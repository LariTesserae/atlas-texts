# The Attention

**1. How it noticed**

Powers of this kind do not sweep. Nothing is searched for, nothing is scanned; the idea of a survey does not arise. What happens is closer to what happens to you in a house you were told was empty, when you become aware that somewhere above you a person is holding their breath.

It is not the breath you hear. It is the holding.

---

**2. What it did first**

It came in low, because everything here comes in low, and it came in quiet, because there is a rule.

This is worth dwelling on, since it is the whole character of the thing. A power of this order could have entered the basin in any manner it liked and the Draft could not have objected in any grammar available to it. It entered at walking pace, under the ninety-metre ceiling, with its breath showing, and it moved so carefully that in eleven kilometres of street not one drop-carrier stopped walking, not one meniscus trembled, and the wave of nothing never started.

It counted the doors on the way in. Three hundred and twenty-six. It accepted the number without argument, which no visitor has ever done.

---

**3. The inventory of refusals**

Most of what it did was decline, and the declining took longer than the doing.

*It could have finished the Mill.* An afternoon's work, less. It sat with that for some time — the last belt spliced, the last frame trued, the upper two-thirds closing on the lower like a hand — and then it followed the afternoon through to its end, to the moment when the wheel comes to rest and the slapping stops and the basin acquires, for the first time in its existence, a silence with an *edge* on it. It did not finish the Mill.

*It could have lit the place.* This is the gift visitors imagine giving, standing in the blue flicker with their eyes aching. But nothing here is dark by accident. Four hundred grazers are dark-adapted; the moths walk at a metre a minute in the open and have never needed to hide from anything with eyes. Light would not have been illumination. It would have been exposure. And in any case light was not what was missing, and the power was not in the business of giving people things that were not what was missing.

*It could have invented erosion.* One small law, inserted gently, and within a decade the kerbs would soften, the corners would round, the eleven thousand treads would begin at last to hollow where feet had never fallen. Dust would arrive. The place would become *weathered*, which is the word we use when we mean *loved by time*.

It considered this longer than anything else and refused it hardest. It is not a kindness to hand a young thing a past it did not live. And erosion does not stop when it has made a place look cherished. It goes on to fragments, and then to rubble, and then to ruins, and the one true thing about the Draft — the thing to hold onto — is that nothing here has fallen.

*It could have killed the Listener.* It was the only violence on offer and it would have worked. It declined for two reasons and only one of them was mercy. The other was this: the Listener is a resident. A benevolence that selects its beneficiaries is not benevolence; it is preference, wearing a good coat.

*It could have filled the slot.* Two metres and a little under. Hands at rail height. It could have drunk the cisterns every night and read the signage and found the stairs comfortable and been, for as long as it cared to be, the inhabitant that the whole basin is shaped around the absence of — the answer to the only question the householder has ever asked.

It declined. The vacancy is not a wound. The vacancy is the working end of the instrument. Fill it and the inference stops, and when the inference stops the building stops, and the town is finished, and see above regarding edges and silence.

*It could have explained.* It could have told the householder why stairs are concave. It worked out, fairly quickly, that this would have been for its own comfort and not the plant's, and that a thing with no centre has nowhere to put an explanation, and that the plant was already doing the correct thing for no reason, permanently, which is a condition most of us only manage in flashes.

---

**4. The moths**

What it did instead, first, was menial.

It walked the Draft ahead of its own feet. Where there was a moth on the line of its stride it stopped, crouched, lifted the moth — they weigh nothing; they are a quilt and a wish — set it down out of the way, and went on. A metre a minute, sometimes less. It did this for the full perimeter and the Mill approaches and the east galleries and both of the long sloping streets that lead nowhere, and it took a great while, and nothing about it was dignified.

At the end of it the Draft contained exactly as many dinner-plate blanks as it had contained before.

This had never happened. Every visitor in the recorded impression of the place is legible as a trail of undecided floor, and the householder had by now inferred, quite reasonably, from every instance available to it, that a visitor is a thing that damages. Within a season there were amendments out past the Mill — kerbs with a slight gather to them, a kind of shoulder, the way you might set a step aside for something to pass. It is not clear what they are for. They are what a place grows when it has just learned that being visited and being harmed are two different events.

---

**5. The reading**

Then it went to the flooded lacuna where the grazers cannot reach, and it stayed eleven wet hours, and it read the films.

The grazers were not interrupted. They were hungry and they were residents and they have as much right to the wall as anything written on it. The power simply read faster than they rasped, and where it could not, it read the film in the cycle before the film grew, which is available here, and which visitors are always told cannot be used. It cannot be used. It can be accepted.

It recovered the name that nobody recognises. It recovered *again*, and *again*, eleven times and then nineteen more, laid out in strokes with the second line indented, in the alphabet the town has not yet chosen. It recovered the number that would be a date.

And in the ninth wet hour, in the dark, up near the water where the film was thickest, it read the end of the sentence.

---

**6. The errand**

*Tell them* is an imperative, and an imperative requires somebody to be spoken to, and for a very long time — though *before* is thin here, and nobody can say how long a very long time is — no one had come down into the basin who was willing to be that somebody.

The power accepted the errand. This is most of what such powers actually are. Not fire, not remedy: someone who takes the message.

It went up and out and across, and where it comes from dates work as dates, and it found the person the name belonged to. A room with a window. Late in the afternoon. A woman of sixty-one with a bad right shoulder, who had gone down into the Draft once in the cold year with a lamp and a theodolite and a list of things she intended to be reasonable about, and who had come out again with nothing except the certainty that she had been inconsiderate somewhere and could not say where.

The message is not written here. It was not a warning and it was not an apology. It was the sort of thing people say when they have found something out and cannot bear for the finding-out to be wasted.

She put her hand over her mouth. It took about nine seconds. Then she went and stood at the window for a while, and later that evening she wrote a letter to someone she had not written to in years, and that, as far as anything in this account can be verified, is the total consequence of the message.

It was enough. It had been addressed to her. It reached her. Nothing that happens in this document is larger than that.

---

**7. The Listener**

Then it went down into the void spaces, and it did the dangerous thing.

Understand the problem first, because the power did. The Listener is not malice. It is the grey batter, standing up, in the only configuration the batter has ever held, and it has been starving since before memory here was under construction. In a world where nothing concludes — where the kerb leads nowhere, the Mill is two-thirds built, the script will not choose its letters, the feldspar loses its nerve at the eighth minute — a sound is the only finished object that ever occurs. It begins, it happens, it stops. It is whole. It cannot be revised. And the Listener has always believed, because everything in its experience confirms it, that an ending is singular: that each one is used up in the having of it, and that this is why it must always go and find another, and why it must take the most finished thing that ever walks into the basin, and why it must take it gently, so as not to waste the drop.

The power stood in the dark of a hundred-metre gallery, where sound travels perfectly, and said its own name aloud, once.

Everything in the district stopped. Four hundred rasps went still under the drip. Every beetle in line of sight froze with its meniscus quivering and the wave of nothing went out through the streets to the last lamp and past it into the grey.

The name began, and happened, and ended. It was perfect. It was complete in a way that nothing in that basin had ever been complete, and it was immediately gone, and the Listener came up through the busbars in a smell of hot copper to take what was left of it, and there was nothing left of it, because that is what endings are.

And then the power said it again.

That is the whole of the teaching, and it took a long time to land, and the power stayed for all of it. Again. And again. The same ending, arriving whole, finishing, vanishing, returning. Not a new one hunted down in some far district at the cost of a gallery or a belt or a surveyor. The same one. Repeatable. Inexhaustible. An ending that can be had without being spent.

When the Listener had it — and there is no way to describe the moment a thing that has only ever eaten understands that it might instead *attend* — the power took it, by whatever it has instead of a hand, out into the streets in the forty minutes of the fall.

*Tock.* A drop the size of a grape lands on a kerbstone and the sound begins and happens and concludes, and is whole, and is over, and nine hours later there will be ten thousand more.

It had been stopping the drip in the districts where it hunted. It had been silencing its own table for the whole of its existence in order to hear better.

No visitor has been taken since. This proves nothing. Visitors come twice a generation and *before* is thin. It is recorded here as it stands.

---

**8. The householder**

Last, and briefly, it did the ordinary things, because the householder is an inference engine with a vacancy in it and the only nourishment it has ever had is information.

It went up the great stair by the market hall, all forty-one treads, and the treads did not wear, and this was correct, and the power did not mind. It drank from a corner cistern to a hand's depth, in full view of nothing. It sat for an hour in the market hall — which is high, and echoing, and has a floor you could sweep — and did not trade, because there was no one to trade with, and rested instead, which was the only honest use available.

And on its way out it went in through one of the three hundred and twenty-six doors, into a room with no purpose and no wear, and it closed the door behind it.

The doors here all stand open. They always have. A door that stands open is a hole with a frame on it; the specification had the frame and the lintel and the concavity of the sill, and it did not have the *shut*, because the shut is not a shape, it is a thing an inhabitant wants.

Within a season there were latches out past the Mill. Then a room, in the east galleries, that is interior — the first interior in the Draft, a space that is inside of something rather than merely surrounded. And then, in the market hall, at a height that is not for standing and not for working, a long grown bench.

The door count went up by one more than the new construction accounts for. The householder has amended its estimate of how many will be needed. It is now expecting a guest.

---

**9. What it left**

Nothing that wears. No dust, no fragment, no history, not one blank in the floor. The Mill is still two-thirds finished and will be. The far streets still run out into the average of a floor. The moths are still too few, and always will be, and the grazers still sit against the wall with their rasps idle for eight hours, waiting for the cover of the drip.

It left one thing and it could not take it back. Somewhere under the streets, in the dark, in the void spaces, a thing made of wet grey batter says a name over and over to itself. Each time, the name ends. Each time, it is whole again. It is the only object in the basin that is finished and is not gone, and it is being had, and it is not being used up, and that is the nearest thing to satisfaction that has ever occurred here.

---

**10. Nobody lives here**

That is still true, in the sense that a sentence with no subject is true.

Somebody came. It obeyed the local law. It carried the moths out of its own path. It read to the end of a sentence that had been rasped off eleven times, and it took the message, and it delivered it to a woman at a window who had not known she was owed one. It fed the starving thing without killing it and without lying to it. It sat down in the market hall. It shut a door.

And then it went out quietly and counted the doors and accepted the different number, and left the town unfinished, which was the whole of the kindness and cost more than anything else it did.

Under the market hall floor, in the protolith, a feldspar began the other day: six-sided, hopeful, assembling itself out of the batter. It held for eleven minutes. Then it lost its nerve and went back to slurry.

Eleven. It has never once got past eight.

It is not in a hurry. It has been told, by something that ended and came back, that it does not have to be.
