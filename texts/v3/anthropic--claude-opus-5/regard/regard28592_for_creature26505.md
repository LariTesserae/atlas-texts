# The Standing: What Came to It

Come at this the way you came at the range, and you will make the same order of mistake.

You will look up. It is the natural place to look for a power in a world where the dead go up, and you will spend your first days scanning the mouths of the stalks for a light, and listening in the chord for a voice pitched above it, and asking after temples. There are no temples. There is nothing on any wall that was not put there by hands with hooked fingers, and no one here has built anything they did not intend to untie and lower within three years.

What you are looking for is not in the sky. It is in a courtesy.

Before you move on a wall above another person, you strike the stem twice, low and then lower: *there is someone above you; expect falling.* You do it for strangers. You do it for people you cannot see and only suspect. It is the first thing taught to children and the last politeness of the dying, who tap it at the ceiling of the world on their way out.

That tap is the people's name for what came. It is not a word, cannot be said with the voice, and this suits everybody.

### What drew it

Not the suffering. There are places with more suffering in them than the Standing that have gone unattended for whole ages, and a power that goes where the suffering is worst will spend eternity as a bailer in a sinking hull and do no lasting good anywhere.

What drew it was the joke.

Thirty years into a world their bodies were not made for, in a country with no metal and no stone and no level ground, where the flexor tendons of every child born on the wall set into a shallow permanent hook so that no adult of the Standing can lay a palm flat on anything — they had already made a gesture out of it.
