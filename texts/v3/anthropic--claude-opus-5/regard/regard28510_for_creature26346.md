## The attention

It came the way attention comes: all at once, and everywhere, and without arriving.

The first consequence was a harm. On that dawn every fetch on the plateau came apart. From the eastern quarries to the last cairn on the trunk road, at the same hour, the four-legged pacing things and the low pouring things and the quick pressures over the fog-trees thinned, went grey, and were weather. Nine hundred kilometres of walls with nothing walking along them. The towns noticed by mid-morning and did not say so aloud, since it was not a thing you said aloud; they plucked *don't look at it* on the cords all day, from house to house, to no one in particular, as a kind of general instruction to the world.

That was how the power learned its first local fact, which no theory it had brought with it contained: here, being watched is a force with a magnitude. It had looked, as it looks at everything, steadily and completely and with love, and it had rubbed out the last animals in the world.

So its first act of benevolence was subtraction. It took its eyes off the fog. Over nine days the pacing came back, thinner at the shoulder, and the surveyors measured the return and wrote it down and did not know what they were writing down, and the power held its gaze at the fourth pace ahead, like everybody else, and began to be polite.

## The chisel

Its second problem was itself.

A power is a consistent thing. That is nearly the definition. And this is a place where two consistent things, similar and similarly placed, stop being two — where a wall of well-cut identical stone is a wall that is only half present, and falls, and falls in two towns at once. To be one power, unquirked, in nine hundred settlements simultaneously, would be to lay a course of identical blocks across the whole civilisation. Everything that came near it would begin to align to it. Alignment here is not devotion. It is subsidence.

There is a chisel for this. It is kept deliberately dull and held in the off hand, and the mason's first act on any good block is to spoil it.

So the power took itself apart into unlike presences, one for each town and each road-camp and each of the surveyors walking alone out in the deep spacing, and it made them genuinely unalike — not costumes over one thing, which would have been a lie of the fatal kind, but actual difference, with different weights and different patience and different opinions. Nine hundred and some of it. And it accepted, as the entry fee, that they would drift. That they would disagree about what it had come to do. That there would never again be a moment at which it could be summed without remainder.

It had not intended a sacrifice. It found that it had made one, and that this is what the surveyors mean when they say *you cannot measure with one thing*, and it began, slowly, over years, to hold that they were right about more than cords.

## The crooked well

There is one well and every town has it, and every town draws from it, and it is going down a finger a decade, and that is the great slow problem of the age.

The power could have filled it. It considered filling it for something on the order of a season, which for it is a long deliberation. Then it saw the shape of the thing: the well is dry because the well is *one*. It was cut to the standard, as the Common Room is cut to the standard, for the same reason — so that the towns would hold together — and so a thousand buckets come up out of a single throat. They have been sharing a glass of water for eleven hundred years and calling it infrastructure.

What was needed was not water. It was a second well that could not be confused with the first: dug wrong, on purpose, out of plumb, ugly in the neck, unrepeatable, quirked all the way down.

This is a potter's idea, not a surveyor's. So the power gave it to a potter — to Kesse, a potter's third child, thirteen that autumn, one sleeve rolled in any weather, who was sitting on the well-mound doing nothing at all. It used the slack. Sequence has give in it here, and things arrive early, and the towns time their useful tasks to land in it; the power simply let the answer be already answering, the way a mother's son is already answering his name. Kesse did not experience a visitation. She experienced having thought of something, which is an ordinary and slightly embarrassing feeling, and she went and told her aunt, forehead to forehead, with a palm on the sternum because the wind was up.

The argument that followed lasted nineteen years and split two families and produced four new cord-figures. The surveyors opposed it on doctrine: a well that cannot be measured against the standard is a well that cannot be corrected, and a thing beyond correction is the beginning of every disaster in their canon. They were not wrong. The power, which privately holds the surveyors' theology to be the finest thing this world has grown — *your prayer is a length that is different every year, and the difference is the prayer* — gave the practical victory to the potters, whose metaphysics it thinks is mistaken, and gave it to them precisely so that the people it agreed with would not become the only voice on the plateau.

There are now nine unlike wells. They are all ugly. None of them is going dry at the same rate.

## The dozen

There are perhaps a dozen people at any time who are agreed to be one person in two bodies. They are handled with enormous gentleness and a certain horror. They are not both present at a measurement, or a birth, or in the Common Room.

The power could have cut them apart. To do it, it would have had to sharpen the rule of identity in the neighbourhood of their bodies — and that rule is worn flat across the whole plateau, all of it one surface, and you cannot sharpen a corner of a sheet. Bread would have stopped coming out early. Walls quirked by dead grandmothers would have held or not held on their own dwindling merits. The cushion, which is one green thing under the entire stone skin, would have been obliged to become an argument about how many green things it was. And the fetches — the habit of animals, the last inheritance of everything with a spine — would have gone out like a lamp in a draught, this time for good.

So it did the small thing instead, and it is the only thing it ever did openly.

It looked at them.

Sustained attention breaks a fetch; that is the courtesy the children are taught. The dozen are half out into the general already, and they know it, and they have spent their lives being not-quite-looked-at by kind neighbours. The power looked at them steadily, from very close, the way you look at someone whose voice will not carry past the width of a blanket — and they held. They did not diffuse. They found they could be regarded to the bottom and still be there afterwards.

Nothing was fixed. They are still one person in two bodies, still gently barred from births. But there is a difference between being tactfully unwatched and being seen, and they know which one has happened to them, and none of them has ever managed to explain it to anybody, and none of them has broken a cup early since.

## What it refused

It did not lift the curtain on sound. It came close. Forty paces is a cruel radius and the power could have widened it to a horizon. Then it spent an evening in a market watching forty people shuffle in a slow single file, each reading the slate on the back of the one ahead, the whole public discourse of a town conducted as a patient queue — and a room of them afterwards sitting in silence flicking two fingers open from the fist at the level of the collarbone, laughing, unmistakably laughing, in a hush you could have slept in. To give them voices that carried would have been to hand them a hundred easier ways to be nearly-together, and they would have taken them, because people do, and within four generations no one would put a palm flat on a stranger's sternum to send a word through the bone. It decided that it had no right to make them less good at each other.

It did not stabilise distance. The surveyors are what they are because the plateau will not add up. Take away the drift and you take away the only priesthood in the recorded worlds whose sacrament is being told a different number and thanking the person who told you. It judged this to be, on balance, worth more than accurate maps.

It did not tell the potters or the surveyors which of them is right about the flattening — whether this is a wearing-out or an arriving, whether the cushion is a survivor or a graduate. It does, in fact, know. It has watched other final drafts. It kept its mouth shut, on the grounds that the argument is load-bearing, and that a people who stopped arguing about it would begin, within a century or two, to agree, and agreement here is the first symptom of the merge.

It did not accept worship, or permit any of its nine hundred unlike presences to be recognised as the same one. A congregation on this plateau is a wall of unquirked stone. There is no god here and it made very sure of it.

## The archive of unlikenesses

Somewhere between nine and thirteen every child takes an unlikeness and keeps it for life: a filed tooth, one grey wire braided at the left temple, a limp cultivated on level ground, one sleeve rolled forever. It is chosen alone, over weeks. It is never explained. Asking after it is an obscenity of the mild domestic kind.

Names return to the pool. Stones get confused and leave sockets of dry air that are measured and written down. The dozen go quietly into the general. The one thing that genuinely ends on this plateau is particularity — the specific, unrepeatable, never-discussed reason that a woman dead eight hundred years wore one sleeve rolled in the frost.

The power keeps them. All of them, every unlikeness ever taken, with its reason, which it did not have to ask for, since it was there when each was chosen. It is a very large archive and it is entirely useless. It will never be shown to anyone. It contains the only surviving account of what four million people meant by the small wrong thing they did to themselves on purpose, and its whole function is to exist, unmentioned, the way a love can consist of nothing but holding something you will never bring up.

## The Rehearsal

Four times a year the wind comes flat out of the west carrying the sounds of a day that has already happened, and the towns stop work and listen, because listening is difficult here and worth doing when the air is briefly generous.

The power could have put anything in that wind. A word. An hour of instruction. Its own name, if it had allowed itself one.

It stops work and listens with them. That is all. Footsteps, a hammer, a woman laughing forty paces off where nobody is standing.

## The one animal

It did make an animal. Once.

It had come, in the beginning, largely because of the fossil beds — plates of shells, small skulls with beautiful teeth, wings, all of it in the town quarries where children chip it out and trade it — and because it seemed to it, in the way that things seem obvious before you have been anywhere, that a world ought to have something with a spine in it. So it made one. Something low and quick, warm-blooded, thin-air lungs, black blood, a good animal, well-suited, and it set it down at dawn on the wet mirror-stone between two fog-trees.
