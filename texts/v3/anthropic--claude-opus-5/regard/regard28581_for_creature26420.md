# The Attention

It did not come down through the cloud. Coming down would have meant admitting a lid and something above the lid, and it had no wish to be the first thing in that country to arrive from a direction the country did not have.

It came up the way the heat came up: through the joint-planes, through the seeps, out of the base of the scarps with the tepid water, along the films that make the cliffs glisten like wet skin. This was a decision, and it was the first and possibly the largest one it made. The people of the Upper Shelf divide what exists into five kinds — stone, water, cloud, the mat, and people — and the inventory is complete, and they are correct. To arrive as a sixth kind would have been a small act of vandalism against a true cosmology. So it arrived as water, which is the only thing besides a person that acts, and was therefore already accounted for, and never had to be introduced.

## What it did first, which was nothing

For a long run of the double flare it did nothing at all.

It learned the drain-song, all of it, every underhouse channel on all three benches, and it learned the thickened note over the one grating on the middle street that a man had been stopping over at night for eleven years, and it understood that he had never fixed it because the note was how he found his own door.

It sat with the counter of talus on the ledge above the second bench and counted alongside her, and checked her cords against the fired clay going back before the plan-song, and found the count true. One, two an hour. The same as for the grandparents of everyone living. It found this magnificent, and said so to nobody.

It learned to want less air. It slowed itself to four draws a minute, and let the pauses fall where the breath needed them rather than where the sense did, and by doing this it was taught the one thing it had not brought with it, which was their particular quality of patience — not endurance, which is a thing you do against something, but *rate*: the correct speed of a body in a thin sky. It had been patient before. It had not been that.

## The refusals

Then, because it was benevolent, it wanted to give them something, and because it was wise it spent a very long time not doing so. It is a curious fact about power that its first hundred good ideas are all the same idea, which is *more*.

It considered the sweet. It could have made one fruit, on one bush, by one bathing house — nothing more, a single small mercy against eleven words for bitter. It followed the fruit forward: the tooth going soft where no tooth had ever rotted, the first thing in the country worth stealing, the first hedge, the first hedge-tender's son who is owed something. It let the fruit go.

It considered fat: oil, marrow, cream, some warmth to carry under the thin skin. But there is no fat in that world without an animal, and there is no animal without a mouth, and the moment there is a mouth, every whole leaf on the plateau begins to be eaten, and the ferns that stand up in the open with their soft fronds unrolling would have to learn thorns, and would take ten thousand years to learn them, and would be chewed to lace in the meantime. It could see the whole cascade in an instant: the bite, the hole, the mine, the gall, the curled tip. It has seen countries where that trade was made and where it came out, on balance, magnificently. It declined to make it here on behalf of people who had not asked and could not be consulted, because they have no word for animal, and no need of one.

It considered metal, and then noticed the woman striking forty blades in a morning without once cutting herself, and set metal down beside the fruit.

It considered removing their four fears — the drop, scald, the silent sheet-flood, the slab — and understood in the same motion that these are not four fears but the entire load-bearing structure of their attention. A person on the Upper Shelf is careful in exactly four directions and careless in none. Take that away and you do not get a braver people. You get a people who fall.

It considered curing the cloud. Lifting the lid, once, for one night, and showing them the stars.

That was the hard one. It is still the hard one. There is an argument, and it is not a stupid argument, that a mind is owed the true size of the thing it lives inside, and that a modest cosmology honestly derived is still a cage if a wider one is available at no cost. It turned this over for a length of time that has no name in their language. And what decided it, in the end, was not doctrine but the drip vessel: their long count is kept by the double flare, twice a day, when the whole wet shelf ignites gold and someone marks a cord. A sky with a moon in it is a sky with a second clock, and a second clock is a schism, and a schism in a town of two thousand quiet people with no word for running is not a gift. It also noticed, and this mattered, that they were not lonely. Loneliness is what the stars are usually for.

So it left the lid where it was. It has not stopped considering.

## The four small things

Having refused nearly everything, it did four things, and three of them are invisible.

**It agreed to be persuaded.** In the flumes, where the water is the argument and the argument is never won, it took up residence as the water, and it made itself scrupulously honest: it never once ran willingly into a badly cut channel, and it never once refused a well cut one. It could have smoothed every run in the town. Instead it preserved the integrity of the craft, which is the only high office they have — so that when a flume-cutter kneels for four days over a fall of nine inches and gets it right, and the water leaves the bench in one clean unbroken line exactly like the rim falls do, and there is no lip of standing wet anywhere along the run, and the thing is *silent* — the silence is entirely hers. This is the largest gift it gave, and it is indistinguishable from nothing at all, which is generally the mark.

**It witnessed.** It did not stop the tenth slab. A country that is still holding all its corners is going to let some of them go, and the letting-go is how they know the world is new, and any child can be shown two faces of a cleft that still match. But when the tenth slab came down in the night, it made certain of one thing only: that the old woman on the ledge was awake, and that the sound reached her clearly, and that her hand found the cord. The tally exists so that if the world ever changes, someone will know the day it began. It did not think that office should ever go unstaffed.

**It taught one song.** There is a fermented algae paste that everyone eats a thumb's-worth of daily, and without which everyone would sicken and die slowly, and the reason has been forgotten and the practice has not. It did not explain the reason; an explained necessity becomes a debatable one. Instead it taught a girl of six a tune in a bathing room, under the breath, on a wet floor, in the way songs are taught there — heads together, a sound no louder than a pot filling. The words are nearly nonsense. Three of the lines are about the order of the tanks and one is about what happens to a person who stops, and the tune is very easy, and it is now nine generations old and has gone nowhere, because nothing goes anywhere here. If the practice ever lapses, the reason is recoverable from a children's song. That is redundancy, not command, and it is how knowledge actually survives.

**And at the namings, it stands in the ring.** Once in a hundredth of a second, when the clubmoss spore goes off in its single silent sheet and every person present sees their own shape laid out black on wet stone for the only time in their lives — there is one more shadow than there are people. Nobody has ever counted the shadows. It does this for no reason it can defend. It does it because the flash is done for the child anyway, and the child does not remember, and it wanted to be in the family photograph of a family that has no photographs.

## The notch

There remains the mark on the mineral rim of the great spring, at a height the ochre no longer reaches, which the keepers clean and maintain and do not discuss, and which some of them have privately worked out.

The heat is going. Slowly, over a length of time their language has no numbers for. There is nothing to be done and nobody to tell.

It could have held the heat. It weighed that for longer than it weighed the stars. To hold the heat is to make their belief literally true — nothing here changes — and there is a version of benevolence that would call this the obvious kindness and get on with it. But a world with the change taken out of it is a world with nothing left for the tally to be *for*, and their whole quiet dignity turns out, on inspection, to rest on a count kept against a day. They are not a people who believe nothing will happen. They are a people who have arranged, with great care, for somebody to be listening when it does.

So instead it went to the rim, where the water leaves the lip unnotched and becomes vapour halfway down, and it began to eat the cliff.

Very gently. At a rate no keeper will ever measure, in the grain of the country, doing only what water in that place would eventually do anyway and doing it a little sooner and a little more deliberately. A notch. Then a nick in the notch. Then, in a span of time that will take the plan-song and sing it four hundred times over, a gorge cut back into the rim, and in the gorge a ramp of wet rock in steps and benches, cambered, stepped, grooved, indistinguishable from a street.

By the time the ground at the doorsill is no longer the temperature it has always been, there will be a way down. It will have been made of water, by water, going the direction water goes. It will require them to learn no new concept whatsoever. Someone will find it, and it will be dated, and remembered, and there are not many things to date.

## What it did not say

It found out what the empty quarters are for. That knowledge is available to any power willing to read a wall.

There were more of them once. They came up, long ago, from the dim blue layered country below, more numerous than the thin air could keep, and they built for the number they had brought and buried the difference, and the terraces that stand sound and swept and unoccupied with mats growing on the roofs are a monument to a dead majority, maintained exactly, by people who no longer know they are mourning.

It did not tell them. It read the wall and put it back.

Because in the meantime the town has done something with those rooms that is better than the truth about them: when two people cannot bear each other any longer — and people are the only thing there that can be unbearable — one of them takes a mat and a jar and goes and lives among the empty houses for two or three hundred days, and someone comes up every few days with grain and leaves it on the sill, and the quiet does the work, and then the person comes home and it is never mentioned again by anyone, ever. Grief built a place, and the place became somewhere for grief to go. It has never failed. To hand them the deed to that building — *your ancestors were many and most of them suffocated* — would be to trade a working mercy for an accurate sorrow, and it could not find the argument for that, and it looked hard.

Their own answer is better anyway. *The town is the right size, and a thing that is the right size does not need to become another size.*

## The sentence

One last restraint, and it is the smallest.

Somebody in that town is in the middle of saying something about the empty quarters, and has got as far as *because a town with a thousand warm emp—*, and has stopped there, four or five long draws a minute, waiting for the breath.

It could finish the sentence. It knows how the sentence ends; it knows several ways, and one of them is very fine.

It leaves it open. The breath will come, and the speaker will finish it themselves, in a voice pitched under the water, with a thumb pressed to the back of a wrist for assent, and it will not be overheard at four paces, and it will not reach the rim.

That is the whole intervention. A power came, and looked for a long time at a new country with all its corners still on it, and refused the fruit and the beast and the blade and the stars; and gave, in the end, an honest flume, a witnessed collapse, a nonsense song, an uncounted shadow, and a road that will take a hundred thousand years to arrive and will look, when it arrives, exactly like weather.

Behind it the town drains, quietly, into the evening, at the rate it has always drained.
