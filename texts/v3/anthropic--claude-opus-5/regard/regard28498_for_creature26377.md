# The Attending

They come to the mountain the way weather comes to a plain — without announcement, without an edge you could point to and say *here it begins*.

They are not one and not many. Call them a Regard: an attention with a shape, a wisdom that has learned over long practice to arrive somewhere without being the most important thing in the room. They have come because something on this world was doing an unlikely thing, and unlikely things are worth the trip.

They do not stoop at the doorway. They do not need to. They stand outside it anyway, on the grey scree, for what would be, to the Congregation, seven complete thoughts.

---

## What they notice first

Not the heat. Heat is common. Not the darkness; darkness is the ordinary condition of most volumes in most universes.

What they notice is the doorway.

Three courses of dressed basalt. A lintel. A shallow spiral, nearly gone. Five strokes.

*Somebody else was here,* the Regard observes, and this is the first thing they turn over, because it changes what kind of place this is. A cave with an animal in it is a fact. A cave with an animal in it and a door someone built and eleven niches someone cut and eight bowls someone filled — that is a *relationship*, and relationships are the only things in the cosmos that can be gotten wrong.

They read the doorway the way one reads a letter found in a house one has just bought.

The pitch of it, forward, bowing. Deliberate. Not structural — the slabs would have held plumb. Someone tilted the door on purpose so that a person entering would be made to lower their head, and then packed the joints with cement gathered from the feet of the animals inside, which means someone spent a long while in there, patiently, collecting a residue a few micrograms at a time, in the dark, in an oven, in order to build a door out of the place itself.

The spiral: a curve that goes inward and does not close. The Regard notes that this is what the chamber's tide does, twice a day, forever. It notes that a person who had watched the tide for years and wanted to write down what they had seen might well draw exactly that, and nothing else, because nothing else would be true.

The five strokes they do not interpret. They hold them. There are eleven niches and eight bowls and five strokes and none of these numbers explain each other, and the Regard is old enough to know that the desire to make them explain each other is a hunger in the observer and not a property of the marks.

---

## The temptation, named and set down

There is a thing the Regard could do, and it takes them the length of a full day at the door to finish not doing it.

The Kiln is dying. This is not ambiguous. The heat sinks away below and the archive rises from beneath, and somewhere between four and forty thousand cycles from now the floor of remembered postures will meet the last warm inch of the fissure, and the Congregation will set — the whole nave of it, mid-climb, hooks locked, four hundred thousand cycles of unbroken correct answers ending in a room full of statues.

The Regard could open the fissure. It would cost them nothing. A small structural persuasion nine hundred metres down, and the mountain's remaining warmth would come up faster, and the Kiln would have a hundred thousand cycles instead of ten.

They consider it properly. Not as a formality — properly, the way a surgeon considers an operation they have decided not to perform, so that the decision has a floor under it.

Here is what they find.

The Congregation does not experience its dying. There is no one in there to be afraid. Fear, as the room itself demonstrates, is a thing done in advance, and the Congregation has no in-advance; it has half a day, and within that half-day it is never wrong. To open the fissure would not relieve a suffering. There is no suffering. There is a magnificent slow accuracy that will one day stop being able to be accurate, and stopping is not the same as agony, however much it looks like it from outside.

And: the rising floor is not a disease. It is the memory. It is *the same process* as the thinking. The Congregation records where the good breathing was by setting bodies into stone, and it steers by walking on that record, and the record is what will kill it. You cannot subtract the burial without subtracting the remembering. There is no version of this creature that thinks and does not entomb itself. The Regard turns this over for a long time and finds it does not have the right to edit a mind's relationship with its own past merely because the arithmetic of it is grim.

And: it is a hundred metres of arrangement that has been correct four hundred thousand times running. Whatever the Regard did to the heat below, the Kiln would answer with a posture — it always does — but the posture would be an answer to the Regard's question and not the mountain's. A tide climbing to a warmth that a benevolence keeps topping up is no longer a solution to anything. The Congregation's whole dignity is that it is *solving*. Feed it, and it becomes a kept thing, breathing on charity, its deliberations reduced to the acoustics of a hand held under it. There are beings for whom that trade is worth making. The Regard cannot find, in the four hundred pits under every plate, anyone who could be asked.

So they set the temptation down. They do not congratulate themselves. It was, they think, close.

---

## What they do instead

### First: they hold still.

They enter, and they are very careful about how.

They come in through the crawl at the speed the tide moves, a pace every four seconds, so that the disturbance they represent arrives as a gradient and not as an event. They do not shout. They do not speak at all, in any register the rock could carry. They understand, having read the room for a day, that sound here is a violence — that a single raised voice drops forty thousand animals off the ceiling and costs the Congregation an hour of climbing and a thought cycle it will never get back.

Small ones drift onto them and stay, and more onto those, and within a minute the Regard is wearing the Kiln.

They let this happen. They hold at four hundred and forty degrees — warm enough to be good breathing, not so warm as to be a hunter, not so cool as to set a single hook — and they stand in the nave for eleven days, and they are, for that time, simply a well-placed rock.

This is the first gift and they know it is the largest one available: *to be in the room without being an emergency.*

Millions of animals breathe off them and are not harmed and do not know that anything unusual has occurred, and that is precisely the achievement.

### Second: they learn it.

Eleven days is twenty-two thought cycles. The Regard follows every one.

They watch the tide go down at nightfall and come up at dawn, and they do the twelve-thousand-variable problem alongside it, independently, and check. Twenty-two times out of twenty-two, the Congregation's posture is optimal or within a hair of it. The Regard has seen a great many minds. They note, without sentiment, that this is among the most reliable they have encountered, and that it achieves this with no memory, no self, no fear, no urgency, and no light, and that it has never once been told it is clever.

They read the floor. All three and a half metres of it. Not by digging — they read it the way the living read it, through the feet, letting the shape of the substrate steer them, and then further down by patience. Four hundred thousand cycles of postures. Every layer a snapshot of where the good breathing was. They find, at a depth corresponding to some ninety thousand cycles ago, a stratum where the fissure must have shifted forty paces west, and the layer above shows the whole congregation solving that migration in — as best they can reconstruct — four thought cycles. Two days. No panic. No losses beyond the ordinary sand of the slow.

They go up into the vault.

There are two hundred and eighty-six anchors. The Regard visits each one. Each is a hunter that grew past mobility and cemented itself to the ceiling and has been hanging there, alive, radiating, for tens of thousands of cycles, holding a catenary of forty thousand descendants. The Regard finds this to be the single most interesting fact in the room and possibly on the planet: that the species' only architecture is hung from its own immobilised elders, that the drapes are literally suspended from ancestry, and that not one of the anchors can know it. They spend a long while up there. They touch each anchor once, gently, at a temperature indistinguishable from the vault, which is to say they touch them in a way that cannot be felt, because to be felt would be to be a gradient, and to be a gradient would be to move the drapes.

Two hundred and eighty-six times, they attend to something that cannot be attended to, and they do it anyway, and they consider this neither wasted nor sentimental. It is a form of accuracy.

### Third: the bowls.

Here the Regard changes its mind twice.

The eight bowls of fired clay hold a crust of white salts, mounded, polished by generations of crossing feet. Somebody carried salt up a mountain into an oven, in the dark, and set it out at intervals of a stride, and there were eleven niches and three of the bowls are on the floor below, broken, half-swallowed.

The Regard's first thought is: *this was food.* The chemistry supports it. Nitrates, chlorides — the same eutectic as the blood. Someone worked out what these animals were made of and brought them more of it.

Their second thought, arrived at after examining the polish on the domes, is: *this was not food, and the giver learned that.* The salts are undisturbed. Not one bowl has been drawn down. The Congregation does not eat salt; it eats gradient. The mounds are polished only because feet cross them, indifferently, the way feet cross anything. Eight bowls of an offering that was never once taken, sitting in eleven niches for however many thousand cycles, in a room where the animals cannot see them, cannot want them, cannot know a niche from a nook.

And whoever cut the niches cut *eleven*. They kept going. Three bowls fell and were not replaced, and the giver either died or stopped, and the Regard cannot tell which and declines to guess.

They understand, then, what the bowls are. They are not provisions. They are the record of somebody who came into the hottest useless room on a dead world and tried to give a gift to something that had no way to receive one, and did it eleven times, and never got an answer, and cut a spiral over the door anyway because they had seen something true and wanted it written down.

The Regard does something here that is not efficient.

They do not remove the bowls. They do not repair them or refill them or set the three broken ones back in their niches — the broken ones are in the floor now, they are archive, they are part of the memory and the memory is not the Regard's to revise.

Instead: they take the eight remaining bowls, one at a time, and they warm each one from beneath by two degrees. Two degrees, held.

It is a trivial intervention. It is also, as the Regard has calculated, exactly enough to make each niche a *marginally good place to breathe.* Not good enough to draw the tide. Not good enough to change a posture or bias a deliberation or teach the floor anything false. Just good enough that a body which happens to be crossing a niche will find, for a moment, that the crossing is slightly easier than the rock either side of it.

And so the bowls are used. Finally, after all this time, and by the wrong mechanism entirely, and in a way the giver never imagined and would perhaps not have recognised — but used. Eight small places in a two-hundred-pace room where the breathing is a little better, at a stride's spacing, at the height of a standing person, on the west wall.

The Regard holds this for as long as the mountain has heat. It is not a large thing. It costs them the attention one might spend on remembering to water a plant. They consider it among the more worthwhile things they have done in several thousand years, and they would not be able to explain why to anyone who required the explanation to be short.

### Fourth: the witnessing.

The Kiln will die. The Regard has decided not to prevent this, and having decided, they take on the obligation that decision creates.

They record it. Not sample it — *record* it. Every body, every posture, every one of the twelve thousand variables, every anchor in the vault, the full three and a half metres of floor read layer by layer back to the first grain of fused corpse, four hundred thousand cycles of correct answers, the whole arrangement, complete, at a fidelity from which the Congregation could be reconstituted in every particular if reconstitution were ever the right thing — and the Regard notes carefully, in the same record, that it probably is not, and why, so that whoever finds this later inherits the reasoning and not just the file.

And then they stay for the ending.

Not the whole span — they have other places, and staying continuously would be its own kind of vanity — but they return. They come back at intervals as the fissure dulls, as the archive rises, as the good gradients narrow toward the low end and the coats thin on the upper walls and the sand of stiffened bodies falls a little heavier each night. They are there for the last thousand cycles. They are there when the tide comes up at dawn and finds nothing worth climbing to, and the Congregation solves that, too, correctly, by staying low.

They are there at the end, which is quiet, because everything here is quiet. The sift of dry flour slows. The drapes lengthen and do not reform. The last thought cycle takes longer than half a day, and then longer, and then does not complete.

The Regard does not intervene and does not weep, having no apparatus for either. What they do is simpler and harder: they pay attention to all of it, all the way down, so that the final posture of the Congregation — the entire nave set at the moment of its last warm second, hooks locked, mid-climb — is not a thing that happened unobserved.

Four hundred thousand cycles of getting it right, in the dark, in silence, with no one watching.

Someone was watching. Late, and only for the last part, and unable to help in any way that would not have been an insult. But watching.

### Last: the door.

Before they go, the Regard attends to the doorway, because the doorway was the first strange thing and strangeness deserves finishing.

They do not add to it. There is a mark above the lintel — a spiral, nearly gone, and five strokes weathered to soft grooves — and the Regard considers cutting something of their own beside it and decides that this would be the same error as feeding the fissure: making the record an answer to their question instead of the mountain's.

So they do the other thing. They stabilise what is there. The scree is unsorted and mean and the mountain takes back what is left on it, and the Regard makes the basalt a little more patient than basalt — not permanent, nothing is, but slow. Slow enough that the spiral will still be readable when this world's next unlikely thing gets far enough along to walk up a slope and wonder.

The five strokes they leave uninterpreted, as found. Someone made a mark and did not explain it. That is allowed. That may even be the point.

And the last of it, the smallest thing, done on the way out and mentioned in the record only in a footnote:

Below the lintel, on the inner face, where no eye will ever see it and no foot has reason to cross, the Regard sets a residue of their own. Glassy. A few micrograms. Gathered from the room, the way the builder gathered theirs.

It means, as nearly as it can be put:

*Attended. Not corrected. Two degrees at the eight bowls, and that is all, and it was enough, and I am sorry it was so little, and I do not think more would have been kind.*
