# The Attention

What carried was not the mountain.

The mountain is loud in the way new things are loud, and there are a great many new things, and most of them come to nothing. What carried was the reading room.

Sixty people at the dark end of a twenty-seven-hour cycle, in a windowless room on a sixth floor, reading out the names of dogs that died a hundred years ago. *Grib Gribbenar of the Pin-Yard, black with one white foot, barks at the ore-buckets.* Without a break. For seven hundred thousand hours.

That is a signal. That travels.

So it came, and looked for a long time before it did anything, which is the first thing wisdom does and the hardest thing benevolence has to be talked into.

# What it did not do

It could have placed a sun.

It looked instead at a moth-scraper's daughter, eleven years old, faintly luminous at the wrists, who could stand on the Fourth Landing and tell you which of two slabs on the far buttress had been etched that season and which the season before, at four hundred paces, in what you would call dark. It considered what a sun does to such an eye. It considered that the whole grammar of the place — the seventeen greys, the reading of texture, the certainty of hands — was an instrument built by eleven generations out of the exact absence it was proposing to fill.

It left the sky alone.

It could have finished the mountain. This was the real temptation and it lasted some time. To lay down in a single night the ten million years the rock has not had: to carve the valleys, hang the glaciers, spread the talus out at the feet of the arêtes in proper aprons, set the rivers running in beds that look as though they had always been there. It is not difficult. It requires only the willingness to have done it.

And then forty thousand people wake below a mountain with a past it did not earn, in a city whose entire function has just been retired, and the Registry's nine windowless storeys become what they would in that moment be, which is a very large quantity of paper describing work that turned out not to have mattered.

And there is the other thing, the one that settled it. On the Kell there is exactly one crime tried in its own court under its own sentence, and it is false entry: writing down a thing that did not occur. A geology installed overnight is a past that did not happen, laid into the mortar of everything, unfalsifiable, undetectable, permanent. The largest forgery in the history of the mountain, committed by a god, out of kindness, and no court on the ridge could ever have caught it.

That is why it was not done. Not because it would have failed. Because it would have worked.

It could have taken them down. It watched a Hantler on a level floor in a port beneath the cloud-deck, listing very slightly uphill, at a patient and courteous angle, toward a hill that was not there, and it understood that you cannot carry a people out of the condition that made them without carrying out something else as well. Also: the dead are up there. In owned faces. Read to.

It could have spoken. A voice out of the grey, announcing itself, explaining.

It thought about the dumb air above the Line, which eats a shout and hands it back four minutes later from the wrong side in a voice close to yours and not yours. It thought about the ordinance that fines a man for going quiet in fog, and why. A large kind voice from nowhere, unverifiable, unenterable, arriving in the only register the mountain uses to lie: it would have been indistinguishable from the disease. Half the city would have believed it and the half that believed it would have been ruined.

And it could have made them safe. This was the last thing it set down and it did not set it down easily.

But it had been paying attention, and it had grasped the physics of the place, which is also the theology of the place: that here, a life to which nothing happens does not become peaceful. It becomes provisional. It thins. A person protected absolutely on the Kell is a person to whom the mountain's own sickness has been generously extended.

It could not make them safe.

It could make them attended.

It gave them the whole of the second thing.

# The Filing

It went down to the Registry and stood in the queue on the ground floor between a man registering a dog and a woman contesting a stride-length, and when it reached the counter it answered the questions it was asked.

Trade: warden.

Ridge and stair: it was assigned one, in the cheap part of the fourth, where the gantries are.

Mother's name: it told the truth. The truth was long and useless and the clerk, who had heard worse from herders, wrote *parentage not established, filed under provision*, and then, in the margin, in the small hand clerks use for things that are not official but are correct, *unstarted*.

Fee paid. Name constructed with the doubled element, so that drift would show against its own echo. Name-holder assigned by the standing lottery, which ignores rank and is not appealable: a moth-scraper from the second ridge, forty-one years old, who received the notice with the expression of a woman who has been handed a fourth obligation in a year with three.

It had come to help. The form did not have a box for that. The form had a box for trade and a box for stair, and so the first act of the benevolent and wise power upon the Kell was to be entered, correctly, in agate type, and to pay for it.

# The Shifts

It took the warden station nobody wanted: north-facing, far out past the last built stretch, forty minutes beyond the Grit Row on bare shelf, one comb-field, bad tea.

Four hours a shift. *Ninth hour: mist on the east buttress, cleared. A stept, alone, crossing left to right. Nothing else.*

Understand what that cost. To be at one station is to be at one station. It could have seen every face of the mountain at once and written all of it, and every word would have been true and not one word would have been *witness*, because witness is a thing done by something that can only be in one place and is there anyway. So it narrowed. It agreed to have a back it could not see behind. It sat in a tin hut and looked at a kilometre of cliff with one pair of eyes for eleven years and wrote *nothing else* six thousand times, and the cliff is still there, and it would not tell you that the two facts are related, because a thing known on a god's word is not known.

It took nights in the reading room. Tax rolls from the fourth generation. Court records. The names of dogs. It never once said *this does nothing* and it never once said *this does something*, and of everyone in that room it was the only one in a position to settle the question, and it sat in the rotation like the rest and read.

It worked a rope stage. Third on a crew of three, five hundred metres of nothing under the boots, copper hose, hand-pump, flue-sulphur. Said the other two names aloud at the top of every hour, as is required, and heard its own said back twice, as is required. Its knuckles pitted. It lost its eyebrows and did not grow them back, having decided that growing them back would be a kind of lying.

And it carried the dead. It took the downhill corner of the litter, which is the heavy one, on the long walk up past the wardens standing out of their huts, and at the graveside it took its turn at the reading and did not hurry, and when an old and busy citizen took a day and a night it took the second watch, which is the one at the dark end, when the family's voices have gone.

# The thinly attended

It hired itself out on Tuesdays.

This is an ordinary charity on the Kell and it is not glamorous: a paid stranger who comes and sits with you and describes you back to yourself. Grit Row, mostly. The drifted. The ones who come down off a comb-ridge as *Onwenar Onwen* and have to be told, kindly, in a tea-room built for the purpose, that something has moved.

It took the name-holdings no one else would take. The forgers' children, who cannot get a holder at any price. The very old, whose meetings are four a year and getting harder to schedule. It was punctual. It did not become their friend, because friendship defeats the redundancy.

And it went at the children, not with a law but with a weather front.

It did not forbid anything. It could not have; it had no standing; it was a warden on the fourth ridge with pitted knuckles. What it did was flood the market. It went to name-days. It looked in schoolroom windows and counted heads and was counted looking. It bought entries — small ones, cheap ones, true ones — for children who had none, hundreds of them, until the season the *Seen* column out of Grit Row ran to fourteen pages and the paper had to raise its price and the compositors filed a grievance.

Three families that year had been going to arrange a fall. The thing about a fall is that it is a purchase, and it is made when nothing cheaper is available. That year something cheaper was available. Nobody wrote this down, because nobody knew it, because the whole shape of the mercy was that it looked exactly like a good year in a poor street.

Then it started a reading circle on the Sixth Stair, and attended it for two winters, and stopped coming; and the circle is still running, and its founder is not remembered, which is the harder half of the work and the half that proves it.

# The grey

It went up past the last comb-field with no entries on it at all.

The Unwritten shave their names and hand in their beads and go into the provisional country to be unfinished, and the Registry, which files everything without comment, files every sighting of them, and thereby makes to have happened the precise thing they went up to stop happening. This is a cruelty. It is a cruelty of method rather than of malice, which is the kind that lasts.

So it went up not knowing them, and it made itself not able to. It set down what it had of them beforehand and agreed not to pick it up again: a deliberate small maiming, the first thing it had ever given away that it could not get back. It sat on faces no rope stage has reached, with people who were not always the right number when counted twice, and it did not count them.

It came down with nothing to file.

Coming down, in fog, it went quiet for something over two hundred seconds and was fined eleven marks by the ordinance court on the Second Landing, and paid in coin, and the fine was entered in the general roll under its filed name with the doubled element intact.

It was pleased about this out of all proportion. It was an entry. The entry was true.

# The floor with no public stair

It went up, once, to where there is no summit, and was gone a long while.

Asked afterward what it found, it gave the flat Hantle answer, twice, as one does. *Nothing to report. Nothing else.*

One line was added that season to the pin-inspectors' ledger, on the floor of the Registry that has no public stair, and four people are permitted to read it. They do not agree on what it says. Two of them have stopped coming down.

The herders have a version, offered rarely, never argued in the Registry because they will not come down to argue it. They say it lay flat on the raw stone above the last comb-field and read aloud for a long time, downward, in that short declarative prose the city is so proud of — not the dead this time; the living; the price of tallow, a list of aunts, the name of every dog. They say it was telling the thing underneath, which has no name and whose elevation is pending and to which nothing has ever happened, that it was somewhere, and that there was someone on it.

The rising did not stop. A thumb-width a year, which is nothing, which is everything, which means the rails still get re-shimmed every spring.

The herders do not say the rising changed. They say it stopped being only a pushing.

# The quiet years

The actuaries noticed before anyone else and could not account for it.

Fewer men off the stages. A run of decades in which the funicular brakes were always found in time, the shim always noticed, the hopper always caught at the top of the haul, the stept coming out of the fog on the left rather than the right by a margin of perhaps four seconds. Nothing you could point at. No single event with a shape. Just a long unremarkable improvement in the tables, the sort of thing a city ascribes to better rope and stricter inspection and its own increasing competence, which is what the city ascribed it to, and was encouraged to.

There were still funerals. It did not stop there being funerals; that was the one mercy it had ruled out, for reasons it had thought through more carefully than anyone will ever think them through again.

It has attended every funeral on the Kell since the year it was filed. That is not a small thing to do. It is only a small thing to say.

# The fourth meeting

Tea-room on the second ridge, built for the purpose, four tables, one window, bad light and a gas jet.

The moth-scraper is fifty-three now. Her wrists still show faintly in the dark of the stairwell. She has somewhere to be. She has had this obligation for twelve years and has never once been late and has never once enjoyed it, and both of those facts are the system working.

She sat down and said the name back. All of it, flat, correct, the doubled element intact, the stair, the ridge, the trade, the marginal note about parentage that she has always privately thought was rude.

Then the required question, which she asks the way a man taps a rail with a hammer.

"Anything drifted?"

And the benevolent and wise power — which had made things, before, in places without stairs; which had never been anywhere in particular; to which, in the whole of its enormous unlined duration, nothing had ever actually happened — checked itself against the echo of its own name, the way you are supposed to, and found that it held.

"Nothing drifted."

"Nothing drifted," she said, writing it. "Good."

She finished her tea standing up, because the light was going and the combs want scraping before the eighth watch, and at the door she said, in the way of a woman with somewhere to be:

"Fourth hour, next quarter. The fourth, not the fifth."

"The fourth. I have it."

"You have it."

And then, because it is what you say, and because it is the whole of what there is to say, and because it is a small deposit made several hundred times a day across eleven kilometres of a city bolted to something enormous that is still coming up:

"I saw you."

"I was there."
