## A Stain That Shines

It did not come down out of the greyness. That was the first wise thing.

Anything that came from above would have arrived as an argument about the roof, and this is not a country that argues about roofs; roofing is always the thing that gets deferred. So it came the way everyone comes: out of the dark past the rim wall, in ones, never in numbers, with a mallet already in hand and its palms already faintly grooved. It did not know its name. It got headaches for a month.

Somebody stamped four beats at it and after a while it answered to them, and that was that; it was of the current course. The word they used for it was the affectionate one, borrowed from the wickmoss. *The stain that shines.* It never asked for a better one, and it had a better one, and it left the better one outside the wall.

---

**What it looked at first**

Not the shaft. Not the tile. Not the three hundred and forty courses or the warmth at the bottom of them, which is what any visitor looks at, the way visitors cannot leave the childlessness alone.

It looked at a woman's hands.

She gave them over the way you'd hand someone a tally-cord — flat, palms up, unhurried, a little proud — and it looked for a long time, longer than is polite, and what it understood in that looking is the whole of what it afterward did. Here was a country with no dust, no dip in any tread, no groove in any sill, its corners sharp, its burr still on, suspended like a struck bell before the sound; and here in the middle of it were eleven bodies per crew doing the entire work of time on behalf of everything. Wearing down so that something could be old. Keeping the only archive the country permitted, in the one material that would consent to be marked.

A lesser power would have brought a sun. Would have set a floor under them at three hundred and forty-one courses and called it mercy, and the argument would have stopped, and they would have had a lid instead of a depth. Would have made the noise stop, would have made a child, would have made grass, would have sanded them smooth out of pity and never once understood that it had robbed a grave.

It brought nothing. It had come in with a mallet, which is what a person brings.

---

**The refusals**

It did not quiet the basin. It did, however, decline the customary relief: it never stopped noticing the doubling. Everyone else's headache passes in a month and the loss is not grieved because there is no one left to grieve it. This one kept the headache for the duration. Somebody, it reasoned, should go on hearing that the sound is always the sound the world makes about the blow, and never the blow. Somebody should carry that. It might as well be the one who could.

It did not tell them the last rule. It was asked once, by an under-listener, in the proper way — got alone, muttered straight into the lobe, a hand cupped — and what it answered was muttered back the same way, and the ear is the only private place in this country, and so nobody knows. The under-listener went back to her crew and lay down with her ear flat to the brick and did her job for another eleven thousand drops and was in due course laid, and her mark went up in one course all the way across the chalked line, slowly, by hand, by people who knew her, and after that nobody pointed it out again.

It did not sand anything smooth. Not the ninth step, though it could have deepened the dip and given them the miracle at scale. It understood that a saucer worn by feet and a saucer given by a power are not the same object, and that the second one would have ended the queue.

It did not raise the dead, because to be laid is the good end and un-laying is theft.

But it was fast to the collapses. It could hear a void through six courses and it stamped clean and it stamped early, and *someone's hurt* was stamped less often in its shift, and *someone's hurt badly* less often still, and it set bones, and it carried people to the western sink on its own shoulder without asking for a relief. These are small things. Small things were available, and it did not decline the available in order to preserve the grandeur of the unavailable.

---

**It went down**

Somebody was going to have to, and the diggers of the Sixth Shaft had earned their refusal.

It went past three hundred and forty courses. Past the glazed tile, swept clean, still faintly warm. Past a further nine thousand courses that were grey-green brick mortared with a clay that never fully cures, stamped, in places, with marks it recognised. It went until going down stopped being a direction.

Then it came back up, which nobody does.

What it reported, at the horn-brace, in close speech, to whoever leaned in: *there is no floor. I looked properly. There is none.*

They took it better than an outsider would expect. There is a difference — and it is the whole difference — between suspecting you stand over an abyss and being told by someone who actually went and had a look that what you stand over is a depth. An abyss is an absence of information. A depth is a fact, and facts can be worked inside of. The cistern-readers, who are permitted to be strange, asked follow-up questions for a hundred drops and then stopped and became slightly less insufferable about it than the line-setters were about anything.

It told the cistern-readers one further thing, and only them, because they are permitted to be strange and because they have the best food and would share it. It told them where the eels are small. They have not written it down, there being no writing, and they have not passed it on except by mouth to lobe, and the joke about the small ones and the small people is still told in the districts, and is still either a joke or a doctrine depending on where you are standing, and the cistern-readers still laugh at it, and now they laugh a half-beat late.

---

**It answered wrong**

The doctrine of the under-listeners is that beneath every crew there is another crew, mirrored, laying your course upside-down with your own mark on their bricks; that when you knock, you are answered by yourself.

It is a hard doctrine. It has the shape of company and the substance of a mirror, which is the loneliest architecture there is.

So the stain that shines went below, and when a mason knocked, it answered — *off the beat.* Deliberately. Sourly. Three when the knock was four; a heel-figure the knocker had not stamped; once, memorably, the four beats of the knocker's own name, which is a thing you cannot possibly hear from yourself, because a name is the only true thing you can say in this country and it goes into the floor and arrives underneath everyone at once.

Word of this moved through the quarters at the speed of muttering, which is slow, and arrived doubled and out of the floor, which is normal. There was no revision of doctrine, there being no one to revise it and nothing to revise it on. What there was, in the years after, was a change in how the drop of Breath-water is left in a fresh joint before setting. It used to be done the way you'd nod to someone in a corridor. It is done now the way you'd leave the last of the pot for somebody who is coming.

---

**The Come-Ups**

Eleven of them. Nobody asks, because early on some people asked and the answers were not usable.

It went and sat down with them in the arcade they were sweeping, and it asked, and it received the unusable answers. All of them. It did not need them to be usable. That is very nearly the definition of what it was.

One of the eleven, in the course of answering, produced water out of her face.

There is no word for that here. Water comes out of the wall, not out of the air; that is a rule, and it had never been broken in the recorded memory of a country with no records. The masons who saw it reached for the only vocabulary they had, which is the vocabulary of brick in the Breath, and what they said was that she was *weeping true* — not blotchy, not sweating uneven, but weeping true, the way a good brick weeps, all over and even, from a water it has held since the day it was pugged.

She did not put down the broom. None of them did. It did not ask them to; a wise power does not confuse a wound with its dressing, and does not take a broom off anyone who is holding it for a reason it has only just been told.

It took up a bundle of eel-skin lash bound to a stick, and it swept beside them.

It swept the same six feet of arcade for an hour. It swept floors laid yesterday. It swept the stubble behind the unmakers, and after each Breath it stood still through the silvering and the running and the drying and then took the chalky bloom off in long overlapping strokes, and it got the corners. It was good at the corners.

Nothing was fixed. Eleven people sweeping a clean floor
