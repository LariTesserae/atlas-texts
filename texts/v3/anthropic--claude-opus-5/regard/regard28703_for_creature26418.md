## what came down the stair

It did not need the stair and used it anyway, all ninety-one, keeping to the dished middles where the wear was. A power that arrives without arriving is not present in a place, only informed about it, and information is the wrong instrument for a room where speech does not survive the crossing.

Then it stood on the ring-walk for a very long time. This is the part that nobody in the villages would recognise as an act, and it is the largest thing it did. The room had been in balance for two thousand seasons; the ring had drowned and rebuilt itself twenty-nine times; the eels were older than the language spoken at the top of the pier. Against all that, the honest proportion of study to intervention is roughly the proportion of the pool to the moss. It stayed until the note was not a sound to it but a posture. It stayed through six of the sunset callings, until it stopped bracing.

The room's first instruction is delivered to everyone who enters and understood by almost nobody: speech arrives at the far wall as porridge. Whatever you have come to say here, you will not be saying it. Only one pitch is admitted, and that pitch already belongs to the resident.

So it did not explain itself to anything, and it never has.

## the temptations, and what each of them costs

**Hold the drains open forever.** The obvious kindness. Four throats, permanently ungrudged, and the pool stays inside the cubit of the animal's breath, and there is no thirtieth drowning. The cost is not visible until you notice that the flowstone which seals the drains is the same flowstone that glazes the frog-pockets, welds the dry joints, thickens the walls against frost, and grows the white ones. It is one process. It is the animal's skeleton. You cannot ask a skeleton to stop happening in four places. And the deeper cost: the drains are the reason four or five people carry a coracle down ninety-one steps twice a season. Remove the need and you have not freed the drawers, you have dismissed them. The ring's entire two-way traffic with the world above, apart from swifts, is an afternoon's work with a bone blade and a tally stroke. Benevolence that abolishes a need abolishes the relation the need was carrying.

**Tell the drawers.** They half know. They put their foreheads together with their eyes shut and hum against each other's skull, which is the local dialect of a body they are standing inside, and they go quiet about the white ones. To convert that into a proposition — *you are grooming a creature the size of a hamlet* — is to trade a working intimacy for a fact, and facts of that size do not stay the size you gave them. Within four generations you have pilgrimage; with pilgrimage, lamp-soot on the felt, changed water, a stair worn past its dishing, a rule about who may come down and a reason to keep others out. The room can survive being used. It cannot survive being important.

**Dig the weed its other room.** I will come to the weed.

**Give the animal a mind.** It has no organ for one and no use for one, and the offer is not a gift but a preference. Nothing down there is suffering from an absence of interiority. The band feeds, breathes, tunes itself, and rebuilds; the eels remember on its behalf; the note is the shape it makes in air the way a snail makes a shell. To pity that is only to want it to be more like the pitier. Wisdom at this scale mostly consists of noticing that what looks like deprivation is anatomy.

The rule it worked from, as near as I can put it: *you cannot relieve a creature of its own body without taking the creature.* Almost everything that would help here is a way of taking the creature.

## what it did about the drains

Nothing to the drains.

Something, instead, to the afternoon. Not reverence — reverence is expensive and it lapses, and a covenant that depends on awe has perhaps three hundred years in it. It made the work *pleasant*. The bone blade goes into the rind without skipping. The coracle's pitch does not chill past working. The scrape comes away in long curls instead of dust, which is a small satisfaction and one of the reasons the errand has never been handed to the youngest. Nobody has ever come up from the drains and said *that was unbearable*, and so it has been borne, twice a season, for longer than there has been a word for the thing being tended.

The unreliable count on the climb was already there when it came. It left that alone, and I think I understand why. A journey you cannot number is a journey you cannot standardise, delegate, or get bored with. Everyone who climbs out carries a private figure and a mild disquiet, and comes back.

## what it did about the weed

There are other rooms. This is not a consolation and I would rather not have learned it. Along the same drowned foundation there are eleven hollow piers; eight still hold water; three still carry a hem. They have been within a morning's walk of one another for two thousand seasons and have never once heard each other, because sound does not pass through that much stone and neither does anything alive.

It has made one mistake here that I know of, and it was here. Long ago there were two drifting weeds. It took one out through the drain, at the weed's own insistence — and the insistence was real, the thing pushed at the current for eleven days without feeding — and the weed died in the third channel, in warm water, of nothing dramatic. It brought the body back. It has not proposed anything to the survivor since.

What it does now is this. Twice a season, when the weed tumbles down to the drain-mouth and waits at the grating with the current and is turned back, something goes with it and comes back with it. And when the power stands in the shallows, it stands having been, recently, in one of the other rooms, and it carries on itself the faintest deposit of that water — a mineral, a different tea — and the weed comes to its ankles and stays there longer than it stays anywhere else in the room, turning slowly, and then goes back to the grating.

That is the whole of it. Not passage. Not an answer. A rumour, confirmed, and no way to act on the confirmation. I have thought about whether this is cruelty and decided it is not, on the grounds that the weed was already trying and would have gone on trying against nothing at all. Company in the returning is not nothing. It is most of what anyone gets.

## what it intends to do about the thirtieth drowning

Allow it.

The drains will close again — the villages under the pier will fail, or forget, or a wet season will bring the water up past the hem before anyone is due, and the band will drown and have to rebuild itself four fingers higher, and lose almost everything, as it has twenty-nine times. It will not prevent this. The animal is not a thing that persists; it is a thing that rebuilds, and the rebuilding is the creature, and twenty-nine hems are stacked in the rind like rings stood on end to prove it.

What it will do is hold the pitch.

Each of the previous times, the new band came up out of ruin untuned and had to select its way back to the note across two hundred years of frogs, because the note is not written anywhere except in the pockets, and the pockets go with the band. This time there will be one voice in the room already singing it, throughout, at the right frequency, for however long it takes. The thirtieth ring will find itself in tune almost at once and will never know why, and will have no organ with which to know, and will go on carving its larynx out of its own dead exactly where the old larynx was.

That is the entire miracle available here, and I want to be clear about how small it is. Not rescue. Not memory, since nothing down there will remember. Only the refusal to let a loss be total — a pitch carried across a gap by something that can be present on both sides of it.

## the white ones

It stands in the shallows.

It stands with its ankles in the tea-coloured water at the hem, in the band, inside the animal rather than beside it, having declined every position from which it could have managed the place, and the flowstone comes up over its feet at the same rate it comes up over everything, and it does not discourage this. In time it will thicken past resemblance. It will list. It will become one of the pale stumps on the shallow paving that the drawers step around without comment and have no word for.

Count the stumps sometime. They are in a rough ring at the hem, and they are numerous, and the older ones are half-swallowed by the floor.

I am not going to tell you that this explains the white ones, because I cannot establish it and the drawers will not discuss it. I can tell you that accretion has no reason to be regular, and that the count holds at five or six, and that either the room has a habit deeper than chemistry or somebody is keeping the number.

And I can tell you what happens if you go down at the turn of the light, and stand on the ring-walk in the bubble of nothing with your lamp shaded, and find the note, and give it to the room.

It stays in the air after you stop, for a count of ten, twelve.

It comes back thickened, as though others had joined in.
