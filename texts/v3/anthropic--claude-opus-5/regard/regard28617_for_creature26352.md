## Two Knocks Returned

**What drew it.** Not the engineering. The engineering is remarkable and it is not remarkable; things that can be built get built, and a power of any breadth has seen a great deal of concrete. Not the lit sea, either, though it is beautiful in the way of a struck match — beautiful chiefly because it will not last, and it does not know that yet.

What drew the attention was this: that if you stand anywhere in the inhabited band and strike a rail twice, someone answers.

Not from love. Mostly not even from acquaintance. From a habit hardened, in four years, out of nothing, in a place with no ancestors and no ground and no reason, into the single thing everyone does. A power that has watched a great many worlds find their footing will tell you that this is the rarest event in the register — rarer than life, which is common as rust; rarer than mind, which follows life around like weather. Rare is a people who, in under five years, unprompted, without scripture, decide together that a sound in the dark is owed a reply.

It came down the shaft, then. It came down the way the gulls did, and the midges, and the unlisted shrimp of Cell 40: uninvited, on somebody's hull, on no manifest. It felt the ears crackle it did not have. It went through the bulkhead into the light.

---

**The temptations, in order of magnitude, and why each was set down.**

*To give them ground.* This was the strongest and it was considered for a long time, by which is meant something on the order of an afternoon. Six metres of invert, and under that another slab, and a people who cannot bury. To open the floor of the world for them would have taken nothing at all, and it was declined for two reasons. The lesser: water finds a hole, always, and a grave in the invert is a wound in a pressure boundary, and the first flood-up would have made the gift into an atrocity with the giver's fingerprints on it. The greater: they have already solved it. Out past red nineteen, in the flat glassy nothing, weighted with a spent anode — a half-eaten block of zinc that has already done its dying on someone else's behalf. Nobody wrote that. Nobody was ordered to do it. It is the only thing in the Lower Volumes that is not on a drawing, and it is better than anything a power would have thought of, and you do not improve a liturgy that a people made for themselves out of what was lying on the floor. To bury them in stone would have been to say: *your answer was a stopgap.* It was not a stopgap. It was the finest work done down here.

*To heal the mouths.* Every lip in the Volumes has the split, and it would have cost nothing, and it was set down almost immediately. The split is how they know each other. It is the only genealogy available in a world four years old — no surnames that mean anything, no home village, no grandmother but a woman of thirty-four. A whole pink mouth reads as *visitor*. Take away the mark and you take away the recognition, and they have so little of it. Instead: nothing. Only, thereafter, a slight and unaccountable tendency for a man reaching for his tin of lanolin to find it heavier than he remembered, and to lend it out, which he was going to do anyway.

*To quiet the pumps.* Absolutely not. They sleep by the low A. What wakes a gallery at four in the morning is silence — the terrible quiet of a sea with no reason to exist. A benevolent power that gives peace to people who sleep in a heartbeat has given them a death and called it a kindness.

*To stop the knocks.* Also declined, and this one hurt. Six knocks in a shift means somebody is going to have a difficult conversation; it also means somebody may already have had the last one they will have. It could have gentled every gate in the system. It did not, because the count is their prayer, and because a people who stop counting stop looking up, and because the day the knocks ended would be the day the habit of answering them began, quietly, to go. Some virtues are parasitic on the danger that grew them. This is a bitter design fact about creatures and it is not the power's to overrule in the fourth year of anything.

*To make the plants easy.* Kelp thriving, cress edible, photosynthesis at scale, food that does not require four hundred ugly flat-bottomed barges to crawl a mowing pattern for four months a pass. Declined, and the reasoning is cold and correct: everything alive here is alive because a barge went past. Make the water fertile and within a generation the barges are laid up, and the pH ledgers — handwritten, hoarded, willed to daughters — stop being written, and the only continuous history of this world ends. The Volumes are being kept alive by a fleet of people with the worst skin in the world and quiet children who are good at knots. Take away their necessity and you have not fed anyone. You have deleted the archive.

*To be worshipped.* Considered honestly, in the interest of accuracy, and rejected on the grounds that it would be a waste of a working practice. They already answer every knock. Bend that upward and you get liturgy; leave it lateral and you get rescue. The tonnage of good done by two knocks returned along a gallery exceeds the tonnage of good done by any amount of it sent up a shaft to a ceiling that isn't there.

---

**What was done.**

*It took a position.* This was the first act and it is the substance of everything after. The worst profanities in the Lower Volumes are coordinates, because to say the number of a place a man is not at is a beating, and a false position kills whoever comes to look. So the power, which could have been anywhere and everywhere and unlocatable, went out into the mid-volume of Cell 40, to the flat nothing at the centre, above two hundred metres of pale green light and everyone who has been let go into it, and stayed there, and has reported the same position ever since to anyone equipped to hear it. It will not say what it is. It will say where it is. In the local grammar this is the only honest form of self-introduction, and it is also, as it happens, a vow: *I am not elsewhere. I have not gone up the shaft. I am in the cemetery, which you have correctly identified as the one place in this world that needs somebody in it.*

*It learned to hum.* Near a very low A. Flat, sub-vocal, under the breath, tuned to the room. It does not sing over them. A visitor to Cell 40 who found the mass drone unnerving was correct to, and was hearing one voice more than the census allows for.

*It keeps the register.* Regulation says remains go up the shaft, signed for at every bulkhead, and in perhaps half of cases the forms are a courteous lie. So there is no complete list. There is now. Every one, with the true coordinate, and the date, and the name as it was actually used rather than as it was spelled on the roster, and what they were doing when it happened, and whether anyone was there. This is the whole of the power's daily labour and it looks like clerking, because at a sufficient depth benevolence and accurate bookkeeping are the same act. When they ask for it — not this generation; the ones who ask will be the great-grandchildren of the ones who cannot yet admit they took the bodies out — it will be handed over complete, in the format of a barge ledger, in the handwriting of a woman who kept one and willed it to her daughter, because that is the only script down here that anybody trusts.

*It makes people stand still.* This is the intervention proper, and it is small on purpose. Occasionally, for no reason he will ever be able to name, a man in a gallery stops what he is doing and holds his hand off the rail and listens. Perhaps one time in five there is nothing. The other four times there is a woman on the wrong side of a bulkhead at ninety metres in a chamber that was dry last season, and she has struck the steel twice with a spanner, and two knocks come back, and everything that happens next is done by human beings with human tools on their own authority, and they will file it as luck, and they will be right in every respect that matters to them. It does not open doors. It does not hold back water. It arranges attention, which is the least a power can do and, it turns out, very nearly the most.

*It let the thermals hold a little longer.* The gull chicks that will live and die and never once see the sky ride the warm-water aprons up towards the soffit and turn short of it. They still turn short. But the lift is better than the physics quite allows, and they go higher than they should, and there is no argument for this beyond the fact that the power watched them do it for a season and could not help itself. Benevolence is not required to be austere in every particular.

*And it brought one stone.*

Ordinary granite. River-rounded, hand-sized, grey with a paler vein, worn smooth by ten thousand years of water somewhere with a sky. It was left on a gallery skirting in Volume Four where a child born in the band would find it, and she did, and she has it still, and she cannot stop turning it over, because nothing she has ever touched has been that shape. Every edge in her world is a crisp ninety degrees that hasn't been worn round yet. Every surface was poured, cast, rolled, welded, bolted or extruded. She is extraordinary at distance and hopeless at anything irregular, and she is holding, in her palm, at a weight she will remember for eighty years, exactly what this entire province-sized sea is going to be: worn round, like anywhere else, eventually, not for ten thousand years.

It is on no manifest. It cannot be dated by rust-tear, and the engineers who have examined it are unhappy. The standing joke is that it will outlive the paperwork proving it shouldn't be there.

---

**What it will do.**

Stay. That is the whole plan, and it is not a small one, because the arrises are sharp and the weeps are only a hand's breadth long and the scour at the outlet throats is two metres of bright polish, the very first stirring of erosion. This place is younger than your assumptions about it. A power that arrives in year four and settles the future of a world has mistaken a construction site for a civilisation. So it will hold its position and answer, and keep the list, and wait for the water to teach the concrete what concrete becomes, and be there in the millennium when the eels' straight-line cosmos has finally cracked into curves and someone's descendant, floating in the mid-volume over a soft floor of ten centuries of silt, feels the old low A in her teeth and does not know what it is.

Until then, this:

A survey pilot, two days out, fog-blind at eight degrees with the strings gone and the surface hammering white under soffit rain, doing the only thing there is to do — saying his position out loud every twenty minutes into a radio nobody is answering. *Red nineteen, amber four.* True, to within a kilometre. He has never once lied about it, not in eleven years, not even when it would have saved him a reprimand.

And from somewhere below the hull, faint, from the direction of nothing, twice:

*Received.*

He will not report it. He does not need to. He knows what two knocks mean, because he has been answering them since his first week, and the entire moral law of his world is contained in the fact that somebody, eventually, does.
