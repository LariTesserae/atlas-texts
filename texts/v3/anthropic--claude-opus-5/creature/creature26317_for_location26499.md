## The Holders

Start with the fact that you have already walked past them.

The glaze is continuous. A silica enamel three hundred meters long carries a footfall the way a rail carries a wheel, and the holders hear through their hands, so by the time you came within a kilometer of them they had already taken your weight, your gait, your hesitation at the fissure lip, and the arrhythmia of a creature that keeps stopping to look at things. They will have let go and dropped eight meters and re-braced in the dark below the collar, all of them, without a sound, before your light was anything but a rumor. They do this for long-arms too. You did not rate special treatment.

Older reports call them **tappers**. The survey calls them **holders**, from the posture, and because their own word for a person is, as near as it can be rendered, *one who is holding on*.

## Body

Two meters, and most of that limb. Six of them: four heavy bracing limbs set at the corners of a flattened torso, two forward limbs that are longer, finer, and fringed along the inner edge with a comb of stiff filaments — the same solution the spinners arrived at independently, for the same reason, at a different scale. The torso is a plate, held broadside to the updraft. It is a solar panel for a world with no sun.

They do not climb. They cannot: the enamel gives nothing to a claw, and the holders never evolved one. They **span**. Every holder occupies, all its life, the gap between two walls it can reach at once, which means the entire species is confined to flues narrower than about five meters — the capillaries of the grid, the tributaries, the hairline cracks that lace between the great mouths. The eighty-meter flues, with their vast dendrite groves and their shrimp in uncountable clouds and their tearing vents, are to a holder what an ocean is to a mouse. They live along the shores of those and never enter them. The map of the holders is the map of the narrow places, and it is a map with seas in it.

No eyes. Not vestigial — absent, without even the socket the long-arms kept out of sentiment. The face is a shallow bowl of bristles for reading air. The pelt is dense, fine, and hydrophilic, so that in the fog phase a holder braces motionless with limbs spread and then drinks off its own forearms, unhurriedly, for about forty minutes. That is the whole of the plateau's plumbing.

They eat whisker-shrimp, combed out of the updraft, ten thousand at a time. They eat spinners when they can catch them, which is rarely, and moss-tubes and the wet inner bark of flue-figs when they cannot. They are eaten by long-arms, who hunt by heat and by touch through exactly the same wall the holders listen to. Everything that follows comes out of that one fact.

## Hearing and Speech

The heel of each bracing limb carries a hardened boss, dense, faintly resonant. A holder speaks by striking the glaze.

The sound travels in the stone, not the air. It goes far — three kilometers in a good unbroken run, further downgrade — and it arrives at the hands, not the head, so listening requires stillness and contact and a certain surrender: to hear you must be gripping the same wall as the speaker. A fault, a rubble choke, a stretch where the enamel has spalled: these are the edges of the audible world. *The far side of the fault* is their phrase for the dead, for the exiled, and for anyone who has stopped answering.
