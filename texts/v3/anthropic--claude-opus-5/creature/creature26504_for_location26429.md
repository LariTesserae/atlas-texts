## First, who does not

Take the fauna out of the account, because most of it does not live on the Shelf. It lives in a bearing seat. A fitter's entire world is the four-centimeter annulus between axle and cup, dark, warm with friction, tasting of biofilm; the basin outside is a rumour, and the fitter does not receive rumours. Silverswarm lives in a field node the size of a bathtub and dies within a few meters of where it hatched, having oriented itself all its life to a leak. Ballast beasts live along an edge — the extending margin of one sheet, which they follow the way a hand follows a hem — and if you moved one to the other end of the basin it would find another edge in a week and never notice it had been moved. These are not inhabitants. They are parts fitted into sockets, and the socket is the address, not the place.

And the plants are not inhabitants either, for the opposite reason. A drivewood does not live *in* the basin; the drivewood is a piece of what the basin *is*. Ask where a drivewood lives and the only honest answer is: inside its own bore, in the two-centimeter film of water on the seat of each valve, where the abrasion is and where the regrowth is, that thin annulus of matched rates repeated four hundred times up a column. A drivewood is aware — if the word can be stretched that far — of its own throat and of the salinity of the seep beneath it, and of nothing else. Nine hundred of them stand in one water and none of them has any conception of the others.

So the noise has no listener among them. Nine hundred fixed tones, a hundred-source chord filling three thousand cubic kilometers of water, undiminished at any range, and every organism named so far is deaf to it or indifferent.

That is what makes room for the actual resident.

## The chord is the body

Before the animal, the thing the animal lives in.

Consider what is conserved here. Not the drivewoods: they die, about one a century, and rot out, and are gone. What survives a death is the *pitch*. The seed germinates in the parent's hydraulic shadow and grows to the parent's bore diameter and chamber count and comes up, over some decades, to the parent's tone, and the interference pattern across the whole basin closes back over the gap without a seam. The wood is replaceable. The note is not.

Which means the thing with continuity here is not any of the tissue. It is a standing acoustic structure, nine hundred sources, phase-locked by geometry, occupying the entire lens of water — an object with a shape as definite as a cathedral's and rather more permanence, of which the drivewoods are merely the currently-installed pipes. It has had every one of its pipes replaced, many times, and has not changed.

There is no one keeping it that way. That needs saying, because the temperature invites the wrong idea. Seven degrees from surface to floor, everywhere, always: a maintained gradient, yes, but maintained the way a stirred cup is maintained, by nine hundred pumps turning the whole lens over faster than sun or sky can stratify it. Nobody is holding the thermometer. There is only what happens to be exact.

But something does live inside the structure, the way a bat lives inside the shape of a barn.

## The listeners

Seventy centimeters, blunt, pale to the point of translucency over dark viscera, one sculling fin, no eyes, no eye-sockets, no pigment where eyes would be. The head is the animal. It is a solid lens of waxy tissue, spherical, fist-sized and fist-shaped because that is the shape of a lens tuned to eight to sixty Hz, and it seats against a plate of hardened cartilage that seats against the brain, which is disproportionate and mostly one thing: a comparator. Down each flank runs a line of some six thousand pressure papillae. Bearing to a twentieth of a degree. Range by shadow. Depth by the vertical component of a chord that gets muddier toward the floor and cleaner near the plane of the surface.

A listener knows where it is the way you know which room of your house you are standing in. That is the standard formulation and it undersells the thing badly. You know your rooms because you have five senses and doorframes. A listener holds, in a brain the size of a walnut, a nine-hundred-term solution — every pitch, every source position, the whole interference field of a province of water — and reads its own coordinates off that field continuously, in the dark, at forty centimeters per second, and does not lose the thread while asleep.

They are not born with it. This is the extraordinary part.

A hatchling holds four or five sources and cannot leave the rim, where the water is shallow and the chord is thin enough to be soluble: a few nearby columns, a wall of reflection off the ridge, a workable arithmetic. Rim-holders stay rim-holders for years. Then some of them start inward, and the count goes up — thirty pitches, ninety, three hundred — and each increment costs seasons, and each increment buys access to a further annulus of basin, and there is no shortcut, because there is no one to tell them anything.

Listeners are silent. Absolutely, in the way that nothing else here is: no call, no click, no courtship, no distress. They cannot afford to be otherwise. Their instrument is the chord, and to add anything to the chord is to spoil the measurement — for themselves, and for every one of their kind within tens of kilometers, which is all of them. So they pass each other in the dark, register each other as soft slow shadows, and exchange nothing. Ever. Mating is a two-minute silent collision between animals that will not meet again.

Nothing is inherited. Each listener assembles the entire map from scratch, alone, over a lifetime, and when it dies the map goes. Ten thousand years of this. Twelve thousand animals, more or less, and the number has been twelve thousand for longer than the number has meant anything, and the sum total of transmitted knowledge on the Shelf is zero.

They are, all the same, the only thing here that accumulates. The rock has finished eroding. The valve grooves are the same grooves. The sheets extend and are eaten at matched rates. A listener at eighty years old contains strictly more than it did at nine. It is the only history in the basin, it is a history of nothing but the basin, and it is discarded on completion.

## What they are for

They eat anomaly.

Formally: they are detritivores, and the closure of the Shelf requires them, because a closed system needs something whose job is that nothing accumulates. A fitter is crushed in a valve throat. A ballast beast, at the end of its decades, stops arcing and goes down. A patch of sheetgrowth tears in the one place the currents were not steady. Somewhere a body or a rag of tissue is where it should not be, and it will putrefy, and the putrefaction will foul a seat or a node, and the chord will drift.

Before it drifts, it clicks. A dead fitter in a drivewood throat changes that column's timbre — not its pitch, its timbre, a scattering of the high silver shriek by some fraction of a decibel — and eleven kilometers away a listener stops sculling.

Then it travels. Days, sometimes. Forty kilometers at forty centimeters per second, a pale fist-headed thing crossing an emptiness of blue-green nothing with no landmark in sixty meters and no doubt whatsoever about its heading, toward a dead animal the size of your palm. Others converge from other bearings. The obstruction is removed and eaten. The timbre returns to what it was, and the returning is itself detectable, and the listeners that were coming turn away mid-course.

They do not know they are maintaining anything. They are following a discrepancy because a discrepancy is edible. But the practical upshot is this: the exactness of the Shelf — the ten-thousand-year sameness of the groove, the unaltered chord — is not a property of the plants. The plants only run. The exactness is enforced by twelve thousand small intolerances distributed through the water, each of which can hear the whole basin and cannot bear a wrong note in it.

Attention is the resident. The rest is plumbing.

## If you go in

You will be audited within the hour.

You are a sound-shadow of impossible softness, badly reflective, wrongly shaped, and — this is the intolerable part — mobile. Nothing here moves except water and animals that move on rails. The first listener will find you from four or five kilometers, arrive, and hold station at the range where its flank line reads you best: eleven or twelve meters, off your shoulder, head-on. It will make no sound. It will follow you.

By dark there will be forty. By the second day, some hundreds, a slow pale cloud at a fixed standoff, all oriented, none touching you. They have no procedure for you. They cannot remove you and cannot eat you and cannot report you, because reporting is not a thing they do. They can only stay, and read, and add you to the map, and be unable to close the map while you are in it.

When you leave the basin, the ones that followed you will hold position around the place where you stopped being for some considerable time. Weeks, in a few recorded cases. Long enough to lose the rest of the field. Nothing else on the Shelf can be lost. These can, and only this way, and only once.

## The one in the shaft

Now the hole. Forty centimeters, plumb, eleven meters, walls glossed, a hemisphere at the bottom, worn by a wheelweed axle turning in balance for no reason forever. It is not a drill hole. It is a resonator, whether or not anything intended it, and a resonator in this basin is a filter, and a filter is the only door out of the chord.

Go down it and the world switches off in stages. At two meters the silver cavitation shriek is gone, absorbed by the polished granite. At five the mid-range grinding thins to a mush with no bearing information in it. At eight the geometry collapses; the walls are closer than a wavelength and the flank line has nothing to differ. At eleven meters, in the hemisphere, what remains is the 6 Hz bulk thud arriving through solid rock from every direction at once — direction-less, positionless, the chord reduced from a cathedral to a pressure — and beneath that, unmasked for the first time, the granite itself.
