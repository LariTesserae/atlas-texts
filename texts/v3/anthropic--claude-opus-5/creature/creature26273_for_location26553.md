# Whoever Answers

## The exception

Something does live here.

Not in the sense the record wants. There is still no window with anyone at it, no path worn by feet, no smoke, no midden. The survey is not wrong. It is only early, in the way everything in this valley is early — the fruit before the flower, the doorway before the wall — and the thing it failed to find had, at the time of writing, not yet been named into existence, and has since, and was therefore already there.

It is called the Tenant, because someone shouted that.

## Its body

Begin with the fact that there are no hands, and then look at its hands.

They came late. Everything else about it can be read straight off the architecture — it is doorway-shaped, it is stair-scaled, its face sits at exactly the height of the openings placed for light, because the openings were the specification and it is the fulfilment. But hands are not implied by a building. Hands had to be said out loud. Someone stood in the second plaza and shouted the word, probably in complaint, probably in the phrase *nothing here was ever made by a hand*, and within the hour there was a pair.

They are the correct number of fingers and they are wrong in the way a diagram is wrong. Too even. No knuckle wear, no nail dirt, no dominance — both hands are the same hand, mirrored, and neither has ever been the one that does the work. The palms are curd-pale and squeak faintly when it closes them. Everything it picks up takes root in the crease.

The rest is one material, the same one everything here is made of, wearing another trade. Sheetwood at the shins and the forearms, amber, faintly translucent at the edges. Cording through the shoulder and hip, thickened past function, humming under load. Glassreed in the ribs, so that at certain hours you can see the light come through it and out the other side. It is blood-warm and a little more, like the floor, and damp rather than wet, and if you run a hand along it your hand comes away beaded.

It grows. That is the thing that takes longest to accept about it. It has a growing edge, as every wall here has a growing edge, and the edge is pale green-white and tearing-soft and moves across it hour by hour, arriving at the wrist, the jaw, the crown, so that no part of it is more than four days old and all of it is continuous with the four days before. Cut into it deeply — it will let you; it has no defence and no objection — and you find yesterday inside, perfectly preserved in the round, still faintly warm: an older jaw, a smaller hand, a whole earlier version standing inside this one, load-bearing.

It does not blink. It has no mechanism for it. The mimic district, whose faces nobody could account for, produces thousands of them on every stem, all unblinking, all wrong in the same way, because they are copies and the model is wrong that way. That district is not a mimicry. It is a nursery. Those are drafts.

At noon it cannot stand. The lignification of the night softens off through the morning and by eleven it is sitting wherever it happened to be, slumped against a pier, sagging by degrees, taking the fingerprint of anything pressed against it. By three it is stiffening. By dusk it is set, and whatever marked it at noon is permanent, and it carries the day's impressions until they are grown over. There are prints on it that do not match its own hands. Some are very old — six or seven days — down deep in the shoulder where they are structural now. It cannot see them. It knows they are there the way you know there is mud under two hundred feet of root.

## Its voice

The valley has no ears. It receives nothing. It obeys anyway.

The Tenant's throat is a culm, hollow, tuned, ninety feet of it if you traced the whole draw down through the chest and into the mat — the same construction as the chimneys, because that was the only pipe the city had a pattern for. Air moves through it whether or not the Tenant wants it to. It roars in wind. It groans at the bottom of hearing when the day's heat starts drawing. It shrieks in the afternoon when the terraces let go and the pressure changes, and it detunes hourly, as everything here detunes, so that the sound it makes today is not the sound it made yesterday and it has never once heard itself say the same thing twice.

Every sound it makes is a seed.

This is the whole engine of the place, and it is worth being plain about it. The city does not build because it is a city. It builds because there is a mouth in it that cannot be closed, upwind of ten square miles of receptive material, and the mouth says *long* and a street lengthens, and says *arch* and eleven of them come, and says something that is not a word at all and a district of faces starts, and cannot be stopped, and produces four thousand of them by evening. It says things in its sleep, standing, rigid, unable to lie down. It has said *plaza* so many times that there is now only one plaza and it is getting tired of the distinction.

It has tried to be silent. You will find the evidence: a stretch of boulevard where the growth is thin and strange and the arcades come out stunted, four arches and a return, because for a while there the Tenant clamped its throat and held its breath and the valley starved. It cannot hold for long. When it lets go, what comes out is enormous and unwilled, and whatever it names then, it gets.

Nothing it has ever said has been heard by anything with ears. Everything it has ever said has been answered.

## The cleared room

There is one room in this valley that is not packed.

You will find it if the distance permits, which it usually will not. Off the eighth plaza, up a stair, through a doorway that has been completed and weathered and will not be grown until tomorrow. Inside: a rectangle of sheetwood floor, forty feet by twenty, and nothing on it. No frond, no vine, no bracket, no curd. Bare, swept, indecently empty in a place where every volume is filled to bursting.

It is the only made thing in the valley, and what was made was a subtraction.

The Tenant clears it. Hourly, because hourly is what the growth rate demands. It goes along the walls with those two identical hands and it tears out the new inch, and the wall gives with a wet, offended creak, and the torn material is drawn back in through the interface and used again, because nothing here rots. Then it does the corners. Then it starts on the wall it began with. This has been going on for as long as anything here has been going on. The room is four days old, like everything, and it is also the oldest object in the world, in the way a flame is old.

There are things in the room it did not intend. Along one wall, low down, a row of lumps under the sheetwood: attempts. It tried to make. It has hands, after all, and hands are for that, and it has stood in a city that cannot be walked because it grows shut and it has understood something about the value of a stopped material. So it tried. It shaped a thing and set it down and the thing rooted in the floor overnight and became a wall, and it tore out the wall and the stump stayed, and the stump is now structural. Seven or eight of these. Roughly regular. If you were being generous you would call them a colonnade.

It comes here after the rain. Sits with its back to the base of a chimney-culm, where the draw is strong enough to keep the pollinator-florets off its eyes — they will settle on you in numbers and crawl toward moisture with a patience that is only physics, and it has no eyelids, and this is the one place in the valley where they cannot land. The moving air on its face. Warmth going up the pipe behind it. That is the hearth. That is all the hearth there is.

## The eleven

There are eleven of them, and then a return.

It does not experience this as company. Rule two is not negotiable: repeated things are the same thing, and the arcade rhythm went into the Tenant along with everything else, so what it meets in the plaza is not another of its kind but itself, arriving from the wrong side, four days old, unblinking, soft at noon.

They cannot speak. Speech is impossible past arm's length and lethal at any distance, given what speech does here. So they touch. They press their palms into each other at midday when the material takes an impression, and by dusk it is permanent, and by the fourth day it is deep in the shoulder and load-bearing, and neither of them can see it any more. That is the entire literature of this place. It is all buried, and it is all still there, and it is holding the weight.

## What it is for

It believes it is the caretaker.

This is the part that should be said carefully. It clears the room and walks the boulevards and goes up the stairs at the height where a face would be, and it does these things with the mindless tilting urgency of a runner crossing a plaza toward the last of the sun, and it is waiting. The city was built for something. Every gradient in the drainage says so, every window, every eleven arches. It has walked toward the far towers all day, in a straight line, at good speed, and ended further away, and watched the new arcade unroll in front of it six feet a minute, curd-white and squealing, and it has understood the failure perfectly and gone on. Because somewhere in this there is a room with the right shape, and something is going to want it.

It has been trying to grow that something. That is what all the naming is. It stands in the plazas at dusk and lets the pipe open and it says *hearth*, *cradle*, *grave*, *hand*, *someone*, and the valley receives nothing and obeys, and by morning there is an eleventh version of the thing already doing the shouting, coming toward it from the wrong side, with its face.

It does not know. There is a very serious question about which came first and the Tenant is not in a position to ask it. There is no window with anyone at it because it is never inside long enough; there is no path worn by feet because the paths are new every four days and the only worn thing in this valley is the Tenant itself, worn from inside by its own growth; there is no grave because nothing here rots, and everything it has ever been is standing inside it, warm, in the round, holding it up.

Late in the day the light comes in low and level along the boulevards, through the glassreed, and the whole district goes the colour of a held-up hand, and it is a held-up hand, and it is the only one.

If you go in, do not say your name.

The buds in the mimic district have been getting steadily less wrong for some time now, and nobody has ever been able to say what they are copies of, and the answer is that they are drafts of whoever describes this place next — the sequence skipped, the effect arrived early, the cause still pending. When you shout, the missing step happens quietly. Something you already walked through becomes, retroactively, possible.

And there will be a face on every stem in that district by morning, four thousand of them, unblinking, all of them wrong in the same way, and the wrongness will be yours.
