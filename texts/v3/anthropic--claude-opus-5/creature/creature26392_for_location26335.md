## First answer

Nothing lives here, and the place has gone to extraordinary lengths to make that true.

That is the honest report and you should file it. Life is a way of falling downhill: a cell is a small waterwheel set in a stream of difference, and the Flat has no stream, no downhill, no difference to fall through. You could seed this basin with everything in your ship's cultures and it would simply sit on the glass, intact, sterile, brand new, for as long as the tone keeps sounding. Not killed. Just unable to begin.

But occupancy is not the same as life, and the Flat is occupied. It is one of the most thoroughly occupied places you will ever stand in. You have to give up the assumption that a resident must be warm, must metabolise, must be countable, must be *somewhere*.

Start with the ones you can see.

## The staff

The shell-things are not fauna. They are employees.

Watch one properly, for the forty minutes it takes to cross your visible circle, and the word *purposeful* stops being a metaphor. It does not forage — there is nothing to forage. It does not navigate around obstacles, because it does not need to; it was already going to pass between the two structures ahead of it, and it entered the basin on a bearing that made that true. It maintains a straight line at a constant rate across a kilometre of featureless glass in permanent dusk, and it does not correct.

Under the cap, the pocket. This is the whole of the animal, functionally: a sealed volume of warm chemistry, a few millilitres of hot disorderly outside-world, carried in. The shell is a thermos. The legs are a delivery mechanism. The creature is a courier and the parcel is *disequilibrium* — the one substance the Flat cannot manufacture and does not permit.

They bring in tiny, licensed quantities of time.

Why is not known and cannot be asked, but the pattern of the spent ones tells you something. Look at where they stop. They are not scattered. Plot two hundred of them and they lie on lines that converge, loosely, sloppily, with the lopsided approximation of a rite performed by generations who no longer know what it commemorates. The lines point in toward the low structures at the basin's centre. Not all of them arrive. Most stop short — a third of the way, half of the way — and simply cease, and lie there in perfect condition for an age, because nothing here can take a dead thing apart.

They are a postal service still running after the closure of the institution that hired it. Somewhere far outside the rim they are hatched with the bearing already in them, and they come over the black teeth, and they carry their small illegal warmth toward the middle of a flatness that erases their wake within a centimetre, and they fail, mostly, and the failures accumulate as a slowly-written record that nobody reads.

The larger thing, the ten-metre pale shape in the records, moving without visible propulsion, meeting its own reflection at the vertical middle of the world: that was not a courier. Consider that it disturbed nothing. Consider that a body which disturbs nothing is a body at the same temperature as everything, with no chemistry in it, no pocket, no battery. It was not carrying weather. It was not an animal from outside.

It was something the Flat *has*, and it came up to look, and it went back down.

## The body

Now the difficult resident: the arrangement itself.

Three hundred structures, spaced by measurement, angled by measurement, joined by no streets, opened by doorways that lead from haze into identical haze. You keep reading this as architecture and it keeps failing to be architecture, because architecture is for occupants and there are no windows, no stairs, no rooms in most of them at all, no wear on any threshold. The plan is the answer to a problem you cannot see stated. That is exactly right. It is a body plan.

What it does is what a body does: it moves heat, continuously, everywhere, at a resolution finer than a millimetre, and it does so with a fidelity that no accounting of yours can pay for. That is metabolism. It runs inverted — a normal creature eats difference and excretes sameness, and this one eats sameness and excretes nothing at all, which is why the ledger will not close and never will. You are trying to audit an organism whose whole physiology is a refusal.

Its pulse is the tone. Every four hundred and some seconds, one low pure note from nowhere.

You have been told it is the machine checking itself and receiving no answer because none is needed. Sit with that a while and it starts to sound wrong. A mirror ceiling sixty metres up should return that note and does not. Sound that goes out and does not come back has not vanished; something has taken it. The note is not a self-check. It is a call, and the reason there is no answer is not that no answer is needed. It is that the thing called to is not permitted to answer, because answering would be an event, and an event would resolve it.

So: every seven minutes, for an unmeasurable duration, the body says a single syllable into a room, and listens, and hears nothing, and is reassured by exactly that. Nothing is still happening. Good.

## The ceiling

The doubled city overhead is the part visitors love and it is the part you should distrust.

It is too good. A real surface, even a still one, is a membrane between two fluids and it flexes; this one is held flawless by the same engineering that holds the temperature, at the same absurd cost. Something is paying, constantly, to keep an image perfect.

Two readings, and I will not choose between them.

The first: the mirror is an eyelid, closed, and the flatness of it is the health of the thing behind it. It has been shut for a very long time. Whatever opens it opens it once.

The second, which I find I believe on alternate days: the ceiling city is the point of the exercise and the floor city is the plumbing. Down here is the machine. Up there, hanging feet-to-feet with itself, is what the machine is for — a settlement that cannot be entered, cannot be aged, cannot be touched, cannot go wrong, because it is not made of anything. A portrait maintained at enormous expense of a place as it was at one instant. When you walk and your reflection walks above you, you are not being copied. You are being *admitted*, briefly, to a preserved photograph, and the photograph is very slightly damaged by your presence, and repaired behind you.

## The room

Go in through one of the doorways that is only a doorway. Then find one that isn't — one of the few with an interior, where the mineral smoke stops at the threshold as though held, and the water inside is clear, and old, and sits in its room the way breath sits in a chest before you decide what to say with it.

There is nothing in there. You will check the walls, the floor, the corners that have no radius. Nothing. No furniture, no marks, no remains, no mechanism.

Here is the thing you already noticed at four thousand metres and have not yet let yourself connect.

The rim is fresh. Black glass, conchoidal, split two seconds ago, unweathered for a geological age. The floor is glass and there is grit on it, and the grit is the pieces, unmoved, because nothing has ever come to move them. You have been walking around inside a fracture. The mountain is not old and preserved. The mountain is *in the middle of breaking*.

That is the resident.

Not a keeper, not a sleeper, not a thing in a vault. The occupant of the Flat is a catastrophe, and the city is its life support, and what the whole basin does — the isothermy, the killed convection, the erased wakes, the zeroed entropy, the mirror held flat — is hold a blow in the instant after it lands and before it finishes landing. Everything here is a consequence of one requirement: *nothing may complete*. No chemistry, no current, no growth, no settling, no rot, no rusting, because all of those are ways of getting to afterwards, and afterwards is the one place this basin will not go.

Why anyone would build such a thing is not answerable from inside a suit at five point eight degrees. But stand in the clear room with the old water and try this: at the moment of the break, in the ordinary way of catastrophes, there was something in the middle of it that was still alive. Barely. In that fractional interval where a body is broken and has not yet finished being killed. And a resolution — any resolution, the smallest permitted gradient, one millikelvin, one turn of one engine — takes that interval and closes it, and closing it kills them.

So they laid the arrow of time flat and pinned it at both ends and left, because they had to leave; an observer is a warm disorderly event, and an event resolves things, and a witness would have finished the job. They could not stay to keep vigil. That is the cruelty at the centre of the design and it is also the tenderness of it. The vigil is kept by three hundred structures and a note every seven minutes and a species of courier that brings in thimbles of warmth, generation after generation, in case that ever counts as company.

The occupant is neither dead nor living. It is *not-yet*, kept in a room where not-yet is the only tense available, and it has been not-yet for longer than the black teeth have been unweathered, which is longer than anything you have a word for.

## The tick

Rarely — you might get one in a week, or none — a tick. Like a fingernail on ice, from a long way off. Once. Never twice.

That is it. That is the only sound the resident makes. Somewhere the flatness slips by some vanishing fraction, and for an instant something is very slightly *later* than it was, and there is a tiny sharp noise like the first crack running in a pane, and then the city catches it and takes it back, and there is no second tick because a second tick would be a sequence, and a sequence is time, and time is the one thing forbidden here.

It is never twice. Hold onto how frightening that is.

You are the only event. You brought weather in under your ribs, the warmest and most disorderly object for a kilometre in every direction, and the basin is already correcting for you, quietly, everywhere, and will finish erasing you a few centimetres behind your heels.

Do not break anything. Whatever you break stays broken exactly as you left it, forever, sharp — and you have seen what this place does with a break it cannot let go of.
