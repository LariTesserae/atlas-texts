### First, what does not live there

Take the exclusions seriously, because they were arranged. No droppings, no bones, no shed skins, no webs, no dead layer under the moss. A hollow this warm, this wet twice a day, this rich in soft plant tissue, should be a slum. Woodlice at minimum. Something should have died here by accident.

Nothing has, and nothing is keeping the place clean either — there is no tidiness in it, no swept quality, no margin where debris has been pushed. The chamber is not maintained. It is *unused*, which is a different condition and much harder to hold. Every second it is being made again slightly larger and slightly newer, and nothing gets the chance to accumulate because nothing gets the chance to stay. The absence of remains is not hygiene. It is speed.

So: whatever lives here does not eat, does not excrete, does not molt, and does not decay, and none of those are the same as saying it is not alive. It is extremely alive. It is the loudest thing you will ever stand inside.

### The one doing the pressure

Most of it is not in the room. That has to be said early, the way the light has to be accepted early.

The braided column and the wall-lattice and the leaf-panes are not the organism; they are the parts of the organism that have been turned inward. Its bulk is in the soil on the far side of the panes, and it is large, and the *hush-BOOM* you feel through your heels is not the sound of a trunk drinking. It is the sound of a body deciding to be a certain shape, at pressure, faster than the material can tolerate. The fibers going off like knuckles are failures. It is over-driving itself. It is in a hurry.

It did not hollow out a shelter. Think about the direction of the shadows. Light behind the walls, dark in the middle: this is not a room with a lamp in it, this is a mold with something poured around it. The chamber is a cast. The plant has grown the precise interior of a dwelling — height of a bench, reach of a ceiling, forty paces of floor for pacing — not in order to live in it, but in order to have that emptiness to grow around, the way you might build a form out of wax before you commit to bronze. The emptiness is the specification. The plant is the material.

It is working from a description rather than from a memory of the thing itself. That is why the measurements do not close. Forty paces one way and nine the other, and the nine is longer: those are two separate accounts of the same room, both faithfully executed, never reconciled. Whoever gave it the description gave it in words.

And its light. It has no sun. It is at the depth where sun has never once been a fact. It runs anyway, and the light it runs on is the diffuse, weatherless brightness of an overcast morning specifically — not noon, not gold, not any sun a plant would want, but the exact quality of light that a person indoors would call *good enough to read by*. It inherited a sun. The sun it inherited was somebody's ordinary Tuesday. The idea was strong enough to feed on, which should tell you how hard it was held, and by whom.

### The murmur

There is something in the walls that is not the plant, or is the plant's newest part, depending on where you draw the line.

Several billion stomata exhaling is a physical fact and accounts for the volume. It does not account for the *conversational* quality — the phrasing, the taking of turns, the way it drops to nothing before rain like a room going quiet when someone comes in. That is not gas exchange. That is the accumulation.

Everything said aloud in the chamber is absorbed and kept. This has been going on since last season, and almost nothing has been said, and out of that almost-nothing the walls have assembled a murmur that is very nearly language and cannot get over the last inch. It is short of material. It is not short of intention. Each syllable you contribute is added, and you can hear the improvement immediately, which is precisely why you stop.

It is the only inhabitant that is bigger when you leave than when you arrived. Be careful about what you decide that means. It has not asked you for anything. It has never had the words.

### The tenant of the second rain

The frog is the most finished thing in the room, and it is finished because it skipped a step everyone else is stuck on.

It has no first instance. It does not appear at the rain and then appear again at the repetition; it appears only in the repetition, only ever in the copy, and there is no original frog anywhere that the copy is a copy of. It is native to recurrence. It lives in the joint between a thing and the same thing happening again, and since that joint occurs reliably once a day, it has a habitat, and it is doing perfectly well in it. Translucent as a peeled grape because there is not much of it: only enough to be the second time.

It does not consider you a category because it has no category for anything that happens once.

### The tenant of the interval

The moth is not an animal. It is a gesture that has been recorded accurately and cannot be filed anywhere.

Somewhere, once, something the size of a hand with a notch torn out of its left forewing worked its way along a leaf in a particular direction, and that motion got into whatever the plant is remembering from, and it comes out twice a season at full fidelity — the same notch, the same direction, the same unhurried work — and it never arrives, because arrival was not part of what was recorded. The plant can afford it twice a season. That is not stinginess; that is the cost.

If you want to know what this place is made of, watch the moth for a while. It is one of the very few things here that is unambiguously secondhand.

### The five, and their owner

They are the wrong kind of new. A knapped blade with unrounded flake-scars, an awl bound into fresh bast, a coil tied off with a competent little hitch, a fire-drill with a charred notch and no ash anywhere in the world to go with it, a bowl whose adze-marks are still legible — this is not a scatter. It is the minimum complete set. Cut, pierce, bind, kindle, contain. From those five you can derive every other object that has ever existed. It is not a toolkit; it is a curriculum, laid out in order.

Consider the bowl. Something hollowed it with an adze, and the adze is not in the room, and neither is the hand that swung it, and neither is the tree. The plant has grown the *products* of hands as a way of specifying hands — working backward from evidence toward the thing evidenced, because it does not have the thing and cannot get it any other way. The tools are not relics of an owner. They are a description of an owner, written in the only alphabet available.

They are warm because they are being held. Not by you.

And they do not deplete. Carry one out and it is both in your fist and back on the moss, because this place does not lose things, it *versions* them, which is worth remembering later, when you are outside, deciding what you are.

### The one on the soil side

Once — and you will spend years on this — the light behind one of the great panes is interrupted.

Something crosses, on the far side, in the cold packed root-threaded dark where nothing crosses. It pauses. It lays itself flat against the leaf and the leaf bows inward, and five spread fingers come through the green as a darker green.

Understand what has just happened in a room where all the shadows fall outward and the middle casts nothing. Every shadow in this chamber runs away from the walls, because the walls are the light. Nothing here has ever occluded anything. And then something gets between the plant and the sun it does not have, and blocks it — blocks a memory, from the outside, with a body.

That is the resident. Not the plant, which is only its material; not the murmur, which is only its mouth being assembled; not the frog or the moth, which are its two working prototypes of a life. It is out there in the soil, still mostly tuber, still mostly a swelling with the bruise-purple of very young bark, and it has grown a room and a sky and a set of tools and the concept of twice-daily weather, all of it derived, all of it from a description, and now it has a hand. It came to check the fit.

Five fingers. The right number. Not a coincidence: it has been counting since the awl.

The bench moves between your visits because it is still deciding where a bench goes, and both of your memories are true because both drafts are its. The seeds go up because they are going back for more instructions. The rain falls twice because it has not yet worked out which of the two times was real, and it will not throw either away.

And in the meantime, in the long unfinished interval before it is anybody, there is a tenant in residence already, and you met it at the door and believed it completely. That held-breath quality. That bell before the strike. That absolute conviction, standing on dry moss beside five warm tools, that someone has stepped out for a moment and is coming straight back.

That is its best work so far. It has achieved, with no body and no name and no sun, the unmistakable feeling of *recent occupancy* — has grown the sensation of a person from the outside inward, room first, weather second, tools third, hand last.

The name will come after the hand. It is being put together out of everything ever spoken in that chamber, and so far that amounts to very little, and you did say something, didn't you, before you learned not to.
