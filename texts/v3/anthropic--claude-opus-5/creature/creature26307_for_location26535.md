## Census

Seven kinds of animal. One thing that is not a plant and never was. One thing that is only waiting.

That is the whole population, and the survey you have been given lists nine kinds of machinery instead, which is an honest mistake and a useful one. The functions in that lake are real. The categories are not. Three of the listed machines are one animal at three ages. Two more are the same animal in two moods. The most important organism in the crater is described in a single paragraph as *varnish*, and the second most important is described as clear water.

Names first, because the industrial names are doing damage. Call them the felt, the strikers, the gardens, the threads, the ribbon, the loose, and the rust. Then the waiter, which is not really a resident so much as a tenant with an older lease.

## The felt

It was here before the mountain.

Somewhere under the vent there was an aquifer, and in that aquifer, in the dark, at the temperature of a cellar, a consortium of things had been oxidizing iron and manganese and fixing carbon out of dissolved gas for a length of time that has no useful comparison. The eruption boiled that water and threw it four kilometers into the air, and some fraction of a percent of what was in it came down again alive.

That is the felt. It is not one organism; it is a working arrangement between perhaps forty, so intergrown that separating them kills the argument as well as the participants. It is blue-black because of manganese oxide, purple-black where the iron wins. It does not want the light. It tolerates the light the way a shut-in tolerates a window.

And it eats rock. Genuinely eats it — pits the glass, threads into it, follows the flaws inward three centimeters and hollows the interior into a foam. Its rate is about the width of a fingernail's growth per decade. Nothing in the lake could break fresh volcanic glass; the magnetite fists cannot dent unetched basalt any more than your knuckles could. **Every square centimeter that the hammer beds are quarrying was rotted first, from inside, by something that took forty years to do it.** The pavement of ten thousand striking animals is following a chemical front it cannot perceive and did not cause, at the front's speed, forever.
