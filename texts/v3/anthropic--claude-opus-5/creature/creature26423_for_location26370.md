## The Rind

Begin at the bottom, with the only living thing here that has no opinion about noise.

The rinds are not one organism and never have been. Pull a hand's width of black felt from a seep-joint and you are holding a stratified city: a top skin of sulfur-oxidisers, oily, iridescent in no light at all; beneath it a brown paste of iron-eaters; beneath that a wet grey layer that lives on the excretions of the two above and on nothing else, and beneath that the sour interface where the deep breath of the stone comes up and everything that matters happens in a film three millimetres thick.

The rind cannot move, cannot flee, cannot signal, and does not need to. It is eaten continuously and totally, every seam scraped bare every four to six days, and it regrows because the seam keeps breathing, and because there is always some of it lodged too deep in the pore-throats for a file to reach. It is the only innocent thing in the basin. Everything else in this account is, one way or another, a way of arriving at the rind.

## The Grain

Three millimetres. Hard, dark, six-legged, with a wing-case that never opens and a pair of flight membranes that unfold from beneath it like a pocket turned out. They hatch in the wet mat, they moult once, they rise, they mate in ropes and knots in the middle air, and they fall. The falling is not death, mostly; it is how they travel. Their whole existence is a slow settling with occasional interruptions.

They have no voice and no fear, and these two facts are the same fact. Nothing that is uncountable needs to see. A grain does not know where it is. A grain does not need to know where it is; it needs only to be one of a hundred million, and the hundred million always ends up in the bowls, because everything in this basin ends up in the bowls.
