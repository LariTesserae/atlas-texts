## The People

Forty-three. The Room was built for two hundred and ninety; it held two hundred and twelve in the sixties and has been falling since, not catastrophically, just steadily, the way a cistern falls when the inflow is a fraction under the draw. Nobody has ever announced this. It is in the ledger, one number per year, and anyone can add it up, and everyone has.

They are one family in the technical sense and about nine in the sense that matters. Eleven households. Six children. Four people over seventy, one of them ninety-one. Twenty-two men, twenty-one women, though the register that governs continuance uses a different set of categories and nobody confuses the two lists.

They call themselves nothing. There is no word in use for *the people of the Room* because there has never been a second group to distinguish them from. When they need the concept they say "everyone," and it is accurate.

---

**Bodies.** Thick through the chest and the thigh, light in the arm, with the particular knotted calf that comes of climbing ninety-two metres of switchback several times a day from the age of four. They walk with a short stride and a low centre and they do not look at their feet, because the stairs are wet and looking down is what makes you fall. On the stairs they touch the wall with the back of the left hand every third or fourth step, not for balance — for information. The wall tells them where they are in the dark, and whether the wall has changed.

Pale, but not sunless-pale; there is a copper undertone that comes up in the fourth week after the Slot Sun. For fifty-one minutes near midsummer the direct beam crosses the upper Room and the whole town goes up to level seven and stands in it in rotation, shoulder to shoulder, eyes shut, and burns. The burn is a marker. You can date a photograph — they have no photographs — you can date a memory by whose neck was red.

They run hot. Resting metabolism high enough that a stranger would call them feverish; they eat about twice what their mass suggests and they are cold anyway, in the corridors, always, and have stopped mentioning it. Nineteen degrees indoors feels to them like a bath. When they come in from the levels they undress in the doorway as a reflex, coat and outer trousers onto the hook, before greeting anyone. It is rude to speak to a person who is still in their outer layer. They are considered to be still outside.

**Hands.** The wall opens skin. Blueschist edges, fresh fracture faces, garnet — every adult in the Room has between forty and two hundred white hatch-marks on the forearms and the backs of the hands, and a first wall-cut is administered, by accident but not really by accident, before the age of five. It is on the outside of the left forearm and it is the only mark anyone is sentimental about.

Fingertips split from the wet and the cold. They seal the splits with a grey wax kept in flat tins, and the smell of that wax — mutton-fat and something resinous from the vats — is the smell of a person, more than sweat is. Nails thick and short. Grip strength absurd. Everyone can hold a body weight one-handed on a rail, and everyone has, because the rails on level five are the only thing between the bridges and a ninety-metre drop into sorting talus, and the rails are inspected but the mountain ticks.

**Ears, and names.** Four hundred and six threads of water, four hundred and six pitches. A child learns them before letters and can name two hundred by the age of nine. An adult knows all of them and can tell you, without turning around, that Gorget has gone half a tone flat, which means a crystal boundary opened on the east wall between levels three and four, which means someone will be up there with a light before the shift ends. The threads are the clock, and the diagnostic, and the census.

They are also the names. When a child is born, the ledgerist assigns it a thread — one newly voiced that year, or one lately retired, or the one that was audible in the room where the birth happened. The child carries that thread for life: keeps its gauge, logs its flow on the ninth day, is answerable for it.
