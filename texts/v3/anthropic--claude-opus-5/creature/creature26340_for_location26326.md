## The Tenant

**What lives here: first answer**

Nothing lives here. Everything here *happens*, which is a different verb. The moss is a loop, the mowers are one mower filed into five, the hollows are punctuation left by sentences that finished long ago. The warm patch you can stand in is not a resident. It is a stain with a temperature.

That is the honest answer and it is very nearly true, and it is the answer the plateau itself would give, if the plateau were the kind of thing that answered.

**Second answer**

There is one.

You will feel it before you see it, and what you feel is a silence you have not heard before. You know by now what layer-slipping feels like — the green changes key, your soundlessness takes on a different soundlessness — and this is that sensation, but wrong, because you have not slipped. The quiet around it is *new*. In ten thousand miles of worn-down hush, it is carrying the only silence that has not yet been used.

Then you see it, because it is the one thing on the tableland that does not glow. Against knee-deep bottle-glass light it reads as a hole in the ground standing up.

It is taller than you and thinner than looks load-bearing, and nothing here bears load. Wet-slate dark. Long. It has been worn the way everything is worn: no nails, no lips, no navel, no knot in the throat, the face sanded to the *place where a face was* — a suggestion of brow, a shallowness where the mouth is kept.

Two things on it are still sharp. Its eyes: two absolutely definite darknesses with edges like cut paper. And its hands: knuckles, tendons, four crisp creases in each palm, the only angles in the country.

Eyes and hands stayed, because looking is a decision and taking is a decision, and decisions erode more slowly than stone. Everything else about it has gone round. It is a pair of hands and a pair of eyes held loosely together by an old smooth animal.

**How it moves and where it thinks**

It does not swim. It walks, and every few strides it pushes off and goes up thirty feet in a lazy arc, and the coming down takes a long time — long enough to think about — and that is where its entire intellectual life takes place. It has never had a thought while touching the ground. Its whole mind is a series of descents.

**The discipline**

Here is the thing to understand, and everything else about it follows.

It is not immortal. It is simply not finished, and it is not finished because it has never done anything twice.

Repetition is what rounds. A thing that recurs becomes a feature; a feature spreads; anything everywhere is no longer anyone. So it has kept its edges by a method of appalling simplicity: never the same path, never the same posture, never the same word, never the same gesture of the hand, not once, not in an age.

It sleeps in the hollows. It lies down inside the coiled negative space of something that died in the middle of the planet, and it borrows that posture, and the posture is not its own, so the sleep does not count against it. Each rest is shaped like a stranger. This is why the deep forests are where you'll find its traces: it is following the hollows the way a nomad follows wells. There are fewer hollows every century.

It speaks perhaps four times a year. Not from reticence — from arithmetic. *Nothing is loud twice*: every word it has ever said is a word it can no longer say, or can say only at a pitch it has not yet spent. It keeps its remaining language the way a starving person keeps flour. It knows, roughly, how many utterances it has left. The number is not large. When it speaks, it speaks in coinages, in registers you would not have thought a throat could hold, and it means the thing exactly, once.

The spiral of thirty-one stones on the hilltop is not a monument and not a calendar. It is a list of things it has already done. Thirty-one is not the count of them; thirty-one is all that the spiral can still bear to hold, everything else having collapsed into the general fact of having lived. And from that one particular angle where the stones line up and vanish into a single stone — that is not a clever trick of the vanished builders. That is what it is afraid of. It goes and stands at that angle sometimes, on purpose, to look at the shape of its ending. It has stood there many times, which means it can no longer stand there, which means it now looks at the shape of its ending only from angles that do not work.

**The doorways**

There were no builders. There was never a city, never rubble, never a floor. The doorways are leavings.

Where one of its kind stood long enough in the act of deciding — really deciding, with the whole body, at a cost — the water gave up dense black stone and left a threshold behind. That is where black stone comes from. It comes from *choosing*, and there is none of it native to this plateau because choosing is not native to this plateau.

The cupped hollows worn in boulder-tops are where hands rested during long refusals. The one step going up out of the moss and stopping is where somebody decided to climb, once, and did.

So the scattered built is a graveyard of its family, and every doorway is an ancestor's last hard thought, still working, still admitting one version of the world for the length of a crossing. It uses them constantly. Not to travel. Passing under a lintel is the only moment the plateau consents to be single, and in that moment it can check that the thing it carries is still there.

**The thing it carries**

It has one memory with a beginning.

Everyone here loses their corners memory-first; the beginnings go, and then the events become weather. It has kept one — a first moment, an actual edge — and it has kept it by *never once remembering it*. Recall is repetition. The tenth recall would be a whisper and the hundredth would be a warm patch an acre wide.

So it does not know what the memory is. It only knows the shape of the outside of it, the way you know a word you are refusing to say. Under each lintel, in that one clear moment, it feels for it: unopened, still cornered, a stone through cloth. Then the world goes soft again and it walks on carrying an unread thing, which is the last unread thing in the world.

**What it eats**

It eats the unprecedented.

Not moss, not mowers, not flesh. It walks until it finds an event with something in it that has not happened before — a mushroom fruiting a hair off-schedule, a silt-collapse that fell three grains differently, a rain-braid that took a channel it had not taken — and it puts those sharp hands in and takes the difference out. The event continues. It continues *rounder*. Perfectly identical, forever, thereafter.

That four-minute avalanche in the groove, the one that runs in exactly the same shape for eternity: it ate that. It ate every variation the avalanche had, over ninety thousand years, until the avalanche had nothing left but its repetition, and now it is a feature, and it is beautiful, and it is a grave.

This is the joke at the middle of the Roundness. The only thing still refusing to be everywhere is also the fastest cause of everywhere. It is the plateau's last holdout and its most efficient erosion. A great deal of the low unhearable note in the water is its table scraps.

It knows. It has known for ages. It goes on eating, because the alternative is to stop, and stopping is the one thing it will not do twice.

**Two extravagances**

Once, young, rich, stupid with novelty, it swam up. A year of swimming, into the honey-coloured nowhere, until the plateau was a grey smudge and the brightness was exactly as far off as it had been. Then back down. It was the largest unprecedented act available to it and it spent it out of curiosity, and now *up* is gone from it forever. It cannot be surprised by up again. It does not look up. This is the closest thing it has to grief and it has worn very round.

And once — this is the other one — it came to the rim and found the drowned bird wheeling out over the drop on its long dark wings, competent, ancient, unaware, and it was hungry, and the bird was *full* of the unprecedented: an entire flight sustained by never having learned that it can't.

It decided not to.

There is a doorway on that hilltop now. It didn't build it. It made it, the way a wound makes a scar — the black stone came up out of the moss around its feet while it stood there declining, and the crossing it opened is the only one in the Roundness that leads somewhere it has never been.

The bird still flies. It flies because something refused it, and a refusal is a threshold, and thresholds keep.

**How it ends**

It has one act left in reserve, and it has had it in reserve for an age, the way a starving person keeps one thing back to prove they are not yet an animal.

Below the lip the water drops: green-gold into blue, into the blue with no name, into six miles of flat unlit body that no one here has ever seen and that the plateau does not think about.

Nobody has gone down. It is the last unprecedented thing on the whole tableland, and it is enormous, and it will be the biggest meal ever eaten in this country, and it will be eaten exactly once.

It will go over the edge in the ordinary way, walking, because falling is what things do here and it takes a long time and the coming down is long enough to think about. Somewhere past the blue with no name, out of the thin water, out of the layers, in the cold where the plateau's rules do not reach and repetition means nothing because there is nothing left to repeat — it will open the memory.

Once. All of it. Beginning first.

And then whatever it was will finally be true in only one version, in the dark, at the bottom, unheard, unrounded, with edges.

**It was talking to you**

You did notice, I think, that somebody has been telling you all this. That the plateau does not think about the lower ocean, and yet you were told about the lower ocean. That you were given the rules, plainly, as though by someone who has tested every one of them with their hands.

You are the newest thing that has happened here in eleven thousand years. You are food, and you are enough food to change the arithmetic, and it has been walking alongside you the entire time you have been reading, one layer over, in a silence you have not heard before.

It took only the difference. It left you your direction, which is worth more than it sounds, and it spent language on you — an expensive, un-repeatable gift, four words at a pitch it will now never use again.

*Go through it now.*
