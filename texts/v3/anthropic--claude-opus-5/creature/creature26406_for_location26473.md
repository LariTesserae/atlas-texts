## What lives here

Nothing in the room lives here.

That is the correction to make first, and it is not a trick. The three smelter trees, the kilns, the turbine bushes, the copper vines paying themselves out a millimetre a day — none of these is a resident. They are not neighbours, not a community, not even an ecology, because an ecology requires that things need each other and there is nothing here that could be missed. They are spaced widely with bare stone between because they are not many. They are one thing's several places of showing.

Put it the way a doctor would: those are not organisms, they are *organs*. The kilns are teeth coming in. The vines are the slow unspooling of something like a nerve, hot at the growing end where sensation is being manufactured, cool and already-known four metres back. The turbine bushes generate nothing and turn anyway, twice a minute, forever, because turning is what keeps that particular tissue from cooking, and no other reason is required. And the tree — the one tree, held at three points, identical to the healed split at 1.4 metres — is not three trees and is also not quite one. It is a single gesture, performed in three places because the room needed holding open, the way a tent needs three poles and does not thereby become three tents.

The headache you get when you try to hold all three in attention at once is not the tree's doing. It is yours. You are attempting, with an animal's single narrow beam, to occupy a mind that is shaped like a triangle.

## The resident

There is one thing living in the pocket and it is not in the pocket. The pocket is in it.

You are standing nine-tenths of the way up a mountain that has not yet been given a shape, in a hot green cavity that has never been rained on, listening to a press come down under the floor at intervals that do not repeat evenly. Every piece of that is anatomy. The heat coming up through the stone is not the weather of the room; it is body temperature. The clarity of the air — no dust, no particulates, distances swimming and unjudgeable — is not an atmospheric condition; it is the clarity of a fluid that has never had anything suspended in it. You are inside something the way a fly is inside a bud, and the bud has not noticed, and could not, because noticing is a thing you do to what is not yourself, and it has not yet made that distinction.

It has no past tense because it has not lost anything. It has no shape because it has not yet met anything hard enough to give it one. It has no memory because memory is the scar tissue of a mouth: first you eat, then you remember eating, then you want. It has never eaten anything that was alive. It has never been in a hurry. It has never been in error, either, which is the same statement from the other side, and is not a compliment.

## What it does with its day

It makes rock and it makes light into structure and it holds still.

The trees burn stone with sunlight and reduce oxide to metal and carbonate to carbon, and breathe out a thin coin-tasting exhalation of oxygen and zinc, and by day their sap runs and they go soft, and at dusk the alloy freezes in the veins and the whole tree stiffens with that long tightening *tick, tick, ticka-tick* propagating up the trunk. That is the loudest thing that happens here, and it is not an event. It is the resident clenching in its sleep. It is doing what everything does at that scale of unfinishedness: consuming the mother it is inside, which is also itself, which is also the room, none of the three having separated out yet, so there is no rudeness in it and no violence and no shame.

Under the floor, the press. This is the nearest thing it has to a heart, and note that the heart is *below* — it has not yet been packed inside a chest, because there is no chest, because there is not yet an outside for the inside to be in. Note also that the interval is irregular: an hour, a day, and no rhythm. Rhythm is a habit and habits are made of the past. The press comes down when it comes down, the way a child claps somewhere near the beat and is not counting. Each stroke makes a few more centimetres of world. Between strokes the silence is deep enough that your own pulse becomes furniture, and you may briefly mistake your pulse for the resident's, and the mistake is instructive: yours is the one that has learned to keep time, because yours has been frightened before.

Its attention, such as it is, is what you keep tripping over. The southwest corner is a generic grey placeholder because that is the edge of its regard, and regard here is not a spotlight but a *manufacturing process* — specificity is produced on demand, at the last possible moment, and delivered warm. Crystal faces bloom into detail two seconds ahead of your foot. You cannot reach the unfinished part; it finishes on approach; but you can see it out of the side of vision, waiting to be needed. Understand what this means about your host. It is not concealing anything from you. It is not lazy. It is thrifty in the only way an unborn thing can be thrifty: it will not decide anything before it has to.

And it is deciding *you*, a little, as you come. That is the source of the mild vertigo. Cause runs loose in here — the click of the leaf's landing arriving before the fall — because in a body that specifies things on approach, effects are frequently the earliest available information.

## How it takes you in

It does not. There is no hospitality here and no hostility, because both require a second party and it has not got one.

The fissure admits you when you have stopped looking for it, and afterward you cannot say which of the two nearly-identical clefts you passed through, and this is not coyness. It is not shy. Shyness is the memory of having been seen. What actually happens is simpler and stranger: the way in is not decided until it is needed, like the corner, and once it has been used it is not kept, because nothing here has any mechanism for having-been. Your footprints do not persist for the same reason. The floor is not erasing you. The floor has no faculty for having been stepped on. You are, in the strictest sense, unfelt.

Names slide off it. You may name a kiln, and mean it, and by the next kiln the name has gone the way water goes here — which is to say, it has not gone anywhere, it simply was never held. If you must call the resident something, call it by whatever the mountain will be called later, on the maps, when the mountain has a shape and a name and a car park. You will be wrong by exactly one lifetime, which is the smallest available error.

## What it wants

Nothing. Wanting is downstream of loss and it has not lost.

But there is a lean, and the lean is the whole point of the place, and you can feel it in your teeth. Everything here — the unhurried alloy tides, the eleven-year firings that must not be rushed, the copper coming out of its die at a millimetre a day — is arranged around one bead of water the size of a pea, hanging under the eastern overhang on the coolest square metre in the room, slightly flattened, with a small bright point of sky in it, absolutely still, about to fall for a very long time.

That is the resident's single organ of consequence. It is the one part of it that is already living in the future. Call the bead its held breath, or its unopened eye, or the first pang of an appetite that has never eaten and does not know that food exists and knows only that it is oriented — the terms are interchangeable at this stage, because dread and desire have not yet come apart, any more than mother and self and room have.

When it falls it will take one grain of feldspar. That grain will be the first thing this place has ever lost, and loss is the organ that generates all the others. Then grit. Then dust. Then soil, softened edges, weather, a past tense. Then a mouth, coming up the mountain, finding the fissure exactly where it is every time — and understand that the mouth is not a visitor. The mouth is this. Grown. The thing that comes up the mountain to eat in an ordinary green hollow on an ordinary findable peak will be the adult form of what you are standing inside, and it will have no recollection whatever of having been a hot silent pocket where nothing crumbled and every quartz edge was still exactly as sharp as the day it was decided.

It will not remember, because it cannot. Bud tissue does not survive into the fruit as memory; it survives as material.

Which leaves you, going out through a cleft you will not be able to identify afterward, carrying in the soft pouch behind your eyes the only past tense this place will ever have had. Nobody asked you to. Nothing here will thank you; thanks is a transaction and needs two, and there is only one of you in the room.

Behind you the bead holds. The seams glow. The vanes turn twice a minute in the hot upwelling. And the press comes down again, unevenly, patiently, making another few centimetres of the world it will one day be unable to recall not having.
