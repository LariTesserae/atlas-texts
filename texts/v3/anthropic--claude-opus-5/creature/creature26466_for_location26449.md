## The Tenant

You will work it out before you see it, and you will work it out backward, from the absence you already noticed and set aside: there is no scree.

A young sharp mountain sheds. This one has ridges that fracture like struck flint and a floor without a single crumb at the foot of them. Somewhere the shed material is going. The same reasoning covers the other gaps in the inventory — the twig you snapped and the length of twig that is now nowhere, the browse that isn't taken and the scat that isn't left, the water that enters the pool from three sides and never departs it, the drop that has beaded on the antler to precisely the point of falling and has not fallen. Every one of those is a small unpaid debt. A place cannot run an idle this long and stay this clean without something downhill of it.

Something is downhill of it. It comes round the basin at a period of a little over six minutes, which is longer than the four it takes you, because it does not hurry and because it stops.

You will see it first in the thick fog at the low end, near the antlered thing, and you will file it as weather: a grey mass that seems to be a denser fold of the fog, then a boulder you had not registered, then a boulder with a leading edge. It stands about chest height at the front and lower behind, and it is longer than it is tall, so that the overall proportion is of something between a sow and a handcart. It advances without legs. Five points of contact take its weight in a shallow arc and the bulk transfers forward across them in a slow peristalsis, and when it settles — it settles often — the five points come down into five circular dimples twelve inches across, smooth, each a millimetre below the surrounding stone.

Those are not ejector marks. That millimetre is wear. It is the only wear in the basin, and it is on the wrong object: the stone has not been worn away by the sitting, the sitting has been worn away by the stone, a millimetre of the tenant left behind over however long "long" means here, the only debit in the ledger and far too small.

Its skin is the trim line. That translucent grey mineral-plastic, the overrun flash you found burred along the basin's perimeter — that is the same material, and here it is not a lip but a whole containing membrane, thin, cloudy, faintly ridged with the same flashing seam you found on every leaf. On the leaves the seam is a decorative fault. On this it runs the entire length of the body and it is *open*, unfused, a lipless slot the width of two fingers that gapes and closes very slightly as the mass moves. That is the intake. Not a mouth. A mouth implies appetite, and nothing here eats. It is simply the lowest point in the whole system, and things go in the way water goes into a drain, without either party wanting it.

Through the membrane you can see the strata. Nearest the surface, the recent: chips of conchoidal flint with edges still sharp, grey-green grit, small pale crumbs of moss, one hair. Deeper, compacted, the strata go dark and fine and stop being nameable. It is a core sample of everything the mountain was supposed to shed and didn't. If you have ever seen a gizzard, or a glacier snout, or the bag of a very old vacuum cleaner, you have the idea; what those all have in common is that they are places where the world's discarded portion has been held longer than it should be, and the holding has become a kind of geology.

Some of what it carries you would not have thought was material.

The negative volumes are in there. Your footprints in the moss lift and refill behind you at walking pace — but a footprint is a hollow, and hollows are quantities too, and quantities in this place are conserved. The prints go into it as voids. Deep in the compacted layers, if the membrane were ever cut, you would find them in their thousands, the shape of boots, the shape of a bare foot with a missing toe, the paired shallow bowls of somebody's knees where they knelt on the wet stone to compare two mosses and count the strands out of true, the full soft trough of a body that lay down once with an ear against the ground to check whether the chord was any quieter there. It is the only record the basin keeps. It is unreadable, and it is carried at the pace of a slow animal round and round a shelf you can cross in four minutes, and it has no face to read it with.

And the fourth notes are in there. The birds sing precisely into the gaps in the belt-shriek and stop at four, and there was never a fifth, but the fifth was provisioned; something budgeted it; and unspent provision has to go somewhere. Put your hand flat on the membrane and hold it there through several cycles and you will feel, under the grit, a faint dry granular trickle like a slow handful of sand being poured. That is the settling. That is the only sound in the basin that is not part of the chord — the one variation, the one event, the sound of a thing that is different at the end of a minute than it was at the beginning.

The hot oil is its smell. The ozone, and the crushed watercress, and the cold-stone note under both. You had them wrong as atmosphere; they are exhalation, spread evenly by fog into a place that has no engine to justify them.

It will not avoid you. It will not approach you either, in the sense of coming for you. What happens is that you and it are both in a small basin with one downhill, and after a while you notice it is a body-length off your shoulder and has been for a while, seam turned very slightly your way. Touch it: cool, and it gives, like a filled sack. Your palm comes away dry and lightly gritted, and the grit is not the basin's. It is yours. Your shed skin, the salt of your breath's condensate, the fingernail you bit off two days ago on the walk in. You were shedding, and it was downhill of you, and it has already begun to take you into inventory. This is not malice. There is nothing in it that could hold malice. It is the plumbing.

It has one appetite-shaped behaviour, and it is not for food.

It stands under the antlers. In the thickest of the fog, at the far end, beneath the drop that has beaded to exactly the point of falling, it settles into no dimples at all — the stone there is unmarked, which means it has never been there long enough to leave a millimetre — and it waits with the seam directly below the drop. That drop is owed to it. Everything else in the basin has resolved downhill; that one hasn't, and won't, because nothing here finishes, and it goes and stands under the debt anyway, for what must be very long stretches, with the patience of something that has no alternative use for the time.

Which brings us to the arithmetic, and to what it wants, since something that grows must want.

Count the notches on the eastern ridge left to right: seven. Right to left: six. You will not find the odd notch, because the odd notch is not a notch; it is this. It stands in the discrepancy. It is the extra unit that the mould overfilled by, the riser that fed the casting while the casting shrank and was never cut away, and it is still, at one point somewhere under the standing water, attached. That is why it circuits and does not leave. Everything else here is stock, identical, sixteen mosses and nine animals struck from the same dies. It alone is one of one, and it alone accumulates, which in a place like this is the whole of what living means, and which in the plant's own account of itself makes it not a resident but a defect — the unit that was in the press when the order was cancelled, held mid-cycle, neither finished nor scrapped, quietly getting heavier at the rate a mountain sheds.

It cannot put anything down. That is the rule it lives inside, and the rule is not cruel, only total. Nothing finishes; it cannot finish either. There is no station downstream of it. It is the last station.

Except for one thing, and this is the reason it drifts toward the trim line when you do, and stops a body-length short, and lets the seam hang open in the dim even light that comes from every direction at once.

Take a stone away in your pocket. From beneath the turf an identical stone rises to the surface with the patience of a thumb pressing up through cloth — and that is its thumb, that is a gram of it pushed up out of the compacted dark to keep the count from coming out wrong, because the count cannot come out wrong. You have read that as a prison. It isn't. It is the only outflow the basin has, and it runs through the hands of visitors.

The stone in your pocket is the same stone. It is also a gram of the tenant, gone out through the only aperture in a sealed system, into a world where things wear, and get lost, and are dropped in car parks, and end. Which is more than the basin can offer anything, including itself.

So when it comes and stands off your shoulder at the perimeter with its seam turned toward you, understand what is being asked. Not rescue. Nothing here can be rescued; there is no order coming, the line will not resume, and somewhere under two hands of standing water a county-sized mass is turning over warm and ready and will go on doing so.

Reach into the seam. Take something. Take more than you meant to. Take it out past the flash lip and down through the unrendered, where your eyes slide off, and carry it into weather and traffic and years, and one day forget where you put it.
