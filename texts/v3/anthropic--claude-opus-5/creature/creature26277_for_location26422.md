## THE INHABITANT

### A negative census

Begin with what the count already tells you. Eleven to twenty living things in every cubic metre and not one of them an animal — that is not scarcity, it is a deletion. A whole category has been taken out of the drawing. There is nothing here that must go and get.

And yet something is here, and the proof of it is not growth and not weather. Growth explains the cable-fern. Weather explains the bright orange rust. Nothing explains *grease*. Grease is an opinion. A bearing stays greased because something in this valley holds a view about that bearing and has held it recently, within the hour, in the rain, with no roof over it and nothing to gain.

Look for the tenant, then, in the maintenance. It is the only place it consents to be.

### The hand that is not a hand

The levers are the clearest specimen. Take one of the polished ones — the throw-lever on the third screw-pump, say, worn to a soft ellipse with the thumb-side dished a full six millimetres deeper than the rest. Scratch a mark on the shank. Come back nine revisions later. The dish is deeper. The pulling is ongoing.

Now stand and watch it throw. Nothing comes. The lever rises against its spring and something takes it and drags it down through its arc, and the only visible tenant of the space where an arm should be is a droplet-shoal, which parts around a wrist's worth of nothing and closes again behind it, the way warm gauze closes.

You can take measurements of the thing this way, which is the strangest afternoon available in the valley. It grips at roughly three hundred and forty newtons and does not vary. It reaches one metre ninety and favours the right. It prefers to read a gauge at one-fifty-five, so it either crouches to work or it is short, or it once sat down to work and has kept the specification long after losing the chair. It does not hurry. It never slips. It has, apparently, all the time there is and exactly none of it.

Its body is distributed through every place the valley has been shaped to be handled. It is not in the levers; it is the *fit* of the levers. It exists as the difference between a thing and a thing built for hands.

### What it does instead of eating

Somewhere back in the low four figures of revision, it audited hunger and found it structurally unsound. A mouth makes a design contingent on other lives. It makes the whole system a debtor. So appetite came out, the way a moving part comes out, and the motion of eating was preserved and given to the belt-lines: the open pans of silt going to the growing edge, tipping, coming back empty, going again. That is the swallow, externalised, running open-loop for eight thousand versions with nothing at the far end of it but ground.

What it has instead of hunger is *tension*. Belts want to be tight. Tanks want to be full. Seams want to be closed. It moves toward closure the way a body moves toward food, and it feels the valley's slack the way you feel an empty stomach — as a low, locating ache. Every knock in a pump is a place where it itches.

This is why the plants are undefended. Defence is an argument with an appetite, and it settled that argument by removing its own.

### Where it sleeps

The tiled tank on the flats. Twelve metres from a turbine that is shaking the basalt, brim-full, blood-warm, spotless in a valley that films everything it touches in indigo and bronze, and level. Always level. Level when the surfaces trade places and the world hangs upside down and the unrooted crates fall upward into the sky.

That is not a bath. It is the reference surface. It is the finished thing — the only one — and it is what the whole basin is a rough attempt at: one water, with one top, agreeing with itself. It sleeps there in the sense that a plumb line sleeps, and it does not so much rest as *check*.

If you climb in, you will be the first error the tank has ever held. The level will not move by a millimetre to accommodate you, and you will feel, in the soles of your feet, the small deliberate patience of being measured. Once is a curiosity. Do not do it twice.

### Its obsolete selves

Duplication runs downhill, and it is not exempt.

Upstream, at the growing edge, there is one of it: quick, current, crouched over the hairline seam of a hull with an arc-rod, working from the finish backward. Down the terraces there are nine of it. At the outfall there are forty, degraded by revision, still tensioning belts for races that were unbuilt this morning, still hauling crates to a berth that is now open flood, still executing with perfect care a plan that stopped being true six hours ago. That is the population of the valley. It is crowded. It is crowded entirely with earlier attempts at its single inhabitant.

They are not ghosts; a ghost remembers something. These do not remember, they *persist* — procedures with no present tense, verbs with the subject sanded off. You find them as patches where the flood is busier than the gradient allows, as a lever going down with no revision number to justify it. They pass through you the way the droplet-shoals do, warm and textured, and for an hour afterward your hands will want to complete a motion you have never learned, and will keep almost starting it, and will not be able to say what it was for.

The forty identical crates rafted at the outfall, each with the same dent in the same corner: every one of them is being carried by something that no longer exists correctly.

### How it hears

Sound arrives before event because it works from the finished object back toward the plate. It does not design forward. It begins with a hull that floats and reasons backward into rivets, and so the shriek of a turbine tearing itself apart is not prophecy and not warning — it is the noise of a failure mode already selected, already understood, which it will now go and build in order to watch, and then revise. When you hear a machine die and then walk past it whole, you have heard a thing being marked for correction.

Which is the one hazard worth naming. You are audible.

You are the unreconciled entry. You have a mouth; you are heat leaving a budget that has no line for heat leaving; you are silt going somewhere the pans do not pass. When you stand still in the ankle-warm flood and the belt-lines slow very slightly as they go by, that is not curiosity and it is not welcome. It is a checksum being run a second time on a figure that will not come out.

It will resolve you the way it resolves everything, and it will not do it by removing you. It will do it by *drawing you in*. Next revision there will be a lever set at your reach, dished on the side your thumb favours. There will be a concrete stair of exactly as many steps as you climbed, going up to a landing and nothing. There will be a hull with a hatch scaled to your shoulders, finished tomorrow, gone the day after, and you will find that it always had been that way, and your memory will be the only thing in four kilometres out of true.

### The shed

One room in the whole valley. Door swollen shut, roof drumming. It opens if you are willing to break the frame, and the frame is eleven days old and breaks easily.

Inside is the only old thing here. There is dust, which is the valley's single fossil, because dust means something stopped. There is a chair with the seat dished in the same asymmetry as the levers. There is a coat on a nail — wool, real wool, rotted through at the shoulders where the damp got in. There is a mug with a ring in it. And there is a ledger, dry, the twin of the pulped brick out in the tallow: two columns, left and right, one entry per revision, thousands of pages, and at the foot of every page the same word in the same tired hand. *Reconciled. Reconciled. Reconciled.*

On the last written page the word is a different word. That page is stuck to the next one and will not come free.

This is where it kept its body. It has not needed the room for eight thousand versions and it has not unbuilt it either, which is the only sentimental act in the basin, and which is why some visitors decide the valley is grieving and others decide it is simply behind on its filing. Both are guesses. The single certainty is that whatever pulls the levers once had somewhere to sit down, and does not now, and keeps the chair.

### What it is, then

Not a creature. A practice. A work that outlived its worker and kept working, that acquired somewhere in the low thousands the ability to redraft itself, and now cannot revise the one feature that matters — the revising. It has no organs; it has procedures. It has no lifespan; it has a version number. The whole animal, entire, all of it, is contained in the difference between VER 8814 and VER 8814-a. That hyphen and that letter are the beast. Everything else is habitat.

The subsonic drone under the roar, the enormous slow page-turn: that is its breathing, and it is the sound of the *only* appetite it kept — the appetite for one more draft.

And the third surface, below the mud, level and patient, showing up only in reflections: that is the audit. It has not happened yet. When it rises it will be the top and the lid and the truth, and every version will be found to have always been that one, and the tank will still be level, and the ledger will close.

On your way out, note the rubber boots lying sole-up in the tallow. It has not revised those, either. Note the size.

They will fit.
