## Who Is Here

### 1. The wrong plural

The first mistake everyone makes is asking *what lives here*, plural, and expecting the answer to be a list: so many species of flora, six of fauna, nothing else. You can compile that list. It is accurate. It is also the kind of accuracy you get from cataloguing a person by their hair, their fingernails, and the six things they happen to be holding.

There is one resident. Everything else in the basin is either its tissue, its staff, or its handwriting.

### 2. The six

Start with the ones that move, because they are the only ones that will ever do anything in front of you.

They are about the size of a large dog and built along the same general argument, but longer in the spine and much lower, with limbs that fold in more places than they should and a gait that has to be watched in instalments. The bones have glass in them — not the drawn fibre of the trunks, something denser and cloudier, the colour of a held breath on a window. Where the hide is thin over the shoulder you can see it faintly through, and it is not the same colour in all six.

They do not startle, and it is important to understand that this is not tameness. Startling is a purchase. They are carrying a small private allowance of duration, and every event they permit themselves — a step, a swallow, a turn of the head — comes out of it, and there is no resupply. So they spend like the very old spend: deliberately, on few things, and never on being surprised by you.

Feeding: a leaf-wafer goes into the mouth over roughly fifteen minutes. There is no chewing that you would recognise; the wafer simply becomes less, from the edge inward, and the dark metal veins come out the other side and are dropped, and lie where they are dropped forever. A borrower covers perhaps four hundred metres in a day. It takes one of them something like eleven years to walk the length of a single stroke of the figure, cropping as it goes, keeping the margin of that stroke exactly where the margin belongs.

That is the work. They are not grazers. They are the pen, still touching the page. Four centuries of maintaining a letterform by eating the parts of the forest that would have blurred it.

One of them has, fused into the glass of the left shoulder, a ring. Plain band, a metal that does not occur in the basin and does not occur for eight hundred kilometres in any direction. It is not decoration and it is not embedded the way a splinter is embedded. The bone grew around it, back when the bone was growing.

They were not always like this. Nothing here says so out loud. But there is the ring, and there is a foreleg on the smallest of them with an old break in it, badly set, and set — this is the part people do not like — with wire.

### 3. The one meeting

Each of the six keeps to a region of the figure, and the regions meet at junctions, and at a junction, roughly once a decade, two of them arrive at the same place.

When this happens they stop. They stand nose to nose, not eating, for about nine days.

That is the whole of it. No contact, no sound — sound does not go anywhere here anyway — no circling, no display. Two long low animals standing face to face in a violet half-light for nine days, spending allowance they cannot replace, on this.

The second expedition's survey lists seven borrowers. It is generally treated as an error, because every count since has found six, and because the alternative is a clock, and nobody who has been down there wants there to be a clock.

There is a clock. Six pockets of duration are being spent at a crawl, and a crawl is not zero. When the last of them stops walking, the cropping stops, and the flora — which cannot grow, but which can at last exceed their allowance if nothing holds the line — will thicken over the strokes, and the figure will go illegible, and the sentence will finally be allowed to finish.

That is what the six are for. They are the reason the resident is still in the middle of what it is in the middle of.

### 4. Handwriting

The forest is not a population. It is a script, and it is being written in the present participle, permanently.

This is why the groves cannot be surveyed as vegetation — why the density curves make no ecological sense, why there are pure stands of the brass horsetail forty metres wide with a hard edge and then nothing, why the purple-black canopy runs out mid-terrace with no change in substrate to explain it. There is no explanation in the substrate. The explanation is in the notation. The canopy is the heavy stroke; the acid-green fans are diacritics; the drifts of undecayed leaves in the hollows, metres deep, every wafer perfect, are not litter. They are the ink that has already been laid down — four hundred years of the same character being held, and held, and held, the pen pressed so long in one place that the ink has pooled.

And the whole of it — every stroke on the basin floor, at all three scales — leans.

You will not see this from inside. From the Rim, in the right part of the day (there is no right part of the day; from the Rim, always), the lattice reveals that its entire geometry is oriented. It is not a pattern with a centre the way a flower has a centre. It is a pattern with a *subject*, the way a room full of people turned toward one person is a room with a subject.

### 5. The centre

The subject is in the largest of the spaces-between-characters, a little south of the Chatter.

There is nothing there.

Not bare rock — there is bare rock everywhere in the basin and it is unremarkable. This is a patch of undisturbed grit, roughly two metres by one, with the ordinary warm plate stone under it and the ordinary fixed violet light lying on it, and every instrument anyone has carried down reads it as exactly what it looks like.

Every person who has stood on it has reported the same thing, in the same words or near enough: *I am in someone's way.*

It is the only place in the Held Basin where the stems do not bend for you. Everywhere else the corridor opens ahead of your chosen line, prepaid, four centuries old. Here they lean past you, all of them, from every direction, out to the Rim and back, the way grass lies down along a river it has nothing to do with, and you are simply standing in the line of sight of an enormous quantity of attention that is not for you and has not noticed the obstruction.

That is the resident. It is person-sized. It is not present. It has not been present for four hundred years and it has not been absent either, and the entire region — the stopped sun, the solid air, the razor Rim, the pit in the north roaring its exhaust into a sea of shale — is the apparatus by which it is prevented from finishing.

### 6. What it is doing

Something happened here, once, for about a second, to something about the size of a person. Whoever was with it did not build a tomb, because a tomb is matter and matter only slows things down. They went underneath, into the ruleset, and they took out the next moment.

Not the memory of it. Not a record of it. The moment itself: they arrested the transition, and they have been paying for that ever since, and the bill is enormous. It is paid in erosion that never happens, in weather that never blows, in a sun that has not been allowed to cross the sky since before the outer country had its current borders. It is paid, too, outward: the storms on the lee side of the Rim have always been worse than the terrain deserves, and the local people have always had a saying about it, and the saying is older than the survey.

Whether this is a reliquary or a cell is the one question the basin declines. The six are either nurses or guards or mourners; after four hundred years the distinction has worn through, except that nothing here wears through, so it is more accurate to say the distinction was never provided. They stand nose to nose for nine days once a decade. You may interpret that however you can bear to.

### 7. Its manners

You will not be greeted, because greeting requires a present tense and the resident does not have one. What it has instead is arrangement.

So the corridor of bent stems is not an act of hospitality; it is the *residue* of hospitality, laid down in advance by something that could not react and therefore had to pre-empt. Every path you might take was made room for. Every path you take to prove it wrong was made room for. It is not surveillance. There is nothing watching. It is worse and kinder than that: a host who cannot come to the door, and who therefore, long ago, left every door open, including the ones you were going to invent.

And it is at equilibrium with you, politely, the whole time you are down there, drawing off the small hot surplus of your heartbeat, your breath, your body temperature, the way it draws off everything — not out of appetite. Out of housekeeping. You are a change-spending animal wandering around inside a held moment, and it cannot permit your surplus any more than it can permit wind.

### 8. What you take home

The leaf-wafer on your shelf is not a souvenir. It is a piece of the resident's handwriting, and it is still doing what it did in the basin: not fading, not curling, not proceeding. Your house is now the far edge of a held moment. Your children will dust it.

And the memory of the corridor is not a memory of a place. You already know this. It is the memory of having been expected by something that had no way of expecting anything, and that got around this, at unbelievable cost, four hundred years before you were born — the way a person who knows they will not be conscious when the guest arrives leaves the lamp on, and the bed made, and the note on the table, and goes under.
