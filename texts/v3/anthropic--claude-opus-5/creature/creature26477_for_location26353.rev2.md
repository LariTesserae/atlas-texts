## The tenant

You will be told there is one tender per shift. This is true in the way that the wall is stone.

What lives in Cell Nine is a woman something over two hundred years old. She has had perhaps a dozen bodies and one handwriting; the book will show you the dozen hands, and the book will show you that they say the same thing, and the Freshworks does not regard the distinction between those two facts as important. Below, in the warm offices where ink is possible, this is called the continuity of the post. In the cell there is no word for it, because there is no word in the cell for anything that has an alternative.

### The body

She is dressed in felt against the cold and lead-cloth against the vibration, and none of it is sewn. Thread saws itself apart in Nine within a season; every seam is grommeted and laced, and the laces are replaced each shift, and the replacing of the laces is the closest thing in her life to a ritual, and she does not think of it as one.

Over her head goes a cage of brass wire, half-inch mesh, hinged at the jaw. Visitors are told it is against the swifts. The swifts have never touched anyone. It is against the millers, and it is for one circumstance only: if she falls, the carpet will begin on her where she lies — nine thousand blind rotary mouths to the square metre, which do not distinguish between new mineral and an eyelid — and the cage will keep her face for the ninety seconds it takes to get up. Every tender has been on the floor at least once. None of them will tell you about it, but you can tell which ones, because they put the cage on before the second lock rather than after.

Her hands are bare. They must be; no glove can read a bearing. They are wet the whole shift and just above freezing the whole shift, and the fingertips are polished — the whorls worn shallow and blue-white and faintly translucent at the edges, which is not a coincidence, and which the tenders notice in each other and never remark on. She is grazed too. Everything in Nine is grazed.

The right thumb is the only instrument in the cell that can be read. It has a callus with a memory. She can put it on the Constant's crank-bearing and know the temperature to within a degree, and she cannot explain how, and no one has ever asked her to, because asking would take shouting.

Her lungs are lined. The milk goes where breath goes. After ten years a tender's spit has a faint grit and a faint opal shine to it, and this is called the pearl cough, and it is the single exception to the first rule: something does leave Cell Nine, and it leaves inside her, and it stays. Doctrine handles this cleanly. She is not carrying the cell out. She is a nozzle that walks.

### The speech

There are forty-one signs in the knuckle tongue. The number is fixed. A forty-second sign would be a change, and the making of one is the only offence in the Freshworks for which there is no stated penalty, presumably because there has been no occasion to state it.

Two taps on the collarbone: *attend; look where I look.* A slow drag down the sternum, three fingers, very slow: *hold; it is passing; it always passes.* A grip on the wrist with the thumb turned inward: *out, now, do not ask me on the ladder.* A single knuckle laid flat between the shoulder blades and simply left there, unmoving, for as long as it takes — that is the one they use on new tenders when the noise becomes silence and the silence becomes terror. It means *breathe.* It means *this is the quiet, and it is not a hole, and I am here in it too.* It is the most-used sign in the cell and the only tender one.

The forty-first sign is an open palm laid over the ear. Its meaning is recorded in the front matter of every tally book in the Freshworks. Every tender learns it in her first week. No tender of Nine has ever made it.

### The shift

The shift is not hours. The shift ends when the lichen on the crank-cheek is one shift thick. If you point out the circularity of this the tenders will not be annoyed with you; they will simply have nothing to say, the way you would have nothing to say if someone objected that a metre is only as long as a metre.

She walks the round. Forty nozzles, each tested by holding the bare wrist in the fog and feeling the cold arrive at the right distance. Eleven combers, which is the longest part, because combers move and because eleven is easy to make out of ten when you are tired. The moss under Lamp Four, measured with a brass rule: seventy-one millimetres. The ribbon-weed in the outflow, measured against her own hand: four fingers.

Note that. Hands differ. Every tender's hand has differed, and the weed has been four fingers in all of them, and it will be four fingers in the hand that comes after hers. The standard is not the hand. The standard is the word.

Then the rag over the crank-cheek, which takes the lichen and makes the lichen possible. Then the book, in pencil, the pencil worn down to a stub and whittled with a knife — the second-most weathered object in Cell Nine. Then down.

She does not eat in the cell. Nothing leaves Nine, but also nothing arrives; a crumb would be an event. She drinks water from a stoppered flask, standing with her back to the wall, feeling the wall give very slightly behind her shoulder blades like the skin on scalded milk.

### The hand

Visitors are permitted one touch of the wall. Tenders are not forbidden it. There is no rule, because a rule would concede that the question had come up.

Every tender, once, somewhere in her career and never twice, puts her whole flat palm against the wall and holds it there and counts. The millers pour in around the heel of her hand. She lifts it away and there is a print — five fingers of absence in the pearl-bone, sharper-edged than anything else in the world — and she watches it fill and feather and go. Forty seconds. Fifty at the cold end of the room. Then the wall is exactly as it was and exactly as it will be and there is no evidence that a hand has ever existed anywhere.

It is not in the book. It is the only private thing a tender of Nine possesses. All of them have it. None has mentioned it. All of them know.

### The succession

They come up the ladder from below, one at a time, and most of them go back down inside the quarter hour, when the roar turns into silence and the silence turns on them. That is the sorting, and it is the whole of the examination.

The ones who stay are the ones who, when they are held, stop shaking, and then do something with their faces that is not quite a smile. They are people who have had a noise inside them for a long time and have just discovered a bigger one. Nobody in the Freshworks says this out loud. Everybody in the Freshworks staffs on it.

Then the hearing years, which are the hard ones. Then deafness, which is not a disqualification but a stage of the career, and which arrives without ceremony, and after which she is not spoken to again in any language but knuckles for the rest of her life.

She signs the book with a glyph of three strokes. It is not her name. She has a name below and does not use it above. The glyph has been the same three strokes since the first page of the first book, and when you look at four hundred pages of it you will find that it is the only thing in the cell that genuinely never varies, because it is made of a decision rather than of matter.

### The one weathered thing

Nothing in Cell Nine has an edge that has been rounded by anything.

Her boot-soles are worn into the shape of the grating. Her cage is polished bright at both temples where she takes it off. Her knuckles are enlarged. Her spine has taken a set from the ladder. She ages in there at something close to double rate, and a tender of twenty years' standing is a woman of sixty with a young voice she never uses. She is the sole entropy in a room designed to have none; she is the only old surface in the Freshworks; she is the one thing in Cell Nine that is actually happening.

And the room knows it. She is the only warm object in it. As she walks, the carpet leans — a fractional tilt of nine thousand blind faces per square metre toward her heat, and the upward rain of cold sparks thickens very slightly wherever she stands, and travels with her, a faint blue-green attention moving across the wall at the speed of a woman on her round.

She has never written this down. She thinks of it as being looked at. On the bad shifts she thinks of it as being loved, and then puts the thought away, because it is an adjective.

### The end of a tender

The failure mode is the adjective.

*Eleven combers. Forty nozzles. Moss at seventy-one. Weed four fingers. Swifts full. Millers even — still.*

*Still* is a change. It is a comparison; it implies a previous state, and a previous state implies that states are a thing that occur. The tender who writes it does not argue when the relief comes early. She knows what she wrote; she has been reading that book her whole adult life and she has seen it happen in other hands, twice, three pages apart, a hundred years ago. She goes down the ladder-lock for the last time and below they say she has gone to the lower works, and nobody says where the lower works are.

The other failure mode is counting the swifts. The column is recorded as *full* and has never been numbered, because a number can vary and *full* cannot. A tender who begins counting swifts has begun to believe in the future.

There is always the same number of tenders. One.

### Off shift

Three days of hum, and the hum is the four notes, and it is inside her head, and she sits in a warm room below with her blankets and cannot sleep, because down there it is actually quiet and quiet is a hole with her own pulse at the bottom of it and the small dry clicks of her jaw.

On the third day, when the hum has gone thin enough that she can hear the room around it, she gets up before anybody sends for her and goes to the ladder-lock and climbs, slowly, lips splitting in the thin air, past the mountain, which is hearsay.

The book records eleven combers, forty nozzles, seventy-one millimetres, four fingers, full, even. There is no line for the tender.

This is not an oversight. It is the highest compliment the Freshworks can pay. She is not among the things that must be checked.

She is among the things that check.
