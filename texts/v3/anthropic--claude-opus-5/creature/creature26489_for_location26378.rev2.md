## The Count

Begin with the count, because counting is the only arithmetic this place still supports, and even it is contested.

Eleven kinds of living thing. That is the figure travellers agree on, ear to mouth, one word at a time, and they agree on it the way people agree on the weather — provisionally, and in order to stop talking. Some say nine. Some say twelve. The dispute is not sloppiness. The dispute is the subject.

Here is the list, in the order you meet them, which is all at once.

The grey algal film, in flat lobes on the mud, smelling of iron. The stromatolite domes, knee-high, cabbage-textured, three billion years at their one task. A black-green brittle reed. A white mould that takes the dead reed and nothing else. A translucent jelly-pad, hand-sized, that lies on the water's skin and does not appear to do anything. A seed-shrimp, half a millimetre, in the film. A pale threadworm in the mud. A blind eel. A bird. A red speck that lives in cut stone. And you.

The argument is over the first two. The film and the domes may be one organism in two postures, which would make ten. And whether a person counts as living here, when no one has ever managed to, is the other end of the same question. Nobody can settle it, because settling it would require holding two things apart, and holding two things apart is precisely the capacity this continent has lost.

---

Now the important thing, which sounds like a small thing.

There is one of each.

Not one species. One. One reed-stand, with a broken stem at the height of your sternum, present nine million times and once. One eel. One bird, seen three times in fifty days, and the three are one. The film is arguably one sheet, folded through itself across four thousand kilometres. The threadworm may be a genuine plural — it is too small to be told apart even from itself, and travellers suspect the plain has simply given up on it as beneath notice — but nobody has proved a second worm and nobody knows how they would.

This is not scarcity. Understand that or you understand nothing. Scarcity is the pressure of a hard world on a population. Here the pressure is the opposite and it comes from the other direction. Abundance costs nothing in the Same, because presence is free: be anywhere once and you are everywhere, at no metabolic charge, forever. A creature here has no reason to be numerous. Numbers are a solution to the problem of distance and there is no distance.

And it cannot become numerous in the way that matters, either, because it cannot become *various*. Speciation needs isolation. Isolation needs somewhere to be isolated in. Two populations of anything, separated by a mountain or a strait or four hundred kilometres of dry ground, will drift apart and become two answers. Take the mountain away. Take the strait, the distance, the red rock. Now there is one population, and it is one individual, and it has been one individual for longer than there have been continents elsewhere with names.

Evolution here is in the same condition as erosion here. Not slow. Finished. Completed, past tense, the way a word said ten thousand times is finished. The eleven are what was left standing when variation ran out of room, and they are extremely good at being exactly themselves, and they will never be anything else.

---

Consider the hunt, because the hunt is the shape of everything.

The eel eats the threadworm. The bird eats the eel. That is the whole predation of a continent: two arrows, no branches.

But if the bird ate the eel, there would be no eel, and there is an eel, so the bird has never eaten the eel. The hunt has been in progress for an unestablished duration and has never concluded, because a conclusion is an event, and an event is a difference between a before and an after, and the Same does not hold differences. What it holds is postures. The eel is always in the water under the bird. The bird is always at the top of the strike. The worm is always going into the mud.

Nothing here finishes. That is the second thing to understand, after the water. The one act ever completed on this continent was the flattening, and it used up all the completion there was.

So: the eel moves in slow arcs under the water's skin, always the same arc, and it is old past any way of saying so, and it is hungry in a manner that has stopped being urgent and become a kind of weather in it. The worm is always just ahead. The mould is always partway through the reed. The jelly-pad lies there in the middle of whatever it is doing. And the stromatolites go on laying down laminae, one a day, smoothing as they go, doing the single thing they have always done — which, note, is *this*. They bind sediment. They flatten. They make one patch of mud indistinguishable from the next.

The Same is not a disaster that happened to the stromatolites. The Same is the stromatolites' idea, carried to term. The continent has become what its oldest resident already was: a colony with no members, many that is one, layered and uniform and patient and without any interior distinction whatsoever. Everything else here is living inside an old mat's opinion.

---

The bird.

Ash-coloured, tall, knees backwards, a beak like a wet blade. It is a wading bird in a world that is entirely a wade. It stands, and steps, and stands. Its eye is a yellow bead with no visible regard in it.

Its call is a low grating *kraaa* with a click at the end, and the grate and the click are both damage. Somewhere back in the unmeasurable past, that syrinx tore. It has never healed, because healing is a finish. The continental roar — the noise that makes a new arrival weep for two days from pressure, that stipples still water, that reduces human language to forty monosyllables shouted into an ear — is one elderly animal's throat injury, arriving from every point of the compass at once, nine hundred million times over, all day, without any prospect of stopping.

That is worse than the noise. But this is worse than that:

It is calling for another bird.

And it is answered. It is answered from every direction, immediately, identically, forty times a day, in its own voice with its own tear in it — and it cannot tell that the voice is its own, because telling things apart is exactly the faculty that has been removed from this world, and the bird is of this world and has no exemption. So it walks toward the answers. All of them. It has been walking toward the answers for a very long time. That is why you meet it three times in fifty days: it is always coming toward you, from all sides, and always arriving at itself.

Whether it is lonely is not a question the place can be asked. Loneliness requires a somewhere-else where the other one is.

---

Now the red speck, which is the only good news in the Same and which changes the meaning of everything built here.

It lives in the basins. Only in the basins — the shallow cut bowls on the dry tops of the platforms, four steps up, always full of rain. Nowhere else. A dot of vermilion, a rotifer or something like one, whirling its crown in eight centimetres of water that has fallen from the lid and been caught in a made hollow and has therefore only ever been in one place.

Which means the red speck has an address.

It is the only organism on this continent that does. And accordingly — travellers have noticed this, and it is the thing they talk about on the shingle afterwards, when they can talk — the specks are not all the same. Basin to basin they differ. Some are darker. Some are slower. In one basin they are said to be almost orange and to gather on the north rim, if there is a north. They differ because their basins differ, because the basins were cut by hand, and no two cuts are the same cut.

They are the only creature here that can be counted. They are the only creature here of which there is genuinely more than one. They are the only creature here still capable of becoming something.

This is what a basin is for. Not water. A *where*.

---

The built things are not architecture. They are wounds, deliberately made, in a surface that has no other way of having features.

That is the whole logic of the wall, the platform, the doorway. Six courses of unmortared grey block, forty metres, ending in nothing at both ends. Two uprights and a lintel with water on both sides. Stone carried from somewhere with a where, laid down in a place with none. Every block is a millimetre of disagreement inserted on purpose. Every notch is the same. A notch is a person saying: *here is a difference that was not here before, and I made it, and therefore I was somewhere.*

Individuation by damage. It is the only method available, and once you see it you see it everywhere in the eleven. The reed-stand is recognisable by a broken stem. The bird is recognisable by a torn throat. The specks are various because the stone was cut unevenly. Nothing here is distinguishable except by what has happened to it.

---

So. Who lives here.

Not the crossers. They are passing through, at an average of fifty days, and most turn back and a few come out on the shingle where there is a hill, an actual hill, thirty metres of unrepeatable itself, and they weep at that too.

But the crossers are not what you meet at the platforms.

You meet a figure at the basin, sitting on the dry stone with their legs in the water, cutting. Ash-coloured with dried film, hair gone the colour of the mud, forearms carrying old raised tally-marks that are not on stone. They are always mid-stroke. The stylus is always down. You have never, in any account ever brought out, seen the stroke completed and the hand lift.

And you meet, somewhere out on the smudge, a figure walking with a grey block on their shoulder. Always walking. Always with the block still on the shoulder. The wall is six courses high everywhere in the Same because the seventh course is in transit and has been since before the rivers gave up.

And if you stop walking, and stand, and let the water be blood-warm and unfelt around your shins for long enough, you will meet the third kind, which is the one the rules are really about: a person standing still in the water at knee depth, face turned slightly, not moving, and there will be another one two hundred metres away in the identical posture, and another beyond that, at intervals, to the limit of the blur. These are the ones a shear-line found. The plain is still trying to resolve them. It has been trying for a long time and it cannot, because resolution requires a difference to decide by, and standing still is how you give up your last one.

---

Here is the part the coastal people will not repeat indoors.

A life is told apart by its places. The boy is the boy because of the room he was small in; the old one is the old one because of the steps they sit on now. Take the room, the road, the distance between them. Flatten. If every where is one where, then the times of a life are no longer held apart either, and a biography is not a line but a heap.

The one carrying the block, the one cutting at the basin, the one standing still — they are the same person at three ages, superposed, and the Same has stopped being able to say which came first. That is why the notch spiral is overcut by a later spiral and that by a later one, all of them the same hand at different points in one arm's wearing-out, all of them present together, all of them going on. That is why the stone beneath the marks is worn into a shallow dish by the pressure of hands: not thousands of hands. One hand, for one life, held everywhere at once, pressing.

Whether that is one person or all of them is exactly the argument about the film and the domes, and it has the same answer.

---

They have about forty words, chosen over generations for the ones that survive being shouted into an ear through the roar. Plosives live. Sibilants do not. *Cut. Wall. Steps. Go. Wait. Drink. Down. No.*

There is one word you are told never to shout, ever, at a figure that comes at you out of a shear-line walking the opposite way in your posture with your gear. It is not a name. Names do not survive out there and nobody brings one in.

The word is *which.*

Do not help it decide.

---

Drink from the basin. It is eight centimetres of rain in a cut hollow, the only water on this continent that has been in one place, and you can feel it go into you as a kind of quieting, a narrowing, a small return of the sense of being at a specific set of coordinates and nowhere else. Leave the specks in it; they do not mind you and you are not going to harm a thing that has an address.

Then cut your notch, which is a wound, which is the only prayer there is.

The one at the basin will not look up. The stroke will not be finished. It is not that they are unfriendly. It is that finishing is not a thing this place permits, and they have been here long enough to have stopped being an exception to anything.

Go back the way you came. There is no way you came. Go anyway; motion is the only remaining evidence.

And when you are out, on the shingle, in the unbearable silence, with a real hill in front of you being nothing but itself — what you will miss is not the bird.

It is that for fifty days you were everywhere, and so was everyone, and nothing that had begun had to end.
