## The Staff

Nothing here breeds. Begin there, because it is the fact that sorts the tenants from the visitors. No eggs, no fry, no roe in the shallows, no nests in the one tree despite the birds, no rut, no season. Everything in the basin is exactly the age it was when it arrived, and it will be that age when the water finally stops standing up, if it ever does. What lives here does not live here the way a population lives in a valley. It lives here the way a hand lives on a rope it is not allowed to let go of.

They are staff. Each of them holds one number still.

**The eel** holds the number one.

It is arm's length, soft grey, the grey of a thing that has never had to be any colour. It lies along the shallows like a seam in the water rather than a body in it. It watches with its whole flank because it has no other way to make attention that large; you will look for the eye for as long as you care to and you will not find it, and the flank goes on regarding you with an evenness that becomes, after a while, a form of politeness. It is on every shore. It is on every shore *at once*, and if two of you walk opposite rims and each of you sees it, and you compare hours afterward, the hours will agree, and you will both go quiet.

There is one eel because one is the number it is keeping. In the nulls, counts are revised — the cairn was always four stones, the sandal always had the extra strap — and something must stand against that, or the basin would eventually have two of everything, then none. The eel does not enter the nulls. It is the only animal here that reads the surface, and it will steer wide of a patch of smooth water at a distance of twenty feet, unhurried, the way you would walk around a hole. When a null overtakes it — they move fast, and it cannot always get clear — the eel is briefly not present. Then it is present. Where it was, for a moment, there is a seam of white silt in the water, hanging, and the eel on the far shore does not look up.

**The shrimp** hold the suspension.

They work the hot columns, transparent enough to be mostly a rumour of shrimp, and their labour is the reason the silt never settles. They go up the column and come down outside it and go up again, all day, all night, thousands of them, and each pass lifts a little of the white dust that the mountain is still making of itself. They eat nothing. Catch one — you cannot, but suppose — and it is chiefly gut, and the gut is full of the same dust it has just handed upward. They are not feeding. They are keeping the water dim. Take away the silt and the depths would go clear, and the underside of the surface is already a perfect mirror, and a basin with two mirrors in it would be a very different kind of place, and no one wants to find out what the fall would look like reflected forever between them. The shrimp are the reason noon here is a sickroom. This is a courtesy.

**The whip-weed** is not a plant and holds nothing, because it is not a tenant; it is a symptom. It grows only in the nulls and it grows straight in a current that should lay it flat, and it thickens where the silence is deepest, and when a null walks away from it the weed is grey and prone within the hour and gone by morning with no holdfast, no root-mark, no scar in the glass at all. Nothing here has roots. What you are looking at is the place where the silence touches the bottom. It is the shadow a null casts. You can measure the depth of a silence by how black the blades are, and the deepest ones are not black; they are a kind of absence with edges, and it is best to be some distance from those.

**The tree** holds the fact of endings.

Drowned to the shoulder on the eastern shelf, dead since before this mountain existed, still putting out small tough leaves from a trunk with nothing living in it. Nobody can name the species. It is from the country that was here before the glass came up through it, and it is the only object in the basin that remembers a completed event.

Its leaves are the only things here that rot. Watch one fall into the shallows: it browns, it softens, it goes to filth, it is gone. That is the whole demonstration. That is the only proof available anywhere on this mountain that a process can conclude, and it costs the tree a leaf every time, and the tree has been paying for it for longer than the ground it stands on.

The birds come to the tree first. They eat the dead leaves. They eat nothing else the entire time they are here.

**The birds** hold the circumference.

They arrive blown up out of the weather, exhausted, the wrong species for this altitude — gulls, a heron once, once something with a beak like a spoon that nobody could place. They drink. They go to the tree. And then they begin to walk, sober as clerks, and what they are doing is paying out a number.

The rim is four hundred paces. It is always four hundred paces. The shore is never twice in the same place, new glass buds and splits at the waterline within the hour, whole shelves vanish overnight and reappear pointing the wrong way — and the walk is four hundred paces, and if you doubt this, count. Watch a gull where the rim has just gained fifteen yards of fresh lobed glass: it hurries, it takes strides no gull should take, it spends its allowance faster over the new ground and then dawdles almost to stillness on the old to make the sum come right. It is not walking a distance. It is holding a figure.

They go up when they are relieved. That is the arrangement. A bird will not leave until another has come out of the weather and eaten from the tree, and then the older one finds a particular spot — they know where; they have always known where — a place where two nulls are crossing and there is, for perhaps four heartbeats, no loudest thing anywhere. And since down is only ever toward the loudest place, there is briefly no down at all, and a body with nothing to fall toward does the only thing left.

This is the sole exit that anyone has ever confirmed. It requires you to have finished your work and to have handed it to someone.

**The walkers** hold the level.

Swim down five fathoms, roll over, and there is the whole basin inverted in the underside of the surface: black rim, white column, dim green sky, and one figure walking the rim upside down at exactly your pace. If two of you go down together you will each see one figure and neither will see the other's, and each figure will keep its own swimmer's pace.
