## The people of the Welt

### What you meet first

You will meet them one at a time and always at the wrong distance. From fifty paces a gutterwright is indistinguishable from the masonry: a grey-brown shape crouched over a channel with one arm out, absolutely motionless, holding a thread. They wear undyed felt so heavily greased it has gone the colour of the rock, and they crouch the way herons stand.

They will not look up when you arrive. They will finish the measurement. Under the roar you can watch a person spend four unhurried minutes deciding that a groove is one hair too deep, and the whole time you are standing there being ignored is, from their side, a courtesy: they are refusing to make a mistake in your presence.

Then they look up, and they look at three things in a fixed order. Your feet — soles first, to see what you are grinding into the rock. Your hands — to see whether you are carrying water. Your face last, and briefly, and with a mild pleasant blankness, as if you were weather.

### The body of a gutterwright

They are small, ropy, and permanently damp. The chest is deep out of all proportion; the shoulders are wide and the arms long, from a lifetime of mauls; the legs are thin and the knees are ruined by ten thousand flights of stone stairs and by squatting, which is their true posture, their resting state, the thing they do instead of sitting.

The hands are the point of them. Hands that have been wet since childhood do not go back: the fingers stay puckered, the pads white and corrugated, the nails soft and short and often absent. Every gutterwright has lost something from a hand — a fingertip to a wedge, a nail to a maul, the use of a knuckle. The forearms and shins are latticed with fine white scars from the glassy edges of new stone, which cut so cleanly you feel it a breath later, and the standard dressing is a handful of sphagnum from the nearest seam, so the scars are all slightly green at the margin for a season.

Their gait is unmistakable and they never lose it. It is the walk of someone crossing ice: weight on the back foot, toe down first, testing, the whole body ready to un-commit. They walk like that in the bathhouse
