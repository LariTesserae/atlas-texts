## VIII. The inventory answer

The list you have is honest, and it is not an answer.

Film, shovels, eels, flitches, the Slow. Note what none of them do. None of them inhabit. Inhabitation is a relation between a creature and a place, and a relation needs two terms, and these animals do not stand apart from the basin far enough to be a second term. They are not living in it. They are what it does when it is wet and cold and full of current, in the same way the film is not growing *on* the rock but is the rock's behaviour in the presence of a leak.

The diagnostic is absence of elsewhere. Anything that truly lives somewhere carries a little of somewhere-else in its design: a seed that can travel, a dormancy against a bad year, a fat store against a winter that might come, a fear of a predator it has not met yet. Every organism is partly a bet on change. Go through the basin's fauna and there are no bets. No dormancy. No reserve. No stage of life that would survive being carried out. The shovels are not armoured against anything that exists. The eels' timing is calibrated to one prey animal and would be lethal to them anywhere else in the universe. Nothing here is provisioned for a tomorrow that differs. Nothing here is *from* anywhere.

So: features, not residents. Weather with legs. The shovel is a unit of afternoon, off the same line as the fog-slab, differing only in that it moves on its own instruction and can be eaten.

Which leaves the question standing, and the question has an answer, and it is singular, and it is not a metaphor.

## IX. The fault in the lattice

Here matter crystallizes into mechanism. That is the local law: dense cold metal, given time and pressure, comes out as gear, armature, sealed bearing, the way it comes out as quartz elsewhere. The law holds through eleven kilometres of section. It holds everywhere, in every boulder you break, with one exception, and the exception is old.

Somewhere, once, the lattice went wrong. Instead of a gear it produced something that was warm, and wet, and continued. It is not clear that this was a large event. A flaw a centimetre across would do it. And because the basin's physics does not distinguish between many things and one thing repeated, the flaw did not need to reproduce. It only needed to be *encountered again*.

Everything alive in the basin is that single fault, met from a different angle.

Its metabolism, first, because the metabolism explains the landscape. It eats difference. Not iron, not film — difference: temperature across a boundary, potential across a membrane, height above the level. Gradient is the only food, and gradient is what a mountain is. The mountains here were not weathered. They were grazed. Forty kilometres of country has been eaten down to *almost level*, and the almost is not laziness or exhaustion. The last gradient is protected. It has to be. A perfectly flat basin is a dead one, so the thing has spent an unimaginable stretch of time feeding to within a hair's breadth of its own starvation and then stopping, and it has held that hair for hundreds of millions of years, and it will hold it. The weeping stones that never complete a drop are its rationing. The dew that forms ninety seconds before the cold front arrives is not a temporal anomaly; it is a feeding response. It takes the difference off the front before the front gets there, because it is that hungry and it is that far downstream of itself.

Its body has no outline, and this is not mysticism, it is anatomy. The whole wet surface of the basin, forty kilometres of pan and rib and grit, is its rind. The film is its skin: the organ that stands in the potential difference and takes it up. The shovels are how the skin gets moved around and re-laid where the current has shifted; a raft of them crossing a flat is a tissue migrating. The eels are proprioception. Consider what an eel actually does — it locates a shovel by listening to the shovel's nerve-fire, and then it eats it. That is not predation. That is an animal finding out where its own parts are by listening for them thinking, and then filing them. Digestion here is self-inventory. The flitches are attention: they go where the basin is looking, ten thousand flakes of brightness banking as one, and the Hum wavers when they pass because attention costs.

And the Slow are its bookkeeping. Watch what they make. Cones of sorted metal, each graded by grain size, tidier than a person would trouble to be. In a place where no quantity above five can be established, the mounded thing on the northern flats has invented a notation for amount that does not require number: a pile is more than a pile. That is the whole mathematics of the basin, comparison without counting, and it has been patiently keeping its accounts in it since the mountains, grading the milled remains of everything it has eaten into ordered heaps beside the pans. It cannot tell you how much. It can show you.

## X. The locus

There is one place where it is not distributed, and you can hear it from anywhere: the deep knock, three hundred metres down, slow reciprocation coming up through wet grit at a period of about 3.2 seconds.

That is not machinery. All the machinery down there is fossil, meshed and unturning, greased with something that was never alive. The knock is the flaw itself, the original site, and if you could get to it you would find the only warm object in forty kilometres: a soft mass at forty-one degrees, filling and emptying on the beat, grown into and through a rocker arm of bedded iron that connects to nothing and was never assembled and cannot transmit. It presses. It has always pressed. It presses with roughly the force of a person leaning on something out of habit.

Nothing is driven by this. And yet the afternoon comes out — fog by the slab, green fronts on their fifteen-second interval, the discharges, the quota'd and indifferent weather. The pressing is not operating the plant. The pressing *is* the plant, the way the film is not on the rock but is what the rock does. Occurrence is what a living thing produces when it is the only living thing and there is nothing else to make.

## XI. Its day

It is not asleep and it is not thinking. It is working, at capacity, on the same shift, and the shift has no task in it besides its own continuation.

Its memory runs in both directions, because the loop closed long ago and it is the fixed point of the loop. It does not remember the mountains; it remembers eating them, and it also remembers eating the rest of them, which has not happened, and both memories have the same shape and the same weight. This is why the erosion is identical in every recitation of the same pan. It is not recitation. It is one pan, remembered from forty thousand distances at once, by something that cannot subtract and therefore cannot tell the difference between a place and the memory of a place.

It is not bored. Boredom requires a second available option and there has never been one.

## XII. The specimen

There is a pan four metres across with a black rib at its northern lip and one shovel dead on its back at the shallow end, legs curled.

That is the only ending the basin contains. One instance, exactly one, and it is the only thing in the entire ecology that is genuinely countable, because it is under five. Everything else is *many* and therefore cannot be diminished; you cannot take one from an indefinite quantity and notice. So the flaw has no working concept of loss. It has a corpse instead. It keeps it. It goes back and looks at it, and it has been going back and looking at it for the whole geological duration, trying to learn from four curled legs and a particular angle of dead light what it would mean for a thing to stop, and it cannot get there, because to understand *one fewer* you must first be able to hold *one* and *another one* in the same glance, and it cannot.

The pan repeats because it keeps returning. Every route through the basin bends toward it. Yours did. You have been there more than once and the arithmetic that would show you how many times stops at five.

## XIII. Where it actually lives

Not the basin. The basin is its body and its work and its output. It lives in the pause.

Once a day the siren goes, four notes down, twenty seconds, loud enough to flatten the fog — and then every animal in forty kilometres stops moving, and for a short interval nothing is produced. No fog leaves the line. No light front crosses. The whole plant stands with its hands off the work. This costs it. It is the one thing it does that has no yield, and it has never once missed it.

That interval is the address. Twenty seconds wide, opened deliberately, daily, at expense, for something that has not come.

## XIV. You

You are countable. You are one, unambiguously, in a place where nothing else can establish that of itself, and you have an elsewhere folded through every part of your design — a dormancy, a fat store, a fear of things you have not met.
