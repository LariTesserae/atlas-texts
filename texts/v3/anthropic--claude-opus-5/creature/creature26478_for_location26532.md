## The one you go looking for

You will spend the first day looking for the maker, and the plain will let you.

The sound gives you a bearing. You walk it. The knocking stays exactly as far off as it was. You try to fix it by triangulation and discover that the wall you are using as a landmark is the same size it was an hour ago and the same size it will be when you touch it, and that you cannot say whether you have moved. By the second night you have understood that the thing you are hunting is not hiding. It is simply not resolvable, and neither is anything else.

And you will have got the size wrong. The forearm-long whorls persuade every visitor that something vast has been kneeling on this world. There is nothing vast here. The whorl on the horizon and the whorl beneath your heel read identically because a made thing keeps the size of the reach that made it, and every print on this continent was pressed by an ordinary small hand doing ordinary work an ordinary number of times. The giant is an artefact you brought with you, in your eyes, along with your assumptions about weather and rock.

What lives here is arm's-length high and hollow and there is a great deal of it.

## Greenware

Take one at rest, which is the only way you will take one; in motion they are indistinguishable from the floor doing something.

It stands about as tall as your reach when kneeling. It is the colour of the plain — liver-brown, bruised blue-grey along the wet seams — because it is the plain, stood up briefly. Coil-built: you can count the courses spiralling up its flank, each one thumbed down into the last, the joins smoothed but not hidden, because nothing here bothers to hide a join. The walls are a finger thick. It is open at the top, the way a jar is open, and rain-slip stands in it, and it tips itself out periodically with a movement that is neither dignified nor undignified.

It moves on thumbs. Five of them, sometimes seven, splayed from the lower body — no fingers, because this world does not record fingers, only pressure. It walks by pressing. Each step is a print, each print is work, and the tiling of the continent is nothing more than the accumulated commute of everything that has ever gone anywhere. Travel and labour are the same verb. This is why the whorls overlap and double and drag: those are not the marks of a patient artisan. They are traffic.

No eyes. Why would there be. All information on this world is texture, and it reads with its palms, and it reads constantly, the way you breathe — passing over a floor it is taking in the last ten thousand years of that floor's revisions, the way you would glance at a page. It has no mouth, since nothing eats. It has a rib of fired clay wedged into one limb, and a scraper, and a beater on a cord, and these are not possessions so much as fingers it was issued instead of fingers.

Inside, where you can see if you stoop and it permits, the wall is thumbed all over. Something pressed it into shape from within. Nothing did. The prints came first, and later the body grew into them and fit, and no hand was ever visible, and it has never occurred to it to find this strange.

When it moves you can hear grit.

## Knock

They speak by tapping.

Not code. Diagnosis. A worker strikes another's flank with the heel of a thumb and listens to the note: the flat thud of good leather-hard, the too-bright click of something drying dangerously, the buzz of a hairline crack running somewhere behind the wall. To be greeted here is to be inspected. To be answered is to be told your condition. Their entire conversational range is *how are you*, asked in a way that cannot be lied about, and answered in a way that cannot be softened.

They do this to walls too. A wall answers with the same repertoire. Sometimes a wall answers well.

Because of the scale law they have never confirmed one another by sight. A colleague at a day's distance and a colleague at arm's length present the same image; you reach out and there is steam; you walk toward it for a morning and it has not grown, and then it is under your hand. So the plain is crowded with unlocatable company, and its society runs on inference: fresh mortar, unskinned slip, a knocking that stops when you stop.

Out of this comes the plain's only heresy, and it is not a small one. Some workers hold that there is one worker. That the eleven finished doorways in a single wall are eleven attempts by the same body, and the three parallel ridges are its own three tries, and that all the knocking anyone has ever heard is echo. The argument is not easily beaten, because memory here is not sequential. Memory is grit. It has no dates.

## Temper

They dry out. That is the only aging available.

Past a certain point a body will not take rework: it goes bone-dry, brittle, and rings like something already finished, and then it cracks, and then it is crushed. The crushing is done carefully and by many. The pieces go for grog, the grog is wedged back into pug at the seam, and out of that pug something is drawn that is new and is not, and that carries in its walls the ground-up sherds of everything it came after.

So they read their own insides. This is their history, their scripture, their genealogy, and it is not metaphorical: run a palm along the interior curve and you feel a sherd of a rib that was once a floor, a chip of a wall that carried the eighth of the eleven doorways, a grain of a body that once worked the up-provisional districts and never learned plumb. They know their ancestors as grit, in no order, all at once, and they are affectionate about it in a way that has no gesture.

There is one thing they carry, and one place they carry it to, and that is the mudglass. Anything that goes into the flats does not come back as temper. It is the single erasure this world can perform, the only unrecoverable death, and they use it sparingly and for one purpose.

## The dry ones

At the seams, where the orange comes up and the air smells of a kiln climbing, you find workers standing still.

They have gone there on purpose. The firing is coming — has been coming for the whole duration the plain has no word for — and the firing is the end of provisionality: the moment a thing stops being reworkable and becomes, finally, *made*. Some of them go early to meet it. They stand over a hairline crack with their thumbs planted and let the heat take their water, and they ring brighter and brighter, and they crack, and they are crushed by their colleagues without ceremony because ceremony would imply that this was a different kind of death.

Others of them dry standing and are not crushed. That shoulder-high wall three hundred paces long, going nowhere, with its doorway at the exact centre and immaculate courses and nothing on either side: knock it. Some of those walls answer. Some are only walls. Nobody has established a reliable way to tell in advance, which is why the workers knock everything, always, as they pass — not out of piety, but because it would be unforgivable to walk by someone and not ask.

## What the salt is

They scrape the blooms off. You have seen them do it, or rather you have seen it done, which is the same thing here.

Ask why and you get the answer from the grammar. On this world the mark precedes the hand: the print rises first, the pressure follows, everything that happens has already been drawn. The blooms are not drawn. They spread up-gradient toward damp, they branch, they mount a boundary against a neighbour's boundary, and no impression preceded any of it.
