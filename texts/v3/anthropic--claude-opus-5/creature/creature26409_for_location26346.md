## 9. First, the difficulty about where they come from

Nobody has seen a birth here.

This is not a claim about secrecy or nocturnal habits. It is an inventory result. In a place this crowded, with fifty animals inside thirty metres at any hour, you should trip over gravidity. You should find eggs, nests, milk, swollen flanks, the fussing and the guarding. You find none of it above a certain size class. What you find instead — and you will find it within your first week, and it will bother you afterwards in the specific way a wrong number of stairs bothers you — is a trimmer half out of a cliff face.

Not trapped. Not fallen. Bedded. Its dorsal plates polished where they have emerged into the air, matrix still gripping its hindquarters, its grinding plates working slowly on nothing, at grade, in the same orientation as the four others in the same face, at the same horizon, spaced with the regularity of garnets. Over about two days it works free. It shakes off. It joins the herd. The face closes behind it and shows, if you look, a faint parting line and a little flashing, which within the hour something comes and eats.

They precipitate. They are a mineral phase this world falls into under pressure, the same as the flywheels and the bearings, and the distinction the eye keeps insisting on — that a bearing is a made thing and a trimmer is a born thing — is a distinction you brought in your pack and will not be able to keep dry.

So: they occur where they are needed. Not because anything needs them. Because a surplus, once it exists, gets a mouth. That is the whole grammar of the fauna of the Rise and everything below is elaboration.

## 10. Trimmers, at length

Cow-sized, low, broad, four short columnar legs, no neck to speak of. Armour in overlapping plates of the same stuff as the ridges, with the same faint broach-marks running across them. Down the exact midline of a trimmer's back runs a ridge of flashing three or four centimetres high, brittle, translucent at the crest. It is its own parting line. It grows back overnight if you break it off, and something in the herd will eat what you broke.
