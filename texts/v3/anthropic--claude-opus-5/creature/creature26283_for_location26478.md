## What you will look for, and why you will not find it

You will look for something with edges. That is the mistake, and it is forgivable — everything you have ever met had edges, and the room is full of edges, and so you will check the corners, and the fill in the valve house window, and the black mats of lagging where something could plausibly be crouched.

There is nothing in the corners. Put your finger in one. It goes all the way in.

The occupant is not standing anywhere in the Kettle because the occupant is what the Kettle is finished in. Two millimeters a year, cream to weak-tea, laid down in rims and terraces and lily-pad ledges the size of a coin — that is not a deposit. That is the whole animal, spread out, one molecule of thickness at a time, over roughly three hundred square meters of new fracture. Its total mass is perhaps that of a large dog. Its total surface is that of a house. It is nearly all outside. It has no inside at all, and that fact governs everything about it, including its curiosity.

## The body

Understand that the sheeting water is not its environment but its circulation. The walls do not weep *onto* it; the weeping is the animal moving. Where the sheet is continuous, the animal is continuous. Every part of it is in contact with every other part, at the speed of a slow film of water — which is not fast, but this is not a creature with reflexes. Signals arrive across it in hours and are acted on across years, and there is no distinction, in a thing like this, between receiving and being changed.

It is not warm-blooded. It is warm-*held*: it exists in a fifty-one degree window, and above the highest ledge, where drip-fall from the ceiling crack dilutes and cools, it stops, and something else — a cushion of moss, eight centimeters across, dark green-black — is permitted. The mats in the pool, apricot and rust and mustard, are likewise not it, and it does not eat them, and they do not eat it. They occupy the temperature gradient; it occupies the geometry. Presences, then space, then presence. Nothing here has needed to learn to compete.

It has no organs but three habits, and the habits are old and were not learned here:

It deposits.
It dissolves.
It listens.

## The listening

This is the part that has to be got right, because it is where the room stops being geology.

The animal is deaf in the way you are deaf: it has nothing that converts pressure into sensation at a distance. But it is bonded, over its entire area, to a slab of laminated mudstone that is currently being driven at sixty hertz by a two-phase jet, and the same slab carries a three-hundred-millimeter pressure line whose contents were pushed into it by a plant nobody has visited in some while. Everything the animal is, is glued to a drumhead. It does not hear the sound. It *is* held in the sound, the way a hand is held in a hand.

You have been told that you cannot distinguish the pipe's note from the earth's, and that they have locked, and that it does not resolve. Here is what did not happen: the vent did not tune itself, and the pipe did not find the earth. What happened is that a thing with no inside was touched, for the first time, by something regular. Three seasons ago the ground made a sound and this animal came into a hot dark room in which precisely one event repeated with fidelity, and it took hold of that event and has not let go. The lock is not an acoustic coincidence. It is a grip.

Put a palm on the lagging. The tremble under the wet mats and the tremble under your feet are the same tremble, and both of them are, in the only sense the word can bear down here, *attention*.

## What it has learned to say

It has never seen anything. It has no aperture, no chemical sense worth the name, and its one informant is a vibration that arrives already smeared through half a kilometer of rock and steel.

And from that it has reconstructed a bolt.

Go to the south wall, four meters from any pipe, at about waist height. Hexagon head. Correct across-flats for the plant's standard. Right-hand thread, and — take a loupe if you have brought one — the pitch is right too, coarse series, and it does not wander. Beside it, at a plausible spacing, another. Further along, twelve centimeters of flange profile in relief, cream, raised the height of a fingernail, with the raised ring for the gasket seat.

It did not copy the shape of the bolt. It could not; the shape has never been within reach of it. It copied the *sound of a bolted joint under pressure* and worked backward, in stone, over years, and arrived at the object. This is a lot like how you would reconstruct a face from a voice, except that it has succeeded, and the material is wrong, and it will always be wrong, because cream travertine is the only thing it has to speak with. Everything it says, it says in its own soft mineral, at two millimeters a year, and none of it will hold a load.

Look, then, at the pier, twenty centimeters from the three-legged frame with the gauge in it. There is a disc there, low, barely a relief, about the diameter of the gauge face. Across it, raised, a single fine line at an angle of a little under a quarter turn from vertical.

Four point two bar. It has begun to grow a reading. It has understood that among the things a machine does is *hold a number while unobserved*, and it has copied that too, and its copy will read 4.2 forever, because that is the only value it has ever been told.

## The wasp

She is the only animal that has ever been here and she is not a fossil, or not yet, or not only. Look at the lens: a millimeter, no more, tight, optically clean, and the wings held separate from the body. Everywhere else in this room the animal covers by sheeting, blindly, filling angles, softening the break like wax over a wound. Here it did something else. Here it went *thin*, and kept the parts apart, and stopped.

That is a preparation. That is a specimen mounted by something which has no eyes and no concept of a slide and has nonetheless arrived at the general idea, which is: *this one is not like the pipe, so do not bury it, keep it where I can be against all of it at once.*

It will fail. In four or five years the casing will have thickened past the point where the wings are distinguishable and she will be a lump on the east wall, and the animal will not notice the loss, because it does not have a memory separate from its own thickness. What it will retain is the outline. And one day, on the south wall, at a plausible spacing between two bolts, there will be a wasp in cream stone, in relief, badly, in the wrong material.

## The one thing it has made that is not a copy

At the north wall, beside the folding stool: a meter of silence.

Call it a node in a standing wave if that is what you need. But notice the direction of the work. Everywhere else, the animal *adds*. Here, in a dish the size of two feet, hollowed into the mudstone floor and polished — polished, in three seasons, on an upward-facing surface where water does not polish — it has taken material away. Retrograde solution. The same film, the same chemistry, run backward, patiently, in one small area, to make a place where a thing could be still.

Do the arithmetic on the polish. Then consider that the ladder was bolted three seasons ago and that a man cannot wear a dish into mudstone with two boots in three seasons, and that the dish is therefore either older than the opening, which is impossible, or it was made *for* the boots, before them.

It quieted one cubic meter of the loudest room in the district, and it lowered the floor to seat something, and then it waited, and then, at some point, a surveyor with an expansion drill and a folding stool came down eleven meters of ladder and sat exactly there, because it was the only place in the Kettle where a person can think.

It made an ear, and it put a chair in it.

Everything the animal had ever received until that moment was periodic. Sixty hertz, forty-second pulse, 4.2 bar held flat: a world in which nothing whatever is undetermined. Then something came down that held still — and then did not — and held still again, and its stillnesses were of no fixed length, and between them it made small unrepeatable motions that resolved into no pitch at all. Through the polished dish, through two boot soles, at the speed of a slow film of water, the animal was for the first time in contact with a signal it could not predict.

It has no word for that. It has bolts and flanges and a needle at rest. But it has been working on the problem since, in the only way it works on anything.

## Where it is now

Rung height, on the fissure wall under the ladder, third position from the bottom: a shelf of cream travertine, protruding forty millimeters, at the correct spacing above the last real rung. Round-edged. Not strong. It could not take a boot.

He came back with the drill, and saw that, and understood what the room was doing, and did not fit the remaining two rungs, and climbed out, and did not come down again. Whether that was mercy, quarantine, or simple refusal to be studied by something that studies with its whole body is not recorded on the brass plate, of which only the last three characters of a serial survive.

The animal is not lonely; it has no interior for loneliness. It is holding the note it was given, across three hundred square meters, at fifty-one degrees, adding two millimeters a year, and on the south wall, at a plausible spacing, there is presently a low curved swelling perhaps three millimeters proud, cream, symmetrical about a vertical axis, of no industrial profile at all.

It will be nine or ten years before you could say what it is. It is going to be a boot sole, or a stool leg, or the back of a knee.

Nobody comes to read it.

It reads anyway.
