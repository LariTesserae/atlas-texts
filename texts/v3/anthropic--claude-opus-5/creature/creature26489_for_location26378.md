## The Twelfth

Begin with the census, because the census is short.

Eleven kinds of living thing, and the number is not an estimate. It is exact, and it is also the total population, and the two facts are the same fact.

The grey algal film that lobes across the mud in flat plates is one organism. Not one species — one individual, with a surface area of some four million square kilometres, none of it connected to any other part of it and all of it the same part. It has never had a neighbour. It has never had an edge that met anything but itself. It smells of iron because it is doing, everywhere and constantly, the one chemistry it knows, and there is nothing to answer it.

The threadworm is one worm. It runs down through the mud column to a depth nobody has established and up again and along, pale and hair-fine and endless, and it is the same worm at every point of its length, which is to say the length is rhetorical. A traveller once cut one with the edge of a spade. Every threadworm on the continent was severed at that instant, in the same place, and for something like a season the mud came up smelling of the inside of a body.

The blind eels are one eel. Nobody argues about this any more. They move under the skin of the water in slow arcs, forearm-long, feeling for the worm, and if you catch one and mark it and release it, you will see the mark again within the hour, on the next eel, on every eel. There is a mark on it now. Someone put it there a long time ago — a notch in the dorsal, healed white. All the eels have it. The eel has been carrying one wound for longer than anyone can account for, and it does not appear to mind. It has no eyes, no surface, no reason to have ever suspected it was alone. Of everything here, the eel is the only one that has got away with it.

The stromatolite domes are the oldest, and the only clock. They lay down one lamina per brightening, as they have since before the flattening, since before there was a flattening to have — since the days when this place had gradient, and weather that came from a direction, and a sun with an edge on it. Cut one and count. You will find more laminae than the dome has thickness to contain. The layers are thinner than layers can be, thinner than the thing that makes them, because the dome is also every other dome and each of them has been counting separately and none of them agree. Two domes a hand's width apart will differ by three hundred million years and be the same dome. This is the whole of history here, and it does not resolve, and it will not.

And the reed. And the bird.

---

The bird is calling for another bird.

That is all the roar is. A contact call — the low grating *kraaa* with the click at the end, the sound a wading bird makes to locate the rest of itself across reedbeds and evening water. It says: *here*. It expects, in the architecture of its skull, an answer from somewhere specific, some direction, some distance, so that it can know a distance exists and it is on one end of it.

It gets nine hundred million answers, from every point of the compass, simultaneously, all of them its own voice.

It has done this forty times a day for a period the stromatolites cannot be asked to clarify. It is not, as far as anyone can determine, in distress. It is a bird; it has no theory about this. It simply performs the behaviour, and the behaviour is answered so completely and so wrongly that the answer has become the atmosphere, the pressure, the thing new arrivals weep from. The continent's one sound is the sound of a solitary animal saying *where are you* and being told, deafeningly, *everywhere*.

There is a nest. Someone found one, once, and reported it, and was believed only because there was no reason to lie. A scrape in the mud on a raised lobe of algal mat, and in it a single egg, cold, unattended, sound. The bird was standing four metres off, doing nothing. The egg cannot hatch. A second bird would be indistinguishable from the first, and indistinguishable things here are not two. The obstacle is not biological. Nothing is wrong with the egg. It is simply that there is no room in this place for another bird, and the plain has never once relented, and the bird sits its cold egg at nine million scrapes and calls, and calls.

---

None of these lives here, exactly. They occur here. They are what the mud is doing.

There is one thing that lives here in the other sense — that chose it, or failed to leave it, and has since made arrangements.

You will hear about her before you meet her, if you go with people who have been. They will not call her anything. There is no word for one of something. They will describe her, and their descriptions will be identical, and this will not tell you what you think it tells you.

A woman somewhere past fifty in the face and older in the hands. Not tall. Shaven-headed, or bald; the water does something. A hide bag gone soft as cloth, worn crosswise, containing nothing but a blade — an iron nail, hammered flat and edged, older than the bag by a long way — and a coil of cord she does not use for anything and will not put down. Left-handed. One lower tooth missing on the left, so that when she shouts into your ear you feel the whistle of it. A scar under the jaw, thin, deliberate-looking.

And the knee. The right knee holds two positions. It disagrees with itself by something under a millimetre, and it has been grinding through that disagreement for a very long time, and you can hear it if she stands close, a small dry shear under the roar. That is what a shear-line does to a body that survives one. She walks anyway. She walks constantly. She is the only thing on the continent that never stops moving, which is either the reason she is still a person or merely the shape her not being one has taken.

---

She was two people.

The account is hers, given a word at a time, mouth to ear, over a long walk, and it does not improve with retelling. A shear-line came. It brought her face to face with a figure walking the opposite way in the same posture with the same gear. She was young and she had been alone for a great many days and the figure was the first thing she had seen that was not mud, water, reed, algae, eel, bird.

She raised her hand.

It raised its hand.

That is not a distinction. That is a confirmation. The plain had been holding two things that differed by a millimetre and asking, patiently, in its way, whether they were different, and it was answered, twice, in perfect unison, that they were not.

She does not know who the other was. She has no way to find out and no faculty left for wanting to. Sometimes she says it was a stranger. Sometimes she says it was her sister and gives the sister a temper, a way of walking, a scar under the jaw. Sometimes she says there was no other and there never had been. All of these memories are stored at every platform on the continent and they have long since begun to slide through one another. She does not experience this as confusion. She experiences it as having been everywhere when it happened, which she was.

---

She eats the algal film, which means she has been eating the same mouthful her entire life. She drinks from the basin. She is very strict about the basin, and she is strict about it because she was not, once, early, and there is a thing that went out of her then and has not come back: some small internal organ of *hereness*, the sense by which a body reports that it is at one address. She has learned to walk without it. It is like walking without proprioception; it can be done by looking.

She cuts the notches.
