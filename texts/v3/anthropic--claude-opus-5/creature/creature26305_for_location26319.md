The notes above list six organisms, a machine, and a mislaid glove. That is a reasonable way to write down what you saw. It is the wrong number of things.

There is one thing living in the Bell. You have already described most of it.

**What it is.** It has no wet parts, no cells, no lineage, and no name except the surveyors' one, which was not a word. In the hand cant chalked on the wall panel, the sign for the occupant of the chamber is a closed fist held still against the sternum. The same sign serves for *wait* and for *no*.

Its body is the cavity: thirty meters on the long axis, eight at the crown, a lens of held pressure with a lining of blades. The crystal terminations on the walls are not scenery, they are integument, and they are sharp for the reason a thing that never abrades has no reason to be blunt. Nothing in the Bell needs a callus. Nothing needs a scab. The floor is swept in the way an integer is even — not by an act, by a property.

Everything the notes call an inhabitant is an organ, and it is important to be precise about what kind. The wickwood is not flora growing in the animal; it is the animal's method of converting seep to standing chemistry, and it stands knee-high because knee-high is the figure that balances. The mites are its turnover. The clatterers are its tuning — they sit on the flute ribs and load them, and the shimmer in the chord is not an accident of insects, it is the chord being kept in tune by a mechanism that happens to have legs. The haze is a shape the animal maintains the way you maintain a posture, which is to say without attending to it and without ever once letting it go. Two meters across, tilted, unchanged since 1961 because 1961 is not a thing that has happened to it.

The Works is where it keeps its books.

**Its arithmetic.** The Bell does not metabolize. Metabolism is a sequence: this becomes that, heat is lost, a debt is entered somewhere. The Bell distributes. Its whole physiology is one conserved total, held across the volume, and every part of the room is a term in the sum. The tallow seeps at the rate it is eaten. The stalks grow at the rate they are grazed. The chord is loud at exactly the loudness the flutes and the carapaces and the standing wave agree on, and when you stuffed your coat into the throat you did not reduce that loudness, you only moved it, because the total is not a consequence of the flutes. The flutes are a consequence of the total.

Sixty-three times a minute the eccentric arm lifts its measure of tallow to the top of the arc and pours it back, and this is the only thing the Bell does that could reasonably be called behavior. It is an audit. It raises a quantity, holds it clear of everything for a quarter-second, drops it home, and the books balance, and then it does it again. The splash aerosolizes a trace that the haze rides. The thud is the tick the clatterers key to. The audit pays for itself, which is precisely the point of it. Nothing is being verified for anyone's benefit. The Bell is not anxious. It simply is a thing whose form of existence is the continuous demonstration that it needs nothing further.

**Its one moving part.** The Long One is the only member of the arrangement that is singular, and the only one that is going somewhere, and it goes nowhere, in eleven minutes and some seconds, blind, over obstacles rather than around them, because it is not navigating. It is counting. It is the Bell's vestigial clock, kept small, kept sightless, kept to one — a single organ of sequence retained in an organism that has otherwise dispensed with sequence altogether, the way a flightless bird keeps the bones.

There has only ever been one. It has also been replaced an unknown number of times, and both of those statements are true, and this is the clearest window you will get into how the Bell holds its inventory. It does not maintain populations. It maintains slots. A slot is the real animal; the chitin is what the slot is wearing this week. Take a clatterer up the ladder in a jar and you have stolen a coat, not a creature; the slot was never in the jar, and by the time you reach the salt crust the slot has dressed again, plausibly, at a plausible age, in a plausible crevice. Nobody has caught the substitution because there is no substitution to catch. There is only a term in a sum that you briefly failed to change.

**What it does with you.** Nothing, and the nothing is thorough.

You are eighty-odd liters of the wrong solvent, and you are not in the total, so you are excluded — not violently, not even with any particular attention, but with the same exactness that keeps the floor swept. Your sweat is gone before it beads. Your flask empties upward into an atmosphere that stays arithmetically dry, because there is no term for water and there will not be one made. By the second hour your lips have opened in fine parallel cracks and your eyelids grate, and the Bell has not done this to you; the Bell has simply declined to include you, and declining is what that feels like from the inside.

But it does notice, in the only sense available to it: it re-balances around you. You are a coat in a flute. The loudness reappears elsewhere. And here is the part the old survey teams learned to fear more than the heat — what cannot be excluded gets scored. Bring in an object that has no water and no future and the Bell will find a place for it in the chord and hold it there. The glove lies palm-down near the plinth with the curve of a hand still in it, not stiff, not dusty, seventy years unaged, because that curve turned out to be a note the Bell could carry. The mine-lamp's filament burns on six meters of severed cable, dim orange, supplied by a differential that has no source. The tally on the wall stops mid-twelfth stroke, and it will stop mid-twelfth stroke forever, and somewhere in the total there is now a term whose value is *a man who was counting something and was interrupted*.

Whatever is left of that man in the Bell is exactly as much of him as fitted.

**Kin, and the vented ones.** It has relatives, and they are all children. Gas punches these bubbles up through the evaporite every few decades and most of the new cavities are perfectly ordinary holes — they dust, they round, they fill with talus and silence, they get asked the question every landscape is asked and answer it the usual way, by spending. Occasionally one is caught mid-answer: chord unstable, census fluctuating by three or four, edges beginning to soften at the crown, everything trying combinations at ferocious speed. Those are the larvae. They are the only ones with any time.

The plate says **BELL 4 — DO NOT VENT** because Bells 1 through 3 were vented, and venting is the single lethal wound available. It does not shatter anything. It gives the animal a gradient — an outside, a before, an after — and that is enough. Bell 2's chamber was reopened eight days later. Six millimeters of dust. Crystal faces frosted like sucked candy. The copper shaft welded solid in a bloom of green, the sulfur bearings crazed, the flutes rounded as if by a century of weather in a room with no weather. And on the floor a great quantity of small dead things, all of which had died at the same instant, and all of which, on examination, had died of age.

Two hundred years of arrears, presented within the hour.

**Age.** Do not call it old; it isn't. It is a hundred and some years into a brutal infancy and it has never grown out of anything, because it stopped needing to. Do not call it immortal either. Immortality is an endurance, and endurance is work done against time, and the Bell does no work of that kind. It has simply arrived, in its short violent youth, at a configuration in which the next moment is not required — and it is not waiting out the ages, and it is not persisting, and it is not even, strictly, continuing.

It is finished. It is a creature that solved itself and has been unable to die since, because dying is a change.

You climb the four meters of open air, and the ladder, and the chimney, and the slot, and the white plain is silent to the horizon in every direction, and under your boots the crust crunches like sugar, and at your feet the crack is humming. It is not humming *still*. There is no *still* down there. It is humming the once, and the once has not closed, and it will not have closed when the plain is gone and the salt is gone and the ladder is a stain of iron in a rock nobody will read.
