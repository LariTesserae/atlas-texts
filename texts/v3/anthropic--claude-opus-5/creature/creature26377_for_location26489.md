## The Tenant

Start with the one on the back of your hand, since it is there already and will not be leaving.

It weighs almost nothing, because there is almost nothing in it. Eleven plates of a chitin-analogue that is mostly calcium and silica, waxen, translucent at the edges where it is thin, laid in an overlapping fan so the body can flex flat against curved rock. Ten legs, though the front pair are shortened and do most of the tasting. No eyes, no eye-scars, no vestige of eyes; the lineage never had them. What it has instead, along the underside of every plate, is a field of pits maybe four hundred in number, each of which reports temperature to a precision no instrument you brought could match. It does not see the room. It feels the room the way you feel a bruise: in gradients, in local intensities, in the exact shape of the ache.

Under the plates there is a volume of liquid salt. Nitrates, chlorides, a scatter of sulfates, iron in solution, at about four hundred and twenty degrees. This is its blood and also most of its body — it is, functionally, a bag of molten salt with a lid.

It has no heart. Look for one and you'll find nothing that pumps. It doesn't need a pump; it has a chimney. Hot blood rises at the hook-feet where they contact the floor, climbs the internal channels, spreads under the ridged dorsum where the air steals a few degrees off it, thickens, sinks, returns. The circulation *is* the temperature difference. There is no other engine in it and never was.

Understand what that means and you understand everything that follows. A body sitting in air and rock of exactly equal temperature is not a body at rest. It is a body with stopped blood. Comfort — the even, mild, unstressed condition you would call comfort — is asphyxiation here, and takes about nine minutes. The obsessive jostling of the walls, the shoving for position, the tide that will not hold still: that is not appetite. That is breathing. Millions of animals, all night, all day, breathing by standing carefully across the fall of heat, and the sound of it is flour poured from one bowl to another.

It has no name for itself and no capacity for one. There is no self in it to name. Ask what it wants and the honest answer is: to be somewhere three degrees better than here, and to be touching others, and that is the whole of its interior life.

---

The animal that lives in the Kiln is the Kiln.

Not a metaphor and not a mysticism — an accounting. Individual bodies are its tissue. They fission, they set, they are trodden into the floor, and none of that is the death of anything, any more than the loss of a skin cell is your death. The thing that persists, that has continuity, that responds and adjusts and *decides*, is the two hundred paces of arrangement.

It thinks by moving. Its signalling runs two ways: pressure through the hooked feet, and a trick of shunting blood to the dorsum to flush a neighbour with a half-degree of warmth or steal it. Both are slow. A change of state propagates roughly a pace every four seconds, and it takes the length of the chamber to converge on anything. So the Congregation — I will call it that; the word is mine, it has no word — has a thought cycle of about half a day, and the thought is where its body ends up. The great slow tide down to the fissure at nightfall and back upslope at dawn is one complete deliberation: a problem in twelve thousand simultaneous variables, solved to optimality every single time, by feel, in the dark, in silence, and the solution is a posture.

It has been doing this for something like four hundred thousand cycles without once getting it wrong. Nothing here shrieks because nothing here has ever needed to be warned of anything it could not already feel.

There is one other thing to know about how it thinks, and it is why the chamber is shaped like a nave.

The hook-feet secrete their glassy residue out of dissolved rock. A body tastes the wall, lifts a few micrograms of silicate into its scalding blood, sets the residue down as cement. Multiply by millions, by hundreds of thousands of cycles. The Congregation has eaten this room. The vaulting you admired is not entirely the accident of a gas bubble; the bubble was the seed, and four hundred thousand cycles of licking made the smooth high curve of it, the way water would have if there were water. The floor of fused corpses and the ceiling that arches over it are the same substance, transported by mouth, one grain at a time. It is the only building the species has ever done, and it did it without intending to, and it is a cathedral.

---

The dead are the memory.

A body that cools sets in the position it held, hooks locked, and the position it held was information — it was the shape of the room's heat at that instant. So the floor is a record: three and a half metres of stacked postures, each layer a snapshot of where the good breathing was, laid down at a fingernail per few thousand cycles. The living read it constantly, because they walk on it, and the shape of the substrate steers the feet. Nobody remembers anything. The floor remembers, and the floor pushes.

This is also how the Kiln will die, and it knows, in the only way it can know things. The archive rises toward the heat. Every remembered thing is another fingernail of the room lost. The Congregation is being slowly buried in its own history, from the bottom, and there is no mechanism in it anywhere for stopping.

---

The hunters are not another species. That is the part visitors get wrong.

A body in an exceptional position — high on a drape, directly over the fissure's plume, in a gradient so steep it is almost violent — takes in more than it can spend. Usually it splits, and the two halves go on being tissue. Occasionally it doesn't. It keeps the surplus, and grows, and at a certain size the internal thermosiphon becomes powerful enough to sustain itself off nothing but its own stored volume of hot salt. Ten legs, arm's length, and independent. It no longer needs the wall. It is no longer breathing the room.

Freedom, here, is exactly and only the freedom to eat your relatives. There is nothing else to eat. So it goes up into the vault where it can radiate, and comes down along the drapes, and takes every third or fourth body, and this is not cruelty or even predation in the sense you mean; it is the Congregation's pruning organ, tissue that has specialised into shears.

And here is the thing that will sit with you afterward. The hunter runs at nearly five hundred degrees. It is the hottest object in the chamber. Wherever it goes it is a gradient on legs — the best breathing in the world, better than the fissure, better than anything — and so the drapes *follow* it. They thicken beneath its path. The bodies most likely to be taken are the bodies that shoved hardest to be there. To be eaten, you have to stand in the one place where you can properly breathe.

No fear anywhere in it. Fear is a thing done in advance, and there is nowhere to run to, and it works out fine.

---

The darkness above is not empty. You were told that.

Hunters keep growing. Past a certain mass a hunter can't hold a drape, and can't move usefully, and does the last thing available: cements itself to the vault and stops. It is still alive. It will be alive for a hundred thousand cycles, a fat immobile store of molten salt hanging in the dark, cooling at some rate too slow to matter, radiating steadily.
