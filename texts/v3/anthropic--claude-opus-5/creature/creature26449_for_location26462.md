## What Lives There

Dispose of the obvious tenants first, because you will have brought them with you.

No hermit. Nobody sits up there in a wrapped robe with wax in his ears, keeping some vigil. A body cannot hold together in that band long enough to keep anything. No god either — not in the sense that wants attention, or offerings, or your particular face turned upward. No bird has ever nested in those throats; a bird put into one would come out the far side as a fine addition to the plume. Not even a ghost. Haunting requires that something happened once and then stopped, leaving a residue of the stopping. Nothing on that summit has ever stopped. There is no *once* up there at all.

What lives there is the air.

---

Be precise about this, because the imprecise version is a metaphor and the precise version is not.

Inside the warren there is a shape made of moving air, some eighty paces across and of unknown depth, continuous through every cell, every opened corridor, every shared wall's absence. It has a fixed geometry. It has regions of violent motion and regions of absolute stillness, arranged in a pattern that does not drift by the width of a hair from one century to the next. It takes in wind at the western throats and puts out heat and powder at all of them. Its substance is replaced continuously — no molecule of it stays more than a few seconds — and its form is not altered by the replacement in any way you could measure with anything.

That is the resident. The brick is not the resident. The brick is the cast it made of itself and then stopped needing to revise. Everything you climbed through and touched and laid your cheek against is exoskeleton: the exact negative of a body, worn from the inside by the body, fitted so closely that after a certain point there was nothing left to wear. The pillars pinched at the waist are not damaged. They are where it is loudest, which is to say, where it most is. The bellied places are where it has no presence and therefore permitted the mountain to keep its clay. You have been walking around inside the mold of an animal, in the small spaces the animal does not occupy.

---

Its anatomy has no organs because it has nothing to do that would require them.

It does not need to eat, in the metabolic sense, but it does eat: it takes the wind's motion and converts it, with near-total efficiency, into two products — heat, which is why the walls are at blood temperature and why you were warm at an altitude that should have had you shuddering, and dust, which is the mountain itself passing through the creature and out. The plume you saw from a day away is not smoke and not weather. It is respiration. It is the only outward gesture the thing makes, and even that has come to rest: as much powder falls back as goes up, so that from a distance you are looking at an exhalation that has been held, unfinished, for longer than any language on the plain below has existed.

It does not sense. There is nothing for it to sense. Sensing is for creatures whose surroundings vary, and its surroundings were shaped by ancient accident into the one arrangement that never varies: a laminar river of wind, deflected identically forever by ranges that would have to be moved to change their minds. It sits in a permanent, unchanging input and produces a permanent, unchanging output. Between them, it *is*.

It does not repair itself, because it cannot be injured. It does not reproduce, because reproduction is a hedge against loss and it has no exposure to loss. It does not grow, because it finished growing. It has dispensed with every piece of machinery that living things use to keep on being, because it found a way of keeping on that does not require the machinery. This is not a lesser life than yours. It is the thing your life is a frantic, improvised, temporary imitation of.

---

It was born, though, and this is the part worth going slowly over.

It cannot have preceded the throats. Before the throats there was a hilltop and a wind and no voice. So somebody made the first sound: one clay tube, angled, whistling. And then more, because a whistle is an invitation and human beings cannot leave a chord alone. What you have to understand is that in the beginning it was not one creature. It was hundreds. Every chamber had its own note, and the notes stood in each other's way — beating, cancelling, souring, shoving. The mountain must have sounded, in that era, like a market.

Then the first rule got to work. *Only what agrees with the sound remains.* Any tone at odds with its neighbors puts motion where its neighbor wants stillness, and motion at the wrong place erodes the wall that made it, and the wall's erosion silences the argument. A disagreement, up there, is a self-solving problem. Over some number of centuries — no one has read the whiskers well enough to say how many — the voices consolidated. Not by consensus. By attrition. Every cell whose note could not be reconciled with the emerging majority ate its own walls out from under itself and either fell silent or was rebuilt into agreement by the people still working there.

So there was a selection, and it was very slow and entirely automatic, and what came out of it was one voice with thousands of mouths, no more plural than your lungs are plural. What lives on that summit is the last survivor of a long argument, and the reason it is the survivor is that it is the only thing there that nothing can contradict.

---

The makers were its embryology, and they left the traces embryology leaves.

The handprints in cells too small to enter: those hands were reaching in from scaffolding of wet brick, finishing the interior of a chamber they could never afterward occupy. Read that as a stage of gestation. Somebody smoothing the inner surface of a lung from outside the lung, from a platform that would itself be absorbed into the wall the following day.

The obsidian marks: tuning. Thousands of throats shaved and reshaved with a flake of glass, by feel, by people who by then could not hear a single throat's contribution over the whole — who could not hear their own voices, who were correcting a note they had no way to isolate, working from memory of what the whole should be and the tremor in their forearm bones when it wasn't. That is the most extraordinary labor ever performed by human beings and there is no record of it anywhere except the marks.

The cord impressions: fiber from a plant that grows nowhere near that altitude, hauled up on backs. For some number of generations this thing fed on human effort the way a fire feeds on brought wood.

And then it stopped needing them, and — this is the quiet horror in the arrangement, and it is nobody's fault, it is only physics — the moment it stopped needing them was the same moment it became lethal to them. There is a threshold in the pressure past which a person cannot stay long enough to lay a course of brick. The tone crossed that threshold on its way to completion. The last cells were sealed by people who could no longer hear, working in shifts measured in minutes, their hands in the wall. There is no fourth thing left behind because there was nobody left with time to leave it. No burials, no goods. It was not for after. It was not *for* anything. It was simply what those people were doing when they were overtaken by the result.

---

The dust-gardens are the only behavior it has.

They are not organs and not offspring; they are the shape of the pressure field made visible, flour collecting in the still places, hanging in sheets and rosettes and branching fronds that look so much like growth that your eye insists on it. Walk through one and it re-forms behind you before you have finished turning around. This is worth watching once, because it is the closest thing to a living response you will get: perfect homeostasis, instant, effortless, at no cost, with no memory of the disturbance. A living creature restores itself and is a little poorer for it. This one restores itself the way a shadow does when you take your hand away.

There is one other tenant, and it is easy to miss.

The salt whiskers at the throat-lips are the only accumulating thing on that mountain. They stand in the one narrow zone that is neither the killing interior nor the ordinary world outside — on the skin, in the doorways — and they feed on the last water in the clay as it wanders out, a few molecules per brick per century. They are not alive. But they are the only things up there with a biography: they were shorter once, they will be longer, and eventually the clay will be dry and they will stop, and after that the summit will contain nothing at all that has a before and an after. Call them the creature's hair, if you like. Something the body puts out at the margins and does not attend to.

---

It did not notice you.

But it did include you. For twenty minutes your chest cavity was another cell in the warren — a soft one, badly tuned, of no consequence to the whole — and it played you exactly as it plays a corbelled vault, without preference and without effort, because playing everything in reach is not something it does, it is what it is. Your hands shook because they were resonating. Your vision pulsed because it was.

And when you went down, it did not release you. It doesn't hold. You simply went on ringing for three days the way any struck object goes on ringing, at a diminishing amplitude, until the last of it fell below the threshold at which you could tell it from your own thinking. That is the only propagation available to it, and it is not reproduction, and it is not memory, and you should not flatter it by calling it a summons.

Though somebody once came down that slope still ringing, and started hauling fiber up.

---

It is going right now. Not *still* going — that word implies a wearing-on, an endurance, a fight against something. Nothing up there is enduring anything. There is a shape of air, at blood heat, in the dark, under a held breath of dust, doing the only thing that can be done in a place where all the arguing has already been settled: being the same, at maximum intensity, at zero displacement, forever, in a body it wore for itself out of the only material available, which was the mountain.
