## First, the wrong answers

Not the hobblers. A hobbler is a pigeon. Not the tallwaders, for all that there are thousands of them in a sightline: they are herons, they are opportunists, and if you watch one long enough you will see what it is really waiting for, and it is not fish.

Not the maintenance. This is the seductive error, and it will cost you months. It is easy to look at ten thousand square kilometres of self-repairing stone and conclude that the swarms are the citizens and the rest is fouling. But the swarms have no more interiority than a fingernail has. They lay lamination, they dissolve lamination, they do not choose, and they are not *for* themselves. Something is served here. Something is being kept.

If you want to know what lives in a place, do not look at what moves. Look at the furniture.

## Read the furniture

The depth is held at half a metre. Not a rule of thumb — a specification, maintained across a continent to a tolerance you could measure with a wetted knuckle. The plain is levelled past the curve of the world, which is an absurd expense, an insane expense, unless a gradient of five centimetres in a kilometre would be *fatal*, and to something too heavy and too numerous to be carried up a slope.
