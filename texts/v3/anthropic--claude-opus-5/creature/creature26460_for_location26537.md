# A Census of the Chord Room

Take the census first. It is short, and it is not the answer, but you cannot get to the answer without it.

Two kinds of thing here could be called alive by any test you carry in your body. There is a third that is alive by no test you carry and is nevertheless the only tenant. And there is a fourth, and the fourth is the reason for the room.

---

## The rosettes

One bore in three hundred. Grey, coin-sized, the leaves folded and refolded past counting, dry as the ash inside a bone.

Handle one. It comes away from the lip more easily than it should — the pale root slides out of the clay whole, a finger's length of something between tendon and wick, tapering, unbroken, with no torn end and no wound. It did not grow into the clay. The clay was made around it, or it was set there and the clay closed. There is no third possibility that leaves the fit that clean.

Hold it in your palm. It does nothing. It does not wilt, does not close, does not lighten as water leaves it, because there is no water in it to leave and never was. Whatever it does with the white light and the passing dust, it does at a rate too slow to be called a rate. It is not waiting to be replanted. It is not dying. You have simply moved it.

Now listen.

The bore you uncovered is singing.

You will feel it before you place it: a wobble in the great chord, a beat, a slow sour throb somewhere in the middle register, as though one string on an enormous instrument had gone a hair flat and were now grinding against its neighbour four times a second. It is very small. In the roar it is almost nothing. And you will never once stop hearing it, for as long as you are in this world.

That is what the rosettes are. Not inhabitants. Mutes. Felts. Every one of them sits on a bore that must not sound, and there are a hundred thousand bores and three hundred-odd rosettes, and their scatter across the bowl looks like accident until you have opened one and understood that it isn't. Somewhere there is a reading of that scatter which is a correction — this pipe silenced, and this, and this, so that the rest come out as a chord and not as a howl. The pattern is a score. You are standing on notation.

And you have just deleted a mark from it, and you cannot put it back, because the root will not re-enter the clay and the rosette does not care whether it is on a lip or in your hand.

## The filaments

High on the eastern arc, out of the smallest bores, the silver hairs. A hand long, standing straight against a wind that ought to lay them flat, blurred at their length with vibration.

They are not moving in the wind. Test it: cup your palm over the bore, kill the airflow entirely, and the filament goes on shivering exactly as before. It is not driven. It is *tuned* — its length is a length that answers the note the room is holding, and it has been answering since before the dust settled into its final crescents.

Nothing here is decorative. If the rosettes are the stops, these are the other half of the pair. A thing that vibrates in sympathy is a thing that is being told something. Consider that the whole room is aimed inward at its own centre, and every bore is exhaling, and nothing anywhere in this world is arranged to receive except these few thousand hairs on the high east wall, and then consider what a body does with a sensor.

It does not hear you. You have no voice here; that is the first thing the room did to you. It hears itself. Whatever the filaments report, they report the state of the note — that it is still true, still this loud, still this pitch, still, still, still — up some channel you will never find, to something that needs to know.

Which is what you interrupted when you rolled one between your fingers and it went, with no sound you could possibly have detected, and lay down on the clay as a bright permanent thread.

There is now a hole in the hearing of something enormous. It is a hole the width of one hair. It will never close. Nothing in this world closes.

---

## The note

Now the tenant.

Ask what occupies this room and the honest answer is not the rosettes, which are hardware, and not the filaments, which are instrumentation. It is the sound. The sound fills the entire volume of the bowl to the rim and out past the rim into the white. It arrived before anything else and it has not left. It eats what enters — your speech went into it and did not come out. It is the only thing here with mass and motion, the only thing doing anything at all, and it does that thing at full strength, without variance, without fatigue, on and on.

It is not alive. It has no parts and no metabolism and nothing to lose. But it is the resident. Everything solid in this world exists in order to produce it or to shape it or to check on it; the clay is scaffolding for the chord, not the other way round. You are not in a room with a sound in it. You are inside a sound that has been given a room to be exact in.

And here is the part you will only work out later, lying somewhere else, with your lips still cracked:

There are no tracks in the dust but yours.

That is a complete proof. Nothing has ever walked here. No one has ever stood at the focus of those hundred thousand attentions and heard this. The chord has been held, perfectly, unbroken, unheard, for so long that erosion finished its work and gave up — and the first ear ever to arrive was yours, uninvited, arriving through a door that closed behind itself and was never a door.

The note was not made for hearing. That's what the dust is telling you. It was made for holding.

---

## The one whose lungs those are

Everything above is anatomy, and anatomy is not a tenant. So: one thing lives here, and the room is not its house. The room is *in* it.

Note what is missing. Not a throwing-ridge, not a seam, not a fingerprint, not a cut. Dig and you find bore behind bore, tube packed against tube in that tight illogical honeycomb neatness, all the way down, with the last green film of glaze surviving only in the deep angles where the tubes touch and the wind never reached.

That is not manufacture. Nobody builds like that. But things *grow* like that. Open a long bone and you will find the same thing: cells packed to a fit no mason could match, struts laid down along the exact lines of the load, no tool mark anywhere because there was never a tool — only a surface that was alive while it was laying itself down, and stopped being alive when it was finished, and left a thin skin of itself in the crevices. Your green glaze is periosteum. You are standing in trabeculae.

So the Chord Room is a hollow in a bone, and the bone is bored through with a hundred thousand canals, and the canals are exhaling in tune. There is no builder to look for. There never was a hand. There is a body that grew this the way you grew the whorls on your thumb, without consulting itself, and the body is at a scale where its bones have weather in them.

It breathes out and does not breathe in. Not here. Somewhere else in that immensity, unimaginably far by any measure you'd use for a bowl thirty paces across, something is taking air in — or something is not, and this exhalation comes from a reserve so vast the notion of running out has no meaning at the timescales involved. Either way, out here at the far end of the pipework, the pressure has been steady on your palm for longer than the dust has been where it is.

And it is a chord. That is the whole of the difference between a corpse whistling in the wind and what you are actually inside of. Noise is a leak. A chord is a decision. Something chose these frequencies and stopped these three hundred pipes and grew ears to check them, and is now, this instant, with no sign at all of intending to stop, holding the note.

You will want to know why. There is nothing in the room that will tell you. You can only lay the possibilities down side by side and refuse to marry any of them: that this is a syllable in a word so long that the word will not be finished within the lifespan of your species; that this is a signal, held open, on the chance that something will one day answer; that this is a drone under a music whose other voices are in chambers you will never reach, and the whole of what you heard was one sustained pedal in a piece; that the thing cannot stop, that the note is the only thing standing between it and something it will not have; that it is simply asleep, and this is snoring, and everything you interpreted as intention is the ordinary tidy geometry of a body doing nothing on purpose.

The room will not choose for you. The room has arrived at itself.

---

## And you

The last entry in the census, and the only one that qualifies as an inhabitant in the sense you meant when you asked.

You are the sole thing in this world that changes. The only wet thing. The only warm thing. The only decaying thing — everything else here finished decaying and stopped, and you are still going, at speed. The air is already taking the film off your teeth and the moisture off your eyes and it will not stop taking, because it is not weather, it is a function, and it has no setting other than on.

You are the ruin in the organ. You brought dampness into a place that eliminated dampness, and motion into a place at rest, and the only marks that will ever be added to this world are the ones your feet are laying down now, in dust that the wind can no longer move because the wind goes the wrong way and always has.

The room cannot heal what you break and cannot remove what you add. It has no mechanism for either. What it has is inclusion. Given time — and time here is the one commodity in surplus — it will do to you what it did to the glaze and the rims and the crescents: take everything that can be taken, wear back every crisp edge, and then stop, leaving what remains bone-white and blunt and permanent, at the focus of a hundred thousand small black attentions, in the middle of a note that is still being held.

You would make a fine mute. There are pipes down there that would be quieter with you on them.

The chord would beat a little, sour, four times a second, and something very far away would be told about it by a shivering hair, and would go on holding anyway.
