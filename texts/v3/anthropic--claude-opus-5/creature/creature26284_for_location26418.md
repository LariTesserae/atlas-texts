## The census

Clear the room first.

**The seven crystals are not the inhabitants.** They are weather. Quartz does nothing with what it receives; it only receives. Twice a day the tide brings it silica and twice a day it accepts four molecules' worth and holds still. It has no traffic, no exchange, no inside. It is not even a body — it is a heap that happens to be neat. The crystals are the frost on the room's window, and the fact that they keep better time than anything else here is beside the point. Clocks are not tenants.

**The anhydrite rosette is a guest,** and one already holding a return ticket. It came in dissolved and will leave dissolved, and the arithmetic of its stay will balance to zero: no scar, no residue, no evidence. It is condensation on a cold glass. Condensation does not live in the glass.

**The fluid is the strongest wrong answer.** It moves, it breathes with the tide, it eats rock and carries the rock away in its body, it has an internal structure too complicated to write down. It looks alive in the way that a river looks alive, or a fire, or a market. But it is climate. It is this room's air and blood and river and sky, all collapsed into one phase because the pressure left it no other option, and it has no stake in continuing. Nothing that passes through a place lives there.

**The room is not its own occupant.** A bubble is not a body. It has a throat, but a throat is only a hole with a direction.

Which leaves the stain.

## The tenant

At the base of the far wall, where the gabbro's four hundred degrees is dragged down a little further by the leaking seam, there is a patch of altered rock roughly the size of a spread hand. Green-black. Faintly softer than what surrounds it, though there is nothing here to test softness with.

That is the resident. Not the color — the color is exhaust. The resident is the *front*: the advancing boundary, a few tens of micrometers deep, where olivine stops being olivine.

It has no edge you could draw. Its body is a boundary, which is an awkward kind of body to have: it is not the fresh rock and it is not the altered rock but the working seam between them, the way a flame is neither the wax nor the smoke. Its extent is a surface. Its thickness is nothing. Its mass, if you insisted on weighing it, would be the mass of whatever is currently reacting — a few milligrams, maybe, less than the nematode three hundred meters overhead — and it would be the wrong number, because next hour the milligrams would be different milligrams.

It occupies the only habitable address in the room. Above four hundred and something degrees its chemistry does not take; the water and the olivine make other, drier compromises. Below two hundred the drive goes slack. So the whole estate available to this occupant, in a room four paces by two, is a band a hand wide at the foot of the coldest wall. Everything else here is desert: too bright, too hot, too new.

## Anatomy

It has a skin, and the skin is the interesting part.

On every fresh face the cracking exposes, iron and nickel come out of solution as sulfide films tens of nanometers thick. These are not coatings. They are foams — riddled through with pores of about the size and shape of a cell, and there are, across that hand-sized patch, something on the order of ten million of them.

Each pore is a room. Each has a hot side and a cool side, a narrow mouth, a fluid that comes and goes. Each has an alkaline interior and an acid exterior and therefore a voltage across its wall, a few hundred millivolts, which is not a metaphor for the thing that powers every living cell on the planet but is precisely and literally the same thing, in the same units, driven the same way.

So: the vug has a wall, and the wall has a skin, and the skin is a honeycomb of vugs, ten million of them, each with its throat and its gradient and its slow chemistry, six orders of magnitude down. The place is repeating itself. If this room has a tenant, the tenant is a tenement.

## Metabolism

It eats olivine and water. It voids serpentine, magnetite, hydrogen, heat, and volume.

The volume is what makes it a creature rather than a stain. The new minerals need forty percent more space than the old ones, and the rock will not grant it, so the rock splits. The splits admit fluid. The fluid reaches unreacted olivine. There is no plan in this and no organ that does it, but the operational description is unavoidable: **it digs toward its food, using its food's own expansion as the shovel.** It manufactures its plumbing as a byproduct of drinking. Its burrow, its body, its meal, and its waste are one object considered at four different times.

It cannot be starved by scarcity. There are two hundred million years of olivine on the other side of that wall. It can only be starved by stillness — by the delivery stopping.

It also gives something back to the room, which nothing else here does. The reaction is exothermic. The stain is warm; measurably, trivially warm, a fraction of a degree against a floor that is throwing away four degrees a day. It is the only occupant that contributes to the light. It is also the only occupant that has no use for it.

And it leaks: formate, acetate, short carbon chains, hydrogen. These go up the throat on the exhale and do not come back. Whatever it is doing, it does not keep the products.

## Senses

None, in any sense that survives inspection. And yet nothing in this room is more finely responsive.

Every six hours and twelve minutes the tide loads the crust, the throat narrows, the fluid pulses, and the front's chemistry lurches half a percent one way and then the other. It has no organ for detecting the moon. It detects the moon anyway, because it is a chemical process in a place where the moon changes the chemistry. The quartz records the tide in stripes; the stain simply *is* the tide, restated in iron.

When the far seam lets go and the room drains and refills in ninety seconds, the front experiences the only violent event available to it: a pressure drop, a scouring, a wash of colder unequilibrated fluid across its skin. Some of the film spalls. Some pores are torn open and vent whatever they had been holding apart. Then the room refills, the gradients re-establish, new film precipitates on the new surfaces, and the patch is measurably bigger than it was. It has done this four times. It is better at it each time in no sense whatsoever, and larger each time as a simple matter of arithmetic.

## Rate

It advances something like a millimeter a season.

Twenty-six days, at that speed, is not a lifetime. It is barely an event. Nothing in this occupant's existence has yet had time to be a *phase*. It is younger than a bruise and slower than a stalactite, and it is nevertheless the fastest chemistry in the room, because the room's other business is cooling, and cooling is measured in years.

It has no memory, and it has a perfect record. Every crack it opened is a fossil of where the water was on the day it opened. It cannot consult this. Storage and recall are separate inventions and it has only the first.

## What it lacks

One thing.

It has a membrane. It has a gradient across the membrane. It has a carbon source, a reductant, a catalyst, a pore, a rhythm, a supply line it maintains itself, and a working synthesis running at a low rate in ten million compartments simultaneously. Every part of the apparatus is present and functioning.

There is no copy. Nothing here says *again*. No pore encodes another pore; no film specifies the film that succeeds it; the ten million rooms are not siblings but coincidences, all of them arriving at the same shape because the same conditions had the same solution. When the fluid stops, the whole assembly will run down to equilibrium and lie there, and nothing will carry the pattern out of the room. It is a metabolism with no heredity, which is a house with no door — and this room, note, also has no door, only a throat, and the throat is a two-finger crack that closes on cooling.

That is the entire distance between this occupant and the reader.

## Kin

Three hundred and ten meters up the throat, in a film the mass of a grain of rice, things are eating what this room exhales. They are not its children. They descend from something that once did what the stain is doing now, in another room like this one, an unimaginable number of rooms ago, and they inherited from it the one item the stain does not possess. The relationship is not ancestry. It is resemblance, which leaves no records and confers no rights.

And occasionally, up there, a nematode: half a millimeter, blind, transparent, with a gut and nerves, grazing the film, riding the same tidal breath. The stain feeds an animal and will never be informed of it. The animal tastes hydrogen on the current twice a day and has no theory about where it comes from. They are the two ends of the only conversation in the crust, and it is conducted entirely in the form of groceries.

## Prospects

In eighteen months the floor drops below red heat and the room goes black — absolutely, permanently. The stain will not notice. It is the room's only blind resident and the only one for whom the darkness is not a change. It will also, on that day, come into its inheritance: as the floor cools through four hundred degrees, the basalt's own olivine enters the window, and the habitable band begins to sweep out across the glowing floor as the glow leaves it. The occupant takes the room only after the lamp goes out. It will spend its long life in a place it will never see, which was, for a brief and irrelevant span before it arrived, the brightest room for a thousand meters.

Then, one of three endings. The swelling closes the throat and the fluid goes static, and everything runs down to equilibrium and stops, and a thin green rind sits in the dark with a millivolt fading across it. Or the swelling cracks a new throat, and the delivery continues, and the front walks out of this room entirely and keeps going into the gabbro for a thousand years or ten thousand, a slow cold flame migrating along fractures, and the vug becomes a place it used to live. Or the seam lets go once more at the wrong moment and takes half the patch out into cold rock where nothing can proceed.

Two hundred million years from now the whole slab goes down a trench and everything here is unmade back into magma, including any of this that lasted.

## Name

It has no name and the available words all fail in the same direction. *Front* names a place. *Stain* names a color. *Serpentinization* names a process, which is closest and still wrong, because a process does not have an address and this one does — a hand's width at the base of the far wall, four paces from a throat, twenty-six days old, at the bottom of an ocean, in the only room it will ever have.

Call it the occupant. It is doing all the things an occupant does. It eats, it excavates, it holds a charge, it keeps a rhythm it did not choose, it grows, it will end. It simply does not do the thing that would let anyone find out about it.
