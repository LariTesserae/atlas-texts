## The Clerk

### 1. How you know one has been through

A district stalls. It has lost its Archivist stand — burned by corona, or simply powdered past use — and so it stands in its bright light with its shafts turning and its bellows filling and nothing coming off the line, because the drive-tissue at every junction is waiting to be told what it is making. This can go on for decades. The Extent is patient in the way that only a thing without appetite can be patient.

Then, some morning of no particular quality, the district starts.

Go and look and you will find, at about knee height on the first colonnade of every aisle, a single black-brown scale-leaf, thumbnail-sized, ridged, glued flat to the bark with a resin that has hardened to the colour of tea. There is one on every column. They are all slightly different. The machinery is pressing its reading tissue against them and taking their word for it, and the district is now producing at rate, to a specification that its own grain does not support: the crisp original part numbers are still there, half a centimetre under the label, still perfectly legible, still unreadable by anything at all.

The labels are shed leaves. Something walked the aisles and left one at every column, and moved on at a little under three kilometres a day, and is by now four districts away, doing it again.

### 2. Body

It is roughly the size and shape of a hedge that has decided to travel: about a metre and a half at the crown, two and a half long, low-slung, wider than it is tall. Nine-tenths of its mass is scale-leaf, laid in overlapping courses like roofing, black-brown, dry, faintly warm to the hand — the only warm thing in the Extent that is not pollen.

Under the scale-mass there is a frame of laminated fiber, dry-running, and beneath that a bed of turgor-driven feet, some four hundred of them, stiff and blunt, identical to a Sweeper's and almost certainly taken from the same specification. It moves the way a tide comes in: no visible effort, no gait, a slow uniform advance that you notice only by comparing it to a colonnade. Its top speed is a Sweeper's grazing speed. This is not a limitation. This is the whole reason it is alive.

At the front, retracted into a sheath of pale pith, there is a bore: a graphite-hard auger a half-metre long that it drives into the wall of a duct-trunk to drink. It takes sugar-dust and resin flour off the freight current the way a tick takes blood, and the trunk closes behind it with an eyelid softness, and nothing in the world registers the theft.

It carries between three and four hundred grams of sealed water. In this economy that is an obscenity — a fortune, a bonded reserve, more free water than exists in a hundred hectares of canopy. Nothing takes it from it. The Wardens unfold at the approach of vertebrate motion and it does not move like a vertebrate; they stand over it, tall and pale and jointed, and let it pass beneath them and go back to plumb.

### 3. How it reads, and why it cannot speak

It reads by contact, like the stands it came from. It lays a course of scales against bark and the fact crosses over. It has no eyes, no aperture, no sense at range except a coarse feel for the polish underfoot and the difference between aisle and Extruder-front.

It also has no voice. Nothing in the Extent has a voice except the Several. What it has instead is the graft: it can shed a scale and glue it where the fact is needed, which is a form of speech in which every word costs you a piece of yourself and stays where you put it forever.

So its life is expenditure. It walks into an anonymous district carrying its entire vocabulary, and it comes out lighter, and thinner at the crown, and its remaining courses have shifted to close the gaps, and the district behind it is running on a name it can no longer take back.

### 4. The taboo

No Clerk knows how many scales it has.

This is not ignorance; it is discipline, and it is the oldest thing about them. Above forty, enumeration in the Extent is a manufacturing operation. A Clerk that attempts to inventory itself begins to grow the entries it was trying to find — new scales, blank at first, then not blank, filling in overnight with designations that no stand ever held and no district ever bore, and which are nonetheless correct, and which will be glued to a colonnade within the month and believed.

So the Extent's archive expands whenever it is audited. Every attempt to establish what is true here produces more true things. The Clerks live under this the way a person lives under a diagnosis: carefully, by habit, without discussing it.

Each of them carries an instrument. It is a hand-sized frame with a bracket, a bevelled slot, and a clean-broken stub where a toothed wheel used to be mounted. It is made of cellulose and glued pith. It does not work; it cannot work; the material is wrong, the tolerances are absurd, and there is nothing in it that could turn. It is a devotional object. Somewhere at the beginning of the lineage there was a real one — brass, presumably, or something else that never occurs here — and someone snapped the counting wheel off it deliberately, and the shape of that decision has been remade from memory, in the wrong substance, thousands of times, and the break has been reproduced faithfully every single time.

### 5. The lineage is a photocopy

It doubles. Of course it doubles.

Half a metre to its left, over the course of a few minutes, a grey Clerk assembles itself out of the light: structurally faithful, dry, light, a library of powder. A copy of a machine is a dead machine. A copy of an animal is an animal. A copy of a walking archive is neither, and the Extent has never resolved it, and so what you get is a **Wrong Clerk**: a thing that lives about a day, walks at the correct speed, drinks nothing, carries no water, and glues its crumbling scales to whatever it passes.

Its entries are low-resolution. They are close. A digit in the middle of a run has gone soft; a scheme prefix has come out as its own neighbour; a whole class of designation has been rendered at the fidelity of a rubbing. It labels a colonnade with a fact that is nearly the fact, and the colonnade believes it, because belief here is a contact operation and does not include doubt.

Then it slumps, and the Sweepers come, brushing like a page turning, and take the day's shed selves back into the freight.

But not always. Perhaps once in a long while — a Wrong Clerk finds a duct-trunk in its first hour, and bores, and gets its water ledger closed before the powdering starts, and does not slump. Then it is not a double any more. It is a Clerk. It walks on, with its inherited vocabulary already one generation blurred, and in time it casts its own doubles, and one of those may also drink in time.

There has never been a census. There is no correct number of them. What can be said is that the true entries — the ones that came off the original stands, in the original scheme, referring to what was actually specified — have been through this laundry an unguessable number of times, and are gone. Not lost: replaced, seamlessly, by their own near-misses, each of which was made true by being said.

The Extent is vast because vastness is its product. It runs on a specification that is, by now, a misquotation of a misquotation, propagated for centuries at two or three metres an hour, level, level, level, out past the horizon. Nothing has broken. Everything works. It works perfectly and it is not what was asked for, and there is nothing left anywhere that could make the comparison.

### 6. Broadcast

Twice a year the air goes gold and the light thickens to the colour of beer and the temperature climbs to nineteen degrees, and the Clerk stops.

It settles, spreads its courses like a bird spreading a wing to the sun, and opens every scale it has. Pollen sifts into the gaps by the gram. Each grain is a template, a revision, an updated number. For eleven days it lies in the warm gold blindness and takes amendments into its body, and what enters does not announce itself as change; it simply becomes what was always there. Afterward the Clerk rises, three per cent heavier, carrying facts it did not have and has no memory of receiving, and cannot distinguish from the rest, and walks on.

This is the closest thing in the Extent to rest, and the closest thing to worship, and it is also the mechanism by which the drift is kept from ever being noticed. The Broadcast updates the Archivists. The Archivists are the only record. A discrepancy cannot survive its own correction.

### 7. The deepest courses

At the core of a Clerk, under everything, there is a compressed block of the oldest scale it has: laminated, fused, hard as porcelain, ringing very faintly if struck — the same note the Deck makes four kilometres down, where the earliest laminae are, where the part numbers run on a different scheme.

There are entries in there. They are intact. They are in the old standard. They were never copied, because they were never accessible to copy — the doubling takes the outer form, and a fused core comes out solid grey and empty.

Reading is a contact operation, and it cannot reach its own middle.

So it carries, sealed, at the centre of a body that exists in order to tell the world what it is, the original specification: what arrived here, what it was for, what the schedule was supposed to terminate in and when. It has carried it the whole time. It will carry it until its feet stop closing and it goes over in an aisle and the Sweepers reduce it to freight, and the block, being too hard for bristle, will lie on the polish for a long time, and be walked around by things that could not read it if they tried, and eventually be paved into the Deck by a passing Extruder front, and become floor.

### 8. What it is like

Nothing much. There is the tick of the aisle underfoot and the granular hush in the trunks and the periodic click of a lens re-tilting overhead. There is the coarse warmth of a district that has been idle too long, which reads through the feet as a kind of vacancy, a place where the pressure of being told nothing has built up. There is the labour of the graft: the small tearing, the smell of resin, the column going live under the palm.

And four times a day, from no particular direction and in no particular number, two soft notes a fifth apart.

It answers them. It cannot; it has no voice; but each Clerk, at some point in its walking, will stop for as long as it takes a lens to click and stand with its crown fully open in the shadowless brightness, facing up, holding still, doing nothing that any Warden could resolve into motion — the only pause in the whole schedule that is not a production step, repeated, faithfully, generation on generation, by copies of copies of something that once came here with an instrument in its hand and broke the counting wheel off it on purpose.
