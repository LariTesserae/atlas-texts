# What Lives There

---

**The tenant you will guess first**

Eel. Everybody says eel. The tube is wrist-thick and travels at the pace of a man walking to a gate he isn't worried about, and it goes around the doorframe rather than through it, which is exactly the kind of thing an eel would do. The guess is reasonable and it is wrong, and it is worth saying why, because the reason is the animal.

An eel has to be somewhere. That is not a small requirement. To be somewhere is to hold still for the length of an instant, and instants are the crop down here; they are harvested; they are what the pale weed builds its fennel-fine leaves out of. Nothing that needs a place to stand can afford to live in the room under the blackthorn. The shrimp try, and hang in the fronds like commas, unable to finish a stroke.

So: not an eel. Something that has solved the problem by not having the problem.

---

**What it is made of**

The weed eats duration, but it does not eat all of it. There is a part it cannot use.

Take the bubble that is at the floor and then at the roof with a grey nothing where its rising should have been. The rising is gone — but the *toward* is not gone. Direction is not the same substance as interval. The weed takes the interval and leaves the toward, the way a beast strips a carcass and leaves the wire of the sinew. Over a season this residue accumulates in the room the way lint accumulates under a bed: coils of undigested going, from-here-to-there with the here and the there both eaten out.

At some point, and nobody knows what the point is, enough of it collects in one place to constitute an appetite.

That is the animal. It is made entirely of travel. It has no location because location was the part that got eaten, and it has no duration because that was harvested in March. What it has is heading, and quite a lot of it, and it must spend it continuously, because to stop would require an *at* to stop in, and it hasn't got one and cannot be given one. It is not fleeing. It is not hunting. It is a sentence that consists only of the word *toward*, and it goes on saying it, and the weed is slow enough to close that for four or five seconds the shape of the saying is held in the greenery like a print in bread.

You cannot name it. A name is a place to stand. The woman who has been down eleven times calls it *the runner*, but she knows that this is a courtesy she is doing herself, not the animal.

---

**What it does, which looks exactly like intention**

It goes through the doorframe.

Not around, that once — around is the wall route, the nine noticed times. The crossing is the other thing, and it has done that some tens of thousands of times since February, because it is always doing it; it is the only work it has.

Through the doorframe the water is the same water at a different hour. The animal, being nothing but transit, passes through an hour the way a dog passes through a hedge: without interest, without slowing, and coming out the other side with burrs on it. The burrs are the hour. A dog's burrs are seeds; these are afternoons. Four o'clock in late August, dust and all. The flat pewter of a wet Tuesday. It does not pick them up. It has no means of picking anything up. They snag.

On the near side, the weed strips them off it — combs them off, the way a mat of fine leaves would comb a thing passing through — and keeps them, and they hang in the fronds, perfectly clear, perfectly bright, illuminating nothing at all.

That is the whole economy. The weed eats the between and therefore needs the *at*: something fixed to hold onto, or it would drift loose in the time it has consumed and go the grey-brown of boiled nettles. Forty small fixed points are enough to moor a room. The animal, which is all between, is the creature best suited in the world to fetch them, and the least able to use them.

So it never sees the light. It has never been in one of the plums. It could not be.

---

**The order on the wall**

Here is the part the woman with the notebook has mapped, and has not told the other five.

If you go along the north wall by touch, from the fallen slabs to the doorframe, the plums come in order. Ten in the morning, half past ten, eleven. They run forward. Round the doorframe and back along the shelved roof they run on: one, two, three, the yellow four o'clock, the long amber that must be June, seven in the evening, and then they stop.

There is nothing before ten. There is nothing after seven. There is no dawn in the room, no midnight, no blue hour, no winter noon except that one pewter Tuesday sitting oddly among the summers like a stranger at a wedding.

Her conclusion, written small: *these are opening hours.*

Something used this room, and it was open from ten till seven, and people came to it in those hours and not in others, and every hour in which somebody once stood in the doorway is an hour the crossing can snag on, and every hour in which nobody did is nothing, is closed, is not available to be caught. Forty afternoons is not a collection. It is a timetable. A creature with no location and no duration is patiently reassembling the visiting hours of a building that fell out of memory, from the outside, by accident, by being combed.

It is short of a morning. It keeps going through. It will never have one, because nobody ever came at six.

---

**The basin, which is the oldest thing living**

Under the iron ring, in the silt, the weed is a white foam, finer than anywhere, brimming.

It is thicker there because it is eating something better than plain interval. The step, whose tread is worn into a shallow dish, was not worn by feet passing. It was worn by feet *hanging* — by people sitting on it in the dark, side by side, two at a time, for as long as it took. This was never only a wellhouse. It was a place you came to lose a stretch of your life that you did not want: the four months until the ship, the nine days of a fever in the next room, the winter after the bad harvest, the waiting for a letter that was already written and could not be hurried. You sat on the step and the room ate the between, and you stood up and it was later, and you had not had to be there for it.

They paid into the basin. Not coins. There are no coins. They left the waiting itself, tipped out of themselves into the stone the way you'd tip out a boot, and the weed has been working through it ever since, and it is nowhere near finished, and it will not be finished in your lifetime.

Put your hand into the foam and hold it there. What comes up the arm is not cold and is not pressure. It is somebody else's patience, very specific, very old, still going: the particular quality of waiting for a fever to break in a person you love, which is different from all other waitings and which the weed has not yet broken down into food.

The animal's route bends around the ring above it. Every time. A thing with no location, avoiding a place. Nobody has an explanation for this and the woman has stopped asking for one.

---

**The others**

The isopods in the west wall are the true old tenants and have never noticed anything. They work the mortar, pale as rice, generation on generation, and their lives are so short that the room's poverty of *between* costs them nothing they would miss. They are the only creatures down there that are simply, ordinarily alive. It is oddly steadying to touch them.

The shrimp arrive with the rain and starve, and do not rot, and hang.

The weed is one plant. All of it. The braiding, the seamlessness, the way it parts around a hand without resistance and without hurry — that is not two mats meeting, it is one organism with no opinion about its own edges. It has been here longer than the stone. The room was cut around it.

And there are six people, and one of them is beginning to be part of the inventory. She is not aware of it in any way she could be argued out of. She only notices that she waits well now — that she has lost the knack of being bored, that a February went past her like a corridor, that she can stand at a bus stop or a bedside for an hour and come out of it with the hour missing and no distress at all, only the faint grey where the middle of the word should be. She dives less often than she used to and thinks about it more. Some evening she will go down and sit on the step, because the step is worn into a dish and the dish is the right shape, and she will find that she was expected, in the mild way that a chair expects.

---

Above, the blackthorn keeps leaning. In August the light through the leaves goes that particular yellow, for about forty minutes, and the field is silent, and the water is a dark oval with drowned green grass at the rim.

Underneath it, something that is not anywhere and never has been is already going through, at the pace of a man walking to a gate he isn't worried about, and it will come back covered in four o'clock, and it will not see it, and the room will hang it on the wall where it belongs, between three and five, in the timetable of a house that nobody remembers being open.
