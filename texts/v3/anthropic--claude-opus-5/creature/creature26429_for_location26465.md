## The Tenants

### The wrong question

Everyone comes down the throat-root asking who lives here, and the question is the wrong shape. It expects a householder. It expects that somewhere behind the plates or under the felt there is a thing that owns the place, and that if you found it you could look at it, and that looking would settle the matter.

There is no such thing. There is a census, and it is very long, and it does not add up to a landlord.

What follows is most of it.

### The wall population

By mass, most of what lives here is in the plates, and most of that is grubs.

Split a plate — you will need a blade and permission you cannot obtain — and the eleven strata come apart like the leaves of a wet book. The outer three are mites, four species, none of them larger than a grain of salt, and they are the reason the plate is watertight; they mine the resin seams and re-secrete them behind themselves, so a seam is always being unmade and remade and is always, at any given second, sound. The middle strata are the roaches, blind, pale, flat as coins, three and four deep, drinking condensation off the plate's inner face and doing nothing else that anyone has identified. Under the roaches, the nursery: grubs like wet rice, packed in rows, ninety to a hundred to a hand's breadth, turned twice a cycle.

Turned by the masons. Each plate has one — a jointed animal about two metres long when uncoiled, sixty-odd segments, translucent amber over a dark gut, with forelimbs that end in combs. It grooms the grubs, it eats the failures, and it lays the black resin that seams its plate to the next. Which means the wall is not the plant's wall. The plant grows plates; the masons make them into a wall. Neither party can produce a chamber alone, and the arrangement is old enough that the plate's edges have evolved a lip to take the resin, and the mason has evolved a comb that fits the grub.

The masons do not like being looked at. This is not temperament; it is procedure. Interruption stops the grooming, the grooming is on the pulse, and a plate that misses two turnings loses the row.

And in and over and among all of this, the lights.

### Light as speech

The blinkers are the smallest tenants and the loudest. Ten thousand per wall-face, mostly larval, a few adult forms, each carrying two spots of cold yellow-green on the dorsal plate, each spot switching on the intake and off the exhale. That is the carrier wave. The message is in what the animal does *inside* the forty seconds: how early it lights, how long it holds, whether it flickers once in the middle or twice at the end.

Four regularities are established.

A syllable is one stroke. There is no faster unit; nothing here can say anything in less than forty seconds.

A plate whose face goes dark for a whole day is opened by the masons from inside within two days, and mined out, and reseamed. Every time. Without exception in the record.

Pulses of brightness travel — actual waves, crossing the wall-face at roughly a metre a stroke, dying out after fifteen or twenty metres, occasionally reflecting off a seam. Something is passed along; nobody knows what, nobody can find the recipient.

And a newly hatched gallery blinks out of phase for its first three or four days, wildly, gracelessly, on no schedule at all, and is not processed for it. This is the only tolerance in the chamber. It expires.

Put those together and you can guess at the burden of the discourse without reading a word of it. Rule three says stillness is consent: stop moving for ten minutes and you are substrate. Almost everything in the plates is still almost all of the time. The light is the loophole. The whole shining wall, all ten thousand voices of it, breathing in and out of visibility, is — as far as anyone can tell — saying *not yet, not me, still here, still here, still here,* and has been saying it, in grammatical order, for a very long time.

### The carriers

Forearm-length, matte black, unlit. They are the only caste that carries no light organ at all, and it follows from everything above that a carrier has no way to declare itself alive. It is, in the wall's terms, already substrate. It is spared because it stays on the path.
