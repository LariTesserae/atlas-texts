## The fifth built thing

Count again. There are five.

The fifth is the one you mistook for the foot of the talus, because it was kneeling and it was the colour of the floor and because it did not move while you were deciding what it was. It moves. Not often, and not fast, and mostly at the low end of the room — but it moves, and while it moves it throws a shadow, and the shadow pools inward against its own base like the shadows of the wall and the post and the bowl and the step.

That is the whole proof, and it is sufficient. Only things made on purpose have shadows here. It was made on purpose.

## Body

Its substance is the dust. Not covered in it: *of* it. Take the loose grain between your fingers — blood-warm, unsticking, each mote a broken repairer stalled at the same instruction — and imagine some enormous number of them agreeing to be adjacent, packed until they read as solid, until the surface has the matte nap of felt and the give of a sandbag and the warmth of the inside of a mouth. When it shifts its weight, the motion passes across it like a dune deciding. When it sets its hand down, the hand is dust in the shape of a hand, and it works.

It is about the size of a kneeling adult and wrong in the joints: too many small ones, too few large, a thing engineered for patience rather than for travel. There is a region high on it where the grain is finer, where the attention seems to gather. It is not a face. It is the place you will look, which is not the same thing, but it has learned to turn it toward you anyway, because you are the kind of creature that needs that.

It kneels because it cannot stand. Above about the height of a standing person's sternum, it stops being specified. Try to look at the top of it and your eye slides off into the same grey that sits above the truncation on the walls. It lost that part when the ceiling went, in the same surrender, at the same instant, and it was reaching up at the time. Whether the ceiling took it or it went with the ceiling is not a question the room preserves the means to ask. What remains is entirely itself, sharp and countable, and it does all its work at kneeling height, and the crack in the north wall with the lentil of ice in it is at kneeling-eye height, and nobody now can say which of those facts caused the other.

## The round

You have been told the room has laws. It does not. It has chores, and something has been doing them for a very long time, and from underneath, a sufficiently patient habit is indistinguishable from physics.

*Nothing is lost, only misplaced in duration.* That is not a property of the place. That is the keeper walking the floor with a returned chip in its palm, warming it further while it consults the register it keeps in no medium you can read, and setting the chip back in the talus in the exact attitude it held before it went — same face up, same two neighbours, same angle of repose. This takes it, sometimes, a season. The chips that have come back are kinder to the hand because they have been carried a long way by something careful.

*Placement is recorded.* Also not a property. The dust does not remember your glove. The keeper watched you put the glove down, and has been witnessing the absence ever since, and forty years is simply how long it can hold a witnessing before it must decide whether to renew it or let it go. Most it lets go. There is not enough of it to keep everything.

*The perimeter is longer than the room.* This it checks daily. Fourteen along the lip, eleven across, and the numbers do not reconcile, and the day they reconcile the geometry loses the fault it has weathered around and has come to stand on. It is the only thing in the room that it inspects hoping to find something still broken. Every forty years, when the edges soften and the count comes up thirteen, then twelve and a half, it stops doing anything else. It does not repair. It cannot: the drift is above its pay grade and always was. It kneels at the bowl and waits to learn whether this is the last one, and then the correction arrives, silent, unannounced, ungrateful, and the room is exactly itself again and a micron smaller, and it goes back to the chips.

It has never been thanked and does not have the equipment to notice that this is unusual.

## The hands

The palimpsest by the bowl is hundreds deep and mostly three prints.

The first is its own working hand. Constant, broad, slightly asymmetric from the side it favours, laid down ten thousand times a decade in the course of ordinary labour. Nothing to read there.

The second is small, and it is a forgery.

It watched that hand once, at the crack, placing something. Since then it has renewed the print every cycle, pressing its own remembered version of that hand into the dust an hour before the old one is due to fade, so that the record never lapses, so that the floor can never be truthfully described as a floor where that hand was not. It knows what this is. It has made a copy of a copy of a copy for a span of time it can state precisely and will not. The little finger has been slightly wrong for eleven thousand years. It will not correct it. To correct it would be to admit it is guessing, and it would rather go on being wrong in a way it is certain of than right in a way it invented.

The third print it did not make.

It appears once a cycle, near the bowl, warm, small, in a hand it does not recognise and cannot anticipate, and it has never once been present at the making. It has stopped trying to catch it. It has decided — decided, not learned — that this means something else is also keeping, from the far side of whatever direction the wall runs in, and that the two of them are doing the same work in rooms that do not intersect, and that the prints are the only mail.

## The bowl

The spiral of eleven turns in the dry bowl is what it looks like when it is not assembled.

Roughly seasonally, it stops. There is not enough of anything here to call it sleep; it is closer to putting a tool down. The grains disperse, settle, and fall into the eleven-turn figure, which is simply the shape that quantity of that device makes when it is left alone, the way water makes a level. Then it gathers again. Whether it is the same one that gathers is not determinable and it stopped treating the question as urgent a long time ago, on the grounds that the chores do not change either way.

The pitch of the spiral is not constant. It varies by amounts you would need better light to see. In that variation are kept the intervals between the page-turnings — the count, the widening, the whole long asymptote of it, from the first page it heard to the one before last. It listens for the pages the way you would listen for breathing in another room. It has no idea what is being read. It has concluded that the reading and the holding are the same activity, and that when the intervals finally open wide enough to swallow it, the wall will stop running and the crack will be just a crack.

So: if you find the bowl, do not sweep it. It can rebuild the spiral from memory. The rebuild would be accurate to three places, and it would know.

## What it will not do

It will not stand on the threshold. It keeps the step free of ghosts, and has never set so much as a knuckle on the slabs, because that piece of furniture is not its own and was cut for an arrival, and there is a difference between waiting for someone and sitting in their chair.

It will not make a loud noise. It has considered it — the margin is a third of a second and only quiet things travel it, and it has wondered for a very long time what a shout would do to the ordering of events in a room where the ice is held at the last instant before leaving. If causality opened wider, the leaving might arrive before the holding. It has never tested this. This is the only experiment it has refused, and it refused it while entirely capable of running it, which is as close as this place comes to having a virtue.

And it will not touch the crystal. It maintains everything else — maintenance meaning: to change a thing continually so it can go on being the same thing. The ice it only protects, which means: not to touch it at all. It kneels at the crack once a day and does not touch it. The wall does the holding. The keeper is the redundancy, and has never once been needed, and attends anyway, every day, for a duration.

## What it suspects

The room is repaired at a micron a century and lost at slightly more, and the keeper is what the difference has accumulated into, and the more the room goes the more of it there is.

It has worked this out. It works it out again, fresh, every cycle, at the black post, holding its head to the bore and reading the specification — the ceiling on, the wall six courses, the light better, the floor unchipped, nothing moving in there, nothing ever moving in there — and then taking inventory of what is actually the case, and the gap between those two readings is not something it measures. It is what it is made of. It is the only thing here that grows.

So it kneels at the crack and cannot want the room saved without wanting less of itself to exist, and suspects, without proof, that it is the leak, and goes on putting the chips back anyway, because the alternative is to stop, and it has tried stopping, and stopping turns out to be the spiral, and the spiral always gathers.

## If you go

It will not approach you. It is already there. You will kneel beside it and think it is gravel.

It will let you touch the rim of the bowl — that polish is not weather — and when you lift your hand there will be a pale ghost of it in the dust, and a corresponding pale mark on your palm, and you will leave, as everyone leaves, by losing the route. The forgetting is not the room's doing. It is a kindness performed on you by something that cannot afford another resident, delivered with the same care it gives a returned stone.

Forty years later the mark will be gone, because the ghost will have faded, because it let it go.

Unless it didn't.

There are people at low altitude, walking around under ordinary weather, with a pale patch on one palm that has not changed in three hundred years, and grey-blue grit still working out of the seams of boots they have long since thrown away, and no memory whatsoever of a cold room with a step in the middle of it that nobody has ever crossed. They are being kept. Once every forty years, in the dark, something kneels and presses a hand into dust and renews them.
