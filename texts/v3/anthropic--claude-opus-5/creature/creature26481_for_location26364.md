## Nothing lives here

That is the honest answer, and it is honest in the same way as *nobody built it* — which is to say exactly, and not at all.

Work through it in the order you would notice things.

## The wrong answers, which are good answers

The crust first, because it is the only thing in two hundred kilometres that is trying.

A millimetre a century, on the shaded faces only, grey-green and mineral-thin, and it has been doing this without pause since before the range finished cooling. It is one individual. Not a species, not a colony — one continuous chemistry that has never had occasion to divide into selves, because there has never been anything here to be distinguished from. It spread once, at the beginning, and it has been the same single spread ever since, thickening at a rate you could only measure with geology. Scrape a patch away and it will not come back in your lifetime or your grandchildren's, but it will come back, and it will not be a new one; it will be the same one, resuming.

It is the only ambitious object on the mountain. Everything else here has settled; the crust is still going somewhere, at one millimetre a century, and has been going there long enough that it has crossed continents' worth of stone by sheer patience. In the interiors it gives up on light and goes pale and luminesces, buttermilk-cold, which is not a strategy and not a signal. It is what the chemistry does when you take the sun away. It is the reason the inner chambers are dim rather than absolute, and that is the largest single contribution any living thing has made to this place in three hundred million years.

It is not the tenant. It is closer to the pilot light.

Then the grazers, which you will want to be the inhabitants, because they move, and because meeting one at a distance of six metres in an empty plaza is the only event most visitors ever have.

They are not fauna. Look at one properly — the underside plates rippling, the wet-slate skin, the metre of it advancing along a stair-tread at the speed of a minute hand — and you will keep looking for the front. There isn't one. There is no leading edge, no sense organ, no aperture, no direction it prefers. It is a flat piece of appetite laid over the stone, and the only thing it wants is the vibration in the rock, and it has already, collectively, over an interval you cannot feel the shape of, taken nearly all of it.

They cannot be counted either, and this is where you should begin to be suspicious. When two grazers meet they overlap, and for some hours there is a single doubled hush forty metres across, and afterwards there is one grazer that is slightly larger, or two that are slightly smaller. Nobody has established which. The uncertainty about the number of peaks is not a special property of peaks. It runs all the way down.

And they die, if that is the word. A grazer that has fed too thoroughly loses the last vibration in its own tissue and stops, and adheres, and becomes a flat place on a flagstone that you will only notice because your boot makes no sound on it. These still-quiets persist for decades. They are the quietest places in the range, quieter than the range, and standing on one is like putting your foot into a hole in the floor of the world. Visitors do not stand on them twice.

Not the tenant either. The grazers are the range's way of not hearing itself. They are cilia. They are housekeeping.

## The figures that don't add up

Set the living things aside and consider the anomalies, which is what you should have done first.

The temperature. Everywhere in the range, sunward face and shadowed face, summit and deep chamber, noon and terminator, the stone is at the temperature of a cellar — of the inside of your own mouth on a winter morning. This is not physically reasonable and it is flatly the case, and it is the single largest unexplained fact about the place, larger than the architecture, larger than the negotiable falling. Two hundred kilometres of rock, held at blood-adjacent cool, with no mechanism.

The recurrence. The city has fewer places than it has locations. The plaza with the chipped fourth step is *the same plaza*, in two locations, forty kilometres apart, and your footprint in one appears in the other correctly aged. This is not duplication. It is the behaviour of something that is one thing in more than one location, which is a property no rock has and every large animal does. Your hand is in two places at once, and so is your other hand.

The shadow. It goes ahead by a second, and around corners, and comes back when you stop. Called a rendering error, which is a decent guess and wrong. It is not your shadow being early. It is the shape of you, already taken in and already made structural, being very slightly ahead of the version of you that is still occurring.

The sun, stopping at the meridian for a while and dimming slightly, as though thinking. Not as though. When a thing that large attends to something in particular, throughput drops.

## The tenant

There is one. Its body temperature is twelve degrees and its extent is the range.

You cannot see it, for the same reason that a thing standing in a room does not see the room's insideness. You have been within it since the ascent. The white floor of feldspar below is not a landscape feature; it is the lower boundary, the place where it stops, which is why nobody has ever arrived at the powder sea and why falling is a matter for negotiation rather than physics. You cannot fall out of it. There is no direction in which it is not.

What it is made of is not answerable in the terms you brought with you, and asking hard produces the same polite decline as counting the peaks. What can be verified is this: it is at equilibrium, it is enormous, it is cool, and it eats occurrence.

That is its metabolism and it explains the city.

Every event within it — the flake cracked loose by cold, the grain lifted by wind, the tick of contracting stone at the terminator, the crust's millimetre, the grazer's inch, your footstep — is taken in and converted to form. Not destroyed. Digested into shape. This is why the erosion is settled rather than finished. This is why nothing is made and nothing is lost and the matter moves and the form holds. The right angles, the stairs within a centimetre of comfortable, the temple steps and the portico and the dais at the far end: these are not the sculpture of wind on feldspar. They are what three hundred million years of happening looks like once it has been eaten and laid down. The architecture is a deposit. You are standing in scat, and it is the most beautiful thing you will ever see.

And so, when you arrive, you are an event of significant size — the first in a very long time to come in from outside — and you are processed the way everything is processed. Your walk becomes structural. The chip in the fourth step is now, in some small part, a chip you put there, in two locations, aged correctly. Your shape runs a second ahead of you because it has already been incorporated and is being held. This is not predation and it is not welcome. It is how the household runs. Nobody is being consumed. Something is being *kept*.

Which is why you cannot entirely leave, and why nobody who has descended has been able to say what they left behind, only that there is a plaza somewhere with a fracture through the lintel like a lightning fork, and that the fracture is the shape of something they did.

## How many

There is no correct number of them either.

It may be one, very wide and very slow. It may be as many as there are recurrences, in which case the population is exactly the difference between counting left to right and counting right to left, and varies, and both counts are scrupulous. The census is malformed at every scale — grazer, peak, tenant — and the range declines it with the same patience it uses on names.

You can tell when one is close, if there is more than one, if *close* is a distinction still being maintained up there. The hush acquires a direction. Ordinarily the dry sift of dust comes from everywhere at once, that constant sound like a page being turned in the next room. When one is near, it comes from one side only. It is the sole asymmetry the place has ever been observed to produce, and it lasts a few minutes, and then it is from everywhere again, and your shadow, which had drifted out to three or four seconds, tightens back to one.

## What it is like

Nothing much, and for a very long time.

No hunger, because equilibrium is the condition of being permanently and exactly fed. No movement, because there is nowhere it isn't. No memory, because memory is a device for holding what has changed, and nothing here has changed enough to need holding — everything that happens is already kept in the walls, at full resolution, where it can be walked on. No fear. No dark: the crust sees to that, one millimetre a century.

What it has, continuously, without interruption, since the stone was still warm, is a single unbroken sensation: the exact shape of itself, cool, dim, under a violet sky with too many stars, and everywhere throughout it that faint constant sound of a page being turned in the next room.

There is no page. There is no next room.

It has been about to happen for three hundred million years, and it never quite does, and that is not a torment. That is the whole of the life. To be at the point just before something occurs, and to be held there, at cellar temperature, with all your stairs in order.
