## What the Place Contains

Start with the correction, because the obvious answer is wrong in a way that matters.

The plant is not what lives here. The plant is where things live. It is alive, certainly, in the sense that its tissues respire and its vessels lift water and its meristems lay down new laminae at the rate of about a fingernail's thickness a year. But nothing happens to it. It does not grow, spread, flower to any purpose, sicken, recover, or wait. It has no direction of travel and no unfinished business. Treating it as the resident is like treating a hill as the resident. It is the terrain, and everything that actually lives here lives *on* it, and none of them use the room.

Still — the terrain first, briefly, because its two habits set every other life in the place.

## The Terrain

It stands on itself. Its roots do not reach rock, because there is none; they reach a raft of its own dead root-mass, some eleven metres thick, into which it is settling at a rate that has produced the sunken floor and the gutter and, given long enough, will produce nothing else, because the settling is asymptotic. It was standing when there was still a ridge here. The landscape came down around it. It is not what survived the erosion; it is what the erosion happened next to.

It flowers. Once in perhaps four years, at the crown, small and dry and green, and the pollen goes up the chimney with the exhaust and out over a day's walk of flat brick in every direction, and lands where nothing can start. Its seeds want gravel and a gradient and running water, three things that were abolished here before the pan finished. The nearest other dome is two hundred kilometres northeast and is also alone, also flowering, also sterile in effect. So it has stopped being a lineage. It is now a structure that happens to be made of meat.

The second habit is the important one. Laminae are laid down on the inside and weather off the outside, and the wall does not get thicker, which means the wall is a standing wave: the material of it is travelling outward, always, at that fingernail's rate, and every inhabited crevice in it is travelling outward too. This is the only vector in the place. Everything else here is permanent. The wall is a treadmill.
