# The Standing: What Lives There

Come at the inhabitants the way you came at the range, and you will make the same order of mistake.

You will look for them on the ground. It is the only place a person makes sense to you, so you will spend your first days walking the plain and the warm floor mats, and you will find their leavings — a cold hearth-basket, a cairn of sift-skulls, a post — and conclude that they are gone, or nearly, a scatter of survivors thinning out of a bad country.

Then something on the inner wall moves eighty feet in a direction you did not expect, and stops, and is not a climber, and you begin to correct.

They are in the wall. All of them. That film on the shoreline, the one the rung-climbers and the braid and the light-grazers make — the people are in it, at the same density, obeying the same rule, which is that there is no living in the middle. You have been standing inside their town for three days looking up at the ceiling of it.

### Bodies

Thirty years is not long enough to change a species. It is more than long enough to change a body.

The ones born here are small, long in the arm, short in the shin, with a chest that looks overbuilt for the size of them and is. Their hands do not open. Not from injury: the flexors simply set that way, in a shallow permanent hook, so that an adult of the standing cannot lay a palm flat on anything. When they want to indicate a lie, or a joke, or an impossibility, they show you the palm and hold the fingers open, and it is visibly an effort, and it is funny to them every time.

Their feet are wide and splayed and the great toe stands off from the others, and they walk on level ground with a rolling, apologetic limp, like men on a wet deck. On the plain they are half blind. Eyes grown up in the dull red-green of the light-tissue open too far out there in the grey; they squint and weep and take a companion by the belt.
