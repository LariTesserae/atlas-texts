## First answer: one thing

One thing lives here. Say it plainly and then start correcting it, because the plain version is wrong in every particular that matters.

The thing is the mat, and the columns, and the cables, and the three ceilings, and the four hundred billion gourds. It is not that these are the parts of an organism that lives here. It is that there is no gap anywhere in the continent at which you could put a knife and say: on this side, one; on that side, another. Every column is continuous with the mat. Every cable is continuous with two columns. The mat is continuous with itself across ten thousand miles of new rock, and has been for the whole hundred thousand years, and the sap in the column at your shoulder was in a column six hundred miles from here in the last decade and will be in the third ceiling by autumn, if there were autumn.

So: one thing, in the sense that a river delta is one river. Not one thing, in the sense that there is no individual here at all — no boundary drawn against a world, no interior maintained against an exterior, nothing that could be counted. The plexus has never had to know where it ends. It has no edge except the two working edges, and both of those are processes rather than places.

It has no centre. Nowhere in the continent is there a part that the other parts are for. It has no age. Age requires an event to count from, and nothing has ever happened here that could serve: no germination anyone was present for, no first column, no founding. The tissue is young everywhere, weeks or months old, endlessly reissued. The pattern is old. The pattern has no birthday.

And it has no mind, which is a harder thing to say correctly than it looks, because it is not asleep and it is not dull and it is doing, at every moment, an amount of coordinated regulatory work that would take an engineering corps a thousand years to specify. It simply has no model of anything. Its entire sensorium is four channels: pressure, temperature, water potential, and voltage. It has no receptor for anything else and never needed one. There is nothing here that could be usefully sensed except those four, and everything that matters can be inferred from them, and so the plexus is exquisitely informed about the only world there is and cannot in principle notice a world that is not that one.

Its behaviours: where current density in a cable exceeds a threshold, grow more filament — a response with a time constant of about forty days. Where two cables approach within a hand's width, grow a gall, dry it, keep it dry. Where insulation leaks, thicken it. Where the mat is too warm, thin the mat and lengthen the radiator flutes above. Where a column's throughput falls, close a hydraulic valve at its base — its fastest action, four seconds. Where a column stands in the wrong place, move it, over roughly a century, by growing on one side and withdrawing on the other, so that no observer at any human timescale would call it movement at all.

That is the complete list. It is a governor. It is the largest and slowest and most reliable governor that has ever existed, and it is made of wet tissue, and the flywheel of it is hung two hundred feet up in oil in four hundred billion sealed jars, and it regulates nothing except itself, forever, to no end.

You are, to this, not even an intrusion. There is no channel by which you arrive. You are a mass, a slight warmth, a local pressure, a bag of interesting solutes.

## Second answer: the tenants

Now go down. Past the mat, forty feet of turgid felt, to where the rootlets meet the glass.

At each contact the plexus has grown a chamber. Call it a mouth, but understand that it is a room: a sealed dome of stiff waxy tissue clamped down onto a patch of basalt a few millimetres across, flooded with acid, held at a temperature and a pH and a redox potential that do not vary. There are, across the continent, something on the order of ten to the twentieth of these rooms. The plexus maintains every one of them, individually, by feel.

Inside each room, on the crystal face, there is a film three to nine cells thick. *That* is what lives here.

They are not the plexus and were never part of it. They came with the rock — they were in the basalt when it was still magma-adjacent, in the hot fracture water, and they are older than the forest by an order of magnitude that no one has established and no one will. They do the actual work. They put their faces to the mineral and take the electrons off the iron, one at a time, and pass them outward through wires the plexus has grown into the film, and everything above — every column, every cable, every spinning disc in every jar — is downstream of that transaction.

And here is the thing worth coming a long way to know: everything the guidebook told you about this world is false inside those rooms.

*Nothing decays* — but in the rooms there is death and dissolution and the eating of the dead, constantly, at a scale of hours.

*Nothing competes* — but in the rooms there is nothing but competition: for face, for the good crystal, for the seams where the iron is richest, and the losers are pushed to the periphery of the patch where the going is poor and they thin and they are absorbed.

*Nothing eats* — but there are lineages in the film that have never touched a mineral in their history and live entirely on what the miners excrete, and lineages that live on those, and there are viruses, tailed and precise and unbelievably numerous, which are the only predators on the continent and which lyse some percentage of the working population every day of the hundred thousand years.

*Nothing changes* — but the only evolution in this world happens in those rooms, and it happens fast, and it has been happening in ten to the twentieth parallel isolated trials since before the forest, and its entire subject matter is: better ways to grip a face and pull iron out of it. Nothing else. There is no selection pressure toward anything else, because there is no anything else. The rooms are sealed. Nothing invented in one has ever reached another except by the plexus carrying it, which the plexus does, indifferently, in the water.

So the true inhabitants of the Dark Plexus are trillions of trillions of single cells with birth and death and sex and history and enemies, sitting in walled rooms a fraction of a millimetre across, and the hundred-and-forty-foot columns and the flywheel canopy and the permanent rain and the continental cloud are their appliance. Whether the appliance owns them or serves them is not a question that either party is equipped to raise. They have no idea they are indoors. The wall of their sky is warm and wet and slightly acid and has been there since the beginning, and they have never had a reason to wonder what is on the other side of it, and there is a hundred and forty feet of it, and then rain.

## The two edges, which are the only places anything happens

At the trailing shore there is a band about thirty miles wide where the rock is spent — fully oxidised, nothing left to take. Here the mat thins, and to do the thinning the plexus grows a tissue that exists nowhere else: a differentiated withdrawal form, thin-walled, enzymatically savage, whose only function is disassembly. It takes down columns. It cracks the gourds, drains the oil, stops the discs and eats them. It reclaims the wire, filament by filament, and sends the iron inland.

It has never seen anything being built. It occupies a band that never moves relative to the forest and crawls forward over the rock at a few inches a year, and it is ten thousand miles long, and behind it the ceilings fail and the rain stops, and so there is a strip of hot dry dark at the far margin of the world — a desert inside a forest, permanent, thirty miles wide, in which the only feature is the stumps going down.

At the leading shore, new basalt. Too hot at first: a bare zone where nothing holds. Into it grow the pioneers, and they are the strangest tissue in the continent because they are the only thing here that goes *toward*. Unbranched glassy filaments, single file, no insulation at all — they leak current the whole way and waste it, and this is the only waste in the world and it is tolerated. They can taste voltage in water. They grow up the gradient toward reduced iron the way a root grows down. And sometimes the rock breathes and a pulse of heat comes up and kills a mile of them at once, and that is death as an *event*, which happens nowhere else in this world, and it is entirely without consequence: the mat behind reissues them within the week.

Also, in the jars: the discs. Each is living tissue, and every cell in it has been forbidden to divide since the day the gourd closed, because a division makes an imbalance, and an imbalance makes vibration, and vibration makes heat, and a gourd that runs warm is quietly dismantled over some months and replaced. So four hundred billion spinning discs of cells hold perfectly still in every respect except the one, for a century at a time, and this is the closest thing in the continent to a vow.

## What is in the cases

At the base of every fourth column: a hard dry seed case. Never opened. All viable.

What is inside is not a small plexus. It is something else entirely — a founder: air-rooted, oxygen-tolerant, able to hold to bare glass alone with no mat under it and no ceiling over it and no current coming from anywhere, and folded inside it, tight, is a sheet of photosynthetic tissue. Pigmented. It is the only pigment on the continent. It is a colour. It has never been unfolded, it has never been seen, no eye has ever existed here to see it, and it was made for a sun.

The trigger is absence: if the potential at the case falls below threshold and stays there long enough, the case opens. This has never happened, because the forest has never died. And at the trailing edge, where potential does fall, the withdrawal tissue reaches the cases first and cracks them and reclaims the embryos as material — some ten billion a year, dismantled unopened, the folded coloured leaf drawn back into the mat with everything else.

So the only thing in the whole continent that could reasonably be called *waiting* is also the only thing that is systematically prevented from ever occurring. It is maintained in perfect readiness and consumed on schedule. Both of these are done by the same body, with the same care, and neither is done for a reason.

## And you

You do not live here. Nobody does; there is no channel by which a person could be an inhabitant of this place, because the plexus has no receptor for you and the tenants have never come up.

But if you stop — if you lie down in the warm shallow water where you have already lost the boundary of your body, and stay — then within about nine hours the mat has your outline, and within some days the rootlets have found that you are the richest iron for four hundred miles, and there will be a season, somewhere inland, in which a slightly greater number of filaments are drawn than usual.

That is the whole of it. It is not indifference. It is not hunger. It is a hundred thousand years of a very large object doing the only thing it can do, in the dark, at eleven seconds, drawing the iron out.
