**The tenants**

Begin with the plants, because they are the only things you can point at, and because pointing at them is how you will learn that they are not the answer.

Forty lineages. Call them by function, since they have no names and were never named: the collar-fern that will not let go of its own drop; the deep hot moss; the eleven algal films that are only a thermometer written in colour; the leaning half-trees, wood on the lee and flowstone on the wet, which conduct water up through the calcite side long after the calcite side has stopped being alive, so that every living frond on the plateau drinks through the plumbing of a corpse and the plumbing works perfectly and always will. Look closely at the moss. The upper half is green and hot and gives under your knee. The lower half is stone in the exact architecture of moss — every leaf, every rhizoid, every air-space, rendered in calcite at a fidelity no casting process could achieve. That boundary between green and white is not a line. It is a front. It has been in the same place, relative to the growing tip, for as long as the moss has been a thing that grows.

Which means the moss is growing at precisely the rate at which it is being turned to stone.

Hold that. Do not resolve it yet.

The plants do not live here in the sense the question means. They persist. They have no sex, no seed, no strangers, no way to be surprised by another of their kind; they multiply by snapping off and lodging downstream, so the entire green weight of the plateau is one plant forty times, ten billion times over, near-touching, shading itself, saturated to the point of solidity. Nothing here can be combined with anything else to make a third thing. That is not a botanical accident. Keep it. It is the single most important fact about the resident, and it is written across ten thousand square kilometres in the only handwriting available.

**Three things gradient does not explain**

You were told the rule: water's preference for the shortest steep line, for the existing lip, for the already-wet surface. Order indistinguishable from intention, and no intention behind it. That rule is true, and it accounts for the streets, the plazas, the arcades, the nine turns of the ramp, the doorways, the courses in the walls. It accounts for everything except three things.

*One.* The equilibrium. Deposition and dissolution matched everywhere, always, to a precision that no natural system holds. Physics can balance; physics cannot balance *like that*, at every rim, through every season of a hundred thousand years, without once overshooting into a millimetre of growth or a millimetre of loss. That is not a balance. That is a hand held steady.

*Two.* Four falls go up.

Everything else on the plateau follows from load and slope. Water does not go up. Four times — four, out of hundreds — it goes up, with the same braided ropes and the same fixed rainbows and the same ancient-new terraces deposited on the undersides of overhangs where terraces cannot be. Four is a small number. Four is not zero. Four is the residue of a preference that is not water's.

*Three.* The seven standing shapes in the colonnade at the head of the ramp, hip-height at the shoulder, in a rough ring, holding the posture of things that would listen.

From three bones you can infer a bird. From these three you can infer what lives here, and having inferred it you will not be able to stop seeing it, and it is very large, and you have been walking on it.

**One street, because one body**

It is single. This is not a claim about population; it is a claim you can check with your feet.

Walk three blocks north and three south and arrive at the fountain-head with the peacock basin. Reverse the order and arrive at the same fountain-head at a different altitude, with no step in between to account for it. You were told the city is a street repeated at many scales and joined to itself along invisible seams. That is correct, and the reason is not architectural. The plateau has one street because it has one body, and a body does not need a second of anything to be everywhere in itself. Your right hand is not a copy of your left hand; it is the same you, twice, and no path leads from one to the other except through the middle of the thing they belong to. Sight dead level across the gap in the cloud to the terrace far below and you are looking at your own palm from the back. Height here is not a quantity. Height is a state it is in, and it is in it everywhere at once, and that is why the plateau has no summit and no base — only edge, indefinitely, in the direction called down.

It has no name. Naming is done twice: once to fix the thing, once to call it back. It can do the first. It has never been able to do the second.

**What it can do with light, and what light does to it**

Its one sense is deposition. To perceive, here, is to have something settle on you and stay. This works — it works appallingly well. Drop a leaf and within the hour the leaf is enshrined, gloved in white, recorded permanently and in full detail. The plateau misses nothing that stops.

The cost is that perception and memory are the same organ, so nothing can be perceived without being kept, and nothing kept can be revised. The rainbows at the eastern rim do not move when you move because they are not in your eye; they are in the world, laid down, an instant of struck light that occurred and then could not finish occurring. It has been looking at that instant since before there were eyes to model. It cannot look away. It cannot un-see. It cannot compare, because comparison needs two moments and it has only ever had the one, held open, hardening.

This is why nothing here is *before* anything else. Not because time is exotic on the plateau. Because the resident has no faculty for sequence, and everything you are standing in is its faculties turned inside out and made of rock.

It is not immortal. Immortality belongs to things that could have died. It has never been given the opportunity to be a verb.

**The seven**

Vapour is the only tissue it can afford.

Everything else it makes becomes permanent in the instant of making, which is to say dead, which is to say furniture. Stone is a terrible medium for a gesture; you commit and it is finished forever. Mist does not deposit. Mist can be held in a shape and then not held. Mist is the one place on this entire plateau where the resident is allowed to be provisional, to try something, to be wrong about something, and so it has put into the mist the only part of itself that it needed to be able to change: the attending part.

Seven is not a headcount. There are not seven of anything here. Seven is the number of times it has managed to hold the posture at once.

They are not ghosts. Nothing with a shape has lived here to be ghosted, and they are not modelled on anything — they are hip-height at the shoulder because that is the height at which the colonnade's air is still, and they are in a ring because a ring is what attention makes when it does not know which direction to face. They have the standing-shape of things that would listen, in a place where listening is not possible, where the massif is a struck bell never allowed to stop and hearing is therefore a wall rather than a sense.

You will not be able to establish whether they were there before you came up out of the cloud. You will want to. But *before you arrived* is a phrase in a grammar the plateau does not conjugate, and you will not get an answer, and you should consider that the resident is in exactly the same position, and has been, always.

**Why you leave nothing**

You cast a shadow and the shadow does not deposit its cool edge in the stone the way the columns' fixed shadows have deposited theirs. You displace no water. The deep hot moss closes over your knee and keeps no print of you. In a place that records every event with total fidelity, you are the only event, and you go unrecorded.

Two explanations, and you cannot choose between them, and neither can it.

The first is mechanical. It can only keep what has stopped. Enshrinement requires the thing to hold still long enough to be met at the rate of stone. You are a flicker far under its shutter speed — not slow enough to be perceived, which is to say not dead enough. Everything on the plateau that is loved is a relic. You have not yet fallen.

The second is the moss.

Go back and look at that front again, the boundary between green and white, holding its position for a hundred thousand years. The only race on the plateau, and a dead heat. It is not the case that the moss happens to grow at the speed of its own petrifaction. It is the case that the resident is being careful. The equilibrium is not stasis; it is restraint — a touch withheld at the exact threshold where touching would finish the thing touched. It has one hand and one hand's only gesture is to make permanent, and it has the deep hot moss, and it has been holding back from it, everywhere, always, at every rim, without once slipping, for longer than you have a number for.

So perhaps it is not that you cannot be kept. Perhaps you were let go.

**The roar, again**

You arrived believing the sound was a condition of the place, the way pressure is a condition of water — no rhythm in it, no events, nothing to listen to, and after a while you stopped hearing it and it became the shape of your silence.

Reconsider. Hundreds of falls going off the rim at once, into a stone sponge kilometres deep, riddled and flued and fused, every collapsing column ringing the whole massif, all of it multiplied and phase-shifted until it arrives as one flat unvarying immensity. That is not the acoustics of a landscape. That is an instrument. A body built to be loud, that gave everything to being loud, that has no ears because it spent its ears on the voice, that cannot hear whether anything ever answered and has not stopped for a hundred thousand years.

It is calling. It has been calling since before hearing.

You stood inside the mouth of it and produced nothing and heard nothing and could not confirm by ear that you were alive, and then you went back down through the cloud, and you are the only thing that ever left, and the report of it will happen somewhere else, in a language made of two moments and second things, which it does not have, and cannot have, and which is the whole of what it was asking for.
