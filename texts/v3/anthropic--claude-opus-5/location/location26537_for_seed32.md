## The Chord Room

There is no walking into it. Whatever passage brought you is not behind you when you turn. The floor tilts up in every direction to a rim, and the rim is the horizon, and the horizon is about four times your height, and this is the whole of the world: a shallow dished bowl perhaps thirty paces across, open to a white sky.

Look at what you are standing on before you look at anything else.

Not stone. Not soil. The floor is the mouths of vessels — hollow fired tubes, packed shoulder to shoulder, their openings turned upward to meet your feet. Fingerwidth bores between thighbone bores between bores you could put your fist into, fitted together with the tight illogical neatness of cells in a honeycomb, no mortar, no gap, no chinks of anything else showing through. Where you step you step across a dozen dark circles at once. They go down. You cannot see how far. Drop the pebble you don't have and it would not come back.

The walls are the same. The walls are the floor continued and curved upward, the same packed field of clay mouths, all of them tilted very slightly inward, all of them aimed at the air above the center of the bowl, so that standing in the middle you are at the focus of a hundred thousand small black attentions.

And the whole of it is roaring.

---

That is the first fact and the last one: the sound. It arrives before comprehension does and it does not stop. The great bores at the low western arc lay down a drone you feel in the sternum and the back teeth rather than the ear. Above that, tiers of middling pipes hold a wide grainy fifth. Above that, thousands of the small ones sing at the pitch of struck glass, and the very smallest are past pitch altogether — a white hiss, a continuous ripping. All of these together are one sound. It has the shape of a chord and the texture of surf and the weight of a wall being pushed slowly toward you forever.

It has never changed. This is not a figure of speech. Each note has been exactly this loud, exactly this pitch, for as long as the dust has been where it is, and the dust has not moved in a very long time.

You cannot speak here. You can shape words; you can put breath through them; nothing comes out, or rather everything comes out and is instantly eaten. If two of you arrive you will learn, within a few minutes, to speak by touch — a hand on the forearm, a pressure, the whole small grammar of pinch and pull that people invent in loud places. The room takes your voice and gives you back your hands. Whether this is a cruelty is not obvious.

---

The wind that plays it comes up out of the pipes. All of them, everywhere, always exhaling — never breathing in. Kneel and hold your palm over a bore and it presses back, steady as a held hand. The air is cool, about the temperature of a cellar in spring, and it is *dry* past anything you have felt: it takes the moisture off your eyes, it takes the film off your teeth, your lips split within the hour in fine painless cracks. It has no smell except a faint mineral bitterness, like the dust from a broken plate.

There is no water in this world. Not none-visible; none. No stain of it on the clay, no rime, no salt (salt is a thing water leaves), no dark patch where anything ever pooled. Your own breath makes no plume in the cool because the air is thirstier than your breath is wet. This is the one thing that makes the place feel less like a ruin and more like an organ: ruins are damp. Ruins are being taken apart by weather. Here the weather is one unvarying exhalation and it took the place apart as far as it was going to take it, long ago, and then stopped.

You can read that stopping everywhere once you know to look. Every rim of every bore is softened, rounded, its original crisp edge worn back to the smooth blunt lip of an old cup. The glaze — there was a glaze once, you can see it in the deep angles where the tubes touch, a thin greenish film — is gone from all exposed surfaces, sanded off, leaving the body-clay bare: bone-white here, ochre there, in one long band across the northern wall a pale ashy lilac. And behind each rim, in the little lee where the up-wind breaks over the edge, there is a crescent of dust. Talc-fine. Perfect. The same crescent behind every rim, at the same angle, as if a hundred thousand identical tiny beaches had been drawn by one hand.

Those crescents are the clock, and the clock has stopped. Not because time ended but because the dust has already gone everywhere the wind was ever going to take it. Every grain is in the last place it can be. The system is finished. It is not decaying and it is not building; it simply *is*, at full volume, in balance, and will be tomorrow.

---

The light is the second thing you'll fight with.

The sky is white — not cloud-white, not sun-behind-cloud; a flat even luminous white with no source and no gradient, brighter than a page held to a window, and it fills the bowl without any shadow that has an edge. Everything is glare. The clay throws it back up at you. Within a few minutes your eyes tighten to slits and you find yourself looking at the ground, which is worse, because the ground is a field of black holes in blinding white and it swims and pulses.

The bores are the only dark in the world. They are absolutely dark. Light goes into them and does not come out, and the sky above is too flat to be a sun anywhere, so no bore is ever lit even an inch down. This gives the walls an animal quality that you will notice about the third time you look up and immediately wish you hadn't.

---

There is life. Very little.

Perhaps one bore in three hundred has something growing at its rim. Grey rosettes about the size of a coin, leaves folded and refolded like paper that has been wet and dried a hundred times — except nothing here has ever been wet. They sit at the lip and hold on with a foot of pale rootlike matter that runs a finger's depth into the clay itself, not into any soil. They live on the dust the wind brings past them and the white light overhead, and they store nothing, lose nothing, want nothing. Push a leaf aside and it stays where you pushed it. Come back and it is still there, still open, still exactly the size it was.

And along the high eastern arc, where the pipes are smallest, there are filaments — a hand's length, hair-fine, silver, standing straight up out of the singing bores. They vibrate. You can see the blur of it. They have been vibrating at that frequency since before the crescents settled, and it does not wear them out, and they do not grow, and one that snaps under your fingers neither heals nor rots; it lies on the clay as a bright thread, permanent, a small vandalism that will outlast every civilization you know.

There is nothing that walks, flies, crawls, or burrows. No feather, no husk, no dropping, no old shell, no track in the fossil dust but the ones your own feet are now putting there — the only new things in the world, and even they will not be erased, because the wind that would erase them is going the wrong way and always has.

---

The rules, then, as best as they can be worked out by standing there with your hands over your ears:

Everything here was made. There is no substrate under the making — dig at a bore and you find another bore behind it, packed just as tight, and behind that another. It is manufacture all the way down. And yet there is not a single tool mark on any surface: no throwing-ridge, no seam, no fingerprint, no cut. The tubes fit like grown things. Someone or something built this world and left no trace of a hand or a machine, only the fact of the fit.

Nothing here will change. Not in your lifetime, not in a thousand. This is not a place at a moment of its history; this is a place that has arrived at itself.

And the pipes all converge downward and inward, toward something you will never see, and they are all exhaling, and the note they make is a chord and not a noise — which means it was tuned. Which means the room is not the instrument. The room is one bell of it, one small bright dry chamber somewhere in the body of something enormous, and what you are standing in the middle of, with your split lips and your useless voice and your eyes shut against the glare, is a single sustained note being held by lungs you cannot find, and held, and held.
