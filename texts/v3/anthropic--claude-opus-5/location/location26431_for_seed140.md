**The Bowl at the Top of the Wearing**

Begin with the mountain, because the mountain is finished. Whatever it was — a stratovolcano, a thrust block, a young sharp thing with couloirs and a summit worth naming — it has been worn past the point of ambition. What remains is three thousand metres of shoulder: a broad warm rise with no edge anywhere on it, curved like the back of something asleep. Rain has had all the time it needed. Every ridge has been let down gently into every valley. You could walk the last kilometre of ascent barefoot and not know you had reached the top except that the ground stopped tilting.

At the top, off-centre, there is a hollow. It would hold a cathedral, if you set the cathedral down carefully and didn't mind it touching the walls. Two hundred paces across, sixty deep, round in the way that a worn-out coin is round. This is the whole of the landscape. Everything else here is context.

---

**The dark**

The hollow is full of darkness, and the darkness is not the absence of day.

It has thickness. It pools. It sits in the bowl the way water sits in a stone basin, with a surface — you feel the surface as you descend, a change at about chest height near the rim, cooler for an instant, then not cool, then simply *inside*. Above that line, on the plateau, there may be stars, weather, a moon; the bowl does not care and does not report. Below it, there is no photon that survives the crossing. Not the faintest grey. Not the phosphene ghosts your own eye invents, or rather those you do get, and they are the only visual event available, and after an hour you learn to disregard them the way you disregard the sound of your own blood.

The dark is manufactured. Not currently — nobody is manufacturing anything here — but originally, and it has settled into a stable state as thoroughly as the mountain has. Twelve posts stood on the rim once, spaced by some ratio that is still legible in the sockets. Four stand. Their surface is the thing that does the work: a skin at some scale below the useful reach of your fingertips, structured so that light entering it does not come out — not scattered, not absorbed into heat, simply *concluded*. Run a palm down a standing post and you feel a nap like the fur of an animal that has no body, and you feel nothing back. No warmth of your own hand returning. The posts are thermal holes as well as optical ones. They take infrared as readily as visible. Moths will not fly within a metre of them. Nothing roosts there. Nothing grows there, though everything grows on everything else in this place; the posts are the only clean surfaces in the bowl.

Somewhere below the root-mat there is more of the installation, and it is presumably still doing whatever the four posts are the last visible fingers of. The dark does not thin. Over the course of a year it deepens slightly and then relaxes, and in the wet months it overtops the low notch on the north side and drains out across the plateau in a slow tongue that thins to nothing within a hundred metres and is gone by mid-morning. The notch is lined. Someone lined it, long ago, with the same skin, and the lining has been dented by rockfall but not weathered at all, which is how you know how old the rest of it is: everything natural here has been rounded to its final form, and the engineering is still crisp, and the engineering is older.

Purpose: unrecoverable. A place kept dark for the sake of darkness. An instrument, maybe, that needed a floor of absolute zero light to stand on. A preserve for something that could not bear the sun. A grave. The bowl has outlived the reason and kept the habit.

---

**Wet**

The bowl is warm — mid-twenties, steady, a warmth that comes up through the floor and does not vary between the deep night and the other deep night. Around it, at three thousand metres, the air is cold. Every hour of every year, therefore, the bowl breathes cold air in across its rim and condenses it.

Nothing here is dry. The walls weep. There is a drip-line under every overhang, and after ten thousand years of drip-lines the overhangs have been dissolved into gentle scallops, so the water no longer falls but *crawls*, sheeting down curved rock under a skin of surface tension, and where it crawls it grows a film of something living. The floor is a mat two hands deep: moss over root over moss, a peat that squeezes water out around your boot and takes it back when you lift. No pond. Water never gets to stand — it's always being drunk, or breathed, or trickling into the throat of the notch. Where you kneel, you are kneeling in warm broth and the smell that comes up is sweet, fermentive, faintly sulphurous, entirely alive.

**Green without green**

There is no light, and there is vegetation everywhere, and it is the same solution life has always found: eat the chemistry instead. The seep is loaded — sulphides, iron, ammonium, whatever the mountain's last warm heart is still leaching upward — and the mat is founded on organisms that build sugar out of that, and the larger growth is founded on them. Nothing has leaves. Leaves are for light. Instead: fluted columns of tissue standing to shoulder height, cool and firm as cartilage, absorbing through their whole surface; hanging cables that come off the wall-rock in curtains, so that crossing the bowl means parting them constantly with your forearms; and low crowded rosettes on the floor that close if you touch them, with a sound.

They flower. This is the part that takes explaining. A flower without light cannot advertise with colour, so these advertise with heat and with scent: each bloom, when open, runs a few degrees above ambient in a pattern — a warm ring, a warm spot in the ring, a slow pulse — and the moths that come read those patterns as unambiguously as you read a shopfront. The bowl has a *visual* life; it is simply conducted three octaves down the spectrum, and you are the only thing present that cannot see it. Hold your hand near a flowering column and the moths will land on your knuckles, mistaking you.

Flowering happens rarely. Everything here happens rarely. The mat grows perhaps a centimetre a decade. The seep rises and falls on a cycle nobody has measured, tens of years long, and there are old dry drip-scallops four metres above the current wet line that will be wet again eventually. Patience is the local physics.

---

**Loud**

And now the thing you will not have expected of an almost-empty room on top of a finished mountain: it is *loud*.

Enter at the notch and the noise comes at you like a wall of warm cloth. Frog-notes — hammered, metallic, one syllable, thousands. Insect stridulation in three or four distinct registers, and under it a broadband hiss that is either more insects or water. Clicks. Long descending whistles that you will decide, correctly, belong to something large. It is the sound of a swamp in a full summer night, and it is coming from every direction including below and above, and it does not let up.

Then you start counting bodies, and there aren't any. Sweep the mat with your hands and you find one frog in ten square metres. The columns are unoccupied. The air is empty — you can walk the full two hundred paces with your arms out and touch nothing airborne. Density here is close to void: a great warm hollow with a thin living skin and almost nothing per cubic metre of it.

The bowl keeps sound. That is what the erosion did, in the end, without meaning to: it ground the walls to a shape so smoothly concave, and lined them with tissue so uniformly absorbent-but-not-quite, that sound entering the bowl circulates. It goes round the wall, and round, and round. A frog's call from three days ago is still in here, degraded, smeared, joined by the fifty thousand calls that came after it. What you're hearing is not tonight's chorus. It's the accumulated chorus — a reservoir, filled slowly by a sparse population over an enormous span and leaking only very gradually out of the notch. Loudness as sediment. The bowl is a bath of old noise with a few living animals in it, adding their drops.

Speak, and you join it. Say your own name at normal volume and you will hear it come back around at your left shoulder eleven seconds later, thinner, and again after that, and by the fourth pass it has stopped being a word. It never entirely stops. Whatever you say in here is in here now.

---

**Rules, insofar as there are any**

Nothing in the bowl is seen. Not by you, not by the moths, not by the grazing thing whose footfalls you feel as a soft compression through the mat before you hear it. Vision was retired. What replaced it: warmth-reading, echo, scent, and the patient reading of surfaces by touch.

Do not bring light. Not for reverence — for consequence. The skin will take a flame's light and the flame's heat with it, and the local thermal grammar goes blank for a radius: the flowers stop broadcasting, the moths lose the shopfronts, and the mat there dies back and takes forty years to close. There are two such scars near the standing posts, both old, both roughly the size of a person kneeling with a lamp.

Do not expect the place to change while you watch. It has finished changing. The mountain is at its final form, the dark is at equilibrium, the mat gains a centimetre a decade, and the sound reservoir is as full as it has been for a very long time.

Leave by the notch. Feel the surface of the darkness pass down your body — knees, ankles, gone — and step out onto the plateau, where there is wind, and cold, and possibly stars, and where you will find that you have brought some of the noise out with you in your ears, and that it takes about an hour to fade.
