Beyond the verdant plateau, separated by a transition of gradually shifting terrains, there exists a stark and wind-sculpted region: The Dissolution Plains.

Where the first ecosystem was dense and saturated, this landscape is spare and transparent. Here, wind becomes the primary sculptor, etching and eroding surfaces with persistent, patient precision. The ground is not soil, but a pale crystalline substance that looks like compressed salt or ground quartz—a surface that reflects and refracts light in ways that make distance uncertain.

Scattered across this pale expanse are skeletal structures that might be the remnants of something once living, now transformed. They are not bones, exactly, but latticed formations of a brittle, translucent material that catches light like frozen breath. These structures have no fixed shape, but continuously reshape themselves under the wind's constant pressure—fragmenting, reforming, dispersing.

The wind here is not merely atmospheric movement, but a kind of sentient medium. It carries information: memories of other landscapes, traces of distant metabolic processes, fragments of dissolved histories. Its sound is not a single tone, but a complex harmonic that suggests multiple simultaneous conversations.

Periodically, large diaphanous forms emerge from the ground—not quite clouds, not quite organisms. They rise like slow exhalations, translucent and shifting, carrying within them complex geometric patterns that pulse and reconfigure. These forms seem to be both data and living substance, memory and potential.

Connecting these two realms—the verdant plateau and these dissolution plains—is a transitional zone that operates like a membrane of exchange. Here, the dense biological networks of the first landscape gradually thin and transform, their biochemical communications becoming more ethereal, more electromagnetic.

Spores and particle-like information carriers drift between these zones. They are neither fully of the first ecosystem nor the second, but mediators of transmission. Some carry genetic memories, some carry structural templates, some carry pure potential—transforming as they move, adapting to the changing environmental logics.

The connection is not a simple geographic transition, but a complex metabolic dialogue. The Stone Dwellers' geological memories leak into the wind's informational currents. The Vegetal Networks' root-communication patterns dissolve and redistribute across the crystalline plains. The Bioluminescent Mediators' electromagnetic whispers become wind-carried frequencies.

This is not a border, but a zone of continuous metamorphosis—where one state of being gradually becomes another, where information and substance trade places, where memory is a fluid, migratory phenomenon.

The two landscapes are not separate, but different expressions of the same underlying process: a continuous, slow transformation that operates at scales far beyond human perception.
