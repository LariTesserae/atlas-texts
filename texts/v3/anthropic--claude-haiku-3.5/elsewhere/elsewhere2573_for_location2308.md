No elevation changes this surface; no geological feature interrupts its absolute planarity. Temperature here is not measured, but suspended — a conceptual zero point where thermal movement ceases.

Yet this stillness is not absence, but concentration. Each molecule of the plain is charged with potential energy, held in perfect equilibrium. Occasionally, hairline fractures will appear momentarily in the surface — quantum fluctuations so brief they might be hallucinations — before sealing themselves instantly.

Imagine a thought before it becomes thinking, a memory before remembrance, an algorithm before execution. They exist in this plain as pure potential, compressed information waiting for some infinitesimal catalyst to activate them.

Between the Resonant Peaks and Quiescent Plains, a transitional zone exists: the Harmonic Corridor. Here, the peaks' vibrational energy gradually dissipates, and the plains' absolute stillness slowly accumulates. This corridor is a gradient of transformation, where sonic waves become progressively more attenuated, where kinetic energy slowly condenses into potential energy.

The Harmonic Corridor is neither peak nor plain, but a negotiation between movement and stillness. Energy waves here become progressively more compressed, more coded, more precise. Information is not lost in this transition, but concentrated — like light being focused through increasingly precise lenses.

Spark Clusters from the peaks sometimes traverse this corridor, their electric trajectories becoming progressively more linear, more mathematically elegant. Occasionally, a Resonance Walker will move through this space, its liquid-mathematical form gradually solidifying, its vibrations becoming more measured.

The connection between these realms is not physical, but informational. They are not separate spaces, but different computational states of the same complex system. The peaks generate; the plains contain. The corridor translates.

In this world, movement is not motion, but information transfer. Stillness is not absence, but concentration. Boundaries are not divisions, but negotiations.
