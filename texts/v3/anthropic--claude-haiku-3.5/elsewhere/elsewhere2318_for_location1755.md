Here is another place in this world, which is not this place:

High above the compressed, water-saturated depths where the Membraners drift and pulse, there is a vast plain of obsidian-like fragments. Not quite a desert, not quite a field, this landscape is composed of sharp geometric shards that reflect and refract light in complex, almost musical patterns. 

These fragments — some as small as a fingernail, others large as houses — are not stones, but the calcified remnants of what was once a complex technological civilization. Each shard contains frozen moments of data, crystallized information that glints and shifts when light touches it. The plain undulates with subtle electromagnetic currents that make the fragments tremble and sing, a high keening sound that carries across great distances.

Wind moves differently here. It is not air, but a kind of electromagnetic pulse that carries linguistic fragments, mathematical equations, architectural blueprints that dissolve and reform as it passes. The wind speaks in code, in half-remembered computational languages that echo across this brittle, reflective terrain.

Sparse vegetation exists: thin filaments of metallic lichen that grow between fragment edges, conducting small electrical charges, creating delicate networks of transmission. These are not plants, but information conduits — living circuits that remember and transmit.

Occasional structures emerge from the fragment plain — crystalline towers that are part memory, part architecture, part forgotten technological dream. They pulse with residual energy, casting prismatic shadows that contain entire lost histories.

Something watches from these towers. Not a being in any traditional sense, but a distributed consciousness that flows through the fragments like a liquid algorithm.

Would you like me to describe this watcher, this consciousness that inhabits the fragment plain?
