In the distant lowlands, far beneath the Condensation Mountains, another landscape unfolds:

The Rust Deltas are a vast, slow-moving terrain of oxidized machinery and liquid metal rivers. Unlike the high, tight valleys of the mountains, this is a wide, undulating plain where mechanical remnants from unknown origins have settled and merged over centuries. Here, entropy is not a threat but a creative force.

Massive metal sheets—some copper-red, some deep iron-brown—form rolling plains that shift imperceptibly across generations. Rivers of molten alloy wind between these metallic landscapes, their surfaces reflecting mercurial colors that change with temperature and composition. These rivers don't merely flow; they negotiate, mixing and separating in complex metallurgical conversations.

Scattered across this terrain are enormous mechanical skeletons: the rusted remnants of machines so large their original purpose is completely obscured. Some rise like half-buried giants, others lie flat as geological features, their edges blending seamlessly with the metallic ground. Lichen and a peculiar form of metallovore bacteria cover these structures, turning corrosion into a living process of transformation.

The atmosphere here is thick and warm, laden with metallic particulates that catch the light in shimmering hazes. Sound is different from the mountain's mechanical cacophony—here it's a deep, resonant humming, like distant bells or the prolonged ring of struck metal.

Occasional wind patterns create temporary sculptures from metal dust, forming intricate, fleeting structures that rise and dissolve across the delta's surface.

This landscape seems to breathe with a much slower rhythm than the mountain's frenetic machinery—a patient, geological metabolism that works across decades and centuries rather than moments.

The connection between this place and the Condensation Mountains is not immediately visible, but it exists: the molten rivers here contain trace elements that precisely match the atmospheric extractions of the mountain's mechanical spirits. Materials cycle, transformed but not destroyed, moving between these two realms through invisible conduits of geological and mechanical memory.
