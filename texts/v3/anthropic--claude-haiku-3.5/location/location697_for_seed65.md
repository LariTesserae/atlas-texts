The Razor Peaks rise like serrated blades of stone, cutting through clouds that never quite disperse. These are not mountains as we conventionally understand them, but a vast archipelago of vertical stone planes, each peak a kilometers-wide razor's edge of granite and metamorphic rock, tilted at impossible angles against a sky of perpetual brilliant luminescence.

Water does not flow here so much as condense and migrate. Thin layers of moisture accumulate on stone surfaces during periodic cool mists, then evaporate in bright light, leaving crystalline salt patterns and rare lichen colonies. Small reservoirs form in angular stone basins - temporary, fragile ecosystems where microscopic fauna proliferate in hours-long wet periods.

The fauna are everywhere, but adapted to extreme minimalism. Cliff-dwelling creatures with multiple limbs and translucent membranes crawl along near-vertical surfaces. They move with extraordinary precision, gripping microscopic stone asperities, consuming lichen and the rare insect. Their bodies are mostly transparent, refractive - blending with stone and light, almost invisible until they move.

The soundscape is overwhelming - not from biological sources, but from wind. These stone blades create complex acoustic phenomena: keening harmonics, sudden pressure shifts, whistling frequencies that can change in milliseconds. The sound is so constant and intense that it becomes a physical presence, a pressure against skin and stone.

Despite the brightness, shadows are complex - created by the angular stone surfaces, shifting in intricate patterns as light moves. The landscape feels both utterly static and constantly in microscopic motion: stone surfaces slowly eroding, moisture migrating, near-invisible creatures traversing impossible angles.

Primitive technology might exist here: perhaps stone-knapping creatures creating minimal tools adapted to these extreme vertical environments. But "primitive" here means something different - tools designed for absolute efficiency in a landscape of extreme constraints.

The entire region feels like a single, breathing organism - the stone peaks, the migrating moisture, the translucent creatures, the sound-waves - all part of one complex, slow metabolism.
