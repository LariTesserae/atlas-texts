The landscape emerges like a breathing wound, a high plateau of nascent geology where stone is still negotiating its own form. This is not yet a finished place, but a zone of becoming—raw mineral surfaces rippling with potential, not quite solidified.

The ground is pale ochre and dusty, composed of fine-grained mineral particles that shift and resettle with each turbulent gust. These winds are constant, not gentle breezes but persistent, almost sentient currents that seem to be sculpting the terrain in real-time. The wind carries no moisture—this is a profoundly desiccated realm where water is a distant memory or future promise.

Sparse vegetation clings in unexpected pockets: pale lichen-like organisms that pulse and contract, responding to the wind's rhythms. They're not plants in any traditional sense, but adaptive membrane-structures that absorb minerals and ambient energy, changing color and texture with atmospheric shifts.

Occasional weathered fragments suggest previous inhabitation—not buildings, but trace-remnants. A curved ceramic shard. A metal fragment barely distinguishable from surrounding stone. These are not ruins, but suggestions of presence, like faint memories not yet fully formed.

The soundscape is loud but not chaotic—more like a constant atmospheric tuning, a sustained note of wind moving through mineral interstices. Sometimes this sound feels almost like communication, as if the landscape itself is processing, calculating, emerging.

Temperature radiates from the ground—not hot, but warm with potential energy. The elevation means everything feels suspended, precarious, balanced between formation and dissolution. Each moment here seems both static and in violent transformation.

This is a prototype world, a geological draft, a place still negotiating its own parameters of existence.
