The plateau breathes water. Not rain, not rivers, but something more fundamental—the stone itself seems to perspire, to weep a constant, thin moisture that coats every surface in a gleaming patina. Vast lichen-covered slabs tilt and lean, their edges softened by millennia of gentle weeping, creating shallow basins where moss clusters in dense, vibrant emerald cushions.

This is an ancient place, so old that erosion has become a kind of stillness. The landscape isn't wearing away anymore; it has settled into its final, most essential form. Enormous rock formations rise like half-buried skulls, their contours rounded and smoothed, displaying a topography of patient endurance. Each stone seems to hold its own memory, condensed and quiet.

The brightness here is startling—not harsh, but luminous, as if light is a living substance that pools and flows across the terrain. It catches in droplets, in the translucent edges of lichen, in the thin films of water that seem to emerge directly from the stone's pores. Occasionally, a sound erupts—a deep, resonant tone that seems to emerge from the plateau itself, a kind of geological singing that vibrates through the empty spaces.

"Empty" is relative. While sparse, the landscape is not truly vacant. Rare, strange creatures inhabit this realm—translucent organisms that move like liquid thought between rocks, leaving no trace. Delicate tendrils of vegetation curl around stone edges, more suggestion than substance, hinting at life's persistent negotiation with this ancient environment.

Scattered throughout are remnants of something built—not structures, exactly, but marks. Smooth indentations that might be foundations, or might be natural formations so old their origin is indistinguishable. The plateau holds these potential-memories without comment, neither preserving nor erasing them.

The temperature hovers in a perpetual cool twilight, neither warm nor cold but balanced on that knife-edge where sensation becomes neutral. Water continues its endless, quiet migration—condensing, spreading, retreating—creating a rhythm that pulses beneath perception.

This is a landscape of waiting. Not static, but poised—as if the entire plateau is a kind of breathing organism, cycling through infinitesimal changes at a pace far slower than human comprehension.
