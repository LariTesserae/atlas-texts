The Living Terraces rise like a wet, verdant cathedral—a vertical ecosystem of such dense vegetation that plants have become the very architecture of the world. Massive vegetal structures pulse and breathe, their tissues flowing with internal waterways, condensation constantly beading along fibrous membranes.

This is a high-altitude realm where heated air carries constant moisture, creating a perpetual post-rain atmosphere. The landscape doesn't merely have plants; it is plants. Imagine terraced platforms of living tissue, each level a different shade of green-bronze-crimson, interconnected by vine bridges and root-tunnels. Broad leaves act as walkways, while stems thick as buildings provide structural support.

Water isn't just present—it's omnipresent. It seeps, drips, condenses, flows. Every surface glistens. Periodic torrential rains cause the entire living structure to shift and resettle, water cascading through internal channels and external contours. The sound is a constant percussion: droplets, flowing streams, the wet sounds of plant metabolism.

Primitive settlements are woven into this living architecture. Small communities of inhabitants have learned to live within the plant-substrate, carving living spaces into stem-chambers, using fiber-based tools, building their culture around the rhythms of this wet, hot, slowly-changing environment. Their dwellings aren't constructed so much as negotiated with the living landscape.

Fauna moves through this world like secondary circulatory systems—insects that are almost indistinguishable from plant structures, small mammals that navigate root-tunnels, flying creatures that blend with hanging vegetative tendrils.

The landscape breathes. Slowly. Patiently. Each cycle taking generations to fully express.
