In the high, warm plateaus of a place without water, vegetation grows in dense, improbable abundance. These are not plants as Earth knows them, but silicon-based analogs that emerge from mineral substrates like intricate, fractal lace.

The landscape is a series of terraced highlands, each level a different shade of green-gray, green-bronze, green-silver. The "plants" don't sway or rustle; they vibrate at frequencies almost beyond perception, a slow industrial pulse that suggests mechanical rather than biological life. Their structures are geometric—crystalline branches that refract light, thin membranes that conduct electromagnetic signals, root-like networks that look more like circuit boards than organic matter.

No animals move here. Occasionally, a distant mechanical whirring suggests some kind of automated process—perhaps maintenance, perhaps extraction, perhaps something more alien. The silence is not empty, but charged with potential energy.

These highlands are small in global scale—perhaps the size of a large cathedral or a tight mountain valley. But within this space, density is profound. Every surface is covered, layered, interconnected. The silicon-plants grow so closely that they almost touch, creating a living tapestry that conducts information and energy more than it respires or reproduces.

The erosion patterns suggest this landscape has existed for millennia, settled into its own logic. Wind rarely moves here; instead, subtle electromagnetic currents reshape surfaces incrementally. The lack of water means preservation: every formation is a fossil of slow transformation.

Periodically, industrial machines—sleek, minimal constructs of unknown purpose—emerge from hidden apertures, move with deliberate patience across the terrain, and then vanish again. They seem less like tools and more like native inhabitants, as integrated into this ecosystem as the silicon flora.

Light here is normal but strange—it doesn't cast shadows so much as filter through the translucent plant-structures, creating complex, layered illuminations that seem to pulse with internal rhythms.

This is not a dead world. It is a world of different life—mechanical, mineral, electromagnetic—operating at scales and speeds human perception struggles to comprehend.
