The Verdant Hollow emerges as a subterranean ecosystem, a living breathing membrane of chlorophyll and moisture, suspended between formation and existence. It is a vast cavern-womb, not yet fully shaped, but already teeming with nascent complexity.

Imagine a space where plants are not merely inhabitants, but the fundamental substrate—a continuous living tissue that breathes, pulses, and grows. The walls are not stone, but dense vegetative matter: interwoven roots, stems, and membranes that pulse with a slow, almost imperceptible rhythm. Bright light filters through translucent plant-tissue, creating a luminescent green-gold atmosphere that seems to breathe.

Water does not flow here so much as condense and migrate. Droplets form on plant-surfaces, slide along intricate vein-like structures, accumulate in small pockets and then evaporate, creating a constant, hushed migration of moisture. The air is thick, almost syrupy with humidity, hot and dense with potential.

This is a pre-landscape, an environment still negotiating its own rules. Occasional structural artifacts suggest something might have been here before—fragments of unknown origin embedded in the plant-tissue like fossil memories. But no clear technology exists; this is a pure biological realm.

Sounds are minimal: a soft susurration of cellular movement, the occasional drip of condensation, a background hum that might be photosynthesis or something more mysterious. Nothing changes, yet everything is in microscopic motion.

Rare fauna drift through this space—translucent creatures that seem more like living condensations than distinct organisms, blending seamlessly with the plant-substrate, moving with slow, dream-like progressions.

The Verdant Hollow feels like an inhalation about to become an exhalation, a world preparing to define itself.
