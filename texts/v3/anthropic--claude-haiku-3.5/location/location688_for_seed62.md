The Condensation Ranges

High plateaus rise in layered, sharp-edged terraces, recently thrust upward by tectonic whispers. These are not mountains but geometric platforms, each level slightly offset, creating a stepped landscape of pale stone and dense vegetation. Moss and lichen cover every surface in thick, vibrant green carpets, so lush they seem to pulse with chlorophyll metabolism.

Moisture doesn't fall here as rain, but materializes—condensing from the cool air onto rock surfaces and plant membranes. Tiny rivulets form momentarily, tracing silver lines across stone, then vanish into the dense vegetative mat. The landscape breathes humidity, though no large water bodies are visible.

Scattered industrial structures emerge from the green—not buildings, exactly, but mechanical artifacts. Rusted towers with complex pipe networks pierce the vegetation. Some appear to be extracting something: mineral, data, energy. Their surfaces are covered in fine moss, integrated into the landscape, more geological than architectural.

The light is extraordinary—brilliant and diffuse, filtering through layers of green and stone, creating a luminescence that makes everything seem slightly translucent. No shadows exist here, only gradients of brightness.

Occasional mechanical sounds drift—a distant hum, a mechanical pulse, a low-frequency vibration more felt than heard. But mostly, silence dominates. A patience radiates from the landscape, a sense of slow transformation happening at geological timescales.

Strange details emerge: some vegetation seems to move with mechanical precision. Certain rock formations have too-perfect angles. The boundary between natural and constructed blurs, creating an environment of subtle unease.

Rare fauna move with careful, calculated movements—small creatures that seem more like living mechanisms than animals. They navigate the dense green layers, rarely visible, leaving only faint traces.

The entire landscape feels like a complex, living machine—slowly processing, transforming, breathing—its purpose obscure but persistent.
