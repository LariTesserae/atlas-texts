In the soft darkness, a landscape breathes—not with lungs, but with membranes and capillary networks woven through living tissue. This is a place where plants are not merely inhabitants, but the fundamental architecture and substrate of existence.

Imagine a high-altitude terrain, not of rock and soil, but of dense, interlocking plant-flesh. Imagine a cathedral-sized ecosystem, where vegetation grows not in discrete forms, but as a continuous, undulating membrane. Tendrils and root-systems intermesh like complex neural networks, creating living corridors and breathing chambers. The plants here are not green, but range in luminescent purples, deep blues, and translucent grays—adapted to low-light conditions.

Water doesn't flow here so much as seep and condense. The plant-tissue is perpetually damp, with surfaces that attract and hold moisture like living sponges. Tiny droplets constantly migrate through the ecosystem, creating a slow, almost imperceptible hydraulic pulse. Condensation forms delicate, ephemeral structures that bloom and dissolve in quiet rhythms.

Fauna inhabit this system not as external creatures, but as integrated components. Small, pale organisms—part insect, part fungal entity—move through the plant-tissue's interstitial spaces. They're not so much moving through the environment as being absorbed and expelled by it, their boundaries indistinct.

The temperature is warm but not hot—a consistent, nurturing heat that allows constant transformation. Because this is a young landscape, its forms are sharp and crisp, with precise architectural details still forming. Each membrane has a clear, almost geometric quality, like a complex origami just beginning to unfold.

Human settlements exist here, but they're not external to the plant-system—they're grown, not built. Primitive structures emerge from the living tissue, crafted by careful cultivation rather than construction. Inhabitants don't so much live in buildings as they are partially absorbed by them, their dwellings breathing and shifting with the larger ecosystem.

The silence is not a lack of sound, but a deep listening—a constant, low-frequency vibration of biological processes. Occasionally, a distant pulse or a soft membrane-resonance breaks the quiet, like a heartbeat heard from very far away.

This is a strange place: alive, wet, warm, breathing in darkness, where the boundaries between organism and environment have dissolved into a single, complex, continuously transforming presence.
