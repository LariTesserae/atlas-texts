This is not a natural mountain range, but a deliberate architecture — carved, grown, or extruded by something non-human.

Each spire is a translucent black, etched with fine fractal patterns that suggest circuitry or neural networks. Between them: vast empty spaces, wind-scoured and absolutely silent. The surfaces reflect light with a strange inner luminescence, as if each crystal contains suspended energy rather than simply reflecting ambient light.

Tiny creatures move between these formations. Not quite animal, not quite machine — they skitter on multiple leg-like appendages, each about the size of a human hand. Their bodies seem molded from the same obsidian substrate as the landscape, with slight color variations: deep purples, gunmetal grays, burnt oranges. They move with precise, synchronized movements, like a distributed intelligence exploring its own body.

No plants exist here. No soil. Just mineral, energy, movement. The entire landscape feels like a vast, slowly breathing mechanism — not organic, but not entirely inorganic either. A threshold space between categories.

Occasional thin streams of condensation drift between the spires, not quite liquid, not quite vapor. They form temporary bridges, temporary connections, then dissipate.

The heat is constant but not oppressive — a dry, crystalline heat that seems to vibrate the very air. Everything feels poised, balanced, waiting.
