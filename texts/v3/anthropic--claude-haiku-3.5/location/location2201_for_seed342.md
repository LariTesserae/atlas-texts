Here's what emerges:

This is Salt Quarter, a low-elevation industrial settlement carved into a mineral-pale landscape where almost no water exists. The terrain looks like pressed salt crystals and pale gray sedimentary layers, folded and cracked like ancient paper. Wind moves through narrow corridors between geometric industrial structures, creating constant keening sounds that oscillate between mechanical whine and organic moan.

Sparse mechanical vegetation — not plants, exactly, but engineered structures that perform photosynthetic and atmospheric processing — rise in angular clusters. These are silver-white scaffolds with membrane-thin collectors that pulse and flex, extracting minimal nutrient traces from the mineral substrate. They're more like industrial respirators than traditional vegetation, articulated and responsive.

The settlement itself is a maze of interconnected mechanical chambers: factory spaces, processing units, strange hybrid living/working modules that merge architecture with industrial infrastructure. Pipes and conduits twist between buildings like nervous system pathways. Everything feels both precisely engineered and slightly improvisational — as if the entire system is constantly remaking itself.

Mechanical fauna move through this landscape — not organic creatures, but mobile processing units. Some roll, some walk on multiple legs, some float on electromagnetic cushions. They're gathering, sorting, transporting — always in motion, always loud. Their movements create a constant percussive soundtrack, a rhythmic industrial chorus.

The light is flat and diffuse, casting no strong shadows. Everything feels simultaneously sharp-edged and blurred, like a landscape viewed through industrial fog. Temperature suggests a constant cool tension — not cold enough to freeze, not warm enough to encourage organic expansion.

This is a place of constant turbulence: structures shifting, mechanisms recalibrating, boundaries becoming momentarily fluid then resolidifying. The entire settlement feels like a living machine, breathing and transforming.
