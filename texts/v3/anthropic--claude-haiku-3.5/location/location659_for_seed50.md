In the high, emergent realm of Resonance Hollow, water is everywhere and nowhere—a liquid architecture that breathes and pulses with impossible rhythms. Imagine a vertical ocean suspended in geological infancy, where water exists not as fluid but as living membrane, translucent and vibrating with deafening frequencies that carve nascent stone.

This is a pocket-world, smaller than a mountain's breath but vast within its own logic. Sparse vegetation clings to proto-surfaces—not plants exactly, but thin crystalline tendrils that oscillate with the sound-waves, translucent and brittle as glass nerves. They emerge and dissolve constantly, never quite establishing themselves.

The temperature hovers just above freezing, creating a perpetual state of almost-transformation. Scattered artifacts—fragments of unknown technology, perhaps remnants of a civilization that hasn't yet occurred—drift through the liquid space. These are primitive tools: carved stone fragments, bone-like implements that seem to have been shaped by sound rather than hand.

Occasionally, a massive shape will surge through the liquid architecture—a rare fauna, more sonic signature than physical entity. Its presence creates localized storms of reconfiguration, where stone briefly becomes fluid and fluid becomes something between thought and matter.

The light here is normal, but normal is strange: illumination seems to emerge from within the water-stone itself, a bioluminescent pulse that reveals and obscures in the same gesture. Everything is turbulent, in constant metamorphosis—a world not yet settled into its own definition.

Sounds dominate here. Not just sounds, but sound-as-substance: waves that sculpt reality, that create and destroy geological features with each thunderous pulse. The entire environment is a living acoustic instrument, playing itself into existence.

This is a landscape of becoming—raw, unfinished, perpetually in draft. A world more verb than noun.
