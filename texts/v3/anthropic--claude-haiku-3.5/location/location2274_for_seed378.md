In the pocket-world of the Lichen Foldings, moisture is a memory. The terrain is a dense, layered landscape of intricate green-gray growths, where vegetation has adapted to near-total aridity. Lichens form complex three-dimensional matrices, growing in tight, interlocking structures that rise and fall like soft, breathing topographies.

This is a cool environment, perpetually dim—not dark, but muted, as if seen through a film of translucent moss. The light filters softly, creating subtle gradients of shadow between the lichen formations. Each lichen colony is a microhabitat, a self-contained ecosystem barely larger than a cupped hand.

Movement is constant but nearly imperceptible. Microscopic fauna weave through the lichen's intricate channels—tiny arthropods with translucent exoskeletons, some no larger than a grain of salt, others reaching the size of a fingernail. They migrate through capillary networks, trading biochemical signals, consuming and transforming the living substrate.

Occasionally, a primitive bone or stone tool emerges from the lichen matrix—evidence of some past inhabitants, now absorbed into the living landscape. These artifacts are not ruins, but integrated components, their minerals slowly converting into new organic structures.

The environment breathes with a strange, almost sentient rhythm. No water pools here, yet moisture cycles through the lichen's internal structures—a closed system of condensation and transpiration that sustains this improbable world. The silence is not absence, but a dense quiet filled with microscopic interactions.

Predation happens in slow motion: one lichen-dwelling creature gradually consuming another, chemical exchanges more prominent than physical movement. The landscape itself seems to pulse with a deliberate, alien intelligence—not mechanical, but organic and profoundly patient.
