The place emerges like a dream of geology — a vast, tilting plateau of obsidian-like mineral surfaces, constantly shifting and remaking itself. The elevation is extreme: massive angular planes rise and fall in slow, violent transformations, like tectonic sheets being compressed and stretched by invisible forces.

There is almost no water, but occasional thin rivulets of something between liquid and plasma trace delicate silver lines across the black surfaces. These lines move with intelligence, tracing fractal paths that seem to map some alien logic of formation.

Darkness pervades, but not a complete absence of light. Instead, a low phosphorescence seeps from the mineral surfaces themselves — pale green and deep ultraviolet hints of energy, suggesting the landscape is less dead than in a state of perpetual becoming. The warmth is not heat as we know it, but a kind of vibrational intensity that makes the entire plateau feel like it's breathing.

No plants grow here. Instead, crystalline formations emerge and dissolve, growing like strange geometrical organisms that bloom and collapse in moments. These are not truly alive, but exist in a state between mineral and living process — growing, changing, responding to the turbulent dynamics of the environment.

Occasional scattered artifacts hint at something having passed through or formed here: thin spires of fused mineral, intricate lattices that might be remnants of construction or might be natural formations so complex they suggest intentionality.

The sound is a low thrumming, more felt than heard — a vibration that suggests massive forces just beneath perception, like the landscape is constantly on the verge of radical transformation. The sparseness means these sounds echo and resonate with vast, almost musical complexity.

This is a world of emergence, of constant unfinished becoming. Not a dead place, but a place perpetually in the act of being created.
