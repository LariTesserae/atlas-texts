The rock formations rise like jagged teeth from a persistent mist, their basalt surfaces gleaming wet and dark. This is a vertical landscape, a series of narrow plateaus and knife-edged ridges suspended between cloud layers, where water seems to breathe through stone rather than simply flow.

Condensation happens continuously. Not rain, exactly, but a constant weeping - droplets forming on rock surfaces, sliding in thin rivulets that never quite become streams. The stone itself seems to be exhaling moisture, porous and alive. Sharp peaks emerge from and dissolve back into fog banks, creating a landscape of momentary revelations.

Despite the height and apparent harshness, life clusters in intense pockets. Lichen the color of old copper clings to rock faces, and strange succulent plants with translucent, water-storing leaves huddle in crevices. These are not soft plants, but hard-edged survivors, their tissues dense with stored moisture.

The soundscape is overwhelming - a constant susurration of wind through rock formations, punctuated by the clicking and chittering of small fauna. Tiny lizard-like creatures with translucent skin dart between stone layers. Winged things that are part insect, part something else entirely wheel and dive through the mist, their calls sharp and staccato.

Temperature radiates from the rocks themselves - not just ambient heat, but a deep, stored warmth that speaks of recent volcanic activity. The landscape feels young, as if it has only recently erupted or been pushed upward by tectonic forces. Edges are still sharp. Surfaces haven't been worn smooth.

This is an ecosystem of proximity and density. Organisms don't so much inhabit the space as interlock with it, layered and near-touching. A primordial intensity pulses here - not violent, but absolutely vital.
