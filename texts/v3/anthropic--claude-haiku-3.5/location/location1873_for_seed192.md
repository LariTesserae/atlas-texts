The Weeping Cliffs of Mist-Breath rise like sodden sentinels, their surfaces constantly trickling with water that seems to emerge from within the stone itself. The rock is young and sharp-edged, as if recently thrust upward by some tremendous geological breath, with faceted surfaces that catch and channel water in intricate rivulets and cascades.

Each cliff face is a living membrane. Moss and lichen colonize every crevice in dense, saturated layers - vibrant greens and soft grays that pulse with moisture. Hanging gardens sprout from impossible angles: tiny ferns, delicate orchid-like growths, tendrils of vine that weep alongside the water-streams.

The entire landscape is compact, no larger than a cathedral's interior, but so densely populated with life that movement seems difficult. Amphibious creatures crowd every surface - small translucent frogs, salamanders with iridescent skin, lizards that seem more liquid than solid. They pulse and shift constantly, their skin glistening in the normal light.

The sound is overwhelming - a constant susurration of water-drip, creature-chirp, plant-rustle that builds to a near-deafening chorus. Wind moves through the tight spaces between rock and vegetation, creating flute-like resonances that seem almost musical.

Warm mist perpetually condenses here, making the air itself feel alive and breathing. Everything feels fresh, recently emerged, as if the landscape is still negotiating its own boundaries and rules of existence.

This is not a place of human construction, but a pure biological infrastructure - an ecosystem so dense and active that individual organisms seem less important than the collective pulse and flow.
