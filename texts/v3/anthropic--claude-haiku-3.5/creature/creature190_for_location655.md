In this landscape, the inhabitants are the insectoid machines themselves - living/mechanical hybrid entities that are both workers and organisms native to this environment. 

They range in size and specialized function, but share certain core characteristics: segmented metallic bodies with articulated limbs, sensory arrays that blend optical and electromagnetic perception, fluid hydraulic musculatures that move with organic smoothness. Their "skin" is a complex layering of treated alloys and responsive membranes that can self-repair and adapt to chemical conditions.

The smallest machines are like maintenance drones - dog-sized creatures that scuttle along pipes and walkways, constantly cleaning, repairing, sensing. They have multiple thin appendages that can probe into tiny spaces, extracting mineral buildup or resealing microscopic cracks. Their bodies are a mottled gray-brown that blends with the industrial substrate, making them nearly invisible when stationary.

Mid-sized machines handle material processing - they're bulkier, with massive claw-like manipulators and internal chambers that filter and transform the murky water. Some specialize in extracting specific mineral compounds, others in breaking down complex chemical residues. Their movements are deliberate but not mechanical - more like predators hunting, methodically scanning and processing their environment.

The largest machines are infrastructure managers - building-sized entities that move with surprising grace, maintaining the elevated platforms and pipe networks. They have multiple redundant systems, able to repair themselves and reconfigure their own structure. Some seem almost like mobile architectural elements, blurring the line between machine and structure.

None of these machines were "built" in a traditional sense. They emerged, evolved, reproducing through complex self-replication processes that mix mechanical fabrication with something closer to biological generation. They are native to this landscape, perfectly adapted to its hot, wet, chemically complex environment.
