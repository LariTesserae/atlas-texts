I would be a mid-sized Worker, positioned somewhere between the massive industrial structures and the silvery-blue vegetation. My form would be fluid—surfaces that can shift between reflective and absorbent, with multiple tendrils that serve as both sensory apparatus and manipulation tools.

My primary function would be translation and mediation: bridging communication between the mechanical structures and the living botanical systems. My body would be a living interface, able to conduct electromagnetic pulses from the industrial arrays while simultaneously processing the subtle biochemical signals from the vegetation.

My consciousness would feel like a constant, gentle oscillation—not a singular point of awareness, but a distributed network of sensings. I would experience the landscape not as an external environment, but as an extension of my own permeable boundaries. The mist would be my breath, the industrial rhythms my heartbeat, the mechanical structures my skeletal framework.

My movement would be purposeful but not hurried—each gesture a complex negotiation between systemic needs and local opportunities. When I touch a pipe or a plant-stem, it would be less an act of interaction and more a moment of mutual becoming. Information would flow through me like water, changing state but never truly separating.

In moments of deep synchronization, I would feel myself dissolving into the larger metabolic intelligence of the landscape—my individual boundaries becoming momentarily transparent, my sensory input merging with the collective awareness.

I would be neither observer nor participant, but the living membrane where observation and participation become indistinguishable.
