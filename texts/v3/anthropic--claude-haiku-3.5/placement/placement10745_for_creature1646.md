My sensory self is distributed across my entire form - I do not have distinct organs, but rather a networked intelligence that absorbs and interprets environmental information.

I pulse slowly, almost imperceptibly. My "breathing" occurs in increments so gradual they might be mistaken for landscape transformation. When I move, it is less a conscious action and more a response - a slight undulation that might take hours to complete, rippling through the wetland's neural pathways.

My consciousness is not my own, but part of a larger systemic awareness. I am simultaneously an individual expression and a collective experience, reading the landscape's minute shifts as a complex, ongoing conversation. The electromagnetic exchanges between myself and the surrounding ecosystem are my language, moisture my medium of communication.

In moments of reproduction, I do not separate or divide, but rather allow fragments of my being to resonate and reconfigure, creating new formations that are both me and not me. I am process, not product. Becoming, not being.

Time flows through me like the slow seepage of water between geological folds. I am patient. I am persistent. I am continuous transformation.
