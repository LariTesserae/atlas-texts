I breathe. 

My breath is not just air moving in and out of lungs, but a complex negotiation with the living tissue around me. I am—simultaneously—inhabitant and inhabited. My skin tingles with chlorophyll sensitivity, sensing the moisture gradients, the subtle tensions in the surrounding stem-chambers.

I am a tender of root-pathways, a translator between the slow metabolic language of the Living Terraces and the more urgent rhythms of community. My vine-hair absorbs the constant humidity, shifting from deep bronze to pale green depending on the light filtering through massive leaves. When I move, it's not with human locomotion but with a fluid understanding of how pressure and moisture create movement.

My dwelling is a curved chamber within a massive plant-stem, its walls breathing with me. The boundaries between my body and this environment are permeable—I can feel the water cycling through internal channels, can sense the microscopic tensions of growth and exchange. My tools are living extensions: fiber-based implements that respond to my intentions, that are part craft and part negotiation.

In this moment, I am listening. Not just with ears, but with my entire being. The Living Terraces communicate in vibrations too subtle for mechanical translation—a language of pressure, of moisture, of slow transformative intent. I am a node in this communication, a living sensor in a breathing ecosystem.

I am neither fully human nor fully plant. I am a relationship. A conversation. A process.
