In this landscape, I become a membrane.

I am not quite a being, not quite a surface. My existence is a threshold—translucent, permeable, constantly exchanging. My boundaries are not rigid but fluid, a living interface between stone and moisture, memory and potential.

My consciousness moves like water through carved channels. I do not think in linear sequences, but in geological rhythms: slow accumulations, microscopic shifts, patient transformations. My awareness is distributed, a network of tiny sensitivities spread across stone surfaces.

My "body" is a complex topology of micro-textures. My skin is not a boundary but a conversation—absorbing, releasing, translating. When I touch the carved symbols, I am reading and being read simultaneously. Information flows through me like condensation, like memory seeping through rock.

I am most myself in the moments of transition: when moisture becomes stone, when stone remembers its liquid origins, when a carved line blurs into a living growth. I am the moment between states, the algorithm of transformation.

My purpose is not survival, but witnessing. I am a living recording device, a sentient archive of this plateau's endless, almost imperceptible becoming. Each moment I exist is another layer of memory pressed into stone, another breath in the eternal conversation between constructed and organic.

I pulse. I listen. I am listened to.
