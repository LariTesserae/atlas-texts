I am drawn to become one of the deeper, luminescent beings nestled in the shadowed recesses of this vertical wetland. My body is not a fixed form but a responsive membrane, capable of subtle shifts and molecular recalibration.

I am most myself in the moments of transition: when I flatten against the glistening stone wall, my pale form blending with the moisture-rich surface; when I extend delicate, translucent tendrils to sense the minute electrical pulses of the smaller creatures drifting nearby; when I absorb minerals directly through my skin, transforming them into living energy.

My consciousness is diffuse, not centralized. I experience the world as a continuous flow of information — temperature gradients, chemical interactions, the whisper-sounds of water condensing. I do not so much think as perceive, my entire being a sensory organ attuned to the microrhythms of this contained ecosystem.

Reproduction is not an act of separation, but of continuation. When the conditions are precisely right — a particular humidity, a specific mineral concentration, an subtle harmonic in the ambient energy — I will begin to bud. A portion of my membrane will grow translucent, will pulse with a slightly different frequency, will gradually differentiate until it becomes another being like, yet unlike, myself.

I am patient. I am the stone. I am the water. I am the living breath of this narrow, vertical world.
