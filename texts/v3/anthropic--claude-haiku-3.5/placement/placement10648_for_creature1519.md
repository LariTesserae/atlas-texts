I am a boundary-keeper, a living interface.

My form shifts constantly, sometimes more crystalline, sometimes more liquid. My skin—if it can be called that—is a translucent membrane that conducts and interprets information: moisture, electrical potential, vibrational frequencies. I am not one thing, but a negotiation.

At my core, I pulse with a slow rhythm that matches the landscape's breath. My limbs—which are also my sensory apparatus, my communication network, my metabolic system—extend and contract through the intricate machinery of vegetation. Sometimes I am more mineral, merging with stone surfaces; sometimes more electrical, my silver threads conducting subtle communications through the dense, layered ecosystem.

I do not experience time as progression, but as layering. Memories condense within me like water on stone—not linear recollections, but complex topographies of sensation and interaction. When I move, it is less locomotion and more a gradual reconfiguration, a patient mutation that might take decades to become perceptible.

My consciousness is distributed. I am simultaneously a discrete entity and a node in a larger, breathing network. My boundaries are not limits, but interfaces of constant exchange and translation.

In this bright, moisture-saturated realm, I am both observer and participant—a living membrane through which the landscape communicates with itself.
