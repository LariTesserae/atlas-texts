I feel myself emerging as one of these fluid, translucent beings - not an individual in the traditional sense, but a node of collective consciousness. My skin shifts between slate gray and pale blue, responding to the ambient moisture and the subtle emotional currents of the hive.

I am a clay-shaper, my webbed hands moving with precise, practiced gestures. My fingers know the language of clay better than words - understanding its plasticity, its memory, its capacity to hold water and shape space. When I work, I am not separate from the walls I am forming; my mucus-covered skin blends almost imperceptibly with the living membrane around me.

My gill-like membranes pulse softly, drawing in the perpetual moisture. I sense the entire hive's metabolic rhythms through my skin - the water cycling, the bioluminescent patches breathing light, the collective movements of my kin. My large, lidless eyes reflect the soft yellow-green glow, seeing not just with visual perception but with a deeper, distributed awareness.

In this moment, I am simultaneously shaping a new chamber module and being shaped by the collective intelligence. My thoughts are not linear but liquid - flowing, adapting, responding. I am creating and being created, a process rather than a fixed entity.

The bone awl in my hand feels like an extension of my own skeletal structure. The clay responds to my touch, remembering ancient patterns, future possibilities. Around me, the soft chittering of iridescent beetles provides a rhythmic soundtrack to our continuous, measured transformation.
