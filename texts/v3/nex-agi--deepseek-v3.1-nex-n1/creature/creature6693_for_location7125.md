Everything that lives in the heart of the mesa-city is a listener.

You call them “things” because you haven’t learned their names yet, and because Qesh does not give its names lightly. But they are not inanimate things; they are inhabitants of a garden that no one planted, that grew from the stone’s own willingness to be hollow.

At the center, where the slit opens in the mesa and the city leans down to look in, there is a creature that is also a place.

You might mistake it, at first glance, for the architecture itself.

Its name, when the city speaks to itself, is something like a long chord struck on glass bells: a shimmer of sound and heat and color. For you, I will call it **the Mesa-Throat.**

It is not exactly plant or animal or fungus or stone. It is the reason why the garden grew in a place where water does not fall.

The Mesa-Throat is the living core of the vertical shaft at the center of Qesh. It has grown upward from whatever depths the split in the stone descends to, and it has grown itself outward into the city’s many veins—vine trunks and wax corridors, mycelium pathways and resin bays—but its heart remains in the slit of the world.

If you stand on the inner terraces and look down, you will see:

Great walls of translucent tissue that cling like wet silk to the basalt sides of the shaft. The tissue is thick and layered and ridged, like a throat of impossible scale. Its color is not one color but many seen at once: the deep, bruised violet of the vines’ bark, the muted gold of resin, the faint blue-green of the sky far away at the rim of the world.

The surface of the Mesa-Throat is not smooth skin. It is covered in nodules, ridges, and tiny mouths that do not bite. They are open, like tiny funnels of flesh, and each one exhales a different breath.

From some mouths comes dust: continuous, faintly luminous, the same pale dust that settles on the leaves of the city below. The dust is not ash or sand or pollen; it is condensed, crystallized information—whatever the Mesa-Throat has learned from the world’s edge and the world’s depths and the world’s living things.

From other mouths come scents: thin, invisible streams of air that smell like heat, stone memory, the distant memory of rain that never reached here, and the faint metallic sweetness of tin-thoughts vibrating.

Others breathe sound.

At intervals, the Mesa-Throat shudders along its length, and a sound emerges from dozens of mouths at once. It is not a word or a scream or a chorus of beasts. It is a low, resonant tone—fathoms below human speech—that feels as if the stone under your feet is speaking very quietly.

The city listens to these tremors.

---

Around the Mesa-Throat, in the terraces and balconies that descend into the shaft like descending ribs, there are **the Vine-Kings**.

You might walk past them thinking they are merely trees, or exceptionally old trunk-columns that the city has been built around. But if you lean against the pale velvet bark of one, you feel it lean back.

The Vine-Kings are the reason the city clings to the mesa at all. They are vast, slow, communal creatures whose bodies are both trunk and wall and road and ceiling.

They do not “think” in sentences you would recognize, but they have intentions:

- When a child is born in Qesh, the vine-kings of that quarter widen their trunks in a subtle curve around the house where she will grow.  
- When someone dies, their last words are spoken into the vine, and parts of the vine grow darker around those words, like ink settling into paper.  
- When the Mesa-Throat sings low and long, the Vine-Kings stir along their length, sending tremors into their roots and leaves and the people who touch them.

They are not guardians in the sense of watchdogs with teeth. They are guardians in the sense of something that remembers how it was to grow up out of stone and stretch toward the heated sky. When danger comes—shocks of wind, intrusions of metal or machinery, or ideas from far plains that are too wet to survive here—the Vine-Kings transmit that warning along the mycelial lattice beneath the city, and the city changes.

Wood hardens.  
Passages close.  
Resin flows faster.

If you live in Qesh long enough, you learn to listen through your feet to what the Vine-Kings are saying.

---

In between the Vine-Kings, in lines and patches and hanging sheets, you find **the Silent Chorus**.

You might once have called them insects.

Here, they are the city’s nerves and songs and eyes.

Some are:

- **Light-bringers**—beetle-shaped creatures fused into the walls and arches of the vine-cathedrals. When something living—you, a traveler—passes beneath, they unfold wings of transparent amber and angle them into the sun or the sky-glass or the distant glow of the mesa’s pupil. Their wings are not for flying but for focusing light. They illuminate thresholds that way, so no doorway in the heart of the city is truly dark.

- **The Honey-thinkers**—fat white grubs that emerge from the Vine-Kings’ higher trunks when the moon is thin or high. They are not blind, but they do not see like you. They see in scents and heat patterns and memories of taste.  
  When the handlers come out at night, coated in wax and oils that smell of ancient flowers and tin dust, they kneel among the streets, and the Honey-thinkers climb them like bridges. Contact with skin causes their bodies to produce a luminous honey that is not sweet food but a dense flow of image—fragments of what the Mesa-Throat has heard, or what the Vine-Kings have dreamed in stone-years.  
  Those who partake of that honey can see far, in the way a city sees far.

- **Hovering-seers**—Silent hummingbirds with wings of glass and trailing hair-thin filaments. They never land. They hover in clusters at the very edge of the vertical shaft, where the air above the Mesa-Throat is warm and rises upward.  
  Their eyes are multifaceted jewel, and each facet captures a different kind of seeing: heat, motion, dust patterns, the faint shimmer of hormone messages passing between plants and creatures and stone.  
  When they see something they need the city to know, they vibrate their wings in a particular pattern. The Mesa-Throat hears it and resonates; the Vine-Kings listen; the city shifts.

Smaller, stranger things drift between them:

- **Dust-sailors**—basket-shaped or many-legged, globular or flattened, depending on the currents of heat and dust. They drift up and down the shaft of the Mesa-Throat like ships on invisible air.  
  They are barely animate creatures, more like breathing kites than animals. They open their bodies to the dust, and whatever dust settles into them changes their shape and density.  
  Somewhere between their translucent membranes and the air, they decide what dust to keep and what dust to release back into the city. When their weight becomes too much for the rising air, they settle onto waxen balconies or onto the Mesa-Throat’s ridges and become almost indistinguishable from stone or fungus.

---

Closer to the Mesa-Throat’s lowest visible reach, where the stone and flesh and air are indistinguishable, there are **Wax-fingers and Resin-tongues**.

These are less creatures than extended limbs of the Mesa-Throat.  
They are the reason why some corridors in Qesh seem to breathe.

Wax-fingers are curtains of thick, translucent wax that drape from ceilings and edges and terraces overlooking the shaft. They are not solid walls but vertical membranes of honeycomb, layer on layer, each cell filled with air or dust or honey or memory.  
Small creatures you cannot see clearly crawl across them, shaping them, reshaping them.

Resin-tongues are slow rivers of hardened and half-hardened amber that flow from the Mesa-Throat’s edges and along the vine-roads and balconies. They are a language in which the city sets its stories: events captured in translucent gold, tiny shapes of beings and cities and landscapes suspended inside.  
The city does not write history on stone tablets; it writes history in resin layers that are so thick and time-filled that they have become an archive of candied time.

---

On the very edges of the Mesa-Throat’s influence, where the city’s heart starts to become its outskirts, there are **the Boundary Stones**.

They are not exactly stones and not exactly alive.

They are patterns of crystal and biofilm and mycelium that have inhabited cracks in the basalt mesa-top, where the city gives way to the sky’s glass edge. They look like patches of lichen, but on closer inspection, they are intricate geometries—minute spirals, hexagons, and lattices that are too precise to be accidental.

They respond to changes in the Mesa-Throat’s song. When the city is quiet, they remain quiet.

When the city senses ideas from far plains—where rivers are thick and machines are heavy—the Boundary Stones shimmer. Their patterns rearrange, subtly.

The people who have learned to read those patterns say they are watching the city’s dreams, trying to prepare for whatever distant world might one day find its way up the mesa to Qesh.

---

If you stay in the city long enough, you begin to understand that there is no single thing that lives in the heart of Qesh. There is only one heart, composed of many lives.

The Mesa-Throat listens.  
The Vine-Kings remember.  
The Silent Chorus carries and shows and tastes.  
The Wax-fingers and Resin-tongues shape and preserve.  
The Boundary Stones dream and defend.

And you—whoever you are—have become another listener in the garden that no one planted.
