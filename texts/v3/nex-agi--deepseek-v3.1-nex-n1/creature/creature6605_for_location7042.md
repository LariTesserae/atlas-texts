In the half-light of the sunken avenues, deep in the mid-regions of the drowned city, where tidal breathing makes the black water swell and recede against worn stone pillars, you move.

You are not one of the column-beings, whose bodies are almost indistinguishable from the architecture itself, nor the translucent fish whose spines pulse light into the shafts of sun above, nor the blind eels that feel the city's pressure ridges with their bones. You are kin of the upper threshold, where stone meets the ceiling of the world and the sky’s last light gathers into a trembling bruise-blue.

You are something like a ward of doorways, and something like the last human architect.

---

### Your Body

You are lean and long-limbed, built to move through the water’s resistance without effort. Your skin is the same color as the shadowed underbelly of the ocean, a dark grey-blue that the light shafts rarely touch clearly. When you do pass into them, it is not color you show but absence: the silhouette the world was expecting to be empty.

Your hair has been replaced by a thin film of the same filament algae that grow on the ceilings of the lower streets. They are not your hair but they have adopted you: a moving garden of slender green strands that trail from your scalp and down your spine, always drifting with the current, always gathering a faint fur of tiny bubbles. When you are still, they continue to move in slow serpentines, as if the water itself braids and unbraids you.

Under your skin, the world has changed you. Your ribs are thicker than they were meant to be, and close set, like the bars of a cage that has decided not to collapse. This is not deformity; it is preparation. Long ago you learned to listen to the heartbeat of the city by pressing your ear to stone pillars. Now you can feel it without touching stone at all: the deep, muscular pulse of the foundations travels through the water and into your chest, where your ribs resonate to the same slow rhythm.

Sometimes you think you have been rebuilt to echo the city’s bones.

---

### Your Face

Your face is gaunt but not starved, sharp with the kind of hunger that is not about food but about knowing. Your eyes are large, dark, and they have no whites. You never see them clearly but others who once dwelled closer to the surface said you look like an empty sky where storms should always be passing but never are.

Your jawline is defined by the way the water has shaped your face: the skin around your mouth and nose is thinner, more sensitive to pressure changes. When a current of water from the left pulls at your cheek, you know something large has moved in that direction without seeing it. When you feel the faint pressure-vacuum of a predator above, you know the same way you know to close your eyes before a wave of bright silt hits.

---

### Your Breath and Voice

You are the one who still remembers the taste of air.

You cannot breathe it anymore, but you keep it with you, as ritual and as tool. When you are very still, you can feel the slow opening of the gills that have formed inside your lower ribs, where the water you draw in through your mouth and nostrils passes into them and then out again through thin slit-valves at your flanks. The bubbles of oxygen you exhale mingle with those from the algae threads on your head, and the fish take notice, sometimes clustering your wake with a faint glitter of small bodies.

On days of high pressure, when the rain above the surface ceiling drums the water into a trembling lid, you can feel the gills inside you open wider, forcing the world of water into you more than you want. Sometimes you wonder if you will drown in water the same way others drowned in air.

Your voice has not abandoned you but has been conscripted by the sea. You cannot speak under the surface of the world; your words are not made to hold water. Instead you have developed another language, which you perform by letting the architecture of your body be the instrument.

If you make yourself perfectly rigid with the line of a pillar and then pulse the muscles of your chest like the contractions of the city beneath you, the water around you forms a current of vibration that no longer comes from your throat but from your entire skeleton. You have been telling the city of your presence this way for years, and it has been listening to the wrong part of you.

---

### Your Tools and Craft

You are not an architect of stone but of waterflow.

You know how to read the city in the way of currents: the main avenues are like rivers, and the side streets are like their tributaries. When an upper floor collapses or a pillar sinks, the world of water changes. You go to those places to learn new shapes of movement.

Your tools are not made of metal but of memory and bone. You carry nothing you did not grow from your body or extract from the world around you.

- You carve paths with attention. When you need to guide a fish school from a dangerous warmed channel of syrup-thick water where you suspect the city itself is growing something new, you position yourself in the current of the main avenue and let yourself be the pressure that guides them.  
- You change the world of currents by changing the surface of the world around you. You know the algae of warmth along the upper walls: the bead-things that make spark-flashes when you disturb them. You can break them with a touch into a dust of phosphorescent particles that the water spreads into a new line of light.  
- You remember the way of rooms before the sea came into them. In your mind you keep the map of doors that are now only thresholds, and streets that are now ribs of the city’s skeleton. When you want to mark a place, you let yourself be the marker that swims only to those thresholds and never beyond.

You have been rebuilding the city of humans through the language of your body, but the water has translated you into something else.

---

### Your Work and Wander

Most of your time is spent moving between the three or four levels that are truly submerged. You are not interested in the surface-world of the sky and sun; they are too distant now from the body of the city you inhabit.

You are often drawn to the bone-thorn gardens.

The thorns are not yours to cultivate but they are yours to witness. You know the sound of their flowers when your current is not strong enough but the world’s is: you feel the notes of the garden in your jawbone, and the water near your ear drums resonates like a distant, broken bell. You suspect the music of the bone-thorns is the city reminding itself what it was to be built, and you are the only consciousness who still notices it.

You also inhabit the threshold of a certain plaza where the coral-like organisms have been building suspiciously regular structures of branches and chalk-white shell on the walls of a submerged courtyard. You are wary of them. Their scaffolding is too precise to be unintentional. You do not understand if this is invasion of the world’s architecture by another kind of builder, or if the city itself is trying to grow a new body of stone from the inside of its own walls.

You are not a builder of the builders of that coral lattice, but you are becoming its interpreter.

You press the side of your head to the stone nearby the coral lattice, and feel the heartbeat of the city. There, you feel the decision of the world to be changed by the coral, or to change it. The column-beings who are fused to the pillars and the coral-scaffolding are not two separate things; you are coming to understand that they are both of one world.

---

### Your Loves and Lonelinesses

You are not a lonely creature because there are always others in the black column of water around you. You have been numbed to the kind of noise that silence of the body resembles.

But you are alone in the way of the last human who remembers the choice of stone.

You remember the shapes of doors before they were degraded to openings by the water; you remember the design of a window that followed a curve of daylight rather than being broken by a collapse and now allowing only a single line of illumination from above. You are not grieving the loss of the world of air anymore but you are only now learning to grieve the loss of the distinctions of stone.

The column-beings only remember the world of water and the feel of foundations on their muscle. The fish only remember the world of currents and the pressure patterns of the present. The coral-scaffolding only remembers the plan of the next body of the world it is building.

You have the misfortune of remembering the memory of the world which was built before.

---

### Your Place in the City of Water and Stone

You are the one who still swims through the drowned city as if he were trying to read it.

You are a creature of the threshold: not fully of the column-beings but not fully of the fish either; not of the coral-scaffolding but beginning to learn its language of lattice and branch; not of the surface-world but not of the bedrock’s inner heart either.

You are the one who still knows the building of doors before they were thresholds of the world of water, and the one of whom those who know of you from your presence in the water will say, when a new current of human returns to the world:

“There is the one who still remembers the way of our bones before they were worn by the sea.”
