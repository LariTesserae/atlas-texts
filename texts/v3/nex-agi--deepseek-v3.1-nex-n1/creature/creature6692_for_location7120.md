On the roof of the world, tiny and bright and damp, live **the Roof-Born**—a people who have turned the art of walking into a language of place.

They are not tall, but length is not their element. They are compact, with short torsos and long legs that let them spring easily between terraces, and broad, pliant feet whose soles are faintly ridged to grip the pale stone. Their skin has taken on the color of the hill—dusty pale where the sun strikes it, faintly bluish where the mist touches it—changing subtly through the day’s light. 

---

### Builders of Stillness

By day the Roof-Born are scattered along the height and curve of the hill like water droplets waiting to gather. They come down from their stone chambers only as far as necessary, and never at the same time unless something is amiss. They vanish into passageways so narrow that you would not think to call them streets, their shoulders brushing the walls of their own houses.

Their **houses** have few rooms—one for the body, one for the cistern, one for the sky. 

- The **sleeping room** is low and round, hewn from the stone of the hill itself. Slats of pale stone form shallow shelves that double as beds; no one builds anything so fragile as a table. The light that reaches inside is always indirect—through slots just wide enough to admit air and the faint color of the clouds.  
- The **cistern room** is beneath the floor, a hollow echoing chamber where their water lives. It is the coldest room in the house, kept bare of anything that might rot or consume. People enter it only to draw, to clean, or on certain days, to **listen**.
- The **sky room** is the roof itself—slanted, with carved channels and a low rim so that no one falls accidentally. From above it looks like any other roof; from below it is both ceiling and public road, and some city laws are spoken only upon it.

---

### The Roof as Language

To walk across a roof in the city of cisterns is to **touch** someone else’s life without entering their house. Roof-Born children are taught before they can speak: **the roof teaches you before the floor does**.

They learn **the roof step** before they are weaned—a soft, two-footed descent where the toes arrive a heartbeat before the heels, testing the stone slab’s strength. For the Roof-Born, sounding the roof is like sight: they listen with the soles of their feet for changes in the **voice of the stone**.

Each roof has its own note:  
- **Resonant** tells them the cistern below is full, its trapped air ringing when struck.  
- **Dry** and feathery taps indicate dangerously low water.  
- **Thick** and **dull**—more alarm than sound—warns of cracked stone or failing support.

They **greet** one another roof-to-roof, not by knocking on walls or doors, but by **walking a signature**. That is: you announce yourself by a particular pattern—step-pause-step, three quick light falls, a long drag of heel followed by a single tap. A mother knows her child on any roof above the city by that pattern alone.

---

### Cisterns as Memory

The **cisterns** beneath their houses are not only reservoirs of water; they are **reservoirs of memory**.

At the **Marking of the Stillness**, once a year in the deepest part of the dry season, the Roof-Born open the **cistern room** and stand silently at its edge. Each person **speaks into the water**—not in loud proclamation, but in the **private** language of the house.

- Some **sing** their name—and the cistern rises gently in echo, amplifying the melody faintly.
- Some **whisper** a regret—and the water swallows it.
- Tradition allows **three** utterances to every person:
  - **what they have lost**
  - **what they have learned**
  - **what they will not do**

The cistern remembers. The Roof-Born believe that **emptiness** is a dangerous weight for stone to bear alone—so they **fill** the emptiness with words and songs, which fall into the water and **lie** there, **heavy** and **true**.

Over generations the cistern water **thickens**, though it never rots. It becomes **living** and **sweet**, and those who drink it say they **taste** time: the faint brine of old sorrows, the **thin** honey of old **joys**, the **clean** hardness of decisions **kept**.

---

### The Rainward

There are **water-bearers** among them, though they **rarely** carry water.

They are **listeners**, mostly—those **few** who **stay** long **enough** on **each** roof to hear **how** it **has** changed **since** the **last** storm.

- Their **eyebrows** are often **dusted** with **pale** grit.  
- Their **heels** are **ridged** from **uneven** stones polished bare by **use**.

They **do** not **speak** the **way** the **others** do; **instead** their **lips** move **almost** silently **as** they **walk**, **naming** roofs to **themselves**:  
- “This one **thirsts**; **channel** west **thirty** steps **will** **carry** some **of** **the** **sorrow**.”  
- “This one **is** **brave**; **still** **useful** though **the** **sound** **has** **deepened**.”

They **knock** **lightly** on **stone** **with** a **short** **chisel** and **a** **round** **hammer**—not **to** **hurt**, **but** **to** **hear**:  

- **If** the **sound** **is** **tight**, **clean** in **its** **sorrow**, they **mark** that **roof** **with** a **small** **white** **circle**—to **say** to **passing** **walkers**:  
  **“This stone** **still** **remembers** **how** **to** **hold**.”  

- **If** **there** **is** a **loose** **note**, **damp** at **the** **edges**, **they** leave **two** **small** **strokes**:  
  **“This** **stone** **will** **talk** **to** **the** **rain** **more** **than** **to** **you**.”

---

### Driftless and Untethered

High **above**, the **winds** **sometimes** **tear** at **the** **city**, **brief** and **sharp** in **the** **thin** air.

- To **small** children **whose** bones **are** still **light**, such **winds** **feel** like **hands** **trying** **to** **pull** **them** **from** **the** roof.

- **So** they **learn** **the** **Walking** **Eyes**:  
  **feet** **always** **knowing** **where** **the** next **step** **will** **land**, **head** **never** **looking** **down** unless **the** stone **has** **spoken** **first**.

Out **among** **the** **empty** **roofs**—those **whose** houses **are** **long** **empty**—children **play** **“Storm.”**  
- **One** stands **very** **still**, **arms** **spread**, **chin** **lifted** to **the** **wind**, **while** **others** **close** **their** eyes **and** **walk** **toward** **that** **voice**. **If** they **arrive** **before** **the** **wind** **drops**, they **win** the **right** to **name** **the** roof **for** a **night**.

There **are** **few** **fallings**— **but** the **story** **of** **each** **one** is **remembered**:  
- **how** **their** **step** sounded on **the** roof  
- **what** **the** **cistern** **said** in **echo**  
- **what** **the** **rain** **did** **when** **it** **finally** **came**.

---

### The Names They Never Say

The **Roof-Born** **do** not **name** **the** **valleys** **below**—not **with** the **thin** word **“world.”**

- **They** **call** **their** **city** **“The** **Roof.”**  
- **They** **call** **everything** **below** **“The** **Downwards**?”

That **question** **mark** **is** **spoken**, **faint** and **true**; **they** **know** **it** **exists**—they **see** the **mist** rise **and** the **clouds** **devour** **the** lower **slopes**— **but** **to** **name** **something** **as** **solid** **as** **“world”** **for** **what** is **down** **there** **would** **give** **it** a **weight** **their** roofs **cannot** **carry**.

So **the** **Downwards**? **remains** **a** **question**:  
- **a** **place** **where** **rivers** **run** **wild** **without** **being** **asked**  
- **a** **place** **where** **people** **live** **un**- **roofed**  
- **a** **place** **they** **do** **not** quite **believe** in **because** **their** **feet** have **never** **tested** **its** **surface**.

---

### Living with Time and Gravity

**Gravity** is **their** **master** **builder**:  
- **It** **chooses** **how** **water** is **shared**  
- **how** roofs **slope**  
- **how** **records** **fall** into **the** **cistern**.

**Time** is **their** **only** **landlord**:  
- **It** **cuts** **the** **stone** **slow** and **soft**  
- **wears** **the** **paths** **over** **their** roofs **until** **they** **glow**  
- **gives** **the** **water** **in** **their** **cisterns** **the** **slow** **power** to **remember** **everything** **whispered** into **it**.

The **Roof-Born** **know** **this** **without** needing **to** say **it**: **they** **are** **temporary** **caretakers** **of** **a** stone **system** that **will** **long** **outlast** **them**. Their **job**—the **one** their **feet** perform **every** hour **of** **every** **day**—is **to** **keep** **the** **roofs** **true** **enough** for **gravity**, **quiet** **enough** for **water**, **and** **strong** **enough** for **time**.

And **at** **night**, when **the** **thin** air **turns** **cold** and **the** **stars** **appear** so **close** **you** **want** to **reach** **them** **from** **the** roof’s **edge**, **the** Roof-Born **lie** **on** their **backs** and **listen** through **the** warm **stone**:  
to **the** **water** **below**, **speaking** **back** to **them** in **all** **the** tongues **it** **ever** learned— **in** the **sound** **of** **their** own **long**-**finished** **parents**, **grand** parents, **and** **the** **first** **foot** **that** ever **set** **its** **step** **upon** this **slant** **of** stone.
