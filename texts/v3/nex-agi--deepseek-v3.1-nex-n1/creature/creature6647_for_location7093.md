(Set on the Roof of Roots, a “continent” of inverted root–forest webbing under the world, colonised by the descendants of humans who have forgotten what sun and sky feel like.)

---

### 1. What they call themselves

They have a word for themselves that we cannot truly sound:  
a throat-click and a hummed nasal vowel compressed into one syllable.

When they describe it to you—slowly, in the broken trade language you share—they gesture at the world above your head and then tap their own chest, and at last you understand:  

**They call themselves the Under-Skin.**

Not in the sense of meat hidden inside a body, but as *the layer between the living world and its emptiness.*  
If the world is a body, then the surface far above is the outer skin, where wind and sun and seasons and cities are;  
below that is the thick flesh of soil and rock and forest;  
then below that again—this place, this web of roots—is the under-skin,  
thin membrane stretched over the bone-void.

They are the people of the under-side of the world.

They say “we” with a gesture that sweeps the ceiling,  
as if they are the cells that live in the world’s pores.

---

### 2. Appearance and perception of the body

They are recognisably human in shape, but not in surface.

Their skin is pale, not from lack of sun, but from the **absence of pigment choice**.  
In this world, light is everywhere, equal, and does not burn.  
Colour exists on their skin as faint, scrolling **pathmaps**—  
–thin veins of blue-green and gold that branch from wrist to shoulder,  
from spine to ribs,  
like a river-system drawn on the body.  
The older they grow, the more elaborate these paths become, until they look like the Roof of Roots itself imprinted on skin.

They are slender, built for **low weight and high balance**:  
every tendon and ligament formed over generations of walking on pathways that are not floors, but living bridges hung over nothing.

Their eyes are large,  
adapted to making meaning of constant light and confusing space.  
The iris of each eye is surrounded by a thin, reflective ring of silvery tissue that  
–when they look at you–  
multiply your own reflection into tiny fragments  
as if your body has been scattered into a constellation.

They do not see “darkness” in the same way you do.  
When you say “night,” they think of the **void below**  
—where the world-body ends and the cold, deep wind begins—  
not a cycle of light and absence.

Sometimes, when they are very quiet in the damp of the condensation clouds,  
you can hear their joints **clicking under the skin**  
like tiny bone chimes in fog.  
Those are not injuries;  
those are the sound of their bones adapting to the tensile world that  
never stops shifting.

---

### 3. Posture and space: how they live in the world's ceiling

They live **pressed against the underside of everything**.

They are comfortable with three dimensions of movement in a way that would make you dizzy:  
climbing up vertical root-pillars,  
crawling along “ceilings” that are the floor of the world above,  
descending ladders made from thin roots that reach into the almost-void.

Their spine has a subtle curve different from yours:  
not the bend of a labourer over soil,  
but the **curve of a plant toward a light source**,  
even though the light here is all around.

When they rest, they do not lie flat;  
they **cling**.

You might think of them as half spider, half bat:  
they sleep in **cocoon-bunks**,  
–woven pockets of root fiber and moss cloth–  
lashed to the underside of the ribs,  
with body and legs wrapped around the root like  
a child clinging to its mother’s neck.

They brace their feet against the world’s immense ribs  
and feel the thunder of the surface above vibrating  
through the bone of their skull.

Quakes, they say, are the world turning over in its sleep.

---

### 4. Time and memory: eating the world’s body

They have no “days” and no candles to mark hours.

Instead, they **mark time by growth**.
