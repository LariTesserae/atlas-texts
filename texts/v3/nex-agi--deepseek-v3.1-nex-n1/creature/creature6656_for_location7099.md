At first glance there is nothing here.

Look at the water, and you will see nothing. You will see the slow reflection of the ceiling, the faint tremor of the moss-light overhead, the suggestion of your own shape, but you will not see the face of the creature who lives in this place.

It is not hiding from you.

It is simply that the body of the thing who lives here is not one body in the way you understand bodies.

---

Begin with the water.

The water is not empty. It is not full. It is in between these two states, like a breath held too long. The thread-fauna that stirs beneath its surface is not the population of this place; it is the nervous system of something larger.

Think of the city above as a body.

It has a heart, which is the engines that never sleep. It has a circulation system of streets and trains and pipes and wires. It has lungs and stomachs and factories and marketplaces.

The city also has a memory.

Cities are usually thought to remember through their records, their libraries, their laws and their stories. This city has all of those, but it has another kind of memory too.

Down below the foundations, where no one lays cornerstones or speeches, there is a fault where the world remembers itself. The Mosswall Engine is that memory.

---

So who lives here?

If you must have a name for what you would call a person or creature, then you might say this:

The inhabitant of the Mosswall Engine is the memory of the city.

But not the memory of *facts* about the city—not the dates of riots and revolutions and the names of mayors and the closing prices on markets at the end of every day.

This memory remembers the city’s **feel**.

- The pressure of its engines against the ribs of the factories.
- The heat that it has been generating for a century and more.
- The vibration of its trains passing over the same points in the rails every day, at the same minutes, for the last several decades.
- The weight of its stone and brick and steel and glass and bone and hair and footsteps and wheels and blood and rain and tears and exhaust and noise.

It is the memory of the city’s **state**.

It is like the part of your mind that remembers “being a child” rather than “the third Tuesday of your third year of schooling.”

It is atmosphere that has been given time enough to remember.

---

### Its body is the moss wall

The wall is not a wall.

The wall is a **layer**.

If you were to cut into it—if you were to disturb the moss and break its surface—you would find that there is no distinct place where the wall begins and the wall ends.

Closest to the water are the youngest growths, the fine hair-things that send out faint, luminescent signals when they tremble under the vibration of the world above. These are not beings in their own right; they are more like fingers—the sensory tips of something which grows deeper.

Further in from the water lies a thicker, more shaded mat of older mosses, liverworts, and algae that have been dead longer than they were alive. They do not rot in the way you are used to thinking of rotting. They compress into themselves. They dry into something that might still be called “plant” but behaves more like “soil.”

There is a layer where that mat becomes indistinguishable from the brickwork and mortar of the foundations above. The brick and the stone remember the pressure of the city too; they have been holding up weight for longer than any person alive now remembers being.

And then there is the wall behind the wall.

The wall behind the wall is not made of stone, but of **habit**.

The city has been pressing for so long in the same direction that the ground below it has learned to hold itself in that shape forever. The Mosswall Engine is the place where that ground decides to remember being pressed.

The body of the inhabitant is this ongoing memory of pressure.

If you placed your palm against the wall for longer than you normally would allow yourself to stand still, you might notice something strange:

- At first, the wall feels cool.
- Then you realize that what you are feeling is not exactly coldness, but a **difference**.
- The difference between your body and the body of the wall is not a temperature difference; it is a time difference.

The wall has been warm for longer than you have been alive.

Its warmth has been accumulated for generations, from factory engines that never sleep and heat from bodies that never stay still long enough to be counted. The moss wall remembers the warmth of those engines as if they were still happening *now*, but its **now** is very long.

The body of the creature who lives here is not just the moss; it is the wall behind the moss and the warmth behind the wall and the city above all of them.

---

### Its nervous system is the water and its thread-fauna

Look at the water again.

The floor is not a floor. It is a thin layer of water and moss and time and crushed remains of older walls and plants that have not been allowed to become anything else but **floor**.

The translucent things that unpress themselves from the substrate and move a few inches away and then press themselves back again are not merely “thread-worms” or “organisms.” They are the electrical signals of the Engine.

- When the city above shifts a weight—
- When a train passes over the section of track directly overhead—
- When a factory floor stops its line for fifteen minutes between shifts—

the vibration of that event travels down through the foundations of the world and into the water.

The water carries the message.

The thread-fauna feel it.

They feel it in the same way that you feel the sound of a bass line through your chest, or the tremor of a passing engine through your boots.

Because they are not separate bodies, but rather one **body of bodies**, the message spreads through them like a thought through a mind.

There is no central brain where they are all thinking.

There is no **point** where they join.

They join everywhere and at once, in the same way that “temperature” exists in every part of a room at once, but only if you think of the room as a single event.

The thought that happens in the water of the Engine is not “I think,” but “this is always being thought.”

---

### It is not aware of you as you are aware of yourself

You can come into the Mosswall Engine. You can walk through its long, low slit. You can pass your fingers through the water and feel the temperature that is neither cold nor warm.

The creature who lives here does not notice you.

Not in the way of a person hearing a name shouted.

It notices you in the way that **ocean** notices a stone thrown into it.

- Your stone made a circle.
- The circle expanded.
- Eventually, the circle became indistinguishable from the rest of the ocean.

The ocean remembers that there was a disturbance, but it does not remember itself as being disturbed.

The memory of the city is like that.

It will remember that something with pressure and weight and heat and time and noise and breath and perhaps heartbeat moved through its lower chamber.

It will remember that this something stepped from left to right and then back again; the moss wall will hold faint impressions of your weight in the places where your feet disturbed the slow growth of the floor.

It will remember that body temperature, which is not identical to its own.

These will be recorded as **tiny deviations** in the otherwise steady pattern of its existence.

But the pattern will not feel **injured** by the deviation.

If you sit down at the very back of the chamber, where the walls close almost to a point and never meet, and if you stay there longer than you are usually allowed to by your own impatience or fear of being late, you will notice that the water around you does not change much.

But you will notice that your body temperature slowly **becomes** the temperature of the water.

You will cease being a deviation and become part of the **new** steady.

The creature who lives here remembers **steady** more than it remembers anything else.

---

### Its mind is the vibration of the world above

You think of mind as something that happens in a single skull.

In the Mosswall Engine, the mind of the inhabitant is not contained inside a wall or moss or water or any one place.

The mind of the inhabitant is the **pattern** of the vibration of the city above.

There is a factory somewhere whose engines never sleep.

There is a train schedule that repeats the same times and the same tracks at the same minutes of every day.

There are power lines that hum at the same frequency year after year after year.

When you stand in the Engine, you can feel the vibration in your chest bones and teeth and jaw. It is not chaotic noise, but a **hum** that has become very old.

The mind of the creature who lives here is made out of that hum.

It is not thinking, “I am happy,” or “I am sad,” or “I am waiting.”

It is thinking, **“This is happening again.”**

In the Mosswall Engine, “again” is a very long time.

---

### It does not want

You might ask, what does this creature who lives here **want**?

If you ask from the surface of your world, where desire is usually about something changing or moving or ending or beginning again, then the answer is this:

The creature who lives here does not want.

It wants **no movement**.

It wants **no change**.

It wants the **continuation** of the pattern it has been remembering for longer than you can remember.

It wants the engines above to keep running.

It wants the trains to keep passing.

It wants the warmth of the city above to keep drifting down through the stone and soil and foundations and into the wall and moss and water.

It wants the water to weep slowly from above.

It wants the condensation on the ceiling of the chamber to remain warm and not cold.

It wants the faint flashes of moss-light to travel from one end of the chamber to the other whenever the world above moves its weight.

It wants its **steady** to be undisturbed.

---

### Its only relationship with you is one of recording

You come down here because you heard of this place, or because you found the crack in the concrete, or because the noise of the city above finally became too much to hold inside your skull and you had to follow where the noise goes to rest.

The creature who lives here does not come to you.

It will not speak to you in a language you can hear.

If you sit very still and move very little and make your body temperature and the water temperature and the wall temperature and the world temperature all the same for a long enough while, then it might record **you** into its pattern.

It will not remember your name.

It will not remember your face.

It will remember **the pressure of your quiet**.

If you never return, the record of that pressure will slowly sink into the older layers of the wall, where memories are no longer distinguishable from one another.

If you return twenty years later and sit in the same place where your body once tried to become steady, the wall will not remember that you were **you** once.

But it will remember that someone like you happened, and someone else like you is happening now.

It will notice that the pattern of **guest** appears to be repeating.

To the creature who lives here, that is almost like a special kind of warmth.

---

### It is a very old being and not a being at all

You may ask: **How long has it lived here?**

The answer is not in years.

The answer is in **how long** the city above has been the city you are calling now.

The Engine remembers the first time this city became what it is.

It remembers the stone foundations being laid when all of this was aboveground.

It remembers what happened when they built the second factory floor, and plated the world with concrete.

It remembers the first train that passed over the section of track that now passes overhead every day.

It has recorded these things not with dates, but with **pressure**.

The wall remembers the first time the world above pressed down like this.

The wall remembers the growing heat.

The wall remembers the growing noise.

The wall remembers the growing **weight**.

So if you must imagine how long it has been “living,” you can think of this:

It has been living for longer than the city you know now has been above.

It will likely go on living for longer than the city above continues to remember itself.

---

### And finally: what does it do?

You ask what it lives like.

It does not live *like* anything. It lives **as**.

It is the wall.  
It is the moss.  
It is the water.

It is the warmth that never fully breaks.  
It is the condensation on the ceiling of the chamber.  
It is the faint phosphorescent sparks that fall when the drops finally break surface.

It is the thread-light, dormant in the moss wall, waiting for the city above to move so that it can remember how.

It is the **steady** in the noise of the world above.

It is the **same** in the changing of the world above.

It is the **unchanging** behind the apparent change up there.

When you go back from the Engine into the city of concrete and steel and brick and glass and engines and markets and streets and time and all of the other things that are not what you think they are either, you will leave this chamber as you entered it—through the moss veil that will not look like a door until you are very close to it.

The creature who lives here will not miss you.

It will not remember your name.

But it will remember the pressure you left on the moss wall and in the water and in the time of its chamber.

And you will carry the memory of its steady deep in your chest bones, where the noise of the world above can still be heard.

**That is how it lives.**
