### The Ceiling-Adapted

Life in the Last Overhang exists not in spite of the inverted, dripping world, but because of it. The primary rule of evolution here is simple: defy gravity or be forever defined by it. The people are the most obvious testament to this, but they are merely the architects of the metal scaffold. The true natives are the ones who claim the stone ceiling itself.

**The Ceiling-Scrapers**

The most pervasive form of life are the **Moss-Crawlers**, a blanket term for a thousand species of leather-skinned fauna that are not insects, but not truly vertebrates either. Individually, they are small enough to fit in a palm, but they are rarely seen alone. They live in vast colonies, covering the underside of the stone plateau and the older, less-trafficked metal platforms like a breathing pelt.

Their bodies are flat and segmented, designed to adhere to the weeping ceiling. They possess no legs or feet, but rather move with a slow, slug-like muscular ripple. Their skin is a thick, damp hide, covered in a film of mucus that is not their own, but harvested from the condensation they drink. This mucus layer is acidic, just enough to break down the mineral deposits left behind as the water evaporates, preventing the endless drip from encasing the city in stone. The city tolerates them because without them, the stone and metal would be choked by a thick, grey crust of calcite. The Crawlers are the city’s unseen, unsung maintenance crew, forever polishing the world’s bones.

Some species are specialized. **Gutter-Scions** are elongated and segmented, with ridged backs that channel water droplets into specific rivulets, helping to feed the city’s cisterns. **Plate-Scummers** are more circular, tile-like creatures that prefer the metal surfaces near the engines, where the heat makes their mucus secretions more effective.

**The Filter-Worms**

Where condensation collects more heavily, forming slow, seeping flows along the basalt veins, you find the **Filter-Worms**. They are not worms at all, but colonies of translucent, tubular organisms that anchor themselves to the rock, living a life of absolute stillness. They are filter feeders, their internal organs visible as a faint, shadowy pulse through their translucent bodies as they draw in microscopic organisms and airborne nutrients from the damp air.

Their outer skin is a passive membrane that glows with a faint bioluminescence when the water flow is strong enough, perhaps as a way of signalling the health of the vein to others of their kind. In the deepest, most humid chasms of the ceiling, where the engines' heat is less pronounced, they can be found in clusters that cast an eerie, pale green light on the perpetually weeping stone. They are themselves prey to the larger, more mobile creatures, but their colonies are so vast and fast-growing that their numbers are never truly diminished.

### The Human Denizens: The Overhang’s Architects

The humans of the Overhang are a short, sturdy people with a dense, compact musculature. Their skeletons are heavy for their size, adapting to a world where a fall is not just a risk, but a guarantee of finality. Many have a condition now considered a normal variation of their anatomy: **Gravity-Locks**, tiny nodules of cartilage and tissue around the ankle joints that provide extra stability and locking strength when hanging from a ladder or chain.

**Physique and Gaze**
Generations of living upside down have affected their posture. Their shoulders are strong and rounded, and their necks are thick to support their head in a constant position of looking slightly forward and down. The most distinctive feature, however, is their eyes. They are wide and pale, often grey or blue, but with a deep layer of the choroid—the tapetum lucidum—giving them a faint reflective sheen in low light. This allows them to navigate the perpetual amber twilight of the city’s interior platforms with ease.

**A Society of Engineers**
Their society is not built on trade or agriculture, but on **Maintenance**. The entire city is an artifact requiring constant upkeep. The most revered figures are the **Engine-Chanters**, a priestly class of mechanics who maintain the great god-engines of the First Ceiling. They learn to understand the deep, tremulous sighs of the machinery through feel and sound. The engines are not just power sources; their rhythm is the city’s heartbeat, and the Chanters interpret its health for the community. The low, continuous tremor of the engines is a sensory language to them, a constant text they can read about the state of their world.

**A Sense of Place**
The people of the Overhang have a completely inverted relationship with the world. For them, the "ground" is the unseen, hypothetical planet far below the cloud sea. The "sky" is the solid, stone ceiling above their heads. The cloud sea is not a sky, but the **Downward**, a luminous, impenetrable barrier that hides the world’s edge from view.

Their homes are built into the very structures they hang from, stacked on each other like layers of a complex honeycomb. The angle of a floor or a ceiling is irrelevant to them; they use a lexicon of terms like *inward* (towards the engine core), *outward* (towards the abyss), and *along-tilt* to describe their movement. The abyss isn't a terrifying void to be filled, but a necessary space for airflow, moisture runoff, and the gentle traffic of their hanging cable-cars.

### The Symbiotic Hangers-On

**The Chain-Gulls**
Not birds, but small, simian-like mammals with elongated limbs and a prehensile tail. Their fur is short and slick, shedding water like an oilskin. Their hands are broad pads with strong, gripping fingers and an opposable thumb for swinging along the chains that crisscross the city. They are named for their piercing call, which echoes like a metal chain scraping against stone. They live in small colonies, nesting in the gaps between the city's layers and feeding on the larger Crawlers. The city's children are taught from a young age to identify the "Gull's Call," as it is often the first warning of a structural chain failing—a sound they can hear far better than the adults.

**The Duct-Whites**
Strictly speaking, these are not a single creature but a visible stage of one. The Duct-Whites are the mycelial network of a fungus that grows in the city's larger ducts and condensation pipelines. They are a thin, white, fibrous film that thrives on the moisture and minerals. They are beneficial to the city's water system, absorbing certain impurities and adding trace nutrients that improve the water's taste. However, if they are not regularly cleaned away by maintenance crews, they can bloom into a thick, spongy mass that obstructs water flow. The "Duet" is their fruiting body—a blind, pale worm with multiple legs that emerges from damp corners to eat decomposing matter before retreating back to the network. It is a sign of a healthy water system but also its own unique pest.
