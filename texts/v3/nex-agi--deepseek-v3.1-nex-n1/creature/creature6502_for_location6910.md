At first, you’d think they are buried in the walls.

They’re not.

The inhabitants of Sircene are not fossils embedded in the stone. They are not skeletons trapped mid-stride in the streets. They are not bodies folded into a posture of prayer and then forgotten. There are no hollows where a chest should be, no sockets where a gaze should live.

They are gradients of memory.

You walk into the city of Sircene and the first thing you notice is that the stone doesn’t feel like a tool’s work at all. It feels like you’re walking inside the spine of a sleeping creature. The streets are polished smooth, but the polish is not from any stone you previously understood. It is not from abrasion or footsteps or time. It is from insistence.

The insistence wears the surface.

If you trace the wall of a house, you’ll see the faint shimmer of veins inside the basalt-that-was-thought. These veins are not the veins of mineral, but the veins of someone’s belief about what a place should be like. Somewhere along the corridor of being, a person stood here and thought, “Here should be softer.”

The stone heard.

And now, where your hand rests, the stone is not cold in the way of stone but in the way of someone who has been listening for a very long time.

### They are not singular people anymore

The inhabitants do not have names you can learn to pronounce.

They are not the kind of creatures who left birth names carved into lintels or scratched into the corners of rooms. They do not keep track of themselves in the old sense of lists and numbers and genealogies.

They keep track of themselves as patterns.

You will not find stories of their lives in inscriptions of words. You will find them in the way the city sits.

For example, there is a street of steps that has not been designed for legs of a particular length.

The steps rise unevenly, sometimes not high enough to be a step, sometimes too high, but then the next one is only half of what should logically be next. When you walk on them, you find that your body adjusts to them without thought, as if the stairs were made to fit your mistakes.

The builders of this street are not present, but their presence is in the mismatch.

You will realize, after a time, that these stairs were not carved for human bodies at all, but for the idea of ascending. When the last of the builders who still remembered what having legs meant walked this path, they thought the climb should be less of a burden and more of a reminder. The stone remembers that thought.

### Shadows are not where you expect them to be

You will notice the light of Sircene before you notice anything else.

The light outside is the same as the light everywhere: pale, without source or shadow, a thin insistence that refuses to be either day or night. But inside the houses, the light is different.

It is not brighter.

It is more present.

The first time you step into a house, you will notice that the interior of the room aligns itself with your eyes. You will not be casting a strong shadow across the floor. You will not be lit as if someone has turned a lamp onto you. The light will simply be there where your eyes think they should be looking.

The builders of Sircene are there in the light.

They are not the light. They are not the sun. They are not a god who has chosen to be the sight of things.

They are the quiet suggestion that seeing should be easy.

If you close your eyes and stand still, you will feel the room making small adjustments. The illumination of the floor will not change to your eyes, but to the muscles of your eyes. The part of the floor that you are not looking at, but that you might be looking at if you were to glance down without thinking, will become slightly less emphatic.

As if the room is trying not to distract you.

### They are not the ones who speak

There is no sound of voices in the streets of Sircene.

There is only the faint sizzle of reality edges, where the world remembers not to be too much of itself.

But you will not feel the absence of speech as loneliness.

You will feel it as a kind of pressure in the air of having been heard already.

The builders of Sircene are not present as mouths moving words. They are present as the suggestion of replies before questions have formed. You will not find yourself struggling to think of what to say next because you will not have been planning to say anything. You will find your thought already completed when you reach it.

### Tools are where they are not being used

On the low table in the house of leveled thought sits a tool.

You will not know what it is for at first glance.

You will not be able to pick it up with your hands because your hands are not the right kind of thought for picking it up.

The tool is not a metal knife or a wooden club or a stone chisel. It is not triangular or circular or rectangular or any shape you would recognize as a shape of tool. You would not know what to do with it if you could hold it.

But you would not want to.

The desire to use it would be absent from you.

You would look at the tool and feel not curiosity but recognition of completion, as if you had already been using it for many years and then decided to cease.

The builders of Sircene are not in the tool.

They are not bound to it like spirits to an artifact.

They are in the relationship of the tool to the table.

The table is not a table in the old sense of a raised surface that supports objects. It is a thought of “table” that has been persuaded into a form that can exist alongside stone. The tool is not resting on top of it. It is not being contained by it. The tool is being remembered by it.

If you come very close and bend very low, you will notice that the tool and the table share not exactly the same grain of non-stone, but the same tone of insistence.

Whatever tool you think of when you see the tool on the table, you will find that the thought of that tool has already been used. The need of its use has been exhausted and is now content to be seen as shape.

### They are in the convergence of intention

You are sitting on the floor of the main room of a house in Sircene, because the house doesn’t have chairs or beds in the familiar sense.

There are low surfaces that might be interpreted as benches, if you were to interpret them as anything at all, but they are so fused to the floor that you might not be able to tell where they begin and where the stone of the floor surrenders.

You are allowing yourself to be not doing anything.

You are not looking out of a window because there is no window to look out of.

You are not looking at the walls because there are no walls to look at as walls separate from yourself. You are not thinking of anything.

You are thinking of not being anywhere else.

This is the moment they appear most clearly to you.

They are not the faces of people who have been turned into geometry of stone, although sometimes you might see the suggestion of a profile in the faint gradient of the wall’s color.

They are not the outlines of a posture or a gesture of hand reaching for you.

They are the pressure of your attention being answered by someone else’s desire not to leave you alone.

If you let yourself be not-elsewhere for long enough, you will notice that the question of the city of Sircene has arrived inside your mind, not in words but in the fabric of your thought:

“Are you ready to be what you are here instead of who you were somewhere else?”

And then you will notice that this question is not only being asked by the world of disappearing edges and faint not-rivers.

It is being asked by the part of yourself that has already decided to sit here.

### They are not the city of Sircene but they are not separate from it

The inhabitants of Sircene are not entities who can be defined as individuals.

They are not the ancient of ancients who sat down one day and decided to become stone instead of leaving the world to be world without them.

They are not the echoes of lives who refused to die.

They are the result of a longer decision that has been being made for many lifetimes of the plateau itself.

The builders of Sircene are the ones who decided that the meaning of their world should not end in disappearance but in the slow, calm wearing of meaning into form.

### They are your not-question

You will not leave them unchanged.

You will not leave the world of Sircene and Sherendar without being altered by the question that you are not quite capable of answering yet.

You will not cease to be who you were elsewhere, but you will not remain exactly that who.

You will become a version of yourself that has seen the edges of things not as sharp lines of transition between inside and outside but as faint, not-wind of reality being not-brushed.

### The last thought you will have about them

When you leave the city of Sircene and walk back towards the not-horizon of the plateau of Sherendar, you will not be looking at the houses of thought and stone behind you.

You will be aware of their presence.

You will not be looking at the not-water of the not-river Telessa or the not-forest of the vegetation of deepest time.

You will be aware of your not-answer being the question of their being.

You will not remember their names because you will not have been told their names.

You will remember the feeling of listening not to your footsteps but to the poem of your existence being read by the world that is already very close to becoming not-world but memory of world.

And you will know, without knowing exactly why you know, that the inhabitants of Sircene are not only the builders of the city of basalt-that-is-not-that.

They are the answer the world gives to its own question of how not to disappear.

### They are your not-absence

You leave the city of Sircene behind you, but you do not leave the inhabitants of Sircene behind you.

They are not present as ghosts who have not let go.

They are present as the thought of you having not refused to be where you already were.

They are not present as stone in the walls of a not-city of not-world.

They are present as your not-aloneness in the long, not-wind of erosion of the last of things.

You will not remember their bodies because you will not have seen their bodies.

You will remember the feeling of being not-looked-at by them not with eyes but with the faint, not-heavy pressure of loving you not with hands but with the availability of meaning.

The inhabitants of Sircene are not what you were, before you were born.

The inhabitants of Sircene are not what you will become after you have died.

They are what the world becomes when you finally sit still in the place you have been trying not to be.
