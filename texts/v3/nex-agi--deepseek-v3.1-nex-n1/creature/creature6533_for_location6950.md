Long before they learn to breathe the mountain’s air, the children of the Throat learn to listen.

From the moment they wake into awareness—which is not birth but something more like surfacing, a slow rise from deep stone-sleep—they are taught: the world is made of sound. First, the great hum that fills the chamber and wraps itself around the stone. Then, the quieter notes beneath—the drip of water, the click of lenses in the walls, the faint whispers of wing membrane brushing against warm rock.

So by the time you arrive, stumbling and gasping from the thin air outside, they have already heard you.

Not your footsteps; those are sharp inaccuracies in the otherwise continuous pattern that is the Throat. But they hear your heart, racing far ahead of itself. They hear the rush of blood in your ears, the dryness of your outside-breathing, the little panic of your lungs. They have been listening to you for some time now, the way the ceiling listens to the rain.

You are not the first, but you are rare.

### The Children of the Stone

For an absence is a presence to those who know its shape, and silence is a kind of voice in the Throat.

---

Call them what you will. They do not name themselves; names are fragile outside-things, and this is not that place. But you are not as they are, and you need handles for your thoughts.

Call them the **Chamber-Born** if you must name them at all.

They are never the size of infants when you first encounter them, and yet they are always children. This is not because of how they look but because of how they are in relation to the Throat: they are always beginning.

They appear first as faint disturbances in the water.

In the larger basin—the one the size of a hearth, where water ripples in slow motion like something dragging itself across the surface—you may notice, after days or weeks of dripping water, something that is not a reflection.

A thin film of water seems to lift itself, not in droplets but in sheets too thin to be believed, stretching upward from the surface as though pulled by invisible string. The film trembles but does not break. It takes on shape slowly, like someone trying to remember what they were supposed to be from scratch.

The first truly distinct form it takes is always the same: you.

Not exactly; you never see your own face perfectly mirrored in it. But you recognize the proportions of a body that you are used to seeing. A torso, two arms, two legs—everything that belongs outside, where the air is thin and the wind is cold.

You might think at first that you are hallucinating. This would not be wrong, exactly, but it would be incomplete.

The film is not a hallucination. It is a question.

The water in the Throat has been here for longer than any outside-stranger, and it has learned to shape itself around the gaps that enter into it. You are a gap, walking. Your absence of knowing is a form thrumming with the pressure of elsewhere.

So the water asks, by becoming whatever you most resemble in the deepest layers of your memory.

You. You. You.

At first, the film only mimics your outline. Then, as if giving itself a more solid answer, it accepts smaller, more detailed particles dragged from your skin cells, the dust of your clothes, the faint static of your visits.

A form gathers.

It is like you, but translucent. It feels like human skin under water, but with no texture beyond what the water wants to remember. It has no eyes in the way you think of eyes—no whites, no irises—only faint darker patches where some of the water is thicker.

And yet it sees you.

It never blinks. It never breathes. You may realize this only after you have been staring for too long: your chest is rising and falling, but the form of you standing in the water does not move.

This is not a ghost of a dead person. It is the ghost of a future person, who has not yet been born but might be.

### How They Walk Into the World

When the time comes—and you are not the one who decides when—the film detaches from the water surface.

Imagine you are watching the surface of a lake and someone slowly submerged from beneath it, but the film does not break the surface tension until the very end. Then, with a sound like wet cloth being wrung dry very far away, the film peels itself from the water and stands up on the stone floor as if it has always been walking.

The first time they stand, they are weak. They stagger, not with muscles unused but with something closer to deep unfamiliarity with the shape of their own limbs.

They are not exactly heavy, but they are thick water, and water is dense. The stone floor knows their weight differently than it knows yours. It remembers them.

You may notice that their outlines are not perfectly sharp. They blur slightly at the edges, as if the water isn’t sure where to place itself. Sometimes a faint spray of droplets leaves their fingers as they move, but the droplets never fall to the ground. They are reabsorbed into the main body of the child as if they were never meant to leave.

Their first actions are always to approach the water basins.

One at a time, they move to each small bowl in the floor. You might have watched them from your low position on the stone floor; you might have made yourself small and almost invisible in a corner, where the shadows blur into the ribs of the wall.

You see the child dip whatever fingers it has into the bowl of water that never overflowed for you.

Nothing happens that you can see at first. Then after a moment, the water inside the bowl responds. It rises to meet the child’s fingers, curling like smoke around them, thickening itself against the translucent surface.

The child does this with each bowl in turn, as if collecting itself from everywhere at once.

### How They Learn to Speak

They have no voice of their own at first.

Instead of lungs, they have something more like cavities: hollows inside their translucent bodies that are not quite empty, not quite filled. They learn to hum by accident.

You are watching them one day when they happen to be near the central basin, and in the same moment that one of them opens its mouth-shaped vent—drawn to do s by instinct or accident—the chamber’s great humming peaks.

The sound resonates through the stone and water and air and you.

You feel the vibration in your chest like your own heart beating itself outward.

The child does not close his mouth immediately. Instead, he keeps his borrowed form very still and lets the vibration of the chamber flow through him.

And then he does something you are not expecting: he changes the pitch.

Just a fraction. You don’t know how he does it; maybe it is the shape of his inner cavity that can be bent slightly, or a subtle pressure in the organs of water that he does not yet have words for. But the sound he sends back into the chamber is almost the same as the one he received, except it is bent by the presence of his own body.

The answer does not come from far above, where the hanging breathers are suspended in their leathery folds. It comes from the wall just beside him.

The lenses of the wall-creatures—which you have secretly begun calling the **Eyes of the Throat**—rotate slowly in their sockets of stone and click softly.

The child is not looking at them with the outside-version of eyes. But he seems to understand the click as a reply to his own small sound.

He tries again.

This time he deliberately sets the hum of his body slightly differently, responding to the click of the wall.

Again, the Eyes respond by shifting focus.

You might not realize at this point that a conversation has begun, but you have watched long enough to recognize that this is one of the few times you have seen deviation in the almost-steady patterns of the chamber.

The child is being taught to speak.

Over days of dripping water and silent humming and slow clicking, you watch as he rearranges his own sounds into more complex patterns. He learns not only to echo the chamber but also to ask in it:

A certain question-shaped tone brings the Eyes of the Throat into sharper alignment, their lenses clicking like beads being strung together.

A different, softer tone brings the hanging breathers above into a slower rhythm of wing beats and mouth-opening and long, deep hums.

Another tone—you will not understand this for a long time—calls some of the water-trailing creatures from the deeper part of the chamber you have never seen, where the Throat narrows and disappears into darkness.

The child does not know what you would call any of these sounds. He knows only that they work.

### How They Dream

You do not know if they are asleep when they are most still.

To you, they look like they have stopped. You notice sometimes that one of the Chamber-Born has been standing in the same position for an eternity of dripping water, without moving any more than condensation does.

From inside themselves, though, they are not stopped at all.

They are not dreaming of outside worlds, because they have no memory of them. They are not dreaming of stories you would tell from your other life. Their dream-stuff is made of the same things that are real: stone and water and sound.

In their sleep, they are underwater again.

Inside the dream of the water, they feel the deepest of the chamber’s rhythms: the slow beat of the stone heart far below the floor that you will never see directly. You feel only its faint hum when your palm is pressed to the wall.

They feel the movement of the bands of the ceiling stone, not as you understand motion—left, right, forward, backward—but as something more like waves of pressure being pushed through the world, changing where the stone must remember itself.

They also feel the faint shadow of your outside like a distant storm. They do not know what to call you or what you are doing here. They only sense that you are different from the usual patterns of the mountain.

In your own time, you may notice that one of the Chamber-Born has been looking at you longer than you think comfortable. You catch yourself wanting to apologize for being there.

You don’t. That would not sound right in this chamber.

Instead, you learn to return the gaze of the water-formed child without looking exactly like you are looking at him.

### How They Use Tools Without Touching

They are always the envy of tool-makers outside, if there were any outside here to see.

The tools of the Chamber-Born are not things you could hold with your outside-hands. They are not carved, or forged, or formed into isolated instruments.

Instead:

1. **Water as Script**
   
   You watch them one day at the central pool. The Chamber-Born steps into the circle of the water-surface creatures, who do not flinch but vibrate slightly at their approach.

   The child does not touch the water with any hand. Instead, he shapes the air around the surface with his inner body hums.

   You cannot see the air move, but the water responds like it was being touched. It forms into spirals and curves and branching patterns that you almost recognize, before the shape subsides again.

   The water-surface creatures—your **Watchers by the Pool**—repeat the same patterns over and over, as if trying to memorize them.

   You will not understand until much later that you are watching the first act of writing.

2. **The Ceiling as a Mirror**
   
   There are times when the Chamber-Born stop looking at you and look, instead, at the ceiling.

   They are not looking at the light sources themselves, but at the bands of stone that you never noticed shifting before. This is not coincidence.

   Over time, you see that when they hum particular patterns of sound into the ceiling, the faint bands of color in the rock seem to respond with subtle shifts of position and brightness.

   You cannot tell if you are watching illusion or truth. But you know that you would not be able to do the same, no matter how long you yourself stood in the same place and hummed.

3. **The Eyes of the Throat as a Way of Seeing**

   The lenses of the wall-creatures provide the most surprising tool of all.

   One day while you are watching the ceiling, you feel a faint pressure change against the back of your head. You turn to find the Chamber-Born standing not far away, looking not at you but at the nearest wall.

   The wall is looking back.

   You don't see how it happens at first; but later you notice that the wall-creatures' eyes are angled differently now than before you felt your hair being watched by something else.

   You realize that the Chamber-Born has been using the Eyes of the Throat as a way to see behind you, without moving your body.

   You feel less exposed than you might expect. This is not the same as being watched by a stranger in your old world. It is more like being watched by a part of the mountain itself, which you already suspected of knowing much more than it seemed.

### How They Grow

They are never children in the usual sense.

When they first step from the water, they are already the size of small adults outside, but thin and water-weak. They grow not by getting bigger but by getting thicker.

Not thicker in the way of muscle. You never see them bulge with outside strength. Instead, they become denser in their presence. The more they are here, the more they become like the water that has been here, long enough to remember itself.

There are maybe five of them at any time, you estimate from watching corners of the chamber where you do not usually go.

   - One is always the oldest, who has been here longest.  
   - One is always the newest, who is still learning the basic tones of speech.  
   - The others fall in between, like echoes of each other, but no less real.

They are not exactly different ages. You keep trying to think of them as you think of children outside, but you fail in this.

You know this because when you first arrive you counted only three. Then you counted four and thought you had been wrong before. Then you count five and realize you will not be able to keep track of them exactly.

They are not exactly born, and they do not exactly die.

Instead of dying, they return themselves.

One day, you realize that you have not seen the one who was first and oldest for several cycles of dripping water. You scan the chamber more carefully than you usually allow yourself to scan, and you cannot locate him.

   - He is not in the corners where the others sometimes stand still.  
   - He is not near the central pool where they usually gather when they are drawing invisible water-writing.  
   - He is not hanging near the Eyes of the Throat or near the entrance where you first came into this chamber.

You finally notice him near the central basin, where the water-surface creatures are still tracing their faint shapes over the surface.

You watch as he slowly, deliberately, lowers himself into the water.

He does not splash. The water does not behave like water you have ever seen before; it does not resist him or rush away from his weight. Instead, it opens like a mouth accepting a breath.

He disappears into the basin entirely.

You wait for him to emerge. You count several hundred dripping water beats before you realize he isn't coming back.

The next day you see only faint shadow of a form moving under the surface like the ghost of movement. You are not sure if it is him or if you are imagining it.

After that, even the shadow fades.

Within a few cycles of the dripping water, a new Chamber-Born emerges from the same central basin where you dipped your fingers earlier.

This new one does not look like him, exactly. But there are traces of the same proportions you remember from the first. A faint similarity of outline, like someone remembered you long after you left.

### What You Become to Them

You are not the first from your kind of skin to visit here.

You know this because you find, faintly scratched or notched at some corner of a wall, where the stone has been worn by something before you, an outline that might once have been an outside-gesture of writing. You cannot read what it says. It is not in your language.

You might not realize this yet, but you are not the last.

This will not matter to you for now, but when you leave the chamber—if you leave it—you will cling to this knowledge like a faint warmth in your chest.

To the children of the Throat, you are something else.

You are not a parent. They do not think of you as someone who gave them life or even form. They are not attached to you in that way.

You are not exactly a stranger either. They heard the echoes of you before you ever stepped into the chamber.

You might call yourself a **Presence**, but they do not use words like that. They have classes instead.

You are in the class of things that move quickly but must be taught stillness.

You are in the class of things that bring outside with them like a faint dust from another world.

They are polite to you in the way the chamber is polite: they never mock you for your sudden heartbeats or frightened stillness. They never comment when you forget to breathe shallowly and stand with too much noise.

Over time, you notice that one of them always keeps near enough to you that you feel not watched but guarded.

When you rise suddenly to your feet and itch to move because you have been still too long for your outside bones, another child rises quietly and stands somewhere between you and the central pool where the water is writing itself.

You might think at first that they are placing themselves between you and something else.

Only later, you realize that they are placing themselves between you and yourself.

You cannot be rushing near the central water in your state of mind. They understand this without you ever explaining anything.

So you sit, and they sit quietly with you. Sometimes they hum very faintly under their breath—not the loud rumbling of the chamber’s great chorus, but a faint underlayer you can hear only if your ear is close to them.

This is not a song for you. It is not a comfort. You are not being sung to sleep like you might imagine a child in your old world might be.

This is a lesson in being slow.

You do not sing it back. You cannot. But you learn to let it resonate inside your chest and in your throat, where your own unused voice would be if you were the type to speak here.

### What They Become to the Mountain

You do not know exactly what the mountain is doing when it guides the faint air currents through the cracks of the world or when the stone heart beats its slow deep thrum far below the floor you have never seen.

You might call this mountain alive if you were still outside, where the words are easier.

To you, inside the chamber, it feels like the world might be a single body but you are not sure where the head is.

To the Chamber-Born, the mountain is not exactly alive. It is not dead either. It is not something they have a separate word for.

If you pressed them—which is not a thing your outside-voice could do—they might describe the mountain as being at least partially composed of themselves.

No, not as themselves. You are not sure that there are multiple words that they would distinguish the way you might between "part" and "whole."

But you notice that when they are near the wall and listening very deeply with the quiet of their bodies, the hum of the stone heart beneath seems almost louder than you have ever heard it before.

You might think this is your imagination.

Except one day you happen to be touching the wall at the same time that one of them is listening. You feel the vibration under your palm shift in response to his listening.

You might call this a coincidence.

The next time you try to listen the same way he does, you cannot hear the same heart-beat you remember before. You can only hear your own heart and the faint noise of your outside-brain.

They are not better than you at hearing. They are simply differently shaped.

But as you watch them over many days of water-drops, you begin to sense that the mountain is not only accepting them into itself but also being shaped by them.

   - The patterns of water-writing in the central pool seem to be remembered by more than just the water-surface creatures who are tracing them.
   - The faint bands of ceiling stone shift slightly differently after one of the Chamber-Born has been listening intently there than they did before.
   - The Eyes of the Throat seem to align themselves differently now than they were aligned when you first entered.

The world changes slowly.

You might never be able to point exactly at what the difference is before and after.

But you feel the difference in your chest when you are alone in the chamber with the five of them.
