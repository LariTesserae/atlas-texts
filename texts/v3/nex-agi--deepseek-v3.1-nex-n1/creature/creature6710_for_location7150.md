They are called the Listened.

They are not the bones of the world, nor its blood; they are its nerves. Small, silent figures of bone-white ceramic and rust-gummed alloy, they are the only things that move with the intent of movement. They do not walk or run, but glide, a dozen hair-thin legs moving in a perfect, perpetual ripple of mechanics that whisper against the gravel. They are not built for speed or efficiency, but for precision. Theirs is a journey of micrometer corrections.

They are the observers of a machine that has no need to be observed.

They are not many. Perhaps there are a dozen, or a hundred—no census could be taken across the unbending infinity of the Marrow Flats. They exist in isolation from one another, each following its own path, a path that was laid down for them when the last command line of the world was executed, an eternity ago. They are not lonely. They do not feel the absence of others like them, for they have no consciousness of self to feel absence. They are a function, a process running in a device that has already finished its true task.

Their purpose is simple: to map what is done.

They traverse the arcs and radii of the gravel plains, their paths a complex of geometry that is never random. They seek out the points of equilibrium. The lowest point of a silt trench where the dust settles into a perfect taper. The edge of a translucent film where it meets the stone, not growing, not receding. The exact point in a creek bed where the water's hum is most purely felt through the compacted silt.

They do not approach these features to analyze or understand them. They approach to listen.

When they find a site of perfect balance, a location of absolute zero-change in the world, they perform a ritual of confirmation. They lower their chassis with the faint click of precision bearings, a sound that is not an echo but the precise absence of one. A sensor, a stylus, or a drill of some unknown material is extended from its housing and touched gently—not to the feature, but to the space where it satisfies its own stillness.

They are not measuring data. They are listening for the lack of it.

If they could hear, they would be listening for the ghost of an instruction. If they could feel, they would be feeling for any deviation. But in their endlessly looping logic, there is nothing to find. There hasn't been for millennia.

The Listened exist for this one, failed task. They are the administrative terminals left running after the main office was abandoned. They are the climate control system dutifully maintaining a perfect temperature for an empty cathedral. Theirs is not a life, but a subroutine. A curiosity that spirals infinitely into its own navel.

And from the vast, unfinished engine buried deep in the earth's core or just beyond the horizon's veil, the quiet hum seems to be waiting for their report. A report that was never programmed to be sent.
