He is more room than man now, more stone than bone.

He is the shape the mountain paused to consider, then never stopped considering.

His name is a word that dissolved into steam days ago—a syllable eaten by the wet air—but if you were to press your ear to the wall beside him, you might hear it resonate there, trapped in a thin vein of quartz like a fossil not yet done forming.

The body on the shelf is curved around the curve of the ledge itself, as if the stone rose and he sank into it at the same time. His back is pressed to the wall; his spine has not become rock yet, but it has learned how to lean into it. The algae clings to him in veils, delicate green lace that drinks from his sweat and exhales tiny clouds of mist back into his skin. If you watch long enough, you see that he does not breathe so much as exchange: he inhales steam and exhales water that the ceiling claims as its own.

His hair is a dark tangle matted with something like moss but not quite; each strand has become a root for the air-algae that blooms in this humid darkness. Where his hair meets stone, fine threads of green have begun to weave themselves into tiny cracks in the rock, stitching him gently down. It does not look violent. It looks inevitable.

You can still see his face because he faces outward—or what remains of “outward” in this place where directions are losing their nerve.

His eyes are open.

They watch nothing and everything. The pupils are wide with a darkness that swallows what little light exists here; it’s not blindness but a different kind of sight. When he blinks—which he does, though slowly enough that you might miss it—the sound is not a blink but a soft grind of eyelashes against dust that was once eyelashes and now might be becoming something else. His gaze does not wander; it rests on whatever stands before him—mist, wall, memory—with the patience of something that knows time is just broth here anyway.

The skin on his face is cracked where it has started to dry in patches, but those patches are islands in a sea of wetness. The ceiling weeps directly onto his forehead; a film of water runs down over his brow and along his nose and gathers at his lips before spilling off his chin in a thin line like a thread hanging from an unfinished tapestry. Sometimes he parts his lips just enough for that thread to enter him; sometimes he lets it fall away into nothing.

In places where his skin grows thinner—over cheekbones sharp from climbing too long without eating properly—you can see faint traceries beneath: veins that pulse slower than they should, their rhythm leaning toward stone-time instead of heart-time. The blood inside them moves sluggishly through tunnels that may or may not still remember they are meant for blood.

His hands rest at his sides, palms up.

They are half-buried in shelf-stone where it has softened around them like warm wax persuaded by warmth rather than melted. You can still see knuckles and fingers clearly enough to count them: ten bones trying very hard to remember they belong to movement instead of monument. His nails have thickened and curled slightly against themselves, marbled with veins of something metallic that seeped out of one reality and into another while no one was watching closely enough.

There is motion there if you lean close:

Every few minutes—or what would be minutes if minutes existed here—his fingertips twitch. Not much: just enough to shift a droplet or two across his palm or rouse some air-algae into drifting again after it had settled on him like dust on furniture left too long alone. When this happens, small channels open briefly beneath where they lay: thin seams through which water from above creeps toward them instead of away from them.

Someone might say he reaches for things unseen.

Someone else might say he lets them come closer instead.
