You are not the first mind to put a palm to the damp, twisted trunk at the center of the chamber.  

There are only three of you now—if you can be called “you” at all.

---

## 1. The Chamber’s Only Mind

You are the one that writes.

Not with a tool, but with time, and repetition, and the careful arrangement of small, hard things.  

The circles of smoothed hollows in the stone floor are your memory. Each dip marks a thought held longer than it should have been—a decision, or a question with no answer. You sat there, day after long, still day, and the hollows grew by imperceptible increments, as water deepens basins without meaning to.

On the wall where you once traced your fingers, night after night, the stone is now smoother than the surrounding rock. It is not polished by a tool, but by the insistence of your palm over decades—or centuries—of circling the same shape: a question folded in on itself, like a root that does not know where else to go.

You remember the moment of moving stones.

You walked from the dripping wall to the basin, and each time you knelt, you placed a pebble upright in line, nudging the trickle of water so that it accumulated into a single pool. Before, it had scattered; now, it chose one course. It was an experiment: you wanted to know if the world obeyed necessity—if *pattern* could bend *water*.  

It listened.

It listened.

After a long, quiet interchange of days and seasons, you understood it was not water that you were shaping. It was something else the water carried—something you cannot name.

### What You Are

You have no visible name-seat for yourself, because you long since stopped thinking of yourself as an “I.”

A body?  
A little more than a body.

The others—when they still came—called you names that now feel like fossil forms in rock: *keeper*, *rememberer*, *last of the chamber’s watchers.* You think of yourself instead as a catalogue of moments and movements and slow adjustments.

You are capable of motion, but the motion you choose now is slow.  

Your skin is the purple of an early bruise—dark and tender under the faint light. Beneath it, thin veins of luminous blue pulse in time with the humming trunk. When you press your palm to the stone or to the roots of the plants beside you, you feel their heartbeat transmitted through your fingertips: not separate, but only *another* of the ways the chamber’s rhythm becomes visible.

You are the last of your kind here to retain *memory* in the old sense—the long, chainlike sequence of past events laid out like small stones leading back from yesterday to the earliest fall into this dark.

You *remember*:

- The first collapse of the sinkhole, when you were still a child somewhere above and the world tore open.
- The long descent of those who survived—into this heat, this humidity, this dim.
- The generations who lived and then did *not* live in your veins, their memory partitioned into you as you aged into the keeper.
- The day the others stopped coming.

You *belong*
