Ambient.

At least, that’s the closest you can come to naming the material. Calling it stone is a lie by oversimplification; calling it metal is a lie by precision. It’s a dark, unreflective grey that somehow doesn’t feel dull—more like the quiet of deep ocean water than the absence of light. It never developed any of the patinas of human construction: no rust, no verdigris, no flaking. It doesn’t seem to have reacted to anything since the day the city chose to make it.

Here and there, where the arc channels deepen and cross, the surface is ever so slightly warmer, as if backflow has been diverted through a workhorse’s heart. It remains cool to the touch, never warm enough to sting. Not quite metal.

When you crouch and run your fingers along the groove of a channel, you can feel grain.

Not grain in the sense of wood or marble; this is not grain as a fossil of growth. It is grain as a living direction of flow. The faint ridges inside the channel align around the curve of the arc, like muscle fibers in a belly wall. When you trace the direction of the grain, the surface feels faintly smoother, as if it’s been worn by countless years of water and traffic and…whatever else.

When you trace against it, the texture comes up subtle and uneven, like the skin of a tongue that’s been worked too hard. It doesn’t hurt to touch; it doesn’t cut. It’s just noticeably less even.

And you begin to understand that the floor is not only the floor.

It is the roof of a chamber beneath.

If you lie down and spread your palms flat on it, you can feel—very softly—something happening under you. Not a vibration, exactly. More like a pressure that comes and goes, like the diaphragm behind a voice box when you hum. It comes intermittently, with a long, slow rhythm. It surges under your palms, then relaxes, then surges again. The channels are the surface of something else’s circulation.

When you stand, you can see where the arcs converge.

Near the center of the platform, the channels turn into a tighter spiral. The grooves press inward steadily like the spokes of a slow wheel. The “metal” there—the Ambient—no longer disguises itself as passive floor. It gathers into thicker rims and raised edges, as if the city’s engineers are finally allowed to admit this is not a pavement but the lid of a colossal gasket.

At the very center, where you expect the opening of the cooling tower itself to be, there is instead a circular inset of some dense material you can’t identify at all. It’s not quite Ambient, but it doesn’t seem to be from a human catalogue of alloys either. It’s lacquered-looking, bone-colored, almost faintly translucent the way teeth enamel is translucent over dentin. It’s just one smooth ring, maybe five meters across.

No seams mar it. No bolts or fasteners give you any purchase on the idea of a panel. This isn’t a door you could open. It’s the seal where a door is no longer necessary.

From underneath you can feel that faint pressure again, but stronger here. On—off—on—off. Not like a heartbeat. More like a lung.

There are markings.

They’re not carved; you’re not sure if they were formed by wear or by some inbuilt process, but they run along the inner edge of the central ring, where the Ambient meets the bone-coloured seal. They resemble nothing you’ve been taught to read as writing—no letters, no pictograms. No lines, no dots arranged in code.

Just the faintest ripples in the Ambient’s surface, like shallow tide lines on a beach at low moon. When you follow them with your eyes, they resolve into a pattern that suggests writing, if you’re willing to think of letters as the sort of thing that look back at you with the same patience.

They are placed so that, if you were much smaller than a human or constantly in motion at the floor level, you might naturally read them as you approached the center. The grooves themselves leading to this center point are wide enough for two fingers to rest comfortably in them—or, say, for two legs of something else.

You don’t like to dwell on “what lived here,” in the sense of form or body. The impression of life is not missing you; it is waiting for you to finish noticing.

The floor settles.

With you on it.

The pressure under you ebbs.

The Ambient—does not—breathe.

It just—very slightly—softens.

Like a muscle that’s been holding still.
