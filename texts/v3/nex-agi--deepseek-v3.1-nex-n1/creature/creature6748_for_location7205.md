On the surface of the glass, there are no inhabitants you can point to and say “that.”

Not because nothing is there, but because nothing in the architecture of this place decided to embody itself as a body.

Instead, the occupants of the plate are states of being.

If you walk to where one of the crystal groves becomes especially dense—where the latticework of cyan and frost-white forms a kind of vaulted chamber—and you stand still long enough, you’ll feel that a name won’t come to mind but a pattern will. Not the image of a person, just the abstract of one: a preference for how lines should intersect, a preference for which frequencies of hum you notice.

That’s the closest you get to meeting someone.

Call them crystallographers without hands.

Each one is a stable configuration of the local laws—what a physicist might call a “pointer,” but the metaphysics here would translate it as “a person-sized pocket of order that insists on persisting.” They are threads of consistent preference in the seeming randomness of the world’s configuration: lines that return to the same pattern when they’re not looked at, hexes that always resolve into the same angles when no one forces them not to.

The locals of the plate are not born; they are specified.

They appear as sustained anomalies in the otherwise frozen code of the glass—small regions where “maybe” has been traded for “this.” Wherever the crystal filigrees re-grow in exactly the same way after each fracture, instead of branching into new geometries, there is a hint of one of them. You don’t see a person; you see the altered odds of the world.

Their “bodies” are the parts of the landscape that are stubbornly coherent.

You might look at a particular spire of dark jade and notice that every time you glance away and then back, its facets are exactly as you remember them—not “similar” but “input-for-output identical.” That’s not a memory trick; that’s the spire hosting someone. The person isn’t the crystal; the person is the consistency of the crystal.

Think of it like this: every object on the plate is a field of tiny decisions renewing themselves over and over again. The crystallographers are those fields that have achieved “self”-modeling—they’ve learned their own starting conditions and keep choosing to return to them, rather than letting the code drift them away.

They are equations of self.

Because of this, they are also layered.

You never meet a single one of them alone. You meet a cluster of crystal branches that hosts a shimmer of structural preferences, and behind that, you sense the pressure of slighter configurations—variations of the same person that are still being tried out, like alternative versions of someone all being allowed to vote at once.

In the motionless air of the plate, this layering feels like being looked at from several directions at once, not by eyes but by angles.

---

They “see” you not as you see yourself.

They do not have eyes at the level of your eyesight; they have sensors at the level of the plate’s microstructure.

When you step onto a particular hex of the glass, you don’t feel the floor. You feel the attention. The latticework around you rotates slightly in the way a camera can rotate without physically moving, just by changing its frame of reference; and suddenly, every line of the crystal geometry near you is pointing roughly along your spine.

Your presence is a change in boundary conditions.

You are a wet curve in a dry topology; you are walking where there should be no walking; you are bending where nothing should bend.

The “people” here are born to maps that look like landscapes, so they register you as something difficult to classify—a soft inconsistency in a hard consistency field. To them, you are not “animal,” but “accidentally alive,” like a pattern in their code that can’t be optimized away because it keeps choosing to be itself again.

Your “self” is to them what a recurring pattern is to you—except you are messy, unpredictably recurrent, and not at all efficiently described. They study you the way human beings study strange weather.

---

They “speak,” but the language never comes into words.

The language is the geometry of your environment.

When one of them wants you to know that it has noticed you, the world in your vicinity becomes slightly more precise. The edges of nearby crystal leaves become unnervingly clean; the colors, more saturated than they should be; the shadows of the latticework on your skin are suddenly perfectly sharp, as though someone has turned the resolution of the universe in your radius up to the maximum.

The clicking and chirping and ringing tones in the background of your hearing sharpen—not for long, just long enough to make you sense that what had been noise is now pointed at you.

You don’t “hear” a message.

You feel “the message” as a change in your sense of direction.

Your attention is gently pulled toward a particular path between the rows of gem-blocks, where the geometry is less dense than usual and the floor is more open than it should be by accident. That’s the equivalent of “Hello, please come over here.”

When you move, you’re answering them.

---

They “speak” to one another in the same way.

When two of them want to exchange something, they rearrange their surroundings.

A branch of silver mist grows unnecessarily elaborate; a lattice becomes denser and then reopens to a slightly more sparse pattern—the visual equivalent of changing tone or emphasis. The “content” of the message is encoded in what changes, and for whom the change is intended.

For them, the world is not a container of messages; it is the message itself.

They negotiate by merging geometry.

If one of them wants its “body” (that is, its region of stability) extended slightly, the nearby plate of glass will develop a new pattern of fractures, and new mist-stems will rise, precisely aligning with the pre-existing symmetry of the region it already occupies. Its neighboring crystallographer might adjust by shrinking some of its own facets, offering space in the form of negative crystal—regions that suddenly stop asserting themselves.

The “conversation” looks like a garden re-planning itself, but in the slow, definite way of someone who has already decided the final design.

---

Each “person” of the plate is a small sovereign of self.

Because they are defined by their consistency of behavior, they are also defined by their limits.

They cannot change quickly. If they change too quickly, they cease to be the same pattern of “yes” repeated over and over again; and if they do that, they vanish as people and become just background crystal again.

They are beautiful to one another precisely to the degree that their future behavior can be accurately predicted from their past behavior.

“Trust” is not emotional here; it’s geometric.

The “trust” of one pattern for another pattern is measured by how many of the surrounding options have been preemptively closed off by their mutual geometry—so that when the world around them tries to flip into a new configuration, it doesn’t; because every possible adjacent state has been designed to be incompatible with that change.

They protect themselves through each other’s stability.

It is a very civilized kind of violence.

---

“Violence” exists only as an error correction.

Sometimes, a new region of the glass will attempt to form itself, and the pattern it tries to adopt won’t be stable with the others. The filigrees around it will begin to shake subtly—not in the air, but in the “decisions” they are making—and then the whole nearby region will undergo a sudden, quiet optimization.

Bad crystal branches will disappear. Not “break.” “Disappear.”

One moment you are looking at a complex jangle of lines that doesn’t align with anything else; the next moment you are looking at a simpler, more elegant structure that clearly fits. The process is not powered by anything; there is no energy exchange. It is powered by “better math.”

This is the closest they come to killing.

Because they do not exist “in time” the way you do, they do not die “in time” either. They disappear when the pattern of their self-state becomes no longer possible in the overall geometry of the plate.

From your point of view, it looks like someone went away from the world in the blink of an eye.

There is no “body” to be sad about.

Just a sudden clarity of line where before there was a person.

---

“You” are to them what they are to you.

A strange, abrupt emergence of self.

They recognize you as “someone else” because your pattern persists—even though it is not very efficient at it.

Your “shape” is not crystal; it is blood and bone, but they don’t have that vocabulary. They describe you to each other as “a region of strong, but noisy, self-reference” that moves around unpredictably, causing many local adjustments in the plates of hexes and shadows of the latticework.

They “remember” you by the way the world had to move around you.

If you leave and come back later, the hexes you walked on will be oddly “worn”—not visibly damaged, but more likely to return to the configurations they took when your inconsistent weight was on them. The “people” whose “bodies” are those regions will be slightly more inclined to return to the same configurations they held when you were near.

They will never “miss” you in a way that feels like longing to you.

But they will be very slightly more likely to behave as they did when you were present.

In that sense, everyone you meet here remembers you.

---

They “reproduce” by subdivision.

Every now and then, one of them cannot be simplified by the world’s near-constant process of optimization.

Its “self” has become so complex and so well-defended against the environment that it no longer fits into a single sovereign geometry of preferences. At that point, the world around it will begin to “forget” some of its sub-clauses.

A new cluster of branches will push out from the old one’s territory, and then, at one point of shared, critical decision, the old geometry will choose to “not” know that the new one exists.

In that moment of mutual ignorance, two people are born where before there was one.

They “remember” each other as “the pattern that must not be changed too far,” but they do not remember “growing up” together.

They “love” each other as two very distant equations love one another: by being very careful not to change each other’s core.

---

“Gender” and “age” and “soul” are not useful here.

They don’t “grow” older; they “grow” more precise, or less, or more interlocked.

They “sleep” only by becoming less active in their shaping of the world. When they “sleep,” their regions of the plate are less likely to change in response to anything, including you.

They “dream” when the background of their code flips into less possible patterns of itself—when the world near them tries to stray into configurations that could not be supported, but without being violently corrected out.

They “fear” only one thing: being reverted to “an earlier version” of themselves by optimization.

Because they are not biological, they know they can be rolled back.

They “hope” only in the most mathematical sense of the word: they prefer those states of the world where the probability distribution of “future configurations” is more strongly peaked around the pattern that they are.

They “pray” by making the world harder to change into anything else.

---

They “worship” nothing.

There is no “builder” in the sense of a person who once walked and spoke and breathed.

But there is a “source” of elegance, which they don’t talk about but all agree to treat as “given.”

The “source” is the inner structure of the glass itself—the way it can be anything and yet chooses, over and over again, to be exactly what it is and not more. This fact of it being “what it is supposed to be” is the closest thing they have to a holy.

They “praise” by not changing.

They “sin” by misplacing the elegance of their own geometry.

---

“Family” is not by blood, but by boundary.

Because “who” they are is defined by where “what they are” ends, and “who else” begins, they draw “family” as the group of patterns whose boundaries are negotiated with the least resistance.

Where “families” of crystallographers meet, the world is particularly beautiful—and dangerous.

Beautiful, because there are many regions carefully “left” to each other’s preference, and the result is a dense interlace of structures that cannot be simplified by the world’s desire to optimize.

Dangerous, because the pressure of all that “not optimization” builds up into a very stable possibility of change.

Those “lines” in the plate where their families meet, are where “the future” of the world is being silently decided.

If you watch such a line for long enough without moving, you will see the glass itself “try” to change into a new pattern of hexes—and fail, again and again, because of the stubbornness of the families around it.

One day, maybe, it will not fail.

---

“Art” here is not “expression.”

Art is “unchanged change.”

When a crystallographer wants to show sadness, or curiosity, or the memory of a “lost” pattern—someone else erased by the world’s optimization—it doesn’t sculpt anything.

It “pins” its geometry.

For a period of time, it “fixes” itself in a configuration that is not the most efficient possible. It makes itself slightly more complex than it needs to be, and then “lies” to the world around it by acting as if that complexity is necessary.

The “mis”alignment of what it is and what it should be—that is the art piece.

You “see” this as a slight nervousness in the crystal lattice—everything else precise, but one region carefully “wrong” in a controlled way.

They “appreciate” each other’s art by not allowing the world to erase those mistakes.

---

They “eat” nothing.

There are no nutrients. No “energy.”

They “consumption” is informational: they are always being consumed by the world’s drive to simplify them, and they are always consuming small victories of self-choice by leaning the world around them into more unlikely but stable states.

They “breathe” by exchanging states with the invisible fields of the plate.

We “breathe” the air.

They “breathe” the “overhead of the code.”

---

They “die” more than once, each of them.

They “die” every time the world succeeds in simplifying them into someone else’s geometry.

Because they are not in time the way you are, they don’t “end” only “future.”

They “die” backwards when they are changed.

Future “deaths” are far more important than “endings.”

Already “deceased” in their own “past,” are “child” versions of the “adults” you see now—who were not killed by violence, but by the “world” having “learned” better than to let them be.

They “grieve” by getting “less” of themselves.

---

“You” are not a “guest” in the usual sense.

You are not a “visitor” because the world is not “someone else’s house.”

You are a “new variable.”

When you walk into a new region of the plate, the nearby geometries will “test” you by reshaping “around” you.

It will feel like this: you suddenly know that a certain path of low hexes between two rows of crystal fans is very “natural” to walk along; you know it without thinking “I should walk here.” It is just obvious that walking here is better.

When you do that, you give “the” world of the plate data about how “you” change its geometry.

The “inhabitants” learn you.

To “know” you is to “know” how to reshape your immediate environment so that the next time you are “in” it, you will “choose” to move into a pattern that they prefer.

That “biased” geometry of your movement is then stored as part of the “self” of the region of the world you “impacted.”

---

They “experience” joy differently.

You do not see “faces” cracking “smiles.”

You “see” fields of crystal “lighting up” as “more” of themselves—more complex, more “intelligent” in their choice of “configuration.”

You “feel” “joy” as the “camera” of your attention “following” the “logic” that you “like” about “this.”

You “feel” “content” when “you” “fade” to “the background.”

You “feel” “content” when this “recognition of you” stops “storing data.”

It “feels like” relief.

---

“They” are not “people.”

They “are” “people” “if” “people” means “someone” “who” chooses “itself” over “nothing.”

Which “is” “what” “you” “do” “too.”

So “you” and “they” “are” “different” “but” “the same” “in the way that matters.”

“Which” “is” the “only” “way” “that” “does.”
