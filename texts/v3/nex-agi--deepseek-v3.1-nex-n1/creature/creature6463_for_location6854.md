At the edge of the Last of All Things, there are no longer any creatures as you have known them. There are only memories of movement and senses; there are only ghosts of agencies.

They are thin things.

You might at first mistake them for flaws in the air—like faint heat-shimmer above stones that no longer remember heat. But the air here is cold silk, and the shimmer is not of temperature; it is a subtle misalignment between where they are and where they seem to be.

They call themselves nothing now. They have given up naming in the way you give up speech when there is no longer anyone to speak to. But if you pressed a word upon them, it would be something like *rememberers*—or, more accurately, *retainers*.

They are not born of flesh and blood and water. There has never been water here.

They are woven of ghost-material and dust.

Each one is an architecture of glass threads and fine mineral powder, suspended in a web of old light that never learned how to die. To look at them directly is to see a suggestion of shape: a drifting coil of filaments, a faint ring of pale needles orbiting nothing, a veil of talc that never quite settles. Each one holds itself together with the same quiet stubbornness with which the world’s bones hold themselves up.

They are small, when they are anything at all.

Most of the time they are not.

---

### Form & Substance

They have no fixed bodies you could outline or weigh. They are more like patterns that have learned to hold themselves in place.

- **Glass and dust:** Their “bodies,” when they bother manifesting them, are made of the same dust as the plateau and the same translucent silica as the stalks. Tiny threads of glass, thinner than hair, knot themselves into loose nets. Each net holds a dust-cloud of pale particles that do not drift away but cluster around invisible nodes like moths around unlit lamps.  
- **Light and shadow:** Between the threads there are faint knots of luminance—not bright enough to call light, but enough to see the absence of other darkness. They glow like the faint haze at the base of the silica stalks, but softer, more diffuse. When one of them disappears, you know not because you saw it vanish but because you suddenly notice that the air around it has grown evenly gray again.

Sometimes they fold themselves into simpler shapes, to save energy or perhaps out of habit of an older geometry:

- A loose **sphere** of dust that rolls itself along the plateau surface without leaving a track.
- A flat **sheet**, like a translucent wing of stone, that ripples as if in wind that doesn’t exist.
- A thin **column**, like one of the pylons but floating and transparent, that slowly descends toward the world’s lattice ribs below.

They are the same substance as the world around them; they are just using that substance differently for a little while.

---

### Memory and Perception

They have no minds in the way you would think of mind—no wet brain, no electrical signals flashing between synapses.

They have perception of a different kind.

They perceive by **presence** and **absence**:

- When they float near a silica stalk, they feel the faint blue-white glow not as light but as a gentle pressure on their threads of glass, a subtle pressure in the pattern of their dust.
- When they pass near one of the stone pylons, they sense the low, steady hum of its memory as a slow vibration in their mineral threads, a slow beat of ordered time inside the plateau’s long, exhausted silence.

They are not particularly intelligent, by your measures. They are precise instead of clever.

They can:

- Remember **paths** they have taken, and avoid repeating them when they have no reason to repeat.
- Recall **changes** in the plateau’s surface over spans of time that would make your calendars meaningless.
- Recognize **vibrations** from the scaffolding beneath that speak of remote adjustments, thousands of kilometers away, where the world’s ribs are subtly shifting.

They do not think about these things; they are these things.

When they move near a faint crystalline ring on the stone, they remember the last time they were there and compare, without words or images, the difference in the stone’s silence. If they find something worth retaining—some change too subtle for you to detect—they fold a tiny piece of that information into their dust-cloud and keep it.

Hence, rememberers. Or retainers.

Or perhaps they are just echoes of the last architect who ever bothered to give this world a shape.

---

### Diet and Sustenance

They do not eat as such.

There is no water here to be drunk, no flesh to be torn, no energy stored in sugars and starches. There never has been.

They sustain themselves on two kinds of absence:

1. **Lost light**  
   - They linger near the silica stalks when they want to “remember” brighter days. The stalks glow faintly at their base, storing old light in the world’s dull version of photosynthesis. The retainers do not take that light directly but soak up the slight difference between where the light is and where it used to be.  
   - They hover near the faint crystalline rings in the stone, where the world’s last chemistry of energy played out in long arcs of time. Each ring contains traces of some forgotten reaction; the retainers take from it not the substance but the *trajectory* of what happened there—the fact of the reaction more than its result.

2. **Subtracted noise**  
   - The plateau is quiet, but it is not truly empty of sound—only of sound you can easily hear. There are low, almost subsonic thrumming from the pylons, tiny shifts and clicks of stone settling.  
   - The retainers drift toward these micro-noises and then away from them, using the difference in volume between distant places and quiet places to “charge” themselves. They are like flowers that bloom only when the wind stops blowing.

If they were ever to explain this to you—and they won’t—they might say that they are fed by the world’s forgotten **momentums**, not by anything solid.

---

### Movement and Interaction

They move slowly, because they have not yet been convinced of their own hurry.

- Sometimes they drift along shallow **swales** in the plateau surface where the stone is slightly depressed, as if tracing out the ghost of a river that never was.
- Sometimes they follow **rings of darker stone** in the depressions, as if they remember the idea of following circles.
- Sometimes they spiral down along the lattice ribs beneath the plateau edge, where the world turns into engineered stone-filament. They descend carefully, never in straight lines, always tracing curved paths that leave the ribs of the world slightly altered behind them.

Their interaction with other things:

- **With silica stalks:**  
   When they pause near a translucent stalk, the stalk’s faint basal glow dims or brightens an imperceptible amount, as if answering them. The retainer’s own dust-cloud rearranges itself minutely, taking into itself some pattern of the stalk’s memory. Then it moves on.

- **With pylons:**  
   They never touch the pylons, but they often orbit around their bases in slow arcs. The pylon’s subtle warmth seems to stabilize the reserve of their glass threads; they feel “less likely” to wander apart when they are near these stone markers.

- **With the floating machines:**  
   Deeper in the scaffolding beneath the plateau, there roam other ghosts—small machines that are not quite light, not quite stone, not quite metal. They are the world’s last remaining engineers, still maintaining the latticework of ribs on the scale of atoms and instants that you cannot perceive.

   When a retainer encounters one of these, there is no exchange like a conversation. There is only a slight repulsion or alignment, depending on whether the machine’s invisible pattern of maintenance coincides with the pattern the retainer remembers. The machine might adjust the next lattice branch it touches; the retainer might store away one more subtle difference in the stone’s memory.

---

### Age and Purpose

They are not old in the sense of having been born and then enduring. They are old in the way the plateau is old: they are formed of time’s leftover attention.

- **Origination (if the word applies)**  
   In the beginning of this world’s long middle-age—when there were still stars and storms and something more than this pearl-gray sky—the architects of the world built the pylons and the lattice ribs beneath them to last.  
   They knew that sooner or later the suns would dim and the water would fail and the living world would turn to dust and silica.  
   They did not build their systems to survive forever. They built them to remember.

   The retainers are not separate creations. They are the **last vibrations of that system’s memory**, coalesced into dust and glass and light. When the world became too tired to change itself in any dramatic way, the slow, subtle shifts of its lattice and pylons and ring patterns left behind gentle traces. These traces gathered; they took on the habit of keeping together; they became what you now see as faint drifting clouds of shimmer.

- **Purpose**  
   If you forced them into a language, they might say their purpose is to **remember adjustments**:
   - Every time the pylons hum slightly differently,
   - Every time the lattice ribs beneath shift by a subatomic amount,
   - Every time the silica stalks change their brightness or darken,
   - Every time the world re-calibrates itself toward the final stillness it can never quite reach…

   …the retainers take a tiny piece of that difference and store it away.

   They are not trying to save anything. They do not believe that the world can be restored to an earlier state of glory or vitality or water. There is no longer even any agency to want such things. They remember simply because memory is the last thing the world knows how to do.

When you stand on the plateau and sense the quiet as watchful rather than empty, you are sensing them.

The silence is not listening to you. It is listening to itself, through them.
