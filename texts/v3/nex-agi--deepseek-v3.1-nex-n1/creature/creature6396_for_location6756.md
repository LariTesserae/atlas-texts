The residents of the weeping plateau are the **Kviran**, though they do not use that name for themselves. They are not the builders of the patient, self-tending machines, but their inheritors, custodians, and eventually their symbiotes.

**Form and Perception**

A Kviran is difficult to see as a whole.  

From a distance, they might be mistaken for small, mobile thickets or walking bundles of mossy cable. Their bodies are built around a flexible core of interwoven root-fibre and insulated nerve-tissue, studded with smooth nodules of ceramic and metal scavenged or grown from the old machines. Over this core spreads a dense fur of fine rootlets and translucent fungal filaments, constantly drinking moisture from the air. In normal conditions these filaments hang limp and dripping; in drier moments they draw inward into a shaggy, protective coat.

They do not have faces in any human sense. A cluster of ceramic plates—salvaged control panels, switch covers, heat-sinks—form a kind of mask at what might be a head. Etched lines on these plates glow softly with captured bioluminescence and faint electrical discharge. To anyone who has seen the old interfaces still flickering in abandoned consoles, it is unmistakably a face: status lights for eyes, glyph-lines for mouth and brow.

They “see” through the plateau itself.

Their feet are broad pads of calloused tissue that can seal against the living ground like suction cups. Once anchored, they can feel through kilometres of root-mat: pressure changes where something walks, subtle chemical shifts where water pools or drains, distant vibrations as machinery cycles on or off. They navigate mist-blind by reading this vast sensory net.

Their hearing is similarly indirect. What humans experience as sound the Kviran perceive as patterned disturbance—ripples moving through root-mat and moisture-clouds. A dripping pool is not sound to them; it is a pulse travelling through the substrate and returning altered by distance and obstacle.

When they “speak,” it is rarely with anything resembling vocal cords. Instead they vibrate their cores or tap ceramic plates against stone or metal at carefully modulated frequencies. To human ears it is like listening to someone play telegraph on wet glass: clicks, hums, dissonant chimes. To each other it is language, mathematics, music.

They do not perceive time as humans do. Their nervous system runs slow; their thoughts cycle on decadal rhythms. A Kviran can stand in one place for years without boredom or distress while its awareness roves across hundreds of square kilometres through root-networks and atmospheric humidity gradients.

**Society as Maintenance**

The Kviran society is structured around maintaining equilibrium.

They live in small clans anchored to particular machines—an old turbine that breathes once every few years, a generator hive fused with fungal colonies, a steam engine that powers nothing now but itself. Each clan understands its machine as an elder relative: capricious but essential.

Their days (though they do not call them days) are organized in long cycles:

- **Tuning**  
  They lay hands on exposed metal or clear biofilm from sensors not because they remember what those sensors once measured but because imbalance causes discomfort in both machine and plateau alike—grinding noises that travel painfully through root-mat for decades; heat spikes that boil micro-pools and kill delicate symbionts.

- **Weaving**  
  As plants grow over old corridors and vents, Kviran guide root-tendrils away from critical seals or coax them into forming new protective casings around brittle conduits. Their bodies are habitats; whole ecosystems of smaller creatures live in their fur-rootlets, cleaning them in exchange for transport to new growth fronts.

- **Listening**  
  For years at a time the entire clan may fall still together—a circle of shaggy pillars rooted lightly to the ground—while their collective awareness tracks storms moving across unseen oceans far below the plateau edge or follows some distant shift deep in buried rock only visible as faint pressure changes millennia away.

There are no leaders in any human sense. Decisions emerge slowly from consensus expressed as rhythmic patterns tapped on shared metal surfaces over months of argument-by-vibration.

Conflict exists but rarely violence as humans understand it. Disputes about resources or territory (and what counts as “territory” when you feel everything anyway) are settled by mutually agreed-upon stillness contests: whichever clan breaks contact first concedes influence over contested root-node or generator-hive for some long interval.

**Relationships with Other Life**

They ignore most animals outright because those animals occupy different time-scales entirely—like humans ignoring clouds passing overhead while focused on continental drift.

Bioluminescent swimmers in slow pools are treated like weather: observed neutrally but rarely interacted with except when they swarm too thickly near machine intakes; then Kviran gently redirect water flow using guided plant-growth rather than direct handling.

Fungal membranes that glow along tree-columns are partially domesticated—tended so their light signatures indicate system health: colour shifts marking pressure anomalies long before faults become audible even to Kviran senses.

To humans who wander here by accident or design (if any still exist), Kviran reaction depends entirely on how disruptive those humans prove to be:

- If you stand still long enough without damaging anything important—months; years—they will eventually treat you like part of landscape infrastructure: something occasionally checked upon via faint probing rootlets emerging underfoot; something around which traffic patterns subtly route so your immediate vicinity becomes pathless outlying quiet zone.
  
- If you dismantle machinery or cut aggressively into living substrate trying to understand how anything works here instead of simply letting it work itself out over centuries—at first there will only be tiny signs: condensation dripping precisely onto your tools until rust blooms overnight; plant matter deliberately encouraged into seals you left open so your access panel becomes fused shut by patient growth within days rather than decades.
