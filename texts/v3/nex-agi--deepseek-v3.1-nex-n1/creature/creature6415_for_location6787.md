What you call “glass-mazes” are like punctuation marks—commas and semicolons scrawled in mineral ink across a stone page.

They are not plants. To call them that would be to misunderstand their purpose. They do not grow. They **settle**.

Each one is a slow theorem of the plateau’s stone.

From a distance, they look like chips of dark glass, all angles and fine edges. Move closer and you see that the glass is not a solid block but a thin blister shell, transparent to a depth of maybe a finger’s thickness. Beneath that shell, the interior writhes with faint smoke—clouds of darker dust suspended in a crystalline lattice.

There are no leaves. There are no stems. There are no flowers.

There are only **trajectories**.

### Surface

The surface of each glass-maze is hard and slick, like obsidian polished by eons of gentle wind. Close inspection, however—or a touch with the fingertips—reveals a texture like the skin of a dried riverbed: a web of tiny cracks in a pattern that is almost but not quite random.

These are not cracks caused by stress. They are **inscriptions**.

The glass-maze is **reading** the rock beneath it—and writing its own response.

Wherever the stone beneath it hums with a subtle difference—pressure, electrical charge, a faint misalignment in the crystal lattice—the glass-maze extends a **filament**: a tiny thread of mineral no thicker than a spider’s web. Filaments thread from the underside of the glass-maze into the stone, bonding with it, dissolving into it.

Nothing flows along these filaments the way sap flows in a tree. There is no vascular system. There is no **movement**.

Instead, there is **propagation**.

### Metabolism

The metabolism of a glass-maze is not about chemistry or organic energy. It is about **structure**.

Inside the shell, beneath the transparent lid, the suspended dust is not inert. It is **responsive**—to pressure, to charge, to the minute stress gradients in the plateau beneath. Over the course of millennia—centuries are too fast—a given particle of dust **adjusts** its position.

Each adjustment is **information**.

The internal pattern of the glass-maze is **message**:  
- A pressure ridge from deep strata in the rock translates into a branching of the internal labyrinth.  
- A subtle difference in static charge across the surface becomes a faint reordering of the dust cloud, a **spiral** instead of a **hex**.

The inside of a glass-maze is **notation** for the plateau beneath it.

In this sense, the glass-maze **eats stress**.

It consumes **difference**.

It ingests **gradient**.

### Reproduction

A glass-maze does not have **seeds**. It does not **germinate**.

It **echoes**.

When a certain threshold of internal **notation** is reached—when the pattern becomes **stable** enough—the glass-maze **induces** a new patch of shell on the stone nearby. Not touching. **Never** touching, except through the stone.

This new shell is **initiated** in the crystal lattice of the rock beneath. Its mineral is **printed** out of the same substance that makes up the plateau—but rearranged, **folded**, into something glassy.

Over **geological** time—not generations—the new shell rises: a thin roof of glass perhaps a finger’s width across at first. Beneath it, something **stirs** from dust that has been waiting since the mountain was still young.

The **parent** glass-maze and the **offspring** shell communicate by **implication**: the pressure-pattern beneath one informs the lattice of the other.

They **talk** in stone.

### Aggregations

Glass-mazes are not **segregated**. They are not **solitary**.

Yet they **do** cluster.

In **colonies**, a dozen or more of them may occupy a small depression in the rock, each a separate shell of labyrinthine smoke. Between them stretches **unbroken** stone—no **filaments** bridging the gap.

Instead, the **rock** beneath becomes **medium**.

The **arrangement** of the glass-mazes is **coherent**: they relate to each other through **thread-like** lines of stress in the stone. A **pressure** under **this** maze becomes a **structural** motif in **that** one, even if they are meters apart.

If you were to map the **patterns** beneath their shells—with instruments that do **not** belong on this plateau—you might find that their **internal** labyrinths are **not** identical, but **mirrored**.

One **reads**.

One **writes**.
