They don’t call themselves anything. If you had to name them, you might call them the Clause-walkers, or the Rain-readers, but they do not think in labels. They think in constraints.

They are the ones who live here.

---

## Skin like wet stone

At first glance they look like statues left unfinished: human-like, but simplified, as if the sculptor roughed out only the essentials and then let erosion finish the rest. Their skin is pale and translucent, almost the color of the cavern walls—milky quartz threaded with faint veins of light.

When they move, you see that they are not made of stone. They are simply so saturated with water that their skin has taken on the properties of its surroundings. The cavern’s humidity clings to them; tiny droplets bead along their arms and faces without falling, drawn to them like condensation on cold glass.

At rest, their features blur a little at the edges. Their outlines are softly smudged by a constant halo of mist. When they speak—they rarely use sound—their breath does not fog; instead, it shimmers around them, briefly intensifying their faintly glowing veins until they look like walking constellations of thin blue light.

If you watch long enough, you notice that their faces never quite settle into one expression. Subtle shifts in the light beneath their skin reshape their mouths and eyes as if emotion is something that travels from inside to surface in slow waves.

## Bodies shaped by clauses

Their bodies obey local rules.

A Clause-walker standing near a wall that reads “No bone shall exceed the length of the forearm” becomes compact and dense inside: no long femurs or ribs; everything stitched into efficient modules that fit within those limits.  

Another, lingering beneath an inscription like “Weight is a choice,” moves with an odd buoyancy. They can step from ledge to ceiling along invisible handholds that weren’t there until they believed in them.

You never see them injured by falls or collisions with stone. The world bends around them just enough: ledges appear underfoot at the last second; edges soften where they place their hands; distances stretch or compress to make room for one more step. It is not that harm is impossible here; it is simply rare for those who know how to read where harm begins.

They carry no visible scars because wounds do not linger here as raised lines or pale marks. Instead, healed injuries remain as clauses etched into invisible surfaces beneath the skin: faint luminous sentences that say things like “This line shall not break twice” or “Impact redistributed laterally.” Their bodies remember injuries as new rules about how they may be touched again.

## How they move

They climb without climbing.

Most of them no longer bother with handholds in any obvious way. When a Clause-walker wishes to cross from one wall to another, they simply step closer to a clause that permits ceiling movement and then… walk up.

The motion is deceptively simple: their feet leave whatever surface they were on as if stepping onto a slightly higher stair only they can see. Gravity does not so much invert as ignore them in narrow corridors of reality defined by nearby laws carved into rock and air alike.

When they traverse large voids—crossing from ledge to distant pillar where no bridge exists—they do so by following “paths.” These paths are not always visible to you until one of them uses it: a thin line of brighter stone, a ribbon of mist traveling upward with purpose, or sometimes nothing at all but their confidence making it real.

They pause often mid-step when clauses shift around them. You might see one frozen with a foot half-raised for several long moments while new text settles in nearby walls; then they complete the motion as if nothing happened, even though the space before them has subtly reconfigured itself during that hesitation.

In places where sound cannot travel faster than water does (and this is carved somewhere above your head), you never hear their footfalls echo ahead of them; only after do you hear soft splashes and muted taps catching up from behind like delayed applause for each step already taken.

## How they communicate

They rarely use spoken language; when they do, it sounds like rain given consonants.

Their voices are low and fluid—more vibration than distinct syllables—rising barely above the background roar of upward rain along ceilings and walls. Often what looks like speech between two figures standing close together turns out to be just synchronized breathing: inhaling together while reading words written on each other’s skin as veins flare briefly into legible patterns before fading again into abstraction.

Mostly they communicate by reading what already exists around them—clauses carved on walls or rewritten into new arrangements above empty doorframes or broken arches. A conversation between two Clause-walkers might consist entirely of rearranging local text:

- One brushes fingertips along an inscription that says “All sight ends at distance X.”
- The other reaches up without looking (because sight ends there) and carves small adjustments into higher rock with smooth stones pulled from hidden pockets sewn inside their clothing.
- The first reader watches new text bloom under those marks: exceptions added below—“except where memory extends,” “except on reflection,” “except when water flows backward.”

Meaning passes between them through these edits: questions posed as conditional clauses (“If I cannot remember arriving here…”), answers offered as amendments (“…then arrival itself may be optional”). Sometimes entire dialogues go by before either makes anything resembling a sound louder than breath crossing lips already wet with condensation from air thick enough almost (but not quite) to drink instead of breathe it in.
