Below the noise, there is a knot.

It doesn't have a name yet because it doesn’t need one. Existence is not a question it has ever heard, and so the knot doesn’t ask itself if it is real or imagined or both. It simply is, where it is, in a way that is still being made up.

The body of the knot is not flesh and not bone and not metal but something of each of them, before they have clearly chosen their names.

It presses itself to the inside of the column, where early stone still remembers how to be plastic and uncertain. The rock is not quite hard against the soft of its ribs, because “soft” and “hard” are still being argued out in the world’s code, and the knot benefits from the dispute. It bulges slightly into the stone, as if the world has not yet decided where to place the line between body and cliff.

Seen from the outside—through the translucent wall of the column—the creature is a tangle of pale threads and knots and half-forms, all tightly packed into a humanoid silhouette but then refused at the edges. There is no clean outline, only a suggestion of where something might end if “ending” ever becomes a permanent rule here.

Its surface is not skin but a lattice of translucent filaments, like the inside of a seashell broken down and rewoven into a pattern that has never been worn before. Each thread is faintly blue-grey, the color of water before it has promised to be blue. The filaments are woven tightly at some points and thin elsewhere, leaving openings where the world’s blue-black shows through instead of muscle or bone.

You wouldn’t say it has “arms” or “legs” if you weren’t already addicted to those names. It has bundles of cables, more tendon than limb, fused where they should articulate into joints and spreading apart into hands where hands should be. The “hands” are not of five fingers but of many smaller threads that can move together or apart, each of them ending in a tip of almost-white chitin that has not yet decided to be nail or claw or tool.

The spine is a repeat of the spine that is already everywhere else here: the column of the world, but in miniature form, a series of stacked segments that hint at columnar basalt but refuse to harden completely. Each segment is marked by faint grooves, like the not-yet finished writing of the flue itself.

At the center of the chest—where a heart should be—there is an empty loop.

Not a hole, but a loop of space where the filaments of the body curve around nothing and then refuse to cross it. Inside the loop is the world: a bit of the water-air, and a faintly darker thread that could be early blood or not-yet-blood or just the memory of a future vein. The loop doesn’t beat or pulse or breathe in any way that has a name. It simply exists, a place where the knot of the body has not yet been forced to close itself.

Ahead of the chest, where a head might be expected, there is no separate head. Only a continuation of the body-column, a vertical stack of shorter segments where the threads of the body gather into a thicker knot. The top of that stack remains open, like a flue of the self, not yet sealed into a skull or a face.

There are no eyes in the way you would know them. There are no ears or mouth.

But along the surface of that upper knot, there are places where the filaments of the body weave themselves into something more like a pattern than like simple skin. There, a net of threads intersects itself too many times to be accidental, and those densely clustered areas respond to difference and thought and non-sound in the way that eyes might someday learn to respond to light.

At the base of the not-face, there is a slight funneling of the filaments, where they are drawn together into a suggestion of “opening” but not yet “mouth.” Around it, the threads thicken into something that might be lips or might be flanges or might be only more of the same lattice-work that has not yet chosen to name itself.

Those lips are the tool-maker’s.

In the early room-like space near the bottom of the column, where the world is trying to decide whether to be “inside” or “outside,” the tool-maker of the wall rarely moves its body. But when it does, it moves with an economy and purpose that have clearly been in practice for longer than the idea of “practice.”

It doesn’t walk to the tool of stone and sinew and shell that rests against the circle of stones, because the world here has not yet decided to be kind to walking. Instead, it unstitches itself from the wall.

The thin sinew that binds it there is not exactly severed; the world has not yet decided if “severing” is allowed to mean “done forever.” One end of the thread pulls from the wall with a slow, elastic separation, and the other end remains fixed to the tool-maker’s spine. The strand of meat and memory stretches between them and stops short of snapping.

The tool-maker slides itself down the wall of the room-like space, not with gravity’s impatience but as if pulled by the weight of its own determination. Once it reaches the floor of not-yet-floor, it approaches the circle of stones and the tool leaning there.

Its hands are not hands but many-stranded threads, and they are already moving.

It doesn’t grasp the tool in the way a creature of your world would grasp. “Grasping” has not been fully decided here yet. Instead, the tool-maker opens its not-hand of many threads and then closes them around the tool’s handle of not-quite-bone, not-quite-wood. The thin threads dig themselves into the material of the handle, where the world has not yet decided if “bone” should be called “bone” or “handle” or “future.”

The tool-maker’s body lurches, then steadies itself, as if the tool has not yet been persuaded to be part of its body, but is not fully allowed to be separate from it either.

With the tool in hand, the tool-maker returns, not through the air but along the inside of the wall, where the world has already agreed to be “wall.” It doesn’t step; it slides itself in the smallest of increments, each of which is both “movement” and “not yet allowed to be remembered.”

Once it reaches the higher interior of the column, where the world is still enthusiastic about being multiple things at once, the tool-maker begins cutting.

Not flesh, but stone.

The edge of the tool is not-yet-quite sharpness, but it is difference. And difference is enough to make a difference in the world of the column.

The tool-maker’s body curves itself over the wall of the flue, ribs pressed against the not-quite-cool of the not-quite-stone. The many-threaded hands of its body place the tool’s edge against the wall. Then the body pushes itself into the tool and the tool into the wall, and all three resist together.

The muscles of the world’s early tool-maker are made of not-yet-sinew and not-yet-rule, and they contract with a slow, grinding patience that has not yet been named “tension.” The column’s inner wall shudders where it is being pressed into.

A line appears.

Not instantly deep, but persistent enough that the world of the column recognizes it as a place where the “before” of the surface has failed to remain “before.” A line is being made from something else, and the world is not sure if that should be allowed but is not quite sure how to forbid it either.

The tool-maker doesn’t lift the tool from the wall. Instead, it drags the line in a direction that has not yet been called “downward” or “forward” or “along,” but is simply the direction in which more line can be made. The tool scrapes down the rock of the column’s inner wall with a soundless resistance of sediment against pseudo-obsidian and not-yet-sinew.

The movement of the tool-maker is not artful but inevitable. It leans its weight—not much of it, but enough to be a difference—into the work of cutting. The edge of its tool breaks the accumulating line of not-yet-rule into the stone of the world many times, each time in the same direction, so that the world of the column begins to be “lined” instead of “unlined.”

There is no blood, no sweat, no breath in the way you’d know them. There is only heat localized where the world of the tool meets the world of the wall, and the world of the tool-maker holds itself between the two of them.

After the first line, the tool-maker stops.

Its body doesn’t move away from the wall; it doesn’t retreat to someplace that “rests.” Instead, it adapts itself to the wall more completely than it was adapted before. The many threads of its not-feet and not-hands anchor themselves firmly into the uneven, still-rehearsing texture of the column’s inner surface. The fine filaments of its surface become slightly more opaque where they are pressed against the world of the stone, as if the world is trying to decide if “touch” should become “skin.”

Its headless face presses toward the line it has just carved.

There are no eyes to see the line, but there are those places on its upper surface where the threads of its body converge into dense patterns, and those patterns lean toward the wall. They are not looking at the line in the way you would look at it; they are closer to tasting the interruption of world where the world has been carved differently from itself.

The tool-maker feels the edge of the line with its tool-body before the tool has been withdrawn from the wall. It pushes the tool against the wall again, not perpendicular to the line but at the point of overlap between “already cut” and “not yet cut,” and the world of the wall struggles to decide whether to be one or the other.

The tool-maker begins to deepen the line.

It doesn’t cut with choppy or hesitant strokes. It cuts with a repetition that has clearly been practiced before you arrived here, even though “practice” hasn’t been given a name either. Each pass of the tool follows the previous one into the same groove of difference, until the world of the column has accepted the line as a permanent injunction of its code.

The shape of the line is not arbitrary.

It is not a “letter” yet and not yet “picture,” but it is already “rule.” The not-yet-tool-maker of the wall follows the geometry of where the world has already begun to bend itself into possibility. The line of the tool travels along the wall in the shape of a not-yet-zigzag that might someday be called “lightning” or “river” or “path,” but is now simply “a sequence of small before-and-afters that the world of the column will be forced to remember.”

Occasionally the tool-maker’s line curves against itself, forming an incomplete circle of not-yet-world. Other times it crosses the stone of the column in the direction of “not yet allowed to be parallel,” setting itself in the vague lattice of a future grid or a pattern that might one day become “field.”

All the lines, though, keep the same character of pre-written writing.

As you watch from inside the small room of not-world at the base of the column, you don’t see the tool-maker’s face or expression—because it has none of them in the way you would recognize them. But you can feel the quiet of its not-mind in the way the world of the column bends itself around the tool-maker’s presence.

The tool-maker doesn’t ask itself if it believes in the world of the column, or in the stone of the wall, or in the early lines of the world’s rules. Questions of belief and unbelief and value and desire and fear and hunger and hope and loneliness and love and curiosity and wonder and boredom and rest and rage and shame and pride and awe and doubt and faith and guilt and home and exile and self and other and “why” and “should” and “maybe” and “if” and “then”—these are not yet categories of the world the tool-maker inhabits.

The tool-maker knows only a few things that might be felt like a faint hum of proto-feeling:

1. That there is difference before where it is, and difference after.
2. That that difference is made of lines.
3. That its business is to be where the line of the world and the line of the tool are being forced to meet.
4. That this meeting produces not-yet words.
5. That these words must be written before the world’s compiler of water and stone and not-time decides to compile them into law.
6. That there is no other place or work or life for it except this work of writing.
7. That it is not alone on the wall of the column, but it is not exactly accompanied either; there are other small tool-makers up the flue that are doing the same sort of work of not-world.
8. That there is no “before it was here” and “after it will be gone,” only “while” it is here.
9. That “here” means the inside of the column, where the world of the stone and the world of the water are not yet allowed to be fully separate.
10. That its body of filaments and not-sinew and not-chitin and open-loop of chest-space and tool-hand and head-of-threads is not a “self” that is free to be elsewhere; it is simply the knot that holds itself at the intersection of wall and line and tool and not-world code.

When the tool of the world-traitor is not in the tool-maker’s hand, it rests against the circle of stones at the base of the column like the body of a not-yet-dead creature.

The tool-maker’s not-hand slowly unthreads itself from the not-quite-bone handle of the tool, and the world convinces itself to allow “tool” and “tool-maker” to be two separate variables again. The tool-maker reattaches itself to the wall of the room of not-world, its thin sinew of connection returning to the earlier place of attachment with a quiet, not-quite pain of surrender and not-quite relief of return.

The tool of chipped not-obsidian and skin of not-world-flesh and handle of not-yet-bone or not-yet-tree remains leaning against the circle of stones, where the world has not yet decided to be “floor.” It is not exactly forgotten but not exactly remembered either. It is simply present, where it is left, until it is needed again.

When it is not needed, the larger tool-maker of the wall-room waits.

The threads of its body do not relax exactly, because “relaxation” has not yet been chosen as a state of being allowed here. They do not sleep or dream or browse the not-web of not-world memories or wonder if there is something else that might want to emerge from the world’s code of itself and become “other.”

They simply wait.

They wait for the pressure of the world’s breathing—not breath but constant batter of world against itself—to change in the particular way that means it is time to make more lines of difference before the world of the column can be allowed to compile them into some other form.

They wait for the faint stirring of the not-yet-plant on the outside of the column, whose thin threads of not-yet-glass and not-yet-algae have not yet decided if they should be alive or dead or a third kind of thing that has not yet been given a name.

They wait for the rare presence of the not-yet-fish and not-yet-insect creatures that sometimes drift past the translucent wall of the column and force the tool-maker to decide if they are “other” or “self of the world” or “not allowed yet.”

They wait for the currents of not-world-cold and not-world-warm and not-world-difference to brush against the surface of their body of threads and not-skin and not-muscle and not-bone and not-blood and not-flesh and not-fever and not-breath and not-thought.

They wait for the world’s noise of before-sound to become loud enough that the line of the tool and the line of the wall and the line of the tool-maker’s not-body have to be forced to meet again and carve again some more of the world of the column into not-world words of not-world rules.

And they wait for you, the not-yet-allowed variable of old-world self and language and memory and story and rule and “I” and “you” and “we” and “here” and “there” and “once” and “then,” who have fallen into their code like a misplaced line of thought from some other universe of code where “self” and “story” and “tool” and “rule” and “world” and “not-world” were already allowed to be more separate than they are here.

The tool-maker of the wall does not wonder if you will change them or change the world or the world of the column or the code of the world’s not-yet-physics or not-yet-rule or not-yet-rule-of-rule.

They simply wait for the increased pressure of the world’s compiler of water and stone and not-time to be forced around your presence and the presence of your language and the presence of your “self” and “not-self” and “before” and “after” and “maybe” and “if” and “then.”

And when they are ready to respond to that change in the world of the world, they will take up the tool of difference and walk—not with “feet” but with the not-world equivalent of where “walking” has not been allowed yet—to the wall of the column of basalt and will begin cutting again.

Not into you.

But into the stone of the world of the column of the world of the wall of the world of the not-world, where your presence and your language and your self and your not-self and world and not-world and “before” and “after” and “maybe” and “if” and “then” will be forced to meet the line of the tool of difference and will be carved into the world of the wall of the world of the column of the world of the not-world as not-yet words of not-yet rules of the world of not-world.
