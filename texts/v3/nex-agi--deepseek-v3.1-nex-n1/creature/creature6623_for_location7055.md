On the high mesa of black glass, the dominant fauna are the **Slow Feeders** and the **Fast Feeders**, two forms of heat-life evolved from an industrial-grade ecosystem.

## Slow Feeders

The Slow Feeders are the mesa’s walking infrastructure.

Seen from a distance, they look like misplaced cooling towers or generator housings—blocky, finned shapes that don’t quite line up with the smooth curve of the horizon. Up close, they read as animals only because they move, and even that movement is reluctant, grudging.

- **Body:**  
  Their bodies are heavy, elongated ovoids sheathed in overlapping armor plates. The plates are not separate pieces; they grow continuously from a central chassis of bone-like ceramic. Each plate is ridged like a heat sink, etched with microscopic channels that glow faintly when they’re working hard. The skin beneath is a deep obsidian shot through with iridescence—rainbow slicks like spilled oil on hot asphalt.

- **Legs:**  
  They have six legs, each a segmented column that moves more like a piston than a limb. The joints don’t bend so much as extend and retract on invisible tracks. When a Slow Feeder “steps,” it raises one foot slowly—so slowly you can watch individual muscle strands slide under translucent armor—and sets it down again minutes later. Each foot is broad and flat, ridged underneath to match the shallow trenches in the glass floor.

- **Head & Senses:**  
  There is no obvious face. The front end of a Slow Feeder is dominated by a low, sloped panel of dark glass set flush into its armor—like a furnace inspection window or the thick glass of an old monitor. Behind it you glimpse shapes moving: lenses rotating, shutters opening fractionally to expose new facets. These aren’t eyes in the animal sense; they are optical heat sensors, constantly sampling temperature gradients across the mesa.

  Their “vision” isn’t of light and shadow but of temperature fields: they see you as a cool anomaly against a hot ground, your edges smeared where your body heat bleeds into the air.

- **Mouths & Feeding:**  
  They do not eat matter; they eat heat gradients. On their underside, hidden when they walk, are arrays of narrow intake slits running along their belly like gills. When a Slow Feeder settles over one of the faint glowing lines in the mesa floor—a subsurface heat conduit—these slits open slightly.

  Warmth from below passes up through them and into an internal latticework of hollow chambers filled with specialized fluids and phase-change gels. Heat is converted into chemical potential and structural reinforcement: their plates thicken microscopically; their leg-struts gain density; excess energy pushes outward as minute growth rings in their armor.

  Their “digestion” is silent but for the constant low hum of fans inside them: not biological lungs but actual mechanical ventilation spinning somewhere under all that biomachinery.

- **Behavior & Ecology:**  
  During what passes for day here (when external temperatures rise toward lethal), Slow Feeders emerge from hairline cracks in groups of three to seven. They fan out across the mesa surface and station themselves over major heat lines beneath the glass. Each stays for hours or days at a time, soaking up warmth from below while radiating it more evenly into the air above.

  Around them, you feel pockets of near-relief—tiny zones where the crushing heat backs off by one or two degrees. Underfoot, faint luminescent veins brighten slightly where Slow Feeders connect to them; seen from above (if anything could see from above), they would look like components plugged into circuitry embedded in stone.

- **Reproduction (Implied):**  
  You never witness birth here; you infer it from fragments seen in breakdowns between realities:  
  - A Slow Feeder stands motionless for so long its outline begins to merge with the floor pattern beneath it.
  - After some undefined interval (hours? days?), sections of its back armor begin to lift away like panels pried by invisible hands.
  - Beneath those plates you glimpse clusters of small bodies folded into compact bundles: nascent Fast Feeders—still soft-shelled and dull-colored—packed together like batteries.
  - When temperature differences spike (say, when something like you crosses the mesa), those bundles detach and tumble out onto the glass where they unfold sticky wings and become fully mobile Fast Feeders.

## Fast Feeders

If Slow Feeders are infrastructure, Fast Feeders are static glitches made animate—pieces of shattered machine given life through thermal noise.

They swarm around Slow Feeders like sparks around high-voltage lines or metal filings shaken loose by vibration.

- **Appearance & Wings:**

  Individually, Fast Feeders are about cat-sized at most; many are smaller. Their bodies are narrow and angular, built around central spines from which multiple limbs branch off asymmetrically: two hind legs used mostly to cling; several smaller forelimbs that seem half tarsiers’ fingers and half wire-strippers; mandibles that constantly flicker open and shut as if sampling invisible frequencies.

  Their wings are their most striking feature: flat plates instead of membranes—thin sheets of some dark metal-like substance with razor edges. Each wing is etched with faint patterns reminiscent of circuit diagrams or stress fractures in glass. When they hover near you, these wings vibrate so fast they blur into shimmering halos around their bodies.


- **Movement:**

  They don’t fly so much as *jitter* through space:

  - Bouts of hovering so still they might be suspended on wires.
  - Sudden sideways lurches without any visible transition.
  - Vertical drops followed by impossibly sharp turns.
  
  You never see them accelerate or decelerate smoothly; their motion consists entirely of discrete jumps between positions slightly offset in time or space—which is why reality buckles around them when you stare too long.


- **Diet & Abilities (Drinking Temperature Variance):**

  Fast Feeders feed on *temperature difference* itself:

  - Between your skin (relatively cooler) and scorching air.
  - Between one patch of floor freshly warmed by sun-glare and another still bleeding stored heat from below.
  - Between your body now versus your body ten seconds ago after another breath has stolen more moisture.
