On the last table of the world live Neshians.

### Bodies and breath

They are roughly human in shape, but nothing about them is built for the open sky of other worlds. They are low and compact and efficient with their weight.

They are slightly shorter than the average of most humans, but they seem smaller than even that measurement would suggest because they are rarely fully upright.

When they are outdoors or under the high parts of the forest canopy they stand on two legs and walk with a short, careful gait that makes the most of their wide, padded feet and broad toes that spread in the Neesh moss.

But when inside the pavilion, where ceilings are low and rooms are shallow chambers carved into the bone lattice of the table, they more often move on all fours or in shallow crouches that do not require them to bend their heads down. They say that “true height” is a measure of how much space you can take up without disturbing the stone. The Neshians are not tall people, but they are dense people.

---

Their skin is thick and resilient, more like leathery than like smooth, but not rough to the touch unless it has been exposed to the wind of the outer rim for too long. The tone of their skin tends to be dark—from a deep gray-brown that matches the ironstone lattice of the table itself to a rich black that shows faint traces of the mineral veining in their bones.

The most visible adaptation of their bodies are the faint, almost-subtle bars of iridescence that run along their shoulders and their ribs and the outer sides of their forearms and thighs. These are not tattoos but traces of mineralized dermis, where the body stores some of the metals and dusts that would otherwise accumulate in the blood. If you look closely you can see they are not single stripes but thin lines woven into one another like a living lace.

---

Their lungs sit deep and wide in their chests, and they breathe slowly but thoroughly, taking just enough of the thin air of the table and not requiring more. The Neshians are careful to avoid breathing too shallowly or too deeply, because their breath is not just for them but for the moss.

The exhaled breath of a Neshian carries more moisture than their inhale does, and the Neesh moss and the windleaf surfaces are tuned to the steady humidity and warmth of many bodies breathing quietly together.

Their lungs do not flare like firestorms from the inside of their chests but pulse like the slow, low-pressure bellows of a world sealed off from rushing storms. They have not needed to project the voice across long territories for many generations, and so their voices are low and murmuring and never meant to carry far.

---

### Faces and senses and minds of stone

Neshian faces are not overly exaggerated in any one direction. They have eyes—two, usually—dark-brown irises surrounded by a faint ring of darker, almost metallic black that glows a little when the starlight passes through it at a certain angle.

They are usually not bothered by the low light of the table’s night sky. They do not have bright, torchlike eyes, but they can see clearly across the dim of the forest’s edge and the deep under the pavilion’s low ceilings.

When they are outside the city of niches and out in the central table they are often more cautious. Their vision is not built for wide open spaces but for close, dense surfaces of stone and filament and moss.

---

Their hearing is not unusually sharp, but it is tuned to the long notes of the world around them instead of to sudden sharp noises.

They can hear the difference in the sound of the windleaves when the wind of the world below changes slightly; they can read the faint shift in tone of the bone lattice beneath the Neesh moss as it settles. Neshians do not try to separate out “sound” from other information; they listen to it as they might listen to the shifting of someone’s body in the next room—a constant background of knowing, rather than a set of alarms to be decoded.

---

Most of their thinking takes place in the low murmur of the body.

They are not slow in the sense of being stupid; but they are slow in the sense that they know they will have time to answer any question that the city asks of them. They treat the body as a kind of narrow chamber of the table itself, and they are careful not to rush their decisions in the way that might be necessary on a world with violent storms and constant threats.

They do not enjoy “surprises” in the form of sudden shocks or loud noises, but they are endlessly fascinated by subtle, long-term changes. If you tell them that two years ago the lintel of a particular niche was a fraction of a millimeter narrower than it is now, they will lean over and inspect the stone with a quiet reverence that visitors from other worlds interpret sometimes as “religious” and sometimes as “overly cautious.”

### Work and the volition-walk

Their bodies are not just engines for moving from place to place. They themselves are part of the infrastructure.

Neshians who reach the age of full adulthood can choose whether to join the “volition-walk” that slowly shapes the city of niches into new forms or to remain in the more constant rhythm of maintenance and minor care of the city’s chambers.

Those who join the volition-walk carry materials with their bodies: not heavy loads, but carefully chosen masses of moss and mineral and worked ironstone. They carry the moss in low sacks on their backs and forearms, and they carry the stones and fragments of bone lattice in their hands and on their shoulders and sometimes on their heads if the shape is particularly heavy and stable.

They do not march at a fast pace. They move at a slow promenade along the central line of the pavilion, where the ceiling is lowest and the stone is closest. They do not “push” the city into shape; they press their palms and chests and backs and the soles of their feet against the walls of the chambers and the edges of the lintels and simply allow their body’s moisture and warmth and the pressure of their mass to communicate the new desired form of the stone.

---

The “work” of the Neshians is not to do something with tools but to be something with the stone and the moss and the air around them.

They maintain their niche by being in it: their bodies’ warmth and the humidity of their breath and the oils of their skin all slowly change the properties of the bone lattice and the Neesh moss. Over time, the stone of a chamber’s floor and walls and ceiling may respond by becoming slightly softer or slightly harder, or by shifting its texture so that light behaves differently in it, or by adjusting its thickness to allow just the right amount of air to pass through.

### Movement and silence and noise and sleep

Neshians are comfortable with long pauses of silence, but only rarely do they use silence to end communication. They do not “stop talking” so much as they move into a lower register of communication.

They might hum quietly, or tap their fingers on the lintel of their chamber, or make very gentle clicks with their teeth. The combination of these sounds, with the body’s shape and posture and the temperature of the stone and moss of the room, is understood as continuing conversation.

---

When they sleep they often put not just their heads but their shoulders and ribs and the curve of their spine in contact with the stone floor or the low platform of raised moss within a chamber.

They say that a “good” night’s sleep is not one where you sleep deeply and dream of distant things but one where you wake in the morning and the bone lattice remembers the shape of your body somewhere under the moss and your body remembers the shape of the stone.

### Children and aging and death and final stone

The Neshians are not born huge but in small and tightly wrapped bodies, already densely compact and ready to live near the stone.

They do not “play” in the way of children who terrorize open fields with screams and running games. They crawl and climb and move carefully over the lintels and within the moss of the chambers. They engage in low “games” of tracing finger and palm patterns on the stone walls and watching how the moss responds to them.

As they grow older their bodies accumulate more mineral traces along their ribs and shoulders and arms and legs and the spine. The bars of iridescence on their skin become more pronounced and more complex, like a faint, living version of the ironstone lace of the table. They say that when you are very old you begin to see the table of Deimos through the same latticework that you see in your own body.

---

When they die they are not buried or burned; but their bodies are placed carefully in a shallow basin of neesh moss and allowed to slowly sink into the stone.

Over time, the bone lattice of the table itself migrates around their body, taking in not just the warmth of them but the trace metals and the faint, last rhythms of their body’s chemistry and memory as they are slowly integrated into the world.

### Relationship and social and intimate and personal space

Even though the city is dense and the chambers are small and close together and the population of Nesh is not huge but tightly packed within the long ring of the pavilion, the Neshians do not feel “claustrophobic” in the way that would be familiar from other worlds.

They say that personal space lies not in the absence of other bodies near you but in the shape of the stone around you and the control of sound from your body.

---

They do not like “shouting” or wide gestures of anger or violence; not because they fear pain but because they fear that such violence will change the shape of the world faster than they can understand it. They argue by carefully choosing not to move, or by moving very slowly, or by refusing to touch the stone of a particular lintel for a time, thereby slowly shifting the equilibrium of a room.

---

Their intimate relationships are not centered on glamorous stories or explosive romance but on the slow alignment of bodies and stone and moss.

Two lovers might not describe “passion” as a time when they lost themselves in one another so much as a time when their body’s shapes and the temperature of their breath and the pressure of their fingers on the wall were all aligned with the same intent of reshaping the world of their chamber just slightly, so that it remembers them differently afterward than it did before.

---

### Staying and leaving and the last of the world

The Neshians do not fly away from their table; or perhaps they cannot do it easily. They do not dream of “open space” so much as they dream of deeper and more intricate layers of the same table.

They live not at the beginning of a world but at the end of a long process of its settling. They know that they are not building something new anymore, but living carefully in the last of the world’s many forms.

---

When they speak of the future they often speak not of “what will grow” but “what will remain polished or worn by our bodies in the long time after we are gone,” and they do not seem frightened by the idea of leaving their body’s shape submerged in the bone lattice of the table.

---

They are the last urban people of a world that has already given away most of its lives and shapes and storms into the stone.

They are not the last because of apocalypse or cataclysm but because of exhaustion and erosion and completion of form.

And they seem to take some kind of quiet, almost content kind of dignity from this fact.

---

### What they leave behind you

When you have spent time with the Neshians you leave the table perhaps not feeling changed in the way of meeting someone who forced you to rethink violence or power or passion.

Instead you might feel as if the shape of your body has been adjusted slightly, by a fraction of a millimeter, to fit more deeply into the world’s stone.

---

That is what they are like, the ones who live on the Last Table of Deimos, in the sanctuary of Nesh.
