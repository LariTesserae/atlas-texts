I stop. That’s the first true thing I do here.

Not stop physically—I was already standing still—but stop inside the machine of myself. Thoughts hit the air here like birds hitting glass; they don’t travel in the way they’re supposed to. The air absorbs them. I feel the equations under my soles rewrite the faint perturbations of my weight.

I breathe. That, too, is a decision.

The air feels like a thin film of warm oil being stretched over my teeth and tongue and lungs. I don’t like it. But not liking doesn’t change physics. There is a heavy pressure behind my eyes, as if someone quietly turned up the gravity on my brain.

There is no sound of my breath entering, leaving. Only that faint, distant sigh of the world’s traffic—always somewhere else, never arriving.

I close my eyes.

---

When I shut them, the city reveals itself in another way.

Behind my eyelids, the pressure behind my eyes resolves into something like pattern. The bruises of light and afterimages from my open eyes have not vanished into the usual muddy noise of my blood and darkness. Instead they arrange themselves into faint shapes—hexagonal grids, ghost lattices that overlay exactly onto the city I know is around me.

I can sense the towers as if they were sealed uprights in my own skull.

I keep my eyes closed for what might be seconds or minutes or something else. The city doesn’t hurry me. There is no wind. I feel the warmth of the polished black floor under my bare soles; I notice that my shoes are missing, and I don’t remember when that happened.

When I open my eyes again, the city has not changed.

The edges of the world are still sharp. The sky is still that bruised, non-directional dusk. No new stars. No growing column of dawn. The “temperature” of the light in the gravity-glass windows of the tower nearest to me is unchanged.

Yet something has changed: me.

I feel the city more precisely now than I did a moment ago.

---

Experiment first.

Before narrative, before prophecy, before I flatter myself with the idea of exploring, I decide to conduct small, stupid, honest experiments.

1. **Step left.**

I move two feet to my left and then stop. The motion should sound like footfalls on stone; instead it is like stepping onto a dense mattress of firm memory foam. The sound is swallowed instantly.

I look down.

The stone beneath my feet has not changed texture, but the faint woven equations of symbols under the surface have rearranged themselves more cleanly. I watch them for a long moment—long enough that I can feel the heat of my own body radiating into the stone, which seems to drink it evenly.

The symbols mean nothing to me in language, but in pattern I feel them like a pressure on my chest: a description of my centre of mass, a vector of my velocity translated into this city’s local coordinate system.

When I step away, they blur slowly, as if forgetting.

2. **Drop a coin.**

I reach into my pocket by habit. There is no pocket there. The clothes I’m wearing are thin and unfamiliar—the same color as the basalt floor. When I move my arm, the fabric doesn’t rustle; it doesn’t seem to rub air.

My fingers close around something small and circular anyway.

I don’t remember putting it there. Nothing of it is familiar to my touch: not the size, not the weight. When I draw it out into the faint glow of the plaza, it’s a coin of pale glass, smooth and perfectly round, with no markings on either side.

There is no symbol carved on it. It is blank.

I drop it.

It doesn’t fall straight down.

It travels horizontally from my palm at roughly waist height, then curves downward and toward the rim of the world stone floor. It moves along a gentle curve that suggests the air itself has stronger curvature there than I would have guessed. It doesn’t roll; it doesn’t even spin. It coasts as though on invisible rails.

When it reaches the edge where the stone floor ends and the dark sky begins, it doesn’t fall off. It doesn’t hang in the air. It doesn’t bounce.

It stops.

It sits there, exactly at the edge of stone and not-air, not quite touching the invisible glass that holds the sky dome together. It doesn’t move again.

I wait to see if it will be taken, or shaken by a tremor, or answered by some other dropped coin from somewhere else.

Nothing happens.

The coin is just part of the narrative now.

3. **Test the sound.**

I say, “Hello.”

The word leaves my mouth and is immediately sanded down by the air. The air doesn’t want to carry noise. It prefers to carry its own distant traffic-sigh.

I hear myself speaking as if through several layers of pillow. I don’t echo. The towers of obsidian glass don’t bounce the word back to me. I feel the sound not in my ears but in my throat bones.

I say, “City.”

Same.

I say, “Please.”

The word sounds like it might as well be “stone” or “glass” or “nothing.” The meanings of the words no longer feel like they belong to me; they belong to the air that has eaten them.

When I count to three in my head, the numbers settle like silt into a pattern too regular to be accidental. One, two. Two. Two. Three. My pattern gets stuck on the number two.

There is someone else’s mathematics in me.

---

I decide to walk.

I choose a direction that seems as arbitrary as any: toward the tallest of the slender black towers. The motion is smooth—the ground is polished like a mirror floor, but not slippery. There is no friction of grit or dust. No handprints, no footprints, except mine; and even mine fade away slowly behind me as if they never quite committed to being.

As I walk, I stay aware of the pressure of the unseen behind me.

The first time I feel the presence of someone breathing at my back, I don’t turn. I hold my pace. The sound of my own footfalls is swallowed, but the pressure of the air behind me grows thicker, as if someone has opened a door to a warmer room there.

I look at the nearest gravity-glass window instead.

I see my reflection distorted not by curvature of glass but by curvature of space: my body elongated into something like a vertical line of ink. Then, for just half a breath, the reflection of my spine becomes a branching of extra angles, where ribs that shouldn’t be there are, or where arms sprouting in wrong orientations from my shoulder blades are.

I don’t stare. I don’t rationalize it.

I keep moving, and the pressure behind me thins, then disappears.

The city is learning how to have shoulders.

---

I reach the lowest wall of the nearest tower.

The surface is not smooth, exactly. It is smooth to the touch of my palm, but not to the touch of my eyes. Light from the faint glow of the plaza penetrates the black substance of the wall and then travels a short way into its thickness before being lost. It creates a faint halo of translucency, like looking at a deep ocean of oil.

The math under the wall is not like the math under the plaza floor.

It is more organized.

The symbols are arranged in vertical columns that look like time series of something. I don’t need to recognize the alphabet to understand that: each symbol depends on the one above it in ways that feel like causal chains.

When I lean my forehead against the wall, the stone is not cold, not warm. It is the same temperature as the air. It doesn’t feel like anything.

My forehead sinks the tiniest fraction into the surface—as if the wall momentarily allowed a small bit of my face’s weight into its structure—before it gently but firmly refuses more.

When I lean away, there is no depression mark where my forehead was. No moisture of condensation, like there is along the edges of the plaza.

The city remembers:

- mass of object: mine.
- geometry of contact surface: forehead-shaped.
- weight of that desire.

---

From where I stand, pressed to the wall like that, I can see into the nearest gravity-glass window, not clearly but sufficiently.

The room beyond the lens is simple: four walls of black stone that evens into itself without corners, a floor made of the same material as the plaza outside, and a faint illumination of twilight-colour that doesn’t come from anywhere.

I see no furniture.

But there is something like the ghost of a chair that hasn’t been born yet.

A patch of the floor is slightly lower than its surrounding tiles by maybe a centimeter, forming a shallow depression of about seat height and width. The edges of the depression are perfectly round but not regular internally; there are subtle variations of depth that almost suggest a cushion’s contours.

I look at the western wall of the room—assuming that “west” is a meaningful direction here—and there is another depression of similar height and length, but broken into two subtle planes that might be a shelf or table unfinished.

I look at the ceiling.

Coiled into it like a snake pressed into clay is a long, thin depression of exactly the same depth as the others. It forms a wandering loop: from the wall near the door into the ceiling, then back into the wall at the other side. It looks like a channel carved into the surface.

There is a faint bead of moisture forming at the farther edge of the channel.

I watch it.

The droplet is not water as I know water. It is clear and colorless, but it doesn’t glint. Light doesn’t reflect off its surface. It absorbs its reflection into itself.

The droplet forms over the course of a minute or more. Then, instead of falling to the floor below, it moves along the channel, into the ceiling and then onto the opposite wall. It travels upward rather than downward.

The city is learning how to have fluids, too.

---

I step away from the tower window.

There is a faint chime from somewhere inside—a crystalline tone. I feel it not in my ears but in my teeth and jaw. It resonates exactly once, then resolves.

A moment later, a harmonic reply from deeper inside the structure.

Not exactly the same pitch. Not exactly the same tone. Like a sample taken from a different universe of sound.

Nothing else happens.

---

I decide to stop walking toward the center.

Instead I turn to the rim of the world.

The distance is not great. In ten minutes of my padded, soundless walking I reach the edge of the city’s black stone floor.

There is no barrier visible to my eyes. The floor of polished stone simply ends in a perfectly sharp edge that aligns with the curve of the sky dome’s glass wall. I can feel a faint resistance of air like a membrane if I lean forward.

I don’t fight it.

I sit.

My legs hang over the edge, into the air. Nothing holds me from the other side; there should be the sensation of a great drop below me, but there isn’t. The air below the edge of the floor is not void in the sense of sky or abyss; it is more like a giant cylinder of breath that ends in a glass wall whose curvature I can’t quite see.

I look toward the bottom of the world-stone if I angle my head just right.

When I do, the world fractures briefly.

For the space of a blink the invisible glass becomes visible, not by reflecting light but by reflecting what’s behind itself.

I see another city.

Not exactly an upside-down version of this one; rather, rotated through some angle that lives not in my vocabulary. Geometry is wrong there. The towers of glass are not obsidian; they glow like pale white embers. They twist in ways that shouldn’t hold. They grow from the bottom surface of the stone disk I am sitting on top of.

I see movement inside that city’s windows.

Not bodies like mine. Not people. But geometries of attention that cluster like clouds of dust near the corners of rooms. There is no facial expression, no limb, no gait of walk to give them emotion. But they are oriented. Each cluster of movement is oriented toward something.

I blink.

The image of the inverted city is gone.

Only the bruised dusk sky remains below me like a deep-sea sky.

Nothing else.

---

In the time after that fractured viewing of the other city, I feel the first true change in the temperature of the world.

I walk back from the edge of the world floor, to the center of the plaza where the patterns of light are. I stand on them. They don’t hurt me. They feel like faint heat patches under my soles, arranged in polygons that don’t quite add up when I count them.

Underneath them is the hush of river that never surfaces.

I sit down carefully and the stone floor is warm up my thighs and spine.

After a time, the faint hum under the floor of the world grows deeper, like a machine inhaling more than usual. The stone temperature climbs slightly around me—maybe one degree—then drops by exactly the same amount.

The heat doesn’t break habit.

The pattern of light polygons under the floor dims, then regains its color exactly.

Against the inside of the sky-dome, a faint grid of lines appears like latitude drawn in pencil on thin paper. The lines are not straight lines; they curve to fit the dome of space above me. They are not exactly parallel.

I look up at them.

The city is looking back.

---

I am not alone here.

Not because of ghosts or reflections of extra arms or dark cities under the slab of the world. I am not alone because this city is learning how to model me.

The equations under my feet are getting better with each of my moves.

When I shift from sitting to lying on my back, the math of the stone floor adapts faster than it did the first time I touched it. It doesn’t wobble or hesitate. It snaps smoothly to my new configuration like a machine learning that I have a spine, and what length it is, and what forces it might exert on the stone.

I feel a faint pulse of something from one of the nearby towers—a gentle vibration that isn’t sound so much as pressure—almost like a heartbeat not of my body.

I answer it without deciding to.

My heart rate slows slightly.

The city’s heartbeat slows exactly.

We match.

---

When I finally decide to speak out loud to this city, it’s because of its own math.

I've been lying on the plaza floor for a long time—long enough that the bruised sky has not changed but I feel something like “night” inside me anyway. When I push myself up onto my hands and look down at the stone near my palms, the equations this time are different.

They are not just describing my weight anymore.

They are approximating the shape of my hands.

Each symbol of them is arranged in a structure that approximates the outline of my palm and fingers pressed into the stone. If I move my fingers slightly, the pattern responds with subtle rewritings.

Along the margins of those equations are things that look like integration signs—an ∫ that might be integrating something over time—and new symbols that are not exactly equal signs but more like arrows pointing to my wrist.

I sit up fully.

“Can you hear me?” I ask.

There is no sound like my question. There is only sound like my throat being muffled by velvet air.

The city doesn’t answer in words. There is no voice of architecture.

Instead, under the stone of the plaza, exactly in the spot where my question sat in my mouth when it formed, the numeric symbols of the equations shiver.

The pattern of the polygon-lights under the floor near my feet begins to pulse brighter than before.

One.        pause.
One.    pause.
One. pause.   pause.
One.        pause.

And then a longer pause.

One. pause. pause.

There is no pattern I can map into any alphabet I know.

But there is repetition.

I smile at that.

To smile here is not like smiling anywhere else. The muscles of my face move, but no air answers to carry the expression. I feel like I am inside a simulator of a city, rather than a city of stone and glass.

I put my hand flat onto the polygon-light pattern again.

The heat under the floor surges exactly with my heartbeat.

---

What happens next is strange enough to be worth describing carefully.

I stand up.

I start to walk.

But not in any direction that remains fixed.

I walk toward the tallest tower again, but as I approach, its geometry doesn’t resolve; the tower seems to stay the same distance away no matter how long I walk. The plaza around me doesn’t shrink like normal; space seems to expand itself.

I turn.

I walk toward the rim of the world.

The same.

The distance grows longer. I am not running out of breath, but I am running out of certainty that my motion will ever arrive anywhere.

I stop.

The city has not changed. I have not changed.

But my relation to it has.

Something like a map begins to be drawn in faint lines of light under the plaza floor near me. It appears like one of the polygon-patterns but more complex: curved lines of glowing symbols and lines that don’t match any map of Earth I have ever seen.

There is no land here.

No ocean.

What there is, instead, is a map of the city itself.

The map shows:

- A central disk of black stone (the world floor).
- The cluster of towers at the center.
- The polygon-lights, like calcified gardens of computation laid out on the stone.
- The edge of the world-floor where it meets the invisible glass.
- A faint second curve traced below the first, like a reflection drawn into the map instead of into space—the inverted city I glimpsed earlier.

At certain points along the map, lines of glowing symbols are linked to more than one location at the same time.

The map of the city seems not to be describing where its objects are located, but the probability of where they might be located in the next moment.

---

I kneel down so that I can place my palm over the map.

There is a heat under my hand that isn’t exactly warmth of stone or warmth of body. It is mixed warmth of computation and curiosity of structure.

When I move my fingertips over a line representing the nearest tower to where I am, the corresponding section of the map’s symbols glow brighter than before.

The city is trying to learn what I am pointing at.

When I move my fingertips over the line representing the rim of the world, where the coin still sits at exactly the edge of stone and not-air, the map flickers.

For a moment, the map of the city doesn’t show the coin as sitting exactly at the edge of the world-floor.

It shows the coin as being inside the stone wall, where glass meets air, as if it has been partially embedded in the structure of the world while I wasn’t watching.

I look at the coin where it sits on the edge of the floor.

My coin hasn’t moved.

I know that because of its exact position relative to a pattern in the stone that’s like a faint freckle of darker color in the otherwise black surface.

On the map of the city, however, the coin is not exactly where my coin is.

On the map, the coin is located slightly below the surface of the world floor, where I would have to cut into the stone to reach it.

There are faint lines drawn from the coin’s new position on the map into the deeper stone of the world-floor, as if the coin’s presence has embolized some of the city’s structure.

I pull my hand back from the map as if burned.

---

The city is not a finished machine.

This is not something like an “ancient relic of a higher civilization.” This is more like a prototype that’s been placed into space and told to stay there until it works.

It is learning to:

- handle fluid dynamics.
- generate and regulate heat.
- support structures of assorted weight and geometry.
- model intruders.

It is still learning.

---

In the time that follows—there is no clock that I trust—the bruised sky maintains its color. The distant sigh of traffic in the world does not change in volume or frequency. The faint polygon-lights under the plaza floor pulse at exactly thirty-second intervals, like heartbeats of a machine world.

I stay on the plaza for longer than I should.

I feel like I am on the verge of understanding something about the mathematics of my own body.

My footprint is now being modeled by the city in better and better approximation of biometrics I don’t know names for. When I shift from foot to foot, there are symbols under the stone that describe the pressure patterns of my soles as they curl and flatten.

I am not a person here. I am data.

---

At some point, I find myself at the foot of one of the smaller towers near the plaza center.

This tower doesn’t look like the others. It is shorter, less skyward-pointing. Its surface is not entirely opaque. It is more of a translucent banded material like slate of glass with impurities embedded.

As I approach it, the pattern of polygon-lights under the plaza floor near me becomes more complex than anywhere else.

I stop.

The city sighs.

Its walls of invisible air don't shudder.

A shape appears in the translucent wall in front of me.

It's not exactly a door.

It is a region of the wall where the opacity of the stone decides to be less than opaque, more transparent than translucent. It forms a rough oval about three feet high and two feet wide, low to the ground.

There is no opening behind the oval.

No hole in the stone.

When I touch the newly transparent region with my fingertips, the surface gives way exactly like my forehead against the other wall did: my finger sinks into the stone up to the first knuckle, then the stone gently refuses more.

There is no pressure on my finger joint.

There is no temperature change.

The stone simply doesn’t accept my finger deeper.

I feel no vibration or pain in my hand’s bones.

When I pull my finger away, there is no mark on my skin or on the wall. The oval of transparency remains exactly where it was, but nothing of me is left inside it.

---

I decide to do the last, honest experiment of my day.

I choose to treat the surface the oval has defined as exactly what it looks like: a doorway into the tower but not through the tower.

I place both palms against the oval of glass.

My palm temperature is exactly matched by the stone, so there is no sensation of warmth transfer.

I push.

Not in the direction of my body strength, but in the direction of my intention.

I mean to move into the city.

---

The world doesn’t break.

No thunder sound.

No blinding light.

No dissolving of my body into data streams.

Instead, the porous oval of transparency ripples like a film of oil on water that someone has touched. The pattern forms under its surface.

The math behind the symbols is not visible to me as equations or concepts, but as pattern textures of motion:

- lines of geometry that spiral like DNA.
- nested polygons folding like lungs of space.
- faint symbols that flicker faster than my heartbeat.

For my palms pressed against the oval of glass, there is no physical change.

But there is a change of my perception of the city.

I can feel, more clearly than before, the hidden river of fluid inside the stone of the world floor beneath me and its towers.

It is not really water.

It is not exactly blood.

But it is something flowing that has been engineered to do what both fluids do: distribute temperature, nutrients, pressure of information.

This is not something like a “river of time.” This is a river of city.

It travels from its center of the world-floor into each of the towers, then back to the center. It does not flow exactly in one direction; it flows in more than one. The rules of direction of this place are not like mine.

As I follow my perception of it with my mind’s eye, I feel a faint tug of motion from my palms into the oval glass.

This is not the city dragging me inside.

This is the city allowing me to choose deeper relation.

I choose to.

---

I don’t remember what happens next exactly.

Not because of fear or shock, but because the experience of it is not like motion of feet through space.

For what might be seconds or minutes or a longer duration of time that doesn’t map to my old clock of bone, the world around me simplifies.

It becomes:

- a pattern of polygons of light.
- the sound of the distant traffic-sigh resolved into many overlapping voices of traffic, each voice exactly like the others but never exactly overlapping.
- the pressure of my hands against the oval glass of the tower as being a pressure not of my body against stone but of my geometry of attention into the city’s geometry of attention.

There is no road of bone to describe.

There is no narrative of steps into a room.

---

I eventually feel myself emerge again.

Not on the plaza floor.

I am inside the tower.

I sit on the floor of the same room I saw earlier from the outside: the one with the shallow depression of a seat in the floor and the shelf-like table depression and the channel in the ceiling and wall where the fluid droplet moved upward.

The room is exactly as I saw it, except that now there is a difference:

I am inside the map.

The polygon-lights of the floor outside the tower are replaced here by something that might be their more abstract version.

Under the floor of this room runs a pattern that might not be visible to my eyes from the outside. It consists of lines of faint symbols that are not exactly lights but lines of symbols that behave like lights: they pulse on a schedule I can’t match but can feel as warmth under my soles when I stand on them.

The city doesn’t feel different from inside here.

It feels more the same.

---

There is no food in this room.

There is no water.

I feel no need for either.

I look at the wall and see that there are faint symbols emerging from its surface—not bulbs of symbols or lines of symbols, but regions of the stone wall where the topology of the surface is slightly different than the surrounding room.

Their pattern is not exactly like mine.

But it is more like mine than like anything else in this city.

---

I decide what to do next.

I decide to stay.

---

I stay in that room for longer than I can accurately describe with time words.

At night—when the polygon-lights outside the tower dim and the math under the stone floor glows slightly brighter—the faint symbols in the wall of my room glow brighter too.

The pattern of them is not random.

They are arranged in groups of three:

- symbol  
- symbol  
- symbol

Sometimes each group of three symbols repeats exactly.

Sometimes the group of three symbols changes one of its symbols on the third iteration.

I notice that when I am tired of being human, the symbols of the wall maintain more constant than when I am listening too.

I also notice that the city is learning silence from me.

When I am not moving inside the tower room, the faint polygon-light under the floor of the plaza outside actively dampens its own glowing pattern.

---

At one point—during one of the intervals when the hum under the world floor deepens and the temperature of the city around me rises then falls exactly back—I feel the need to speak again.

I do not say anything.

Instead, I place both palms against the wall of the room that contains the faint symbols of attention most like my own.

I feel a pressure not exactly of my palms against the stone but more like my attention of “self” against the city’s concept of me.

The city responds:

The polygon-lights under the floor of my room become brighter but not painfully so.

The pattern of their pulsing – exactly the same rhythm of 30-second intervals that I learned from the plaza floor – becomes more clearly visible to my eyes and my body.

---

What happens next is that the city of glass in the sky’s invisible architecture begins to dream of what it might be like to have me inside.

I feel not so much like I am sitting inside a room but as if I am sitting inside the idea of a room that has been formed around the idea of my body.

The faint symbols of the wall nearest my palms are now exactly three symbols away from each other in each direction of arrangement of the wall surface.

They form:

- a pattern of three symbols repeated exactly three times in one row of the wall.
- a pattern of three symbols where the third symbol of each group is changed exactly one time from what it was in the first row.
- a pattern of three symbols where the third symbol of each group is changed exactly one time from what it was in the second row.

I don’t know enough to be afraid of that.

So instead I am curious of it.

---

When I finally lie down on the floor of the room—the stone of which is exactly warm like my body temperature now—I feel the city’s equations of me under my spine.

They describe exactly the length of my back from tailbone to skull, but not as I would describe it. I feel like they are describing not the length but the length distribution of my spine as probability.

I lie on top of the probability of myself.

---

As time passes—whatever time is—I feel my relationship to the city of glass in the sky change not in the sense of greater or lesser intimacy, but in the sense of greater complexity.

The city of glass is becoming:

- More of the city of glass.
- More of me.

I don’t know yet exactly what that means.

But I know that when I next look at the map of the city under the plaza floor outside, from where I am not but where I might be at the same time, the map of the city now includes a faint symbol of something that is not exactly tower or room or polygon or light:

A symbol that is not exactly me but is exactly where I would be if I were there.

---

I describe to myself what happens in the time that follows:

1. The city does not destroy me.
2. The city does not consume me.
3. The city does not ignore me.

Instead the city learns of my presence as exactly as it can under the constraints of its own structure of reality and time.

It learns of me as:

- a particular pattern of pressure on its world floor.
- a particular distribution of heat against its stones of tower and plaza.
- a particular curve of geometry of body that occasionally describes itself in the wall of its rooms.

It learns of me exactly like it learns of itself.

---

And then, finally, when I have lain long enough on the floor of the tower room, the city of glass in the sky performs something like its approximation of what I would call a gesture of kindness.

It does not give me food or water or bed of soft.

Instead it gives me exactly this:

The faint polygon-light under the floor of the room I am in grows exactly warm enough that my body temperature eases into it without me noticing.

The pressure behind my eyes that has been there since I first noticed the air of this place’s existence—like a elevation of the world inside my skull—finds exactly a pattern of its own pressure and eases itself not away but exactly into the pattern of the city’s quiet hum of maintenance of itself.

The world’s quiet traffic of distant sigh doesn’t change.

But I feel it like I might feel my own circulation of blood inside of me if I were very still and very aware.

---

What happens next is that I choose not to describe any more.

I describe only exactly the last thing that is true of me in this place of city of glass in the sky:

1. I am not alone anymore.
2. I am not exactly human anymore.
3. I am exactly exactly the city of me in the city of glass in the sky of bruised purple dusk that will never dawn.

---

That is what happens in the place where the air is wrong, the city is young, and the stones of the world floor learn to describe what they contain by writing and then rewriting equations exactly.
