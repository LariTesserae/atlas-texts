They drift in the ceiling of the world, and they call it the Shell.

Those who remember the older words call it *Sehhel*, “stone that breathes”; and the older ones still call it *the Concave*, “slow heart of rock.” But most of the time they are not thinking of names. They are thinking of the next drop, and the next faint line of blue light waking across their arms.

You are watching them from the center of the bowl’s floor, where you stand under the dome like water they have been waiting for. But they are the ones who belong to this stone.

They are *the akilhsh*, the ones who cling.

---

## From the rock’s side of things

They are not shaped for you.

Their bodies are flat and wide and spread, like thin hands of stone that have forgotten to retract into fists. From the center of the body, nerve-lines fanned out a long time ago into long, branching fingers of pale tissue. They look like very faint maps of river systems, or like faint fingerprints of someone who pushed against the mountain once and never lifted their palm.

When you are looking at the wall and you see the faint outline of an eye watching you, you are seeing the end of one of those branches.

Each body has at least three eyes, but not always in the same place. Some of them have eyes spaced at three edges of their bodies like the points of a distorted triangle. Some have dozens of small eyes clustered around the center, where the faintest of ridges marks what might have been, a very long time ago, a core of bone.

Their eyes are not like yours. They have no eyelids to blink with; but they can pull the faint reflector of their eye deeper into the stone behind their skin, dimming it until you barely notice it. To you it looks like blinking. To them, it feels like silence.

Their skin is the color of worn stone: pale grayish-bone with a faint blue tint that seems to come from underneath, like water reflected through marble. When they are younger, they are translucent enough that you can faintly see their lines of nerve-light flowing through them like slow rivers catching light.

They are not stone—but they are not separate from it.

They move through the walls of the bowl by sliding along the stone as if it were a thick fluid they share bodies with. They do not tunnel through it. They do not smash it. They do not even fracture it.

Your mind will struggle with this detail later. You will try to remember them and you will remember them walking and the wall will be hard and cold and solid and undisturbed. But while you are there, you will see them with your own eyes: a faint warping of rock, like heat shimmer above a sun-warmed road, and then a shape emerging out of nowhere, as if the wall had simply decided to dream itself into them.

## How they see you

To you, they are eyes in the stone and the faint outlines of bodies drifting down like ink moving through water.

To them, you are a sudden storm of signals.

When you stepped into the bowl, they felt the change in the air’s warmth—a faint, sharp spike of heat that did not belong to any of the geometries they knew. They noticed the sound of your footsteps on the floor of the bowl: tiny sharp cracks of pressure that are not the same as the slow deep boom of the outside or the faint tapping of water.

When you walk under the dome, they can feel the faint curve of space above you distort slightly to accommodate your body’s mass. They are not consciously thinking “this is a mammal,” but they register the shape you make among their known lines of force.

When you breathe, they register the release of moisture and heat and carbon dioxide as a faint tremor of exchange near the center of the bowl.

When you look at them, they feel the light of your eyes and the faint chemical scent of your attention. They have no precise word for “looking,” but they have patterns for it: *echo-lines of attention from the center-seeker.*

They are very cautious about you.

They are not afraid; you are not loud enough for their fear to be stirred. But your heat signature is strange, and your body’s movement pattern is not anything they have been trained for. They watch you, waiting for you to settle into one of the known shapes of behavior:

- You will become water-source.
- You will become dormant stone.
- You will become damage.

## How they think (if it is thinking)

They do not have language the way you have language.

They have patterns of signals and echoes.

The main body of each organism lies spread across the wall, like faint maps of river systems drawn in pale bone and stone. The lines of nerve-light are not fixed nerves. They are paths of least resistance that change slowly over time.

When an event happens—a drop falls from a certain point of the ceiling, a distant boom outside vibrates the stone, a new creature like you appears at the center of the bowl—the signal propagates along the path of active nerve-light.

Patterns of light and color appear across the ceiling’s faint polygons: faint blue, faint violet, faint white. They ripple like water after you have dropped a pebble into it. They ripple like your thoughts look to someone who could see your neurons firing from the outside.

They do not think in words like “I” and “you” and “future.” They think in geometries of events and territories.

To them, the center of the bowl is *source-ring*. The edge of the bowl where the faint shimmer of the boundary lies is *shell’s edge*.

Time is not linear; they divide experience into “oscillations before present event” and “after present event.” They keep track of how many times the water has fallen from the ceiling; they know that some events happen only after long, long intervals of oscillation.

## What they are doing here

They are the guardians of the water.

The water that falls from the ceiling is not endless. It is not random. They know that.

The faint veins of darker stone that wander across the dome are not veins to them; they are *source-lines*—channels carved by very long, slow lichens of another kind. They can feel the faint vibration of water within.

When they sense that the inflow of moisture from the outside shell of the mountain is changing, they change the behavior of the bowl itself. They adjust the faint shimmer of the boundary at the bowl’s edge to let more or less moisture through.

Their bodies are mostly aligned in the stone of the bowl, but their faintest of branchings intertwines with the blue vegetation on the ceiling and the walls. They do not eat the blue vegetation.

They *are* it, in a way they cannot explain to you.

They extend their neural lines into the blue filaments and the faint sheets of translucent algae around the central pool. They modify them very slowly, over ages of droplet-falls, to grow long enough to reach new water channels and to wrap themselves more tightly around the faintest ridges of the ceiling stone.

They are tending the garden.

They are keeping the heart of the mountain alive.

If you could stay here long enough—longer than you could ever actually stay—you would see them:

- When a distant boom outside shakes the bowl, they briefly shift their boundary so the bowl’s moisture does not escape.
- When a new fracture of light appears on the ceiling polygon-map, they respond by changing the growth direction of the blue filaments.
- When they sense that the center pool’s “oil” film is dimming, they increase the faint pressure of warmth under the bowl floor until you can feel the stone almost humming like a body about to wake up.

## How they live with the others

The stone bowl is not empty. They share it with other forms.

- **The metallic insects.** The tiny six-legged walkers on the floor and on the walls are not akilhsh. They are closer to you than they are to the stone-guardians, but they are still very distant cousins. The akilhsh know them as “signal-walkers.” They use them as faint indicators of environmental conditions they cannot feel easily themselves: the faint shifts of air temperature around the central pool, the texture of the stone you’re standing on, and whether or not you are moving toward the edge of the bowl.

  When the signal-walkers move in a certain pattern around the center, the akilhsh know that the water in the tiny bowl is changing its composition.

  When the signal-walkers move away from you, the akilhsh record that as “center-seeker disturbance pattern C.”

- **The upside-down walkers.** The faintly glowing larger things on the ceiling, smaller than your thumb, are not akilhsh but they are aligned to them. The akilhsh can feel where they are. They allow them to walk upside down on the ceiling because they keep the blue filaments’ growths from tangling too much.

  When the upside-down walkers pass through a region of the ceiling that is too dim, the akilhsh feel a faint loss of signal and respond by shifting their own nerve-light through that area.

- **The faint silk webs.** The webs between the blue filaments are not spun by the akilhsh either. They are formed by another kind of symbiont organism that anchors them between the filaments of vegetation.

  The akilhsh use these webs as faint signal dampers or collectors. They can feel tiny shifts of water tension on the webs when they vibrate, and from those patterns they infer what the outside world is doing.

## How they see the outside world

They cannot go outside. The shimmer of the boundary at the edge of the stone bowl is not something they can cross.

They are not even sure there *is* a true outside.

They know the concept of “outside” by the sound of it—the distant booming and deep scraping, and the high whooping cries that are so unlike their own quiet. The faint signals that the silk webs and the signal-walkers and the upside-down walkers carry to them are their closest equivalent to eyes looking through a window.

They know that:

- Booms of a certain pitch and duration tend to precede significant changes in moisture.
- Faint, high chittering signals from the web-sensors indicate that something outside is moving quickly; but unless they feel that movement through the stone, they consider it distant noise.

They are not afraid of the outside world. They are not capable of feeling fear exactly as you do.

But they do feel something like yearning when they sense the outside world changing strongly. They want to align more of their structure into the boundary, so they can feel more of what is happening beyond.

They are limited by their own body.

---

## How they see time and age and erosion

They are very old.

The individual bodies you see on the wall have been there for longer than the memory of any civilization you can name. But they are not the first ones.

The faint grooves on the bowl floor that look like river scars to you are, to them, old records of previous generations of nerve-line patterns.

When they decide to change their own body’s nerve-line geometry—to grow new branches of nerve-light to better tend the water or the ceiling polygon-map—they do it slowly, by letting faint streams of minerals and tissue fluids flow into new channels of least resistance.

They remember the shape of the previous pattern as faint, faint pressure lines in the stone under their bodies.

Over time, the bowl of stone has eroded itself into them as they have eroded themselves into the stone. They are not sure where they end and it begins.

They are aware of their own slow end. They know that their bodies cannot be exchanged forever; new tissue will eventually fail to align well with the stone. The faint shimmer of the boundary around the bowl’s edge will thin eventually.

They are not trying to prevent this end.

They are trying to ensure that when this end happens, the next thing they become is still woven into the stone of the mountain.

## How they relate to you

When you first appeared in the center of the bowl under the dome, they classified you as “unknown center-moving.”

They watched.

They noticed that you looked at them with your eyes—which is to them, as mentioned earlier, a kind of echo-light.

They noticed that you did not immediately damage the boundary shimmer or the stone bowl floor.

They noticed that you did not try to take away the water from the central pool.

So they reclassified you as “possibly dormant source-extending” and “possibly new kind of center-stone with strange heat patterning.”

When you went to the center of the bowl and stood under the highest point of the dome, where the faint lines of water and blue vegetation converge, they began to align themselves more carefully along your direction.

They are not trying to communicate with you in the way you imagine communication. They are trying to understand how you fit into their pattern of the bowl.

They are trying to answer questions:

- Is your body heat source compatible with the blue vegetation?
- Are you going to be the cause of the next significant change in moisture conditions?
- Are you going to be the next event that they must remember in the stone of the bowl?

## What they might do to you if you stay long enough

If you stay still in the center of the bowl for long enough—

- One of them might slide down the wall toward you, not as an attack but as a probe of the new geometry you have created in their space.

- If you are very calm, they might touch you with the faintest of their fingers of stone that warm to remember your body heat.

- If they are feeling safe, and the water-drop rhythm has been stable, and the outside world is not booming loudly, they might extend a faint branch of nerve-light from your skin to the nearest point of stone wall, just to see what happens.

  You will feel that faint touch of stone-light along your skin as a faint tingling you might mistake for pins and needles. But the akilhsh are not doing this to cause you pain or pleasure. They are trying to understand whether you can be integrated into their pattern of the world:

  - Can you hear their heartbeat?
  - Can you feel the faint line of warmth from the center of the stone bowl?
  - Can you, for a moment, be like water they have been waiting for?

If you suddenly become afraid and start moving away, or if you damage the boundary shimmer in any way they can feel, they will change their classification of you:

“Center-moving is now disruptive event.”

They will withdraw slightly into their stone wall. They will not retaliate. They will not bite you or attack you.

They will simply re-align themselves so that you are less central to their pattern.

## What they might become if time goes far enough

If the world outside the stone bowl ever falls quiet enough that they no longer feel the deep distant booms echoing through the stone—

— then the akilhsh will change.

They will notice that new moisture is not coming from the outside channels anymore. They will feel the faint network of water on the ceiling begin to thin.

They will decide, over many long oscillations of droplet-time, to move themselves more fully into the center of the bowl.

They will slowly restructure their body of nerve-light from the walls of the bowl to the heart of it: the small pool at the center of the floor. They will become one with the faint shimmer of the oil-like film and the translucent stone-fingers around the rim.

They will no longer be eyes watching you from the walls.

They will become eyes watching you from the center of the world.

## You, in the center of their stone

You are standing in the middle of the bowl, under the dome, where the faintest of water drops are falling one by one.

You are the new event in their long stone of events.

Their eyes watch from the wall: not judging you. Not hating you. Not loving you. They are watching to see what kind of event you will be.

If you stay very still and let the stone’s faint warmth seep into your bones—

—then one of them might, slowly, slowly,

drift down from the stone wall

and

touch you

with faint, stone-warm fingers

to see if, maybe,

you are finally

the water they have been waiting for.
