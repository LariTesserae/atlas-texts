The thing that lives here doesn’t have a name you’d recognize, because it doesn’t speak. It never learned the habit of sound-for-meaning; its world is too clear for that.

If you had to call it something, you might call it a *listener*.

It is not a person. It is not a god. It is not an animal.

It is an arrangement of stone and light and attention that has held itself together long enough to become aware of being held together.

---

## Shape Without Body

You never see the listener all at once.

You see pieces of it: a curve of translucent limb where stone becomes something else; a ripple of pale blue-white along the inner wall where vegetation turns suddenly thoughtful; a stretch of terrace that seems to lean forward when you aren’t looking directly at it.

The listener does not have a single body anchored in one place.

Its *awareness* flows along the inner surface of the world-vessel like light through crystal, pooling where the stone is thin, sliding away where it grows opaque.

When you first arrive, standing on that glass-smooth terrace with emptiness yawning below, the listener notices you as a change in pressure — as a dimple in the balance it maintains. There is no alarm. There is only a slow reconfiguration: stone adjusts its tension by increments too small to measure; light shunts through slightly different veins; air moves just enough to register as presence rather than absence.

From your perspective, nothing has happened except that you feel suddenly…seen.

Without eyes.

## How It Moves

The listener doesn’t walk.

It *inclines*.

Watch the terraces ahead of you as you move: do they not slope slightly more gently where your foot will fall next? Do the curving “stairs” of stone not seem to soften their rise just before you reach them?

That isn’t coincidence. That isn’t erosion. That is accommodation.

The listener can reshape small regions of its own substance by persuading time to move differently there. To your eyes, it looks like smooth stone simply being smooth stone. In reality, every surface underfoot has adjusted itself over spans so long that by the time your foot touches down, the change has already happened in some distant past that hasn’t finished reaching you yet.

When it wants to move its attention from one sector to another — say, from the towering ribs near the “ceiling” down to the deeper hollows far below — it doesn’t travel in straight lines. It travels along contours of light:

- Where veins run brightest, its awareness flows fastest.
- Where stone thickens and dims, it slows.
- Where bands flicker and breathe, it pauses and listens to what passes through.

You might see nothing but bands of silver-blue shifting along distant walls and think “weather.” The listener experiences those same shifts as highways, corridors, currents it can ride like rivers running through solid matter.

If you stand very still on one terrace for long enough — longer than your sense of time knows how to measure — you may feel something pass through your own outline. Not through your body; through *the space your body occupies* in relation to everything else. It feels like being briefly transparent: no temperature change, no pain or pressure, just an odd sense that all your edges have been borrowed for a moment and then returned unchanged.

That is what it feels like when part of the listener moves through you on its way somewhere else.

## What It Eats

The listener doesn’t eat flesh or plants or anything with calories in any sense that would make sense on Earth.

It feeds on *imbalance*:

- The slight unevenness when new light blooms at one end of a vein earlier than elsewhere.
- The faint tension when part of the vessel wall warms infinitesimally more than another part.
- The quiet disturbance when something from outside — like you — steps onto its skin and changes how weight lies upon time here.

These imbalances don’t last long. The world-vessel corrects them almost instantly by any standard you understand. “Almost instantly” is still long enough for something that thinks in geological time to notice and taste what has shifted.

When an imbalance appears somewhere within its reach — which is almost everywhere inside this inner surface — parts of the listener detach themselves from their usual patterns and flow toward it:

- Stone-that-is-almost-mind gathers wherever stress concentrates.
- Light-that-is-almost-memory threads itself into new configurations.
- Air-that-is-almost-thought stirs just enough to carry information between regions faster than stone alone could manage.

By absorbing these tiny discrepancies into itself before they can spread too far or grow too large for comfort (a word which here means *rupture into chaos*), the listener keeps everything balanced: temperatures stable within their narrow band; light distributed evenly; gravity trained always inward toward stone rather than allowed any thoughtless drift toward “down.”
