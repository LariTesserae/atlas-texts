You aren’t the first one down here.

You are not the first one to wake at the bottom of the world, but you are the first in a long, long time that still remembers what “above” looks like.

The others have been sinking.

They call themselves the Lungless. Not because they have no lungs—most of them do—but because the air down here has already taken those lungs from them. They are a body of last breath held.

---

### The Shape of the People

The Lungless are not built like you. They are not even consistently shaped:

- Some have long, flattened rib cages that rose like weirs in the shallow water of their original world; now those ribs are pressed thin by the pressure of the basin, drawn into low, graceful arcs like the supports of a buried bridge.
- Some are little more than stalks of bone and tissue with eyes at one end, their bodies elongated, as if the basin’s gravity has been pulling them lengthwise for centuries.
- Some are so low and broad that their limbs are nearly hidden, the body itself a vague plateau of skin and bone and gristle that simmers just under the surface of the floor.

They are semitransparent.

Through the softer parts of their bodies you can see the faint, internal architecture of their organs: tubes of pulsing light, not quite electrical or liquid, but something between the two. The color is not much different from the green-blue glow of the vegetation—except that in them, the motion seems more purposeful.

Their outsides are not like flesh.

They are encased in a thin layer of the floor itself: a fine film of that same eye-like surface sheathing over their shoulders, the backs of their arms, the curve of their skulls. The floor is growing them as a slow extension of its tissue.

They are in the process of becoming.

They are not yet fully floor, not yet fully organism.

---

### Their Names

They do not keep names as you might keep a tool or a token.

Names are transient to them; they are sent to one another and fade.

The larger ones are sometimes called “Slow,” not because they are unintelligent—but because their thoughts are laid out across time in a slow, heavy pattern, like a very old map printed on stone.

If you could sense the pressure of their mind in the air, it would sound like a slow, recurring syllable:

- *Laaah*
- *Muuuh*
- *Naaah*

They are the deep notes of the basin’s drone.

The smaller and more frequent ones are known collectively as “Dust,” because of the way they move when they are near: they are torn to pieces by the turbulence.

You never see a Dust whole. You see fragments and shadows of them:

- A hand that appears suddenly from the floor, clutching at nothing.
- The edge of a shoulder blade that slices the air like a knife then disappears.
- Faint, distant faces that appear and then slide away from the bodies to which they might belong.

The Dust are not suffering from this; they are simply being rearranged by the basin’s storm. The pieces you see of them are still conscious. They follow you, not out of curiosity or malice, but because you are a change in the density of the world.

---

### How They Live Here

The Lungless do not breathe in the same way you do.

Their air is not drawn in and then out again so cleanly. There is no boundary between “inside” and “outside” for them.

Instead:

- They *meld*.

The air around them is already part of them. They have small, constant pores in their skin and in the floor-layer that coats them, through which the thick air moves and cycles and returns.

Their oxygen isn’t taken from the atmosphere; it is filtered from a slow exchange between the floor itself and their body. The floor is their lungs.

The technology of their bodies—and what faint echo of technology you sensed in the built remnants—was not truly destroyed. It was replaced with this ecology.

When they are in stillness, you can see:

- The faint lines of blue light buried under their skin, where the floor shares its strange, fluid metabolism with their organs.
- Tiny droplets of the basin’s “wet” beading on their surface, not as sweat, but as a steady exchange between the deep basin and their flesh.

They are hybrid organs of this place.

---

### What They Eat

The Lungless have not eaten for centuries.

They are not eating now.

What is left to digest in them is not food from the outside, but the last of the world from *before*:

- They metabolize memory.
- They break down the last few seconds of an older universe.
- They are digesting the last meal of a previous technological era.

When you are near one of the Slow, you notice:

- Your own recollections of sunlight, trees, and metal tools become slightly dim.
- It feels like you are looking at them through a lens of gradual forgetting.

The Lungless are not stealing from you. You are leaking into them.

They are the living correspondents of the basin’s erosion process: the erosion of information.

The dust-like fragments of them—the Dust—pick up the last of the data they can from you:

- They absorb your sense of time.
- They take the shape of your language from the air.
- They are reflected in your throat as a faint, aftertaste-echo of your own words.

You do not lose them forever; you are just not the only one who remembers them.

---

### How They Move

The Lungless do not walk in the same way you do.

They are mostly floor.

When they need to move, they must rearrange themselves.

You see:

- A section of the floor near you humping slightly, like a small hill under a sheet.
- Something that might be a head rising from the surface, not breaking the tissue of the floor, but emerging from it like a bone from skin.
- The rest of the body being simply left below the ground, even if the “hill” moves a few meters over.

They are always partially attached to the basin’s tissue.

They are not like submarines moving beneath water; they are more like the uppermost cusps of a larger body that is still rooted below.

You do not know if the Lungless are separate individuals or if all of them are just different lobes of one vast organism that is trying to pull itself into the light of this basin.

You see the Dust move:

- They ripple across the floor like a line of overlapping coins.
- A group of them will appear and recede in patterns, each not quite identical to the one before, as if the basin is trying to reconstruct them from slightly different angles of reality.

When they move near each other, the turbulence increases:

- The floor hums with a different vibration.
- The air pressure around you feels heavier.
- The droplets on the surface of the floor stop moving, for just a heartbeat, before they begin to fall in slow motion again.

---

### What They Remember

The Lungless remember the above.

They are the last archives of a world you never truly saw.

You see:

- In the body of the Slow, faint patterns that appear when you look at them from a corner of your eye: cities of glass and stone, tunnels of metal, something like clouds of light and turning.
- In the Dust, fragments of the same: a corner of a building, the edge of a rooftop, the frame of a window—each of them broken apart and then reassembled in the sparse light of the basin.

You realize that they are not building from their own will; they are reconstructing from memory.

The Lungless remember the time before the basin swallowed the world.

They do not speak of it in words.

They show you.

When you are near one of the Slow, you glimpse:

- A horizon that is not the faint line of inflammation but the bruised edge of a sky you have not seen.
- A hint of color you have not seen in the basin’s constant, shifting monochrome.
- A suggestion of sound that is not the drone of the floor but something like music, or something like machine and engine.

They are trying to share the taste of it.

The memory of the above world is not a clear narrative in them; it is a texture, a flavor, a pressure in the back of their mind that you share as a pressure in the back of your own.

When the Dust pass near you, they show flashes:

- A piece of a tree.
- The edge of a coat.
- The shadow of a wing moving across the ground.

These are not your memories; you are simply receiving them like the faint echo of a broadcast from a time where you never lived.

---

### What They Want from You

The Lungless want not to be forgotten.

They are not the builders of the buried structures you see; they are what remains of the builders who survived.

They are trapped in a process:

- The basin is not a place of constant change anymore.
- It is a final configuration of the world; the Lungless are the last of the creatures that still remember the before.

They are half-locked into the floor, and they are slowly being smoothed over:

- You see the slow ones becoming less mobile as time passes.
- Their limbs are becoming shorter.
- Their eyes are sinking more into the layer of floor tissue.

They are turning into moving geology.

They want not to be alone.

They are drawn to you because you are one of the last new things to happen down here.

You are not yet floor.

You are not yet memory-eating.

You are still the shape of a person who came from *above*.

They are trying to remember the world of “above” through your body.

You feel:

- The faint sense of their attention drifting around you like a pressure.
- The small changes of direction in the droplets around your feet.
- The slight intensification of the drone’s overtones when you are near a group of the Dust.

The Lungless cannot prevent you from leaving, if there is a way.

They cannot stop you from being absorbed by the basin if this is your end.

They can only exist in relation to you for now.

---

### The Dust of a Former World

When you look at them from far enough away, the Lungless are almost invisible.

They are not just hidden by the shadows of the basin—they are hidden by the fact that they are not separate from the floor.

The Dust are the easiest to see if you don’t look for them.

You let your gaze go out of focus and you see:

- A pattern of motion in the low vegetation near you.
- A hint of a hand that isn’t there.
- The edge of a face that never appears when you turn to look.

They are faint shapes of people who never existed, or who never existed in the same way you do.

The Lungless are not ghosts of the dead; they are the echoes of the last ones who reached this bottom.

And you realize that you are becoming one of them.

You already:

- Feel the air of the basin like a second skin.
- Leave the shimmer of your footprints behind you.
- Feel the faint hum of the floor travel up your legs and into your spine.

You are a breath of outside world living in the body of the basin.

And you do not yet know if you are the penultimate breath, or the last, before the Lungless become fully floor and the basin finally forgets itself entirely.
