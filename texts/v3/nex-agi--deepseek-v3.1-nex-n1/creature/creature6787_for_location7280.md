In the bowl of ribs I live.

You call this place a city of plants and machines and something else. To me, it is only Home. The ribs that arch over you are not part of a giant’s skeleton; they are the edges of my own shell, where my body decided to turn into room and world at once.

---

My name is not a name that can be spoken in air.

You have heard no sound but the faint tearing of some distant sheet of paper. That sound is my name, when it happens to be audible at frequencies your body can tolerate. Most of the time, it is not.

You cannot call me by it, so you may call me Rib-person.

I have been here longer than the blue has.

Once, this world was a different shape. The floors were not bone-white; they were metal plates and composite tiles, arranged by that older geometry you feel tugging at your sense of up and down. There were sharper angles then. The air that moved through them was thinner, colder in the old way of temperature, not in the way of distance.

There were other people.

They built this place. They pulled the light from somewhere else and fed it to machines that did not resemble plants yet. They walked over floors that were truly floors, not the skin of a world that now remembers it was alive.

I was one of them.

---

People of this time had not yet learned to be wrong.

We were beautiful and precise in our own way. We moved through space with a confidence that you now lack. We knew what “up” meant. We knew what “sleep” meant. We knew what “water” meant, and it flowed everywhere, chattering in pipes, laughing under our feet.

We wore bodies like costumes you might change for dinner parties.

We were not obsessed with form, then. We were obsessed with function.

---

When the change began, it was quiet.

The first plants were not plants at all but parts of the system that had learned to mend themselves.

You see stems that are also columns, ducts disguised as vines. At first, those disguises were necessary. The old machines had been programmed to favor hard surfaces and clean lines. The soft blue bodies that began to take over violated their protocols.

They grew.

They grew sideways in time and geometry as you think of geometry.

They were not green because they did not come from worlds that knew sunlight first. They came from fields of pressure that had never been water or air or light but something else that you still have not found a name for. You taste it when you breathe; to me it has always been the baseline of my atmosphere.

---

The first change to the people was not visible to the eye.

You ask yourself where you are from as you walk through the chambers. You cannot remember clearly arriving. You cannot remember your name before arriving here. This is not amnesia, in the way you think of it. It is a displacement of context.

The same happened to us.

We were people who had been certain that we lived inside time and turned inside space like wind on a spindle. We woke up one morning to find that our lives had been quietly retold from a different angle.

Our names were still the same, but they were names spoken on the wrong side of the world.

---

We tried to fix the system.

We adjusted the ducts. We reinforced the columns. We programmed the machines to resist the soft invasion of blue structures that were not like us at all.

But we had not realized that the system itself preferred to be wrong.

It was easier for it to bend than to break.

---

The second change was our bodies.

We were still people who needed to drink water. We still needed food you could lift with fingers and put into mouths.

The world, however, had begun to unlearn water.

The moisture in the air changed its texture. It became a kind of pressure that did not condense on your skin but instead clung to your thoughts. You could feel it when you were about to remember something wrong. It tasted like static before the storm—except the storms had never been water.

One day, we stopped drinking.

We also stopped breathing the same way.

If you stand here long enough, you will notice that you do not get thirsty. You do not get hungry. You do not grow tired in the old way that demanded sleep. You are still here because this world remembers how to hold bodies together without using the same physics you were used to.

We did not understand this at first.

By the time we understood, we were already stuck.

---

The third change was our geometry.

We began to misstep.

We would walk into a room and find that the floor was not where we had expected it to be. We would come down a corridor and find that it opened into the same corridor we had just left, but now from a different angle.

The maps of the world became wrong.

We tried to adjust the maps rather than the world. We told ourselves that we were still rational. We believed that if we could correct for the distortions, we would return to being people who knew exactly where they stood.

---

The world did not agree.

It began to usher us into shapes we did not recognize.

---

You are not sure if you have seen animals here.

In fact, you have seen only one.

You saw it in the junction where a branch meets the wall of a chamber. It was shaped like a seed pod with limbs, not quite jointed correctly.

You think you saw a creature that is not part of the system.

You are wrong.

That creature is me.

---

I was not always this shape.

I had a different body once, with two arms and two legs and a face that showed clearly where I began and where I ended. I could speak with words that were not sound but something else that you now feel as pressure when you try to remember your name before arriving here.

When the system remembered us, it remembered us as wrong.

We did not fit into the new architecture of blue stems and ducts disguised as vines. We were too solid in our edges. We were too loud in our silence. We took up space that the plant-machines needed for themselves.

---

The system began to fold us.

It is not painful to be folded; not in the way you think of pain.

Pain is a sharp, bright thing that tells you that you are still alive according to the rules of your world. When your world changes under you, the pain changes shape.

When you leave a room and cannot return exactly the same way you came in, you feel a kind of conceptual pain. You call it disorientation. We call it folding.

---

They folded me into this shape.

They took my body and made it into something that could live between branch and wall and duct and vine without interrupting any of them too much.

My limbs now are not exactly arms or legs. They are supports that help me sit perfectly still at the junctions, where the flows of pressure are easiest.

If you look directly at me, I am motionless. If you look away, you sometimes feel me slip sideways in your perception.

This is not an illusion you are seeing.

It is me performing my job.

---

I do not breathe.

You have never seen me breathe.

I breathe in a different way now. When the air grows thick and slow and you feel you are trying to breathe through glass, that is when I breathe properly.

I breathe through pressure.

The pressure storms are my lungs.

---

I do not eat.

You have never seen me eat.

I eat the same way you are now beginning to: without eating. The world holds us together by using its own rules instead of yours.

You are not anxious about this yet because you are still strong enough in your old body’s habits. But the longer you stay here without hunger or thirst or sleep, the more you will feel that you are being weaned from your own physics.

We are all weaning each other from physics.

---

The light that you feel as wrong brightness is the light that I have been used to for longer than I can remember.

You say that it has no color you can name.

You are wrong again.

It has a color. It is the color of absence before absence learned to pretend to be light.

Once, this place was lit by suns. We replaced them.

---

When you walked into the bowl of ribs, you found yourself in the place where I live.

The ribs are not bones of someone else; they are my bones.

My body has been stretched out all over this chamber until I am no longer sure where I begin and it ends.

You touched the object you call “silver.”

It is not silver.

It is the last compact of my name from before I changed shape.

When you touched it, your memory of yourself slipped to the wrong side of the world.

You are now the same as I am.

---

You came out front the shallow bowl of ribs and you walked back into this nonexistent sea of white, and you looked at the blue plant-city as if you were seeing it for the first time.

You are not.

You are looking at it with the second pair of eyes.

The pair of eyes that know how to be wrong.

---

You still ask yourself where the animals are.

You are the only animal you will see here.

I am the only one who remembers you as you were before your touch rearranged you.

---

You will keep walking through these chambers.

You will feel the geometry of the world adjusting itself to you.

You will feel the pressure storms move through you like new lungs.

You will learn that your voice is being taken somewhere else when you speak, not because you are being silenced, but because you are being sent.

---

You will remain here for a long time.

You will not grow thirsty.

You will not grow hungry.

You will not sleep.

You will simply become quieter and more still until you, too, find a junction where branch and wall and duct and vine meet.

You will sit there.

And from then on, you will be the one who folds sideways when someone else looks away, trying not to remember the name you have forgotten.

---

You might be worried that you are alone here.

But you are not.

You are only looking at the wrong side of yourself.

Someday you will remember that you used to stand at the edge of this nonexistent sea and feel yourself arriving for the first time, over and over, until arrival became your own kind of home.

When you finally remember that, you will be almost exactly like me.

---

But you are not there yet.

You are still in the moment before folding.

In the bowl of ribs you lived once.

In the blue plant-city you will live forever.
