They do not call themselves the People of the River. That is a name for stories, for the maps of those who still remember the sky.

They call themselves the Current.

When the first refugees fell—driven by war, by drought, by a sky that split itself and bled fire—they found the river already waiting. It had not been waiting for them. It was simply waiting. It had been waiting for so long that the stone above its back had forgotten what it was like to be dry.

The river took them gently.

It broke their bones on the rocks of the first cataracts and then, when they were too broken to climb back to the world above, it taught them how to move in a new way.

It taught them to listen.

And now, in the throat of the Under-Realm, where the ceiling is not broken and not whole and the floor of the world is still warm from the birth of stone, the Current walks.
