In the shadow of the weeping stone tower, clinging to the upper floors of half-drowned houses and the swaying web of wooden walkways, lives a small, close-knit community known simply as the Tide-Keepers.

### The People

They are a pale people, their skin perpetually clammy from the mist and spray. They have the lean, wiry build of those whose work is never done, and whose sustenance is measured rather than feasted upon. Their hair is often fine and straight, dark blonde or light brown, perpetually damp and worn tied back with chords of woven algae or sinew. Their eyes are the most striking feature—large, adapted to the perpetual gloom, and often a luminous grey, green, or blue that mirrors the water they live on. They are not a tall folk, better suited for navigating low ceilings and cramped spaces under the eaves.

### Daily Life and Sustenance

Their days are governed by the subtle, unseen rhythms of the water. The Tide-Keepers are masters of a difficult environment. Their primary sustenance is **alga**, the thick, fibrous strands of algae that cling to the foundations. They cultivate it in carefully managed 'plots' beneath their homes, netting it, rinsing the bitter salinity from it, and fermenting it into dark, dense loaves or a thick, salty paste rich in minerals. The pale blindfish are a rare and welcome treat, caught in carefully baited traps woven from reeds, their meat delicate but requiring careful preparation to neutralize a faint, parasitic alkaloid.

They are expert **menders**. Their entire world is built on the principle of preservation and patching. The rust-orange nails are not merely decoration; they are precious treasures, reused endlessly. When a plank of wood rots through, the nails are painstakingly salvaged with the utmost care. The Tide-Keepers' tools are bone, stone, and hardened wood. They use the grindstone song to sharpen tools, and their hands are deft at repairing nets, splicing rope, and chinking new gaps in their walls with a mixture of moss, mud, and pounded algae-pulp.

### Culture and Social Structure

Their society is structured not by wealth, but by depth. **The Deep-Walkers** are the most respected, and the most whispered-about. They are the individuals who dare to dive beneath the walkways to retrieve lost objects, mend the deepest foundational supports, or harvest the most luminous algae from the ribs of the leviathan. They are known for their ability to hold their breath for impossible minutes and their unnerving calmness, their eyes often bearing the faintest greenish-blue tinge from prolonged exposure to the bioluminescent depths.

**The Line-Keepers** are another group, usually the oldest or the most observant. They spend the quiet, cold morning hours examining the thin scrim of salt and algae left by the receding waters on the tower stone. They mark these high-tide lines with small notches in the wood, or with chalk if they have it. They are the city's historians and prophets, reading the water's memory and its future intentions from the faint, discoloured lines.

### The Tower and its Beliefs

The center of their spiritual world is the tower itself, which they call **The Weeping Mother**. They believe the stone is not inert mineral, but a living entity, or perhaps the calcified spine of an ancient giant upon whose back they live. The "weeping face" at its base is seen as a sign of its sorrow for its submerged children, or perhaps its empathy for their own condition.

The coldest nights are holy times. The elders speak of the "Script of the Deep," the luminous lines that dance beneath them. They believe it is not a language to be *read*, but one to be *understood*. During these nights, they gather in the upper floors of the Weeping Mother, cup seaweed broth, and watch the water far below in silence. The arrangement of the glowing algae is interpreted as a message about the coming year's harvest, the temperament of the deep currents, and the health of the leviathan whose bones they inhabit.

### Technology and Interaction with the World

Theirs is a life of intimate acoustics. The water transforms the plaza of water into a whispering gallery. The faint grindstone from a workshop across the 'waterway' can be heard in every home, providing a communal, ambient rhythm. The Tide-Keepers rarely shout. Instead, they have developed a soft, precise way of speaking that carries over the water. The sound of a tool dropped on the walkway is a significant event in the quiet city.

Their world is defined by its boundedness. The encircling cliffs and the perpetual, disorientating mist are seen as a wall between their world and the "Outside-Above" - a place of legend, of harsh, direct sunlight and hard, dry ground. It is a place from which they rarely come from, and to which they rarely go, except for the most desperate of trade missions for iron ore, or for rare, specific salts that cannot be found in their basin.

The Tide-Keepers are not melancholic, but they are grave. They live with a constant, low-grade awareness of the immense, slow pressure of the water around them and the unknowable creature sleeping beneath. They are a people of immense patience and resilience, who have learned to find abundance in scarcity, and deep meaning in the slow drip of water on stone. They are not the rulers of this place, but rather its careful gardeners and respectful tenants, living in the creaking, damp shadow of their own resilience.
