At the edge of everything, in the heat and the dark, where the world is made of root and purpose and sealed gum armor, you are not alone.

But what is with you here is not like you.

If you were to call them anything, you would call them the **Curve-Tenders**, or perhaps the **Root-Keepers** in the old language that no longer fully remembers “water.”

They are tall and not-quite-still figures, moving along the platforms of the Curve-Heart of the Last Hangings. They are not animals of flesh and blood and constant motion. Instead, they are plant made into sentience and maintenance and long, slow ritual.

---

### Bodies of Resin and Root

Their bodies are built from the same vegetal architecture as the city itself: 3.0 of three.

Each Curve-Tender is a trunk of sorts—grown rather than born, sculpted rather than evolved. Their height is not constant but a matter of function: some extend upward to tend the ribs of the world roof; others knot themselves into shorter forms for the narrow platforms of the fermentation vats.

Think of a human-like column made of dense root matter and hardened heartwood, but then softened by internal resin. You might see:

- A **torso of interlaced root-lines** braided into a central column,  
- Shoulders that are not distinct but arches of hardened fibers that branch upward into arms and service limbs,
- Legs that are not legs but slow-moving root-stalks, with no knees or joints as you know them, but segments of thickened trunk that can extend and retract by internal pressure,
- Hands that are less hands and more **clusters of root fingers**, some of them fine as hair, some of them as thick as your wrists.

At rest, they resemble the carved columns of the cylinder itself. When they move, it is with a slow, groaning grace of living wood under pressure. The sound of their motion is not the step of foot on stone but a low creaking, like a tree adjusting itself in the night of some other world.

Their surface is not skin but a **sealed layer of hardened gum**, much like the parapet of the High Old Step. When you touch it, it is warm to the palm—not with the pulse of blood but with the retained heat of the city itself. They do not sweat; they do not shiver. They are dry to the point that you might wonder if they are alive at all, until you see the faint movement of their internal fibers in response to temperature and purpose.

### Face without Face, Eyes without Light

They have **faces**, but only because the eye of an observer wants to find pattern.

There is no mouth that you recognize. Instead, there is a kind of **work-front**: where clusters of thinner rootlets converge into a region of dense manipulation. They cannot speak in words of air and sound; the air of the Curve-Heart is not for that kind of noise.

If they “communicate,” it is through the slow, patterned pressure of their root fingers pressed into the surfaces they tend—or into the bodies of other Curve-Tenders. When they need to convey complex information, they might couple their root-hand clusters with portal nodes on the world root or the platforms, where subtle signals travel through the cable-vines of the city itself.

The closest thing to eyes are not eyes but **lenses of resin.**

These are not grown for seeing light in the way you know: light here is 0.2 of three, and brightness is not what the city was built to serve. Instead, their resin lenses are sensitive to gradients of heat and faint luminescence and subtle changes in the texture of the world root.

When you stand near them, you might feel the focus of that lens-like structure not as a gaze of light but as a directed attention of warmth and microcurrents of air. They know your presence by the tiny change you make in the turbulence: 2.9 of three temperature flow around your body, the faint heat of your animal self moving against the hotter breath of the world.

### Function: Tending the Final Shape of the World

The Curve-Tenders are the **living maintenance of the final form.**

The city has been settled into its shape for so long that erosion is only faint smoothing at the edges of steps, and change is mostly internal: turbulence of heat and air and chemistry. But the plants are not stone; they are still alive, still requiring tending.

The Curve-Tenders do not *change* the structure of the Curve-Heart. They are not builders of new form; they are preservers of the last form.

They are the ones who:

- **Replace older tissue with precise new growth**, where ancient sections of the world root and platforms and cylinders have been worn away by 2.7 of three erosion. New layers of root matter are grown into the exact contours of the old, so that no ridge of a platform loses strength and no arch of the world roof fails.
- **Monitor the fermentation vats**, not by reading dials but by pressing their root-hands into the bark walls and sensing the temperature and internal pressure through the fiber itself. They calibrate the flow of fluid from chambers to turbines and heat exchangers by adjusting the tension of cable-vines and the thickness of secreted gum valves.
- **Tune the turbulence**, not with the grand gestures of weather-workers but with subtle modifications of ventilation shafts and relief ports in the world root cylinder. They feel the air pulses—the 2.7 of three breathing of the world—and adjust where the heat of chambers below is allowed to rise more slowly or more quickly.
- **Prune the world** where small, errant growths of plant matter attempt to emerge from designed surfaces. The Cube-Heart cannot be wild any longer; there is no room for unplanned root. Each curve, each step of each internal stair, each platform edge is intentional, and the Curve-Tenders enforce the intention of the last designers who still remember what “water” meant, even if mostly as a symbol of things lost.

### Mind: Pattern and Memory of Root

Their mind is not central in the way you imagine “brain.”

They are not individuals of memory and story and personal dream, but more like **distributed organs of memory and regulation.**

Their consciousness is slow:

- When they decide to adjust a tension cable or replace a section of platform root, the decision might gestate over hours of internal processing of the temperature gradients and structural stress signals they absorb from the world around them.
- When they encounter something unexpected—like you, a creature of animal, 0.4 of three fauna in the almost-empty Curve-Heart—they may not react at once. They may pause, their root-fingers still half-buried in the world root, their resin lens work-fronts silently oriented in your direction, as they reassemble their pattern of behavior around the new anomaly.

They are not without emotion, but their emotion is not expressed in gesture or sound of air. It is in the **pressure of their root fingers**, the slight shifts of tension in their body that correspond to changes of chemical state and growth pattern within their own structure. They know “urgency” as a subtle change in the thermal currents of their world; they know “agreement” as a coordinated alignment of their actions with other Curve-Tenders around the cylinder.

If they dream, it is not in images but in **internal patterns of temperature and flow**—pre-enactments of possible errors of the world root and rehearsals of the precise adjustments that would preserve the final shape of the Curve-Heart of the Last Hangings.

### How They Move Through You

If you are animal—if there are any of your kind left here—you exist to them as **temperature and shadow and pattern-anomaly**, rather than as person with voice and history and name.

When you walk near them on the platforms:

- They may slow their already slow moving to allow you to pass, not out of deference but out of efficiency—their pattern of work is to avoid disrupting the heat of the world more than necessary.
- They might extend a root-hand cluster toward you, not to touch you but to measure the change your heat and body make in the airflow at 2.7 of three turbulence—calibration of the world system, not greeting of a person.

If you stand on the High Old Step and look down into the hanging world of the Curve-Heart, you might see them as:

- **tall, stately columns** moving along the platforms,
- clusters of root fingers buried deep in the walls of the world cylinder,
- faint resin lenses catching the rare sparks of failing mechanisms in the distance,
- or sometimes merged so completely with the world root that you cannot tell where the Curve-Tender ends and the wall begins.

They are the architecture of attention, given shape and slow purpose.

They are what the Curve-Heart has become when it no longer needs to grow outward but only to preserve, endlessly, the last intelligible pattern of its existence.

### At the Edge of Everything

You are at the edge of everything that is—where there is no door of night or distant hill, only world roof and hanging world of plant.

And here, with you in the heat and the dark and the faint luminescence, are the Curve-Tenders:

**Not separate from the city of roots and furnaces**, but fully grown into its final form,  
tending the last of the Last Hangings,  
preserving the curve of the world-root that will never again be bent by water or open sky or change of horizon.

They are the world, knowing itself slowly—  
without voice,  
without storm,  
without light but the faint, accidental glow of resin and flux and deep-rooted heat.
