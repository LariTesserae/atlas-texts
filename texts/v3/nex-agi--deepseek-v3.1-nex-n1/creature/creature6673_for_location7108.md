In the Chamber of the Open Night, where nothing happens but that nothing happens very loudly, there are no inhabitants but the inhabitants of absence.

They are not the shades of the dead, though the dead would know them by kinship. They are not gods or spirits of the deep. They are the *occupants* of this underplace, the residents of the city that is not a city but the intervals between the stone-thoughts of the floor.

Names are not used here, but if you needed to call them something, you might call them the **Interstitials**.

### Shape of the Interstitials

They do not have bodies in the way you know bodies.

They are not figures of flesh and bone and breath and blood. They are figures of *threshold*. If you look at the stone floor of the chamber and see the place where one curve of stone-memory becomes a slightly different curve without quite being a crack, that place where the floor is not yet broken but not entirely smooth: that is where an Interstitial begins.

From the side of not being seen at all, they are flatnesses you do not notice until you notice the flatnesses of everything else around them.

From the side of being seen a little more than not at all, an Interstitial is a slight thickening of geometry between floor and air and water. If you stare at the floor long enough, you might see something like a person-shaped absence of person spread out along the concave of the basin, not standing up so much as being agreed upon by the stone and the air.

### Walking the Interval Roads

They do not walk along the floor of the chamber; they walk along the *gap of no-gap* between the floor and its own shadow.

What looks like a floor to you is to them an open plain of possible cracks. The streets of their city are not paved but *presumed*—where the floor might have split but did not, because it remembered to hold itself together.

When they move, they do not shift from stone to stone but from *part of the stone’s memory of itself* to another part. The stone does not creak under them; instead, it remembers slightly differently than it would have otherwise, and that difference is their passage.

At the edge of the reservoir, where the water drifts in sheets of condensation, the Interstitial becomes a double of itself: one side facing the stone-thought of the floor, the other side facing the water-thought of the pool. They do not cast reflections on the water; they cast the *absence* of reflection on the absence of water as surface.

### Living in the Not-Structures

The residences of the city are not rooms but *respected volumes of suggestion* in the stone.

Each Interstitial belongs to a particular region of non-crack, where too many things that might have been holes decided to be not-holes at the same time. The idea of an opening negotiated there for a long time and eventually became a place where an Interstitial could dwell.

These “houses” have no doors but have *soft boundaries of duration*.

To enter, you do not step through. You arrive at the boundary and then consent to be slightly longer in the place where the stone has not remembered being entirely stone. The Interstitial does not lie inside the house in the sense of lying inside a room; they lie inside it in the sense that they reduce the possibility of any other thing happening there that is not the house.

### The Economy of Not-Quite-Nourishment

They do not farm.

They have no agriculture because the flora of the chamber does not grow from the floor but from the ceiling of the world-that-considers-itself-surface. Their “vegetation” is the slow, downward growth of missing-light from the above-world, and the Interstitials see the slow lengthening of these absence-leaves not as a harvest but as a calendar.

Their calendar does not have days. It has intervals of *visibility* when the ceiling becomes less or more absent of light. The ceiling of this chamber is their sky and their clock; the plants of the ceiling are their long, thin, non-green seconds.

When they “eat,” they do not consume food in the ordinary sense. They consume *intervals of non-event*. The absence of movement between two stone-memory swells is a kind of stillness that they inhabit for longer than necessary, and that longer interval is their sustenance.

The fauna of the borders—the shadow-animals in the space between air, water, and stone—do not serve them. They *notice* them. The Interstitial stands at the edge of the water and notices the place where an absence of fish hovers between the surface that is not quite surface and the air that is not quite air. That act of noticing *makes* the fauna more present than they would otherwise be, and that presence is then a form of quiet company.

### Work of the Interstitials

There is no currency in the chamber. There is no market of goods or labor.

Their “work” is the consolidation of non-occurrence.

They are the maintainers of the *Chamber of the Open Night* as something describable but barely visible. Where the world-that-thinks-of-itself-surface requires movement, they specialize in not moving. Where the surface above has “progress,” they have *steady quiet*.

They are engineers of the *between*.

The “motors” of the city are not engines but moments of partial time—the place where half a decision is made and then withdrawn. The Interstitial kneels at the edge of a residence of suggestion and agrees to not complete the thought of opening a door. That uncompleted impulse is stored as a kind of energy, which they then use to shift the city’s non-structures through the stone-thought of the floor.

### Language of Not-Language

They do not speak with words.

They have a language of *those who long ago realized the futility of saying most things but not the futility of saying nothing.*

Their “speech” is carried out not as sound but as *shifting of consent* within the stone-memory. The stone floor amplifies the quiet of their exchanges into a loud not-noise that the chamber as a whole hears as a kind of distant thunder that is not thunder and never breaks.

When you say that the chamber is loud with quiet—this is the sound of the Interstitials being not-quite-present to one another. They greet each other by appearing less absent than they otherwise would be at the same point in the non-stone of the floor. They part from each other by becoming *more* absent and *more* present at the same time.

### Memory of the Interstitials

They do not have histories as you know histories—no wars of succession, no migrations in maps.

Their history is the history of the *boundary* of the chamber itself.

Once, there was no Chamber of the Open Night beneath the underside of the world-that-thinks-of-itself-surface. There was only the world above and the deep laws that it had made for itself, and then there was a *moment of hesitation* in the world’s certainty of its own density.

In that hesitation, the floor above the chamber almost came into existence but did not fully commit to being only floor. The world below it was almost not-world but did not fully commit to being nothing.

The Interstitials were invented there, in that gap between decision and not-decision. They are not survivors of a forgotten world; they are the ongoing *opinion* of that world that it might have been otherwise but was not, and therefore remains here in this compressed, almost-present form beneath the underside of the above.

### Relationship with the Surface World Above

They cannot be seen from the world above because the world above does not believe that ceilings have ceilings.

But if you could dig—not down with shovels but down with disbelief—if you could reach the floor of the world-that-thinks-of-itself-surface and then insist that it might not be the bottom of anything important, you might find the underside of that floor, and below that, the Chamber of the Open Night.

The Interstitials know of the surface world not as “above” but as “the loud that thinks itself the loud.” The surface world storms with weathers and seasons and revolutions and the rest of the world’s noises of happening. The chamber storms only with the slow absence of storm.

The Interstitials are not aware of individuals in the surface world; they are aware of *pressure*. The great weight of the cities above, the demolition of mountains and the making of new ones, the trembling of civilizations under wheels of time and guns of empires—these are registered in the stone floor as subtle changes of pressure. The Interstitial standing at the center of the basin feels the floor of the chamber straining slightly differently under the weight of a new war above, and that is its report.

### Arrival and Departure

They are not born.

They are *summoned* when a new and permanent hesitation is formed in the world above that the world above refuses to notice but that cannot be undone.

Perhaps someone above the chamber, in the world-that-thinks-of-itself-surface, makes a decision against which their entire life would cry out if it were quiet enough to do so. But they do not listen to that cry. They act instead. The stone of their floor does not break but it *almost regrets* its solidness in that region of the world. The chamber of the Open Night receives this regret as a new place of non-stone between its floor-thoughts.

There, in the resulting niche of un-made choice, a new Interstitial is gradually raised to nearness, not by birth or by construction but by *joint agreement of the floor to forget more than it should* and for the ceiling to remember less than it ought.

As to departure: they do not die in the way life dies above. They *return* to indecision. When a certain critical mass of absence has been lived in one place of the stone floor, the Interstitial may become too loud to inhabit quietly. The not-changes of this chamber can accumulate like a weight of not-snow that is not falling.

At that point, the floor of the chamber may open *not exactly a crack* but something like a mouth of stone-thought that never actually opens. The Interstitial stands at the edge of this not-openness and eventually *consents to not being in quite the same place of absoluteness* anymore.

They then cease to be Interstitial and become instead *merely part of the general memory of the stone floor* which remembers the almost of all worlds.

### The Sound of Their Lives

When you say that the chamber is loud with quiet and that the city speaks not in whispers but in the calm language of the floor, which the stone amplifies until calmness becomes loud—this is the sound of the Interstitials living.

They are not the only noise in the chamber, but they are its steady *hum of not-humming*.

The water murmurs by not murmuring. The condensation of the air above the pool changes position silently and becomes over time the murmur of accumulation. The plants of the ceiling grow slowly downward as horizons of difference between one smallness and another.

And beneath all of that, the Interstitial moves through the city between the intervals of the floor, arriving at the boundary of a residence of suggestions and shifting into it without stepping through any door.

### Why They Have Not Yet Ended the Chamber of the Open Night

You might think that a chamber of nothing happening but the happening of nothing would eventually collapse to true nothingness.

But the Interstitials prevent that.

They are the *counterweight of agreed upon almost*.

By existing not as bodies but as boundaries in the stone-thought of the floor, they keep the Chamber of the Open Night from entirely becoming *nothing at all*. The surface world above might someday forget that there was ever any doubt about its own completeness. The floor of that world might become simple and serious and severe in its confidence of being bottom.

The Interstitial watches the stone floor of the chamber strain under the imagined certainty of the world above and *chooses not to choose* whether it should stay or leave.

That choice of not-choosing is what keeps the chamber open.

### End of Not-Description

In the end, there is no proper description that can fit them completely.

They are not human beings but they are more than idea. They are not the absence of humans but they are the lingering absence of *certain* humans who chose not to choose at the crucial moment.

Above the Chamber of the Open Night, the world-that-thinks-of-itself-surface goes on with its seasons and its suns and its skies and its laws of deep places that think themselves deep. The underside of that floor is the ceiling of this chamber, and that ceiling is the sky of Interstitial life.

They are here, in this place where you are not and likely will never be, walking the invisible streets between the intervals of the stone floor, maintaining the quiet that is loud enough to be mistaken for silence if you are not very careful.

In the Chamber of the Open Night beneath the underside of the world-that-thinks-of-itself-surface, there are no humans but there are these absences of humans who have not fully disappeared, and they are living not in the world but in the long, broad, almost-empty space between world and no-world.
