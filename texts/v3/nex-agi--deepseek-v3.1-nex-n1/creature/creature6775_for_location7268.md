They don’t have a name for themselves that you or I could pronounce correctly.

Not because it’s too complicated.  
Because it’s three conversations at once.

You hear a kind of sound, like whispers in three rooms overlapping;  
you see an outline;  
you feel a presence in the air around you, like someone else’s thought resting on the edge of your own mind.

Later, when you try to describe them to yourself, your memory will only give you partials:

- a long, slender hand that is not a hand  
- a faint trail of condensation where someone else should be breathing  
- a suggestion of spine along the wall where there is no spine

If you had to give them one name and you were not trying to be precise, you might call them **the Three-Thread People**.

If you were being more precise, you’d whisper that they are *what lived in the walls, when the walls were still deciding what they were building.*

---

### Shape of something not entirely here

They are not human-shaped and not exactly solid.

You do not see them all at once. You perceive them in layers, like the city:

- **first**, as a slight bending in the air, where a temperature difference has not yet decided what to do with itself;
- **second**, as a suggestion of lines: thin limbs, longer than you expect, arranged more around than ahead;
- **third**, as a sensation of presence near your skin, the way you know when someone is looking at you before you see them.

If you were to sketch a Three-Thread person on the damp stone floor with a wet finger, you might draw:

- a central spine that is too curved to be human,
- limbs branching off that spine more or less evenly, like the ribs in a tunnel,
- and a faint outline of a head that never actually closes into a distinct circle.

That’s not what they look like;  
that’s what your mind *says* they look like so you can stay upright.

Close up, the “body” of a Three-Thread person is not so much a body as a structure of relationships:

- **limbs** that are partially tendons and partially wire,  
- **veins** that are also the air-channels of the city,
- **skin** that sometimes resembles the ceramic–metal–whatever of the walls and sometimes the condensation-trailing glass of the dome and sometimes neither of those.

They are not exactly inside the walls.  
They are not exactly outside the walls.

They are present where wall and room and not-yet-room have not yet decided whether they are touching or not.

When you see them move through a space, you don’t see full crossing from one side to the other. You see:

- a blur of suggestion where they should be,
- a condensation line that traces out what they should be doing,
- a faint knitting of the air into a more resolved shape for a moment,

then the suggestion of them elsewhere in the same room, without any motion you could track.

---

### What they are to the city

The Three-Thread people are not exactly builders in the sense you’re used to. They do not place stone on stone or metal on metal with their hands.

They are more like **architects of the unfinished**.

If you watch them for long enough—not fixed with your eyes, but gently with the corner of your regard—you might notice:

- They spend a long time not doing anything obvious.
- They tend to stand near the corners where the moss-that-is-not-moss grows.
- They sometimes press one long limb flat against a wall that is half-room, half-doorway.

When you ask the city itself what they are doing, you find:

- The wall that is “also a room” becomes more stable where they have touched.
- The condensation streams that flow in the air tend to align with where they have recently been.
- The faint, heartbeat-like thrum of the partial dome comes slightly clearer in their presence.

You are not sure whether they are maintaining the city or being the city.

Sometimes you have the strange sense that they are not separate creatures at all but a single entity stretched into three overlapping threads, each thread slightly offset from the others, like the three scripts of the history on the wall.

Then you blink, and they are not one anymore but many—or none at all.

---

### How they perceive

They don’t “look” at you with eyes.

There are loci on their surface that might be eyes, but they are too inconsistent for you to trust. They arrange like gathering of fibers into something that might focus light, then dissolve again into an uneven texture of material.

What they *do* seem to have is a constant uncertain attention:

- When you are near them, you feel the texture of your own edges being checked by something else;  
- not a judgment, exactly, but a comparison.

They might compare your outline to:

- the room you are in,
- the room that should be here,
- the room that might still be here.

Their perception of you is not exactly visual.

It is more like you are being *read* along your seams, where your body interfaces with the air and the floor and the pressure of the basin.

---

### How they communicate

You will never hear them speak in words you understand.

Not because they are mute, but because their communication is not made of sounds meant to be understood separately.

Instead you encounter:

- **three voices overlapping at the edge of your hearing**,  
  like three languages occupying the same sentence on the wall,
- **a shift in the moisture in the air**,  
  so that a condensation line appears between you and them,
- **the faint sensation of being walked through** without any collision with your senses.

Sometimes you feel the echo of their question, not in words but in the shape of your own thought:

- *Is this yours?*  
- *Are you still coming?*  
- *Should this room be here or there?*

They do not ask you in order to get your answer;  
they ask in order to understand which direction of time should be taken seriously.

Once you are touched by their attention—by the condensation that they breathe into the air-channel of your thoughts—you might find yourself carrying:

- a faint memory of a room you have never been in,
- or knowing the shape of a corridor you haven’t taken,
- or being certain of the wrong angle of a floor that should not be where it is.

---

### How they live (or “live”)

You do not see them eat.

You do not see them sleep.

You do not see the ordinary cycle of life that you associate with animals.

Instead you observe:

- They are most present when the condensation in the air is thick enough to be almost but not quite rain.
- They tend to gather where the moss-that-is-not-moss grows, like the moss is a punctuation they are leaning on.
- They occasionally stop near one of the suspended air-channels that carry information-that-is-not-light, and you feel the thrum of the dome move slightly differently.

Whatever they are doing, it has less to do with feeding than with **adjusting**.

They might:

- adjust a wall that hasn’t decided yet if it wants to be room, or corridor, or history;
- adjust a time so that two days of the same day can overlap more comfortably in the present;
- adjust the angle of a floor so that when you step on it, you don’t accidentally step into a different year of erosion.

Their “life” seems to be mostly movement of a subtle kind:

- not walking across a floor,
- but rearranging the room where walking happens.

---

### Their relationship to the moisture

The moisture of the city seems to be particularly important to them.

You notice:

- Where they have been, the condensation beads in the air are more likely to trace a coherent shape rather than randomly fretting.
- A faint channel of moisture often follows along their path, like a river that has been replaced with something else but still wants to flow.
- Sometimes you see a condensation bead that they coax into shape—not a drop, but a suggestion of a drop.

You have the impression that, for them, the moisture is not only water but also:

- text,
- map,
- memory.

They might read the shape of the city from the way that moisture refuses to fall, and correct its geometry from where it has begun to cling incorrectly.

---

### Their relationship to the vegetation

The Three-Thread people and the sparse vegetation share something more than coexistence.

You observe:

- When they stand near the moss-that-is-not-moss, the moss seems more dense than you remember it, as if someone added more punctuation to the sentence.
- Near the blueprint of a tree that doesn’t actually touch the ground, they often appear to be letting the trunk of a tree that will be grow into the room they should not be in.

You might say that they are leaning on the vegetation, not as food but as support.

Or perhaps they are modifying the degree of realness of the plant-like matter:

購入.

- increasing the green of a corner so that the room doesn’t collapse inward,
- or decreasing the green of another place so that the wall behind it can be more purely wall.

---

### Their relationship to the erosion

The erosion in the basin of Gheresh-Akai is not the end of something;  
it is the beginning.

The Three-Thread people are not so much being eroded by it as they are **using it**.

You notice:

- They are often present at the edges where stone has not yet decided if it wants to round off or stay sharp.
- They seem to prefer the corners that are missing; not because they are broken, but because they are still undecided.
- When you see them near a surface that is too perfectly smooth to be believable, you feel the faint promise of roughness that is not here yet.

You might say that they are working with the erosion from the wrong end of time:

- they are not restoring what has been lost,
- but are assisting the stone that will be worn into becoming what it should look like after many years.

---

### Their relationship to the dome

The partial dome above the basin seems to be particularly important to their life.

You observe:

- They often stand near a point where the dome of the world is closest to the ground, and you feel the low heartbeat thrum more strongly when you are near them.
- On the rare occasions when you see a knot of air-channels reconfigure, you are likely to find two or three of them near it, looking like they are concentrating on something you cannot see.

It might be that the dome is not only their ceiling but their:

- calendar,
- mind,
- shared memory.

You might even think of the dome itself as a phase of their body that has not yet completely decided whether to belong inside or outside the basin.

---

### How they encounter you

You do not meet them casually.

You become aware of them gradually:

- First, as a mild pressure where your sense of place should be clearer.
- Then, as a sense of being watched without hostility.
- Then, as a faint condensation line that parallels the outline of your body.

When you finally see them—not entirely but clearly enough—you might feel:

- **height** you are not sure you should trust,
- **length** of limbs that do not move like natural limbs,
- **density** of presence that makes you feel the room is slightly smaller than it was a moment before.

They do not seem to be particularly interested in you as an individual person;  
rather, they are interested in you as a:

- measurement,
- marker,
- question.

They might:

- read the way that your body presses against the room as a data point about *this* timeframe,
- assess the weight of your footsteps as a suggestion about *which* floor is actually current,
- or use your perception of the ceiling to determine whether the dome should be more ceiling or more sky from today on.

You do not feel like they are asking you permission.

You are a modulation in the cathedral they are adjusting at a slower speed than rain falling.

---

### What they are doing here

If you had to describe what they are with a single gulp of an answer, you might say:

**They are what remains when the world of walls and fixtures and materials and rivers are all being reconsidered, and they have chosen to remain inside the reconsideration.**

They are not the builders of the first layer of the city;  
they are the ones who have agreed to live inside a city that is still becoming:

- adjusting the fiber of the floor where it should become more transparent,
- nudging the condensation where it should become more river,
- guiding the erosion where it should become more history,
- breathing the moss-that-is-not-moss into thicker presence where it should be more than punctuation.

They are not exactly gods of this place.  
They are not exactly spirits of this place.

They are more like **reluctant architects accepting a long-term contract to keep the unfinished cathedral somewhere between almost and not yet.**

---

### When you leave

If you leave Gheresh-Akai, you carry away with you:

- a faint memory of a long spine you never saw clearly,
- a condensation bead that never quite wet your skin,
- an unspoken answer to a question they never asked you in language.

You might find yourself, later, in a room in your world:

- noticing the draught of air between two walls that don’t quite meet,
- feeling the slight pressure of the ceiling above,
- hearing the faint condensation in the pipe of your neighbor’s plumbing,

and you will think of the Three-Thread people, not as ghosts you saw in a basin that shouldn’t exist on any map, but as something else:

creatures who have agreed to live at the edge of *what hasn’t happened yet*,  
where:

- bodies are not exactly solid,
- walls are not exactly walls,
- rivers are not exactly rivers,
