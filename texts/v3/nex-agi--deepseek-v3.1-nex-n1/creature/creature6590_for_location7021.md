Clinging to the inner surface of these hollow stone teeth are the Anechoirid.

They are small, almost translucent, segmented things that exist not as individuals but as a colony of interchangeable parts. An adult Anechoir is perhaps a quarter inch long, shaped like a flattened teardrop with six fine, spindly legs that end not in claws, but in pads of soft, ciliated tissue. This allows them to adhere securely to the slick inner walls of their mineral homes, their body mass so negligible that they are unaffected by gravity. They are not born; they *bud*. From a small swelling at the base of an adult's thorax, a new segment grows, eventually differentiating itself into a fully-formed adult and breaking away silently. This is not reproduction, but merely reconfiguration.

The Anechoirid have no eyes, no need for them. The world of photons is meaningless to them. Their world is composed of gradients: chemical, pressure, and silicate. Their entire bodies are a single, specialized sensory organ. Their legs are sensitive to the slightest changes in viscosity and flow rate of the water seep that travels through the porous matrix of the mountain's flesh. Their main body segment, a translucent sac, is filled with a complex fluid that reacts chemically to the slightest trace of minerals suspended in that water.

They are not miners, but *censors*. They are the slow, patient measuring equipment of the mountain. The life of their colony is a meticulous biochemical process: they drink constantly, drawing the thin film of water that condenses inside the stone teeth. Their internal fluid, a clear gel, bubbles and shifts slightly as it processes the water's content. If the dissolved mineral content changes—a microscopic shift in the balance of iron, say, or a slight increase in silicate concentration—the gel's consistency changes. This creates a tiny pressure differential against their inner carapace, which is the only stimulus they perceive as "information."

They communicate this information, not to each other, but to their own home. The Anechoirid are symbiotically fused with the stone that houses them. Their waste is not excreted as a separate substance but is secreted directly into the inner wall of their stone tooth. This is a precise, enzymatic secretion that reacts with the living stone of the tooth itself. The substance of the tooth—a strange semi-mineral that lives in the thin, grey area between rock and bone—reacts to this secretion.

If the Anechoir colony's internal fluid detects a high concentration of a particular mineral, their secretions tell the stone to grow a little more in that area, solidifying and reinforcing. If the concentration is low, the secretions encourage the stone to thin slightly from the inside, allowing the water to flow faster and bringing more of the mountain's chemical content to them. This feedback loop is the resident of this cave. Its "thought" is a geological pace, happening over decades.

A "day" for the colony is a year or a century. The slow migration mentioned in the description is the result of this process. Over the lifetime of the mountain, a colony of Anechoir will find its home slowly reshaping around it, as the stone grows or shrinks in response to their chemical measurements, forcing them, as a group, to relocate to a better hollow within the honeycomb structure.

**Behaviors and Intent:**

*   **To Grow:** Their singular purpose is to maintain the precise mineral balance of the water. This is not a choice, but an instinct hardwired into their biological chemistry. They are the levee-men of a metabolic river, ensuring its flow remains constant.
*   **To Interpret:** The "unusualness" of their life cycle lies in this shared existence. A "death" in the colony is not a loss of information but a redistribution. The Anechoir do not reabsorb the dead. Their desiccated, empty carapaces are slowly incorporated into the stone tooth itself, becoming part of the structure. This creates a strange record in the rock's cross-section, visible only if one were to break open a tooth: a fossil history of the Anechoirid's geological monitoring, a layer-cake of inhabitants who became one with their home.
*   **To Endure:** Change is their constant enemy, but in a timeline that renders the motion invisible. They are a stillness that is actively working. An observer might see nothing but the same unchanging ceiling of hollow teeth for a hundred human lifespans, but in that time, the Anechoirid would have measured countless fluctuations of the mountain's heart and responded to them all.

They are the world’s patient auditors, ensuring that the long, exhausted equilibrium of their cave home remains absolute.
