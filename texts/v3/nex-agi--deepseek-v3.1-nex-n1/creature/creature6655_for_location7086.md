In the Bellow Chamber of the Cavern of the Bone Tree, the only permanent resident is a single human office-holder: the Sage of Breath.

They are not chosen for wisdom, though wisdom often comes. They are chosen for lung capacity.

To be precise, they are chosen for their ability to listen without breathing.

This is a more difficult art than it sounds. In a chamber where every shifted footfall on bone-dry wood rings like a cracked bell, where every swallow echoes against the resonant trunk-column and sings back from the glass-stone benches, normal human respiration is a constant percussion. A nose whistle becomes a flute; a sigh becomes a storm.

The Sage of Breath must learn to exist within that soundscape without polluting it.

Their training begins long before they descend to the Bellow. In the upper canopies of the tree-city, they practice in silent rooms lined with felt made from fallen bark-fibers. They learn to breathe so shallowly that a feather held before their lips does not stir. They learn to slow their heart through meditation until its thudding retreats from their ears. They learn circular breathing not for music, but for silence—drawing air in through the nose while pushing the last reserve from their cheeks, smoothing the transition until there is no break in the stillness.

By the time they are ready for the Chamber, many candidates have fainted half a hundred times from hypoxia. The one who does not faint, who learns to run on almost nothing, is chosen.

When they first descend into absolute darkness, they go alone and barefoot. The wooden steps down from higher levels have been worn into smooth troughs by generations of single-file feet. There is no railing; touch is everything.

Their feet become maps. The grain of the floorboards speaks to them of age—of dry years and drier, of centuries when the trunk contracted and expanded in breaths so slow no living thing would notice if not trained to it. Their hands trace walls that are at once bark and ribcage, feeling where old scars in the wood have healed into resonant ridges, where knots vibrate with inner harmonics.

The Sage’s body alters over time.

Their chest expands and deepens; their ribs flare wide to accommodate lungs that learn to hold air like cisterns hold water in other worlds. Their skin grows pale from lack of light until it resembles sun-bleached driftwood. Their eyes never adjust—there is nothing to adjust to—but their hearing sharpens until they can distinguish between:

- The low groan of mountain rock compressing by microns over decades.
- The faint creak of cellulose fibers tightening as moisture (what little there ever was) finally leaves them forever.
- The deep thrum traveling up through root-networks from planetary mantle currents more sluggish than glaciers.
- The tiny clicks that mean some distant branch high above has shifted by less than a millimeter.
- And beneath all that, underneath even those layers:  
  The slow inhalation and exhalation of the tree itself.

The Sage’s duty is twofold:

First, they are listeners.

They sit on one of the glass-stone benches—never always on the same one—and let their bones resonate with what passes through them. They do not interpret immediately; interpretation comes later in written reports brought up by blind runners who memorize symbols traced on their palms before ascending in silence.

Instead they catalog:

The pitch of planetary breath rising from below.  
Its rhythm: days-long sighs followed by weeks-long pauses as pressure equalizes somewhere deeper than any root has ever reached.  
Variations: inflections which might mean distant geological shifts or something like tectonic dreaming.

Second—and this gives them their name—they are punctuators.

Once per long cycle (measured outside by stars; measured here only by hunger), when certain harmonics align within trunk and stone and sky-shell far above, when gravity’s subtle pull tugs at just such an angle… then and only then does breath become bellow.

The Sage rises from bench or floor or wall-lean and chooses a place upon root-turned-column or bench or ceiling-wave where resonance will be strongest at that moment’s frequency configuration (for nothing stands still). With hands trained over years without sight guides alone – feeling texture like braille – they position themselves so lungs can empty with maximal effect into chamber shape around them rather than into open space above chest cavity below ribs all tuned now together as if strung on invisible wires drawn taut across centuries…
