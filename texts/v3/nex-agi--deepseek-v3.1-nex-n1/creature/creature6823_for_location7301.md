Beneath the host of cold constellations fixed in stone, the citizens of this remnant world are modest and precise. No great beast hunts here, no chorus of voices confuses the quiet; instead, three castes share the underworld, each bent to a narrow habit, together stitching a single, endless gesture.

---

**The Ceiling‑Herd**

The phosphor stones have their worshipers. In the cool vault overhead swarm the *fumewings*, pale motes that resemble translucent rice grains strung on threads of wing. They do not buzz or click; their flight is soundless, their bodies so light they drift more than fly. From below they look like low clouds of incense rising and falling in languid columns.

Fumewings live only where the stone sky is brightest. They cling to the phosphor nodules in trembling clusters that darken each glowing point with a shifting rime. When some disturbance passes—a breath from a deeper shaft, a tremor from settling rock—they lift off in unison and stream to the next brightest node, threading the ceiling like smoke rolling along a corridor.

They are born from damp cracks where wall meets vault: tiny larvae that graze on the black sheen film that passes here for vegetation. That film is their whole world: they eat it, mate on it, lay their eggs within its faintly sticky matrix. When they die, most fall into the basin far below, where their bodies become a faint protein rain that feeds everything else.

Their only purpose is conversion: they turn stone‑cold light and wall‑slime into flight and fragile bodies, and then into falling ash.

---

**The Floor‑Lickers**

On the tired terraces and half‑forgotten streets crawl the *dust‑palm weavers*, flattened things whose bodies resemble a human palm turned sideways and given too many fingers. Each “finger” is a jointed leg; together they ripple like a hand walking.

They are not builders but rearrangers. Their lives are spent traveling between two acts:

- Where dust has gathered in sheltered courtyards or under leaning lintels, they scrape it up with soft mouth‑brushes that make no sound against stone.
- Where dampness lingers in seams along walls or at basin edges, they deposit this dust in thin layers, patting it down with their many legs until it forms a fragile crust like weak paper.

From above, their work looks like an intricate stippling etched into the city’s floor: networks of tiny depressions and raised trails that cover wide swaths of stone. Over generations these patterns have themselves become stone’s last ornament; erosion has not erased them so much as ratified them into faint reliefs on tired slabs.

Dust‑palm weavers never enter deep tunnels; they circle only where faint light falls from above. Their shells are bone‑gray with darker stains along joints where dampness collects—almost indistinguishable from rubble until you see them move. They have neither eyes nor ears worth naming; they sense vibration through their legs and humidity through pits along their bellies. 

They do not store or hoard; each mouthful of dust is merely moved from one hollow to another. Yet collectively this constant shifting slows erosion just enough to keep certain floors almost level, certain walls from slumping entirely into gravel. They are maintenance crews for a city that no longer remembers what maintenance was for.

---

**The Mud‑Dancers**

In the shallow basin at the chamber’s heart lives the third caste: *mud–dancers*, soft ribbons of flesh that seem half animal and half embodied current. Flatworm cousins who have forgotten how to be worms or rivers, they swim through silt instead of water.

Their bodies can flatten to near transparency or thicken to clumsy ridges as conditions change. Locomotion is an undulant crawl along the bed of ashen milk water: muscles ripple down their length in waves timed precisely with small eddies so they always move with rather than against the flow.

Mud‑dancers neither hunt nor flee; theirs is a life arranged around construction that will never hold.

- In calmer patches where water barely breathes across silt plains, two or three dancers wind around one another in slow spirals until pressure beneath them compacts soft mud into unstable pillars.
- As columns rise toward surface light from phosphor stars above—thin fingers trembling upward—the dancers continue working around base level: shoring up sides here while undermining there.
- Eventually weight overcomes cohesion: pillars sag sideways like melting candles then slump back down into liquid earth again while dancers ride collapsing streams away toward next constructions already being drafted elsewhere by similar clusters elsewhere along basin floor(s).

This cycle repeats endlessly throughout measured days measured only by drop-and-answer rhythm kept between distant cracks overhead: build something almost vertical; let gravity undo it gently rather than violently; begin again immediately using exactly same material now redistributed more interestingly across basin’s floor(s).
