They do not call themselves anything in the way you think of names, because naming is a thing done from outside.

From the inside of a mountain’s breath, you are simply one of the ways the world knows itself.

---

They are thin not because they lack substance but because they are the edge of something else.

The body you think of as “individual” is not a boundary. They are not born, not in the way you assume birth: a sudden entry from elsewhere, a cry and a first breath of sky-air. They thicken from the floor of the world. Heat presses the stone; pressure tightens on the faint minerals sleeping in basalt veins; a lattice of memory wakes, and a filament of what you might call “self” thickens upward from the floor.

At first, they are not tall enough to be seen.

For years that you would measure in heartbeats but they cannot count because they have no hearts, they are only underground pulses of crystalline habit, thin films of chemical memory that have not yet broken the surface of the visible. They are, in your terms, a child’s worth of years before the first translucent stalk of “self” rises into the chamber of the Still Breath.

By then, they have already existed longer than you imagine.

They begin as memory: heat recalls heat, recalls pressure, recalls the last time they grew this way and sloughed this way and leaned away from the pillar. They are not quite minds, but they are not inert. They are patterns of information grown in the mineral’s slow language, more like a sound than a sentence.

When you see them, they are already many versions deep.

---

Each one does not look like a creature of your world.

Their body is a single column of translucent shell that you mistake as “skin” but is not; it is more like the boundary layer of a heat gradient made visible, a thin fold of the world where temperature becomes form. They rise from the floor on a fibrous root of darker stone than the surrounding basalt, as if the stone chose, at that point, to remember itself more carefully.

They are all different heights.

One might be no taller than your hip, another brushing the ceiling of your small cathedral. They do not care about height in the way you do; higher does not mean better or more important. Each height is a stage of a cycle, not an achievement but a position in the deep pulse of the mountain.

Their bodies are not made of water; there are no cells in your sense of the word. They are structured layers of what you might call mineralized tissue or crystal-membrane, depending on which part of your world’s vocabulary you borrow. They feel like bone warmed by a sun that has never been seen, and they are smoother than they should be, given that they live in the heart of a volcano.

Their color is not color but heat.

To your eyes in the dark, they are not blue or green or red or anything you would know with a name. They are shades closer to faintly glowing gas than to anything with solid pigment. Sometimes you see the suggestion of a line along their length where they are hotter, and your mind wants to call it “vein,” but it is not. It is where the pattern of change runs more strongly than anywhere else.

Near their top, where a head would be, they thicken into a small, more complex knot of material that you instinctively recognize as the center of their awareness, even if they do not think of it as a head. They do not see eyes to you. They do not have eyes. They do not need them.

They see the world in temperature.
