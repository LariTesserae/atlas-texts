On the threshold of the high city, between the root-vault and the cloud-pressed canopy, life is not a single breath but a layered murmuring.

This basin is home to *mossflesh* and *step-tree*, *rootwrights* and *ghost-lichen*, *drip-beasts* and *silent wanderers*—each of them a slow current in the same cold river.

---

### Mossflesh

The floor that you walk on is not only ground; it is body.

What looks like a carpet of moss and lichen is a single organism-in-pieces, a modular skin of living flesh. Its true name is unpronounceable in a tongue that requires air, but you might call it *mossflesh*.

Each patch of green and silver-gray looks independent, but underground rhizomes and fungal hyphae weave them into a shared nervous system. The mossflesh spreads across the basin in a dense sheet many inches thick, its layers interleaving: a living pelt of moss and mycorrhizal mycelium and water-holding algae, all fused into one soft entity.

It does not think in human words, but it *registers*.

- **When you step on it**, your weight compresses the layers, releasing a faint breath of warmer air and a trace of scent—like crushed ice-logged fern.  
- **When you stand still**, the mossflesh remembers your pressure as a slow pattern of electric signals diffusing through the web of fibers, not like pain but like a conversation held at glacial speed.

The mossflesh is the memory of the city. It records:

- **Temperature**: tiny shifts in warmth around tree trunks, marking where step-tree crowns have thickened or thinned.  
- **Water**: where condensation gathers and channels, the mossflesh become thicker, greener, more profusely veined with white fungal threads.  
- **Footsteps**: your weight, over time, leaves a ghost trail—a faint, preferential channel of cells that may grow slightly differently for years afterward.

Because erosion here is young and ongoing, the mossflesh constantly adjusts. It thickens where a new water channel forms under the surface, thinning where the stone beneath dips or crumbles. It is not just living cushion; it is **living map**—a chart of the plateau’s changes written in damp body code.

If you could look down through the layers of mossflesh, you would see:

- **Top:** soft, fine shoots and filaments, perpetually dewy, glowing faintly along veins of bioluminescent algae.  
- **Middle:** tougher mats of interwoven mycorrhizae and rootlets, a boundary between light and dark.  
- **Deep:** where older mossflesh has partially broken down into a spongy peat, still connected by living threads to the surface, but on its way to becoming a new substrate.

The mossflesh feels **cold** at your fingers and **warm** at its inner heart. It is not warm enough to change the air temperature much, but enough to keep the slow fermentation of organic matter below it from stopping.

Beneath its surface, tiny **drip-beasts**—small, translucent invertebrates—swim through the liquid gaps between fibers and fungal nets, grazing on algae and spores. They are the ones who make the faint trembling patterns you feel underfoot when you stand very still.

### Step-Trees

The columns and vaults of this cathedraal-city are **step-trees**: highly specialized, co-evolved organisms that are **architecture**, **tool**, and **organism** at once.

They share a common ancestor with the world-girdling giants of older forests, but here they have been sculpted by something else into shapes of precise utility.

Their **trunks** are not straight by accident:

- They grow vertical and **columnar**, with minimal taper, so that neighboring trees can form regular walls of living wood.  
- Their **bark** is smooth, not to repel water but to **channel** it. Regularly spaced ridges—like etched furrows—run down each trunk, guiding condensate and sap in predictable threads.

Embedded in their trunks are **staircases**: not carved grooves or nailed boards, but **grown** features.

- **Spiral stairways** weave up the trunk, each step a slow bulge grown by the tree’s cambium layer, over years of guidance pressure from climbing rootwrights and their own internal patterning.  
- Where the stair passes, **micro-aqueducts** of bark and sapwood guide water, keeping the surface always damp—but not too damp—to prevent ice cracking.

The **canopies** of the step-trees interlock far above:

- Leaves are **unusually planar** and **two-sided**:  
  - The upper surface is **broad** and **faintly luminous**, collecting whatever weak light comes through the cloud tongue and reflecting it back down.  
  - The underside is **dark** and **absorptive**, retaking whatever light the lower world needs.  
- Branches do not grow randomly outward. Instead, they angle **horizontally** on **two** main tiers:
  - A **lower platform tier**, whose branches are thick, flattened, and interwoven to create **walkable** surfaces between trees.  
  - An **upper canopy tier**, whose interlacing branches form a **ceiling**—the roof of the city.

These **platform** branches are smooth enough to walk on, but no animal here does so casually. They are **manipulated** branches, grown to particular shapes and reinforced by tension and internal lignin.

The **step-tree** life form does not live “for itself” in any simple way. Its function:

- **Support:** It bears the weight of platforms and other layers.  
- **Transport:** It channels water from mist to roots to air.  
- **Communication:** Deep in its cells, chemical signals travel along sap lines—hormones marking “too much shade,” “not enough water,” “new branch growth here.”

They are **living infrastructure**—as much as highways are to a human city—except far slower, and more intimately alive.

### Rootwrights

The true architects of this place—if anything can be called architect—are **rootwrights**.

They are not trees, not exactly, and not animals. Rootwrights are **plant-scale** and **pod-based**: each “rootwright” is a connected **system** of nodes:

- **Body:** A bulbous, low-slung trunk, small enough to be hidden among the step-tree roots and the understory layer.
