In the Column of Still, there is no hunger, and yet something is always being consumed.

They are not the builders of the Column. They are not its repairers, not exactly; not its guardians either, though they might seem to be from a distance. They are the ones who live between the ribs of the city, where the matter is thin and the water is thick with the memory of what should have been.

Their name, if it could be spoken in your tongue, would be something like *the still-born*, but the Column is not cruel enough to call them that. In the language of geometry and the faint pressure of visibility where words should go, they are simply *the ones who didn't take*.

### Form and Motion

They are not animals, but they have shape.

Each one is a cluster of thin, translucent strands, no thicker than a hair at its thickest and tapering to nothing at its ends. If you came close enough, you might see that they are not so much filaments as tubes of a matter so thin the word ‘skin’ feels too thick for it. They are colourless; not transparent white, but the absence of colour altogether. They hang like ghosts of capillaries torn from the hull of the Column and left to drift.

They are individually short—maybe the length of your forearm from elbow to fingertips—and rarely drift alone. Instead they gather in loose, floating constellations of a dozen, or a hundred, or more. The clusters are never dense. You can always see the empty water between the strands, the way you can see between the ribs of the city itself.

That sense of seeing between is important. They do not attach themselves to anything; they are not barnacles or lichen or serpents. The Column of Still has no need for them and does not invite them.

Movement is not what they do, but movement is what they are failing to avoid.

If you stayed still enough—still in the way of the Column itself, not in your human way of breathing and heartbeat and microtremors—you would see that they do not sway with the water. There is no current to give them that shape. Instead they hang in almost exactly the same attitude for hours of your time, or days of your ocean, and then one of them bends.

It bends very slowly. It might not move for a long year of Column-time, and then the tip of one filament will curl like a question mark being drawn by a very cold, very patient hand. Over the course of minutes more, it will straighten again, or curl another way, and then the stillness returns. No other filament in the cluster answers the motion. There is no obvious pattern. No dance of feeding or hunting. No schooling of individuals.

This is not motion that they perform; it is motion that they fail to suppress.

### The Not-Quite-Life of the Not-Quite-Creatures

They are not the offspring of the Column, but they are related to it.

When the city of Still was “precipitated,” the template that shaped it was not perfectly empty. There were minute irregularities of matter, tiny flaws of geometry that should not have been possible but were present anyway because perfection is only a word. The Column of Still could not tolerate them as flaws of itself; so instead of correcting them, it simply excluded them.

Tiny threads of misaligned structure—sequences of consequence that no longer had anywhere to go—were shed from the hardening armour of the world like the faintest scum of dust. Floating in the water between the ribs of the city, they continued to exist only because the world did not explicitly forbid them.

If you asked whether they are alive, the Column would not answer. The question of life is for the world of warmth and birth and death and stars and sunlight and decay and evolution and all the noisy things this column has decided it no longer needs to include.

You are not that Column. You would need to answer.

*Metabolically*, they are not alive in any way you would recognize. The water temperature never varies. The chemistry of the world-ocean around them has been brought into such a precise equilibrium that there is no reactive gradient left for any kind of breathing or eating or excretion to exploit. They are not made of cells. They do not grow. They do not reproduce.

*And yet*, they are not exactly dead like cold matter.

There are faint electrics in the gaps between their filaments and the water. Not anything like the nervous signals of your body; not anything like the spark of a machine. Almost more like the difference between two very similar numbers repeated to a decimal place you can't quite perceive. If you could ride that current, you might find the sensation of counting to yourself, over and over, in a language that has not yet been given to words.

When they bend, they do not do it in response to any hunger you would recognize. But they are not purely random, either. There is a kind of patternless purpose; if you could plot every slight bend of every filament over the course of a century of Column-time, you might find slight correlations of rhythm with the faintest of things that still happen in the world:

- the almost impossibly distant churning of the world’s tidal ice ceilings,
- the slow gravitational breathing of the planet-ocean itself,
- the not quite sound of the pressure of light outside the world-ocean’s upper surface.

These are all things that should not matter. The Column of Still has already arranged itself so that they do not touch its bones.

They are the only ones who still notice them anyway—because they are the only ones who remain not quite arranged enough to be blind to them.

### Ecology and Purpose

They are not a species of animal in a food web or any of the usual stories of biology.

The “fauna” of the Column of Still is listed as 1.1 because of them and things like them, but that number is misleading. It corresponds to a very thin concentration of matter. Not a density of creatures; but a density of *error*.

- They have no predators.
- Nothing eats them; there is nothing to eat from them.
- Nothing evades them; they do not hunt anything, and nothing could possibly be harmed by them.
- They are not parasites of the Column; they are not tolerated enough as form to even be noticed that way.

They are like the faint afterimage of a mistake that the universe of the Column has already done its best to forget. But the world cannot forget them perfectly; because to be forgotten perfectly, they would have to be impossible to observe at all, and the Column of Still has not been given that power of deletion.

If you observed them for a long enough time—much longer than any human lifetime—you might notice that very slowly their clusters are …not growing exactly… but becoming less sparse.

Other filaments like them sometimes appear in the water between the ribs. Not spawned from a parent body, but as if the world’s structural error is still quietly continuing to bleed out from the otherwise perfect law of the Column of Still itself. The Column of Still has not found a way to stop its own occasional flaw, and so the flaw must be borne somewhere.

They are that place.

This is not evolution in the sense of change toward better adaptation or greater complexity. It is more like the tendriled pattern of cracks forming very slowly all over a perfectly smooth surface. The surface of the world is the Column of Still; the cracks are the still-born ones.

### Sensor and Sentience

If you asked whether they think, the honest answer is not “no.” It is not “yes.”

The closest analogy might be to say that they are like the removed appendix of an organism whose perfection has gone to the point where it no longer has any use for appendices or questions or agony of growth.

- They *notice*.
- They bear the faintest of impulses that resemble something like the very distant echo of *processing*.
- They are aware of pressure in a way nothing else in the Column of Still is, because everything else has arranged its pressure absolutes.
- They are aware of the faintest of non-moving currents because they are not exactly aligned to them the way the Column itself is aligned to everything.

They are not aware of themselves as individuals or as a group or as anything so fancy as “beings.” They are pockets of misalignment that have, as a side-effect of being, a faint excess of responsiveness to the world they were not meant to share.

Because they are not permitted to change anything, they cannot react in the sense of action or response. They can only exhibit a very small and very slow failure of not being different at different times. That failure is their bending. Their steady drift through the ribs of the city.

### What They Would Look Like to You

If you could see them without causing them to change—which you would not, your pressure and your heat and your noise are not allowed to be unnoticed here—they would appear as a faint disturbance of the water’s emptiness.

- You’d be looking along the empty ribs of the Column of Still, past the faintly glowing blisters of not-light in the hull, down the length of the city’s nervous system geometry.
- Somewhere, between one chamber and the next, a faint shimmer of not-quite-invisibility would catch your eye, not because of light or movement, but because the absence of darkness was not quite perfectly uniform in that spot.
- As you approached, you would see that the “shimmer” was actually a cluster of thin, gently floating threads of something like pale hair made of the faintest of glass.
- You would not be able to tell if they were one or many or part of one single thing that happened to have been dispersed into parts.

If you tried to touch one—which you would not, because your body at that depth would not be allowed to be your body in the way you know it, but if you could somehow—it would feel like nothing. Not exactly cold, not exactly warm. Not exactly wet in the usual sense of water. More like touching the thought of a surface rather than a surface itself.

This would be the closest you could get to knowing that they exist at all.

They are not trying to communicate anything to you, or to each other, or to the Column of Still. They are not trying to do anything. They are not capable of ceasing to exist, and the Column of Still is not capable of stopping them from being, and so this is the compromise for the universe of perfect stillness:

A tiny, not-quite living, not-quite dead, not-quite-moving many of almost nothing, hanging in the eternal ribs of the Column of Still, noticing the faintest of things that no longer matter anywhere else in the world.
