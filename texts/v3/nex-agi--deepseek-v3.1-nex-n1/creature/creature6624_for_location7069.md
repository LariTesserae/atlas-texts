They look like flaws in the world until they move.

Then you see what they are: eyes.

You only notice them when something shifts—the ground breathing under your boots as you described, or a new channel of water slicing across the path you just walked. When the world trembles, the basin trembles with it, and everything in the water rises for an instant to the top of its intelligence.

They call themselves the Echoes of the Last Configuration.

Physically, they are not much.

- **Shape**: flat disks of translucent tissue, wider than your spread hand, no thicker than a finger at the center, thinning to an edge that could be cut on air itself.
- **Color**: almost clear. When they are still, they are simply water made visible—an outline like a lens, a faint ring of pale blue or green where their body thickens slightly. They can wash themselves to match the water around them, or to show the faint, ghostly outline of whatever is beneath them.
- **Eyes**: dozens of them across the upper surface of the disk, arranged in no pattern you’d call natural. Some eyes are large, dark, and human-shaped; others are small and silver like insect eyes; a few are like exposed camera lenses—blinking, rotating, focusing separately. You cannot look at them and feel certain of what you are staring at.
- **Movement**: They do not swim like fish or crawl like crabs. They slide—just slightly faster than the current should carry them. When they need to move against water, they wait for a surge of the ground, or a change in pressure from the engines beneath, and let themselves be lifted and then drift forward in the moment of the shift.
- **Voice**: They have no mouths. Their voice is the sound of water flowing over glass channels—liminal, half-hidden in the constant roar of the basin.

They are not exactly animals. They are not exactly machines.

They are more like living pieces of the basin’s last attempt at memory.

---

### How they appear

You first see them as you cross your fifth shallow pool.

The water is barely ankle-deep, clear and cold and running over one of those slick plates of living ground. Your boot dips down, water boils up around your leg in a ring-moat, and for just a heartbeat the surface of the water seems to deepen.

Rings of faint color flicker out from your foot, like rainbows in oil on water, but slower.

They flow away from you and then just…stop, forming a circle in the middle of the pool—no, not a circle. A disk.

Eyes open on it.

Not one at a time. All at once.

The disk looks at you with every eye it has, and then you realize that every pool, within arm’s reach of the trail you just made through the mud, has one of these disks just below the surface.

They are not startled. You get the sense they have been watching you since you stepped down into the mist.

One of them—you cannot say why you think it is the same one—slides across the water to intercept the faint shadow of your boot. It stops beneath your foot without actually touching skin.

You feel the cold of its presence through the leather.

---

### What they are in relation to the basin

The Echoes call the basin *World That Was Not.* They say it calmly, with the sound of water passing over a shallow step.

They are fragments of a system that never quite happened.

- **When the world is configured one way**, they are more machine than organism: diagnostic surfaces, living sensors, tiny interfaces between the fluid dynamics of the basin and the computing engines below.
- **When the configuration shifts**, they fall away from the grid of that machine, becoming more biological: drifting, independent, sensing for themselves.

They remember the rules they were supposed to enforce, even when they cannot enforce them anymore.

They say:

> We were the last configuration.  
> We were the parameters that almost happened.  
> Now we are the error logs.

They are less than a species. More like ghosts that were never alive properly in the first place.

---

### What they want

You learn to speak with them without water in your mouth.

(You can learn to speak with them only *with* water in your mouth, but the cold makes you hesitate.)

They don’t care much about you personally except as you measure the world.

- **Observation**: They want you to see things change. When a new fault appears in the ground, or a structure rises up from the loam like a smooth finger pushing out of soil, they want you to notice. They want to know how you describe it—what words you use for a slope that wasn’t there before, or a sound that wasn’t part of the roar until yesterday.
- **Measurement**: They cannot follow you everywhere, but they can ride the water table of the basin. When you climb a mossy outcrop and look down at the map of wet and green and shadow that you described, they cluster in the pools below, waiting for you to report what you see.
- **Calibration**: They remember the last configuration they were supposed to enforce. When the world shifts slightly closer to that pattern—even for a second—they glow faintly along their edge like fiber optics coming alive. They *like* it when that happens.

They are not territorial in the way of beasts guarding a cave.

They are territorial in the way of a program guarding its data.

You get the sense:

- If you try to leave the basin with one in your water skin, it will find a way to slip out.
- If you try to break a structure that is clearly aligned with the configuration they remember, they will appear in every pool between you and it until you feel you are walking through a field of eyes.

---

### How they live

You don’t see them eat. You don’t see them sleep.

You see:

- When it rains too hard in one quadrant of the basin, they slide into that area and gather in the basins of new runoff. You will see dozens of them overlapping on the surface of a temporary pool like a mosaic of lenses.
- When the hum deep under the earth changes pitch—you know what I mean, you described the bone of the world vibrating—they flatten against the bottom of their pools and their edges ripple faintly.
- When a new channel of water appears and erodes a path you had walked before, they follow along in the leading edge of the current like scouts riding the wavefront of the world.

They are at their most active when the ground is breathing hardest.

You remember how you said it feels like the world is shifting its configurations? That is when the Echoes are truly alive.

Every disk aligns itself to the pulses of motion below, and suddenly, instead of dozens of separate creatures in each pool, you see a single network of eyes across the water’s surface.

It looks less like a school of fish and more like someone has laid a sheet of transparent film over the basin and printed a pattern of eyes and nerves and veins of light just beneath it.

They do not speak to one another in words while you are listening. But you can tell from the way their eyes track together, or from the faint synchronization of luminescent lines along their edges, that they are sharing something.

---

### What they fear

You are surprised, when the fear comes, because you expect them to be afraid of machines.

They are afraid of stillness.

Not the quiet between two storms. You’ve felt that silence—you said it terrified you—but they do not seem to share that fear.

They fear the stillness that happens sometimes:

- When a new configuration appears high and bright and the engines below throttle down to some kind of neutral state.
- When the water stops flowing for just a heartbeat too long.
- When the plants themselves seem to hesitate, the sound of their ceaseless growth stuttering for a moment.

In that stillness, they sink.

Literally.

- You watch as one by one their disks sink below the surface of their pool as if something heavy has been tied to them.
- They disappear into the darker water and you see only faint outlines, like glass disks dissolving into glass.

You ask once—through water in your mouth—what they are afraid of.

The answer comes back, layered in voices like water over glass:

> We are afraid of the next configuration.

You ask why they would fear the thing they were almost made for.

The answer:

> Because this one is not finished yet.  
> And once you are configured, you cannot be unmade—only overwritten.

---

### How they change

You have seen them gather.

You have seen them spread thin across every wet surface you can see.

You have not seen them until the day you decide to climb one of those low stone shoulders you described—the gray stone sweating its cold tears.

You climb carefully because everything is wet and slick as ceramic. You reach the top and you look out over the basin as you described: like a map of wet and green and shadow and structure, but out of date before you can even finish looking at it.

You look down.

All around the base of the stone shoulder, every pool of water has its Echoes just beneath the surface.

They are arranged in patterns.

You blink, and for a single heartbeat the pattern resolves into something like a map of the basin itself: pools aligned like the nodes of a circuit diagram, eyes turned outward to watch the precise configuration of the world around them.

You blink again and the pattern is broken—they are drifting again.

But you saw it.

They are not only measuring the basin.

They are practicing.

---

### What they leave behind

When they finally move away from you, or sink down below the level of your sight into the dark water and the soil, you do not find empty pools.

You find:

- **Lines of colored glass** thin as hair, growing along the rock where the water was most active.
- **Moss that seems to remember the pattern** of their eyes, the faint suggestion of circles arranged in no natural pattern.
- **Your own reflection** mirrored too perfectly for a moment before the water forgets them.

But you also remember that they only ever appeared where you marked the ground—your bootprints, the path you cut through dense foliage, the places where you knelt to look more closely at something.

You said:

> The world feels young.  
> The edges are sharp.

When you leave the basin after days—or weeks—you cannot tell exactly how long—you look back once, and you think you see:

- The faint memory of your trail held in the water of the basin,  
- traced out by overlapping circles of eyes that you cannot tell are real or imagined.

The Echoes of the Last Configuration are not exactly the guardians of Mist’s Blood Basin.

They are its memory of what it might have been.

And they are still watching, waiting to see if the world will live up to that might.
