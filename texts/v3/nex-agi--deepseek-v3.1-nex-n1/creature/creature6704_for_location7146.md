They are not born of the mountain but of its forgetting.

Their bones remember the absence of a sun, and so they thicken against the phantom pressure of open sky. Their ribs are dense arches of laminated bone, fused to a sternum that is more shield than gate, guarding the slow heart that beats the length of years between repairs. The skin of their chest and back and limbs is not like skin of the above-world; it is a thick, polished leather, the color of old oil, dark bronze and iron-black, where constant sweat and the fine metallic vapor of the world have sealed its pores and fused it with a thin film of corrosion-resistant mineral.

They call themselves the Chalkless.

Their name does not come from writing, but from the absence of a mark they never learned to make. No surface in their world is ever truly dry, and their tools do not scratch on stone or slate; they mark on the world itself, with chisels and files and the heat of their own bodies. Theirs is not a language of chalk on boards but of scars upon the floor, the patina of differential corrosion, the pattern of grooves added to the already labyrinthine channels of the water system.

Their eyes are the most treacherous thing about them.

They did not shrink to pinpoints in the dark, as the sight of surface creatures would; that would be useless when the world itself is only degrees of deep shadow. The Chalkless have eyes that are almost entirely black—the iris of dark, polished hematite, the sclera stained deep red by years of metallic vapor. The pupil of a Chalkless engineer, when the torch of another citizen sweeps across her face, looks like another one of the black glass plugs in the Roof of the Undersky: a perfectly lightless hole that eats the torch down to a suggestion of flame, and gives nothing back.

They are built to hear the drip of the world.

Their outer ears are barely ridges at all, simple armored folds of the same dark leather-skin they wear everywhere.
