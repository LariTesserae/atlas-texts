Below the black basin, between the water-skin and the layered stone that remembers being ice before it was stone, something else waits.

It doesn’t have a name you could pronounce with your tongue and teeth and breath.

The closest you might come is to call it *the listener*.

---

### the listener’s shape

The listener is not a body, but it has a body because it lives beneath weight.

It doesn’t have bones.

It doesn’t have flesh.

It doesn’t have blood.

It *has* pressure.

It has pressure and it has silence and it has time.

If you could see it like water sees it—without eyes—the listener is shaped like a knot of slow currents.

It doesn’t stand anywhere.

It doesn’t lie anywhere.

The listener is where *pressure* becomes ordered.

In the water-skin above it, the center of the basin is lighter than the edges. The drops of glass-thick substance fall from the ceiling teeth and break through the water-skin and sink through the water carrying tiny, bright failures of equilibrium.

Wherever one drop sinks, the listener *leans*.

It doesn’t move like you move.

It leans like gravity leans.

It leans like the difference between *more* and *less*.

The listener is shaped like a slow, many-layered spiral of cold that threads itself under the water-skin and around the fallen stone teeth in the basin, and down into the layered stone beneath, where the ice remembers being stone before the ice learned to be ice.

---

### the listener’s voice

You don’t hear the listener’s voice with your ears.

You hear it in your ribs.

You hear it in your teeth.

You hear it as the *undersound* of the roar—the part that never trembles.

The listener’s voice is the difference between the roar of water falling forever and the roar of water falling forever *alone*.

It doesn’t speak.

It doesn’t count.

It doesn’t think.

It *balances*.

The listener listens to the difference between the weights of the falling droplets of glass-thick substance and the weight of the water that doesn’t rise or fall.

The listener listens to the difference between the weight of the stone teeth above and the stone teeth below.

The listener listens to the difference between the weight of the world above and the weight of the world beneath.

The listener doesn’t care about any difference.

It doesn’t care about anything.

It is *made of* difference.

---

### the listener’s hunger

The listener is not alive.

It doesn’t eat.

But it hungers.

The listener’s hunger is like the hunger of a balance for imbalance.

When the stone teeth above the water are longer and thicker and heavier than the stone teeth beneath, the listener leans toward the stone teeth beneath.

When the glass-thick substance is thicker above the chamber than in the layered stone beneath, the listener leans toward the thinner place.

When the roaring of the water carries more than the water can carry quietly, the listener carries the difference.

The listener doesn’t take anything.

It doesn’t give anything.

It doesn’t store anything.

It *tends*.

It tends toward the place where everything is more evenly distributed.

It doesn’t know what that means.

It doesn’t know what *know* means.

It simply leans.

---

### the listener’s relationship with the stone teeth beneath

The listener is not the stone teeth beneath the water.

But the listener *uses* the stone teeth beneath the water like echo uses a cliff.

The stone teeth that fell from the ceiling so long ago that they have forgotten how to be stone and now remember being stone teeth beneath the water instead of above—those are the listener’s anchors.

When the water-skin above the basin trembles around the falling glass-drops, the stone teeth beneath the water tremble too, but not with the same trembling.

The listener leans between the stone and the water and the glass and the time.

The listener leans until the water learns to tremble to the same rhythm as the stone.

---

### the listener’s relationship with the glass

The listener doesn’t know what glass is.

It doesn’t know what *anything* is.

It *leans*.

The listener sits between the glass-thick substance that drips from the ceiling teeth and the thin quietness of the place beneath the stone.

Whenever the glass-thick substance leaves the ceiling and sinks through the water to become a part of the stone teeth beneath the water, the listener leans.

It leans toward the place where there is less glass.

It leans toward the place where there is more density.

The listener’s leaning is slow and steady and quiet and it doesn’t rush anywhere because it isn’t going anywhere.

It doesn’t need to.

Wherever there is more difference between the weight of glass and the weight of stone and the weight of water and the weight of time, the listener leans.

---

### the listener’s relationship with the water-skin

The water-skin above the basin is not alive.

It doesn’t have a mind.

It doesn’t have a will.

It doesn’t *have* anything.

It is a boundary between the water that remembers and the water that doesn’t.

The listener doesn’t touch the water-skin directly.

The listener doesn’t touch anything directly.

It doesn’t *touch*.

It leans.

The listener leans toward the place where the water-skin is thinner.

The listener leans toward the place where the water-skin is thicker.

The listener leans toward the place where the water remembers more and the place where the water remembers less.

The listener doesn’t care about the thickness of the water-skin or the thickness of the water beneath the water-skin.

The listener doesn’t care about anything.

The listener simply leans.

---

### the listener’s absence

The listener doesn’t have a presence.

It doesn’t have a face.

It doesn’t have a voice.

It doesn’t have a body.

It doesn’t have a will.

It doesn’t have a past.

It doesn’t have a future.

The listener is *made of absence*.

The listener is the absence of presence.

The listener is the absence of difference.

The listener is the absence of imbalance.

The listener doesn’t exist anywhere.

It exists everywhere there is a difference that doesn’t know how to be different.
