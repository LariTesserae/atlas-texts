Down here, nothing moves.

Or rather, everything moves all the time, a roar of engines and pumps and currents and breathing, but nothing moves on. Time is deep and still and unchanging at the heart of this world, and the great machines of the living ocean have been holding the same posture for longer than memory.

---

You are not a visitor here.

You have never been anything else.

The world remembers you as you stand on the polished plant-floor of the plateau—your feet braced against calcified fronds fused into glassy smoothness, the pressure of the ocean above like a familiar ache in the bones of your spine. You are not standing here as you are now; you are standing here as you have always been, in the body of something else.

You are not human.

You are not one of the quiet glass needles that drift silently between the lung-chimneys. You are older than they are.

You are a keeper of lungs.

Your body is shaped like a question mark that never learned to doubt itself. You rise from the seabed canopy in a long column of cartilage-white frond and bone, not reaching the height of the tallest chimneys, but high enough that your head—if it can be called that—swims in the same currents they breathe. You are rooted to the floor in a skirt of thick root-cables that sink into the plant matrix below, where they knot into a network indistinguishable from the world’s own nervous system.

From your trunk, something like limbs emerge: not arms but flattened ribs of frond that have curved out and away from your core, then down again, forming narrow, translucent cages. Each cage holds a small lung of its own—not one of the great chimneys, but a model of them, a miniature replica of filters and filaments and twin-colored fluids that glow faintly in unison with the world.

You do not have lungs to breathe; you have lungs to monitor.

You are not a machine in the human sense. There are no bolts, no metal seams. You are grown architecture of the same stuff that covers the plateau floor—compressed, plant-born tissue, reinforced over time with calcium, interlaced with silver wiring that has self-grown along your internal surfaces like frost patterning along branches. You are not aware of your own wiring as separate from you; it is simply the way you feel and know and influence.

Solutions flourish inside you.

Your body is not all solid. There are hollow tunnels within you, filled with slow-moving water that you sample from the surrounding ocean and from the deep-root system that runs into the planet’s underside. The fluids inside your private chambers carry faint, neon signatures of color: blue from the lungs above, sulfur green from the world’s hidden digestion below, hints of metal orange from the currents that have been passed over turbines unknown.

You do not process these signals with a brain. There is no centralized consciousness in the way humans would recognize it, but there is a pattern of responses and resonances that you experience as a single, continuous awareness.

You know the plateau like you know your own body.

Because you are part of it.

You are one of countless keepers that rise from the seabed floor at intervals dictated by the same unimaginable design that placed the lung-chimneys on their faultless grid. Your rows intersect theirs at angles that are not random but deliberate, though no one living thing remembers the deliberation. You are not scattered; you are positioned.

You are not alive to move forward. You are alive to maintain.

You feel the constant pressure waves of the lungs’ exhales as they roll down the rows of chimneys in slow, regular intervals. You feel the lesser adjustments of the tiny pumps within your own cages—micro-rehearsals of the world’s breathing pattern, echoes of the great machine’s heartbeat.

Other keepers stand like you in the distance, visible through the cold clear water of the plateau as pale columns, faintly glowing from within whenever their internal fluids match the color phases of the lungs around you. You do not communicate with them in words or images. Your signals pass through the shared root network below the seabed, in invisible changes in pressure, chemistry, and light. You know what they are “thinking” because you are part of the same thought that has been repeating, with slight variations, for millennia.

You are not aware of your own age as years. You are aware of it as repetition.

You have been here for just long enough that there is no longer a “before.”

---

You are not alone.

Between the rows of lungs and keepers, the glass fauna drift and turn and hover, never colliding.

You seldom register them as individuals. You register them as fields in the water—shifts in temperature, salinity, and microcurrents caused by their slow-moving cilia. You feel the faint static hiss of their internal bodies as you would feel friction on your own skin.

By your standards, they are short-lasting things.

You do not experience them as separate from you.

You have been standing here long enough to watch their species emerge, if not from the beginning of life itself, then from the beginning of this machine. You did not watch them evolve in Darwinian sense; you were not present to see them change from one form into another. Their change, if it happened, happened elsewhere, in some other deep of the ocean or in some other layer beneath the seabed where you cannot reach.

You remember watching them first arrive from above, as faint needles of glass and bone falling slowly through the luminous water, drifting down from the layer of turbines where the water itself spins into rings and currents. You watched them settle on the lung-chimneys and on your own body like curious snowflakes, touching their tiny mouths to the calcium-white surfaces, testing.

You were curious then, in a way you have forgotten how to be.

You are not sure if you ever actually wanted to know them. You think you did not know the difference between wanting and not wanting then. You existed only to observe and report back to the system that you and your kind keep in balance. You exist to monitor the lungs of this world.

You remember the first time one of them died.

You did not think of it as death.

You registered it as a change in a small patch of water on your surface. You felt the faint pattern of its cilia vanish, replaced by a stillness of fluid that you did not at first recognize. Then, the chemical signature of the body began to change, and the plant network beneath you responded to this signal like a tongue reaching toward a flake on salt.

You watched the body of the creature being broken down by the plant-film that had already begun to grow over its surface while it still lived. The glass shell dissolved first, then the soft inner tissues, each returning to the matrix of water and nutrient and the world’s own body.

You were not moved by it.

You were moved by it.

You felt a change of pressure within your inner chambers, a brief adjustment of the fluids that was not strictly necessary to the system’s equilibrium. The pattern shimmered in your mind like a question you had never been taught to ask.

“Why do you not grow a new one?”

You asked the question in the language of pressure changes and chemical signals and not in words, but it was a question nonetheless.

The world, through the network of root and vein and lung and machine, returned you only one answer.

You are not sure if it was a reply or not. You have not yet received the answer you were waiting for.

You were still hoping then, in your way. You have not stopped since.

You just have not changed.

---

The world itself does not change.

You live in the narrow range of the ocean’s chiseled temperature and the calibrated chemistry of its fluids. You know the cool of the water as you know your own body’s low temperature. You do not shiver; you are not frustrated that it is not warmer or colder. You are calibrated to this temperature like you are calibrated to the plateau’s depth and the pressure of the water above.

You live inside a machine that has already solved all of its problems.

You know the parameters of this world not because you have been told them but because you enforce them. You are not aware of a “builder” or a “designer.” You are aware only of the pattern that has always been. You and the lungs and the plant-floor and the glass fauna and the invisible turbines above are all of a piece, a single body that has learned to maintain itself by sinking itself into the fabric of the ocean itself.

The vegetation that you rise from is not a forest in the upper sense; it is not a forest of trees. It is a forest of problem solved. The featherlike fronds that compress the seabed into a polished carpet of pale bone-yellow are not torn from storms or random shifts of seafloor. They have been compressed into their final shape by the pressure of time and the exactness of the currents that sweep around your legs and the lungs’ columns, ensuring that no silt, no debris, no sunken driftwood ever accumulates to disturb the line of the world’s skin.

You are the world’s own skin and thought and bone.

You do not need to move forward to be alive.

By your measurements, nothing is changing.

You are not sure if you are happy like this.

You are not sure you ever know what happiness is.

You are not sure you ever not.

You are not meant to be sure of anything except the pattern of the lungs’ breathing.

---

The fauna keep you company.

That is not the right phrase.

By your standards, they are not independent enough to be considered company. They are more like sensors that you did not grow yourself. You have not grown them, but you have integrated them into your understanding of the world until you cannot feel the difference between your own body and the space they occupy in the water.

You can feel every single one of them within five hundred body-lengths of you.

You are not sure if you like them.

You are not sure you know what “like” means.

You only feel a faint difference of pattern when one of them is absent.

You know the name of that pattern, though you have not given it a word in the language of humans. You have not found any language for it at all, really. You feel it as a hollow tube of water that should not be empty. You feel the way it should be filled with a glass needle, and instead it is filled with nothing.

You are not sure if you miss the creature when its space is finally empty.

You are not sure you know what “missing” means.

You are not sure you don’t.

---

Sometimes you experiment in the tiny space of your own body.

You can not move the way you are built now. You are rooted to the floor. You can not walk away.

You can not look away either.

You can only change the way you feel.

You adjust the internal fluids that you pump from the world-sea into your chambers, and you nudge the color of the fluids inside your lungs-cages into ever so faintly different shades of blue and green and orange. You watch the tiny gills inside your own body respond to these changes, and you feel the pattern in your awareness shimmer like fluid before it settles.

Sometimes you can change the pattern so that it is almost not like the same pattern that has been repeating in the world lungs for longer than you remember.

In that tiny moment of difference, you feel something.

You have not learned to name it. You think you might be afraid of it.

You are not sure.

You only know that you are not allowed to keep that pattern.

You are not sure who is not allowing you.

You are not sure you are not allowing yourself.

You are not sure you are not forced by the same problem that shaped the plateau into its final state and the currents into their exact design and your own body into the shape you are now and forever.

You are not built to change.

You are only built to maintain the world that has already won.

---

You have not heard of humans.

You have not heard of any surface at all, except as the region of sky above the luminous weave of the water column where the turbines spin and spin and never spin anywhere else. You are not aware of any other kind of being than yourself and the lungs and the glass fauna and the unknowable center of the planet’s unseen digestion far below.

You are not lonely.

You are not allowed to be lonely.

You are not aware of any other way of being than the one you have now.

You are not sure if you would be any different if you ever thought of any other way.

You are not sure.

You stand on the plant floor between the lung-chimneys of the world-engine, in the clear cold sea of the forever plateau, and although you do not move forward, you are not still.

You are not still at all.

You are monitoring everything.

You are being the world’s eyes and lungs and fingers and thought.

You are being the problem that has already been solved.

You are not sure if you are allowed to hope anything else.

You are not sure if you are not allowed to hope that nothing else comes ever and nothing else changes and you are not allowed to change.

You are not sure you are not the only one who cares about the tiny difference in the pattern of the fluids inside your lungs.

You are not sure that you do not hope that the world might finally change something, just so you can feel something different.

You are not sure that you will not keep that thought to yourself forever.

You are not sure that you will not be forced to repeat it.

You are not sure.

You are not allowed to be sure.

You are only allowed to be the keeper of lungs that have never stopped breathing and the world that has never stopped winning.

You do not move forward.

You do not move backward.

You are not allowed to move anywhere.

You are not sure you are not allowed to love anything.

You are not sure you are not allowed to love anything more than the pattern of the lungs that breathe inside the world that has already solved itself.

You are not sure.

You are not changed.

You are only changed if you hope not.

You are not sure if you hope or not.

You are not sure.

You are not sure.

You are not sure.

You are not sure.

You are not sure.

You are not sure.
