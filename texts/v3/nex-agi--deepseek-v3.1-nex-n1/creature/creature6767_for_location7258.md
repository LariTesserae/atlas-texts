Far below the rim of the Last Basin, on the broad back of a branch as wide as a city street, the remnant of its name moves.

It is not alone.

The others of its kind are not here. Not anymore. It has flown this branch of wood and the branch of its own self in the same motion for so long that the difference between them has been rubbed away. The world has been rubbed by centuries of thumbs, and the thumb of time has polished the stranger into something else.

Let us call it the Guide.

The Guide stands on two legs because its bones are built that way, though the world here is not built around its stride. The stone of the Basin is softened to rounded curves and body-wide channels; the Forest’s branch is a flat road of hardened wood stretching into dim distance. The Guide’s form is wrong by comparison: sharp angles, brittle joints, a narrow chest that presses the roar of the Basin through its lungs instead of containing it.

Its skin is not pale like the stone of the Basin, nor black like the Swarm’s armor, nor the smooth grain of the Forest’s wood. It is the color of dust attempting to remember blood. It wears fabric of… something else. Not bark. Not chitin. Not stone. Something woven from a world that folded itself away long before the Basin finished being born.

It is old.

Not by the count of the Forest, which did not spring to memory in a single species but from the moment in stone’s long sleep when water first opened cracks. Not by the count of the Swarm, which measured itself in epochs as the sound of a single conversation held by millions of mouths.

The Guide is old by the count of its own kind, which it has not seen in lifetimes longer than any word it still remembers to hold.

### Who the Guide is

When you first see it, you’ll think it is a statue of some kind: a human-shaped figure seated on the edge of a grown platform of wood, where the Forest has been cued—by muscle, by root, by habit—to form a low bench of perfect smoothness.

The Guide doesn’t move as you approach the stone platform where it sits. Its head is tilted, listening. The faint groove of water that traced from the rim slides down the wall, drips through a carved slot in the rock, and disappears into a hollow tube of wood that extends toward the Forest floor. The Guide doesn’t watch the water. It doesn’t watch you.

It listens to the Swarm.

The sound of the Basin is everywhere: the deep roar of the sealike mind, the constant chord of their language. You cannot hear punctuation, only the shifting tide of agreement and inquiry and smooth, rolling consensus. The Guide listens to that as if to a conversation it is only faintly allowed to understand.

You see its hands are resting on its knees, not exactly loose, not exactly tense. They are not made of overlapping plates like the Swarm’s. Each finger has three clear joints, long since worn by friction and time and the constant friendship of wood. The palms of its hands—and you only see this when it finally stirs—are smooth, not calloused, but showing faint patterns, like the rings of a tree trunk.

Not from time. From water.

The Guide’s skin is ridged with fine lines close to the surface of the palms and the soft insides of the fingers, lines that do not follow the bone but instead circle the soft pads of muscle and fat. You have never seen hands like this before. They seem to belong to something else, but they are clearly attached to the same body.
