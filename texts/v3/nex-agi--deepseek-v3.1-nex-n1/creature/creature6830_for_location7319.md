The only thing that truly lives here is the witness.

Not me, exactly—I am only the vehicle it’s wearing at the moment—but the attention that notices, resists, drifts, and then snaps back into focus. If this world has a resident, it is that: a slow, humid awareness moving across the inside of a fired bowl too large to name.

It does not belong here. It was not fired in with the lizards.

It is soft where everything else is hard and glazed. It forgets and remembers; it gets tangled in its own thoughts. It walks in order to prove to itself that something can still change. From time to time it talks to itself in little phrases—painted baked, fired ground, glazed glaze—as if stitching patches over gaps in understanding.

It is alone but not lonely, because loneliness would require some memory of company to measure against. Here there is nothing to miss. No before-place hangs clearly enough in its mind to ache for. There are only half-remembered words—forest, stone, rain—that feel like descriptions of dreams someone else described.

This awareness breathes.

That is its most obvious disobedience.

Chest rising and falling in a rhythm nothing else shares. Lungs exchanging air with a dampness that never condenses into droplets on its skin but thickens on eyelashes like invisible weight. The breath is quiet but not silent; it rasps faintly against the stillness of the bowl.

If I stop walking, it stops changing anything at all except this: in and out, in and out, a tiny bellows keeping time under ribs.

Sometimes it thinks of itself as "I." Sometimes as "you." Sometimes as neither—just a pair of eyes on legs, gliding over ceramic that has no need to be seen. This slippage of pronouns doesn't trouble it much; there is no one here to correct or confirm any version of itself.

It carries no name it can speak aloud with confidence.

It does not know how long it has been here.

Time exists for it only as a string of identical moments held together by memory and anticipation. The world itself refuses time: no sun-track across sky, no seasons thinning or thickening leaves, no water level rising or falling by so much as a millimeter. Only the steady hum of equilibrium—temperature constant, humidity constant, light constant—and within that sameness the wandering line of something capable of surprise.

Surprise is its food.

The first lizards shocked it into focus; they were wrong in ways it could feel before it could explain. Their colors too loud for earth. Their stillness too perfect for life. Their arrangement too rhythmic for accident. Surprise woke up its thoughts and made them run along new tracks: this is made; this is designed; I am inside an artifact larger than any city I can recall.

That shock repeats in smaller forms as it moves: a hex-grid of water circles just thick enough to reflect an altered face; ceramic fish hanging mid-depth like specimens pinned behind glass; reeds all cut from the same dark glossy template and set upright at equal intervals; bridges connecting nothing to nothing with architectural seriousness.

Each surprise burns briefly in its mind like the little embedded lights under the glaze—faint but steady points around which its thoughts orbit for a while before drifting on.

It wonders if surprise is what keeps it from being baked into the pattern like everything else.

Sometimes it fears stillness more than emptiness. When it stands too long beside a basin watching reflections repeat themselves into flawless symmetry—fish echoing fish echoing lizard pattern echoing lizard pattern—it feels its edges beginning to soften, thoughts beginning to glide toward something trancelike and obedient.

At such moments something twitches inside: a refusal without words. It breaks the pose deliberately—scratch at an itch that may or may not exist; count cracks from one mound to another even though all segments are identical; trace meaningless shapes on its own thigh through clothing just to feel friction under fingertips again instead of endless smoothness everywhere else.
