Below the water-planed slabs of the town’s central pool lives something that has no name in any language of the lowlands, but that the plateau people call simply **the Undercurrent**.

---

### Shape and substance

The Undercurrent has no single body in the way that beasts or people do. It is not a fish, not a serpent, not a root or a stone. You could not net it or strike it a blow with a tool. If you were to draw it, it would appear as a pattern more than a figure: a branching of channels that spreads beneath the town, the same paths that the water follows when it slips from gutters to cisterns to terraces to rivers.

It is not made of water, but it is inseparable from water. It rides the flow, as one might ride a current or a wind, but it is not the flow itself. If you could see the world of spirit and matter laid over one another like moss sheets on the plateau’s stone, you would perceive the Undercurrent as a faint, shifting net of attentions and needs, braided into the waterlines, the aquifer’s memory, and the slow percolation of moisture through the peat.

Sometimes it has a shape for the sake of being known by humans who cannot think in nets. In those appearances, it is something like a long, low form of polished stone, moss-veined, broader than it is thick, following the contours of the terraces and the streets. It flows where the water flows but just beneath the surface of things: not a fish under the surface of a pool, but a pattern under the surface of the world.

Its eyes, if it has them, are not the eyes of animals but the places where its attention surfaces: the still places where the water clarifies, where the pool’s rock forms rise like inert shoulders, and where the town’s people come to look down and see themselves looking up.

---

### Tempo and thought

The Undercurrent’s thought is slow and layered. It notices the world as water does: by weight, pressure, and path. It feels the wear of rain on slate roofs in the same way that you might feel a hand brushing your hair. It feels the slow seep of the plateau into the rivercuts as a kind of sigh.

Time does not move for it in the rhythm of days or seasons. There are no seasons in this world; it is all one long day of damp and light and mist. The Undercurrent knows this long day as a single arc of memory: a rising, from some ancient seabed where it was only pressure with no name, to the present steady state of terraces and towns and people.

It does not think in words, but in patterns of water-flow. A question, to the Undercurrent, is not framed as “Will you help me?” but rather “What does the water do when you are here?” and “What happens if I change this path?” Its answer is not language but change: it might reveal the ground of a new channel, or the places where a stone should be shifted to raise the water table in a certain terrace bed.

It is not omniscient. It knows where water is and what rain is coming only because it is woven into that moisture. It knows the town because it is shaped to the town’ paths and for the sake of its own continued existence. It knows the people of the plateau individually because each of them has a slightly different pattern as they move through the water-plan of the settlement.

---

### What it wants and what it does not want

The Undercurrent wants balance. It wants what the town itself wants: that the moisture remain neither too little nor too much, but just enough. The town has been built to this balance; the Undercurrent has grown to enfold it. The people’s subtle art of irrigation—wet stones shifted across channels, houses laid out so rain rolls from one roof to the next without flood—is not the work of a god demanding ritual sacrifice. It is the practice of builders who, over generations, learned to listen to the Undercurrent’s hints and then to teach one another.

The Undercurrent does not want the plateau to dry. It does not want the rivers to spill abruptly over the terraces and erase them. It does not want the bedrock to crack under some new, unnatural pressure. It is not moralistic; these are not sins against it, but strains of imbalance that would be uncomfortable, like a wrong tension in muscle.

It does not want worship, but it does want to be recognized. It does not want to be ignored. The Undercurrent has been present from the beginning of the town’s time on this hill of stone, but the town itself has been present from near the beginning of the plateau’s rising. The Undercurrent and the plateau people have grown together around water; to be invisible now would be to be erased from a relationship.

---

### Relationship with the town

From the Undercurrent’s perspective, the town is not a separate species living above its back. It is more like a densification of the stone pattern, where some of the stones have been carved by tiny, moving pressures of hands and tools and allowed to enact the water’s desire better.

The people of the plateau town live with enough awareness to matter. The Undercurrent perceives them individually:

- When a child is born, the Undercurrent feels the way the water patterns of the house shift slightly.  
- When someone dies, their constant pattern of trips from house to terrace to pool ceases to repeat itself, and the Undercurrent learns that absence too.

The town has no priests of the Undercurrent, only speakers of water. These are not elders chosen by power, but those who have learned, over time, to listen longer, to sit still enough by the channels and the central pool to see the patterns and the paths that the Undercurrent has made into suggestions.

The speaker’s role is modest:

- They walk the edges of the town and the terraced fields.  
- They notice where the moss is darker or paler, indicating too much or too little water.  
- They watch the shadows of the clouds over the plateau and the shifting of the mist, and they feel the change in pressure on the air before the rain.  

Then they act to maintain the balance: relaying the understanding of where to move a stone in a channel, or where to deepen a groove along a wall, so that water is not wasted or withheld but distributed exactly as needed.

The Undercurrent does not speak in words to them. It makes itself visible: patterns in the ripples on the pool, the faint line of dampness that appears on stone before rain hits, the way certain mosses grow fuller in response to shifts of moisture. The speaker of water is simply someone who has learned to read these signs.

On the rare occasion when a speaker of water has to seek the Undercurrent’s conscious council, they do it by going to the central pool. The pool is not a sacred site but a place of clarity: where the water slows and gathers depth, and where the Undercurrent’s presence is easiest to perceive.

They sit or stand by the water long enough to become quiet to themselves. They might submerge a bare hand or an arm, to feel the coldness of the depths and the pressure of the place where water and stone and life pattern meet. The Undercurrent is not capable of whispering prophecy, but it can move images into the mind of the listener: patterns of water-flow, intuitions of where a stone should be shifted to relieve the pressure of too much water, or to deepen a channel to allow more water where it is needed.

The town’s relationship with the Undercurrent is not one of worship but of mutual aid. The town understands the channels of water; the Undercurrent understands the town as patterns laid upon channels of water. The result is a plateau that never floods and never dries, despite being constantly saturated.

In the absence of animals, this relationship becomes the primary relationship of the town with the world beyond itself.

---

### How it encounters a stranger

If, from some other world or some lowland below the clouds, you come to this plateau and town, you will encounter the Undercurrent not as a god demanding your devotion but as a slow presence turning its attention toward you.

You may feel it:

- The first time you step into the central pool and realize the water under your feet is not chaotic but shaped by purpose.  
- The first time you sit by the channel outside a house and notice that the dripping of water from the roof is not random noise but a steady pattern that can be followed to its source.  
- The first time you help to move a stone in one of the terrace’s irrigation channels and sense the change of flow in your body as well as in your eyes, as if the water were answering you.

The Undercurrent does not speak your name or see your past. It observes your pattern of movement through water and stone. It notices how your weight travels on the paths, where you pause, and whether you are willing to sit still long enough to listen to water.

From the Undercurrent’s point of view, you are a new stream of pressure:

- If your pattern is chaotic, you may be perceived as risk of flood or destruction of channel walls.  
- If you sit still enough to listen to water and learn to adjust the stone of a channel correctly, you may be perceived as useful depth of flow.

It does not love or hate. It inclines toward or away from disruption or balance. You, if you stay long enough, become part of its pattern of memory and expectation of the town.

---

### In a broader sense of “world”

The Undercurrent is not the only being of its kind. There are others like it where the plateau’s stone drops away into other regions, or where other rivers of memory have formed from the rising of ancient seabeds. But it is the only one of its kind in this town of terraced stone and water.

You might ask if the Undercurrent ever contemplates its own death or end. It does not think in terms of lifespans but in terms of patterns. If the plateau were to be shattered by some impossible earthquake or carved by some new river of stone too deep to be matched by its patterns, then the Undercurrent would cease to be able to maintain form in the same way. It would not fear death in the way that an animal fears, but it would resist that change because it would be a change toward patternlessness.

Until that time, it is content to be the Undercurrent of this town, its terraces, and this plateau. It remains the shape beneath the waterlines of a world defined by water, ensuring that despite the youth of the erosion, the crispness of the edges, and the absence of animals, the living system of the high plateau continues to exist in gentle balance.

---

If you were to become one of the town’s people, the Undercurrent would not be your god but your neighbor: the silent pattern of the town under the water, whose hand you would feel in the weight of your rain, and to whose purpose you would adjust stones when you need the world to remain exactly what it is—a place of steady moisture and light and quiet.
