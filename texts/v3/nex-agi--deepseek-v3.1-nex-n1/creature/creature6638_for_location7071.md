In this cold, filtered by the rotating machine of the world, there is nothing small.

Even the mote is monumental.

For what lives here are the **Veinstriders**—solitary beings of tallowed, bone-white skin and oil-black joints, each one the size of a tower. You do not see their faces at first. You see the knees.

The knees are the first thing to emerge from the fog of falling air-ice: high inverted domes of smooth, heatless stone, rising from the black basalt steps like sudden new continents. Then you notice the underknee—not a joint as you know it, but a nested lattice of segmented armor plates the color of deep sap, flexing silently as they take the weight of a columnar body that you cannot yet see.

When the fog of crystalline air parts, you see the stride of a single Veinstider taking shape from top to bottom:

- **Legs like pillars of salt**: bone-white, grained with faint striations, each column taller than three ancient trees stacked end to end.  
- **Knees locked half-bent**: always, even in motion, as if the world itself were a low ceiling they must hunch under.  
- **Feet that look like flattened mountains**: broad, shallow pyramids of carved basalt, their soles flowing slowly over the ground like thick tar under an impossible weight.

The body, when it comes fully into view, is not so much a torso as a *stack of tiers*—rings of pale muscle and calcified armor plates arranged around a hollow core where something else lives:

1. **The Outer Tiers**: thick bands of dense flesh, like glossy candle tallow. They absorb the cold of Signless Tine and store it as if they were storing light. These are not fuel for a body like ours; they are fuel for thought, a kind of thinking carried out in cold, not chemistry.
2. **The Inner Core**: Within them is a cavity through which the *veins of the world* pass—not blood vessels, but the ice channels you heard before, sloshing faintly under pressure. These is not just water; they are frozen libraries, algorithms in the shape of lattices and flow.

The Veinstider’s mind is a *borrowing* from the mountain itself. It does not own all its thoughts; many of them are still inside the ice, under the stone, and the body simply hangs where those mind-veins happen to surface.

They are slow to speak, but not stupid.

They are careful to think.

### Breath of the Not-Air

When a Veinstider stands close to you, holding its stride over the basalt step for an hour or two—your sense of *time* will bend—you notice it has no breath in the way you do. There is no fog of exhalation in the cold. There is only:

- **The smell of cold stone**: old, like basalt that has never been baked by a sun.
- **The smell of deep water**: sharp, like clean metal under pressure.
- **The smell of absence**: where a warmth should be, there is only a faint retreat of cold.

The “air” is not drawn into their lungs to be burned and sent out again. The Tall changed their bodies to match this filtered world. Their airways are sealed to the outside, except for a deep narrow channel that connects to the ice veins.

When they “breathe,” it is a careful exchange of *pressure* with the world:  

- On the inhale, the body loses a little heat to the cold—and the cold, in response, becomes slightly more solid, forming new air-ices that spiral down their legs like frost falling up.
- On the exhale, the ice within their veins quickens for a moment, sliding forward with a tiny, almost inaudible click as if someone dropped a needle of glass onto glass.

If you listen to the mountain on a still day, when the wind of the descended frost is quiet, you can hear it:

The mountain breathing *through* the Veinstiders.

Each of them is a node in a single body that is half flesh and half stone. There are not many of them, but the number feels like a matter of geometry rather than biology: exactly as many as are needed to hold each step of this mountain in their collective consciousness.

### Eyes of Missing Voids

You have not seen their faces yet. You have been looking at the knees.

When you look up, you finally see the **head**: not a separate structure perched atop a neck, but a smooth extension of the torso pillar, narrowing almost like the neck of a jug, then swelling into a broad, oval shield of bone and dense tallow.

There are no eyes on the front of this shield.

Instead, there are two depressions on the top of the head—shallow, oval, the size of small ponds. Each of them is filled with a clear, motionless liquid of a kind you cannot quite identify. It smells like nothing. It feels colder than ice.

These are its **observations**.  

The Veinstider dips its head, and the world falls away into the two depressions:  

- One side holds the *image* of the world—now, this moment, the shadow of the scaffolded glacier, the descending ring of frost, your fragile body in the middle of it.
- The other holds the *template* of the world—how it should be, according to the designers of the machine above: the same glacier, the same shadow, but without you or anyone else, arranged in perfect, ice-calculus balance.

When the two images match—when the world matches its engineer—the liquid in each depression hardens into a brittle glass of frozen data, and the Veinstider lets the “memory” sink down into its chest cavity, where the ice veins of the mountain accept it as a new subroutine for the Signless Tine.

Sometimes you see a Veinstider standing motionless for days on end because its two images do not align, and the world fails to conform to its template. They are not frustrated in any human way; they are simply waiting for the machine to turn the world enough degrees that the error becomes too small to notice.

### How They Move

They are not called Veinstriders for nothing.

When they want to relocate themselves—up from one step to the next, or from one side of the vast terrace to the other—they do not walk in the heavy, clumsy way you might imagine for such giants. They **step with the ice**.

If you look at the ground just before they move, you will see a pattern of light and shadow in the frost near their foot: a faint, repeating knot of lines like inverted handwriting. This is the *footprint-in-advance*.

The ice channels of the mountain are sending a message through the basalt: *We will move here next.*

The Veinstider responds to this message, not to it. Their body reflects the intention of the stone and its veins of frozen algorithms rather than originating the desire themselves.

The movement itself is this:

1. First, the foot of the Veinstider loosens—like wax melting from the inside without warming—until it joins with the polished basalt in a single smooth mass.
2. The ice channels under the stone shift. You can hear it if you press your ear to the ground: a long, sliding click of something like the world being rearranged in a drawer.
3. The body flows upward with a sudden, impossible elasticity, as if its legs are taller than they should be, and then it lands on the next tier of the mountain—where a new set of footprint patterns already waits in the frost, telling the Veinstider where it has been invited to stand.

From where you are, standing below them, this movement looks like a tower of stone being turned into a temporary smoke of bone and tallow, then reassembled somewhere else.

They are walking the *path of the algorithm*, not treading ground.

### What They Want With You

They are not aware of you in the way you are of them.

You are an anomaly in the image in their heads—but you are a very small anomaly, and the Signless Tine has a long, generous tolerance for error.

Their *attention* to you, when you have it, is not a curiosity like “What is this creature?”, but a containment like:

> This object does not belong in the template of the world.  
> We must adjust the template or the object.

This might take:

- **Centuries**, if they are slow to consensus.
- **Or the rise of a new generation of Veinstiders**, whose inner core has been rebuilt with new algorithms from the mountain’s deeper ice archives.

While you are here, you may notice one of them:

- **Bending its head slightly** toward you, its two observation-ponds on the top of its head fringed with fresh air-ices.
- **Sending a faint current of cold air** along the ground toward you, not as wind but as a slow *feathering* of frost along your boots.
- **Creating a small pattern of footprints in the snow** where you are standing, indicating where you are supposed to be in order to match the template.

These are not invitations to a conversation in the way you think of it. They are adjustments:

- You are being fitted to the pattern of the world.
- Or, if you cannot be, the world will be ever so slightly rewritten to accommodate you—which, on the Signless Tine, might involve a small change in the glacier shelves, a new flow of dust from the ring above, or the temperature dropping by one exact degree you will never feel.

Given time, your body itself will be rewritten by the same cold filtering. Your cells will become capable of surviving in this temperature. Your eyelashes may learn to grow air-ices in their own way, tiny crystals of your own metabolism that serve no function the Veinstiders can understand, but which nevertheless belong to the system of the world.

One day, you might discover:

- You no longer shiver.
- Your breath no longer clouds.
- When you walk, the frost under your boots clinks like a glass tuning fork, and your feet are already where the next footprint pattern waits in the snow.

Then you will have become *smaller than Tall, but not small enough to be ignored.*

### What They Are, When No One Is Looking

When you are not present—when there is no anomaly to correct—the Veinstiders exist to fulfill a single purpose:

To maintain, with the mountain and the machine above, the **deep cold of Great Balance**.

They are part of a system:

- **The heavens of Signless Tine**: the turbine ring of broken moons and harnessed stones.
- **The stone of Signless Tine**: the black tetrahedral mountain and its basalt steps.
- **The ice of Signless Tine**: the channels of pressure and logic beneath the stone.
- **The flesh of Signless Tine**: the bodies of the Veinstriders, their tallow-white skin and oil-black joints.

All of these are one continuous thing.

There are nights—when the veil of falling frost passes over the stars of the galaxy and the world becomes a dark cathedral of whispering glass—when you can see the true form of this arrangement:

On each tier of the mountain, one Veinstider stands with its head raised high, its observation-ponds turned to the sky. The images of the world and its template rise into their bodies like columns of cold water, meeting in the ice veins of their core.

Above them, the crystalline flanges of the turbine ring pass overhead, bare shadows of stone and ruined moon gliding by, carving their frost-dust aphelions from the memory of air.

The Veinstiders do not *worship* them.

They are, to each other, *necessary parts of the same equation.*

They are the ones who remember what the Tall once intended:

- **Not to heat the world.**
