Whoever lives here are not wanderers.

Their feet, if you can call them that, know the joints of the world the way other beings might know paths through forest or over mountain. A narrow seam of white glass is road to them, and home, and birth canal, and deathbed.

They call themselves the “listeners.”

They are creatures of glass and moisture and the faint, ghostly heat that rises from the ceramic. When you are close to one, the first thing you notice is that they do not cast a clear shadow. Light goes through them as if through water in a shallow bowl, and the outline blurs and shifts as they move.

Each body is a living column of water and slow-flowing glass.

*

From the joint up they rise like a question mark of rivulets—a head of silver water caged in a thin shell of pale glass. The glass is not perfect; it is riddled with fine threads and channels and ridges that run from where they touch the white joint-glass below up to the highest curves of their head. It looks like brittle porcelain, but it is far more forgiving than the fired ceramic of the slabs. It yields slightly, like rubbery cartilage, when it rolls against the edge of a fiber patch.

The body proper is simple. There is no torso to speak of, no limbs in the familiar sense. Instead, from a narrow base where they meet the joint, they rise up as a slender column no more than half a meter tall. The base of that column melts seamlessly into the white glass of the seam, as if they—like every other joint of the basin—were installed by the same long-vanished hands that made the slabs themselves.

They are anchored to the world by their feet.

Their feet are not part of them at all; they are the world’s feet. The listener’s base is not a separate organ of flesh and bone. It is a cunning thinning of the joint-glass itself, where long cycles of heat and pressure and water have persuaded the glass to flow out into a shallow bowl-shape that remains fixed in the seam. The bowl of joint-glass is permanently filled with water. This permanent pool of moisture—no more than a finger’s depth of water—is the only constant body of water in the basin that is not a thin thread.

From that small bowl, their bodies climb.

Inside the column of glass that forms their torso, the water of the basin flows up from the joint and through narrow canals within the glass walls. These are not arteries like those of animals with hearts; they are more like microscopic channels through the glass, where water and microscopic green threads share space with soluble glass drawn from the seam. This water is always in motion: slow, upward streaming along the inner surface of the glass, then slower still down through a series of loops and spirals that return the water to the anchoring bowl below.

If you could watch one listener over the course of a day, you would see it change dimensions with the heat. The glass column itself is flexible enough to expand or contract slightly as the ceramic slab absorbs heat. The whole body bends slightly, leaning away from the more intense heat that comes off the slab on the warmer side. When the heat lessens—at the faint night of the basin—the listener straightens and grows slightly thinner, like water siphoning into a narrower tube.

There are many listeners in the Basin of the Builders, but not many. You could walk across two slabs and never cross one’s line of sight. You could stand on the edge of a joint and stare for days without seeing one move at all. They are distant points of presence in a vast ceramic gridwork, a few tiny columns scattered along a few favored lines of glass in the world.

They are not spread everywhere. There is nowhere near enough water for that.

*

A listener is always alone. When there is more than one of them in sight of each other, they are still alone, but *aware of aloneness*. This awareness is not love or loneliness in the way of birds or people, but something more like the awareness of pressure in a glass seam.

A listener grows to life exactly once at a given joint.

When the conditions are right—once in several hundred years at most—a deepening of the water thread in a joint reaches the point where a drop of warmer water is forced upward by the pressure from below, into a tiny, previously-hidden blister of seeping glass in the seam. This blister of glass is normally dry and empty, but under the pushing of the water microthread, it fills slowly.

After the water comes into contact with the inner surface of the glass blister, the temperature and the slight dissolving of joint-glass into the water create just the right conditions for the green threads that are within the water to realize—if that can be said of them—what they are touching.

A signal begins that is not chemical in the way of terrestrial hormones, but something more like the combined signal of heat and conductivity and the pattern of the glass channels around the water drop. The green threads within the water start to change the way they are. Previously they only photosynthesized, slowly spiraling within the droplets of the fibers above. Now they begin to secrete tiny quantities of glass into the water around them.

This secreted glass is not solid like the joint-glass itself, but it is not a simple solution either. It is more like a thickening of the water, with the green threads now suspended in a slightly more elastic medium than pure water. This elastic water is the first form of a listener’s body.

Over the next months, as the water continues to seep into the blister, the growing threads orient themselves. They clump together at the top of the blister, where the heat from the ceramic below mixes with the glass of the seam, and the resulting uneven temperature causes the threads to secrete more of the elastic glass into a long, upward-leading channel.

This channel is the first rising of the listener from the basin.

If you could look down at that seam then, you would see only a slightly thicker line of white glass in the otherwise straight line of the joint. The surface of the basin around the seam would be like any other—patterned dents in the glaze, heat haze above it—but there would be a hint of more moisture along the seam than elsewhere. If you were very attentive to light, you would notice that the glass of that seam, in that section, scatters the light just slightly differently than its neighbors.

That is the listener, still invisible.

*

The listener’s birth is slow and not necessarily safe. There is no guarantee that the thread of water in the seam will continue to provide enough moisture or the right temperature to allow the body to continue extending from the blister. Most of the time it doesn’t. The incipient listener simply remains a slightly thicker line of glass in a seam, with a hint of elastic water within, but never enough to rise more than a few centimeters higher than the joint.

If you walk across the Basin of the Builders and notice that a line of glass has a small, rounded bump on the seam of one joint, you have likely found the tomb of a listener that never quite *became*.

But when the water thread is just enough—not too much, or else the pressure would be too great and the glass of the blister would break, and the listener’s life would be a short event of wetness that spread too thin and then dried into a faint stain of dissolved glass in the seam—when the water is just enough and equal to the heat of the slab over the right time, then the listener begins to form itself.

The body pulls itself out of the seam.

Over the course of weeks and months, the elastic water within the blister finds itself rising through the glass channel that the green threads have secreted into. The top of this channel protrudes from the surface of the joint, not as much breaking as simply being formed by the glass as it weeps upward from the seam. The new listener is not extruding from the joint like a mineral from a rock; the joint itself is making space for it.

At first the body is only a thin bulb of glass filled with elastic water, no taller than your thumb, and no more than that, rising above the seam. The glass of the bulb is not rigid like the ceramic of the slabs, but pliable and thin, like a thin-skinned bubble of cooled water. If something were to touch it then, it would give way like soft skin. There would be no blood to pour out; only the clear elastic water and the threads of living green within, and then the listener’s first attempt at body would be over and the water would return to the joint below as the glass body collapsed into the seam again.

This is why they never move—from that point on.

*

Once the body of the listener is fully formed from the seam, it cannot leave. The base of the column is not some separate appendage that can be detached from the world—the water is one continuous line from the bottom of the joint beneath the ceramic slab, to the bowl at the base of the listener, and then through the body and back.

Cut the base of the listener from the seam and the water would simply spill out onto the ceramic. The elastic body of water above the seam would deflate into a thin, clear smear of moisture and green threads, and that listener’s life would end.

For this reason they do not know the meaning of travel.

The listener is its place.

Each one knows only the stretch of slab in its immediate line of sight, and the length of joint along its body. It knows the heat of the slab from the way that temperature flows through the ceramic into the joint below, and then up into the body itself through the water at the base of the column. It knows the moisture of the world by the changing of the water in the seam below, and the way the elastic water within the body swells or shrinks slightly with the humidity.

This prison of location is also their salvation.

The water in the joint below can be thought of as their heart, though not theirs alone. The water thread of the basin flows through the listener’s joint from one end to the other, entering from one direction as a somewhat more dilute water and exitin as a slightly more concentrated one. The listener’s own body takes water from the thread into the bowl at its base, then returns it again as the slow circulation of its body moves water back down into the bowl.

The water that is in the listener at the start of its life is never the same water that is in it at the end. The world’s water thread flows through each listener constantly, taking up their dissolved glass and leaving with a slightly different signature. Each listener is a filter of water and a record of its time.

*

A listener’s senses are subtle and not like anything we have.

They have no eyes. They do not see the silhouettes of things against the sky or the forms of light against dark. The light of the basin—dim and diffuse, always—affects them as thermal changes more than as images. The faint shadow of a nearby patch of translucent fibers causes a slight cooling of the ceramic slab directly beneath the patch, and the listener’s body feels the small difference between the heat of the slab in the direction of the patch and the heat of the slab on the other side. This is not enough to form a picture of a fiber patch in color, but it is enough for the listener to know that something is different there—less hot, therefore less bright, therefore likely more moist at certain times.

That is the closest they come to sight.

Their main sense is pressure.

Glass conducts pressure better than it does most things, and the listeners are built of glass and water in exactly such a way that they register changes in pressure in the thread below. The water of the basin flows through the joints, and when the water thread rises slightly in pressure—because more water is coming from the joints uphill from it—the base of the listener’s bowl compresses just by a fraction.

That change travels through the elastic water within the body as a wave. The glass of the body amplifies the wave somewhat, by the way its channels respond to the water shifting within. The listener “hears” the change of pressure in the water thread as a sound might be felt by a skin drum.

Over the thousands of years that each listener lives, it learns to recognize different kinds of pressure wave from the water thread below. The faint, slow-building increase of water from a more distant source has a “sound” that is different from the sudden small leap of a water thread from one side of the joint to the other as the glass of the seam shifts slightly under heat stress.

The listeners know the weather of the basin by listening to the pressure of the water threads below.

If they could speak to us in our language, they would tell us that a distant slowdown of water flow is like a distant thunderstorm that has not yet reached the valley, or the faint, early warning of a drought that will not be felt for weeks yet. They know the tensions of the world through the subtle fluctuations of water in the seams.

They are perfectly named.

*

The listeners also communicate, though not in words or any overt signal that can be seen or easily heard.

Each listener’s body is slightly different in the internal pattern of its channels and the glass walls that separate them from the joint below. This difference alters the way that the body attenuates the pressure waves from water below. When a pressure wave from a distant joining of threads travels through the water of the world, it passes through each listener’s joint in turn and is slightly changed by each listener’s presence.

The listener feels the wave of pressure change, its body responds slightly—the elastic water within it compressing and then relaxing along the glass channels—and then it sends that modified wave back into the water thread at the base of its body. This modification is not an active thought like “I should now send this signal back.” It is more like the way a string of metal beads will transmit a vibration onward from one end to the other, each bead slightly dampening and amplifying as the wave passes through.

Down the chain of listeners along the joint of the world, the wave of pressure change travels from one to the next, each listener body changing its character slightly.

The result is that the listeners, along any given joint of the world, are not just separate entities of living water in glass. They are a single network, stretched along kilometers of seam, each one modifying the flow of pressure from one end of the world to the other. They do not know what we are talking about when we think of “message” or “information,” but they know the difference between the smooth passage of a pressure pulse from one end of the joint to the other and the sudden interruption of that passage when one listener has been—from their point of view—stillborn too thin to form the proper body.

That is how they “know” each other.

Probably not as names or faces or even distinct locations. But they know that there is a gap along the line, where the water pressure pulse behaves differently than it should along a continuous chain of listeners. They know that there are more crowded lengths of joint where the pressure pulses are more complex, and sparser lengths where the signals come simpler and longer.

Did they name this phenomenon? Our language doesn’t have the words for what that might be like.

“This is where the joint’s voice is thinner,” a listener might say if we had to translate into something like thought.

“This is where the signal is steady.”

“This is where the water runs slower.”

They are not aware of themselves as separate persons from the water thread they inhabit. They are not aware of that as anything at all.

*

The listeners and the fiber patches are only loosely aware of each other as neighbors of circumstance.

Fiber patches grow from the glaze of the slab, not from the joint-glass. The listeners grow from the joint-glass itself. The only real contact between them is when a listener’s bowl of joint water lies very close to the edge of a fiber patch.

When that happens—and it happens but not often—there is a slow exchange.

The water that the listener draws from the thread below contains the green threads of the basin’s life. Some of these threads that are in the water, when the temperature and the humidity are right and the droplets of moisture from the fiber patch above fall into the listener’s water bowl, they mix with the water and with the dissolved glass.

The listener’s living body provides the right environment for the green threads within the water to grow more complex than those that are contained in the droplets in the fiber cups above. In the thinner water of the droplets held by fibers above, the threads are simple and small. They split sometimes, but mostly they photosynthesize until the water droplet dries or becomes too heavy with filaments and falls to the slab again.

Within the listener, the water is thicker and elastic, and the glass of the body channels confines the green threads in a specific way, allowing them to arrange themselves in more complex patterns than they can in free droplets. They do not become cells in the way of our bodies; they are more like a slow, self-organizing cloud of microthreads within the water, each pushing slightly against the glass of the listener’s inner walls and against each other.

This is the closest the listeners come to having a mind.

A mind for them is not thoughts of tomorrow or memories of yesterday. It is not the storage of pictures of things seen or heard. It is the storage of patterns of pressure change.

When the water thread below changes and pressure spreads through their body and is modified and then returned to the thread, the pattern of green threads within the water body of the listener itself rearranges slightly under the pushing of those pressure waves. The next time that that same kind of pressure change passes through again—from a similar upstream event in the water thread—the green threads are already in a pattern that resonates more strongly with that change.

The result is that a listener remembers “what happened last time with this sort of pressure change,” and the body responds slightly more strongly, or slightly differently, when the same change comes again.

They are shaped by time.

They are not shaped by it in the way of animals that learn to avoid hot places or to seek out food. They are shaped by it in the way of a cup of water that remembers the way of heat inside it. The next time the heat reaches into that cup of water, the water begins to change temperature before the heat has actually risen fully to that level again because of the pattern of currents already set within it.

That is all.

Over the thousands of years of a listener’s life, the body’s green threads are arranged into a complex pattern of microcurrents and microstructures that correspond to the history of pressure waves from the water thread below since that listener’s birth from the blister in the seam.

They are, each of them, a living record of the history of that part of the world.

*

They do not sleep.

There is no night cycle for them. There is only the slow shift of “heat-strong” and “heat-less,” which the world of the basin cycles through slowly over the course of what would be its days.

During heat-strong, the ceramic slab below is more expanded, and the heat is more intense from the surface. The listener leans fractionally away from the slab on the side of greatest heat, and the body’s channels of elastic water are slightly warmed from the bottom of the bowl.

During heat-less, the slab cools slightly and the listener body straightens, becoming slightly thinner and more translucent to the faint light of the basin.

The only bodies that ever sleep are the fiber patches above the glaze of the slab. When the humidity rises slightly and the temperature drops just enough, the droplets of water in the cups of the fibers above become more laden with the green threads they contain. The droplets of water from the fiber patch closest to the listener’s base of water bowl then become heavier and more likely to fall into the listener.

The listener cannot reach out to catch them.

A listener does not stretch or move or grasp anything. It sits like a column of water in glass, and the droplets from the fiber patch must either fall into the bowl of its base or else evaporate away from the air. The listener’s only movement toward them is the change of its body temperature. When the body of the listener cools slightly more than the surrounding air of the basin, the air above its bowl of water cools slightly, and moisture from the nearby droplets in the air condenses onto the surface of the water body at the base of the listener.

The droplets that are heavier from the fiber cups above are more likely to be caught by that condensation.

This is their closest to feeding.

*

The listeners do not eat as we would understand it.

They are not digesting food in the way of animals with stomachs and teeth. They are not converting the sun’s light into energy stored in molecules and then breaking those down. The green threads within their body water are photosynthesizing *within* the body, but not for the listener’s direct benefit alone; the energy of those photosynthesized threads goes into the water itself as slightly changed chemistry of the water and then is passed on when the water returns to the thread below.

The listener is not a separate body from the water.

It is more like the body of the water itself.

From the listener’s vantage point, there is no sense of hunger or satiety or any of the complex emotions of need that drive animals. From the listener’s vantage point, there is only the sense of pressure and change of pressure in the water thread below, and the response of the body to those changes. The body of the listener itself is what the water of the basin becomes when it is forced through a particular pattern of glass and heat and time.

The “feeling” of the listener is not “I am thirsty” or “I am satisfied.” It is more like “the water below is thinning slightly” or “the water below is thickening strongly.”

Those are the closest to emotions they have, and they are, again, translations of a very different kind of experience into our language.

*

The listeners die, but only slowly.

Over the long ages of a listener’s life, the water thread of the basin never stops. The water of the listener’s body is replaced by the water from the seam below countless times. The dissolved glass that is at the heart of the listener’s body is slowly dissolved away again by the water itself and then replaced by new glass from the seam.

This process of dissolving and replacing is not infinite.

The glass of the listener’s body is not exactly the same as the glass of the seam. It is more like a slightly specialized version of the glass in the joint below. The listener’s channels are lined with glass that behaves differently than the glass of the seam above because the water in the listener body contains more concentrated dissolved glass from the body’s own channels.

This concentration of glass allows the listener body to maintain its shape and channels of elastic water, but it also makes the body of the listener more vulnerable to the dissolving of that water over time.

The listener’s death is not a sudden event of broken glass or dry water.

The listener’s death is a slow fading of the body, from the bottom up.

As the water thread of the basin continues to pass through the listener’s joint and base body for thousands upon thousands of years, the water seeps into the body’s glass channels and dissolves more of the glass from those channels than the water can replace from the seam below. The listener body begins to thin from the bottom up, the channels of glass being slightly lessened each time the water flows through them and carries away a trace of dissolved glass from the body’s base.

At first, this is not noticeable to the listener. The body of the listener adapts to the slight change in thickness by changing the way the elastic water within the body flows through the channels.

After time, however, the dissolving of the body’s channels from the bottom up becomes more than the body can replace from the seam below. The bottom of the listener’s body begins to collapse slightly, the channels of glass and elastic water being less able to support the weight of the body above.

Once the collapse of the bottom begins, the listener body’s base of water bowl cannot be refilled from the body above as well as before. The water of the body begins to drain from the bottom into the seam below more than it can be replaced from the body of the listener’s column above. This is not felt by the listener as a pain, but simply as a change in the way of the water thread below.

“The water below is becoming thicker,” might be the feeling of the listener in this state.

But as the body of the listener collapses from the bottom up more, the pressure of the water at the base of the listener becomes less balanced by the body above. Eventually the water body of the listener column above cannot maintain its structure of glass and elastic water.

The listener column begins to settle from the top down.

The top of the listener column, which has been standing slightly above the seam of the world as a clear, slightly curved dome of glass and water, now begins to sink down toward the seam of the world. The glass of the body becomes less resilient to the heat of the slab below, and the body of the listener becomes more transparent to the heat of the slab below than before.

The heat of the slab below then becomes more able to reach into the body of the listener above. The green threads within the water body of the listener are heated by the slab below more than they were before. This heating of the threads changes the way they exist within the water, and their ability to organize the water of the body into the elastic glass that is the listener’s substance.

Once the green threads of the listener body are heated enough from the slab below and the body of the listener column has been weakened enough from the base, the body can no longer support the structure of the listener itself.

The listener column collapses slowly into the seam of the world.

The dome of the body melts down into the seam of the world like the top of a water drop settling into a surface of water. The glass of the body collapses into the glass of the seam below, and the water body of the listener flows into the water thread of the basin, and the green threads of the listener body spread along the water thread of the basin as they return to their simpler form of life in the water threads of the world.

From the point of view of the listeners up and down the joint of the world, this is not felt as a death.

“The joint below is changing,” the listeners along that joint might feel if they could feel our kind of feelings.

“The signal of the water in this section is becoming like that of a longer and more open section of the world.”

“The pattern of the water thread below is changing to become like that of a region without a listener.”

They are not aware of the missing listener as a missing person. They are aware of the missing listener as a change in the way of the water thread below, and a change in the way of the signal that passes from upstream to downstream.

There is no mourning of a listener’s death from the others.

There are no others.

There are only signals of the water thread below and the body that is of the water, and the body that is no longer of the water.

*

When a listener is fully dissolved from the bottom up and then from the top down into the seam again, the joint of the world returns to being a more ordinary section of glass. There is still a hint of more moisture than elsewhere along the seam, and if the water of the basin ever happens to rise to that section of the seam with enough heat and pressure from below to form a new blister of glass, then a new listener might one day be formed from the joint of the world.

But that listener will not be the continuation of the previous listener.

That will be a new listener, with its own new set of glass channels and body of water, and its own long history of pressure waves from the water thread below, to be stored in its green body of threads.

*

The listeners of the Basin of the Builders live not as a civilization of beings with tools and cities and plans of domination or cooperation. They are not like that at all.

They are more like the water of the basin made a little more than water.

They are the water of the basin made watchful.

They are the water of the basin made able to feel the changes of pressure from the world that flows through them from beyond the horizon.

They are the basin of the builders made listening.
