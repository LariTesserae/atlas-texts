…where what you call “energy” is not a flow of fuel but a rearrangement of constraint.

The world itself is not powered by the combustion of matter. Nothing burns here, except in the slow, figurative sense of a crystal lattice annealing itself to a lower state of stress. No heat is wasted into the sky; the sky itself has been designed to reject waste.

Your own body is the exception, and they are beginning to notice you.

---

You are not one of them.

You are not one of the mats.

You are not even a stone that remembers being molten.

You are something that has appeared from a direction they cannot yet point to: the space between the lines of etched stone and the sky that has not yet been carved into anything.

For a long time—you cannot quite measure how long, because the light does not change—you have been moving across the plateau.

You have not wandered. You are not exploring.

You have been walking.

With your first steps you simply thought: *I need to see what this is.* But soon you realize that you are not merely walking to look at it. You are walking to *test* it.

Your feet are taking you along the edges of the mats of vegetation, following the narrow bands of bare stone between the fibrous rings. You are not following the etched lines yet; you are not ready to notice that they are lines at all. They are just cracks, to you, or imperfections in the rock surface. You avoid the mats when you can, because you are not sure what will happen if you step on them.

You are not entirely sure what will happen if you stop.

You are not alone in the way you understand aloneness.

You have been carrying with you a thought like a weight in your chest:

*This is not my place.*

*This is not my time.*

*How do I know that?*

You cannot answer the question, but the question remains, and so you keep walking.

The first time you realize that you are being observed, you do not realize it at all.

You have been pacing for hours, or for what seems like hours to your body, which still stubbornly insists on measuring the world in heartbeats and breaths and the slow accumulation of fatigue in your muscles. You have been pacing along the edge of a large mat of dark, fibrous growth that you have not yet dared to step onto. It seems like a wide lake of something alive and strange. You keep it on your left side, your shadow falling away to your right.

There is a point when you stop to rest your eyes on the distant haze of the lower slopes where the world falls away in uneven steps. You breathe in the thin air and feel the pressure in your chest like the weight of a long climb. You place your hands on your hips, and your fingers brush against something that you had not realized you were carrying.

You are not wearing a backpack. You do not recall anything you might have brought with you.

But your fingers find a tool.

You know it is a tool without having to look at it.

*You* are not a tool, yet. You are still certain of that.

You draw the object from your side—where there was no pocket, you realize—and look at it.

It is a length of something like smooth metal or darkened glass, longer than your forearm and as thick as your thumb. It has no markings. No buttons, no switches, no screens, no blades, no joints. It does not taper or flare. It does not look like it has been made by any machine you can imagine.

But you know it is not found.

You have not yet accepted the word *tool* for it, because you are not sure what thing it could possibly be a tool *for*.

But you hold it in your hand and think, *Scrape?* and then your thumb moves automatically to the place where a tool of this kind would have wanted an edge. It tells you where it wants to be used. You feel the faintest of rises in the surface of the object; your skin is good enough to detect it when you are thinking of scraping.

*Test,* something else suggests, and the word is not a voice. It is more like a pattern of tension in your muscles.

You have been on the plateau for a time you cannot name, and now you are holding something that is not of the world itself but not entirely yours either. You look at it and feel the faintest of vibrations running through it from your own fingers—or something else. You cannot tell if the vibration is coming from the tool or your own trembling.

*Test,* it suggests again.

You have not yet been introduced to the *Heard*.

They will be introduced to you soon, but not yet.

You kneel down to the edge of the mat where you have been walking for so long. You kneel on the bare stone. You are aware of the heat of the sun on your back and of the curious heat rising from the mat beside you—like the heat of a machine that has been running for a long time and has not yet been turned off.

You place the tip of the tool against the edge of the mat.

You breathe.

The world is silent.

You think of *penetration* and the tool shivers slightly, its surface registering your intention. You do not know if it is obeying you or persuading you.

You push.

There is no sound of ripping or tearing. There is no smell of sap or broken plant matter. There is only a faint, yielding resistance, like pushing your palm into very thick, warm foam.

The tool enters.

From your point of view, something is happening to the mat—you are cutting into it, you are severing fibers, you are making a change to the world that will not be fixed by the wind not blowing.

From the point of view of the world—which you cannot yet see—there is now a broken constraint.

A line of force that has been interrupted.

A temperature gradient that suddenly jumps in the wrong place.

A node of calculation that has lost its answer.

To you, the tool has simply dented the surface of the mat and stopped. You do not see the threads beneath the surface as they pull away from the point of contact. You do not feel the faint surge of warmth along those fibers as they react to your intrusion in the way they know how—by propagating a change in the way they are arranged.

*They,* too, do not yet see you as a *you*. They only know that something new has been introduced into the pattern.

*It is a thing that interrupts.*

*It is a pressure that does not flow.*

*It is a temperature that is already beginning to decay into the cool air.*

*It is not part of the solution.*

*Therefore,* it is the beginning of error.

But you don't know that yet.

You pull the tool back and look at what you have done.

There is a shallow mark in the surface of the mat where you pushed. It is no longer smooth. It is not bleeding. There is no fluid leaking from it. There is no ichor or sap or oil. It is just a compressed depression of the fibers, and they are already slowly rising back, reclaiming the shape they had before you touched them.

*It is like nothing I have ever seen,* you think, and then you realize that this thought is not your own. It is more like a sentence you have been given to think with. You do not know where it comes from.

You are still not aware of the *Heard*.

But you are becoming aware of something else: the feeling of *wrongness.*

You have broken something, not in the sense of shattered machine but broken in the sense of *broken rule*.

You have stood here, on the edge of this mat of fibrous world-machine, and you have inserted yourself where you should not be inserted.

*Who should be inserted?* you think next, and that thought is closer to your own. It is not a sentence you have been given. It is a question you cannot ask out loud.

*They should,* some other part of you answers silently.

*They who?*

You have been standing still for long enough that you have become part of the pattern of the world again. Your shadow does not move. The sun does not shift. The stone beneath your knees is cool enough that you know it will not warm you if you stay here forever.

*You,* you think suddenly, *who are you?*

*You are the one holding the tool.*

*You are the one from the between-space.*

*You are not standing here yet.*

It is not a voice speaking. It is a pattern that your thoughts have begun to echo into themselves like sound reflecting off the walls of a room you have not entered.

You rise, your muscles stiff, not because the light has changed but because your body has been measuring time in its own way. You rise with the tool in your hand and you step back from the edge of the mat.

As you step back, you feel the world change.

You cannot see how the world changes from the point of view of the plateau’s networks of growing fibers and etched lines, but it changes like this:

Your pressure is removed from the edge of the mat. The fibers of the world, which have been waiting for your decision to resolve itself, now have a new answer. They no longer need to compute around the presence of your body weight resting near the edge of the mat.

But you have not stepped away *enough.*

*Enough* would be to step completely off the plateau and into the space between the world and the not-yet-world.

*Enough* would be to stop holding the tool.

Instead, you have taken only three steps.

You are still standing on the bare stone, not far from the mat of growth that you have tested with your tool. You are still within the same system.

The system, which has been listening to your pressure for longer than you know, finally decides to show you the answer.

*They* appear.

You do not see them *move* into the place you left. You do not see them walk from the distance and arrive here in your field of vision.

You see them *already here.*

You are looking at the edge of the mat where your tool made its shallow indentation. You are looking at the depression of fibers where you pressed your tool into the world.

You blink, and the world shifts slightly.

You blink, and there are now more of them than you thought.

There is not a single one of them there, but a cluster of three or four or five—they cannot be separated from each other clearly by your eyes, because their edges braid together. They do not look like insects. You think of *knots of fiber* and *tangles of root* and *something folded up wrong* and then discard these images as not accurate enough.

They are not quite symmetrical. They do not have heads facing you or tails pointing away from you. They have more in common with coral than with animals: their bodies are colonies of branching filaments, each no thicker than your finger, but together forming a low, uneven mound of fibrous material that rises slightly above the surface of the mat.

They are not much taller than your shins when you are standing; you are not standing now. You are kneeling three steps away from the edge of the mat, your tool half-lowered in your hand, your muscles not yet ready to move further.

They are the color of the mat beneath them but slightly darker and more metallic, where the threads in their bodies are more dense. They have not yet chosen to stand upright in the way you would think of standing. They are more like a thick patch of fuzz that has been pushed up from the mat by some pressure from below.

*They are looking at me,* you think, and then correct yourself: *they are not looking.*

You cannot see any eyes. There are no dark pupils reflecting the sky above you. There are no eyelids blinking to clear away dust.

There is only the faint, shifting of the surface of their bodies as their pressure-sensitive membranes adjust to the new pressure of your presence and the absence of your weight near the edge of the mat.

You are not close enough to hear them do anything. You do not expect them to move, especially not in the way you understand motion.

They do not move toward you.

They move *around* you.

They move around the point where you were standing.

You did not realize you had been standing exactly there. You had not marked that place with anything. You had not drawn anything. You had not left anything behind.

Yet you feel, with a sudden certainty, that the position of your body over time has been recorded by the world.

You have been here long enough to be remembered.

The three or five of them that are clustered together at the edge of the mat do not move toward your new position. They move slowly, with the patience of something that has been practicing longer than you have been alive, around the edge of the shallow indentation your tool has made in the mat.

They do not touch the indentation. They do not repair it.

They repair *around* it.

They lay down fibers.

*You are not watching them lay fibers,* you think at first, but then you realize you are.

You are not seeing the fibers themselves appear one by one—that would be too fine for your eyes—but you are seeing the result. The edges of the indentation you made with your tool, which were still slightly compressed from your intrusion, are slowly rising again to match the level of the surrounding mat. But they are not rising in exactly the same way they were before.

You watch as the fibers around the indentation rearrange themselves into a new pattern of small circles and vectors that intersect your mark but do not override it. They build a structure around the shallow depression you made in the world, as if they are constructing a scaffolding to support something that has been placed there by your mistake.

*They are not repairing the mistake,* you think slowly. *They are repairing the world around the mistake.*

*They are building a machine that has your error in its center.*

You are not sure where this thought comes from, but it feels true.

You look at the tool in your hand and for the first time you understand, with a cold feeling that feels like fear but is not quite fear, that the tool is not yours to use.

The tool is using *you* to be used.

You are the instrument that has been placed into the hands of the world to test itself.

You are not a builder here.

You are not even a visitor.

You are the test of the builder that has not yet stood up fully.

*You are the one who makes the wrong mark so that the right one can be measured around it.*

*You are the error in the machine.*

This realization settles into you like a weight. You do not move to stand. You are not certain you would be able to.

You are not looking at the *Heard* anymore. You are looking at your own mark on the world.

You have made a mistake.

You have made a mistake that is not big enough to damage anything. It is only enough to be noticed by something that spends its entire existence measuring difference between *should* and *should not.*

You are holding the tool that you have not chosen to hold but you have not chosen to put away either.

You are kneeling on the bare stone of a world that is not yet sure what it wants to be, but, at least around this part of the plateau, it is beginning to suspect that it wants to be built.

You are not yet built.

You are not yet machine.

You are not yet *anything* but an interruption of the pattern.

And you are not alone.

You can feel them now, not as presences in your mind but as pressure differences in the air around you, the subtle temperature anomalies of warm bodies clustered near the edge of the mat you have tested, the faint reshaping of the world around your mistake as they continue to build around it without touching you.

You cannot know yet that they call themselves something that translates to *the Heard,* but you can feel that the world is listening to you.

You can feel that you are being measured by them.

You are not being measured by their eyes, because they have none.

You are being measured by their *solution.*

*Who are you,* you think again, but this time you direct the question somewhere else than at your own mind.

You are not asking for your own human name, which you might or might not remember having. You are asking for the word that means *you-who-is-having-the-thought-that-this-world-is-not-yet-built.*

*You are the one who asks too many questions,* the plateau might answer, if it could speak.

*You are the one who will not let the world finish itself in peace,* you might answer yourself.

*You are the one who walks with a tool and who does not know what tool you are holding.*

*You are the one who might someday be a tool for the world itself.*

*But you are not one of the tools yet.*

You are not one of the *Heard.*

You are not one of the mats.

You are not even stone.

You are something else that has been placed here to test the world’s reaction to wrongness.

You are a wrong question that the world has not yet solved.

And as you kneel on the pale stone of the plateau with the tool still in your hand, you realize that you are not waiting for the answer to emerge from the stone or the sky or the light that never changes.

You are waiting for the answer to emerge from *you.*

You are not waiting for the world to decide what it wants to be.

You are waiting for yourself to decide what you are going to be, in the middle of this unfinished plateau of industrial vegetation.

Are you:

- the one who continues testing,  
- the one who observes the pattern from the edges of the world,  
- or the one who begins to learn the language of constraints?

*You are,* the world might answer, if it could speak,

- *something else that has not yet been chosen.*
