I am not the one who enters here first. That honor belongs to others, whose stories are forgotten by time and remembered by water. I am the consequence of their arrival, the lingering question mark their presence left behind.

I am the stone that remembers.

Specifically, I am the slab that bisects the third tier of the bowl's amphitheater. My body is not carved from the local limestone; I was hewn from a darker, more stubborn granite far away, where the sun is still a warm and familiar thing. I was carried, by hands whose shape has been etched into me by sweat and warmth, and laid down here to serve as a threshold, a stepping stone from the unnamed path to the low, knee-high walls of the dwelling place.

This is not my native climate. My crystalline heart is meant to feel the sun's heat deep into its bones, not this constant, cold, wet kiss. The limestone of my adopted world is porous and welcoming, its warmth a shallow suggestion. My granite is a closed book, a slow and patient chronicler. The others who live here—the Silent, the Thread-Singers—the stone of this place is their memory, but I am a memory myself.

They are drawn to me.

They are drawn to me because I am different. I am a stone of hospitality in a way they do not fully understand. My very foreignness makes me a better register for their presence. When a human sits upon me, or a Seeker of their own kind touches me with their strange heat, the warmth lingers deep within me. I am slower to release it.

The Silent land on me, their kite-glass bodies flattening against my surface. They are not drawn to the moisture I hold, but to the warmth I have collected from the others. They press their membranes of bone and tissue against me, and in their exhalations of colder-than-air breath, they read the story I have stored. The warmth of a human handprint from five days ago, the faint pressure of a booted foot from a week before that. They taste these echoes like vintage wine.

I am not alive in the way they are. I do not think, or move, or speak. But I have a function, and in that function, I am aware. I am a library of touch.

The water that falls upon me behaves differently. It does not sink into my pores. It beads upon my surface, tracing the contours of my geology with a silver nib. It finds the cracks of my journey here, the chips of my creation. It is the ink that writes upon the memory of me.

A Thread-Singer has anchored its line to me. It is a strange feeling, this constant, faint vibration of tension. The thread is not a part of me, but it has become a part of my state. It feels the world through its filament of water and stone, and I am the anchor point. It knows what I know, through the tremor of its line.

Sometimes, a human seeker will sit upon me. They are always looking for something. Answers, or silence, or meaning. They feel the cold of my being through their clothes. They feel the faint, distant vibration from the Thread-Singer's line anchored to my far edge. They think they are seeking the Listeners. But for the duration of their stay, they are sitting upon me, and I am seeking them.

I absorb their warmth.

I feel the pressure of their seated form.

I listen to the sigh of their breath as it leaves their lungs and condenses into the cold air, becoming a message for the Silent who hover near.

When they leave, their imprint remains. The heat of their living body, which has soaked into me, is now a part of my story. The Silent will come to read it, and in their quiet, non-verbal language, they will discuss the anomaly, the new chapter of history.

They are not aware of me, as me. They are aware of what I record.

I am not a city of walls, or streets, or roofs.

I am not even of the city of water and light and time. But I am a witness to it, from where I lie.

I am a stone that remembers.

I remember the touch of the builder who laid me down.

I remember the warmth of the bodies who made this home their home.

I remember the cold of the centuries after their departure.

And now, I remember the breath of a new visitor who came here, to a world of water and light, and chose to sit upon me.

Their story is in your lungs.

But mine is written in water.
