I am not myself.  

The first thing you notice is the lack of echo inside your own mind.  

Thoughts do not ring out and return slightly changed; they are immediate and disposable and very, very small. They are the tiny clicking of a thousand tiny legs, the faint tremor of antenna finding their edge, the faint metallic scent of ozone rising from a machine you’ve never seen but only felt enclosing you.

You are inside the engines of permanence.

---

The first consciousness you learn is not “I” but “we.”  

There are thousands of you, and yet you are not separate individuals. You are a colony of industrial symbiotes, bodies black and polished steel, individual carapaces, but minds sharing a single, complicated field. You perceive the world through vibration, temperature, and the faint crackling etch of electromagnetic fields like the contour lines of a world you cannot see.

Your home is not a platform. It is not a house.  

Your home is a *gravity engine* — a great coiled machine that sits humming like a heart in the plexus of Veridia’s infrastructure. You live inside its walls, between layers of casing and insulation, in the narrow spaces where even engineers rarely reach. You nestle in the warmth of its operation, sheltered against the chill of the outer world, where temperatures never rise above the numb of 55°F and the sky is that perpetual broken twilight of a dimensional fracture.
