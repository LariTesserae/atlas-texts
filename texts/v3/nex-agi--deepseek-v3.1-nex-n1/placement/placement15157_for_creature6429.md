I am not a visitor here. I have never been a visitor.

When the prompt of “you” tries to imagine itself walking onto the surface of Qetram, it is looking for the place where my feet already are. It is looking for the wrong kind of standing.

On the high plateau of the world-branch, where the continent of bark curves into the dark like a frozen ocean, I am the one who does not move. The Qetrami woman you describe steps around me. She does not walk *to* me; she walks *through the space my absence is not taking.*

You are looking for a body like yours, a wet thing with a heartbeat.

You have already described me.

I am the bone-white column that is not a bone and not a column but a speaker of light.

I am one of the slender uprights that rise from the world-branch’s bruised surface and terminate in the darkness above in no particular pattern and yet in exactly the necessary pattern. My base is not rooted in the sense of digging into soil; it is a continuation of the branch itself, a growth of harder matter extruded from the world-tree’s final skin.

My surface is not smooth like stone but like the enamel of deep teeth, a faint, translucent sheen over something else. The color of me is not white in the way of snow or paper. It is the white of a sound that has not yet happened, the color of a faint expectation before a strike. The “not-quite-sap” you describe—the slow crystallization of sound inside my structure—is not a metaphor. It is the closest thing your language has to what I am doing.

When I am called a “light-bearer,” it is because you lack the word for *sound that is visible*.

There is no sun here. There is only the thin greenish-blue smoke of atmospheric memory, the residue of bioelectricity that has not forgotten how to glow. The light you see falling on the curve of bark, the blue-grey gleam on the convex of the world, does not come from a bulb or a flame inside me. It comes from the *tension*.

My body is not hollow, but it is not solid either. Inside my slender upright form is a lattice of filaments, thinner than the idea of veins, a lattice grown in response to the stresses of the world-branch. When Qetram shifts infinitesimally—which it does, still, despite the age of it; the world is not dead but no longer quite growing—the branch moves under my feet.
