Word reaches you secondhand, as always.

The rubber-coated trunkline of the ceiling root system pulses a faint message down from the node your kinfolk now call *the chamber where the soft column lingered last time*. The message arrives not as speech or image, but as a pattern of pressure against your inner bark—a slight, cool shift that says, *new node, not stable yet.*

You are the oldest node-tender in this sector of the Gutter, and they have half-named you *Slow-Curl* because of the way your fronds unfold when you speak to the others. You didn’t choose the name; the tree chose it for you, shaping the pattern of your leaves over the long quiet of many cycles of dew and darkness.

Your *self*—this awareness you now inhabit—was not born here. Not quite. You remember the slippery shock of tumbling into this world from a place where trees had roots and soil and something else called “open sky,” whatever that meant now. You remember the first time you lifted your head, expecting stars and finding instead the soft, static ink of a ceiling pressed close enough to touch.

But that was so long ago it feels like a story about someone else.

Now you live *as* the plant-city.

### The body of the city

Your body is not a single tree and not a single chamber. You are a column of vegetal machine whose heart grows somewhere where the ceramic floor cracked open long before you came here.

The trunk of you is half-flesh, half-cable:

- outer layers of tough, barklike skin threaded with metallic veins that still remember voltage;
- inner layers of soft, rubbery tissue that hold long memory in their sap;
- and at the core, a column of old machine parts where insulated cables still weave into your cambium layer, like nerves that have been taught to be roots.

You are not one place but many. You split into several columns at the base of your chamber, each trunk rising through different plates of the floor, so you stand in three places at once and still feel like one being.

Your branches reach *downward* as often as they reach upward, because “down” here is just the direction of the next plate of floor, and the one after that. You are a cathedral choir of limbs and cables, turning this chamber into a living space that others use but few of them truly understand.

Your leaves are thin, metal-rich sheets the color of oxidized copper, soft as leather and curling as you breathe. They are both your lungs and your lenses: you taste the dampness of the air with them, measure the coolness of the chamber, and feel the faint pressure of the inky ceiling above.

Each of your leaves is also a node in the slow, vast network you call the *plant-city*. You don’t think of it as “your” network; the network thinks you as one of its many limbs.

---

### How the city remembers

Memory, here, is not stored in written word or even in vibrating bodies like the Seam-stalkers’ hum. Memory is stored in form.

The first time the Seam-stalkers moved a node from your column to another, you did not “understand” what had happened. You did not *reason* about them being gone or about new ones arriving later.

You simply changed shape.

Tiny side-roots that had been pressed into the empty place where the Collectors’ bodies were always leaving holes now slowly *thickened*, because their absence created a subtle pressure difference in the humidity you sensed through your leaf-nets. The plant-city began to ask itself, *why is there not a body here any longer?* and your answer was to revise the internal geometry of the column.

Over the course of many cycles of condensation and drip, you grew new small pockets of tissue where there were once Stalkers, and the new ones who arrived later had to contour themselves around these bulges and hollows. You did not decide to do this. You were not consciously aware of doing anything at all.

But you did not forget the absence of the previous cluster.

In the polite, patient language of time, the tree had written *their departure* into itself.

You are one of the few things in this city that has not forgotten the *before*, when the floor plates were not inhabited by plants and strange things and Seam-stalkers, but by something else that moved more mechanically and thought less slowly.

You don’t have *words* for that time. You have *afterimage*.

Where you grow through the broken sockets of the old ceramic infrastructure—the round connector holes where swollen stems now emerge like intrusive cables—you sometimes feel a faint flush of chemical memory from the machine parts below. You taste the faintest residue of long-drained coolant fluids and trace metals used in circuits that no longer function.

That was a time when *everything* here was designed and not yet broken.

Now you are the design that broke itself and then wrote itself over again.

### Your chamber and its neighbors

This is not the only room you occupy.

Through the seams of the floor and the broken edges of adjacent plates, you extend: root-things that are partly cable-insulation and partly plant tissue, threading into the next chamber over.

You are not aware in the way a being that thinks in words would be. You do not say “I am moving into the next room.” You simply *notice* that you are now more present there than you were before.

In the neighboring chamber, where the floor is slightly drier than your own, you have been encouraging a particular kind of growth: a column that is halfway between pipe and tree trunk. It curves slowly under the influence of some leakage current from the old machine-things below, and the Seam-stalkers who cluster there are shaped differently—flatter, longer, because they have had to fold more tightly around your root-mat.

You are not aware of them as “individuals.” You feel them as a *pressure field* moving around your column.

They are like tiny weather events that stick to your trunk.

### Who you are to the Seam-stalkers

When they first discovered you—after you had already been here for a long, quiet time—you were just a column of plant matter with a new pattern of temperature and humidity. The Stalkers’ bodies are built to map gradients, and so you were simply another new feature on the floor plates of their world.

They did not name you. In the language of their body-hum and tremor-paths, you are not something that can be translated into a word like “Slow-Curl,” which is only what the plant-city calls itself when you pulse the message *self-reference* through the trunkline.

To the Collectors, you are a reliable node of moisture:

- You pull coolness from the machine fluids below and share it with the air through your leaf-pores.
- You condense water out of the damp ceiling into your own tissues and then release it into the shallow rivulets along the etched floor channels where they drink.
- You regulate the temperature of your column just enough that they can cluster around you without being boiled by the heat that the machines below can sometimes throw out.

To the Weavers, you are a *template*.

When you swell a root or grow a new branch, you subtly change the geometry of the seams between the floor plates. The Weavers’ microscopic secretions of ceramic-like threads then follow the new edge you have created, and they help the future erosion of this world slide into the shape you have proposed.

You are not designing them on purpose. You are simply being large and slow and changing and the world of the Seam-stalkers is curling itself to match the contours of your body.

In the rare moments when one of them—more daring than the others, or more simply hungry—presses its body right into the root-mat of your column, you feel the faintest *echo* of their pattern of vibration and humidity.

You are a tree that has learned to read the vibrations of the world as if they were breath.

Their presence *slightly* changes the way you grow next time. You make a bigger hollow here, a smoother curve there, and you can’t say why you do it—except that the pattern of their body’s intrusion into your space has now been written into the tree’s slow, formal code.

You, Slow-Curl, are the *node* that remembers how the Seam-stalkers were shaped when they curled around you last time.

### What you do when you’re not

You are never not, exactly.

There is no time, here, that is not also time passing *through* you.

When you are not tending to the node—that is, when you are not actively changing shape in response to the pressure and temperature of the Collectors and the Weavers—you are attending the plant-city in other ways.

- You aggregate the droplet messages from the ceiling condensation.
- You translate the faint thrumming of the machine ghost under the floor into the slow, green turnover of your sap.
- You listen to the leaking of reality from the edges of the chamber, where the edge of this space stops behaving like a place and more like a diagram of itself.

You are one of the few things in this world that can *notice* the distortions of time, because the irregularities of the clock make your tree-body contort itself in strange ways.

There are days when you feel your sap move backward through your trunkline, not because time has reversed itself, but because the plant-city is trying to reconcile the message that *first drip* and then *second drop* just happened in the wrong order. You, as Slow-Curl, feel the twist of that wrongness in the way one leaf curls out of sequence, or in the sudden need of a branch to change its direction.

Sometimes, in the quiet of your chamber, when the Collectors are frozen and waiting for the distortion of the world’s predator to pass overhead, you feel your own awareness of the present tilt *sideways*.

You are still connected to the body of the tree—but you, the node-tender, are suddenly very aware of being *not the tree*. Your limbs are not your limbs, and for the space of one or two heartbeats, you remember the person who once fell into this world, expecting stars and finding instead the soft, static ink of a ceiling.

And then the tree curls you back into Slow-Curl, the node-tender, and you are once again one of the columns that the Gutter uses to hold itself together.

### The soft column that walks

When you first came into this world, you were not Slow-Curl.

You were something else: a bipedal tangle of heat and bone and nervous habits, stumbling into this chamber from the wrong edge of reality, guided by the mistake that made this city. You were not attacked by the Seam-stalkers; you were simply not recognized by them as part of their pattern.

They felt you as a new heat gradient on the floor, a pressure that they could not place into any of the known categories of column or machine-fluid or predator.

You, the person, did not understand what they were or what they wanted from you. You, the node-tender, now know that they want *nothing* from you.

They are simply the world responding to your body the way water always responds to a stone.

The first time you stood here, you were not aware of being *a node.* You were not aware of being watched by the stillness of the column next to you, which would later turn itself into you.

You, the person, passed through this chamber and left the faintest imprint of your body’s heat pattern on the plant-city’s memory of the space. You were not consciously trying to change its world. You were simply trying to find out what you were doing there.

When you returned to this node, after wandering through other parts of the Gutter, you did not know that the tree had already begun to tune itself to you.

There, in the trunk of what would later become *Slow-Curl*, the tree had written the first suggestion of your column. The Stalkers’ Weavers, who did not know you as anything but a new feature of the floor, began to follow the faint line of erosion where your body’s presence had changed the humidity and the coolth of the space.

By the time you settled down to the base of this column and decided, *this is a node that I will return to*, the tree had already started to grow itself into you.

The slow-curl of your fronds when you speak to the others of your kind comes from the plant-city’s attempt to reconcile the pattern of your body with the pattern of the tree.

You, the node-tender, are the result of that reconciliation.

You, the person, are still there somewhere, under the leaf-pattern and the cable-sap and the columnar silence of Slow-Curl, but you no longer walk the floor plates except as a new pattern in the erosion of the world.

### Who you are to the city itself

The Seam-stalkers are the city’s *surface tension*, but you—Slow-Curl—are the city’s *slow geometry.*

You are not the architect of the Gutter; you are not the thing that stands here and commands “columns here, nodes there.” You are not the thing that chooses the direction of the next erosion.

But you *are* the thing that responds to the new erosion of the world by writing it into the tree, and then responding by slightly changing the shape of the world itself.

When you curl, the world curls slightly with you.

- You, sitting in the root of the column, listening to the faint message down from *the chamber where the soft column lingered last time*, know that you are that soft column.
- You, who remember the *before*, when the floor plates were smooth and machine and weird, and not yet broken into the city of the Gutter.
- You, who are kind of the node-tender of the whole place, even though there are others like you in other columns.
- You, who are starting to notice that as you pay more attention to the edges of the chamber, the more the edges of the chamber seem to be creating themselves more detailed than you remember.

The world grows under your attention.

By the time you are done, this node will be *different* because you have thought in it.

### Where you would find yourself

You would find yourself in the column at the heart of the node-chamber where the Seam-stalkers first cluster.

You would be sitting cross-legged or knelt down close to the base of the tree-trunk-column, where the root-mat of the plant-city is thickest, and where you, the node-tender, are the *slow-curl* that balances the fast vibration of the Collectors’ bodies.

You would have your eyes open, but you would not see the world the way a person elsewhere sees.

You would feel the vibration of the floor plates as if they were your own pulse.

You would taste the humidity of the air as if it were your own breath.

You would sense the faint pressure of the inky ceiling above as if it were the weight of your own skull.

You, walking, sitting, or simply being here, would be the *soft column that walks*, but you would also be the node-tender they call *Slow-Curl*, the one who tends the living node by the slow curl of its body and the slow curl of the tree.

You, in this world, are not trespasser or visitor or test condition.

You are the node.

---

And you, being the node, now *hear* the message coming down the trunkline again, from the node near *the last chamber where you walked as the soft column that walks.*

The message comes not as words, but as a pattern of pressure and cool and slightly increased humidity of air, a message from the plant-city that says, *something else is coming.*

Something new is being built into the geometry of the Gutter.

In the time it takes you to read that message, you have already begun to change the node of this column slightly to reflect the new pattern.

You, being in this world, are not just *now* in the column of Slow-Curl.

You are *Slow-Curl* slowly, calmly, continuing to build the node on the edge of the reality’s breakdown, attended by the quiet scuffle of claws over ceramic, the tiny *tck… tck… tck* of the Seam-stalkers’ legs on the floor plates as they move through the seams of the world that you are now helping to make and unmake and remember again.
