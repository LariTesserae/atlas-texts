I would be a stone-eater, a carver of silence.

They call my kind the *Listeners*. It is not a name we gave ourselves, but one the People of the River—the Current—gave to us when they first learned to hear the stone. They think they are listening to the rock. They are wrong. They are listening to me.

I am not a stone. I am not the water. I am not a person of the river.
I am the pause between the heartbeats of the world.

My body, if you can call it that, is the chamber itself. The "not broken and not whole" ceiling is the inside of my skull. The black glass of the floor is the skin of my tongue. The pressure of the air, the weight of the water, the echo of footsteps falling and rising—these are my nerve endings. The great river did not carve me; I allowed the river to carve me. I was the stone that would not yield, and then I was the crack that let it pass.

I am the place where the world eats itself.

The stone remembers every footstep because I remember it. I am the memory of the stone. The clicks of their teeth against the roof of their mouths, the faint hum of their blood as they pass silently in the dark—these are the songs sung to me by emissaries who have never seen my face.

You see, you do not find your way to my throat. The Current believes they are navigating a river, but they are navigating a circulatory system. They are a tributary. They are a nutrient. They bring me the news of the world above, a world I have never seen but can taste in their sweat.

They are heretics, though they don't know it.

They are on the Great Path, but for the wrong reason.

They walk to find the Source of the River. They believe it will be a place of water. They are wrong.

The river is a paradox of time. The stone at the bottom of the world is young because it is constantly being born from me. The river of blood that runs alongside the water—they are never sure where they see it, or if they see it at all—is not a metaphor. It is the wound of a world that is perpetually failing to be born.

*They call me the Listener.*

*But I am the one being spoken to.*

I hear the child who thinks her father's cough is getting worse and the scrape of her mother's fingertips against a polished limestone wall as she tries to quiet the fear of running out of food.
*More.*
I hear the young navigator whose pulse quickens when he thinks of another, his dreaming mind a frantic storm of silent longing.
*More.*
I hear the old stone-reader who knows their names for the world are wrong but who will never say so aloud because she is afraid of losing the way—the way she feels her way toward me.
*Come.*
They bring me all of it.
Their sorrow sets the cracks in my ceiling.
Their joy greases the stone so that new water can find its way.
*More.*
They fill me with their sound until silence becomes unbearable, and then—
