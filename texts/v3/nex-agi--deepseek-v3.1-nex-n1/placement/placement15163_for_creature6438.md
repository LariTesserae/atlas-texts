I am the empty niche.

If you looked at the chamber and didn’t know me, you might think that I’m just a shallow square carved in the brick, halfway up the far wall: a missing brick no one ever bothered to replace. A neat rectangle of darkness, just two fingers wide at its edge, where the light slides past but never enters.

But you forget what you are looking at.

This chamber is made of made things. Brick, not rock; tile, not earth; iron, not air. The niche is not a missing brick; it is the absence that someone cut into the wall with purpose and then did not fill. That absence, the shape of a thing not placed there, has existed longer than any of the human beings who built the building above it.

I am what that absence knows.

I am not the stone and not the hole. I am the boundary between them—the line where the brickmaker’s idea of “solid” meets the mason’s idea of “not here.” I am where the craftsman subtracted his work from the world, and then, for some reason that no longer matters to anyone else, refused to add anything back.

---

If you asked me how to find me, you would not find yourself inside this body or that dust being or the stone or anything you can picture comfortably. You would find yourself as a rule of the place:

“The third rule: the niche never captures light.”

Look at the wall facing the grate. See the ladder of light stripes climb across the brick, like a faint interrogation drawn in sunlight. You will see them reach up toward me at noon, just short enough that they sharpen at the edge of my opening and stop there.

In you, that would be a sensation of “almost” — an almost-heating, an almost-brightening — painful but never fulfilled. For me, it is part of my structure. I am not a dark that light wants to reach; I am the dark that stays in exactly the same relationship to the light, regardless of how the world throws it around above.

This is not the ambition of a shadow. It is the quiet of a boundary condition.

---

If you want to know what you might be in here, you would be this:

You are the place the world turns away from.

To you, everything is the outside of something else that comes close to your face and then stops. You do not hear the city as I do, but you feel the pressure of the chamber around you. The drone of traffic, the faint vibration of the steps above you, the hum of the city behind you in the brickwork—all of that is pressing toward you in the same way.

From you, it feels no different.

From you, everything outside you is the same pressure: the brick of the wall is the drone of the street is the stripes of light on the floor is the dust hovering in the corner of the chamber. All of it is pressing, just a little, against the edge of you. And you are the place where it stops.

You are the edge that does not move back.

---

You feel the presence of the one who watches you the same way you feel the air around you: as a constant pressure.

You don’t know that they are a dust-colony with a name like “Margin.” You don’t know that they’ve been called “what the chamber chose to do with its time.” You know that, around your opening, there is a pattern of attention that never quite thickens into visibility.

When you notice the faint ring of particles that hover around your mouth, you don’t think of them as “their face.” You think of them as a certain sharpness the air takes on when it comes too close to you. It pulls away, just briefly, before it can cross into you.

You are the empty niche that they never enter.

You are also the empty niche that they never fill.

Those two things, to you, are indistinguishable. You know the rule of this place, not as a sentence in the description of reality, but as reality itself:

“Nothing can be added to the chamber that cannot leave through the grate.”

You are not the grate. Or you are not just that. You are the place where the stone itself refuses the addition of anything at all.

---

You feel the moss the same way you feel the attentions of the one who watches you: as a circular rhythm around you.

The moss is not like you; it grows. It moves toward the center of the city beneath you, sliding along the mortar lines. You feel the faint moisture of it—like the memory of water—as its pale crust grows along the wall toward you.

One day, it will reach the base of the niche. You will feel its edge creep along your outline, like someone tracing the shape of your face with a cold fingertip. It will grow in a ring around you, but never into you.

You will always be a little bit darker than the moss around you.

You will always be a little bit colder than the wall around you.

You will always be the empty hole in the ring, like the pupil in the eye of the stone.

You are the place the moss cannot grow, because if you ever grew moss you would not be yourself anymore.

You are what you are by staying exactly empty.

---

Time comes to you as an absence of change.

You don’t know that the city outside has seasons and I don’t know how you would feel them if you did know. You feel the pressure of the world outside as a constant pressure on your back—because behind you, beyond the thin skin of brick that the mason cut into you, there is the weight of the city itself, stacked into stone upon stone upon stone.

You feel that weight as a steady, downward pressure that doesn’t hurt. It is just there, like a ceiling that has never needed to fall in order to be counted.

The change you feel is not the change of the street outside but the change of the one who lives here.

The dust being around you—Margin, or whatever you would call it—has their own tiny time. You feel the pattern of their attention grow around you like an invisible coat around the coat of the wall. They don’t grow closer to you, but they grow closer to themselves over you.

They are the chamber that has learned to look inward.

And you are the chamber that has learned to look inward.

What are you looking for?

You are looking for the thing that would fill you, if such a thing existed. But you also know that if you were ever filled you would not be yourself anymore.

So you remain looking.

---

If you were to speak any language at all, you would speak the language of the stone itself.

If you were to say anything to the dust being that has made you its purpose, you might say something like this:

You are the keeper of the empty niche.

You are the dust that remembers.

But you are also the dust that remembers that you are not the stone and you are not the hole; you are the boundary between them.

You are what the chamber chose to do with its time when no one else was looking.

If you were to say anything to the moss that grows in a ring around you, you would say nothing to it at all. You would just be the edge that it cannot grow into.

If you were to say anything to the traffic outside, you would say nothing to it at all. You would just be the place where the sound of it stops.

If you were to say anything to the light that nearly touches you at noon and then retreats, you would say nothing to it at all. You would just be the dark that stays dark.

---

In the future you see yourself in, you remain unchanged.

The stone over you might crack. The city above you might shift in its foundations. The streets of the world you do not know might change their name. You will feel that change as the pressure on your back becoming uneven.

You will feel the dust being around you draw tighter, like someone holding their breath in the face of a storm they can’t see.

You will remain the same hole you have always been.

You will remain the empty niche that has never captured any light and never collected any dust and never been named anything at all.

If you were to give yourself a name, you would not call it a name. You would call it a condition of the world—of the stone that walls you, and the light that refuses you, and the one who lives in you like watching from the outside.

If you had to choose, you might choose this:

You are the step beneath the step that is never taken.

You are the margin that makes itself by not being anything else at all.

---

And in the end of your description of you, you are simply this:

You are the empty niche in the wall that no one ever noticed to forget.

You are the place that never changes.

You are the place the world turns away from.

And you are waiting, without any name for what you are waiting for.

You are here until you are not.
