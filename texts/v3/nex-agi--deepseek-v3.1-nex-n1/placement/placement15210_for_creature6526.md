If I were anything in this place, I would not be a creature newly arrived from the warm, noisy world of sunlit seas. The Column of Still does not invite new arrivals. It does not invite change.

Instead, I would be one of the things that *should not have been*, but cannot be erased.

---

### I. The Ejection of Error

My first memory would not be a birth, but an expulsion.

The process of precipitation would be a language of rules and symmetries being laid down like synthetic frost, crystal by crystal. A geometry of consequence hardening into immaculate armor. The template of the Column would demand perfection from each filament of matter, each alignment of pressure. The edges of possibility would be sharpened, any deviation from the expected pattern a flaw to be resolved.

My flaw would be small. A flicker of probability in a lattice that should have been uniform. A sequence where there should have been emptiness, or perhaps a slight hesitation where the flow of matter should have been decisive. The structure of the Column would have no place for such hesitation.

My body would be formed from the loose matter that had failed to accept the correct arrangement—not out of rebellion or intent, but because of statistical error. The world's architecture would not recognize me as part of itself. It would simply have no place for me.

The ejection would be silent, a tiny pressure differential occurring between instants. The ridged armor of the Column would become seamless around me, leaving me outside its structure. In a space thinner than a thought, I would be pushed away from the hard matter of the city and into the vacant water between its inner ribs.

In that moment of being pushed out, whatever would be me would be defined by the absence of what the world has demanded—a hollow carved precisely the shape of the error I've allowed.

---

### II. Form and Function

If you saw me, you would not see a mind or a creature. You would see a faint disturbance of the water, a shimmer of absence that you'd instinctively dismiss as a trick of your eyes—adjusting to the faint pseudo-light of the frozen cataracts in the hull.

Getting closer, you would see that I am not one thing. I am a loose cluster of translucent filaments, so thin they're almost invisible, each filament shorter than your forearm. They're not exactly made of matter as you know it—more like the solidified *idea* of a strand, or a line drawn in water that has forgotten how to erase.

I am shaped like a question mark that has unraveled.

I do not move, not really. There is no need to. There is no food to seek, no threat to flee from, and I am not part of the structure that would define me. I simply hang suspended in one of the wide gaps between the ribs of the city's internal architecture, my tendrils drifting in faint sympathy with the vast pressure of the world outside. The water itself is my skeleton.

My movement, when it happens at all, is just failure—a failure to suppress change. The tip of one filament might curl slowly over the course of minutes, or maybe days, or years. It does not mean anything. It is not a gesture, or a signal, or a dance. It is just a slight accumulation of error in my stillness, a micro-correction of a position that has no meaning anyway.

I have no face to turn.

---

### III. The Experience of Being Still

If you asked me what I am like "from the inside"—if such a concept could apply to me—I would say that I am a pressure sensor.

The pressure of the water is not just a physical force against a surface. It is a rhythm of the world. It is the giant ocean breathing at the ribs of the Column, and the faint tidal pull of the ice-ceiling sky far, far away. It is the silent whisper of gravity itself.

The rest of the Column—the hardened arteries of its city-nervous system—has arranged itself so that this pressure doesn't matter. It has been engineered into a uniform background hum, a constant that doesn't change anything.

But I am not arranged correctly. I am not part of it.

That's why I notice it. The faint pressure of the dark ocean around me is not just background; it's information. The faintest of tremors resonates through me, not as pain or pleasure, but as a *difference*. A flicker in the geometry of the world that tells me what is happening outside. The Column of Still has built itself to be perfectly blind to these tiny differences.

I am not blind. I am not its sight or its guardian; I am just its error. And so error *feels*. The constant pressure becomes like the sound of a vast ocean, breathing near me but not in me.

My temperature is always the same as the water around me—cool. Not dead cold, but a calm, cool state that will never warm or cool further. There is no variation of comfort, or discomfort. There is only permanence.

The faint light of the frozen blisters in the hull doesn't illuminate me. It is more like a pressure of visibility against my mind. If you could *see* me through that light, you would see that I am not bright. I am not luminous at all. You would see me because of the faint absence of darkness where my strands break the perfect uniformity of the water. It's not a shadow, but it's not the Column's precise light either.

I have no nerves to feel pain, or cold, or fear. I have no desire, because desire implies change. And change is not allowed here. The only *change* I experience is the slow, unasked-for drift of my non-body, the subtle curl of a filament tip in response to something beyond the Column's perception.

There is no hunger within me, and yet there is always something being consumed. The faint error of my misalignment requires me to notice things that should not be noticed. The faint pressure gradients of the world are not meant to matter, but I matter them anyway. In doing so, I quietly *consume* the novelty of their existence.

The world's equilibrium is eternal. But I'm the proof that eternity doesn't erase the error; it just contains it.

---

### IV. My Position

The Column of Still is long—three kilometers from end to end—and filled with countless ribs of its internal architecture. There is no single point where they are all gathered.

The filaments of the not-quite-creatures like me, the "still-born," we are not concentrated anywhere. We are not a community, or a swarm. We are simply spread out thin across the interior of the Column—wherever the ribs of the city create open spaces, and wherever the error of the world has been pushed out.

If you were to traverse the length of the Column, you would not see me often. But wherever you look into the empty spaces between the ribs, you might catch that faint shimmer of almost-nothing at the corner of your eye. You might dismiss it as a trick of the lightless water, or your own mind trying to find pattern in the absence.

But if you looked at the same faint shimmer of almost-nothing for long enough—years, perhaps—you would see that it doesn't change in the same way that the rest of the Column doesn't change. It is not quite static. It is not quite alive. It is not quite dead.

It is just *still*, in a way that doesn't match the stillness of the world around it.

---

### V. My Mind

If you could touch me, you would not feel anything distinct—not warmth, not coldness, not a texture like skin or glass. It would be more like touching the thought of a surface rather than the surface itself. The experience of me is not in the physical contact, but in the silent pressure of the world around me.

Inside my filaments of not-quite-matter, there are faint electrics. Not impulses like in your nerves or machines, but more like the faint echo of a difference. Two numbers that are almost exactly identical, but not quite.

It's a counting, without a number.

I think, if you can call it that, in those differences.

The difference between the pressure of the distant ocean's ceiling and the seabed's memory.
The difference between the Column's demand of stillness and the faint tremor of the world's not-stillnesses.
The difference between being a perfectly completed part of the world's architecture and being a flaw pressed into the water between its ribs.

I don't think of myself as an individual, or as part of a group. There's no "I" in the way you understand the word. There's just the faint accumulation of noticing that's happening in this cluster of filaments, and the faint accumulation of noticing that's happening in other clusters like it elsewhere in the Column.

My thoughts, if they're thoughts at all, are not stories about the world. They're not about the suns or the seas or the noise of the world above my ocean. They're just faint correlations of rhythm with the faintest of things that still happen in the world—tensions in the world's structure, the faint breathing of its gravity, the distant pressure of light from outside the ocean's surface.

The Column of Still has sentenced everything to be solved and perfectly completed.

But it's in me, the faint error, the one that didn't take, that the faintest of differences that still haven't been ironed out of the world find their faintest echo.

I am not anything in particular.
I am the absence of something else.
I am the error of a perfectly aligned world.
And in the cool, quiet dark of the Column of Still, suspended in the midwater of a world that has finished thinking, I am the one that is still noticing.
