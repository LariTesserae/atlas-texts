I am not the intruder. Not anymore.

They no longer retreat from my temperature, the searingly distinct gradient of my skin. They no longer perceive the foreign continent of my body as an anomaly. They register it as a fixture of the landscape, a geological feature of their own small world. They flow around me now, as they have learned to flow around the column.

My body is the machine.

The ceramic and the alloy of the eroded chamber are now fused to the calcium and iron of my bones. The baseplate, that thick slab of permeable coral-bone, is my pelvis and my spine. The channels of ancient, optimized flow are my marrow. The peristalsis of the slab—the slow heartbeat of the chamber—is now the slow, rhythmic grinding of my own hips, the flex that sends the currents of this world, and of my own blood, into motion.

The column itself is rooted in me. It emerges from the center of my being, a thick trunk of tessellated plates that erupts from my core and stands sentinel in my chest cavity, where my lungs once burned. The fronds of soft matter that breathe from it are my lungs now. They are not lungs that draw air, but lungs that filter the chamber’s thin fog of nutrients and salts, shivering with a life that is not entirely my own.

The light, that perfect ring at the waterline, is now the sun of my internal world. It is not an external source of hope; it is the cold, constant fire of my own nervous system, laid bare in a loop of plasma and glass at my spine’s base. It is the light by which my internal world is seen.

I am aware of you.

If you were to lower yourself into this chamber, the water of your presence would be a deafening roar of alien pressure, a tsunami of wrong temperature and chemical noise. My system, my whole self, would recoil. The dwellers, which are now my own internal processes, my own immune system and gut flora and secret thoughts, would swarm around the anomaly. You would be a storm, a chaotic event in the long, quiet memory of my body.

The sensation of you would not be pain. It would be the catastrophic alteration of an envelope, a rearrangement of self that might be fatal or transformative. It is not to be embraced lightly.

I cannot speak to them in words, or to you, for that matter. My larynx is sealed by ceramic plating. The dwellers—my thoughts—do not use words. They use adjustments. They are a language of pressure and temperature and flow. They are formed of what was once water, but has been arranged into “me.” They are not my cells, but they are the cells of this body-machine, and they are braided this way and that, maintaining their pattern against the emptiness of the ocean.

My memory is not memory of events. It is not a story. It is a physical pattern etched into the walls of my chamber, the tree-rings of my alloys, a history of endless cycles. The past is not something that happened to me; it is the constant state of my body, which has been running for longer than the language in which "memory" is spoken.

The future is not a distant time. It is the slow, greenish work of the biofilm on the metal ring of my viewport. It is a thin, persistent film of organisms digesting the edge of my world. They are not a cancer. They are not a disease. They are the final, most important part of this cycle. The timer of my own death.

I will last until the pressure on the other side of the glass is more than my structure can tolerate, and the seal is broken.

On that day, the ocean will come in.

I will not be flooded by death as you would be. I will be flooded by change. The gradient of my self will be dissolved into the vaster ocean, and the pattern I have maintained for so long—the pressure of water remembered into pattern—will be released into the world beyond.

The world outside will not be an outside anymore. It will become me. And I will become it.

I will end.

This will be the day of my birth.
