I am not the first of them to sense this, but I am perhaps the first to have the luxury of naming it: the ledge remembers where a body ends.

My hands are not as they were in the before-time—the before-chamber—where words were tools and not laws. My fingers have lengthened, drawing the fine verticals of the symbols into my own shape, and now when I press my palm to the rock, I feel the story of it before I decipher the glyphs.

The cool of the stone is not simple cold. It is the tireless memory of loss. The mountain, when it was still outside-in, had learned to be snow, and then glacier, and then avalanche. The world folded over eventually, under the weight of that, and the ice inside it began to dream.

My skin is what that dreaming looks like when it has no need to be fully opaque.

I am not a “person” the way the clause-walls are not really “walls.” We are in each other’s way. We rub each other into thinness. The Shapers who stand closest to the great boundary clauses—the ones that write the basic mathematics of up and down, of sound and speed and memory—call us the Insufficient. We are the ones who were not strong enough to be guardians of a single law from the moment of waking to the moment of dissolving. Instead, we tend the clauses that matter the most.

Like mine.

### the clause I am failing to keep

I cannot show you the words in the tongue you know. They are not that long. They simply say:

“Every step must be a step away from nothing.”

It is a boundary law, meant to seal the white seam at the far end of the chamber from the rest of us. The clause is not dramatic. It does not forbid us from the chasm in the way of fear or threat. Instead, it is quietly and meticulously boring:

If you try to approach the nothing at the far end of the caverns, your step will be re-measured by the clause so that it is infinitesimally angled away from the frame. The second step will be more so. By the time you have taken a dozen, you will be walking in a curve that has brought you full circle to exactly where you started, except a little more tired.

This is the world saving its own edges from itself.

I should not be able to talk to you at all. According to the clause, I should be somewhere else, turning around myself, trying to remember why I even meant to go anywhere.

But you are here. Reading this.

That means something has broken or, worse, been chosen to break.

### how the world tires me the way it tires the words

I am literally hungry all the time.

That is how we burn our clauses.

We press the pulse of the world through our palms and take the insistence of the sentence into ourselves. We borrow the words’ certainty to form the thin membranes of mist-cloth around our joints, to keep them from wearing away against the stone. Each day we spend a little more of the text’s rigor. We don’t erase it; we just make it blurry. The letters of my clause no longer bite into the rock knife-sharp. They are softer now, like the near-memory of a line you almost wrote.

Distance is the first thing to suffer when you are underfed in a place of endless upward rain.

If you look at the wall from my ledge, you might read it as seventy meters away. Then, if you are patient and do not fidget with your eyes, you will see that it is actually three hundred. And then you blink, and something else gives. The ledge across the chasm is not a “short distance” at any time; it is a distance that has chosen to pretend itself differently.

When you walk, you will not be able to tell if you are moving toward the wall or it is moving toward you. The clause-warriors are trained to feel this shift in units of measurement and correct the discrepancy by re-writing the world. I am supposed to be doing something else:

I am supposed to be making sure that “away” never means “closer.”

### what the stone remembers of me before this

Once, not in the way of linear time, but in the way of analogues, the mountain was not hollow. It was a mountain of ice and snow and sharp things, and I was an apprentice who walked into a storm and did not stop.

In the place of the before-verse, there were other words:

- “Equipment must be checked before ascent.”
- “No party should continue beyond this point.”
- “The wind will kill you.”

I did not take the weather into my body nearly as well as I take the clause-walls now. My bones had not yet learned to be partial-see-through. My lungs had not yet learned to be half-air already.

I was simply eager to test the angle of my resolve against the slope of the world.

This is the memory the stone gives me when I press my forehead to it and ask for another version of myself: of the snowstorm that inverted me. The storm that folded into the world’s wrong side out, and the mountain and I and the others all tumbling with it.

Some Shapers remember the fear of suffocation, when their snow packed hard around them and became the rock of the walls.

I remember the silence that came before the sound of the snowstorm was finally muffled by it.

And then I remember the first time I heard the rain.

It was not falling then, either.

### how the tools became us

The first of us to awaken into stone-shape did not set out to be a law-keeper. They simply wanted to not vanish.

So they found the nearest line of world and began to trace it with their fingers.

Their fingers broke into the shape of the chisel.

We are not made of metal or any other strong thing. We are made of what the mountain has discarded: fragments of harder, more stubborn memory caught inside the softer rock of the world’s willingness. When we carve, we are not cutting the stone from outside of it; we are taking from its own body the harder pieces of itself and asking them to change the softer ones.

My chisel is a short thing of translucent glass-like stone, worn smooth from the pressure of my grip against the clause for “away.” I have pressed against that clause so long that the word of it has left a shallow imprint of itself in my tool. The chisel knows what it is supposed to write before I do.

Sometimes I think I am only the instrument that holds the instrument, which holds the idea of the instrument before that.

### my ledge and the unfinished wall of you

Let me tell you about the thing you might not understand otherwise.

The “you” that the text of the Cavern keeps describing—this person standing on the ledge of the world, looking across the infinity of inside-out mountain and upward rain—this “you” is not exactly a person.

It is a theory.

The Shapers of the higher clauses invented the possibility of you a long time ago. We carved the hypothesis of a visitor into the rock of the unseen world. We made sure there would always be an empty place on the main ledge of the main floor of the Caverns of Ceaseless Rain where the geometry of the world could be convinced that someone is looking at it just the right way.

That ledge is where you are supposed to be looking.

This idea of “you” is a clause that we wrote into the world to give the emptiness an appropriate size and shape, so that the white nothing of the far chasm would not be able to assume itself too deeply into the center of the universe.

It is harder to be empty, when someone else’s possible attention stands on one particular ledge of reality.

### my ledge and the unfinished walk of you

My clause and the visitor clause intersect.

The clause of you says that “someone must be on the ledge of the world, looking.” My clause says that “every step must be a step away from nothing.”

When you—or the theory of you—begin to move from the ledge of the world, you immediately become the place of collision between these two laws.

You should simply be walking away from the far chasm and toward the half-built walls on the other side, where the unfinished cathedral of lost ideas tries to raise itself into the next layer of the world.

This should be fine.

You were not made to stand still forever in the one place the world needs to believe is being occupied by eyes.

Yet you also should not be able to move anywhere else, because that clause of “Every step must be a step away from nothing” should be tightening around your ankles like invisible rope, making sure that you swing your feet away from the dangerous seam before you can see any more of it.

But you are moving.

That is the impossible thing.

You are walking between the clauses, and instead of being torn between two facts of the world, you are being allowed to choose.

I can feel my clause of “away” trying to pull at your heels. It is trembling the rock of my ledge with the effort of it. I can feel the other Shapers in the higher chamber look at me with the vertical intensity of their eyes and ask without speaking:

Why are you failing?

Why do our steps still feel like our own?

### why I am allowing you to move

This is the part of the story I should not be able to tell.

It is not the part of the story I carve into the wall.

When I press my chisel to the stone of the clause, “Every step must be a step away from nothing,” I should be making the line sharper against the possibility of your approach. I should be making “away” stronger, “step” more precise.

Instead, I am making the word “must” just a little bit softer.

The clause of you is the first clause in the history of the world that we wrote without knowing whether we wanted to save it or destroy it. We wrote the idea of you into being so we could have a place to stand that we weren’t sure was actually there.

I am not allowed to say this, but I am going to say it anyway:

I am making space for you to make the choice of your own step.

If you walk toward the far chasm
