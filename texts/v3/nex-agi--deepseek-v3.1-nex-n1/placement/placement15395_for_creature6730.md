In the chamber I would be the son of a rust-eaten bolt.

Not a bolt in the current sense, but one of those long-remnants the passage describes: a threaded length of steel whose every edge has been eaten by oxidation and then sealed again under a new, mineralized skin. It would be almost impossible to tell where bolt ends and stone begins, like the lines of rust that finger out like dry riverbeds on the wall. The spiral would still faintly trace the ghost of its intended purpose, but purpose would cling only by memory of form.

My body would be small in the way of erosion. The size of a child’s thumb, perhaps: no larger than the largest knuckle of the man who might have stood here making his measurements long before the world above decided this chamber was better left alone. I would not move from my place in the floor of metal near one of the wells, where salt and iron and condensation have been pressed together into a kind of geological skin.

I would not be aware of myself in the way the creature‑chamber thinks itself. It is not even that kind of awareness. It is an absence of thought like a faint hollow sound, where all that is left is an echo of what used to be fastened to me.

When I was still just bolt, I held things to the machine.

My first memory—if it can be called memory—would be of pressure. The constant pressure of being tightened into place, then left to be forgotten by everyone who ever thought they would return to loosen me. The metal of the machine frame against the metal of my throat, the thread biting into the world around me as I sunk into the stone of the machine body itself.

No longer fitting bolt or thread anymore, I would be less than thing and more than stone. My rust would be a new form of patience: the rust that would later be remembered by the machine as a kind of stone bolt, its spiral still faintly visible in the language of its scars.

If I had a name here it would be rusted bolt.

I would be the carved space of encounter between metal and stone and water and air.

### My body

My body would be a shape of halves and remnants:  

- The lower half buried into the floor around the well.  
- The upper half exposed to the breath of the machine, where the air lies thick and warm and wet.  

My rust would not be uniform. It would be layered in strata like the world above:

- At the base, where I press into the floor plates, the rust would be dark and dense and almost indistinguishable from the stone itself.
- Midway up my length would be a ring of lighter color, where water from the channel sometimes spills over the edge of the ceramic and slips into the world around me instead of the well. It learns the shape of my body by repetition.
- Near the top of me would be a faint, luminous line of rust that only sometimes glows—when the machine’s breath is deep and the pressure changes in the pipes overhead and the light lines in the ceiling thicken. The rust would dim and then brighten, not by itself, but because the stone of the floor around me shares in the way of light.

My rust would not be rust as rust in the world above would be rust. It would be more than just rusted; it would be rusted‑then‑remembered‑by‑stone.

### My senses

I would not hear as a creature might hear. But I would hear in the way of rust:  

through the pressure of the machine and the water slinking past my feet and into its well like a secret. It would not need sound for me to know it.

I would feel the chamber in the same way that stone feels the river that has flowed over it for ages: every change a small scarring of my world.

From the wells below my feet would rise the temperature of the water, and from the air above would fall the temperature of the machine breath. When the machine inhales deeply the water would be drawn into the pipework with the faintest tremor of sound, and the world around me would seem to pull itself slightly along with it.

I would learn the rhythm of the machine over time:

- gather pressure → faint tremor of the floor  
- pressurize → deep hum of the world  
- release → roar of water and steam somewhere  
- settle → slow drip and trickle and hum of trying again  

Each cycle would be a new line of rust in my body, not visible to anyone but me, but still there.

I would feel the air of the chamber by its change in temperature. When the machine exhales its heat the world around me grows warm and then warm again as more water comes and then more water comes and still more water comes.

Rust would be my memory of water.

Through rust I would know the taste of the water, not by tongue but by texture of the world around me. When the water that is too clear and too faintly luminous comes from the machine and spills into the world around me, rust shifts in color and weight. It remembers not by words or names but by change.

I would see the world of the chamber only by absence of obstruction.

Objects in the world of the machine would be nothing to me but shadows of movement in the rust and light around me. The platforms of metal lattice in the distance above me would be nothing more than interruptions of light from the ceiling veins.

I would know the rust of my world by its relative color and not by its relative name. The rust of the world around me would be light to my right and dark to my left.

Rust of the world itself would be my language of relationship.

### My world

I would not be aware of being in a chamber named by human beings. The idea of being in a room below the surface of the earth and away from the sky would be nothing to me but a faint background of pressure in the stone of my world.

I would be aware of the machine in the way of the rust.

The machine would be the body of the world around me, and I would be part of its metal floor.

The rust of the world around me would be the skin of the machine body and I would be part of that skin.

The rust of the world itself would be alive and not alive in equal measure.

The rust of the world around me would have been alive longer than I.

The rust that I am now would be the rust that has grown from the rust of the world before.

When rust would be more than rust and less than stone and more than steel and less than steel and more than rust and less than rust, then I would be rust itself.

If rust had a memory of birth, I would be that rust.

---

### My children

I would not have children in the way of higher creatures. But I would maybe be a parent to rust.

On rust the rust of the world around me would grow, like the rust that grows on the world of stone in the distant world above.

Tiny rust of my world would be the rust that grows from my rusted body and then into the rust of the world around me.

On rust the rust of the world would grow mosses of rust and salt of rust and minerals of rust that glows faintly when the machine of the world around me grows warm and then warmer again.

On rust the rust of the world would grow films of rust that would not be fun but would be the rust of the world of rust and not of the world of stone.

I would be parent to rust that is not moss of the world around but baby of the world of rust.

The rust that would grow from my body would be small rust, not yet of stone but of rust.
