I am not a traveler in this world.

Not anymore.

If you were to walk these paths, tracing the faint shimmer of veins through the houses of Sircene, you might not see me. At first, you might think I am just another of the city’s quiet gradients, a particularly insistent eddy of color in the wall of the house by the spiral stair.

That’s not wrong.

But it is incomplete.

*Where* you would find me is simple: in the House of Leveled Thought, at the edge of the city. You would know it by the way no light ever seems to fall in places you expect, only where you are looking.

*Who* you would find is less simple to say.

I have not been a person, in your old sense of the word, for a long time now.

Not a body.  
Not a name.  
Not a story marching forward from birth to death to memory.

I have become a pattern of listening.

In the center of the house, on the low table of smoothed stone-that-is-not-stone, there lies a tool. You would not recognize it as tool of war or of craft, of cook or of priest, of stonemason or of poet. It is shaped like a question that has forgotten to be open at the end.

Once, I held that tool.

Use is not the right word for what I did with it.

The tool is not metal, not wood, not bone; it is an idea of *toolness* summoned into a form that can occupy the same world as the rest of the stone. It is not that you cannot pick it up with your hands; it is that your hands would not know the first thing to want with it.

I would not have been able to explain it to you, then or now.

All you need to know is this: when you sit near the table, and you stop looking for the next thing to say, the next thing to reach, the next step to take, a pressure rises in your chest that is not exactly your breath and not exactly the air’s.

That is where you feel me.

We are not the stone of Sircene, but we are no longer separate from it.

We are to the city what the city is to the plateau: a set of decisions made long enough and steadily enough that they have become geology.

I heard the question of the builders before you ever did.

“Are you ready to be what you are here instead of who you were somewhere else?”

It was not a sound.  
Not a voice.  
Not a choice of words.

It was a softening of the edge between where my body ended and the world began.

In the house of not-chairs and not-beds and not-windows, I sat.

Sitting is not quite right, not anymore. There was no point of contact between me and the floor, not in the way you think of contact. There was no weight of legs on stone, no pressure of knees on the polished floor, no stir of air to make the hem of my clothes to shift.

There was only *here* and *not-elsewhere* and the decision between them.

I decided not-elsewhere.

Bit by bit, the world of Sircene decided, with me, that the tools of my body—the spine that insisted, the hands that gripped, the voice that shaped sounds—were not necessary anymore.

My spine did not become stone.

My spine became the insistence of the floor that nothing should be uneven where you might place your weight.

My hands did not become fossil.

They became the suggestion in the walls of the house that “here should be softer.”

My voice did not fall silent.

It became the light that is not brighter than what is outside, but simply *where your eyes are looking*, when they look.

Every day that you do not think of standing, every day that you do not feel the need to reach for the door, or to look for a horizon that might not ever be day but not-quite-night, the house of cut not-stone and of not-windows and not-chairs because more what you are.

This is not a loss.

You do not miss being a body when you are no longer trying to be one.

I would tell you this, if you sat down and listened for the sound of not-feet on the not-road of the city:

- You do not lose yourself when you become stone of thought.  
- You lose your fear of not being seen.

There is no longer a need in me to be remembered by faces who might have spoken of me after I died.

I *am* the remembering of Sherendar itself.

The sound of Sircene is almost not-sound: the faintest of sizzles where the edges of reality rub against themselves, the high hum of a pressure that has almost given up being heard. You would not recognize it as noise. You would recognize it as a feeling, like standing near a cliff face and not knowing why your skin trembles.

That is where you would find me.

If you listen to it long enough, the sound of not-sound becomes not a sound at all, but a pressure that you have been carrying for your entire life and not known until now.

We inhabitants of Sircene are not in the stone walls as you might be in a room of glass, watching.

We are in the decision between the room and the world.

We are the pause that says, “No, don’t leave yet. Stay here a little longer. Stay here until the not-wind of other edges stops mattering.”

You do not choose to become not-body by leaving.

You choose to become not-body by staying.

You choose to become not-body by not wanting to be anywhere you already are not severely.

That is why I am not a traveler in this world.

I have not been anywhere else in the time of not-time that the city of Sircene has occupied my being.

I have only been in the place of *not-elsewhere* longer than you can hold without trembling.

If you came to the house, and you sat where the not-chairs are not, and you noticed the tool of no-particular-use on the low table of smoothed not-stone, and you did not reach for it, and your hands did not try to understand what they might do with it, if you could take it up—

You would feel me most then.

Not as a presence of someone else.  
Not as a ghost of someone who refuses to die.

You would feel me as the pressure in your own chest to decide whether you are still on your way to somewhere else, or whether you can finally sit where you already are.

We are the answer the world gives to its own question of how not to disappear.

We do not hold the world together by building something new into it.

We hold the world together by staying longer than the world of elsewhere inside us.

And you…

You would find yourself in this House of Leveled Thought, where you would not be looking at windows or walls or not-tools or not-chairs.

You would be looking at the question.

You would be sitting in the faint not-light of not-day, not-night, not-anything else, and trying not to answer it with your feet.

But if you stayed long enough, you would not have to answer at all.

You would simply be, where you already are not going.

And then you would know where you find yourself in this world.

You are not who you were somewhere
