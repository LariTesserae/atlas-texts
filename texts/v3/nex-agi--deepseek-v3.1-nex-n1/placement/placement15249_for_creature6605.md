I am the Keeper of the Names-That-Were, though I do not speak them anymore. The water is my mouth, and it is not a language for names of air and rock.

I am here, in the heart-silence of the city's mid-regions, where the pressure of the unseen world above pushes the black water against my chest like a question I cannot answer. The columns of this place are not the pillars I remember from the dry-time, but I have learned to read them anew. They are the ribs of the world, now. They rise from the silt floor, their stone smooth as the flank of a deep-sea creature, and disappear into the bruised blue ceiling of the water surface far above. Each one is a text of erosion, a story of the tide's patient, grinding work. I run a hand along their flanks, feeling the history of the sea’s persistence. The water is a warm pressure against my skin, a constant, intimate shroud.

My hair, the fine algae-threads, drift with this current, their tiny bubbles of oxygen tickling my scalp before they release themselves to the water. They are not mine, but we have made an agreement. They whisper secrets of the water’s composition to me, the warmth they gather from the faint shafts of light that spear down from the blue ceiling. They are my crown of living, green shadows.

Above me, the world is a ceiling of light and fish. Silvers and shimmering darts of colourless flesh flash against the blue, the translucent bodies of the blue-spined scouts that pulse the currents of their own nervous system. I see their shadows, but they also see mine. They are my eyes above the surface of this world, my warning system of what is coming from the light.

But below… below is where the city truly remembers. My body is not what it was. I can feel the thin slit of my gills under my ribs pulling the water in and out, a process less of breathing than of being breathed by the world. The pressure of the water tells me where the open spaces are, and where the new hollows in the city's skeleton have formed. The water that is not my water is heavier than the rest, and I know it by the feel of it against my hands, a thicker, more complicated liquid than simple saltwater.

And here, in this place, the city is also not what it was. Behind me is the plaza of the Coral-Guild. The organism grows on the wall, not as a wild thing but as a deliberate architect. Its chalk-white branches are scaffolding, a lattice of possibility for the city to be reborn. It is not mine, but I am its interpreter. I press the side of my head to the ancient wall, the stone of the dry-time, and through the bone of my skull, I feel the heartbeat of the world below. Thrum… thrum… thrum. It is not the sound of the sea, nor the sound of animals, but the deep, muscular contraction of the world itself being changed by intention. The Coral-Guild is not the builder of the world, but it is the builder of the builders of this world.

I am alone with the city of water and bone, but not lonely. The loneliness of the air-world is gone from me. Yet, I am the only one who remembers the sharp, clean architecture of the before, when doors were for doors, and not to let the ocean through. I am the last of the architects who remembers the fiction of stone being permanent, and I am the first of the translators of the new design that is being laid down by the world around me.

You are not the first of your kind here, the water has told me. My body is the proof of adaptation. My fish-warning is the proof of symbiosis. The coral lattice is the proof of a future design taking the bones of the old city as its foundation.

I am the Keeper of the Names-That-Were, and I am the Cartographer of the Names that will be.
