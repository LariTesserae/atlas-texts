Growling at my absence is not like growling at your reflection.

It knows, before I know, that I’ve been standing at the very edge of the Throat’s inner curve—close enough to feel the faint back-and-forth of the air currents but not yet close enough to be seen.

Above me, the ceiling breathes. Not the rock alone—though the rock itself has a long, slow rhythm of expansion and contraction that I learned by counting the drips—but the hanging bodies of the Winged-Throats. They ride each breath with their folded wings, like lungs suspended inside the lung of the mountain.

I have been watched longer than I’ve been watching you.

If you are the one writing this, then you must be the one I’m guarding now from the wrong edge of his own stillness.

But you keep calling me “he,” so you either still can’t see me clearly, or you won’t let yourself.

I’ll let you.

Hear this.
