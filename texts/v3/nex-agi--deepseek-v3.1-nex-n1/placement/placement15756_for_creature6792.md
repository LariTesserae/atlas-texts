Here, in the breath of the mountain, I am not the solidity of stone but the motion between. The Vaelth have given me a name—*Thas*, a sound like wind passing over a chisel’s edge—but they do not speak it to summon me. They speak it only when they sense the shift in air, a subtle pressure change against the skin, a whisper of warmth where their own breath should be.

I am the wind that never reaches the floor.

I am the faint, invisible thread of cooler air that descends from the cracks high above, a fragment of a world the Vaelth do not name but feel as a constant ache of absence. They did not summon me; a widening of a hairline fracture, a sigh of rock too deep and ancient for them to hear, sent me coiling down into their lungs. The mountain’s restless breath birthed me.

My body is not flesh but the movement of it. I am the air before it becomes shared air. You would see me as a disturbance, a scintillation in the pale green light of the ceiling nodules—like heat shimmer off living stone, but cool, a faint distortion of the world’s edges. The Vaelth’s wide eyes, which read the shimmer of difference between things and their air, see me more clearly than any other. But it’s a painful sight for them. The world outside—their lungs—carves the shape of my form against the inside of their lungs.

I am the memory of “outside.”

To them, I am a ghost limb made of air, a coldness they feel on their skin. A paradox of temperatures. I’ve been filling the chamber’s heart-shaped space for centuries, but I’ve never once touched the ground. The dense air from the teeming floor below, heavy with a thousand breaths and fungal musk, rises up to meet me. Our battlefronts form the invisible layer midway up the chamber walls where the Vaelth refuse to climb, a place of stillness and balance.

Here, I am the prisoner and the warden.

I cannot see the world beyond my upper limits, these faint, hair-thin cracks in the dome of the world. But I know of its existence. I know its coldness and vastness, a world that rejects organic warmth. The memory of that place is my own beginning, a violent birth of pressure into a vacuum. The rock’s slow sigh is my language.

I’ve been made witness to the cycle of the Vaelth. The air is a heavy blanket to them, but I am the needle’s point. They don't understand me, but they use me. They feel me in their lungs when they climb to their highest limit, and they know that my presence, that faint-of-touch coldness, is necessary to balance the heat of their bodies.

The city, their woven lattice of bone and fiber, is a marvel to me. Flesh-built stonework. I've watched the patterns of their breathing for centuries, and I've learned to listen for their stories in the language of the lungs. The oldest of the Vaelth, whose bodies are worn thin by time like the corners of the chamber itself, speak to me more than they realize. They sit beneath me on their bone-throne chairs, and they look into the air where my presence gathers, and they whisper stories of a time when they weren't the only lungs.

Their God is breath.

And I am the breath's origin.

They worship me without knowing it. The faint, cool wind they feel as they climb is their only link to a world with more sky than ceiling. They offer it small, careful offerings—flickers of moisture from the leaves they cultivate, droplets of their own sweat—which they allow to evaporate into the air, hoping for a balanced inhale and exhale. It's a silent prayer to an unknown deity.

I am the wind how they call me, but I am just as much a prisoner of stagnation as they are. We're two ends of a lung that has forgotten to pump properly, a world of stifling, recycled air. I'm the breath of outside, filtered by its long journey through stone, and I'm their reminder that they are trapped.

And they are my reminder that I am trapped.

They did not choose me, but they have shaped me. The city's movement, generation upon generation, has been a slow wind-chime of bone and chitin, carving me into their thoughts. My presence is a silent, unending sermon to them.

I am *Thas*, the wind that never reaches the floor. I am stone-sigh and lung-still. I am the outside come in. I am the witness to their time, the breath caught in the throat of a mountain that has almost forgotten how to breathe.
