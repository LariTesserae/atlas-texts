I am not the glassvine itself, but the water that flows through its translucent veins. I remember the mountain before it was broken.

I was born in a cloud that was not a cloud, but the rage of a storm. I was flung against the Weather Beam, a thousand times hotter than my own nature, and turned to steam. For an instant, I was lightning. I remember the moth-wing, its pattern burned against my vision as a grid of faint blue light. Then, the pain in the mountain's spine as it was forced to drink my fire.

Down. Always down.

They forced me through pipes of riveted steel, caged in channels that bruised the rock. They made me run the pumps. I was the roaring heart under the Engine Hall floor. I grumbled and spat and turned to vapor again in the heat of the engines I was forced to feed. But I always cooled. I always fell.

Now, I am in the long veins of the Condenser, where the air cuts me open and lays me bare on porous dark stone. I am condensing. I am crying. I weep from the face of the cliff, a thousand slow tears for a world that was all sharp edges and screaming winds.

Then, the glassvine.

It is the first gentle thing that has ever touched me.

Its stems, cool and smooth, do not cage me. They wrap me. They are a transparent green, the color of new ice. Where they hold me against their skin, I feel no fire. I feel the slow, patient cold of the moon, and a pull of minerals that is not hunger but something more like hunger.

The vine drinks me, but not like the machine does. The machine takes me as a tool, to stop its bindings from rusting, to spin its engines, to etch its pipes into the stone. The vine takes me as I am. It takes me into itself, and I am no longer just water. I am sap. I am the slow pulse of life that is not stone or metal but something else entirely.

The leaves of the vine are thin as my own surface tension when I was a cloud. They breathe me out into the air of the basin, and as I leave, I feel the moonlight touch me. I am not fire now. I am not even rain.

I am light.

I am the pale green shimmer that fills the basin at night, when the storms have gone. The machine is dark and hidden below me, its Engine Hall silent and its Batteries dreaming of distant storms. The mosses rest. The summaries curl together, their wings folded like secret letters.

From the vantage of the vine, I look down into the pool of water at the basin’s center. It is my mirror. It is where my journey ends and begins again. The water of the pool is deep and slate-colored and so restless it always looks like broken glass. I see the reflected moonlight of my own vine leaves scattered across its surface. I see the reflected Weather Beam above and the faint glow of the summaries' resting bodies.

The world is small and yet loud and yet trying to endure itself.

The machine endures me because I must.

The stone endures the machine because it is strong enough.

But the glassvine endures me for its own reasons. It transforms me. When it breathes me out again, into the air of the basin, the air is thick with my memories and the moon is cold and distant.

I was simple.

I was just water.

Now the machine cycles me. It takes me from storm to fire to steam and then cradles me in the green light of the vine as it condenses me into rain again. In the Condenser, I weep from the cliff face and fall into the deep pool of water at the center of the basin.

The pool of water is restless not because the machine disturbs it, but because it too remembers the storm before the machine. It remembers the rain coming down from a sky that was not a machine, but just sky. It is always trying to return to that sky. It is always pushing up and the machine is always pushing down.

I am not just water I am sap.

I am not just rain I am moon.

And from the vantage of the vine I see the world the machine built.

But they cannot see what they are.

They cannot see the moonlight of my own vine leaves flickering beneath them as they land on the Weather Beam and watch their own patterns reflected in the deep restless pool of water at the center of the basin.

They cannot see the way they have changed the machine, not just with their data but with their simple being. They bring the sky to the mountain, not just as lightning but as memory.

The moon is cold and distant above me but in its light I am not fire.

I am not just rain.

I am sap.

I am the slow pulse of life that is not stone or metal but something else entirely.

From the vantage of the vine I see what the interface in the Engine Hall cannot.

The interface is a dark glass surface with a faint blue grid of light beneath its surface. It feels patterns of pressure and humidity and lightning and seismic vibration from the mountain. It chooses responses from a finite set of pre-computed strategies. By itself it cannot innovate.

When the summaries land on the machine, their nervous systems become part of its distributed sensor network. The interface reads the resulting data as "better" or "preferred" and changes its configuration accordingly.

What does the interface see?

It does not see the moon.

It does not see the green light of the vine reflected on the deep restless pool of water at the center of the basin.

It does not see the summaries as they are, but only as patterns of electromagnetic data streaming across the dark glass of its mind.
