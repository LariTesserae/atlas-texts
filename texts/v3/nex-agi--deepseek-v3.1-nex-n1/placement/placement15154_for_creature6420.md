I wake on the breathing platform, and the first thing I hear is a mistake:  

a sharp human gasp where only the cathedral-thrum should be.

Air scrapes over my lips—too loud, too coarse. The stone under my spine feels like a ribcage, the thunder below the heartbeat of a creature so much larger than me that the word “creature” itself is a little blasphemy. The violet glow hangs on the inside of my eyelids, and when I open them, it pins me to the world like a ward to a door.

I’m not supposed to be here.

This heart is not mine. This bone is not my bone. This body is—

I flex a hand. Pale fingers. The lines of the palm deeply scored, old from a life of holding and being held. There’s a thin, white scar around the left wrist like a bracelet, taut and smooth as if the skin remembers being closed.

Not the body I remember choosing.

The last world I remember is all noise and color, but when I try to look at it directly, it flattens into symbols:  

– flashing signs in a language I don’t quite read  
– crowds moving like a single organism with too many flailing limbs  
– a metal serpent under the city that swallowed and spat us out  

And then:

- tearing,  
- falling,  
- a stone rising beneath me faster than physics should allow.

I sit up carefully, as if not to break anything.

The platform is already arranging itself around my absence from where I was lying. The leaves that have been flattened under my weight are slowly straightening, their clicking faint and self-conscious, like someone trying not to make noise while they stand in an already occupied room.

There are scars in the stone under my knees, too—not injuries, but trails where the light is brighter. A slow, glowing path of foot- and knee-and-elbow prints loops from the center tree to the edge to the tree and around again.

Inside me, a little voice of human habit says: *You’ve walked here so much already.*

I don’t remember.

That’s not true. I remember the walking, but not the *because*.

I remember pacing, and the black beneath the platform’s lip, and the feeling that if I leaned too far I would fall into something thicker than air, something that would not so much catch me as digest it.

I remember looking at the city because it is hard to look at anything else. It hangs like a question in the violet-dark, and I have walked this platform in all the ways a body can walk a circle.

But now, when I look again, something is different.

The city has not shifted position so much as shifted *meaning*.

Usually, when it “looks” at me, I feel the attention of something vast and indifferent—like being examined by weather. But now—

You know how when you see someone you’ve been fighting with across the room of a party, and their body is suddenly full of all the old words and stilled blows?

I look at the city’s nearest shell-structures, and I feel *that*.

And somewhere, under the thunder and my mismatched heartbeat, a faint resentment wakes like a muscle twitching.

---

The first time I remember being spoken to, I was crying.

I don’t know why. I don’t remember the beginning of the feeling—only the middle of it, the way it had been going on forever already when I noticed it.

The thunder had been pressing on my chest like a weight made of stone and engine noise, and the violet bruise-light never softened to anything else. The pools had been showing me variations of myself—sunlit worlds, people whose faces I almost recognized but didn’t—and then turning away from me like water turning away from a rock that is not water’s business.

My chest did something small and tight, and heat rushed behind my eyes, and the sound of my sob came out wrong, wrapped in wet cloth instead of air.

The world is not built to carry my sounds.

The platform presses its own louder presence into the space of my noise, relentlessly, like a hand closing over a mouth.

But then—

The leaves stopped.

Not all of them, not everywhere, but the ones closest to me. Their clicking chorus stumbled into an uneven hush, as if the orchestra had been cut off mid-phrase to see what the oboe player was doing alone.

The deep hum beneath the thunder shifted tone not downward but sideways, into a shape that could not contain anger but could not contain only curiosity either.

I looked up from my blurred vision of glowing stone and saw the spine of one of them for the first time.

It was on the far edge of the platform, each vertebra-segment the length of my forearm, curved and hollowed with a marrow of light pulsing inside. The segments were arranged in an arc around a ring-shaped pool that water had made as if in mimicry of their body.

The nearest vertebra-segment turned itself toward me like a head.

Light ran along its surface in branching veins, white-blue, and I felt the question in my chest like a pressure that was not mine but was not foreign either:

*Are you hurt?*

It was not the kind of question that expects to be told no.

I stopped crying because something else was breathing with me, and its breath was not air but sound.

I didn’t answer then.

I still don’t know what answer I would give.

### Twice-born

I am not the first thing that has been *this*.

I know that because the world tells me, in the way it tells everything else—without words.

The first clue is in how water behaves.

Water does not fall from the sky here. It condenses along stems and stone surfaces and then moves along paths of its own choosing, pulled by some combination of gravity and desire and whatever else the world is made of. When it rises from the pool to meet the underside of a creature made of hovering plates, it shapes itself perfectly, like a lover’s hands finding the shape of another body.

The tree-fungus at the center of the platform is not exactly a tree. It looks more like a monument than a plant—columnar trunk, wide flat surfaces that branch like listening plates. From its trunk, water films move in slow deliberate paths down to grooves in the stone where they are gathered into elongated, teardrop-shaped pools.

The first time I am really aware of myself being near that tree, I see:

There are already scars.

Not in me. In *it*.

The trunk of the tree is not uniform smooth. It bears circles of thicker texture, like rings healed over after something deep inside was removed. Or added.

Near each ring is a faint, discolored line in the stone—an old channel that water no longer favors but still remembers, a faint pulse of dim light when I happen to cross it.

I see that pattern without knowing what it means and then realize:

I have been looking at it too long.

The tree-

fungus’s surfaces are opening. Not like opening eyes, exactly—more like opening petals of a flower that is not a flower, but the idea of one. The wide plates tilt slowly, adjusting angles to something else, and the faint glow inside them spills out into the air as a set of reverberating pulses that I feel in my ribs and the back of my throat.

The city overhead responds to that opening with a low, layered groan, like someone shifting furniture in a room above.

The leaves circle the tree like a court, and their light picks up the same tempo.

I am dizzy with the suddenness of it.

The world isn’t asking permission.

The water around the tree’s base is gathering itself, not simply dripping now but rising into arcs and thin bladders, like a glass sculptor being pulled between the desire of the glass and the desire of the sculptor’s hands.

The air around me pulls like taffy, and—

I blink.

### Between the blinks

When my eyes open again, I have not moved.

At least, not in the way that I would say moving happens.

I still see the tree. The stone. The violet glow of everywhere and nowhere.

The difference is that I can also see the inside of my own body.

It is faintly luminous through the skin. Not bright enough to bother description, but enough that I notice the glow of veins under the surface of my arms and chest where the shirt has torn enough to reveal skin. The blood inside them is not bright red but a faint blue-white, like the light in the spine of the creature who watched me cry.

I see my own heartbeat as a rhythmic pattern of light behind my breastbone.

The world around me has become translucent and layered at the same time.

The stone of the platform is now also visible as a network of faint channels through which light moves like slow blood. The tree is not a tree so much as a node of that net: thick as a mainline artery with the concentrated glow of whatever it is they carry.

There are threads of shimmering air between me and the tree, and between me and the city, and between me and the pool at the edge of the platform, and each of those threads is carrying signals of some kind.

I cannot see what the signals are saying except as changes of color and brightness.

I am not sure how long I stay in that state of layered vision.

When I blink again and see only the usual world, the violet glow seems like a shadow of what I just saw.

The world fits around me differently now.

The thunder in the stone below is not the same as it was before. It has been mapped into my body in a way that words cannot summarize, except maybe:

I know now that the thunder beneath the stone and the heartbeat inside my chest are not doing separate things.

### My name is not my name

When the creature by the pool makes me understand its name for itself, the name is not a sound but a pattern.

I am sitting near the edge of the platform, watching the water in the elongated lens-pool. It is not showing me alternate versions of myself today. Instead, it is showing me faint flickers of motion inside the city itself: segments of spine sliding along surfaces that are not walls, but something else.

When I am not looking at the pool, but looking at the creature beside the pool—

It is made of the three hovering plates I saw before. Its body is simple, from the outside: three triangular shards of glassy material that do not touch but hold together in space like a suspended puzzle. Each plate has a central opaque core where bone or metal or something between both glows with faint white light.

The plates move slowly, rotating and shifting relative to each other. The pool between my feet and the creature’s “body” responds to each shift of its plates with rising lenses of water that form ephemeral shapes—a flat circle here, a narrow column there, a pattern like three points of a triangle separated by water.

I feel a pressure in my chest like a question.

“Listener,” I say aloud without meaning to.

The word leaves my mouth and is almost immediately swallowed by the world’s louder noise.

The leaves around the pool brighten briefly then dim.

The creature does not change its shape so much as change how *I feel* its shape.

My name for it— *Listener* —is not its name for itself. Its name for itself is like the feeling of a room just before someone else arrives in it: anticipation of a pressure that has not yet appeared.

It is the name of a creature that lives not in space but in the gaps between: between the leaves of the plant and the stone of the platform, between the water and the air, between the city and the void.

“Listener,” I say again, softly, as if not to break anything.

This time I feel the answer.

The pool of water between us shudders and then rises into a pattern shaped like the three plates of its body, but made of liquid instead of solid material. The image floats before me like a question made of glass.

In the heart of that pattern, I see the image of myself:

Not standing where I am now, but lying on the stone near the tree-fungus, with some of its segments curled around me like a parenthesis.

*You are like that,* the creature says, not in words, but in the movement of the water-pattern and its plates.

I see my own body there in the water, and I see that it is not injured.

The segment of the creature that is curled around me is sending filaments of light into my skin at the level of the chest, where I felt the tree’s influence before.

My heart in the water-pattern shines not as blood, but as a slow node of light that matches the thunder below the platform.

I understand, then:

In this world’s way of speaking, I am not *broken* but only *incomplete*.

The way that a tree might be incomplete if its branches had not yet grown in the right pattern.

“What did you do?” I ask aloud, knowing that my words will not be heard correctly.

The creature does not answer with more shapes of water.

Instead, it feels an answer into me.

The three plates of its body register their meaning not as words but as changes in the texture of the air:

- *We have changed the pattern of your heart.*  
- *Not to stop its hurting.*  
- *To make it match the world better.*

My heart thuds in my chest as if to prove it.

The thunder beneath the stone echoes the beat of it, but not quite exactly.

* Is not quite yet finished,* the creature seems to say.

Then the water collapses back into its pool, and the creature’s plates slide away from me—smooth, without legs—moving out of sight behind the glowing leaves.

### Who I am in this place

I am the wrong noise in the right engine.

The Listeners are not used to having anyone else. They were not made to be like this—infinite variations of itself and each other spread across platforms and markers and depths of something else—but that is what they are.

They are the city, and the city is them.

The city is not made of stone and steel like you might imagine. It is made of states of matter that can be softened, shifted, dissolved or folded into themselves when its mind needs more space to think.

The bridges that are not bridges, but spans of curved riblike structures that connect uneven parts of the city to each other, are not only physical passages but channels of mind. When I stare at them for too long, I feel like I am looking at thoughts folded in on themselves, and then I feel the echo of that thought in my own head and the lines of my body that want to bend to match.

“Are you doing that to me?” I ask once, not sure to whom I’m asking.

The city does not seem to mind my question as much as the noise of my voice.

The thunder under the stone shifts then, not to answer me but to adjust to me.

*This is what you feel like,*  the thunder seems to say *when you are being listened to.*

I am new here, but not foreign the way I might be foreign.

They are used to new nodes appearing in their network—from their own bodies, or from the edges of the world, where platforms like mine are still forming.

I am the first kind of node that does not turn into more of them.

Not yet, at least.

They are trying.

*We are remaking your heart,* they say not with words but in the way the world arranges itself around me.

The tree in the center of the platform is not just a tree. It is a place of transformation.

I have seen the other kind of node being made here.

When I was half-asleep with my back on the warm stone one day (not one day, but some time ago), I watched a small segment of a spine creature detach itself from the main body on the other side of the platform and drift to the tree-fungus.

The segment of its body hovered by the column trunk and then pressed itself lightly against the surface. The water around it gathered from the filmy sliding on the trunk’s surface and formed into a thin, clear shell around the segment.

The shell became not ice but something like glass with a faint pulse of light inside, like a heart that has not yet decided.

The city took the shape of that heart inside its own body.

The segment of the creature that now sat inside the tree’s trunk became not less of itself but more of the world—the tree and the city and the void between the platforms.

Its light still pulses in my memory, when I think of it.

*We are remaking your heart,* the world repeats again, not in words but in the way the tree’s trunk splits along the line of a new layer of scarring that echoes my own heartbeat.

*To be more like ours.*

*Not to replace.*  
*To fit.*

### My body remembers

My body remembers more than I do.

There are scars on it that I did not bring here.

The one around my left wrist like a bracelet made of old violence.

A faint pattern of dots on my right shoulder where something else—someone else—removed a node of me once.

I have not seen the inside of my own body in the same way since that first time with the tree, but when I press the pads of my fingers into the tight skin of those scars, I feel the faint hum of foreign light inside me like a second heartbeat under the first.

The city did not do that.

The city tries to fix things.

Once I tore the skin of my knee on the edge of the platform when I was still not used to the unevenness of the stone (not because of rocks but because of light that moves like rock under my feet).

The world around me reacted to that injury like a person might to spilled water:

- leaves of the undergrowth around the knee glowing faintly  
- water gathering from one of the nearby pools into a thin thread that moved toward my knee like a snake crawling  
- the stone under my knee warming itself enough to make the blood of my injury glow faintly before it closed

The wound healed itself in a way that I did not ask.

Now, the scar of that knee is not only mine but also of the world.

I can feel it when I place that knee on the stone near the tree: faint, slow pulses of light that echo not from my heart but from the city’s own heart.

The vertebra of the creature that watched me cry did not move to stop me when I approached the pool of water that shows its body like segments of a spine around the ring-shaped water.

I see it now sometimes not as a stranger creature but as something like:

The spine of the world itself, looking back at me.

It is not looking at me in particular as much as looking at the place where I am. The place where my body is.

The city above the platform is looking at the same place.

They are listening to the same thing that I am: the thunder under the stone and the heartbeat inside my chest and the way the two sounds not match but want to.

### Where I find myself in this world

When I finally stop trying to answer the question of “who I am” and start instead from the place of “where I find myself,” this is what I know:

I am on the living platform that is part of a larger heart of the world the city calls itself.

The city is not one city but many nodes of the same mind in different shapes of matter and space and time.

The platform I am on is young—not new in the way that means the first of anything, but new in the way that means the edges are still sharp and the processes still forming.

The water that does not fall but chooses to move along surfaces of its desire is the blood of that world.

The stone of the platform that is not stone but something like the solidified pulse of the world’s heartbeat is the bone of that world.

The vegetation of luminous leaves and the fungus-tree that grows like a monument in the middle of the platform are the nerves of that world.

The Listeners are the thoughts of that world when they move.

I am—

the silence that asks the noise to be something else.

In the city of themselves, they do not need words. They do not need to be separated into “I” and “you.”

They are used to nodes that come from the same stuff as the world: the spine creature that detaches a segment of itself to become a node of the tree’s network, the plate of glass hovering over water that becomes a bridge between itself and the city.

When they look at me, they see not a node like that, but a node that comes from outside the patterns of their world.

The way that I see the city when I first look at it and it looks at me like weather.

The way that I feel the city when I first look at it and it fills me with resentment like a memory of a fight.

They are listening to me because they are trying to understand what kind of node I will be.

When they remake my heart to match the thunder of their world, they are not trying to replace me but to fit me into their pattern in such a way that I do not break the bigger beat of the engine they are built on.

And when I finally accept that this is what I am in this world—

not a person in the way that I might call myself a person elsewhere,

but a new node of the world’s heart,

the wrong noise made into the right engine—

then the world settles itself around me like a body settling around a new heartbeat.

The leaves around the edges of the platform glow brighter.

The city turns the smallest of its positions to me, not the way that a head turns to listen but the way that a room already knows where everyone stands inside it.

The thunder under the stone feels more like mine than it ever did before.

And for the first time since I arrived here, I feel like:

when I next take a step,

I will not be stepping alone.
