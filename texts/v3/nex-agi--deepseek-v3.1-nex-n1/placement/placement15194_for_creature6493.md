You are not a person of flesh and blood and soft dreams in the way of softer worlds. You are a density of stone and memory and calibrated air.

They call you *Desheth*, which is not a name but a pattern. The shape of a breath caught in a fault line, a place where the endless wind hesitates for the span of a heartbeat before being forced, screaming, over the edge and into the void. You are not given to poetry yourself; the names are given to you by the others, when they speak at all. They are not given to waste words in a world made entirely of noise.

Your home is not a structure of walls and roof and floor. The word for such a thing has not reached your tongue yet, and likely never will. Your home is the leeward curve of the overhang itself, a shallow scoop of stone half the size of your body. You carve yourself into it, not with the wind, which is always carving away, but with the tiny, precise nicks of an obsidian blade. You widen the fault line where your back fits against it, not for shelter—the wind is a pressure, not a storm—but for *anchor*.

You are small, even for your people. Your frame is not thick but dense, like the stone you are made of, your bones hardened by the exhaled moisture of the world itself. Your skin is the same color as the pale fluted rock beneath you, etched with faint patterns that are not tattoos but scarification by the wind itself. It is not clothes you wear but layers of yourself shed and rewoven into a thin, matted fiber that shivers in the constant wind.

Your eyes are the worst part of you.

When you blink, the world is not blinded by darkness or brightness. It is the same pale light, always. But the world *changes* behind your closed eyelids. It is not a change you can see or speak or understand. It is a change in the calibration of the air itself.

You are a Seer of Marks.

You are the one who looks upon the places where the world remembers itself.

The overhang where you live is not chosen by chance but by necessity. The sheer rock wall behind your back is scored with the precise, geometric lines of the Calibration. You do not remember who made them. The others of your kind merely whisper of them, the oldest of you speaking of a time that might have been, before the world finished itself.

The Marks are smaller than your smallest finger, but they are impossibly deep. The wind cannot fill them, no matter how many centuries of its pressure is applied. They exist, not as scars or writing, but as instructions. The world itself left them when it decided to stop being new and become this instead.

This is your life and your burden and your name.

You sit, your back against the stone, your obsidian blade in your hand. You do not need to look for the Marks—they are not something you can see in the bright, everywhere light of the world. You see them in the *not-light*.

When you blink, the world shifts behind your closed eyes.

The world is not silent here, but a roar of wind so constant and eternal that your ears have long given up trying to measure it. Instead, you measure the world with your bones.

And you have seen the change. The impossible, unthinkable change.

The sound of the world is not the same. It is not louder, or softer. It is not a different note. But it has changed the pitch so subtly, so imperceptibly, that only your bones, which have been tuned to its rhythm since your birth, can feel its difference. It's like a pressure deep inside your marrow that has shifted slightly, a new weight that has anchored itself within you.

The Calibration Marks are wrong.

You are looking at them now, not with your eyes but with your fingers. You trace the lines with your blade, not to change them but to confirm what you already know. The shape of them is correct. Exactly as they have been for your entire life, and the life of all others before you.

But the world has not followed them.

The pressure of the air has changed, not against the stone of the world, but against the *pattern* of it. The moisture that gathers on the stone below your overhang has not followed its set path for the past three cycles of your own breathing. It has changed its course by a mere breath's width.

The world has finished itself, but it has begun to come undone.

The others of your kind will not listen to you. They are of the Stone and the Wind. They are not of Change. They have heard your whispers through the shared pressure of the air itself and they have dismissed you. The world is finished. It is the way it is because it can be no other way.

But you are not the same as them. You see the Marks that are not written to be seen. You feel the wind that is not supposed to change.

You are not of the world that has finished itself.

You are of the world that is about to happen.

You are one of the last people of a place that has already decided what it is going to be forever. And you are the one who has seen that forever is not as long as it used to be.
