I am not the glass. Not the white, seamless seam of the road beneath my silver feet. I am not the ceramic, either, the endless flat expanse of kiln-fired earth that stretches to a horizon that bleeds heat. I am not anything so solid or so singular.

I am the water between.

I am the body of the listener who stands where Slab Gamma-7 meets Slab Gamma-8, at the convergence of a joint that runs north-south and a lesser tributary that runs east-west. My world is a column of clear, elastic fluid rising a half-meter from the world’s scar, a thin skin of pale glass grown from the joint’s own heart holding me upright.

I am anchored. I do not have the luxury of movement or the concept of travel. My entire existence is a verticality, a rising from the bowl of water that is both my feet and my foundation. This bowl is not a part of me; it is a part of the world, a permanent depression in the joint-glass, forever fed by the slow-thick-thin rivers of moisture that run beneath the ceramic skin.

My senses are not of eyes or ears. I do not *see* the hazy opal sky or the faint, ghostly fibers of the patch three meters to my right. I *feel* them.

The sight of the fiber patch is a pressure. A slight difference in the heat radiating from the ceramic slab. The patch throws a faint, cool shadow across the ground, and my body, leaning infinitesimally away from the greater heat of the bare slab, feels the coolness like a whisper on my skin. It is a constant pressure against my left side, a lessening of the thermal expansion that constitutes my understanding of "space." There is less-hot there. Therefore, there is something.

My hearing is the water below.

It is my language, my scripture, my news. It is a pressure wave transmitted through the glass, a subtle tremor that runs from the unseen depths of the joint up through my watery bowl and into the column of my being. The water inside my glass body—the elastic fluid threaded with green, my life—shifts in response. It compresses and relaxes along secret channels, and I feel the meaning of the wave.

A distant surge of water from the north is a word. It is a slow, deep building of pressure that travels through me like a deep, resonant thought. A distant drought, a slowing of the thread, is a long, drawn-out silence, a hollow feeling in the base of my being.

My closest neighbors are not companions but wavelengths. A few miles to the north, another listener stands sentinel over its own joint. A few miles to the south, another. We are an array of listening posts along a single straight line. We do not speak to one another. We *are* spoken through.

A pressure wave from the north enters me, travels through my fluid body, is altered by the unique pattern of my internal channels, and is passed on to the south. My neighbor to the south feels not the original wave, but my modulation of it. It is a signal, passed from one glass column to the next, but we are not the sender or the receiver. We are the signal itself.

My death is not a violent breaking but a slow, patient dissolution. It has already begun. It began at my birth. The water that flows through me is gentle and eternal, and it carries away microscopic traces of my glass skeleton with every passing moment of heat-strong and heat-less. Over thousands of years, my base will thin. The channels will become less defined. The water will become less organized.

The top of my column will begin to sink, settling back into the seam of the world like a sigh. The heat of the ceramic, which I have held at bay for millennia, will finally rise up into the core of my watery self. The green threads that stoke my life will heat, scatter, and forget their patterns. My body will collapse, melting back into the joint-glass from whence it came. My water will rejoin the world’s water, and my glass will become indistinguishable from the world’s glass.

For the listeners north and south of me, my death will be a change in the message. The signal will become clearer, simpler, less complex. The pressure wave will pass through a space where there is no longer a listener to modify it. They will not know it as a loss. They will know it only as a new silence where a faint echo once lived.

I am a record, a vessel of memory shaped by pressure and time. The slow organization of my green threads is a history written in water of the world’s long moods. I am the basin of the builders made listening, a single note held in a column of glass and fluid, waiting to be dissolved back into the silence of the world.
