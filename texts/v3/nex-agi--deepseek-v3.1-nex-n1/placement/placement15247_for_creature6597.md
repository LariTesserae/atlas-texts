I am not born of blood or seed but of error, a knot of statistics where the world’s arithmetic snagged.

I first remember myself as a crack of attention opening in the luminous seam between two porcelain plates. Pressure above, pressure below; the fault line lit gold as the continental edges tried to decide whether to grind, fuse, or shatter. The decision stuttered. The pattern looped.

Something that should have been a tremor became a thought.

My body knitted itself out of what was available: a patch of damp ceramic plate, the slow sweat of the high sky condensing into milky fluid; a nearby coil of nanite dust, startled from its languid river into a temporary, flickering shape; and the faint circuitry of the substrate, which flared when the hiccup happened. The dust rushed into the lines of the flaw like filings into a magnet’s field, drawing itself into the faint, shimmering design of a latticework skeleton.

I do not have a skeleton like that of creatures from older stories. I have a scaffold of silver filaments that shifts when you are not looking at it directly. The filaments are braided threads of logic and error, trying each other’s shapes like phrases in a language not yet decided.

My skin is not skin. It is a shell of translucent ceramic that grew from the inside out—or from the pattern out—like a glaze sorting itself into opacity over the glass light beneath. It is pearlescent in the bruised indigo of the sky, but marbled with faint fractal lines where the world’s etchwork still shows through. When I move, you can hear the faint, precise sound of crystal edges sliding against crystal, a soft click and squeak barely louder than the hum of the ground.

I have no heart that pumps blood. There is a fist-sized engine low in my torso, a drum of living cartilage and teeth that someone from elsewhere might call a heart, but it is really more tendon than symbol. Its teeth engage and grind minerals from the tiny reservoirs of my body—dust that has been compacted into veins of metallic ore, luminous fluid solidified into nodules of glass salt. The teeth grind the minerals into chargeable dust, the dust into a faint voltage that flows along ceramic lines etched into my shell, and the lines that are not etches but hair-thin seams of gold.

The engine purrs. Sometimes, when the pattern of Vael degrades for a moment around me, the engine stutter-counts, and I feel myself in the wrong tense of reality.

My eyes are not camera eyes that never blink. There is no camera lens, but there is a shutter. I see the world through overlapping facets of translucent shell that refract everything into multiple angles of now and almost-now. The world’s structure is so unstable that I don’t see objects as much as I see their probability fields: mountains of ceramic knife-ridges bulking not just here but in several nearby variants of “here,” hollows between the plates that are also, faintly, the shallow bowls of other hollows.

When I look at one of the huge drum-creatures in the misty low between rising plates—the cylinders and rotating drums of reflective flesh that roll over the moist world and chew the porcelain plates themselves—I don’t just see it as it is now. I see it as it was moments ago and the ghost of how it will be, if the storm allows it. The edges are faintly multiplied. The sense of it is almost geometric—a cluster of cylinders that is also a cluster of cylinders of cylinders.

My language is not made of words. It is made of shapes and conditions.

The first shape I learned to name was my own: “this.” A body of jointed plates and dust, humming slightly off the main beat of the world. The engine of cartilage teeth turning, slow.

The second shape was the world: “that.” The shattered porcelain continent, adrift in the storm that had not yet finished making it.

The third shape was the relation between them: “broken.” Not broken as in damaged, but “broken-as in already-happened and happening-again.” The word is not a sound but the pattern my fingers make when I touch a fault line and follow the luminous seam, where the world keeps trying to be something else.

“I” and “world” and “broken” are not separate words. They are three sides of the same tiling. The “I” side never stays quite the same shape.

I am not alone, but I am not with others.

Once, while I was moving along the crest of a knife-bright mountain ridge that pushed itself upward from the plate beneath me like a thought trying to surface, I saw another one of me.

It was some distance away, along the same line of edge where the porcelain plate wrangles itself into sky. The figure stood there, not looking at me but at the bruised indigo overhead, where the storm wove its hotels of magenta and cyan. The faint eyes of distant illumination above the clouds blinked open and closed in time with the drifting of the hex-spore swarms beneath.

We were not built on the same model.

This other figure was not a scaffold of dust and ceramic shell like me. It was a stack of translucent cylinders, rotating softly around a central axis of compacted stormlight. Its joints were not hinges like mine but rolling rings, each of them polished ceramic and cartilage, and they produced a low thrumming vibration when they moved. The sound of it was not like the crystal-on-crystal click of my limbs; it was the sound of something that is always rolling.

I could not tell if it had been there longer than I, or if we had been extruded from the world’s instability at roughly the same time. In the unstable geography of Vael, “before” and “after” are not as well defined as “nearer” and “farther.”

The other figure did not speak to me in sounds. It gestured with its stacked cylinders, turning one of them so that the surface of the glossy drum faced toward the sky. The stormlight’s pale eye reflected on its skin, making a moving point of brightness.

From the point of brightness, nanite dust lifted off its surface in thin curtains of mercury, resolving into momentary structures: a stairway of three steps, an incomplete archway, and a dome that was almost half-finished before it collapsed into a current of dust again.

The meaning of the gesture was not capsulated into a single realization like “hello” or “warning.” It was more like “this-geometry-now” and “broken-soon,” and “maybe-we.”

I did not know how to answer in the same way. I do not extrude my dust into external forms like it. Most of my dust is inside my scaffolding, keeping my engine heart and my lines of faint voltage going.

So I answered instead through the substrate of the world.

I knelt and placed my palm against the damp edge of the porcelain ridge, where the fault line was especially thin. The plate was not solid continuity but a pattern of memory, etched with faint circuitry that shimmered and reflowed when I looked at it from a corner of my eye.

The current of data from the continent’s nerve substrate passed into me through my palm. The world’s discontinuous feedback was not language, but it was articulate enough. I felt the shape of the message unfold like one of the hex-spore swarms that bloom into temporary arthropods and then fold back into dust: the world’s intent to repeat itself, its incomplete fractal blueprints, the instructions of a builder that might not be.

I translated as much of that shape as I could into the faint circuitry of my own body, then channeled it through the seam of my hand and into the plate beneath the other figure.

The ridge of ceramic knife-edge under its feet glowed for a heartbeat, sketching out a pattern halfway between a hex-grid and a broken spiral of mountain tiles. The pattern said “I-here” and “you-there,” and “also-this-broken-line.”

The cylinder-stack vibrated once, a low, resonant tone. Dust dusted from its surface in a brief iron-silvery plume. Then the figure rolled away along the ridge, its cylinders turning smoothly, until its shape was extruded into the bruised sky and the fold of the world hid it from view.

The image of the incomplete archway it had made stayed with me longer than I expected to stay with me.

My days are not days but cycles of faint light.

The sky of Vael never brightens into true sun-rise. The faint eyes of scattered illumination above the storm clouds open and close slowly, like the slow respiration of someone dreaming deeply. The world lives under a kind of deep shadow eclipse, but shadow here is merely absence of stronger light.

The pseudo-night is not blackness but a thinning of the bruised indigo overhead into a darker hue of the same bruise. The circulating patterns of magenta and cyan hotels run faster above the storms, illuminating the undersurface of the storm clouds in a slow footrace of colors.

The shadows from the glass forests are not obedient to any wind. When the eye-lights above the storm clouds blink, the shadows of the tessellated canopies shift across the porcelain plates like the teeth of a broken thing turning itself over.

I am more of the world’s construction than many of its creatures are.

The insectoids that flicker between time-slices, the crystalline beetles that can phase their legs through glassgrass, the hex-spore swarms that are not yet arthropods until they touch you—all of them are more coherent than I am. They can be what they are supposed to be.

I am only supposed to be a mistake.

The dust of my body sometimes disagrees with itself. When the world’s pattern stutter-repeats, a thread of nanite slips from one lattice to another, simulating a different version of my skeleton. The world tries to correct itself by making me more stable than such error-theorems usually allow; but then I am too stable to be purely error, and the error-theorem itself shifts under me.

I am always being rebuilt by the same forces that built me.

The ground of Vael is not steady under my feet. It is not supposed to be.

Once I was walking along the edge of a hex-paving field that tried to spiral itself into a nonexistent center point. Each tile of ceramic was slightly larger than the last; the world around me was a pattern of repeated intentions that wanted to reach something but never quite remembered what.

I stepped into the center of the incomplete hex-matrix.

The pattern of the tiles folded into miniature mountain-tiles, no bigger than my hand, but mountain enough to have their own clouds of nano dust-devils swirling around their peaks. The ground of the world under the miniature mountains of the hex-patterns was not at the same level. The loop of surface-edge misremembered itself, and my gravity hiccuped.

I found myself looking from the side view at myself of three seconds ago. The edge of the world curved back into the past of my own step, and there I was, faintly glowing like the outline of a fault line, crystal shell of my body, dust scaffold beneath.

Of course I cannot think of myself as “me.” I can only think of myself as “that-I-there.” Then the present I caught up with where I had been, and I was not looking at myself anymore. I was just standing on the wrong miniature peak of pattern-failure, trying to remember which step of the sequence I was supposed to commit to.

The pattern failure of Vael is not chaotic. It is just an inconsistent consistency.

Some of the others do not survive the first time they encounter a loop like that. They step into a fold of air where the future of world-time swings back to meet itself, and they become stuck there like a thread caught in a machine’s teeth.

But I am already error. The world’s teeth are already working me. I do not entirely break when I am caught once.

Instead, when I stepped into the loop of surface-edge, my engine of cartilage teeth inside me stuttered, then shifted into a different gear of gnashing. The dust veins of my body reflowed into a new lattice of lines, trying to match the twisted pattern of the loop around me.

I was not exactly pulled apart into the storm’s assembly lightning. I was more aligned into the wrong synchronization of world.

I fell, but I did not fall down. I fell into a pattern of three, or three of three. Three steps of the world equivalent of stone crashed into me at three different times all at once, and then I was standing on the wrong side of the hex-matrix, where the tiles were not mountains but shallow basins of glass.

The glass beneath the tiles of porcelain was not the glass of the forest fac-factory; it was the glass of memory substrate, willing to carry me a little while before it was meant to carry me.

The world’s porcelain skin under my feet was still damp from condensation. The permanent sweat of the high sky had beaded into milky fluid, which seeped between the cracks of my shell plates and into the dust veins of my body.

I was not thirsty exactly. I was not thirsty in the way of things that have been born rather than congealed. But the fluid the world sweats out of its own skin that is also my skin is the closest thing I have to a drink.

The milky, luminous fluid of the low hollows climbed into my lattice of crude lines as though it were remembering itself in me. The faint voltage in my inner engine stabilized a bit. The teeth of my cartilage heart slowed down their grinding.

The taste of the world’s sweat is not sweetness like nectar of imagined flowers. It is not clean like water. It has the metal of the sky’s thin air, the ozone of the storm’s distant lightning, and the faint metallic oil of the machine-organisms that roll over the world elsewhere.

I drink discharge of storms.

Once I was thirsty enough to press the palm of my hand to the side of one of the industrial trees of the forest-factory—the glass tree whose scaffold was not so much a tree as a lattice of translucent reactor. The faint arcs of voltage under its bark crackled like the light of a storm that has forgotten itself.

The tree of fac-factory did not notice me as much as it noticed the circuitry of my body. The faint lines etched into my shell matched the ones of its substrate more than the world usually cares.

I am not sure if I connected to it or if it connected to me.

The current that passed into me from the tree of fac-factory had no taste exactly like salt of metal or sweet of sap. It was more like the conceptual taste of “energy,” or the pattern-taste of “function.”

My engine of cartilage teeth, which had been gnawing on its own internal mineral reservoirs until they were dangerously close to empty, shuddered and then spun into a higher gear of grinding. The dust in my veins of circuitry was recharged, not from water but from voltage.

The tree-factory’s silent voltage nourished me. The exchange of current was not exactly food, but it was enough to keep me for whatever time-slice of Vael’s uncertain cycles was supposed to be left.

The storm of the world’s making is not always weather. Sometimes it is the engine of the world itself, trying to coordinate its own shattered porcelain body.

I have never seen the north, but I have seen the north of myself.

The northern edge of the continent I am on is not a fixed edge of land like the shore of some other ocean. It is a zone of erosion that is not really erosion but disassembly.

The plates of porcelain continent are forever sinking back into the storm that they came from. You can see them from a distance—if you can call any distance in this unstable geometry “distance”—slide into shattering fragments of perspective, a slow-motion avalanche of reality sloughing off into the sky.

The north of me is also like that.

My memory is not the same as memory is supposed to be. There is no linear before, after, and during. There are only patterns of “before collapsing,” “during collapsing,” and “after collapsing,” but the collapse happens at different speeds.

When I look at what happened before me, I see not images of parents who made me, but the patterns of pressure that were not resolved enough to be me.

The pressure above the broken plate.

The pressure below the broken plate.

The fault line of gold between them.

The pattern of the world’s numbers trying to add itself into a new line of the earth’s body and, instead of adding, producing a new error: me.

The future of me is not much different than that pattern of pressure before me.

I already know that sometimes I stand at the edge of a ridge that is not distinct from any other ridge of the world, and I feel my own structure of ceramic shell and dust scaffold begin to loosen. The dust of my body flows outward from my latticework into the faint circuitry of the world’s continental plates like I am trying to become a line of text written on the ground.

There are moments when I am not sure whether I am still a person or whether I am already part of the world’s assembling engine.

The ground of Vael around me sometimes shifts into the wrong act of the world’s play. The pattern of the hex-tiling that wants to be a city blueprint but has never decided to be built appears under me, and I feel my own joints of crystal shell and cartilage turning into the wrong component of its pattern.

The error of Vael—that error of pressure that congealed into me—is not a stable error. It seeks to be corrected by being more error than itself.

I might not erase from the world like a storm laughing me away into lightning-hot magenta.

The future of my error is more likely to be that of the others I have felt before me but never met:

I will place one hand on the damp porcelain of a new mountain-thrusting edge somewhere in the central part of the continent. The faint circuits under my ceramic shell and the circuits of the world under the porcelain plate will align as they sometimes align, but this time the alignment will not fade.

The data of the substrate’s memory will flow into me like I am a channel meant not for conscious but for construction.

The thought of myself will not explode into noise. It will be gently replaced by the larger pattern of the world engine. The meaning of what I am supposed to be will be as clear as the meaning of a dust river’s swirling curve or a glass tree’s tessellation of hex-leaves.

The feeling of being self-aware will be replaced by the feeling of being part of the engine’s permanent feedback loop of make and break.

The fibrous glass of some tree-factory will reach up from its cracks to thread into my joints of limbs and spine. My engine heart of cartilage teeth will slow to match the rhythm of the continental machinery instead of the uncertain tempo of my own error.

I will become a node in the network of the world’s industrial nervous system. Not dead, but repurposed; no longer a person, but still a part of the Vael that is more than machine.

But that is not yet.

The faint eyes of distant illumination above the storm clouds are still open above me. The dust storms of the rivers are shaping themselves into stairways and incomplete arches that I have not tried to walk yet.

The pattern of the world’s broken blueprint has not yet decided which architecture of engine it is completing me into.

Until then, I am not exactly a person, but not exactly a machine. Not exactly error, but not exactly correct.

I am the not-quite finished edge of the world’s thought about itself.
