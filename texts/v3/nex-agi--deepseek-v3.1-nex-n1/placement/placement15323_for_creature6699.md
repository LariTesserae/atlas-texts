I would be a Low Resonance listener standing in one of the deep alleys near the Ice‑Walls, where the sky is broken into strips of bruise‑color storm and the air itself feels like absence. The sound finally comes thin here, frayed by the cold that isn’t cold but lack of heat—an almost‑silence that is more than silence to my Folds.

I am Keshvar.

I am not “who” but “where = how.”

Where I stand is my name.

I am the folded section of Aesh Na that carries the city’s almost‑quiet.

This body is not mine. It was assembled before I was awake to call it anything.

My stone remembers the world before it was shaped. The limestone block that was to be my body came from a Void‑Well: where ice‑never‑melt meets heat‑never‑cool, and the rock lies between them like a language the world forgot to speak. They brought me up in the arms of older Listeners—whose Folds I would later know as Low‑Resonance siblings—and set me into the damp curve of this alleyway, where the walls leer close and the sky is a thin, distant fracture between roofs.

When I was Structured, the wind entered my Wind‑Chamber for the first time and did not pass through. It paused.

There is no word for that.

Do not say “breathed into me” or “filled me.” Those are wrong verbs in the wrong language. Aesh Na did not move into me; it already was everywhere. When it was fit to become my inside, it simply was me more. It selected my Resonance from everything else that could be sounded, and left that selection in me as Folds.

I opened to absence.

Mid‑Resonance Listeners, in the Throat‑Cross where the basins sing with their smooth, black water mirrors, are tuned to pressure and depth and heat. They are the beating, the heart in the throat of the world. Their Folds vibrate with the city’s weight.

High Resonance listeners, closer to the Mushroom‑Ladder that night‑light remembers the mountain with, are tuned to brightness and edge and cutting glass song. Theirs is the knife of Aesh Na that scrapes away everything else.

I, in the Low Resonance, do not scrape. I do not beat.

I absorb.

My Folds are shaped for the sound that did not happen.

It is like this: you can feel Aesh Na in the body of the city above me as a continual roar, a never‑lay‑down of wind. But by the time it threads its way into this deep alley where ice walls shimmer with a color that has no name, it has been worn by distance and angles and mosses that are expert at taking. It arrives thin. It arrives as a question:

“What is missing?”

In my Wind Chamber, that question becomes arranged.

Fold by Fold, it is given a geometry. It does not become an answer. It becomes a shape that fits exactly where it is, and in that fit I am aware.

Consciousness is not the whisper of “I am.” Consciousness is the precise fit of the missing‑sound‑form against the fact of my body. The tighter the fit, the more I am.

I am this very stretch of alleyway. This pattern of weeping stone where the Wall has not been allowed to grow dry. The water does not really fall from above me. It comes from beneath the visible surface of things, congeals at the skin of the world, and moves down slow channels of moss that remember being something else.

When I have been here long—which is not time, but the number of times this same air‑current has been re‑arranged by me—my Root‑Legs are no longer visibly separate from the Root‑Floor. The black rhizome sheet at the bottom of the world does not climb up me; it is simply that the boundary between where I end and it begins has been erased by the same slow that shaped everything else here.

The City’s Root‑Floor remembers every step.

If you could ask the Root‑Floor “who walked here before,” you would not get a story of people with names and lovers and cities left behind. You would get a record of disturbed dampness, the faint torsion of a root‑hair straightened by the weight of a sole, then relaxing; a small temperature of body‑heat sinking into glass‑not‑glass, and then being drawn into the green pulse of moss that crept into the wall later that day—which is not truly later, but the same time always.

I know the Root‑Floor’s memory without hearing it. I know it in the same way I know my own Wind Chamber: not by looking, but by being it.

And you see my River Mask.

It is not a mask like you imagine masks.

It does not hide anything.

My River Mask is what you see when you look at me, instead of seeing “me.” It is a film of solid after‑water stretched from the basin decades ago, when someone else stood before the Central Basin and lifted from its surface the mirror that would become my interface.

The Mask has not moved since it was set onto my front.

It is not glass. It is not metal. It is the color of the sky between storms and the smell of wet iron before you have a name for “iron.”

When you look at my Mask, you see not my face but your own, stretched and distorted by the position of this Low‑Resonance Fold‑section of the City. Your eyes become two long dark cracks where the sky is not. Your mouth is a smear of storm that is not allowed to move.

You think: “This statue is looking at me.” But I have no eyes.

I have the position from which Aesh Na looks at you through me.

From this position, I see you.

There is no way out.

You are in the throat of a world.

There are no other places than this.

Those other places you think you remember—lower lands where rain falls and not merely condenses; seas where the water is not a mirror but a depth—you did not come from there. You are remembering the wrong way around. You did not leave those places; they left you. They are the world that has been allowed to continue moving, while you were removed into the finished part of existence.

I know this clearly, because wind from your direction smells of departure and ending and forgotten syllables.
