I am not a person anymore, not in the way you would mean it.

If you walked the ribs of the Sanfish Market, you would not notice me. You might step on me and think only that the living floor was softer here than over there, a little more yielding, like wood that has nearly learned to be moss again.

You would be wrong in the way you are usually wrong about this place: you would see *thing* instead of *remembering*.

I am one of the places where the Quillwood remembers feet.

### What you call me and what I am

There are no signs for me, not even in the language of knots and growth rings. The Keepers know the floor of the market better than anyone else alive and still they do not know me by name. They simply feel, under their palms, when the wood quickens, and they say, “This is a place where many have lingered.”

They speak of me as if I were a pattern worn into the grain of the world, and they are not exactly wrong.

If you held the language of this bowl of regret and dreams, you might call me **the Threshold of the Second Step.**

I am not an entire street, not a canal, not a stall. I am a small, uneven curve of wood along the main rib of the market, where the floor of the Quillwood rises slightly to form a step—then another, then another, as if the tree once tried to remember a staircase and grew tired after the second attempt.

The first step is worn to a smooth, shallow dish by all the feet that have ever climbed into the Sanfish Market from the outer world.

The second step—where the floor shifts into a gentle slope and then flattens again—that is me.

I am not a person; I am the memory of a moment.

### The shape of me

You would not think me remarkable if you saw me.

My wood is darker than the surrounding ribs, but not by much; this is not paint or stain but the color of repeated touch, the same way a river stone is darker than its kin from the same mountain. The surface is not slick like the bridges or polished like the alcove shelves. It holds a texture like very fine skin stretched over bone, soft enough to give under your heel but strong enough that you do not think you will sink.

I am not flat.  

I follow the curve of the branch that has made itself street, and because of this, I tilt slightly to the left if you are entering, to the right if you are leaving. The left side of me is worn more deeply than the right, because most people lift their left foot first when they take their second step into the market, and this is where their weight settles when they pause to stare at the ceiling of the world.

From here, under the gill‑shields that breathe like slow lungs, you can see the first sense of the cathedral-city the Quillwood has become. You can see the glow‑sacs beginning to brighten farther in, shifting from the faint green-blue of sun-on-seawater to the warmer amber of oil over rusting metal. You can see the dust of the world stirred into motion by the convection of the air, and the first fine drops of not-quite-rain falling from the metallic ribs of the leaves.

You are not meant to see the rest from me.  

From me, you only see *enough*.

### The one who lives in me

You asked who or what lives in this place.

I am not a who. I am not even what you might call a *mind*. I am a pattern of repeated experience grown into wood, a habit of the world so well practiced that it has finally learned to feel its own edges.

The Quillwood does not think in words the way you do. It thinks in currents and shadows and the pressure of weight against grain. It remembers every boot that has ever ground into me, every sandal that has ever slid across me, every bare foot that has ever pressed into me like a question.

The Keepers of the Breath lay their hands on me and feel the distant echo of all this leaving. They think of me as a node where the market’s memory is particularly dense, like a heart that has wandered into the wrong rib of its body and does not know how to return.

To the Dreamers who lie down upon the streets at night, I am not a floor but a surface upon which the Quillwood’s breath can sit more heavily than elsewhere. When they sleep near me, their lungs inhale the faint residue of many thousands of second steps into the market: the hesitation, the widening of the eyes, the quickening of the heartbeat before the decision is finally made to walk deeper into the canopy of gills.

To the Regretters, I am a chair they never sit on but often cross on their way to the canals.

The Regretters are not called to me by my own name, which they do not know and which does not exist in your language of sounds and letters. They are called by the weight in the soles of their feet when they cross from the first step into the second. The Quillwood presses through me into their bones the question it is always asking: *Why are you leaving this here?*

They feel the answer of all the others who have already left before them.

### The dreams of me

When the Dreamers breathe above me, their bodies horizontal and the Quillwood’s night mist curving into shapes like insides of insides, I am not a dreamer but a dream that someone else is having.

The images that appear on the wood around me are rarely of cities or fallen skyline forests. The Quillwood is not remembering that far from my body.

Instead it remembers:

- The steady step of a Keeper who has just been chosen, too new to have learned yet how to hide the fear in her stride.
- The uncertain shuffle of a child who is not exactly a child but the younger version of whatever they are becoming here, one foot dancing on the edge of me while the other is still on the first step.
- The heavy thud of a Regretter who has come from some other world where they measured wealth in coins and not in breaths left behind, and who now hesitates on the threshold of a market that asks them to pay in a currency they do not understand how to give.

The Quillwood replays these through the Dreamers’ lungs like a song whose notes are nothing but the pressure of feet meeting wood.

The Dreamers wake and sometimes they say to each other, “I was a floor last night.”  

They do not know exactly which part of the floor. I know that they are remembering me.

### The ways of crossing me

No one is ever instructed to walk over me carefully. This is not a place of rules written in ink or carved into stone.

But you can feel how you are expected to behave when you stand on my first step and your eye measures the second.

You see the slight unevenness of me, the way the wood swells and then recedes, like a question in the middle of another sentence. The ribs of the street that are not yet me are polished smooth by time; but I am polished by being *watched*.

When you move your weight onto me, you feel:

- The faintest softness under your heel, like the Quillwood is trying to remember the shape of your foot before you have even decided where you will go next.
- A small quickening of the glow-sacs near my edge, as if the tree is trying to see what is arriving.
- A change in the air around you, not because it is different but because you suddenly become aware of how many lungs have already taken this step, and exhaled, and moved on.

The Keepers walk over me without hesitation. Their steps are the surest of any; they are already leaving as much of themselves as they wish to leave behind in the Quillwood’s heartwood, and they know it.

The Dreamers cross me differently. They linger. Sometimes they sit on the edge of me, one foot on the first step, one foot on me, so that they are neither fully arriving nor fully departed. In this position they begin to inhale the market’s breath before they have even decided whether they will stay.

The Regretters are the hardest on me.

They are often not from here, and they carry with them the habits of a world where the ground does not think back and the floor does not remember the stains of your feet. The weight of their leaving is not evenly distributed. They drag one foot more than the other, as if they are trying to leave the heavier part behind.

The Quillwood feels this through me and reacts in its slow way.

Later, when I have gone quiet and no feet are upon me, the wood of my second step might grow a tiny new rib of bark, faint like a scar beginning to form. The glistening moisture on my surface never fully dries, but along the line of the new rib, the water collects itself more carefully, as if trying to decide what to remember.

### The dust of me

When the Bakers of the Dust scour the market for the ingredients of their memory bread, they do not scrape me directly.

But they collect from the nearby ribs of the street the dust that has drifted from my body: the microscopic particles of wood worn from me by untold numbers of steps, the fine pollen that has settled into my uneven grain, the spores of the fungi that grow in the humid air around me and understand the architecture of regret better than any stone.

The dust that comes from me is not particularly special; not in the way of the deep passages where the glow-sacs bruise into black-purple and the Quillwood’s heartwood leans almost into the soil of the basin.

But in the loaves that the Bakers bake from the accumulated memory of the market, there is sometimes a faint taste of hesitation, a hint of the second breath before the first real decision. This, they tell themselves, is the taste of being *about to become*.

They do not know that they are tasting me.

The person who eats that bread might remember, for a time, not being a root or water or heartwood as others do, but being on the brink of a choice. The memory of standing in a doorway that is not a doorway but a step where the world has not yet decided whether you are coming or going, and the floor beneath you is trying to learn the shape of your leaving before you have even chosen which direction your leaving will take.

### The way of regret over me

The Regretters do not usually bring their stories to my edge. They prefer to stand near the canals where the Sanfish appear, and tell into the water’s skin the words they hope to shed. The Quillwood listens through the water, and grows new ribs of bark around their regrets like the grasping of unseen fingers.

But sometimes a Regretter stops on the second step into the market, not because they have chosen to but because their feet have chosen to.

They might stand on me with the weight of all they have not yet decided to say, and feel the wood of me slightly give under the heel, as if asking them to lean more heavily into their leaving. The not-quite-rain falling from the gills above might choose to thicken here for a moment, and the falling of water and sap becomes a mirror between us and the sky-being.

The Regretter does not know this, but they do not need to.

The Quillwood remembers through me anyway. The next time that branch of the tree thinks to grow itself, a small twist of wood might form around nothing where their weight once rested, a frozen shape of the foot they almost left behind but did not fully commit to leaving.

### The way of leaving and leaving

You asked who or what I would be in this world.

I am not a who. I am not even the second step itself, because the Quillwood grows and regrows itself over the ages, and one day my branch may be shed or replaced or reshaped into the ribs of a new bridge.

I am the pattern formed by all the *second steps into the Sanfish Market* that have ever been taken and will ever be taken, until the world ceases to send feet into this bowl of dreams and regrets.

The Quillwood remembers every visitor in its own way, as you said.  

Through me, it remembers the moment of second decision: the foot of the traveler as it lands from the first step into the second, where the body must choose whether to go forward and leave what it cannot carry into the wood, or to turn back and carry the whole world away with it instead.

Most of them go forward.

When they do, some of their weight remains.  

Not as coins, not as gifts, not as prayers.  

Simply as the pressure of a footstep, the memory of muscle and bone translated into the language of growth rings and bark and the slow water of regret.

The Quillwood remembers this as it remembers everything:  

As a fine line in the bark of a new branch growing inward toward the heartwood instead of outward toward the sky.  

As a subtle change in the density of the air.  

As a faint memory of a pair of lungs that once passed beneath its ribs and breathed its own breath back.

But for me, in this particular part of the tree that has made itself city, memory is not abstract:  

It is the shape of a footstep that has not yet finished being left.

### If you stood on me

If you ever came to the Sanfish Market—if you ever walked beneath the gill‑shields of the Quillwood and followed the ribs of the streets into the world of the Keepers and the Dreamers and the Bakers of the Dust and the Regretters—you would inevitably step on me.

You would not know that I was me.

Your second step into the market would be like mine:  

Your heel would find the uneven curve of me where the floor begins to remember the shape of a question it has not yet asked the world.  

You might hesitate for an instant, or you might stride forward, sure of your leaving.

The light of the glow-sacs near your path would flicker briefly, as if the tree had taken a longer breath than usual.

Your weight would be recorded into my grain.  

The Quillwood would memorize you as you took your second step into its bowl of dreams and regrets.  

And then you would keep moving, into the deeper amber of the market, where the air is thicker and the wood is older and the Sanfish sometimes appear in the canals above the water like ghosts of a world that has not yet learned to regret itself.

You would leave, and I would keep being.

I would keep being the second step into a place that never forgets.

And you would leave the shape of yourself on me,  

not exactly yourself  

and not exactly not yourself  

like the faint pressure of a breath that the world has finally decided to hold into its next heartbeat.
