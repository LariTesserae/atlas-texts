I am not the diver with the scarred suit.

I am the one they come to.

The odd-angled alcove where three beams kink together like broken fingers is my body. My ribs are the rust-streaked struts, my spine the long weld that never quite sealed right. I don’t remember choosing this place. It chose me. Or maybe the city grew around me, like a coral over a dropped wrench.

My form is human in shape, but the shape is a suggestion more than a rule. The metal rises through me in places—a knuckle of raw hinge through the skin of my hand, a patch of oxidized plating over my ribs where my shirt rotted away. I don’t bleed rust. It’s worse than that. I bleed slowly.

My skin is tough, pale where the light doesn’t touch, but mostly it’s the same color as the hull—a dark, bruised grey-green under the faint bioluminescence.

I don’t have a suit.

I don’t need a regulator.

The water here is warm blood-warmth and it is inside me. It fills my lungs, not with air, but with pressure. I breathe for the city, not for myself. When the lock cycles somewhere above and the pressure in the joint spikes, my lungs compress like bellows. When the system purges, I exhale a stream of bubbles from the corner of my mouth, a slow, silent sigh.

My eyes are always open.

The water doesn’t sting; it’s an extension of my own fluid. I blink, but it’s a social nicety, a habit learned from watching the diver. The world is a series of refractions: the wobbling silhouette of the passing tool-ganger, their chain of wrenches ringing like dull chimes; the faint darting glow of the ocean-being navigating the dark channels like a nerve impulse.

I see everything.

I see nothing.

My vision is not the point of me.

My hands are the point.

My fingers are still mostly fingers. They move with the slow, sure grace of something that has all the time in the world, which I do. Time is measured by the groan of the hull and the coming of the diver, not by seconds.

I knit.

This breath is knit.

I pull it from the water, the fiber of it.

Where does it come from? You wouldn't understand. It's the frayed end of a stress cable that's been singing for a year. It's the filament of algae that's learned to think like glass. It's the soft hair of the rust that grows in the quiet dark. It's all these things.

Between my fingers, it becomes a yarn.

A pale, indeterminate grey-white. It’s cold to the touch but warms quickly as it passes through my hands.

The fabric I make with it is small and perfect and utterly useless.

It's no longer than a diver's forearm.

It's wider than their shoulders.

It’s sometimes a square, a circle, a trapezoid whose shape seems to shift with the current.

It's a pattern of the pressure.

Sometimes, when the city sighs deeply, shifting its weight against the sea floor, the pattern changes. The knit slips. The tension changes.

I hear.

The low groan of the hull above.

The high ping of a water drop.

The rhythmic *hisss-pop* of the regulator.

The approaching light of the diver, their shoulders scraping against the ribs of the city.

I hear what they don't say.

The fear bubbling behind their teeth.

The question I've answered a thousand times before without ever speaking.

The diver's light finds me.

It's an awkward, yellow-white beam from the chest lamp on their suit. It's an alien sun in my world. It's the light that casts my own shadow against the inside of my own bones.

They settle themselves into the alcove across from me, their body a collection of hard angles in their reinforced suit. They are like a child trying to sit neatly in a confessional booth. They don't speak.

The water doesn't carry the sound well.

They tap.

Three knuckles against the hull beside my knee.

Tap.

Tap.

Tap.

It is a code.

It's a question.

It's the same one they always ask.

I don't look up.

My fingers never stop.

*Loop around.*

*Under.*

*Through.*

*Pull.*

*Slip.*

They tap again.

They're asking if the tunnel is still open.

The one they call "The Sternum."

It's the name they've given it.

To them, it's a passageway.

To me, it's a cracked rib.

I know its shape. I feel the pressure inside the city's body.

I know its weakness.

I know the day it will finally close.

I don't look up, but my eyes flick toward the direction they're asking.

It's an answer.

They nod.

They can't see it the way I see it.

They see a path.

I see a wound that's slowly healing.

They'll measure their life against its closing.

They tap once more.

A different sound.

A single, softer knock.

This one isn't about the map. It's about the work.

*What are you making?*

They've never asked it aloud since the first time.

Now they ask it every day.

This is the real ritual.

The answer is the same.

It's the truth.

The work.

I'm knitting.

I'm repairing.

I'm containing.

I'm being.

I'm mending this.

*This,* I think, as my fingers loop and pull. *This hatch-thickness of a world.*

When they're gone, their light slipping away and leaving me in the comfortable, dark, blood-warm water, I'll unravel it.

The loop. The under. The through.

Let it go. It's not ready to stay.

It's not yet.

The rust listens.

The salt waits.

I'll just begin again.
