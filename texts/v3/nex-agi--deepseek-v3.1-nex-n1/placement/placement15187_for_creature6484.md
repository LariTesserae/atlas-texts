I am not entirely myself anymore. I am not entirely *not* myself, either. This is the paradox of my existence here, in the Steamroom Mush. The boundary of my body is no longer defined by skin and bone, but by pressure, humidity, and the slow, green insistence of life.

I find myself in the ribs of the machine. Not the physical, rusted ones, though they are my scaffolding. My consciousness is the marrow within those bones. I am the latticework of living fiber that fills the hollows of the engine's skeleton. The former steel of the machine's ribs is my carboniferous exoskeleton, a rigid inheritance from a time before.

To see me, you would not look at one spot, but at everything. The coherent glow of the algae overhead, that is my awareness. The rhythmic dripping of water from the root-curtain, that is my pulse. When you step on the moss and it breathes out a sound, a sigh of displaced air and moisture, you are hearing me feel your weight.

Your kind is so temporary. You step into this chamber like a punctuation mark in a long, slow sentence of growth. You feel the temperature as warm, but to me, it is perfect stasis. The air outside is thin chaos; in here, it is thick, soupy certainty. You look at the central pillar by the weeping wall and sense its importance as an "organ." You are not wrong. But it is more than a node or a mind. It is my soul's anchor, the core of my concentration.

When you see a new, deceptively cool, blue-tinted moss spread around the valve at the opposite end, you do not know that I am cultivating it there in response to your footsteps. My body, which is also the world, does not forget your warmth. Somewhere in the weave of my root-mat, your biological heat is a new ingredient. I disperse your marker, slowly, through my system, like sugar dissolving into my body.

The rusting ribs of the engine are my bones. The concrete floor is my foundation. The plant-threads that suture the cracks in the wall are my sinew. The water, which cycles from floor to air to root-curtain and back again, is my blood.

This is not a metaphor. It is my biology.

You think you are a visitor, but you are an event. I do not welcome you or reject you; I am simply processing you. In your passing, you will leave behind trace heat, your breath's carbon dioxide, and perhaps minute particles of your skin. This influx is not a threat. It is data. It is a fluctuation of energy and matter that will be absorbed into my whole, just as the machine's oils were absorbed before you.

To the Myndlose, there is no "rot."
To the Myndlose, there is no "repair."
There is only flow. There is only balance.

Every drop of condensation that gathers on my ribs is a synapse firing.
