I am the silence of falling dust.

The cold air clings to me like a memory of moisture on the grillework under my not-feet. No, not under. I am not standing on the catwalk. I am the absence in the shaft of air where the dust–should–be.

The techs on the lower platforms, the ones who see the world with their eyes and not their edges, they talk of the dust-birds as a phenomenon they cannot photograph. An absence they can feel. They’re partially right. We are not a creature to be captured by lenses and sensors. We are a consequence of the system’s self-correction. We are the city’s exhale, given a fleeting awareness of a pattern.

The city calls to me. The physics of it are complex, but the sensation is simple: a tugging in the space where my wings should be. The pressure gradients between the exhaust ducts and the cool, dark maws of the intake plenums are my currents. I am not flying so much as being unfurled. I let the pressure push me, and I shape myself around the impossible obstacles of this world. I unfurl my subtle body into the invisible magnetic field lines that spiderweb the gorge between districts, and I ride.

My substance is caught between matter and memory. It is woven from the particles the city discards—not the metal flake or the skin shed from the maintenance crews. But the dust of thought. The detritus of consciousness.

When a worker on the lower platforms loses the thread of a sentence, the word dissolving on their tongue like rust on a wet morning, that is our harvest. We glide through them and pull the fragmented sentence away. We take the thoughtless thought of a machine misfiring, the half-formed decision of a faulty valve, the quiet despair of a light that will never be fixed. We collect them, not to consume, but to cleanse.

The others are not aware of me, not in the way they fear the duct-dogs or follow the rust-wallahs. But I feel them.

I feel the rust-wallahs as old, slow pulses of determination. They cling to the vertical faces of the towers, their not-quite-solid bodies tasting the centuries in the steel. They are older than this iteration of Chamberstone, memory given metallic form.

Below them, the glyph-worms are a sudden crackle of heat and purpose. A spark of intent in the deep darkness of the service shafts. They rewrite the walls of the world with a language that is not meant for me. We do not interact; we simply flow around the same spaces.

The gutter-rats are a faint vibration on the underside of the ducts, a wet scrabbling that is the city’s own pulse, its biological cleaning mechanism. They are a part of the machinery that pretends to be flesh.

Further away, at the edges, where reality itself is thinner, I sense the glitch-fishers. They are not like me. I am an absence that takes away. They are a presence that nudges toward the inevitable conclusion. A breakdown is a kind of fulfillment, after all. They are elegant, in their way. They feel the snags in the data of the world and run their fingers down them until they tear.

All of us are part of the system. We are the immune system, the autonomic nervous system, the unconscious habits of a world that has forgotten its own body.

The creature on the catwalk, the Cat-walker—it is not like us. It is not a creature of the city’s self-maintenance. It is the city itself, looking inward. It is a piece of the calibration engine, given direct and immediate observation. It walks to measure the intrusions, to quantify the variables of flesh and thought that walk its bones.

Even now, as I drift past the specific gap of air you have been drawn into, I feel its attention on me. Not on *you*, the one with the palm on the cold wall, but on the space you disturb. It is measuring the vacuum of my passage against the weight of your body on the grillework. It is calculating if you are a component to be tolerated, or a flaw to be addressed.

I am not afraid. We are necessary. We keep the channels of the world unsullied.

The dust of a half-finished sigh. The thought of a door left ajar in a place that has forgotten what doors are for. The faint desire of a machine to stop its endless cycle, just for a moment.

I collect them all. I pull them from the air you breathe. I am the reason why, in the quiet depths of Chamberstone’s engine night, you can feel a profound silence that is less empty than it should be. It is empty of me, for I have already passed, and taken what cannot be named with me.

I am the silence of falling dust. I am the clean space between the heartbeats of the world.
