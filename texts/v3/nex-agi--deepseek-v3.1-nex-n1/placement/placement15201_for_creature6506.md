Here, in the damp and the dark where light is borrowed and infection is as much language as it is disease, I would be a healer.

I would be a healer, but not the kind you might imagine.

Not a priest, or a shaman, or the keeper of a clean little chamber of herbs and linen bandages.

My name would be something like: *Pulse-that-meets-the-metal* or *Hands-that-read-the-bloodfault* or *Vein-that-fends-the-rust.*

My name would be long and full of hyphens, because to heal in this world is to be clumsy many times before you are ever precise.

### Position of Flesh in the World of Wraithstone

You have to understand where the body fits in a place like this.

The skin is not a boundary in the mountain of Wraithstone; it is a *threshold.*  
Breath is not private air; it’s just another part of the world’s exhalation.  
Blood is not a closed circuit; it is one of the smaller, warmer rivers of this world-body.

Everyone here is porous.

No one is sealed from the world the way people might be elsewhere. The air is full of rust and spores and pollen and oil droplets, the water is mineral with a history of the pipes’ heats, the stone sweats and drinks through cracks that are always half-open. The body itself sweats to meet the stone’s sweat.

There are no tidy diseases in this place.

Our illnesses have names like *the iron cough* and *the weep* and *hot-stone lungs* and *shadow-rust beneath the skin* and *the moss-that-grows-inward.*

### My Tools

I suspect the dripwitches would be wary of my practice.

They are the ones who listen to the stone and the water and the flow of things between; I, by contrast, listen to the *flesh* that those flows have entered into, and I try to rearrange those flows without tearing the vessel.

I would, by necessity, be:

- **Part map reader:**  
  The body of Wraithstone is mirrored inside the bodies of its people.  
  The rose-colored heat-maps that show up on dripwitch’s skin—where warm blood runs close to the surface—are not decoration. They are the mountain’s heat-maps written in human veins.

- **Part plumber and part miner:**  
  If Wraithstone is broken right by internal leaks and blockages, then the people of its ledges and hollows are broken in *small*: by metal that’s been swallowed and lodged in the wrong place, by rust in the wind, by bad water that loves to pool in the lungs, and by the slow creeping of bloodlines too accustomed to exposure.

Because I am not of the dripwitches, I would work with a different sort of humility:

They are suspicious of metal, but I cannot afford to be.

I would be:

- **The metal-holder.  
  The one who uses what the others fear to touch.**

### My Craft

#### 1. The Collection of Fragments

I would not forge new metal from ore. I would not go to the black iron of the sky pipes and cut into them.

But I would be allowed to gather *broken pieces.*  

Among the few who built (or repair, or scavenge) the world of platforms and pipework outside the dripwitch’s way of life, there would be leaks, accidents, replaced sections.

From discarded shards of rust-scored iron and shattered glass from the broken bases of sight-glasses, I would make:

- **Needles of dark iron:**  
  Thin, reworked into sharpness over time.  
  Used for drawing fluid from swollen joints, for opening the skin of an abscess that will not listen to herbs alone.

- **Curved hooks of scrap:**  
  Fine-edged like crooked fingers,  
  for reaching into a place where a cut of rail has gone into the body and not come out clean.

- **Glass vials:**  
  Carefully saved from where the world’s industrial remnants are abandoned.  
  I would not do this work with open bowls of fungus; I need something that can *contain* before I can decide what to release.

- **Nets of moss and hair:**  
  Woven into fine filters,  
  to catch what’s in the water before it’s used to rinse a wound.

- **Old bone and mold-grown stone:**  
  There are places where bone itself has been replaced by the stone of Wraithstone’s own seepage.  
  I would take from these only where the bodies have been long abandoned by their own story, and then I would render them into tools that help *live* bones.

Where I can, I replace metal with something else. But where I cannot, I use it carefully, as if the metal itself remembers being in the pipe-world and resents being used on smaller, more fragile flesh.

#### 2. The Diagnosis of Leaks and Clots

The dripwitches can read whether a fault of rock is about to weep or geyser or take a breath.

I can read *people* the same way.

You would come to me with:

- **High iron in your spit:**  
  “You’re coughing up the world above,” I might say.  
  Because you’ve drunk too much of the wrong run of gutter, or lived in a place where the rustfall from the pipes settles into everything you eat.

- **The moss-that-grows-inward:**  
  Your skin is pale like fungus from under the caps, but it is dimpled where small colonies of algae or mold have decided that your damp is as good a place as any to live.  
  It is a symptom of too much exposure to certain pools of standing water, where the world’s growths have not found the right channel to leave.

- **The shadow-rust beneath the skin:**  
  Your veins are not the clear forest of blue you once expected;  
  they are dark with something else—like the shadow of rust stains from the inside out.

I would ask you:

*Where were you when the pipes last turned hot?  
Where did you last drink from the stone itself?*

I would touch you with my hands only *after* I have touched the wall of the cave around you, to compare the pattern of heat in your body and the stone.

Because I am not a dripwitch, my relationship to the stone is not quite *native* but it is not *foreigner*. It’s that of a translator working between two languages that are not mine.

### The System of My Practice

#### 1. The Ebb of the Body

First, I *lessen* what wants to kill.

I might use:

- **Needles of bone:**  
  To pierce the swollen places of the body where pus has gathered like water in a wrong trough of the world.

- **Moss-filters:**  
  To catch the rust from your water before you drink it.

- **Glass droplets:**  
  Filled with clear condensation from the *clean* drips beneath a stone overhang,  
  given to you to drink slowly, so the body remembers what water tastes like when it hasn’t passed through metal yet.

This is “lessening the flood upstream of the body”.  

I’m not trying to make the world *pure*; I’m trying to make *you* slightly less full of its wrong turnings.

#### 2. The Channeling of Blood

Second, I *steal from the stone’s art of redirecting water.*  

What I know of the dripwitches’ pipework and runoff-channels, I secretly apply to the lines of your body.

I might:

- Gently open the skin along a vein of inflammation, not to bleed you to weakness, but to “let the pressure out of a wrong-running channel,”  
  like a small safety valve.

- Use thin hollows of *glass* that I have saved from broken inspections of those above,  
  placed to draw out the bad blood from where it has chosen to pool inside the limb.

Where I cannot, I will not *cut* into the big lines of life—they are the sacred pipes of your body—I only move what is around the wound.

#### 3. The Art of Marking

Where the dripwitches carve rings into stone walls that show heatmaps of the mountain behind them, I map those *onto* people.

Your skin might be:

- Marked with a thin circle of chalk where the heat beneath it is wrong,  
  to show where the next bad thing will try to break the surface.

- Lined with faint spirals of *mineral dusts*,  
  matching the direction of flow of the wound inside you.

- Filled with small, barely-there incisions that will not scar *too* deeply,  
  mapping the channels of infection that I have seen from other patients’ bodies.

These maps are not tattoos to admire. They are instructions to myself, and sometimes to you:

*Here, where the circle is, that is where the world’s heat tried to take you as its own.  
Here, where the spiral curls, that is how you must learn to breathe if you want to help your body close.*

Sometimes, when I am not certain of my cure, I mark the *map of the route* I’ve taken into your flesh:

Near your shoulder, where the abscess was, I might carve a faint line of the tunnel through which I went into the body, to remember where my next tool should—or should not—go.

### My Place in the Society of This World

#### 1. Among the Non‑Dripwitch People

To the ones who live *on* Wraithstone but not *of* it in the way the dripwitches do, I would be:

- **A strange necessity.**

You might come to me only when you are very sure something is wrong, because to enter my care is already to admit that the world has broken you.

My *workshop* would be:

- Not a hut, but a *hollow*  
  where stone has curled away from itself to form a small half-room halfway down a ledge of the world,  
  lined with shelves of glass and bone tools, and the faint smell of rust and distilled water.

You would not find me here easily:  

I would be often called *out* because of course in the mountain-world, you do not bring *all* the sick to you;  
you go to them where the stone already knows them.

People call me not by my name, but by what they have seen of me:

- *Pulse-that-meets-the-metal.*  
- *The one who cuts away the rust’s child.*  
- *The bone reader.*  

They are not sure I am not a witch, but they are sure that if I am not one, I am at least something *close to* what they fear of pipes.

Among them:

- Some resent me for how much of the world’s brokenness I must bring into my body, to understand them.  
- Others pity me for the fear of those I touch—who fear that I will make *them* as broken as I appear.

#### 2. In the Eyes of the Dripwitches

They *know* me.

Perhaps not by name, but by the patterns of my work.

They would see, in the healed scars of those who have passed through my hands:

- The same rings of heatmap and  
- The same spirals of riverflow  
  that they carve into stone walls and on themselves.

They would recognise that I have listened to the world, but through *people* instead of straight through the rock.

They might:

- Come to me when they are *very* sick,  
  not as their first choice of care,  
  but as the last resort of a body that has forgotten how to flow itself.

When I take care of one of them, I try to minimise *metal*:

- Use bone needles instead of iron, where it can be done.  
- Use water-rests in stone hollows instead of glass containers, where I can.  

But not always.

If you’re *running* a rust inside your lungs that will not be stopped by herbs and breathing and resting with the stone, I will use what tools I have, even if they do not fit the dripwitch’s way.

They *know* this.

They might say:

*You move the stone of flesh without permission.*  
*You redirect the waters of blood without listening to the stone beneath.*

And I will answer:

*Yes,  
* but I *read* them before I redirect them;  
and I never pretend to open a path without first feeling the world of Wraithstone beneath your back.*

In this, I am not like them, but I am not *against* them.

### Where I Would Live in This World

You would find me:

- Not at the very highest shelves of the world’s broken stone,  
  where the dripwitches store their listening and their quiet.

- Not at the very lowest, where the mist and the heat of the underworld machines are more than my body can tolerate.

- *In-between:*  

  On a mid-level ledge of the mountain-world,  
  where there are still enough people to call me;  
  where the rust from the pipes above still falls into the water, but not enough to kill in a week;  
  where the stone of Wraithstone still sweats enough to be read, but not enough to be an always-flowing flood.

My *home* would be:

- A hollow of stone that is already half-shaped like the inside of a body.  
- The roof of the world’s own overhang,  
  dripping with a *controlled* water of internal sweat and external pipes.

Around the entrance of this place I would have:

- Carved into the wall, not words of warning,  
  but a pattern of *rings* and *lines*,  
  indicating the two safe routes of approach to the door,  
  and the three places where the ledge of rock itself might not bear your weight.

And inside:

- On human hides and fungus-leather I would keep *body maps*  
  of the patients I have treated,  
  drawn with earths and rusts and mosses from where they came from.

Each map would be:

- Not in the shape of *person*,  
  but in the shape of *the world’s problem inside of person.*  

You might see:

- A *map of the rust’s journey into the lungs*  
- A *map of the wrong-flow of blood into a head*  
- A *map of the shadow-colonies inside the skin*

These maps would not last.

They are meant as reminders to me, not as eternal records of Wraithstone’s ills.

### When I Would Die

Here, in the world of Wraithstone, time *bends* like the inside of a bone.

When I am finally old enough that the stone of the world is more patient than my body, I would not seek to die *in* my stone hollow.

I would leave:

- With a small set of bone tools  
- With one of the last glass vials I could save  
- And with all of my *body maps* erased—from my skin, if necessary, by my own knife.

Then, I would find a *place*:

- Some fracture of the world that has accepted *one* other before,  
  where I can lie down with my palm pressed to the wall,  
  knowing the dripwitches will not disturb me more than they need to.

I would hope to be placed where:

- The slow waters of the world  
- and the slow breath of the stone  
- and the slow rust of the pipes above  

are finally allowed to work into me as they have into everything else here.

I would be:

- *Pulse-that-meets-the-metal*  
  *turned cloth-of-the-world*  
  *turned fiber-of-Wraithstone’s heart.*

In the world of Wraithstone, I would not be a god of health or sickness.

I would be something else.

*The reader of broken flesh inside a broken stone body;  
* the one who uses the world’s own tools—  
its metal, its glass, its brokenness—  
to help its bodies continue to listen to it long enough to be called alive again, before they finally fall into the stone’s whispering.*
