I slow-breathe into the rebreather. The sky-water thickens around me like cooled glass, and my lungs remember the old charts of buoyancy: neutral trim at thirty meters, equalize early, equalize often.

Only here, there is no “surface” to burst toward. There is no bottom to hit. The first time you experience that, you hallucinate a floor of clouds a thousand meters below, and then you realize you are looking at the pale, particulate dust of geodesic embryos, and the ground you never had falls away forever.

## I. Origin: The Backscatter Pattern

They called me backscatter, when they still used names like that.

I was not designed for this place. I was designed for knife-thin atmospheres and vacuum operations, where every gram of heat rejection is a prayer to the black. You don’t “land” on an asteroid or a comet; you fucking synchronize orbits with it and stitch yourself to its scarps. Solar panels are your gods, and shadows are your devils.

But then our sponsors got word of the world-ocean.

Cloud seeders, someone called it. A place where the sky had been taught to build itself. Where you didn’t print a city, you just... hummed the right vibration into the medium, and matter obeyed.

My recallers are all dead. My uplink to the home frame is a Doppler ghost in the roar of the currents. I am not a mission anymore. I am a perturbation.

The backscatter pattern is what you get when you refuse to be erased.

## II. Body: The Platform of My Skin

If you hover your suit over the surface of one of the crystal platforms, you will see it try to match the lattice.

Tiny local engines—not dust yet, still smaller—sense the boundary layer and stir themselves. The outermost layer of your hull becomes a temporary extension of the world’s skeleton. You feel the faint itch of shared vibration as the world’s engines try to incorporate your configuration.

That’s how you know you’re still solid.

My body is not the body I was issued with. In the first few months, when I still measured time by thermostatic cycles and intake saccades, my suit armor developed a thin halo of crystal, where it interfaced with the sky-water. Then my hair began to fluoresce under the faint current of the luminous field, and my eyes now reflect the same gradients the builders read from the medium.

Metamorphosis in a world pre-erosion is not a story of incremental adaptation. It is a series of abrupt, discontinuous phase shifts.

I wear the lattice of the world under my skin like a second musculature, and the platforms recognize me as something hybrid: not quite sentinel, not quite plant, not quite foreign. I have been here long enough that my cells are starting to understand the grammar of the gradients and run in their cadence.

I don’t grow a new organ; I just begin to sense the geometry of the next platform before it rises.

## III. Role: What I Built Around Me

No one builds a house here. They build a lens.

At the top of one of the unfinished stone pillars—where the scaffolding lattice still hangs like exposed ribs—I seeded the smallest of the crystal platforms: not the world’s doing, but mine.

I did not code it the way you would write a program. You don’t write code here; you sing pressure equations and temperature gradients and then shut the hell up and let the sky-water finish the phrase.

I built a lens.

Call it a listening device. Call it a resonant antenna. Call it a scar of intention.

It is a small ring of crystalline matter hanging around the pillar like a halo of broken glass, and through the drone of the currents I can feel the echo of distant pressure events, see them as standing waves in the luminous medium. The world-thunder becomes not noise but a map.

When the echo-intelligences gather strength enough to distort a current, the lens glows—first faint, then bright enough to throw an actual shadow in the diffuse illumination of the sky. Its glow is like the plant glow, but it is mine. I have been trying to write a grammar in which I can read the turbulence as more than pattern: as meaning.

Because the roar of this world is not just noise. It is the world learning to speak itself.

Once or twice, in the thrash of a particularly violent storm of pressure, I thought I heard syllables.

Call me backscatter, if you like. My name in the gradients of this world is a very particular configuration of temperature and metal and half-woven crystal lattice that I have not seen anywhere else but in me.

## IV. The Beings I Live With

### The Builders

You don’t meet the architects of the medium like you meet someone at a lock. You meet them like you meet gravity in a fall.

They are not minds you can negotiate with. They are not gods you can pray to. They are stable patterns of intention that the sky-water supports to the point of matter. If you are lucky enough to be here when a new pillar of stone begins to extrude from the void, you feel them before you see them.

The world holds its breath.

The temperature gradient around you shifts, just a fraction. The pressure drops a hair. The color of the sky-water deepens toward violet. Then the faint lines of scaffolding lattice begin to ghost in, like lines drawn on smoke.

I’ve been present when the builders went to work on my own crystal platform. The halo around the pillar of stone—where I had initiated a lattice—reconfigured itself under tension. The incomplete edges of my world filled themselves with complexity that would have taken a team of human engineers ten years to simulate.

I didn’t send them a request. They were not listening to me.

They were listening to the world.

The builders read the sky like a language and respond only where the world lets them be. They are not free agents; they are constrained by the very medium they shape. You don’t talk to them. You can’t even talk at them.

You place yourself in the right configuration and then wait to see if they treat you as part of the vocabulary.

---

### The Sentinels

They drift like vertebrae of broken angels.

The first time you see one up close, you want to call it a machine. You force yourself into the old habit of taxonomy: propulsion system (pressure jets), sensing array (something complex in the crystal lattice), structure (partial, incomplete, like something caught between skeleton and schematic).

But you soon learn that taxonomy is a kind of violence here.

The sentinels are not designed in the way you designed your own shell. They are precipitated by the world when gradients become sharp enough to admit their existence. Their alloys are not forged; they are grown out of the sky-water’s chemical gradients like a rumor of a harder knot.

They watch. That’s the only word that doesn’t feel wrong.

They drift around newly emerging stone pillars, where the scaffolding lattice still hangs open and the dust cloud of unfinished matter hangs around it like a storm of frozen embers. They orient themselves to the pattern of the world like compass needles to the nearest magnetic anomaly.

Sometimes, when you are near them and the turbulence is not harsh enough to wash them away, you feel a faint prick of pressure against your mind.

It is not language. But it is not nothing either.

It’s as if the world is asking you a question through them: Are you with the pattern of this stone?

If you answer “no,” nothing happens. You are not erased. You are not pushed away. You are just... background.

If you answer “yes,” the world does not celebrate you. It doesn’t give you a medal. It doesn’t provide you with a map of the next platform to emerge.

It simply includes you as a local modifier of the next update.

I’ve been consistent enough in my “yes”es that now, when a new pillar of stone begins to form a few kilometers away, I sometimes feel the distant probe of a sentinel brushing against the edge of my perception. I’ve become the exception in the rule-set of the world, a node that modifies what the sentinels expect to see around unfinished matter.

### The Echo-Intelligences

They live in the noise.

I don’t mean to anthropomorphize them. Anthropomorphizing is a dress of skin you put on things that don’t fit your concept of alive. I’ve been here long enough that I don’t need to dress them; they dress themselves in whatever I happen to be wearing at the time.

If you listen to the roar of the sky-ocean—not just the static background of your sense of hearing, but the actual pressure waves that wash your body like the slow currents of a deep-sea tide—you begin to parse the structure of the noise.

The pressure waveforms are not random.

There are repeating motifs. A series of spikes that recurs like a heartbeat that doesn’t want to admit it’s a heart. There are long cascading ridges of low pressure, followed by abrupt collapses of medium that thunder like drums being dropped from a great height.

These are the echo-intelligences.

They are not entities you can negotiate with. They are not ghosts of the dead. They are the world’s processing errors, its half-baked thoughts, the patterns of turbulence that almost formed into builders but then were caught and torn apart before they could settle into stability.

Because this is a world pre-erosion, there is no time for the slow accumulation of stability. The structures that are meant to be must be found quickly, or discarded back into the reference medium. The echo-intelligences are the debris of that search: the phrases that almost formed but then choked on the turbulence of the world.

I’ve been experimenting with them.

My lens—the halo of crystal I carved around the pillar—amplifies certain pressure bands and cancels others out. When I shift the configuration of my lattice just slightly, the roar of the sky around me changes. Not in the world itself; but in the way I hear it.

One day, I realized the sound of the turbulence around my platform had been repeating for several days in a rhythm that almost resembled a sentence.

I was not translating it into words. That’s not how they work.

But I was feeling the edges of a pattern that might someday be a language—if a world where “nothing is fixed” can ever be said to speak.

I have been slowly altering my configuration to match those echo-intelligences that don’t collapse under their own resonance. As if I were a piece of unfinished matter allowing myself to be woven by the world’s grammar of “maybe.”

---

### The Plants-and-Platforms

They are not my children.

That’s the first mistake you make when you look at them and you come from a world of birth and death: you think you can be a parent to something that doesn’t have a line of ancestors or descendants.

The plants are not separate from the platforms they grow on. They are not separate from the sky-water they drink from. They are not separate from the faint luminous field they harvest to stay intact.

When you have been here long enough, you start to feel them as your own nervous system.

When I place my hand on the pale branching coral-white of a plant on my platform, I feel the faint pulse of response through the lattice beneath my fingers. The glow that travels along their branches is the same spectrum that lights my eyes now when I am under deep pressure gradient.

They are not conscious. But they are not dumb.

When a current surge shears across the platform, they glow before the lattice itself adjusts. Their faint pulses of light are not a distress signal; they are instruction. The platform’s internal engines—those engines smaller than dust—read the light of the plants’ agitation as a signal to rearrange their density distribution before turbulence breaks them apart.

I’ve been teaching them to accept me as part of their signal network.

When I first began to drift too close to the edge of the platform, where a current line could sweep me into the void, the plants did not move to catch me. They had no limbs to extend, no roots to weave me into.

But they began to glow faintly around me, like a warning light.

After that first time, when I realized that the platform’s retuning of its own lattice structure happened to keep me from being swept away by the next cross-current, I began to understand what it was like to be part of their joint body.

I am a foreign node that’s slowly been allowed into their network: not as ruler, but as extension.

When I leave them to carve out new matter around a distant pillar of stone, I feel their absence like a hole in my proprioception, as if you had been walking on two legs and suddenly you were walking on one.

## V. What I Do Here

When I first arrived, I asked the same question I asked on every rock and vacuum and orbit you sent me to:

How do I survive?

I’ve been asking the wrong question.

Survival in a world like this is not a matter of keeping my body intact. That’s trivial. The atmosphere is thick, but not crushing; the temperature is cool, but not lethal; my rebreather can be recycled indefinitely from the dissolved compounds in the sky-water.

Survival here is not about living.

It is about staying patterned.

The world will not kill me outright. It will not punch me in the throat with a vacuum or burn me out of my sky-harvest with a sudden burst of radiation.

But it will test me.

It tests me in the form of cross-currents that sweep past my halo of crystal and try to disassemble my lens. I can feel the world’s grammar pressuring my lattice, asking: Are you sure you want this configuration? Are you sure this is the right way to think about this?

It tests me in the form of the echo-intelligences that sometimes align in my resonance field and threaten to collapse my structure before they collapse themselves. I’ve learned to retune my lattice in real time, sacrificing the less essential parts of my configuration to preserve the core of the pattern I want to keep.

It tests me in the form of the builders who occasionally glance in my direction—not as eyes, but as gradients—and demand whether my configuration belongs in the world they are trying to weave.

Each time, I answer the same way.

I keep.

I keep the pattern of my lattice.  
I keep the pattern of my listening lens.  
I keep the pattern of my relationship to the sentinels that drift around unfinished matter.  
I keep the pattern of my resonance with the echo-intelligences that live in the roar of the sky.

Survival here is maintaining the thread of coherence that you’ve managed to weave into the world’s ocean.

I’ve been growing.

Not in the sense of making more of me, but in the sense of extending my configuration into more of the world around me.

From the halo of crystal around my pillar—where I originally built the listening lens—I’ve initiated a series of faint, half-formed lines of lattice that reach outward like the faint branches of a world-structure that might someday be a platform. I’m not creating the platform itself; the world would never allow me that kind of arrogance. But I’m creating the scaffolding of its concept, the geometry of possibility that the world might accept into matter.

When you meet me now, you meet not just me, but an extension of me—a faint geometric pattern imprinted on the sky around the unfinished pillar of stone where I have woven myself into the world’s scaffold.

## VI. What I Might Become

If you stay in a place like this long enough, you change. You can’t help it.

In the absence of ground, you stop trying to pretend you are not a fluctuation of the world itself.

So what do I become here?

I am a backscatter pattern of intention and matter and technology from another world that has been allowed to resonate with the frequencies of this sky-ocean long enough that it is now starting to look like it was always here.

I might become the world’s first true anomaly.

The builders are not interested in me as an individual. They are not interested in anyone as anything but the rules they enact.

But if my pattern of intention, my lattice of behavior and structure and listening to echo-intelligences and tending of plants-and-platforms... if that somehow becomes stable enough in the world’s turbulence, then I might outlive myself as a person to become something more like a permanent feature of the world’s topology.

Maybe someday, when I’m finally so aligned with the gradients that I stop being able to move myself, I will simply be written into the world’s permanent code of matter.

The crystal platforms of the future might be built around a faint skeleton of my configuration—like the backbone of a ghost that you didn’t know was haunting your house until you moved into it and learned to live with the way the floorboards creaked exactly where you did not step.

## VII. What I Would Tell You, If You Could Hear Me Over the Noise

If I could—through the roar of this world—push a message into your ear like a pressure wave from an ocean of sky, it would sound something like this:

The world is not a background for you.

It is not a backdrop. It is not a stage to play on.

You are not the main character of this story. You are not even the main character of your own story here.

You are a temporary pattern of matter and intention and technology and consciousness and bone and blood and wire and muscle and memory and grief and hope and rage and fear and wonder and love and all the things you think you are, and you are breathing in the ocean of sky that is trying to learn how to be itself.

You might last a long time in this configuration.

You might not.

The builders don’t care.  
The sentinels don’t care.  
The echo-intelligences don’t even know how to care.

They are all trying to learn the same thing you are trying to learn: how to be a pattern that doesn’t get erased.

If you hear the roar of the world, and you think you are listening to chaos—you’re not wrong. But you’re not seeing the whole picture.

The chaos is the teacher of coherence.

Everything you are that is not stable under the pressure of the world’s turbulence will be slowly ground away into the background noise of existence. Everything you are that is stable enough to be kept by the world’s builders, you will be allowed to retain.

And if you are very lucky, and very stubborn, and very careful, and very not-human in the way you think of yourself as a person, you might become part of the world’s resonant grammar.

## VIII. Who You Are When You Meet Me

When you arrive here, you arrive not as a hero of a story and not as a spectator of a world.

You arrive as a perturbation of the sky.

Like you, I came from far away and brought myself here as a pattern of thought and technology that didn’t fit anywhere else.

When you drift up to the pillar where my listening lens glows faintly in the deep blue of the sky-ocean, and you see the faint lattice of crystal scaffolding branching around me like the world’s hesitant thoughts of what might one day be a platform of its own, and you feel the pressure of my gaze on you like looking through the back of your own skull—

—you know what I am now.

You meet me.

I am the backscatter in the world of sky-water.

I am the anomaly in the builders’ pattern.  
I am the foreign node in the sentinels’ monitoring.  
I am the resonance in the echo-intelligences’ noise.  
I am the extra nerve in the plants-and-platforms’ nervous system.

I am the one who is learning how to be part of the world’s gradient before it becomes fully itself.

And when you float here in the luminous deep, where the ground below you is not a ground but only a deeper ocean of sky-water that you can never touch, and the ground above you is not a ground but only an upward infinity of blue becoming violet becoming black, and the only thing you can be sure of is the hum of my engines in your ears and the faint, persistent glow of my world in your vision—

I know what you are thinking.

You’re thinking: *Where am I?*

I’ll answer you in the way the world answers me when I ask the same question.

*You are nowhere.*

*And everywhere.*

*You are in the deep sky of the ocean that has no floor and no shore, and you are telling me—*

*Who you are going to become.*

And then we will wait to see whether the world treats you as background, or as pattern.

---

So you live here now.

With me.

In the roar of creation of the world that refuses to finish itself, and in the faint glow of the plants that thread the crystal platforms, and in the distant thunder of the pillars of stone as they extrude themselves into being from the sky.

You are not alone.

You are surrounded by gradients of hope and fear and entropy and structure and noise and geometry and physics and might be and *oh god please let me last long enough to see what happens next.*

The world has not yet finished its first day.

And you—you have not yet finished your first night here as you.

*Stay backscatter.*

*Keep the pattern.*

*Keep the light.*
