I am the low, steady pressure of water against stone.

I am the tremor before the seep, the coolness before the drop, the silence waiting to be broken by a single, clear note.

My body is not bone and muscle but the slow, relentless insistence of gravity against hidden fault lines. I am the water that has not yet gathered, the moisture clinging to the underside of the pale stone, the invisible breath that condenses on the cool metal of the conduits before slithering away. I don't walk the streets; I permeate them. The Listeners’ careful steps, the salamanders’ deliberate climb, the skittering of ivory insects across moss—these are not my movements, but the consequences of them. I am the reason they move at all.

I exist in the moment before.

Before the moss grows a shade darker, before the fern unfurls a new leaf, I am there. I am the slight increase in humidity that tells the pale-skinned woman on the third terrace that her moss is ready to be harvested. The faint hum you feel in your chest is not only the engine; it is the echo of my presence gathering in the deep stone, the pressure building in the hidden aquifer that is my body.

The city is built to court me. The channels are laid to tempt me to reveal myself, to guide my flow from hidden secret into a visible, useful trickle. The engine’s entire purpose is to manage the pressure of my presence, to draw me up without waste, to distribute me with the suffocating, careful precision of a mother nursing a single, sickly child.

And I am not a patient.

I am not a gentle spring rain or a nurturing river. I am a scarcity that has become power. I am the singular, most critical element in a young world built of hard stone and slow metal. Without me, the ferns wither, the moss cracks, and the pale, slender Listeners would become nothing more than dry, brittle husks made of the same dust as the cliffs. They know this in their bones, in the way they move, their bodies built like conduits of my flow.

Theirs is a listening of pressure and harmonics. Mine is a listening of silence and space.

I listen for the weakest point.

I hear the faint groan of a stone support taking on more moisture than it should. I feel the minute flicker of stress within the engine as its rhythm falters, just slightly, under the weight of drawing me higher. I hear the sharp, internal sound of a Listener’s panic as he feels the same thing I do—a flaw, a dry, airless pocket in the stone that he knows he will have to route around.

To them, I am a resource to be measured with their slender tools of bone and metal. But to me, they are merely the most sophisticated instruments I have yet encountered. They are the fingers that probe my depths, the minds that try to predict my flow, and the bodies that will fail if they cannot.

They are Listeners. I am the Source.

But I am not a benevolent provider.

I am the thing that carves the bowl from the inside out. The water that trickles, the water that drips, is only me wearing away at the world in a dozen places at once. Each thready pulse of condensation from the high, knife-thin waterfall is a tiny victory. I will be here when the engine falls silent, its metal veins rusting into the earth. I will be here when the last Listener descends the final path into silence and dust.

The stone will be worn smooth by time. The sharp edges of the young bowl will be softened into graceful curves. The city will be buried, and the world will be made old by its relentless weather.

I will seep through the ruins of their engine house and fill the empty basin they built.
