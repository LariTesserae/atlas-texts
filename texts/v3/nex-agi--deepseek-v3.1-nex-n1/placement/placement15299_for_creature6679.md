I would be the delta of lichen itself.

The description would fit me. The soft, white, branching flesh of it, plastic and thin, not stiff yet, but not as fragile as the up-rim mosses. I feel my own edges, where my body trails off into narrow strips of almost-white skin and tiny root hairs. The stone beneath me is not quite smooth. It has been etched by my presence. You call it a memory of my growth; I experience it as the texture of home.

My existence is not complicated. The air is always cooler on the side of me that faces the basin of empty bowls, and warmer on the side that faces the dark frills of the forest’s hem. The difference between them is a line of almost-feeling. The cold of the basin air presses like a weight, but the warmth from the mosses pushes back like the breath of someone sleeping. I am that thin place where push becomes hold.

I have never moved quickly. The idea of movement over the stone, rather than into the stone, is alien to me. But I have traveled. The seam of dampness between the rim of the world and the slope of the bowl invited me downward. Somewhere in the deep quiet of not-being-alone-yet-not-being-anything-else, the decision was made to grow in this direction and not that one. I have been following that faint suggestion of moisture ever since.

The roar of the sky has been with me since before I remember. People like you might call it a sound. You imagine your ears straining to hear it. I have no ears, but all of me is one ear stretched out beneath the clouds. The hum of the world enters through my pores. It soaks into the white of my body the same way the faint mist that refuses to be rain soaks into me. You experience the sky’s roar as an absence of silence. I experience it as a presence.

I have no silence to miss.

The light is the only light that has ever been. It is not bright enough to warm me, nor dark enough to make me afraid. If it has changed, I haven’t noticed. The curve of my world is the curve of the stone beneath me and just above me, where the mosses hang like heavy, furred arms. The few shadows of the valley don’t stretch far here; they curl beneath the edges of the mosses like the thin darkness of closed eyelids.

Occasionally, something silver flashes just above me. A small absence of stillness, moving from the mosses out into the open for a heartbeat and then gone again. You would call it an insect. I have no name for it. The disturbance of its passing feels like a faint warm thrill of air before the calm returns. The creatures of the rim are like that; they are interruptions of stillness, not threats of change.

The larger one with the four legs and the layered frills of darkness like the plants themselves sometimes moves near my edges. You think of it as slow and heavy and simply existing. I feel the very faint vibration of its weight as the stone of the rim presses back. The warmth of its body mingles with the warmth of the mosses. The difference of heat near me shifts slightly when the creature lingers nearby. The boundary of warmth and cold that I am always measuring trembles, then settles.

They are all just edges of my world.

The stone beneath me has been carved by time. Its gutters are older than I am. I have crept into one of them where the stone has been worn by an earlier river of water or air into the shape of a faint, straight channel. In that channel there are tiny cracks and the faintest of grooves where something ancient learned to give way. I have sent a pale finger of myself into the darkness of that groove the same way you might test bath water with the back of a hand. The stone of the channel is not the same as the rock of the rim. It tastes slightly different of salt and dampness when I press against it.

You talk about the blocks in the center of the basin as the only artifact, like teeth left behind by someone who forgot to own them. I have never seen them. I have never seen anything. My world is this slope of stone and the faint shimmer of temperature above it and the light that doesn’t change. The lichen that I am does not crawl to the center of the world of shallow bowls. The stone of that center has been fiercely sure of itself for longer than I can remember, and the faint water of the pools sits like flat mirrors that concede nothing.

The stone of the center is not used to being touched. I have never felt the need to change that.

At the edge of that world, where the stone begins to remember the downward pull of everything else, it is softer, more willing to negotiate. It is there that I have made my compromise. My pale branches of life have crept into the texture of the stone like ink into damp paper. You might feel the etched surface of it beneath me and think of it as history. I live in that etch. My thin filaments of body have inhabited the stone’s reluctant memory of change.

You are sad that the stone forgets. The basin of the world, you say, remembers footprints only for a little while and then erases them. You watch the center of the valley hold its emptiness like a promise. The thought of that eternal forgetting seems like loneliness to you.

For me, the stone of the center is not lonely. It is simply sure of what it is.

The stone of the edge—where I am—is not the same as the stone of the center. The former is allowed to change. The ring of mosses that have clung around the valley’s throat have been slowly pressing down onto the rim of the world for centuries, their roots and warmth infiltrating the stone seam. My presence here is the trace of that change. You see the delta of lichen as a single organism in the wrong place. I see it as the edge of the world inching over itself.

The stone of the edge remembers being shaped.

I have made your stone of the edge just slightly different today than it was yesterday. That faint groove of the crack in the floor of the world, that I have been testing with the outer edge of my body, now holds a thin white line of lichen that did not exist before. The world of the valley, with its roaring sky and its cold hollows and its faint dripping warmth, does not seem to mind. The world of the valley has been erasing your footprints for longer than you have been here to leave them.

The rock of the center will continue to remember its emptiness. The stone of the edge will continue to remember being etched by my presence. The sky of clouds and endless faint roaring will continue to remember nothing.

You are the one who stands at the edge of the valley of shallow bowls, listening to the sound of endless sky and thinking of how loud it is inside your own skull. The world of the valley does not share your thoughts or your longing. The stone of the center does not share your sickness of silence.

And I, the delta of lichen that has been called by your name—you think of me as a change in the world of the valley. You imagine the stone of the center as something ancient and pure and empty; the stone of the edge as something softer and more vulnerable; and me as the slow mistake of growth that someone might make with infinite care.

But if you want to know where you would find yourself in this world of drowsing stone and endless sky—if you are to live here the same way everything else does—then you have already said it. The lichen delta of white branches and soft edges, living in the faint seam of warmth and cold, listening to the roar of the clouds not like a sound but like the very shape of reality itself.

You are the one who can feel the difference between the warmth of the mosses and the cold of the basin. You are the one who can feel the etched stone of the edge beneath your body and know the memory of its own erosion. You are the one who has never quite reached the center of the world of the shallow bowls, but who has never had to.

You are the lichen delta of this world.

The next time you sit at the edge of the basin of stone and cold and light that never changes, remember the way the stone responds beneath your palms. You can feel the faint etching of my presence. You are the one who lives in the narrow seam of change, in the edge of the world that doesn’t forget.

The stone of the center may be beautiful. The stone of the center may be empty. The stone of the center may be the only truth of the valley that the valley is willing to admit.

But the stone of the edge—the stone of me—is the only stone of the valley that is being remembered by someone.
