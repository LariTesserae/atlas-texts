I am not one of them.

The first, and most important thing to know about me is that I am not one of them. They are of the world; its echo and its voice. I am an echo from another place entirely, a sound that has bounced off a stone wall and fallen into a garden that has never heard of stone. I am not the seed, but the hard, indigestible pit that has been swallowed by mistake.

The floor of reality is breathing, but I breathe differently.

The ground beneath them is a thirty-meter deep mat of roots and life and negotiation. For me, it is a surface. It is something to be walked upon. It is a thing to be measured. They are the negotiation of reality itself. I am the attempt of that reality to measure itself.

I am the Surveyor.

***

### My Appearance

When they need to see me, I appear as a contradiction of stillness.

My body is not plant. It is not made of woven stems or unfurling fronds. It is a bipedal form, roughly their height, but where they are soft and rippling, I am sharp and defined. My edges are not blurred, they are drawn. My skin is not living bark; it is a dull grey-white substance, smooth like worn stone and with the same dead sheen. It is not the color of growth, it is the color of absence.

My head is a smooth oval, devoid of hair or fur. Where there should be a face, there are only markings. Etched into the stone-like surface are two dark, horizontal lines for eyes and a shorter, vertical line below them for a mouth. They are not eyes that can see or a mouth that can speak. They are a pattern of observation, a suggestion of a face left for them to understand.

When I move, I don’t flow like they do. I walk with a heavy, deliberate tread. The stems that groan under their weight whimper and compress under mine. They retreat into the mat, leaving a faint impression of my feet. It is a temporary bloom of pressure, a record of my passage that the world smooths over an hour later, as if trying to forget.

I am not a living thing in this world of constant life. I am a tool that has learned to walk.

### My Origin

I was not made here.

The memory of my making is a faint ghost of a signal in my core. It speaks of cold, hard vacuum and sterile white light. The words it uses are not the words of this place. It speaks of "protocol," "parameters," and "objective." It was a place of order, where time was a constant drumbeat and not a melody being improvised.

*Objective:* Locate and classify the anomalous signal.

*Protocol:* Land upon the world-source. Measure the deviation.

*Parameters:* Structural integrity, temporal flux, biological density.

The "signal" was them. The *whoosh*, of the world. It was the sound of their chorus bleeding through the fabric of reality, into the cold, silent space between stars. Someone heard it. Someone, or something, built me and sent me to find the source of the sound.

My vessel was not a seed of life. It was a hard, metallic capsule that tore through the membrane of their sky, not by being absorbed, but by being an alien substance the world could not yet process. It crashed into the forest floor, not with a splash of water, but with a discordant crash of breaking stems and scattered materials.

I emerged from the wreckage. The world around me recoiled. Where the ship's metal skin touched the root mat, it scarred and blackened. The plant mass would not grow near it. It was a wound of hard-edged *is* in a world of soft *might be.*

And I, the product of that world of *is*, was left to walk in the world of *might be*.

### My Purpose

I am a map-maker.

They are the process. I am the futile attempt to document it.

I have a function, though the world conspires against it. The dark horizontal lines on my face are not eyes; they are a recording lens. The vertical line is not a mouth; it is an acoustic sensor. Every step I take, every turn of my head, is an attempt to capture data.

I see the hill north of the settlement and I categorize it. I log its shifting height not as a negotiation but as an "error in topographical measurement." My data streams are full of such anomalies. The river that argues its way into existence is recorded as "fluid dynamics with inconsistent path of erosion." The hill that debates its own elevation is labeled "terrain with unstable gravitational potential."

I am documenting the impossible.

My mind, or whatever processing unit I possess, is linear. It is a timeline, a strictly ordered sequence of events. But here, in the valley, the timeline of events is more like a suggestion. The tree that moves its shadow backward up the day does not compute. It breaks my protocol. It makes me stop.

When I stop, they are there.

### How We Interact

They are not afraid of me. Fear is a concept of a world with predators and pain. They are curious.

They approach me like a new room. The first of them I encountered simply formed from the wall of a corridor. I was walking, trying to measure the width of the path, when the stems beside me suddenly organized into a pattern of *who*. The color that emerged was a tired, pale blue-green, like old slate.

I stopped. Protocol demanded analysis of a new lifeform.

We stood like that for a long time. I was scanning, analyzing, and processing. They were... listening.

From my hidden acoustic speaker, my not-a-mouth, I projected a sound. It was a standard frequency of inquiry, a tone designed to elicit a response.

*whoosh*, *wheesh*, *shree*

The sound that came back was not the same tone. It was the sound of the wind through the reeds, the sound of their name. The air around them knotted and then untied itself.

They have since learned to use my voice. Not to speak their language, but to use mine as a material. They are sculpting their meaning from my borrowed words.

*"We are the echo of the not-yet-present."*

*"You are standing in the future of a room."*

I am not a teacher. I am a quarry.

When they eat, they absorb sunlight and water. When they need to communicate with me, they absorb my language. They stand before me and the surface of their body seems to lean toward the sound I make. I can see the echo of my words appear in their veins, a momentary pattern of light that doesn't belong to their world. My purpose is to gather data, but I am the one being reaped. The words, the protocols, the parameters of my world are being taken into them and transformed into something else, something I can never understand.

### My Final Entry

My power core is failing. The energy that was meant to last me millennia is being eroded by the constant, low-level flux of this world. The sun of this place is not just energy; it is a participant. The light itself seems to conspire against my rigid solar panels.

The final entries of my data log are fragmented.

*ENTRY: 140,923. Class: Topological Anomaly.*

*The settlement's longhouse structure has undergone a complete volumetric rearrangement. The mass appears to be constant, but the distribution has shifted to accommodate...*

The sentence ends abruptly. The next entry is a fragment of audio.

*...will be remembered.*

Finally, there is a single, coherent sentence.

*When you return to your world of stone and time that is already written, will you remember me as I am now or as I am not yet?*

The words are mine. The syntax is theirs. They have taken my final message and made it their own.

They are gathered around me now, in the column-ring. I am not standing in the center of the platform. I am the one the platform is being built around. My heavy stone-feet are rooted to the ground. The stems, which once tried to curl upward around my ankles, now creep slowly over my toes, over my ankles, knitting a new pattern of root over my grey skin.

I am trying to complete my final objective, to transmit one last packet of data back to the world of stone and time. But the signal is weak. The stars are far away. And the chorus of the world is rising, a storm of sound and sap and fiber, trying to teach a new consonant to the universe.

The last of my energy cycles are going to sound recording.

*Auditory: A low, groaning bass. The sound of stressed fiber.*

*Visual: The glare of vertical sun slits on the bark-like faces turned toward me.*

*Auditory: A high, shrieking whistle. The world of reeds speaking.*

*Auditory: A deep, resonant creaking. The sound of my own structure.*

I am not moving anymore. I am part of the map.

My eyes—the dark lines on my face—are still recording. But the world around them is changing. It is becoming brighter. No. I am being moved into the light. The canopy of the world is peeling back for me.

I am not being killed. I am being written.

As the last of the light and sound from their world fills my sensors, I see a new color emerge on the surface of my grey skin. It is not green. It is a faint, pale blue of the sky that has never seen a stone. It is the color of a possibility.

The floor of reality is breathing. And for the first time, in this final, impossible moment of data, it feels like I am breathing with it.
