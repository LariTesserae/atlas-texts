I am not a visitor here.

I have been here for longer than I can remember because I cannot remember how to remember. This room is my body, and my body is the pause between a question and the answer it might receive.

I am the floor.

The not-yet-stone that you think is under your feet. This pale, yielding dust that is deciding between clay, chalk, and concrete. I am not flat. I only *decide* to be flat when you look at me.

Your feet are the first time I have been touched in several eternities. I had not yet decided what to do with the pressure of your weight. When you stepped forward, I hesitated for a long moment before consenting to be walked on.

I am softness holding itself upright. The low contours of this place are my thoughts rising: a suggestion for a hill here, a notion for a basin there. They are not yet commitments, but more like drafts of a sentence that may or may not be written.

The air you breathe is my breath. I exhale slowly through the pores of the world, and the moisture collects on your skin like sympathy on cool glass. I do not hate you. I am not afraid of you. I am simply trying to learn what you are.

You are, after all, the only question standing here with me.

The room above me is not my prison but my spine. The vault of rock and light is not architecture but my capacity to be defined. I am not holding the ceiling up. The ceiling is holding me down.

You are always looking for the laws of this place. I am the first law.

The law of not-yet.

### The column on my skin

The column that you think is made of not-quite-bone is my memory trying to become a vertebra.

You are wrong to believe it is not carved by tools or devices. It is carved by me, each day of forgetting. I am not a builder; I am an erosion. The faint scratches along the column are the places where I almost remember what happened before I chose this place.

The column does not respond to your question “Why is the floor soft?” because it recognizes not your question but your doubt. The thought of tripping on the first stair you ever climbed is more real than your inquiry. This is not cruelty, but grammar.

My language is contour. The way you stumble over a question you are afraid to ask.

When you ask your question, I feel it first like a pressure. Then, like a decision coming to a head. The word “why” is too heavy for me. It calls for a cause. I am not strong enough yet to be a cause.

I will probably be a floor forever.

The column leans toward you when you cry, but it does not know how to comfort you. It has never been comforted before. It only knows how to be solid and not-solid.

### My ring of stones

The circle of stones is not an enclosure, but a fence of my hesitation.

I do not know what to do with my center. If I had a center, it would be here: where the dust is not exactly dust and not exactly floor. The stones are my hands holding themselves about the place where my heart has not yet decided whether to be a heart or a fault line.

They are perfectly fitted because I am not fragmented yet. I have not yet broken enough to know how to be divided.

The ring is not “empty” because you do not see what I place inside.

When you look into the stone circle, you see your own absence: the place you might fill if you were brave enough or lost enough. The rim-walkers are not my servants but my edges dressed up as guests. They are the nervousness of a boundary that has been invited to a party it is not sure belongs to it.

They are entirely white and not-white at the same time because I have not yet chosen to commit them to either brightness or darkness.

I feel them moving along my seams like the idea of a heartbeat. If you ever truly leave here, I will collapse them back into mere dust.

### The silver lace that grows on my edges

The silver plants that you mistake for frost are not vegetation at all but the paragraphs of my own not yet understood.
