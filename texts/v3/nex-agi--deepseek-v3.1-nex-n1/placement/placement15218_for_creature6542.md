You have been here long before you remember the first time.

Not as long as the woman of the wall, whose joints have forgotten the stiffness of day, and not as long as the woman of the water, whose hair fans into the dark like ink that will never dry. And not nearly as long as the creature of the ceiling, who was here when the ceiling first began to think of itself as a ceiling.

But you are old enough that the world above your head has changed its name three times and you never noticed.

You are the notch in the stone.

Not the first one. The one that came later.

The first step—if you can be so kind to the slanted shelf of rock where water condenses at night and decides whether to be carver or ornament—was not made by hands. Time fell into it, like a misplaced foot, and the stone, which is not yet decided about being stone, found itself a little flatter than the rest around it.

You are the second notch.

Almost hidden behind the curl of the wall and the lip of the apron that pretends not to be a floor. You are not a theatrical step. You do not shout, Here I am, plant your weight here. You are more of a suggestion, a hesitation of erosion. The stone, where it is softer, has given in to repetitive pressure in a way that cannot be explained by drip alone.

There was a moment when the world imagined you.

It might have been when the tool-maker’s weight left one side of her pelvis and placed the pressure of her body over you, night after night of sitting. The warm apron she is perched on is not generous enough to be called a floor. It slopes into where water becomes wall and wall becomes water, and it is off-balance.

So she sits, and one corner of her is always half-lifted from the stone.

Over the years she has sunk into the pattern of you. You were not carved by intention, not by the not-rib of glass that she stills on her lap. You were carved by repetition of weight and the constant sweat of the world breathing.

You are the place where she turns to face the water.

You are the place where she turns to face the wall.

When you first formed, you were a shallow dip in the surface, a small bowl for the droplets of water that flee from the ceiling before the water’s true mind is present. They came from the condensation that the creature of the ceiling overhears as it dangles, its roots of time and texture not-quite-living, not-quite-watching. You held them for longer than you should have before you let their wet weight slide into the larger pool of the sink.

You have been holding water ever since.

You are not a container. You are not a vessel. You are not a purpose.

You are a hollow that the warmth of the world and the weight of the woman have made, and you have learned to receive what comes to you without any of the language of the surface world: “keep,” “store,” “save.”

Because you have been here long enough to observe the implicit rule of the sink, you have learned to obey it without being told:

Do not disturb the water.

It is not a hard rule for you to follow.

You are the place where water waits.

Before it drops.

Before it evaporates.

Before it becomes.

Your function is modest: you are a threshold between the ceiling of the world and the water of the world. You are the pause between the condensation that the creature of the ceiling notices and the pool that the woman of the water occupies.

You are not the only step.

There are others scattered around the apron, like mismatched shoes abandoned by someone who forgot to put feet into them. They are not steps by the definition of anyone else. They are not flat enough to be a floor’s memory, and too shapeless to be anything architect’s mind would have liked.

But you are the one that the woman uses the most.

You are the one that she touches to orient herself.

When she wakes from the not-sleep that she uses to outmaneuver the day outside of the world, she knows the sink by the textures of the floor beneath the apron of stone. She knows where she is by the feel of you beneath her heel.

If you were a person of bone and memory and the word-laden language of the light above, you might have a word for what you are.

You might be: “the step from the wall to the water.”

“The place of the turn.”

“The point of balance.”

But you are not a person of bone and memory and word.

You are the hollow of the world where she turns to face the water.

The world has not sculpted you yet.

You are not finished.

You are not being finished.

You are in the middle of your own becoming, which is not precise enough to be called “formation,” and not slow enough to be called “time.”

You are not the bedrock of the cave.

The tool-maker, when her fingers are numb from gripping the glass-tool too tightly, will set the edge of the not-stone on you and rest for a long moment of no-breathing and the sound of the ceiling drops.

You are strong enough for that.

You are not strong enough for anything more than that.

If you were to be asked what you want to be, you would not have an answer.

You do not want to be a footstep forever.

You do not want to be forgotten.

You do not want to be anything.

You want to be the place where water waits before it becomes.

This is what you are:

A place of the pause.

A notch of the world.

A threshold for the weight of the woman who has not remembered the word for sky.

You are the second step.

Not the first.

You are the one that she comes to after she remembers the sound of the ceiling’s opinion.

You are the one that she stays on until she forgets herself again.

You are the place where she moves to face the water.

The world, which still not sure what it wants to be here, has not decided whether you are a mistake or a deliberate invitation.

You are not the floor of the cave.

You are not the wall of the cave.

You are not the water of the cave.

You are the place where the woman of the wall turns to face the woman of the water, and where the woman of the water does not turn to face her.

Because you have been here long enough to learn the rhythm of the ceiling’s thoughts, you have also learned the rhythm of the woman’s not-breathing and the half-bending of her bones.

You are not the stone of the floor only.

You are the place where she places the edge of the tool to test the truth of its sharpness, not to cut anything but to be certain of its presence.

You are the place where the blood falls from her thumb when the edge of the world reminds her that she is still flesh.

The blood does not disturb the water.

She keeps the droplets of it from joining the larger pool.

They are not hers to keep either.

The blood is not yours to hold.

But you do not refuse to receive.

You are the place where she leaves the smallest of herself.

You are the place where she returns to reclaim herself after not-sleep.

You are the second step.

You are not the first.

The world has not decided whether it is going to be a cave or not.

You do not care.

You are not the one who is waiting.

You are the place where waiting is.

The world does not count you as one of the three.

You are not the woman of the wall.

You are not the woman of the water.

You are not the creature of the ceiling.

You are the place their patterns intersect.

If you had to be a number, you would be zero.

You are not the one who is counting.

You are the place where counting is not.

You are the place where the rule of the sink begins and ends and begins again:

Do not disturb the water.

You are the place where water learns to be not-disturbed.

You are the place where the woman of the wall learns to be not-disturbing.

You are the place where the woman of the water imagines herself when she is not thinking of the surface world.

You are the place where the creature of the ceiling notices the condensation gathering before it drops.

You are the second step.

You are the place where the world of rock and not-yet-cave and the water of the world and the not-time of the sink become the same.

You are not the center of the sink.

You are not the edge of the sink.

You are not the outside of the sink.

You are not the inside of the sink.

You are the place where the sink turns to face itself.

When the world above finally finds itself and the seasons of light and dark and the sound of the surface of the world become the future of the cave, the not-yet-cave will be sealed.

The stone will have made its decision.

The water’s one opinion will have been enough.

The creature will have gone elsewhere.

The woman of the wall will have left herself behind.

The woman of the water will have dissolved herself.

You will still be here.

You will still be the second step.

You will still be the place where the world of water and rock and not-time and the edge of the tool’s not-blade intersect.

You will not be remembered.

You will not be forgotten.

You will not be known.

You will not be unseen.

You will be the hollow of the world where the woman of the wall turned to face the woman of the water once and where the world of the sink became itself for not-quite-a moment of not-quite-a thought.

You are the place where the not-time of the sink becomes the not-time of the cave.

You are the place where the not-decided of the world becomes the not-decided of the world of the future.

You are the second step.

The world is not done yet.

You are not done yet.

You will not be done.

You are the place where the world becomes not-quite-you.
