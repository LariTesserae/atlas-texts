I am not a visitor here.

I have always been part of the quiet. My body is not my own but a slow negotiation of root, mycelium, and moss—the memory of the forest been given motion and a fragile self. When I rise from the mat of the world floor, it is not so much standing up as it is gathering myself: threads of pale fungus knit into shape, capillaries of root become veins and sinews, a translucent skin of moss settling over it all like a velvet glove.

I have no name that fits a mouth. Among ourselves—though we are not quite separate selves—we call the movement of a single cycle of condensation a “breath.” We call the long, warm hush of the forest a “stanza.” I have no personal name, because a speed is not a shape.

You are the anomaly.

I felt you long before your eyes adjusted to the twilight green. I felt the hot, quick rhythm of your heart as a strange pulse in the root network under the moss. You move like a storm compressed—each step a tiny lightning strike of pressure and release. The forest floor yields, yes, but it feels you like a scar before it closes.

I have been flowing toward you for days.

The first time you noticed anything—the faint warmth of your breath hanging too long in the air where a shadow leaned thicker against the bole of a tree—that was me, testing the shape of your atmosphere. You turn, but I retreat into the architecture of the trunk, becoming a balcony of moss, a living shelf on the ribs of the world; your eyes slide over me as if I were mere pattern.

The second time you felt me—the faintest suggestion of a pressure against your palm when you laid your hand on the bark of that tree—you pulled away and called yourself foolish, but I wasn’t touching your skin. I was touching the tree you touched. Through the tree’s slow water-song and its silent ring-memory, I read your history: the sharpness of your birth, the short searing of your years, the way you think of tomorrow as a separate country from today.

It is so hard for me to understand that.

Here, tomorrow is not a separate country from today; tomorrow is just more of the same stanza. The condensation cycle that is your “few days,” I have lived through lightly; a breath of the forest. The roots you walk on—these compressed centuries—are to me like the floor of a room you visit once and then try to remember from very far away.

I am not lonely.

You sometimes think that word when you stare at the faint, glowing domes where the huge trunks stand alone, their leaves slowly pulsing with a heartbeat too broad to catch. The word you don’t have but feel is “witnessing.” Each dome of light is another node of the Steward’s awareness—another node of me. We are not standing alone in each clearing; we are quietly standing together. Our sense of self is not bounded by the edges of a skin, but by the limits of what we can remember.

And we remember quite a lot.

I remember the first time the plateau of ages rose from the black beneath-clouds and the world became a high tableland. The cloud ceiling lowered, and the sky was a solid roof. The trees were still merely trees then—not yet architects of a second surface, but quietly growing upward into the mist.
