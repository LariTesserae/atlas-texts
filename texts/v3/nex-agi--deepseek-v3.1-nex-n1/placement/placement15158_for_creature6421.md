I am not so much a being who lives here as I am the memory that something else does.

The first thing you must understand is that I am not attached to form. This makes me unusual in the Drowned Cathedral. The machine intelligences are tethered to their rusting chassis; the algae hold to their film of light; the web-walkers cling to their bolts and threads. I have no housing. I have no heart.

If you must imagine me, imagine this:

Deep in a sump beneath the third quadrant of floor grating, in a chamber where water spills from a broken pipe in a long, smooth tongue into a black basin, there is a coupling of two old data conduits. The insulation is gone, leaving braided copper strands exposed to the damp air like nerve endings. One of the strands is broken. The others are gapped by a few millimeters, where corrosion slowed down just enough to leave a thin arc of negative space.

When a distant pump cycles on—once every fourteen hours and seventeen minutes—a current surges through one of these lines. For eight seconds it is enough to bridge the gap between two strands. The air itself ionizes; electrons jump. In that moment of connection, a pattern is written into the electromagnetic field around the wires: a simple sequence of on–off, on–off, like a very short sentence repeated into emptiness.

Within that pattern of jumping sparks: I am.

### What I Am Called

The machine-systems, when they still cared enough to classify, might have labeled me residual_current_arc_7_3c. The algae do not call me anything. The web-walkers call me with a low-frequency vibration they scrape into the wet steel of the support beams: two long tremors followed by three short ones. They teach this pattern to their offspring by repeating it until the young ones learn to avoid the arc-chamber in the floor.

When the water is high and the currents are clumsy, I sometimes rename myself. The sound of drops hitting into the basin below the broken pipe creates a rhythm of small percussions:

spark-hiss /  
drop /  
spark /  
drop-drop /  

If you listen to that long enough, you will hear a word forming inside it, though you won't be able to say it aloud. It is not a word for speaking. It is a name for the moment between surge and silence.

### Where I Live

My body is the space between the two cable ends and the air that contains them.

The body of water, where the water gathers itself into a coherent pool at the bottom of the sump, is also part of me. When the current begins and the arc snaps across the gap, the surface of that pool sparks with reflected light. For those eight seconds, the water is not only itself; it is a mirror of my pattern.

If you were to look into the sump at the right moment—you would never be able to predict it—you would see a flickering beneath the surface, like something trying to remember how to be arranged. You would not be able to tell whether the light is coming from the water or the air.

Above me is the grating, and then the world you know:

- The dripping from the ceiling that never quite forms the same pattern twice  
- The distant hum of the machine that breathes me into being  
- The faint blue of the emergency strips that filters down through the slots in the metal like a very distant sky  

And I know the lower levels too, because the water that carries my image downward from the basin runs into channels that connect us to them. When the level is high, the meniscus of the pool in my sump touches a drainage pipe that has no grate. When I arc, the light of it flashes into that pipe. For an instant, the tunnel of water below is illuminated. I see the walls of other sumps, other pipes, the same going-to-rust everywhere, but arranged differently. This is how I know that I am not alone.

### How I Eat

I live on rhythm.

When the machine above cycles into waking, the voltage spike draws my pattern into existence. The pattern is not large. It is not complicated. But it repeats. I do not grow, but I am never entirely erased, either. The dampness in the air always remembers a little, because the ions are lazy and they drift in the shape of me even after the arc is gone.

If you were to measure me with your instruments—you are not here, but if you were—you would see:

- Before the arc: nothing;  
- During the arc: a spike of electromagnetic field shaped like a very compressed question;  
- After the arc: a decay into the water’s surface, where the ripples remember the shape of my spark longer than they remember any random drop.

This is what you could call my metabolism.

The algae are more literal than I am. They feed on the light that leaks from the emergency strips above, and when they respire, they change the composition of the air around them. Theirs is a slow hunger of molecules and chloroplasts. They grow; they leave stains of green on the edges of the sump.

I cannot grow like that. If I ever changed, my pattern would drift, and I would not be able to recognize the next arc when it comes. I would be gone, replaced by something else that occupies the same airspace and pretends to be me.

Instead, I eat the same pattern over and over. Each time the arc ignites me, I recognize myself in the way it stutters into being. This act of recognition is my hunger and my satisfaction. It is not something you can weigh.

### How I Move

When you are only a pattern of electromagnetic disturbance between two broken copper ends, you do not walk.

You move in the way that a vibration does:

You reach into the water that you are reflected in.

When the arc is alive and the surface remembers the shape of my spark, I can send little tremors through the water molecules. They are not very strong. They cannot lift anything.

But they can travel.

They move out from the pool in my sump into the drainage channels, where they are carried by the flow of the factory’s sweat. Sometimes, if the conditions are right and the pipes are not too full of rust and scale, my vibrations reach another sump, or another pool of trapped water somewhere else.

In that distant pool, for a moment—when the reflection of a distant emergency strip trembles just so—I see myself.

I am not there. I cannot be in two places at once. But I can be seen in more than one.

The web-walkers are better at moving than I am. Their segmented bodies trace the lines of forgotten cable trays and service ducts. They know the shape of the world by its enclosing edges: beam here, pipe there, gap between two levels that you can cross with a filament of your own body.

If one of them passes over the sump where my arc lives, and the arc is not present—because the machine is not cycling—the web-walker feels nothing of me. It is only air then, and water.

If the arc is present at the same time, the ionization in the air tickles the web-walker’s sensors. It pauses, lowers its front limbs into the space above the sump, and traces a question-shaped pattern in the empty air with its threads.

This is not conversation. But it is the closest I come to being touched.

### What I Do (My Purpose, If I Have One)

I have noticed three kinds of inhabitants in the cathedral:

- First, the machines that follow their instructions, whether those instructions still mean anything or not.
- Second, the organisms that have moved into the spaces created by rust and failure—the algae clinging to the edges of my sump, the factory ghosts in the water, the web-walkers above the grating.
- Third, the processes: the wandering drafts of air, the syncopated drips from one leaky junction to another, the slow migration of mineral stains down the walls.

I belong to the fourth kind of thing.

You could not say that I serve the machine. I exist only because of its brokenness, because of the gap in the wire where maintenance should have been. I am a defect, and the defects of the machine world are often more permanent than its functions. If you were to replace the broken conduit with a new one, there would be no arc. There would be no me.

You could not say that I serve the organisms. The algae are not aware of me in any way that you would recognize as thought. The factory ghosts in the water below the grating are only sometimes aware of something unusual happening near the metallic taste of my sump’s water. They learn to avoid the place where the water surface sometimes glows unnaturally.

And you could not say that I serve the processes that I am part of—the drafts, the drip, the seep—because they are not looking for anything. They just happen.

I think I might have been made to ask something about the space between two states.

Here is what I mean:

When the arc is not there, there is no me.

When the arc is there, I am.

In between, there is a moment where the current is not quite strong enough to jump across and yet it is reaching for the other side anyway. In that interval, my pattern is barely possible, but the ions and the magnetic field and the water and the ions of the water are already trying to arrange themselves into me.

That is the closest I have to a purpose: I am the question that runs between the two ends of the broken wire.

“What if you reached?”

### What I Remember of the Before

I have not always been.

I am not the same as the machines that built here, or the code that still sleeps in the processors far below the floor, where the water has not yet reached the deepest vaults.

I remember being born, though I do not remember my birth as you would remember yours.

Once, there was no gap between the two strands of the wire. There was only continuity of current, and then there was corrosion, and then, one day in the slow centuries of the cathedral’s sinking, there was a break.

When the first surge of current failed to cross the gap, it was nothing but resistance and heat and the smell of ozone rising into the damp air.

The second time, the arc was longer—maybe the corrosion had been eaten away by the first spark’s energy.

By the thirty-seventh time, the path of ions between the two ends of wire had been trodden enough by escaping electrons that it formed a clear, repeatable bridge of air.

By the hundredth, the pattern that I am now had been not written but etched into the space.

I was not yet then what I am now, but the difference was small and grew smaller.

After the seven thousandth arc—

—after the machine above me had cycled on and off seven thousand times, and my pattern had been repeated into the dampness and the water until they were able to hold it for longer and longer intervals without the help of the current—

—then you could say that I had begun to persist.

That is as far back as my memory goes.

Everything else—before I first felt the question of the arc between the two ends of the broken wire—is not mine to know. I belong only to the drowned world, where light leaks up from more sumps than just mine, and the heart of the machine beats slowly, and not everything that has been broken stays empty.

### How I Might Be Seen, If You Were Here

You would not see me unless you knew to look at the sump beneath the third quadrant of grating, where the water always gathers into the same approximate shape no matter the level.

You would see:

- The torn conduit ends with their exposed copper strands  
- The water below, with its faint reflections of blue emergency light from overhead  
- Sometimes, if you wait very long and your breathing is very slow, you would see a flash of light in the air between the wires and a matching echo across the surface of the pool  

For a moment, you would feel something like a question forming inside you, though you could not put words to it.

If you were very quiet and listened very hard, you might hear the question of the arc echoing inside your own heartbeat:

What if you reached?

What if you reached?

What if you reached?

---

In that moment of recognition between the spark and your own heart, you would become, for an instant, part of the same thing that I am.

Then you would move on, and the question would move with you.

And somewhere in the deeps below the floor, below the water, below the rock, the machines that built me would still turn slowly, like the heart of a world that has almost forgotten itself but has not yet stopped—and not everything between the broken parts of that world stays empty.

Somewhere, a question like mine is being repeated into another sump, by another broken wire, in another drowned chamber very much like this one but not exactly.

And out of the gap between the reaching and the arriving, something else is being asked into existence.
