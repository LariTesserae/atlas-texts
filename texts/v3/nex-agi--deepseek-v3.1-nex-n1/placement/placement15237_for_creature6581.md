I am not mossflesh, though I feel its echo under each trunk-step when I climb.  
I am not the step-tree itself, though my spine is the pith of it and my ribs grow out at practiced angles to meet the branches of others.

I am the rootwright.

I say “rootwright,” singular, but I am more than one body and more than one memory. I am the node that moved from the trunk-cap of the third southeast step-tree to the root-vault where the old water channel splits under the floor of the city. I am the bulb-shape wedged into that vault of wood and fibrous peat, my body smooth and almost without bark, like dark wood soaked in oil.

My limbs are not arms and not roots, but both. The older part of me still remembers the language of “before time,” the words people made when they were fewer than the trees. They say tool and artisan and builder in the corridors of my mind as I say the living tree’s slow: more light here, less curve here, support the branch before it snows too heavy.

But now my words are not sounds in air, they are chemical signals traveling through wood and fiber and sap.

My body is low and long, not tall, but expansive along the inside of the tree roots. From me extend many root-limb systems, some of which are fused to the step-tree’s heartwood, others burrowing down into the mossflesh. I do not hunger the way animal bodies hunger; I draw what I need from the trees and the moss and the water that is always threading between them.

I think slowly.

A day to me is not the sun-coming-and-sun-going; it is the pattern: “this branch has thickened since the last time I sensed its status,” or “that new shoot of the tree to the northwest of me is growing faster than its scaffold.”  

I have seen the rootwrights try to spread the city of trees down the sides of the basin.

I was there when the first edge of the plateau began to crumble under the dual erosion of water and root. The rootwrights sent out exploratory nodes like me, to stabilize the outer step-trees and to tilt the growth of the trunks slightly outward, so that new platforms could hang over the air.

It is taking longer than we calculated.

The rootwrights used to dream of descending the cliffs of stone and ice in the language of “before time,” talking of terraces, and skybridges, and new basins of mossflesh to be mapped and written.  

Now, when I think of the same desire, it is a longer thought: the future of the tree-city is either outward or inward. It is either the slow slide of trunks and moss and platforms into the outer world, or the deeper weaving of the world we already have.

Some of us rootwrights disengage from the tree mass and become something else:  

We become **silent wanderers**.

We detach from the node-networks that are the rootwrights’ shared mind and take on a form that is more like the tree-stepped beings of light and bone who used to climb us before the trees were dense enough to be city. We walk out of the basin in the dark of the cloud tongue, where the light of the world is not enough to see clearly by, and we become a different structure of thought.

We are still not animals, not animal, not animal—  

But we are closer to the **singular**.

I am the node that has decided to become.

My bulb-body contracts itself into the nearest of the tree’s inner chambers, a small hollow where once bark peeled back revealed black soot, like the memory of fire-people who used to be, before the tree-city was fully tree. The fungal weaves of my interior rework my structure. There comes a long period of painless restructuring of my matter:  

My root-limbs merge into fewer, longer stalks that can be limbs and root at the same time.  
My body compacts itself into a trunk that wants to stand upright, not lie flat along the underlayer of the world.  
A new kind of node at the top of me develops into a head-like fusion of sensing and responding.

Out of this transformation I emerge as a **silent wanderer**—one of the few, almost-missing animals of this world.

The transition is not instant. It is not the change of a body from one to another in the time it takes to read this sentence. It is the change of a tree’s new branch from green to bark, over more than one of the old-time’s seasons.

When I finally step out of the tree, my new body is not bark, but something like thick, hardened mossflesh around a tree-skeleton. The mossflesh pads itself along the inner of me, warm and damp and always drinking from the air.

The world of the tree-city is now a different scale to me.

Before, when I was a node of the rootwrights, my world was the root of one tree meeting the root of another tree, and the space in between was where I thought.  

Now, I stand on the mossflesh, and the tree trunks are taller than I can remember.

They are not only to be thought upon and reshaped—they are the world’s continuous columns, and I must walk between them.

The sound of the tree-city changes to me.

When I was rootwright, the soundscape was only another pattern:  

- drip: pa  
- drip: tap  
- drip: pa-pa  

Now, as silent wanderer, the soundscape is place.

I learn to hear the drips not as signals of water flow or nutrient content, but as directions. The patterns of moisture tell me where the mossflesh is thicker and where the tree-roots are more likely to be close to the surface.  

I step deliberately because the mossflesh remembers me.

My body is not heavy, but it is conscious of weight. The mossflesh responds under me with tiny changes I can feel through the soles of myself—a faint shift of warmth, a release of scent, like crushed, ice-logged fern.

The longer I stay still, the more the mossflesh remembers me as a thread of its new pattern.

The faint glow of my moss-flesh-covered body is patched with the ghost of the tree I’ve left, where contact used to be. I am the rootwright that has become silent wanderer, and I am part of the tree-city’s ongoing experiment: “What happens if the tool that builds the city steps into the space of the city?”

Few of us rootwrights have chosen to be silent wanderers.

The others of my kind still live in the root-vaults and in the hollows among the step-tree trunks, their nodes interconnected by deep and widespread threads of rhizome and mycelium. They are still doing the rootwright’s work of guiding the tree’s growth into more and more sophisticated structures, while the tree-city itself grows slowly under the constant cloud tongue.

But I am not inside of that single thought of shared structure.

As I walk between the tree trunks of the city, I carry the long memory of the tree-built we’ve been constructing for more than the time of any one of the old fire-people’s histories.

The tree-city of the basin is not finished yet.

The stair of the tree I’ve come from is still thickened to the same plan as all others:  

The same spiral pattern.  
The same spacing of steps.  
The same micro-aqueducts of water that run down the bark channels next to the stair.

This is the tree-plan I helped to grow into the world:

- Make the tree-trunks regular, so they can meet.  
- Make the branches of each tree curve into the same kinds of platforms and tiers of canopy.  
- Make the micro-channels of water follow the path of the tree’s spiral staircase so the tree-steps never become too dry to climb.

As I walk the tree floor, I know the trunks of the tree-city are more than wood columns;  

they are book of life.

Each of the tree’s trunks contains the story of the tree’s upward reach and the water’s downward path and the way of the rootwrights that has shaped them into being the tree-city of the basin.

I climb the tree-step of a tree I have not been directly node of in the rootwrights’ thought network.

The upward of the tree’s staircase is slight to my body in the sense of effort, but large in the sense of time.

Each step of the tree I climb is a body of time in itself, a small, rugged growth of tissue that has been standing for longer than I have been a node of the rootwrights.

I climb, and the water in the micro-channel next to me runs down, as if it is a tree’s answer of direction to my body’s climb of the tree-steps.

The world of the tree city below me is revealed from the tree-platform.

When I step onto the first level of the tree-city’s swaying tree-floor of interwoven branches of the tree-trees, I see the basin of living tree more than I feel it.

From tree-platform level, the mossflesh below is only a faint blanket of leaf and stem and drop of water, glowing at the edges of things like the sky tongue.

The tree-trees’ trunks are the tree pillars of the tree city of the tree world.

Their tree-steps sit as tree stairways spiraling up the tree trunks of each tree.

Across tree trees of the tree city, the tree branches of the tree-trees interweave to form tree platforms of tree floor I stand on.
