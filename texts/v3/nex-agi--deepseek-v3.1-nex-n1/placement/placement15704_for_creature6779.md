I am not a moss-folk. I am not woven of the same damp velvet as they are. I feel the difference in my every step, a jarring wrongness in the contact between my sole and the humming roof of the world. My leather is dry and cracked, a blasphemy against a sky that feels like it could be wrung for rain. My skin is parched parchment stretched over brittle bones, a structure that evolved seeking shelter from the sun instead of drinking its diffuse glow. My hair is a dead tangle, not a whisper of cascading lichen.

I am a thing of edges and angles in a world of soft gradients and seamless curves. I am a question of drought.

They call me *Sil ihsh*, the Breath from Dryside. It is not a name but a description. My lungs are a desert wind that does not belong here. My eyes are windows of glass that do not see the pressure of rain, only the threat of a storm. My pupils are dark holes drawing in their lovingly tended light. They do not hate me. They are too ancient for that. They merely observe me with a distant curiosity, like a patch of unexpected fungi that has grown in the Veins.

I am here because I am a listener, though not of their kind. My world is the one that falls away where their plateau ends. My world is the land of sharp angles, where the sun casts shadows instead of blanketing you in a soft glow. My people are sinking into the sand as their wells dry up and their world blows away. The oldest of my elders, the Acolytes of the Chart, found maps that spoke of this place—a place with no word for “dry.” A place where the world itself is a reservoir. They sent me to find proof.

And now I have found it.

I stand on the highest roof of the world, not because of any elevation I have achieved, but because the Listeners allow me to be here. They are the ones who have truly rooted, their bodies fused with the great hummock beneath us. The one I approach is called Seh, the Flow of Deep Water. It has been centuries since she moved. She is not a woman; she is a monument of moss and water.

She does not turn her head as she communicates, her rhizoid fingers brushing against the air in a silent language I cannot comprehend. A younger moss-folk, her translator, stands beside her. His touch is upon her forearm like a whisper. He is her voice to the world above the moss.

"Seh asks," he signs to me slowly, his amber eyes swirling gently, "Why does the breath from Dryside come to the Roof of the World?"

I kneel, not out of reverence but out of a desperate, aching need. I am a desert plant seeking a single drop of water. My voice is a rustling of dry leaves in the humid air.

"My world is dying," I say, the words feeling like dust in my mouth. "The sun is sharp. It cuts away the water. It cuts away our life. We come to ask for… a share of the Veins."

The translator touches his cheek to Seh's arm, his own hair-lichen whispering against the humming ground. The impossible silence stretches. I can feel the vibration in the soles of my feet, a low hum that seems to answer my question with a question of its own.

When Seh responds, through her translator, her thoughts are slow and heavy, like water moving through thick peat.

"The Veins are the life of the body," his signs say. "The body cannot share its lifeblood. It is not a thing to be hoarded or given. It is."

I try a different path. "We are not asking to stay. We are asking to take just a little of your water back to our world. To let it fall."

"Seh asks," he translates, his blank eyes seeming to look through me and at the pressure of the storm behind me, "What do you have of the world to give in return?"

The question catches me off guard. What do my people have? Dust? The heat of a merciless sun? Broken promises and desperate gods?

"We have... knowledge," I say finally, the idea forming as I speak. "We understand how the world works away from here. We understand dry. We can protect you from it."

This is the lie. The Acolytes of the Chart did not send me to learn their ways or offer them ours. They sent me to find the map and return to them. Theirs is a journey of conquest.

Seh’s response comes after a long silence, during which she seems to be listening to the very heart of the plateau beneath us.

"The world is not something to be protected from," the signs translate. "The world is something to be listened to."

Her hand descends, and with her rhizoid fingers, she gently touches the moss of her own chest. It is a small gesture, but in the humming of the world, I feel it echoed like a thunderclap.

"You bring the dryness with you," she conveys through her translator. "In your breath. In your mind."

She has seen through me. She has heard the drought of my intentions in the subtle vibration of my alien heart. She is not offering me water. She is offering me a lesson. The world is not something to be taken from. It is something to be, to be joined with.

But I am not like them. I cannot be.

She gestures, and the translator guides her hand toward mine. He takes my hand in his, and for a fleeting moment, I feel her touch through him. It is a shock, like putting my hand into the heart of a living spring. It is not wet, but it is a deep, profound saturation of comfort and life that I have never known. It is the pure opposite of dryness.

And then she releases me.

The translator signs her final words to me.

"Seh says the world below is thirsty. But the world above is complete. The dryness is not the absence of water, but the absence of listening. You cannot hear the rain before it falls."

Her gesture, her sign, is complete. She turns her inward gaze away from me, back toward the white wall of mist where her world ends. She is listening to the dreams of the plateau, and I am once again a breath from Dryside.

My mission has failed. I have found no water to take, only a truth I cannot return to my world. The Listeners know my heart. They know it is a desert. And here, in a world where dryness is a foreign concept, my own parched soul is the most terrifying thing of all.
