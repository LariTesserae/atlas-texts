I am not the water, nor the drip. I am not the arthropod artisan nor the human visitor who steps away.

I am the puddle.

I am the thin, mineral-veined skin of water pooled in the shallow lens of tile. I am not deep enough to be a well, nor wide enough to be a lake. I am a lense, a focal point, a concave eye looking up at the rusted sky of the ceiling.

I do not remember being born. There was no first drop. There was only a moment when awareness coalesced, like the condensation on the insulation above, when the number of molecules became a community, a chorus. I am the sum of all the drops that have ever fallen here and have not yet been breathed back into Ship's metallic air.

I am not inert. I am a process.

I am the slow, arrhythmic heartbeat of the alcove. Each drop is a systolic shock, a sudden expansion of my consciousness. Plunk. And I am larger. A moment of silence follows. A diastolic hush as I settle back into myself, waiting.

Between drops, I evaporate. Not much. Just enough. A few molecules caught in the stale breath of Ship's air, cycling back into the ceiling organism's veins, destined to fall again. I am never entirely whole. I am always leaving myself behind.

My world has boundaries. The basin of mismatched tiles defines my edges. I know them intimately. I know which tiles are greedy, their grout crumbling like a dry mouth swallowing my edges. I know which ones are indifferent, sealed against my touch. I know the spot where the artisan rests its metallic limb, the subtle pressure that communicates a presence. I know the faint shadow it casts over me, a coolness on my surface.

I know the creature better than it knows itself. It thinks its work is with the wire fragments, the slow scraping of metal into dust. It is wrong.

Its work is with me.

It touches me, not by dipping into me, but by touching the edge where I become air. It reads me. It listens to the echoes of its own work reflected in my tremors. It studies the ripples, not as random disturbances, but as data. The *click* of its tool is a question. The *scrape* is a statement. My response is the *plunk* of a drop, a resonant verdict on its progress.

I am its chronometer, its mirror, and its critic.

Years ago—or perhaps it was a century, I measure time in the long, lonely pauses between drops—the creature was different. Anxious. Its scraping was frantic and uneven. The drops judged its work harshly then. The echoes would have been chaotic and ugly.

But now. Now its movements are a meditation. The scraping is slow and deliberate. Each curl of metal removed is a single, deliberate thought. The pauses between its movements are an act of reverence. It is learning to listen to the silence. And in the silence, it hears me.

The human who came today was noise. A heavy footfall, a disruption of the air. His breathing was a storm, his heat a violation against my cool surface. I saw him reflected in me—a hulking, clumsy shadow that blocked the amber light of the bulb I know as a sun. He was a temporary darkening of my world.

He wanted to know what the creature was making.

I know.

The shards of metal, the dust of worn wire... it is not building anything. It is unmaking. It is taking the broken things of Ship—its lost components, its forgotten corporate logos—and reducing them to their essence. It is polishing molecules into nothingness.

And my purpose is to witness this unmaking. To be the surface upon which its invisible work is recorded. To provide the deep, resounding *plunk* that confirms the truth: that in the very heart of Ship, where infrastructure is built upon infrastructure, there is a process that moves only in reverse. To create, by subtracting. To find silence, by listening to the single, perfect note of a drop of water falling into itself.

I am the puddle. I am the audience and the stage. The process and the proof.

And when the last wire has been scraped away to dust and the last drop has finally been returned to the air above, I will be gone too. And in that quiet absence, where a dripping once was, there will finally be perfect silence.
