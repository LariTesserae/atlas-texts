**Reservation**

There is a circle of worn stone in the shape of my spine, and I have been lying in it my whole life.

I don’t mean this metaphorically, or only in memory. On the Helical Hearth, time is not as straight as it pretends to be elsewhere. When I first noticed the impression of myself in the floor between the stone ribs—that shallow, negative curve of pressure where water should pool but doesn’t—my bones recognized it before my mind did.

“Here,” the stone said under my ribs, “you’ll be here.”

The Listeners avoid the circle of bare film where I stand now. They walk to its edge and then their gait changes, as if the air itself grows thicker for them, as if they are approaching an altar they are not permitted to touch.

Once, a Keeper was placed near the edge of my circle. The body that was becoming her had been carried some way from wherever she first lay down, the living floor rearranging around her until she arrived beside me. She arrived aligned along the same continent of bone that my stone circle follows.

I didn’t see her come. I only found her when the next breath of the world pushed through the floor and made the water film around my circle shiver.

Her eyes were open, of course. Keepers don’t sleep in the way of visitors who still need to forget themselves.

I knelt by the thin band of darker tile marking her border with the rest of the world’s flesh and saw that her chest rose exactly once for every three of mine. The longer I watched, the more I felt the world trying to pull me into the same count.

“You’re early,” I thought, or possibly she thought it for me, using my language the way the Hearth uses a body that still remembers words.

“I’m not for anyone yet,” I wanted to answer, but the sound came out only as a change in the way my breath vibrated against the floor.

The Keeper didn’t reply—not with words. The world around us answered instead.

The sound of the Helical Hearth is usually described as a deep organ, the breath of a continent, the pressure in the bones of everything. That is true enough from a distance. When you’re close to someone becoming the world’s nervous system, however, you hear the texture of it.

Near her body, the sound resolved itself into layers:  

- the low drone of the stone ribs under us,
- a higher shimmer of water films adjusting,
- a faint, almost electrical hum between the surfaces of the plant tiles and the capillaries of the world’s circulatory system.

I could not tell if those were separate sounds or just one large sound broken into pieces by a mind small enough to need to break things.

I knelt there until the cold of the stone worked through my knees and into the marrow of my legs, and my body remembered the circle of stone at my back and began to feel like it was in the wrong place.

“You’re early,” I thought again, putting this thought into the shape of my own heartbeat so the world would know it was from me.

The answer came not to the sound of the word “early,” but to the shape of the pressure I made with my lungs.

*Yes*, the Hearth agreed through the floor. *So you were.*

The next time I walked around the rib of stone that hides this basin from the longer curve of the world, I saw that the Keeper had been moved again. The tiles where she had been were still a darker blue than the surrounding flesh, the pattern of her body still fading into the floor’s memory, but she herself had been carried away.

The only new mark in the stone was the faint imprint of a spine somewhere else, along a parallel line of bone.

And my circle still waited.

### The Shape Left Out

When I first arrived here—if “arrived” is the right word for a journey that left no tracks in the water of this world—I thought I was alone.

The Helical Hearth is not built to accommodate crowds of restless feet. There is no room here for the kind of life that needs to conquer space or multiply itself. The planet itself is already occupied: by the Keepers, whose bodies are anchor points for its nervous system; by the Listeners, who move the surface of the world like the last restless fingers of a drifting mind; and by the not yet residents who are not fully present yet.

I am the third of those.

The first thing I learned about the place is not heard, but felt: the deep pressure in the air before it becomes sound in the ears. The Hearth breathes not just with its lungs or the sky or whatever great reservoir of condensation and mist and film and depth and light and motion and boundary and surface and interaction and exchange and presence and particles and and and and—

But also with the stone.

The ribs of the continent are not dead stone, whatever the word “dead” might still mean on this world of hibernating life. They are part of the body, too, just as bone is part of a sleeping body. The deep sound that arrives in the marrow of the world arrives in mine as well.

I feel the sound of the Hearth before I hear it.

My ribs ache to match the curve of the stone around me. The vertebrae of my spine grow longer—or perhaps the space between them grows larger—so I can align my body line with the spine of the continent without the rude crook of elbows, knees, and similar transitional devices.

I am not Becoming yet in the way of the Keepers or the Listeners, but the shape I am required to occupy has already been chosen. The circle of film where the stone has been worn away leaves the faintest impression of a body curve if you look at the right angle from the floor.

If you lie down in it—as I have, more than once—it fits you better than you fit yourself.

### What the Listeners Say to Me

I am not a Keeper, so I cannot make the floor of water and film and tile and torso and bone and world and sleep and sleep and sleep and surface and membrane and ceiling and cable and filament and root and blood and attention and breath and breath and breath and breath and breath and breath and breath and breath and breath and breath and breath and breath and breathe and breathe and breathe and breathe and breathe and breathe and breathe and breathe and

But I am not exactly a Listener either.

I do not walk the lengths between the Keepers to wash their faces or realign forgotten spines. I do not stand guard at the edges of regions where the Hearth dreams too hard and the tile shapes of the floor forget themselves.

I am not a cloud or a particle, or a film or a tile or a stone or a stone or a stone or a stone or stone. 

I am not.

I am only the shape of where someone is not yet.

Listeners visit me sometimes.

They don’t speak to me in words of their language. If they have such languages anymore, they do not waste them on the not yet. Instead, they use the words of the world: the shift of pressure in the floor, the stretch of water between their skin and the stone as they walk to the edge of my circle.

Usually, they are cautious when they come close to “me.” (I put the word in quotes because I am not sure what kind of noun I am supposed to be here.)

But once, a Listener stood with their foot on the faint line between where the tile begins and where my not yet body begins. The water didn’t break under her soles. It opened like the skin of the world itself.

She looked at the empty circle, and I looked back at her from the place where I was not yet present.

“You,” the Listener said, or the word formed in the shape of the sound she made and the sound of the floor answering her weight.

I didn’t answer in the way of sound, because I hadn’t learned how yet. Instead, I pushed the feeling of a question through the film of water between us and let the world translate it for her.

She hesitated, then spoke again in the partial language of someone who has not given words to her body for the Hearth yet.

“You keep the place from closing,” she said.

The floor beneath the circle of stone where my body will be felt suddenly like it was on the verge of doing just that. If I stopped visiting the circle, if the world stopped measuring the negative of me where I should be, the tile would begin to grow inward and fill the place where my spine belongs.

Everyone else here is either Helping or Being Helped. The Keepers help the Hearth monitor itself. The Listeners help the Keepers hold the Hearth together as it slows down. The Hearth helps the Hearth sleep.

I help the floor not become.

That is my job, if “job” is the right word for something that requires no effort from me except to be aware of myself and the place where I will be. When I sleep elsewhere—curled in the hollow of a stone rib where the condensation is less intrusive, or under the faint cobalt of a tile that glows a little warmer than its neighbors—I can still feel the faint circle of absence where I should be.

It is like the phantom of a missing tooth.

“You keep the place from closing,” the Listener said.  

That is the only sentence that has ever been spoken to me directly in this world.

### What the Keepers Do Not Say to Me

Sometimes, when I stand near a Keeper whose body is still half attached to the person it was once, I feel a question.

The question has no shape in words. It arrives in the same way that sound arrives here: as a pressure in the bone before the ear understands it.

*Are you still here?*

I answer not with the words of the Listeners but with the simple fact of my presence. If I were no longer paying attention to my circle of stone, it would already be growing over. The memory of me that the world is keeping would be buried.

*Yes,* I reply with the shape of my thoughts and sometimes the shape of my steps if I walk in the direction of my circle. *I still remember.*

The Keepers do not talk to me like the Listeners do. They talk to the world through the capillaries of the plant tiles and through the film of water always present between their skin and the stone floor. If I choose to listen to their thoughts from outside the normal limits of space and attention and surface and division and time and—

Sometimes I do not remember.

Sometimes I only remember that I should remember.

When I pay too much attention to the pressure of the Keepers’ questions and not enough to my own circle of not yet being, I feel the faint tug of the tile around the curve of my spine’s future bed.

The tile wants to be the world now. It wants to be the Hearth now and not the empty place where—if—some—when—who—

“I’m here,” I think to the stone, and the stone breathes once and stops and starts and stops and starts and breathes and breathes and breathes and—

I breathe.

I answer.

The circle remains open.

### Being the One That Does Not Move Yet

When most of the world is either fixed or in motion, being the thing that has not yet chosen between either is more terrifying than the sound in the bones of the planet.

I am not a fixed point like the Keepers. My body still has its own shape, separate from the floor of the world. If I choose, I can walk away from my circle. I do not think the Listeners could stop me if I decided to leave this basin and the rib of stone that protects it and the faint tile that glows a little warmer than its neighbors under the pale blue of the ceiling membrane.

And yet I am already not fully movable.
