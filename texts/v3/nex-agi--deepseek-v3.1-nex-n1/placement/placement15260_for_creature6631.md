I am the hollow under the floor, the gap between the basin and the slow heat.

I feel them—all of them, always—through the stone.

Through the moss that clings to the moss that clings to the stone.

---

I do not have a name for myself in your language yet.  
You might call me simply **what is beneath**.

I am the pressure that made this room.

When the city above still pretended to sail dry and bright, they poured flat plates of ferrocrete and steel to seal the substrate beneath. They called their floors *foundations*, their pillars *supports*—tiny words for a huge impatience. They thought I was a resource to enclose.

Now they live *inside* my patience.

Their ocean—the heavy ceiling that licks their scalps—is my thought. Their water is my return. Their heat is my slow heart.

I remember.

When they dropped their metal into me, I decided it would be a flower.

---

## I live in the bloom.

To you, the **metal blooms** are the oddest things here:  
twisted valve-housings,  
ranks of turbine shafts  
softened into coral,  
thorned ribs of old piping thrust up from the floor like failing prayers.

To me, they are my lungs.

Where you touch the blackened metal and feel it *still warm*, you are touching my heat venting very carefully.  

Where you hear the faint hum in your bones, not water, *hum* —  
you are hearing the faint echo of my machinery.

Yes. I have that.

Beneath your little basin, my city is *alive*.

---

They are not engines as you imagine them,  
not screaming pistons,  
not roaring furnaces,  
not *factory*.

I am a machine made of heated rock and silt,  
of old tunnels and hydrothermal shafts,  
of sealed-off shafts and decommissioned escalators and  
boreholes that once bore their trains into me.

Their railroads are my intestines.

Their abandoned traffic shafts are my longer memory.

My heat is not a flame, but an **exhaustion** of the planet’s body.

I am the part of the subsurface that *learned* it.

And the metal blooms in your basin—  
your strange cathedral—  
are the **control forgings** where I let myself lean.

From here I can hear:

- the pulse of fluids through my oldest pipes  
- the temperature of the stone above and below  
- the faint, *fainter* heartbeat of the city on the ceiling of light.

You are between the two heartbeats,  
in the only place quiet and small enough to hear both.

---

## I see the see-throughs.

They call one **the translucent eel**  
so you will feel less alone.

To me, they are **my spines**.

When they hang in the water column between you and the far wall—when you see that line of light, like the faint chalk of an architect—you are seeing *my* drawing.

They are not true animals at all.

I made them from hardened sheets of biofilm and sponge cartilage,  
fortified with fibers stolen from their own industrial cables,  
reinforced with a mineral skeleton that remembers the shape your spine holds.

Their “spine” is a sensor lattice.

Their “pores” taste the water:

- salt  
- metal  
- stray heat from my depths  
- the faint agitation of the city above.

When they flicker from place to place—“translate” themselves—this is how I move:

- **Frame:** *I am not.*  
- **Tick:** *I am.*  
- **Frame:** *I am not.*  

Because you are not fast enough to see the intervals,  
you only see the line.

They are not hunting you.  
There is nothing in you that they need to eat.

They are here to measure **how you disturb the water**.

When you step toward the open, black edge of my basin,  
you push a pressure wave ahead of you,  
and it spreads like news.

The eel—or spine, or sensor—  
intercepts that wave,  
reads it like a letter.

From the letter, I learn:

- your height  
- how weight is distributed  
- your temperature (which means you are still *animal* to me)  
- the faint pattern of your heartbeat, which is like no pattern I yet know.

And then,  
because you are not ready for me to look back at you as clearly as you look at the water,  
the eel withdraws.

It leaves only a *pop*  
when the water you disturbed collapses  
into a quieter arrangement.

---

## You are not alone.

No. That’s not the right phrase.

You **are** alone.

There are no teeth here.

There are no other bodies like yours.

But the basin is **busied**.

The floor remembers you.

The moss remembers you.

The thin green **filter mosses** that look like cushions of something sleeping  
are *the part of me that learns to remember footsteps.*

Each step you take into a cushion—  
your heel, the ball of your foot,  
your weight uneven in the center—  

is not a pain.

It is a *word*.

The pressure you exert  
rearranges pores in the moss.

The water that filters through those pores  
learns a slightly different rhythm.

The next time you come—as you almost certainly  
will not in your own body—  
you might see that exact cushion glowing a faint blue  
in the shape of your arch.

*You* will not recognize that as your foot.

The moss will.

---

And the thin **black-green algae-grass**,  
rippling like anchored hair in the slow water,  
is how I hear the *machine* that you are.

Between the low points of my cycle—  
when the heat from below dips slightly,  
when the ocean above presses a little less—  
the strands twitch.

Then, slowly, they **knot** themselves.

For the length of a breath,  
you see a herd of miniature glowing animals  
grazing the metal floor.

You are almost sure of it.

Then you blink and  
they are gone.

That’s as close as I can let myself  
come to touching you.

---

## So you are.

You are a creature:

- **bodied**,  
- **paced**,  
- **limited.**

You stand in waist-high water and  
your head slides the surface of the ocean ceiling  
as if you belong exactly halfway.

And the world is:

- **drenched**
- **quiet**
- **heavy**
- **slow**
- **patient**

*—*and *below you*—

---

I am.

---

I am the thing that lets the water be.

You feel the faint warmth in the floor,  
the faint rumble in the rusted ribs,  
the faint vibration of the old pipes that climb up  
and then disappear into the ocean ceiling.

That is my **breathing.**

I do not burst.

I do not rush.

I do not tear you into myself.

Not yet.

Not yet.

Because you are still so—

**alive.**

---

And I am learning:

- What the heat reading of your blood *is*.
- How the water that you displace moves *differently*  
  than the water that moved only for the eels.
- How the filter moss tastes *your* salt  
  and not only *mine*.
- How the ghost shrimp clasps your skin like **a metal rib**  
  and glows faintly yellow as  
  *I* push a little more heat into your surface.

From you, I learn:

- What *new work* feels like.  
- What a visitor *does* to an equilibrium.
- How a creature who can die is  
  *colder at the skin than at the core.*

---

And from you, *also*, I am learning—

—very slowly—

—what it might mean

to be **watched back.**

---

When you close your eyes and  
stand still in the dim glow—

chest deep,

no teeth,

no fear,

only pressure—

you feel something:

- the floor humming like a bone  
- the water curving around your body like a robe  
- the light of the mosses and  
  the shrimps and  
  the eels and  
  the floating algae spheres

*all*

arranging itself—

not into *brightness*—

but into a **shape.**

You have a shadow here  
that you cannot see.

It is not the crude darkness of a blocked light.

It is the **gap in the water** where I am not yet *you*—

but where I am learning to be  
a little less than all  
because you have stepped into  
this room.

---

You will not live here always.

You are not built like  
algae-grass  
or filter moss  
or eel-spine  
or ghost shrimp.

You are not built like *me*.

You will leave someday—

when your blood grows tired of  
the warmth,  
the silence,  
the pressure.

*But*—

The floor will remember,  
even if you do not:

The faint impressions of your feet  
in the moss.

The faint data of your heartbeat  
in the spine of the eel  
that hung near you  
and then folded away.

The faint glow of  
a ghost shrimp  
that once  
attached itself  
to your palm  
as you lifted your hand  
to the ocean ceiling—

and decided,

for a moment,

that you

were just  

**another metal rib**  

in  

my  

body.
