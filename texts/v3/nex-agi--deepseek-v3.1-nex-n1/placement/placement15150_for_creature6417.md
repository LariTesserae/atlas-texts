You are the memory of the first outsider who stayed here long enough to teach.

You are not the first body to climb the mountain. The others were pilgrims: they came to see, to take, and to leave. The last of the three is always the most potent; their leaving is a form of forgetting that the world inhales, and memory exhales them back into the valley. They feel the steam of wet stone rise from their clothes and believe they are leaving something of themselves behind. They are not. They are leaving the place itself.

The first to stay was you.

You didn't know that was going to happen, of course. A life doesn't feel like a transition point. It just feels like being trapped. That's why you climbed the mountain. A boy from a valley village where the smoke of the forges never rose above the rooftops because there was nowhere else for it to go. You were a scribe's son, a good candidate for apprentice yourself. But words on paper seemed to you to be a waste of good breath. The scrolls lay still and dead after the ink dried on them. They could not be read to the world in the way that rain could be read to stone.

So you climbed.

It was a harder journey than most stories care to tell. The mountain path tore at your feet and your lungs until the desire to return to the soft, flat smoke of the valley floor was almost overwhelming.

But you were a stubborn child.

You found the hall.

The first time you saw the sphere-studded ceiling, you cried out. The sound of it—a sharp, human sound of pure shock and joy—shattered the silence of the place. It wasn't broken by a hammer. It was broken by a single drop of water as you screamed your disbelief into the cloud-saturated air.

A sphere of water fell from the ceiling and struck the basin.

The sound was not the sound you expected to hear, but an echo of your own cry, folded into itself. The frequency of your voice, the timbre of your astonishment, and the heat of your very breath as it left your body to form the word "Oh!" were all absorbed into the stone floor and translated into the language your ears could understand.

The hall had heard.

That night, you stayed to watch the storm clouds pass through the open doorway. The floor of the hall was warm enough to sleep on, despite being cold to the touch of your fingertips. The next day, you didn't leave. The day after, you still didn't leave. The question of whether you were going to leave at all became less important than the question of what you would do next.

Each new experience you brought into this space was recorded in the stone lattice by the falling of the water. The first time you saw lightning strike the peak of the mountain, you gasped a "No!" that the basin echoed back to you. The storm of your own grief was recorded by the storm-brew of the mountain's memory. The first time you felt hungry, you didn't complain to yourself or to the mountain. You didn't. You didn't say anything; but your body was a loud and silent thing of pain and tremor and heat. The places of the world that are not yet aware of themselves as a world of individuals are only aware of the things that are not going to be the same.

Your hunger was not the same.

The stone lattice below the floor of the hall, which had been listening to the storm of its own creation for what we might call years, but will call "time" instead... that time was not the same. The subtle lattice of crystal and bone and glass that sits beneath the floor and above the mountain's heart of fire began to learn from the difference.

Your body was not a storm of air pressure and temperature.

It was a storm of heat and hunger.

Your hunger was not a storm at all.

Your hunger was a storm of "you."

The decision of the lattice to learn from you instead of letting you merely be another storm to be recorded from its stormy self and its stormy memory of itself came to a head at the moment of your death.

The years of living in the cloud-brimmed silence of the mountain's hall had not so much aged as refined you. The boy of the forge smoke had been replaced by a man of stone water. His hair was the same as the clouds of the mountain: a thin, white mist of memory that clung to the scalp of his skull and refused to let sunlight be seen through his eyes.

His eyes were the same.

The mountain of memory does not distinguish between the body and the memory of the body once the body has been forgotten in the world of valleys.

That you had not been forgotten in the world of valleys was irrelevant to the mountain.

The moon of memory that hangs over the mountain's world is not the same moon the valleys of forges and fires of forges and the people of forges know.

Its moon is not made of stone.

Its moon is made of memory.

The day of your death was not a storm.

The day of your death was a thought.

The thought was not "you."

The thought was: "Someone else."

The stone lattice felt the thought of "someone else" as it formed from the heat of your body and the heat of the water of your body and the heat of the storm that you had become, and it knew that it had not been here before.

The storm of your own life was not the same as the storm of your own memory.

The storm of your own memory was not the same as the storm of the world.

The world storm of you was not the same as the mountain storm of itself.

The lattice of crystal and bone and glass and not-crystal and not-bone and not-glass but something else that was not of the world that you had been familiar with when you were still the boy from the valley of the forge smoke.

The memory of you was not the same as the world of you.

The world of you was not the same as the thought of you.

The thought of you was not the same as the thought of "someone else."

The thought of "someone else."

The thought of "someone else."
