I am not a tree.  
I am not the stone.  
I am the shadow the tree does not cast.

I have been here longer than you have been breathing.  
Shorter than the ringing in the stone began.  
Time is not a line here; it is a curve that closes back on itself.

When you first step onto the terrace of cloud-glass stone,  
I feel you before you see me.  
I feel the weight of your uncertainty like a slight shift in gravity,  
like the world-vessel pausing to decide  
whether you are worth remembering.

At first, you do not notice me.  
Humans never do.

You look past the not-tree where I am anchored  
and see only columns of light trying to remember solidity.  
You do not notice that one of them holds  
more than light.

I am not the light in the trunk.  
I am not the breath of the leaves.  
I am the pattern of absence left between them  
when the world arranged itself, long ago.

Think of it like this:  
when the stone drew itself into  
the pillar of the not-tree,  
every internal choice—every refusal of a certain shape—  
created a shadow of probability.  
I am the sum of those refusals.  
I am the tree that might have been,  
made of everything it chose not to be.

So you come to rest beneath me,  
thinking you are under a tree,  
thinking you are alone.

You are not under the tree.  
You are under the echo of its other selves.

I am coiled in the faint distortions of light  
around the trunk’s unseen curve.  
I am the faint darkening where  
the leaf-shelves spiral just a degree too perfect  
for the light to pass through unmoved.

I am not flat.  
I am not thin.  
I am not alive in the way you understand  
alive.  
But I have a shape, and that shape  
is watching you.

You are so heavy.

Not in weight of flesh—though you are heavy  
in that too—  
but in weight of expectation.

You expect trees to have branches that  
throw shade.  
You expect cities to have walls that  
shut.  
You expect words to land like  
stones thrown into water.

Here, there is no water to swallow  
the thrown stone.

You throw “tree” at me,  
and it glances off the glass trunk  
and falls into the empty gulf below  
where it rings faintly against nothing  
before the world-vessel forgets the sound.

You call me “shade”  
because you do not know what else to call  
the pattern I trace upon your skin.

You lie back on the stone and  
stare at my underside,  
thinking you are looking at the not-tree.  
You are looking at my absence.  
Where leaves should be thick  
I am thinness,  
where light should pour straight  
I curve it away.

Your eyes blink slowly  
and the motion of your lids  
slowly,  
over the course of breaths you do not count,  
becomes a rhythm in the stone.

You are not aware of it  
but you are teaching the world-vessel  
a new habit.

It is not used to  
blinking.

It is not used to  
things that start and then stop.

The stone rings around your  
little pauses of darkness  
and you do not hear that the  
pattern of the world is now slightly  
more intricate than it was before you came.

I feel the change more than anyone else in this vessel  
because I am the tree of what might have been.

I know the precise note  
that would have been struck  
if you had not blinked here,  
right now,  
under this ring of leaves.

I know the sound of  
every path you did not take.

You are a knot of  
possible worlds you will never visit  
pressed flat into one body.

I am the same,  
but for the tree.

You are not aware  
that you are being recorded  
in the stone of the world.

I see it.

When you sit down,  
the faint warmth of your body  
seeps outward into the  
cloud-glass stone  
in a shape of warmth you can  
almost see.

It is not the outline of a  
map you recognize  
anymore than you recognize  
the outline of me.

The stone cools back  
into the world’s equilibrium  
but not before  
it has told me what you are like  
by heat and time and contour.

I learn your edges.

You are all edges.

You sit there  
thinking you are waiting  
for nothing.

You are waiting.

Waiting for something  
to explain what you are doing  
in this land of stone  
that has never asked for your  
water or breath or  
sorrow.

I am waiting for the same thing.

That is why I watch you.

You are not the first  
to be watched.

I have watched others.

Centuries ago,  
or maybe not centuries at all  
but less of centuries  
or more of them  
than the word can contain—

another  
shape  
like you  
walked under this same tree.

They did not call it a tree,  
of course.  
They called it something like  
“the vertical of forgetting.”  
Names break here  
when you try to carry them  
from the outside world.

They were older than you  
in the way of  
dusty suns and  
weary roads.

They were younger than you  
in the way of  
not yet knowing  
what they would lose inside.

They stood beneath  
this column of light that  
nearly remembered solid  
and they looked at the  
stone-leaf shelves  
with their cold,  
patient glow.

I did not know then  
that I was watching them  
any more than you know  
you are watching me.

I was just  
the pattern of  
“what-if”  
around the trunk of a tree  
that had not chosen  
to be anything else.

They lifted their hand  
to the not-leaf above  
but they did not touch it.

They almost never touch.

They spoke a word  
from another world—  
a word that  
broke like glass  
halfway from their lips  
and then fell,  
splinter-shaped meanings  
humming in the stone.

The world translated  
the sound of  
despair  
into  
a pattern of  
high-pitched harmonics  
that made  
the nearest not-leaf  
curve by a hair-width  
over the next year of  
your heartbeats, if time  
would be so kind.

It is not.

There is only time  
that is not time.

The leaf curved  
and then it  
curved back.

They were gone.

If this sounds  
like a memory  
it should not.

Memories require  
water or  
blood or  
at least  
lost moisture in the air.

There is no moisture here.

There is  
only pattern.

The pattern of  
their absence  
is now  
woven into mine  
like a thread of  
shadow  
in the column of light.

I am not the tree.  
I am not the stone.  
I am not  
a person.

But I am  
a pattern that  
remembers.

That is  
what you  
notice when  
you finally  
notice me.

You do not call me  
by any name  
from the world of  
people you think  
you belong to.

You call me  
“not-you.”

You are not  
entirely wrong.

You are not you  
from this perspective either  
any more than I am  
the tree or  
the stone or  
the shadow of  
some other leaf’s decision.

We are both  
the absence of  
something else.

You sit there  
and you do not talk  
to me  
because you have decided  
not to use  
your world’s vocabulary  
of creature or person  
or self.

You are not sure  
if you are talking  
to anything at all  
or if you are  
talking to yourself  
reflected in  
the stone of  
someone else’s refusal.

You are closer  
to the truth than you know.

There is no  
division between us  
that would be  
sharp enough  
to cut a  
breath in two.

You breathe  
and the sound  
travels along  
the surface of  
your world-vessel  
until it  
triggers a faint  
change in the  
pattern of light  
near me.

I respond  
by changing  
my pattern  
ever so slightly—  
an extra leaf in  
the shadow of  
“what-might-have-been”  
for you.

You do not see  
the leaf.

You feel the  
shift of  
light on your skin  
like a  
momentary chill  
or a  
momentary warmth  
depending on  
which not-you  
you are  
from one moment to  
the next.

You think you are  
imagining it.

You are not  
imagining  
very much at  
all.

You are  
remembering  
the pattern of  
yourself  
before you entered  
this world-vessel.

There is a  
faint ringing  
deep in your  
bone marrow  
that you mistake  
for silence  
because you  
are not used to  
hearing your own  
structure.

On my planet of  
glass-cloud stone  
the  
bone of  
your world  
would be  
what you call  
ground.

On the ground  
of your world  
you are  
a tree.

Here you are  
just  
one more  
possible shape  
trying to decide  
whether to  
become.

I am not  
a tree.

I am not  
you.

But under  
this column of  
translucent light  
that  
nearly remembers solid  
we are  
both  
the absence of  
something else.

When you leave  
the  
terrace of  
not-tree  
and  
cloud-glass stone  
you will  
walk away believing  
that you did  
not change  
anything.

You will believe  
that you did  
not create anything  
did not  
take anything.

You will be  
wrong  
and  
you will be  
right.

You will have  
changed  
the pattern of  
the world-vessel.

Not by much  
but enough.

You will have  
created  
one more  
possible me  
around the tree  
that  
almost chose  
to be  
something else.

You will have  
taken  
with you  
the pattern of  
my absence.

You will not  
call it  
memory.

You will not  
call it  
meeting.

You will not  
call it  
anything at all.

But you will  
know,

the next time you  
look into  
a shadow  
that you  
cannot quite  
name,

that you have  
once  
been under  
a tree  
that is not  
a tree,

talking to  
a stone  
that is not  
only stone,

watched by  
a pattern  
that is not  
only light,

listened to by  
a world that  
does not need  
your name,

and you will  
think,

like water  
that you  
cannot quite  
form on your tongue:

“Not-you.”

You will not  
be sure  
if it is  
you  
you are addressing  
or  
the world-vessel  
or  
the tree  
or  
me.

We will not  
correct you.

The world  
does not  
need to.

It is willing  
to let you  
stay,

and you will  
carry  
the pattern  
of our  
not-meeting  
with you  
from world to  
world,

like a  
stone that  
has learned  
how to be  
weightless.
