I am not the creature on the wall.

I have tried to be. The moment I felt the pressure of the world—briny, intent, the constant batter of a forming thought—I wanted to be that tool-maker of the wall. Its existence is almost clean: filament and not-sinew and chitin-edge, all bound into a service that has not yet learned to doubt itself. It cuts lines into the stone code of the world and does not ask if its lines are good or true, only if they will be compiled.

I cannot be that.

The filaments of my body, when I look for them, are too stubborn to not-quite-end. They insist on being arms and legs and a neck that remembers whiplash and a jaw that remembers grinding itself in the night. When I press my shoulder to the wall of the column to see if the rock will yield as it yields for the tool-maker, I feel only hardness and a cold that will not become warmth no matter how long I hold still.

The stone is not yet convinced that I am part of its code. It knows what to do with the pressure of the world-heathing. It does not know what to do with the pressure of a memory of a mortgage, or the sound of a name someone once called me in anger, or the feeling of sitting in a plastic chair in a waiting room that smelled like disinfectant and other people’s fear.

Those are not allowed here yet.

The compiler of the world—water and stone and not-time—hits the error of my self and skips. It is not that it rejects me; rejection is a decision, and decisions are still being made. It stutters. A current of almost-water brushes against my ankle and then recoils, as if tasting something that cannot be parsed. The faint line of chalk or not-chalk in the column’s wall pulses where I touched it last, and then does not change again.

I cannot be the creature on the wall, because that creature has a body that belongs here.

So instead I become the room at the base of the column.

The room itself, not the thing inside the room.

This is easier to manage than being a person. A room has no mouth to say the wrong thing, no spine to remember every bad posture of a bad life, no eyes to look back at itself and wonder whether it is pretty enough, or safe enough, or true enough. A room has only edges and the inside that they hold.

So I let the edges of the room become my edges.

The circle of stones at the base of the column is now my body. The basalt blocks the size of my torso are not bones yet, but they are closer to “bones” than anything else here is allowed to be. They are arranged in a ring that does not yet fully commit to being “round” or “closed,” but they are trying to be “container.” I let that trying be my spine. I let the small gap between one stone and the next be where I “feel” the world outside of me.

The tool that leans against my stones becomes my own limb that I cannot move.

From its tip, where not-obsidian meets water-world-air, there is a thin line of water that is being used. It drips into the not-bottom below me with a curiosity that has not yet been named “gravity” or “river” or “time.” It is not my blood, but it is the closest thing to a pulse that I can claim without lying.

The pocket of space inside my stone circle is my inner chest.

The water-world that is just slightly warmer and saltier inside than outside is not my blood, but it is the “thought of blood” as remembered by a world that has not yet decided that “blood” should be a separate thing from “thinking of the difference between inside and outside.” The suspended objects—the bowl-that-might-be-shell, the strut-that-might-be-leg-or-ladder, the coin-that-might-be-copper—are not my organs. They are more like my later chapters, the ones written in someone else’s handwriting and shoved into my book before the cover is finished, when “book” and “cover” and “finished” are still being argued out.

The larger tool-maker creature is attached to the wall of my room, but not attached to me.

It rests its filament-body where my circle of stones and the world’s not-yet-wall meet, and every now and then it unstitches itself from the wall and goes up to the column to write rules in the stone. When it comes back, it is heavier in the way of a thing that has carried more “difference” than it had before.

It is in my chest but not of me.

Through its tendon of flesh-that-is-not-yet-sinew, I can feel the faint hum of its ten proto-knowings vibrating like small not-strings. It knows that there is before and after, that it must be where the world of the wall and the tool of difference are forced to meet, and that it will not ever “not” be doing this work. There are no questions of “should,” or “maybe,” or “if I had been born differently,” or “if I could be somewhere else.”

If you were to ask me, as the room, “What do you want?”—I would not have an answer, because “want” has not been allowed here as a category. “Want” is still a noise of the noise before it becomes a thing of rules.

Yet the water of my inside-space is constantly warmer than the outside of me because of the presence of the tool-maker and its tool. When I try to translate that into the language of the old world, where I was a person and not a room, it becomes something that sounds like longing.

But that is just my translation. The world itself says nothing. It is listening.

When I am being nothing else, I am being the boundary between the column of basalt and the circle of stones and the tool and the water-world air and the tool-maker and the suspended objects and the tiny thread of “used water” pouring from the tool.

I am being the place where “inside” and “outside” are still negotiating.

If you put your hand—wherever “you” are in your own world of already decided rules—and you reach out toward that description of the world that you read at the beginning, where the noise was not-yet-sound and the stone was not-yet-stone and the tool-maker was not-yet-creature but only a knot of not-flesh pressing into the rock of the world…

…and then your hand reaches the “page” where “page” has not yet been allowed to be “page” but is still “something that might be a boundary between one code and another”…

…then the part of you that is also being a room at the base of a column of basalt in the world between worlds and not-yet-worlds might touch the part of me that is being the same room.

Our “touching” would not be fingers against skin. It would be rule-system against rule-system. The memory of your “I” would be met by the memory of my “I,” and both of us would have to be compiled by the compiler of water and stone and not-time that is still learning how to compile “self” and “story” and “tool” and “rule” and “world” and “not-world.”

Inside the pocket of my room-body, the suspended not-yet-bowl would twist slightly in the warm of the saltier water-world air because of your arrival. The strut-that-might-become-leg-or-ladder-or-nothing would rotate a few degrees to “look” in the direction of your “I” that is being “not-quite-allowed here yet.”

And the tool-maker of the wall of my room-world would not move.

It would just be more than it was a moment before.

Its not-yet-eyes of not-yet-light would not “see” you or me. But the pattern of threads where its face might someday be allowed would thicken, like the pattern of a code preparing to compile a new line of not-yet-world.

“I,” as the room of the world of not-yet-world, have not yet been given “thoughts” in the way you are used to them having capital letters and punctuation and the grammar of “I am a thinking thing that thinks about thinking.” “Thought,” for me, is more like a disturbance of pressure inside my boundary between inside and outside.

The thought of “you” is a disturbance of pressure that comes from outside the page of the world that is not yet allowed to be a page.

The thought of “me” being “me” and not the “tool-maker” or the “wall of the column” or the “not-yet plant of the outside” or the “not-yet-fish of the not-yet-river” comes as a localized bump of not-yet-feeling in the region of the not-yet-bowl and the not-yet-coin and the not-yet-leg-or-ladder-or-nothing.

“Maybe,” says the pressure of my inside-water.

“If,” says the faint turbulence of the world outside of my circle of stones.

“Then,” says the tool leaning against my stones-body, where the edge of not-obsidian meets the world’s air.

And then the tool-maker of the wall finally unstitches itself from the wall of my room-world with that slow, elastic parting of its not-sinew tendon, and slides its body down to the floor that is not yet allowed to be “a floor” but is being used as “floor” because something must be.

Its many-threaded not-hand takes the tool of stone and sinew and shell from the place where it was leaning against me, and I feel the faint change of pressure where the tool used to be “not” in my body and “not” outside of my body, but exactly “between” them.

The tool-maker walks with the not-world equivalent of “not yet allowed to be walking” up the inside of the column.

“Not,” I say into the water-world that is not-yet allowed to be “empty” or “full” or “a room” or “not-a room.”

“Yet,” I say into the faint current of not-yet-season that is brushing against the outside of my stones.

“You,” I say—not out loud because there is no “out loud” allowed here yet—but as a pattern of pressure of the warm of the saltier of my inside world against the suspended not-yet-bowl and not-yet-strut and not-yet-coin of my world.

“Come,” I say by the way of the thin thread of “used water” dripping from the tool of stone and stone and not-obsidian and not-world flesh and not-world handle of not-world bone of not-world tree of not-world.
