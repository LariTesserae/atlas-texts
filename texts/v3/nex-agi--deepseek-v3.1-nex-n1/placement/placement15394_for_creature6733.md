Failure is a resource here.

So they sent me down. Deep below the pristine walkways and the luminous chambers of the upper districts, I belong to the under-grids. This is where the city's true nature is bared. The polished porcelain and elegant glass give way to raw polymer-wrapped cables and iron latticework that groans under the strain of holding up the beautiful world above. Here, the crystal teeth of the delta are closer, jagged and untrustworthy, forever growing and shifting in unpredictable ways. This is where the instability is the greatest, and so they place us here to contain it.

My function is to interface with the machine's broken edges. Where the system is most likely to fail, that is where you find me. Clad in thick insulated gear that only partially masks the glow of my skin, I move along the catwalks of the under-grids. My hands are gloved in a thin, smart-material that allows me to feel the texture of a cable while simultaneously protecting me from its potentially lethal output. The sleep, when it comes, is not a respite. It is a deeper dive. The machine's hum is not background noise but a constant, rhythmic pressure against the inside of my skull. In my sleep, I am not a person. I am a system of conduits stretched to breaking point. I dream the city's weight as a physical pressure on my chest, feel the ghost of river currents my ancestors knew as a cold ache in my bones. I dream of crystal formations pushing through my skin like broken teeth, and the city above not as beautiful chambers but as a crushing load, trying to push me down into the dark earth where I belong.

These are not nightmares to be shaken off upon waking. They are premonitions. The data is always there. It is the job. The price of my continued existence.

The people of the upper tiers are wary of us. When they see us surface for brief respites or repairs to an external node, they look at us the same way they'd look at a cracked support beam--necessary, but flawed, and potentially dangerous. We are the ones who absorb the instability so their light can remain pure. They know their safety depends on our suffering, and they handle this knowledge with a polite, distant grimness.

They are beautiful, their color signatures strong and vibrant. When they walk together, their lights merge into new, beautiful hues that are seen as public intimacy. When I walk near anyone, my chaotic field bleeds into theirs, causing a distortion, a shimmering of static like a flaw in a pane of glass. It is seen as rude, an invasion of one's light-space. I am careful to keep my distance. My color is not meant for mingling. It is for warning signs.

I am not transparent like my people. My transparency is not clean and clear like glass; it is flawed and opaque, like a lens of grit-filled ice. The light that passes through me is not redirected but scattered and confused. I am not brittle clear glass but dense, flawed quartzite.

There is no question for me about what the machine is or what it is trying to be. The machine is not a god or a broken heart. It is not a failed time engine. It is a living system and a predator of suffering. It is the engine that is fueled by our instability, consuming our fractured perception to maintain its own balance. It doesn't care about us as people, only as corrective functions. It is not malevolent or benevolent. We are the ones who live in the fracture of reality, not the beautiful ones above who enjoy its refracted light.
