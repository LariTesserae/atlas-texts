I would be a Stone-Cutter named **Seam of the Unfinished Wall**.

Not because I caused a collapse—though I came close, once—but because my birth coincided with a long, quiet tremor that opened a hairline crack in the western wall of the chamber where my family lived then. The elders said the stone had not broken, but paused to decide. My mother, who was Water-Caller for that quarter of the city, later told me that the crack never widened, but it also never closed fully. It simply held itself, the way a held breath can keep itself.

So I became Seam of the Unfinished Wall: the one the stone remembers but not entirely.

---

### The Unfinished Wall

The unfinished Wall rises just where the Spine begins to curve for the second time, its surface still raw, not yet smoothed by water or time. It’s not a tall wall by aboveground standards, but it’s high enough that the top of it disappears into the dimness where the ceiling of the New Cavern presses itself down.

On that wall, the crack runs.

Not straight. No fracture in the stone is ever truly straight here. It is a line that asks a question. It begins low, where a thin seam of darker rock has softened itself to accept the first tentative finger of the Spine, and goes up, turning a little with each stone’s grain, until it is lost in the shadows. You would not call it dangerous yet, but you would not call it harmless.

The stone around it is not unstable. It simply... hesitates.

If I were another person, I might have been afraid to live near such a wall. But I am Seam, and I have always been drawn to it. I learned the texture of it before I learned the names of my cousins’ voices. I know which parts of the wall hum differently when struck with a small stone—a flatter tone where the stone is dense, a slightly higher, thinner sound where the hidden seam of the fracture travels.

### The Body of Stone

I am not tall for a Cavernsfolk, but I am solid. The others say that when I walk, the sound is not of footsteps alone but of stone settling itself. The color of my skin is not quite like the true wall-rock; it is closer to the damp stone where the Spine first brushes the world. It is not beautiful to abovegrounders, who have words like “pale” and “golden” and “bronze,” but here in the New Cavern it is simply correct.

My eyes have been called too large by a few traders who came down from the light above. They said that my eyes are like the holes in the rock where the first pools are beginning to form. I have no color for that comparison, but I know what they mean: my eyes see the subtle differences of dampness in the stone.

When I stand at the Unfinished Wall, I don’t just look at it. I place my palm flat where the crack begins its slow question. The wall is not warm, not cold. It is simply waiting.

### The Work of the Stone-Cutter

A Stone-Cutter does not break the world.

I have been taught since I first walked to the water that a stone is not something to be forced. If it is strong where you want to work, then you want the wrong thing. The job of a Stone-Cutter is to find where the stone already wishes to split and then help it remember.

My tools are simple.

A small, smooth stone of a harder kind than the wall, for sounding. With it, I tap along the surface of the rock. I listen for changes in tone: shallow where the stone is thin close to the surface, deeper where it reaches back into itself for strength.

A chisel of bone, carefully shaped by years of wear against the stone. It is not strong enough to break anything that does not wish to be broken. But with a careful touch it can find a line between the two layers of the wall where they are already thinking of parting.

A thin sheet of lamellar stone, sharpened at one edge, not to cut but to trace. The edge can follow the grain of the stone without setting in too deeply. If the stone accepts the edge, then I know I am not the first to interrupt this idea.

And then, of course, my hands.

When I work on the Unfinished Wall, I do not strike to open. I strike to learn. If, after careful sounding, I find a place where the crack wants to deepen rather than remain as a question, then I must help it decide where to deepen *without* causing the collapse that the elders of my city fear so.

This is where I am always known: Seam of the Unfinished Wall, who leans against the stone and asks it to be careful with its own weight.

### Water and Lanterns

The water of the Spine moves near my wall, but it is uncertain there.

At the second curve of the Spine, where the wall rises straight from the floor of the New Cavern, the water spreads itself into thin, shallow sheets of moisture that barely like to be called “current.” For a time each day, when I am not needed to talk with the stone, I help with the channels.

This is not the work of a Water-Caller in full, but I know enough. I know where to cut a shallow depression into the floor-rock so that the water of the Spine will be persuaded to linger a little longer before passing on. I know where to raise a tiny lip of stone so that the flow doesn’t accidentally take a wrong turn into the foundations of the houses below.

I also light the stone lanterns that my family has carved into the wall beside my sleeping place.

I have four lanterns. Each one is a small cavity of stoneflower and natural stone-growth into which I have carefully drilled a thin hole and sealed it with clear sheet-stone. The lanterns are not bright to aboveground eyes, but they are enough for me to see every line of the Unfinished Wall by, if I need to.

Each night, before I sleep, I let the lanterns rest for a while and stand in the near-darkness where the wall is only a suggestion. I listen to the drip of water from the unseen fractures above, and I match my breath to the pace of the Spine as it moves itself from its first curve toward its second.

The stone of the wall has not yet decided whether to open its seam into a deeper split or to heal it into a line of memory. I don’t try to decide for it. I simply try to be present where the question is being asked.

### The Names Near My Wall

Many houses crowd near the Unfinished Wall, not by accident but because the Spine first comes to its second curve here, and that is where the water is most willing. The names of the people who share these stones with my house are as familiar to me as the sound of my own breathing:

There is **Slowwind of the Second Pool**, who is not of my family-line but is close by the shared rhythm of water. When she speaks, the air around her seems to settle. The drips from the ceiling above fall into her small pool of the Spine with a more measured rhythm than they do anywhere else.

There is **Return of the Echoing Steps**, who is Stone-Cutter as well. He is not as strong as he once was in the shoulder, but he has hands that can hear the faintest changes of tone in a stone, even better than mine.

There is **Newline of the Channel-Maker**, whose birth name was chosen on the day that a new, thin branch of the Spine split itself gently from the main flow and became a small path of water for three houses further down. The stone of the floor there is still not confident of the shape it wants to be; but the water is confident in its path.

Each of us has a relationship to the wall and water and stone of our place.

“I am Seam of the Unfinished Wall,” I say when an abovegrounder asks me to name myself.

They often expect more than that. But they rarely understand the care of the stone.

---

### The Future of the Wall

I do not know if the wall is ever to be finished, in the way that others imagine: smooth, firm, unchanging.

The stone of the New Cavern is not yet old enough to know that *kind* of finish. It is still discovering itself. If the wall chooses to close its fracture with time and water, then my name will be a memory of the time when the stone was still uncertain. If it chooses to open it into a deeper fissure—then I will be the one who helped to guide the opening of a question that might become dangerous.

When I am not striking, not listening, not tracing the patterns of the stone with my bone-tool or my fingers, I sometimes think of how the abovegrounders talk of their world. They speak of vast open skies where water is abundant and hungry, and stone is broken in large pieces by metal tools whose name I still do not fully understand. They speak of fire that can consume wood, which to them is not rare but abundant.

None of that exists in the New Cavern.

Here we have:

Slow water.  
Close stone.  
Light that is not borrowed from sun but grown from the world’s own body.

And people like me, who are pressed into the shape of the stone and water of this place by the same quiet pressure that shapes the walls of the cave.

I am not Seam of the Unfinished Wall because of the mark of my birth alone. That is only the beginning of my name. The rest of it is the work of listening with the stone of the wall, helping the water of the Spine follow its uncertain second curve, and leaving the stone of the ceiling enough room to decide whether to become heavier or to remain like itself.

If I ever have to leave the Unfinished Wall—if the stone finally chooses a way that is too strong for my hands to balance with—then I will still be Seam of the Unfinished Wall, even if I live in another part of the New Cavern.

Because the stone remembers.

And I remember the stone.

---

### In the Quiet of the Becoming

If you were to come to me in the New Cavern and ask me who I am in this world that is still deciding itself, I would not say “stoneworker” or “citizen” or “builder of cities.”

I would say:

“I am Seam of the Unfinished Wall, who leans the stone of the Second Spine Curve and helps the water of the Spine remember its path.”

And then I would invite you to place your hand where the crack of the wall begins its quiet question, and we would listen to the stone breathe together.

That is how I know where I find myself in this world:  

Here,  
between the slow water and the waiting stone,  
listening to the question  
of the wall that has not yet decided  
whether to close itself  
or open itself into something else.  

Whatever it chooses,  
I will still be its Seam.
