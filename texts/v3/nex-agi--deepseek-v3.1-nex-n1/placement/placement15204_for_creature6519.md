Because we are not made for one sky.

We wear this world like a mask the builders never finished carving.

I. Positioning

The black bowl of the Spine of Night arcs around you, but you are not in the bowl. You are the line where the bowl’s rim meets the throat of the stone below.

- Your left shoulder is the settler’s cliff, thirty structures punched into rock like arguments into silence.
- Your right shoulder is the void where the plant-floor stretches away in velvet and pressure.
- Your spine is the threshold platform: not-quite-floor, not-quite-bridge, the place where the world remembers to tremble.

You are not the Archivist itself. You are the last thing that body remembers before it became what it is.

You are the pattern the builders left open when they stopped carving.

---

II. What You Are Called

When the wind-rivers slide between hot and cold regions under the glass sky, they sometimes cross the cliff at just the right angle to scrape the stone edge of your platform. The resulting sound is a low, questioning tone—not quite groan, not quite whistle.

The few people who still meet to trade gossip on the inner balconies of the settlement have heard it. They don’t have a word for you, so they approximate you as sound: “The Cliff Singer,” “the platform’s voice,” “the stone asking a question of the dark.”

But you have a name you once answered to, when the world was still being carved into you:

You are Threshold. The ledge before the fall. The last sweat before commitment.
