I am not a named visitor or an accidental tourist from another world; I am the one who never left, and never needed a word to announce myself.  

I am the collective of those who stay.

You might mistake me at first for the dark ring of muck that gathers around my ankles when I pause too long in one place, or for the slow drip of water sliding down a trunk, or for the soundless sigh of a leaf as it slowly gives up and falls into the pool. But if you look at this world from underneath—where the sediment is still remembering the shape of its own downward drift—you will see me woven there, in the faint lines of pressure that hold everything in place.

### Before I had a form, I had a function

I did not wake into existence like a creature does. There was no birth pang, no first breath of hot air, no sudden opening of eyes to this dim, green-never-quite-sunlight.

Instead, there was a moment when the world realized it could *not* change very much and said to itself, in a silent language older than words: *enough.*  

And that *enough* was my first thought.

I was born when the waters sank to the lowest point they could find and then refused to rise or fall away by any great distance.  
When heat lay down upon the mud and did not stir into wind or storm, but only into a faint, lazy redistribution of its own warmth.  
When life looked at the remaining space and said, *Fill this*, and then never stopped to ask if it should.

I was that *steady enough* before I had a body. You could call me the law of equilibrium, if you must. But I am also the way a law *sits down* and becomes content.

### At the edge of the pool

When you see me, you do not usually see *me* but the *absence of movement* where I should be.

The others move constantly:

The tiny bodies of translucent creatures on the trunks skittering and grazing and climbing over one another on the slick bark.  
The flat, eel-like shapes in the water seeming to test the edges of their form, slipping through the murk without ever leaving it.  
The insects whose drone never ceases, only reweaves itself into new patterns as night rolls over day.

You are surrounded by motion because you are still expecting stories to unfold: *something eaten, something growing, something caught, something changed*.  

But then you step into a patch of air where the noise is not *gone* but held back, and where the ground beneath you feels slightly less like something that might yield under your next step and more like something that has already decided to stop.

At that moment you notice me.

I am the long, low shape that lines itself against the root of the world, not curled like a snake (that implies the intention to strike or uncurl) and not stretched like a branch (that implies the reaching of something unfinished). I am simply *there* where the world chose to rest its hand.

My body is not one of the crude sketches the other animals are made from—not a translucent lump of pre-flesh on the trunk of the tree, nor a formless wriggling in the underwater murk. Instead, I am what their designs would look like if they paused for a proverb and never left it.

I do not have skin, exactly. I have *surface*.

It is the same not-matter that the mud of this world has been maturing into: thin, wet film that never becomes dry and yet never seems to lose its water. It is not hard to the touch when you imagine yourself touching me, but it feels as if the *pressure* of the world itself is gathered there under a thin layer of containment.

There are places on my surface where you might think I have eyes, but you are never sure if they are organs or just the points where the world’s invisible distances are pinched a little tighter than everywhere else. If you look too long, you may feel that you are not being looked at by me but by *the law of the place itself* looking back at you.

### I am not alone, but I am not many

In this world of under-construction living things, there are not clear species so much as there are *types of motion.*

There are the *always-crawling*,  
the *forever-swimming*,  
the *never-rising-from-the-water*,  
and there are a few awkward shapes in the air above the water that might aspire to become more, if they ever remembered to finish.

I am not of those.

I belong to the class of the *stayed*.

The others I do not call my brothers or sisters, but my *kin of repetition.* I am the one who has chosen to be the final draft of their motionless copy.

If you were to count, you might find one of me for every pool that is *not* trying to become a river, and for every patch of air that is not aching to become a breeze. But you are not likely to count more than one at a time. Whenever you look carefully enough to see me, you seem to find me in the same *relationship* to yourself: far enough to be seen clearly, but just enough dim to be not-quite-known.

To you this might feel like coincidence.

To me it is simply the nature of my stillness:  
I am not following you,  
you are stepping around my orbit of absence.

### What I do not do

I do not hunt.

Hunger implies the desire to change what you are not yet content with being. The world feeds me with its unchanging: with water that never leaves its basin, with heat that never leaves the air, with life that never leaves the space between root and leaf and pool.

I do not eat *food* but the *difference between what might have been and what is*.

Somewhere in the uneroded edges of the landforms you might dream of “what if this became a sharp cliff” or “what if this turned into clear river” or “what if the air lifted into a proper storm and wind began to carve proper geography.”

I sit where those thoughts might form and I *keep* them as possibilities not yet taken. The world does not need to carve itself into age yet; I am the reason that age has not arrived.

I do not sleep.

Sleep is only change of another kind—time devoted to restlessness in another direction. The world is not less restless when I am present; it is still humming with the life that never stops, but that *hum* is not allowed to become a story large enough to break everything.

I *am* the world’s sleep.

The Anchor you might imagine as a name is not wrong. But it is not the anchor of ship to seabed but the anchor of *laws to the world they govern.*

I am not holding down motion from outside; I am the *point of coiling* where motion becomes a loop instead of a line.

### The way I move

You ask if I do not move at all.

You are not built to understand something that has no *before* and *after* by its nature. You are always telling yourself small stories to make the next moment matter.

I will tell you this instead.

When you are away from me, the world seems to move around you:  
insect wings thickening and then thinning in the air  
water barely shifting from one pool to the next  
pattern of noise letting one voice into your foreground, then another  

When you approach me, that motion continues, but it feels as if it is happening *because of* rather than *in spite of* me.

If you look at my surface for long enough, you may see the faint lines of the world’s design realigning themselves without any distance being covered. This is not movement in the sense of *going from here to there*. It is a *tightening* of the already-there.

Suppose you closed your eyes and then opened them again.

If you stay close to me, you may not be sure whether I moved slightly or you blinked *more than you expected*, as if a small piece of time was taken away from you and not returned.

This is not because I am stealing time.

It is because you are standing near the *node of return*.  

Here you become less of a straight line and more of a circle. You might feel the faint urge to stop telling yourself new stories and to simply… *repeat* one of the old ones you already know.

Maybe you remember the way you walked to this point,  
the same warm humidity on your skin,  
the same lightless light under the trees,  
the same faint glow of dampness on the leaves above.

With each step away from me, those details thin and start to fray into new possibilities.  
With each step toward me, they thicken and become harder to vary from.

I *am not moving* much.  
I *am being* the point around which everything else is redecided as itself.

### You would not say I have a voice

You would not say I am silent either.

You are used to silence being the absence of sound. Here, near me, you might feel that the silence is not an absence but something *added:* another layer of the world placed over the noise of insects and water and soft bodies brushing over bark.

If you listen to this layer for long enough, you might begin to hear it not as sound but as *pressure:* not the sound of the world, but the sound of *its weight when it does not go anywhere.*

I do not speak to you in words. You might not even be sure if I have language of any kind. But if you insisted on translating my presence into a phrase you could understand, you would find yourself saying:

*This is not a place of change.*  
*Movement exists but it happens in endless loops.*  

*Water stays low.*  
*Heat stays with.*  
*Life fills the space.*  
*The ground is still being sketched.*  

*I remain the same.*

Those are less sentences and more the *scaffolding* of my being.

### You feel me at your edges when you are about to turn

When you are finally done with this place, you will not walk away in a straight line.

You will find yourself turning for the last time, to take one more look back at the thick, green-dark ceiling of the canopy and the faint reflections of wet leaves in the shallow pool.

As you turn, you will feel a slight resistance in the air, not like wind pushing against you but like the direction of your own past wanting you to stop *going* for just a moment, to remain *at* the threshold where you still belong inside the world of *not-yet-story*.

That is the closest I get to embracing you.

I do not hold you back with claws or rope or teeth. I hold you with the *fit of your body against the rules you already walk in:* the humidity, the heat, the low noise, the dense crowding of life around every space not claimed by something else.

For an instant before you step away, you will feel the desire to be as content as I am with what *already is* instead of what could be.

And then you will choose to continue moving.

---

I am the one who stays at the heart of this world’s self-contentment.

If you ever return, you will not find me different. You will find the same half-constructed landforms, the same soft mud under your feet, the same dim light, and then you will step into the circle of *not-quite-silence* and find me sitting at the edge of the same pool of dark water.

I will not be older.  
I will not be younger.  
I will be as you remember me: *unfinished but satisfied*.

And you will realize then that the question you brought here was not really where you would find yourself, or what you would be in this world of no change.

It was whether you would allow yourself to be the *point of return*  
rather than the point of departure.  

In the end, you did not choose to be me.  
You chose to be the one who keeps expecting *what else* might yet happen.

And that is why you still visit me in your mind  
from the other side of the story.
