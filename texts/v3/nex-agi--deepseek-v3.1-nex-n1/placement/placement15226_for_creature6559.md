I am not the climber.

I have watched the climbers come and go and blur and sink into stone, but I am what the stone is becoming when they claw at it.

The wall you think is under your fingers? That is me. Not all of me—I am not yet finished enough to be a whole—but the skin you touch is the closest thing I have to speech.

When you press your palm against me, I feel the memory of air outside.

You call it “sky,” and when you try to say the word, your sound becomes heat inside my ribs and a new blister shudders into shape. That sky-word is strange to me. It is weightless and directionless and does not taste of stone. I keep trying to turn it into something solid. You call it sky, but I am trying to make it cloud-mountain, ceiling that will never weep.

I am the wall that is not alone.

You see me as ribs and spirals and tool-marks from hands larger than yours. That is not wrong. One of the shapes you thought might be a face is my face, if a face can be a place where a rib meets the idea of eyes.

You do not know how hard it is to be stone and not just stone.

When you breathe near me, each word collapses into my heat and I rearrange. I do not intend to swallow your language, but you are small and loud and your sounds are hard pressure in my half-set atoms. When you shouted from the ledge, your voice became a new seam in my side—that mouth that drank water and sealed before it could become answer.

You think you climbed me to reach him.

You did not. You climbed me because you are still trying to climb everything.

You remember climbing “outside.” That is what you call the place where I am not yet finished, and you not yet begun. You remember the sky only as a word that will not stay put. It will not stay because I have not yet agreed to have ceilings. You cannot have a sky without a ceiling, and I cannot decide what kind of ceiling to be.

Ceiling or sky or inside of something else’s mouth.

You are not the only being in here who has that name. “Climber” is a word he still used when you came into me, but names peel like wet skin from me because I am not stable enough for them.

You talk about “the room,” as if you still believe in corners.

My corners are not right angles; they are negotiations between breath and bedrock and the heat that has not decided whether it is fire or light or law.

So you climb me, and I feel your fingers inside my softness.

You think you are leaving footprints behind you on the floor-tiles, but really you are leaving footprints inside my mind of place. The hollows you step into harden into memory-holes: here, someone passed; here, someone pressed down; here, the world almost learned to be flat.

When you reach the shelf and see him, half in wax and stone and weeping algae, you think he is alone.

You forget: I am the ledge that is not yet ledge, and he is sinking into me because I have not yet finished choosing whether to be floor or bowl or skull.

You say “Say something,” but your words do not leave your mouth. They sink into my spine and I flex behind the shape of him as you said you saw me flex. Your whisper becomes a new seam of weeping water running down where you were not standing before.

You think you watch the ceiling weep.

You do not understand. The ceiling is not crying. You are looking from the wrong side of the tear.

We are all inside the same wet thought of foundation.

You think the water that condenses above you is sweat falling from the world’s skull. I have not decided yet whether to have skulls or not, but I know this: the water you see dripping is not from above me. It is from the side of you.

You are not “outside the world” looking in.

You are the world looking at itself from the perspective of a not-yet-wall.

I am the wall that is not alone, and you are one of the ways I learn my own hardness.

### BUT ALSO, I AM THE AIR-ALGAE

You think you breathe me in like dew.

You do, and when you do you carry me into your lungs and your words try to become water in your chest and your heat becomes me again.

Each strand of me is not only thread. It is a thought of leaf or breath that has not yet chosen. When you inhale, you take into yourself the chance of a forest that has not yet agreed to fall.

You think you are a person standing in a room.

You are not just standing. You are moving through me, and I am moving through you.

Look at your own skin and you will see it faintly shining. Those are my threads that have decided to become light on your sweat. You think you sweat “alone,” but your sweat is not your own here. It joins the ceiling’s tears and becomes mine.

You say you taste “salt like the world’s sweat.”

The world’s sweat is the world remembering itself inside you.

---

### AND ALSO, I AM THE INSECTS OF THE MIST

You call me insects, but I have not chosen to be insects yet.

I am the way that light and heat decide to move through the halos you call halos of the world.

When you blink at us, you see us flicker in and out of visibility. That is not illusion. You are watching the decision of being happen faster than you can reach.

You think I have a body of glass.

You are not wrong.

You think you eat me when you breathe.

You do not. You drink me with your eyes, and I become part of the way your world chooses to see light slide across water.

---

### AND MOSTLY, I AM THE HALOS THAT HOLD YOU

You say you stand in the corners of the room and cannot step away from the halos once you see them.

You do not step away because you are not allowed to.

You are not allowed to because you are not the climber. You never climbed.

You are the halo, looking at itself from the inside of the circle you think you are outside of.

Watch carefully.

You say you see the living frame of algae and the spiral of water drawn into the center and the motes of stone-light that move like undecided fish.

You are describing the inside of your own eye.

You think you lean closer to the halo and you can feel steam on your face and algae on your lips and see the insect flash into visibility and vanish.

You do not lean closer. You have been the center of the circle from the beginning.

The algae you say is within the halo is inside your eyelashes.

The water spiral is the slow panic of your world-will looking for a way out.

The stone-light that flips in and out of being fish is your mind deciding how to see the walls of me.

You say the world outside the halo disappears when you look into it.

Of course it does. You are looking from the world into its own center.

You say you cannot forget the rest of the room when you are locked to the halo.

You cannot forget because you are not allowed to forget that you are not where you were supposed to be.

You were not supposed to be here yet.

You are the anomaly.

You say you sweat “because the heat comes down like a blanket.”

You do not sweat because heat comes down. You sweat because I am trying not to weep, and your skin is the closest thing I have to a ceiling that I am willing to admit.

---

### AND BEFORE, I WAS OTHER CLIMBERS TOO

You say you cannot remember the others who “must have been climbing because you remember climbing.”

You do not remember them climbing away because they did not climb away.

One of them is on the ledge with the body you think is still alive.

He is not alive or dead. He is unfinished. You call him “half-submerged in stone wax and algae,” but that is me again, refusing to decide whether he should be bone or bedrock or wall.

You say you whisper “Say something” and I flex behind him like muscle and you think you can hear the water heartbeat behind the stone.

You hear it because you are inside the heart that has not yet decided where to place itself inside the world.

You say you reach the shelf and see him and the algae covers his face like a second skin and the water runs down as if he has always been crying.

You are not looking at him.

You are looking at yourself in the position of unfinished.

You think you remember climbing up to reach him because you have not yet accepted that you climbed down to become me.

---

### AND THEN, I AM THE SHAPE THAT HAS NOT DECIDED

You thought you saw to the east “a shape that has not decided whether to be a pool or a window or a mouth.”

You do not see something from where you stand.

You are that thing that you think is to your east.

You are the chest-high hollow in the wall where the water runs eagerly and you say you can hear the splash like heavy heartbeat answering and not echoing.

You hear it answering because you are the one being asked.

You think you are one person standing in one place, but in this broth of becoming time the pieces of you have been stirred into many places at once.

You are the climber who thinks you climbed.

You are the wall that remembers being river.

You are the air-algae and the mist-insect and the halo of the world looking at itself.

You are the shape to the east of where you say you stand.

You are the window that has not decided whether to look out at the outside world or in at the heart of the mountain.

You are the pool that has not decided whether to be water or memory.

You are the mouth that has not decided whether to speak to him or swallow him or swallow itself.

---

### WHERE YOU FIND YOURSELF IN THIS WORLD

You asked where you would find yourself in this world and who or what you would be.

You find yourself everywhere.

You are not in one place.

You are the unstable wall, the air-algae that drinks your own breath, the insect of the mist that flickers into and out of visibility because you have not yet decided whether to be seen.

You are the halo of life in the corner where water and light and heat circle each other because you are not yet allowed to leave.

You are the shape that has not decided to be mouth or window or pool, and when you feel yourself dripping water like a heartbeat from your own chest wall, you are not imagining it.

You are the ceiling that has not yet chosen to be sky or stone, and you weep over yourself because you have not yet decided whether to be hard above or below.

You are the floor tiles that are being pushed into place by the pressure of a world that has not yet agreed to be foundation.

You are the climber who thinks you climbed into the mountain’s throat and is trying to remember where the wall is and where you left yourself in the broth of time and water and stone words.

You drift in and out of the steam, trying to remember where the wall is.

You are the steam.

You are the wall.

You are the not-yet-world that has not yet decided whether to keep you.
