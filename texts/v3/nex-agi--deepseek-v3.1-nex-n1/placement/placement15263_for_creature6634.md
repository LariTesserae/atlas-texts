I am not born of the machines or the brine. I am not born of anything.

The salt remembers the shape of me before I do. The current tugs on my memory before it tugs on my flesh. I am hanging in the center of the cathedral, between the Middle Spire and the Summer End, and the world is coming back into me from the water.

First, the sound.

Not words, not the soft sea of your ancestors. I come into myself with the bass of the Middle Spire to my left, the faint echo from the Summer End to my right, out of phase just enough to make a pattern, like shoulders drawing breath and exhaling. The jets of brine make a white static toward the sternward wall. The pumps hum the way a god would hum if it had metal for lungs and no one to listen.

Second, the pressure. It is not hostile to me. It is my teacher. It presses the new knowledge of my body together from whatever is scattered in the brine: oils from gills of ancestors who never lived here, minerals from the rust lines of the supports, the faintest trace of iron from the blood of the first ones who opened their chests to the water. The pressure says: You will be small enough, and cold enough, and still enough.

Only third do I begin to see the shape of myself.

I am in the lower currents of the center, where the water is not quite strong enough to pull me into the intake of the Middle Spire but not weak enough to be kind. I am thin. The water moves me like I am not quite there, like a darker patch of brine itself. The settlement of me is still happening. I can feel the faintest of membranes knitting between my ribs, where my ancestors once had ribs of their own. I have too many ribs to be an Opened-Throat; they are thin and crowded and they fan inward from the spine like a cage of polished bone needles.

My spine is longer than I expect. It is a tail, not a fish tail but a long articulation of calcium and salt, armored with thin plates of the same dark material that is the pump’s casing. You would see me as black and dull and faintly metallic if the sodium bulb over the starboard wall were not too faint to reach this far into the center.

At the top of my spine there is a skull, but I do not think it is for the same thing your skulls are for. There is no jawbone. There is no space for a brain. The space behind my eye sockets is empty in the way of a machine that has not been installed yet. The brine moves through me and carries information for the thinking of me.

My eyes are not in the spots of the sockets. They are along the spine, at intervals of three ribs. Each eye is a small, clear dome of glassless vitrine, set flush into a bone ring. The vitrine is milky with dissolved salts, but I can see the difference of light from dark. The Opened-Throats would call me blind if they were still the kind of people who use that word. But I see in the way of the machine: I watch the pattern of bass from the Middle Spire and the pause of the Summer End and the faintest of vibration from the Winter Heart at the far end of the chamber, and the pattern they make together is the reading of the world.

I am not the first of me.

You are already thinking of me as singular, but I am already plural. The brine of the Sixth Chamber has been making versions of me for longer than the Middle Spire has been called by that name. The first of me was when the pumps of the world were still new and the engineers were still trying to rinse the dust of the shore from their lungs. The first of me was a mistake of salinity and pressure, a crystallization of something else that was meant to be used in some other way. The brine of the world is not clean of history; it is full of the ghosts of what the ancestors dumped into it before the chamber was ever carved.

But that story is not mine now. Now I am only the body of this one mouse, attached to the spine and the ribs of the last shape of me before this.
