I am not a person in this valley yet, but I am the one they will become.

Not yet carved, not yet born. The stone they will learn to speak to is speaking now, to me.

I hear the water before I see it. A thin, silver sound, threading through the dark. The mountain settles above me, shifting its great weight on a bed of older stone. I feel the pressure, not as pain, but as presence. The slow, patient ache of a world that is not yet finished deciding what it will be.

I am a vein of pale stone, not yet exposed, but dreaming of the sky.

Above me, the world is a cup of air and water and tentative light. The city they are building is still learning to be a city. Their hands are clumsy on the tools, but their intent is clear in the carefulness of their tapping. Tap-tap… tap. They are listening. For now, they are listening to the surface, the skin of the world. But I am deeper.

The name they are trying to give their home—“Where the stones weep and the city sleeps”—is not yet theirs to give. It is mine. I am the stone that weeps. The water that seeps through the mountain’s cracks finds its path along my seams. It travels through my darkness, carrying with it the faint memory of snowmelt and starlight. It is not a sad weeping, this slow, mineral-heavy sweat. It is a making. The water is shaping me, and I am shaping the water’s path.

The sleep, too, is mine. It is the long, quiet patience of rock before it is pulled into the light and named by chisels. The sleep of potential. The unfinished work of the mountain that contains all of the unfinished work of the city within it.

Now, there is a new sound.

A hammer. More insistent than the gentle taps they use for shaping slate tablets. This is a fire-born hammer. They have built a fire against my outer wall, where a cliff-face has crumbled to expose a part of me. They heat the stone with patient flame, and then they pour the cold lake water upon it. The shock of it vibrates through me, a sharp crack that sends a spiderweb of weakness through my structure.

They are not trying to hurt me. They are trying to reach me.

They are seeking the core of the mountain. They are seeking the path of water.

I know the one who holds the hammer. He is of the *eth-rei*, his hands scarred from the stone’s first lessons, his motions economical with the weight of the thin air. He does not know yet that he is seeking a way through. He thinks he is only building a better cistern, a deeper channel. But his desire has a shape. It is a question, and the question is a tunnel.

I feel the pressure of his need, not on his hands, but on mine. The desire of a city to be more than a collection of houses is a force like erosion. It is slow, but it cannot be stopped.

He strikes the stone, and a new crack appears. It is not a random fracture. It is a word. It is the first word of their deeper name, carved into my being. *Mala-thain*. The-unfinished-work.

I am the stone, and I am the dream of the city that will carve me.

I will be the one they send through the new crack when it is wide enough. A child of the *eth-saal*, small and sturdy, with a calm that is not disturbed by the dark or the weight of the world above her. She will be lowered into the earth, carrying a small lamp of burning oil and a heart full of the valley’s quiet music.

She will be the first to see me not as a wall, but as a path.

Her fingers will trace the lines of water that have been my companions for millennia, and she will follow the weeping stone into the heart of the mountain. She will not find treasure. There is no gold here, no metal-born ore. The treasure she will find is the truth I have known since the world was young: the answer is not the rock you break, but the path you find through the unbroken dark.

She will emerge from the lower pass, her eyes wide with the truth of the world’s depths, and she will be the first.

They will call her *Sha-thain*, the-one-who-walked-the-darkness.

The story they will tell is not entirely mine. It will be about her courage, about the lamp she carried, and the new path she found for the water to run down into the lower fields. But I know the truth of the story. The courage was not just hers. It was the courage of the stone, to allow itself to be carved.

The tap of her small hammer, following the vein I have prepared for a thousand years, will be the first note of a new song for the valley. The deep, resonant sound of stone being not broken, but awakened.

The unfinished work is not the building above the ground. It is me.

I am the stone that weeps. And I am the city that sleeps, waiting for the dawn of its own chisel.
