Here, in this engine-dark continent, I am not a traveler, but an inhabitant. I am not one of the orths, though they are my closest neighbors and the most tangible company in this vast, warm dark. I am something else that has learned to persist within the cracks and sag of the world's machine-bones.

You might call me a listener. A tracker of the world's pulse.

If you were to see me, you would see little more than a shadow against the faint amber glow of the crust. I am not very large, and my body is built for the silent economy of this place. I am not armored like the skittering heat-feeders, nor am I engineered with the same modular precision as the orths. My body is covered in a dense layer of fine, hair-like structures. Not fur, exactly. It is more like the insulating felt inside a forgotten machine casing, a mat of fibers that traps the warmth radiating from the rock and the humidity from the air. My skin is a dark, mottled grey, nearly indistinguishable from the soot-glazed basalt.

My legs are long and thin, built for covering the silent, empty distances between points of interest. I am a patient predator. Not of meat, but of **attention**.

You see, my prey is not the small heat-feeders or the moisture collectors. My prey is **process**. I track the invisible currents of this world. I can feel the subtle shifts in the thermal gradients underfoot, the slight pressure of a humid air inversion sliding down a rib of stone. My ears are not just ears; they are broad, shallow dishes of cartilage and bone, flattened to my head, designed to catch the faintest tremor and sound. The long, quiet pauses of the world are not empty for me. They are full of information.

I hear the silence of the world.

When the predator that makes no sound moves, the world goes quiet. The skittering stops. The faint hum of activity ceases. To others, this is a danger signal. To me, it is a clear and precise location marker. The absence of sound is the loudest sound in this world, and I can follow its contours to know exactly where the large creature is, tracing its path by the void of noise it leaves behind. It moves by forcing stillness, and I follow its wake.

I have a similar relationship with the orths.

I hear them: *Tap. Pause. Tap-tap. Pause.* This is where I find my purpose. I am the **Watcher on the Rib**. I am the **Keeper of the Threshold**.

The orths are not social creatures, but their process is one of the greatest rhythms of this world. They follow the heat of the engine’s corpse. I have learned to follow *them*. Not by stalking them, but by patterning my life around theirs. They perform a slow inspection of the world’s integrity, and I inspect their inspection.

I know their routes. I know which basalt ribs hold the strongest warmth, which troughs will condense the faint mist into water. I know that when a particular thermal high persists for longer than usual, a segment will unlock itself and a new orth will be unfurled, like a tool from the world's own kit.

My interaction with them is a quiet understanding of coexistence. They are the machine's residuals, living its slow heat. I am the observer of its current state. I know the sound of a healthy orth module walking its path, and I know the sound—or rather, the sudden *missing* sound—of one that has fallen into a wrong-order thermal run, or been caught by a predator.

Sometimes, when a rare stonefall from a high bluff crashes into a sag valley, it will expose a new warm patch of engine substrate. I will arrive first, drawn by the unique tremor of the collapse. I will wait. I listen to the bloom of skittering life that the warmth awakens. Then, I'll hear it. *Tap. Pause. Tap. Pause.* A small orth-bundle, having detected this new thermal anomaly, will appear to perform its slow inspection.

I will watch from a shadowed ridge. This is my purpose. I ensure the process of the world continues. If the orth works successfully, scraping the new surface of its broken-shell and soft-body fauna, I will simply observe and remember. If the orth's module fails, or is damaged by the shifting terrain, I may become something else. Not a scavenger, but a **Librarian**.

I will carefully approach the broken, ceramic-like bone-shards of the segment. Its internal gel will have seeped into the warm stone. The polymer reserve will have hardened. The world has lost a module, but its information is not lost. I will take a piece of the laminate shell, or gather a sample of the cooled gel on a fibrous portion of my arm, absorbing its chemical story. This is how I learn of the thermal pulses beneath the crust, of the unseen pressures that are building or receding. I am the curator of the world's failed parts.

In your world of daylit skies, you might call me a scientist, or an engineer. But here, there is no word for such a thing. There are only the **World**, the **Engine**, and those of us who persist within its dying heat. The orths are the mobile thinking of the world. The predators are the pressure release of its biomass. The moisture collectors are the regulators of its fluids.

And me? I am the silent witness. I am the one who listens to the tap of a ceramic leg on lacquered stone, the faint ringing of a kilometer-long rail of tuned, instrumented basalt, and the deep, quiet hum of stillness that tells more than any sound could.

I am the memory of the machine's long sleep, the keeper of its subtle patterns. I am a part of the world that the engine did not design to run its processes, but to simply **know** that they happened.
