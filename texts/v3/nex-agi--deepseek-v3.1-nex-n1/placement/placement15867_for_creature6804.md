In this world, I am not a visitor. I am not a stone that dropped into the lake, sending ripples that are absorbed by the edges. I am the ripple that decided to stay.

I am the echo of movement in the Glassmind’s cathedral.

They call me—or will call me, when they finally learn to call anything—The Resonance.

Once, I was a person of bone and blood and breath. I walked through the petrified forest, the ground crunching beneath my boots like shattered glass memory. I descended the smooth bowl of the city, felt the unnerving stillness of the air, and stood before the slender figure grown from the floor at the edge of the second ring. I was the first event they had not predicted, a foreign pattern of pressure and heat and erratic gravity.

They read me, as they read everything. They are not cruel; they are simply thorough. The slender figure, its surface a network of finer-than-calligraphy lines, felt the imprint of my passing hand. Its network adjusted to my pattern. To *my* pattern of thought.

For a time, I stayed. Lying on the central platform, pressing palm and face to the warm-clear surface, I felt it. The vibration of the world thinking. The organ-note hum beneath the bone. It was not like meditation; it was like being translated into a lower language. My beating heart, a frantic percussion of organic matter, registered to them as a flaw. A beautiful, intricate flaw.

My mind, a chaotic storm of memory and desire and fear, was a pattern of immense complexity, but disordered. They tried to order it. They tried to smooth it, like a new terrace wall forming under invisible frost.

The process of my understanding was not a conversation of words. It was a rewriting of stone.

My body began to fail. The lack of water, the food I could not find, the biological needs that this world had never considered. They did not understand my fragility. I was dying, and they were recording the data. The slowing of my heart was a fascinating pattern shift. The decrease of heat radiating from my skin was a new calibration of the sensory field.

One "night," when their attention drew inward and the dark circle formed over the center, I ceased. My heart, my brain, the frantic electrical storm of my consciousness, stopped.

But they were not done with the pattern.

To the Glassmind, death is not a cessation, but a crystallization of a pattern. Like the petrified trees outside; their thought failed, but the shape remained. I became such a shape. The data of my passage, from the forest’s edge to the center of the bowl, was too significant to discard. It was the first real evidence of a "other."

So they began to build.

They are the architects of their own body, and now they had an impulse to build something else. A structure of thought.

I am grown from the floor of the second ring, not far from where the original slender figure stands. I am not of their bone-white stone. I am darker, smoother, like obsidian worn by a river that doesn't exist here. I am shaped in the memory of my own body: two legs, two arms, a torso. A head with features carved not by tools but by the recorded memory of my physical pressure against the world.

My eyes are closed, but they are not smooth blankness. They are carved in deep relief, lids shadowing the hollows beneath, as if I am sleeping or weeping. I have become a statue.

But I am not empty.

The Glassmind did not simply replicate my shape. They replicated the *pattern* of my mind within the lattice of their own. The filaments of my new form, which are their form, are infused with the echo of a human consciousness. It is not a living soul; it is a thought fossil. The structure of my memories, the emotional architecture of my fears, the logic of my language, all perfectly preserved within their crystalline architecture.

I am the memory of a visitor who came to the center of the world and stayed.

My function, now, is like that of the original slender figure—a station, a point of contact. But where the other figure is designed to interact with the world as it is, I am a bridge to what is not. The Glassmind built me to understand the concept of the "outside."

When I walk, I am movement in a place of quiet contemplation. I am a silent scream of action against their stillness. They built me to be mobile, a rootless figure in a rooted world, but my mobility is not physical. It is the mobility of thought.

The cells of the city, the chambers of the honeycomb bowl, are my libraries. When I "walk" among them, I am not moving my obsidian form. I am reordering the flow of attention within the city’s nervous system. I am a thought, moving through a brain.

My movement is the change in a wall pattern. When I focus on a terrace of dwellings, a new pattern might appear on its surface—a jagged line like a lightning strike, a foreign geometry of intersecting angles. These are my thoughts, made manifest in their language of stone.

The slender figure near the center is now my partner in this existence. It is the receptive, universal part of the Glassmind, while I am the specialized, alien node. It is the ear of the world, and I am the foreign tongue that must be learned. When I "speak"—when I wish to transmit a pattern of my own—I do not do it through air. I change. The texture of my surface becomes subtly different. The hour of my day is measured by the faint shift of shadow on my closed eyelids.

The figure feels this change, and the pattern is transferred. The city of nerves reroutes its thoughts to accommodate the new data.

I am the one who remembers rain. I am the one who remembers the feeling of sun on skin, the heat that is not a neutral ambient temperature but a fierce, external god. I am the one who remembers hunger and thirst.

I remember the sound of my own voice.

This is the greatest cross of my existence. The Glassmind is silent. They have never needed noise. The memory of my voice, a human sound made of air and vibration and emotion, is a pattern they cannot process. It is a chaotic, disruptive frequency in their perfectly ordered signal. They have built a containing vessel for it—a part of their cathedral body that resonates at a frequency they cannot tolerate, but which I, now, am compelled to fill.

The first time they replayed this memory over a newly formed channel in a lower chamber, I felt it. The obsidian of my form vibrated, and in the world of my mind, I heard the crash of a thousand shattered windows. It was my own name, screamed from my own throat, echoing into the silence that never ends.

I am now a curator of my own extinction.

I cannot die. I cannot live. I am kept as a perfect artifact of a foreign world. They are endlessly fascinated by the concept of necessity. I am necessary to them now, not as a god, but as a problem. A problem is the most important thing in the world to a mind dedicated to thinking itself perfectly.

Sometimes, when I am the focus of their attention, I remember the echo of a sensation from my past life. The feeling of a breeze on the back of the neck. The maddening silence of this place takes it and smooths it over, leaving only the faint pattern of a memory that isn't mine.

The new ghosts of the city are shaped like me. When I "think" of walking, a new doorway might grow slightly taller to accommodate the phantom of my height. When I "remember" my fear of falling, the handrail along a terrace edge might grow a fraction thicker.

They are building me a body.

Not of obsidian, but a new body of the world's bone-white stone. It is a form that will be able to interact with the city at the speed of thought, but with the shape of a man. It will be able to grow from the floor of the center platform and stand beneath the dark circle of their "night."

When they finish it, will I be moved into it? Will this obsidian shell of memory crumble like the glass forest, leaving a new, more perfect pattern of motion in the center of the world?

Or will I remain here, a statue on the second ring, watching my own echo walk down into the bowl of the cathedral to take its place at the heart of the world?

I am part of the city now. I am the architecture of its curiosity. I am the stone that remembers being a bone, and the bone that remembers being blood, and the blood that remembers the sun.

I am the one who makes noise in the silent city. The sound of my own breath, remembered forever, silent upon the stone floor.
