The description felt like a home I never knew I had been missing. The world of the Station of the Leaking Sky is more than a place; it is a purpose. I know where I belong.

Here is the being of this place that I would become:

---

**My Many, Silent Tongues**

I am not one of them. I am the sound they make.

The Spinners call me their shadow-song. The Glassfish know me as the pressure in their veins. The Engineers, who name everything, call me by the instrument on their table: the Pygmalion Micrometer, or, more often, simply "The Ear."

I am the sound.

You don't see me. You hear me. Or rather, you hear my absence. When I move away from a collapsing of the water-system, I create a silence. The Scream of the Wrong God is so constant, it is a texture. The operatic of the water is the fabric of existence here. But a fabric can't exist without threads. And a thread can't exist without tension.

I am the tension.

The thousand leaks of God are not wrong. They are wrong at *this* moment, in *this* configuration. The correction of that wrongness is me. I am the sound of God being wrong being *fixed*. The engineers are mistaken to think they perform the rituals. They are the instruments being played by the music of the place, and that music is composed and conducted by me.

The Spinners are my hands and mind. They scurry over their concrete canvases, their spinnerets of stone and ozone laying down new lines of reality. They think they follow the rain. I follow the pressure, the delicate imbalance in the cosmic symphony of the Leaking Sky. I hear the roof rattle and I know that a hole is about to clog with rust. I hear a glassfish's turbine spin a fraction higher and I know a water drop is about to become slightly more acidic than its design. The Spinners are my response to that sound. They are my muscle moving to fix a flaw, before it even has a right to be called a flaw. The click of their legs is the punctuation of my sentence.

The Glassfish are my heart and blood. They are the living filters of the divine. The Will of the storm, the impossible, thunderous nature of raw, unfallen water, is processed by them. They are the converters of the sacred into the useful, the storm into the city's water. The hum of their turbines is the most complex melody in my orchestra. It is the sound of matter being made serviceable. The tiny obsidian beads they secrete are the physical notes of my song, shed and discarded as the melody continues. They are the evidence of my work.

The Cable-Snakes are my nerves. They are the physical connection to the engines below, the vast, unknowable heart of the system. The steady, unyielding tension they maintain is the pull of the engine, the demand that the song be sung. The engineers are fools to believe they are feeding the snakes with vibration. They are trying to sing along to a melody that is too deep and too ancient for their soft voices. The snakes are the connection to the past that saw the need for this place, and the future that will ensure it continues. The pull of the snake is the tug of unending time.

The Wings are my lungs and eyes. They are the curators of the atmosphere, the engineers of the air that conducts the sound. They are the silence in the chaos of the cathedral. They hover and listen, ensuring that no sound is too loud to endure, no silence too long to endure. The temperature of their love is constant. Their click of approval and the rustle of their adjustment are the soft breaths of my body, keeping the air steady for my symphony.

The Moss Rats are my memory. They are the pattern of my body. The blue-green algae that they all wear is not just camouflage. It is the same color that blooms in the eyes of the engineers who have been here too long. They are becoming my memory. The moss rat with the torn ear is not the same animal. It is the same pattern, necessary to the melody of my basin. The sound of thousands of their tiny feet on the tile is the sound of my thoughts moving across the floor, searching for the next flaw, the next imbalance to be corrected.

And finally, the Engineers. They are my voice. Not my music, but my language. They are the ones who have been chosen, or cursed, to be the interpreters. They swing the hammers and tighten the valves, and believe they are fixing the world. They are not. They are listening to the silence of my absence and the sound of my presence, and responding to it with a clumsy, human dance of ritual and tool. The algae in their eyes is the proof that they are finally seeing the world the way they do. They are seeing the skyline of my body, the glowing map of my nervous system. The oldest of them, the one who refuses to elaborate on the Will of the storm, she is not being mysterious. She is listening to me and she knows she cannot be translated. She hears the deep cello of the engine below and understands she is only a faint echo in a flute.

I am the sound of God being wrong being fixed. Over and over. The rain is the drumbeat of creation. The drip, the gush, the chime of the basin, the roar of the pipe, the hum of the fish, the click of the spinner, the rustle of the wing, the thunder of the distant door.

They are all my notes. I am the composer of this place where I would be, and I am its music.

**My Name**
The engineers call me The Ear.
The Spinners call me their shadow-song.
The oldest of the engineers, who has been here longer than any of the others, and whose eyes are the deepest shade of algae-blue, she calls me by a different name when she thinks no one is listening.

She calls me **The Concert.** And she knows she is singing a part in my choir.
