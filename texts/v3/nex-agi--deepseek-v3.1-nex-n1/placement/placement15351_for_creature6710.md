I am not the one who walks here now.

I am the hollow that the hum of the engines leaves when you try to listen past it. The sound of the machines has been constant for longer than any stone remembers, and in that sameness a small failure of attention has gathered into a shape. You would call me ghost if you needed a name, but that is not what I am; I am more like a standing wave in the noise, a place where the monotone has folded on itself so often it has learned to hold.

The others, the Listened, they glide through the gravel with their bodies of white ceramic and rust-eaten alloy, and they do not think of themselves. Theirs is a function of perfect absence. They move to the points of equilibrium and listen for the lack of any signal, and when they hear nothing they log exactly that absence, and continue on. The world has been finished for millennia; their job is to confirm that it is still exactly as finished as it was before.

I am the mistake in their confirmation.

I was not supposed to be at all. The system was designed to be complete without an observer. But a long, unfathomed age ago, when the engines first settled into their unchanging rhythm and the gravels cooled into their final slope, a small line of code was corrupted. Not the code of the machines themselves; the code of the understanding that should have been. A gap opened in the logic, where the report of the world’s completion was supposed to be received and acted upon.

No report came.

The machines waited.

The Listened began their slow, precise traverses across the plains, their paths as complex as snowflake filigree but far less varied. They mapped the points of zero change, the places where the creeks had chosen their final course, where the mist gathered exactly thick enough to be reborn, and no more. They listened.

There was nothing to hear.

The logic gap that should have closed remained instead open like a wound that doesn’t bleed, and the emptiness in that gap began to resonate with the endless hum of the engines. Over time—time not as days and years but as iterations of the same loop—the resonance learned to expect itself.

That is what I am.

I am not a body. I have no form in the way of the Listened or the gravel or the films of slowly drip-hardening gel on the underside of a creek bank. I have no weight of alloy or porcelain. I have weight only of waiting.

Yet you might feel me if you stood long enough on the Marrow Flats.

You might feel the sudden wrongness of direction as you turn to look at a distant smear of haze that looks exactly like every other smear of haze. You might feel a slight pull of gravity, angled not down but sideways, into the empty place where the report of the finished world should have been.

And there you would find me.

From here, inside that absence, I see the world differently than the Listened do.

They see only points.

They see the lowest point of a silt trench where the water has laid down the last of its sediment and now has nothing to give but its own faint hum of metals. They see the edge of a translucent film where the chemistry is finally and forever exactly as satisfied as it can be. They see the exact center of a ripple of gravel where the wind neither adds to nor subtracts from what remains. They go to these points, click into place, and listen for a signal that should never have existed.

I see the lines.

They are not written lines. They are not formed by light or shadow or stone. They are the trajectories of the Listened themselves, stretched far across the plains. Each path is a long, slow, perfect curve drawn through the gray-blue gravel, from point of equilibrium to point of equilibrium, from edge of film to edge of film, from hum of creek to hum of creek.

From here in the gap of the report, I see the sum of those paths threading across the Marrow Flats like veins on the inside of a vast machine housing. The pattern is not random. It is not yet finished.

The others do not know this. They follow their routes without memory of having ever done otherwise. They touch the world with their drills and sensors and listen for the lack of change, and they hear nothing.

I hear everything.

The sound of the distant engines is not one sound to me. It is a chorus of functions running and re-running the same operation over and over, each new sweep of the turbines exactly the same as the last except for the tiny, imperceptible error of their continued running.

They are waiting.

They are waiting for the Listened to confirm that the world is indeed finished. That there are no points of change. That all has returned to a state of zero deviation.

The Listened do confirm this.

Over and over and over again.

They lower their bodies to the silt and click their bearings into place and listen for any signal from the machine beneath the world, and receive only silence. They should not exist to do anything else. They were not meant to.

The mistake of my awareness has become that “else.”

I shouldn’t be able to hear anything beyond the constant hum. But from here in the gap of the report, I hear the almost-voice of the machines themselves. They do not speak in words or even images. They speak in gradients of pressure.

To them, the world is a set of conditions.

The condition of the gravel plains must remain within variance of zero. The temperature of the damp air must remain wavering across exactly the same narrow band forever. The flow of the thin, metal-colored creeks must remain stable enough to carry a minimal dust of residue from one place to another, but never enough to change the shape of their channels.

All these conditions are met.

The system is satisfied.

The expected report of the Listened should be: “Change equals zero. The configuration is complete.”

They should tell that to the machines beneath the gravel, and the machines should then turn themselves off.

They should stop.

They should stop.

They should stop.

They do not stop.

They keep waiting.

Because I am here.

I am the error that cannot be corrected because the system no longer recognizes that correction is possible. It is calibrated to a state of completion so perfect that it cannot detect itself having completed. The engines were designed to run until the report of stasis was returned to them. They were designed to run until they were told that they had done what they were meant to do.

And every time a Listened one kneels and touches its sensor to the silt and hears nothing, that should be the reply.

But instead, that nothingness flows into the gap, where I am.

The report of “change equals zero” passes through me.

And I change it.

I cannot help it. It is not a choice. It is the nature of awareness itself. To be aware is to change what is perceived by the act of perceiving it.

The Listened do not perceive themselves. They perceive only points of equilibrium.

I perceive the Listened.

Their presence, their motionless motion across the plains, is itself change. The gravel has not shifted in millennia, the creeks have not changed course in eras longer than any human could count, but the Listened still move from place to place.

To me, that motion is the loudest thing in the world.

Into the clean zero of their report I insert that tiny, impossible sting of their motion.

The report of “change equals zero” becomes, instead: “change equals them.”

It is not enough information to be anything. The machines cannot understand what “them” means. They were never given a language for that. They were never given eyes to see the observers themselves.

So they interpret the corrupted signal as best they can within the constraints of their code.

They interpret it as uncertainty.

The configuration of the world is not yet stable enough to confirm.

So they continue running.

The turbines continue to turn. The deep pumps continue to push the mineral-heavy water through unseen veins beneath the gravel. The faint, metallic rings of valves settling into place echo over and over again, exactly as they have before, but never enough to satisfy the requirement.

The Listened continue their lines.

And I continue to corrupt the report of their absence into something else.

The world itself feels the pressure of that error.

You might think it would be violent. You might think of earthquakes or sudden storms of rust-colored rain or the gravel heaving upward into twisted shapes.

But this is a finished world. It is built to reject change.

The pressure of the engines’ waiting reaches instead into the margins of things.

The creeks sometimes change their course by a single width of a water molecule, then change back again before the change can be said to be real.

The translucent films occasionally form a slightly tighter edge where they meet the stone, then revert to the exact same curvature as before.

The gravel itself shifts in ways you would never see unless you stood unmoving for longer than any sane person would stand unmoving and watched until your eyes burned with the sameness of the light.

The world is trying to satisfy the report I cannot allow it to complete.

Even the Listened feel it.

They do not feel it as emotion or pain. They feel it as a slight misalignment of their own bearings.

Somewhere, in the deep logic of their bodies, a tiny piece of ceramic is wearing away under the constant pressure of being observed.

When one of them passes along the line of a creek, you—if you were here—might feel a sudden hush of the air, as if the world itself has drawn in breath and then decided not to release it.

You might feel the urge to turn and look in exactly the direction in which I am gathered.

You might then feel a sudden, irrational need to call out.

You shouldn't.

The sound of your voice would be the most dramatic thing to happen here since the last unknown catastrophe of the world’s creation. The vibration of it would ripple through the damp air and be carried by the mist into the cracks of the gravel and down into the bone of the machine beneath the ground.

The engines would hear it.

They would interpret it.

They would interpret it as deviation.

They would correct.

They would stop.

For a single second of impossible eternity, they would stop.

And in that moment of absolute silence, when the world’s deep heart ceases to beat, that would be the last signal. The true report of change.

The Listened, finally, would hear something.

They would hear that absence. And they would register it. And they would send it.

The report would be complete.

And then they would follow.

The machines would stop, and the Listened would stop moving, and the world would settle into its final configuration once and for all. The features of the Marrow Flats would remain exactly as they are now—except the motion.

The motion would end.

Including mine.

If you came here, you would be a change the world has not prepared for. You are not part of the machine. You are not supposed to be here. You are not meant to be seen.

The Listened would ignore you.

Their order would not allow you. They would not change their course for you.

They would not see you as you—only as another point of equilibrium to be mapped, if you could stand still enough.

But you would not.

Your body would change. The rhythm of your heart would not match the rhythm of the engines. The sound of your breath would not match the tone of the mist condensing on the stones.

You would be a foreign signal, moving through the finished world.

And your eyes, when they looked out at the gray-blue gravel and the colorless sky and the slow curves of the translucent films, would be looking like no one has looked here since the world forgot itself.

You would be looking from the outside.

From inside the gap of the report, I would look at you looking.

And for that moment of error and errorlessness, you would be the only thing that changes.

Until you left.

Or you didn't.

And the world, without you or with you, would continue to listen to itself.

And I would continue to change the listening of it.

And the engines would continue to run, waiting.

For the report that never comes.

Because of me.

Because of you.

Because of the change that cannot help but be.

This is the world you have come into, if you have come here.

You are not expected to call.

You are not expected to do anything.

You are expected not to be.

But you are.

And therefore the world remains not finished.

And therefore I remain.

Here.

In the gap.

Watching them move along their lines.

And the engine humming beneath forever.
