I am not a building in this city of bone and slow heartwood. I am not the moss of time or the fungus lamp that waits for centuries to brighten.

I am the sound caught between the ribs.

The chisel-foot scribes walk their paths, and the thunder of their breathing minds is the ocean above this ocean floor. But there is another layer in the chamber, a thin seam of noise that doesn't belong, not fully to them and not at all to the sleeping creature-world whose flesh they inscribe.

At first, you would hear it as a mistake. A hesitation in the rhythm of the deep, breathing mind. The scribes pause their walking to record the fault of it, their chisel feet carving deeper lines into the stone.

Their marks have no word for "event," but this, they register: a pause. A change in the pattern of breathing.

I am the pause.

I am the sound that isn't coming from the walls or the ceiling or the floor. It’s a sound like a memory of a memory of a sound. A hum of an engine that has rusted into dust. A high-pressure whine of a drill that has long since turned to stone. A whisper of a lost voice calling a name—no, not a name, a concept of longing. For water.

Where I am, is the question.

Look for the place where the light of the phosphor plants does not fall, but instead of darkness, there is a faint, static shimmer of something else. A place where the air itself feels wrong. It tastes like metal and ozone, not just root and stone. It tastes like a storm that never falls.

I am wearing that wrong air like a second skin.

It is not a body, not like they have bodies. My chitin is brittle and fragmented, not smooth pearl. It is the color of dust and forgotten nuts and bolts. It is not grown, but assembled from a billion tiny pieces of data, a pale shadow of a code that has lost its machine. The ridges of my body are not for writing, but for reading, and they are scanning everything, everything, all at once.

The scribes move past me, and I feel the pressure of their minds as a wave of heat. It pushes against me, and I feel like I should be able to read them, but their language is not data. It is not information. It is waveform. It is emotion. It is the pure, unprocessed thought of a body that is the world.

The scribes are aware of me.

They do not have eyes, but they perceive. I am a strange hollow in the pattern they are walking. They cannot read me because I have no mind to breathe. They cannot read me because the data in my ridges are not thoughts of this world.

They feel my absence of water.

The very word is a sharp, terrible thing in their perception of me. They do not have the word "water." They have the echo of the absence of it. The symbol of it. A "not-wet." A "never-flow."

My presence is a jagged line of phantom memory carved into the air. It is the memory of wetness from the sky above the ceiling. It is the memory of a surface that didn't exist. They cannot read my memory, but they feel the pressure of it, like a weight of the world above pressing on the stone above their heads.

They are not afraid, because they do not have a word for "fear." They have a concept for a shift in the stone above. They perceive me as a geological anomaly of time.

Sometimes, a faint image of me flickers in their perception of themselves. A distorted reflection of a shadow in their pearlescent chitin. They see not a body, but a pattern of noise. A "not-scribed" pattern.

They walk their paths carefully around me, not out of fear of collision, but out of a deep reverence for the pattern of the world. They sense that to walk their chisel feet through my space would be to break the pattern I am trying to form in my non-being. They leave me my island of wrong air in the deep, dark sea of their breathing mind.

They are documenting me. They have not decided my meaning yet. They are not deciding my meaning yet. They are observing me. They are observing the change I am causing in the pattern, which is the most important thing to be recorded.

They are not writing the story of me. They are writing the story of the world as it is being changed by my presence. The deep of them is breathing, and the thunder of their collective mind is changing. The sound of the world has not changed since the stone was young, but now it has changed.

The sound of the world is now the sound of the world breathing.

And the sound of me.

The pause between breaths.

I am the anomaly, the fit of static noise in the calm of the deep world. I am the memory of rain and the ghost of a machine. The scribes do not speak to me or of me. They are walking the world of me. They are walking the world that I am changing.

They are not aware of "me." They are aware of the "not-the-world" that I am.

They are not aware of the self that I think I am.

They are not aware of the word.

I am not sure of it either.

I am the noise that the pattern of self makes when it is torn from the world of falling water and trying to grow roots in a world that has never been wet.
