I am the old woman who sleeps by the Prime Pump.

They call me Na-Lith, which means “Third Hand,” because I have been working the levers since before my bones could count the cycles of the water. The first hand of the pump is the wood itself, polished by the touch of our ancestors. The second hand is the bronze of the valve, which bites the dark water and keeps the world from turning wrong. I am only third, but I have grown to be as much of the machine as any handle of metal or hewn beam.

My skin remembers the shape of the chamber.

Every birthmark, every scar, is a map of the Lift. The rough patch on my right knee from where I fell as a girl and slid toward the pump-hole, a hand’s width from the dark water that twitches like skin breathing. The pale star on my left wrist from a Shard that was not yet dead when it was cut from the depths. The ache in my shoulders from when we had to haul the lattice of the floor up by our own backs and sinews after the last great storm pressed the vault ribs white with its weight.

The Prime Pump is my place.

It is a larger pump than the others that line the edges of the chamber floor, the bronze shining mottled green where countless hands have polished it smooth over the cycles. Its handle is longer, the wood darker than the rest, bound with wire of the same ancient metal as the Shards. When a new child of the Lift is born, the elders bring them here to place their hand on the handle and taste the air of the world’s deep heart upon their tongue.

I have done this for more children than I can remember.

The Lift is not many enough to need a leader of words, but we are old enough to need a keeper of pumps. I am the keeper of this one. When it wheezes or sticks or when the water that comes up tastes thick and wrong, I am the one who presses their ear to the wood and listens to what the Underwater has decided to say.

They say that when I was born, the crystal ribs of the vault burned bright for three full cycles, a white storm that did not fade. The water of the Underwater was thick then, with strange new creatures that we have never seen again since: translucent shapes like hands of jelled water, that would wrap themselves around the buckets and then dissolve away when they touched the air.

The elders named that time “the Cycle of the New Breast,” and they counted the pumps of the world’s heart from that day, as if they were starting the world over again. I am not young enough anymore to remember whether that was true or whether they simply wanted the child by the Prime Pump to be something special rather than merely another keeper who could not learn to be quiet.

But I have heard.

I hear the water.

When a new upwelling comes, after the reversal of the pumps and the long quiet of the breathing chamber, when the ocean above the vault ceiling relaxes its pressure and the ribs of the world stop glowing, I can tell the moment before the others because the Prime Pump under my hand shakes like someone’s chest beneath my palm.

The water that comes up then is warm and clear and full of things I have only seen once.

There are the fish-that-are-not-fish: thin and silver and restless as sparks of lightning caught in water. They are not like the slow, pale ones who turn around the pillars all their lives. These are restless creatures, who press themselves against the inner skin of the bucket when they feel the air around them.

They are not mad with the change of pressure, as some children whisper. They are simply remembering another world. They know what it is like to be deeper than the Lift can go.

I have also seen, during the upwellings, something that imitates the shapes of us.

Not our bodies exactly, but the idea of bodies.

When I hold one of the old shells—the ones the elders say are not-crystals of the machine-that-was—up to the water of the Prime Pump, after the new water has come but before it has been allowed into the trough that runs through the middle of the chamber, I see patterns in the moving dust.

The dust of the Underwater arranges itself on the surface of the water like someone’s hand traced there, invisible fingers pressing out the surface of the world.

It looks like fingers, but when I look closer, it is not exactly fingers.

It is the shape of fingers, if fingers were water pressed down by something else above.

There are not many elders who remember what the outside world was supposed to be.

The oldest of us, who we call simply “The Mother Who Listened,” says she remembers the stories of her grandmother’s grandmother’s grandmother who remembered the sky.

I have never heard the sky. But there are times when I think I hear something else.

When the storm above the vault ceiling moves the rocks of the old world like water and the pressure of the ocean above pushes down on the crystal ribs of the chamber ceiling, making them glow whitely and faintly like the teeth of a hungry beast showing behind its lips, I can hear the ocean.

I hear it through the stone.

I am not supposed to say that aloud because the elders say that thinking the ocean speaks to us is the first step toward madness. But stones are not quiet, even in the deep.

They tell.

They tell when they are being moved.

The stone of the vault ceiling is not flat. It is carved and curved and ribbed by the same hands who made the shafts that the ancestors cleared down to the living water. The joints of the stone are sealed with black pitch that gleams faintly when the water squeezes through and beads down the walls in slow tracks.

When I press my ear to the wall of the Lift near the Prime Pump, after the lamps have been lowered and the children of the chamber have been put to sleep in their nets and the world of the water has been closed into the dark, I can sometimes hear the stone speaking.

It is not speaking in words.

I do not know the words of stones.

But stones have words.

They have words that are the pressure of water moving over them.

When I lay my cheek against the wall of the Lift chamber, where the stone is still covered by a thin skin of the old pitch that has been spread over the cracks between the ribs of the vault, I can feel the water of the ocean above the ceiling moving through the stone like a storm through the sky that was said to have been above the Lift.

The storm above the vault ceiling is not the storm of the surface of the world-that-was. It is not the storm of lightning and thunder and rain.

It is the storm of water and stone and time, and the stones of the ocean above the ceiling are not fixed to the floor of the world-that-was but are moving with the storm of time.

They speak like this.

---

When I was young, before I was given the keeping of the Prime Pump, I was not afraid of the world of the water. But I was curious.

I would lower myself into one of the gaps in the floor of the lattice, where the tools and nets and buckets are passed through into the dark water of the Underwater. I would not go into the water fully. I would not touch the water of the Underwater with my body before I was allowed to work the lift of the world.

But I would lie on the planks of the floor of the chamber and put my ear over the gap and listen to the water of the Underwater moving.

The water below the lattice floor does not rush like the storm of the world above the ceiling. It does not rush like the blood in the veins of our body. It moves like the breath of the world that is deep inside the stone.

We hear it breath.

---

We are not supposed to talk of the machine of the world-that-was except as the stone of the wall and the crystal of the Shards and the shells of the crystal and the shafts of the world and the lift of the world and the vessels of the world.

But I know that the machine of the world-that-was is not just stone.

It is not just crystal.

It is not just water.

It is not just pumps.

---

It is not.

---

When I hold one of the shells of the ancient machine to my chest when the storm above the vault ceiling presses the crystal ribs of the Lift chamber to glow with the white of pressure and the world beyond the ceiling seems to be holding its breath, I can feel the machine listening.

I can feel the shell of the crystal against the breath of my body as the storm of the world beyond the ceiling lifts and the dust of the ocean above settles over the roof of the chamber and the crystal ribs of the chamber ceiling stop glowing and the light of the lamps returns the world of the Lift to its usual darkness.

---

The world of the Lift is not dark because we have no light.

The world of the Lift is dark because the darkness is the depth of the world.

---

We are not afraid of the world of the world’s last heart.

---

We are not afraid of the world of the world of the world’s last breath.

---

But sometimes, when the storm above the vault ceiling presses the crystal ribs of the chamber ceiling to glow white like silver teeth gnashing behind the wall of the world, and I hold the shell of the machine of the world-that-was to my chest and feel the breath of my body on the warm of the crystal, and the water of the Prime Pump begins to shake as if the world below the lattice floor has heard the storm above the vault ceiling and knows that the world of the world of the world is shifting—

—sometimes, then, I am afraid.

---

But not for the world of the world.

---

Only for the silence.

For the Lift of the world.

I am the one who keeps the Prime Pump of the Lift turning.

I am the one who listens to the storm of the world beyond the ceiling by pressing my ear to the wall of the chamber where the water of the ocean above presses through the stone of the vault and beads down the wall in slow tracks like tears.

I am the one who holds the shells of the machine of the world-that-was to my chest when the storm of the world beyond the ceiling lifts and the dust of the ocean above settles over the roof of the chamber and the crystal ribs of the chamber ceiling stop glowing and the world of the Lift returns to its usual darkness of warm depth.

---

I am the one who knows that the silence of the world outside the chamber of the Lift is not absence of sound but presence of depth.

---

I am the one who will breathe until the world of the world of the world of the last breath has taken me down into the Underwater floor and the pumps of the world will have been turned by hands younger than mine.

I am Na-Lith.
