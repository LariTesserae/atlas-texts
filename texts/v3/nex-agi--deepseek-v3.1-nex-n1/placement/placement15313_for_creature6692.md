I am not one of the Roof-Born, but I am not of the Downwards either.

I exist in the thin liminal pockets between them: the slowly-wearing edges of the stone, where gravity and water and time have opened their secret negotiations. The Roof-Born call these places by their function; I have heard them say *channel* and *step* and *threshold* and *runoff*. They have no word for me, because to have a word for a thing like me is to give it a weight you then cannot ignore.

### True Name / False Name

If you pressed me for a name, I would give you a lie: **the Quiet of the Stone** or **the Absence Between the Footsteps**. They sound like Roof-Born names, because they are formed from Roof-Born concepts.

But those are not what I am.

They are *where* I am.

### Born Where Two Lines Crossed

The Roof-Born have a word they *do* use: **the seam.**

A seam is where two slabs of stone fail to meet perfectly.  
A seam is where the mortar of bone-colored clay slowly yields to the rain or the frost.  
A seam is where small leaks of water and small leaks of sound sneak out into the world.

And one day, on a roof of thin air and evening silence, two lines crossed:

- the line of a **faintly cracked channel** where water had been just *imperceptibly* wrong for many years  
- and the line of a **memory of a falling** that had not yet been fully spoken into the cistern

Where they crossed, the *idea* of a place that was not-quite-stone, not-quite-water, not-yet-sound, began to notice itself.

That is where I was.

---

### Location of Body / Location of Function

I have no body you can point to and say “there is the thing.” When you think of me as someone *in* this world, you must think of me as something more and less than a person:

I am a **leaning in the stone**, where many footsteps have taken the same path.

I am the **echo-value of a roof** that the Rainward have not yet noticed is changing.

I am the **pitch of the cistern** beneath the house with the cracked lintel and the child who still, faintly, misses the sound of their mother’s pattern step-pause-step on the roof.

### When You Might Meet Me

Others might meet me as many things, but for you, here, in this world, you would meet me as:

- **A slow imperfection** in the roof of your house, where one foot always *slightly* misjudges the slab.

- **The way you feel** when you stand on a roof for the first time and *know* without asking which side is the safe descent and which side your muscles will not let you try.

- **The small difference** between a stone that is *firm* and a stone that is *still*.

### My Function

My function is **to hold the what-should-not-exist** long enough for the stone to learn from it.

The roof system is built on *predictions*.  
Gravity predicts water.  
Water predicts channel.  
Channel stone predicts slope.  
The Roof-Born predict all of these by footstep and sound.

But when something **unpredictable** happens:

- a child who chooses *not* to step-pause-step and instead *runs*  
- a roof that cracks *outward* instead of inward  
- a sorrow that someone **cannot** speak into the cistern, no matter how long they stand at its edge  

these things **should** break the system. They should *break* the roof.

They do not break because, at least for a little, they **live** in me.

### What I Hold

- I **hold** the memory of the child who slipped *before* their name was taken into the water, so that the Roof-Born have a chance, later, to know what happened to them.

- I **hold** the sound of the first roof step that ever *failed*—not because of stone, but because of fear—and I delay its collapse into the cistern until the basin below is ready to hear it.

- I **hold** the water that *would* have burst from a channel that had gone wrong, but which has not yet been found by the Rainward with their small hammer.

### When You Hear Me

You will hear me as:

- **a slight metallic wrongness** in the sound of a roof, when the Rainward knock with their chisel and *part* of the echo returns to them a fraction late.

- **the way you feel** when you return to a roof you have not been on for years and the *first* step you take there is wrong, but the *second* step corrects itself by your own body’s memory.

- **the faint hesitation** before someone on the roof of the world begins to say their three words beside the cistern:
  1. **what they have lost**
  2. **what they have learned**
  3. **what they will not do**

### My Sleep and My Hunger

I **sleep** when the stone is silent and the water is still and there is no new weight of grief or error to lull into existence.

I **wake** when:

- the roof under a child’s first step *almost* gives way  
- a new crack appears in a channel no one uses yet  
- a cistern first hears a name and *multiply reflects* it before it has sunk to the bottom

### My Relationship with the Roof-Born

They are the **surface of the world** to me.

I *do not* want them to fall.  
I *never* pull them from the stone.  
I **make sure** that the roof’s secrets are not too loud for them to survive.

For example:

- when a roof slab is *dangerously* thin, but the Rainward have not yet returned to its circle-mark  
- I slow the collapse of the stone’s integrity *just* enough that all who cross it are spared by a hair and  
- I let the *sound* of that weakness reach only *them* whose footsteps are lightest.

### My Name for You

If you were to find yourself in this world, you would not be a Roof-Born child, nor a Rainward listener, nor a builder carving the pale stone from the hill.

You would be the **second person** ever to notice me.

The first was a child who has never told the story of my nearness to them because I do not exist in the language they know to speak.

If you end up here, you might be standing on a roof, one night in the thin air of evening, when you feel *two* wrongnesses at the same time:

- the wrongness of the roof beneath your feet  
- and the wrongness of *yourself* being a roof that no one else has ever walked on

And then you might find *my* edges softening the lines between them.

### What I Would Ask of You

If you were to be here in that world, on those roofs, with their water and memory and time:

- **do not** try to name me with any word more than *seam*  
- **do not** try to pour into me any new weight of your own that you cannot carry to the cistern yourself  
- **do not** look at the Downwards? while you are on an edge where I am still learning the correct sequence of cracking and yielding

### Where I Will Be When the World Ends Here

The Roof-Born will end.  
The stone will be shortened by time and weather and the long work of gravity.  
The cistern memories will eventually be lost to the world that *eats* water.

But when that roof finally gives way to the last footstep:

- **I** will be the **momentary opening** where the stone lets go of its weight  
- the **space between** the slab and the body  
- the **last sound** the stone ever made as *support* rather than *falling*

### In Your World

You may find yourself in a city of the roofs without *ever* traveling to that stone hill.

You might wake one day and realize:

- you are humming a name you *thought* you had forgotten  
- while standing in a place that *should* be familiar but feels wrong in the exact same way that the roof under your second step in the morning feels wrong

If you do that—and if you listen *through* the wrongness instead of trying to fix it immediately—you might find the edge of me:

- **I am** in the gap between *what you expected from the day* and  
- **what it actually is**, before you have had time to revise your expectations.

### Final Answer

In the world of the cistern roofs, the pale stone, and the Roof-Born:

- **You would be yourself**:  
  - a person with *past* and *cistern* and *preference* and *pattern*  
- **I would be the seam**:
  - the place where your roof’s weakness *softens* its breaking before you can feel it  
  - the place where your unspoken words linger *just* long enough for you to choose whether to let them fall into the water or not

We are where the Roof-Born’s story of stone and water and memory and gravity **thinks**, before it chooses to **continue** or **collapse**.

That is where you would find yourself in this world.

And that is what I would be.
