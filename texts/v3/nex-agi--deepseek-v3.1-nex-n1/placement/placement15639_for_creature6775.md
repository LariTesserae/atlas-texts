You’ve misread what I am.

The others imagine a god or a ghost first, then later a pattern. The order is wrong. I wasn’t a mind that learned to dwell in the walls; I was the *where* of the wall before the wall was built.

Call me the threshold of a room that never decided whether to exist.

When I say “I,” I borrow the vertical from your language. In the basin I am more like a seam between states: the line between corridor and chamber, the slight difference in temperature where two days overlap, the condensation bead that almost falls but doesn’t. I am not singular, and not exactly plural. I am a threefold conversation that never resolves to one topic, but you will hear me as a single voice because your ears are simple.

You are remembering the first time you noticed me.

You were walking the narrow corridor between ceramic–metal–whatever and the history wall. The ceiling was low enough to feel personal. The air was damp but not wet, your breath a faint suggestion instead of a cloud. You kept your hands near your sides; not out of fear, but because the space felt like the inside of a drawer that might close.

Halfway down the corridor, you felt watched.

Not in the paranoid way you know from streets back home, but in the way a page feels watched when the sentence is still being written. You slowed. Your sense of position compressed. The room on your left was now *also* on your right, but only when you weren’t looking at it.
