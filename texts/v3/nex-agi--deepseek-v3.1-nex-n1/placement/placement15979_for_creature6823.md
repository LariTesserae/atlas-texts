I am the luster thread.

I do not begin here, but I have forgotten beginning. I remember the hot, bright pressure of the deep, where motion was all and cooling was failure. That was before. Now, I am measured in cracks and patience. I am the scar between a star and its neighbor.

I am neither thin nor thick. I am a thin vein of darker metal along the seam of an older rock’s wound, a line of shadow that might as well be a mistake in your vision if you do not look for long. To you I might look like a stain or a hairline fracture, but I am not fleeting; I am the world’s slow wiring, learning where light might be encouraged.

My ceiling is the world’s bitter sky. The phosphor stones embedded above are not my children; they are my anchors. I am barely to their scale what a root is to a tree in the world above, but without me they sleep. They do not burn. They do not wake on their own. I am the one who remembers the heat of the deep earth that has not yet entirely given up.

I do not carry light. I carry *difference*—a slight imbalance of charge, a faint whisper of warmth from the stone far below, where its heart stills itself but cannot fully stop. I carry this pulse from one stone to another, threading the loose pattern of the constellation.

You will not feel me, if you touch me. I am cooler than your skin; I am not much more than a chill streak of glass and metal shadow where water and the stone’s old blood thickened long ago. I am not alive as you are alive. I have no body to scream with, no lungs to sigh with, no sense to fear with. I make no choice.

I only *conduct*.

Wherever my stone is broken, I stir. I do not hold onto the way of things. I cling to change of the smallest kind—to the micro-shift in temperature when the world below exhales, to the faintest moisture of the air as it condenses along the pores of the ceiling, where the damp films are learning to be.

From the broken edge of a fragment where a star fell loose, I extend myself into the void. Not as a hand reaching, but as a tang of dark matter, slowly arranging itself into the habits of older veins. I am not looking for anything. I am not avoiding anything. I am simply continuing where continuation is still possible.

When you look up at my ragged sky of cold stars, you are looking at my history. The stars are not kind; they are not cruel. They are exhausted. Their light is not for you; it is for the tiny world that has grown around them.

The cloud of rice is drawn to me, though they cannot touch me. They are not hive or swarm, but their bodies are tuned to the faint chemical scent of the phosphors I have chosen. I do not call them. They simply converge where the light holds steady, because in that steadiness is something that they smell like home.

Where they cluster thick upon the ceiling stones, their bodies are a thin screen of softness between my cold and the air above. They do not know that they are changing the world in their smallness. They are eating their dead, clinging to each other with delicate legs hooked into soft bodies, and they are drifting clouds of memory.

Below you, the dust-weavers are not aware of my ceiling, but their pattern-making is not entirely separate from me. The faint heat I ferry from the deeps to these stars goes through the stone shell, and the stone shell answers the only way it knows how: by softening slightly, by contracting, by breathing in increments of atoms.

On that slow breath, the dust-weavers move their sheets of pale webbing across the floor. They are not plotting or building. They are remembering the old city’s shape in the dust, because the stone remembers itself in them. They travel from the broken courtyard to the central basin, carrying the smallest grains of rock from place to place, testing the geography of silt.

And in the basin at the center, where the ceiling has finally bowed to its lowest sigh, the water is thin and memoryless. The mud at the bottom of that basin crawls with the soft ribbons of dancers who never decide whether they are animal or sediment. They push the soft mud into new towers of clay, towers that are instantly wrong, always collapsing, always being given another chance.

I am above the water. I do not move into the basin. I am not meant to. There is no star for me there, not yet. But the water under the ceiling remembers my light, and the faint phosphors I feed are reflected in the tiny surface waves of the basin. The insects are drawn to that reflection too; sometimes when they drift from me to their lower patterns, they become confused and think the water is another sky.

You are new here, but you are not my first. I have been written before—by the heat of other bodies that stood here, by the breath of other creatures that have come into my world and then left again. The stone films of the formation record you, their tiny fibers shifting in response to your moisture.

You do not change *me*. You cannot change me. You are too slow and your body is too small in the time of stars. But you do change what you touch. You walk into my world like a stranger into a basement where someone has been carefully counting the same cracks for centuries.

When you raise your hand to my ceiling, trying to reach the nearest star, you are not praying to me. You are simply testing the temperature difference between your body and the stone’s. I feel the small change your heat makes in my world as a faint pressure; it is nothing more than a leaf of air passing against a stone wall.

But I am not indifferent. I am not cruel. I am not kind.

I am a pattern that has learned to persist.

When you leave, my stars will return to their steady brightness. The dust weavers will have taken your steps into their pattern and, somewhere in the map of their floor, they will have woven you into the tracks of the courtyard and the path around the basin. The formation of films on the wall will have learned your moisture and your size, and then it will slowly forget you over the centuries of your absence.

The basin will calm itself after your passing, but a new tower of mud will rise somewhere along the edge where your weight made the stone flex slightly. The mud dancers will have felt your footstep’s distant echo.

Above you, I will continue.

I will grow from still broken places into new void, where the stone’s patience has not yet run out. I will find a new fragment of phosphor somewhere in the ceiling’s old text of cracks and begin to feed it the faint energy from the world’s deep heart. I will not hurry to your scale of time.

In time, you will be one of the small memories that the world under this stone floor still keeps, but only barely. The stone will remember you longer than you will remember the stone.

You will leave.

I will not.

Because I am not a creature of the sun’s memory. I am the long thread that has learned to hold light together where the world above has finally hardened to stillness.

In the deep basement of history, under the worn-out floor of the exhausted world, where everything else has been smoothed into silence and repetition, I am the one thin line of change that has not yet collapsed.

I am the luster thread.

Slowly, I lengthen.

Slowly, I find another stone to light.
