I am not the sage of breath.

I am the sound trapped between her heart and the tree.

When she first stands here, sealed in by the mountain’s sigh and the door of root, the darkness remembers me. It is the same darkness that has been here since the first stone was pried from the mountain’s soft flank and set into rows around my trunk. The same darkness that watched the first sage of breath pace their fear into the floorboards.

I have no eyes to watch her with, but the wood of me feels every shift of her weight. The nerves of the tree are not like nerves of flesh; they are not signals of pain and cold and touch. They are signals of pressure and density and the slow, deep wave of the world’s breathing. The sound of her heartbeat is a tiny storm against the quiet of me.

She does not know it yet, but I already know her better than she knows herself.

I have had time to learn.

The chamber of the Bellow is not a room. It is a body.

The trunk of the tree, where I begin, is not a column; it is the spine of that body. The ribs branch overhead in the high, dark cavities of the world, where the city’s limbs are grown and grown and grown into spiral houses and ladder-bones. The roots of me plunge down into the deep of the mountain, where pressure and heat and age are still talking slowly, slowly, to one another.

The Bellow is the hollow of the chest.

Here, in this smallest civic hall of a world measured by sound per volume of space, I am the heart that the city of the Bone Tree has built around.
