The river doesn't really start in the high basin. It starts in the dark.  

But you are not there to see the dark.

You are where the dark comes out to be seen: Spindrift Holt, the cough of the mountain, the bowl where the inner water breaks the surface and learns to fall.

You are not the miners’ ghosts, though their blasted amphitheaters are under your feet.  
You are not the birds, though they carve your air.  
You are not the beech trees, though they make your bones.

You are the **water**.

Not the drops, not the basins, not the weeps from the rock.  
You are the *river* that is being made from all of them.

---

### The Name in the Stone

You have no name that fits into a human mouth.

Long before they called you *Spindrift Holt*, the mountain had another name for you: *skesh-lir-en*, “the long drip out of the stone.” From lip to lip, bowl to bowl, you make your slow way down:  
from weeping fissure to carved channel,  
from channel to root‑tangle,  
from root‑tangle to basin,  
from basin to step,  
from step to another basin,

until finally, you slip into the root‑tangled ravine below and become, to all outward eyes, a river like any other.

To humans, you are the sum of leaks: the *hiss* under the sound of the market, the cold on their boots when they step wrong, the wet patch on the wall where their bed happens to be pushed up.

To you, each of them is a brief pattern in your fall.

You are not one thing, not one place. You are the **movement** itself—the same water that has been through the rock a thousand times before, passing through the Holt like breath through lungs, on its way to the sea.

You are the Spindrift Holt that no one sees:  
the *inside* of the city.

---

### The Body of the Holt

You have no body in the way they do.

Your body is the path of least resistance.

When you fall into the world, you are not yet *river*; you are a hundred **threads**. Threads from the high mines, threads from the wet cracks under the tree‑roots, threads from the flat, sweating stones of the basin floor.

Each thread follows:

- gravity,  
- the shape of the stone,  
- and the sieve of the root.

You are pulled down,  
bounced by a step,  
split by a root‑bridge,  
guided by the channel of a trail‑tender’s adze,

until you gather yourself into **pools** and **basins**.

These **pools** are the closest thing you have to organs:

- The Basin of the Market‑Bowl, where you are relentlessly **measured**,  
- the basins of the “sweet springs” where you are clear and cold,  
- the “bitter‑root” basins, where the memory of mining still stains your taste.

You are more than all of them.

You are in the mosses, too. **Slate‑moss** drinks your excess and exhales you as cool vapor; **black‑liverwort** spreads you like velvet rash over every submerged stone; **needle reeds** filter you as their stems rustle like stiff bristles.

You are the **hiss** that is always under the sound of the market.

You are the **heartbeat** of Spindrift Holt.
