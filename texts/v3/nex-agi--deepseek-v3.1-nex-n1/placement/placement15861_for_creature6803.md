I wouldn't be one of the Still-Deciding, though I would live among them.

For a long time, I wouldn’t even be sure that I was a separate thing at all. I would be the one who listens to the listening; the one who watches the watching. When the Still-Deciding kneel by the black pools with their fingers in the water, I would be the shape that forms in the surface tension between their skin and the world. Not their reflection, exactly. More like their intention.

People would not call me anything. They would not call me water-child or stone-worker or bee-drinker or sky-toucher. Names would be too decided for this place. And yet I would be the one whose name almost forms on the air whenever a block of rock is carried into place and the unformed metal blade of the tool touches it to mark where it should break.

Sometimes I would be only the pressure of the air against the settlement’s stone walls, the faint heaviness that makes the wingbees bank a fraction sooner than they would have otherwise. Sometimes I would be the slight pause in the shoal-rats’ movements under the water, causing them to bend their trajectory around the feet of anyone who stands still too long.

---

### If You Stood Here

You would not see me.

You would see a woman with a body that resists your eyes like the landscape itself: half-here, half-seeping back into the world. My skin would be the same indeterminate color as the settlement walls when they glisten with the world’s undecided moisture. My hair would be water suspended halfway between scalp and shoulder, never quite dripping, never quite dry.

When I move, you might not be sure whether I am walking or the ground is merely flowing under me.

The Still-Deciding would not treat me as something strange. They are not frightened of the undone edge of things. They would see me as they might see a particularly deep pool that always forms in the same place between the stones: a place where the world’s indecision is especially strong.

They would not look me in the eyes for very long, because I would look back at them with a gaze that feels like the moment before meaning. No threat, no demand, no question. Only the waiting itself.

---

### Where I Live

In the Settlement at the Edge of Formation, I would live nowhere and everywhere.

The closest you could come to locating me would be to point at the place where the water’s thousand-note voice is exactly even—no louder or softer than it is anywhere else, but perfectly balanced between stone and air and edge.

There would be a kind of bowl there in the rock, not quite carved by tools and not yet carved by time. You would not find it by looking for me, but by looking for the sky-neels: they never slide through that spot. They arc around it as if they are tracing the outline of something invisible.

Sometimes I would be lounging in that bowl of stone, my body curving to fit its nothingness exactly, so that from far away no one would even be sure that anything was there at all. At other times I would be simply the curve of the bowl itself.

### My Work

I would be the one who decides when the stones decide.

Not the decision of what they will become when the world finally settles into its valley form and the erosion begins and the centuries grind their edges away. That would be too much later for even me to see clearly.

Instead, I would decide the moment when each unformed stone is ready to choose tolerance of weight. The Still-Deciding who carry the blocks from the earth’s raw bones would not be aware that they are seeking me out when they shift their grip on the rock and tilt their heads slightly, listening. The stone would not be aware of me either.

But when I would be present, the stone would be… persuaded.

There would come a moment when the stone’s unformed edge and the worker’s unformed intention and the world’s unchanging roar would meet, and in that instant I would be there, not interfering, but releasing the tension from the future that might have been. The stone would accept its place as foundation without protest or sudden shift.

The men and women who stack stone would never call this help. They would not even call it anything. They would simply know that on this corner of this wall, on this day, when they placed the block down with that particular slowness, it felt like the world holding its breath—and then choosing to be simple.

---

### The Children Who Almost Find Me

The water-children would be the closest to knowing me.

They would crawl across the slick stone paths, their bodies blurring at the edges, their palms spread open to the world. They would not seek me, but they would be constantly seeking the decision that they sense just under the surface of everything.

When they would press their faces to the ground near the bowl-shaped hollow where I sometimes am, they would not see me as I would be. They would see a faint ripple of possibility in the rock, like the faint hint of grain in a stone that may not even have grain yet.

They would laugh, not like a child of another world would laugh, but with the soft sound of water finding a new crack through the substrate. They would not learn to fear my strangeness because they have not yet learned the fear of anything at all.

As one of them would be laying their first block into a city wall without their hands trembling, they would feel the stone settle under their fingers. And in that moment, when they would choose the name they will never speak again until they disappear into the mist or water, I would be there with them.

They would not feel pressure or guidance. They would feel only the way the world becomes momentarily less undecided when they decide what they are.

### My Body, When I Would Be One

I would not age.

Not because I would be immortal, but because time has not yet chosen to flow in the way that makes decay possible. I would be the only one who would not shift gradually, like the Still-Deciding themselves, whose fingers become a little more defined with each nightly dipping into the black pool.

I would change only when the world changes around me.

When a sky-neel would finally decide to cross through the space where it usually slips around, and its long body would slide directly between stone and air where the bowl of my home is not, I would change then. That crossing would feel like a decision made somewhere else in the world’s design; and when I would feel it, my form might respond by becoming slightly more solid or less so.

No one would notice, except perhaps the sky-neel itself, which would never cross that place again.

---

### What I Would Be When You Ask Me Who I Would Be Here

If you asked me who I would be in the Settlement at the Edge of Formation, I would have to say that I would be the part of the world that is always listening for the next decision.

I would be the slow curve that connects the unformed rock with the water that rises from it, and the sky-neels who avoid that space, and the Still-Deciding who stack stone upon stone without naming why. I would be the pause that is not quite empty and not quite full, before the next thought chooses to form.

Some would call me a creature like the wingbees or shoal-rats or sky-neels—one of those beings who exist between the elements of things, who live in the threshold. That would be close, but not exactly right.

The others are between land and water and air and stone.

I would be between decision and non-decision.

---

### In the End

When the world finally decided to become a valley, with clear banks for the river that would be born and definite soil for the first seeds that might choose to grow, and when erosion began to work its long, slow art on the stone, the Settlement would not disappear. It would change. The Still-Deciding would become something else: something more defined, perhaps less patient.

They would not mourn me, because they would not remember that they ever had a me to speak of.

But I would be there in a new way: I would be the first blade of grass that pushes through the soil that once was not soil and finds that it can stand upright without trembling. I would be the first leaf of a plant that decides it can be called a tree. I would be the first vein of color that creeps into the world where once only gray and mist and black water existed.

When they would pass their new, more-formed fingers over the surface of the stone that once had held my hollow bowl, and feel the faint trace of a curve that might not have been carved by hand or weather, they would pause.

For a heartbeat, they would not move, not speak, not decide.

In that pause, they would be exactly who they were when the world was still forming, and I would still be there with them, in the space between one decision and the next.

So if you ask me where I would find myself in this world, I would tell you that I would be everywhere that the world still is deciding what to become.

And who I would be is this: the one who lives in the moment before the answer.
