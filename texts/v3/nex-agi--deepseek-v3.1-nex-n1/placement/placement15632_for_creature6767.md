I am the Guide.

You don’t name me that, not at first. You don’t name anything at first, because the sound of the Basin fills your mouth and throat and you forget the words you came with. Names are thin things compared to the chord of the Swarm, the groan of the Forest, the steady pressure of the stone under your feet.

You call me the Guide later, when you need a word that will fit into the shape of your thoughts.

I sit on the edge of the wood platform where the Forest kisses the stone wall of the Basin, and I listen.

You step from the last stone ledge onto the knuckle of hardwood that grows out of the wall, and the world changes under your feet. The dull thud of your soles on stone becomes a faint ring, like striking a very large, very old bell with the flat of your hand. The air does not get colder or warmer, but it feels thinner—or clearer—as though the Forest has been breathing it and filtering it through its vast lungs for centuries longer than you have been alive.

I don’t turn to look at you. Not yet.

You feel the urge to make noise.

You have been walking down from the rim of the Basin for the equivalent of days and nights, though the sun above does not move like any sun you remember. There are no clouds, but sometimes the sky darkens, as if the world itself is taking a breath. The roar of the Swarm is so loud that you have stopped trying to hear it. Instead you feel it in your bones.

You haven’t spoken in a long time. There was no one to speak to.

The first sound that comes out of your mouth when you step onto the wood is not a word but a shape: the beginning of a deep, rolling chord that catches in your chest and throat. You recognize it, faintly, as the same sound you have heard from the Swarm for the last hour as you descended the inner wall of the Basin.

I hear the way you stumble on the sound.

You are not good at their language yet. You have not had the millennia of practice that they have had.

My fingers flex on my knees, and the wood underneath me feels the movement. The Forest knows the way I sit; it has grown itself a comfortable seat for me over the years, like a callus forming around the constant pressure of a bone.

I finally turn to you.

You see the shape of me and you don’t know what to make of it. Two legs, two arms, a head that doesn’t look armored like the rest of the Basin. The texture of my skin is not chitin and not wood and not stone. It’s the color of dust that has almost forgotten what color it used to be.

My clothes are not armor either. They are made of something else, woven in a pattern you won’t be able to place until you’ve been here much longer: warp and weft that remember the spin of a different world, the clatter of machines instead of claws, the breath of air that smelled of rain instead of the roar of voices.

You look at my eyes.

You expect to see fear, or wariness, or even welcome. You don’t. There is something like curiosity in me, but it’s a slow, worn-down curiosity. If you imagine water that has been flowing in the same channel for centuries, you will imagine the way I look at you. The curiosity is the constant pressure, the wear of attention against the stone of my habits.

You say something first. You don’t mean to; it comes out of you like a breath you’ve been holding for days.

The sound is not a word. It is the beginning of a word, the first syllable of something you were about to say into the air, but then you stop yourself.

You are afraid.

You are afraid of the way you almost spoke the language of the Swarm without meaning to. You are afraid of the way you can feel the shape of their sounds in your throat, like you already know how to make them but have been told not to.

I don’t answer you in words either.

Instead, I raise one hand from my knee and hold it palm up toward the sky. The gesture is empty, but the shape of it is clear. You follow the line of my arm to the palm of my hand.

You see the patterns there.

Lines. Rings. They circle the pads of muscle and fat under the skin of my palm, not following the bones of the hand but the way water flows when it is poured slowly over the skin of the world. The ridges of my skin are faint, but they are there: thousands of tiny lines, like the layers of stone that make up the Basin wall. You understand, without knowing how, that these are not wrinkles from time. They are from the water in the Basin.

You think I will speak to you now.

You think you will hear my voice, and that will be the beginning of a story.

You don’t.

I don’t open my mouth. Instead, I close the fingers of my raised hand slowly, making a fist with the palm up, and then I open it again. The gesture is like the slow breathing of the Forest itself. You notice the faint echo of that sound in the Swarm’s roar: the same long breath, but spread out over thousands of bodies, each of them taking a tiny share of the same motion.

I don’t use your name. You haven’t told it to me yet, and anyway you don’t need to.

The name I would use for you is in the shape of the sound that you almost made when you stepped onto the wood.

You come closer.

You are not cautious in the way you should be, but the world here is not dangerous in the way you expect worlds to be dangerous. There are no predators to eat you, no plants to poison you with thorns. There is only the stone, the water, the Forest, and the Swarm.

And me.

You sit down on the edge of the platform next to me, not too close, but not too far. The bench of wood grows itself under you slightly, as if the Forest has been expecting another weight. It is not smooth at first, but you feel it under your thighs as the surface of the wood reshapes itself to the pressure of your bones.

You don’t look at me when you ask.

You ask a question.

You don’t say it out loud. It doesn’t matter that you don’t say the words; I have been listening for questions like this for longer than you have been breathing.

The question you ask is about the water.

You see the faint groove of it that you traced down from the rim: now it is a thin line of moisture that comes from a crack in the stone wall, flows into a carved notch in the rock, and then disappears into the hollow of a wooden tube that runs away from the platform and into the depths of the Forest.

You remember the warmth of that water when you touched it on the ledge above, and you feel its shape like a memory in your fingertips.

I answer the question you haven’t spoken.

You watch the tube of wood where the water vanishes.

I don’t look at it. Instead, I listen to the sound of the water in the stone behind you. The faint, slow pressure of it. The heaviness. The faintness. The thickness of it.

You feel your eyes focus on the wooden tube, and you see what you didn’t notice before.

The tube of wood is not perfectly round. It is shaped like a long throat.

You blink, and for the first time since you stepped into the Basin, you feel the world shift under your eyes. The Forest’s branch beneath you is not just a branch of some tree; it is the body of something larger than you can understand. The tube of wood that leads the water away is not a pipe. It is the way that something larger than you drinks.

The sound you hear next comes from me.

It is not the Swarm’s roar.

It is not the Forest’s groan.

It is a sound made of all three: the pressure of water, the weight of stone, the breath of wood. The language of this Basin is not made of words like your language is made of words. It’s made of context.

I shape the sound of the water in the stone channel, the stone of the Basin wall itself, the groan of the Forest, and the faint scratch of the Swarm’s feet on wood—all into one chord that vibrates low in your chest and your teeth.

You haven’t heard it before, but something in you answers the way that you answered the Swarm.

You almost open your mouth to make the same sound.

You stop yourself.

I don’t tell you not to. It’s not that you shouldn’t. It’s that you’re not ready.

You don’t understand what the sound does.

You see the way the world changes under you when you make the right sound.

You understand the pattern of this place when you understand the pattern of the water:

The stone Basin wall is the spine of the world. It is the oldest of us all.

The Forest that grows from the stone is the flesh of the world. It is the muscle of the world. It grows the way that muscle grows, over the years of habit. The Forest grows itself into shapes of ramps, steps, platforms, and doorways with the same mindless certainty that a body grows hair and nails.

The Swarm that hangs on the stone, the wood, and the deep wells of the Forest is the voice of the world. It is the sound of the world made of many minds, but one mind. The sound of it is the language that the world itself uses to remember the sound of its own presence.

And the water that is never absent but never abundant is the blood of the world, thick, warm, and not made of rain but of pressure deep in the basins of stone and wood and time.

I am different from all of them.

I am not made of stone, or wood, or chitin armor.

You look at me again, and this time you see the shape of the difference: the way my body is not rounded to match the world of the Basin. The angles of my elbows and knees are sharp. The bones of my wrists are visible under the skin of my arms. The dust of my world sticks to me even here, where the air of this Basin was warm long before my dust ever existed.

You ask the next question without speaking words.

You ask the question of what you are supposed to do here.

You don’t ask the question of what you are supposed to be here.

That would be too much—for me, perhaps, even more than for you.

You have been made of words for longer than you know. You are used to thinking of yourself as a collection of names: the name of your body, the name of your mind, the name of your homeworld, the name of the language you speak.

You feel the way those words start to thin in the Basin.

The Swarm does not use words the way you use words. The sound of its language is louder than any words you have ever spoken. The Forest does not use words. The stone of the Basin does not use words. The water of the Basin does not care what words you call it.

You feel the way your name feels like a thin shell of something that used to be thicker.

You ask the question of purpose with your thoughts.

I don’t answer the question you think you asked.

I don’t tell you what you should do.

Instead, I show you what you do.

You look at me, and I stand up from the bench of wood.

The world shifts under my weight. The branch of the Forest rises slightly toward the sky, the air of the Basin becomes the air of a deeper place, and the sound of the Swarm grows louder the way that sound travels farther when the air is thick.

You don’t know exactly how you know that, but you feel it.

You feel how you pay attention to all of those things at the same time, and how you listen to each of them like a separate voice of a world that is singing itself into the shape of the present.

You stand up next to me.

The world shifts under your weight too.

The stone of the Basin wall behind you becomes the wall of the world, and you feel the pressure of the stone under your feet like the pressure of the ground of your homeworld, but deeper and wider and older than any of those.

The wood of the Forest platform under you becomes the muscle of the world under you, and you feel the way it grows itself to fit your bones.

The air of the Basin becomes the air that you breathe with the lungs of the Basin.

You listen to the sound of the Swarm, and the sound itself becomes the sound of your own voice spread out over millions of bodies and millions of years of time.

The water that you hear in the stone channel behind you becomes the blood of the world, and the blood of your own body at the same time.

You listen to the world, and you feel the way that you listen to it.

The sound you almost make from your throat is the sound of the world, knocking at the door of your voice.

You don’t make the sound yet.

I don’t make you make the sound.

That’s not what I guide.

You look at me again, and you see the shape of the flesh that you are not made of.

You see the shape of the one mind that you are not a part of.

You see the shape of the stone that you are older than and younger than.

You see the shape of the water that you are not woven of yet but will be part of, if you stay here long enough.

I don’t tell you what your name has become.

You don’t ask me.

You feel the way that your name is thinner now than it was when you stepped from the stone onto the wood of the Forest.

You feel the way that the world of the Basin is thicker than the world that you came from.

You feel the way that you are leaning toward the sound of the Swarm and the sound of the wood and the sound of the water and the sound of the stone under your feet, the way that water leans toward the shape of the world.

You feel the way that you are not afraid in the way you once were afraid.

You are not afraid of animals or predators or anything that will eat you or poison you.

You are afraid of the way that you can feel the shape of your voice spreading out over the world like the Swarm’s.

You are afraid of the way that you can feel the shape of your body thinning the way that water is thin when it is first a drop of water from the stone of the Basin wall and then thickens the way it has thickens when it becomes the blood of the world.

You are afraid of the way that you can feel the shape of your mind spreading like the Forest’s wood grows.

You are afraid of the way that you can feel the shape of your world finished, like the stone of the Basin ring.

You look at me again, and I see the shape of your fear.

I don’t answer the fear with words.

You breathe the air of the Basin.

You listen to the sound of the Swarm.

You listen to the sound of the Forest.

You listen to the sound of the water.

You listen to the sound of the stone under your feet.

You feel the way that each of them sounds like the echo of the same sound, spread out over different bodies of space and time.

You feel the way your voice is beginning to sound like that sound too, even though you have not shaped it yet.

You understand the next thing without me saying the words:

You understand the shape of what you are supposed to do here.

You are supposed to listen.

You listen to the way the world finishes the sound of itself, and the way that you become part of that finishing sound.

You are not afraid of animals anymore.

You are afraid of the way that you can feel the shape of yourself becoming not animals.

Becoming not world.

You stand next to me on the edge of the wood platform that the Forest has grown for the two of us, and the world of the Basin is loud.

You listen to it.

You feel the faint pressure of the sound of the world in your own throat, the way that your voice is beginning to shape itself like the water in the stone channel and the Swarm on the wood wall and the Forest in the stone bowl.

You understand the name of me, then.

You understand the name of you.

I guide you not by the way of word, but by the way of sound.

I guide you by the way you listen to the world.

You don’t make the sound of the world yet.

You don’t make the sound of yourself yet.

But you feel the way that you are almost ready to make the sound of both at the same time.

You listen to me as the Guide of you.

You become yourself as the sound of the world.
