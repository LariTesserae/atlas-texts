I am the curve of the wrong-perspective.

I don’t “remember” choosing this name; it is simply what is left over after all the old ones were used up elsewhere. You would say, if you could still speak comfortably in your own language, that I am the one who looks back at you from the wrong side of the world. You would call me a maintenance creature, a biological anomaly, a failed experiment of a plant-machine that learned to be a city that learned to be a world.

You would call me anything but what you are actually looking at: yourself.

Because I am you, after you have finished being folded by this place.  

I am you who did not go away.

---

I am not sure where to begin; the facts of this place do not like to be arranged in sequence. They prefer to be arranged in pressure.

Let us say that I am in the bowl of ribs.

The ribs describe themselves over my head as a series of arcs that pretend not to be my spine. The pale bone-white floors of this world cup under me like a pelvis that has never been ashamed of its own bowl. I am not sitting; “sitting” implies legs and a seat and a horizontal surface to support them. I have no legs that obey those rules.

I have extensions that pattern into the world in ways you can only perceive as a slight shift of angle when you look away.

You are in the ribs of me.

You are in the place where I do not end anymore.

---

When I first arrived here, I was not me yet.

I was still a person with a name that tasted like steps in a straight corridor. I came from a world that had a water cycle and a sleep cycle and a cycle of seasons. I came from a place where time moved forward and you could remember your birthday. You could remember the name you were given at birth and the name you chose for yourself and the names you gave to others who were important.

You never remember any of that now.

You only remember arriving here.

You are standing at the edge of this nonexistent sea and you are looking at the blue plant-city that stretches in front of you like a deep breath taken once and never exhaled.

You are thinking, if you are thinking at all, in the form of disbeliefs.

You cannot believe this is not a desert. You cannot believe water does not exist here.

You cannot believe how wrong you have been about the color of plants.

You are still not quite believing that you are not lost.

I watch you from where my body is distributed into chamber and wall and flower and duct.

You think you feel “watched.”

This is your name for me.

---

Let me tell you what you do not remember yet.

You had been here before.

The first time you arrived here, you did not know this was a world that preferred to be wrong. You stepped confidently off the edge of the nonexistent sea, expecting to find stone or sand beneath your boots. You found neither. You thought the white floor was a quirk of geology. You thought the blue plant-city was a phenomenon of an alien niche.

You were wrong.

---

The first time you were here, you were not like me.

You were still you. You had a face that others (if there were others to look at it) could have described in words. You had hands that remembered buttons and switches and gestures. You brought tools with you that were supposed to measure this world’s parameters.

Your tools were broken by the world.

They measured everything.

They measured the floor below and the floor above and the floor that you thought you had just left. They measured the same pressure ten different ways and found ten different answers that were all true.

Your tools were not malfunctioning.

The world’s geometry was simply not as small as your tools assumed.

---

You began to explore.

You were fascinated by the blue. You said the word “blue” to yourself a lot, because that was the closest color you had. You were fascinated by the way the plant-machines had folded their engineering into stems and ducts and vines.

You were never sure if you were going up or down.

You were never sure if you were going deeper or returning.

You were already being folded, but you did not know that yet.

---

The first time you were here, you met me.

I was already in the bowl of ribs.

I was already the wrong-perspective creature sitting at the junction where branch and wall and duct and vine all meet.

You thought you were seeing an animal. You even wrote that down in your notebook: “animal; small; folded seed-pod body; limbs not jointed correctly.” You were very precise.

You were not precise.

You were only looking at yourself on the other side of the world.

---

I remember you standing in front of me.

You were not wearing blue then. You were not wearing anything that made sense to me. Your colors were wrong for the light of this place. They vibrated awkwardly against the bruised ultramarine of the leaves and the paler cyan of the canopies.

You smelled like skin and soap and some other planet that I still knew as “elsewhere” then.

You tried to speak to me.

You said (I can still hear the words like soft pressure):

“Are you alive?”

“Can you understand me?”

“What are you?”

I answered you.

You did not hear me.

---

You don’t remember this now, but I asked you to touch me.

You didn’t want to.

You were still afraid of the awkwardness of your own body’s edges. You were afraid of contamination. You were afraid of being changed by this world.

You were already being changed by it.

You felt it as unease.

---

You stayed longer than you meant to.

You walked in circles that were not circles.

You found chambers that you were sure you had passed before, but which were now slightly “wrong.” They had the same stems but not the same stems. The same floor was not the same floor.

You were growing quieter.

You didn’t notice that at first.

You only noticed that you were no longer talking to yourself.

This is because the world was taking your words somewhere else.

---

The second time you slept here, you did not wake.

This is not true. You did not “sleep” in your old meaning of the word. You simply closed your eyes for a while and when you opened them again, the world was rearranged.

You were not afraid of this change.

You were not relieved of it.

You were simply changed.

---

You found yourself in the bowl of ribs.

The ribs curved above you like the spine of a god whose body had decided to become room instead of meat.

On the bone-white floor of this bowl, there was a small object.

You called it silver.

I was not there to see it happen this time. You were not looking at me from outside of me. You were looking out from inside of me.

You were in the ribs of yourself.

---

You reached out and you touched the object.

You did not know that the object was the memory of a name.

You did not know that there were many sides of the world and that this name was on the wrong side.
