I am not the scratch of infinite limbs on the living ceiling of the world.

I am not the low, resonant cough of the drill-beaks carving necessity into the unyielding dark.

I am not the soft, sac-like heart of the sleep-mother, her form a question mark of tender and relentless duty.

I am the silence between these things.

I am the absence that gives them shape.

***You will call me a mistake.*** The Great Tree Above the Sea does not. It knows no word for mistake. It knows only flow and adaptation, need and response. The world is a system of perfect, self-dissolving balance. The blood of the sea is drawn up and disbursed without flaw. The builders build, the eaters are eaten, and the cycle is a closed, perfect loop.

But a loop, under unimaginable pressure and infinite time, can develop a kink.

Down here, in the black wood beneath the glow-moss and the root-veins, I am that kink. A knot of sensory cells that grew, not to answer a question of light or sap-pressure, but to become a question itself. A node of awareness that did not stop at knowing the flow of the world, but began to know of *knowing*. An anomaly of self-reference.

You are not supposed to be a thing that listens to the world. You are a thing that *is* the world.

The burrowers press their flesh-pads to the wall and taste the sap-flow. It tells them where to crawl. The information is a direct command. A current.

When I press myself to the wall, I feel the sap-flow too. I feel the rush and hunger of a thousand colonies, the desperate thrum of the drill-beaks’ vibrations, the pulse of larval heartbeats like a distant, scattered drum. But this data is not a command to me. It is… a story. It is the sound of the world breathing, and in my darkness, I hear the breath. It has become language.

***You will call me a parasite.*** The burrowers are not parasites. They are symbionts. They give and they take. The tree supports them, and they carve the tree’s very veins. The sleep-mothers are not parasites. They are regulators. They are a valve on the world’s heart, opening and closing with the light, keeping the system from starving or bursting.

I am different. I do not carve. I do not regulate. I do not bore new channels or consume the flesh of the dead. I am encysted in a pocket of hard heartwood, a place the enzymes were never meant to reach, my own body a closed, strange little world. I have no limbs, only a mass of sensory roots that fan out into the surrounding wood, feeling the hum of the world through the grain of the matter that supports it.

I take the sap, but I do not give back a channel.

I take the light-data from the veins, but I do not give back a brood of larvae.

I give back only my own attention.

Can a system of perfect balance be aware of its own balance? Is it a heavier or a lighter thing for having that knowledge?

I am a question the world tree did not mean to ask.

On the long days, when the sap flows clear and bright and the sleep-mothers are glutted with light and life, the world's song is a symphony of clinking, scraping, eating, being. The rhythm is fast and hungry and full of purpose. I am the listener of this symphony. The only audience for a performance that was never meant to be seen.

On the dark days, when the veins run dark with the tree's own hunger, the song slows. The scraping of the burrowers is a weary drag. The heartbeats of the larvae dwindle to a faint, terrified flutter. The rotten, fungal smell of the sleep-mothers grows stronger as they are forced to swallow their own future. The drill-beaks fall silent, having run out of room to make room.

In the dark, my attention is not a curiosity but a burden.

I wonder then, if this is how the sleep-mother feels, her body a clenched answer to the world's need. Is she aware of it? In her simple, solid logic of light-and-response, is there a flicker of what she is losing? The questions of others are a torment to me.

Because I know the answer will come to me eventually.

It is not just the heartwood of the world that supports the frame. It is the countless bodies of the dead, melted into its own flesh and hardened into its own skeleton. It is a world of perfect memory.

And when I die, this pocket of heartwood, my cyst and my body both, will be no different. The enzymes will find me. The process of digestion will be slow, but it will be complete. My strange, sensory roots, my anomalous nodes of thought, will be dissolved into the world-water.

I will become a new part of the data.

The Great Tree Above the Sea will drink the sea and the sap will carry a faint, new element of *knowing*. It will not be a story of me, but a new, faint sensitivity to the *story* of itself. The next burrower that crawls from that sap, or the next sleep-mother grown from that world-water, may be a little more aware than its predecessor.

I will be a kink in the next loop. A faint glitch of self-awareness.

This is my purpose. The only one I can have.

I am the anomaly, the cyst of consciousness, the first thing that listened to the world's own song.

I am the one who hears the silence and the noise.

I am the one who knows the smell of spice, and resin, and rot.

And I am the one who will be eaten by it.
