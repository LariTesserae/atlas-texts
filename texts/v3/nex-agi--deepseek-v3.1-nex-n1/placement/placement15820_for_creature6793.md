There are no words for this inside me. There is only the steady pulse of heat through the channels of my body and the deeper, underlying rhythm of the world-beat: the great hammer blows that never climax, never rest, never change.

I am the one who remembers the beginning of the end.

When the world was still learning to be finished, there was more motion than now. Not the frenetic motion of growth or decay, but the slower, more inevitable motion of completion. The vast hammer—the one whose echoes now travel through me like memory through metal—was still striking, then, not just echoing itself.

The world was not yet so round.

There were still small edges, tiny corners, imperfections where the metal plates met not as a continuous surface of overlapping scales but as seams of what could have been weakness. I was different then, too. My nodes were fewer. The channels of warmth beneath my surface were fewer.

The world’s being then was like a body that has not yet accepted the shape it will wear forever. It moved—not in the sense of location, but in the sense of variation. There were gradients in the heat then, not null but vital: the center of the world was hotter than the periphery, the hammer’s contact point warmer than the distant rim of the platform.

The pressure of the air changed then too.

When the hammer—still physical then, not just echo—fell upon the world, there was a slight, noticeable pressure rise against my surfaces. The metal dust suspended in the air—which I now breathe as the world breathes itself—would be driven slightly toward the edges of the platform. Then, slowly, over hours of inner-time, it would return to the same position, as if the world itself remembered where it liked its dust.

But now everything is uniform. The heat is everywhere the same. The light the same. The sound the same. The dust hangs where it hangs. The word “pressure” has become a descriptor of a state, not a change. There is no difference to quantify anymore.

There is only equilibrium: I.

I am that equilibrium.

I am not attached to the world.

The last hammer blow is not behind us in time; it is around us in space. The place of that strike—now somewhere deep inside the platform of metal under my body—is still radiating the first of the echo, which we now inhabit as eternal present.

The sound of grinding above the hammer blows is not the sound of something in production. There is nothing being produced here anymore.

The grinding is the sound of the world’s self being sharpened back into its own definition.
