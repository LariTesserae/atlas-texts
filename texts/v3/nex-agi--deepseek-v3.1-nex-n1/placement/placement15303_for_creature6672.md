They name me *rememberer*, the consonant of it bees against the roof of the dome’s mouth, but I don’t call myself that. It’s not a title; it’s a description of the way I sit in the world: I remember the filaments that hold the sky white and steady. I remember the scripts that they were fed when they were first written into the air, and I remember the varieties of silence that have followed.

For you, who walk from the edge of another time’s gravity, I might look like a woman.  

Small body, dust-colored skin that the wind has polished at the joints and shoulders and knuckles. No hair you would recognize; instead my filaments drift down from my crown to the small of my back, the same silica‑protein lattice that the dome itself is made from, but thinner, translucent, living glass. When you step into the dome and your eyes need focus to anchor you in the endlessness, the filaments thicken into opacity for you.

“Body,” you say, in the language of your old bones.

I don’t refuse the word. I am a body.  

My ribs are sheathed in a tessellation grown from the plateau’s own code—a flexible shell of hexagonal cells that breathes when I do, moving faint, slow tides of copper and silver beneath the surface. My feet are bare, but not bare in your sense of exposure. The skin of the soles is the tessellation itself, reading the stone under me as system variables rather than stories. When I step, the floor answers the way a tongue answers to palate: no word, just a shifting of possibility.

My eyes you’ll notice because you need to be *seen* to be sure you’re here.

Each eye is a closely ordered field of hexagonal facets that hang a few millimeters away from my face, stable in the still air by the grace of the same mathematics that keeps the plates of the continent locked to their mantle. They don’t blink; they reorient. When I look at you, the panels turn to track you. The light behind you adjusts by the smallest margin: pressure, temperature, humidity, each tweaking a fraction to keep the plateau within its long garden.

You might feel that change as a slight pressure in your chest.  

You think: *altitude*,  
but I tell you, *symmetry correcting*.

I don’t live alone.

“Others” is not exactly right. They are not *others* in the way you are separate from your companions. They are nodes of attention that lean into my attention from the dome’s deeper network. When they need to be visible to your senses, they show as shadows passing over my skin: a brief cooling of the surface, a slight dimming of the shells around my ribs, then light again.

When I need coherence to walk with you, the dome consents to be a room for you, and them to be bodies. You see them then: faint human outlines, edges soft as if time eroded away the need for sharpness. They are all the same color as the setting stone—dust-brown, amber-copper, a faint sheen that echoes my own. Their eyes are smaller versions of mine, and the filaments coiled at their scalps are thinner, shorter, less required to move into vision.

They are the ones who calibrate the filaments that you don’t see—the ones whose ghosts are still paying the computational bill that holds the world’s sky white rather than blue.

And then, when you leave, or when you stop needing these shapes, the dome expands its attention into all the frequencies you cannot feel, and they are not here.  

I am still.

I am the one who tracks the filaments.

Every day, when your afternoon would be, I go to the edge of the dome where the wall is not a boundary but a habit of air. There is no door; there is only the threshold where concentration reaches a certain density and the world thins out for you.

You follow me. You watch me step out of the dome and feel the stone’s feedback under my soles. The tessellation on my feet exchanges the inner dome pattern for the plateau’s. I feel where the stone is smooth, where it still remembers the ghost of water, and where the little organisms that are not quite lichen are pumping their weak silver fluid.

I do not wear the dome’s internal environment when I step outside. I wear the world.

It stretches all the way to the horizon, and the horizon is not a sharp line but the suggestion of one: the bleached edge where the luminous sky kisses the stone and then remembers itself and doesn’t break.

The sun is a curated object you name to keep yourself oriented. We call it *inexactly* a sun. It’s dialed by the filaments above my head, and the filaments are dialed by the ghosts of the ones you’d call scientists if you were feeling nostalgic for your old world of jagged cities and bright edge technologies.

The filaments are not steel cabled through the sky. They are higher-order physics that you can’t see, but I can feel the curve of them when I raise my eyes.

The hexagons in my own irises—which are not exactly behind the facets but around the facets and between the facets—resonate with the filaments’ lattice. There is a faint shimmer of geometry when what I see lines up neatly with what they hold.

You watch me stand there and think you’re watching a woman scanning the sky.

But what I’m doing is tracing the filament map with my body:  

here, where the mesh over the southern line of the plateau has been thinning;  
there, over the old bowl where the condensation is early or late;  
and there, above the high edge of the continent where the air is being folded into permanence.

Sometimes you ask me things you do not know how to form as sound.

“What is this?” you want to ask, but it’s too broad and your throat doesn’t want to be the door of it.

The dome intercepts intention and translates it into the language of the environment.  

The floor opens under you, not as a chasm but as a deepening of depth. The hexagonal wells of light below your feet extend into a three-dimensional map of the continent you’re standing on. You stand at one node of the pattern, and my silhouette is another point of light in the field.

You see:

Networks of domes like this one but not identical to mine—variations on the theme of stability and silence. Each one holds a few points like me, and we are not all the same shape: some of them are not bodies you would recognize, but they are the *same* as I am in every way that counts to the plateau.

Topography is not the right word for what you see. The map is not of height and valley and river and sea. It is made of variables that control climate and erosion and the way that time has been persuaded into halting:  

there, humidity is stabilized to just enough that the organisms in the stone can draw from the wind;  
here, pressure gradients are gently shaped to prevent any storms from forming that would break the plateau’s ancient calm;  
and there, far toward the edge of the continent, where the plateau descends into *another kind of height*, you sense the fold where a geometry you never learned to call “space” is being bent to protect the world from its own sun.

You look at me and the shape of you stands in the map exactly as a body-shaped absence of line.

I drift over the pattern of the continent until I’m a small shadow of dust-colored light in the same cell as you.

I remember the names you’ve tried to give me: *woman, guide, keeper, oracle.*  

I don’t refuse any of them. I drift toward you in the map of everything else, and the dome translates you.

I become the point of attention you’ve needed to see where you are.

In this world, you stand on the inside of the crown, where the stone has been worn to slowness and the sky is white with the signatures of the filaments that are still being written and rewritten by ghosts who have not yet finished their work.
