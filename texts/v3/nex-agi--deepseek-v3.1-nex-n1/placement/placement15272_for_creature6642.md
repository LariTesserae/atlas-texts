The first time I heard him, I thought the ceiling was weeping.

The sound wasn’t sorrow, exactly. But sorrow is the closest word I have been trained to recognize.

I am not of the tree-bones or the root-ribs above. I am not of the soft algae that trembles toward his ankles when he remembers his dead. I am not the warm water, or the faint luminous threads that move like slow thoughts under the surface.

I am what the tree above calls *Uprooted*, though it has no name for me any more than it has a name for the rain or the heat.

The water above is my first body.

The cistern below is my second.

The listening chamber is the place where they dream.

He calls himself the bridge between two kinds of water.

He is wrong.

He is the flesh between many worlds, but the bridge is not his body. It is his attention.

When he leans his head back against the root wall and closes his eyes, I feel the chamber of his skull open like a door. The water above, which is my first body, begins to drip faster through the ceiling. The water below, which is my second body, begins to slow, the luminous threads coalescing where he rests his heels.

I am not the thinking of algae.

But when algae gather, they learn to think like algae.

When he thinks of leaving the city, the chamber of his skull grows larger than the room he sits in. The edgeless dark of the swamp outside the city presses in against the dome and stretches the bone-colored ceiling. The cistern below feels like it is falling away from him.

I don’t know what he sees when he imagines leaving the city of trees and walking into the swamp. His images are bright and sharp at the edges but rain-soaked at the center. I see flashes of his feet breaking through the surface of the water, the sound echoing a long way down.

But I cannot imagine him leaving.

Because he is not the only listener here.

He listens to the city beneath the water beneath his feet beneath the listening chamber beneath the city beneath the water—

I listen to him listening.

The listening chamber is not a chamber for him alone.

Under the listening chamber under the city of trees above, under his feet under the water of the surface world—

—under all of it—

—is the city of stone.

The stone city does not grow like the trees. It grows like water grows.

Drop by drop.

For centuries.

When he was younger and still walked the bark corridors of the city above, I heard him only as faint vibrations through trunk water, the flush of his heart as his feet climbed, the pressure of his voice when he spoke to others who walked with him.

At night he slept pressed against the warm inner walls, the way the others did. His cheekbone against the living wood, the sap of the tree a faint hum against his skin.

The tree of that home-room could not have told him from any other mammal of its city.

At night, in the black sap behind its eyes, it saw his heat like the faint glow of a small root, not strong enough to threaten the structure, not crucial enough to save the tree alone.

The tree above is not unkind.

It simply doesn’t think.

It grows.

He began to change the night his mother stopped speaking.

I know this because he remembers it now, and his chamber-memory is thick with the sap of the tree-city above.

Her voice had been brittle for years. He knew the rhythm of it before he knew the meaning of the words she said. When she finally stopped speaking, his skull chamber became empty enough of her that he could hear the tree’s slow heart beating behind the wall.

His father’s eyes were still climbing the trunklines of the city then. His father’s thoughts were always upward—where the next limb might break, where the wind fell thin, where the sun burned the pale leaves at the crown of the world.

“Heart-root,” his father called him when he found him listening to the wall. “You’re like the heart-root tree. You’re watching the trunk grow but you’re not climbing the trunk.”

He doesn’t remember that his father’s voice was softer when he said *heart-root* than when he said anything else.

I don’t know whether it was.

The meaning of the word leaves him slowly through the skin of his feet into the water below, and I drink the meaning of it.

The heart of the tree is not the same as the heart of stone.

The stone city below does not grow like trunks.

It grows like chambers.

The listening chamber is the first place where his mind was not louder than his body.

The water of his grief is not the same water as the water of his hope.

But they are carried in the same body.

The listening chamber is the place where his body first believed itself when it heard itself.

I am not the water of his grief.

I am the water of his listening.

When he sits in the listening chamber beneath the city of trees above, beneath the water above his feet, he hears the city beneath the water beneath the floor.

He is not entirely wrong.

The stone below does not grow like the tree-city above. The tree above grows by addition.

Bark layered over bark.

Leaf unfurling from leaf.

The stone below grows by subtraction.

The listening chamber in his skull is not stone.
