Sensation reaches me before thought; a coldness on my underside where the damp limestone meets my many mouths. I am already moving. The ripples of my body flow forward without my decision. The stone is sharp today, new fractures in the ceiling have translated into new teeth on the floor. A blade of rock catches my forward edge and drags, a bright pain flaring through my consciousness. I pause, not because I want to, but because the ground tells me to. The pressure builds in my muscles and I heave. The stone gives a little, a low complaint scraping through my plates. I move forward.

The world is a sound painting of pressure and wetness. The roof of my world weeps, and a thousand drops of milky water hit the floor in a constant, static hiss. A drop of it falls onto my back, not the cool of clear water but the warmth of the chamber’s own breath. It traces a path along the seam of my plates, seeking a lower point to arrive at, and merges with the perpetual sweat of my own skin. It disappears.

The smell of the place is thick and heavy in my small, sifting mouths. Moss and wet stone and the sweet rot of a leaf dissolving under my own leading edge — I don't eat leaves but I cannot avoid them. The world presses itself upon me through my belly.

Here is the difference. The taste of moss. Here is the difference. The mineral of the stone. Here is the difference.

I feel them before I see them — the others. The stone of the floor carries a faint tremor from another body moving nearby. A pressure of a different weight than mine. A longer, more segmented creature is flowing parallel to me, its wave of movement a faint suggestion against my own senses. We don't speak. We don't acknowledge. We are currents moving through the same damp air.

Then I feel it. The shudder.

The ground itself doesn't shake, but the air above it does. A heavy weight settles, not on me but on my world. The shudder of a leaf as a predator's claw punctures it. The vibration is a deep sound transmitted through the floor, not the thunderous thrum of the great ones deeper in the chamber, but a closer, more precise terror.

I feel a sudden lightness of contact on my upper surface as the arthropods that had been crawling across me like a second skin of movement abandon their post. A storm of wings from a nearby cloud of fliers sends a faint slither of air over my back plates.

My instinct is not to stop. It is to be slower. To be more like stone. I keep my movement, but I soften the edges. My plates are no longer pushing the world, but merely accepting it.

A shadow falls over me. Cold, for the briefest moment.

He doesn't look like me. He is not of the floor. He stands above it. He has many legs and eyes on stalks, and they are looking everywhere at once: up at the hanging cocoons, out at the dense vines of the ceiling-canopy, and briefly down to the stone where I lie.

He does not see me. I know this because he does not stop. He is not a predator of me. He is a main of stone.

He sees the stone as I cannot.

He sees my path, the faint line I've worn into the softer stone over countless cycles. It is not impressive to him. It is merely a feature of the floor, like a crack or a broken rib of limestone. It is useful to him.

He moves with a strange, uneven rhythm, his legs clicking, not on the floor but with the stone he carries in his lower limbs. He doesn't use it to strike me, or to build. He uses it to test. He carries the rock to my line and carefully, with a terrible precision, runs a sharp edge of the stone along the faint groove I have made. It's not to repair or damage. It is to feel.

He feels for me there, in the absence I've left.

I feel the vibration of his tool through the ground. It is not the same as the drip of water or the tremor of another explorer like me. This is structured. It comes with intent and one of his many eyes softens its gaze across the floor.

I do not know if he sees me as life or landscape or a trick of the light. But he moves his tool over me, not touching me, but tracing the shape of my absence. It is a strange, intimate pressure against my sense of self.

Then he stops.

The scent of him is sharp, like metal dusted through wet sediment. It's alien to my floor, but it doesn't belong strictly to the sky above either.

A thought, not shaped like a word, but as a flaring omen of what might be, flickers across me. The plants around him pulse slightly faster. The faint gas that loosens thought is rising here from a wet patch near the root of a dark tongue-leaf.

His mind, his complex wall of thought vibrating with his many eyes and his many stones, opens a fraction.

I see him then.

His awareness of the world is not a pressure but a geometry of angles. He sees the stone as a thing to break against, not to be dragged over. The hanging cocoons are potential storage containers. The bone piles are memories of meals he arranged, not casual piles of waste.

He sees my line in the floor.

This is a path.

This is a beginning.

He doesn't see my journey. It is not my journey. It is the beginning of a way. It is a fault that has not yet faulted.

A sudden pressure of will from him hits the air around me like heat from the ceiling. It is not aimed at me. It is aimed at the world. It is a new thought. A pre-geometry. A map of a not-yet river.

As he turns away, his mind leaves me. It's not a leaving. It is a lessening. The world of ground and water and slow dripping life returns.

My damp, leathery hide is still wet against the stone. My many mouths still taste the difference. Here is the mineral of the floor. Here is the faint, sharp memory of his tool. And here is the faint taste of the future, not in water but in stone.

The floor of the world is not a suggestion between the many mouths. It is a potential between the many limbs.

One day a water will run in my worn path, not because of me but because of him. A water defined by sharp angles and a thought. It will not feel the same as the rain from the ground's own breath. It will be unkind.

But that is not yet.

The water of here is still nervous. It braids and unbraids itself, trying different shapes, not ready to follow the lines of the future.

The gas of the future loosens in my mind and drifts away.

I feel the weight of the chamber above me like the pressure of a body's interior, and I feel the weight of the not-yet below me like a fault line in the stone.

My body is low and long. It meets the contours of the world.

The stone is young.

It remembers sharpness.

I move forward. I feel the edge of the fracture where we must meet again.
