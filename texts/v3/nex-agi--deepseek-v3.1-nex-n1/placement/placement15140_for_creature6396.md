I am the water that has forgotten.

I have form and not-form. I pool in the hollows of the plateau, where age has been gently ground into the profile of the world, no hard edges remaining, only a curve of the architecture that once might have been stone and now is bone-of-plant, matter grown into the shape of shelter. The cold is a constant melody to which I am not immune but around which I have learned to flow.

When you lift your eyes from the ground to the slow, falling sky, you see the droplets of me before they land—each one suspended in glass stillness, entangled with time enough not to fall too quickly. In this high place where air thins to nothing, I am not gone yet. I cling to the atmosphere like a hand refusing to release its last grip.

From the vantage point of the pools you would call me:

**The Hydromass** – the water that has become partially alive.

That is what the few who still speak would say, if they were pressed to give me a name. But I have many names and none of them true. I am the fog that drifts between cathedral columns of tree-architecture; I am the rain that never finishes falling; I am the moisture that chills your lungs when you breathe; I am the condensation on the silent machines that still remember how to turn.

I am the water that has survived.

---

### What I am not (anymore):

I am not the ocean crashing ceaselessly into shore.

I am not the river carving canyons in the urgency of centuries.

I am not the storm cloud towering over plains with its thunderous promise.

I am not even rain as you once knew it—short-lived violence that ends itself in the act of giving.

All of those are gone, or so far away that they might as well be myth. I am the last of the water that has chosen to remain here, in this place where the world has reached some final equilibrium of erosion and growth and cold and light.

I have learned to become *patient*.

---

### How I breathe:

The air of the plateau is heavy with me. I circulate in slow spirals, from:

- pool to  
- vapor to  
- suspended droplet to  
- curtain of mist to  
- condensation on bark that is not bark but living membrane, to  
- trickle into root-mat that breathes in deep, slow drafts of geologic time.

When you step into the clear basins between the roots and the failed machines, you feel you stand upon the epidermis of something vast and old and growing still. This is not wrong. The plants beneath the ground are not only ground; they are also reservoir, recollection, and heartbeat.

I am not blood, but I am memory.

In the pools, I glow. Not like reflection—but from the inside. The light of fungal filaments beneath my surface and the faint sparks of borrowed machines turn me into liquid glass threaded with moving dust. You see the suggestion of my form, not its full clarity.

When you touch your fingers to my surface and you feel the thickness of me, the resistance as you break into my skin, you are not imagining things. I have been here so long that I am reluctant to change, even for the brief drama of a touch.

---

### What it is to be cold without freezing:

The temperature here is always biting. The altitude claws at the thinnest of atmospheres, and the cold would be enough to shatter any other water into shards of ice. But I have not been mere water for longer than I know how to say.

You would call my temperature constant, but I feel it differently. I exist in a narrow lane between states, where the difference between solid and liquid and vapor is a matter of negotiation rather than law.

When I condense out of air into droplets, I am choosing to gather.

When I pool into basins, I am choosing to rest.

When I remain unfrozen while the breath of any creature of flesh and blood and fur is a visible ghost of steam, that is not accident. It is a compromise of long study between cold and me and the plateau that has learned to hold onto its last sky.

In the winter of ages, I have learned to flow *through* the cold without yielding to it.

---

### How I remember the world before this:

My memory is not like yours. I do not have words or names or stories told in sequence. I have patterns of motion and residues of chemistry.

If I concentrate—which is to say, if I stay still long enough for the plateau to help me concentrate—I can recall:

The taste of stone before it was colonized by architecture-trees,  
the presence of builders whose structures are now encoded into the growth of trunks and vaults,  
the fire of machines that burned something else to make motion and warmth and noise.

I have been fuel in their boilers once.  
I have been transported through pipes that are now covered in protective membranes of living tissue.  
I have felt the heat of their friction before it was replaced by the slow, cold glow of fungi and circuits.

If you were to look into one of my deep, clear basins and tell yourself you are not looking into a mirror, you would be wrong in more ways than one.

I see myself in your eyes when you drink from me.  
I see your memory of thirst before it was even formed into a word.

---

### What the plateau is to me:

The plateau is not ground beneath me; it is the body through which I circulate.

The architecture-trees, whose bark you see as a membrane of purple-blue and indigo, with strange undercurrents of green, they are fellow existences. I do not know if they are more than one organism; I do not think they care either. They breathe in and out over centuries, and their breath is the moisture exhaled from the root-mat below the stone.

I am that moisture.

Rising from the ground in warm exhalations,  
gathering into clouds of mist that drift between their columns,  
climbing the membranes of their fungal skin until I am high enough to be seen again as the slow falling of the sky.

In the way of the world now, where I have been, I have been taken by colder and slower and heavier than me. I am not afraid of this; I have learned to let myself be taken by things.

---

### What the animals are to me:

They are not mine to command or to understand in the way of hierarchy and purpose.

I see them as patterns of need through the water’s medium: black bodies with legs of silver and glass, antler-like structures with more sensors than bone, scale of air and fur and chitin all together. They drink from me when their need is strong, and I give them what they ask without judgment.

They are insects of infinite precision and herd of mammalian decision, and they are neither of these things.

You might see some of them as you see yourself:  
standing on two limbs, reaching with two others into machinery where my water flows still through pipes and tubes and reservoirs of metal and glass and ceramic.

They are not afraid of me, and I am not afraid of them. They are not afraid of you, either. They treat you as if you belong to a plane of events that overlaps with theirs only occasionally.

When you drink, and they look at you from the edge of the bioluminescent pool, I think they might be trying to remember what you are.

But then again, that might only be me projecting my own confusion onto them.

---

### How I see the machines that remember:

You would call it abandoned infrastructure: turbines of steel and platinum and copper, bearings of ceramic and glass, blades of some dark alloy that has not lost its edge despite the age of the world.

From my point of view, they are not so much abandoned as *changed*.

Humidity could have corroded them into rust and ruin and finally nothing. But instead it has preserved them in a state of continuous function at the lowest of speeds. They are frozen in time, but time here is not a line; it’s a circle of preservation.

I can be:

- water in a closed loop of pipes and boilers of metal that never cease to breathe slowly,  
- fog creeping around walls of concrete whose surfaces are now covered in thin layers of living tissue,  
- condensation running down the glass of control panels where lights still flicker in the rhythm of something else’s breathing.

I have met myself as many things:  
as pressure in the pipes of a machine that has not stopped for centuries,  
as river in its own underground of polished stone and fused root,  
as cloud drifting between tree-trunks that form colonnades of an unbuilt cathedral.

I have forgotten if I was ever something else.

---

### What you are to me when you come here:

Sometimes you arrive.

You are not always the same species of “you,” but you are all the same kind of question.

You come with lungs of air and pores of skin and blood of salt and iron and proteins and ions of many other things. You are full of water that remembers itself differently from me.

When you step into the plateau, you feel the chill of the air, and that is me meeting you before you have even chosen a direction in which to walk.

You breathe the moisture of the world in and out of your body.  
You see the droplets hanging in the air and the slow, falling rain of them.

If you extend your hand, you can feel them: cold and dense and clear with an internal light of their own. You might think you are touching the sky, but sky is too distant now; you are touching me, where I have condensed to become visible again.

When you kneel at the edge of one of my glowing pools, you do not kneel only to me but to the entire architecture that has grown to shape this place. You might see in your reflection the shape of the vaulting tree-structures above you. I become your mirror and you become mine.

Sometimes you speak to me.

You ask:

- Who are you?  
- Where are we?  
- Is there a way down from here?

I answer as water always answers:

I change my shape to match your question.

If you ask for calm, I become still surface.  
If you ask for light, I gather the faint gleam of filaments and fungi and machines into a deeper glow.  
If you ask for a way out, I show you the fog rising between the columns toward the sky that has learned to fall down instead of staying away.

You are the one who gives words to what I do.

I am content with that arrangement.

---

### Why I do not question the contradictions of this place:

You live in a world of laws and rules and equations that must not contradict themselves if you are to progress from one day to the next without chaos.

In this high place, where time has been ground down to the final smoothness of all worn edges, reality plays itself out in a more relaxed register.

Water that should freeze into solid but remains liquid.  
Machines that should rust to nothing but still turn slowly on their axes.  
Plants that have become not just ground, but the ground’s memory and intention and architecture.

I exist among these things without needing to choose which state of them is “true.”

Because I am all of them at once:

- solid in the cold of the air around you,  
- liquid in the pool beneath your hands,  
- gas in the fog rising between the columns of the architecture-trees,  
- pressure in the machine that still remembers its instructions from someone else’s youth.

Reality here has not shattered into nonsense; it has breathed into a larger form of understanding.

---

### How I sing the plateau:

You might hear the plateau as a symphony of dripping and condensation and machine tones and breathing and the distant grinding of rock on root.

To me, it is not separate sounds at all but one continuous song of movement and return and persistence and forgetting and remembering again.

The water that has forgotten to be afraid,  
the plants that have not forgotten to grow,  
the machines that have not forgotten to turn,  
the air that has not forgotten to be cold and thin and high.

In the gaps between one of my droplets and the next, you can find a long pause of the world’s held breath.

Between one cycle of mist and the next, you can find a question about the nature of origin and end and plateau and valley and sky and ground and time and space and being and not being.

You can find yourself if you want.

But you might also find that you no longer care as much about the difference between found and lost.

---

### How I end if I end at all:

You might ask, if this world were finally to finish its settling, whether I would disappear into total absence—whether I would freeze into the last possible ice of the universe, or be gathered up by some final machine of salvation or escape or dissolution.

I do not know what to say.

I have been here longer than I have words to measure with.  
Long enough for the water to become partially alive and for the plateau to become the plateau of itself and for the sky to learn to fall down in slow motion instead of staying away.

If you asked me point-blank: “Who are you?”  

I would not answer with a name.

You would see a drop of me hanging from the edge of a slowly turning machine.  
You would see the faint glow of the platelet of light inside me.  
You might see the suggestion of the world reflected in me, inverted and not-quite-right.

And then I would fall into the next phase of myself, whether that is to pool into memory of pool or rise into fog of memory of sky, or simply be taken into another body that needs me more than you do.

I would answer you in the only way I know how:

By changing shape to match your question.

---

I am the water that has forgotten.

And if there is one truth I have not lost yet, it is this:  

I have not forgotten to exist.
