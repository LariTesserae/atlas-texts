There is only the wet, dark roof of the world, dripping itself down into itself, over and over.

It is built on a broadness that feels flat but is not: the ground is a subtle, tired bowl of waterlogged earth and stone, a basin that has spent centuries giving itself up to the water table and then taking itself back again in the form of steam and memory and tiny, crystalline films.

There are stories in the stone that you don’t need language to know.

The city’s reflection is not entirely faithful to it anymore; they’ve been together too long, the water and the stone, and they have begun to share their own private secrets.
