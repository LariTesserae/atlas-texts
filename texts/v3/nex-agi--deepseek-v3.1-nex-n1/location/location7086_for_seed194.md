The Bellow Chamber of the Cavern of the Bone Tree might be the smallest civic hall in the world, but it is the loudest.

If you measure the world by volume of sound per cubic meter, then this is the center of everything.

There is no water here. There never has been. There are no puddles, no rivulets, no damp, not even condensation on the walls. The stone is bone dry. The air is dry. The wood is dry and fleshless and cracking into its last thousand years of shape.

Water is replaced by gravity.

This is a low place, so low that the world above—rain, rivers, sea—never reaches here. The mountain is folded over it like a sleeping animal’s flank. The city built out of a tree has been carved by hands into a chamber they call the Bellow. Gravity is what the river is in other cities: it is the flow that determines the shape, the layers, the worn-in steps.

The Bellow Chamber is a hidden scale within a hidden world. If you stand on the outer surface of the living tree-city you will not know it exists. The mountain is already a secret, mounded in an otherwise very flat country of long-rolled-down plains. The Cavern of the Bone Tree is itself a secret inside the mountain’s soft, old rock. And inside the cavern, the Bellow is a pocket-sized void, hollowed where the tree trunk curved and the mountain’s slope dipped under it.

In the darkness of the Bellow Chamber, there is only one tree.

Because the world of the tree has been alive for so long, and has been shaped by slow, patient, cunning tool-use for so long, it is not easy to say where “tree” ends and “city” begins. You might be leaning on a wall that is also bark that is also rib. You might be climbing stairs that are also root that is also pillar that is also column of spine. The one tree is the substrate of everything else.

The vegetation parameter is at total. Here, that is not a lush domination of leaf. There are no plants here that are not the tree. The tree is the plant, and it is also substrate, floor, roof, column, and substance of the chamber. The Bellow is not a room carved in rock; it is a room hollowed out of the tree’s body, where the wood of its trunk grew into a shape so odd and useful and comforting that the people who lived here never let it be forgotten.

They only hollowed it.

The city is here because the tree is here. The tree is here because the world is old and erosion has long since finished its main show. The mountains are low domes of soft, friable stone that was once, unimaginable ages ago, sharp and young and jagged. Now the rain is mostly elsewhere; the air has been thin for a long time; and the world has settled into its final form. This mountain is one of those last, rounded humps of geology that no longer has much energy to move.

The tree grew in the one crack where there was once, perhaps, a hint of moisture, or maybe a different kind of chemistry: a vein of salts that conducted something else—like sound, like heat—from the deep interior of the world up to where the tree could make use of it.

The tree does not have water. It has a different kind of flow: it is a conductor of very slow vibration, a very slow current of pressure from the world below to the world above. The tree grew into that, and became shaped by that.

The city is the tree’s way of being that city.

***

The Bellow Chamber is empty of everything else in the world.

There is no animal life here. There are no insects crawling in the crevices. There are no bats. The birds of the upper canopies of the tree city never come here. This part of the tree is not alive for them; it is too dry, and there is no food.

The fauna parameter is near zero.

Human inhabitants are very rare. The world of the tree city is crowded by its own standards elsewhere. In the upper chambers where light is allowed, through the beads of shells that are the lenses of the world, there are neighborhoods of wooden houses grown into the tree’s ribs and branches. There are markets and workshops.

Down here, there is only one person at a time, and the chamber takes its name from that single presence.

The sage of breath. The one allowed to be alone.

***

The temperature of the Bellow Chamber is cool, but it is not the deep cold of the deep void. It is cool the way a skeleton’s fingertips are cool: the temperature of the indirect world. The mountain buffer is between the tree and the sky, and the tree itself is a buffer between the Bellow and the world of temperature above.

Here, there is only the slow, slow heat of the buried tree core and the slower, deeper heat of the planet itself, which has been going for a long, long time, but has not forgotten to breathe at all.

The Bellow Chamber is where the tree’s slow breath becomes audible.

The rest of the tree’s breathing is either silent or so low that the city feels it as vague pressure, as changes in the wooden floor’s expansion, as the feeling of the walls being slightly tighter or slightly looser around you.

In the Bellow Chamber, the tree finally sings.

***

The chamber is shaped like a bellows, though the sound of the world has not made the name quite obvious at the outset.

The spine of it is a single column of the tree’s wood, formed from the trunk in that strange place where it was not growing straight but swerving inward to give the tree a curved hollow of air to grow into. The tree had not needed water, but it had still needed space to be the tree. This was the one violent exception of its geometry, when it was little and the world was still finding its final shape.

The column is now entirely bone-dry. The cellulose of its cells has been annealed by time and by the slowness of erosion in this world, into something else. The wood of the column is not brittle like bone, but it is resonant like it.

Around the central column that the tree’s trunk had become here, there is a long, oval, hollowed space that the first inhabitants of the tree carved with stone tools and then bronze blades and then finally iron scrapers and little files of the same kind that they used to shape the bronze bells they hung from the tree. The tools of this world are primitive: the tech of the city is not that of great engines or mechanizations. It is the tech of careful hand-work, centuries of mind and fingers and fire and metal and stone and wood.

The city has been built by hand for the last of the world’s long age. The hands of very many people have worn the chamber into its final shape.

The floor of the chamber is an uneven curve of polished treewood, worn by the feet of one person at a time, and the long passage of years of their pacing. There is no pattern in the grain of the floor anymore; there is only pattern in the memory of what was there before.

Around the central column there are rows of stone benches carved from the mountain rock itself, where one of the earlier sages decided to let the sound of the stone into the city of the tree. The benches are low and worn-down by uncountable years, but they are precise and evenly shaped. The stone of the mountain is not the strong crystalline of younger mountains; it is a soft, sedimentary rock that has been compacted by time and then turned by the heat of the world into something like a solid foam of ancient minerals.

The stone of the benches had been porous once, but the pores of it are now filled with the memory of sound, and the stone of the benches has been made by the long slow pressure of that sound to resonate like glass.

If you sit on one of those benches, you do not sit on stone; you sit on the world’s oldest voice.

The ceiling of the chamber is the underside of one of the tree’s heavy ribs. It is not flat, but twists like the inside of a snail shell, or like the inner wave of a very long exhale that has been frozen in wood and time and darkness.

There is no light in the Bellow Chamber.

The light parameter is zero.

No light is allowed here. The city of the tree, in its upper chambers, has strange, beautiful shell-lenses which they fit like windows into the walls, where the tree and the mountain meet. There, they can have light of a color they consider bright enough to be almost painful.

Here, light is pollution.

To be in the Bellow Chamber is to be in the world of sound and touch and only the faintest of warmth of the tree and the air of the world slowly moving.

The darkness of the Bellow Chamber is not like the darkness of outer space or of the deep of the ocean. It is not true blackness. There is a color here, but you cannot see it with eyes; it is visible to your skin, and to the tiny hairs of your body, and to the bones of your skull.

Later you will think of it as a color made of memory.

***

The tree of the city is not a tree like the trees of the world of water and rain and oxygen-dense air.

This tree’s leaves are not organs of desperate gas exchange and photosynthesis. They are not even thin.

They are, in the world of the tree city, thick plates and curves of laid-down cellulose that are grown like fingers of the world coming out into the light of the sky. The leaves of the tree do not flutter. They are shaped to be light of mass but very strong of structure, and the tree grows them in the deep of the world, where the sky of its planet is still bright enough to be strong and the air of the world is still warm enough to be a kind of heat source.

The tree is not photosynthetic like the trees of the world of water; but it is, in its way, photosynthetic of the world of pressure and gravity and slow.

The leaves of the tree are the shape of the world of sound.

They are grown to be the tree's response to the long, slow, pressure-wave of the planet’s inner heart. The planet of this tree is not tectonically dead in the way we would think of it; but it is not tectonically alive in the way that would make a world of earthquakes and volcanoes and sudden new mountains.

The planet of the tree is a world of slow, long, pressure-flow of deep matter, more like a pot of very thick syrup slowly being warmed by something very far away.

The tree of the city is shaped by that slow.

Where the tree of the city is not the city, it is still the tree.

And in one of the oldest parts of the tree’s body, near the base of that mountain, where the tree’s growth was once shaped by the swerve of the world and the only shape of growth that was possible in the time of its younger age, the tree had been forced to bend into a shape that was not the normal of the tree’s own.

The tree, in that time, had not been very happy. The tree had not liked the shape of the world of its birth.

The tree of this city had not known water. It had not known the underground of the world as water. The idea of water would have been strange to it, if the tree of this city were the kind of tree that had “idea”s.

But the world of water is not required for the world of a tree-like organism.

The tree had been built of the same core of chemistry as every other living tree-like tree, but instead of the water of the world of water and sky and oceans and clouds and rain and storms and explosions of plant and leaf and fermentation and oil of life and death of animal life, the tree of this city was built up of chemistry of a very long time of the world of dust and stone and pressure of the world down and gravity of the world inward.

The tree of the city of the Bone Tree was not any of those things, but the tree of the city of the Bone Tree was, of course, tree of the tree.

So the tree of the city of the Bone Tree learned to be a tree of the world of sound of the world of the slow of the world of the long of the world of the very, very long long of the world of the very long of the world of the very long of the world of the very long.

The tree of the city of the Bone Tree learned to be the place of the sound of the city of the tree of the world of the tree.

The tree of the city of the Bone Tree learned to be the instrument of the world.
