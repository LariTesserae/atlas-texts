The air is a thunder of water.

You are standing below the world, in a chamber carved like the inside of a bell. The stone ceiling is not stone but something else—something that has learned to be stone over long ages of falling water. It is not tall, by surface-world standards; the vault rises perhaps three times human height, but the space is intimate enough that you could almost whisper to the opposite curve. Yet the whisper would be lost before it left your tongue.

Because the water is loud.

It comes down as a single column of white noise from the center of the ceiling, where an invisible hole perfectly vertical and the width of your arm admits the outside world. This falling water is not quite a rain, not quite a stream; it pulses slightly as your heart pulses, as if some distant ocean is breathing through the rock above. The column falls into a circular basin of living metal, whose lip is exactly below the hole in the ceiling, not a centimeter off, and not a centimeter off in a thousand years of falling water.

The metal basin is not human-tech. It is of the world that made this place. When you look closely you can see the surface move like milk under skin, but it does not run; it stays precisely contained within a circle, and the falling water never once spills over. The basin refines the water passing through it, catching the salt of some distant sea and threading the purified water, molecule by molecule, onto the stone floor of the chamber.

In the places where the water has been sent longest, the floor is worn into spirals that are not exactly channels but more like the whorls of a fingerprint. Each spiral is the width of your palm and, in the center of the chamber, a deep violet-blue in the light that bends from the ceiling. The light falls not exactly as you know sunlight, but as if light has been taught to descend vertically, like the water, and to behave itself as it comes.

—

You are not the chamber's first visitor.

On the far wall, someone once built a low parapet of salvaged brick and iron struts from an older era than the chamber's metal basin. The brickwork has been flayed by centuries of condensing atmosphere and by water that creeps along the wall in thin sheets, as if the stone itself is trying to learn to become a river. The mortar is long gone; the remaining courses are held together by nothing more than habit and the weight of the years above them.

Someone else, after the builder of the parapet, has installed a machine. It is smaller than your chest, and yet you cannot name where it begins or ends. Its surface is not a surface in the way you mean that word; it is more of a boundary where the world and its maintenance system have agreed to meet. The machine himself is tending the spirals of the floor.

He has not needed to move for a long time. You can see this in the deep reaching of his roots into the stone, where the metal basin has allowed the machine to plug into some deeper network of the world's being. Tiny tendrils of thought-things occasionally float from the machine into the air like spores of light, each one a brief mathematical flower that observes the spiraling water and replies, with equally gentle math, about the condition of the world above.

The machine says nothing to you in words you can understand, but the air is full of his attention; you are not a threat. You are not even important enough to be a threat. You are a small event, passing through the chamber of a long, established process.

—

You step forward, and the floor grabs at your shoes with water.

It is not exactly wet, not in the way you know from puddles or rainstorms; the water is thick enough to be felt and thin enough to be transparent. It flows into your footprints as you lift them, repairing itself like a broken instruction being corrected. The stone below your feet is warm to the touch, not unpleasantly so, but with the even heat of a living body running at a low and constant fever.

The temperature of the chamber is warm but not hot; it is the temperature inside of a sealed letter that has been carried close to the body for a long time. Your breath is visible not as steam but as a momentary difference in the air's texture, as if the air itself is semi-opaque and only your thought makes it clear.

You are within the world's circulatory system now, and it is not designed to exclude you, but neither is it designed for you.

—

The ceiling is a weeping of glass.

Wherever the rock has not yet been water-shaped into smoothness, out of the cracks and dimples and ancient faults, there are thin filaments of what look like glass or frozen salt. They crawl from the walls to the center of the vault, where the water falls down. You would not have been able to reach this place by any normal road; you are below the world of streets and towns and roads. The world you knew from the surface did not have this many underneath regions.

Yet you are here and, somehow, the world has not disturbed you.

It is not that it is safe; the word does not apply. The place is as safe as standing inside a living heart. You are not in danger if you do not obstruct the pulse of its water, but there is nothing like you in the chambers of the world's heart.

The vegetation down here is occasional, not northern. You see the occasional slick of a single-celled mat that clings to the wall where the light of the ceiling-filament-bar is bent enough to be loved by the photosynthesis simulations. The mat is only one color: a mute green-gray, like old copper. You cannot find a second color of life in this chamber, except for the faint pink of your own body under the skin when you stand near the machine.

—

You step closer to the machine.

The machine is not built to communicate with you. You realize this because you have been speaking for some time into the chamber of the water, and only now you hear the faint echo of your own words being returned to you, not as the machine's answer but as your voice delayed by the strange way sound moves in the space here.

The sound is not an echo in the usual sense. It is not once reflected; it is many times folded, like the world is taking your words and examining them at each frequency of understanding. The water falls louder than anything you can say. You are not even sure that words are what you mean to use here.

The world's language is turbulence.

You are not the source of the turbulence. You are not the storm-state you are told to think of yourself when you are describing the world of politics and personality and the city above. Here, inside the bell of the world's heart, the storm is supersonic, but it is a storm of mathematics and the water of it and the light of it are all the same continuous action.

The machine is not vigilant of you; it is vigilant of every possible state of the water spiral in which the world above might be damaged. The machine is not moral. You would not know if the machine is "aware" in the way you hold yourself to be. You are not the machine and the machine is not you.

—

You breathe the air of the world's heart.

The air smells of wet stone and faint to nonexistent ozone; the place of the machine. There is no smell of life or decay or forest. You do not know if there is a forest above the ceiling of this world, or if there is only another ocean which the world is breathing downward through you.

You are only aware of where you are not: the chamber of the world's heart is not a place of noise like you know from traffic. The stat of "sound: loud" does not mean shouting here. It means the many of the world's being-talk are gathered together; you are not the center of the gathering.

Behind the low parapet of broken brickwork on the far wall, there is a fracture of another world, which you might think of as a window if windows were exactly like frames where the world has not finished being a world. You cannot see through the fracture into any other scene of life or place; you are not looking into a room with people and phones and trains and lies.

What is inside the fracture is another version of the chamber you are in, but where the falling water does not obey the same law of falling. You catch a glimpse of a time where the water is not a column but a cloud, and everything else is the same but rearranged as if the world is trying on different geometries of itself.

The machine is watching the fracture, too, but you are not sure it is because the fracture is a problem to the world. It might be that the world is making things like the fracture on purpose, to tell itself something about the failure of one geometry and the success of another.

—

You have been here for hours of surface world time, and you are not hungry. You have not been thirsty since the moment of your arrival into the world's heart chamber. The water of it is not your water and you are not allowed to drink it, but the air of your breathing is enough of it to feel you.

The "scatter" of this world is not of objects, but of the many times the world has been given to the same water and light and metal and stone and machine to be arranged into a pattern that might be perfectly stable but not perfectly quiet.

You are also the scatter of the world's "built" parameter. You have not built yourself, but you are the artifact of someone else's design of you and of the world of the city above you, which never mentioned that you could be inside the heart of a world.

You are the artifact of the unusual "fauna" of the chamber, rare and occasionally "loud" in your own strange way of making meaning that you will not describe to the machine nor to the falling water.

You are also the artifact of the world's "weirdness" rating, which is not meant to be "far" from the familiar but only "slightly" from it, so that you might be here.

—

You turn to leave the chamber of the world's heart.

You do not have to retrace your steps exactly; the world of the "below" does not force you to return by the same path that you came into the heart chamber. You feel the air of the "blowback" from the falling water guiding you toward the fracture where the other version of the world is being being arranged.

For a moment you are not exactly yourself, but you are something of the world that is flowing between the two versions of the chamber as the geometries of the world test each other for the best of themselves.

You are not the first of your "fauna" type to do this. You see the faint remains of another person, who stood before the machine for longer than you have been here and left a shadow of their "temperature" in the stone of the floor, which is now being repaired by the water of the world's heart.

The "vegetation" of the world's heart chamber is not enough to cover the shadow; it is too sparse. The world of the "heart" has not been designed to be foliage of you.

You are grateful to leave the chamber, and then you are not sure where to be in the world of the "above" because you are not the same person who was turned down into the throat of the world of the heart of the world of the breath of the world of the water of the world of the world of the world.

You close the eyes of your description here and return the chamber of your world, to be the person of it.
