You first breathe in before you see anything.

The air coils thick and hot into your lungs, carrying the smell of crushed leaves, sawdust, and something else—like wet stone and lightning. It is so heavy with moisture that your tongue can taste nothing else, yet the water-scale of this world reads 0.1. Whatever clouds are overhead, they are not made of ordinary rain.

You are inside the great body of something still deciding what shape to take.

## The Shape of the Place

You are below, but not underground.

The ceiling is a mass of roots and ribs and roots that have become ribs—arching plant-bones woven from pale, woody tissue that pulse and thicken even as you watch. They branch from a central spine that runs away into dim distance like the backbone of a continent. Everything here is the plant, and the plant is everything: floor, walls, pillars, corridors, chambers, all of it.

The floor under your feet is a dense carpet of living material, green and black and bruise-purple. It is not a single species but a single *organism*, or something like it—a superorganism that has long since forgotten how to be individual. Each part of it is connected to every other part by a mesh of fine, white, rootlike threads you can only see when you lean close. The ground feels slightly springy, like muscle under tension. It gives just enough to notice, but not enough to trust.

The place is cavernous, but a *living cavern*—a cathedral under construction by a god with too many nervous systems. High above, where roots form into vaults, you can see the faint outline of what might be shafts of outside world: light, not strong, but enough to show the color of the air. The air itself is a warm haze, tinted greenish by the countless pores of the plant-surface exhaling slow, hot breath.

## Sound and Motion

You hear the world before you understand it.

The sound parameter is 2.9/3: deafening. It is not a single noise, but a layered chaos of life. First you notice the highest registers—tinnitus-like trills of unseen creatures hidden everywhere, like a whole chorus of invisible insects trying to tune their instruments at once. Then the lower registers: groans and deep, rhythmic ticking like the heartbeats of old trees, but multiplied by thousands, overlapping out of sync.

Then there are the impacts.

Thuds from somewhere above, heavy and blunt, like boulders falling into leaves. They are descending—a series of crashes from distant to nearer, a cascading punctuation of the world reorganizing itself. Between each thump, there are softer clicks: bodies of all kinds snicking, scraping, tapping against plant matter. Every surface of the world is alive, and every surface is being walked over, eaten, or grown into.

The motion is *turbulent*—2.8/3. Nothing is ever fully settled.

Roots close around you like a slowly forming hand. Tendrils slide from above, thickening and then thinning as they reabsorb. New corridors of green matter bulge out from the walls as though the world is trying to grow new rooms in front of you. Entire sections of the floor swell into hummocks and then flatten again, like a waterless ocean floor bed under storm.

You are inside the forming of a continent, not yet fixed.

## The Plant-As-World

Vegetation 2.7/3: *total*.

Everywhere you look, there is plant, but not in a way you recognize.

**Walls** are not stone but living trunks interwoven into thickets so dense they become planes of tissue. Names are approximations here, but you see:

- **Weaver-boles** — columns whose bark looks like woven rope, constantly twisting tighter or looser in response to unseen cues. Gaps open between the strands, then close again; small, glittering animals scoot into these openings and disappear.
- **Vein-falls** — curtains of vine-like growths, hanging from the ceiling like drapes of green moss. But they carry a faint pattern of branching, glowing veins in a softer green, like slowed-down lightning. When something passes through them, the veins brighten momentarily, then fade.
- **Bone-pavements** — sections of the floor that are not green but bone-white. These are not hard like stone; they are firm, ridged surfaces like petrified cartilage. When you step on them, they give slightly, then re-form behind you, leaving a faint pattern of toes where your foot was.

There is no soil. No soil that you can see. There is only deeper plant, deeper layers of the same thing, becoming denser, more organized, or more chaotic depending on where you look. The world is made of different densities of itself.

The **erosion** parameter is 0.4/3—emerging. This is not a worn-down world; it is a world being assembled from scratch.

Growth spurts ripple outward. The floor swells slightly beneath your feet; then cracks, new filaments of white matter tease out and knit into each other. The ceiling bulges downward like an inverted mountain forming. Then, abruptly, a section of it collapses inward—strands of root matter tumble down in a slow avalanche, dragging bits of living light and animal shapes with them.

You are witnessing the birth of a geography that doesn’t care to hold still.

## Fauna Overwhelm

The animals are **overwhelming** — 2.7/3.

They are everywhere: not filling the space like a flood, but *composing* it.

You cannot walk without stepping on or around something alive. The ground is not simply ground; it is a carpet of:

- **Armor-plates** — small disk-shaped creatures whose carapaces look like the overlapping scales of pine cones. They trundle slowly across surfaces, scraping algae-like growths from the plant matter with rasping, mouthless edges. When you touch one, it doesn’t react like an animal so much as a muscle—it tightens, then relaxes, as if the world itself flinches.
- **Whip-birds** — threadlike vertebrates with no wings but ribbons of membranous filament that ripple along their sides. They hang from the ceiling in threads so thin they are almost invisible, until they move, and then you see that the ceiling is a shivering curtain of them. When something larger passes below, they spiral away collectively like smoke drawn into an invisible chimney.
- **Cluster-scops** — tiny fauna with too many legs and too many eyes, clustering in hollows of the plant walls. When disturbed, they explode outward in a sudden cloud of hard legs and soft bodies, filling your ears with a brief rain of clicks like hail on glass.

Then the larger ones.

A **massive** organism moves through this space—not a mammal, not an insect, but something like a tree-slug the size of a bus. It glides along the bone-pavement with its under surface rippling in waves, leaving a temporary depression that the plant floor slowly fills back. On its back are entire forests of miniature plant-like growths, and along these micro forests crawl countless smaller arthropods and wormlike things.

You see:

- Eyes that are not specialized organs but modified plant nodules.
- Limbs that are clearly jointed but jointed in *more than three directions*.
- Organs that are not inside but braided along the exterior surface, like flowers growing from the wrong side of the skin.

This is not a world where animals are distinct from plant. Animals are… specialized movements of the plant-body. They are what happens when the substrate wants to change speed faster than its main growth would allow.

## Built Fragments in the Green

Built: 1.0/3 — **scattered**.

Not everything here is natural.

You see **scattered** structures that are clearly not the plant’s own doing, but faint, tentative marks of something else.

You find a **step-tier** carved into a wall of weaver-boles. The wood itself has been cut—crude, not clean, but cut nonetheless—and then allowed to re-grow. Steps now bulge into odd shapes as the plant tries to close over the cuts. Tool-marks of the same kind as you might find on stone elsewhere are preserved here in wood-flesh, like wounds healed incompletely.

Further along, there is a **platform** made of deadwood and lashed together with fibrous strands taken from the living plant. It is attached to the side of a trunk like a crude shelf. It looks like someone’s workbench. There are objects on it:

- A bowl carved from a hard, dark growth of plant matter, polished by use.
- A few stones of foreign mineral—veined, not like anything else naturally here—knapped into rough edges and then used as scraping tools.
- Something like a primitive bone-crank or lever, made from a segment of fallen animal. It has irregular teeth carved along one edge; where they have been worn down, you see the plant has already begun to incorporate the tool back into itself.

Beyond that platform, there is **no village**, no city, no real architecture except what the plant itself has made. The tech level is **primitive** — 0.7/3. Early tools, yes. Maybe metal in some distant form—but here, stone and bone and plant and animal are all interchangeable raw materials.

## Rules of the World

As you move deeper into the plant-world, you begin to feel the **rules** by which this place operates.

### 1. Everything is a temporary shape of the same substance.

You are in a system where **growth** has outpaced **decay**. The plant-mass is the substrate: everything that grows, grows *from* it or *on* it. Animals are mobile extensions. The scattered tools, the platforms, are little more than temporary anchors thrown into a river of flesh.

When you cut a plant here, it **bleeds** but does not die. It seals around the cut and grows thicker there. Wounds are opportunities for the world to reshape itself.

### 2. The world rebuilds around you.

The erosion value is so low not because nothing is worn away, but because **new growth replaces the old before it can erode into stillness**.

The floor is not smoothed by centuries of wind and rain. It is formed by rapid surges of growth, then dissolved by reabsorption when the plant-world decides to allocate elsewhere. The sound you hear of groaning and ticking is the world collapsing itself so it can conceive new geometry.

### 3. Water is rare but not absent.

Water parameter 0.1/3: **no water**.

You do not see rivers, lakes, or rain falling. But moisture is everywhere: in the air, condensing on the surfaces of the plant, weeping from wounds. This is not water in the sense of open oceans or even rivers. It is **bound water**, locked into the biochemistry of the plant-mass.

The chemistry here is non-aqueous, but not waterless. The plant uses its internal fluids as **mobile solvents** rather than free-flowing water. When you break a surface, you sometimes get a clear, thick fluid that might evaporate slowly or not at all, depending on where it is exposed.

You drink at your own risk.

### 4. Time is local.

You cannot measure the age of this place by looking at layers. There are no stable strata, only **currents** of growth. Some chambers might be older in the sense of continuous occupation by the plant-mass, but visible scars are healed over in days or less.

The **dynamic** parameter means time here is not the steady ticking of a planet’s rotation but a storm of **construction events**. You might be standing in a chamber that is only minutes old as a defined space, even though the plant that forms it began millennia ago.

### 5. Scattered intelligence, but no clear city.

The built structures are too scattered and too small for a large population. Yet you see **patterns** that suggest some intelligence is at work:

- Lines of cut notches in the same trunk, spaced in a rhythm that is not random growth.
- Woven baskets of plant matter that are still partially alive, growing new roots into the base of a platform.
- Fragments of what might be symbolic marks carved into surfaces—then quickly overgrown by plant tissue, as if the world itself is trying to erase evidence.

Perhaps intelligence does not build here because **anything built can be eaten**.

## Atmosphere of the Weird

The overall **weirdness** is 2.5/3.

This place does not obey the geometry you are used to.

Landforms you would recognize as mountains or valleys appear **inverted**. Great valleys are not gaps between highlands, but enormous ribs in the plant-world’s ceiling, curving down like the ribs of a whale whose body you walk inside. Peaks are not far-off points you reach by walking; they are nearby pillars of growth bulging upward, or clusters of ceiling formations that form **living stalactites**.

The temperature: **warm** — 2.2/3. It’s a heat that doesn’t come from a sun you can see directly, but from **metabolism**. The world itself breathes warm.

The light is **normal** — about 1.5/3. It is not bright, but it is steady enough to see clearly. It comes from everywhere and nowhere: filtered from shafts above, mixed with faint **bioluminescence** from the plant’s own veins and from certain animals that wear light like jewelry. When a large creature passes close, its own patterns of light shift the color of the air around you.

You cannot be sure if you are underground or not. You are below surface by elevation value, but the sense of enclosure is not that of a cave. It is more like being **inside the body of a god**.

## The Ecology of Noise

The **sound** parameter does not come merely from volume, but from **density** of sound events.

- **Low**: Deep groans of structural trunks shifting under their own weight.
- **Middle**: The constant skittering of countless small feet, legs, and bodies dragging across surfaces.
- **High**: The shrill chorus of something that might be singing insects or might be stressed plant tissue vibrating under tension.
- **Impact**: Thumps from above as entire sections of ceiling collapse and rearrange, or as animals the size of small structures smash themselves into new sections of the world.

The **density** parameter is 1.7/3 — *many presences per volume*. None of the sounds ever belong to a single creature. They are always the sum of thousands, overlapping into a single roar of life.

If you stand still long enough, you realize that some of the sounds are **structural**. The world is not just making noise because animals are loud; its own body creaks and snaps as it grows, like a house expanding while you are inside it.

## Time and the End of the World

You cannot stay in one place for long.

The floor you are standing on grows **thicker**. The plant mass under your feet becomes too soft to be safe. You see **new** passages shaping themselves nearby as the plant-world rearranges itself to accommodate pressure from somewhere deeper.

Everywhere, **scattered** artifacts—an isolated tool, half-plant; a broken platform of carved wood and bone; a faint pattern of grooves carved into a trunk that might once have been a map—are being swallowed back into the world. They will be remembered as nutrients in plant tissue, not as stories in stone.

This is a world in **pre-formation**, not in its old age.

Its erosion value is low because it has not yet reached the stage where erosion dominates. It is still building itself. If you could stay here long enough—which you can’t, because you would be consumed by the growth—you would see this chamber **torn down** and rebuilt into something else entirely. Maybe into a new valley of plant-forest. Maybe into a nest for something larger than the tree-slug organism.

You might, eventually, see **water** as it becomes more common in the future—if the world ever chooses to form pools rather than keep all its moisture bound inside living tissue. But that has not happened yet.

## Final Image

Before you leave the description, you stand still long enough to see this:

In the middle distance, a **section of ceiling** collapses inward—not violently, but with a slow, organic crumbling of roots and layered matter. It falls into a chamber below you cannot see, but the sound is like an entire forest being dropped into itself.

As the debris hits, a swarm of **whip-birds** erupt from the edges of the collapse, spiraling upward in threads of light and motion. A smaller contingent of armor-plates along the wall near you suddenly converge on the fresh edge of the collapse, scraping at new surfaces exposed by the fall.

From the torn plant tissue below, something new **bulges upward**—a pale, dome-shaped mass of tissue that doesn’t immediately look like anything recognisable. It pushes itself through the tangle, then **climbs** onto a nearby weaver-bole trunk like an animal escaping its own birth.

It is not entirely plant or beast. It is something the world has just *made*.

It is this place in miniature: a living **geology** emerging without waiting for erosion to finish it. The world is not wearing itself smooth. It is tearing itself open and starting again, louder by each heartbeat.

If you stayed here, you too would be absorbed—not erased, but turned into one more moving pattern in the plant-world’s restless body.
