Here, the world is still assembling itself from raw ocean, and my small city of bundled coral is one of the first places where things have begun to stick.

### The ocean around us

Water fills almost everything: 2.9 of 3. Out there, beyond our platform, it’s an ocean that has no floor and perhaps no far shore. Not a stormy ocean; it’s calm, massive, and patient. It’s not deep sea in the way of abyssal plains; it’s deep in the sense of limitless reach — a water-sky below and, in places, above.

Rain is a constant presence: sheets and veils of it, turning near distances into noise, then back into shape. Some days it’s a fine, misting wall that passes through our bones and makes every stone sweat. Some nights it’s a dense, warm lull, heavy drops that raise small jewel-bursts on the surface of the water.

Sound is low: 1.3 of 3. The ocean itself is quiet. Waves don’t crash in breakers; they’re long, low breaths of water that rock the entire world platform on a cycle so slow you can only measure it by watching algae lines on the pillars. The rain is a hushed hushing. Voices carry far. Music blends with the hum of dripping water. It’s not silence — it’s a low, oceanized murmur that never quite goes away.

### Light and warmth

Light is dim: 1.1 of 3. The sun — if we still call that faraway disc a sun — rarely breaks through in full. Most often it’s a diffuse glow beyond the clouds, small changes in grayness that tell time. Dawn and dusk are long, slow smearings of light: charcoal to pewter to a faint, bruised bronze. No sharp shadows. No sudden noon. The brightest things are not the sun but what phosphorescence collects.

Temperature is warm: 2.5 of 3. Not hot, never cold. The air sits against the skin like a damp towel left out too long. Even at night the water and air are nearly the same warmth — they’re constantly trading small margins of heat. This warmth accelerates the growing of things and slows our minds. Decisions become thicker.

### Standing in the water: a high, half-built island

Elevation is 2.4 of 3 — high, but not high above mountains so much as high above the formless water. We live atop a broad platform of fused stone and shell that rises some distance out of the ocean, like the back of a giant turtle not yet fully surfaced. The city’s lower edge is always lapped at by small waves; the upper reaches are rarely touched by water.

Erosion is 0.4 of 3 — “emerging.” The platform of our island is astonishingly young. Little has been worn away. The rock is not smooth or rounded yet, but porous and ragged-edged, with small cavities where seawater glints inside. There are no rounded cliffs, no deep river valleys carved over time.
