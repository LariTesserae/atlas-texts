You stand on a threshold of stone that is not quite stone, and not quite floor. The Spine of the Night stretches around you in a bowl so wide it begins to curve, like the inside of a planet-sized vessel.

Above, there is no sky.

There is only ceiling.

---

### Ceiling and Scale

The world is roofed over.

From the horizon all the way to the horizon opposite, the land is covered by a vault of black glass that never quite comes to rest. At this scale, it is not a sky; it is another surface, distant but definite. The “roof” of the world is a slow-motion ocean of glossy, obsidian-like sheets that slide over one another in plates leagues wide and unfathomably thick. They tug, grind, and buckle in time spans so long that human eyes would see only change without motion.

You are at high elevation – in the upper tiers of the world’s geography. The vault of glass is lower here than down below, where continents of black plant-matter and fossil rivers coil under ever-thickening atmosphere. Distance presses the sound out of things; the roar of the world below is faint but constant, like breathing heard through a stone wall.

Everything is vast.  
The scale of the physics itself stretches: things that fall fall strangely, slowly. Air moves in sluggish rivers that are not wind so much as climate-plumes. Time and weather are interchangeable; each storm is a climate event.

---

### Cold Light and the Living Roof

The temperature is cold – but not the cutting cold of a snowblown peak. This is a cold of deep absence, a constant temperature slightly below what a body wants. It settles into clothes, stone, and bone with a patience that has outlived mountains.

There is light, but almost none of it is honest.

What little light exists comes from three sources:

1. **The Glass Ceiling Above.**  
   Where the black plates grind thin and edges kiss edges, there is a faint, oily blue-violet glow – like electricity trapped under skin. At conflict points between ceiling plates, where pressure is immense and release impossible, tiny stress-fractures phosphoresce. They are not stars; they are wounds of the world’s immobile lid.

2. **The Canopy Below.**  
   Under you, the land is not rock. It is plant.

   The entire surface of the planet below this elevation, as far as you can imagine, is covered in a single interlocked organism of vegetation that has been growing for millions of years. At a distance it looks like a black ocean of fine, hair-thin branches woven into a mattress of forest so thick it has no clear floor. Each branch is thinner than a matchstick and many kilometers long.

   This deep vegetation releases a faint, intermittent heat and light in its upper layers to drive photosynthesis when the ceiling cracks allow it. This manifests as a dim, teal-green glow shimmering across the surface of the land far below, like moonlight reflected in oil, but without moon.

3. **The Aurora That Travels Across the Floor.**  
   Occasionally, a glowing wave half a kilometer tall and thousands wide rolls across the vegetation far below: ribbons of blue-green shimmer like a ghostly tide. These are not lights in the sky; they are lights on the floor. They are thought to be massive pressure discharges from the ceiling’s grinding plates being transmitted into the plant network beneath.

   The aurora illuminates your platform of stone and the scattered buildings of the settlement here for a few heartbeats. Then it passes, and the darkness gathers tighter than before.

---

### The Plant-Architecture: A Forest That Is the Floor

The vegetation of this world is not a covering of the ground;  
it is the ground.

From below, to the horizon, you look over a forest of near-black, filamentary growths. Each stem is incredibly thin and flexible, but they are packed in densities of millions per square meter, all interwoven into a living matrix that behaves like foam and felt and bedrock.

From a distance it looks like velvet stretched over a continent.

As you approach, you realize that the “ground” here consists of:

- **The Surface Met:** Woven vegetation, compressed and partly fossilized into micro-layers, creating a surface that creaks underfoot but holds your weight.
- **The Inner Void:** Many kilometers of empty, air-filled space beneath, where the plant matrix grows thinner and thinner until you reach the true ground floor of the world – not rock, but a root-like ocean woven into a living floor that has been processing wind-borne soil and bodies of everything else living for a geological era.

In other places there are stone peaks – the true bedrock of the planet that managed to push through the plant-sea. They are stegosaur-like plates of dark rock with edges sharp enough to draw blood from a glance. The rock itself does not show smooth curvature of erosion; it is jagged, fresh-looking, as if newly extruded.

---

### Water in a Cold, Damp World

Under the ceiling, there is no open rain.

Water exists here as:

- **Condensation Weather.**  
  The upper ceiling surfaces are slightly colder than the atmospheric column below, because heat escapes from stress fractures and surface phytochemistry. The air up near the glass cools slowly and moisture condenses in droplets that roll along ceilings, until they find a flaw – a crack, a thin seam – and then they rain downward.

  This creates “rain” events that are not storms, but long, vertical sheets of water falling for kilometers, from ceiling plate to plate, until they strike some vegetation or stone surface.

- **River Walls and Water Falls.**  
  Where the moisture collects on the rock’s faces, or in the matrix of the plant-bed, it flows in thin, winding channels that sometimes pour over the edges of cliffs or run along the walls of cavities like living veins painted silver.

- **Clouds Below Your Feet.**  
  At lower elevation, under the immense blanket of the plant strata, the moisture condensing from the breath of the plant-mass creates a cloud layer under the ceiling you see. Sometimes clouds rise up through gaps in the vegetation and roll under the ceiling in slow, ponderous banks of mist.

---

### Built Things: Hanging on the Edge of the World

The density of the world is misleading.

There is almost nothing between presences here on your high stone platform. The air feels empty. The volume feels huge, like you are standing in a hall large enough to contain a country.

But the density of *built things* is scattered:

- **Single Buildings Clustered Against a Stone Face.**  
  A small human settlement clings to the side of a cliff that is itself one edge of a massive plateau of stone thrusting up through the plant mattress. There are perhaps thirty structures.

- **Materials:**  
  The buildings are made of:
  - Dark stone blocks from the local plateau,  
  - Lacings of long plant-fiber rope from the vegetation far below (hauled up by manual winders),
  - Panels of translucent material from some local secretion – like resin hardened over decades.

- **Position:**  
  Most structures are not free standing; they are carved into, or hung onto, the rock face, or built onto stone platforms that are extensions of the cliff. Some platforms jut over the edge, allowing direct view of the plant ocean beneath.

- **Architecture:**  
  - Narrow windows face the warm horizon glow where the ceiling stress fractures are brighter.  
  - Flat roofs and shallow basins to collect condensation.
  - External balconies without railings – because in this low-density, cold world, falling air is slow and a man’s falling time is long enough to be reconsidered.

- **Age:**  
  Everything looks newly made. The edges of carved stone are still sharp; tool marks are clear. Vegetation has not yet softened the corners. This is a settlement cut from a landscape that has only recently exposed itself, by people who are not long here.

No roads are visible between structures. Footsteps, handholds, and occasional rope bridges between platforms serve the role of paths.

---

### The Technology That Is Present Here

Technology is primitive and selective:

There is **simple metal**, but not much of it.

- **Tools**:  
  - Stone blades shaped with extreme care,  
  - Knives of worked metal reserved for cutting and shaping the plant fibers from the lower world,
  - Hand-tools made of wood and bone and sharpened stone.

- **Machines**:  
  - Rope winches driven by muscle and leverage, to haul cargoes from the plant-world below.
  - Ventilation flaps that can open and close to allow air through tunnels carved into the stone cliff.
  - Basic water-collection systems that rely on gravity and condensation.

- **Knowledge vs. Tools:**  
  There is evidence of careful observation of the environment:  
  - Scored grooves on walls marking the arrival time of aurora events,
  - Symbols painted near windows where certain light patterns from the ceiling are expected,  
  - But no visible mass production of anything.

### Sound and Noise in a High Quiet

Despite the darkness and stillness, the world is loud.

- **The Ceiling Groans:**  
  When pressure adjusts between vast plates, it radiates as sound: a low, grinding groan that vibrates in stone and chestbone at the same time. Sometimes you feel it before you hear it and your teeth ache before the sound arrives.

- **The Vegetation Ocean:**  
  The plant-mass below has a sound like a forest with every leaf falling at once, but stretched over a scale so large it becomes a continuous noise: a soft roar, like ocean heard in a shell.

- **Wind Rivers:**  
  At these high altitudes, wind is not local gusts; it is massive currents of air moving between hot and cold regions and under the ceiling. The signatures of these wind-rivers are audible as long, sustained tones of different pitches – like bass strings being plucked across a continent.

- **Occasional Events:**  
  - A sudden crack from pressure release between ceiling plates can be like thunder in a metal box.  
  - A massive avalanche break from the plant ceiling of a lower valley is heard as a drawn-out roar and series of crashes swallowed over the horizon.

### Fauna and Ecological Processes

The fauna of this world are **abundant**, but not crowded in the way of animals on a savanna.

The ecosystem is **active**, but its time-scales are longer than human lifetimes.

- **Gliders of the Plant-Ocean Surface:**  
  There are immense creatures that surf the surface of the plant forest below like birds over an ocean. They never land because “land” is not a surface, but a mesh so thin their bodies would break through.

  These gliders ride:
  - The pressure difference between vegetation regions,  
  - The updrafts from slight heat differentials in the plant mass,
  - The occasional shockwaves of movement in the plant matrix, when some huge portion of it detaches and falls.

- **High-Inhabitant Animals:**  
  Up near the ceiling and rock platforms, where the air is thin, there are:
  - **Sky-worms**, slow serpentine forms that float on the wind currents like eels in water, using thin air as a medium.  
  - **White owls**, or what seem like owls – but they are perfectly blind, their eyes sealed over, navigating instead by pressure, air temperature, and the sound of the world itself.  
  - **Small, scuttling things**, everywhere that stone meets plant, living in tiny cracks and eating dust, condensed moisture, and the spores released from the vegetation matrix.

- **Processes:**  
  - **The Migration of the Aurora.**  
    The massive floor-auroras travel along predictable latitudinal lines across the vegetation surface and can be seen in the distance as moving waves of light and current.  
  - **Predator–Prey Cycles.**  
    In the plant ocean below, invisible to you from here, there are huge, slow predators that follow the aurora paths like vessels following highways, because the electromagnetic currents of the auroras stimulate the plant matrix to produce small, mobile prey organisms in response.

- **Time:**  
  The “dynamic” aspect of this environment does not express itself as frantic motion. It’s more like a huge machine that operates slowly; but when you take into account the sheer scale of the processes operating – pressure plates grinding, air currents thousands of kilometers long, the plant-mass itself being a living river that advances over old stone – the activity is immense.

---

### Atmosphere: The Feel of the Place

You stand on a stone platform that projects over the edge of the cliff.

The stone under your feet is cold and sharp-grained.  
Condensation has collected on the rail-less edge of the platform, forming a thin layer of frost that you can scrape off with a thumbnail.

At the horizon, under the ceiling’s black glass, the faint blue line of stress-glow runs like a wire across the world’s lid.

Sometimes, when the ceiling plates groan and the pressure shifts, you can feel the air density change in your eardrums.

Sometimes, when the floor-aurora below moves closer, you see your own shadow stretched across the stone platform – black on black – cast by a light source from beneath.

Always, there is the sensation of standing on the edge of a huge, hollow, cold, living continent beneath a glass sky that never breaks open but never stays quiet.

---

This world is not empty.  
It is just **emptied**, spread thin over an immense volume of cold air and warm plant ocean under an unmovable, unforgiving ceiling of living glass.
