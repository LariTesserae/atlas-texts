**The Chamber of the Tireless Engine**

The first thing you notice is the sound—a low, industrial moan that never wavers, never quickens, never slows. It's the sound of machinery working perfectly, without resistance or friction, without the irregularity of wear or the hesitation of fatigue. It’s a sound that doesn’t so much fill the air as replace the concept of silence altogether.

You stand in a vast, cavernous chamber, perhaps half a kilometre long and three hundred metres wide. It feels like the inside of a colossal factory, but one abandoned by its architects and left to the tenants of this deep, warm earth. The air is thick and stagnant, a humid blanket that smells of ozone, hot oil, and something else—an acrid, almost metallic tang that coats the tongue.

The ceiling is low, a jagged dome of dark grey basalt. It is not smooth, having been recently formed by geological violence, and its surface is a dense latticework of spars and tiny, crystalline outgrowths. These are not the slow, patient drips of a limestone cave. This place is **young**; it is a scar in the world's skin, a new chamber beneath the surface of the planet, yet its volcanic birth was not the chaotic event one might expect.

The light is **dim**, cast by a single source. The fourth wall of this chamber is not rock but a seamless, monolithic drum of blackened, heat-resistant ceramic. It is the **Engine**, and it is the heart of this place. It stands nearly thirty metres tall, a perfectly smooth cylinder, its surface scored only by faint, glowing blue lines that seem to trace paths of immense internal pressure. There is no visible fire or exhaust, only the heat radiating from its immense surface in visible shimmering waves. It is **industrial**, yet its purpose is unfathomable. It doesn't drive pistons or turn wheels. It is the engine itself.

The floor of the chamber is a dense, **dense** ecosystem of metallic life. There is no water here. No pools, no drips, no humidity condensable on skin. This is a **non-aqueous chemistry**, a biome of rock and metal and the heat that is its lifeblood.

From the floor grows a sparse forest of **scattered** structures. They are not built by hands, but grown. Stalagmites of dark, polished metal rise from the ground, not in smooth cones but in intricate lattices of hollow tubes and sharp angles. They look like the skeletal remains of brutalist architecture, or the cooling towers of a long-forgotten power plant. These are the **vegetation** of this world, the **built** artifacts that are not built at all. Thin, translucent conduits, like fungal mycelia or the roots of a mangrove tree, weave across the bare stone floor between them, shimmering with faint, internal light.

The **fauna** is **abundant**, but not in the way of a surface jungle. There are no bodies here. Instead, the air is thick with a **strange** life of a different scale. **Loud** clouds of tiny metallic shards, no bigger than a thumbnail, constantly swarm around the Engine. They glint in the blue light, a shimmering column of kinetic dust that perpetually orbits the heat source. They are insects, but not of carbon and water. They are of iron and rare earth elements, their "flight" a form of controlled electromagnetic levitation, their sound a high-pitched, crystalline hum that is woven into the lower moan of the Engine.

Occasionally, a larger creature will detach from the lattice of a stalagmite-structure. It is a **warm**, slow-moving beast, perhaps a metre across. Its body is a segmented armoured shell of glossy ceramic plates, caked with fine dust. It doesn't have legs; it has a complex of articulated arms that it uses to move itself across the rock floor with a slow, soundless grace. Its purpose is unknown. It may be a grazer of the metallic minerals that leach from the rock, or a predator of the swarming shards. It is **never changing**, its motion as slow and predictable as the tide of a world with no moon. It moves to a new position and settles, becoming a part of the architecture itself once more.

The world of the Chamber of the Tireless Engine is a **small**, closed **cathedral scale**. Its boundaries are absolute: the jagged walls of raw rock, the impassive face of the Engine. It is a **stable** ecosystem, an **equilibrium** of heat and metal and motionless time. There is no day, no night, no seasonal drift. The **strange** balance of this **industrial** heart **below surface** is **frozen**—a perfect machine that has achieved its end state and will not change for a thousand years, or a million. The animals are silent, metal cogs. The plants are static, industrial spires. And the sound, that endless, unwavering sound, is the prayer of this perfect, metallic god.
