At first glance, the Water-Cathedral of Genna’s Spire looks like a mistake of geology.

It is a high place, all of it: a column of dark volcanic rock that punched up through an ocean of leaves, millennia ago, and then hollowed itself out as it cooled. Wind scours the summit of the spire. A notch of sky, perfectly framed, lets in a cool, constant light. Everything else—the floor below, the walls, the air itself—is taken.

By water.

Not a flood, but a permanent column of it, suspended in the heart of the mountain: a slow, vertical river of saltwater that has learned to climb. It rises from the dizzying depths of the ocean far below, up through the trunk of the spire, as if a current thousands of leagues long has been peeled into this single, narrowing, echoing chamber.

The stone of the chamber—smooth as obsidian glass, worn by patient ages of water—forms a cathedral-sized cylinder, maybe eighty meters high and half as wide. The central column of water climbs it not as a single torrent, but as a sort of transparent, spiraling staircase: a helix of currents and whirls, braided around one another with the unhurried precision of a slow storm.

It is a **submerged** world in the sense of total immersion, but it is not dark or deep in the abyssal way. The light that filters through the notch of the sky-well has a pale kelp-green tint to it, softened by passage through a roof of living mangrove-like canopy far below. The air is **cool**, almost always; the temperature of the ocean below, moderated by the height and the wind, but it never freezes and never warms into anything tropical. This place has settled into a long **established** balance, with **slow** and **patient** change.

What makes the Water-Cathedral truly **urban** and **total** in **vegetation** is not the stone itself, but what clings to it.

The **plants** here are not scattered: they are the **substrate**. Everywhere water meets stone—the rising column of sea, the inner walls of the chamber, the ceiling high above—there is a city of moss.

Not soft moss, but a **pure** ecosystem of mosses, algae and moss-like kelp-forms that have adapted to live in both air and water. They **fill** the space vertically, horizontally, and three-dimensionally along the flow of the current.

The **scale** of the inhabited space—judged by the human eye—is **small**, almost **groomable**: a cathedral, a grove, a coastal town compressed into a single vertical chamber. You can stand on the narrow stone ledge that encircles the interior wall and **see** it all: the spiraled water-column in the center, the moss-covered walls around, the sky above, the ocean’s reflected ceiling below.

But **density** is **sparse**, surprisingly so. There is **much space** between each presence of life, between each moss-cluster that chooses its territory on the rock wall. Wherever the moss does not thrive, there is **emptiness**: bare glass-burnished stone, blank water surface, a hanging **space** that allows every sound to resonate.

The **build** here is **urban**, but **primitive** in **technology**. The city is not carved of metal and stone; it is carved of **moss**.

A **layered** architecture rises from the cliff walls: **organized** lines of thick moss-carpets cascade down like roofing shingles, arranged in **settlement-scale** terraces. Each terrace is a **district**, a **hamlet-size** neighborhood of moss-aggregates. The **infrastructure** is **simple** and **hand-tool**: crafters have long ago manually **fabricated** a **town** here by pruning, training, and **simple** irrigation of the moss itself.

The **tech** level is **early-tool** at most: **pre-metal** blades of sharp shell and **simple** stone chisels are used to **manually** carve **paths** and **rooms** into the moss. **Fabrication** is **primitive**—knotted cord of kelp-fiber, **hand-built** levees and **weirs** shaped from **stone** and **bone**—but **organized** and **dense** in **construction** despite **low** technological sophistication.

The densities **balance** one another: **sparse** fauna and **sparse** structural mass **per** square meter, but arranged **infrastructure** on **urban** scale **within** this **grove** of **living** architecture.

Into this **submerged** **high** city **woven** with **all** **plant** **mass** comes **abundance** in **fauna**.
