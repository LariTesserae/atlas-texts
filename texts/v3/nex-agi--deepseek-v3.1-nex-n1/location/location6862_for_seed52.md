**The City Beneath the Root-Shadow**

Only the roots are truly visible from the ground floor of the world.

Massive trunks of the city-tree *Veridonum sp.* rise out of the synth-latice floor in that warm darkness like the pillars of some abandoned celestial temple. Each trunk is wider than a three-lane highway from the old-world, tapered in smooth, barkless curves of engineered chitin-plastic that faintly glow, casting a faint amber light that blends the dusk of extinction with the glow of new beginnings.

There is moisture here, but not the rain you're thinking of.

The dim light above coalesces into something else. You can't call it sky; it's a low ceiling of interlocked hexagonal leaves, each the size of a hovercraft. They don't drip rain. They *secrete*. Every so often, a quiver runs through the canopy, and a slow, silent sheet of water, thin as a soap film, descends. It slides down the trunks, collecting in engineered runnels, filling transparent reservoirs embedded in the roots themselves.

These reservoirs are not glass. They are material grown from the metabolic waste of the tree-city, a clear, self-mending skin of translucent cellulose that allows you to see the water within, faintly luminous with whatever microorganisms the city cultivates in its own closed circulatory system.

The air is warm, not with the heat of the sun, but from something else. The city itself is warm. It exhudes a faint metabolic heat that keeps the temperature a constant, pleasant 27 degrees Celsius. The humidity is high, but not oppressive because it's never truly cold. The warmth feels internal, like your own body heat, but magnified and stretched over the subterranean valley of root structures that you walk on.

This is not dirt. You walk on the root system itself, a spongy, slightly resilient gray surface that has been flattened into something like cobblestone by the passage of time and feet. It's faintly damp with condensation that appears from the air itself, as the air is slightly warmer than the surfaces it touches.

Vegetation explodes from every available surface of the city-tree, but not on the ground floor. The life is up there, in the dark canopy of leaves that hangs, maybe only thirty meters above you. The canopy of *Veridonum sp.* is not green.

It's a deep, indigo black.

When your eyes adjust to the dim glow of the trunks, you see the canopy is not a single mass of black leaf, but a fractal explosion of dark purple veined with faint cyan. The canopy is not photosynthetic in the ancient way. It doesn't need light.

It's radiation.

The city-tree is a radiation-metabolism plant. The faint, radioactive heat of the planet's core, or perhaps some arcane reactor buried deep within the root system, provides the energy for the city's metabolism. The leaves are not collecting light; they are collecting radiation particles, converting them into biological energy that allows the city to grow and shape itself. This is why the light level is so low, but the warmth is so high.

The fauna here are rare but unnerving when you finally see them.

They're not animals in the traditional sense. They're more like components of the city.

You see a movement out of the corner of your eye, something that you first dismiss as a falling leaf. But it doesn't fall right. It's too slow, too deliberate. It's a disk of chitinous material, about the size of a dinner plate, hovering on the faint electrical field generated by the radiating trunks. It's not flying. It's *supported*.

It's not a creature of flesh and bone, but of engineered material and perhaps some symbiotic gut-flora working in concert. The disk-creatures graze on the dust of the city, collecting spores of microorganisms that settle on the surfaces, or maybe they are just a part of the cleaning system, keeping the surfaces pristine as they hover around the trunks like silent, oversized, biological cleaning drones.

You almost don't notice the humans until you're right among them.

The buildings are not separate from the trees. They *are* the tree.

The city's built environment is the result of the *Veridonum sp.*'s own growth cycle. Certain sections of the trunk have been coaxed, by means of nano-biological engineering far beyond anything you can imagine, to grow cavities into their structures. These cavities are not random holes. They are windows, doorways, spiral staircases built into the trunk-wall itself that leads to higher levels of the city, where the "population" resides.

The "streets" are not true streets at all. They are the spaces *between* the colossal trunks. These spaces are lit with a faint, amber glow of the trunk bioluminescence, complemented by torches that are actually not flames at all, but more like glowing veins of fungi that wrap around specific columns, providing a faint, walking-level light source that doesn't break the darkness too much.

The "people" you finally see are not like you at first.

You see a silhouette of a figure tending to one of the root reservoirs. They are not carrying water in a bucket or turning on a faucet. They are touching the surface of the reservoir with the palm of their hand. The translucent skin of the reservoir *responds*. It parts like a membrane of living soap film, and then it begins to change color, a faint swirl of data indicating levels of hydration, nutrient content, and anything else the city's biology "wants" to tell its inhabitants.

The inhabitants of the city-tree are not just "living" here. They are in symbiosis with the city.

You might say that the city has not really been built by them. It has been built *for* them. By what? That's the question that the strange, quiet world of the root-shadow forces upon you.

The air is quiet.

The sound is not loud here because there are no engines, no cars, no wind machines. The dominant sound, if you can call it that, is the very faint *hum* of the city-tree itself. It's not the hum of electricity running through wires, but the deep, cellular hum of massive structures growing, very, very slowly.

Occasionally, you hear a sound of water, not from rain, but from the city itself. A drop of moisture collected from the air *condenses* and falls into a reservoir. The sound of that single drop hitting the surface of the reservoir of the city's root-district system echoes like a single rain of water in a deep dark cave.

The city stretches into the dimness as far as you can see.

The trunks of *Veridonum sp.* fade into the dark, not like a forest of discrete trees, but like the columns of a single, vast organism. They are evenly spaced, not random like the old forest of the world that might have been, before the radiation-cities of the root-shadow were ever conceived.

You are standing on the root-floor of a city that is simultaneously alive, intelligent, and built.

The floor beneath you heaves slightly. You freeze. It's not the ground. You're standing on a root. The city, as you walk, responds to your steps. It's not aggressive. It's not even defensive. It's just... aware. The city is aware of you, and in the dim warm darkness of the world, you are just a small, carbon-based creature walking on the epidermis of a mind that stretches in kilometers of quiet, root-shadow silence.
