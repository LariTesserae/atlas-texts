The sky of this world is not a sky. It is a ceiling of ancient root and bone-pale, calcified tendrils, hanging low like massive, inverted trees. They weep. A slow rain falls from them, not water but a thin, translucent sap that glows faintly with its own internal phosphorescence. It is the only light. It is a light that doesn't illuminate so much as stain the air, a constant amber twilight that clings to everything and hides the far horizons.

This world is not a planet, but a structure. The word "architecture" is too small, but it is close. It is a vast, inverted mountain of living wood and stone, a hollowed shell that hangs in the great emptiness. The air is warm, thick, and still. It smells like wet earth, decaying honey, and something metallic like blood after a rain. The space is vast beyond comprehension—a continent exists within the single trunk of this cosmic tree—but it is almost empty. The density of matter is terrifyingly low. What you perceive are the pillars that support the ceiling, and these pillars *are* the world.

They are called the Pillars of Deep Matter. They are not stone, but living entities of bulbous, fungal wood and dripping mineral growths. They are not close to you. They are not close to each other. The distance from one pillar's edge to the next is a vast, empty abyss of dim air and falling rain. It is a journey of seasons, to stand in the lurid glow of one pillar and see the impossible shape of the next, a faint smear of darker color in the dim amber. This is the vegetation of the world: not a ground cover, but a structural system, a tectonic-plate of fused, arboreal architecture.

The ground beneath you is not ground. It is the top of another pillar, far below the one you stand on. The entire floor of this world is a ceiling, viewed from a different angle. The "surface" you walk on is the upper surface of a lower pillar, a mesa of continuous matter that stretches beyond sight, but is itself just one node in the vast, hollow, honeycomb structure of the Tree-world. You exist on a thin layer of reality between two immense voids of different densities.

The erosion here is not of rock and wind. It is the erosion of memory. The world is old, established in its form, but that form is not stable. It is being undone by narrative. The world is made. It was constructed, by what or by whom is impossible to say, but every surface of every pillar bears the marks of it: carved channels that divert the weeping rain to unknown cisterns below, perfectly smooth facets of wood that could not have grown that way, and open-air chambers that flow like rooms into the void. The Tech is primitive. The tools are simple. The builders who shape this world are not the ones who made it. They are the Humble, a small-framed, diffuse people. They carve with sharpened spalls of bone and basalt, and they live with the constant knowledge that their world is a machine.

They are not the main fauna. The builders are busy insects in the shadow of leviathans. The fauna is abundant, but not numerous. It is vast, empty, and alive. The main creatures are the Kinderswallows, immense, slow-moving things that are less like animals and more like walking weather systems. They are the size of small fortresses, with translucent bodies of shimmering tissue that billows like sails. They feed on the light, the phosphorescent rain falling from the ceiling-world high above. They drift between the pillars, trailing their bodies like silent clouds, their gentle movement causing the only air currents in this otherwise still world.

But the reality of this world is breaking down. It is a thin film of matter over a deep, terrible emptiness. The Kinderswallows are not entirely animals; they are ghosts of potential matter. They change as you look at them. A creature that is a thing of skin and membrane might become a collection of floating stones and driftwood for a moment, before resolving into a cloud of pale leaves. It exists not as a single animal, but as a collection of possibilities that have never quite fully collapsed into a single reality. Predators are not beasts that hunt you, but fractures in the air. A shimmer in the dim light might be a beautiful iridescent sheen, or it might be a place where the world of the pillar simply stops and the void beneath you begins. It is not a place to be, but a place to be averted from.

And sound is absent. The rain falls silently. The Kinderswallows move through the air without a whisper. The Humble build with their tools, and even though you can see their arms strike stone with bone, you hear nothing but the faint rush of your own blood in your ears. This is not a silence, but a quietness of such deep and desperate pressure. It amplifies the smallness of your own being. The environment is active. The processes of this world are immense. The sap flows in its channels, fertilizing deep root-systems. The Kinderswallows migrate in slow cycles from the warmer regions of the Tree-world to the cooler edges of the trunk, their movements causing the faint, amber "light" to thin and thicken like seasons.

But beneath it all is the void. The wet world you can touch is a thin skin on the skeleton of something else. The pillars of Deep Matter are not truly matter; they are the solidified thoughts of a being sleeping in the emptiness beneath, and the rain that falls from the ceiling is not sap but its leaking memories. This is a world built on a dream, and the dreamer is beginning to wake. The edges of things are not what they seem.
