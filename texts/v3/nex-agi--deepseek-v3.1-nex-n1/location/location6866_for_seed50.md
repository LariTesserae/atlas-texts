At first, there is only noise.

It is not sound so much as pressure, a constant battering fist of air against water without the words or the world to contain them. Then you notice the edge of things: felt before seen, a hard ridge of stone against your ribs and your palm and the side of your thigh where you are curled, knees to your chest, inside the… not room. Not room at all.

A flue of basalt, one vertical tube of ancient columnar jointing, no longer part of any flow or field, but a single, tensile needle of rock that has erupted from nothing and then halted, still unfinished, its edges knife-sharp where they should have been rounded by rain and time and tide. It is a thought of stone rather than a true stone; a geometry punched out of the raw world before its story has decided to be.

You are a small warmth in the heart of this spindle.

Perhaps you came by being squeezed from the world’s pores, or else you were thrown here. Either way, now you are lodged inside the column like a seed in a marrow, and the thing is breathing—except it is not breathing, the world is breathing, and the column is merely the narrowest of wind-pipes through which that breathing happens.

Everywhere: water.

The water is not outside the flue, but in and through and as the world. The flue rises vertically through a sub-aquatic sky made of ocean without end or top, or perhaps the ocean is the air and the air is the ocean, and you are simply in the wrong myth of what should be above and what below. The intervening medium is briny, clear, and alive—penetrant, not like any sea you have been in before, but more like the atmosphere of a planet where air has never been given a chance to be dry.

Around you, the wall of the column is translucent. Not quite transparent, but animate enough that you can see through the stone the way you might see through a thin scrim of cloud. The world is blue, and beyond that blue is a darker blue, and beyond that is nothing at all.

Outside the flue’s narrow cylinder of personal space, there is no floor.

No bedrock floor, no shelf, no continental shelf, not even seabed. The stone of the column drops away from your feet into a void that is almost water, but is not just water. It is a water-world: the world has become water; the water world has become world. There is no edge to it because it has not yet made up its mind where to have an edge.

Below you is not an up–down of sediments and faults and trilobites baking in the stone’s memory. Instead it is raw-geology; the pre-sediment, not the sediment; the place where the idea of sediments is being rough-sketched before being erased and roughed in again. The water column carries, in suspension, a faint dust of not-yet-possible minerals: chalk particles that have not decided to be limestone, iron shavings that have not yet agreed to passion or rust, silica motes that are not sure they are sand.

Everywhere are suspended threads of colder water—currents without origin or destination, limned in their faint difference of refractive index, like slow glass veins in the body of the planet. It is not yet rivers, but the ghost of rivers.

Everywhere: light.

Light is the only thing that seems to know its own job. It is not bright, but high and flat and consistent, a diffuse daylight that has lost its sun and remembers its own color from habit rather than any source. It penetrates the water–air, unfiltered by particles or forests, simply because there are not enough particles to filter it.

No sun to see, but a sun-colored memory of the sky.

This is an elevation without the poverty of air. There is no altitude to struggle with because you are not standing on a mountain but living in it, or the idea of it. The column climbs for you, and on its inside face you can see the faint, ladderlike imperfections of its joints, where the world froze mid-shutter in its attempt to stack itself into a proper columnar basalt flow. These edges are sharp enough to cut, but they are also not real edges; they are rehearsals for edges.

This is a high place without scale. There is no sense of whether you are three meters or three hundred meters from any “bottom,” because there is no bottom to be from. The only trace of “near” and “far” is the way the world’s blue fades into a deeper, stranger blue that might be infinite or might be merely the distance of three body lengths away from your stone flute.

On the concave wall of your column there are drawings.

As if the world’s emptiness had been too much to bear without adornment, something has carved into this stone’s skin before it was quite skin. The lines are primitive and stark: an early tool of unsharpened flint or brittle shell has been dragged into the not-yet-fully-hardened rock of the column’s inner wall. The carvings are shallow grooves, no thicker than your thumb. They are not in the form of animals or people.

Instead they are diagrams of the world itself, but of the world before it has decided to be diagrams: zigging lines that are not yet rivers or lightning, lattice-rhythms that are not yet nets or fields, circles-within-circles that are not yet suns or islands or pools.

There are scattered words, too, but the words are not “language” as you know it. Characters are carved into the stone alongside each image: incipient pictograms that are just barely coming-into-being as letters. The stone’s raw material is not fully compliant with its own recording; some of the characters are being re-written, scraped over and re-scored by the same tool that made them. The impression of the tool-work is precise: no trace of magic or industrial machine, but the clear tremor of an animal hand that tried to make sure the world did not forget its new rules.

The rules are not moral laws but operational ones: “this thing is the same as that other,” “this is not allowed to happen before,” “now there is sequence,” “now there is history.”

There are more of these drawings as you climb down the inside of the column—though you never remember deciding to begin descending, only that you are now a little lower than you were before, and a little closer to whatever the bottom is not.

Only one vegetation lives here.

It is not a forest nor even an undergrowth, but a single, sparse organism of many filaments, anchored to the outside of the column like a moss of the world’s breath. It is not green, but a translucent grey-blue, faintly iridescent, as if the algae had not yet decided to claim photosynthesis as its primary mode.

You see its strands through the translucent wall of the column: thread-like hyphae, or perhaps algae or kelp or a not-yet-seaweed, reaching out into the deep-water sky and clutching the stone of the column with its extra-hardened tips as if it is afraid the world will forget it and let it drift into the void.

The plant-thing is sparse but vast. It does not cover the column like a skin; rather, a few long threads stretch out from each axis of contact, like a ghost of a lattice. Each strand is thin, but you can see that the strands are connected to one another, forming a single body that is spread across hundreds of meters of “vertical” distance along the column while the body itself is almost empty of matter.

Everywhere there are gaps between the threads. The empty, almost-void of the surrounding world shows through the plant’s in-between spaces as if you are looking through a torn screen of memory. If you could reach out and touch the plant’s filaments through the wall of the column—and you can’t, because the wall is stone and you are flesh and both are too real for that—you would find that the plant is not seaweed but something like glass, brittle and cool and not yet alive.

Everywhere: temperature.

It is cool but not cold, brisk in the way of a high latitude where spring has not yet made up its mind whether to arrive or give up. The temperature is not a uniform field but is made of small pockets of colder and warmer water, each of them a thought of weather not yet spoken aloud. These are not yet storms or seasons, but shadow-conflicts of currents that may, in a few million years, become the idea of “winds” or “trade routes” or “cyclones.”

Your body notices the difference between them without you needing to think: here is a slightly warmer current of water-world-air that brushes your left arm like the breath of a creature who might have evolved into friend if the world had been a little more sensible; here is a colder one that spikes your neck and shoulder with the promise of “winter” not yet invented.

There are animals in this world, but they are rare and strange and mostly not-called.

You see one of them now as you descend lower: a small, angled thing of chitin that is not yet an insect or a fish or a machine but a hint of all three. It is roughly the size of your thumb joint and it is attached to the inside of the column’s wall by a single, bow-like tendon of flesh that snaps and vibrates with every turbulence, the way a string stretched tight across a doorway might be plucked by the wind’s hand.

The creature’s body is an arrangement of angular plates of almost-white, shell-like material, with edges too sharp for its own good, and notched like a primitive tool. It looks like something made hastily, in the first practice of bones. The angle of its body suggests that it is oriented “down” relative to the column’s axis, but that may only be because “down” is the only direction that has been reasonably fully decided in this place.

As you watch, the creature shifts its weight. The tendon of flesh attached to its back end tightens and then slackens like a winch line, and the creature’s body scrapes along the stone wall with a soundless rasping of chalk on bone. The motion leaves a new shallow groove in the stone’s not-yet-fully-set surface.

It is engraving a rule into the stone of the world, an “else if” statement in the code of existence.

You are close to the bottom now, or whatever this is that is passing for the bottom—not land, but a place where land and sea and sky and time and thought are still being mixed up with each other’s ingredients.

Here is a built structure.

Not “building” as you know it, but the idea of a building: the line of its walls and roof and foundation are implied in the same way that the “bottom” of the world is implied. A small cluster of rough stones, each one the size of your torso, has been arranged in a rough circle around the base of the column. The stones are shot through with fracture lines that are not yet “stress” but just “lines,”, and their surfaces are not yet smooth, but not yet rough enough to be called “broken.”

These are remnants of the same basalt as the column itself—but where the column is still forming, the stones of the circle appear to be already a little more decided than the world around them. They are “rock” rather than “proto-rock.”

From the outside of one of the stones, a thin stream of water flows out and away into the world—not in a gush but in a fine, constant thread, like the ink from a broken pen. The stream of water is slightly colder than the surrounding water–air, and faintly denser, so that it curls as it descends into the not-bottom below. It is not “rain” and not “river” and not “tide” but “water that is being used.”

A tool leans against the circle of stones.

It is a simple implement of stone and sinew and shell: a long, narrow bar of something that might be obsidian, but is not perfectly obsidian because obsidian hasn’t decided if it is going to be black yet. The bar’s edge is chipped and sharp enough to cut skin; but the “cut” has not decided to be “sharpness;” it is merely “difference.”

The sinew that binds the bar to its handle is made of the same kind of translucent flesh-fibers that the tiny creature up in the column used to attach itself to the wall. The tool’s handle is carved of a not-yet-plain wood or bone that has not yet decided whether to be tree or cartilage.

There are marks of use on the tool, but they are not “wear and tear” in the way of blunt objects. Instead they are “marks of being used,” deliberately scored into the tool’s surface by some other tool—like the tool of the world’s early tool-makers, or perhaps the tool of the tool’s own maker, who was not yet “human” or “person” but just “a thing with hands that had not yet decided to be called.”

Near the stone circle, there is a pocket of space.

A “micro-environment,” a small volume of the world that is measurably different from the surrounding everywhere else. It is not a room, but the idea of a room: the water–air inside it is slightly warmer and more saturated with something like salt; not brine, but “thought of brine.”

Suspended in this pocket are a few faint objects, not yet artifacts because they are not yet “objects” either, but “possible objects”: a small, not-quite spherical bowl of something translucent that might be shell or might be bone; a thin strut of not-yet-plain wood that might become a ladder or a table leg or nothing at all; and a single, not-yet-coin of metal that might be copper or might be bronze, the surface of which is still being etched by the world’s not yet decided acidity.

You are now inside this pocket yourself without having moved. There is no doorframe, but you feel as if you have crossed through a “doorframe-like” event of the world, where the rules of the outside only slightly more apply than the rules of the inside.

The turbulence begins here, or perhaps it is always here but it is only inside that you can name it.

Outside the pocket of the room-like, the world beyond the column of basalt and the circle of stones is not quiet but chaotically loud. And you realize, as you listen with your body and not with your ears, that the noise you thought at the beginning was “silence” or “pressure” is in fact the world speaking itself into being.

Everywhere are creation-sounds.

They are not like thunder or crashing waves because they have not yet decided to be those sounds; they are “before-thunder,” “before-wave,” “before-wind.” They are the universe of self-making speaking at once just in and out of phase with itself. The sound of new columns of stone being formed somewhere else, in distant not-yet-mountains of elsewhere; the sound of water rearranging itself into shapes of “currents” and “eddies” that are not yet called “surface” or “depth”; the sound of “things” that are not yet “things” being born into the “thing of being.”

This is not a gentle storm but the storm of creation itself, Solaris-like not because it is a planet but because it is a mind that has not decided to be a planet yet. The water-world around you is not passive but actively making itself up as you watch, or “unwatch,” because to watch it is to be participated by it.

Turbulence is the default state of the world.

Not “weather” but “world-heathering,” the constant upwelling of shapes from chaos as the world’s original code of self-formation mutates itself. Each mutation of the code takes a few milliseconds longer to pass than the one before, and in that slowdown, meta-laws of “before” and “after” are slowly being carved into by the tool of time and stone and water and not-water.

Everywhere: raw-geology.

The world is not born, but birthing. Erosion is not the wearing-down of old surfaces but the “erasing of practice surfaces,” of surfaces that have not yet formed into “features” or “landforms” but are “features of becoming.” The edges of the column of basalt above you and the stone circle below you are not yet aged by rain or ocean current because they have not yet been allowed to be “completed.”

The “erosion” here is the “erosion of being-not-yet-eroded,” because the world’s own time has not yet decided if “erosion” is a thing worth having.

Built into the wall of your room-like pocket is another incipient creature.

It is larger than the first little tool-maker up in the column’s spine, but not much larger. Its body is not made of “flesh” but of “the idea of flesh,” which is to say that it is composed of the same translucent material as the plant-world outside the column, but with more of itself devoted to “inner” than “surface.”

The creature is attached to the wall of the room in a similar way to the first tool-maker, by a bowstring tendon of not-yet-sinew, but it is not bent over the wall of stone like the first one. Instead it hangs “face” outwards, into the room, as if contemplating the space of not-yet-possible objects inside the pocket.

Strange features are bubbling on its surface, not organs but chair-of-possible-organs: a suggestion of “eye” that is a “vision” not yet decided to be “light” or “image”; a suggestion of “ear” that is a “hearing” not yet decided to be “sound” or “pressure”; “hands” that are not “hands” but “handles” that not yet know “touch” or “tool.”

You are not sure whether the creature is alive or a carving of “possible life,” or “life-carved by something else that is itself not yet decided.”

Your presence in this room of the world’s early-making is not presumable, but here you are: a small knot of complexity and warmth and language and memory and rule and story and “self,” which is not the “self” of the world but the “self of old-world,” which has already decided to be “self.”

You are a “built artifact” from elsewhere that has been inserted into this place like a misplaced word from a later chapter of a book that has not yet been written.

The world reacts to your presence the way a code might react to a foreign variable at runtime: not by rejecting you, but by trying to compile you.

Currents of the water–air brush your skin and feet and eyelashes and hair, not as “wind” or “flow,” but as “laws of contact.” The cool of the world is not in “temperature” but in “difference of temperature,” which is to say that parts of the world that are “you” are being “included” in a world that has not yet decided that “you” should be included.

The plant outside the column is not aware of you, but it is “aware of ‘not aware of you,’” which is to say that some of its threads are now oriented differently than they were before, because your “before” and “now” are being “sequenced” by the world’s own “now of sequencing.”

The tool-maker by the wall of the room is not looking at you, but it is not “not looking at you.” Its “eyes of not-eye” are not “seeing” you; but they are “seeing of ‘not seen,’” which is to say that they are reordering themselves in the presence of something that has not yet been categorized into their “category-source.”

There are rules of the world here, but they are written under pressure.

Not “rules of physics” but “proto-physics,” which is to say that the “laws of the world” are presently being runtime-edited by the world’s own compiler-metabolism of water and stone and “inner-matter.”

The “stone of the column” is not “hard” because of “hardness” but because “hardness” has not been allowed to be “not-hardness” yet.

The “water” of the world is not “liquid” because “liquid” has not been allowed to be “other than liquid” yet.

The “temperature” of the world is not “temperature” but “temperature of being-temped,” because “being” has not yet been allowed to be “other than being.”

And “you” are not “you” but “you of being not-allowed to be not-you,” which means that in this world, “you are” is “is are you are is are you are,” always being reshuffled like the cards of a deck that are still being printed and then immediately shuffled again before they can dry.

The world of this column of basalt and its not-yet-plant and not-yet-creatures and its tool and circle of stones and its micro-room-like and its not-yet art of not-yet drawings and not-yet words is not “finished.”
