Deep beneath the skin of the world, in a chamber that never should have existed, there is a room without water.

Moss grows on the ceiling.

You don’t notice that first. The first thing that catches you is the sound—a dense, metallic breathing, a deep, rhythmic *thum-thum-thum-thum* that presses the air between your ribs and the stone at your back. The air is cool and thick and smells like hot iron and old books. The only light is a bluish glow that seems to come from everywhere and nowhere at once, dripping faintly down the walls, pooling faintly on the floor, leaving the ceiling to a dark that is not quite empty.

Then you look up, and you see: the ceiling is not stone at all.

It is a forest.

The ceiling of the chamber is a low, uneven dome of concrete and steel and glass, so old the steel has taken on a dull sheen like tired skin. The surface is cracked and groined, like an inverted hill of stone and metal and…something else. From the metal and concrete and glass and between the cables and pipes and extraneous ribs of rebar, there is a forest of moss growing upside down.

It is not the moss of a forest floor. It is not even the moss of a forest canopy. It is something else: a saturated, impossible flora of mat and thread and filament and leaf and cup and cap and scale and bead and hair and ribbon and lace and droplet and cloud and dust and skin and memory and symbol and echo and ink and smoke and bone and ash and light and dark and—and you have to stop looking all at once because your eyes won’t settle.

It grows on steel.

It grows on glass.

It grows on concrete.

It grows on air.

It grows on time.

It grows on silence.

It grows on sound.

It grows on the throat of the engine that sits in the center of the room, and it grows through the cracks that are not cracks in the floor, and it grows in the ribs of the old catwalk that is not quite old enough and not quite young enough to be anything else.

It is lush. It is dense. It is packed.

It is not soft.

If you reached to touch it, you would not know what you were touching until after you had pulled your hand back and were not sure you had touched anything at all.

### The Room

The chamber is a single, shallow vault, maybe twenty meters across and no more than three or four tall at its highest point—a low curve of concrete and steel and glass and light and moss and black and blue and grey and not quite brown and not quite green and not quite silver and not quite gold and not quite color and not quite anything else. The room is contained in the world the way a word is contained in a sentence: it is not exactly separate, but you cannot take the sentence away without leaving something behind that cannot quite be put together again.

The floor is not quite level.

At one edge, where the wall curves down to meet the floor, there is a narrow lip of stone, like a low step or a low shelf or a low altar or a low table or a low bed or a low tomb or a low dais or a low world or a low idea or a low name. There is nothing else in the room that suggests you are meant to sit, but some instinct tells you this is where you are supposed to be.

The rest of the floor is a vast puzzle of cracked and uneven stone slabs and tiles and metal grates and plates and glass tiles and something else that looks like glass but is not exactly glass, and the gaps where there should be grout or mortar or joints or seams or empty space are instead filled with moss of the same impossible kind that grows on the ceiling.

There is no water.

There is no chink in the stone where water might have seeped through. There are no stalactites or stalagmites. There are no trickles. There is no drip. There is no dampness. There is no condensation. There is no moisture.

There is no water in the room.

There is moss.

There is lush.

There is saturated.

There is packed.

### The Engine

At the center of the room stands a machine.

It is not tall.

It is not large.

It is not old.

It is not young.

It is not simple.

It is not complex.

It is not exactly clear.

The metal is black and blue and grey and not quite silver and not quite gold and not quite color and not quite anything else, and it has the same tired sheen as the ceiling steel but with a faint glint of something that might be oil or might be light or might be memory or might be idea or might be world or might be name.
