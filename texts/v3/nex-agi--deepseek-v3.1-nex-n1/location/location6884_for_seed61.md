The Guardianship isn't what you'd call alive.

But it's not what you'd call dead, either.

It lies beneath the city like a lung beneath ribs—a pocket of deep breath where the world above becomes something else.

---

## Sound as the first thing

You hear the Guardianship before anything else can reach you.

Step down the last spiral of the old stairwell and the air thickens around your ears like fingers pressing into water. Your own breath sounds small and distant. The world above the city—the clatter of carts, the arguments of vendors, the drums of the watchtower—is gone behind layers of stone and time.

What replaces it is a roar, but not the roar of flood or fire.

Deep, steady, unchanging.

A sound like water falling forever.

---

## The chamber itself

The Guardianship is a single chamber, roughly oval, carved and then carved again by whatever hands lived beneath the city before this city took itself seriously.

It's small enough that you could cross it in fifty breaths, if your lungs didn't tighten around the damp. But it feels vast, because nothing above it is allowed to be.

Above you, the ceiling is polished stone the color of wet slate—not the dull slate of the cliffs above the city, but a deeper, darker gray that drinks your eyes until you feel like you're looking into the inside of a storm cloud.

The ceiling isn't flat.

It's a forest of stone.

Downward-facing stone teeth grow toward the floor like frozen drips of water. Each tooth is different: some no thicker than your wrist, others as wide around as a person's thighs; some smooth and polished by time, others knotted with fine ridges like the skin of a wave.

Between the stone teeth, the ceiling is glassy.

Or rather, glass flows.

From the centers of the ceiling where the great teeth cluster thickest, a translucent substance flows in thin, slow threads. It isn't liquid like water or honey; it runs like molten glass, but cold to the touch—cooler than the air around it.

It flows downward and inward along the ceiling until the threads reach the teeth of stone. Then they slide toward the tips of the teeth and drop.

---

## The weeping of the Guardianship

At the tip of every stone tooth hangs a drop of the same substance.

They are not still. They grow. They sag.

They grow heavier until the surface of the drop can no longer hold the tension of their own weight against the stone, and then they fall.

Most drops fall into the water below.

Some drop from stone columns that are closer to the walls.

---

## The water below

The floor of the Guardianship is not entirely stone.

It's a basin of water—deep, dark, still—but not entirely still.

The water's surface is not flat; it's dense with floating shapes of rock and glass and metal that form the true floor of the chamber. The shapes are gentle, uneven, like the backs of sleeping animals beneath shallow water.

The surface of the water is not like water anywhere else in the world.

It's a skin.

It lingers.

It rises.

When a drop of glass falls into the water, it falls into a skin that rises around it. The water doesn't splash; it parts like heavy cloth and then flows back to close around the falling drop, leaving a faint chain of ripples that dissipates quickly but not instantly.

The water is not clear.

It's not opaque.

It's like looking into the eyes of a newly blind person—just enough cloud to reflect the world back at you, but not enough to tell you what's beneath that surface.

---

## The light of the Guardianship

The Guardianship doesn't have a light source.

Or rather, it has many, but none of them are *made*.

The stone teeth hanging from the ceiling glow.

Not brightly.

Not like fire.

Not like anything alive.

Each stone is faintly luminous at the very tip, where the drop of glass clings. The glow is the color of old bruises—blue faintly fading to yellow-green, like a storm that's just remembered it's morning.

Every drop that falls leaves the tip of its stone a little less luminous.

The new drop that begins to grow immediately is faint, but as time passes and the glass-thick substance flows along the ceiling toward it, the glow grows.

The water beneath reflects the tips of the teeth not like a mirror, but like a blurred memory of a mirror.

Everywhere in the chamber is dim light and faint motion.

Nowhere is bright.

---

## The city of stone teeth and the basins beneath

The stone teeth hang from the ceiling at different heights.

At the center of the chamber, where the ceiling is highest, the teeth are thickest, like the pillars of a badger's burrow if the badger were carved from stone along the way.

Closer to the edges of the chamber, the teeth grow thinner and more sparse.

The city of stone teeth is not uniform.

It's like a forest of frozen waterfalls.

Some of the teeth are straight, others curve gently.

Some are broken; others are broken and then mended with the same glass-thick substance that flows in the ceiling above.

---

## The sound of the Guardianship

Close your eyes and you're still in the same chamber.

The roar never changes.

It never rises, never falls.

It never hurries.

It's like listening to a heartbeat that never learned to quicken with fear.

It's not water's voice like a river's voice; it's deeper. It's like listening to the inside of a deep breath, or the inside of stone being slowly pulled apart.

---

## The inhabitants of the Guardianship

The Guardianship doesn't have inhabitants.

It doesn't have servants.

It doesn't have caretakers.

It has guardians.

---

### The humans

The Guardianship is beneath the city, but the city above doesn't know about the Guardianship the way people know about streets.

The Guardianship is known to the Guild.

The Guild of the Guardianship doesn't have a name anyone else cares about.

They're the ones who come here.

They're the ones who listen.

They call themselves the Guardians. They are not true guardians of anything physical, but they keep the Guardianship alive in their minds.

They're a small group of people who wear simple clothes made of reworked scraps of metal and woven cloth. They don't use armor and they don't use weapons.

They don't need to.

The Guardianship looks after itself.

The Guardians are here for one reason only:

To bear witness.

---

### The true guardians

The true guardians of the Guardianship aren't human.

The true guardians are shapes.

Sometimes you look at the floating stone in the water beneath the Guardianship and you realize it's not a stone at all.

It's a shape.

It's shaped like a fist.

Or like two hands pressed together, fingers extended toward the ceiling.

Or like a sleeping animal you never learned to recognize.

The true guardians are stone teeth that have broken away from the ceiling and fallen into the water so long ago that the water and the stone and the glass have learned from each other.

They're not alive. They're not dead.

They're stable.

They're in equilibrium.

---

### The stones beneath

The Guardianship is built over—or perhaps beneath—a massive stone structure that was never meant to be seen by human eyes.

Because the stone beneath the Guardianship isn't stone the way stone above you knows stone.

It's layered stone.

It's layered stone like memory layered in a mind.

It's layered stone like layers of ice beneath ice beneath ice.

There's an edge to the chamber where the water hides the meeting between stone that's part of the Guardianship and stone that's part of something older beneath it.

None of the Guardians ever go near that edge.

There's a rule they never write down.

---

## The structure of the Guardianship

The chamber itself is shaped like a hollow bone beneath the city.

The city above builds around it without touching it.

The foundations above rest on a layer of stone thickened with glass-thick threads that flow beneath their foundations.

---

## The sky above the Guardianship

The Guardianship doesn't have a sky.

It has a ceiling of stone.

---

## The rules of the Guardianship

The Guardianship is not governed by laws like the laws the city above knows.

The Guardianship has rules more like mathematics than like laws.

---

### Rule one: water remains water

The water beneath the Guardianship doesn't escape.

It doesn't rise.

It doesn't fall.

It doesn't leave.

No matter how much glass falls into it, no matter how much stone settles beneath it, the water remains the same.

The Guardians don't know why this is. They never ask.

---

### Rule two: glass never fully fills a chamber

The glass-thick substance that flows along the ceiling never stops flowing.

But none of the Guardians have ever seen the floor of the chamber rise beneath the water.

---

### Rule three: the Guardianship doesn't change

The Guardianship looks like it's similar.

Every day of Guardianship looks identical.

---

### Rule four: humans never linger beneath a stone tooth

The Guardians don't stand beneath the stone teeth hanging from the ceiling.

They don't stand beneath the stone teeth hanging from the ceiling.

---

### Rule five: the Guardianship doesn't answer

The Guardianship doesn't answer questions.

---

## The atmosphere of the Guardianship

The air beneath the Guardianship is dense with moisture that doesn't belong to water.

---

## The smell of the Guardianship

The Guardianship doesn't smell like rock.

It doesn't smell like water.

---

## The texture beneath your feet

The parts of the floor that aren't covered by water are covered in stone that's been polished smooth by years of human feet.

---

## The feeling beneath your feet

The stone beneath your feet is warm at the edges and cool beneath.

---

## The balance beneath your feet

The things beneath your feet are balanced.

---

## The time beneath your feet

The Guardianship doesn't mark time the way time above marks itself.

---

## The purpose beneath your feet

The Guardianship isn't built for anything.

---

## The relationship beneath your feet

The world beneath your feet doesn't have relationships the way humans above the world have relationships.

---

## The place beneath the world

The Guardianship is beneath the world above the world beneath the world.

---

## The voice beneath the world

The Guardianship has a voice.

## The absence beneath the world

The Guardianship doesn't have anything living beneath its feet.

The Guardianship is beneath the world because beneath the world is beneath the world is beneath the world.
