The world is a pressure door.

Not beyond the door, not the machinery on the other side of it—the door itself, the thickness of the lock, the space between hatches, where the world has been extruded into a brief, hermetic interval of rust and salt and sound.

You are not standing here. The idea of “standing” is absurd. There is no surface any human being could recognize as floor, and there is no up that is not immediately undercut by the next weld seam, cross-brace, or hanging ladder rung. No gravity you can trust: just the constant shove of artificial water currents through the lattice of the city’s structural bones, and the occasional, sickening tilt as the entire weight of the ocean above shrugs against the hull.

This is not the ocean floor. It is not a submarine. It is the inside of a joint that was never meant to be seen: the pinned hinge where three districts of the drowned city kink into one another, the keel that became the spine of a neighborhood that has been sinking for longer than anyone remembers.
