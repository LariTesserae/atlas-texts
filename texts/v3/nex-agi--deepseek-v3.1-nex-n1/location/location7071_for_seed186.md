Think of the planet as a slow turbine.

High above what you would call the atmosphere, crystalline flanges of rock turn through a vacuum thick with ice. Where the flanges graze the edges of low space, they carve rings of blown frost and dust that fall down toward the spinning planet beneath in a loose, spiraling veil of white.

This is the sky of the Signless Tine: not a dome of blue, but a deep black well out of which snow descends forever.

### The Mountain That Pierces the Night

You stand on a slope of black basalt polished smooth by the continuous fall of ancient ice. The stone underfoot is not cold to the touch—or rather, it is, but your nerves are too stunned by the scale of things to register it properly. Everything is immense and far apart.

This is the world of the Tall, and the Tall do not build things smaller than themselves.

The slope rises in front of you in a series of unnervingly regular steps, each hundreds of strides deep, each tier darker than the last. The mountain itself is a tetrahedron so sharp-edged it seems like a cutout of reality rather than a geological object. It does not so much reach the sky as *replace* it: the peak vanishes into a throat of permanent darkness so high that the whole upper body of the world looks like it has been snapped off and lost into space.

Yet the sky is never entirely empty. Mile upon mile of scaffolded glacier hang beneath the mountain’s upper reaches—ice cantilevered out over thin air, arranged by invisible hands into lattices that hold the snowpack in place like living shelves. The weight of these ice terraces bends the rock itself into the flat steps you see below you.

Over and above the mountaintop, the turbine of the world turns slowly: a ring of broken moons and sculpted stones that were once closer satellites, now harnessed and repositioned into a single engine of catastrophic moderation. Its purpose is clear to the eye—but the eyes of the Tall, long gone from this surface, left the wheel turning with their absence.

### Temperature and Time: The Deep Cold That Remembers

You are very cold, but not in the way of merely harsh weather. This is cold as a physical state of the world itself—cold as a deep, stable memory of the universe.

The air clings at your clothes and skin with the petulant grip of a very heavy child. It is not a thin, sharp alpine cold; pressure is high enough, but the molecular life of heat has been leeched out of it by the planet’s extreme altitude and the quiet energy of the machine above. The warmth of your body is an obvious, rude anomaly in a space that prefers zero.

On your eyelashes, tiny six-sided air-ices form—not frost, but delicate polyhedral crystals of solidified air, that sometimes drift off your lashes and float down to the basalt as unmelting diamonds. When you step, they crinkle under your boots like glass sugar. Within the crystal of O2 is a dim glow like trapped twilight; within the crystals of N2 and the trace noble gases, deep black and almost purple, as if swallowing light.

The temperature here is always dancing within the same range: from just barely above the point of collapse, where gases would turn into transparent supercritical fluids, and a few degrees colder. The turbine seen above is the regulator of this dance. It circulates heat from the deep crust of the world into the atmosphere and then bleeds it back into space—slowly, precisely, over uncountable millennia.

In other words: the cold of the Signless Tine is not an accident of the weather. It is a *filtering*.

### Water: Conditional Dampness

Water plays here, but only under the strict rules of the Tall and their machine.

You see no lakes, no open oceans. The planetary scale is too dry, too high for that. Yet the ground is not sterile desert. Deep inside the stone steps, under channels of ice that you do not at first recognize as channels, you hear the faintest sloshing—water kept under high pressure, never freezing all the way through, because the molecules of ice itself are *structured*.

These ice channels are not like rivers or regular pipes. They are more like veins of frozen memory. The ice of the Signless Tine is patterned with curves and spirals that, looked at from the right angle, resolve into information: flow patterns, logics of rotation, the syntax of ancient control algorithms. The Tall never wrote in words; they wrote in ice.
