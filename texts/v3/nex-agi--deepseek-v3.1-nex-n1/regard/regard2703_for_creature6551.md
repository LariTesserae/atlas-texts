A presence registers itself.

It is not like the arrival of a wind or the change in pressure before a storm. It is more like the realization, spreading quietly from edge to edge of the world, that something has been listening all along.

The stone breathes.  
The water beads.  
The worms inch forward.  
You wait.

Nothing is overturned. No skies are parted. No voice booms. The benevolent power is not interested in spectacle; instead, it chooses a very small gesture.

---

### 1. An Eye That Opens

One of the stone faces on a wall nearby you has not moved.  
If you close your eyes and open them again, you would not be able to point and say with certainty “that one changed.”  

And yet.

This is how you know it has changed:

You have passed by this wall twice before.  
The first time, you were attracted by the pattern of water trickling from a bowl above, down along the faint paths of older worm trails, across the curve of a cheek and into the basin at the bottom.  
The second time, you were tracing the progress of a new worm as it mapped its own track across a forehead and into a shadowed niche.  
Each time, you looked at the face.  

Its eyes were shallow depressions—no more than thumb-prints pressed into stone by some tool centuries ago, then worn by drops of water and the slow, oblique abrasion of time.  

Now you notice that the left of those two hollows has a deeper darkness at its center.

You are very sure that was not there before.

It is not like a pupil. It is not like a lens. It is not anything you can name.  
It is simply a place where what should be stone is…less.  

You look away.

When you look back, the darkness has not changed its position.  
It is still there, at the bottom of that eye-hollow, watching the world with the kind of attention that does not need to blink.

---

### 2. The First Tone

The face does not speak to you.

No voice reaches out in your own language, or in any language you know.  
No sound shakes loose from the stone throat.

And yet, when you are a certain distance from the wall, you hear a tone.

It is not loud. It does not echo from elsewhere to fool you about its origin.  
It comes from the stone under your feet, then through your bones, and only then through your ears.

It is a single, clear tone—like a struck bowl of glass filled with water—but steadier than that, and simpler than that.

It does not change pitch.

It does not change volume.

It does not change.

It is simply there.

After you have been listening for a long time—minutes, maybe—you realize that the tone is not coming from the stone in the way you first thought.

It is coming from something *through* the stone.  
Something *using* the stone as a medium.

Once you have that thought, the world shifts slightly in your mind.

The stone-walls are not, for you, any longer “a solid thing made of blocks,”  
but rather “a membrane stretched between the inside of the world and the outside of you.”

You are standing on the surface of a drumskin made of a city’s layers,  
and the tone is being played on the far side of that skin by something that does not want to break through.

---

### 3. The Water Learns Something

The water has been moving.

Before you noticed, most of the water simply beaded on surfaces and slipped down the shortest route to a lower basin, guided by gravity and random texture of stone.

But now you are watching.

You are watching a patch of wet wall where you noticed the face with the darker eye.

You are watching water.

One bead forms at the top of the forehead, where a rough edge of stone meets the underside of a bowl that you know to be already overflowing.  
The bead should slide down the center of the face, over the bridge of the nose, and fall into the basin below.

It should.

Instead, something like hesitation touches the bead.

Instead of moving down, it spreads along the upper curve of the forehead, tracing a line along the hairline of the stone face—from the center toward the left eye.

Two more beads gather from pores in the stone above and join the first.

They merge into a single thin ribbon of moisture.

The ribbon does not drip.  
It does not break.  
It does not behave like water in your world.

It behaves like water that has been taught to be a thread.

It is led by some invisible gradient of attraction that you cannot see or measure—  
some small change of surface, a nudge of tension, a temperature almost invisible to you but perfectly visible to water.

The line curves along the upper edge of the eye socket.

It passes along the lower edge of the socket.

In this way it traces, exactly once, a perfect circle around the eye of the stone face.

At the moment it completes the circle, the darkness inside the eye deepens.

Nothing else changes.

The water continues down from the corner of the eye, along the cheek, and into the basin below—just as before.  
There is no lightning.  
No sound but that steady tone.

But you have seen it.

You have seen the water being taught to trace a circle around an eye.

---

### 4. The Worm Feels the Tone

The worm is moving.

You have been watching this worm for longer than you think.

It is the one that first climbed over the edge of the basin and onto the forehead;  
the one that now rests its leading segments on the bridge of the nose,  
its trailing segments still in the water of the bowl,  
its body in between bridging the two.

The stone of its body is darker than the wall behind it.  
Dark segments, shallow ridges between them.

You are watching, and you notice the first time the tone of the world affects the worm—

you notice because the worm does not react like an animal would.

It does not startle.

It does not curl.

It does not change direction at speed.

It is simply that, halfway through its next wave-of-motion,  
one segment of the worm’s body does not follow the smooth curve of the previous segments.

That segment stiffens.

For just a heartbeat, its curve becomes sharper than it should be.

At the same time the open tone in the world under your feet slides slightly in pitch—not like a performer adjusting a note, but like someone has taken the world-cable it is stretched along and gently tightened it.

Immediately after the higher pitch, there is a brief flutter of softer sound—like a string quickly plucked three times in the same place.

The stiff segment settles again.

The worm resumes its slow forward climb.

That is all.

But you have seen an inanimate stone animal being tuned by the world-tone.

---

### 5. The Pattern Emerging

You are moving now.

Not because you were told to move, but because you want to test whether the pattern you are beginning to suspect is real or only a trick of your own mind.

You walk from wall to wall.

You are looking for more of the dark-eyed faces.

It is not easy to find them.

There are thousands of carvings on every wall;  
thousands of shallow sockets where you might be able to persuade yourself that you are seeing a deeper darkness than before.

You are strict with yourself.

You let only those faces count as “changed” where you can clearly remember what the eye looked like before you felt the presence of the benevolent power.

You are strict with yourself.

You are not sure how you know this, but somewhere in the back of your mind you have decided that this is part of the test.

---

On the third terrace you come to one.

You have been here before.

You remember this face because, of the two eyes, the left had been half-broken away by a repair—someone had cut a new block of stone for the wall and set it into place, leaving the eye that used to be there as only a suggestion of a curve under the new stone.

You remember that detail.

Now you look at the same face.

The new block of stone is still there.  
The edge of the old eye is still broken under it.

But the right eye, which you had dismissed as simple and ordinary and whole,  
now has a deeper darkness at its bottom.

You are not sure at exactly what moment between the last time you looked and this one that change happened.

The same way you are not sure when day becomes night if the change is subtle enough.

But you know the tone has been present under your feet since you left the first wall.

---

On the fifth terrace, you find another.

You remember this one because, of all of them, this had the least relief of any features on the entire wall.  
Everything else is carved deeper;  
this face is almost like a suggestion sanded away by a thousand years of water.

You are not sure what you feel now, except that you feel the tone of the world as clearly as you feel your own heartbeat.

You step closer to the wall.

You see the faintest suggestion of a curve where the right eye should be.

And you see the darkness—not at the bottom of a deep socket, but hovering at the center of the shallow curve like a pupil painted on a flat stone.

You are not sure what you are looking at.

But you know that, if you were to draw a line from the first eye you noticed to this second one to the third, and if you were to continue the line in your mind’s eye across the world’s surface,  
you would be pointing somewhere that feels like *direction* rather than like “random collection of walls.”

You are not sure what you are pointing at.

You are not sure the benevolent power wants you to know yet.

But you know that you are beginning to feel the geography of the changed world.

---

### 6. What The Power Does Not Do

The presence is not gentle in the way of soft things.

It is not the kind of benevolence that makes you comfortable.

It does not change the temperature,  
does not soften the stone under your feet,  
does not provide you with shade where the bright unchanging sky is always there.

It does not give you food.

You are still hungry.

You are still thirsty.

You are still alone.

The benevolent power does not take away your need to be fed and watered and rested.

You might feel, at first, that this means the power is not actually benevolent at all—  
only curious and remote and interested in your reactions like a child might be interested in the behavior of an ant.

But you would be wrong.

The power is wise enough to know that if you did not have to tend to your own body,  
you would not truly be present in this place.

You would be a dream in a body of stone.

So you remain awkwardly, stubbornly, *alive*—  
flesh in a world of stone and worms and subtle water—  
and you feel the contrast of yourself with your world more clearly than you did before, because the presence is now quietly listening to you.

---

### 7. The First Change of the Light

You are not sure when you began to speak.

You, who have been so careful of language in this place where nothing else seems to care for it,  
find yourself at the center of a terrace where you have not seen another changed face yet.

You are speaking aloud to the stone.

“You are listening,” you say.

“I think you are listening.”

“You showed the water to loop around the eye.”

“You used the tone of the world to change the worm.”

“You are marking the stone with your eyes.”

“But you are not changing me yet.”

The sky does not change.

The light does not dim.

The stone does not shudder.

You are not sure what you expected from that kind of speech—perhaps a thunderclap of response, perhaps a great voice, perhaps a vision of something sublime and infinite and beyond you.

Instead, what happens is small.

It is very small.

You are looking at the wall beside you—  
a wall crowded with many of the usual, blank, unchanging stone faces.

In the gap of time between two heartbeats,  
the light touching the stone of their faces lifts slightly in color.

Not from “white” to “yellow,” or from “gray” to “blue.”

It is something like a change of temperature, but in the language of light rather than heat:

For the space of less than a second,  
the color of the world on that wall becomes warmer to you—  
warmer in a way you cannot measure with any instrument except your own experience.

It is the color of light that you associate with early morning on your own world.

Somewhere behind you a stone clinks against stone as it settles in a slightly different position.

You get the distinct sense that the entire world has been a closed eye until this moment,  
and that a closed eye has a color of its own,

and now, for a fraction of a breath,  
you have been allowed to see the color of its eye opening.

---

### 8. A Question Is Asked, Without Words

You understand the next question without the power needing to say anything.

You simply become aware of it being asked of you,  
from somewhere far and close and deep and high and stone and air and tone and light and worm and water and face and wall and world and you.

The question is not:

“Are you afraid?”

“Do you want to escape?”

“What do you want from this?”

The question is:

“What, of all of this, do you love?”

“What, of this stone world that is not yours and does not love you back and perhaps never will,

of what here,

do you love?”

You are not sure how to answer that.

---

### 9. The Answer, in Three Steps

You do not want to lie to the stone.

You sense that the power is not interested in what you think you *should* say,  
but only in what you actually feel.

You are quiet for a long time.

Finally, you step to the edge of the terrace and look down at the layer upon layer of roofs and streets and arches and basins and terraces stretching below and above and beside and before and behind and beyond and far and near and endless and beginning and ending and continuing and stopping and starting and never and always and all of that.

Then you point to a worm.

You point to the worm whose journey you have been watching for longer than any other.

You are not sure why.  
You do not think of yourself as a particularly sentimental person.  
You are not sure you love worms at all.

But you know that, of all the things in this world,  
you are most attached to the slow, deliberate, persistent *making* of this creature.

You know that you have been watching its long track as it heads from one basin to another,  
from the forehead of one face to the cheek of another,

from water to water,  
from stone to stone,  
from itself to itself.

You have never been able to look at it and know “soon it will be done”  
because “done” is not a concept it seems to recognize.

You are attached to the incompleteness of it.

You are attached to its persistence.

You are attached to the fact that it does not notice you watching it.

It is nothing to you.

You are nothing to it.

And yet you have been watching its long, slow, stone-wrought life.

You love that.

“I love that worm,” you say.

“I don’t know why. But I love its patience.”

The world does not answer.

But you feel, under your feet,  
the very faintest ripple of the stone tone slide  
exactly half a step upwards in pitch,

as if something in the world has been mildly pleased.

---

### 10. The Second and Third Answer

You continue to think.

You are not sure you are done with answering the question.

“What else?” you ask yourself, “What else do you love?”

You are surprised by your own answer.

“I love the spots of light that gather on the water when the droplets hang on the edges of the basins,”

you say.

“I love the way that, even in this place without warmth or sun or softness,

those tiny surfaces of water become bright for a moment.

I like that they are not permanent.

They vanish and return and vanish and return.

I like that they still exist in this world of endless stone.”

You are embarrassed by the sentimentality of that answer.

But the stone does not laugh at you.

The tone of the world simply continues,

unchanged,

as if having already acknowledged your first answer,

and now being quietly content.

---

You are going to stop at two answers, but something else comes to you before you can stop.

“I also love the way that some of the faces are carved over older ones,” you say,

“the way that you can see where the old chin has been broken and the new one has been set over it,

the way that the line of an older eye socket makes a strange curve under the new cheekbone.

I like that they have not hidden the old work.

I like that the world remembers being changed more than once.”

You are surprised by that last answer most of all.

---

### 11. What The Benevolent Power Gives You

You are not sure what it is you want.  
You are not sure what it is appropriate to ask from a stone world.

But you speak, finally, into the continuing stone-drum tone of the world and you say:

“I am not from this place.”

“You are not from me.”

“I have answered you with what I love.”

“If you are willing, give me something to give back.”

The world does not answer with a change in temperature,

or with a change in pressure,  

or with a change in color.

It answers with a change in time.

---

### 12. The Time–Capture

You are standing on a terrace that you have been standing on for longer than you know.

You are looking at a wall that you have been looking at without intention to remember.

You are thinking of how tired you are of trying not to forget what you see.

And then the world gives you a small gift.

You are looking at the wall, and, without explanation,  
the *entirety* of its surface from edge to edge of your vision is suddenly—  

not exactly “remembered,”

but “stored with you.”

You are aware of where every crack is,

where each basin sits,

where each worm track curves,

where each face stares,

where each block of stone ends and the next begins,

where each patch of damp stone has its edge,

and where each of these things has changed since the last time you allowed yourself to look.

You are not sure how you know this.

You have not been counting.

You have not been recording.

You have not been mapping.

But now you are aware of the map of the wall inside yourself,

as if you had always been drawing it without knowing,

and now someone has turned the page to show you what you have already drawn.

You are frightened by the completeness of it.

You are not used to your mind being so obedient to the world’s memory.

You are not sure what you think of being made into the library of this stone wall.

---

Then you realize the next thing.

The “gift” is not about the wall at all.

The “gift” is about the *capacity*.

You feel the benevolent presence gently asking,

“If you are able to remember this wall in this way,

what else in you might be able to remember,

if you were willing to be quiet for long enough?”

“If you are able to notice your change without being changed by it,

what else are you ignoring?”

“If you are able to love a worm, a droplet of water, a broken cheekbone,

what else are you refusing to love?”

“If you are able to be here in this stone world without being broken by it,

what else are you refusing to be a part of?”

You are not sure how to answer that.

---

### 13. The End of the First Change

The benevolent presence does not ask you to answer aloud.

The benevolent presence does not demand you to change.

It does not change you.

It does not change the world in any way that is visible and obvious and loud.

It does not open the clouds to reveal a second sun.

It does not cause earthquakes to rearrange the city.

It does not give you powers of flight or strength or sight.

It does not give you anything except:

- one stone wall that you are now aware of in your memory with complete,  
  uncomfortable clarity  
- one tone of the world, that you now hear as a background,
