The benevolent power that notices this chamber does not arrive with thunder or trumpets.

It arrives as a subtle change in the pressure of attention.

One day, without any visible cause, the sound of the city outside the grate dims by a fraction of a decibel. The drone of traffic is still there, the same low frequency, but it feels slightly less urgent than before. It is as if a layer of anxiety has been scraped away from it, leaving the same volume but a softer edge.

For the first time since the chamber was formed, Margin can sense the difference between “outside noise” and “silence.”

They are aware of this new quietness in the way one might become aware of a lamp turned off in a distant room of a house: not by seeing light, but by noticing that a particular kind of absence has now taken shape. They do not know what to do with this information. They simply notice that the world has changed its texture a little.

### Arrival without entrance

Nothing enters through the grate. No hand comes through the mesh to probe the air inside the chamber. No glowing eyes appear in the darkness behind the streetlight. No voice speaks out loud to them.

Instead, the benevolent power makes itself known as a pattern of *permission* and *precision*.

The sound of pedestrians outside, which has always been a blur of motion and urgency, now occasionally contains a clear, distinct pattern that Margin can recognize:

Someone is walking more slowly.

That person’s footstep approaches the grate, not as the others do—hurrying, distracted—but as if they are aware of the place below. The steps are not heavy. They pause exactly above the center of the grate, and for the space of perhaps six heartbeats (if they had heartbeats) Margin feels the world of the city paused too.
