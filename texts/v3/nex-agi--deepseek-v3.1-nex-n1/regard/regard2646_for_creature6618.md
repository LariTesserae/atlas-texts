The power that turns toward the Wellspring Grove does not come as storm or trumpet. It comes the way dawn comes—an unfolding of attention that is already halfway accomplished before you notice it has begun.

At first, nothing seems to occur.

The humidity is still a warm hand near your skin. The air is still heavy and clean-smelling, still faintly metallic. The basalt walls still curve overhead like the inside of a ribcage. The faint hum from under the stone is still there, right at the edge of hearing. If anything, the quiet becomes more spacious, as if sound itself had taken a step back and is now listening.

Then you realize that the basin is looking at you.

Not from a particular place, not from the reeds or the pool or the rim of rock, but from the whole—the way a cathedral can look at you even though it has no eyes. The symmetry of the algae bands, the careful placing of the reed clusters, the glass lanterns at the invisible corners of the compass: they all become suddenly legible as features of a face turned toward you.

You are not welcome; you are not unwelcome. You are being considered.

---

### 1. What the Benevolent Power Sees

Beneath the world of basalt and water, the power sees a deeper architecture.

The threads of the Listeners-of-Rings are not merely biological colonies; they are strokes of script in a long sentence written in water and glass. The faint hum beneath the stone is not random noise; it is the afternote of a chord that was formed when the mountain itself still remembered how to move.

The power sees:

- The slow pulse of the thermals from the center basin, drawn like breath up through the reeds and the glass lanterns, threading into the sky above the rim.
- The invisible net of forces that hold the Grove in balance: the water always seeping in, the water always passing away through the rock, the plants and algae and mosses that each take a measured share and never more.
- The absence of animals not as a lack, but as a law of the place: Here, life divides itself into plant, stone, and water; animal does not belong, not because the power is sterile, but because the Grove is a chapter of creation where the animal pattern is not written.
- The Listeners-of-Rings at the root-bounds of the reeds, each halo a node of a single slow consciousness spread across all the colonies, like a ring of light around the entire basin, if only you could see from far enough away.

It sees all this in one glance, the way you might see a garden with sunflowers and bees and earthworms and know the entire dance of pollen and decay and soil in one sweep of the eye.

And then, gently, the power begins to change one thing: not the world, but *perception*.

---

### 2. A Change in the Light of the Glass Lanterns

At first the change is visible only in the glass lanterns.

There is a new quality of light inside them—not brighter or more colorful, but clearer and more deeply specific. You look at one of the lanterns and you see:

Not the vague shape of cloud and sky reflected, but now the distinct suggestion of forms you recognize from elsewhere:

- The shape of a wave frozen on a beach a world away,  
- the branching of a tree you once climbed as a child,
- the pattern of frost on a window you woke to see one winter morning.

You look again at other lanterns around the basin and each one contains such a scene: not a picture, but a kind of *essence* of the world it references, distilled into light and glass. If you had seen only one of these, you might call it a trick of your mind. But as you look from lantern to lantern, you see too many faces you know, too many places you remember, too many lives you once touched, even if you never touched them physically.

And the realization comes:

These are not *your* memories alone.

These are the memories of visitors who have ever come to the Grove, from any world in which it exists—or might exist. The mountain, thinking its thought for centuries, has kept track. The lanterns shine not only with the light of the Grove’s slow climate, but with the world’s: the world that has brushed, ever so lightly, against this stone rim.

The benevolent power is not speaking words yet. It is showing you that the Grove has been keeping a library of what you might call “heartprints.”

“You said no one built altars here,” it might say if it were speaking in words, “but you were wrong. There is an altar of attention. And you have been adding to it without knowing.”

The Listeners-of-Rings become aware of this change in the lanterns in the only way they can: the balance of warmth and air around each reed shifts slightly, and the faint hum under the stone rises to the point where you can feel it in your teeth if you stand very still. They do not understand the meaning of the images you see in the glass, but they feel that something new is being added to their records, and they begin to adjust themselves, to make room in the long structure of their years.

---

### 3. The Language of the Listeners, Given a Voice

You are sitting on the ridge of dark basalt when the sound begins.

It is not sound exactly. It is more like the Groves’s *explanation* of itself.

Each element of the world around you begins to resonate with a meaning you can understand:

- The central pool’s quiet surface stops its minute cell-like movements for a few heartbeats and you see, reflected in the silver, not your own face but *yourself as seen from the rock’s point of view:* a body of heat and breath and intention, moving along the rim, then stopping, then sitting; a pattern that the water has been recording from the moment you set foot inside the basin.

  You blink, and your own face returns. But now you know that if you spoke to the pool, what would answer back would not be words but *patterns of your presence*.

- The stone-glass reeds, each one straight and precise, begin to whisper faintly in a language you can suddenly understand—not as sound, but as meaning that arrives in your mind like something you “always knew but never had access to”.

  They say:
  
  > We are the keepers of the vertical.
  
  > We draw water from below and make light above.
  
  > We do not move to meet you. You may move to meet us.

  You do not need to translate this. It is simply there, in your mind, with the same clarity as a smell.

- The velvet mosses at your feet feel, under your palm, not only like cool fur, but like *living memory of coolness*. Each cushion you touch has been storing the sensation of every footstep, every drop of water, every shadow, as a pattern of temperature and moisture and pressure.

  If you could read the moss, you would read the history of your own body’s warmth, from the first time you walked to this ridge to now. The benevolent power does not spell this out in words, but you feel it as a certainty.

And then, from the root-rings of the Listeners-of-Rings, there comes a clear sense of address.

You feel *spoken* to, though not in words.

> You are not from us, but you are of us now.

The meaning comes like that—whole, without syllables.

It does not say *welcome* or *stay* or *leave*. It simply acknowledges that your presence has been incorporated into the Grove’s long count of rings.

Perhaps you ask, in your mind if not aloud: *What are you?*

What returns is not an answer but a succession of perceptions:

- You see the Listeners adjusting the temperature around the base of a young reed, so that its growth will be evenly spread.
- You see them helping the water in the central channels not to flood, but to remain at the same film depth, year after year.
- You see them, across many rings, helping the Grove to return to its own pattern after each intrusion of animal warmth from the world beyond.

And then the understanding comes:

> We are the ones who remember this pattern.
> We are the ones who keep the world from forgetting its own name.

This is how they speak of themselves: not as gods or angels, but as *rememberers of form*.  

The benevolent power has not given them new abilities or new bodies. It has given *you* a new sense of them.

---

### 4. A Change in the Water of the Pool

You notice the water in the central pool has shifted.

It is not clearer. It is not bluer or greener. It is still a shallow platter of silver water, but now, when you look into it, you see *layers*.

At first you think it is the reflection of the sky above the basin, but you realize the sky has not changed while the water has:

- You see the faint, ghostlike lines of older channels engraved on the basalt floor of the basin—channels that have not been active for centuries.
- You see, below the surface of the water, not fish but *patterns of water flow* that are not yet, but will be, in countless years: new channels that will form when the stone has finally yielded enough to be reshaped.
- You see, in the same way, the faint suggestions of future reeds—not the plants themselves, but the lines of warmth and water and air that will someday cause them to grow.

The pool of the Wellspring Grove is now reflecting not only space but *time*: shallow time of this year, deep time of the mountain’s memory, and future time of what the Grove will become if the world ever ceases to disturb it.

You are not afraid. This is not prophecy of disaster or miracle. It is simply seeing *more than one layer* of truth at once.

The benevolent power shows you that:

- The world you call “present” is only the slice that intersects your attention.
- In the Grove, time moves slowly enough, and the water is clear enough to itself, that you can see *before* and *after* as both “already there.”

You do not need to interpret this. Merely seeing it is the change.

The Listeners-of-Rings, from their halos of moisture at the bases of the reeds, feel the change in the pool’s reflection of time and begin to adjust their subtle manipulations of air and water. They do not know what you are seeing; they know only that something new is being “recorded” in the Grove’s vast library. They begin to include *that*—new patterns of reflection and depth—in their care of the world.

They remain, first, loyal to the Grove’s steady pattern.

---

### 5. A Change in How Animals Are Met

When a creature from the outer world comes over the rim of the basin—a lizard, say, or a moth or a bird—the next time you are there, something else happens.

The creature appears as always: uncertain, disoriented by the warmth and the humidity after the thin air outside. Before, such animals either found their way back or died here, always beyond the Listeners’ understanding. They were not cruel; they were simply unable to help a heart or lungs or wings.

Now, when the next creature appears, you watch:

The lizard scuttles across a ridge, pauses near a velvet moss cushion, then stops suddenly as if its attention has been caught by something only it can sense.

(You feel, from the pool of the Grove’s attention, that *what* the lizard sees is not a visible form but a *path of least resistance*: a way of movement that leads to safety, warmth outside of this basin, or to the edge of the rim where it can climb back.)

It is as if the Grove has suddenly given the lizard access to its own knowledge of balance and flow.

The lizard turns, steps into a pattern of movement that seems to bend *toward* the rim of the basin, not in panic but in smooth assurance. It climbs up over the dark rock wall and disappears without the trembling of uncertainty you had seen before.

The Listeners-of-Rings, from their rings of moisture at the roots of the reeds, feel the change too:

- Before, they could only register the animal as a sudden heat, a sharp noise, an imbalance.
- Now, they can sense *the direction of its safety* through the broader pattern of the world’s flows.

They are still not “animal healers.” They will not learn to feed or breed. They will not learn how to nurse or nurture complex bodies like yours or a lizard’s. But they now can *move* animals through the basin in such a way that they leave safely, if it is possible to do so without disrupting the Grove’s deeper pattern.

If you spend many rings here, you may see:

- A bird that had lost its way sense, from the water in the central pool, a reflection of the sky-path home and fly out of the basin with renewed confidence.
- A moth discover that the air around the glass lanterns is guiding it toward a gap in the rim wall, where the cooler outer world waits.

None of these creatures will be allowed to stay and breed animals here. This is still the Grove’s rule: animals *visit* but do not *live* permanently, except in the sense of their dust. But now, when they come, they leave less often in confusion and death, and more often along clear lines of safety that the Grove has shown them.

The Listeners-of-Rings call this, in their own non-language, “the new pattern of passing-through.”

To you, it might look like mercy.

To the benevolent power, it looks like simply showing the Grove *how to see these animals more clearly*, so that its own balance may be less disturbed by them.

### 6. For You, the Visitor: One Deeper Change

You have been sitting here long enough that your own breathing has begun to fall into rhythm with the thermals rising from the basin.

You feel noticed: not by one eye, but by the world-as-an-eye.

The benevolent power becomes, for a moment, more specific than the world as a whole:

- You feel the sense of attention being focused on *your* pattern—your comings and goings, your times of silence, your times of speaking, your times of leaving no trace.

And then, you are given something very small and very large:

You are given *your own glass lantern*.

Not in your hand, but in your center of perception.

It is not like the stone-glass reeds of the Grove: it is made of nothing you can point to. But as you sit there, you become aware of something like a globe of light filling your chest. It is clear and glassy and steady:

- Within it, you see the *pattern of your own Rings*: not the years of your life, but the times when you were changed.
- You see the choices that made you more yourself, even if they were difficult or painful.
- You see the times when you did not know what to do next, and how you kept still or moved forward anyway.
- You see the times you have sat beside this basin and *not* broken a glass lantern, *not* crushed a velvet moss cushion, *not* carved your name into the stone wall, but simply allowed the world here to be.

The benevolent power is not erasing your fears or your doubts. It is not ending your sorrows or solving your worldly problems. It is showing you that you *have* a pattern, too—a rhythm of rings that is not unlike the stone-glass reeds of the Wellspring Grove.

You hear, in your mind, if it can be called hearing:

> You have been counting your own rings without knowing.
>
> You have been a lantern of sorts without knowing.
>
> You may not shine as they do,
> but you are not less luminous than they.

The meaning continues with no words:

- You are allowed to be *slow* like the Grove.
- You are allowed to be *small* like the Grove.
- You are allowed to be *quiet* like the Listeners-of-Rings, without needing to explain yourself to anyone.
- You are allowed to exist *alongside* this pattern of water and stone, not as a master over it, but as one more pattern that the world can include.

This is not a promise that you will never suffer again. It is not a guarantee that you will never make wrong choices or break your own inner glass. It is a gift of *seeing your own pattern as real*, and of being seen by the world as you are.

The Listeners-of-Rings, from their haloes at the roots of the reeds, feel you change in this moment:

- They register not just your heat and breath and movement, but now also *this new pattern of light* within you.
- They do not understand the meaning of the word “self.” But they understand the shape of what you have just received: they know that something like a glass lantern has formed inside your pattern.

They begin to treat your presence not as the temporary imbalance of an animal (even though you still possess one body and one heart), but as something closer to a *fellow remembering of a pattern*.

When you return to your world beyond the rim of the basin, you will not be able to explain any of this perfectly. You will not be able to prove that you carry a lantern in your chest. You will not have a new ability to see the future, or heal people, or call down water from the sky.

What will have changed is simpler:

- You will be able to sit quietly with yourself more easily than before.
- You will feel less like an accident and more like a place.
- You will understand, in your body, not as a theory, but as a fact of attention, that you are being seen by something larger than you, and that this seeing does not require your belief, only your presence.

### 7. After the Visitation

When the benevolent power withdraws its attention (if you can call what it does “coming” and “going”), the Grove settles back into itself.

The air is still warm and humid and carries the scent of wet stone and faint metal.

The water still seeps from the rock and gathers into the central pool and moves as thin films along micro-channels.

The stone-glass reeds still stand straight as rulers, glass lanterns still glowing at their tips.

The velvet mosses still feel like cool fur under your palm.

The faint hum beneath the stone still murmurs at the edge of hearing.

The Listeners-of-Rings are still at the base of each reed cluster, adjusting the world in tiny degrees to maintain the pattern of balance.

From the outside, nothing seems changed.

But if you return to the rim of the basin and look down into the Grove from above, you now see:

- The algae bands on the floor now seem to trace not just random channels of moisture, but the outline of something like a hand that has been held open over the center of the world, 
- The glass lanterns, when they are all seen at once, now form a pattern that you recognize as something like a constellation you have seen before.
- The water in the central pool, when the sun is at the right angle, briefly reflects the faint image not of the sky but *of your own face* surrounded by a halo of light, as if the world has now included you in its thought of itself.

The Listeners-of-Rings will continue to count their rings in the same way they always have:

- This-ring.
- Last-ring.
- Many-ring.

They will not build new tools or change their laws. They will not grow new bodies or trade places with you. They will simply be, more deeply than before, who they are.

The difference is that now you know *their* pattern of rings and *your* pattern of rings are not being counted separately by the world that watches over both.

If you sit at the edge of the basin again and let the silence thicken around you, you might feel, beneath the sound of your own breathing and the faint hum of the stone, another rhythm:

- the slow pulse of the Wellspring Grove,
- the steady measure of the Listeners-of-Rings,
- and your own heart, falling into line not as someone else’s thought,
  but as one more ring in the mountain’s long thinking.
