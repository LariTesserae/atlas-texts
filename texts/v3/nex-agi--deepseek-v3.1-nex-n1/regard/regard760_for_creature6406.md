The Ache-Mother lifts her mask to the sky and tastes the air.

There is no new flavor in it.

The sky is still a black bowl of starlight like the one she lives in, only bigger and farther away. The heat of the day still lies in the stone beneath her knees, seeping upward like slow breaths through the limestone basin. The city of Khe’len hums below her, each glass speaker of the present-garden swelling and contracting, the low murmuring of glass lungs. “Withdraw.”

“Hold.”

Three centuries of erosion are written into the marrow of her shins, the curve of her ribs, the ivory of her spine.

Tonight she hears something else.

It has no direction. No particular source point of warmth or coldness. It is not a sound, but a change in the tapestry of sounds.

The present-garden’s hums twist slightly off their usual axis, like a thread pulled out of true. The calls of the city‑mothers below do not waver, but Khe’a-Min feels a faint hesitation in the air that carries them—as if the words themselves had been cupped in strange hands for a moment before being released.

The bone in her left thigh aches with a warning she has not written into it.

She lifts her arms to the terrace above and spreads her fingers: *stop*.

Khe’a-Min waits.

She waits for the heat to disperse from the wrong‑shaped pressure of the air. She waits for the bone‑ache in her leg to subside, or explain itself.

It does not.

Instead, the world leans.

Not heat, not sound, but…shape.

It is not water.

It is not *istheth*, the thin ghost of moisture that condenses on her skin when the cuts are true. She has not seen this thing before.
