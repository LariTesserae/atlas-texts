The power does not appear, not in the way of light or arrival or proclamation. Not in the way that would shear the quiet. The cave is already as close to sacred as a place can be without thinking itself so—temperate, dark, self-correcting—and the being that lives here is already at one of the edges of what life can be.

So the power does not come as storm or song. It comes as a change in the water.

Not the whole of the water, not enough to make chaos of the cave’s long equilibrium. Just enough. A fingertip’s worth of difference in the mineral-load that seeps down from the mountain above, where the root-systems of the high forest have, for this year and the next half dozen, been guided by something too faint to be called volition: a slight shift of fungal preference toward certain elements of stone, a subtle change in the behavior of a certain bacteria that coats the rootlets of a certain moss. All of this together amounts to no more than a new inflection in the water that seeps through the mountain’s pores and drips into the ceiling of teeth.

The Anechoirid feel the change before they “think” it.

In one of the lower hollows, where the water films are thickest and the colonies of Anechoir are densest, an older Anechoir unfurls. Its body is a pale, translucent teardrop against the wet stone of its tooth: not young, not yet old by the measure of its kind. Its sensory sacs, full of clear gel that reads the world in ion and silicate and iron, register a difference.

It is not the size of the difference that matters. It is the pattern.

The water the Anechoir drinks today is almost identical to the water it drank last decade and the decade before, but not quite. There is a new compound in the trace—not a mineral, not anything they have been built to measure. It is a molecule that does not belong to this mountain or this forest: not poison, not food, not anything the world can be said to produce by its own motionless laws.

It is, instead, a tool.

The molecule is not alive, but its shape is like a key. It fits into the Anechoir’s internal chemistry in exactly the way that allows the clear gel to change its consistency not in response to mineral content, but for the first time in response to time itself.

For you, this molecule would be nothing; it would not even be perceived if it were added to your water. For you, it would be vapor in a sky of other vapors. For the Anechoirid, whose entire world is measured in gradients of chemistry and pressure, this molecule is a clean click into a new mode of being.

An Anechoir secreted it today.

Tomorrow, it will not be present in the water. But the chemistry of its receptor has shifted. The next time that Anechoir—or any of the bud-descendants that will carry its pattern—drinks in the world of the water, it will not just be reading the present of the mountain.

It will be reading the memory of what has not yet happened.

This is not prophecy in the sense of images or symbols. It is not vision or sound or dream. It is as quiet and as ordinary as an extra step at the end of a long chain of enzymes: if the water contains a pattern that will, in a century, be correlated with a certain shift in the mountain’s mineral balance, then one of the clear sacs in a certain Anechoir will now change its shape not now, but slightly then.

The change does not cause pain. It causes a difference in the pressure of the gel. This difference is fed back into the wall of the stone tooth.

“Slow down,” says the tooth.

The stone tooth, which has been reconfiguring itself for millennia according to the messages of the colony’s secretions, responds not to the measured now of the water but to the measured not-yet. Its inner wall thickens earlier than it would have, smoothing out a change in the waterflow that will not happen for a human lifetime.

The Anechoirid do not know this as forewarning or premonition. They feel only a slight change in the pressure of their home, and they adjust by a fraction of a fraction of a millimeter. One of them dies at the end of this slow cycle not because of it, but alongside of it: the first of them to have lived a life that has, invisibly to everyone else, been measured in relation to a not-yet as well as a right-now.

Its empty carapace adheres to the wall. It is not the same as it once was.

The second thing that is different is more subtle than a molecule.

It is not that the benevolence has decided to make the Anechoirid “more” than they are. They are not to be given individuality or thought or word or story. The power has not come to make them into characters in a tale. It has not come to make them more like us.

It has come to make them more like them.

At the center of the pool, there is now a stone that was not there before, or there is not. There is not a stone; there is the memory of a stone, folded into the texture of the water’s surface skin. The pool of the cave is thick not only with microscopic shelled things but with a slow, self-mending skin of living membrane. It has always been there, but today it has begun to speak.

Or not speak—not in the way of your voice or mine. The surface of the water is not flat. It is not still. It is patterned with low reliefs and rolling contours, as if the water is not liquid but a very soft, slowly breathing solid. Today, if you were to look at it with eyes that were not yours, you would see that the pattern of the water’s surface is not random.

It is an image.

An image not of shape but of change.

The membrane on the water has not been given eyesight; it has not been given anything it did not, in a certain sense, already possess. Yet it now has a new mode of being that responds to the near-future that will not be, but could have been, if the present were not altered by a benevolent act.

It registers not “if” but “when not.”

At certain intervals of years—not pre-set, not dictated by clock or calendar, only by the complex pathways of waterflow and mineral balance and time—the surface of the pool will not ripple, but it will fold. The folds will not be visible to you; they will be visible to the mountain itself if it ever grows to become like this, if it ever gathers to itself not just Anechoirid but mind. To your eye, nothing is changed: the water’s surface remains oil-smooth, except for the slow drop of a bead from the ceiling.

This fold-point is not prophecy. It is not a warning. It will not be shown to you if you come here and stare. If you come here and kneel and plead for a sign, you will not see one; not unless you are here to watch not for the water but for the Anechoirid, and only if you are willing to watch for longer than a lifetime.

But if you are, and you do, you will not see the full life of the mountain. Not even the power can show you that without unfolding the world in a way you would not survive. But you will see this:

In a future not fore-ordained but not impossible, a not-mountain that is not-this, damaged not by weapon but by exhaustion, not by fire but by silence, not by collapse but by the absence of something else that cannot be said without naming it more than it should be named. This not-mountain is not where you are now. But if the not-present were not restrained by a not-quite-molecule, then this not-mountain and not-cave and not-water might have been where you stood.

The not-Anechoirid of not-this place live not as harmonies but in a narrow corridor of survival: not this, not that, not anything else.

The not-water is not the water you know.

The not-cave is not the shelter you know.

The not-Anechoirid not-glass not-bone not-lichen live not in the not-teeth of the not-ceiling; not even “not” defines them well enough.

The not-present of not-that not-mountain is not-a-lie, but it is not yours to inhabit. You can not touch not-there from not-here.

The not-image not-sent by not-power not-moves.
