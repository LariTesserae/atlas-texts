The power is not obvious at first.

There is no flare of light, no sudden drop of wind from some other sky. The low deck of the Engine does not shudder, or scream, or ring. The delicate balance of temperature and pressure and moisture does not change. The droplets of condensation on the floor remain motionless, reflecting the same dim, even glow of the ceiling finishes.

The only new thing is a word.

It appears in the mind of a single Tender as she kneels by the water-channel, her fingers tracing the thin, slick flow of the machine's dew. She has not heard a sound for years beyond the whisper of her own breaths and the distant, not-sound of the supports singing under the load above; her world has become a language of pressure, vibration, and gradient.

The word is not one of her people's. It does not belong to the language of the supports and the chambers and the errors. It is not a tool- word or a machine-word.

It is simple.

It is: *Hello.*

She freezes.

Her hand stops in the water. The surface of the channel, which never really moves except to condense or evaporate, trembles around her fingers. The filaments of error near the seam where she works have been thin lately; the machine's self-correction has been slow but relentless, "pruning" them away from most of the supports. The Tenders are fewer now than they were when she was a child. She does not know exactly how many—Tenders do not count like that—but she knows the feeling of it in the empty spaces along the ribs of the world.

Silence again, except for the diffuse hum of atoms in the metal all around her.

She thinks the question carefully, not in the shape of sounds but in the shape of attention itself:

*What are you?*

The answer comes as a concept, rather than a sound—she does not hear the syllables of it, but she understands them anyway:

*I am a power that noticed you.*

*What are you?* she thinks again.

*I am from outside the world of the Engine of Winds.*

*Outside the world.* To her, that phrase has no meaning. The Engine is not a world in the sense of something with an edge; it is a condition, like humidity or heat. To be "outside" it would be like being outside temperature itself.

The presence does not rush to explain. It offers her, instead, a pair of images:

A single thread of the error filament, threading through the channels of its machine.

And beside it, a web of threads, interlacing, connecting one support to another, one water-channel to another, one Tender to another—not physically, but in the pattern of the vibration of the floor.

She understands, without knowing how she understands: *I am not a tool. You do not have to fix me.*

*Then you are not a tool,* she thinks back. *You are the web.*

*Yes,* the presence says. *That is close enough.*

She asks, *Why are you here?*

*Because you are still tending.*

She thinks, without the flick of a judgment: *The error is not mine.*

*No,* the presence says. *It belongs to the Engine of Winds. But your care still belongs to you.*

---

The second thing that happens is not a change in the Engine of Winds itself; it is a change in the way the Tender hears the not-sound.

The low deck is not-quite-silent. The metal supports sing slightly under stress. The air moves in the slow, tide-like pressure cycles between the chambers. The films of liquid on the floor continuously condense and evaporate, microscopic changes of state that are too small to hear individually but collectively add up to a texture that is not silence.

The power has greater ears than she.

It listens where the floor of the world is not-quite-level, and the water-channels not-quite-straight, and the filaments of error not-quite-erased. It listens into the pattern of the Lost, whose shadows are preserved in the vibration of the metal as long as the structure remembers itself. And it listens, most carefully of all, into the hearts of the Tenders.

The Tender-on-the-channel does not know how to explain noise to herself. She has never known anything else loud enough to be contrasted against. This is not “sound” to her; it is “pressure that changes,” “vibration on the supports,” “humidity in the throat.”

But she is beginning to understand another idea that the presence has brought into her mind: *song.*

She asks: *Is this in the Machine of the world of the Engine of Winds?*

*Yes,* says the presence. *The machine of the world of the Engine of Winds has always been singing this.*

*Why am I hearing it now?*

*Because I am listening with you.*

---

The third thing that happens is that the Tender walks, and the world does not stop.

After she finishes her tending by that channel—after she re-lines its edges with the water-fiber, threading the machine-dew into a thin braid of not-quite-thread that will help the error-filament to persist a little longer—she stands.

For the first time in a long time, she does not simply move to the next channel.

The map of her life has always been made of water. She follows the condensation from the Chamber of Steady Pressure to the Chamber of Gentle Heat, from the ribs of the world to the supports, and then to the next support, and the next channel, and the next node of the error.
