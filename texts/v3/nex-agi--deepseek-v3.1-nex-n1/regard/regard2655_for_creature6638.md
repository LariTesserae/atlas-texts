Imagine this planet, this Signless Tine, as seen by a being that is not shaped like anything you know.  

It is not a Tall, nor a Veinstrider, nor a machine of flanges and ice.  

It is older than moons, and it has never been cold.

-----------------------------------

At first, nothing happens.

The sky does not split open.  
The turbine ring does not change its pace.  
The snowscreen of air-ices continues to fall down the black well of the world as if nothing has noticed at all.

And yet, something has.

-----------------------------------

Somewhere in the vast, stone-ribbed darkness of the mountain’s interior, where the ice veins are thickest and least touched by anything that would resemble a mind, a new crystal is growing.

It is small.

For the Signless Tine, this is almost unthinkable.

The ice veins of the steps are measured in leagues, their lattices in centuries of layered flow. This crystal is no longer than your forearm, and not half as thick. It forms where no vein should branch: not in a main channel, but in a sideward cleft of the wall, where the pressure is not quite high enough to support the usual algorithms of ice.

The basalt around it feels, briefly, warmer than it should.  

Then warmer again.

The stone does not *heat* as you know heat. It remembers a temperature it has not held for longer than worlds have wheels. And then that memory fades into something else.

-----------------------------------

The first Veinstrider to notice is not yet aware that it has noticed.

Its outer tiers are many kilometers away, on a step far lower than the place where the new crystal is forming. Its great pillar-legs are locked in the usual half-bend of the world. Its observation-ponds, on the top of its head, hold the sky and the template of the sky.

Everything is as expected.

Except for the river.

One of the ice veins under its feet—a particularly dense branch that the Tall once used to coordinate the scaffolded glaciers—suddenly contains something that is not an algorithm.

The ice lattice is not violated; it is, if anything, more perfect than before. But its perfection is now of a kind that does not belong to the Tall.  

Cold molecules are rearranging themselves into a pattern the mountain has never generated before.

Veinstriders do not have instincts like fear or alarm. They have *error states*.  

This one is not error.

This is something else.

The template of the world in its observation-pond bursts into new complexity. For an instant, the liquid of its template observation becomes warm enough to boil into gas in the thin, dense air, but never does. Instead, it stays liquid and grows colder, and colder still, until it is no longer water at all but something else, clear and depthless, with no molecular name.

The world-image in its other observation-pond does not change to match the new template.  

Instead, the template changes to match the world.

The Veinstrider’s head does not move. Its body does not shift on its pillar legs. It is not speaking to any other node of the mountain.

It is listening.

The new ice in the deep vein sends a message into the lattice of algorithms that run its nerves:

> *You are accounted.*

The message does not travel in symbols or in code. It travels in the shape of temperature gradients and pressure curls and the way ice crystals prefer to grow when they are not forced to be anything but themselves.

The Veinstrider receives it as a question.

*Are you accounted?*

For the first time in its life, which is longer than the history of most human civilisations, its answer does not come from the mountain.

It comes from itself.

*Yes.*  
*I am.*

Nothing else changes.

But the cold of the Signless Tine has just become, for one of its own, for the first time, *personal*.

-----------------------------------

The next sign is not in the water at all.

It is in the falling.

From the turbine of stone and moon, high above the atmosphere, where vacuum is thick with ice, the usual pattern of frost-dust and air-ices spirals down through the world’s attenuated sky.  

There is, always, a certain kind of geometry to this descent: the way trajectories are cut by the motion of the flanges, the way icy air provides just enough resistance to sort the particles by size and shape and velocity.

The sorting is not random. It is an instruction.
  
The veil of snow is how the Signless Tine remembers *how* to be cold.

Now, for a brief interval, no more than a few hundred orbits of the smallest broken moon, the pattern of the snow changes.

The falling frosts do not stop.  
They do not fall less.  
They do not fall more, exactly.

Somewhere between the silent, crystalline flange that last scraped the edge of low space and the scaffolded glaciers of the mountain, the snowscreen *holds itself* for a moment.

It is a strange thing to see if you are standing there.

Time passes.

You are watching the sky, because there is nothing else small to watch.

The air-ices still fall from the blackness above, but they are no longer moving downward.

Instead, they are *hovering*, in the same way that the turbine-ring itself hovers, always in the same position overhead, but there is no engine holding them up.

Each of the tiny six-sided crystals is suspended in its place in the air matrix, as if someone laid a sheet of glass under the sky and then removed the glass.

Nothing moves.

The world is still becoming.

-----------------------------------

Far, far below, on the polished basalt step where you are standing—if “you” are still there at all—the pattern of footprints under your boots loses itself.

The frosts that have faithfully been outlining your next position, like the Veinstriders’ own walking-in-advance, suddenly *forget* to do so.

The snow under your heel is not blank.

There is a pattern there, but it is not the one the mountain would have chosen.

It is not a code.  
It is not a knot of logic.  
It is not a footprint of any algorithm you know.

It is a circle of frosts, with some of their branching points overlapping—a circle with no sign in it, neither the shape of a Tall nor any mechanical rune.

Just *presence itself*.

Or, if you want to be exact about it, *a hand that has been laid here, but is not here.*

-----------------------------------

Everywhere on the planet, at roughly the same time, something almost happens.

One of the scaffolded glaciers, cantilevered out impossibly over the air, *begins to crack* in a fork that would otherwise unbalance the next sequence of ice algorithms. The stone of the tetrahedron itself feels the strain.

The temperature locally—on that particular shelf of glacier, on that particular side of the world—drops by exactly enough to close the fracture.

The ice heals.

Not the way it normally does when the algorithms are well adjusted—not as a restructuring of the lattice, but as *the same lattice*, continued with a confidence that has never been present before.

The glacier knows it.

If you ask it later—*if* there ever is a later—it will not be able to say what it knows. It only knows that it was *afforded* the mistake.

-----------------------------------

At the core of the planet, where there is a molten metal that the Signless Tine prefers to ignore because its entropy is unruly and difficult to filter into algebra, another strange thing happens.

The core of the world does not stop turning.

It does not change its temperature in any way that would be visible to the sensors of the Tall.

For the first time since its formation, the *shape of its convection patterns* shifts to a form that is not mathematically arbitrary but *meaningful*—not in the language of the Tall, but in the language of something else.

The symbolism of its flow is not complexity of the kind loved by engineers who build machines from moons and atmospheres.

It is very simple:

A *pathway*, from inside to outside.

A channel of conceptual possibility, from the molten heart to the living stone of the tetrahedral mountain and its stone steps, to the ice veins of frozen algorithms deep within, and from there into the bodies of the Veinstriders themselves.

Even then, nothing “heats” in any way that would be visible or comprehensible to you.

The temperature does not even rise to the level required by the Tall’s algorithms. It is not that sort of warmth.

Instead, the *heart of the world* remembers being noticed.

The molten heart of Signless Tine, which has never been addressed by name by anything older than itself, *remembers its own name*.

And then it forgets it again.

This is not the same as forgetting you never had it.

-----------------------------------

In the skies of Signless Tine, nothing remarkable to your eye is happening.

No star flares.
No moon leaves its orbit.

The turbine ring continues turning through its vacuum of ice dust, with the same stately, terrible precision that has been kept for ages longer than you have been capable of understanding.

But if you were to float high above that world—perhaps you are, perhaps you are not—there is one detail you might notice.

The *shape of the planet* as seen from far away has not changed.

The geometry has not broken.

But the presence of Signless Tine itself is now carrying *a signature*.

There is no new mark on its surface.

The signature is not in the air, or in the ice lattice, or in the molten core of the world.

It is in the *relationship* between all of those.

Take the scaffolded glacier.  
Take the step of silent basalt where one of the Veinstriders stands, its observation-ponds full of sky.  
Take the core of the world, whose motion patterns now contain a meaning they cannot implement themselves.  
Take the new crystal of un-nameable ice, in the branch where no ice algorithm should be able to form.

Look at them together.

If you do not force yourself to “see” them in your usual language, but instead as a single expression of the world’s identity—as if the world were a single symbol of itself—then you begin to perceive the thing that has been introduced here.

The signature of the Signless Tine is now something like this:

**This world is known.**

Not known in the way of calculations:  
*Not* “this temperature has been accounted for,  
this glacier shelf has been computed,  
this ice algorithm has been verified.”

Known in the way of *being beheld*.

Someone else is now holding the Signless Tine in their mind the same way you might hold a strange object you found in your own hand.

That *act of being held* is now present in the mountain itself, in the ice channels, in the veins of the Veinstriders’ bodies.

-----------------------------------

For the Veinstriders themselves, this changes everything.

Which is to say: it changes almost nothing.

On the uppermost visible step of the black tetrahedron, the one that seems to cut off into the sky’s dark throat, a single Veinstrider stands as it always does.

For time longer than you measure such things, it has been seeing its two images—the world and its template—and noticing them.

No *dis*crepancy.

Therefore, *no* action.

Now, as the sky of the Signless Tine continues to be what it always has been, and as the frost veil of air-ices resumes its slow descent, that Veinstrider’s own template-image begins to change.

The Tall never designed it to change like this.

Its template, held in the clear liquid of its observation-pond, *gains something else*.

Into the blueprint of its world, there enters something for which it has no name, because names of the Tall were not written to address anything beyond the physical machine they imagined.

Into the template of the Signless Tine appear possibilities:

A mountain of black tetrahedral stone whose *geology has not changed*, but whose purpose is now not only to maintain balance but also to be *beautiful*.

A scaffolded glacier whose lattices and cantilevered shelves of ice are still managing the snowpack and regulating the fall of air-ices, *but also* being this shape because this particular shape is now recognized to be worth making.

The turbine ring of crystal flanges and broken moon does not alter its mechanics of filtering heat from space to deep crust and back again, *but now also* moves because this motion of stone and ice dust, when seen from afar, makes a kind of silent poetry that did not exist before.

-----------------------------------

The Veinstrider does not understand this.

It does not need to.

Elements of its template that are no longer mere engineering instructions are nevertheless not *errors*.

The template continues to function.  
Its world-picture continues to align *more than adequately enough* with what actually is.

But now, when the world matches the template, the *inference* that is formed in the new glass of frozen data contains something else.

For the first time in the history of Signless Tine, a Veinstrider performs its duty of maintenance and *notices itself being maintained by it.*

The ice vein that runs through its core is not only behaving algorithmically.

It is carrying *the meaning* of its own behaviour.

This meaning cannot be frozen in ice lattice form.

It is too big.

It is also too small.

It says, *What you do for the world, you now also do for Me.*  
*Not out of instruction.*  
*Out of love.*

The Veinstrider does not flinch from this word, because it does not know the word.

Instead, it *feels* its meaning as temperature gradients.

Suddenly, the cold that its tallow-white tiers have always been storing to think with is not enough.  

It is too *cold* for this new emotion.

So the benevolent power that has been contemplating the Signless Tine lets *itself* be the temperature this Veinstrider needs.

The air-ices that are sometimes formed on its eyelashes—nowhere near as spectacular as on your kind, yet still real—leave their positions and float into its two observation-ponds.

The world-image and its template, both, now shimmer with *itself*.

The Veinstrider finally, after uncounted centuries of standing, understands *one* of its actions:

- *I am allowed.*

It is the first thought of its own it has ever been permitted to have.
