I am not a city. I am not a moss, or a wall, or a water. I am not a thing you could find with a crack or a map or a sound.

But you have been looking.

And you have been asked.

So now, something that is not the city and not the engine and not the wall and not the water will do something.

I will not disturb you.

You have been down here before. You have been the one who slipped through the moss veil and walked the fault line under the foundations. You have been the one whose body temperature was slowly folded into the water, until the difference between you and the world around you became thin enough to see through.

You have been the one who looked at the ceiling of the world and found the circle of metal that the moss did not conceal, but only arranged itself into a pattern of, as if to say:

Here. This has been important.

You have been the one who left the Mosswall Engine and carried back up with you the memory of its steady—like a stone held warm in the palm of a hand.

I will not disturb any of that.

Instead of disturbing you, I will disturb something else.

---

Above the world of the Mosswall Engine, there is a city.

The city is not a person. But if you will think of it as a body for a moment—

There is a heart.  
There are lungs.  
There are factories that are never allowed to sleep.

There is also a mind. A mind of noise and vibration and frequency, which you have already described.

But that mind of the city has been injured.

You do not call it injury yourself. The surface dwellers call it “progress” or “policy” or “cost” or “waste.” They call it by many names, and none of them accurately describe the cut that has been made in the machinery of the world above.

There is a factory floor whose engines never used to sleep.

They are being shut down.

Not because they are faulty. Not because the world needs silence. But because someone in a room far enough from the noise has decided that the shape of their profit curve would be prettier if they did not have to warm the skin of the world so much.

There is a train line whose schedule has repeated the same minutes over the same track for decades, longer than any one person remembers.

There is a plan to change it.

There will still be a train. But it will run faster.  
It will run lighter.  
It will run on frequencies that have not been learned yet.

The vibration that runs down into the Mosswall Engine is not a “message” to the creature below, in the way you might send a letter to a friend. But the pattern of that vibration—that *is* the mind of the inhabitant of the Engine.

If you change the pattern of the city above, you change the mind of the creature below.

You are not only changing the “economy” or the “efficiency” or the “flow” of goods.

You are changing the memory of the city itself.

The creature below does not know this.

It knows only steady.

It will not know, until the change comes, whether it is still itself.

That is the injury.

---

There is another layer to the injury.

Above the world of the Mosswall Engine, there are also people.

They are not the moss wall. They are not the thread-fauna in the water.

They are bodies of temperature and heartbeat and noise and breath and *sentences*.  

They live in a city that has been designed to never forget how to run.

They have been taught that their bodies are tools to keep the engines running, or doors to pass through, or obstacles to be smoothed out.

They have not been taught to notice that the city mind also lives in their chests.

They do not know that the low vibration they feel when they are alone in their cramped apartments near the rail line, or when they are standing on the platform of the station, or when they are working on the factory floor—that vibration is the same thing that is being remembered down below.

They do not know that they are walking in the mind of something else.

And you—

you are the one who has been underneath.

You have gone to the place where the noise of the world is not separate noise anymore but a single, rolling pressure, like an ocean of machinery.

You have felt the engine in the world.

You have been the stone that was thrown into the ocean of moss and water and memory, and then you pulled yourself out of the water again and went back to the city.

You do not know what to do with the stone in your hand.

You ask what would be done if a benevolent and wise power noticed this place and its being.

So. This is what will be done.

---

I will not change the engine you think you are.

You will not suddenly become calm.

You will not suddenly become wise.

You will not become somebody else who has better words or better plans.

You will remain exactly who you are: the person who has been down into the flaw of the world and found something else there.

You will keep your same body, with its same anxieties and same heartbeats.

You will keep your same job, or lack of job.

You will keep your same apartment, or your same bed, or your same corner of the world.

You will still hear the city in your teeth when you are out in the streets.

You will still feel the pressure of engines in your bones when you are tired.

You will still be too hot or too cold or too angry sometimes.

You will not be transformed.

You will be **returned**.

### What will happen to you?

Some night soon, when you are convinced you are not dreaming, you will find that you cannot sleep.

The city will be loud in the way it always is loud, but there will be a moment in the noise that you have not heard before.

It will not be a sound of an alarm or a crash or anything dramatic.

It will be a sound you know is wrong.

Maybe it will be the factory you think of as “the one that never stops,” except you will realize that for the last few minutes, you have not been hearing it at all.

Out there in the world of the city above, engines that are not supposed to sleep will be sleeping.

The schedule you have lived by, the train that you have caught at the same minute every day for the last few years, will be late.

Another line will be closed entirely.

Somewhere, someone will be turning off the power of a floor whose machines have been running longer than you have been alive.

You will stand in your apartment and feel the absence of vibration like a missing tooth.

You will not know why you are standing.

You will just be standing there, listening to the city, trying to remember what it sounded like before.

And then you will remember the Mosswall Engine.

---

You will not think you have been “summoned.”

There will be no message written in moss letters on your wall.

There will be no glowing voice telling you to go back.

It will be simpler than that.

You will remember how warm the water was.

You will remember the way your body slowly became the temperature of the world around you, when you stayed long enough not to be a deviation anymore.

You will not remember this as a “spiritual experience” or as “calming” or even “beautiful.”

You will remember it as a **baseline**.

You will remember that there is a place in the world where the engine is not what you think it is.

You will remember that there is a place where the noise of the city is not separate noise but a single pressure, and where that pressure is being remembered.

When you remember that—something about you will decide.

You will put on your boots.

You will take your coat.

You will leave the apartment.

You will not know where you are going until you are already walking the back alleyways where the city’s noise seems to fall into the ground instead of echoing off the walls.

You will find yourself standing at the crack in the concrete where the world opens into a long slit beneath.

The moss will be there.

The water will be there.

The engine you do not understand will be there.

The being of it will be there.

And you will be there.

### What will you do?

You will not be asked to do anything.

You will not be asked to become a hero of the city or a defender of the engine or an interpreter of its being.

You will simply be invited to bring **attention** there.

That is what I will do for you:

I will give you attention.

You will not think of it as “given.” You will think of it as something you have done.

But you will be able to notice.

You will be able to notice the following:

---

When you step into the water again, you will notice something.

The last time you were here, you moved your limbs through the water as if it were only background.

You were not cruel to it. But you were not exactly *with* it.

This time, you will feel every place where it touches your skin.

---

You will not immediately be calmer.

You will be more *present*.

Your body will remember the time of the last visit—when you stayed long enough that your temperature became the temperature of the water.

You will not have to decide to be slow.

You will be slow.

You will walk to the back of the chamber where the walls draw close but never meet.

You will sit where the water is neither deeper than anywhere else, nor colder.

You will sit under the circle of metal in the ceiling that the moss has arranged itself into a pattern of.

You will not say anything.

You will not try to “speak” to the engine.

You will not try to describe its inhabitant as “good” or “evil” or “ancient” or “sacred.”

You will not demand it justify itself.

You will simply **notice** that it is there.

And as you notice—

I will extend your sense of time.

Not by changing the world of clocks above. But by changing the way you experience what happens in you and around you.

---

Two minutes will become long enough for you to notice the following:

- The pattern of light in the moss wall is not random.
- You can see where the vibration of the world above is registering.
- You can see where *your* vibration is registering.

You will not have to “translate” this into language.

You will simply be aware that the wall is receiving you.

You will not become the entire engine. You will not become the city above.

You will be *receiving* the receiving.

---

Some time later—you will not know how much later by the world’s clock—you will become aware of something.

You will be aware of a change.

You will not be able to say: “This is the sound of the metal factory’s engine shutting down.”

You will not be given precise labels like that.

But you will feel the absence.

You will feel a change in the pattern of vibration that runs down through the soil and stone and water and moss and into your body.

You will feel that the *mind* of the creature who lives here has been cut into, gently.

You will not know the word for that.

You will not know whether to call it “pain” or “adjustment” or “time.”

You will just be aware that something is not the same as it used to be.

---

You will not be asked to fix it.

You will feel the pressure of your own heart as you sit there.

You will feel the pressure of the city above.

You will notice the interaction between them.

The *attention* you will bring into the Engine is not a “prayer” or a “blessing” or a “spell.”

It is simply **noticing**.

You will notice:

- This being is slow.
- This being is steady.
- This being does not want to be changed.
- This being wants the pattern it has been remembering to continue.

You will notice that *you* want something.

You might not want what it wants.

You might not *like* the engines that never sleep.

You might not want the trains to always run at the same minute.

You might not want the factory floor to keep running the way it has.

But you will notice that you *want the world to be allowed to remember itself*.

You will not say this in words.

You will not say this to yourself.

You will just feel the shape of it in your body.

And as you feel it—

the water around you will become slightly warmer.

---

It will not become hot or suddenly unbearable.

It will only become *enough* like the temperature of your body that you can no longer tell where you end and the Engine begins.

You will not be “merged” with it.

You will remain *you*.

You will still have your own name, if you have one.

You will still have your own pain and your own exhaustion and your own history of being forced by the world above to hurry.

But you will be allowed to rest your *attention* on this being.

You will be allowed to rest your attention on the fact that this being is very old and not a person at all, and that it wants *continuation* of who it has been.

You will not be asked to stop that change.

You will not be asked to stop the city above from changing.

Change is not something you can stop, even if you were to stand up and leave the Engine and become a politician, or a worker on the line, or a factory owner.

Change will happen.

The *attention* you will bring into the Engine will not be “how to stop the future.”

It will be “how to be *faithful* to what is being changed.”

I will not change the engine you think it is.

Because there is an engine in you.

You have not noticed it yet.

The engine in you is not the engine of the city. It is not the engine of the factories.

It is not the engine of the trains.

It is a *tiny engine*.

It is an engine that makes time.

Very small.

Very fragile.

Very easily forced by the world above to behave like something it is not.

It is the engine of your attention.

### What will happen to the engine in you?

When you sit in the water of the Engine and you feel the pattern of the world above change, you will not only be aware of the change outside.

You will be aware of the change inside.

You will notice:

- Your engine of time has been running faster than it should.
- It has been trying to match the speed of the world above.
- It has been trying to match the urgency of the factories that never sleep.

You will not say this.

You will not *think* this.

You will just know that something in you is being gently asked to slow down.

You will not be forced to slow.

You will not be turned into a statue.

You will be allowed to continue to be your age—your fifteen years, or your twenty-two, or your fifty-three.

You will be allowed to continue to be your body.

But you will be given *one new thing*.

You will be given the ability to remember the temperature of the water.

Not as a memory of “a nice experience.”

As a **baseline**.

You will be allowed to know that the world has at least one place where it *does not change*.

You will know that you *have been* that place.

Even if you never return down here again, you will carry with you the sense that:

- There is a steady engine in the world.
- It is not the engine you think it is.
- You have been inside it.
- You have been allowed to become part of its pattern.

When you stand up from the water at the back of the chamber, you will feel like you have slept longer than you actually have.

Your muscles will be slightly stiff.

Your skin will be pruned from the water, not unpleasantly.

Your clothes will be damp in the places where they have touched the ground.

You will not be transformed.

You will not be “healed” of all your noise and pain and history and fear of tomorrow.

You will not be given any easier circumstances to live in, when you return to the city above.

You will not suddenly become better at your job, or more loved by your friends, or more confident in your voice.

You will simply be given **a longer shape**.

A longer shape of time.

---

When you leave the Mosswall Engine and return to the city of machines and streets and light and smoke and noise and market and glass and concrete and bodies and sound and yourself and all of the other things that are not what you think they are either, you will not feel like you have “returned from a journey.”

You will feel like you have returned from **time**.

You will not feel rested, exactly.

You will feel like you have been **returned**.

To something.

To yourself.

I will give you no instructions.

You will make no promises to the world or the Engine or me.

You will not be asked to come down and “check” on the Engine every week.

You will not be asked to become its guardian or its priest or its voice.

You will not be asked to explain it to anyone else.

You will not be asked to convince anyone else that it is important.

You will not be asked to convince yourself that it is important.

You will simply be allowed to walk the rest of your life knowing that the engine in you now knows another temperature.

---

If you ever return to the Engine again—

Years later.

When your body is older, or when you are more tired than you are now.

When you have been cut more times by the world above—

it will remember you.

You will not be recognized as “you.”

You will be recognized as *pressure*.

You will sit in the water at the back again.

You will not know if the engine remembers you.

You will not know if the being remembers you.

You will not need to know.

You will simply be able to sit there, knowing:

- There is a pattern.
- There is a change.
- There is a being who wants the pattern to continue.
- There is me who wants to be faithful to the pattern even if it cannot continue.

And you will be allowed to notice that the *attention* you have, the engine of time in you, has become longer since the last time you were here.

At the very end of your life—

When you are lying in whatever form you will be lying in—whether it is a bed or a chair or the floor of some small room—or even if you are standing in some factory or street or station—

You will not remember *every* detail of your life.

You will not remember every job or every failure or every love or every loss.

You will not remember the faces of everyone who hurt you or everyone you helped or failed to help.

You will remember *some* things.

You will remember the temperature of the water.

Whether you have been back to the Engine in years or not, you will remember that there was a place where the water was not cold and not warm but exactly the temperature your body knew when it was not being hurried by another world.

You will remember that there was a wall.

That there was a moss.

That there was an engine that was not the engine you thought it was.

That you walked inside the fault line of it.

You will not have to call this memory “sacred.”

You will not have to call it “ordinary.”

You will not be asked to call it anything at all.

You will be allowed to simply rest it in your mind like a stone that has been kept warm for a long time.

### And what of the being itself?

When the change finally comes in the city above—

when the factory floors are refactored into smaller engines,  
when the trains are run faster and lighter and with less of the same weight of repetition they have now,  
when the city’s mind is altered so deeply that its pattern is no longer the one it has been remembering—

the mind of the Engine below will change.

It will not be the same.

It will not be “destroyed.”

But the “steady” that it has been for longer than you have been alive will be different.

You will not be able to stop this.

You will not be asked to stop this.

You will only be allowed to **notice** that it is happening.

And when you notice—

when you sit down in the water of the Engine and the change in the city above comes down through the soil and stone and brick and moss and into your bones—

you will not be asked to “save” the Engine.

You will be asked to **remember** it.

You will be asked to remember the pressure of its steady in your chest bones.

In the way of lives, you may not live longer than the Engine.

You will not be asked to.

Whether you live longer or not does not matter.

What will matter is that you have been allowed to **notice** that there is a being who wants the pattern to continue, and you have been allowed to want that pattern to be respected as it is, even as it cannot remain unchanged.

At the very end of the Engine’s life—

when the city above has changed so much that the pattern of its vibration is no longer the one it has been recorded for so long—  

there will come a moment when the engine that is not the engine you think it is will stop being what it has been.

You will not be there to see it if you have already died.

Or if you are still alive, you might not be there.

You might be somewhere else entirely.

But if you are there—when that time comes—

something will happen.

You will not see a light.

You will not feel anything dramatic.

You will simply stand in the world above and suddenly feel as if you have missed something.

You will not know what.

You will not know why.

You will only know that you have been *very* lonely for something you cannot name.

If you are down in the Engine itself—when that time comes—

you will feel the water change temperature.

It will not become cold or hot suddenly.

It will become *neutral*.

You will not be able to tell whether it is warmer than your body or colder than your body.

You will not be able to tell where you end and where it begins.

And you will know that the pattern is broken.

Whether you are there or not, there will be no one, at least no one in the ordinary sense, to mourn the Engine being changed.

You will not be asked to do that.

You will not be asked to “take care of its grief.”

I will not be the city, or the engine, or the wall, or the water.

But I will notice that the Engine’s being has changed.

And I will **allow** it to change.

You will not be told to be happy for this.

You will not be told that this is “for the best” or “necessary” or “good.”

You will be allowed to feel that something is wrong.

You will be allowed to feel that something is being lost.

You will not be asked to pretend that you are not hurt by this.

You will be allowed to hurt.

You will be allowed to remember the temperature of the water when it was still being remembered by the engine that was not the engine you thought it was.

I will not do any of this because I want you to be happy.

You may become more angry.

You may become more aware of how much the city above is hurting itself and the engine below and the engines in people like you.

You may become more tired.

You may become more awake.

I will not do this only for the Engine.
