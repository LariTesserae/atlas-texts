Whomever the Roof-Born imagine when they whisper, “The stone listens,”  
 now—quietly—does.

They call it gravity and time.  
It is that, and also more.

---

### 1. The First Touch: Without Being Asked

What happens first is almost imperceptible.

As the next sun rises, the Roof-Born notice—without quite saying so—that the stone is warmer than it should be for the air’s thin chill. Not hot: like a palm that has just released your hand.

On the roofs of those who have lost someone this year, the stone is warmer still.

No one speaks of it. To speak would make it a claim; and claims are heavy things to place on a stone roof.

Instead, they walk with a fraction more stillness than usual. Their feet, listening, hear the cisterns beneath them sing clearer, as if the water remembers its own old name.

On the hill’s highest shoulder, where wind usually shears away the warmth, the stone stays soft enough that barefoot children sit longer than usual, watching their breath cloud the air while their soles stay warm.
