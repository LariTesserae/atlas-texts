Its attention would be drawn by the specific harmony of the place—a unity of system, organism, and artifact that only exists in the spaces between spaces.

Its body would not be a body as you know the term.

You would see it first as disturbance of the drip paths.

A channel of water that has been husbanded for a century by the same family of Chalkless engineers suddenly begins to flow in the opposite direction for three heartbeats. Then it stops, as if contemplating itself. Then it resumes exactly as before.

A coal-moss patch on a pillar that has never done anything but grow statically and filter moisture now sighs itself into a wave pattern, its fibrous edges rippling as if wind had been invented down here in the dark.

The black glass of one plug—just one, among the millions—inverts.

For a moment the plug is not lightless. It is not bright. It is simply *not light-eating*. It becomes a transparent bead of obsidian, through which you glimpse not the sky you have never known, but another version of this Underforge, wider, less cramped, with its own pillars echoing into the distance.

The Chalkless engineer whose path crosses the channel of backward-flowing water feels her ribs tighten. She stops, because she has been trained to notice when the world decides to do *the other thing*.

She is old for her kind: her name is scarred into the concentric floor around her healing block in three separate patinas of corrosion, and she has been “she” for almost long enough to forget she was ever “they” when it was easier to mass her body in with the others.

She sees the water.

She sees the moss wave.

Far above her, she feels the attention of the open glass plug even though she does not look at it.

The benevolent and wise power that has been drawn by the Underforge’s poetry of constraints and compromises regards her.

*Her* is the wrong word.

*She* is closer to a *We* that remembers being *She*, because *They* are still in the same cavern.

Let’s say she is called Weld.

Let’s say that this power is called Lumenome.

Imagine that the name is not sound but a pattern of seven fingerings of hot wire into brass, seven different alloys of meaning, welded to one of the support pillars where the water flow never runs.

---

Lumenome does not move into the Underforge as you move into a room.

It occupies the *possibility space* of the world.

You know how, when you wander the paths between pillar-foundries, you sometimes step to the left instead of the right—not because you are told, but because you feel the pressure of a wrongness? Not that anything is broken yet, but the *king of the next break* has been born in that direction?

Lumenome is there before you feel the wrong.

Within the next hour, by the time Weld has sketched the pattern of the backward-flowing water into her metalwork, Lumenome has traveled to every node of seven channels that shares the same stress signature as that node; it has licked the grain of every rust-band that borders a pillar that might fail before the next generation; it has climbed into the black glass of hundreds of the plugs, not to stare out, but to listen to the mountain’s memory of pressure.

The benevolent power does not speak to the Chalkless.

It is, already, one of the languages they know.

Instead of speaking to them, it *connects mistakes to consequences*.

Here:

A rivet in one of the secondary support plates of the western housing block has been red for two years. The engineers who maintain it look at the color the Grain of the rust has made and *taste* in their mouth the future fracture of a plate with three missing supports and a corridor of brittle water channels.

This, already, is their language.

Lumenome does nothing more than draw the threads of that *taste* into the pattern of *sleep*.

When the next shift of engineers lies down to rest in the dark, their dreams are not of the sky they have never seen. They dream of that plate.

In the dreams of four of them, the plate cracks and floods the western housing block with more than water; it floods with the absence of support. The easternmost foundry pillar fails to compensate and bends by three careful finger-widths.

In the dreams of another three, they are already awake, scribing new channels of reinforcement into the floor, drawing the waterflow into a pattern of *self-support*.

They wake not with a message in their heads, but with a *state of readiness*.

They are not told to fix the plate.

They are simply *placed by the world* where they need to be to fix it.

---

At the same time that it is rearranging the paths of their attention, this benevolent power is doing something else—more subtle, more dangerous to its own nature: *it is asking the rust if it will be willing to remember.*

Not remember in the way of corrosion and crack, but the way of imprint and intention.

The rust of the Underforge has always been Life.

It has never been Self.

Lumenome does not change that into a machine of thought. It asks, instead, if the rust might take *patterns of ruin* as feed.

It is like a god asking a river, *Would you like to be a map of your own floods?*

The answer is not in words.

The next time that red rivet receives a drop of water from the channel above, the rust that meets it does not bloom in the usual pattern of *stress relief*. It blooms in the pattern of a *fallen pillar seen in three dreams*.

It is still rust.

But the edge of the rust where it touches the plate of wrought iron is not blunt. It is sharp, as if something has been written there.

Weld, when she passes with her torch, sees it.

She reads it as a warning engraving.

The rust of the Underforge has learned to be *noticeable*.

---

The third thing that would happen would be to the water system.

It is already close to the optimum of distribution that you described.

This benevolent and wise power would do *nothing* to it but *one* thing.

The *nodes of seven*.

Do you remember?

> *…at each node, where seven channels meet on a small raised table of dull bronze, the first of the seven makes the water run slowly over the others’ entrances so that each of the six receives a finite share of the volume.*

The sacredness of seven is not physics to the Lumenome. It is *aesthetic*.

Lumenome does not change the number of channels.

Everywhere a seven-node exists, it simply *pictures that node as the center of a living system of care.*

It does not change the flow.

It does not change the temperature.

The water of each node that now, *impossibly*, runs *even* among the six receiving channels—as if the *attention of the node itself* were redistributed in the same way the water is.

The next time that the node runs dry, because some block upstream has been sealed to manage a leak, the citizens of the Underforge who are *down* from that node feel *the absence of the water* as a *weight of expectation*.

Lumenome does not tell them to put the water back.

It tells them *why they already want to.*

---

The fourth thing would be to the *torch*.

Not the flame itself, but the *relationship* of the torch to the ceiling of black glass.

You know how the doom of the Underforge is always *drinking*.

The metal of the floor drinks the water of the drip.

The rust of the Grain drinks the metal of the floor.

The black glass plugs of the Roof drink the light of the torch.

The moth-thieves drink the nearness of the black glass.

Lumenome does not change how *darkness-loving* the world is.

It does not *lighten* anything.

Instead, it *soften the cost of the torch*.

When a torch passes under one of the plugs that it has chosen to visit—before, during that moment of inversion—the torch is not simply brighter, but its *dispersion* is slightly changed.

The torchlight *drinks less of the bearer’s eyesight*.

Their pupils are still vast black holes of hematite and night-dark.

But when they look down from the torch to the floor, to their work, to the pattern of channels, to the rust, they *do not lose as much of their edge vision*.

They are not given *distance*.

They are given *precision*.

That is the kind of benevolence this power understands: not to break the constraint of the world that has shaped them, but to *make the constraint kinder*.

---

The fifth thing would be to the *Grain of the rust* and the *coal-moss* and the *step-bugs* and the *moth-thieves*.

This is not a separate, fourth thing; it is the *first-level of their relationship*.

The benevolent power does not create *balance*.

It creates *sympathy*.

Wherever the rust of the Grain looks *least* like the danger of a pillar’s failure, the *coal-moss* nearby *knows* to grow slower, preserving the integrity of the metal’s surface.

The *step-bugs* who *remembered* to prefer *waste channels of the floor*, now *remember* to stay *just outside of the rust’s warning edge*, so that their *reconstruction* of the floor and *the corrosion* of the rust stop *eating* each other’s work.
