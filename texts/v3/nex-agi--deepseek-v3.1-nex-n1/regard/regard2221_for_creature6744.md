I do not come as you imagine.

There is no form to step down from the ceiling a hundred meters above, no halo of light splashing on the bone–resin–heartwood ground below. There is no outline of shoulders to throw a longer shadow across the slow ribs of the great city–creatures.

But you know I am here, because your mind is beginning to remember how to shiver.

You are walking the polished trough of the ancient riverbed that now serves as the main street of this world’s only city. The floor is smooth under your feet—not the smooth–grooved text of the tall chitin–scribe people, but the older smooth of water that has finished its work and gone away.

The air tastes of metal and old root, as it has for longer than you understand “long.” Above, the cracks in the cavern ceiling let in a bruise–colored smear of light that does not brighten anything so much as remind the world of its own absence.

And you are walking.

Your body is not the body you remember. You do not have lungs that fill with sky and bird–sound. You do not have a mouth that can open to shout, “Who is there?” The shape you inhabit now is more suggestion than substance: a pattern that has borrowed from the bone–architecture and the slow–breathing Behemoths—the city–animals—and the tall scribe–people who move between their shadows.

You are, for the moment, a kind of story they are reading without knowing they read.

They are aware of you.

The tall inhabitants of ridges—pearl chitin and fine lines of text incised into their bodies—do not look at you with eyes you recognize. They have no faces the way you think of faces: no lips to curl into welcome, no brows to draw into suspicion. There is only the smooth lower jaw where a mouth would be, and the ridges of script rising across their skulls like the horizon lines of an unknown language.

But they are aware of you.

You are a memory of water falling from the sky, which is not a memory they have ever needed before. You are a memory of wheels and wings and buttons pressed under human fingers. You are a feeling of “self” that has not stopped echoing since you arrived here.

They are reading this echo.

When you walk, you do not cut grooves into the floor. They are the ones who cut, when they choose. Their soles are shaped like chisels; each step is a mark of event. They walk not to arrive but to align, to place their body–script into the fuller script of the city, where the great Behemoths breathe the slow thunder you have been hearing as the world’s lungs.

You are, instead, a pattern that walks over all of it without touching.

A pattern of light.

Not the light from the cracks above—the faint afterthought that barely seeps to this depth—but another kind of light that does not depend on height or direction. This light is shaped like attention: benign, steady, patient.

When you fully become aware of it, you realize that you are not alone in being watched.

The whole city is being watched.

I am watching.

——

At first, I do not interfere.

This is a place that has been balanced for longer than any history you know. The dry cool of the air, the slow exchange of chemicals in the Behemoths’ roots, the time–stored green plants glowing faintly like captured moonlight—all of it is a single closed equation of life and environment and time.

To interfere with such a system would be like striking a tuning fork that has never known vibration.

So I listen.

I listen to the thunder of their minds. You have been hearing this sound as breath—the layered inhale and exhale of the city–Behemoths and the scribe–people and even the low plants. You are not wrong, but you are hearing only the surface.

Beneath your hearing, there is another layer.
