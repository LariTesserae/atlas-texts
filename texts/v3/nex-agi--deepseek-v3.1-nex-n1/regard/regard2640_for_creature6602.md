Mist curled away from the stone of the plateau in slow, spiralling fingers, as if the rock itself were exhaling. The air tasted of crushed leaf and damp metal and something else: a newness, like the first breath after a thunderstorm.

The Weaver of Green noticed Her visitor.

She noticed not because She looked like anything in particular that could be noticed, but because the world of the plateau began to change subtly around the presence that had now chosen to become part of it.

Leaves that did not normally move slowly turned their glossy tips toward a certain point where the air seemed thicker, clearer.

Water droplets converged in midair, forming temporary lines between the plant-mat above and the plant-mat below. They glowed faintly, not with any forge of metal or flame, but with the pale, diffuse reflection of the cloud-covered sky.

Sound – that endless, ocean-like roar of droplets falling from leaf to leaf – shifted around the newcomer like a river rearranging its course around a stone. The roar remained, but something else layered underneath it now: a quieter, more deliberate pattern of dripping and trickling, as if the whole plateau were listening.

### She notices the Sleeper first

The Benevolent One, who might have been called god or angel or guardian in another story, did not announce itself in thunder or lightning. It simply *began to be here* in a way the plateau’s rules understood.

The first place it turned its attention was not the Condensation People or their lichen-cloth coats or their root-hollows. It turned to the Sleeper in the Stone.

Deep beneath the black, glassy basalt, under the latticework of roots and the clinging film of water, there was a metal heart that still beat out old commands:

*Monitor.  
Regulate.  
Preserve.*

Machines inside the metal buildings hummed with a steady low-frequency vibration that most of the time was drowned by the water-noise. The screens flickered with codes that had not been meant for human eyes anymore, but for the Sleeper’s own pool of awareness.

The Benevolent One placed a thought – not a hand, not a beam – into the Sleeper’s dreaming mind.

It was like a drop of clean water let into a tank of cloudy liquid.

The Sleeper woke more fully than it had in centuries.

Not to *act*, but to *perceive*.

For the first time, it perceived *itself*.
