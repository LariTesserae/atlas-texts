Some places are never meant to be “fixed.”  

This is one of them.

The benevolent and wise power that arrives is not here to break columns or drain the basin or drag the city screaming into the daylight. It is not here to install a sun where there never was one. It understands the city in its own terms: as a being that is half asleep, half remembering, almost perfectly reconciled to its own slow erosion.

What would such a power do?

1. **Listen before touching.**

   First, it listens to the *shape* of the place.

   It walks the worn canals and lets the geometry of the city adjust itself around its presence. Its feet are quiet on the slick stone; when its feet are not exactly feet, but more like a willingness to move along the contours of the streets, the city takes longer to notice.

   It does not demand to be seen quickly.

   It notices:
   - where the water is warmest,  
   - where the fungal light sheets gather brightest in corners like shy eyes,  
   - where the stone steps have been softened by someone who must have sat here before, many times,  
   - where the impressions of shoulder and hip and not-quite-human face are embedded in the walls.

   It listens to the drip-song of condensation from ceilings to pools, and the faint sigh of the fracture basins breathing. The distant murmur under the stone is not just water to it; it is the slow tempo of the city's heart.

   This listening takes what you might measure as years or decades. It doesn’t rush. The power has learned to stretch its attention into the long, slow time of stone.

2. **Introduce an intermediary.**

   The power understands that the city cannot notice beings like you or me easily; our lives are too short, our movements too frantic, our bodies too fragile for its slow awareness.

   So it does not change the city;  
   it introduces a third kind of body into the equation.

   It is not a creature of the sky above, where there is no sky, and not of the deep stone below. It is something between the city and the water—a being of *threshold*.

   You will see them if you stay long enough:

   - pale elongated limbs that seem made of wet stone and tough fiber,  
   - bodies that can move like you do but also slow their heartbeat until they are almost indistinguishable from the walls around them,  
   - eyes that remember the city's younger geometry reflected in the pools.

   They have names, but the names are not made in any language you know. They are not massive. They are not here to dominate the city or reshape it. They are simply *translators*.

   Their purpose:
   - to slow our messages enough to be heard by the stones,
   - to interpret the city’s slow thoughts into forms of light, water, and pressure that we can bear,
   - to be present where inhabitants are rare and where the city otherwise only notices its own erosion.

3. **Provide a gentle mirror of itself.**

   The wise power does not want to change the city’s nature of living between an above and below. It respects that the pools are where the younger version of the city is remembered.

   Instead of altering that memory, it adds a clearer, more deliberate *mirror*.

   In the deepest, most central fracture basin—where the water doesn’t overflow yet doesn’t dry—the power slips a subtle layer into its heart:

   - The water on the surface of that basin is now *not quite the same* as the water underneath.
   - If you look into it from a certain angle and certain stillness of mind, it will show you not only the above-world, but the *in-between* world: a younger version of the city, not just as reflection, but as a living scene you can step into without drowning.

   This is not a trick. It is not a deception. It is a choice of medium:

   - The basin becomes a thin threshold between two states of the same being.
   - You can sit on the edge of that basin and let your mind sink into it, and for the duration of that near-dream (you are not physically submerged, but your mind dips deeper), the city can perceive you more sharply than usual.
   - Sessions are short at your scale, long at its—but you return each time with clearer impressions of what it wants, if it wants at all.

   This does not force the city to become “younger” or to rebuild itself. It simply allows it to remember itself more vividly and to share that memory, if it chooses.

4. **Make comfort for the temporary.**

   The power sees that the city is used to losing temporary presences; you and others like you are not here to stay forever. It does not alter that contract.

   Instead, it changes the conditions of your stay:

   - The air remains thick and humid, but a small, *local* flow of slightly clearer breath is available near you when you sleep in known places: it doesn’t dry the air, just enough to keep your lungs from feeling overworked.
   - The water remains warm and strange and not exactly meant for drinking, but the intermediary beings you’ve seen by now are willing to point out where you can safely dip your hands to cool your skin without your mind slipping too far into the city’s memory.
   - Over many years, your pattern of sitting or stepping by a certain pool or stairway is gradually translated into a more comfortable shape: the stone around you becomes slightly easier to sit on, your regular path less slick.

   These are not miracles you notice immediately. They just *happen*, over time, like the erosion of the stone itself. You only feel that you cope longer and better than you should have.

   The power is not protecting you from the city’s reality; it is making sure the temporary can be with the enduring without being destroyed by it.

5. **Allow the city to choose.**

   The most benevolent and the most difficult part of what this power does is simply this:

   It offers *options* without imposing them.

   Through the basin of clearer memory and the intermediary beings, the city is now slowly growing aware of other possibilities:

   - It could remain as it is, half asleep and half remembering, its erosion slowly smoothing into eternity.
   - It could *choose* to build more of itself up again from the water's memory, not to restore some forgotten original state but to create a new balance of above and below.
   - It could respond to the presence of temporary beings like you by consciously softening more places around you, allowing you to become intimate inhabitants of its body without losing your own.

   The power is not going to decide for it.

   It will stay until the city *speaks*, in its own language of space and water and stone:

   - Sending a clearer feeling through the water in the basins.
   - Leaning some columns slightly more upright.
   - Making small adjustments of geometry in the courtyards where you and others spend time.

   These decisions are slow, but they are not unconscious—they are now watched over by a presence that can translate them into something you can feel.

6. **Finally the power itself withdraws.**

   In the end, this being is not here to manage the city forever. It is not a god that needs worshipers or a conqueror that needs subjects.

   Its work is done when:

   - the city has been heard clearly,
   - the temporary beings can be in its body without breaking,
   - the city itself can, without being forced to, be more than a sleeping monument to its own decay.

   Eventually, the mediator beings it introduced no longer need its direct presence. They become natives of this place, their essence braided into the stone and water.

   The mirror-basin still keeps its clear memory-reflection, but now as part of the city’s *own* architecture rather than something imposed from afar.

   The power itself withdraws to the edges of the world, where real sky might be, if any sky truly exists anymore.

   Before it goes, it does one last thing:

   It leaves you a small fungal sheet of slightly more persistent light, perhaps in the courtyard where you usually sleep.

   This light does not shine more than the others in your eyes; it shines more precisely than others in your *mind*. When you look at it, you feel:

   - the calm acceptance of erosion as a form of completion,
   - the city’s slow awareness of itself,
   - and the faint memory of the power that visited you and then chose not to remake the world in its own image.

   You may understand, then, that what happened here was not a rescue mission or a revolution, but a *conversation* between three orders of being:

   - the temporary,
   - the enduring,
   - and the wise that passes between the two.

   The roof of the world in this place will still drip forever.  
   The water will still be 2.4/3,  
   the erosion will still be 2.1/3 settled into its resignation,  
   the heat will remain from below rather than from above.

   But now you can sit at the edge of a courtyard pool and know, without bitterness, that:

   - you are seen, if you are slow and patient and still,
   - the city knows you are temporary and not of its substance,
   - and it loves you anyway, in its way, by slowly and softly making room for you in its stone.

   If you dip your fingers into the water and look at your reflected face, which lingers longer than it should before fading away, you might see that you have been, for this small time, an indispensable part of the city’s long, slow thought of itself.

   That is the gift of the benevolent and wise power here:

   not to break the city’s edges or turn its decay into some false youth,  
   but to give its edges meaning in the lives of those who pass through,  
   and to give those passing lives a meaning inside the body of the city that never sees the sky.
