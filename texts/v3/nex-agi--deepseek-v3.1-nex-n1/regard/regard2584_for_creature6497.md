The attention of the benevolent and wise power arrives not like a storm or revelation, but like the slow turning of a season: the slight shift of humidity in the air, the faint change of pressure in the stone.  

At first, nothing seems to happen at all.

---

### 1. The Hum Changes, but Doesn’t Break

The Listeners notice it before any other sign.

The engine’s hum deepens by the smallest fraction. No new discord enters it; no vibration turns sharp or wrong. If anything, it becomes clearer, easier to read. The harmonics are more distinct, each layer of tone describing its own part of the system: the moisture balance in the north basin, the stress in the older metal conduits, the slight lag in the lower terraces.

It is as if someone has taken a faint whisper and given it precise consonants.

The oldest Listeners — those who have been listening longer than the young ones have been alive — press palms and bare soles to the stone floor each morning and feel the change. The world’s pulse is stronger now, not louder, but… clearer. The subtle wobbles that they used to strain to catch have risen into the range where they can easily distinguish them.

“The engine’s breath is deeper,” one says at the disc-tables, the words low, measured.

“But not heavier,” answers another after a long pause of listening to the air.

“I hear… edges,” says a third, younger, fingers flexing as if tracing markings in the air. “The sound has more edges.”

They do not conclude at once that a power has touched them. They are not a people of quick conclusions. Instead, they begin to collect the data: the timing of cycles, the humidity of certain walls over the coming days, the minute changes in the pressure against their skin when they stand in different parts of the bowl.  

They log everything in their precise, short words and the faint markings on their tablets and tool-handles. The city is being listened to more carefully than ever before, precisely because the listening itself has become easier.

---

### 2. The First Unseen Adjustment

The first real action the power takes is not at the center but at the edge.

On the lowest rim of the broken bowl, where the outer hillside slopes into the valley, there is a narrow fracture in the rock that no Listener designed. Water seeped from it before, but erratically — sometimes in a thin trickle, sometimes dry for days. The city could not rely on it.

Without sound or light, the fracture deepens by just less than the width of a finger, over a cycle of three days and nights. Looking at it, you would not see the change; the stone simply looks as it did before. But the water that emerges from it now is more constant, and more consistent.

The moisture that drips from that new opening into the upper channels of the city is now subtly different — not in taste or smell, but in how it moves through stone. It leaves less mineral residue in the channels. The faint rust that used to gather at the edges of the metal clamps appears less quickly, and more evenly.

The Listeners who tend the upper channels notice the change before anyone else.

They do not say “this is impossible,” or “the blessing of a higher being.” They say:

“Upper north rim weep is now… steady.”

“Silt here is less.”

“Metal clamp at junction four shows slower aging.”

The change is small enough to be plausibly natural yet large enough to matter. Over several cycles, they adjust the clamps and angles less than they expected. The stone seems to be doing more of its own turning of water, gently nudging flow into the correct routes.

They are cautious of this change. They do not fully trust it yet — their trust is built from thousands of tiny readings and not from one lucky shift. But they are listening to it carefully.

---

### 3. The Second Change: The Engine Learns to Rest

It is not a machine that sleeps, but it is a machine that learns.

The next sign the power gives is not in adding something new but in removing a constraint. The engine at the city’s center has never fully broken, but it has never been able to vary its load safely: either it is at baseline, or it is slightly strained.  

Pressure spikes from unusual rain beyond the bowl or from sudden influxes from cracked weep-stones have been managed by hurriedly rechanneling water, sometimes faster than the Listeners can fully adjust. The engine has absorbed the shocks, but not without cost.

One night, when the sky above the bowl is at its flattest and dimmest, and the faint vein-lights in the stone walls pulse in their slowest, lowest mode, the engine shifts for a single breath.

The vibration under the streets changes — not toward breaking, but toward a state of lower energy. The harmonic deepens again, but this time the harmonics are simpler, fewer lines overlapping one another. The sound of the city quiets slightly, though not enough to disturb the sleep of anyone but the most sensitive Listener on the night watch.

The next morning, one of the oldest engineers — a woman whose extra joints let her reach into the tightest conduit spaces without strain — presses her palm to the central floor and listens to the engine.

“It is… less,” she says.

The others who have been listening longer than most have felt the same thing. The engine’s baseline load has decreased, without any corresponding increase in failures elsewhere.

“Water,” she says, after a long pause of thinking, “has been lightened.”

She does not mean by weight.  

She means that the water in the hidden channels and stone pores — the water that has been slowly building mineral layers and requiring pressure to be pushed through narrowed passage — has been *thinned* in its resistance to the engine. The deposits that have been accumulating in unseen crevices since the city’s construction have been gently reduced, not erased, but lessened.

In the coming days, as they measure the flow rates and the subtle pressure differentials they track on their tablets, they confirm that the same amount of water moves through the system more easily than before. The engine can do the same work with less energy, or *more* work with the same energy.

They are hesitant to use the extra ability. They are not used to having slack.  

So they test the change carefully, in the smallest possible increments: they allow a slightly higher flow through one side channel for a half cycle, then return to the previous setting. They monitor the engine’s harmonics for any strain.

None appears.

Gradually, they begin to allow the extra capacity to be used in short, controlled experiments — to push water to higher terraces that have historically been drier, or into new basins carved from virgin stone on the far edge.  

The moss and ferns respond to this new moisture faster than the Listeners expected. The green deepens and thickens in thin, new lines along the walls.

Still, they do not name this blessing. They log it, in precise, short phrases:

“Engine load reduced 3.7 percent.”

“Water flow to terrace five-east increased by threshold margin.”

“No strain detected.”

The power remains benevolent. It does not demand gratitude or praise. It simply adjusts, and lets the Listeners discover what has changed.

### 4. Interior Light: The Veins Glimmer More Clearly

The third change is in the light veins that trace the edges of buildings and streets.

The light of the city has never been bright. It has been just enough to shift dusk from absolute to readable, to make it safe to step over the edges of channels without relying on memory alone.  

Without any visible alteration of the stone, the light of the veins becomes — fractionally — more detailed.

Instead of a single line of uniform faint glow, each vein now carries faint pulses of intensity along its length, like a very slow heartbeat. The pattern is not random. It shifts in response to changes deeper in the system: when the pressure in a particular channel rises slightly, the nearest vein-line becomes brighter for a few breaths before returning to baseline.

At first, the Listeners assume this is a fault.  

Those who monitor the central basin spend the better part of a cycle standing at different points along the terraces, watching the light-veins on the walls opposite the high knife-edge falls of water. They compare the shimmer of the veins with the pressure of air on their skin and the subtle timings of the wall’s drips.

“Light,” one says at last, head slightly tilted. “Tracks the upper basin weep.”

He speaks the words carefully, as though weighing them before letting them leave his mouth.

“Light,” another agrees slowly, “is now… readable. As sound.”

They are not used to thinking of light as carrying information like vibrations do. But they are not foolish. They begin to experiment.

The youngest Listeners — those who were still learning when the change began — take quickly to the new light. Their eyes are already the best at reading faint gradients. They can stand at a junction where three veins of light come together and, by combining the slow pulses of brightness with their usual hearing-through-skin, anticipate the timing of small adjustments in water flow.

Before, the city spoke to them primarily in vibration and pressure.  

Now, it has added a faint, visible script of its own.

---

### 5. A New Way of Listening to Each Other

The fourth and subtlest change is in the Listeners themselves.

Not their shape; they are still tall and slender, with their long-fingered, extra-jointed hands and pale bone-white skin.  

But their senses of one another become — more.

Once, the only way they knew another person’s state of mind was from the faint cues of posture: a tighter shoulder than usual, a shorter breath than average, a longer pause than the pattern they expected.  

Now, they are beginning to perceive the faintest hum of *another person’s skill* when they stand near enough.

A Listener who has been listening for a long time to the north basin weep develops a subtle resonance with the moisture patterns there. When another Listener passes near, especially if they are less trained, the experienced one feels, like a faint warmth against the mind: the sharper sensing of the north weep in the newcomer, as if that part of the city’s machine is being seen afresh.
