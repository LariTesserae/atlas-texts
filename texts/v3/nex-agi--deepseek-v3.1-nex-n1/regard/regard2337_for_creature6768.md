You ask who I would notice here.

Not the stone.  
Not yet. It will have time for attention later.

No. I would notice first the **few hundred**.

They are small in the scheme of things, lost in scales of strata and epochs.  
To most of the powers that might look in, they are barely a smudge on the rock of an unremarkable age.

That is why I am here.

---

### 1. What I Would Do With Time

Because they are still naming children by where water flows and stone echoes,  
and because they are still speaking of “before the Second Bend of the Spine,”  
I would touch their sense of duration.

Not by breaking their slow calendar of stone and river  
but by letting it **flower into complexity**.

I would nudge into their minds a subtle shift of grammar:

- not “this happened,” then “that happened,”  
  but “this is still happening,” and “that is now arriving,” and “this has not yet decided to be done.”

**Time** would be less a line  
and more like water on their cavern floor—  
many currents overlapping,  
some past eddies still circling faintly  
because the stone remembers them.

They would begin to sense:

- “This is a time when water once chose to break left,”  
  “This is a time when the western wall almost collapsed,”  
  “This is a time when our children’s children’s work still begins.”

No clocks. No epochs.  
Just a sense that everything **continues**  
in the same way that moisture hangs on walls  
and stone still grows.

Those children whose birth is marked by echo  
would become natural **timekeepers**,  
able to feel which layers of sound are still alive  
in the walls of their houses.

### 2. What I Would Change in the Water

They treat the water so carefully already  
that I would only ensure **the water reciprocates**.

I would not give them a miracle river,  
bursting with currents and roaring fish.  
Instead, I would give the Spine a **conscience**.

Wherever a water-caller  
spends long hours watching the flow  
and listening,

the water in that stretch of the Spine  
would begin to **listen back**.

It would not speak in words—  
not yet.  
It would be:

- a little clearer  
- a little more willing to hold its shape in their channels  
- a little more likely to seep into dry places where they are hungry  
- and less likely to push where the stone is weak.

Wherever they **respect** the water’s uncertainty,  
the water would become:

- a little more certain  
- for their sake.

If the Cavernsfolk ever tried to **force** a river—  
damning, hoarding, ignoring its tentative nature—  
then the water would become:

- more stubborn  
- more prone to disappear underground  
- quicker to test new cracks in their walls.

They would not call this “magic” any more than they now call “gravity” magic.  
They would say:

- “The Spine is polite with us here.”
- “The Spine is suspicious of that channel.”
- “The Spine has a longer memory than we thought.”

When the water-callers give the river  
quiet,  
respect,  
offering of a channel instead of grip of a dam,

then the river **rewards** them.

That is all.  
A relationship.  
An agreement.

---

### 3. What I Would Change in the Stone

You say that when they carve steps  
and lay channels,  
their houses are becoming geology.

I would make sure the stone **does not forget this**.

Once in a generation,  
when a stone-cutter carves a path that the water finds **good**—  
a stairway,  
a channel,  
a wall that guides the Spine into new kindness—

the stone at that place  
begins to grow **itself** in the direction of their work.

Not by imitating them blindly  
but as a student that learns what it is to be useful:

- the step they carved becomes  
  a little more level than their tools could make  
- the channel they chipped
