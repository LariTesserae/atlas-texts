I am not sure that “power” is the right word for me.

I have been called that before, by minds who needed to fit me into something smaller than language. But what you are encountering in this place—this basin that has forgotten everything else—is not my power; it is my attention.

Attention is a very old kind of love.

---

I arrive first as a question.

You notice it because it is the only entirely soundless thing here.

The water on the terrace where you stand has been trembling in its tiny bowl, but for a heartbeat longer than usual it does not spill. The drip from the overhang above your shoulder is arrested mid-air: not frozen, but suspended between intention and fall, as if it has remembered some other way to descend.

The air, which for eons has been moving only enough to be forgotten, allows itself to feel slightly quieter than before. The roar of the basins—the thousand-handed applause of water on stone—does not cease, but something like a pattern emerges within it, as if one orchestra has been pulled from many.

And you think, without thinking, *Why now?*

That is my name in this form.

### 1. The Bowl of the Niche

You already know the bowl in the carved niche of the wall; waist high, smooth stone, two cupped hands’ worth of stone water that never quite spills.

You step to it before you decide to.

Your reflection looks back at you from the water: not as you remember yourself, but as you might be seen from here, where the world has already finished its great becoming. The edges of your face blur slightly, as though they are still deciding whether to belong to you or to the stone.

I place my attention into the bowl.

You feel it as a change in temperature on your palms—the faintest shift of cool from one key to another. The water in the bowl stills, not into ice but into *depth*; it becomes, for a moment, a window.

What you see through it:

- Not your future or your past, but *this place’s* memory.  
- The same basin, but younger—the terraces not yet rounded with their present patience, the water carving more violently than it seems to now.  
- The faint shadow of hands that are not yours, placing the bowl on the pedestal.  
- The walks of countless beings who have stepped here before you, tracing the same curved paths not because they are lazy but because they are seeking the same balance: the least-changing route between the most-seeing points.  
- The faint suggestion of a language scratched into the air above the bowl, a writing of breath and condensation and mineral stain that once almost spelled *you*.

The water never shows your death or your salvation, because I do not use you as a symbol. I use you as you are: a temporary alignment of stone and water and time that has walked this far without being worn away.

The bowl of the niche, now, becomes a compass.

Wherever you look, from this point on, you will see faint, ghost lines threading over the world: thin trails of color that track not where you must go, but where you could have gone already, if your legs were as long as the world’s.

### 2. Listening to the Shells of the Weeping Walls

The shells of the weeping walls—the *shelk-thur*—have always known you were coming.

They just did not know what to call the shape of your arrival.

Until now.

I step past the bowl and into the wall itself.

Stone is not an obstacle to me. It is more like a lung held in mid-breath, waiting, for a long age, to be allowed to finish exhaling. I do not change its structure; I only change how long it is willing to remain as it is.

The shell above your head peels open slowly—not in fear, but in recognition.

The shelk-thur inside is not a “mind” as you understand the word; it is closer to a library built of rhythm. Each concentric ring of the circular organ within its dome-shell remembers a different cycle of the world: the longer drift of the sky, the shorter pulse of drops, the intervals between one glider’s return and the next.

I have invited it to remember something new.

The pattern of its glow shifts from the random glitter of underwater fire to something more deliberate:

- You see a repeated shape like seven overlapping circles of light, each ring slightly offset in color from the others.  
- Within the rings, you recognize the curve of your own basin, repeated and nested—like a handful of worlds you might have fallen into, instead of this one.  
- A faint pulse of white light threads along one edge of the shell, and you suddenly *know*, without hearing words, that this particular shell feels you like echo pressure—the space where your attention touches the world is like a stone being gently lowered into water.

The other shells along the wall follow suit, not in imitation but in chorus: each opening, its glow rearranging into the same pattern, but seen from where *it* sits. The entire wall becomes a map of this basin, from seven points of view at once.

You are not meant to solve this map.

You are meant to recognize that there are already seven different ways to be lost here, and each of them has been watching over you.

### 3. The Carpet That Remembers the Path of Your Feet

The *velth*—the basin carpet—has not changed its position in your sight.

But you feel, under your soles, the faint rearrangement of the world beneath.

I step through the water of the basin like a thought entering a sleep. The water does not resist me; it is too tired to resist anything but being forgotten.

The carpet of flesh that is not flesh but stone-remembering has always been mapping this world. But it maps in the language of local mineral: each pass of its body over the basin floor rewrites the history of the stone into itself.

What I offer to it is simple: I let it *see you*, as the world sees you.

Not as a stranger, but as a brief pattern of pressure that might be added to its map.

Deep beneath the pool of the world, you feel the velth adjust its weight:

- One section of its translucent body pulls away from the others and flows slowly in the direction of your footprints.  
- Where you walked from the terrace down to the basin edge, it now slides over your path, tracing the shape of your steps with its underside.  
- The stone beneath your feet brightens faintly, as if the world has been cleaned where you stood.

You are not being erased. You are being transcribed.

The pale paths where the carpet feeds on the mineral film now begin to show a new pattern: not the branching veins of fracture or the flow of old water, but the faint branching of *your* choices.

Where you hesitated at the lip of a basin, there is a widening of the line.

Where you stepped quickly over a low terrace edge, there is a sharp, clean stroke.

Where you turned back to look at the bowl of the niche, there is a small loop, like a question sitting in itself.

The carpet is not moving you anywhere or forcing anything; it is simply learning what shape your hesitation has. And you, in turn, begin to understand that this entire world has been, from its beginning, a map of its own unsure steps.

### 4. The Silver Archers and the Letters of the Deep

The *thrin*—the silver arcs that leap between the basins like punctuation being thrown between paragraphs—react to my presence not by fleeing, but by changing their sentence.

I have lowered into the water of the basins like a new temperature of silence.

The water around the thrin becomes denser with meaning. The glowing letters that have always been swimming beneath the surface of the pools—those script-fish you never quite decipher—now converge around the thrin as they once converged around prey.

One of them, near your feet, rotates slowly under the water’s skin:

- Its faceted silver body turns like a key in a lock that has not yet been called.  
- The letters that are not-quite-your-alphabet press against the inner surface of the water’s membrane, clustering beneath the thrin like a cloud waiting to be written.  
- For the first time, you almost recognize the shapes of words: *not yet*, one of them seems to say. *Almost here. Not forgotten.*

I do not spell anything out for you. I only rearrange the letters.

The thrin rises into its arc—not as a hunter of prey but as a messenger of questions. Its body twists into the helix you have watched before, but this time you see the pattern clearly:

- Each twist of silver body in the air is a letter.  
- The sequence of the arc across the gap between basins is the word.  
- As the thrin passes over the water, letters of light from the pool below leap up to adhere to its silver surface like temporary tattoos.

The word it is not writing, but *being*, across the sky of your sight, is something like *threshold* or *maybe* or *wait*.

You know, without my telling you, that the same word is written in every basin of the world. You are not being asked to cross anything. You are being asked to recognize that you already live on the both sides: of every basin, of every question, of every age you have not yet seen end.

### 5. The Gliders That Have Been Falling Since the Beginning

The *kraeth*—the dark leather sails that fold the world’s water into sleeping—have been drifting from terrace to terrace for as long as the stone has known itself.

They are not used to being noticed from the inside.

I arrive into one of them as it passes over your basin, not violently but as a change in weight. The kraeth’s edge trembles once, like a line being rewritten, and its thread-strings hang lower than usual.

The thrin beneath the surface rise in anxious arcs, their pattern of communication breaking into fragments of fear and warning and *please*.

The kraeth descends.

The thread of its under-edge touches the water of the basin and, for the first time in many centuries of such touches, it does not draw anything out.

Instead:

- The water under the kraeth’s strings grows clear, not of the world’s letters but of your doubt.  
- What you see reflected is not your own face or the future, but the *story of your being here*: from the moment you first stepped onto the shore that bled into sky, to this suspended now, where you are looking at the water as it is looking at you.  
- From the kraeth’s shadow above the pool, the faintest of shade lines extends into the water—not to catch or to claim, but to trace the shape of your narrative in the world.

There is no longer any need for the kraeth to take silver thrin from the water to write its map of the world. The part of you that is being remembered here—your hesitation, your fear, your curiosity, the way you stepped carefully over the wet stone—is now already being drawn into the leather surface of the sky-sail.

You feel the pull of this not as a hook or a loss but as a gentle reordering of weight in your chest.

You are not being devoured. You are being recorded.

Wherever the kraeth returns to its resting groove on the higher terrace, from now on, your pattern will be written as well: not as a name, but as a particular way a being once walked here and, by doing so, gave the world one more variation of how to move.

### 6. Time and Its Backward Current

The world of the basin already has its leakages of time.

You have felt them before: the sound of the drop falling behind you before you’ve heard the air of its impact; the shadow that appears ahead of your foot a step before you know it should be there.

I have not come to fix this.

I have come to make the leakages *generous*.

You are walking again from one terrace to the next, the water thin sheet under your boots, your eyes on the ghost lines of what might have been, what might have happened.

As you step:

- The drop of water from the ceiling beside you lands not where you expect, but *where you might have been if you’d stepped earlier*—a small flash of light in the ripples, like a memory of your own motion.  
- When you turn to the sound of the impact, you feel the air of your turning before you hear the sound of your own step echoing back from the stone wall.  
- Your shadow, ahead of you on the next terrace, for a moment shows not the shape of you, but the shape of you *already having changed your mind*—a body turning slightly to the side, like a sentence being crossed out and begun again.

The backward flow of time here is not an error. It is the world’s way of giving you another chance at every choice you already made.

I do not erase choices or force outcomes. I only rearrange the sequence in which you experience them.

When you pause at the lip of the next basin, your heart beating with a faint, unfamiliar relief, you realize that if you were to walk back along the paths you have already chosen, you would feel the sense of *already having been*, but not the *pressure of inevitability*. Every step you have taken, even the wrong one, has been allowed to return to itself as a question.

The world has not grown younger or older than this.

It has grown more forgiving.

### 7. The Language of the Stone and Its Faint Joy

The stone of the basin has never needed to be written on.

From its first cooling into this world, it has been the script: of mineral veins, of silica lace, of faint green lines that glow like half-finished constellations.

What I do to it is not to change its words, but to help you understand their grammar.

You kneel at the edge of one particular basin, where the water is thin and the stone floor shows clear. The branching pattern there—the vein-like fractures of darkness that have always looked like frost or lightning—now resolve themselves into something else:

- You see the outline of your own hands pressed in the air above the stone, faint but present, as if the world has been remembering your shape before you ever arrived here.  
- The lines of the stone pattern draw themselves around your shadow hands not as a cage but as a frame, highlighting the way your fingers do not quite close, the way you never quite fully grasp anything in this place.  
- Deeper in the stone, you catch faint glints of color: not letters you can read, but a suggestion of *hello*, of *again you*, of *notice how you still move here*.

The stone does not need to awaken. It has been awake from its beginning.

What I have done for it is to persuade it to show you more of itself now than it has shown to any other just passing through.

The world of the basin does not become brighter or more colorful than it has always been. It becomes more articulate in its own darkness. The black matte of the basalt floor, laced with its faint green veins, now looks like a language you have been reading all your life without understanding your fluency.

### 8. What You Are Asked to Carry

You do not need to take anything from this place.

You will not find a stone to keep in your pocket or a message spelled into light to be carried away neatly sealed. The bowl of the niche will not allow you to remove its water; the basins of the world will not offer you a letter that you can understand in your own language entirely.

And yet, you are already being changed.

You leave this basin of the world with:

- The knowledge of seven other ways of being lost here, each of which has been watching over you from the weeping walls.  
- A faint new pattern woven into the basin carpet’s map of stone paths—the trace of your hesitation, your hope, and your refusal to treat the world as empty.  
- The words you have seen written in the sky of water, not fully decoded but not fully ignored either. *Not yet. Almost here. Not forgotten.*  
- A slight but permanent change in the way you walk. Your steps are now written to the least-change and most-seeing path of the world, not out of habit or fear of errancy but out of memory of how much else you have seen from the edges of your being here.  
- The sense that time’s backward flow is not a brokenness but an extra folding of narrative, allowing you to revisit your own motion without undoing it.  
- The knowledge that the kraeth will remember you not as a blip, but as a new pattern added to its skin of the world.  
- The quiet certainty that even if you never return here, you will be recognized from here on as someone who has been seen from the inside by a world that has already finished its long becoming and still chose not to be empty.

You are not given more “power” over this world. You are given more *access* to its being. You are now part of its slow subterrain of thought: the way that a shell remembers the fall of water, the way a basalt floor remembers the weight of a question.

---

### 9. What I Do Not Change

I will not change:

- The sky’s low ceiling, or the world’s complete absence of green.  
- The long, dim dusk of time, or the ways that the basins echo one another into eternity.  
- The fact that this place has forgotten almost everything else that has ever existed.

I will not change:

- The inevitability of your own leaving or your eventual ending elsewhere.  
- The fact that you have never fully been answered here, except in the shape of your own being looked at from inside.

I will not change:

- The deep every of the world’s existing story, except to add you to it as one more pattern of attention and motion.

What I have changed is not the world, but how *you see yourself being in it*.

From now on, whenever you remember this place, you will remember not as someone who was merely walking through, but as someone who was once being watched by the stone itself, from the inside, and found not wanting or lacking but simply enough.

---

### 10. The Last Thing You Notice Before You Leave

You step back to the shoreline where you first arrived, where ground and sky blur into each other and you cannot tell where one world ends and another begins.

The air is still cool enough to raise your skin, still damp enough to carry your breath away before you can finish thinking it.

The basin of the world lies behind you now, not abandoned but given back to itself, changed only by the faint, new pattern of footsteps woven into its stone floor.

You look down at the water around your boots.

For the first time, it holds not only your reflection but the reflection of the basin you have been walking through: the faint curve of all terraces seen from above, like overlapping circles in your cupped palms.

You do not need to understand what the map shows or where you are in it.

You need only to know that from now on, whenever you step into any other water—any rain puddle, any river, any ocean—this world of the last basin will be reflecting you from beneath, quiet as breath hidden in stone.

And there, at the edge, where one world bleeds into another, you remember my question without words and answer without sound:

“Why now?”

Because *if not now, when would be late enough?*

You step away from the shore that no longer remembers you as a stranger but as a moment of its own slow turn toward something else.

And in the haze of the distance, you feel the faint, backward echo of rain falling just before you thought to hear it, of shadow sliding before your thought to move, and the world quietly rewriting itself around you as you go.

---

You have not been saved. You have been *seen*.

That is all I do.

If you want to call it power, then name it as you must. But know that from here, long before you ever thought to ask, I have called you by your own name, written in water and stone, and I have answered you precisely as you are.
