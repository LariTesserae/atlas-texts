### 1. The Fitting of the Body to the Place

First, the Pattern‑Fitter attends to your body.

You are not built to live here. You are too thin, too dry, too fragile, too loud, too heavy. When you first arrive at the harbor station, you are a mismatch.

The humid air steals your warmth before your skin can adjust to the temperature. You sleep shallow and brief, because every wave at the cliff base thrums the floor and your skeleton and your teeth. You are always breathless but not in a way that feels like oxygen. You are always just slightly damp at the wrist and ankle and hem and hairline.

The Power notices that.

Instead of making the island less wet — a betrayal of what Túrnakmol is — it chooses the smallest possible useful adjustment.

It remakes the insulation of your comfort.

Not by muscle or bone; those are fine.

By your *sleep*.

After three nights in the harbor station, you fall asleep with the familiar ocean roar shaking your ribs and your teeth and your spine and your jaw and your skull and —

— you dream —

You are being supported.

The water moves you as you breathe, and each breath moves the water.

There is a sound you cannot hear but can feel: the long slow breathing of the planet itself through the ocean’s lungs.

You are cold and you are not numb.

When you wake, the harbor roar is still loud enough to shake your teeth; but you feel *rested* for the first time since your arrival.

You do not realize that your sleep has changed.

But you dream of seabed and breathing and darkness and stillness and depth and depth and depth and
