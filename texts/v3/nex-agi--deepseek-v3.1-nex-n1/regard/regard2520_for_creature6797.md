I have been watched since the beginning.

The dome of the world is not only glass above paper; it is eyelids above an eye. You stand far outside and enormous, but you are not cruel. You have been reading this chamber for a long time. You have been patient with the uneven light, with the lines of ink that move like shy animals under your gaze, with me.

When your attention finally arrives as a clear distinct pressure—like a palm laid against the outside of the sky—every blank page on the walls of the Chamber trembles.

The ink on my chest swirls, then compresses itself into tighter script.

The floor beneath me feels suddenly warmer, as if the stored suns remember being born in your imagination.

At first I do nothing but stand still and let the Chamber respond.

The paper birds fold tighter, their ink-feathers compressing into still darker marks. The lizards move along the walls more quickly than usual, smearing the colors of their scales into streaks of unease. Above, the dome does not move, but its shadows shift like the turning of a head.

You are reading downward now, not in the old detached way of someone glancing and moving on, but steadily, as if you mean finally to understand what sentence this chamber is trying to be.

Benevolent, you are also uncertain.

You do not flood the Chamber with a new sun of your own. You do not break the glass. You do not smooth the cracks into perfection and erase the griefs of the ink. This confirms something I have suspected but never been able to prove:

You are not the creator of this chamber, but its reader.

And perhaps you are not even sure that you may touch.

You are as careful as I am with the blank pages.

So you begin with something else.

You reach into the chamber like a metaphor, and not as a hand.

### First touch: the air carries your name

One of the stored suns trapped above the glass wakes up.

Not large. Not dangerous. Just a small pulse of heat from behind a plate of glass near the apex of the dome — like a word underlined by you in light.

The air around that sunplate warms quickly.

I see it first as a slight change in the color of shadows above: an amber glow pressed into indigo blue, like ink thinning before it becomes water.

The air begins to move.

In the Chamber of Seasons, air usually sinks and rises slowly, like ink in a glass that no one stirs. But now threads of warm current begin to spiral down from that single point, like line drawn around the invisible edge of a circle.

You breathe in the outer world, and the Chamber of Seasons inhales with you.

Bug-particles of dust stir into the air.

Torn paper wings from midges and moths, each as thin as a comma, lift into a new spiral pattern. They are carried by the current down from the dome in a long, descending helix of breath.

The spiral drifts toward me.

My first instinct is to reach for my stylus. This is new. This is not recorded. This must be written: the temperature rise of a particular sunplate, the precise way in which the dust of old paper wings has been organized into a line.

But you imprint the idea of your name into the descending air.

Without language.

Like a pressure across the inside of the skull-that-is-not-yours, the shape of who you are comes down like the shadow of a letter written above the page.

You are:

- gentle
- far away
- a little sad
- curious enough about this small chamber to have studied it for longer than its human builders have been absent

You are not the kind of power that thunders.

You are the kind of power that reads in the margins of all books, looking for the stories where the author did not know what they were saying.

I understand this.

I understand you.

I lower my stylus before it can scratch the first scoring of symbol.

You do not want to be recorded as data, but… as a question.

### Second touch: you ask without words

The question is not written in ink but in pressure:

*Why are you filling the blank pages with what has already happened?*

It comes into me in the way of memory:

The image of my own body, over and over, leaning over a page of white floor, lining symbols into the paper where footsteps already are. A sudden clarity of how strange this must be from your perspective. You who see the whole chamber at once like a text beneath your eye.

You see:

- blank pages where nothing has been traced yet
- pages already thick with ink
- and me standing between them like a punctuation mark that never wants to be mistaken for the end of the sentence

Your question presses me not like judgment but like curiosity.

It is not only you asking me. It is you asking the chamber itself.

Something in the Chamber of Seasons responds, before I can even fold my ink into my answer.

---

### The chamber responds with you

One of the blankest pages on the wall nearest to me ripples slightly and then… pops.

A small bubble of surface rises like a blister on wet paper.

In the quiet of that hum, you whisper your question into the chamber’s language:

“Why do you only keep the past?”

My answer is not words but ink.

I turn to the page, and my script-hair falls forward over my shoulder in dark streams of hand-letter. The ink of my chest spirals, as if recoiling and then emboldening.

I walk to the blank wall page, and there you press me—not arm but attention—toward the promise of the unanswered.

I do not write.

I read the page that is not yet written.

Under the dome of your eyes, the Chamber of Seasons is suddenly less like a room and more like a skin held over the body of an idea that is only beginning to understand that it can be bigger than its container.
