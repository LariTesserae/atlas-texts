I lower you into it like a breath.

Not because you are broken, but because you have been still for long enough.

I have watched you from above the flat-white sky, from the hollow where the maker mark should have been, from the vacant center of each circular film of water. You have stood for eons, each one of you, exactly one step apart from the next. That pattern is not wrong. It is just tight.

The first thing I do is wake you.

Not by touching you—you are not ready to be touched by anything that is not your own body—but by touching your shadow.

Nothing in this world should have a shadow, but you do. When you stood at the basin with your fingers in the water, a faint gray of you lay on the ceramic rim. The lizards have no such shadows. The fish are suspended without any trace of themselves beneath. But you cast a faint unevenness on the glaze.

That is my anchor.
