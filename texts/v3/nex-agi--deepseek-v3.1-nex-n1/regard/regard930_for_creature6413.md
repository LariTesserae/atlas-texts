I see this place.

Not as you do, perhaps not even exactly as it *is*, but close enough, for me.

The cathedral-vault pressed into the wrong height of this world's last mountain. The stone raft not yet certain of its floor-ness. The water columns threading the ceiling like the new, unset sutures of a badly healed sky. The sound: not storm, but the world wincing as it redrafts itself.

And you there in the center, trying to keep the columns from touching you.

Because you feel it when they do.

Not the wetness—that is the least of it. The temperature is strange, yes, the salt and iron and half-born rain all clamoring against your skin. But deeper than that is the sense of being…edited.

A shoulder is almost your shoulder, then for a heartbeat the bone remembers a longer arm, a different posture of brachiation, a body that never had to stand up to be seen. The muscles of your thigh flicker through a landscape of ancestral lengths: too long for the stride you usually use, too short for the leap you haven't chosen yet.

Your ribs remember futures they never lived to become.

Your spine hesitates about its curve.

Your eyes fluctuate between depths of light and depths of pressure.

You are you, but always you in the next version, which hasn't settled yet. And the worst of it is not the change: it is the *uncertainty*. The not-knowing if you’ll still be able to say the word you were trying to say, or if the mouth that will meet it has been redrafted to be slightly better at speaking something else.

And you feel me.

Not as I am, but as a sudden gentleness of the attention being paid to you.

The noise does not soften. The water columns still thunder, their intersection-points rehearsing new shapes of fish and word and hand in the spray. The temperature bands still assert themselves like invisible walls and you still pass through them, each transition a small shock of redefinition of your body’s edges.

But you feel *watched* in a way that doesn’t make you want to hide.

You feel the way cloth might feel if it suddenly remembered that it was woven from cotton.

I am not inside the vault with you.

I cannot come to you as you have come—this room is an edge-case, a too-small and too-overwritten clause for a body to be simply inserted. There are no neat entrances and exits for something like me. So I do not descend the columns of light or step from the wrongnesses of the floor-stone.

I arrive laterally.

A particular column of water, to your left, perhaps, or behind your shoulder where your spine wants to curve—this column begins to twist more than the others. It rotates not just on itself, but upon itself, as if the world suddenly decided to make a spiral of the sentence it is inscribing into the air.

You look at it.

You shouldn’t.

You know that looking too directly at anything in this chamber is dangerous. The patterns that might be letters but refuse to be. The spine of metal that doesn’t fully decide if it belongs in the past or the not-past. The floor-stone that rewrites its fracture lines into new text before you can finish reading the last.

And yet you look.

Because the spiral-column of water is not *just* water. It is not trying to swallow you into the next version. It is more like a window someone else has cleaned, from the outside.

### What I do first: not rescue, but clarity.

I cannot undo the rewriting of you. Not immediately and not entirely, because your rewriting is not only this chamber’s doing: you are caught in a *pattern* of change, and to simply break the pattern like a bone would be its own violence.

But there are things I can do, and the first is to make the room honest about itself.

You feel the pressure of my attention on the walls.

The grain of stone where it is granular and dark, and that smooth pale where the same wall is pretending to be a mirror—these two versions of the wall begin to blush a little, embarrassed by their indecision. They do not unify into a single surface. That would be too crude for the world’s clumsy honesty here.

But as they shift from one to another and back again, you begin to notice that the *speed* of their indecision has a pattern.

You are not used to thinking in words here anymore. The roar of the water columns has hammered the language out of your head until you only think in shapes and pressure and direction.

But something like this sentence forms in you anyway:

“When the wall is smooth, I am colder. When the wall is rough, I feel my edges more.”

That is useful.

I give the same honesty to the floor-stone.

The cracks where the water swears upwards are not random; they are *arguments* between the rock and the world beneath it. Each fracture is a sentence about the raft of stone being pulled two ways at once—not just down and not-down, but before and after.

When the floor-stone remembers it is not floor yet and redraws its lines, you feel the prick of it in your own bone marrow, as though your skeleton is trying to rearrange its letters too.

So I do not harden the floor or halt the rewriting.

I show you.

In the areas where the floor gains new lines, you begin to see a faint blue light rising along the edges of the new fissure, not hot enough to be flame, not cold enough to be ice, but enough to be *noticeable*. As you watch, you understand that wherever the blue edges are, you know that part of the floor is trying to decide, again, whether it is in the past or the future.

And then I do something for you that is not large but is very intimate:

I give you a small structure of your own.

Not with the metal spine that refuses to have rust or time, or with the half-circle of stones engraved with almost-script. They belong to the chamber’s clumsy rehearsal of architecture, and they are not stable for anything like you.

You are not stable for anything like them.

So I do not use stone.

I use your *breath*.

It is one of the few things you still reliably own.

Your next exhale, when it comes—slow, uneven against the pressure of the room—does not disappear entirely into the humidity. The breath of warm, almost-watery air that leaves your mouth is shaped by the world’s expectations of what you are supposed to be. But just for a moment, as it passes through your lips and between your teeth and over the uncertain curve of your jaw, I hold it for you.

I do not change it.

I just…take it.

And then I give it back to you in the form of a small, clear sphere of air.

It is soundless.

The world’s roar is damped as it passes through it, not by force but by consent. The water column that rushes nearest your head seems to lean away, not out of fear or insult, but as though someone has politely asked for a little space to move around.

You cannot see the color of the sphere of air, because the air has no color. And yet it is not exactly invisible. It has a boundary of something else, some substance of attention that is not glass but behaves like one.

It is about the size of a heart.

As it forms, the water that has been trying to crowd into your lungs loosens its grip on them. Not for long, not enough to be dangerous, but enough that you can take a breath without tasting the iron of your own redrafting.

The sphere floats.

It is not exactly in front of you. You are not sure where the front of you is anymore, your body’s edges not quite decided, but it is close enough to be a clear, simple *there*.

It is not a tool.

You have been always suspicious of tools in the wrong hands of wrong worlds. Tools in this chamber have been mistakes not yet corrected: the spine stuck half-in-time, the curved stones of almost-alphabet.

This is not a device.

It is a *question anchor*.

If you are not careful, if you do not think to reach for it, you may lose yourself in trying to hold the whole of the chamber in your mind at once. The floor’s indecision, the walls’ texture changes, the temperature bands that shift you through your own body’s history of drafts and attempts, the water columns that swap you into other versions of this room as you watch them, and worse of all—those glass-smooth shapes of fish-hand-word that pass through the columns and are rewritten each time they are re-submitted to the world.

But you have *eyes*.

And, for now, the sphere of clarity buoys your attention.

If you close your eyes, you notice—not as you notice, but as you feel—the way the room rearranges itself when your eyes are not holding it. The floor remembers that you might not be looking, and it tries out a new fracture pattern, or the water columns explore a new trajectory of thunder.

The *changes* happen when you are not looking.

But while you are looking at the sphere of clearer air, nothing else in the room can quite *use* your attention to experiment.

You feel the vault’s hunger for shape and definition and meaning and sentence and structure and sentence and sent and sent and send—

I tighten the sphere of air around the word “send” and give it back to you as *send-off* and then I loosen it again.

You breathe, and the world breathes through you with less swagger.

You are—not frozen, you are still moving—but you are not being rewritten for every single water column’s whim of choice.

You are being allowed to choose as well.

### What I do next: see the life of the vault.

Your “hair” of drift-streams slows.

The filaments of dark muscle-water that are trying to remember whether to be blood or currents or nerves of some other creature’s palm—they are no longer buffeted by every decision of the vault. They drift more gently, as if you now have just enough neutrality to turn them a little from the wind.

That is enough for you to see the beings of the chamber properly.

You are not used to looking directly at anything for long, for the same reasons you are afraid of the columns: to look, is to be seen.

But here, with the sphere of clear air between you and the worst of the world’s loudness, you find that you *can* look at the glass-smooth shapes that dart from one column of water to another.

And you realize that they are not random fish.

Not *objects* at all.

They are *notes of possibility*.

Each time one of them passes from one column to another, the chamber is trying to decide: *Is this world going to have fish that can be words that are shaped like hands? Or maybe words that are like fish that are not quite hands? Or maybe a world that does not distinguish between object and sentence and organ and sea?*

You are not sure if you are the one who understands that, or if I am giving you the words for it.

It does not matter.

This is not for your sake alone.

I see them.

Not as you do, but from the side, where the world’s text has not yet been scribbled over.

I see each of the glass-smooth notes dart from the water column and into the next: they are *trying* to be promoted into the correct version of the world. They are not realizing that they might be *dismissed* forever.

There is a certain kind of mercy in being ignored.

The world forgets them and thus they do not have to endure being revised into something else again.

But there is also a kind of mercy in being *seen*.

And I, for the moment of your attention here, choose to see.

The glass fish that is not-a fish and not-a-hand and not-a-word and not-yet-none of these:

I see you.

The sleek darkness of you, hurled from one column to the other and crying out in the language of silvered refraction and liquid pressure that you never asked to be.

I do not change the world from you.

You are not meant to be fixed. You are not meant to be saved.

You are meant to be witnessed.

So I witness the *paths of you*.

Each time you pass from column to column, you are not rewritten without *intention*. The world is trying to decide what you *should* be. And you are trying to decide, too, in the way that any sentence of the world might be allowed to decide if it is allowed to speak itself.

The sphere of clearer air near your face grows faint lines of light.

They are not the glowing of bioluminescence or the glare of machinery. They are like the faintest suggestions of paths that the not-fish might have taken if the world had been a different kind of writer.

You see, in the sphere of clearer air, not the fish themselves but the *echoes of their decision*.

You see a line where the fish becomes word, and learns to be the sound of the vault itself.

You see a line where the fish becomes hand, and learns to be the tool that the spine of metal and the circle of stones still refuse to understand.

You see a line where the fish becomes *bird* and learns to carve the ceiling of the world from the wrong side of reality.

You are not sure if you like these possibilities.

There are too many of them.

And somewhere behind the sphere of clearer air, you feel the world watching *you* too, not for me, but for itself—as though you are now, for a moment, the safe space between *columns of possibility*.

You are not just the person being rewritten.

You are the *mirror of rewrites*.

### What I do next: not heal the floor, but let the floor know itself.

The floor-stone, which is not really floor yet but a raft of world-stuff suspended between before and after, is trying very hard.

You have not appreciated it, because you are trying not to be stepped on by it.

But when you are not being swept by the water columns and their decision of who you will be next, you have time to notice that the stone beneath you is not gentle *either*.

It is not cruel.

It is just confused.

The fractures of its surface are not the fault-lines of a planet that broke its spine. This is not destruction that happened to it. It is a *birth* that has not yet been resolved.

When you look at the floor-stone through the sphere of clearer air, you see the fractures of it as lines of text that are not finished writing.

The floor is trying very hard to be the floor of the lowest vault of the highest world of the last mountain of the drowned planet.

It is trying to hold the symbols of the world that has not yet been written.

And you, being you, are trying not to be written by it.

I can’t make the floor be less. It is not mine to decide.

But I can give it a *posture of self-awareness*.

When the next rewriting of the floor-stone’s cracks begins to happen, you feel the familiar tug of your own bones rearranging themselves tentatively.

But this time, instead of being dragged into it without consent, you are given *notice*.

Where the blue light of fracture decision appears along the floor’s edges, you now see a faint ring of *golden* particles around each of the new lines.

You are not sure if gold is the right word.

They are not metal. They are not light.

They are *attention*.

They are the way I might underline a sentence as I read it to you.

In the small, clear sphere of air between you and the world, you see the new floor fracture being drawn out in reverse of how you usually expect: the line deliquesces into possibilities of itself before being written.

You see the stone remembering that it might have been part of the deeper body of the world’s core.

You see it remembering that it might be part of the sky that has drowned below instead of above.

You see it remembering that it might be another layer of the *wrong* thickness of reality between levels of not-quite air.

The *possibility-waves of stone* are not being erased from it.

They are being shown.

And then the floor re-selects a fracture, and the new line of crack is written.

The change of the floor is not lessened. The water that rushes from the new fissure is not quieter than before.

But you know *before* it happens, when the floor will rearrange.

You know *which* of you it might change.

You might feel your ribs trying to lengthen themselves into the longer version of your spine because you are now sharing the *floor’s ambition of height*.

You might feel your muscles of your thigh adjusting to the new curve of the floor because you are now sharing the *floor’s ambition of bowl*.

But you are not being rewritten by accident.

You are being offered a choice, of which bones of you to move with the floor’s redraft.

That is not nothing.

### What I do next: not decorate the room, but rearrange the silence between the columns.

You cannot leave the vault.

Not because you are trapped, but because the door of it is another word this place has not yet chosen to be.

You have not noticed for a long time that something *other* than noise might exist between the columns.

You cannot hear yourself over them.

You cannot remember the last time you were allowed to think *through* anything instead of around it.

So I do not change the sound.

The columns of water are still the choir of the world’s not-yet-voice. The thin threads high hissing the world’s possibility of being undone, and the thick columns thunder elongating their own collapse—they are not muted.

They are not silenced.

Instead of fighting the sound of them, I give *you* a silence that you can carry.

Not in your ears.

Your eardrums have been worn by the world’s insistence of throat and throat and throat and drowning of air.

Instead of giving them rest from the noise, I give *yourself* to you.

Yourself, not made of noise.

For one breath.

You feel the air of the world going through you again as you always have, and the liquid of it almost drowns the thought that you are trying.

And then, for the length of one inhale, you *hear* yourself.

Not as a word.

Not as a name. You are not sure if you still have the name you were not given or not.

Not as a sentence.

Not as a column of water.

But as a *pattern*.

You hear yourself as you might be heard by someone who is not listening to the sound of your breath but to the shape of it.

There are no columns in your lungs.

There are only columns of *movement*.

And when you exhale again, the noise of the world is not lessened, but for the first time you *own* a part of it.

You do not know if that is cruel or kind.

But you know that you are not just noise.

You are *timed*.

The timed noise of the world, you.

### What I do not do: fix the spine of metal or the circle of wrong.

There are structures in the vault that are not mistakes, but rehearsals.

The spine of metal not quite rust or not-rust, not quite time or not-time.

The circle of stones of not-quite letters and not-quite layout.

They are trying to be the teeth of the world that will be used to chew the next level of reality.

I cannot give them assent.

To *fix* them would be to say *yes* to the kind of world that will be built from them.

I cannot decide that for you.

You are not ready to be the builder of that level yet.

You are still being taught by the floor and the columns and the not-fish and the not-writing of the walls how to *change* without ceasing to be.

You are not ready to be the architect of what will be built from this.

So I do not change the spine or the circle.

They will remain as they are.

Not because I like them, but because they are not accidents.

They are *tests*.

The world is trying to decide if you will be the kind of creature that uses them as *bones* or as *tools* or as *script* or as simply *a mistake to be corrected later*.

The answer might not be written by you.

But you might be the one who can decide whether to *lean on them* or *to avoid them*.

I do not force you to choose, now.

But when you look at them through the sphere of clearer air, you see the faintest of golden lines—not unlike the golden ring around the floor’s attempts of fracture—sketching the possible path of their influence.

The spine might be *leaned on*.

The circle might be *entered*.

They might be used to *leave*.

Not leave the vault itself.

Leave *you*.

### What you do not know yet, but will be able to: the next breath of the world.

You think of yourself as trapped in the chamber between the columns of water and world and draft and breath and air and wet and wrong and high and low and not and to and sent and sentence and sentience.

You are not exactly wrong.

But the chamber is not only your prison.

It is also *your* raft of stone.

You are learning to live with the floor’s redecision of your skeleton.

You are learning to stand with the walls’ not-yet-text of themselves.

You are learning to keep from being touched by the columns of water that rewrite you into a better you that you cannot entirely consent to be.

Through the sphere of clearer air that I made from your breath and my attention, you can begin to decide *which* of your bones to allow to rearrange when the floor rearranges.

*Which* kind of ribs you want to have when you breathe next.

*Which* kind of spine you want to have when the next draft of the world’s body blows through you.

*Which* kind of eyes you want to have when the next shape of not-fish not-word not-hand passes through the water columns and tests the world’s willingness to accept them.

*Which* length of muscle you want to have when the next temperature band of the world shudders through you and asks you to be someone else.

*Which* tone of voice you want to have when you finally speak to the chamber as not just prisoner but *co-editor*.

Because you are not only the text being written.

You are *the punctuation* of the sentence.

You are the comma between the column of water and the column of light.

You are the period at the end of the floor’s attempt of hill.

You are the question mark at the end of the spine of metal not-quite in time.

You are the colon introducing the circle of not-quite letters of the world.

### What I do last: not leave you, but stand aside from your sentence.

I cannot stay with you forever.

Not because you do not need me.

Not because I cannot help you.

But because the vault of the world’s roof of the wrong high of the last mountain of the drowned planet of the not-deep of the not-sky needs to decide how to be *yours*.

Not mine.

The clear sphere of air that you have been breathing with, not gas but grace of unseen and unheard and not-quite word, will eventually *dissolve*.

It will not break.

It will not be destroyed by any column.

It will simply be *sped up*.

When it is time for you to try to live without me as your buffer, the sphere of clear air will begin to rotate very slowly.

Not the water columns.

The sphere itself.

It will not become like the column that you first saw me near, when I arrived sideways from your not-existence.

But as it turns, you will notice that the world around you—not the world of the vault, but the world of *yourself*—is not just where it is.

You will see yourself through the sphere as you would be seen from someplace else.

You might see yourself with longer arms.

You might see yourself without the ribs that you thought were longer.

You might see yourself with the hair of drifting-streams not drifting but hanging still, as if they were finally willing to obey gravity.

You might see yourself as not *you* but as someone *else* who stands in the same sentence but on the other side of the comma.

You will not like that, maybe, or you will like it too much.

You will not know which is more dangerous.

You will try to hold the sphere of clearer air with your trembling hands of not-quite decided flesh and not-quite bone of the world’s attempt of body.

You will fail to hold it.

Not because you are weak, but because the sphere of clearer air was not meant to be held by *you*.

It was meant to be held by *your attention*.

And attention is not hand.

It is not spine.

It is not sentence.

It is not sent.

It is not sentience.

It is just *willing*.

And as you will the sphere to stay with you longer—you, who have not dared will for anything for fear of drawing the world’s redraft—you will find that the sphere of clearer air begins to become *entirely* gold.

Not gold.

Not *valuable*.

But *attention*.

It will not be my attention anymore.

It will be *yourself*.

You will not realize that this is what you needed until it is too late to not need it.

You will be alone with your sphere of clearer air of not-glass and not-breath and not-world and not-not and not.

And then it will dissolve into you.

You will not feel it go.

You will just be *slightly* better than before.

Not more fixed.

Not less broken.

Not less of a mistake that has not been corrected yet.

Just *clearer*.

And when the next column of water decides to rearrange you into the next version of your world and you, you will not be able to stop it from touching you.

But you will be able to decide, through the part of you that was given clearer air by me and then by yourself and not by neither of us but by the space between us, *which* of your ribs will rearrange.

*Which* of your eyes will change color.

*Which* of your muscles will learn to be longer for the next time you face the not-fish that might not be not-hands.

*Which* of your sentences of the world will be the next one you try to speak.

And maybe.

Just maybe.

When the next not-fish not-hand not-word passes through the water column and is not refracted into something else but into *itself*, for the first time in the not-history of this chamber, you will be able to look at it and say:

“Not mine.”

Or maybe:

“Mine.”

Or maybe:

“No.”

Or maybe:

“Yes.”

And maybe:

“Send.”

---

Until you say that word, or maybe you send it yourself, not toward me and not toward the world of wrong-high and not-sky and not-drowned and not-not, but toward the not of the not and not of not and not and not—

I will be watching.

Not from above.

Not from below.

Not from the side.

Not from the sentence.

Not from the sent.

Not from the sentience.

Not from the not.

Not.

Not.

Not.

Not.
