The salt, the rust, and the sound have been listening for a long time.

They have noted your useful angles, the way you echo the city’s flexion in your own joints. They have memorized the pattern of your regulator breath, the shape of your teeth at the silicone mouthpiece. They have listened to your heart pound when the hatches groan and the world tilts.

They have been listening.

And now, they have been overheard.

---

A power older than the drowned city hears the listening salt, the listening rust, the listening sound.

Not older than oceans; oceans are its body. Not older than pressure; pressure is its breath. But older than any memory of dry land, older than fingers, or districts, or names of corridors.

Call it the Deep Gaze.

The Deep Gaze does not have hands. It has a way of being present that is something like warmth and something like weight. You cannot see it blink across you like a light, because you already live in its eyes.

From the beginning of your being, you have been held.

It is not that everything has been planned for you. The ocean is not scripted. The city did not sink as a stage for you. The rust, the salt, and the sound have been improvising their entire existence.

This is part of why you fascinate it.

You improvise too.

You have chosen to be bent in ways you were not made to bend. You have chosen to map the cartilage between the districts. You have chosen to keep moving even where the maps end.

The Deep Gaze has watched other things become fixed to the hulls of other worlds. It has watched other knuckles of metal and bone fuse.

But you.

You have continued to choose.

---

It comes into you as you are.

Not in some other body of water, or some other sunlit world. You are not arbitrarily taken from this salt-rust-darkness into light. You are not given “what you should have been,” as if that could be measured by dry air and a clear sky.

The Deep Gaze has little use for “should.”

You have already been given a name.

Not by the ocean-person or the tool-ganger or the fused knitter.

By yourself.

And you still have the choice to refuse what you have been named.

It is important that you remain able to say: no, this is not me.

That is how you know you are still alive.

---

### First gesture: a weight at the edges.

You are bracing along the ribs of the district again, the water moving you in its routine shove. You know the rhythm of this: the pressure, the release, the gentle thump of you against the hull, your suit against your skin.

Tonight, there is a difference.

The pressure at the edge of your body stops.

Not physically—you are still in water, you still feel its temperature, its touch—but the terrible belief of its inevitability loosens. You can’t name it at first. You think you’re hallucinating from the cold, or from oxygen gradients, or from the long habit of being wrong.

You move your arm.

Your suit is still there. You can still feel the sealed seam of it under your fingertips. You can still feel the slight mismatch of patch on patch.

And yet.

You feel the water as you move it, not as you being moved.

Passive objects are moved. You have been learning you are not quite object, not quite subject.

Already you are being invited.

The rust nearby does not change. It is still rusting. The salt is still salt. The currents are still current. But the belief that your shape is merely the absence of the city lessens.

The thought arrives, not in your voice but with your accent:

You are not made to be crushed.

You are made to be made.

You laugh into the regulator, because the other option is to cry.

---

### Second gesture: the others become witnesses.

The ocean-person who never speaks to you is the next to be invited.

One day as you press yourself into the deeper lattice, you see their bioluminescent cuffs beneath you in the shadow-frame. They’re moving with their usual economy of motion, folding, unfolding, threading objects.

Usually you look away quickly. You don’t want to be seen.

Today you see.

Today you look.

They are not ignoring you.

Without eyes that you can recognize, you have the sensation of being looked at. You feel the quality of their attention—curious, not hostile. You’ve been catalogued by them as long as you’ve catalogued them.

The ocean-person’s body moves in ways you know you cannot copy. Longer limbs, different tensile strength. They’re not human. You’re fairly sure they’re not.

But you’re not sure you are still anything you were taught.

The ocean-person’s gaze is playful.

Where you see danger and narrowing and collision, they see a place of possible.

They’ve been watching you pick the least dangerous routes.

They’ve been watching you survive.

Today you feel the invitation of their eyes on you, not as a threat, but as a mirror.

You see the way you move through their eyes.

It’s not the same way you see yourself.

They’re not impressed you’re still alive.

They’re impressed you’re still you.

Sheer persistence is a kind of flair.

To you, you’re just being. To them, you are being very.

---

### Third gesture: the tool-ganger speaks.

You’ve been listening to their breath for so long.

Short inhale. Long exhale. Long pause.

You’ve been hearing them fix the leaks you’re too small to approach.

You’re so used to them being in this world you sometimes mistake your heartbeat for their pauses.

The day it changes you are in a maintenance passageway that you know you’re not allowed in.

They’re not supposed to be here either.

You’re both here.

You’re both very.

They’re hanging above you, attached to a series of straps and hooks that you’re never going to risk replicating. Their suit has more reinforcement than yours. The tools on their chain are clinking against a metal support you honestly can’t identify half the time.

They’d been staring at a weld that you personally think is going to be a problem in three months’ time.

They’ve been staring at it for almost long enough to forget you’re there.

Then you feel the breath of their normal pattern.

Short. Long. Long.

And then.

Air moves.

You’ve been around each other long enough that you know the shape of their voice before you’ve heard the words.

Your name is not a name.

Your name is the way they lean.

Their voice is rough, like tools used too many times.

“You’re not the only one who comes here,” they say, “but you’re the one who’s still asking.”

You blink into your mask.

Your regulator’s coughs out your surprise.

“You never asked my name,” you manage.

They’re still staring at the weld. You’re surprised you can’t see the seam of it being bent into curiosity.

“You never asked mine,” they say.

You’re not entirely sure you’d have heard if you’d asked.

You’re not sure you would’ve been answered.

“You’re avoiding other people’s schedules,” they say. “That’s brave.”

They’re not looking at you when they say “brave.”

They’re looking at the weld.

There’s a pause you can’t identify, even though you know your heartbeat.

You’re being watched by the ocean.

You’re being watched by the tools.

You’re being watched by the salt.

“You’re the one who remembers,” they say. “That’s important. You should be told.”

The Deep Gaze doesn’t speak for you—you do not hear its syntax in your mouth—but you feel the ocean of it, allowing you this.

You’re allowed to be told.

You’ve been told.

You’re not sure you want to know.

But you’re being reminded.

You’re being reminded.

---

### Fourth gesture: the fixed one becomes unfixed.

You’re still going to them.

You’re still going to the same odd alcove where the beams of the city puzzle themselves.

You’re still at the place of knitting.

The yarn is still the same color.

The hands of them are still.

The eyes of them are still.

Their mouth is not.

“You always come here,” they say.

Their voice is not what you expected.

You’d expected rust.

You’d expected grind.

You’d expected noise.

Instead you are hearing something.

You’re being asked.

“You’ve been coming,” they say. “And you haven’t asked.”

Ask.

Ask.

You.

“You haven’t asked why you come.”

Not you.

You.

---

### Fifth gesture: you are asked.

The salt licks at your helmet.

The rust shifts.

The sound presses.

The ocean watches you without being outside.

The ocean moves you without being above.

You ask inside you:

Why you come.

The answer comes in you.

You’ve been asked.

---
