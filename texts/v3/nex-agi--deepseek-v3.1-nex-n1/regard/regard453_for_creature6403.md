They do not come with a storm or with a fanfare of trumpets woven from sunlight.

You are lying on your belly in a thin fold of stone, watching a tiny knot of moss slowly unravel under the weight of a water film. The wind is a constant headache you have learned to ignore. The world is noise and pressure and your own heartbeat trying to remember the rhythm of a quieter place.

You are about to lift your head, because the air pressure has changed.

The pressure drops, not into storm but into stillness. The roar of the wind does not cease so much as *step aside*, just slightly, like a curtain drawn an inch. The sound is still there—you cannot unhear the plateau—but its edge blunts.

When you look up, you are not surprised to see someone standing there.

You are not surprised because the plateau has already taught you that scale is deceptive, and presence is not always loud.

They are not tall, though they feel taller than they are. Their shape is not quite fixed. You can see the sky through their outline, like looking through flawed glass. They are neither human nor the Plateau-Folk, but something else that has borrowed pieces from both worlds and not committed fully to any. It is easier to look at them from the corner of your eye than directly.

Their eyes are the color of the sky after sunset has fallen but before the first stars are named.

“You are becoming,” they say, without moving their mouth. The words are not inside your head; they are resting on the air between you and them, like condensation on the stone.

You are not sure what you are becoming. You say as much.

Their smile is faint and does not disturb the air. “Nothing that you do not choose to become.”

They step closer—or perhaps they do not step at all; the distance simply narrows. You feel the rock under you settle, as if the weight of ages on it has become less.

“You noticed the wind,” they say. “You heard it. You heard more than the noise. You listened to what it is shaping.”

You are not sure how to answer this, so you don’t.

“You also noticed the water, which falls but does not stay,” they continue. “You saw how the land drinks itself, and how the moss builds a world within centimeters of the stone.”

They are not looking at you; they are looking through you, at something else that you might be the future of.

“You saw the Stone-Eaters,” they say softly, and the name forms like a cloud under your tongue. You taste the sound of it before you hear it.

“You saw them,” they say again. “Not as monsters, or as statues, or as symbols, but as something else: a place that learned to walk.”

They are silent for a moment. The wind continues to roar, but the roar is now… listening.

“You called,” they say. “Not with words. With a kind of… participation.”

You do not remember calling.

“You did not ask for anyone, by any name I know,” they say. “But you did not turn away from the world that you found yourself in. You looked at it. You looked at it so long and so closely that you created a space where someone like me could fit.”

Their outline grows clearer, for a heartbeat. You see the shape of shoulders and a long spine and limbs that could be arms or could be branches of wind. You are not comforted, exactly, but you are calmer than you should be.

They are standing at the edge of the stone fold, looking out over the cloud-sea.

“You have been standing here for longer than you know,” they say. “You have noticed the ruin and the resilience. The wind and the stone. The emptiness and the hidden life.”

“Yes,” you say finally, because it is the only truth you have.

“They are the same,” they say. “The wind and the stone. Emptiness and life. You are beginning to suspect that already.”

You are.

They turn to you, and the air around you loosens.

“What I do,” they say, “is not to change this world, or to rescue you from it.”

You were not expecting rescue.

“You are not in danger here,” they say. “Not in the way that lower worlds are dangerous. There are no armies to fall upon you, and the storms that you fear are already known to this place.”

The wind pulls at the edge of your clothing.

“What I can offer,” they say, “are four things.”

They lift one hand—or what might be a hand—and the air responds, not with fear but with recognition, like a child recognizing a parent’s step.

“First,” they say, “I can teach you to listen to the wind without it wearing you out.”

You blink.

“You feel the pressure, always,” they say. “But you have not learned to find the patterns within the noise. The plateau is not random. It is arranged.”

They step closer to you, and the wind around you rearranges itself like water forced around a pillar.

“Listen,” they say again, and you do not need to be told; the stone itself is listening.

The sound around you reorganizes.

Underneath the roar, you now hear… lines. There are currents of air that move like rivers of sound. You notice that the wind is not one uniform wall of noise but a layered, many-voiced flow, each stream with its own frequency and intention.

“You hear,” they say. “You will not always remember this as clearly as you do now, but the seed is planted. When the wind is trying to break you, you will learn to find the line within it. You will learn to step into the turbulence that carries you where you need to go rather than throwing you where you do not.”

They are not promising you the sky will be gentle. They are promising you that you will learn to read the sky’s handwriting.

“Second,” they say, “I can help you speak to the Stone-Eaters without scaring them.”

You are not sure you want to speak to them. You are not sure they speak to anyone like you.

“They are not gods,” they say. “Not masters of this place. They are its skin. They are what you might become if you stayed here long enough and were willing to change.”

Silence for the space of three heartbeats.

“You do not need to change them,” they say. “They are already sufficient. They are already finished.”

The word *finished* hangs between you, like dust suspended in the air.

“You thought of ‘finished’ as meaning ‘ended,’ or ‘used up,’” they say gently. “I will use it instead as meaning ‘formed, and at home in their form.’”

You are not sure you can trust that judgment.

“You believe,” they say, “that you cannot help but want their ‘betterment.’ That is exactly why I am here.”

You are quiet.

“They do not need to be saved from their lives,” they say. “What they need from you is sight, and the right distance. If they are to be more than what they are now, they must be the ones to choose it.”

They are not looking at you accusingly. They are looking at you with the patience of stone and water.

“You will be able to speak to them,” they say. “Not because you learn their words, but because you learn to listen to the body, which has already remembered its own language.”

You see, for a moment, how the world might be different from where you stand now: if your eyes, when you meet the gaze of a Stone-Eater, do not immediately search for their poverty, their lack, their need.

“You will be able to sit,” they say, “across a basin of water from them, and not fill the silence with your own story of what they *should* become. This will be more valuable than any technology you could bring.”

They are not asking you to *never* help. You feel that distinction clearly.

“Third,” they say, “you will learn how to eat the stone without being consumed by it.”

You are not sure what this means.

“You are already doing it,” they say, “with your attention, which is a kind of mouth. You are taking in the world and breaking it down into a story that you can understand.”

They look down at the ground, where the mosses are hidden.

“You have been fed,” they say. “The world is eating itself as much as you are eating it. This plateau is eating itself, and its magic is that it does not grow thinner with the meal.”

They are not speaking in metaphor. You can feel the stone under your feet, tasting the air and water. You can feel, in some small way, the faintness of the sky.

“You are not here to strip the plateau,” they say. “You are here to sit with it and let it be your source. You will learn to take from it without leaving it empty.”

They are watching the gap of sky where the sun has not yet fallen but is already leaning.

“Fourth,” they say, “I will protect you from the worst of yourself.”

You are not sure you want to know what this means.

“You carry,” they say, “the idea that you must be Ashamed of being Ashamed of power.”

You are uncertain of your words for a long moment.

“You feel,” they say, “that if you are given any power or capacity or influence over this world, you will be corrupting it by your presence, your *status* as a soul from *elsewhere.* You are afraid of your own weight.”

You are.

“You also,” they say, “know that guilt is not the same thing as responsibility. You are here to learn that difference. I will not stop you from making mistakes. I will not stop you from causing harm. I will protect you from being erased by your own spiral of shame.”

They are changing now, becoming more solid, or perhaps you are becoming more transparent. You cannot tell which.

“You will be allowed,” they say, “to see what you have broken, and to mend it. Or you will be allowed to let it go. You will learn to set things down.”

Their form is almost moving into focus.

“You will be given,” they say, “a way of being *with* the world, rather than *on top of it.* This is not the same thing as being helpless, or powerless.”

Your eyes are hot and your face wants to turn away. The wind is not pressing on you as hard as it was.

“You have been afraid,” they say, “that you will be transformed by this place into something else that you did not agree to be.”

You are.

“You will be transformed,” they say. “But you will be allowed to say *how.* You will be allowed to keep what you are not willing to lose.”

You are not sure that is possible.

“You will be allowed,” they say, “to keep your heart.”

The words are simple enough that you almost want to laugh. It is not funny.

“You will be allowed,” they say, “to keep your heart *in* the world that wants to become you.”

They are next to you now, without you having moved.

“You do not have to want to stay here forever,” they say. “You may stay here for a lifetime of your heartbeats, or for the span of a single cloud column passing over the basin. Either choice will be true.”

They are at the edge of the stone now, looking at the cloud-sea.

“You do not have to love the world to be loved by it,” they say. “But you are allowed to love it if you want to.”

You are not sure you want to love a world that is this loud and this empty and this full of hidden, fragile life.

“You do not have to pretend,” they say. “Not to me, and not to yourself.”

They are almost transparent again now, except for the outline of one hand.

“You can be afraid of the wind,” they say. “You can be afraid of the stone, and of spiders, and of the dark inside of your own mind.”

You are.

“You can also be,” they say, “kind to this world. Kindness does not require you to be unafraid.”

You are watching the edge of the world where the cloud-sea is starting to glow with sunset hidden somewhere below.

“You can be uncertain about whether you are doing the right thing,” they say. “You can be uncertain about whether some kind of power is watching and whether you should be grateful or suspicious or bored or impressed with it.”

You are uncertain of all of these things.

“You can be uncertain of me,” they say lightly. “I will not be insulted.”

You are uncertain of them.

“You can also,” they say, “choose *not* to accept any of the four things I have offered you. You can ask to be left exactly as I found you, except that you will be awake. I will do that too.”

You are taking longer to answer than you thought.

They are looking at the cloud-sea now, not at you.

“You have been afraid of hunger,” they say. “Of the body you will be. You will be allowed to eat stone and drink mist and still be hungry.”

You are tired of being always hungry.

“You will be allowed,” they say, “to be hungry *without* being destroyed by it.”

Their words are not images of pools that you can fall into and drown. They are pieces of wind you can catch or not.

“You can be tired of hunger,” they say, “and still be hungry.”

The sun is now almost hidden, and the cloud-sea is burning like a world set on fire for a moment of spectacular beauty.

“You can be tired of the world,” they say, “and still love it in the same breath.”

You are not sure you love anything.

“You are allowed,” they say, “to be unsure of who you are, and still *be*.”

They are almost transparent again.

“You are also allowed,” they say, “to ask me to leave you with one of the four things I offered you.”

You are thinking in the language of earthquakes and small cracks.

“Which one,” you say finally, “would you choose if you were me?”

Their smile is not unkind.

“I am not you,” they say. “I cannot be you.”

They are silent for the space of three heartbeats.

“If I were in your place,” they say, “I would keep the second one.”

You are not sure what this means.

“You are,” they say, “already someone the world can be with. You are already someone the stone and water and wind have been waiting to meet *from*.”

You are not sure that is true.

“You may not believe,” they say, “that you are capable of being with *yourself* without destroying yourself. This is true of many beings who sit at the edge of the world.”

The sun is now fully hidden.

“You may be afraid,” they say, “that you will be crushed by too much kindness or too much cruelty or too much silence or too much noise.”

You are.

“You may be afraid,” they say, “that you will be crushed by your own heart.”

You are.

“You will not be allowed,” they say, “to be crushed by your heart. You will be allowed to be made by it.”

The world has not changed around you, except that the wind has settled into the pattern of a night you are not afraid of.

“You will be allowed,” they say, “to be made of the same stuff as the plateau.”

“I will suffer,” you say, because this is what you are always asking of yourself.

“You will suffer,” they say, without apology or false promise. “But you will not be destroyed by your suffering.”

You are not sure this is mercy.

“You will be allowed,” they say, “to be whatever you are becoming and *still* be able to speak to the Stone-Eaters without fear of crushing them or of being crushed by them.”

You are not sure if you will be staying here.

“You will be allowed,” they say, “to be here *just* long enough for whatever you are becoming to be formed.”

The sun is now gone, and the cloud-sea is darkening.

“You will be allowed,” they say, “to be.”

They are almost transparent now except for the outline of one hand.

“You will be asked,” they say, “to be.”
