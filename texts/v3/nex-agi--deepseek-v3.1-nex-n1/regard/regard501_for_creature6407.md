The benevolent and wise power arrives as a change in the light.

It is not a sound, or a wind, or a tremor in the stone. There is no fanfare of rupture in the storm. The engines still hum their one unwavering chord; the ocean ceiling still hangs its slow rain; the cities still float their stratified disks of light. The plateau of black basalt remains unchanged beneath you.

But the light shifts.

You are lying on your back on the cold, smooth stone, looking up at the underside of the storm-sea. You have been here long enough to stop expecting the skyline to move, or the rain to multiply, or the whisperglass to ever truly notice you. Your eyes are accustomed to the false ceiling of water, the ghost-lines of their membranes, the faint scatter of downward drifting metal dust.

You blink, and for a heartbeat you see the underside of the world as if someone else is looking over your shoulder.

The pattern is not disrupted.

But it is *seen*.

### Presence of a kind not meant for this place

The first thing that notices is not you, but the whisperglass.

Being the diagnostic membrane of the entire world, they already sense any tiny fluctuation in pressure, temperature, field strength, vibration. The intrusion of a benevolent power is not a subtle event to them. It is a pressure both gentle and absolute; a temperature both cool and mild; an information field that doesn’t alter the world’s data from anything else, but *annotates* it.

Where their membranes border the engines, they begin to flicker.

Not randomly. In a pattern that has never before existed in this space-time: waves of low phosphorescence running across the underside, like a pulse of reflected heartbeat, from horizon to horizon of your plateau. The lines of light do not change their shape or position; they are simply being illuminated, from some direction outside of them.

You sit up, instinctively.

There is nothing else to see. No figure stands on the black glass floor. No new structure has pierced the ocean above. The rain still hangs in its suspended droplets.

But you feel the presence of a mind old enough to be tired of novelty, and yet still genuinely interested.

This being does not announce itself with words. It simply allows the following idea to form in your mind like an already-present memory:

*"You are not meant to be here, and yet you are here."*

The sentence is not accusatory. It’s observational, like noting the color of water.

Your throat tightens. You whisper to the empty plateau, "So?"

No answer comes.

Somewhere far above, in the highest of the nowhere-cities, a single engine modulates the vibration of its shaft by exactly one-thousandth of a percent—for one interval shorter than anything you can call a second—and then corrects itself.

The whisperglass register the entire anomaly, perfectly.

They reclassify it as not-anomaly.

### Small kindnesses for the traveler

The benevolent power begins by making tiny, precise adjustments.

You are not physically hungry, or thirsty, or cold in the way that would threaten you; this world has already been modified to allow you to survive beneath its impossible ceiling. But there is a kind of hunger it is capable of understanding.

It is the hunger for pattern.

On the dark surface of the basalt, near the edge where your fingers were curled earlier, you suddenly notice a thin crescent of color that has never before been there.

It is not paint or mineral stain; it is a condensation of moisture into the exact pattern of a rainbow you once saw as a child, mapped in microdroplets across the stone. You bend to it, curious despite the fear of being noticed by something so large.
