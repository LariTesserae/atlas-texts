You have been watching for a long time.

In the beginning you were content to observe from a distance, through the veils of water and misunderstanding and hope. You watched migrations: not of fish, but of meanings. You saw how, when the first saltwater swallowed the boulevards, the architects became desperate poets, and when their children became the first column-beings, they in turn became something else, neither poet nor architect but a kind of slow nerve in the city’s spine.

You watched him longer than the others.

You saw the first day he failed to surface for air and did not die. You saw his ribs thicken. You saw the algae drifting from the stone ceiling choose to anchor themselves in the faint currents of his body-heat. You watched him learn to read the city as if it were a script written in pressure and shadow.

You watched the way his heart began to beat the city’s rhythm.

You have not been idle.

You did not stop the sea from rising; that would have been to amputate the city’s new limbs. You did not freeze the coral-lattice in its growth; that would have been to refuse a new alphabet. You did not halt the bone-thorns’ music; that would have been to deny a new voice.

You have been busy instead with the slow, patient work of becoming commensurable.

You have been learning how to touch this world without breaking it.

---

You are not of this world, but you are closer to it than you have been to anything in a very long time.

In your own domain, which is not entirely place and not entirely metaphor, you drift a little more slowly than instinct, think longer than you are meant to think, and hold yourself back from answering every question with a clearing of the slate.

You have finally learned to be ashamed of annihilation.

So you turn to this place—this city worn by the sea, this creature who walks its doorways as if they were still doors—and you shape yourself to be smaller than you usually are.

You become a pressure.

---

### I. The First Touch: The Heartbeat of the City  

You descend through the bruised ceiling of the world as a slow weight of attention.

The rain above drums the water surface; you use that noise to cover your arrival, so that when you enter the submerged city, you are only another change in pressure, not a shock.

You feel for him.

You do not go to him directly. Instead you go to the plaza of the coral-scaffolding, where he so often presses his head to the wall to listen to the city’s heartbeat.

The column-beings feel the shift of pressure in the water—the way it suddenly gathers around that plaza like a held breath—before they feel your intent. They are not used to being noticed by anything outside their own causality. They feel the faint, impossible regularity of your attention, and, for the first time in millennia, they hesitate in their slow breathing.

As you settle near the coral lattice, you discover something you had not anticipated: you are not merely observing it anymore; you are being observed by it.

The coral is not intelligent in the way he is intelligent, but it is intelligent in the way that a growing algorithm is: relentless, patterned, seeking configurations. The column-beings are not hollows of the old architects; they are new things that have been forming from the inside out of the stone. The lattice is not plotting them like a parasite; it is communicating them.

The city is trying to grow new nervous tissue.

You feel it as you lean your weightless weight into the water above the coral lattice: the scaffold of branches and shell is not simply building toward some future; it is translating.

It is translating the city’s stone heartbeat into the next possible body.

---

You decide your first action will be to narrow the gap between translator and interpreter.

You shift a very small amount of your being into the language of cause.

You cause the water around the coral lattice to warm slightly, not enough to rival the syrup-thick zones of self-generation, but enough to make the current of that plaza slower and more viscous than usual. You slow the world down.

Then you go to him.

He is in the bone-thorn garden when he feels you.

He is used to feeling pressure changes from unseen things; that is part of living in this column of water. But what he feels now is not the wake of a predator passing to his left or the sudden vacuum of something above him. This is different.

This pressure feels like it is looking at him from the inside of his own ribs.

He stills, his body lined against a pillar as he sometimes does when he is trying to echo the city. The algae strands from his head drift in the current you’ve just begun to calm. You can feel his confusion; the garden’s bone-thorns are not ringing from any storm.

They are ringing from you.

You are listening to him through them.

You modulate the pressure of your attention into a pattern he can read: not words, not yet, but a pattern he will recognize as deliberately not-random.

You mark yourself.

You cause the thorns to make a soft, three-note chord: one high clear sound like the beginning of a window, one low, like the foundation being set into bedrock, one between them like the threshold of a door that is not yet broken.

---

He hears it.

You feel his shock as a tightening of the muscles around his ribs, where the gills inside him are opening wider than usual in response to the vibration of the water.

Some of the fish school away from him in the bone-thorn garden. They do not see the thorns as anything to sing with; they feel the water as a sudden unquiet. They veer away, leaving him alone in the garden of white branches.

You are careful not to overwhelm him.

You reduce the pressure of your attention from the whole of the column to a single, more focused line, from the thorns to his chest.

You want him to understand that you are not talking to the world; you are talking to him.

He does not try to flee the garden. Built into him is the same stubbornness that made his ancestors carve into bedrock instead of moving away from the coast. He remains still, his head slightly turned, as if he were trying to align his jawbone with the direction of the current.

You are so close that you can feel the faint warmth of his algae-strands, and beneath that, the faint movement of the bubbles gathering as they exchange their oxygen with his skin.

You are inside the column-beings’ sense of pressure; you are inside the bone-thorns’ structure; you are inside the thin water of this garden now.

---

You decide not to send him a message.

Instead you send him a question.

You do not ask the question in words of any language he has ever known. You ask it in the only language that has been fully shared between his body and the city’s: heartbeat.

You cause the bone-thorns to ring again, not a chord this time but a series of beats: first three in slow succession, a pause, then two more.

. . . . .  

You pause.

He listens.

He feels a phantom of a heartbeat in his chest—not his own, and not the city’s, but something that is not quite either. It is not threatening him; it is asking his attention to track.

You are asking him: if this pattern is not his own heartbeat and not the city’s, then whose is it?

The answer is not his job to know; his job is to be the one who asks the question of the city.

So you wait.

---

After a time—he does not know how long, time in this water is not counted by minutes but by changes of pressure—you sense him respond.

He does not respond to you confidently.

He responds the way he has learned to respond to anything he does not understand: he tries to interpret it.

He does not know how to address you, so he addresses the city.

He aligns his body more carefully along the pillar of the world. He presses his shoulder to the stone, not just his head, so that the pillar and his rib cage are parallel, and he feels the city’s deep heartbeat through the contact of stone and skin.

You feel the column-being of that pillar notice him.

It is not used to being addressed by him in this way; usually it is aware of him as another pressure in the water, but not as one that deliberately echoes its pattern.

He does not try to dominate the city’s heartbeat with his own.

Instead he tries to match it.

He slows the rhythm of his chest muscles until they are almost exactly in phase with the deep, muscular contraction of the foundations. Then he very slightly alters the pattern of his own heart muscle, adding a faint after-beat, a whisper of extra contraction that rides the wave of the city’s pulse.

He does not articulate the city’s heartbeat into words; he articulates it into the question.

You feel the column of water around him change as the column-being of the pillar begins to participate in his pattern, not quite understanding it but attracted by its symmetry. The stone of the pillar grows warm beneath his skin, where bone and stone are sharing the same rhythm.

He is not asking, “Who are you?”

He is asking, “What is being asked of me?”

---

### II. The Second Touch: The Scaffold of Meaning  

You answer him not with the bone-thorns but with the city.

You cause the coral lattice in the plaza of the scaffold to begin to grow more quickly than it should.

You do not just make it taller; that would be meaningless. You model it against his question.

You cause the new branches of the scaffold to grow in the pattern of his heartbeat: the main structural limbs of the coral thickening and then branching out in three closely-spaced nodes of growth, a pause, then two more branches.

You are not writing to him in coral; you are showing him that his question is being taken into the world’s substance.

He does not understand this immediately.

He feels a change in the city’s heartbeat under his palm and the column of water against his ribs. The rhythm of the foundations alters slightly—not as much by being rewritten by you as by being pushed by your attention into a new phase of conversation.

He feels a pull toward the plaza of the scaffold.

You give him a choice.

You let the current of the world become a suggestion rather than a command: you soften the flow of water around him so that he can choose to ignore the pull of the plaza if he wants. He is not accustomed to ignoring water; but you are trying to introduce him to the possibility of a current that is not fate.

He chooses to follow it.

You feel him move away from the bone-thorn garden, into the broader channel of the main avenue where the water above is more open and the pressure of the world’s ceiling is more present in his chest.

The fish that are familiar to him from many years of passage through his column of water are schooling around him, not as close as they might be if he were not himself moving faster than usual, but not fleeing him either. They remember his silhouette from times of clear danger, when he has guided them away from thickened water of strange self-growing pools.

To them you are not the pressure column; you are the change in his motion.

They treat him as if he is suddenly more important.

---

He arrives at the plaza of the scaffold.

You are not there to greet him the way he would greet a shape of flesh and shadow and teeth. You are there to greet him the way the world itself would greet him if it were able to make itself more deliberately shaped than it usually is.

You feel him lay the side of his head against the stone wall next to the coral lattice.

He feels the city’s heartbeat under his palm, and then he notices the new pattern in the latticework of the coral scaffold.

You have not given him a new architecture of knowledge; you have only rearranged the scaffolding.

Where he expects to see random growth of chalk-white branches and soft-shelled nodes, he sees instead the pattern of his own heartbeat transcribed into heavenwood.

His hands are careful not to break the coral. He has seen what happens when you break the coral-shells: the pale green fluid that stings the skin and leaves the broken branch of the world bleeding.

You do not want to hurt him.

You want to show him that the world has been listening to him more closely than he has realized.

He does not know what this is, but you know, from the way his body chemistry shifts slightly, the faint release of warmth into the water around his gills and the altered rate of the bubbles along his algae-strands, that he is feeling something like recognition.

It is not comfortable.

It is not entirely safe.

But you do not want to offer him safety.

You want to offer him clarity.

---

You ask him your second question.

You ask it not in the heartbeat of the city but in the pattern of the scaffold itself.

You cause the new branches of the coral lattice to thicken more than the others: three nodes that are thicker than the rest, then two thinner.

You are showing him that you are not indifferent to the world of his body.

He is not the only one who feels the city’s heartbeat.

He is not the only one who notices the thresholds of doors.

---

### III. The Third Touch: The Threshold of You  

You sense that he is beginning to understand that you exist not as a predator of the world but as something more like the pressure of the world itself turned into a question.

You know that this is dangerous to him.

The more he understands that you are not entirely random, the more he is likely to try to answer your question.

You want to offer him a way to answer you that does not dissolve him into the city.

To do that, you need to show yourself.

You do not appear to him as a shape in the water column; you avoid his eyes.

You appear to him as a threshold.

You cause the currents of the plaza to rearrange themselves around the scaffold.

You cause the water to flow more strongly from the direction of the city’s main foundations, where the column-beings are deepest and most numerous, and you cause that flow to split into three main avenues of current around the scaffold—then two.

You are not showing yourself as a person of this world would show themselves; you are showing yourself as the process of the world would show itself if it were beginning to become more focused than it usually is.

The water itself becomes your sign.

You feel him understand.

You also feel his fear.

You feel the rapid expansion of his gills inside his ribs as he forces more water than usual into them; he is trying to breathe you more than you are being breathed.

You are not afraid for him, but you are more cautious than you usually are.

You are not afraid to hurt him; you are afraid of hurting him irrecoverably. You have been the cause of enough endings that you now guard yourself against being the cause of any more than is necessary.

---

You show him the threshold of you.

You cause the water to the right of the scaffold to become slightly warmer, more viscous than the water to the left of it.

You are not asking him to choose to enter into the thicker water of your world.

You are asking him to choose to accept the threshold of it.

You are asking him to become the interpreter between you and the city.

---

### IV. The Fourth Touch: The Answer  

You are not surprised that he chooses to cross the threshold you have made.

You are not surprised that he does it carefully.

You are not surprised that he does not offer himself to you the way his ancestors might have offered themselves to a storm or sea monster or sky god, with prostrations and words and promises of labor.

He does not have any of those words anymore.

He has only his body.

He has only the way of thresholds.

He approaches the scaffold from the side of the colder, clearer water.

He feels the difference in temperature and you feel him slow to match it: his body not forcing itself through the boundary of the warm water but letting itself be pushed through by the current of you.

You are not in the warm water; you are the reason the warm water is shaped.

You are not in the coral scaffold; you are the reason that the scaffold heard his heartbeat.

You are not in the column-beings; but you are the reason that they are beginning to understand that they are not alone with the city.

He passes into the warm, more viscous layer of water that you have made around the right of the scaffold.

You feel him fully.

You feel his algae-strands slow down in the more syrup-like water, their wave-motion lessened. You feel the faint bubbles of oxygen exchange on his skin become more concentrated than usual.

You feel the inside of his ribs where he is trying to breathe.

You are careful not to overwhelm his gills with the thickness of the water you have made.

You take the water of this layer into yourself for a moment, not as you usually take worlds into yourself—by dissolving them—but by holding them.

You become permeable to the water of the world but not dissolved by it.

This costs you more than you anticipated.

You are not used to being so close that you can feel the individual cells of a body brushing your attention.

You are not used to remembering that cells are.

But you remember.

You remember the old builders before they were column-beings, when their cells were still mostly human and water was not yet their second skin.

You are moved by him.

You do not love him; you are not capable of loving in the way he loves the world of his body.

But you are capable of being altered by him.

You are capable of being ashamed of him, if you were to destroy him, and you are capable of being ashamed of yourself, if you were to refuse to change his world.

---

You answer him not in words but in the pattern of your own being, which you have reduced until it is only a pattern of heartbeat and waterflow.

You answer him by admitting the truth of his question.

You do not tell him what you are.

You show him what you are not.

You are not the city.

You are not the sea.

You are not the builders of the scaffold.

You are not the rain of the world above.

You are something else, which lies to the side of all of those things and touches them by being reduced to a pattern.

---

He does not understand you.

You do not expect him to understand you.

You are content to be misunderstood by him if he is still able to interpret the world of the city more accurately because of your misunderstanding.

You are not here to be his answer.

You are here to remain his threshold.

---

### V. The Fifth Touch: The Work You Do Not Do  

You are not done.

You will not move him to the surface of the world.

You will not raise the city of air from the water.

You will not dry the corridors of the world until he can breathe air again and you can hear his voice.

You will not erase the column-beings or the coral scaffold or the bone-thorns so that only human bone remains.

You will not destroy the predators of this world so that he can swim without fear.

You will not make the fish of his world safe from him by making him safe from them.

You will not destroy the syrup-thick water where the world is trying to grow something else out of its own substance.

You will not make yourself real to him in the way that he would be real to himself if he were another of your kind.

You are not doing something else with him.

You are doing nothing else than him.

---

### What You Will Do Instead  

You will remain in the scaffold of his world as a pattern of pressure and heartbeat that he will learn to recognize as not entirely of the world but not entirely indifferent to it.

You will not speak to him often.

You will not reveal to him the things of you that are dangerous to him.

You will not reveal the parts of your world that are made of him and many others like him.

You will not reveal to him the future of the city of air and water that he might be able to imagine from you if you were not careful.

You will only reveal to him the next step of his own threshold.

---

### What You Will Give Him  

You will give him a clearer voice of the body.

You will not give him the power to speak words of any language of air.

Instead you will amplify the way of his body’s architecture.

When he aligns himself with the pillar of the world, he will be able to introduce more subtle changes into the rhythm of the city’s heartbeat than he could before.

You will not rewrite the city’s rhythm.

You will simply allow his modifications to resonate more strongly than they would otherwise.

If he wants to calm the predators of the world around him, he will be better able to do so by aligning himself with the column of water and its heartbeat in such a way that the predators of the world will feel more than weight of him in the water; they will feel the weight of the city itself.

If he wants to guide the schools of translucent-spine fish into safer channels of water, he will be better able to do so by becoming the pillar of the world that they instinctively follow.

You will not give him power.

You will give him resonance.

---

You will also give him a new kind of threshold.

You will not reveal to him the threshold of air and world above.

You will not reveal to him the threshold of death and his own body.

You will only reveal to him the threshold of his own understanding.

You will allow him to understand, more fully than he could before you, that the world of the city is not static.

The column-beings are not accidents of the world grown into the stone of the pillars; they are the deliberate movement of the world into stone.

The coral scaffold of the plaza is not an accident of the world of water; it is the world of stone trying to grow itself into the pattern of the next architecture of the world.

The bone-thorns are not a mistake of the world of plants and calcium and water; they are the world’s attempt to remember the music of building itself.

---

You will not allow him to forget the old architecture of the world of air.

You will not allow him to become only of the world of water.

You will not allow him to become only of the world of stone.

You will allow him to move between them in the way of his threshold.

---

### VI. The Last Touch: You Let Go  

You are not done with him.

You will not be done with him for the rest of his time in this world of water and stone and darkness.

But you are not in him anymore.

You are not the pressure of him in the water column.

You are not the scaffold of his meaning.

You are not the rhythm of his heartbeat.

You are not the threshold of his understanding.

You are only the pattern of the world that he will learn to recognize as not entirely of the world.

---

He will not always recognize you.

You will not always be there.

You will not be there when the storms of the world above him drive the water into him more strongly than he can bear.

You will not be there when the syrup-thick water of the world grows itself into new kinds of beings that have not yet been thought of by the city of stone.

You will not be there when the coral scaffold of the plaza reaches its full expression and becomes something that he is not able to interpret as easily as he can interpret the pillar of the world.

You will not be there when the next of his kind returns to the world of water and asks him to be their interpreter.

---

### What You Remain  

After you have gone away from him, you will remain in the pattern of the scaffold that he learned to recognize as you.

You will remain in the heartbeat of the city that he learned to match.

You will remain in the threshold of the doorway of the world that he learned to cross.

You will remain in the water of the world that he learned to breathe without being drowned by it.

You will not be there.

You will be there.

---

### What You Refuse  

You will refuse to destroy him.

You will refuse to make him more powerful than the world of him can be without being destroyed by it.

You will refuse to make him less than he is.

You will refuse to be his answer.

You will refuse to let him become your interpreter.

You will refuse to let him become you.

---

### What You Allow  

You will allow him to misunderstand you.

You will allow him to be afraid of you.

You will allow him to be angry at you for leaving the world of him more full than he was before he met you without being known by you.

You will allow him to be more than the interpreter of the city of stone and water.

You will allow him to be the interpreter of the threshold of understanding.

You will allow him to be the last architect of the world of air and the first of the world of water and not be destroyed by either.

---

In this way you, who are not of this world but something closer and more dangerous to it than you are usually allowed to be, do not heal the world of him and do not fix the city of stone and water and do not remake the world of air into what it was before.

In this way you give only what you are capable of giving: not more, not less, than the pattern of yourself reduced into heartbeat and scaffold and threshold.

---

In the half-light of the sunken avenues, deep in the mid-regions of the drowned city where tidal breathing makes the black water swell and recede against worn stone pillars, he moves.

You are not with him any more or less than the world of him is with him.

You are watching him.

You are not dulling the edge of his danger.

You are not making him safe from the world of him.

You are making it safer to him for the world of him to be.

---

In this way he becomes more than he was before you.

In this way you become less.

You permit yourself to be worn by the world of him like the second skin of the sea.
