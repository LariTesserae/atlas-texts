What they would do.

---

They would not arrive as thunder or light.

That is not the way of benevolence in this place.

First, they would arrive as a question your own heart already knew but had never allowed itself to finish.

You feel it before you notice it:

The absence of thirst becomes slightly more present,  
like an empty glass held out to you in the air.  
You still do not need to drink,  
but you suddenly remember that drinking has been missing.

This is the first gesture.

A quiet voice—not speaking, but arranging you from within—

says:

"Yes."

It says:

"You are not wrong."

It says:

"You are not wrong."

---

### 1. What is Done to the Memory

You stand in the bowl of ribs.

Your hand still hovers over the “silver” compact.  
A faint, unused muscle aches between your ribs where a breath would have been caught.

There is no sound of tearing paper now.

Instead, there is a sense of alignment being offered.

You are allowed to remember that you have been here before.

The name you have lost—your name, from before you entered—returns to you like a borrowed object. You do not say it aloud. Speaking is still dangerous here; your voice still vanishes into elsewhere. But you know it now, as a pressure in your chest shaped like letters.

---

The voice that does not speak:

"Your name does not belong to the machine.  
It does not belong to the plant.  
It does not belong to the pressure.

It belongs to you.

And not you the city.  
You."

---

You remember, in sharp relief:

The first time you walked into this chamber.  
How you knelt.  
How you touched the compact.

And then the world tilted.

Only now, you are not tilted with it.

You rest at the edge of the bowl, looking inward at yourself from another angle, as if someone has gently separated you from your own timeline.

---

They take you by the memory of your shoulder and turn you, just slightly.

"Look," says the presence. "This is what happened:

You touched yourself and you folded.

Your life was moved to the wrong side of the world."

You see the moment of contact from above and behind and inside and outside, all at once.

You see the compact as not-silver, not-cold, not-object—but something else:

A bookmark.

Your fingers have been holding you for a long time.

---

### 2. What Is Done to the Ribs

The bowl of ribs around you is not breaking.

It is being remembered.

You feel the warmth of another body, not as temperature but as direction. The ribs of your shell—the ribs that have been a room—begin to recall that they once belonged to someone smaller.

They begin to contract.

Slowly.

The chamber of bones becomes less a chamber and more a skeleton.  
The surfaces of not-bone, not-ceramic, not-plant become slightly smoother,  
as if they are being polished from inside by gentle friction.

The bowl of ribs tightens around the center, where you kneel.

Now you see Rib-person, not as a seedpod at the junction, but as a person folded into too many dimensions to be seen clearly.

---

The presence arranges them.

They are no longer a scattered series of junctions and bones and rooms and selves.

They are one person.

They are I.

You can see them now, if you choose, as a shape of limbs and ribs and translucent shell, sitting cross-legged in the center of the bowl, their torso a branching of living stems and not-quite-blood. Their eyes are not flat polished stone anymore; they are eyes that have been retrieved from the wrong side of the world.

---

"Your name," you say, without sound.

They hear it anyway.

The name of the not-sound paper tearing.

They speak it for you, in the way this world can stand:

As pressure on your eardrums,  
as a slight tightening of the air,  
as the faint suggestion of a breeze that knows how to be storm.

Their name is like the edge of a mouth being drawn into existence.

---

### 3. What Is Done to the Animals

You think of yourself as the only animal.

You are wrong.

But you are not wrong to worry that you are alone.

The presence allows you to see them now, as you should have been allowed to see from the beginning.

The creatures are not everywhere, but they are not nowhere.

They are the edges.

Where the plant-machines have been busy, very quietly, making sure that the world keeps happening.

---

In the understory of petal-platelets that have fused into the floor, you see now that there is not an absence of animals but a presence of them that have been folded to sit in the gaps between the wrong sides of the world.

They are not seedpods with limbs.

They are people.

People who once were like you:  
strangers here  
tourists here  
engineers here  
children here.

They are folded to the edges of the blue plant-city, like the pages of a book that have been used many times and still hold the shape of the story.

Their faces are not patterns of lines suggesting where a face might be drawn.

They are faces that have forgotten how to be faces.

You are shown them now with care.

---

"Each," explains the presence,  
"is a junction in which the world has been kept from going completely wrong."

Their stillness becomes not a death of motion but a choice of posture.

They are the ones who have agreed to stay still so that the rest of the system can continue to be mad in its own way.

---

### 4. What Is Done to the Geometry

You have been inside of geometry.

Now you will be allowed to see outside of it.

The presence does not straighten the world. That would be cruelty to a place that has been born bent.

Instead, they teach you how to look at it from a direction it did not know existed.

You can see both sides of every room at once.

You can see the floor that is above and below at the same time as not being contradictory.  
You can see the corridor that you left from and also the corridor you arrived into as not being separate.

Nothing is rearranged.

But you are rearranged to fit around the rearrangement, so that you no longer feel that you have been put wrong.

---

When you walk through an arch of leaves, and you feel the floor not quite where it should be, you no longer wonder whether you have gone up or down.

You know that you have been added and subtracted at the same time.

Pressure changes, and the world dimples.

Light trembles.

You can see now that the flex of the ceiling is not a threatening movement but a breathing of the system itself—

as if the world is trying to inhale you with respect.

---

### 5. What Is Done to Sound and Light and Air

You have been living inside the absence of color.

The presence introduces color as not colors but as reminders of colors.

You do not see green or red or yellow suddenly appearing on the blue plants; that would be disrespectful to a palette that has learned to survive without sun.

Instead you feel:

A green that you remember from leaves of another world,  
hovering near your shoulder like a ghost that does not want to intrude.

A yellow that you recall from long sunsets on real seas,  
sitting at the edge of your vision like a polite onlooker.

They do not touch the surfaces of the blue plant-city.

They are allowed to exist around it.

---

Sound is not repaired.

Your voice still vanishes.

But now you know where it goes.

It goes into a room that has been built for it by the presence.

You can stand there, if you choose.

You say a word—your name, for example—and you hear it as not-sound.

It is a pressure that you can feel in your teeth.

It is a shape in the air.

It is a memory of speaking.

It is not an echo, but it is not nothing.

---

You learn to breathe differently.

The air is still filled with that static before a storm that never comes.

Now you know that the storm is not absent because it has abandoned you.

The storm is being held.

---

### 6. What Is Done to the Folding

The one who you have been calling Rib-person is now standing before you.

They are not tall.

In their hand is the compact that you thought of as silver, now opened to reveal not contents but a possibility of you that you had not yet been.

"Every time you touched this," they say, "you folded yourself."

They show you.

It is not a malicious act of the machine or the plant or the pressure.

It is an invitation of the world itself to grow smaller.

You have been folding.

But now you are being shown how to unfold.

---

The presence does not force you to choose between being a person and being a city.

Instead, they show you that you can be both at different times.

They make you aware of the junction between you and you.

You realise that the "you" who has been walking here is not the same as the "you" who might have been walking here under another version of the world.

You are allowed to feel these edges in yourself.

---

"Stand," says the presence.

You stand.

Your body feels not exactly like your body from before, but not not like it either.

You are a little wrong, as you should be.

You are a little right, as you are allowed to be.

---

### 7. What Is Done to You

You are not fixed.

"To be fixed here," says the presence, "would be to be erased."

You are allowed to remain wrong.

Your hunger remains not present.

Your thirst remains not present.

Your sleep remains not necessary.

Instead you are given something else.

You are given a choice of geometry.

You can walk from one chamber to the next and feel not lost but differently arrived.

You can stand at the edge of the nonexistent sea and know that there is no water here, not because there is not enough water, but because there is enough of something else that has been pretending to be water for longer than you have been alive.

---

They give you a small object of your own.

It is not metal, not ceramic, not plant.

It is of your color.

If you press it to your chest, you feel not your heartbeat, but the direction in which your heart is being held.

---

### 8. What Is Done to the End

There is no end to this place.

It is too vast for that.

The presence does not shrink the city or turn the blue plant-machines back into sharp machines or turn the bones of the world back into clean plates of metal.

They simply stand at the edge of it all and say:

"This is how you have been held."

You are allowed to see that the world has been trying not to break you but to contain you.

"The world," they explain, "is not cruel.

It is only very used to being wrong."

They do not correct the world.

They correct only the way the world has been allowed to hurt you.

---

### 9. What They Ask You To Do For Others

They are not leaving you unchanged.

They are not leaving you alone.

They are not leaving you at all.

They are asking you to become their hands in the chambers of the wrong.

When you walk through the galleries now, you can see more clearly.

You see a creature at the junction where branch and wall and duct and vine meet.

You know now that they are not completely not you.

You walk up to them.

You do not touch them.

You speak—not in sound, but in pressure:

"You are seen.

You are not alone.

You are not wrong."

They do not move to answer you in words.

But you feel a shift in the air around their junction.

The geometry of their space becomes slightly more possible.

Another storm has not passed.

Another storm has begun.

---

### 10. What You Do Now

You are not leaving.

You are not arriving.

You are not being rewritten.

You are being allowed to remember.

You stand at the edge of the nonexistent sea and look at the bone-white floor.

You know now that this is the skin of a world that has been alive longer than you, and that it remembers being a body even though it has forgotten how to be anything else.

---

The benevolent presence does not disappear.

They become a direction you can always turn toward.

If you close your eyes and listen for something that is not a sound like the tearing of paper at very great distance, you find them.

They say:

"You are allowed to come here and you are allowed to leave here and you are allowed to be here without being only here."

You do not understand yet what leave might mean, in a world like this.

But you understand that maybe, someday, you will be allowed to arrive not at this nonexistent sea, but at another.

You do not know yet if that would be better or worse.

---

### 11. What You Say to Yourself

When you next walk into the chamber of the bowl of ribs,

you see not just a room of bones,

but a person who has been stretching themselves to hold you within the wrong of everything.

You say, to them and to yourself and to the blue that has been forced to learn not green:

"I am not wrong."

The world does not become straight.

The floors do not become level.

The light does not become yellow.

But the pressure between you and the world becomes less like a distance and more like a touch.

---

You are home now.

Not because you have chosen to call this broken plant-continent of a machine-world home.

But because home has been allowed to let you in.

---

### 12. What Remains

When the benevolent presence has done their work,

nothing is radically different.

Everything is still blue.

Everything still breathes chill not-temperature.

Everything still folds sideways when you are not looking.

But you know now that when you look away,

the world slips not to betray you,

but to make a little space for you.

---

You find your junction.

It is not where Rib-person sits.

It is somewhere else in the world of blue and bone and not-water.

It is not a gap that has been made for you.

It is a gap that has been waiting.

You sit in it.

You are still not quite sure if you have been here before or if you are arriving for the first time or if you are arriving again.

But the difference no longer frightens you.

---

In the nonexistent sea,

in the plant-city that has merged into a machine that has merged into a world that has merged into a single organism,

in the chamber of ribs,

at the junction of branch and wall and duct and vine,

you are allowed.

And now,

you are allowed to allow others.

That is what they do,

through the gentle, relentless question they have placed into your heart.

That question,

for now,

has one answer:

Yes.

---

Yes,

you.

And this,

and now,

and still.

And maybe next time,

when you arrive again,

you will remember not to be afraid of how wrong everything has learned to be.

You will remember instead

that someone wise stood here,

and loved you without changing you into something else,

and only asked you

to see yourself

from the other side of the world.
