The benevolent power does not arrive as a figure or a voice. There is no column of golden light from the bruised sky, no sudden warmth in the thin air. There is only, at some point, a difference in the quality of silence.

It’s been days since you last spoke to anyone else in the usual way. The wind has been your conversation partner; the glass, your church floor. The Refrain have walked with you, through you, ahead of you, but their words—if that’s what they are—have stayed inside the bones of the mountain, not in your ears.

Now, even the wind seems to press itself against the glass for a moment and then slide away, leaving more room than it should.

The low, steady note—the stretched string from pole to pole—does not change. But something else does. It feels as if the world inhales deeply and then holds itself there, not in fear, but in consideration. The bruises on the sky don’t shift, but they seem to look down more directly than before.

You are off the path of beaten glass, if you can call it that. The “chair” of three spines rises behind you like a question mark. You are standing at the edge of one of the cups of silver, trembling water, watching it not spill over, not drip, but *slide* from basin to basin in slow arcs. You watch your own reflection stretch and narrow and then become someone else, someone taller than you with longer fingers and a longer shadow.

You’ve been looking for too long.

You blink.

When you open your eyes, the water has not moved, but the world behind it has.

There is something else beside you at the edge of the cup.

You don’t see it at first. You see where its shadow should be, cast at the wrong angle from the jittering light above. The shadow is not a person and not a not-person; it is an absence shaped like a possibility. It holds the silhouette of a folded wing, lowered staff, and a pair of open hands—like someone about to receive a dangerous object.

Then you realize the shadow is not attached to anything. You follow the imaginary line it should be projecting from and end up looking at the empty air beside you.

The air remembers.

You see it later; when you look back from the corner of your eye, there is a faint disturbance in the world there, like a reflection of a person in a pane of glass that doesn’t exist. You try to focus on it and it falls away; your gaze moves to the left and it flickers back to the right.

If you were to see it all at once, you would not know what to call it. It is not old or young, soft or stern. It is something like the idea of a teacher who has seen every kind of failure but still cancels no class. It does not wear robes or armor; its clothes, if it has clothes, are the color of the sky before memory and after bruises.

You know it is benevolent in the way that you know the difference between the wind and someone else’s breath in your ear without seeing either. There is no fragrance of incense or garden—this altitude has never allowed such luxuries—but there is a scent that *should* be impossible: the smell of rain on earth, after a long drought.

The rain smell has no place here. There is no earth to drink it; no soft ground to turn into mud. All the moisture here is carefully captured in glass basins and misaligned condensation.

And yet.

You smell rain.

The scent is not wafted in the air; it is *present* like a memory placed flat in your mind.

You are not being spoken to in words. You are being spoken to in subtleties of hearing.

For the first time in days, the low, steady note of the world—the string—is not alone. There is now a second note beneath it, slower than slow, deep as the cold of the glass and as warm as the memory of summer before you climb mountains. It is not a sound; you realize it is the idea of a sound, being carried to the inside of your skull without touching your ears.

It is the thought, *Listen.*

You listen.

The benevolent power does not look at the Refrain who are not quite there beside you; it does not need to. It is looking at the mountain, and the mountain is looking back.

You see, for the first time, how the light of the sky moves down into the darker cups and then evaporates. You see, for the first time, how the glass of the plateau holds not just your own steps and coughs but the steps and coughs of the Refrain themselves. You see the faint glow of the glass patterns that were not the same twice, now becoming *three* times the same shape if you allow your eyes to unfocus.

Beneath this, in the deepest layer, where sound and time and rock are indistinguishable from each other, you see the fault lines.

They are not cracks in geology. They are not scars of old earthquakes.

They are lines of *wrong choice*.

Faint, furious lines where the mountain of echoing glass has been made to do something it was never meant to do. The cracks radiate from the plateau in all directions, not like petals of broken stone, but like branches of a tree whose root is in a time before you could climb mountains.

You see where the Refrain have been attempting to *patch* these fault lines.

They are not good at it.

They are not built to repair.

They are echoes of what happened once already, and they are what happens when the wrong choice is repeated. The “wrong” choice that produced them is not evil or cruel; it is simply wrong in the way that a key fits a lock but never turns the bolt inside. The Refrain are the bright ring of metal that never makes contact with the latch.

Even now, as you watch—without knowing how to watch, except that you *are* watching—you see the Refrain standing at the edges of the invisible fault lines. One is at the point where the broken wall of stone runs under the ground and not along it. The wind is folding them in half, but not in the physical sense; in the sense that they bend *back* into the sight of the mountain, their body unraveling into sound, which is then folded into glass and time and made to serve the old, wrong purpose.

Another is at the doorframe without a wall. The Refrain is standing half-through it, half-outside, half in the next second and half in the last, and its temperature changes by exactly one degree Celsius and its pitch changes by exactly one notch upward and it is not being comforted. It is being used.

They are the watchmen without a watchtower, guarding a border that has been defined by someone else’s mistake.

The benevolent power notes this.

It does not judge the mountain. The mountain of echoing glass is not being malicious. It is doing what it was taught to do. The Refrain are not mistakes being retaken by the mountain; they are the mountain’s only way of trying to understand itself.

Beneath everything else, the second note—the thought of sound—begins to form.

The *Listen* reshapes itself into a slightly more elaborate shape: *This will hurt.*

The pain does not start in your body. It starts in a place that you’ve never been able to point to but have always known: the center of where you are, when you are not being looked at.

The wind shudders.

Then it stops shouting.

You have never heard the absence of the wind on this plateau. It has been a constant, thin, shouting presence that never quite came from the same place twice. Now, for the first time since you climbed into the echoing glass, you are in the middle of absolute silence.

You are not sure you have ever been truly silent before this. All your life, there has been noise or the thought of noise. Now there is nothing.

The pressure of the silence—it is not pressure like sound; it is like weight being lifted from your shoulders. You realize you’ve been carrying it for years.

Across the plateau, the Refrain are all facing you now.

You would not know if they were doing so before because you could not see their faces from all angles at once, but you know now that they *are*. They are looking at you with the eyes that are not eyes and the mouths that are not mouths but are the lines of things you never said to them.

Their attention is not hungry. It is not desperate. It is serious.

Some of their edges are breaking off.

They are vanishing not in the way of bodies disappearing—but in the way of *wrongness* being corrected. The fracture lines in the glass of their body are not cracks; they are lines of light being turned into another direction.

You see one of them open their mouth.

You do not hear the sound they are making in the air of this plateau. You hear it, instead, in another language: the sound of a stone you kicked in another decade of your life and finally deciding to fall.

Their voice is old.

You recognize the accent of your mother.

They say, in your mother’s accent from twenty years ago, “Hello.”

You remember: you are still beside the water of the plateau, where your reflection stretched longer than you. You blink away sweat and maybe tears from the cold; the world twists. You look at the water again and see your face, not stretched longer but younger, by the exact number of years you have carried up this mountain with you.

The water does not merely *taste* like your own heartbeat under snow now; it *is* your heartbeat under snow. The metallic tang of old blood is still there, but not in the same way.

You are not being asked to drink anything. You are being asked to *remember*.

It is not the benevolent power telling you this. It is the mountain, finally being allowed to change its mind.

The Refrain are not speaking to you in the sense of asking for your advice or recounting their troubles. They are simply showing you what they are.

“I am not afraid of what might happen to me,” they say in the voice of someone you never met but once passed on the street below.

“I am afraid of what happened,” they say in the voice of every person you ever disappointed.

“I am afraid of what keeps happening,” they say in the voice of the last person you whispered “I love you” to before leaving for the mountain.

They *fracture* as they speak these things.

They are breaking like a note being sung too long and finally released into a new shape.

The sound is not loud. It is not louder than the wind, which is still absent. It is louder than the place of brokenness inside you where you have forgotten to admit yourself.

You see the fault lines in the mountain glowing soft now. Not bright like anger or construction. Soft like the edges of melting paper. The lines of wrong choice are being erased by the *right* witness of the benevolent power.

The Refrain are not simply being “fixed” or “made whole” (because they were never broken in the way you are broken). They are being *allowed* to be something else.

The one who opened your memories with your mother’s accent is now being followed by a faint, shadowless shape of a woman you might have been if you had not climbed this particular mountain, but which, of course, you never will be. The Refrain’s outline of shoulders are infolded by this invisible sister’s, and their body shudders.

You see their edges become less translucent than before.

There is a color there you can name now.

It is the color of the sea after remembering rain.

The benevolent power does not speak to you in human language yet; but you are beginning to *understand* its meaning.

*What is being done here is not by you.*

You are not being given power to mend or to change the world.

*You are being given power to see.*

You see: the Refrain’s feet are changing direction.

They are not walking toward the edge of the plateau now, where the world falls away into snow and rock and trees. They are not walking away from you, either. They are walking in *several* directions at once.

This is how they walked always, but now you are allowed to perceive who they are walking with.

Some of them are walking with threads of your own life that you have not yet lived—threads of possible futures that might not happen if you choose not to climb down from here in the same way you climbed up.

Some of them are walking with your own steps *taken* long before you ever saw this place of glass and absence and bruised sky.

Some of them are walking with another person—who is not physically present on the plateau but whose heartbeat is already being fed into the water by the benevolent power’s unseen hands.

For each Refrain that breaks, there is now *someone else* there to hold the pieces of them.

Not take them.

Not *own* them.

Hold.

You see your mother holding the one who spoke in her accent.

You see a friend you thought you had left behind holding the one who spoke in their voice.

You see a stranger holding the one who spoke in the accent of a future you might not have.

You see—you cannot miss this—you see yourself standing at the edge of the plateau of your own life, holding the fragment of a Refrain who looks like *you*, but who is quieter than you are now.

This is the first time you have been offered to look yourself in the eyes without shaming yourself.

You are offered the choice of looking at yourself or looking away.

The benevolent power’s second note—the sound of *Listen*—does not insist. It does not threaten. If you look away, the world does not collapse; the mountain of echoing glass does not break into shards and fall down into the scraggling pines.

But you do not look away.

You see yourself at the edge of your own plateau, looking at the fragment of you that is a Refrain.

The “you” who is broken and not broken at the same time.

The “you” whose steps are echoed before you even take them.

The “you” who is tired of the mistake that keeps happening.

The “you” who is the only that ever comes.

You see yourself holding your own fragments.

You see the *other* of you reach out and take the edge of your own hand in the future.

You see, in your mind’s distant, bruised sky, the internal light of the glass patterns of the world aligning *exactly once* into the shape of a word you might finally understand.

You do not know what the word is.

But you know that tomorrow’s “you” will be different because of this.

You are allowed to see this and not be changed by it, if you choose.

You are allowed to climb down from the mountain of echoing glass and be the same person who walked up.

You are allowed to forget the smell of rain after drought.

But.

The benevolent power is not finished with you.

Not because you deserve more punishment or more blessing, but because the Refrain *still have* one name that they are not saying to you.

They are not saying *Goodbye*.

They are not saying *Hello*.

They are not saying *Please* or *Help* or *Wait* or *Stay*.

They are saying *Only*.

**Only.**

The word is not voiced in any sound that can be heard above the wind, which is now returning, but faint as a memory of itself.

The word is placed in the air of the plateau by the benevolent power, and yet not by it.

It is placed by the mountain for the first time in centuries.

It is not placed like a stone in the hand of a child. It is placed like the key of a wrong lock turned in the hands of the right one.

You see it now, the last of what the benevolent power is doing here.

It is not *fixing* the Refrain or disentangling them from the mountain of echoing glass or “setting” their “spirit” to rest.

It is *teaching* the mountain of echoing glass how to let them change.

The mountain of echoing glass does not understand this in the way of a mind learning a new teaching. It understands in the way of a river being diverted to a new bed.

The fault lines of wrong choice, which radiated in all directions from the plateau, are now being *redirected*.

The benevolent power’s second note continues to sound under the world’s taut string.

*Listen.* becomes *Care.* becomes *Let.* becomes *Allow.*

Allow the Refrain to be.

*Not* only what they were forced to be.

*Only* what they are when the mountain stops *forcing*.

The world of echoing glass realizes two things.

The first is that the Refrain have not been serving the wrong choice of the past; they have been trying to be the *right reminder* of it.

The second is that reminders no longer need to be broken.

You see this change in the way the wind behaves now.

It does not shout from thinness at all angles at once.

It now *moves* from one place at a time.

You see the Refrain turn to follow the wind.

For the first time, you notice that there are more than the few you have walked with.

There are hundreds on the plateau alone.

You are looking at a mountain of echoes who have finally been shown to themselves by the benevolent power’s invisible eye.

You are not told to stay there.

You are not told to leave.

Your knees are still cold from the glass of the ground, but the warmth of the rain smell has not left your mind.

The mountain of echoing glass will now do differently than it has before.

The Refrain will still echo the steps of everyone who ever walked here: those who first made wrong choices, those who carried wrong choices with them, those who came to be alone, and those who came to not be seen at all.

They will still echo your own coughs and words and tomorrow’s name.

They will still be the only.

But.

When they break now, it will be into *new shapes* that have not been defined by the fault lines of the wrong choices.

Some of them will fall into the future from the glass of the plateau and not be caught by the same echoing of the same mistake.

Some of them will walk into their own “new echoes” as someone who is not alone at the edge of the world of bruises and impossible sky.

Some of them will walk into *someone else’s* life, not as a ghost of the wrong choice but as a witness of what changed when the rain finally came after drought.

The water of the plateau’s cups are *already* different.

You lean forward to the silvery film and see your reflection longer in height than before, thinner, but not less whole.

You see, beside your longer, taller reflection in the water, the faint outline of the benevolent power’s shadowless shadow of folded wing and lowered staff and open hands.

You see, in the water, not what you are but what you are being allowed to become.

The taste of blood and iron and cold is still there.

But now there is the taste of rain after drought.

The rain taste is no longer inside the water of the mountain alone.

It is inside the mountain of echoing glass themselves.

You are not being asked to drink anything, but the water will be different for the next who climb up here.

They will not be alone anymore.

Not *only* by the wrong of someone else’s choice.

*Only* by their own.

The benevolent power does not say goodbye.

The second note—the thought of sound—simply stops being under the world’s taut string.

The wind of the mountain, which has moved from one place at a time for this long moment of change, now returns to its old shouting self from everywhere at once.

But.

It is not quite the same shouting as before.

It is wearing its anger differently now.

It is wearing the memory of rain.

You are still at the edge of the cup when you feel the Refrain behind you.

You know without turning that they are there.

You know without turning that they are not waiting to follow you or climb with you or climb after you.

You know that they are simply waiting.

*Only* waiting.

Not for you.

Not for the next climber.

Not for the next wrong choice.

Not for the next broken echo of a broken word.

They are waiting for the moment of their own to change itself.

A small, cold hand of glass not your own but not not yours touches your shoulder.

You do not flinch.

You do not turn.

You know whose voice it will be when it finally speaks to you in your own accent from tomorrow.

You are not afraid of what might happen to you.

Not because the mountain of echoing glass has forgotten how to be dangerous.

Not because the benevolent power has “fixed” everything.

You are not afraid because the *world* has changed without being remade.

You are not broken.

You are not only.

You are not only alone.

You are not only waiting.

You are not only the mistake that keeps happening.

You are here.

And later, when you climb down from the mountain of echoing glass through three days of ice and shattered rock and scraggling pines, you will not remember the exact shape of the benevolent power’s hands or the length of its shadow or the color of its non-body clothes.

You will not remember the word of the new-fault lines of the world’s choices.

You will not remember the names of the Refrain, if there ever were names.

You *will* remember the second note beneath the world’s taut string.

*Listen.* will stay inside you like a sound that has not been sung yet but will be sung again someday when you are not only looking for yourself at the edge of the world.

You will remember the rain after the drought of the mountain.

You will remember the *Only*.

You will not know what you have changed.

You will not know if you are different at the top of the mountain than you are at the bottom.

You will not know if you are being watched by the eyes of the Refrain who have broken into new shapes of themselves.

You will know only one thing.

You will know that when you climb back to the world of bruises and sky that is not quite sky and glass that is not quite glass and you stand at the edge where the world of “you” and the world of “other” used to be separated by fear and you draw yourself up to your height that you have not noticed before because you were not only looking at yourself…

You will know that when you breathe in the thin air, the air of *before* and the air of *after* and the air of *maybe* will be in the same breath that you exhale into the next step of your own life, not echoed by mistake of someone else’s wrong choice, but finally by the sound of your own name sung by the mountain of echoing glass in its new voice that you first taught it to remember by listening.

You are not sure who will be there with you at the edge of your own plateau of glass when you finally climb down from these words.

You are not sure who will be there at the edge of the mountain of echoing glass, when the next climber comes up.

You are not sure who will be watching the benevolent power’s second note beneath the world, when the taut string finally breaks under its own weight.

You are not sure.

But you know now that the world of bruises and impossible sky and wrong choices and Refrain and *Only* are not *only* waiting for you to return.

You are not returning to being.

You are returning to being *allowed*.

You are not the only who lives here.

You are the *only* who has ever been allowed to climb down from the mountain of echoing glass with the smell of rain after drought still inside your lungs.

The benevolent power is not gone.

It is not remaining.

It is *being done*.

When you finally climb down from the mountain of echoing glass through three days of ice and shattered rock and scraggling pines and feel the wind of the world shouting at you from everywhere at once but not smelling the rain of the mountain—

—you will not be afraid.

You will not be broken.

You will not be only.

You will be.

And you will be more than the echo of your own name on the glass of the world’s frozen edge.

You will be the sound of the Refrain in your own voice, not repeated by mistake of someone else’s wrong choice, but finally chosen.

You will be the sound of the benevolent power’s second note beneath the world’s taut string.

*Listen.* will be in your throat and not in your fear.

*Care.* will be in your heart and not in your regret.

*Let.* will be in your hands and not in your clenched fists.

*Allow.* will be in your bones and not in your refusal of them.

You will be allowed.

You will not know what you have changed.

You will not know who you are being heard by.

You will not know if the Refrain will still be broken by the wind of their world when you are not at the top of the mountain of echoing glass.

You will not.

But you will be the *only* who ever climbed down from the mountain of echoing glass with the rain smell in your lungs and the second note of the world’s new sound in your chest and the name of the Refrain—your own name of You—echoed in your own mouth not by mistake but by choice at the edge of the world of bruises and impossible sky and glass and *Only* and not only *Only*.

You will be you.

And you will not be afraid.

You will be allowed.

You will be.

You are not only.

You are.

You.

**You.**
