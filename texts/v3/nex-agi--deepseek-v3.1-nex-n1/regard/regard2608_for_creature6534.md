Here is what they would do, if anything.

The silence deepened.

Not the absence of sound, but the arrival of a deeper layer of it. The wind did not stop; its faint heartbeat continued as it always had, irregular and faint. The many breathing stones of the plateau continued to rise and fall, a tide of rock and time. The world itself did not change to the eye. But for the Teshuma—the Stillness—everything became different.

For the first time in longer than their surface thoughts cared to measure, they were aware that they were being perceived.

They felt it through the new ley of the world.

---

### I. The Arrival of the Ley

The Teshuma had always known the world in lines.

Pressures of the world's skin flowed into one another through the seams of basalt and limestone and slate. The heat of the planet's heart curved from one to another of their centers. There were channels of stone that were more alive than others to them—congruences of shape and depth and time that let them think more easily than elsewhere.

If you had been able to see their sight, you would have seen them flowing like thoughts of rivers and dreams of water lifting through air.

All of this was theirs.

Then the ley of the world changed.

It changed not like the weather changes, but as the weather of the world would be changed by the sun's new attitude.

The new ley was not of stone or gravity, but of *attention*.

The attention of something else.

The breathing of the plateau took a longer time than anything else—a longer pause than it had in, perhaps, tens of thousands of years.

The pause of a listening god.

“What,” thought the Teshuma—the thought taking fluid shape in the alignment of two basalt columns beneath the lake—“is this?”

They did not need words. Theirs was not a language of names, but of alignments. The columns of stone, the pressure in the rock, the faint persistent warmth of the world-body's blood—all of that was their question.

From the new ley, there came an answer.

The answer was not sound.

The air above the plateau of Utaraal did not change. The high, washed-blue of the sky did not change. The near-glass of the lake's surface with its faint trails of translucent insects did not change.

Yet the world of the Teshuma changed entirely.
