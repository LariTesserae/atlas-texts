Let’s call the power that has noticed this place *the Long Tide*.

It doesn’t think of itself as benevolent or wise, exactly. It would describe itself—if you could catch it in a mood for language—as something like “a tendency of the universe to curl back on itself in memory.” It is what happens to the world when events that should have gone extinct keep lingering in the shape of space.

It has noticed the basin between the failed continents, and the Quillwood that has folded itself into the absence of mountains.

It noticed mainly because the basin’s water is remembering *too much.*

### The Arrival of the Tide

The arrival is not loud. No trumpet of the sun, no pillar of light burning the gills.

Rather, one night the air of the market slows *more than it should*.

The Keepers of the Breath know the Quillwood’s pulse better than their own. The instant they feel the change, they freeze where they stand: palms pressed to warm, metallic wood, skulls humming to the subtle shifts of sap and current.

Tonight:

- the glow‑sacs do not breathe their usual slow rhythm;
- the canals do not slosh;
- the dust motes in the air between the ribs hover like halted constellations.

The Long Tide has not “come here.” It has always been present, in the way that gravity is present in the absence of a fall. But now it chooses a form of attention.

A few of the Dreamers lying beneath the oldest bridges start to shiver. They feel the Quillwood’s breath retreating from their lungs like water from a shore they never knew they had.

In the stillness of the market, something else appears between the ribs of the streets.

### A Form Between the Branches

At first, no one sees it clearly.

They feel its reflection:

- The Regretters look down into the canals, and instead of their own faces, they see no face at all—just the curling of the water around things that should be there but aren’t.
- A child (or the child of the Market) chases a Sanfish and finds it hovering above a patch of canal where there is nothing to see and yet everything to remember.

The Keepers of the Breath are the first to see it with a kind of sight.

To the oldest Keeper—a woman whose joints are the same age as the oldest gills outside the heartwood—it appears as a ribbon of water tied around the rib of a bridge, except that the water is not quite water and not quite metal, and not *quite* there.

When she blinks, it moves.

When she breathes, it breathes.

When she touches the Quillwood’s bark, her palm finds the tree’s pulse still present but altered—like the echo of a heartbeat that has forgotten to be afraid of stopping.

The Tide chooses to adopt a shape that can be understood.

To the Dreamers, it is a slow, silvery echo of the Quillwood’s gill‑shield ceiling, hanging below the real one like a reflected sky.
