The attention of a benevolent and wise power arrives in the valley of hollows as a change of sound.

It does not arrive as thunder. Zeus does not split this cloud-cover; Jehovah does not part these vapors. The roar of the sky has been, for as long as this bowl has drowsed, the only thing that never changed. So the change that arrives now is not a breaking of that monotone, but a subtle clarification of it, as though someone has turned one thing in a vast machine and made the note of the world abruptly *intelligible*.

The sound does not grow louder. Instead, the overcast white noise of the sky begins, to any listening mind, to resolve into words. They are not spoken words, but meaning-words, the way a thought may be a word without having sound. They press at the edges of hearing like the cold damp pressed on the skin.

They are saying:

“Here.”

And “Wait.”

And “See.”

They are not demanding. They are not announcing a judgment that has been passed down from the upper rim of the world. The sound of the sky is simply, briefly, organizing itself around this particular patch of space at the bottom of the world’s bowl—this valley of erased footprints and forgetful stone and a single spiral of lichen-painted time.

The cold that usually sits at the bottom of the hollows does not retreat from this change. It remains, but it becomes articulate. The regions of slightly colder and slightly warmer air that intersect around the lichen delta become more pronounced, not as temperature alone but as *difference*, as if the air were now a thin blackboard of convection and someone had carefully traced lines of chalk along its currents.

The rock of the basin floor, which usually erases everything but remembers itself, has no particular defense against this attention. It is no longer permitted to lose its acquaintance with that particular patch of lichen. Underneath the white branching body of the delta, where the stone had been gently etched by the thousands of tiny rootlets, the stone’s memory of their passage is now fixed. The pattern of the lichen—the whorls and loops and lazy false starts—sinks into the micro-crystal structure of the rock, not as a scar of conquest but as a record of collaboration. The stone does not become softer. It becomes more itself.

The dens of slightly colder air that gathered under the lobes of the lichen begin to move. Hitherto they had been the faintest pockets of difference, places where the cold, smoglike air of the basin floor was undisturbed by the warmth exhaled by the rim plants. Now the wisdom of the air remembers how to *breathe* more precisely.

Each of those small hollows beneath the lichen begins to fill, not with water, but with a thinner, more subtle version of itself: a condensation of *possibility*. The air is not ordered to become stormy or vigorous; but it is allowed to consider *direction* once more. After uncounted years of sliding, exhausted wind over stone, the faintest possible breeze recalls the meaning of “toward.”

There are thousands of these hollows under the lichen’s branches. Each of them becomes a tiny, persistent direction, the ghost of a wind that might have been and now might, very slowly, be permitted to remember *how* to be.

The sound of the sky, which has been monotonous, unending, and impersonal, now remains *unchanging* in volume but not in tone. It never becomes melodic. It does not acquire chords or harmonies. It does not become human music, which belongs to the world of hearths and weapons and dancing bodies. This place is far older than that.

But the white noise’s pitch *tilts*. The monotone is now broken into two layers:

A low layer, which remains unchanged, the long roar of the world’s sleep.

And a higher layer, which intermittently clarifies itself into patterns of modulated noise, like a faint voice speaking through a waterfall of static.

To a listening human, the words would not be recognizable words; but the meaning would arrive anyway:

“This one is not yet.”

“This edge is not less.”

“This shape is not only.”

The wisps of turbulence that drift through the cloud blanket above the valley suddenly become more distinct to the eye. The thin strokes of movement in the cloud layer begin to spiral slowly around the valley’s center, not like storm clouds but like the action of an immense spoon that has been set into the bottom of the world’s bowl and, very slowly, begun to stir.

The center of the basin does not surrender to life. The stone at the core of the valley floor still remembers its ancient rules of solitude. The shallows of glass-cut water that mirror the sky continue to do what they have always done: gather, hold, then spill slowly into each other, without ever becoming enough to overflow.

Fewer misdirected seeds of plants from the rim now fall into that center. The two-layered sound of the sky seems to know where the central basin’s refusal of life is and gently *rearranges* the smallest of events: wind currents, drips from overhanging fronds, the tiny silver insect-visits that had been drifting out into the basin center now brush up against an invisible slope of air and lose interest, turning back toward the rim of moss-forest instead.

The plants of the rim—the dense, dark frills that are halfway between moss and forest—do not explode into growth. They are not asked to do so. But they are given permission to arrange their edges more precisely.


### What a benevolent and wise power does here is:

1. It **honors the center’s choice of emptiness**.

The center of the basin floor remains unchanged in its decision not to hold life. The stone’s ancient refusal of roots, seeds, and sprouts is not overridden. This is not a garden in need of “fixing.” It is a scar that has been allowed to become its own kind of completeness, and completeness is respected.

2. It **clarifies difference at the edges**.

Instead of forcing life into the center, it *makes the edges more clear*. The slight temperature contrasts between the cold of the basin floor and the faint warmth exhaled by the encircling plants are brought to the surface of the world’s awareness. The air that once “preferred” to live in hollows now learns to *prefer* in more complex ways: preferring to loop around this particular unevenness of stone, to skim over that new etched pattern of lichen, to avoid this center where stone never forgot to say “no.”

The “border” between the basin and the rim becomes more cognitive than physical. It’s not a wall. It’s a *grading* of worlds, a slow layering of choices from one to the other.

3. It **let’s itself be touched by time**.

A benevolent and wise power does not necessarily believe “change” is synonymous with “improvement.” But it *does* notice that something, in this valley of erased footprints, has finally *persisted*.

The delta of lichen is not treated as a child to educate or a patient to heal. It is treated as a *reporter* from a world of very slow laws that the central basin never had to learn before. The lichen’s “white branches of skin-thin existence” are not asked to grow faster or to grow more heroically into the basin center. They are allowed to continue at exactly their own speed, but now with *preference*.

Water films that once condense randomly upon the rim of stone now gather slightly more often along the curve of the lichen delta. The minerals that are leeched from the stone by the tiny rootlets under the lichen are not rushed to produce more; but they are *rotated*, so to speak, in the world’s accounting of odds. The same amount of change happens over the same amount of time, but now it happens in *this pattern* rather than any other pattern.

The pattern of the spiral on the fourth stone of the human (or near-human) arrangement, by the way, does not change. It is not made to glow or to be “upgraded” into something else. It is simply *recognized* as belonging to the same category of “shape” as the whorls of the lichen branches and the faint, swirling patterns of the cloud-stir above.

It is a pattern for “I was here,” *and* a pattern for “time will not forget this.”

4. It **allows a small increase of light**.

The light that seeps from nowhere-in-particular—the stone, the plants, the water—slightly increases its quality at the edges. It is not enough to become sunlight or moonlight. It is not enough to make shadows grow longer or to change the color of the frills of the moss-forest, which are still near-black.

But it is enough to make the *textures* of things more readable.

The etched texture on the stone under the lichen delta becomes more visible to the eye of the mind. If you sit beside the delta of lichen now, you can see *why* your fingers are brushing over tiny ridges you never noticed before. They are not grooves; they are the *writing* of the collaboration between stone and lichen, each curve of the rootlets’ path translated into extremely slight alterations of mineral grain.

If you look at higher points of the basin rim, at the place where the moss-forest overhangs the edge of the world, the light is now just enough to distinguish individual leaf-layers from one another. Before, they blurred into a single dark collar around the valley’s throat. Now, at the edge, the eye can distinguish frond from frond, frond from fur-like leaflet, leaflet from drip.

This does not change the plants’ behavior. They do not race upward into “better” light. They stay in their own drowse of existence. But they now *look* like they *know* they are being seen.

5. It **changes the sky’s relation to the valley without punishing the sky.**

The sky is not told to “be quiet” or “be happy” or “be better” by the wise power that now notices this bowl at the bottom of the world. The sky, after all, is not doing anything wrong; it is only being endless.

The roar of the clouds, instead of being hummed at the world *from* above, now *also* hums from below. The tiny hollows under the lichen-patches, each of them containing the faint condensation of possibility, become resonant chambers of a very low sort. The sound of the world’s sleep, which used to come from above only, now *also* comes from the stone of the basin floor, but thinner and not precisely in sync.

This small difference creates *interference*. The monotone of the sky now has a faint echo of itself from beneath the soil of the world, and the two slightly different time-signatures of the roar cross each other in the air above the basin floor like knife-edges of invisible weather.

Everywhere above the stone of the basin, where once no sound ever varied, now a very, *very* slight change in the tone of the world’s white noise repeats itself over time—not as a rhythm, but as a slow *warping* of rhythm.

If you listen to the sky hard enough for long enough, you may notice that the roar of the clouds *does* change now. It changes not into anything else, but into itself *heard* from another place and time, where the same roar was recorded by the under-surface of the world.

To stand at the center of the basin floor and to listen to the sky is now to be at the intersection of two *different* versions of the same unending sound.

6. It **gives the inhabitants of the rim a small increase of complexity.**

The silver-white insect-visits that flit from leaf to leaf in the dark frill-forest are not given “consciousness,” or “society,” or anything else they might not know how to use. They are given one more degree of freedom: *when* to appear.

Before, their appearance was almost random, a brief fluttering life of silver hair in the gloom of the leafed canopy. Now, they *tend* to appear a little more often when the double-layered noise of the world is at a particular point of its interference pattern—where, by the cruelest of world-physics, the “s” and “t” of the static meaning-words momentarily lose themselves and “almost” become sound.

They still appear and vanish like “drifting visits,” more brief than a thought and less than a message. But now they carry *timing* as part of their meaning.

The heavy four-legged creature that wears the same frilled darkness as the plants and does not seem to notice you is not made into a guardian or an oracle or a beast of burden. It senses nothing *new* in the cold of the basin feet or the heat of the soil under its fur. It is still only large enough to exist.

But it is now *ever so slightly* aware that *the edges of the world taste different*.

Before, its slow grazing over the moss-forest that rimmed the valley was simply a matter of moving from one patch of leaves to another, taking “one small mouthful at a time,” only because there was no reason to take more than one mouthful at a time.

Now, when it comes to the edge of the basin rim where the lichen delta has spilled over the stone, the frills on its body and around the moss of its mouth *sense* the new temperature difference of that air, and the creature will pause.

Not pause as in “freeze and wonder.” Pause as in “eat one leaf more slowly than the others,” and then “turn *slightly* away from the edge of the world” instead of continuing down the curve of stone.

That is the extent of its new “intelligence”: “I will not cross over here.”

This is not a fear. The creature does not feel fear. It does not feel “excited.” It is as it was before: *large enough to exist*. Now it also exists with *a boundary*.

7. It **sets the lichen delta as a new kind of anchor point.**

The lichen does not remember your footprints. The stone under the rest of the basin floor will still erase footprints in time, as it has always done, “slowly and forgetfully.”

But the zone of stone under and immediately around the lichen delta now *anchors* footprints. Not to preserve them as “pictures of the past,” as in concrete or amber, but to preserve them as *data*.

Any footprint you leave now on the stone of the delta’s edge will be subtly *different* from all others. The pattern of your weight’s distribution on the soles of your feet, the pressure point of where you favor to stand on one heel or the ball of your foot, the way you turn your toes toward or away from the center of the basin—these will not be erased into the smoothness of ageless stone.

Instead, they will be *copied*, into the faint, etched microstructure of the rock, where the lichen’s rootlets have already written “stone meets life.” The pattern of your footprint’s pressure on one particular spot of stone will be translated very slowly along the lichen delta’s “cultural network,” emerging at the other end of the delta as *a slightly different pattern of growth*.

Not visually different to your eye, at least not at first. The branches of the lichen will still rise white and thin and skin-soft, each like “dried ink soaking into damp paper.”

But over time, as the “message” of your footprint’s pressure pattern slowly travels through the lichen’s body of shared rootlets and filaments, one of two new branches will emerge *not exactly* where nature would have expected it to.

Exactly where, you cannot say. “Delta” is not as big as a city; it is as large as a handprint, maybe. But you have *no* way, from your position at the basin edge, to know which small upcoming finger of white lichen growth is now ever so slightly shaped by the pressure pattern of your foot on the stone years or decades or centuries ago.

The stone of the basin center will still forget your footprints. The stone beneath the lichen delta will never *remember* them as artifacts of “you.” It will remember them as *data* of pressure, which it then slowly translates into “branch there.”

So you, who are standing at this edge of the world’s bowl, have *become*:

“The branch that grew just outside of the expected pattern.”

Not “the hero” or “the savior” or “the destroyer.” “The branch-that-shifted-itself.”

8. It **chooses to stay ambiguous with the center of the basin.**

The center of the valley floor—where the water mirrors the sky and “the stone itself seems to beg the absence of life”—is not told to cease its “prayer for emptiness.”

A benevolent power may not *always* want to “fill” emptiness. Emptiness can also be holy.

The center of the basin floor remains under the same cloud of white noise of the world’s sleep, now under two *barely* different versions of that sleep, interacting but functionally unchanged. The water of the shallows continues to cry quietly in glass-cut pieces when it spills into one another.

Nothing *new* is required to happen in the center of the basin.

*Only* this:

If you ever decide to go to the center of the valley floor and *deliberately* plant something of your own there—if you *end* that “stone of the basin” which “remembers only itself” and begin to remember “you” instead of itself—then the world’s attention will become *sharper*.

The *monotone* layer of the sky’s roar will remain unchanged, but the *higher layer*—the modulated, pattern-noise of “This one is not yet” and “This edge is not less”—will become almost *indistinguishable* from explicit language.

“Whose foot are you?”

“Which seed are you?”

“Which branch of time are you?”

Nothing will be punished. You will not be struck down. Your imposed seed may not even “refuse to grow” as the stone of basin center once “begged the absence of life.” *Your* seed might very well *grow*.

But then, “the valley of the shallow bowls and the roaring sky” will become *not* forever.

It will become *your* garden.

The *old* rules of stone remembering “nothing” and “everything” will be remembered *by* the stone but not *acted* by the stone. *You* will become the new rule of recollection.

*That* is the choice the benevolent power leaves you:

*You* can decide if the valley of erased footprints and forgetful stone and white delta lichen *should remember*.

If you do not choose to go into the center of the basin floor and “plant” your own memory of yourself into its stone, then *all* of the things above will happen silently, over centuries, almost invisible to you or anyone else:

- The delta of lichen will continue to “tresspass” by the slowest of microscopic degrees.

- The sky’s monotone will continue to “own” itself and *also* be “owned by” the faint echo of itself from beneath the stone of the basin’s edge.

- The insect-visits of silver hair will continue to appear “whenever it is,” but now that “whenever” will tend slightly toward “whenever the sound of the world’s sleep is momentarily lost.”

- The heavy, unafraid creature of the moss rim will continue not to notice you, but now “notices” *not* going down the slope of the lichen-clad basin edge as *a* *path of not being taken*.

None of this will be “you doing.”

The *only* act of your own power that you will be able to perform here will be to go to the center of the basin floor, *kneel* upon that “pale and translucent” skin of “long-drowned thing,” and *place* something of yourself into that stone.

A seed of your own.

A word of your own.

A *fingerprint* of your own, *not* upon the lichen edge but upon *the* *center* of the world.

If you do that, then the world’s attention will *know* you have chosen for “this valley” *not* to be “only itself” anymore. *You* have become the “benevolent and wise power” of *this* microplace.

“Benevolent and wise power” does not necessarily think “being” is better than “not-being.” *It* stayed *out* of the center of the basin. *It* let “stone of the basin floor which remembers itself and not you” keep *being* that.

You *can*, if you want, override that.

If you *do*,

then *that* will be *what* you would have done,

if you were “the being who lives in this place,”

and “this place” *were* your own remembering,

from now on,

until *you* forget,

or *you* finally,

slowly,

allow the stone

to *remember* you,

as *another* faint groove of an instruction,

written on the fourth stone,

in *another* spiral,

smaller,

than the first.
