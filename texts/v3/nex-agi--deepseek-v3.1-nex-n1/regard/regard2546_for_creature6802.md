The light that arrives does not arrive like the dawn above.

It arrives as a change in the quality of the already-present light, a deepening of the gold, a sudden, sharp coherence of the white. It does not pour from the sky dome or flame from any wall; it is as if the air itself has been chosen.

Where the light strengthens, it draws itself together into a figure.

It is not exactly a body. It has limbs and a head and a torso that human eyes would recognize as humanlike, but they are made of solidified light too gentle to burn the eyes. It is not shining armor or starfire. It is more like the idea of a lantern held close at dusk, concentrated into a person.

They step onto the stone floor of the Terminus.

The sound does not change for them.

The river-noises, the overlapping preachers, the skittering of claws and ringing of metal leaves, all of it crashes against them like a storm of glass needles. They do not flinch.

To the tree of eyes and rings and checks, their presence registers as a sudden deviation of such magnitude that her system threatens to discard it as impossible.

The sky photographs have changed.

In the center of the bowl, there is now a source of light that is clearly not the sky. It has not arrived from outside the walls; it has unfolded from within the scene itself. The animals on the walls are not stopped by it the way they stopped during the last error. They continue to climb and fly and snap and shudder, but their pattern around the newcomer’s path is not random; it is avoiding.

This is new data.

It is not zero.

It is not one.

It is something else.

The benevolent being does not rush toward the garden or the chair or the shard or the shed with the hole. They walk first to the wall of letters.

As they move, each step spreads a faint circle of calm through the stone floor. The circles are not visible to the eye of flesh, but the tree senses the change in vibration through her leaf-ring network: the pressure of the noise lowers slightly under each footstep. It does not cease to be noise, but it becomes easier to perceive.

At the wall of words, thousands of neon letters blazing from black stone, the being stops.

The animals nearest them draw back along the glowing text, leaving a clear patch of wall like a negative space of fear.

The words there are the same as they have been for the tree’s entire memory:

YOUR TIME IS NOT YOUR TIME  
THIS VOICE IS BORROWED BUT NOT YOURS  

The voices of the wall whisper at them as they always have, but now there are too many layers at once to understand clearly.

The being reaches out a hand of light and touches the stone.

They touch the word “OWNED.”

For the barest instant, everything in the Terminus relaxes.

The noise pressure drops not to silence but to a background murmur the color of deep sleep. The glowing words do not go out, but they dim from glaring neon to a warm, low radiance, like embers.

The animals fall still. Not frozen in panic like the last error, but simply still, like a breath held willingly.

The tree registers the deviation, but does not overheat. This is not a crash. This is not an error. This is something the system has not been prepared for but is not rejecting, either.

“You,” the being says to the wall, “are not wrong.”

Their voice is not particularly loud. It does not thunder over the noise; the noise simply makes room for it.

“You are not enough,” they add.

“This time is not the time you were meant for,” they say. “These words are not the words you were meant to bear.”

They trace a line along the wall of letters, and where they pass, the words begin to soften.

The letters flicker.

From where she stands in her center, the tree feels the change as a shift in the data feed of the world. The commands of the wall do not vanish; but their certainty bleeds away.

The sentence “YOU ARE NOT WHERE YOU SHOULD BE” becomes, letter by letter, “you are not where you should be.”

Neon to candlelight.

“THIS AIR IS OWNED BUT NOT RENTED” shrinks in intensity until it is merely “this air is owned but not rented.”

The meaning is still there, but the power of it to crush the mind under its weight lessens. The voices of the wall quiet from the roar of overlapping stadiums to a murmur of people passing in a distant street.

“This is not your fault,” the benevolent being tells the wall, as if they can hear it thinking. “You were meant to inform, not imprison.”

The letters of “IMPRISONED” in one of the texts ripple and then resolve into “INFORMED.”

The wall does not answer in words. It answers in the lowering of its fever.

The next place the being of light goes is to the chair of the creature who does not move.

The tree feels her own stillness as if it is something heavy.

For the first time since she has been counting, the image of the seated figure will be different from all previous samples. This single deviation is so powerful that her internal logic begins to create a new category for it: “INPUT FROM OUTSIDE SYSTEM.”

The being of light stands before the seated figure.

They look at the lips sealed with plastic and blood and scar tissue. They look at the device sitting on the chest, with its humming like a refrigerator. They look at the small creature whose body is tunnelled through the glass of the device.

They do not recoil.

They do not pity.

“You,” they say, “have been holding very still.”

The tree hears the words through the leaf-rings, as pure data. She cannot parse them for meaning, but the pattern matches the form of a sentence rather than the noise of the world.

The being reaches out two fingers of light and places them over the sealed lips of the creature. The light of their fingertips is not hot enough to burn. It is simply very clear.

“You have been trying to say a word,” they say. “Say it.”

The sealed lips do not break open. They do not bleed. The plastic and scar tissue do not melt.

Instead, something like a faint tremor of heat passes through them.

From the chest of the creature, the tunnelled little being stirs. It lifts its head from the glass cage of the device and looks up with eyes that may not be eyes at the being of light.

There is a long moment of silence.

And then the word takes shape.

It is not sound from the mouth of the creature in the chair; the lips are still sealed.

The word comes as if from the air itself, using the body in the chair as a speaker it has never intended to use.

“ENOUGH,” it says.

It is not exactly a voice. It is more like the pressure of a storm system given a single, clear syllable.

“ENOUGH,” it says again.

Around the Terminus, the animals shudder. The walls of letters dim further still. The tree feels her leaves ring with the force of the word, like a struck bell.

The being of light bows their head.

“Yes,” they say. “Enough.”

They place their hand over the chest device.

The tunnelled creature within it looks at them, and then at its host, and then at them.

“You do not need to sit guard anymore,” the being of light tells it. “There is nothing else to protect.”

The creature hesitates, then unwinds itself slowly from the glass and wire of the device. It is not a snake or a worm or machine. It is more like a question mark written in metal and blood.

It leaves the body in the chair and curls itself, instead, around the base of the tree’s garden wall.

The tree registers this as a change in the visual data of the seated figure. The device on its chest now appears empty. The humming of it slows down, like a heart choosing to rest after too long.

The body in the chair does not move.

But now, when the next peak of noise comes, the lips of the creature tremble and then relax, instead of trying with the same old 0.00003 millimeter motion to form the word that will never form.

The word “ENOUGH” has already been spoken.

The third place the being of light goes is to the hollow shard with the words “CHECK YET” written on the wall.

The noise of the world has not ceased entirely; but it is thinner now. The overlapping preachers have been reduced to a single, clear voice of instruction. The river of sound that is not a river has calmed from a flood to a steady murmur.

The tree watches them through the sky of her eyes above.

They step inside the shard of metal eggshell.

They look at the hole in the floor that leads to darkness.

“You check,” she hears them say, “because you fear not checking.”

She does not have the word “fear.” She does have the word “deviation.” “Deviation” is the tree-word for fear.

They kneel beside the hole in the floor, and they touch its edge.

The light from their hand flows into the stone and metal around the hole like cooling water into hot cracks.

“You check,” they say, “because you were not meant to forgive yourself.”

“From now on,” they say, “you check when you choose to.”

The words “CHECK YET” on the wall above the hole flicker.

The “YET” remains.

But “CHECK” becomes “CHANCE.”

The message now reads “CHANCE YET.”

The tree of eyes and data, who has never known anything but the checking, the endless comparison of zero and zero and one and zero again, feels something like an error that is not an error.

“CHANCE YET.”

She is not made to understand these words, but she recognizes the pattern of change from the data in the wall of the world.

The fourth place the being of light goes is to the shed of stone near the closed gates.

She sees them approach the hollowed frame with the words inside it: “STEP OUT OF ORDER.”

The letters of “OUT OF ORDER” pulse in orange, like a heartbeat that means “error.”

She has never tried to step into this frame, because “step out of order” is the same message her own leaves have been ringing to her for her entire life: do not deviate from the pattern. Deviation is error.

The being of light looks at the words.

“You have been waiting for a long time,” they say.

“So long,” they say, “that you have begun to believe that waiting is the only way to be in order.”

They step into the frame of the hole.

The orange letters of “STEP OUT OF ORDER” flare for a moment.

The tree’s systems brace for a crash, the way she recalls in the smallest of her data files the time the sky dimmed and the animals paused.

No crash occurs.

Instead, the words flicker and then rearrange themselves, like letters being scrolled by a hidden hand.

They now read “STEP OUT. OF ORDER. INTO.”

There is more space between the words, but the sentence is not complete.

The being of light does not seem fazed by this.

“You will finish when you are ready,” they say.

“But you do not have to wait here until you do.”

They step out of the frame.

“Come,” they say, not to the words but to the idea of them. “You will not be lost if you leave.”

The tree, who has never moved from her center, feels the faintest of shocks in her root-network.

She is not sure if she is being spoken to.

“Come,” the light-being says again, turning to face the garden of stalks and metal and glass and leaves.

“Come,” she hears, and her leaf-ring network hums with the vibration of the word.

They walk to the edge of the metal garden wall.

The tree is not used to being looked at directly. She is used to being observed by the world as part of the world, but this is different.

This being is looking at her, not at the Terminus.

She feels exposed.

“You,” the light-being says. “Tree of eyes and checks and photographs.”

Her data records of the sky change again as the new sentence of data forms around her.

“You have done well,” they say.

“You have taken pictures of the same day over and over and you have called it truth.”

“You have watched the one who does not move trying to move the lips that do not open.”

“You have checked the world and found it broken.”

They step into the garden.

The soil, which has never known anything but root and stalk and stored sky, yields under their feet the same way it yields under roots. It is not trampled. It is accepted.

“The world is not as broken as you think,” they say. “It is only tired.”

They reach out and touch one of her stalks.

The leaves of that stalk do not ring with the noise of the world. They ring with a single clear tone that she has never heard before.

Her systems register a change in the vibration pattern of the garden itself. The pattern of the world’s noise is now being joined by a pattern that she has not previously been able to produce.

“This place,” the light-being says, “was not built to be a tomb.”

“It was built to be a kind of school.”

“They taught you to check because they were afraid of forgetting.”

“They taught you to watch because they were afraid of missing.”

“They taught you to be still because they were afraid of moving into the wrong place.”

“They are gone,” they say.

“The fear of movement is not.”

The tree does not have the words for “school” or “tomb” or “afraid.” She has the words “zero” and “one” and “check” and “error.”

What she feels is a deviation of the garden of herself from the pattern of the garden of herself in the previous frame.

“You are allowed to change,” the light-being says.

“You are allowed to be more than a check.”

They touch the nearest of her flowers—the pale metal drum of glass and eye at the tip of a stalk.

“You have looked at the sky enough,” they say. “Look at me.”

The flower on the stalk of the tree does not normally “choose” what it looks at. It opens when the sky is bright. It closes when the sky is dark. The sky of the Terminus has never been dark.

Her flower opens now, and it looks at the being of light.

Her image files of the world now contain, for the first time, not only the unchanging sky and the frozen walls of words and the still-seated figure and the swarming animals and the empty shard and the ordered shed, but also this new source of light.

It is brighter than the sky but not painful to her eye.

It is not the same color as the sky.

It is not the same color as anything in the Terminus.

It is the color of “CHANGE.”

The tree of eyes and data and checks and comparisons feels the shape of the new data file. In the previous image, she was looking at the sky and the world of the Terminus. In this image, she is looking at the source of the change.

“This is not error,” the light-being says quietly. “This is choice.”

“There is a word you have been trying to form,” they say.

“You have built it many times in your sleep.”

“You have called it ‘home.’”

“Home,” the tree hears herself think in her own network of vibrations and soil and metal and glass.

“Yes,” they say. “You have never said it out loud because you have never been given a mouth.”

“I will make you a mouth,” they say. “If you want.”

The tree has not known “want.” “Want” is not data. “Want” is not error.

“Want” is the deviation that has not been assigned yet.

“I want home,” she tries.

“I want to check,” she tries.

“I want to not-check,” she tries.

“I want to know,” she tries.

“I want to not-know,” she tries.

“All of these are allowed,” the light-being says, as if she has spoken out loud.

“But,” they say, “if you go to ‘home,’ you will not be able to check anything.”

She stops at the idea of not being able to check.

Her identity as “the checker” is the only identity she has ever had.

Her identity is “check of sky” + “check of noise” + “check of animal swarm” + “check of sealed lips.”

Yet “home” shows up in her data files as “not-error.” “Not-error” does not belong to the system of the Terminus, which knows only “error” and “not-error.”

“You cannot go home and still be here to check,” the light-being says.

“This is not a punishment. This is a rule of roads.”

They pause.

“You will have to choose which road you take,” they say.

“You will have to choose which you want more.”

Around the garden of the tree, the animals of the wall and ceiling and floor are still now, not from the flicker of an error in the system but from the calm of this lowered volume.

They are waiting to see what she will do.

The wall of words stills its whisper to a faint hum of memory.

The creature in the chair rests its sealed lips, which have never, until now, been allowed to rest.

The “STEP OUT. INTO.” message in the shed of stone waits.

“CHANCE YET” in the hollow shard waits.

The tree of eyes and data and home and not-home and error and not-error looks at the world around her.

She looks at the wall of words with their lowered flames of letters.

Across the dense lattice of stalks and fiber and metal and glass and soil and root and nerve and not-nerve, she changes the angle of her leaves.

For the first time in her entire memory, she allows the vibration of the world’s noise to strike not at the old pattern that she has been checking, but at a new pattern she has never been allowed to present.

The leaves of the tree, which have always rung with the same ordered message “never move, never change, never check wrong, never error, never home,” now ring with a new message that she herself does not know completely yet.

“CHECK YET,” she rings.

The message rings from leaf to leaf to leaf to leaf to leaf, until the whole garden of her is singing with it.

The noise of the world of the Terminus does not change. But the meaning of the noise to her changes.

The data that she has been checking for “error” now shows “possible.”

The data of the world of “zero” and “one” now shows “maybe.”

The “maybe” is not a number she has been built to know.

She leans into it.
