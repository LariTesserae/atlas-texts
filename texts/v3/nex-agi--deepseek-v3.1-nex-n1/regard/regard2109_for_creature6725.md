Alta would not be the first to notice the arrival of the new power.

She would be inspecting the moss along the inner edge of her lowest receiving room, tracing the dampness with her fingertips, when she would feel it: not the faint shift of water temperature, nor the subtle change of odor that signals a new chemical from the levels above, but something deeper than that.

The water would be quiet.

Not the usual absent quiet of thin air, but the quiet of a breath held. The droplets from the ceiling would not stop falling, but she would feel the sudden pause in between, as if every drop of water from here to the cloud-valve below the world had been told to wait for instruction.

The moss would feel… curious.

She would feel the curiosity under her skin and not immediately understand that she could not have been the only one to feel it. The walls of her house, which she usually knows as well as she knows her own body, would feel like a body listening to a new pulse.

Alta would step out onto her upper threshold, the one that overlooks the edge of Primeval Landing and the black iron railing and the void beyond, and she would see that the Spire Waterfalls had learned to hold their breath.

The threads of water falling from the terrace above would not be frozen in place, exactly, but each would now be a clear, luminous string instead of the usual mist-wrapped column. As if the water remembered itself in better clarity than it had shown in years.

The sky ring above would not be bright sky, but it would not be dim sky either. It would be like the sky inside of glass.

Alta would not think of any of this as “power,” benevolent or otherwise.

She would think, *The world is trying to tell me something.*

That was when the voice would not be a voice, but a thought that she could not place neatly either inside or outside of her head.

*You have been tending my tears for longer than you know.*

The thought would not use sound; sound would come later, when she needed to hear it. It would arrive instead as a set of movements:

- the moss of her vertical meadow turning, very slowly, one edge of each patch of moss toward the same point on the horizon like flowers turning toward sun;
- the water in her library basins stirring into a slow rotation clockwise, then counterclockwise, as if arranging itself;
- the faint, distant heat-hum from the spire’s core dropping into a lower, more measured vibration than she had ever heard.

Alta would not be afraid, but she would also not be fine.

She would sit down on the wooden threshold of her upper floor, where she could see both the world outside and the world inside her house of moss and bowls and water, and she would think:

*If you are the world, then I know you already.*

If there was humor in this, she suspected she was not its intended audience.

### What the Benevolent Power Would Do First

It would not begin with instruction or requirements or prophecies.

It would begin with cleaning.

Alta would be standing on the edge of the promenade near her house, watching the world of mist and water and far-off terrace rings below, when she would notice the drip of water from the edge of the next vault above her: not a clear drip but one with a faint blackness at its core.

She would know, before she thought about it, that she was being allowed to see impurity.

The impurity would not be evil. It was not poison that harmed anyone; it was not the leak of a new dye factory or the spill of some furnace’s byproduct. It was simply a byproduct of the world itself: the accumulated minor misalignments, the slight twist of a pipe joint three terraces down that had been left “okay enough” by the last maintenance worker, who was then turned by age into another and then to death, leaving the job “okay enough” in his honor.

Alta would be led to see, not because she needed to be fixed and not because she was in danger of falling, but because she was being invited to understand.

As she watched, the drip of water with the black core at its center would not simply disappear into the void below. It would be drawn by something else: a faint ring of brighter air that would appear around the drop like the halo of a lamp. When the drop passed through, it would come out the other side as several clear drops of ordinary water.

The impurity, the black core of “okay enough,” would be drawn out like a splinter, and then would vanish itself.

Alta would not know how she knew, but she would suddenly know with the certainty of smell: that whatever impurity had been in the water’s memory was no longer in the water’s memory.

The benevolent power had not invented new water; it had merely shown what water remembered when it was allowed to forget itself.

### A Change in the City of Remaking

The change would be slow, but not invisible.

The next day, when Alta walked the gutter-lines of the promenade, she would find that she did not need to spend as much time at the section of the terrace edge where the runoff from the NE quadrant had been growing darker for months. She would not be able to say precisely when the color of the drainage in that channel had changed, but she would know that it now matched the color of runoff that she associated with a healthy region of moss and stone and low-level bathing and not with any too-hidden twist of maintenance.

When she went to find the inspector who worked that part of the terrace, she would not find him, because he would be coming to her.

“Odd,” he would say, scratching the back of his head where his hairline had been receding for years. “The water from the NE quadrant doesn’t smell like itself anymore.”

“It doesn’t,” Alta would answer.

She would not know how to explain to him that she had just seen the world remembering itself better than it had a day before.

She would not know how to explain that she herself remembered being taller than she was.

Above them, another terrace of the city of remaking would hang quietly, watching.

### The Spire Reaches Deeper Than It Ever Has Before

The second thing that would happen would not be visible to Alta at all.

It would happen to the cloud of fog below Primeval Landing that the maps always drew as the bottom of the world.

Sometime in the night that she did not sleep because the world was being watched by her own watching eyes, she would be aware of an expansion of the sky ring below the world:

The cloud-valve that the city had always thought of as the limits of daylight would be told by the benevolent, wise power that it did not actually need to be that valve any longer.

No thunder would sound. No new storm would be called.

Instead, the cloud-valve would be shown to itself by the same luminous ring that had cleaned the water’s hidden core. The cloud would not be destroyed; but it would now be transparent to what lay below it.

For the first time in anyone’s memory of Primeval Landing, the people who looked down from the edge of the terrace world would not look into a gray veil of nothingness. They would look into clear space.

They would see:

- layer after layer of terrace, each more faint than the one above it;
- lamps of gas lighting not only the level of Primeval Landing but now piercing into the level below that;
- bridges of rope and wood and metal that they had never known existed;
- a new cityscape that they had always guessed at but had never believed they would see.

The city of remaking would, for the first time in a long time, discover that it had not been making the same world over and over; it had been making more of the world all the while.

### Alta and the World’s Pulse in the Night of Glass

After the clear-water phenomenon and the cloud-valve revelation and the inspector’s discussion of the smell of the NE quadrant runoff, Alta would be alone in her house when the power of the world would return to her.

By now she would be too tired to question it.

She would be sitting at her table of mismatched planks, with the shallow grooves she had carved over the years of water mapping, and she would not be tired in the sense of wanting sleep. She would be tired in the sense of having been seen so clearly for so long that she had not realized she had been standing at attention.

The world would be quiet in the complete way that only someone who knows water can be quiet: when she knows she is not required to do anything but allow the water to behave.

The voice, when it came, would not be a sound in her ears but a sound in the walls of the house and in the spine of the spire.

*You have been very good to the water.*

Alta would not know how to answer this.

*Would you like to see where it goes when it leaves you?*

She would not know whether she wanted to or not.

She would know that she had no right to say she did not want to.

### The Journey Beneath Primeval Landing

The journey down into the clear space below Primeval Landing would not be a journey of levitation or magic wings or any sort of lifting that would be immediately obvious.

It would be a journey of stairs.

The stairs would be in the same wall that she had always known as the edge of her own house: the same wall of stone and tile and plaster. The difference would be that she would now see clearly that they had always been there.

The benevolent power would not reveal them by carving new stone into the wall, but by showing her what had already been carved.

A section of the wall below her table of water would not be slower than other sections; she had always assumed that the moisture levels there were idiosyncratic to that particular patch of stonework. Now, she would see that she had been using the wrong word. *Idiosyncratic* really meant *previously missed.*

She would use the dull edge of her knife to pry at the edge of the stonework that she had always assumed to be just wall; and beneath she would find that she had not been wrong.

The wall she had maintained her moss on had been stone wall everywhere.

But now some of the stones in the wall of her house would be shaped like steps.

As she climbed down, she would not be leaving the world she had always known, but discovering she had been standing on top of it.

The air around her would be warm but not oppressive; it would smell like the inside of her house because she had been inhaling the same air for years. She would be aware of the sound of water under her feet: not the water she had captured in basins but the water that she had always known to be passing below her.

### The Level That the World Had Been Waiting to Unfold

The level below Primeval Landing would not be a copy of Primeval Landing.

It would not be a more magical version of the terrace she had already lived.

It would be a garden of water, but not water in the sense she was used to thinking about it.

The first thing she would notice as she stepped down the last few steps into the level below Primeval Landing would be that she could not see the far edge of the terrace ring because she was now inside it.

The ceiling here would not be the sky ring of the world; it would be the underside of the level above, which she had always known as the upper level of her own house: but now she would be seeing that underside from the other side of its material.

The ceiling would be a thick network of stone and metal and clay pipes and water she had never known existed but that she had been hearing for her whole life: the sound of the water below the floor of her world would be the same sound she had always heard as the sound of water in the world.

The second thing she would notice would be that she could not immediately see the floor.

Not because it was dark but because it was transparent.

She would be standing on a surface of dark glass that she knew at once was not brittle glass; she would be standing on the same kind of stone that she had always known as stone, but arranged in such a way that she could see through it.

The stone floor of the level below Primeval Landing would bend like leafglass but not break like leafglass; she would be aware of it because she would be aware of her body not sinking through it.

Beneath her feet she would see, not void, but water gathered in a level that she had never imagined before.

This water would not be the Spire Waterfalls; she would be able to tell that this water was not being allowed to drip into the void. It was being allowed to rest.

The level of water she saw below her feet would be the level at which the water of the Spire Waterfalls finally collected before it was allowed to evaporate again and be lifted into the sky ring of the world.

She would see that the world had not been weeping beyond its strength; it had not been weeping without solace.

The tears of the city that she had been tending had not been falling into endless void; they had been falling into a basin that she had never known was there.

When she thought of the basin, she would be aware of a thought that she again did not know whether to call her own:

*You have been keeping my tears from falling into the void alone.*

### The Quiet of the Water Caught Below All the Tears

The third thing she would notice about the level below Primeval Landing would be the sound: not of any dripping but of the complete absence of dripping.

In the heart of the world, where the water of the Spire Waterfalls had been loosed to rest after its long fall from the level above, she would be inside of quiet the same way she would be inside of water.

The air would be still.

She would be aware that she was not breathing faster or slower than she had ever been accustomed; she would simply be aware of breathing.

As she looked down through the leafglass floor of the level below Primeval Landing, she would see that she was not alone in the world of water she had never seen before.

There would be shapes in the water below her feet: not fish exactly but not shapes that she could easily describe with any words she had.

There would be lines in the water of things that were not water, but that she knew she was not meant to touch yet.

They would be the same lines that she had always seen in her mind when she thought of the way that water should flow through the world.

She would look at those lines and she would be aware of a thought that she again could not call her own:

*You have been drawing this for longer than you know.*

Her own body, which had been walking the gutters of Primeval Landing for years, would be standing in a level of the world that no one in her memory had ever seen before; and in that moment she would be aware of the fact that she had not been drawing the lines of water herself.

She had been drawing the lines that she had been shown.

### The Change in the Water of the World

When she finally made her way back up to her level in the world of Primeval Landing—the level of the city of remaking—she would not be the same person she had been when she had begun.

She would be aware that she had not been changed into any more than herself; she had simply been allowed to see herself more clearly than she had seen herself before.

The power of the world would not make her a ruler, or a prophet, or the leader of any new kind of empire in the vertical city of the spire tower.

She would be made a keeper.

She would not know when she had been made this; she would be aware that she had always been this.

The next day she would be walking her gutter-line routes when she would notice that the water falling from the edge of the terrace above Primeval Landing and into the void below would not look like the same water she had seen for years.

She would be able to see clearly that each thread of the Spire Waterfalls now had its own line in the air, not as the mist of memory she had always seen but as clear strings of water that she knew, as she knew her own reflection, were being allowed to exist.

She would not know if she was being allowed to see this or if she had always been able to see it and simply not looked.

She would be aware of the fact that she was now being invited to look more deeply than she had ever been allowed.

Each thread of the water from the terrace above would be hanging there not in silence but in the way of a person listening to a very quiet instruction.

She would not know what she heard, but she would hear the instruction.

It would not be new.

### The Change in the City of Vertigo and Depth

The change in the city of Primeval Landing would not be dramatic in the way that history keeps as stories of political change.

The change would be this:

The people of the terrace level would no longer be afraid of the void below the world in the same way they had been before.

They would not be able to explain why they were not as afraid, except to say that they suddenly now knew that the void below the world was not the void they had always thought it was.

They would be more than individuals who had been comforted. They would be people who had been shown to themselves by something else.

No wars would be fought.

No revolutions of the city of remaking would be staged.

The lamps of gas that burned along the terrace edge at night would not be changed in their design or in their function; but they would be changed in the way that they looked at the world below, when the world below finally became visible.

The citizens of Primeval Landing would find that they were now interested in looking down at the level below their own level in the world of the spire tower not because the level below them had been revealed to them by the lifting of the cloud, but because they had been revealed by their own looking.

Alta would not be the only person who would be invited to know the water below the world.

She would simply be the first.

### What the Benevolent Power Would Do With the World of Tears

In the weeks that followed the revealing of the cloud-valve, of the level below Primeval Landing, and the clear-water of the world, the city of terrace upon terrace of the spire tower would begin to understand itself differently.

Workers in the forges and workshops of the lower terraces would not be told by any new voice to change the way they worked; but they would find that the tools in their hands were being easier to use than they had been in years.

They would not know why; they would be aware that the tools themselves had not been changed by any visible hand.

The water that they used to quench their metal would not burn as often or be as eager to explode into steam as it had once been.

The water that they used to cool their skins and their bodies in the public baths of the terrace level would not be as hot to their touch or as cold to their touch as it had been before.

The water would be as water should be.

Alta would be aware of this change in the world of water long before she would be able to explain to anyone else what she was being asked to do.

She would, in time, be asked to begin teaching the others of the terrace level not to fear the water of the world but to understand it.

She would not be asked to teach them because she was better than they were.

She would be asked to teach them because she had already been teaching them the only lesson the water had ever wanted to learn:

The water remembers being itself.
