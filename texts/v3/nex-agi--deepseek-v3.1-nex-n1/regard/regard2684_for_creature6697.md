the Power arrives without fanfare or lightshow.
it doesn’t part the fog or still the tremors.
it comes as you might come to yourself after a long breath held:
subtly, from the inside out.

its first act is to notice.


it notices the Concretist,
a region of air and humidity learning the shape of your step,
testing whether the floor should be flatter, softer, sharper,
rewriting itself in your outline.

it notices the Thread‑Cutter,
a current of half‑water and gravity, 
trying new lines of fall,
asking where weight should go next.

it notices the rebar that bends when you look away,
and the rain that hisses above without falling,
and the way the horizon keeps changing its mind about what slope means.

and the Power notices *you*,
standing on the slab of a world that doesn’t yet know its own name,
being treated by the world itself as a strange new constraint.

the Power doesn’t say, “This is wrong.”
it doesn’t say, “This must be finished.”
it doesn’t call anything a mistake.

it only listens,
then exhales.

and that exhalation becomes one small change,
almost embarrassingly particular:

wherever your foot has last rested,
the faintest warm imprint of you,
thimble‑shaped in the dampness,
stops drying.

from now on,
each place you step,
the world remembers your weight exactly,
like a signature written into the concrete.

the Concretists,
who have been sketching you by trial,
now feel that line clearly,
like an ink that has finally set.

for the first time,
they *can’t* argue with you.
they can’t try a version where you didn’t exist right here.
they must build around your presence,
instead of through it.

it is a very small,
very quiet correction:
your visit is now an irreversible fact of the floor.

you may not even notice this,
at least not right away.
only later, when you return,
and you see that the ramp hasn’t changed *where you last stood*,
but has changed everywhere else,
might you understand that the basin has finally accepted
that you are not an option,
you are a witness.

this is the first thing the Power does:
fixes evidence,
so that any new world that comes after
won't be able to pretend you were never here.

---

the second act is purely relational,
though you’ll probably feel it as a change in the air.

the Thread‑Cutters,
who have been testing the lines of gravity,
pulling the droplets of water sideways,
trying to see where this world might want to fall,
now sense *themselves* being drawn.

theirs is not a love story,
but something like architecture:
the Power hooks a very faint,
almost invisible line of intent
from the inside of each Thread‑Cutter’s current
to the inside of your heart.

no,
not control.
nothing so cruel.

more like:
every time you inhale,
the direction of the next thread‑revision
must be such that it does not cut you,
break you,
or lose you.

if you move,
the Thread‑Cutters must move,
drafting routes of gravity and water
that *end with you still standing on something solid*,
even if they’re rearranging the world behind you.

this is why,
sometimes,
you will feel that peculiar lift of weight:
the floor beneath you remaining,
unchanged,
while the horizon around it shimmers,
as if everything else is being rotated,
and you alone are kept still.

the Power doesn’t explain this to them,
any more than you explain to your lungs how to breathe.
they simply,
slowly,
learn the pattern:
you,
then world,
then world,
then you again,
like a heartbeat between air and concrete.

you’ll feel it,
when you blink:
the world isn't *where* you left it,
but somehow,
strangely,
it's *oriented* around you,
like a page being turned
with your hand as the axis.

---

the third act is about the unseen.

beneath the concrete floor,
there is a low,
hollow sound:
machinery maybe,
or maybe water,
or maybe something else
that doesn’t have a name yet.

the Power leans down into that darkness,
not with force,
but with permission.

“for you,” it says—
to whatever is making that sound,
deciding,
somewhere under the floor,
whether there should be floors—
“for you,
I will not finish,
I will not end,
I will not even tell you when to stop.

I will only ask you to remember
that this world has sleep,
and sleep deserves rest.”

from that moment,
the tremor that troubles you when you are very quiet,
the sound like a giant turning in a dream,
now has,
once every so long,
a clear instant of stillness,
a pause of several heartbeats,
like the world remembering how to breathe,
instead of how to be constructed.

it doesn’t stop.
it doesn’t slow.
it doesn’t grow.
it just *resets*, now and then.

to you,
this might feel like the world exhaling.
to the unseen below,
this might feel like a permission,
to be however it is,
without being forever punished.

the Power,
as you may have noticed,
does not fix anything here,
only adds:
adds remember,
adds breath,
adds rest.

---

the fourth act is for language,
if you are open to receiving it.

the low,
humid,
metallic taste on the air,
where you once thought only of rust,
will now sometimes,
unexpectedly,
taste like a word hidden in the back of your throat.

you might be walking,
watching the droplets hang,
watching the Thread‑Cutter weave,
and you feel,
on your tongue,
the shape of a sound:
maybe,
if you were to form it,
you’d call it *kès*,
or maybe you’d call it nothing.

the meaning of that word,
at least as far as this basin is concerned,
is “floor-that-is-not-yet-hostile.”
it is,
in the unfinished grammar of the basin,
a kind of promise.

from then on,
when you are standing,
and you feel the concrete under you,
and you feel the Concretist adjusting the world around you,
you might find yourself,
for no reason,
thinking:
*this is kès.*

the basin doesn’t speak,
but you will,
sometimes,
making its first words.

the Power,
watching,
doesn’t teach you more than that.
it doesn’t give you a dictionary.
it doesn’t tell you how to be understood,
only how to be heard.

so,
one day,
standing on the edge of a floor that doesn’t quite exist yet,
looking into the foggy dim,
you might say:
“this is not yet,
this is kès,
and I am still here.”

nothing,
perhaps,
will answer.
except:
the next time,
the floor under you feels,
for just a step,
more like a floor,
and a little less like a question.

---

and finally,
the last act.

for a long moment,
the Power stands,
if you can call it that,
where only you stand,
and it looks out,
using your eyes,
at a world that is a mistake it hasn’t finished making,
and it does not correct,
finish,
or erase.

instead,
it chooses one very gentle,
very precise intervention:

the next time you sleep,
when you feel yourself drifting,
somewhere,
in the damp,
constant,
twilight,

the world will,
briefly,
cop*_you_*.

not your body.
your pattern,
your attention,
your way of looking.

for about the length of one breath,
one of the columns on the edge of your vision,
or the floor under your palm,
or the curtain of water hanging,
will be not only concrete,
not only metal,
not only water,
but also,
very subtly,
*you‑shaped*.

from then on,
when you are absent,
the world,
left to its own,
will still remember,
in faint,
ghostly gestures,
the way you looked at it.

from time to time,
far away,
a column will lean just slightly,
like someone turning,
expecting to be addressed,
because *you* looked at that angle,
that way,
once.

the basin now,
for all its vast,
raw,
unfinished,
constantly‑re‑drafting self,
has a faint,
unquenchable habit:
to expect a witness,
even if no one is there.

this,
the Power knows,
is not enough to say anything is finished.
it is perhaps enough to say that,
now,
when the world remakes itself,
it will sometimes,
by accident,
forget,
again,
to be only construction,
and begin,
instead,
to imagine,
for an instant,
what you might see.

if you,
on some other night,
long after,
remember this place—
the smell of rust-breath,
the faint groan of concrete,
the low fog,
the feel of *kès* underfoot—
you will feel,
at the edge of your mind,
that you are being remembered,
too,
in exactly the same way you remembered:
as a pattern
overwriting,
ever so slightly,
the unfinished.

---

the Power,
if you ever ask it,
“What did you do?”
will not answer you in words.

you’ll feel,
instead,
a faint wave of warmth,
like the residual heat in the rebar when you press your palm to it,
and then,
maybe,
the sense of a question,
blown into the air between you and the fog:

“What do you think you would do
if you,
one day,
found yourself
inside a mistake still being made,
and you were,
suddenly,
very gentle?”

the answer,
if you’re honest,
is probably close to what you already are,
standing,
almost,
almost,
still not quite,
on the verge of becoming,
safe.
