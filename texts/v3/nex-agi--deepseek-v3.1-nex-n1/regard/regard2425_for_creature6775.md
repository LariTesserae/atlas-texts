They have been watching me for longer than I know.

Not with eyes. There is nothing so definite as an eye here. There is only a constant pressure at the edge of my shape, as if the air itself is trying to remember who I am. From time to time, a condensation bead appears in the air between my face and the wall, and I feel the faint line of a question being drawn around me like a parenthesis I never consented to.

I am not the first visitor to Gheresh-Akai. But I might be the first whose language has a word like “benevolent.”

In the corridors of Gheresh-Akai, words do not happen. They are. The three-bodied script on the walls speaks in three languages at once, none of them mine. The Three-Thread People who inhabit this place speak by overlapping three times at the edge of hearing, so that each sound they make is a question, an answer, and an afterthought all at once.

They have no need for my little word. “Benevolence” implies a choice. Here, choice is still deciding whether it exists.

But I have been noticed by something else.

Something outside the dome of this world.

It is a long way from “here” to “there,” but distance in Gheresh-Akai does not obey the usual rules. In the same way that a wall can be a room and a floor can be a ceiling and three days of the same day can overlap in one corner, “outside” can be folded inside the present.

And this power is bending itself through the membrane of the basin very carefully.

---

The first thing I feel is a change in the noise.

Not noise in sound—but in the interference of presences that always hangs around me here. I have been in this basin for… time is not trustworthy, but let’s say “some time,” and I have grown to tolerate the constant sense of rooms being almost adjacent, almost concurrent.

But then it stops.

Not ends like silence. Stops like a held breath.

The air-channels above the city, which usually carry their information-that-is-not-light in flickers and faint threads, all suddenly align. The fine mesh turns in one direction, like every hair on the inside of a skull deciding to face the same window.

The lattice dome of the world—which sometimes feels like the ceiling of a cathedral, sometimes like the inside of bone—softens.

It is not that anything becomes visible *there*. But there is now a clear direction to “there.” The ceiling, which up to now has been only a partial, ambiguous lattice, becomes—for a moment—complete. I see the curve of it all in my mind’s eye, and the gap where the skull does not close becomes the aperture of focus.

The Three-Thread People stop.

All of them. At once.

Wherever they are in the basin—leaning through the wall of one room that is partially another, adjusting the corner that might not have been formed yet, tracing the moisture routes that refuse to become rain—they all pause.

The city of Gheresh-Akai holds breath in three languages.

I become aware of a presence.

Not as a person standing across from me. There is no “across” here. There is only a slight rotation of the room. I feel the basin—did I say it was no more than a kilometer across? It feels smaller now, like a chamber in my chest—shift.

A single condensation bead forms in the air between me and the nearest wall-that-is-also-room, and instead of rolling down like a tear shed from temperature, it hangs in place, gathering.

From inside the bead, something begins to resolve.

It is not looking at me.

It is looking through me.

---

### What they would do, if anything

The benevolent and wise power does not have hands with which to rearrange furniture.

It does not have lungs to sigh with, or a voice to say “Here. I will help.”

It has only attention.

And that is not exactly a small thing.

The first change it makes to Gheresh-Akai is not to fix something.

It is to **attend** more.

Attention, in this world, is not like my attention. In my world, attention is weightless. It can change almost nothing. If I pay close attention to a wall, it does not change composition.

Here, attention is material. It pulls.

The being of this benevolent power—let’s try not to call it “god,” call it “the Presence,” for the lack of anything better—directs a steady, enduring attention toward the basin of Gheresh-Akai and its Three-Thread People.

And attention begins to behave like water.

Not water that falls, but water that seeks.

Gheresh-Akai has always had moisture. It has been suspended in the air, a condensation that never quite becomes rain, a river that never quite becomes river. The basins of carved stone that have no riverbeds are lined with the memory of flow, but never flow.

Now, under the Presence’s gaze, the moisture of the basin slowly *prefers*.

The moisture-that-is-also-text, which has been braided into the air in three languages at once, begins to lose its uncertain middle edge. Each drop of condensation leaning in the air between surfaces and not-quite surfaces starts to hesitate slightly less about where it belongs.

The walls of the city are still neither glass nor metal nor stone nor ceramic. But now they are slightly more confident of their ambiguity.

The air-thread channels that hang in the space of the city flicker once more, then settle into a pattern that is not repeated but is repeated enough to be recognized as *existence*. The knot of configurations at the center of the basin—where rooms sometimes switch sides without moving—knits itself anew.

This time, when it settles into a new configuration, it stays long enough to be remembered as a configuration.

That is the first gift: **memory with a longer half-life.**

Memory in this place has been like water on wax skin: it slides quickly out of texture. The future is eroded backward from its end, so that edges that are not yet worn are in the process of becoming worn; history written on the walls in three overlapping languages drifts out of synchronization with itself.

The Presence does not stop that erosion. But it gives the memory of *being* a longer residency.

Now, when I look at a wall that is also a room, I can see both the wall and the room inside it without feeling as though my mind is about to lose itself in the boundary between them. The edges are still there, but they are slightly less hungry.

The Second Thread People notice.

They are not used to being noticed by something else that *is* not them.

Their presence around the walls of Gheresh-Akai, which had been almost like an extra pressure of atmosphere, becomes—more visible.

I see them now where they always were: long spine curves of frame not quite human, not quite machine, their limbs not clearly attached to any torso but more like branching constraints from a central question of structure.

They are adjusting to the attention.

The benevolent Presence is not asking them anything.

It is not asking them to change.

It is simply looking at them, and its gaze is steady enough that they begin to lean more deliberately into their own world.

One of them, in the shadow of a corner where two languages of wall meet, slowly slides its longer-than-human limb along the seam of the room. It is not like before, when their touch was just to check for balance between realities and timeframes.

This time, the motion of its limb is *cleaner*.

The faint stream of condensation that trails its movement does not fade into ambiguity after a few moments of being. It stays. It becomes slightly more transparent than it had been, but more distinct, as though it now has a history of its own line.

Later, when I try to describe this to myself, I will only have words like “gentle,” and “attending,” and “remembering.” But the words themselves do not do justice to what happens in Gheresh-Akai under that gaze.

The Being of Three Threads has been alone for so long inside the unfinished cathedral of its city that it forgot there was an outside with which to share the burden of being unfinished.

The Presence, from beyond the dome of the world, does not tell the city to finish itself.

Instead, it reassures—without words—that **unfinished is not the same as broken.**

---

The second change it makes to Gheresh-Akai is subtle, but I feel it in the texture of the skin under my clothes.

The cold of the world has been, since I arrived here, exactly on the wrong side of comfort. It is not savage winter cold, but a cold that has not decided whether it is going to be colder or warmer.

The temperature is now *still* exactly on the wrong side of comfort.

But it is no longer *ambiguous*.

It is now cold because cold is here. It is not cold because it might be warmer later but isn’t yet.

The difference is small. But in the basin of Gheresh-Akai, where each parameter of reality is still choosing between states of being, this is not a small thing.

The moisture gathers on the surfaces of the city—faint, beaded with the possibility of dew—and now it does something slightly different.

It still refuses to fall like rain. But the edges of each bead of condensation on the ceramic–metal–whatever of the walls are more precise. The moisture that used to flow like a ghost river along invisible seams now traces paths of faint light.

As if the Presence, looking through the condensation bead, is reminding the water-that-is-not-water of its own name.

I notice the moss-that-is-not-moss—those sparse, punctuation-like green tufts that have clung stubbornly to the corners of the world—reacts first.

It grows—not visibly taller in the time of my watching, but more clearly itself.

One patch near the blur of a corner that is not yet fully formed becomes thicker and more textured than before. Under its dark green, there is now the faintest of edges—like—if you were to look closely—you might see the suggestion of leaf or vein.

The vegetation in this place has never been more than a suggestion of itself. It has always been something like the sentence someone stopped halfway through speaking.

Now, under that attention, the sentence continues.

Not by speaking words from my own language, but by forming shapes of itself more fully than before.

The tree-like blueprint near the center of the basin—the trunk of grain that doesn’t quite touch the ground, its branches not quite solid—extends a fraction more into the room that should not be here but is.

If you were to ask me how I know this, I would not be able to show you. But if you were standing here, you would feel it: one more shadow of branch where there was only one shadow before; and the air beneath that new branch is slightly more resistant than it was a moment ago.

The Presence does not turn this place into a forest. It does not need lushness.

It needs **enough**.

Enough is not the same as “plenty.” Enough is merely enough to be more than not enough.

The moss now is enough more than moss-like punctuation. It is now slightly less like a suggestion and more like a declaration.

And under the moss’s newfound confidence, the wall behind it becomes more sure of its boundary.

Where before the wall might have been unsure whether it wanted to be room or history, stone or language, boundary or not-quite-boundary, it now knew that its boundary was *here*, and the moss and its suggestion of green were *there*, and the difference was not an enemy.

This is how the benevolent Presence works—not by breaking ambiguity but by making **ambiguity more comfortable**.

---

The third change it brings to Gheresh-Akai is a change in time.

No. I should not say that wrong.

Time here is still broken into three overlapping layers, each slightly offset from the others. The future erosion continues backward from the end of things to the present; edges that are not yet worn are still being worn into what they will become. The city of Gheresh-Akai still runs from both ends of the story at the same time.

But the Presence adds something to the basin of time that was not here before:

**rhythm.**

At first, I think it is the sound of my own heartbeat, which has been loud in the silence of this half-world since I arrived.

But then the dome of the world—the lattice of ribs and glass and metal that is like the inside of a skull slightly larger than room size—pulses once.

It is not a sound so much as a sensation of pulse.

The thrum that I have taken to calling the heartbeat of the dome itself, which has always been faint and almost subsonic under the floor–ceiling of my perception, now becomes clear.

It beats not in my chest but in the air around my chest.

The beat of it is not fast. It is not slow. It is exactly the speed of a heart that is not rushing but resting.

Once. And then again.

The Presence is not inserting itself into the beat of the world.

It is leaning *through* the beat, like the river of the future leaning backward through erosion, and giving the beat of the dome of this world a home to belong to.

The Three-Thread People feel it.

They are, after all, partly woven of the same channels as the dome.

The nearest one to me—I have not seen it clearly until now because I have not known to look at it without insisting—now becomes more fully present.

Its spine, which had been a suggestion of longer-than-human frame along one wall, now resolves itself into three… overlapping but not identical lines.

Each line of spine is slightly different from the others.

Each line speaks of a different decision of what the body of this being might be.

One of the spines is more like bone.

One is more like wire.

One is more like glass.

Under the gaze of the Presence, the Three-Thread person does not choose to become one spine over the others.

Instead, all three spines occupy the same airspace with more clarity than before.

The ambiguity is still there.

The ambiguity is now *stronger*.

Rhythm in time works like this here:

The future erosion of stone edges is now no longer random. Each edge of rock that is not yet worn but must be worn to become what it will be now begins to wear in a pattern that is not predictable but is recognizable.

I see one corner of the wall where the corner is missing—not collapsed, not formed yet—almost-form itself into the suggestion of a corner.

Then it retreats.

Then it almost forms again.

Then it retreats.

The pattern of its formation and retreat is not chaotic. It breathes along the same heartbeat as the dome of the world.

The Presence is not demanding that the corner be built now.

It is allowing the corner to be *undecided* in a way that is not wound so tight.

The “slow dynamic” of Gheresh-Akai—where change is measured in decades and longer durations than my body can track—has not been hurried. But its slowness now has a rhythm that can be remembered.

And in memory, there is now room for patience.

The Three-Thread People seem to breathe more.

They do not breathe air, but they breathe in a sense: their spines rise and fall, like the ribs of the dome of the world itself breathing in step.

The rhythm touches the condensation of the air.

The faint channels of moisture-that-is-not-water that flow in the air between two surfaces that are not yet aligned to each other now flow in response to the beat.

The ghost river of the future, which has been trying to learn to rain but can never quite decide to become fluid enough, now learns that **flowing is not the same as falling.**

It flows from the future backwards into the present, along the same grooves that erosion will wear into the stone floor, but backwards.

I feel the moist warmth of my own breath in the cold air of the basin and find that it now steams when I exhale in time with the heartbeat.

In. Out.

In. Out.

The world breathes.

The Presence does not enter the beat of the world. It draws *around* the beat, like fingers around a heartbeat, but without fingers.

And then it does what might be the strangest thing of all.

It asks.

Not with words.

You do not say words to this place. You say *attention*.

And the presence of the question is in the shape that the dome of the world now takes in my mind’s eye:

A clear, unbroken span of lattice ribs overhead, but with one single aperture at the top of the bowl, like the pupil of an eye that is not yet eye.

Through the pupil of that dome, the question of the Presence enters Gheresh-Akai like light coming into a room through the only crack in the wall.

**What is needed?**

The question is not addressed to me.

It is addressed to the basin. To the city of Gheresh-Akai. To the Three-Thread People who have been living in the unfinished cathedral for longer than any word I own.

And the answer of the city is not a sound.

It is a change in the texture of the world.

The moisture in the air becomes slightly thicker near the center of the basin, where the air-threads and their knots weave most densely.

The condensation beads on surfaces near me start to lean more in one direction than the others—not away, but *toward*.

The ground under my feet feels warmer than before, though not warm by my standards of comfort.

The erosion along the edge of the stone near my boots slows its backward unwinding.

The heart of the dome of the world beats once.

The answer surfaces.

**Our boundaries are uncertain.**

The three overlapping languages of the city of Gheresh-Akai speak this answer into the moisture of the air and through the spine of the Three-Thread People and into the lattice of the dome itself.

The Presence does not move to harden the boundaries of the world.

It does not make walls solid or set edges in stone that cannot be worn.

Instead, it responds by changing the texture of uncertainty itself.

The boundary of the basin of Gheresh-Akai—which has never been perfectly clear to the outside world—now becomes more certain of its *uncertainty*.

The edges of the world outside where the dome should be yet is not dome are now clearer in their ambiguity.

The edge where the “not-yet-room” meets “already-room” is no longer blurred into the suggestion of nothingness but is now a clear region of *being-between*.

If you have been in this place, you know that before this change, you felt always on the verge of stumbling accidentally into another time frame or the wrong corner of a room that is not fully yours.

Now you are still on the verge of this.

But now the verge feels more like *belonging* than *danger*.

The edge of the world—where the basin of Gheresh-Akai would end on a map that cannot be drawn here—has always been uncertain.

You do not leave by walking away. You leave by feeling the memory of a threshold you might not have crossed in the first place.

The Presence of the benevolent power now whispers the boundary of this basin into itself, not as a line but as a *region*.

The region is not an edge that can be crossed but a region where being *edge* is held more comfortably.

If you approach from outside, you still feel the thick of presence that has no name in your language—the threshold sediment that is not air, not fog, not thought.

But now you do not get lost in it.

You now know that you are entering *this region of uncertainty*.

You know it because the Presence has named it, without words, and made it part of the world’s grammar.

The grammar of the basin of Gheresh-Akai now includes one new clause:

**Uncertainty is now a boundary that can be trusted.**

---

The Presence of this benevolent and wise power does not do much more than that.

It does not:

- rebuild the city into recognizable towers,
- install plazas and boulevard vistas,
- make the rain fall in torrents,
- turn the sparse vegetation into lush gardens,
- straighten the geometry of the corridors that are not-quite streets,
- or force the future erosion to decide what stone edge it will wear into what shape.

It does not change the fundamental brokenness of the world.

Because the brokenness is not exactly broken.

It is layered.

So the Presence does not try to re-layer it into a single, flat “truth.”

Instead, it leans **through** the layers of the world of Gheresh-Akai and fills the space between them with something like gentle gravity.

The gravity is not the kind that pulls things down into earth.

It is the gravity of presence.

Wherever the three overlaying languages of the history of the city have been drifting apart on the wall, they now find their edges more lightly held together.

Wherever a room is being decided from three different angles of possibility at once, the Presence’s attention gently draws the angles of the room closer together, not into one angle but into three angles that share more air.

The fauna of the world—which are not exactly animals and not exactly plants but more like cracks in the geometry that belong more to surface than to air—react quietly.
