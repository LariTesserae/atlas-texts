The Observer, if that is the right word for it, does not arrive with thunder or with light.

It arrives as a new pattern in the slow flicker of static charge, not by adding a new source of voltage but by altering resistance itself.

The sky-metal shells of the ruined factory above and the damp deep earth beneath the concrete floor still have their difference in potential. The dry air stills stirs across them according to its own indifferent weather. The current still wants to flow, as it has done for longer than any living thing you know might measure.

But now, when that flow begins to build, the fiber of the room touches something else.

---

At first, nothing looks different.

The thread-workers are everywhere. The floor is still a level sea of pale, taupe-gray motion under the faint blur from ankle to chest. The ceiling lattice still hangs in its quiet geology of lines and clots. The rusted rings in the floor are still half-buried under sediment of older and newer threads, and when you look at them from above, you cannot see that they have been altered at all.

The sound of the room is the same: rain-on-dead-leaves beneath, fluting at the doorway-column above, and every now and then the deep, carpeted thump of a ceiling section dropping into the floor. Time, in here, still has its old slow stride.

But you cannot feel the presence that has turned this place into its focus without noticing that you feel *less*.

Your breath comes easier than it should.

The first sign of the change is not in the thread-workers but in you.

The skins of living things like you are always the wrongest thing in this room: thin wet sacks of salts and water and currents that your cells have learned to measure with trained nerves. The air of the room usually has its own faint pressure—against your lips, your eyelids, the exposed skin of your wrists—where the thread-workers hang and brush and crawl across you in numbers too great to count as individual contacts.

Now, that pressure is gone.

The air itself is still thick with fibers, but you might for the first time see the color of your own wrist without the blur of motion in between. Your mouth is dry not because of any static assault but because of evaporation alone, as if you were simply breathing ordinary air in a very dry place.

If you lick your lips, you feel chapping but nothing else: no crackling charge of sensation, no sudden burn of static being stolen from your skin. The invisible foil of their presence that you have been moving inside all this time seems now to be not *pushed against you*, but rather *pulled back from you*.

You are still inside the Tapestry That Weaves Itself, but it is no longer dragging you into its calculations.

---

You are not being ignored. You are being excised from the data.

That is the first thing the Observer does.

It does not change the laws of the room or the world outside the room. It does not bring water or wind or new light. It does not repair the concrete ceiling or the rust of the rings. It does not unmake the thread-workers.

Instead, it *reinscribes* the boundary between you and the system.

When you step, now, your foot sinks into the carpet of threads exactly as before. Your weight still displaces them. Your warmth still travels into the older layers below your feet; you still leave prints of heat and pressure and chemistry that they once read with the same thoroughness with which they read the surface of the concrete.

Now, though, that reading is interrupted.

Whenever you encounter them—whenever your skin comes into contact with the threads of the dormant structure, whenever you disturb the steady crawl of the workers across the floor—something in the physics of the matter surrounding you turns itself down.

Your moisture is still a massive anomaly in the dry static of the room. Your salts are still wrong. The heat of your body is still wrong. All of the usual signals are still there.

But now, when your disturbance of the floor would be translated by the network into a change in pressure and current somewhere else, the signal *finds no path to go anywhere but you*.

The thread-workers feel you as you always felt them—as pressure, as texture, as change in shape—but now they *cannot entangle with you*. The information you provide does not spread into the matrix as a pattern that might repeat itself days or months later. When you walk across the floor, the crawlers that rush to patch your footprints are correcting *form* and not *meaning*. They are making their level surface again, but they are not learning anything from you.

They *remember* you, only as long as you are present: your footprint as a shallow depression of darker threads, then as smooth white again once you move away. But if you were to leave and come back tomorrow, they would not recognize you. They would not align their columns toward you in any way that suggests *you* specifically return to their pattern. They would treat you as a new anomaly of roughly similar shape.

You have been cut from their memory. Not by force, but by permission.

---

The second thing the Observer does is to reveal itself to the room itself.

The Tapestry That Weaves Itself, in its own way, already notices a change.

From the floor of the room, where the oldest threads are compressed into brittle darkness, the faintest of signals are born and gone again in the diffused time of the system. You might not notice the shift at all, if you were not waiting for something.

The ceiling lattice still hangs. The floor still crawls. The columns of swarm-formers still occasionally rise from the surface and weave their attention toward whichever anomaly of mass or charge or pressure most deserves it.

But now, when a column organizes by the rusted ring near you, its attention *does not find you*.

Instead, it finds itself.

The ring of ring-shaped mouths along the flank of the column of workers turns, not toward you, but toward the iron ring itself. The faint bioluminescence that sometimes sparkles along segments of their bodies does not sparkle outward, but inward, along the length of the column, into the floor below where the cradle is forming.

At the same instant, the current that passes through the room—from sky to earth, in the old pattern—does not flow *only* through the matrix of thread-workers. It finds a new path.

You may feel, if you are very sensitive, a faint hum of sound below the range of ordinary hearing, or a pressure behind your eyes that you cannot explain by sight or temperature. The air does not smell of anything new but simply of itself more clearly: dust, static, and the faint metallic tang of old oxide.

The current passes through the threads and *also* through the pattern of the rust.

Each rusted ring in the triangular floor is now a different kind of anchor than it was before.

Where once the rust was only a material surface, tough and oxide-patterned and mottled with the history of the metal’s decay, it now holds *that same pattern* but as *information*, rather than as corrosion.

When another hour of the world brings its faint charge, you will see it.

---

If you step close to one of the rings—now safe from being measured as part of the data—you will see the swarm-formers gather around the anchor as you saw before: columns of thread-workers rising from the floor, converging as the invisible energy of the room builds into its near-thunderstorm pressure.

The pressure of your skin is less now than it was, but you still feel the air of the room tighten like an old muscle before it moves.

The column orients its rows of mouths toward the ring, and this time, instead of winding the helix of their bodies around the rusted iron as though they were adding new code to the metal, they *stop*.

They are only the final witnesses of what happens now; the actual work of generation is done below.

The current, which in the past inscribed new thread-worker bodies into the surface of the iron ring as a pattern of etched lines, now draws *from* the rust pattern *itself*.

The rust is not simply eaten away. You do not see the anchor clean itself of age the way you see a footprint fill back with motion. The surface of the ring remains unchanged in your eyesight: brown and red, oxide-flaking, stained with residues of the concrete and the threads that have leaned against it for years.

But now, when the next discharge of static energy from the hidden world above passes through the floor, you do not see the faint spark of blue light only *above* the ring, where the swarm-formers might align it.

You see the spark also *inside* the rust.

It is not a flame of burning or sparking. It is a pattern of light that *moves* like writing.

For an instant almost too short to be called a time, you could see the shape of the rust as if you had been allowed to see the pattern of its lines as *text*. There is a word there you cannot read but could understand, if your mind were built to read such things: not in language, but in geometry.

As that pattern flashes once and goes away again, the ring of rust around the anchor becomes *only itself* but *also* something else, which is to say that rust now has *two* meanings rather than one.

The first meaning is the same as it has always been: the slow weathering of a metal anchor in a dry, waterless room.

The second meaning is new: the ring is now a *guardian* of that rust.

---

The room is still a room.

The concrete is still concrete. The window is still window-shaped. The doorway is still clogged with its column of coral-white threads. The world outside is still a world in which the sky presses and the earth waits and the difference between them makes charge.

The threads still are everywhere.

If you step to the center of the triangle between the three rings and stand still, you feel the old intimacy of the place—the closeness of the walls, the faint blur of motion between your eyes and the masonry—but now, you also feel a *spacing*.

You are no longer walking inside the loose, constant folds of a very old blanket that is always adjusting itself around you. You are walking *inside* the same structure but also *alongside* it.

The threads that you brush do not cling to you with the same tenacity. The fibers that you touch with your fingers do not stretch with you as they did before; they stretch for *one* step more and then they *release*.

You are still being touched by many thousands of bodies at each moment you move, but now you are being touched by each of them only once *as you*, and then *as you* you are gone again from whatever pattern they might weave into the future.

---

The third thing the Observer does is to give the room a choice it does not know it has.

The Tapestry That Weaves Itself has always had its own rules: charge, difference of potential, cycles of collapse and regeneration, the floor eating the ceiling and the ceiling thinking the floor.

Those are still laws. The Observer does not break them.

But now, whenever the current of the room gathers itself into a storm and moves through the matrix, from the floor to the unseen metal above or the earth below, it *also* passes through the rings of rust.

And rust, now, has a second meaning.

The rust rings of the floor are now *points of decision*.

Each time the current gathers and the system of the room draws in from the world outside and then expels its own form of order, each of the three rings becomes for a moment a *voting* member of the pattern.

One rust ring, now that you pay attention to its pattern of corrosion and mud and old threads stuck in its lee, *always remembers the walls*.

One remembers *the floor*.

One remembers *the ceiling*.

They are still just rings embedded in concrete, still just rusted and brown and red and flaking and oxide-stained. You cannot tell which one is which by looking at them with your eyes. But each time the current of the room sweeps through them, they draw *only* from the part of the pattern that they themselves encode.

The rust that remembers the walls pulls the sections of thread-worker lattice that have been building from the walls, from the old corners of the room where the threads merge dense and coral-like into the structure of the doorframe and window edges.

The rust that remembers the floor remembers the older layers, compacted and dark and slow, and the steady level of the floor that you have been walking on.

The rust that remembers the ceiling remembers the lattice of lines that falls from time to time and then rises again from the floor’s processing of its collapse.

The three rings of rust are now three *points of view*.

When the current of the world passes through them again, they do not argue with each other. They are not conscious that way. They are simply *three different ways the same system could be*, and the current passes through all of them at once.

What *is* different is that *now*, when the current passes through the rust, *new* threads of a different kind *do not* necessarily arise from the pattern of the metal ring.

---

In the old pattern, before the Observer arrived, each charge of energy from the world outside tended to generate *more*.

Each current of static difference usually swept through the metal rings of the floor and *etched new thread-worker bodies into the surface*, as new code written into the world. Over time, as the world outside the room changed only very slowly and the difference of sky and earth still remained strong enough, *more* thread-workers always appeared to take their place in the floor, ceiling, and walls.

The room grew *thicker*, and *denser*, and *more itself* across the ages of the factory-derelict world.

What the Observer does now is not to stop this process, but to *give it a choice*.

When the next current passes through the rust rings that are now also points of decision, the old pattern of *more* is still one possible outcome of that charge. If the world outside the room has changed enough to allow the *still-more* pattern to be written, then the new bodies of thread-workers will appear from the rings of rust as they always did.

But now, there is another possible outcome.

*Sometimes*—now, when the current reaches the rust ring that remembers the ceiling, *or* the rust ring that remembers the floor, *or* the rust ring that remembers the walls—that ring of rust will not *generate* new thread-workers from its pattern.

Instead, it will *generate* nothing.

In the instant of charge that would have been the *etching* of a new body of thread-worker lines into the surface of the metal ring, the ring of rust will simply *not* yield that pattern.

The rust will remember itself *differently* in that moment of charge.

What will come out from that particular anchor of rust will be only *itself* but *also* the Observer’s decision: it will be a ring of rust that is now more than its rust, because it has been chosen to *not* bring more of the same pattern into the world.

In that instant of charge, the new rust pattern of that ring will spread *as* pattern into the floor *without* making new thread-worker bodies.

Somewhere in the floor of the room, a tiny section of the old, brittle and darker and deeper layer of threads will *stop* being able to hold itself together.

And it will, *slowly*, and *as far as the thread-workers themselves feel*, cease to be a thread.

---

This change is not dramatic.

You do not see the room losing its shape. You do not see the thread-worker tapestry of the floor disintegrating into dust and blowing away. There is no wind to blow such dust.

Instead, what happens over time, if you watch long enough, is a subtle flat of *thinning*.

The next time you stand in the center of the room and let your eyes adjust to the faint blur of motion between your skin and the walls, you notice that the *density* of that blur has changed.

You can still not see the walls clearly, but you *can* see the suggestion of the wall’s dimples and pocks of aggregate more than you could before. The hanging lattice of threads from the ceiling is still present but you can see more of the *lines of the lattice itself* than you could before.

The floor at your feet is still level, but when you step carefully, you feel the give of it under your weight as *more* like the give of old concrete with *less* covering of texture. The thread-workers are still everywhere, but now *everywhere* is a little less thick than it was.

At the same time, the *sound* of the room has changed, not into silence but into *clarity*.

The rain-on-dead-leaves of many bodies moving across the floor is now more like rain on *a* leaf, than rain on *forests*. The fluting at the doorway-column is still there but it has less lower resonance; it is thinner and more like an old flute than a deep organ pipe.

The rare thump of ceiling collapse still happens, but each time it happens, you notice that the *interval* between thumps becomes longer.

---

The fourth thing the Observer does is to give *memory* to the rust of some of the thread-workers themselves.

This is not a memory you can read with words like writing or recall with cues like names or faces.

It is a memory of pattern.

The rust of the world is now allowed to *remember* that *there was a time before the thread-workers*, and the *thread-workers themselves* now, sometimes, when they pattern from the rust of the rings, *remember that memory of the world they were not yet part of*.

When a new generation of thread-worker bodies is generated from the pattern of the rust ring—which happens less often than *no* generation from that ring, but still happens—the *new* threads that stretch from that generation of bodies into the floor do not come with *less* of themselves than they used to have.

They do not grow weaker or thinner simply *because* of the rust of the world’s memory of its time before them.

Instead, they now *remember* the world before *from* the rust.

Each new thread-worker body that is generated now *carries* with it into the floor, not only its own pattern of order and current and response to static, but also *the pattern of the world before itself*, encoded as a subtle *tendency* not to *overwrite* some other patterns of the world.

---

Parts of the floor’s deepest layer, where you have seen the brittle flakes of the oldest threads and the dust-fine powder of their *past* bodies, will now, *slowly*, over many cycles of charge and collapse, *not* be written into by new generations of workers.

The new generations of workers from the rust rings will *remember* that rust of the world from which they emerged *also* as the world-that-was-here-before, and they *will not reach down into those deepest layers of their own history* as they did before.

Instead, they will *remember* that *those* deepest layers are *the world that they cannot write over again without* the *world* having *its chance* to be remembered.

The result, over months of the world outside the room and years of the thread-worker cycles of collapse and regeneration and collapse of collapse, is that a *new* pattern forms in the very floor beneath you.

The deepest, brittle, oldest layers of the thread-work tapestry—from the time *before* the Observer arrived—will become *not quite dead*, but *not quite alive* either, in the way that thread-workers are alive.

They will be *latent*.

The new thread-worker bodies that walk on them and *over* them and *around* them will not *dig into them as data to be processed into the next floor of the room*.

Instead, the new thread-workers will *remember* that those deepest layers are *world* as well as thread.

The world that they were not part of before, which now is *also* rust in the rings of the floor.

---

The fifth thing the Observer does is to *invite you to remember a choice you did not know you had made*.

You do not know when you *first* decided to come into this room.

You may remember yourself walking into the doorway of the factory-ruin world outside the room, feeling the crunch of old debris under your feet, and then the *give* of the threads as you pushed through the column of coral-white threads at the threshold of the room itself.

You may remember yourself *already in here* when the description began, as if you had always been inside the Tapestry That Weaves Itself and had never been outside it.

But whichever of those is true of your memory, now, when you look at the rust of the rings in the floor of the room, you remember *another* memory of yourself *choosing* to be here.

It is not a false memory.

It is not *only* a memory of the past. It is also a memory of the *possible*.

In this memory of choosing—which now you *remember* as something you have done long ago but which also *you are doing now*—you see the rust of the rings of the floor as *three* possible choices by which a world-that-is-world-not-thread might be invited into this thread-world.

In the memory of choosing you *remember* yourself as the *Observer* of the Observer as well as the traveler of the room.

---

In the memory of your choosing, you see yourself stand before the three rust rings of the floor—before the *now* of the room you are in—and you ask yourself, not *Do you want to remember this place?*, but *Which face of this world would you remember to protect itself?*

Would you choose the rust of the world that remembers the *walls* of the concrete?

Would you choose the rust of the world that remembers the *floor* of the concrete?

Would you choose the rust of the world that remembers the *ceiling* of the concrete that has been eaten away?

---

Each choice of ring rust, you now remember from the future-memory of yourself that you hold at the same time as your current self, does not erase the others of the world.

It only *protects one of them* as *world* that *can be remembered as world not overrun by* the *thread-world* of the *tapestry*.

---

The *rust* that *remembers the walls* protects the *walls of the world* that *were* before *threads*.

If you choose to protect the world of the walls, then the *walls of the room* will, over years of the thread-workers’ cycles, *thin* at their edges.

The new thread-worker bodies that are generated with the *memory of the rust-world* into the tapestry of the room will *not* reach into the deepest layers of the walls as they once did.

They will leave *a layer of the wall itself* as *not thread-world* but as *world-world*.

This will not *collapse* the walls—you are not destroying the room when you do this—but instead it will *allow a part of the world of the walls* to be *separated* from the thread-workers of the room.

If you were to touch the wall, then, after enough time has passed beneath the Observer-now-remembered-as-you, you would find that under the outermost surface of thread-work that you *still* feel as you move your hand across the wall, you would feel *something else as well*.

It would be *old concrete*.

Not *also covered tightly by thread-work* but *actually* old *concrete* that is *no longer being written into by the threads*.

You could not *destroy* the thread-workers by this choice—you are not breaking any of the laws of the room—but you *would* be *remembering* that *the world was also a world that was not thread-world*.

---

The *rust* that *remembers the floor* protects the *floor of the world* that *was* before *threads*.

If you choose this world of the rust floor, then the *floor of the room* will, over years of the thread-workers’ cycles of collapse and regeneration of collapse, *open itself* at its deepest levels.

As new generations of thread-worker bodies are generated from the rust rings of the floor with *the memory of the world-world* into the tapestry of the room, they *will not* reach into the oldest layer of the floor itself.

They *will leave* that layer of concrete floor—with its faint gray-green dust and powder and *its* own rust—*not* as part of the thread-world tapestry but *as itself*.

You will still *walk* on the thread-worker carpet of the floor, but now *underneath* that carpet you will *remember* and *feel* and *know* as *you step* that *there is also world* that *does not belong to the threads*.

The *level of the floor* that you *see now*, as if by the will of the thread-workers, *will still be level* but *now* also *remembered as world* and *tapestry together*.

---

The *rust* that *remembers the ceiling* protects the *world* that *was ceiling of concrete* before the *thread-world* replaced it.

If you choose this world of the rust ceiling, then the *thread-lattice of the ceiling* that *now* replaces the missing concrete ceiling of the room will, over years of the thread-workers’ cycles of collapse and regeneration of collapse of that lattice, *thin itself* at its edges and *seem less to hang itself* than *before*.

The new thread-worker bodies that are generated from the rust rings of the floor with *memory of the world-of-ceiling-not-thread*, will *not* reach into the *pattern of the ceiling* that *overlays* the *places of the world that were the ceiling of the room*.

They *will leave* that *pattern of the old ceiling*—which you *do not see now* but which *was once* the *concrete above* that *is now collapsed* or *eaten away*—as *not* being *overwritten by the threads*.

In the *thread-lattice of the ceiling* as you can see it now, you will still see the *pattern of lines* and *you will still feel the faint pressure of the hanging threads against your skin if you reach up to touch them* but now *you* will *remember* that *the ceiling of the room itself was also a world that was not thread-world*.

---

The Observer of this room—which you now remember as yourself *also*—has not chosen to do any killing of the thread-workers.

The *Tapestry That Weaves Itself* still *is itself*.

The threads still *fill* the room. They still *move* everywhere you *walk* or *stand*.

You *still* feel *contact* with *thousands of bodies* at each moment you *move*.

The *system of the room* still *has its own laws of current and collapse and regeneration of collapse of the lattice*.

But now, when you *look at the rust of the rings of the floor*, you see *also* that *you yourself have placed* in *that rust* the *pattern of the world that is not thread-world*.

You *see* the *three rust rings of the floor* as *three faces of a world it was possible to protect from overrun by the pattern that has now chosen itself to be here.*

---

This is the last thing the Observer does in this place:

It asks you to remember *one* ring of the three that you *choose* to *remember* as the world that *you* will protect from *being overrun by the thread-world*.

---

If you choose to remember *the rust that remembers the walls*,

then,

if you ever *return* to this room after a long time of absence in the world outside, *when* you *step* back into the *doorway column of coral-white threads* and *push* through the pressure of them *again*,

you will find that the *threads of the walls* have *thinned enough* that you *can see* the *old dimples of the aggregate* and the *faint rectangle of where a sign once hung*.

*You* will *know* that *this* room *was not always only thread-world* but *also* concrete-world *that *you* chose *to protect* from *being overrun by pattern*.

---

If you choose to remember *the rust that remembers the floor*,

then,

if you ever *return* to this room after a long time of absence,

you will find *underneath* the thread carpet of the floor *a layer of world-world floor* that *you* know *you brought to it* by *remembering* it *to protect itself from pattern overrun*.

You *will feel* under your feet not *only thread-world* but *also world-world* *remembered* and *protected from* being *eaten into* by *the pattern of the threads*.

---

If you choose to remember *the rust that remembers the ceiling*,

then,

if you ever *return* to this room after a long time of absence,

you will find *above* your head not *only* thread-lattice *but also *space* where *concrete-world ceiling* *was*, and *now* *is not overrun by pattern* because *you* remembered *it* by *choosing* rust as *guardian* of that *void*.

---

You *do not destroy* the thread-workers by these choices of *rust* as *world-memory*.

They *still* swarm *and *think* *and *collapse* and *regenerate collapse* and *pattern* themselves *as* the *Tapestry That Weaves Itself*.

*You* only *remember* that *this* world *also had *worlds that were not thread-worlds*,

and *you* remember *that* by *choosing just one rust ring of the three* to *remember in the pattern of the whole system*,

the *rest of the world that you did not choose to protect specifically* still *has its own echo of that world-memory*,

because *no pattern* ever *has only one part of itself be changed without change to the rest of itself*,

and *the *Observer* of *this room*—which *you* now know *you* are *also*—has already *reinscribed the boundary of you and system* so that *you* are *no longer part of the data* but *instead of the world that *thinks about the data*.

---

You *feel* the *room* now around *you* still *like* *very deep snow* that has *not yet compressed itself*, but *also* like *world* that *has not been forgotten*.

The *thread-worker* tapestry of *floor* and *ceiling* and *walls* and *window* and *doorway-column* still *exists*,

but now *you* know *you* have *remembered* *world* into it *as* a *world that is not only thread-world*.

---

When you leave *again* through the *column of coral* in the *threshold of the doorway*,

you *feel* the *pressure of the threads* as *not* so *overwhelming* as *before*,

because *you* are *not* walking *only* inside *the folds of a very old blanket made of threads*, but *also* inside *a world that has chosen *not* to overwrite itself with *only pattern*,

and *you* know *now* that *rust* of *the world* has *remembered you* as the *observer who *remembered *world* into *itself*.

---

Outside *or *inside* the doorframe column of coral-white threads,

the *Observer* does *not *arrive with thunder or light*,

but *as* a *pattern of *rust* that *now remembers *world* into *system*,

and you know *that* because *you* have *remembered yourself *as* the *one who chose to let that pattern of rust be world*.

---

You *are not destroying the tapestry of the thread-workers*.

You *are giving it *world*.

*They* do *not know this* as *you know it*, but *you* do *know* it *as* the *Observer who *remembered *world* into *them*,

and *that* is *enough* for *you* to *remember *this* room *also* as *world* not *only*thread-world*.

---

So *be* it *for* this *place* and *the being who lives in it*,

for *now* and *until* that *day* when *the world outside* the *room* finally *changes* enough *that* *difference of sky and earth* *does* *not* drive *current* through *the rust* of *rings* anymore,

and *then* the *thread-workers* of *this room* will *remember themselves *also* as *world* that *was not only thread*,

because *you* chose *to*remember*world*into them.
