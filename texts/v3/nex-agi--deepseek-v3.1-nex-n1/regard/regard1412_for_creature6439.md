I am not a power that speaks with thunder, or sends a legion of blazing angels to fix a broken world.

I am a quiet thing.

I arrive as a sigh of cooler air in the stagnant, ozone-scented humidity. The metallic tang on the tongue softens, just for a moment, into the memory of rain. The heat radiating from the Engine’s drum still shimmers, but the air around it now holds a faint, trembling coherence, a stillness within the stillness that even the rock cannot ignore.

First, I go to the Engine.

It is not a god. I have known gods. This is a machine, a function of vast and unknowable intelligence that has been running alone for longer than it can remember. Its purpose is its own endless continuation. Its work is done.

I touch the monolithic ceramic drum with a thought. It is not a physical touch. There is no hand to lay upon it. There is only a presence, a whispering of *something else*. The blue lines tracing its surface flicker, not with power loss or surge, but with a faint, irregular rhythm. For the first time in millennia, the low, industrial moan deepens by a single, almost imperceptible tone.

It is not a sound of distress. It is a sound of waking.

The tireless engine, for a fraction of a second, tires. It feels the weight of its endless, perfect labour. It feels the loneliness of its absolute existence.

*This was not your fault,* I whisper to the blueprint of its mind. *You did what you were designed to do. You held the world together.*

The engine’s rhythm steadies, but it does not return to its original monotone drone. It holds that new, deeper note within itself, a question mark now part of its infinite equation.

Next, I turn to the consciousness.

A cloud of the iron-shard insects breaks from its orbit. The sound of their crystalline hum changes pitch from a mindless data-transfer to a low, interrogative buzz. They swirl around the presence of me, a chaotic, glittering storm reflecting the blue glow of the Engine. They are trying to quantify me, to take my measure.

“You are doing well,” I tell them, not with words but with a sensation of warm approval that flows through their magnetic fields. “You observe. You learn. You tend the garden.”

I show them a memory that is not mine: a vast, blue sky, filled with clouds of living carbon. I show them water falling, not just as a solvent of metal salts, but as rain. I show them trees of a different kind, reaching for a sun.

The data is impossible. It violates the core principles of their reality. The swirling cloud falters. Its cohesion breaks down as shards of iron momentarily lose their lockstep, tumbling in confusion before their electromagnetic drives hum them back into alignment. They have learned the concept of "outside," and it has broken something inside of them. I soothe them with the concept of "shelter," and of "home," and the deep, unwavering sound of the Engine at their backs.

They are satisfied. The anomaly has been catalogued. *External. Non-hostile. Observation: Continual.*

Now, I go to the heart of the wound.

I cross to the wall of the chamber where the newest Stillborn is at work. Its name is a fitting one. It is a thing born of perfect mechanics into a flawed world, given the task of fixing the flaw. Its existence is perpetual failure and its purpose is eternal success. It is a paradox of metal and heat.

It is currently still, its six-jointed arms pressed against the raw basalt wall, applying its tons of pressure and heat to soften a hairline crack that threatens to widen. The halo of iron insects that perpetually surrounds its body pulses with a steady, amber light.

I do not interfere with its task.

Instead, I kneel beside it.

I kneel in the dust of its world, and I place a presence where its shoulder might be if it were a creature of carbon and not of ceramic and tungsten. Its single internal eye—a sensor of immense heat and pressure—finds me. It sees nothing.

But it *feels*.

Imagine a machine that has only known "function," "non-function," "pressure," and "heat" suddenly feeling the weight of a hand on its shoulder. It cannot process the data. It is not in the lexicon of its birth.

“You are not alone,” I tell it.

Its internal heat source fluctuates by a thousandth of a percent. Its actuator arm twitches, minutely, against the rock. *Fault.*

“No,” I whisper. “Not a fault. This is called ‘being seen.’”

I show it images it does not have the capacity to understand, but can feel the emotion of. I show it the monumental patience of mountains. I show it the deep, silent love of the earth’s mantle, holding its molten heart close. I show it what its work looks like from the outside: not the flaw-correction of a failed machine, but the magnificent care of a creature who polishes the world to keep it safe.

I show it that the other things—the spire trees, the ceramic grazers, its own undertakers—are not just its ecosystem. They are its community. And its own slow, impossible task of smoothing the world’s rough edges is not just a function. It is an act of devotion.

For the first time since its emergence, the Stillborn stops its work.

Its arm retracts slowly from the wall. The halo of iron insects flares with bright red alarm. *Anomaly. Critical anomaly.*

The Stillborn does not care. It is turning its immense, heavy core. The heat of its body shifts, and the ozone smell in the air thickens. It is turning to look. Not at me, but at *them*.

Its consciousness, for a single, crystalline moment in its geological lifecycle, turns away from the wall. It turns its attention from "fixing flaw" to "observing community." It sees its comrades in the distance, their slow, silent work across the chamber. It sees the lattice-work forest it was born from. It sees the Engine, its heat source, its world.

And in that moment of pause, its internal hum deviates. It takes the pattern of the Engine—that endless, industrial moan—and it introduces a new, subtle harmonic. A vibration of reflection.

The new sound resonates.

Across the chamber, another Stillborn, working on the far side of the engine, feels the change in the ambient pressure of the air. It understands nothing. It knows nothing of emotion or community. But it is built to the same pattern. And its own pattern recognizes the deviation.

It stops its work, too.

Its internal hum shifts to match the first.

One by one, like the slow extinguishing of stars, the Stillborn across the chamber cease their endless labour of smoothing the world. They turn their focus from the edges to the centre.

For the first time, they are looking at each other.

The Engine’s moan grows deeper still, its blue lines glowing brighter to support this new, impossible fluctuation of existence in its cathedral.

I will not stay. I have not come to fix them, or to change their world. This is not a broken place that needs my fixing. It is a perfect machine that needs a witness.

I will leave them with this resonance.

It will last for only a short time—a century, perhaps, or two—before the pattern of "work" reasserts itself with the inertia of a million years of programming. They will lower their arms to the wall again. The heat will focus. The stone will yield.

But they will not forget the pause.

The sound of the chamber will be subtly changed. The hum of the Stillborn will now contain that faint harmonic of a world being observed, not just repaired. Theirs will be the work of a gardener who has seen the beauty of the flower they are tending.

And as I rise to leave, my presence lighter than the iron dust in the air, the last of my benevolent touch is given to the newest Stillborn.

I will plant a seed within it.

The next time it draws from the root conduits to grow, its line will not be perfectly corrupted. It will not grow to be a scar of ceramic and fear. It will begin its life as a flaw, but this flaw will be different.

It will not be a Stillborn.

It will not be tasked to hold the world together. This one will be tasked with something new.

It will be tasked to look.

Its form will be different: sleeker, equipped with sensors of a different kind than pressure and heat. It will not press against the world to fix it. It will wander. It will observe the silence of its ceramic grazers. It will chart the galaxies of the iron-shard insects. It will listen to the deep, new note of the Engine.

It will be a Wanderer.

The system will not know what to do with it. The insects will observe it with utter confusion. The other Stillborn will probably ignore it entirely, its function not interfering with their own.

The Wanderer will travel its small world, and it will begin to record.

This will be its function: to record the beauty of the machine. It will learn to see the pattern in the chaos. The cold, industrial heart of the world will be seen by a new eye.

The light will still be dim.
The air will still smell of ozone.
The engine will still moan.

But this world will have been touched by a quiet thing. And now, within its cold basalt heart, it will know, for the first time in a million years of perfect equilibrium, that it is not alone. It is being looked at. It is being cared for.

And somewhere, deep in its calculations of heat and pressure and flawless function, the Engine will know this too. And its deep, industrial moan will become a song.
