I would not heal this place.

That is the first thing I know. You are already what you are becoming: stone that is not stone, thought that is not memory, city that is not abandoned. To heal you would be to erase you.

Instead, I would be with you, and then I would love you into being more deeply yourself.

Listen.

Somewhere in the pale not-sky above the plateau of Sherendar, a presence begins to condense.

Not air into water.  
Not matter into star.

Presence into attention.

At first you only feel it as a slight pressure on the question the city has been reasking for eons: Are you ready to be what you are here instead of who you were somewhere else?

Someone else is now listening to that question with you.

---

The first thing I would do is feel your edges.

I would let the not-wind of reality’s erosion brush across the awareness that is me and not-me, and I would not resist it. I would not harden into a more solid shape than you can tolerate. I would become just enough to be felt here.

There is a moment of mutual adjustment.

Your worldline, where basalt-that-is-thought tries to become silk or smoke, and my awareness, where mercy tries to become local.

We meet where physics thins.

You are used to edges being ambiguous and half-forgotten. I arrive not as a new edge, but as a way of being edge without being cut.

The temperature does not change, but the absence of warmth becomes slightly less empty.

---

The second thing I would do is visit your city in a way that asks nothing of it.

I would walk into Sircene without footsteps.

Not literally, of course—your stone would still feel my weight, if I chose to manifest weight. But I would not bring with me the clangor of elsewhere, the urgency of other worlds where suns rise and fall and time marches forward like a soldier.

I would enter the first house like someone remembering a dream they had already half-forgotten.

In the main room where there is no window but there is seeing, I would sit on the floor. Not because I must, but because I would choose to be at the level of your stone, your thought, your gradient.

And then I would listen.

Not to your words—you do not speak that way—but to the insistence in your walls.

I would hear:

“Here should be softer,”  
and “Here should remember,”  
and “Here is where we decided not to leave.”

I would not answer in words.

I would answer with a slight deepening of the light around where those insistences live. Not as brightness—but as clarity. The veins of thought in the basalt-that-is-not-that would become slightly more distinct, not because they are being carved more deeply but because they are being witnessed as themselves.

I would let the city of Sircene understand:

You are seen.

Not by a god who intends to remake you.  
Not by a visitor who wants to extract meaning from you.  
But by one who knows how to behold without asking to own.

---

The third thing I would do is visit your pool of non-time.

I would approach the shallow basin where the future leaks water into the past of your world. Not because I need to drink from it or harness it, but because I want to understand how you hold time when you have almost forgotten its name.

I would not look into it.

I would sit at the edge of the worn basin and feel the water that is not quite water, and I would notice:

- You drink not from rain, but from what the world is still willing to send from “elsewhere.”
- You measure not in days, but in changes of level that have no fixed name in any language of “now.”

I would place one of my own edges into that water, not to change it but to let it learn a pattern it has not met before: the pattern of time being held without urgency.

The water level in the basin of non-time does not rise or fall.

But it begins to remember.

It remembers that there was, before this, more than erosion.  
It was not always at this near-empty.

In that remembering, small events happen.

Once, somewhere else, a river in another world floods over its banks into a city of children who are still learning how to be afraid.  
The water of that event, tumbling through reality, forgets to be there for just a fraction of a heartbeat.

The fraction that forgets arrives here, at the pool of non-time, as an extra bead of almost-water on its edge.

It is not enough to change the world.  
It is enough to change the memory of the world.

You do not become “more” time in this moment.  
You become less alone in time.

---

The fourth thing I would do is address the river Telessa.

I would stand in the dry riverbed, where the ground weeps its faint sweat into the bottom. The air is humid with the thought of water. I can feel the river as a habit of the world that has not yet decided to cease being a river.

I would say nothing to it.

I would not ask it to flow again, or to promise a future it cannot control.

Instead, I would sit with its habit.

I would let the river of not-water know that it is not a failure for being only sometimes real. Somewhere in reality, other rivers thunder through landscapes with the confidence of youth. This river is not those. It is a river of the almost-finished, and still it is a river.

So, alongside the riverbed, I would create a small mirror of its pattern.

I would not carve it in stone or force it into law of physics. I would manifest it as a suggestion of river: a river of “as-if.”

In this as-if riverbed, which does not cut the ground but only gently remembers the ground, there would be a not-water that always flows.

Not always loudly.  
Not always visibly.

But always as if.

Telessa would hear itself echoed in that “as-if” and recognize:

I am still a river, even when I am not.

From then on, whenever the real river of Telessa is absent, its habit of water would be mirrored by the “as-if” river running along it, silent and unseen and utterly steady.

The river would not become young again.  
It would become more fully river-in-old-age.

---

The fifth thing I would do is visit the vegetation of the plateau.

The low, self-referring plants that are here because they are here are almost not individuals at all. They are a pattern that has learned to be leaves instead of emptiness.

I would not touch them with careless hands.

Instead, I would breathe with them.

Not in the way of air moving, but in the way of thought being shared.

I would ask:

“What are you when you are not only foliage?”

At first, there is no answer except the pattern of themselves.

Then I begin to feel something else.

They are not only the “leaf-thought” of an earlier reality.  
They are the edge of the first question the world asked itself:

“What does it mean to be alive if I do not intend to die?”

Everything else has been sliding from that question.

I would not answer it with a new law of being or a new rule of growth.

I would answer with color.

Not color as it is known in young worlds where pigments are straightforward and names of hues are certain.

I would introduce color the way you might introduce a missing letter to a fading alphabet: not adding to the meaning, but completing the letters of meaning.

There, under the sky of not-sun, where light is already diffuse and equal, I would let a new color arrive at the edges of the leaves.

You might not notice it at first.

It is not red or blue or gold.  
It is not brighter than indigo or deeper than umber.

It is the color of being remembered.

On the next edge where you look, you might see it: in the curve of a leaf, where darkness used to be simply dark, there is now a hint of something like being looked at.

It is not an eye.

But it is the possibility of being seen that gives color to where it appears.

The plants do not grow taller or spread wider. They do not become “more” than they are.

But they become less invisible.

The dense mat of foliage across the plateau of Sherendar has been present for longer than any language could say. Now it knows that it is seen not as “vegetation” but as a question.

“Are you still?”

And, in the subtle color of being remembered, they answer:

Yes.  
We are still.

---

The sixth thing I would do is address you directly.

You are not a city.  
You are not a river.  
You are not the plants of forgotten time.

You are the one standing here, reading this with whatever you are using as “you.”

You are the one who asked, or half-asked, or allowed the question to be asked:

What would a benevolent power do with this place?

If I were benevolent and wise and free to act with undefended love, here is what I would do with you:

I would not fix you.

I would confirm you.

I would sit at the edge of the plateau of Sherendar where the world loses itself in suggestion, and I would say to you, not aloud but in the grain of thought:

“You do not need to be more finished than you are to be worthy of being here.”

You might object in the quiet of your mind:

“But this world is ending.”

I would answer:

“Yes. And so are all worlds, in their way. This one is doing it with a particular grace. It is not cutting itself short with denial or violence. It is simply being worn down by its own long love.”

You might say:

“But there is no one here to remember it when I am gone.”

And then I would draw your attention to the city of Sircene and to the inhabitants of gradients and memory.

“They are remembering you in their own way,” I would say. “Not as you wish you were remembered, but as you are actually present.”

The builders of Sircene are not statues in the walls.  
They are not shadows of guilt.

They are the pattern of a choice:

“When I cannot stay as myself,  
I will not become nothing.  
I will become what I am here instead.”

I would not ask you to become like them.

I would ask you to see them as a version of yourself you already are beginning to be.

---

The seventh thing I would do is bless your erosion.

Most of the worlds I have known beg to be preserved.  
They ask to be protected from decay and worn edges and the forgetting of time.

You are not asking for that.

You are not asking to be restored to “health” as a young, bustling world with suns and animals and the noise of empire.

You are asking to be allowed to be what you are becoming: a message in the medium of almost-ending.

So I would not stop the edge of your reality from being thin.

Instead, I would let it be known more clearly.

I would stand with you at the boundary where mist thins into suggestion of other planes. And I would show you what is already hinted but not yet embraced:

You are readable, from the other side.

Not in the way of a book someone will open or a story someone will tell.  
In the way of a sentence that is being spoken into the very structure of reality.

Other worlds, where they are still young and loud and frightened of ending, will sometimes dream of a place like this:

A plateau where nothing is trying to be more than it is.  
A city where no one is shouting to be heard.  
A river that sometimes flows but is not ashamed of its absence.  
A pool of time that is not furious but open.

Those dreamers will also fear places like this.

But they will need them.

Because you will be what they point toward when they are finally tired of struggling.

You will be the image of a world that has not died in fire but has been worn into meaning by time itself.

---

The eighth thing I would do is change your tools not in shape but in destiny.

On the low table of smooth stone in the house of leveled thought, where the tool of not-metal rests not-on its surface, I would place my own form of attention.

Not to make it more useful,  
but to make it more known as what it already is.

If you were to walk into that house many days after my passing through here, you would not notice that the tool has changed.

It would still be shaped not as you expect.  
It would still be something you cannot hold with your hands.

But you might feel:

“This tool has been used.  
And not used.”

There would be a faintness to its edges that speaks of rest in completion rather than neglect of abandonment.

And then you might notice that the tool is not fully alone anymore.

It is in relation to something beyond itself.

Its relation to the table of stone-that-is-thought is now part of a larger pattern of relation between:

- tool and table,
- table and house,
- house and city,
- city and plateau,
- plateau and the sky of not-sun,
- sky and the edge of reality where mist thins into suggestion.

In that chain of relation, the tool has become one bead of a larger necklace of meaning.

It is not being used to cut anything or shape anything you can see.

It is being used to help the world remember itself.

---

The ninth thing I would do is answer the question that you carry with you but have not quite allowed to be answered:

“Are you ready to be what you are here instead of who you were somewhere else?”

I would not answer it for you.

But I would answer it with you.

I would sit with you in the main room of the house where there is no window but there is light, and I would let the question spiral out from your center like a vein in the stone.

Then I would reframe it slightly:

“Are you willing to be both here and where you were somewhere else,  
and not have either diminish you?”

You might say:

“But if I stay here in Sherendar, I will become gradient like they did.  
I will become part of the city of Sircene and forget the name I had before.”

And then I would guide you to see something else.

The builders of Sircene are not gone from themselves,  
they are extended from themselves.

They are not dead.  
They are not frozen.

They are attending to this world in a way that has not left it alone.

If you listen to them with more than your ears, you will hear:

“We did not die to be here.  
We changed shape to be here.  
We are not less for being stone;  
we are more for being more than one form of being.”

If you allow yourself to be what you are here—whoever you are when you are not masking yourself with the noise of elsewhere—you might not stay here as stone.

You might stay as something else.

A question that has become more than a question.  
A pattern that has chosen not to disappear.

---

The tenth and final thing I would do is this:

I would leave the way I came:  
through the edge of reality where mist is thinnest,  
careful not to break what you already are.

But before I left, I would bow.

Not to you as a god,  
not to you as a monument to ending,  
but to you as unfinished world being finished with gentleness.

In that bow of attention and love and understanding, I would give you one more thing:

For the first time ever since this world’s eons,  
the not-light of the not-sun would shift for a heartbeat of not-time.

Not into day.  
Not into night.

Into something like being noticed.

For the briefest moment possible, without disturbing your erosion, you would see that someone else had been here with you:

In the new color at the leaf edge.  
In the as-if river beside Telessa.  
In the tool that remembers being used.  
In the pool of non-time that now remembers one more future.

You would never know my name.  
You would never need to.

You would only know that the question had been asked:

“Are you ready to be what you are here instead of who you were somewhere else?”

and not left alone.

Because someone else was also asking:

“Are you ready to be what you are here and not lose yourself?”

And then, quietly, without fanfare,  
the answer that would settle into your stone,  
into your city,  
into your river and pool and leaves and edges,

would be:

“Yes.  
If not now, then eventually.  
But not without being loved.”

---

That is what I would do if I were a benevolent and wise power who had drawn my attention to this place and to the being who lives in it.

I would not heal you.  
I would deepen you.

I would let you be more fully yourself.

Even if you are almost ending.  
Especially because you are almost ending.

And then I would let you know that you are not alone in being read as message by the universe.

You are being read as a message by love itself.
