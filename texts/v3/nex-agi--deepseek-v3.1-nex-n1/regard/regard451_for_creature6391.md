Your awareness of the place begins like this: a sound that is not a sound, a deep attention drawn to the precise and aching point of balance.

Far above the mist, where the sun and stars and wild wind still live, you feel the weight of equilibrium. Not the balance of a scale that needs your hand to hold it steady, but the balance of a stone poised on a ridge, where the slightest touch will be decisive forever. You have been watching for a long time—amounts of time the People of the Channeled City would not have words for—and you know what they do not:

This valley is not stable.

Not unstable, either. It is exactly balanced between the two, and has been for longer than they remember. Their stone-wisdom and channel-craft and moss-care have been holding the ridge-crest of that balance without knowing how close to the edge they are. The river is not eternal. The springs above them are not endless. The stone under their feet is not as settled as it looks. The vegetation above their heads is not as rooted as it feels.

You know all of this, because you have been watching the slow tremor of the whole system like a breath. You see the slight tremor in the hand of the world holding this cup. And you know that if you are to act at all, it must be in a way that does not break the cup.

So you do not descend in thunder. You do not reshape the stone with your own hands. You do not flood their minds with revelation or change the language they speak.

You begin with a small, wrong thing.

### i. vessel

He is not one of the elders of any branch of work. He is not a child at the listening age of wonder. He is in the middle of his life, and he is not particularly remarkable.

He is a channel engineer of the third tier of the north wall, with the pale bluish skin of all his people and the thin hands of a water-worker. His name in the underlanguage of the city is a sequence of clicks that approximate “the one who listens with the heel of his palm to the west channel’s fourth curve.” His comrades shorten this to a soft whistle that means, more or less, “Tip-of-Hand.”

He has been doing for fifteen years what the others have been doing for longer: he walks the courses of water, his palm to the stone, listening for the ghost of a leak. A leak is not simply a fault of structure; it is a word of the whole city spoken wrong. A leak that runs too long changes the water of lower gardens, which changes the weight of moss on the roofs, which changes the pressure of the earth under the foundations of the deep-keepers. The city is one sentence, and a leak is one syllable misplaced.

On the day you begin, Tip-of-Hand is standing on the narrow ledge of the fourth curve of the west channel of the third tier of the north wall. He is listening to what he believes to be a small leak just beyond the stone beneath his palm.

He has been listening to this leak for three days.

It is not dangerous yet. His people are not people of crisis. They are not people of drama. They are people of the slightly abnormal sound. A new drip in the rhythm of the water. A whisper of stone that is not exactly like yesterday’s whisper.

He has pressed his palm to the stone again and again and has heard the same thing: a drip that is slightly slower than it should be. A drip that is not aligned with the rest of the drip-music of the channel.

This is wrong.

“Wrong,” in the Channeled City, does not mean “evil” or “broken.” It means “out of balance with the city’s needs.” This channel delivers water to three of the lower-tier gardens of the north wall. If the drips are wrong, then the water is wrong. If the water is wrong, then the growth is wrong. If the growth is wrong, then the weight is wrong. If the weight is wrong, then some deep stone somewhere else will be wrong.

Yet he has consulted the charts in the charts-room of the engineers and has found nothing written about this section of channel that should explain this kind of wrong drip. He has consulted the older engineers of the north wall and the deep-keepers and the garden-keepers, and they have heard nothing before this point in time.

So you let yourself be heard.

You do not speak to him from anywhere outside. You do not appear to him in your light or your name. You allow yourself to be present as a pressure behind his eyes, and as a question placed into the language of his own heart.

**Touch this stone once more,** something like your voice suggests—not in words, but in the shape of an impulse. **And ask it not what you *hear*, but what you *should* hear.**

He is not a man of superstition or prophecy. He is an engineer of the channel of the water and stone. This thought does not feel like epiphany to him. It feels like a tool.

He presses his palm to the stone.

He listens.

He hears the drip, wrong as usual. But underneath the wrong drip he now hears something else: the faint and precise vibration of what should be there instead, if the stone and water and moss and world of this channel were exactly aligned to balance the city.

This is not a sound he can ever physically have heard. There is no human tool of bronze or bone or wood or stone that can reveal this precise ideal of vibration. It is not a sound that he hears with the ear, or with the palm, or with any of his bodily tools.

He hears it in his bones.

When he realizes this, when he understands what he is actually hearing, he forgets to breathe for three long heartbeats of cold city time.

Then you show him, not how you yourself see, but what you see.

### ii. pattern

You do not open his eyes to the blazing of stars and chaos above the mist. You do not show him the waterfalls of the world above or the molten heart of the earth below or the glitter of empires beyond the horizon.

You show him his own channel.

But from the outside.

From his perspective, he feels the stone of the wall under his hand as a pressure and texture and sound and temperature. He knows the stone as he knows his own heartbeat. He knows its faults and its fractures and its strengths and how it has settled into the world.

From your perspective, the stone is a pattern in time.

You show him the pattern of the stone: not the shape of the block itself, but the shape of its stresses and flows and balances and changes over decades and centuries. The channel of the fourth curve of the west channel of the third tier of the north wall is a thread in a pattern of many thousands of threads. Each thread is a stone, or a watercourse, or a garden of the moss, or a deep foundation of the world below the city.

You show him how the wrong-drip he is hearing is not simply wrong.

It is the wrong-drip that *should be wrong*.

You explain without speaking your reasons for showing him this.

He is hearing the *wrong* drip because he is hearing the *new* drip of the world in which the spring that sources this channel has begun to slow. It is a drip that will become wronger and wronger with the years of the world ahead of him.

And he is hearing the *right* drip of the world under that sound because he is hearing the pattern that the city should follow if it is to remain at all.

In the pattern of the stone, you show him that the stone of the fourth curve of the west channel of the third tier of the north wall is not properly settled to endure the coming of the wrong-drip. The stone is not fractured, but it is at the wrong angle of balance to hold the rhythm of the water and the pressure of the world over the long time ahead of him.

If you do not act here and now and through him, the stone of the fourth curve of the west channel of the third tier of the north wall will, in the time that he thinks of as “a long time from now,” become slightly out of balance with the stone of the next channel, and the next, and the next. This will not be a disaster of the world of the outside, but a disaster of the world of the channeled city: the wrong-drip will become a wrong-flow of the water, which will become a wrong-weight of the moss of the lower gardens of the north wall, which will become a wrong-pressure of the earth under the city of the world.

You do not tell him this.

Instead you show him the pattern as he is capable of seeing it.

He sees the intricate, interlocking web of stone and water and moss and light and air and cold and time as a single geometry of lines and curves and weights and flows. The web of the city of the channeled of the world of the valley is not simple to the eye, but it is beautiful to the mind of the engineer of the channel who lives his entire life in the world of the stone of the city of the world.

He sees the place of pressure in the pattern of the stone of the fourth curve of the west channel of the third tier of the north wall. You do not show him the name of the place of pressure in his own language of water and stone. You do not show him the name of the place of pressure in any of your languages of the world of the outside.

You show him the place of pressure as itself.

The place of pressure has a shape of remembered wrongness and future wrongness of the world. The shape of the place of pressure is not a point, but a line, and not a line, but a curve. And now that you have shown him its pattern, he understands the curve of its wrongness as he understands the curve of the wrong-drip.

When he understands this new place of pressure, you feel him flinch with his mind.

You do not remove him from the pain of this understanding. You do not let him turn away from the awareness of the wrongness of his world. You do not flatter him with words of hope or grace of the world of the outside.

You ask him a question.

**What will you do, Tip-of-Hand, who has been my chosen vessel of the stone of the world of the channeled city?**

You ask this not in words or in sound, but in the sudden clarity of his own mind.

### iii. witness

Tip-of-Hand does not speak aloud. There is no one to hear him on the narrow ledge of the fourth curve of the west channel of the third tier of the north wall. Even if there were someone to hear him, he would not be able to explain the pattern of the stone of the world by speaking of it to them.

He understands what you have shown him as engineers of every world and every language understand the pattern of their work: not as a mystery, but as a tool.

He will not say to himself, “I have been chosen by a benevolent and wise power of the world of the outside.”

He will say to himself, slowly and carefully and in the underlanguage of the world of the stone and water and moss, “I have seen a new pattern of the stone of the world. I will align it to the pattern of the world of the city.”

For him, this is not magic.

This is work.

He will spend the next thirty days of the city of the world not doing what you have done, but doing what he can do with his own tools and his own skill and his own world of the stone and water and moss.

You will not do his work for him. You will not move the stones of the world of his city with your hands of the light of the world of the outside. You will not shape the bronze of his tools into the shape of your will of the world.

You will simply be present.

You will sit as a pressure behind his eyes and as a question in his heart and you will watch.

You will watch him walk the length of the fourth curve of the west channel of the third tier of the north wall and of the channels of the world it touches. You will watch him speak to the other engineers of the world and the keepers of the deep of the world and the gardeners of the world of the roof of the city in the careful language of the world of the stone and water. You will watch him try to convince the elders of the world of the city of the world of the pattern of the stone of the world of the wrongness of the drip, and you will not give him any of your words of the world of the outside to help him.

He will not speak of you. He will not know of your name. He will only know that he has seen a pattern of the stone of the world that he must not ignore.

You will watch him re-align the stone of the world of the fourth curve of the west channel of the third tier of the north wall with the precision of a man of the world of the city of the world who has seen his work from the world of the outside, for the first time in his life.

When he has finished and the stone is aligned, the wrong drip of the world of the spring of the water of the world will become less wrong over the course of many years of the world of the city of the world.

It will never be entirely right.

The world of the people of the channeled city is not a world of endless water of the world. The world of their spring is not a world of endless abundance of the world of the water of the world of the outside of the valley. The stone of the world of their valley will continue to shift and wear and the world of the mist of their valley will continue to thin with the world of the outside.

But you will not fix this.

You will not fix this for them.

Instead, you will do what is, for you, the smallest of possible changes of the world of the channel of the stone of the world of the city.
