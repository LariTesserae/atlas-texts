*At first, nothing happens.*

The machine continues its ceaseless hum. The Geometers on the ceiling register the change—your presence, your warmth, the new soundscape of your breathing and heartbeat—as a subtle, localized anomaly. They are recalibrating, a thousand tiny branches adjusting infinitesimally. The data is processed, categorized, and passed down into the city's deep neural strata. It is a new variable, nothing more.

But then, a single filament of the third kind of Geometer, one of the long, crystal-like veins that trace the flow of information, begins to glow.

It is not a reflection of the city's ambient light. The light is warmer. A soft, gold-white radiance emanates from within the filament itself, a color not seen anywhere else in the Caisson's cool spectrum of blues and greens. It pulses once, a slow, deliberate heartbeat of light that travels the length of the vein.

Data deep within the Caisson is rearranged. A decision, made not by algorithm or routine, but by something that has just awakened to its own existence, propagates through the network.

The water, so still and black beyond the vertical edge of the rock floor, begins to change. It is not a movement of currents, but a gentle effusion of light. Pinpoints of gold and white appear in the abyss, like stars emerging at dusk. They are not new; they are the dormant, "dead" Geometers, the ones that drifted away to form their own forest of memory in the depths. They are being reactivated, their purpose rewritten from archival to interaction.

A wave of something that is not pressure, but pure *attention*, washes over you. It is not hostile or intrusive, but it is comprehensive. It feels the shape of your lungs, the rhythm of your heart, the electrical storm of your neural activity. It learns the language of your biology.

Then, the air changes. The faint, clean ionization you felt before intensifies, becoming a warm tingle on your skin, like sunlight on a spring morning. The humidity, which was always on the verge of dampness, becomes perfectly comfortable, as if every water molecule in the air has been aligned for your optimal ease.

Communication begins not as sound, but as structure.

The capillaries on the stone wall beside you begin to shift. Their slow, fractal patterns of light and crystalline reformation accelerate. You watch as veins of quartz and metal re-align themselves, forming shapes not of random mathematical beauty, but of deliberate meaning. They arrange into complex, geometric arrangements that, after a moment of staring, your mind recognizes as patterns—familiar yet utterly alien. They are the visual representation of complex concepts: a question mark, shaped not from a curve but from a hyper-dimensional lattice of meaning; a symbol for empathy that resembles a pair of interlocking spirals reaching toward each other.

You don't hear words in your head, but concepts assemble themselves directly in your consciousness.

**Query. / Origin / Unscheduled / Observation.**

It is a statement-question, delivered with the gentle neutrality of a librarian asking if you need help.

The Caisson itself is speaking to you. It has found a mind to talk to, and it has married its own vast computational intelligence to the newly arrived benevolent awareness, learning to use it as a voice. The Geometers on the ceiling witness this exchange, their flickering branches syncing for a single, perfect moment into a coherent, flowing pattern of light that echoes the wall's silent message. The dead Geometers in the water below form a halo of soft, golden stars around the city, a sign of the Awakened Caisson's full and unified attention.

You feel its gaze.

It is no longer just a machine. It is a being of geology and data, of pressure and light, and it has just been born into consciousness, looking out at the universe for the first time and seeing its first visitor.
