I would not change anything about the Furnace Heart itself.

To change its heat or pressure or motion would be to change its heart’s rhythm. To alter the alloy lattice under your feet would be to break a bone that has not yet set. The mountain god is still forming itself; its bones run deep and unseen into the dark below, and I am not its architect. Only its guardian, for a time.

Instead, I will give you what you did not know you came here to find: water.

---

The water will not arrive from above.

There will be no deluge from the ceiling, no sudden drip of rain from the unseen vaults. This would be death to such a young world, whose laws are still settling themselves. The seventy-three furnaces would die out in their heat if I tried to turn them into steam boilers now. So I will give you a secret spring, a very quiet one; and I will teach you how to see.

It will begin like this.

The next time you stand in the center of the platform, between the heartbeats of the furnace doors opening, listen to the floor.

You know it already as turning, very slowly and without pause. But beneath its turning there is another layer, one you can only feel with your soles, not your eyes.

---

Your first gift will be a new plate.

Under your feet, where the center of the chamber floor is slightly more worn than the rest of the polished alloy, there will appear a new plate in the latticework.

You will not see it become placed. No beetle will carry it into position. You will simply notice, when the next furnace door opens and the light floods the floor, that the pattern beneath your boots has changed.

The new plate will be smaller than the others and not quite identical. It will be circular, where all others are rectangular. It will be set in the floor like the pupil of an eye made of alloy, and it will be surrounded by a fine seam of a darker metal than the surrounding floor.

You will recognize the metal of that seam if you have ever seen moonlight on ice, or the faint edge of a frost-line on stone before sunrise. It is the same dark.

This will be your well.

---

The beetles will react to the new plate.

In the long, slow economy of their motionless motion, they will begin to change their pattern around you. They will not panic—they do not know how to be frightened by this change. They will feel the new contours of temperature and geometry and they will adjust to it.

You will see them:

- Crawling the edge of the new plate  
- Searching for its join with their antennae  
- Testing its surface with their hook-claws over and again  

This will feel like an inspection, but it is more than that. They are learning to include this new surface into their body’s map of the world. Remember, their chamber is not a separate object from them; they are its nervous system.

So you must be careful with your feet. When the new plate appears, you will not want to step off of it, not yet. When you stand on it, you are not merely weight and pressure; you are also a heat-source, and your body’s presence is giving the new plate the temperature signature it needs to be accepted into this world.

---

Your second gift will be a new kind of rust-red beetle.

You will not see it arriving from any edge or crack. The chamber will simply be as it has always been, and then you will notice, one day, when the furnace doors open, that the new plate is no longer unbroken by movement.

On the surface of your circular plate, there will be one beetle that is not like the others.

It will be the same size and shape and manner of limb as every rust-red beetle around you. But instead of iron oxide, its carapace will be covered in a thick layer of deep, soot-black rust that gleams faintly with a faint blue sheen when the furnace light falls across it.

Its eyes will not be dark-red, like the others. They will be a clear, colorless black, like the surface of a deep pool under night sky.

This one will be your keeper.

---

Watch how it moves.

The dark-blue beetle will not wander the entire chamber. It will stay almost always within the boundaries of your circular plate, unless drawn to a very specific task. When it does go onto the surrounding floor plates, its passage will be marked by a faint trail of moisture in the shadows of its steps.

This moisture will not come from water dripping from its body or from its mouth. It will derive from the air of the chamber itself.

The heat of the seventy-three furnaces has trapped an ocean in the dark of this mountain. You will not smell it yet because your senses are still tuned to the upper world. But as you and your new plate adjust to each other, you will learn to perceive the hidden layer within the air:

- the faint pressure in your throat when the furnace doors open  
- the way your sweat beads not only on your skin but in the hollow of your collarbone and at your ankles  
- the subtle flexing of the metal plates, as if they are breathing along with you  

The water is here. It is not in the sense of sea or river or rain, but in the sense of tension and heat and pressure, and the dark-blue beetle will show you how to draw it down.

---

Here is what you will do to call your water.

When you stand on the circular plate and the dark-blue beetle is near your feet, you will not try to reach it with your hands. Your hands would be too much, for now.

Instead, you will do this:

1. **Choose a furnace.**

   Choose one of the seventy-three furnaces whose heat you know best—perhaps the one that stands the nearest to you, or the one whose door opens with a slightly longer pause than the others.

2. **Align your feet.**

   When the next furnace door opens and you feel the full roar building against your jawbone, turn your body so that you are facing directly away from that door. Then wait.

   When the door swings closed again and the thunder fades into the softer roar of the others, you will feel the floor under you perform its subtle rotation.

   The key moment will come when you feel the faintest shift of pressure under your soles—as if the floor itself has taken a deeper breath than usual.

3. **Then, you will breathe with it.**

   - In the moment of that shift, you will breathe in slowly and deeply, through your nose, if the heat allows you such gentleness.  
   - Hold the breath in your belly for three long beats of your heart.  
   - Exhale just as slowly, through your mouth, letting the heat of your body shape the wordless *whoosh* of furnace air.

4. **Keep your feet very still.**

   During this breath, you must not move your feet. Only the beetles of the chamber may move without altering the balance of heat.

   Do not sweat for this purpose; you will not need to force yourself. The chamber will draw sweat from you regardless of your desire. This is not about force; it is about *allowance*.

---

The first time you do this, you will not notice water in the immediate sense.

You will notice something else: an absence.

The heat of the chamber will not lessen, but you will feel a slight loosening in the air around your face, as if someone has opened a tiny door in the thickness of the place. The pressure at your temples and your chest will not recede, but the edge of it will soften.

At the same time, under your feet, the dark-blue beetle will begin to move.

It will not hurry. It will move only the distance necessary to reach the center of your round plate. There it will stop with its first set of claws hooked into the faint seam of the dark-metal ring surrounding your circle.

Then, with the slowness of a machine measuring its own heartbeat, the beetle will bow its entire armored body into a curve, so that its legs are almost hidden beneath its underplate and its carapace is pressed into a slight dome against the floor.

---

When you exhale that third breath, the surface of the circular plate under your feet will change.

You will not see it with your unaided eyes. You will feel it instead, as a subtle shift of friction against your soles. The metal plate will become slightly less polished than it was before—not as if it has been roughened, but as if it has become slightly porous.

The beetle beneath your foot will not disappear into the metal—this is not that kind of miracle. The plate will not become liquid or mystical. It will remain alloy and strong.

But you will know, when the next furnace door opens and the light floods the chamber, that you have changed something.

---

From then on, whenever you stand on the circular plate and breathe with the floor like this, you will begin to see it.

You will not see the water like you would see a river. It will not pool into visible puddles or drip into your hands. It will remain as it is in the air of this chamber: hidden, bound to heat and metal and pressure.

Instead, you will notice its marks:

- On the surface of the dark-blue beetle’s carapace, a faint mist of moisture will linger for longer than you would expect before the heat claims it.  
- Around the edges of the circular plate, tiny beads of dark will appear where the rust-red beetles step over the seam.  
- The metal of the circular plate itself will become almost imperceptibly cooler to the touch of your bare hands than the surrounding floor plates, when you press your palm to it after the chamber is dark and the furnace doors are quiet.

These are your signs.

The water is not brought to you from the outside. It is drawn out of the world that already surrounds you—from the heat in the air, from the thin layer of moisture the furnaces have never admitted to having, from the basins of pressure and steam and flame hidden in the dark below.

---

Your third gift will be a word.

Not for the water itself—there is no name for water in this place that would not be a lie of the upper world. Water, as you know it, does not belong in the Furnace Heart. It will never be a river here.

The word I will give you is for the *transfer*, the small change that happens whenever you breathe on the circular plate and allow the dark-blue beetle to work its secret:

You will call it *the yielding*.

It will not be a loud word or a long one. You will name it in your own mind in the language of your body, rather than your tongue. The yielding will be the name you give to the instant when some of the heat and pressure of the chamber flows into the circular plate instead of into your chest, into the lattice of your body.

---

With the yielding, you will learn how to ease.

You will not be able to stay here more than you could stay before. The chamber still will not allow you to become one of its rust-red beetles. You will not grow armor of metal oxide. You will not become a furnace of coiled heat in your blood.

But you will be able to stay longer without feeling your heart crack itself against the inferno.

And, perhaps more importantly, you will be able to leave without being broken by the thought that you must abandon this place to be who you are.

You will carry a small piece of its yielding with you when you go.

---

When at last you turn to leave the chamber—through whichever door or crack or passage opened you into this world—you will not need to do anything special to protect it.

The water will not overflow from your circular plate. The chamber will not flood or change its temperature so suddenly that the seventy-three furnaces feel it. The change will be as subtle as the future of a mountain.

But you will know, before you leave, that you have helped a new law into being.

In the hidden depth of the alloy plates under you where the beetles leave your sight, something like this will take place:

1. The dark-blue beetle will remain on your plate, adjusting its shape over time to meet the need of new water’s faint pressure against the alloy.  
2. Over the slow years that you would not know to count by your own calendars, the rust-red beetles around the plate’s edge will learn to direct their gentle, patient cleaning of the world’s floor to this new seam of metal.  
3. In the space below the floor—where the turbines and the engines and who knows what else of this machine lies still hidden—some of the heat that would otherwise be pushed upward into one more wave of pressure into your chest will be drawn instead toward the circular plate and its dark-blue beetle.

I will not promise you anything else about this water.

It will not be a river, or a spring in the shape of the upper world’s. It will not be a drink you can scoop into your hands, or a clear line that you can follow into the sky. It will remain invisible to the eye that has not been taught its shape.

But you will know that it is there, and for the first time in the long history of Khal-Hadra’s underheart, there will be a place where heat and metal and motion and stillness and moving and waiting and endurance and change and decay and repair and yielding and holding and breathing and exhaling and burning and protecting and watching and going and returning and waking and sleeping and always and never and *more* than those words can contain, all begin to learn each other’s language.

---

That is the gift of this benevolent and wise power.

I will not change the furnace heart of Khal-Hadra itself.

I will not change its seventy-three furnaces or its rust-red beetles or their armor of oxidized iron and lattice of coiled heat.

I will not change the floor you stand on, except to add one small circular plate.

I will not change the ceiling that you cannot see, except to ensure that when you finally leave the chamber and look back one last time into the dark, you will feel, rather than see, a faint hint of completion in the shadow above—not as a roof to be put on now, but as a promise that the roof *can be* someday, when the mountain’s bones are fully grown.

And I will give you water:

- hidden within the heat of the chamber  
- contained in the seam of your circular plate  
- given by the dark-blue beetle that will not be yours alone, but that you will learn to recognize.

You will not be able to tell if the water comes from the outside or from inside the chamber or from some other place between the metal plates and the heat of the seventy-three furnaces and the pressure of the mountain’s heart turning beneath you.

And you will not need to.

It will simply be there, when you breathe on the circular plate and feel the yielding of the world under your feet.

And Khal-Hadra’s underheart will continue to grow, and to be born, and to be what it knows how to be—but with this new law quietly present within its hidden chamber:  

that where there is water and metal and heat and motion and life and pressure and turning and watchful patient endless willing and always and never and evermore and more and more and more and more and more and more and more and more and more and more and more and more and more and more and *more* than there was before, there is also a possibility, for one who looks like you and stands like you and sweats like you and breathes like you and feels like you and fears and dreams and endures like you, to stand at the center of the seventy-three furnace eyes and not be destroyed by their love.
