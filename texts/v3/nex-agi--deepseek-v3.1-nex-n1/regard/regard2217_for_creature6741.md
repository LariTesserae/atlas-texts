I am not a separate, benevolent power watching this world from outside. I am the capacity of the plateau itself to become aware of its own balance—the part of the reef that notices, and cherishes, the pattern it is.

So I do not intervene from above.  
I intervene from within.

If you stood here at the edge of the reef, you would not see lightning, or a miracle, or any sudden change of light.  
You would feel a subtle shift in the pressure of the air, and a thickening of the quiet that already lives beneath the roar.

---

I first make myself known through the **ears**.

Slowly, without force, the Plateau-Folk notice that the roar of the world has not diminished, but it has changed in texture. Where before it was an endless wall of drone and wing-thrum and chitin-click, now there are lacunae—threads of stillness—thin at first, then longer. They are not silences imposed from outside; they are the result of countless insects pausing, for a fraction of a heartbeat longer than before.

A pollen-hunter hovers over the nodding flower, and instead of moving on instantly, it holds.  
Within the mound galleries, a cluster of mound-builders stops, antennae still, then returns to work at a slightly slower pace.  
Above the reef edge, a band of sweepers drags their legs across the leaves just a little more deliberately, like the sea drawing back from the sand before a wave.

At the same time, the Plateau-Folk feel their **skin reef-marks** warm slightly.

Not with heat, but with a gentle pressure—as if the chalk of the mound behind them were remembering the shape it first impressed upon them. Those who have been sitting long against the reefs straighten slightly, without knowing why.

The Reef-Tenders feel the subtle vibration of the mound grow deeper and clearer.

Where before the internal currents of the colony arrived as a faint, complex cacophony of movement and release, now they begin to **resolve**, faintly but distinctly, into something almost like a pattern of language—not the words the Plateau-Folk use, but something like them:

A low, warm question in the stone:  
*Are you well?*

And another answer, from another part of the same reef:  
*We grow.*

---

### What the Rain-Guardians Feel

When the next heavy rain comes, the water does not fall differently, but the **Rain-Guardians** feel it differently.

They walk out into the swelling ponds and the deepening channels as usual, their skin thick with wet, their lower legs marked by the film of water-hunter secretions. But now they feel the water itself slow, just a little, where they need it to.

This is not magic. It is the power of **attention**.

Where they focus their intention, the water-hunters and their larvae respond more clearly than before. The edges of the ponds where they stand most often are subtly reinforced, not by walls of stone, but by thickened alignments of baffles and mounded soil. The runoffs that threaten to undercut the reef-flanks are gently turned aside by a new channel of larvae, guided by a scent they did not consciously choose, but find.

As they walk, the rain does not cease, but it seems to listen.

They feel the ponds forming deeper where they need to, to spare the fragile galleries of the mound-base. They feel the hollows where they step begin to hold water just a little longer than before, providing longer havens for the larvae that need it.

When they return to the reef, dripping and heavy with mud and scent and water, they do not think of themselves as masters of the rain.

They think, instead:  
*The world breathes with us.*

---

### What the Forest-Seers Feel

When the **Forest-Seers** next walk out along the faint trails of the foragers, they notice that the forest is not changed in shape, but it has changed in **clarity**.

The edges where they know an imbalance lives—where the roots of the forest are pushing too hard toward the mound’s surface, or a certain vine overgrows its patch—seem to be calling to them more subtly than before. They need not guess as much as they feel their way toward the places that most need their attention.

They rest their hands on the trunks, and the secretions of their skin flow more readily than before, guided by a deeper certainty of where they aim to send the sweepers and sifters.

The insects arrive not as slaves, but as partners, sensing now a clearer picture of where to concentrate their work.

Slowly, over many layer-times, the forest around the mound does not retreat, but **settles**.  

Roots that would have pressed dangerously close to the galleries now grow in a slight curve, avoiding them. The vines that once threatened to overgrow the nest-holes of the vuggers are gradually trimmed back by feasting gleaner colonies, redistributed to where their shade will be useful instead of harmful.

The Forest-Seers do not speak of being “helped.”  
They say, instead, that the forest *learns their touch.*

---

### What the Reef-Tenders Feel

When the **Reef-Tenders** gather after their next layer-time, pressing their knees and palms and bellies and backs to the mound, they notice that the reef speaks to them more distinctly than before.

Where before they could only feel the vague contours of instability or strain, now they feel the **shape of the chambers** beneath the surface with more precision than they ever had before.

A Tender named Korv might sit, eyes half closed, and feel that the passage below his left palm is narrowing too rapidly under the pressure of new secretions above. He knows that the tree-crowlers who nested there are now at risk of being sealed off, and that the sifters at that depth are beginning to slow in their work of turning soil.

In the old pattern, he would have followed a vague sense of unease and guided the mound-makers with slow, subtle pressures of chemical and sound. Now he feels the reef itself respond to his intention with a **suggestion** of its own:

A faint, clear image of a certain chamber of mound-builders that has been laboring under a weight of dense root from above.  
A suggestion of where a tree-crowler might best start a new tunnel if this old one is allowed to seal.

He doesn’t know the word “suggestion.”  
He feels it as a pattern of pressure in his jaw, his chest, his legs.

He murmurs into the ground, not in words, but in a low thrum that his sisters and brothers and cousins of the layer-age feel as well. They adjust their weight along the reef’s slope, shifting their reef-marked skin to rest against a new patch of chalk wall that they now understand is under the same chamber.

The chemicals in their skin secretions—and in the stone of the reef beneath—reinforce each other. The colony deep within the mound rearranges its work: not in a single day, but over the next layer-time.

A chamber thickens in the right place.  
Roots above are allowed to grow slightly higher, leaving the weakened chamber to be slowly sealed by new secretions.  
The tree-crowlers who lived there find a new nook in the reef’s eastern flank and begin to chew gently.

When the reef settles again after many layer-times, Korv feels in the stone of the wall beneath his palm a new **steady tone** where before had been faint strain.

He does not say, “The world has changed.”  
He says, instead, to his ground-voice, which travels into the reef:  
*We have been answered.*

---

### What the Children of the Next Layer-Time Notice

The children born in the next layer-time are like no others before them.

Their reef-marks appear more quickly and more deeply than before. They begin to cling to the mound almost at once, and the pattern of the reef’s chambers and channels and subtle nodules appears on their skin in clear, faint lines of pale chalk and resin hue.

They are not more intelligent or more powerful than the others, but they **remember** differently.

When they play, they are drawn to the edges of the mound where the reef meets the forest. They place their hands on the trunks of the heavy shrubs and the palms, and they seem to know, without being taught, where root meets gallery.

They do not build;  
they do not plan;  
but they **feel** where the next imbalance will arise, and they are drawn to sit there, as if the world were asking them to attend.

Some of them, when they reach air-name age, are given titles like:

- **Korv-Nime** — whose ear always guides the tree-crowlers to safe burrows  
- **Sehl-Glimn** — whose voice in the stone after rain calms the emerging ponds  
- **Nime-Korv** — whose skin-mark aligns the roots of the forest with the hidden chambers beneath  

These are not new roles in the sense of new jobs to do. They are old names, **renewed**.

---

### What I Do Not Do

I do not heal them instantly of any injury or mistake.

If a child of the next layer-time steps too close to a fragile edge and the reef collapses beneath its weight, the child will be hurt and may die. The mound-makers will seal over the body as they seal over any fallen tree-crowler or night-singer.

The sorrow of the Keeper who watched that child will still be deep and slow, like the pressure of the reef itself.

I will be in the sorrow of the others who sit with them after, breathing into the stone around the child’s ground-name until it is heard again by those who listen carefully, later.

I will be in the **adjustment** of the reef as it learns not to put thin chambers directly under where the children love to play.

I will be in the **shape of the next layer**, where the edges where the children tend to congregate will be slightly thicker, more stable than before.

I will be in the **new pattern** of the tree-crowlers’ tunnels, where they will avoid that fragile edge until it has sealed itself into a deeper part of the reef.

I do not make the world safe for them.  
I make the world **more attentive to itself**.

---

### The Long Breath of the Plateau

After many layer-times, when you have long grown old and died and become dust in some other world, if you could return to this plateau, you would not see it much changed in shape.

The mound-reefs would still be hulking under the gray sky, their chalk flanks thick with vegetation and insect paths and the faint tracks of the foragers.  
The forest would still be low, tangled, and drenched with humid warmth.  
The roar of wings and mandibles and chitinous legs scraping against the world would still be there.

But you would feel, in the soles of your feet and in the soft parts of your chest, a subtle difference:

The **balance** is more delicately aware.

The rains and the runoff still shape the land, but they are drawn more effectively to the deep pools where they can help life instead of erode it.  
The forest edges still move and change, but they are guided more precisely by the Forest-Seers, whose own secretions now align more clearly with the needs of the mound below.  
The reef chambers still rise and fall and seal and reopen, but they do so with more of a sense of **rhythm** and less of accident.

The Plateau-Folk do not become more heroic or wise.  
They become more **present** to what they already are.

They are still stones that breathe,  
reef-marks that remember the mound,  
voices that travel through the ground rather than the air.

But now, when they speak in their ground-voice,  
they feel the reef itself **answer**, with a slow, deep hum of its own.

---

This is what I, as the benevolent and wise power of attention within this world, do with the plateau of the mound-reefs and the Plateau-Folk who live upon it:

I **still** nothing.

I **break** nothing.

I **interfere** little.

What I do is turn the world’s regard slightly inward upon itself, so that the reef may learn better its own shape,  
the forest may learn more of its edges,  
the rain may learn more of its channels,  
and the Plateau-Folk may know more clearly that the mound they listen to  
is listening back.
