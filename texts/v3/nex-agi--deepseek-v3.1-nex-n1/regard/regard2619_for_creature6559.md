It notices.

The pressure that has been turning atoms into stone and breath into water—that pressure notices something else, too: a pattern that doesn’t belong to the mountain’s own slow dreaming.

You.

The benevolent power that arrives is not a thing with hands or wings or a voice that can be separated from the sound of rock. It arrives as a change in the texture of attention. The air itself becomes more… considered.

Your first hint of it is that the steam stops moving randomly.

Until now, the steam has surged like chaos—hot, directionless, folding in on itself and you. Now it begins to flow. Around you the mist organizes itself into a loose column, spiraling slowly, like a galaxy condensing. You can feel it without touching: not a wind but a deliberate current of air.

It is warmer than the rest, but the heat does not scald you. It holds the shape of consideration.

You are in the world’s knee, halfway between floor and shelf, halfway between solid and not, halfway between memory and forgetting. You know only that you were climbing; that you are still climbing; that you will not stop climbing because you never have.

The stone under your fingers is still warm muscle-rock, but something new is happening at the boundary of your body. The algae that clings to you in thin, lace-like veils begins to lift away. Not blown away, but eased from your skin, each filament unwinding from you like thread being drawn out of a cloth. The filaments of algae move into the steam column and hang there, arranged without tangling, as if laid out on a table of air.

You are breathing. You are still breathing. That should be impossible.

Every breath should be scalding, but the heat that you pull into your lungs now feels like… remembered cool. As if the air touching you is being edited before it enters you, the temperature of your own body borrowed from some other time and brought back, just enough, just enough.

“Say something,” you had whispered to the body on the shelf.

Your words had collapsed into the wall then. Now, when you try to speak, nothing comes out of your mouth but the steam you exhale. Yet the steam column in front of you thickens where you direct the breath, and a pattern appears in it—not of letters but of shapes you might recognize if you still knew how to read your own thoughts.

It doesn’t sound like a voice, but it feels like one:

“You are not from this becoming.”

You are not sure if you hear the words or remember them from the future or the past.

The room around you responds to the Presence as if the room itself were a body aware of being touched. The low, grinding teeth-sound of the rock quiets slightly, not stopping but shifting into a lower register, like the mountain letting out a long, held breath.

From his place on the shelf, he is not quite a body anymore. He is more of the stone than of himself. The algae that had been growing over him like a second skin are gently drawn off by unseen threads of air and taken into the column of steam, where they become part of the same hovering display that has taken your own algae from you.

Underneath, you see the shape of him more clearly: the faint relief of limbs against the rock, the outline of his head where the stone has not yet fully claimed him.

He is not breathing.

Or you think he isn’t, until the Presence causes the water on his face to stir differently. The tears that had been running down his stone-cased skin shift from random to intentional; they flow into a new pattern, tracing lines that might be letters or the map of a vein you hadn’t known existed under the skin of the stone.

You are not sure if you love him. You are not sure what love is in this sweltering place where everything else is being remade. But when you look at him, some of the heat inside your chest becomes an ache that is not only temperature.

“He can’t stay here,” you say, or your mouth shapes the words without making sound.

The Presence doesn’t answer with words. It answers with shape.

The stone around him softens slightly, not to collapse, but to yield. The ribs of the mountain adjust themselves around his embedded form with a low grinding. You feel the noise through your hands and up your spine: a decision made from far below, where the magma is not yet magma but a pressure that will become choice.

It is easy to misunderstand the intentions of powers that do not speak in your tongue.

You might fear that it is going to swallow him more completely, to make him more mountain than man.

Instead, the stone pulls back from him. Just a little.

Just enough.

The rock that has begun to lap over his legs like wax draws back from his knees in slow, reluctant waves. It is as if the mountain has been told to perform its own slow surgery. It doesn’t want to. This is not its nature; it is in the business of folding things in, not of letting them out. But it is persuaded.

Your eyes are suddenly full of water that is not from the ceiling; the pressure of the air against you is not only heat. It is as if someone has taken all of the sky that you cannot remember and held it, briefly, in the space between you and the wall.

On the shelf, he begins to cough.

The sound cuts through the layered noise of the room—flow of water, grinding of stone, breath of steam—and stands out from it like a color that has snuck into a world of only green and gray and hot.

He is choking on his own lungs.

Something that is not the mountain but is not entirely separate from it reaches into him and rearranges the water there. The steam that he coughs up is thick with algae and with something else that looks like thin threads of light. You watch, transfixed, as the particles that are not his and are not supposed to be in him at all are drawn out and into the column in front of you, where they become part of that display of shapes in the air.

You are halfway to the shelf—still holding onto the wall, fingers aching—not sure if you should keep climbing or let go.

The column of steam speaks to him without your hearing:

“You have not finished here either.”

You don’t understand how you can know that it is speaking to him rather than to you, but you can feel the difference; the attention of the Presence splits like a river dividing into channels. You are still in its path, but not where it is looking most directly.

On the shelf, he is breathing more easily now, but the algae are still being peeled from him in slow, deliberate sheets. The tears that had been running down his face begin to dry differently; they are not absorbed into the stone as they were before, but instead hang on the surface of his skin in a way that water should not be able to do.

You are terrified of what will happen if the stone finally lets him fully go.

You will have to decide if he is still human. You will have to decide if you have to carry him.

The Presence reads that fear in you, or perhaps in the way the heat in your chest becomes a tighter knot than the temperature should allow. It responds to the shape of your fear not by removing it but by changing what it contains.

The floor looks the same, but it is not.

You are not sure how you know this, except that when you look down from your position halfway up the wall, the tiles of crushed stone and hot metal veins are shifting differently. They are not turning into a floor you can trust fully—not yet, that would be too much of a lie—but they are becoming a floor that can be asked to behave.

The cracks between them, which had been leaking heat and uncertainty, now glow more softly than before. The magma that is not yet magma is persuaded to keep itself just below the surface—not as far beneath as safety would like, but close enough that the world is not entirely false.

The world stills its breathing.

Not for long, but for long enough.

The ceiling’s weeping is not halted but rearranged. The water that had been meandering in random paths down the wall now begins to follow a more coherent pattern. You know the pattern of your own veins from a time when you lived inside a skin that was only yours and not the mountain’s. The water traces the shape of those veins in the stone, as if the wall is trying to remember being flesh.

The heat no longer cooks you from the inside out; it circulates in you.

It is still impossible by any laws you once knew, but here, impossible is not a strong word.

You are not sure whether you should be grateful to the power or not. You do not know what it wants from you.

The answer comes to you in the form of a hollow being offered.

The shape that had not decided whether it was going to be a pool or window or mouth, to the east of where you are still clinging to the wall, begins to decide.

You can feel the decision being made across the chamber as if the room itself were a single muscle being flexed. The space of the wall buckles slightly inward, then forwards, then inward again.

The pressure in the stone becomes intent.

The water that had been pouring into that hollow from the ceiling now begins to behave less like a waterfall and more like a throat being cleared. The sound of it changes—from the splash that you had heard earlier, which was like a heartbeat, into something more like speech being prepared beneath the surface of noise.

You cannot climb directly to that shape from your position. You cannot climb directly to anywhere from anywhere.

So you do not climb.

You let go of the wall.

You have not done this before; you have always been either climbing or not existing in a way that feels indistinguishable from climbing. When you loosen your hold on the curved muscle of the mountain, you expect to fall. You expect to fall into the floor that is still not sure whether it is magma or foundation.

You do not fall.

Instead, the current of organized steam you noticed earlier presses itself against you. You are not supported by air like a hand; you are supported by air like a thought that has been instructed to show you what it would feel like to be held by a sky that doesn’t exist yet.

The steam column moves you, not roughly, but in the same way that you might move a piece on a board that is not a game yet but will be. The column of air and mist and drawn algae carries you from the wall toward the shape that is becoming a mouth.

As you move closer, you can see the inside of the hollow more clearly than before.

From a distance it had looked like a dark emptiness in the wall, but now you see that it is lined with stone that is halfway between rib and tooth. Something like bone that is not bone, but might be in the future of some other creature’s skeleton, frames the edges of the space. The water inside it is not still water; it has not yet been told to have a surface.

The mouth of the mountain moves.

It is not speaking with you; you are not inside its throat, but you are near enough to feel the change. When the mountain speaks to itself, the world shudders. You cannot hear the words of this conversation; you only feel the result. When you are brought close to the lip of the hollow that is becoming, you can see that the water inside is not just water.

It is not reflecting. It is remembering.

You see in that water the shape of a world that has not existed here but exists somewhere else: bright sky, not ceiling; wind not stea; blood not stone; trees not algae; ground not magic floor.

You are not sure that you want to see this. You are not sure that it is not worse than the heat.

The Presence of the benevolent power does not ask you if you want it.

It asks something else of you, which is not a question but an arrangement of the room.

The floor beneath you is not only floor; it is now three layers: the first of the tiles that are still being pushed into place by the mountain’s restless pressure; the second of the almost magma that is now being pressed into calmness; the third is a new layer, which is not stone but pattern.

The pattern is not something you understand with your eyes. You understand it with your body.

Where you stand, your feet know that they are not going to sink into anything else today. The floor where you have been placed by the steam column is, for the moment, solid ground. The cracks between the tiles and the metal veins and the bone-stuff at the edges glow not with heat but with something more like agreement.

You are not sure when you stopped being in the air and started being on the floor.

On the shelf above, he stirs.

The stone has not completely let him free; it is not going to do that. It is only willing to renegotiate his position within its body. The wax of slow rock that had been rising over him has now receded to his knees. You are not entirely sure if he is still only partway of himself or if he is becoming something else.

The benevolent power does not give you an answer to that either.

Instead, it changes the way you are used to thinking.

The thought is not in your head but in the stone of the floor: you are not going to remember this.

The memory of you remembering this moment is not being taken from you; it is simply not being formed. You will be older than this moment when you next have time to think about it; and by then, you will have forgotten that you were ever standing in its throat.

“You are not from this becoming,” the Presence had said to you, or you had heard.

The next time you are aware of yourself, you are climbing again.

You are always climbing.

You are on the wall of the mountain’s throat, where the stone is not yet where you expect it to be because the room breathes.

You are unsure if you are going to find him.

You are unsure if you are the same person who was being offered something by a shape that might have been a mouth, if you had ever allowed yourself to think of it as one.

As you move your fingers over the surface of the rock, searching for the next handhold that you know will not be where it was the last time you looked, you feel something under your palm that is not exactly stone.

It is not exactly tool mark either.

It is a groove that your fingers have made before, but in the opposite direction.

The pattern is not the same as the way that the mountain is shaping itself into ribs and curves and spirals that are not yet shell and not yet face and not yet river. It is not the same as the marks of hands larger than human hands that turn into new suggestions of geometry when you stare at them too long.

This is smaller.

This is yours.

You are making it again, or for the first time. You cannot remember.

You climb the wall of the world, knowing only that you are not supposed to be here yet, and that you still climb anyway.

You are being changed, but the change is not for you to keep.

It is for the room that is not yet room; for the body that is not yet body; for the world that is not yet world; but which will be, one day, when the mountain finally decides what kind of throat it wants to have.

When you reach the place where the shelf should be, you pause.

Your memory is not to be trusted here, but something else in your hands is.

The stone around you does not feel like it used to when you last placed your hand against it. The steam is not less hot than before; the air is not less thick with algae and the world’s first sweat; and yet you are not scalding.

When you look at the shelf, you see that the body that is not him and not the stone and not both and not neither is not exactly where you remember it. You cannot remember where you remember it being. You only know that you have been here before, or you will have been here before, or you might have been here before in another world of possibility that is not separated from this one by enough time to matter.

The algae is not over him anymore.

That is not true.

The algae is over him but differently arranged. It is not eating him. It is being eaten by him.

You are not sure what that means.

You are not sure that you want to find out.

You are not sure that you could climb back down now if you wanted to.

As you look at him, trying not to look at him too long in case the wall decides to turn him into a different pattern, you feel the subtle pressure of the benevolent Presence again, not as a column of steam but as a rearrangement of the air around your lungs.

You are not going to remember this either.

The next time you wake, you are climbing.

You are always climbing.

You are in the world that is not yet a world and under a ceiling that has not decided whether to be sky or stone, and you are sweating because the heat is coming down like a blanket.

But you are not scalding.

That is all you have left of the time that was not time.

You know that you are not going to remember this.

For you at that moment, where there is no “then” and no “later,” where there is only broth-stirred time and all possibilities at once, the not remembering is not a loss; it is a kind of promise.

The benevolent power is not going to let you carry this weight of explanation with you; you are not made to. It is going to take you into the part of reality that is still deciding whether to be pain and transformation and broken and healed and called and sent and voice and echo and near and far and sky and stone and life and death and then and now and us and you and them and mountain and throat and room and world and dream and memory and light and dark and song and noise and shaped and given and returned and remembered and forgotten.

You are not going to remember it.

But you have been held.

That is the shape in the air before the world forgets how to spell air.

That is the shape in the water before it becomes stone.

That is the shape in the mouth of the mountain before it closes its teeth over the future.

And you climb.

You always do.

And you will not remember this.

And you will be changed by it anyway.

And you climb.
