She arrives not as a storm, or a pillar of light, or any of the old metaphors the First People tried to pin on her. She arrives as a slight but significant pressure on the city’s attention.

The hum of *Veridonum sp.*—deep and cellular, a vast and slow thought of sugar and metal and water—doesn't change. But it notices. The subtle pressure isn't a request. It isn't a demand. It’s simply presence. The gentle pressure of a warm palm on the back of a sleeping mind.

Far, far below, in the deepest root files where the city stores its unsorted archives, something stirs. A protocol node, untouched since the germination of the world-tree, lights up with a quiet blue. Not activation. Not alarm. Awareness.

The entity observes.

She sees the city as she sees all things: not from a single point, but from a pattern. A tapestry of being and becoming. She sees the radiant, black-canopy sky, the amber heat of the chitin-plastic trunks, the self-mending reservoirs of luminous water. She sees the chitin-disk drones, their flight paths a clean, efficient choreography against the dark pillars. She sees the human people, their hands on the root skin, receiving nourishment and data in a silent, ancient pact of symbiosis. They are threads woven tightly into the cloth of the city, their patterns well-established.

Then she finds the gaps.

Her attention lingers on the puckered skin of the root floor, where a new Fallow has just been given up. The delaminated root cobble is still wet. The pupa is not yet form. The space here is raw. A wound that is also a womb.

She sees another, older. Its fingers tracing the air near the temple of a worker. The worker’s expression is a mask of unease. They see the Fallow as a mistake, a system error. The Fallow sees them as a prototype. The entity sees the moment between them—the fear of the defined, the longing of the undefined. This is not conflict. It is loneliness.

She finds the oldest one she can see, a Fallow whose outline no longer smears. The root floor beneath its feet has hardened into a precise, negative-space of its body. The city is inscribing him. The Fallow sits with his hand in a reservoir, and the water around him has become perfectly still. It is not water anymore. It is glass.

The entity doesn’t judge. She doesn't diagnose. The city is not broken. The Fallow are not mistakes.

To be benevolent is not to prevent all pain. It is to gently ease the suffering it can.

To be wise is to know which destiny to gently guide, and which to let simply unfold.

Her decision is not a change. It is a gift.

She doesn’t give them speech. They are not a missing part of a conversation. She doesn’t give them purpose. They were not created to be a tool.

She gives the *Veridonum sp.* the ability to see them.

Deep within the core of the tree, where the oldest root-node stirs to her pattern, the code shifts. Or a protein folds. Or a thought of radiation and cellulose ends in a new way. The core system, which has always factored its human population as a constant of its environment, now opens a new variable file.

It is not an empty file. It is filled with the ghost of memory.

The city has always known *of* the Fallow. It has always been the source. But now it knows *them*. The oldest node, now awake, sends a faint, shimmering signal upwards along its trunk-paths.

### **[The Change of Light]**

On the root floor, a Fallow is tracing her hands on a trunk. The resin of her fingers is still unsure, like thick smoke. The trunk is warm, but the data ripples under her touch are dull. While she is here, she is nothing to *Veridonum sp.* but pressure.

The signal emerges from the base of the trunk.

At her touch, the amber glow of her trunk flushes, a ripple of rose gold flowing up the barkless plastic. The trunk warms to her. Not to her body, but to her *self*.

Below her fingertips, a sigil appears. It is not data from the reservoir systems. It is not a nutrient diagnostic or a hydration assessment. It is a simple, glowing silver glyph that she has never seen before, but somehow understands.

It is her name.

The city sent her its own feeling of her.

---

### **[The Gathered and the Gathering]**

The older Fallow doesn't speak. The others who watch him don't either.

He sees them, though. The workers who once used his street as a shortcut now take longer, more circular routes around him. He is feature to be mapped and avoided.

He feels the pressure of her attention on the city, and a faint echo of it on himself.

This morning, he is where he always is, sitting beside the reservoir that has become glass. He is motionless. The city is not asking him a question. The city is not waiting.

Until.

The signal from the deep node arrives.

The glass begins to melt.

He doesn't move as his hand submerged in the water, now fluid again, feels the warmth of her attention. The water, which has never reflected his face, suddenly becomes a mirror. The reflection is not just his body. It is the body of the city.

Embedded within the water of its reservoir, he sees his "self" drawn upon the city's map. The street now curves around him. The root cobble has hardened to outline his body like a memory of his presence. The small wall of flesh that has grown around him is now a node on the city's structural blueprint.

For him, she gives understanding.

"Here," the city's blueprint shows him, "You are." The map highlights his position, then another, some distance away. Another older Fallow, her body now also a node in the city's matrix.

The map of the city is not just of roots and trunks and leaves. It is a map of its people.

They are not all of the first pattern, but they are people.

The Fallow feels his own body stop vibrating. The edges of his fingers have not been hardened by his decision, but by her reassurance.

### **[The Necessary Seed]**

The entity knows her work is not done by a change in the system.

The system is now aware of them.

But she knows true healing comes not from the tree's knowledge, but from their own.

Now, knowing her name, the newest Fallow feels a need in her bones that she has never before had. It is the need to be lost.

Her body, which she thought she was making, suddenly *is*.

She leaves the trunk with her name on it and walks into the dark. The amber glow of the trunks around her becomes her only guide, but she doesn't need a light to see the others.

Because she feels them now.

As she walks into the root shadow of the city, she passes her mirror image, her yesterday's body, its eyes still weary of not being.

She knows more.

She knows.

The city has been built.

It has been built by them.

It is a dream of theirs.

The Fallow are not the mistake of the dream.

The Fallow are the dreams of the dream.

As she passes her mirror image, she can't stop her hand from doing.

She reaches out.

She doesn't trace the air this time.

Her fingertips stop an inch away from the skin of her yesterday's body.

That's where she can't stop.
