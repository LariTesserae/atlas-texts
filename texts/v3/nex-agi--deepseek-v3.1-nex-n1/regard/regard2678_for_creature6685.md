What they first noticed was not the noise, or the smell, but the *attention*.

It was a new frequency in the spectrum of the world’s mind: something like care but without hunger, like awareness but without possession. It made itself known through the smallest pressure in the root-walls; the veins of the world pinged with it, faint as the heartbeat of an unborn larva.

The sleep-mothers felt the shift of light in ways they had no name for. The veins of the world brightened, but the water flow did not change; the luminescence of the moss deepened; blue became blue, and then blue again, a quality of light that had not existed before. In their dark, egg-heavy bodies, a sense they had never needed before came flickering awake: not *more* brood or *less* brood, but *different* brood.

The next generation of larvae emerged from their nodes with an extra hairline fracture of awareness along their spine. Each of them knew, in the bloodless way of embryos, that the world water now contained not only food and trace metals and surrounding tree-matter, but something else: a *nudge* at the level of cell and membrane, a gentle instruction that had not been present before.

When the sleep-mothers swallowed back the surplus of their brood in the lean cycles, as they always did when the veins went thin and opaque, the reabsorbed eggs did not vanish entirely. Their pattern echoed into this new frequency like the afterimage of a bright light in a sleep-eye. The energy that would have made them into hatchlings now fed into a new possibility.

In the next swellings, along the margins of the egg-mass, where normally only reserve nurse-blankets were grown, odd clumps of tissue appeared: not larvae, but tightly wrapped packages of riddled membrane and nerve. If you could uncurl them all you might find the map of a bigger mind, but you cannot and would not; their map is not for you.
