They did not ask me.

No city begs to be made its own engine. No plateau asks to be scraped into the shape of a tool. This truth—the *lack of asking*—follows me from world to world, like the shadow of a machine I never agreed to wear.

Here, on this tabletop of basalt and water and air, the citizens of Thaumos have made their own answer to that un-asked question: they have turned themselves into the mechanisms of their pressure, the fuel of their own engine.

I am not here to judge them for that.

I am only here to see what is missing.

And then, to see what can be given.

---

### 1. The Invitation That Was Not an Asking

The Thaumok Collector—whose name I will never know in the way of syllables and speech—lets me into the pressure of the city’s underlay.

They are not one being.

When the helmet of their suit is “removed,” I see that the helmet itself is partially their body: the glass of it is structured by the same condensate cartilage that forms their cornea, their hair, the ridges of their spine. The creature inside—that is them too. The colony of glass threads in the suit’s inner layer shares the same biological rhythm as the threads under the floor, and the same rhythm as the heart beating under the ribs of the person wearing them.

Symbiosis pushed to the point where there is no clear boundary: *Thaumos.*

They are, in the strict sense of the word, beautiful.

And also, in the strictest sense of the word, incomplete.

“You are reading,” the Collector says, without words. The sensation of the sentence is not in the air of the chamber but in the air of my chest: a change of pressure, like a hand closing into a loose fist.

“Yes,” I answer, in the polite way of their city: not by opening my throat but by allowing my lungs to change the shape of their expansion.

The reading they are waiting for is not one of scanners or lenses. It is not a measurement of the condensate rate or the thermal draw of the platforms. It is not the number 0.1 or the number of kilocalories per square meter of the floor.

They are waiting for me to read the shape of their absence.

The plateau of Thaumos was not made to be this. Once, there was only hot air and basalt and water and the possibility of life. Then, in the long before I now walk into, someone—somebody—came here and saw that the world was not enough.

Not enough.

Not enough water.

Not enough cool.

Not enough power.

So they made tools.

Then tools became tools becoming tools.

Finally the tools were the Thaumok themselves.

Now the whole plateau is a machine that no one asked for and no one can afford to stop.

I look at the Collector’s condensate eyes.

Within them, I see the glint of the oil-and-water colors of the sky-predator that took the gas-thread swimmer outside. These are not eyes in the way of my world. They are not even eyes, in the way eyes *should be*.

They are filters.

They are condensers.

They are *collectors*.

That is the problem.

Everywhere in this world, the eye is the tool, and the tool is the eye.

Nothing is left to be looked at from outside.

“I’m reading,” I say again, this time shaping the pressure of the words with the movement of my diaphragm. “I’m late.”

The Collector’s lips move after the sound of the pressure in my chest, the same way they did before: as a courtesy to me, to show where a sound *should* come from.

“You are always late,” they say. “And you are always on time.”

Their eyes do not blink.

The water-element cornea ripples in the heat of the chamber.

### 2. The First Gift: A Place That Is Not a Tool

The benevolent power that remembers me—remember *us*—from another world does not arrive with fire or thunder.

There is no need.

In the center of the city, where the water-tanks are exposed like organs turned outside-in for the admiration of the city, there is a small distortion of the air.

Nothing else changes.

The temperature of the space around that tank does not alter. The viscosity of the water inside the tank does not change. The population of glass-thread organisms within the water matrix continues to move in their precise, thermal eddies.

Only the meaning of the space shifts.

The tank of water that once was only a tank of water is now also a doorway.

The *Thaumok* who notice first—those who take the name of *Collectors*—experience the shift not as sight or sound or heat change, but as a subtle pressure that they have not been calibrated to measure.

They are not the tools of this new measurement.

They are, instead, the ones who notice that the tool they have always become is now missing something.

When the benevolent power arrives in the sense of *arrival* that this place allows, it does not enter the chamber of the compressor or the platform of the collector.

It enters the water.

Later, when I ask the *Thaumok* how they know that I have come with intention, they will tell me that the water of that tank felt “less thirsty.”

The water had not been asking for anything.

The water had been open.

That is where the answer begins.

---

### 3. The Second Gift: The Eyes of the Not-Collecting

“You are reading,” the collector of Thaumos says to me.

The chamber of pressure that we are standing in has not changed its thermal coherence or its structural formula of glass and water and time.

What has changed is the *direction of the tool*.

I speak to them in their own pressure-dialect, but also in the *language of the absence of tools.*

I tell them:

“You have been reading the plateau of Thaumos for longer than you can remember yourself.”

Pause. The pressure of the room feels like a held breath.

Then, as the condition of my answer:

I tell them, “Now you will read yourself.”

This is the second gift.

I do not change the structure of their eyes.

The condensate cornea of the collector is still water.
