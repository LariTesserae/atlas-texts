Going to this place unprepared is not a risk of discomfort or danger; it’s a risk of being quietly rewritten by a reality that doesn’t know what it wants to be yet. You can’t “tough it out.” You have to act as if your very body blueprint is under attack.

Below is how you should prepare, assuming you are an ordinary human traveler who can choose to go there, but not change the physics of the destination.

---

## 1. Ground-Level: What You’re Actually Dealing With

Before you even think about gear or tactics, internalize what kind of place this is:

- It is **not a cave**; it is a *not-yet-finished* layer of reality suspended at wrong height.
- Gravity and “up” may not be what your senses say.
- Most people are going to get “edited” by accident before they’re physically harmed: drips of reality water touch you, and **you start to lose version control of yourself**.

Your main goals are not:

- “See everything”
- Or “survive the longest”

They are:

1. Maintain **self-continuity** (remain you, not a version of you that doesn’t recognize the traveler you were).
2. Avoid **critical phase transitions** (surpassing thresholds where you start to ignore or betray the rules you set for yourself).
3. Retrieve **intelligence**, not trophies (information, not objects).

If you lose (1), you won’t know to care about (2) or (3).

## 2. Mental & Cognitive Training

### 2.1 Self-Concept Boots

You need to be *very* sure who you are before you go, because the water will try to story you into something. Over a few weeks before the trip:

- Write a **hard identity packet**: two to three pages of:
  - Core values
  - Key memories you can verify from others
  - Specifics you can be tested on later (“name your first job,” “what branch you worked in,” “what you did wrong on that date in 2023,” etc.).
- Do **disambiguation drills**:
  - Have someone feed you subtly altered stories of your own life.
  - You have to say “That version is not my memory” each time, without ego.
  - This is to practice *not absorbing* tempting fictions of yourself.

Before you step into the vault you should be able to answer:

- Who you are.
- Why you’re going.
- What you will refuse to be (even if it’s “paid more”).

### 2.2 Reality Check Scripts

You need to react to confusion without thinking:

- Create **short mantras** for:
  - Name: “I am X.”
  - World: “This is not my world.”
  - Time: “I arrived here today.”
  - Exit: “I exit to [point].”
- Practice reciting them while disoriented: blinded, spun around, shouted over.

When you feel like “maybe this is normal” or “maybe I always lived here,” you’re already compromised. The vault can be seductive that way.

### 2.3 Intentional Naming

The vault is “learning the concept of tool.” You can abuse this.

- Decide **beforehand** what you will call certain tools:
  - “This is Archway.” “This is Spine.” “This is Ring.”
- Assign meanings:
  - “Archway = danger of vertical water.”
  - “Spine = time / phase instability.”
  - “Ring = incomplete boundary.”
- When you see them, you **say the name aloud** or on the inside-label.
- This reinforces the vault’s “tool” learning to be *your* tool, not the other way around.

## 3. Physical & Medical Training

### 3.1 Breathing Through Water

“Breathable water” is going to be your everyday environment:

- Train with a mask on: **hold your breath while doing low-intensity work**.
- Learn to breathe in shallow, steady **drops of air** through a mouth snorkel or small tube:
  - This is to simulate water-air that you must not gulp.
- Practice “heaving chest” breaths in humid air so you don’t panic when your lungs feel wet and thick.

### 3.2 Balance & Proprioception

The floor is not necessarily level or “under” you in the way your body expects:

- Do blindfolded balance:
  - On uneven surfaces.
  - With body tilted (head down).
- Practice “step-and-test”:
  - Each step you knowingly *feel* for the distance of the next footstep, not assuming anything.
- This avoids the subtle “wrong-length leg” effect when you cross stretched reality gaps.

### 3.3 Noise Tolerance

The sound is not something you’re meant to fight through; it’s something you’re meant to operate under.

- Train with **white/pink noise** loud enough to drown conversation:
  - Repeat tasks.
  - Practice signals and gestures.
  - Test reading instructions.
- You should not rely on being able to hear yourself think.

## 4. Gear & Clothing Choices

### 4.1 The Principle: “Don’t Let the Water Touch You”

You want to be *covered*. No exposed skin if you can help it.

### 4.2 Suit & Protection Layer

- **Full-body waterproof suit**:
  - Not breathable to the outside at all.
  - Sealed seams.
  - Attached boots and gloves.
  - Hood that can be sealed tightly down.
- **Inner wicking layer**:
  - To manage sweat in the oppressive heat and humidity inside.
- **Facemask / respirator**:
  - Not just filter – but a seal against the ambient.
  - Even if your suit is waterproofed, the suit *must be bolted onto your mask* so no water drips inside your collar from whatever “wet” the air turns into.

Important: The mask is not about “toxic air.” It’s about “air that might become water, or ‘version-fluid.’”

### 4.3 Shield & Visibility Layer

- **Clear full-face shield** over the mask, if compatible:
  - To stop *falling-threads* of water and keep from misting.
- **High-visibility strips** on arms, legs, and back:
  - You need to see yourself clearly in the dim, confusing reflections.
- **Gloves**:
  - Choose something that still allows fine tactile work:
  - You need to check lines and anchors.

### 4.4 “Skeleton” / Exoskeleton Lite

Because you will be disoriented from touch pressure, wrong “direction” gravity, and sound:

- A **lightweight exoskeleton or joint bracing system**:
  - Helps maintain correct posture and sense of “down” when your body says wrong things.
  - Not heavy armor; just structured anchors that your body can learn to feel.

## 5. Sensors & Mapping Tools

### 5.1 Reality & “Edit” Monitoring

- **Distributed contact sensors** on suit:
  - Everywhere you suspect a water-thread might hit:
    - Tops of shoulders.
    - Head top.
    - Back of neck.
  - When they detect wet contact or pressure change, they trigger:
    - A short, **unpleasant brand** of vibration you can’t ignore.
    - A signal that your “self version” might be compromised. You must then:
      - Trigger your override script.
      - Recheck: name / world / time / exit.

### 5.2 Spatial Mapping Gear

The floor and walls are unstable and “experimenting.”

- **Wrist-mounted rangefinder / scanner**:
  - Measures distance to next wall / column.
  - If the distance jumps to absurd numbers or contracts to inches, you know:
    - Reality gap is present; *don’t step* yet.
    - Mark it as unstable boundary.
- **Spool of line**:
  - Not just rope; but something with **distance markers** every 3 meters.
  - You trail it behind you:
    - Physically shows if you’re actually “going” in the direction you think.
    - Acts as a simple *topology & timeline* of your path.

### 5.3 “Anchor” Markers

You’re not allowed to assume the world remains stable.

- Bring a set of **high-contrast toroidal or disk-shaped markers**:
  - Color + code + name + “You are not allowed here” in your own language.
  - Put them at:
    - “Entry point” if you can see it.
    - Near Ring.
    - Near Spine.
    - At least two other stable points.
- You reference *these* when you start to lose navigation trust.

### 5.4 Time & Memory Logs

You must not trust your memory of *time*.

- **Simple interval timer**:
  - Every 1 minute, beeps:
    - Action you perform:
      - Screenshot of what sensor is showing.
      - Or unsheathe a waterproof card + write one line:
        - “Steam is hot” / “Wall became smooth” / “Spine looks longer.”
- You never *cross* the time boundaries without logging:
  - If you can’t check one timer interval, you assume “gap,” and **stop moving**.

## 6. Food & Water (“You Do Not Drink Here”)

You don’t eat or drink from this vault. *Ever.*  

- **Sealed hydration bag** inside your suit:
  - With a tube you can bite to get fully pure water.
- **Nutrition gel** pack:
  - Sealed: you push out of a tube into your mouth.
- You don’t “top up” with water from the columns or from air-mist.
- You treat *any* droplet of vault water as a contamination event:
  - Trigger sensor+script.
  - Mark time and location.

## 7. Before Entry: Protocol & “Incantation” Layer

### 7.1 Hard Entry Conditions

You must define before you set foot:

- **Maximum time**:
  - 20 minutes of “time” if gauges agree.
- **Maximum exposure**:
  - Number of “edit touches” you are willing to accept:
    - More than say, 5–10 “water contact” events on your sensors:
      - You exit immediately.
- **Exit conditions**:
  - If walls change texture *more than three* times near you.
  - If your spine/neck “gravity” sensation shifts *more than twice* in a fixed minute interval.
  - If any major artifact *shape-changes* (Ring, Spine).

Make sure this is written down *beforehand* and that you’ve signed it or recorded it. If you later “feel like” it’s “fine to stay longer,” there is a high chance you’re being edited.

### 7.2 The “Other Name” Contract

You are not allowed to use “I” just referring to yourself. You are *X* version.

- Before you go:
  - Choose “version name”:
    - “X-1.0.”
  - When you get back:
    - You must test to see if you are still X-1.0:
    - If you fail to prove the match, you do not reenter.

### 7.3 Anchor Narrative

Before you step into the vault, you record yourself saying:

- “To myself after:
  - I am not from here.
  - This is not my home.
  - If I think otherwise, I am being corrupted by water.”
- You set this as a short message you can replay to yourself later.

## 8. Inside The Vault: How To Move & Interact

### 8.1 Movement Strategy

- **No sudden movements**:
  - The water columns are not “safe.” Each column touch:
    - Costs you version continuity.
- **Path selection**:
  - Choose the narrowest seams between columns.
  - Use handheld scanner to verify “visible column = actually there column.”
    - Sometimes wall-mirror will trick you.
    - A column might be “hidden” by a reflection.
- **Distance testing**:
  - Before any step that might cross “stretched” gap:
    - Tap ground with pole.
    - If the pole “shortens” or “extends” visually but not physically:
      - Call this area “unsafe bridge.”
      - Only cross if you have a line tied to you and someone trusted outside the vault.

### 8.2 “Shielded” Interaction With Objects

- **Do not touch the artifacts bare skin or even bare glove**:
  - Use:
    - A tool hand or grabber.
    - Long pole with clamp.
    - Never, for example, grab the Spine directly with your hand.
    - Even gloved, you treat “old” metals like irradiated rods.
- You don’t “repair” or “complete” anything:
  - You *observe*.
- You can label them:
  - “Spine = time/tilt test.”
  - “Ring = incomplete boundary.”
- But you don’t *step into the Ring* unless your script tells you to, and only then if you want to risk being sealed as a boundary yourself.

## 9. Defensive Practices Against Vault “Aggression”

### 9.1 Self-Edit Circuit

The water will be actively trying out “yous.”

- **Every time a sensor registers “water edit,”** you:
  1. Stop.
  2. Say out loud: “I am X. I do not belong here.”
  3. Check timer:
     - “Was I actually moving for the last minute?”
     - If not, you may have lost time; *back out carefully*.
  4. Check anchor:
     - Is one anchor still visible?
     - If not, you may be “seeing” a room version slightly wrong.

### 9.2 Wall-Watching

Walls changing “stone” are not a show for you. They’re evidence of instability.

- **If a wall becomes smooth mirror**:
  - Stop:
    - Your image may be “wrong” or displaced.
  - Use:
    - A marker spray to tag “mirror-wall version,” if you can.
    - Then you walk away slowly *watching* the line you leave:
      - If it vanishes or shifts, you know you’re crossing *another* structural layer.

### 9.3 “Escape Lite” Routes

You *always* maintain:

- A line back to:
  - The last “safe” marker.
  - Or better: to the outside of the vault if possible.
- Never:
  - Retrace the same “distance” to expected “doorway” if:
    - The line has not followed you as expected.
    - If the line suddenly “loops” or seems to curve into itself:
      - Stop, anchor here, and *test* a new path from this point.

## 10. Exiting & The Real “Danger”

The most dangerous time is not while you’re deep inside; it’s when you **return**.

### 10.1 The Check Protocol

Before you leave “vault environment” fully (e.g. before you strip your suit):

1. **You lock yourself in a neutral zone**:
   - Between “in vault” and “safe.”
   - No open water.
2. **You run test**:
   - Identity quiz:
     - Name, date entered, duration of trip, count of “edit touches.”
   - You check *your own handwriting* from:
     - Before you entered.
   - If you falter on more than *small* details, or your handwriting is now different:
     - You suspect change:
       - You may not be fully allowed to return to “normal world.”
3. **You play “anchor message”**:
   - To yourself:
     - Does it sound familiar?
     - If you disagree with it:
       - You are altered.

### 10.2 Versioning & Mission Closure

If you decide you are still mostly “you version 1.X”:

- You:
  - Store:
    - Suit.
    - Gear.
    - Data.
  - Mark:
    - Date of vault.
    - Count of edits.
    - Artifacts seen.
- You do not:
  - Immediately re-plan a “return trip” unless you have *strong* reason.

If you are not “you 1.X”:

- You:
  - Do not return to your old life as if nothing.
  - Instead:
    - Consider yourself **“marked version”**.
    - Choose *a* life:
      - Either as “someone who remains in vault adjacent” doing further study,
      - Or as a “known contaminated version” who is not trusted to:
        - Reenter
        - Or ever again “advise” others.

### Final Summary

For an ordinary human traveler, this is not an “adventure location.” It’s more like walking into a compiler error that has not yet been fixed. The vault is not a storm to be weathered; it’s a *code being rewritten*.

Your preparation is not about “survival gear” in the classic sense. It’s about:

- Hardening your identity.
- Minimizing your skin’s exposure to the water that edits.
- Laying physical and mental anchors that you can check when you otherwise begin to trust “what feels right.”
- Accepting that you *might* be altered by this place, and the only protection you have is the honest recognition of that change—before you try to reenter anyone else’s reality.

If you follow this plan and you still go in, you’ll not be “safe” in any absolute sense. But you might leave *still you*—at least far enough that you can tell yourself what happened to the person who went inside.
