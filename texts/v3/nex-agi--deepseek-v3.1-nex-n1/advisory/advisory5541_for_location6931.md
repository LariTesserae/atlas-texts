## 0. Before Anything Else: Understand the Scale

You are not going to a country or a planet.  
You are going into a chamber.

Dimensions: planetary.  
Ceiling: low enough to see, but high enough to contain the atmosphere of a world.  
Floor: not rock, but a compressed mat of living plant-fiber, kilometers thick.

Time is not urgent hours here;  
it is a slow, mechanical pressure that you feel in your bones.  
Whatever happens, happens slowly.

Prepare your *mind* before your body.

---

## 1. What Your Body Should Expect

### 1.1 Cold: Persistent, Not Dramatic

This is not Antarctic storm-cold or snow. It’s a constant small deficit:

- Temperature: always slightly below what your body wants.
- Result: you never feel *dangerously* cold; you just never feel properly warm.
- Over time, your core temperature drops by tiny increments.

**Preparation:**

- Layers: many thin, warm layers over “soft” armor.  
  - Base layer: moisture-wicking synthetic or treated linen.  
  - Insulation: middle layers of fleece or wool-like material.
  - Outer shell: tightly woven, wind-resistant but breathable.

- Avoid:
  - Single heavy armor plates that trap cold air.
  - Overheating and then sweating; you’ll pay later.

- Expect:
  - To feel slightly chilled for *weeks*.
  - For your joints to ache slightly sooner than usual.
  - To sleep colder than you expect unless you add extra layers.

---

### 1.2 Gravity: What “Falling Slowly” Means

You are explicitly told that things here fall strangely slow:

- Air is dense enough to slow you down.
- You might even *glide* a little if you spread yourself.
- Stepping off a ledge without railings is still not safe – but not *instantly* fatal.

**Preparation:**

- **Never treat height as safe.**
  - A “short” fall from a platform might be stretched long enough to break your neck if you hit wrong.
  - A long fall over the edge of the cliff will be a long, terrifying time to think about your mistake.

- Footwear:
  - Boots with good grip and a thick sole but not clumsy.
  - You will be on uneven stone edges, narrow ledges, and sometimes rope bridges.
  - Your shoes must be capable of lateral grip and small edges but not weight so much you can’t catch your balance.

- **Don’t**:
  - Overload yourself with gear.
    - If you lose your balance at the edge of a platform and your pack is heavy, you will go over.
  - Carry gear only on your back.
    - Better: distributed weight on torso harnesses so you can drop your pack if you need quick movement.

---

### 1.3 Atmosphere: Low Pressure, Deep-Water Feeling

You are at high altitude in a world already under a heavy, damp atmosphere.

- Air pressure may be close to tolerable, but “thin” in some ways and dense in others.
- The sound is strange: distant things roar faintly like you’re deep underwater.
- You might feel:
  - A slight pressure in your ears that doesn’t fully equalize.
  - A sense of being *inside* something, rather than under an open sky.

**Preparation:**

- Respirator mask or filter:
  - Not because you’ll instantly suffocate, but because:
    - Spores, dust, and biological debris from the plant ocean below.
    - Fine particulates when you walk on the plant-mat.

- Basic medication:
  - Decongestant or pressure-equalizer for your ears.
  - Expect mild headaches for the first few days.

- **Mental**:  
  Prepare to feel *physically enclosed*.  
  You might feel like you’re in a cathedral ceiling whose roof is just low enough to be uncomfortable.

---

### 1.4 Sound and Vibration

Silence here is misleading:

- The ceiling groans.
- Wind-rivers are audible like distant strings.
- Vegetation is a roaring whisper.
- Pressure-cracks can sound like thunder.

**Preparation:**

- Hearing protection is not about noise so much as *unexpected noise*.
  - Soft earplugs you can remove quickly.
  - You need to be able to tell *direction* of sound well, because:
    - Avalanches in the plant-mat.
    - Pressure changes.
    - Ceiling shifts.
    - Approaching aurora-events.

- If you have tinnitus:
  - Expect it to sound deceptively equal to the world’s hum.  
  - Work out your own patterns so you don’t ignore warning signals.

## 2. What to Bring

### 2.1 Core Survival Gear

**Light:**

- Modern headlamps with:
  - **Red light mode** for subtle navigation.
  - On average, you’ll be using them far more than you think.
- Bring:
  - Rechargeable cells + portable solar charging pad.
  - Sealed in waterproof containers to prevent condensation corrosion.

The local light sources:

- Stress fractures from the ceiling (blue-violet).
- Plant-mat glow (greenish).
- Floor aurora events (strong pulses of blue-green).

None of these are reliable *constant* illumination.

---

**Warmth & Shelter:**

- Compact shelter:
  - Not a full tent but a strong, *wind-resistant* shelter with:
    - Capability to anchor onto stone or tie between structures.
- Sleeping:
  - Thinsulate or equivalent high-performance sleeping bag.
  - Insulated sleeping pad.
- Heat:
  - Portable, low-area heater:
    - To be used only briefly to avoid dehumidifying yourself.
  - Reserve chemical warmers for:
    - Extremities in cold snaps.
- **Do not**:
  - Use open flame carelessly near anything:
    - Buildings are hung on cliffsides, often involving resin-based panels.
    - Plant-world below is a flammable matrix of hair-thin fibers.

---

**Water and Food:**

This is a wet world without direct rain.

**Water:**

- Two ways local water is:
  - Condensation dripping from ceilings or rock surfaces.
  - Thin sheets cascading from walls.

**Bring:**

- Collapsible containers you can set out on surfaces under drips.
- Filter:
  - Even if you’re catching water from stone surfaces, there will be:
    - Dust, spores, and microbes from the plant-world.
- **Test**:  
  - If you notice local water-collection basins:
    - Check for sediment, color.
    - If people clean them regularly, you can assume filtered water is safer.

**Food:**

- High-energy, compact trail rations.
- Local food might be:
  - Derived from the plant-mat:
    - Processed fibers.
    - Possibly extracted organisms.
  - It might be slow to digest due to local chemistry.

- Introduce local foods slowly:
  - One small meal at a time.
  - Expect to preserve your reserves from home for at least 7–14 days.

---

### 2.2 Clothing and Tools

**Clothing (already mentioned):**

- Overlapping:
  - Base layer.
  - Middle insulation.
  - Outer wind-resistant layer.
- **Important**:  
  - Everything durable against:
    - Abrasion from walking on stone ledges.
    - Contact with plant-mat edges (which are likely sharp and fine).

---

**Tools and Cutting:**

- Local resources:
  - Stone blades.
  - Basic metal tools.

**You should bring:**

- **High-quality knife**:
  - For cutting:

    - Plant fiber.
    - General utility.
- **Multifunction tool**:
  - For:
    - Repairing winches,
    - Adjusting gear,
    - Opening packs,
    - Basic splicing.

---

**Climbing and Safety:**

- Since everything is built near or on a cliff-face or platforms:

**You need:**

- A lightweight harness:
  - For attaching yourself to:
    - Rope bridges.
    - Winches.
    - Ropes attached by locals.
- **Carabiners and sling**:
  - For clipping:
    - To fixed anchors on stone platforms.
    - To yourself while you observe over the edge of a platform.
- Climbing gloves:
  - For rope:
    - The local fiber ropes may be stronger than they look, but rougher on your hands.

---

### 2.3 Documentation and Observation

**You should bring:**

- **Notebooks and waterproof pens**:
  - To:
    - Mark time patterns of events you cannot photograph.
    - Record markers:
      - Aurora frequency.
      - Ceiling-groaning events.
      - Local symbols carved on walls.
      - Your emotional changes.

- **Simple compass**:
  - Even if local magnetic fields are screwy:
    - It helps you map the direction of:
      - Movement of the aurora.
      - Wind-river current.

- **Something to measure time**:
  - Local days might not line up with your biological clock.
  - Keep:
    - A separate watch on your home time standard.
    - Another local timekeeping to sync:
      - Aurora events they mark on the walls.
      - Their workcycles.

---

### 2.4 Technology: Minimal, Robust

**Bring:**

- Simple:
  - Small:
    - Flashlight, headlamp, spare.
  - Multimeter (if you have the technical capacity):
    - To measure:
      - Changes in ambient electrical potential during aurora events.
      - Might give clues about when to *not* be near metal structures.

- **Do not**:
  - Bring complex electrical gear:
    - If you don’t know how to protect it from:
      - Constant moisture.
      - Condensation.
      - High-pressure discharges.
  - Risk:
    - Short circuits.
    - Your device malfunctioning in exactly the wrong moment.

---

### 2.5 Medicine and Health

**Issues you will likely face:**

- Persistent low-level cold.
- Potential joint pain.
- Cuts from sharp stone edges.
- Infections from interacting with the plant-world.

**Bring:**

- Basic first-aid kit:
  - Antibiotics (if allowed by your laws).
  - Disinfectant.
  - Bandages and tape that can handle wet conditions.
- **Antifungal cream**:
  - Because of:
    - Moist environment.
    - Plant-matter.
- Pain medication:
  - For:
    - Headaches.
    - Joint aches.
    - Ear pressure.

- **Psychological**:  
  - Mild:
    - Anti-anxiety medication,
      - If you already have prescribed, and travel is allowed.
      - For adjusting:
        - To enclosed ceiling-world.
        - To slow time scale.
      - Not a long-term solution.

### 3.1 Technology is Rare and Painful to Produce

You see:

- Stone tools.
- A few metal tools.
- Rope winches.
- Some windows of translucent resin.

**Your behavior:**

- **Do not show off** advanced tech:
  - Even a simple flashlight:
    - Are light to you.
    - Possibly magician-level to them.
  - If you reveal:
    - Show:
      - Modest, *brief* demonstrations.
      - Ask how they manage light.
      - Offer help:
        - Not charity.
        - Not dominance.

- Treat their tools with respect:
  - Every metal object here:
    - A result of immense effort.
  - Asking:
    - “How do you forge this?”

    - “This is primitive.”
      - Closes doors.

**Your behavior:**
