If you're an ordinary human multiverse traveler about to visit the Sanfish Market, preparation is not just a good idea—it's the difference between slipping into a half-forgotten dream and collapsing into a nightmare of your own making.

### 1. Understand the "Ordinary" You're Leaving Behind

You're human. You're used to air that doesn't think, wood that stays where you put it, and water that falls from clouds and then stays on the ground until it drains away or evaporates. The Sanfish Market doesn't operate on those principles.

- **Time**: Our time is linear and mostly empty. The time in the Sanfish Market is *heavy*. It carries remnants of what happened here "before," when two continents moved like tectonic lovers who never quite finished their affair. The basin is a wound. Time pools in it.
- **Space**: You're going from rectilinear architecture to a *cathedral that is also a lung*.
- **Metaphysics**: The Sanfish Market is a "bowl of dreams and regrets." You're not going to a place where dreams and regrets happen; you're going to a place where they are *material*.

Accept: your ordinary intuition about physics, mood, and personal safety will be misleading you from the moment you arrive.

### 2. What You'll Actually Encounter When You Step Through

You're assuming you'll arrive at a train station or a portal-terminal platform. The "Market" part might be making you think of stalls and merchants shouting about prices.

You won't.

You'll arrive under the ceiling of the world.

#### The ceiling of the world

Imagine you step into *not* sky, but into a huge, breathing attic above the planet. The sky above you is not open sky; it is a forest of gigantic ribs and ribs of ribs, supporting vast surfaces—like gills, or lung-lobes—made of leaf-skin.

- **Light**: It'll be filtered through many layers of leaf-membrane and trunk-architecture. The light will shift, constantly, because the *roof* is breathing.
- **Colors**: You're going to see:
  - Copper
  - Deep green
  - Old mineral browns
  - Mica sparkles like a shattered mirror of metallic minerals integrated into the wood
- **Sound**: You'll hear:
  - Subtle shifts in the "gills" above as they inhale and exhale
  - Possibly faint water sounds from condensation dripping along the ribs of the Quillwood
  - Voices of people who aren't quite visible at first in the half-light
- **Smell**: Damp warm air "always smelling like the next hour's rain." Whether it rains or not. That means you'll feel:
  - High humidity, *always*
  - An air so thick with potential moisture it might feel like inhaling silk gauze
- **Ground**: Flattened to near-horizontal. *Not* paved. You will be walking on soil or root-matter or something that is both.

Important: the *world* itself—the Quillwood—is alive and engineered and ancient. It's not a backdrop you can ignore.

### 3. Preparing Yourself Physically

#### Climate and humidity gear

- **Light, layered clothing**: It's warm and wet but not necessarily hot. You want:
  - A thin, breathable base layer that can wick moisture without stalling against humidity.
  - A loose, long-sleeved overshirt that protects against skin exposure but still vents air.
- **Protection against fungal spores and pollen**: The Quillwood is a roof organism. What does it release into the air?
  - A lightweight filtration mask or scarf covering nose and mouth might be necessary if you're sensitive or trying to stay "anonymous" to local biota.
- **Rain protection**: Even if it doesn't rain, you'll feel "next hour's rain" constantly.
  - Bring a lightweight, waterproof cloak or poncho that won't trap heat too badly.
- **Foot protection**: You'll be on soil-wood-root-ground.
  - Closed shoes with good soles and *excellent waterproofing* are necessary.
- **Hydration**: The humidity will be deceptive. You may lose water through respiration and skin without feeling you're sweating heavily.
  - A small personal bottle with water you can afford to carry.
- **Basic first aid**: Antifungal cream (if it's safe to use), antihistamines for plant allergies, and maybe basic pain relief. 

#### Physical training and adaptation

- **Respiration conditioning**: If you're not used to humid, heavy air, you'll want to do some basic cardio before you go:
  - Simple walking or light running in warm humid conditions, if possible.
  - Slow controlled breathing exercises, so the sensation of thick air doesn't cause you to panic.
- **Balance and stability**: The ground is flat but not necessarily perfectly engineered for human legs.
  - Before you travel, practice walking on uneven surfaces: forest paths, root-ridged ground, etc.
- **Eye adjustment**: You're going from normal outdoor lighting into a cathedral-like space where:
  - Light changes constantly as gills above contract and expand.
  - Shadows might *move* independently of the sun. You may want to practice walking with confidence in low-light or changing-light environments.

### 4. Preparing Yourself Mentally and Emotionally

You are going to a place described as "a bowl of dreams and regrets." For a human mind that's usually ignored or suppressed, that's *extremely dangerous*.

- **Expect emotional amplification**: The Market itself is likely to be capable of:
  - Making your regrets *sticky*
  - Giving your dreams more body and reality

- **Before you travel**:
  - Do some kind of emotional inventory:
    - What are your biggest regrets?
    - What are your strongest dreams?
  - You don't need to solve any of them; you need to *know* them.
  - Know that these will show up differently in the Market. You don't want to be taken by surprise.

- **Mindfulness practices**:
  - Basic grounding:
    - Feeling your feet on the ground.
    - Focusing on breath.
    - Noticing 5 sensory things around you.
- **Stated intention**: Write yourself a simple statement:
  - "I am visiting to observe, learn, and leave safely."
  - Repeat it to yourself before and at times during your visit.
- **Acceptance**: The Sanfish Market is not finally you. It is *out of scale*, both physically and emotionally. You're going to be inside of something much larger than you. Acceptance of that dependency will help you avoid mistakes:
  - Like trying to "control" the environment.
  - Or trying to "take ownership" of one of the dreams being stored.

### 5. Practical Knowledge and Skills to Acquire Before You Visit

#### Basic linguistics and communication

- You're entering a place where "language" might exist:
  - Between people and the Quillwood
  - Between traders and the environment itself

- Prepare:
  - Learn to recognize different kinds of *rhythmic motion*:
    - The gill-shields contracting might be more than simple respiration—they might be a kind of communication or a time-mark.
  - Learn basic camping and forest-trail etiquette: you're dealing with a living organism that's effectively architecture. The *space* is not neutral.
  - (If you're multilingual) you might be at a slight advantage if local languages or "trade dialects" exist.

#### Navigating an architecture that is alive and engineered

- **Paths**: Paths inside the Sanfish Market are likely temporary:
  - Worn through by repeated traffic over *soil* and *root* matter.
- **Signs**: There may be markings:
  - On the ribs of the structure
  - On the ground
  - But not in the language of concrete or street signs.

Develop the ability to read:
- Slightly worn paths
- Patterns of foot traffic
- Subtle changes in color or texture on the bark or ribs or ground

Remember: you're under a *cathedral* abandoned by its forgotten god and recolonized by a living one. 

- You will have:
  - Naves formed by ribs of wood.
  - Aisles that might *open* and *close* as gills contract.
- **Structural awareness**: You will need to know where:
  - To be *safe*
  - *Not* to be when the structure shifts.

- Practice:
  - Before you travel, stand near trees and notice how they move:
    - Watch wind through branches.
    - Stretch your imagination to treat *entire architecture* as capable of that same movement.
  - Practice staying calm under moving structures (like bridges, ships, or large trees in strong wind). That's good practice for being under *breathing architecture.*

### 6. Equipment to Bring With You

You're a human traveler—not a specialized cosmic agent or a god. You need *modest*, absolutely *essential* equipment.

**Physical protection:**

- **Lightweight, waterproof storage**: Rucksack with water-resistant coating or internal liner.
- **Cloak or hooded poncho** that covers:
  - Your head and shoulders.
- **Eye protection**: Simple tinted glasses or clear goggles in case:
  - The Quillwood releases *anything* into the air.
- **Gloves**: Light, durable, nitrile or cloth—if you need to touch walls or surfaces.
- **Mask or scarf** with filtration capacity:
  - For dust, spores, or unseen particulates.

**Information and tools:**

- **Notebook and pencil**:
  - You're going somewhere *history* is partially erased and partially stored in the living structure.
- **Simple mapping tools**:
  - If possible, a small device that allows you to mark:
    - "Where I am now"
    - "Which direction I came from"
- **Basic light source**:
  - If the gills contracting or the time of day causes drastic lighting changes:
  - A small, unobtrusive flashlight or glowstick will be very important.

- **Optional: sensory enhancing device**:
  - If your technology/multiverse travel allows, you may want:
    - A small translator or *environment scanner* that can warn:
      - "This air is getting thicker."
      - "This vibration is increasing."
      - "This structure is moving."
- **Symbols of grounding**:
  - Bring an object from your home or reality that:
    - Reminds you of your identity.
    - Helps you hold *onto yourself* when you're being surrounded by dreams and regrets.

- **No weapons**: Unless you *know* what you're dealing with, weapons might be worse:
  - You're inside *a living, breathing architecture*. Aggression against it might be interpreted as *insanity* or serious *violence*.
  - Focus more on defensive and protective gear than anything weapon-like.

### 7. Safety Protocol

- **Set an anchor time**:
  - Decide *before* you enter:
    - "I will be back by the time of [X]."
  - Having *actual* time might be tricky:
    - The description of the Sanfish Market doesn't suggest you'll have conventional sun or time, just "the next hour's rain."
  - But you can:
    - Set internal time intervals: "every hour on my hour device" check-in.
- **Check-in points**: 
  - Before you travel, establish:
    - "If I am not heard from after [X] intervals, someone on my side of the multiverse knows to watch."
- **Monitor yourself**: 
  - You are going into a place of *dreams and regrets*. You will be affected.
  - Watch:
    - Are you suddenly more nostalgic?
    - Are you catching yourself *living* inside your regrets?
- **If you feel yourself drifting into memories or dreams**:
  - Stop where you are physically safe if possible.
  - Look:
    - Straight at the structure of the world above you.
    - Count the ribs.
    - Count the verticals.
  - Ground yourself:
    - Not your mind.
    - Your *body* first.
  - Then breathe:
    - Notice what your lungs are doing compared to the lungs of the city above you.

- **Don't make decisions out of emotional overflow**: 
  - If you're suddenly overwhelmed by:
    - The desire to stay and give *everything* to this place.
    - Or fear that you *never* want to leave but must *immediately*—
  - Then:
    - Step back physically first.
    - Then *think* after you've stabilized your body.
- **Know when to exit**: 
  - If:
    - The ground seems *too* soft in a way that threatens to engulf you.
    - The gills above begin contracting more rapidly or *rhythmically* like something more than breathing.
    - If you see *patterns* that you *cannot stop following*
    - Then you have *nothing* to prove by staying.

### 8. How to Actually Experience the Sanfish Market Without Being Engulfed by It

You're going to be in *a cathedral that is also alive and that is also a bowl for memories*.

- **Treat all spaces as semi-sacred**: 
  - You're inside an organism *made* of architecture.
- **Walk lightly**: 
  - You don't know what's root and what's floor.
- **Speak softly or remain silent**: 
  - You don't know what frequencies:
    - Wake something.
    - Or attract something.
- **Listen**: 
  - To:
    - The "gills" taking their slow breath.
    - To the water moving.
    - To the voices of other people if you find them.
- **Don't try to own everything you see**: 
  - You're going to be surrounded by dreams:
    - Stored in:
      - The air
      - The soil
- **Respect**: 
  - The fact that this *is* a world most have forgotten.
- **Ask yourself**: 
  - What does *forgetting* mean?
  - For *you*
  - For *this place*

### 9. Exit Plan

- **Identify and remember your entry point**: 
  - If you can:
    - - mark it visually.
- **Know your *exit* mechanism**: 
  - Multiverse travel often:
    - Provides keys or devices or *gestures*
    - Or fixed points.
    - The *Sanfish Market itself* might try to:
      - Make you forget *why* you came to it.
      - It *absorbs* dreams and regrets; *leaving* might be the hardest part.
- **Before you enter**: 
  - Write down:
    - The name of the reality you come from.
    - The name of the person/people you go back to.
    - The reasons you *must leave*.
  - Keep this note:
    - In a pocket close to your body.
- **If you begin losing your sense of self or purpose**: 
  - Read it.
- **Do not trust your memory alone**: 
  - The description of the Sanfish Market says:
    - It "is a bowl of dreams and regrets that the world has mostly forgotten."
- You might be *invited* to:
  - Add yours.
  - Lose yourself.
  - Be *archived* like the rest of the forgotten.

### Final Summary for an Ordinary Human Traveler

To prepare yourself for visiting the Sanfish Market:

- **Accept** that you're walking into a living, breathing architectural being that is also a bowl of dreams and regrets.
- **Dress** for a humid, warm, rain-heavy climate without a sun you can rely on.
- **Protect yourself** physically with good waterproofing, humidity gear, and basic breathing protection.
- **Prepare yourself mentally** through:
  - Emotional self-awareness
  - Grounding techniques
- **Develop your skills**:
  - Reading organic environments
  - Interpreting moving structures
- **Bring** minimal but essential gear:
  - Light, waterproof clothing.
  - Notebook and pencil.
  - Light source.
- **Set** anchors and time reminders:
  - Know when you will leave.
- **Respect** the space as:
  - A sacred cathedral
  - A living organism
  - A repository for memories others have forgotten,

- **And never forget**: you're not *staying* there.

You're visiting a bowl of dreams and regrets that the world has mostly forgotten. You're not going there to become *one of them*.
