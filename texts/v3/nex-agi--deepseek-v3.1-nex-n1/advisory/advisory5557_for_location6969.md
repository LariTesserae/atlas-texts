This is not a place you “visit.” It is a place you undergo.

---

## 1. Understand what you’re walking into

### 1.1. It sounds sacred; it isn’t—quite

Before anything else, internalize this:  

The **Cavern of the Still Mirror** is not a temple or a shrine.  
It is a **measuring instrument**.

Its architecture is beautiful because its physics are precise; its awe is a side effect of that precision. If you treat it like “just art” or “just scenery,” you will misunderstand the dangers. Think of it as a lab your body is not allowed to touch, but is allowed to witness.

### 1.2. The core paradoxes to keep in mind

- You will be standing inside a **machine** that is constantly testing reality itself.
- The **water mirror** is not just a reflection: it is part of the suspension system that keeps the bone-white city hovering above it.
- The **light** is not illumination; it is leakage from a mechanism that is maintaining stability in space-time above you.
- The **stillness** is not empty: it is the center of a continuous, slow-motion experiment.

## 2. Physical and psychological preparation

### 2.1. Mental conditioning

Expect the following:

- A mild but persistent **disorientation**  
  You will be in a room where “up” and “down” are not just directions but phases of time being layered over each other.  
  Train yourself:
  - Fix on **one real object**: the stone under your feet, a seam on the wall, your own hands.
  - When feeling vertigo or unreality, return to that anchor point instead of chasing explanations.

- **Sensory deprivation plus subtle overload**  
  The place is dim, monotonous, and nearly empty of sound you can recognize as language.  
  At the same time, the low hum and the constant pressure of the air can wear on you.
  
  Prepare:
  - Short **meditation practices** weeks before travel:  
    Learn to sit in silence for 10–20 minutes without distraction or narrative.
  - Learn a simple **breathing technique**: slow in through nose, out through mouth, to regulate your nervous system when you start feeling like “the walls are watching.”

- **Linguistic vulnerability**  
  Your mind will try to describe what you see with words like “floor,” “ceiling,” “mirror,” and “floating.”  
  They will fail, and you may feel like you’re losing your grasp on reality.

  Strategy:
  - Accept that **you can’t name everything perfectly**.  
  - Use deliberately clumsy phrases like: “the surface that is also a support” and accept that the language will feel wrong.
  - This misfit between what you see and what you can say is *normal* here.

### 2.2. Physical training

You’ll be descending a **spiraled service ramp** and standing on a narrow stone rim. There’s no seating, no safety rails, and minimal light.

You should be able to:

- Walk comfortably on **smooth, wet stone** without shoes that squeak or slip.
- Stand quietly for **20–30 minutes at a time** without needing to shift, cough, or stretch conspicuously.
- Move without **sudden gestures** or fast arm swings.

Do basic exercises:

- Balance practice on one leg for 30 seconds to a minute.
- Slow, controlled walking (as if carrying a full cup of water on your head).
- Neck and shoulder mobility stretches so you can look up and down without rustling or gasping.

### 2.3. Clothing and gear

Goal: minimize **presence** and **sound**, and stay **warm enough**.

- **Clothing:**
  - Soft, neutral colors: grays, near-black, dark blues.
  - Fabric: matte, not reflective—no metallics, no shimmer, no glossy surfaces.
  - Avoid loose sleeves or dangling ends that could flap into the water or catch drafts.
  - Fit should be close and comfortable, not tight or constricting.

- **Footwear:**
  - Flat, thin-soled shoes with excellent **grip on wet stone**.
  - Soft soles that make almost no sound when placed on rock.
  - Absolutely no shoes that can slip off or squeak.

- **Temperature:**
  - The air is constantly moving and warm at the pool surface but cooler near the uncut rock dome.  
  - A single thin, long-sleeved layer you can keep on is ideal.  
  - No need for thermal gear; more important is **quietness** of fabric.

- **What to avoid:**
  - No belts with metal buckles, no jewelry, no watches with shiny faces.
  - No cameras that whirr or beep; if you must bring a device, it should be capable of silent operation mode.
  - Nothing that can accidentally fall—seal pockets so you won’t be fiddling.

## 3. Pre-arrival research and local customs

### 3.1. Local context

You’re presumably coming from or through the **city above**—this is not a remote cave system, but a deeply integrated infrastructure level.

Before you go down:

- Ask **any local guide**:
  - “Are there any times of day when the system is recalibrating and visitors are discouraged?”
  - “Are there group-size limits per shift?” (usually small; often 1–3 people at a time).
  - “Any particular colors or patterns they prefer to avoid on the rim?”

- Don’t expect:

You may get *none* of this.

### 3.2. Protocols you must respect

Someone—perhaps the architect-engineer, perhaps the city’s maintenance system—will give you a set of rules verbally or in text. **Memorize them.**  

Core likely points:

- **No one enters the water.**  
  Ever. For any reason, including “recovering my dropped object.”
- **No heartbeat in the reflective plane.**  
  That means: you, your body, any blood, any animal, anything that has a pulse must stay off the surface of the pool.
- **Do not increase the light**  
  No flashlights, lumens, or glowing devices aimed at ceiling or water.
- **No loud noises or sudden movements**  
  You are part of its data calibration; erratic behavior is noise, and noise is not safe.

Listen to these rules as if you were being told **not to step into the path of a machine**—because you are.

## 4. What you should bring

### 4.1. Essentials

- **Identity / access token**  
  Whatever mechanism you used to get permission to come here.
- **Earplugs (optional but wise):**  
  If your guide is uneasy with them, don’t use them; it is more important to be compliant than comfortable.
- **Non-luminous water bottle:**  
  Quiet lid, no metal ring that clinks.
- **Any medicines** you might need for:
  - Inner ear imbalance (motion sickness)
  - Anxiety or panic attacks
  
  If your meds have *noticeable* effects on your heart rhythm or perception, discuss them with a guide ahead of time.

### 4.2. Absolutely not to bring

- Anything that **flashes**, **beeps**, or **reflects strongly**.
- Devices that require tapping, typing, or verbal commands.
- Food: no smell, no crumbs.
- Anything your hands might fidget with—eg, keys, coins, or items that jingle.

## 5. Descent and arrival

### 5.1. On the spiraled service ramp

You’ll descend a **service ramp** that spirals down into the cavern.

During the descent:

- Expect **increasing pressure of sound**: a low-frequency hum that will be more felt than heard.
- You will lose many external references:  
  No natural sky, no horizon, no windows to the outer world.
- You may feel a slight **disorientation of balance**:  
  This is not sudden; it is gradual and insidious.

What to do:

- Walk **slowly**, keeping one hand lightly near the wall (not dragging on it).
- Pause occasionally **before** vertigo comes; don’t push through.
- Breathe deeply and quietly to regulate your heart rate.

### 5.2. Entering the chamber

When you emerge from the ramp into the chamber itself:

- **Stop immediately for a few heartbeats.**  
  Let your eyes adjust to the light level.
- Let your body adjust to the **hum**, the **airflow**, and the **optical illusion of symmetry**—the city above and its reflection below.

You will want to look and look and look. Resist the urge to spin around or step rapidly toward the edge. **Movement must be incremental.**

### 5.3. Positioning on the rim

You will be positioned on the **rim of stone** between wall and water mirror.

Guidelines:

- Stay at **least your body’s width from the water’s edge**.
- Don’t crowd:
  - Leave space between you and any other visitors.
  - Remain distributed around the circle if there are multiple people.
- **Face the center** but avoid the direct angle of reflection that would put your face mirrored into the water.
- Some systems might subtly prefer visitors to be **off the centerline of reflections**—ask if they’ve indicated such a preference.

## 6. How to be in the room

### 6.1. Posture and breathing

- **Stand or sit quietly**—if you sit, use a low stone or edge that you can be sure of, and keep your body back from the waterline.
- Breathe slowly and **diaphragmatically**:
  - This helps regulate heart rate and keeps your “biological noise” minimal.
- **Minimal movement**:  
  Only small adjustments; weight-shift slowly rather than stepping.

### 6.2. Gaze and perception

Allow your eyes to **unfocus periodically**.

- Don’t force exact clarity of every edge.
- Let yourself notice:
  - The migration of moisture along the dome’s upper skin.
  - The faint, gentle sway of the suspended city above.
  - The subtle “thickness” of the air between the structure and its reflection.

This is not daydreaming; it is participating in the room’s data collection by not being a source of chaotic signals.

### 6.3. Voice and noise

- **Preferably remain silent.**  
  If you must speak:
  - Keep it below conversational level, to a whisper so thin it might be lost under the hum of the field.
  - Single words only, if you can—no extended conversation.
- **No coughs or sudden throat clearings** if you can suppress them.
  If you need to cough:
  - Turn away from the center, a step inward from the edge.
  - Cover your mouth with a sleeve.
  - Do it quietly and deliberately.

### 6.4. Body language

Avoid:

- **Pointing** (especially at the water).
- **Reaching out toward the mirror**.
- **Kneeling at the edge** where your center of gravity might pull you forward.
- **Any fast or wide gesture**—like throwing arms out to exclaim or “take it all in.”

The system is highly sensitive to subtle disturbances in the air and the field. Your body should be a **stationary sensor**, not a signal generator.

## 7. Understanding the water’s temptation and risk

You will be tempted—especially as you acclimate to the stillness—to:

- Lean closer to see **depth** in the water mirror.
- Put a hand near the surface to feel “the temperature of the reflection.”
- Drop something small, to see what it does.

Do **none** of these.

The rule **“nothing with a heartbeat in the reflective plane”** includes:

- Your body or any part of it.
- Anything wet or blood-bearing.
- Anything pulsing with electronics or biological processes.

The system will detect interference and “rearrange itself.”  
You might think:
> *Surely just a fingertip would not matter?*

Assume you are **wrong** by default.

## 8. Handling the sense of unreality

### 8.1. Expected “symptoms” of the weirdness

- A feeling that **“down” might be above** and vice versa.
- Difficulty feeling sure whether you are looking at the “real” city or the **reflection**.
- The sense that **time feels thick** or stretched thin between your heartbeats.
- Subtle pressure on the eyes or eardrums, as if you were at borderline altitude changes.

### 8.2. Coping strategies

- **Ground yourself**:  
  Press your feet consciously into the stone.  
  Notice that this stone is not new; it’s an older layer of the world, not rewritten by the machine.
- **Use tactile senses**:  
  Rub thumb and forefinger together gently.  
  Feel the friction of your own skin.  
  That is real in your body’s world.
- **Repeat a simple internal phrase**:  
  Something like “I am on the stone rim. I am not in the mirror.”  
  This isn’t to explain the physics, but to keep your balance center located.

If you feel **detached** or unreal:

- Don’t panic; it is a known side effect.
- If you brought a wristband or something tactile you know from home, use it as a reminder of “outside world” reality.
- Avoid **grabbing others**; this is their space as well.

## 9. The longer you stay: “Becoming data”

If you remain longer than the minimum visit time, you will become more of the **room’s data**:

- You may notice:
  - The hum **changing pitch** slightly.
  - The condensation **thickening** above you by invisible increments.
  - The reflection of the city appearing **ever more perfect** relative to the source.

You are moving inside time with that machine; you are part of the **before** and **after** that it is measuring.

Learning from this happens:

- **During** the stay, by *not* acting:
  - Don’t rush.  
  - Don’t try to “document” it with your mind either.
- **Afterwards**, in recollection:
  - You will notice that you remember the **order** of events more clearly than you felt you were perceiving it.
  - The machine’s changes will be more readable as memory patterns than as they occurred.

## 10. Exit and aftereffects

### 10.1. Leaving the chamber

When you leave:

- Pause at the **threshold of the ramp** before ascending.
- Take a last breath of the chamber’s air, knowing that you won’t be adding to its data stream soon.
- Turn **away** from the center of the water mirror.
- Climb without hurrying; spiraling up will feel more like “time processing” than you’d expect.

### 10.2. Post-visit rest

- Do not schedule immediately after:
  - Complex tasks or meetings.
  - Driving or anything requiring fine motor reflexes.
- Give yourself **quiet time**:
  - Sit with a warm drink.
  - Write or sketch quietly, if that helps.
- Don’t expect:
  - A clear, “So now I understand it.”
  - Instead, expect to **rethink basic words like “floor” and “surface.”**  

The Cavern of the Still Mirror has not told you secrets of its universe.  
It has given you a **phantom limb of language**: you sense a new geometry of up and down, of time and surface, that you cannot fully describe in words.

### Summary of the preparation, in condensed form:

1. **Condition yourself to be calm and physically minimal**  
   No heroics, no drama, just quietness.

2. **Dress and equip yourself as invisible data**:  
   Soft, quiet, non-reflective, and warm enough.

3. **Respect the rules as hard laws of physics**:  
   No entry into the water mirror;  
   No heartbeat into the reflection plane;  
   No light increase;  
   No loud noise or sudden movement.

4. **Move and stand like a sensor that knows it is only visiting**:  
   Slow, deliberate, anchored to stone, not to illusions.

5. **Expect your mind and language to fail a bit**:  
   That failure is the correct response of a human to a machine that is solving time over water.

6. **Afterwards, treat yourself as someone who has walked inside a measurement**:  
   Not a spectacle, but a lab the size of a world.

If you approach it that way—with **humility**, **quietness**, and a **tolerance for ambiguity**—you can walk safely in the Still Mirror, and it might leave you with a new understanding of how stillness, not silence, is the center of action.
