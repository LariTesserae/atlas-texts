Prepare like you’re taking a trip into a living manuscript that hasn’t decided what kind of story it wants to be.

Below is a fairly thorough breakdown, from mindset to gear to in‑world behavior.

---

## 0. Core understanding (before you pack anything)

You’re entering a world where:

- **The ground is not data; the ground is being written.**  
  Topography, rivers, “houses,” and even time’s flow are under negotiation from moment to moment.
- **The plantmass is the only ecosystem.**  
  It is soil, water, architecture, and possibly “mind.” There are **no animals**, no insects, no obvious microbes—yet you are still a biological object.
- **You are not separate from the process.**  
  The world will try to learn you, integrate you, or at least respond to you.

Your goal as a traveler: *visit without being rewritten* — physically, temporally, or cognitively.

## 1. Physical and medical preparation

### A. Baseline health check

- **Full physical exam** including:
  - Heart, lungs, neurological function.
  - Any chronic conditions (asthma, autoimmune, etc.) must be stable and medicated.
- **Allergy screening + greenhouses test.**  
  You’re going to a world that is nothing but plant tissue. Before you go:
  - Spend time in sealed greenhouses with dense, humid foliage.
  - If you show even mild respiratory allergies or skin reactions, reconsider going or bring very strong mitigations.
- **Immunizations tailored to a plant-world risk profile:**  
  The exact world-specifics will be moot, but:
  - Up-to-date on tetanus (because of cuts and scrapes in a wet organic environment).
  - Consider broad-spectrum antivirals/antibacterials if you can get them.
  - Antifungals—especially if you have any history of fungal infections.

Because you never know what you’re inhaling.

## 2. Breathing in a world where “air” is not just air

### A. Airborne conditions

- Expect:
  - **Constant humidity**: 90–100% relative humidity, but without obvious rain.
  - **Condensation on everything:** droplets of water that *reabsorb into leaves* instead of falling.
  - **Airborne plant matter:** microscopic pollen, spores, sap aerosol, bits of fiber rearranging themselves.

### B. Respiratory protection you must bring

**Minimum for any short stay:**

- **Half-face respirator or mask with P100 filters.**  
  - Organic vapor + particulate filter.
- **Replacement filters** enough for:
  - At least one pair change per day.
  - More if the mask ever feels “heavy” or damp inside.
- **Nasal irrigation kit** (saline, rinse bottle).
  - Use at least morning and night.

**If you can afford or get one:**

- **Light, powered air-purifying respirator (PAPR)** with:
  - Full-face mask or helmet with hood.
  - High grade filter cartridges.
  This serves:
  - Oxygen enrichment.
  - Protection against spores, pollen, sap.
  - A steady “personal” atmosphere in your breathing zone.

## 3. Dealing with constant humidity and heat

### A. Heat and humidity tolerance

Expect:
- **Temperature:** like a warm greenhouse, maybe 25–30 °C (77–86 °F).
- **Humidity:** feels like breathing inside a sponge.

### B. Clothing and moisture management

- **Base layers:** synthetic or treated fabric that wicks moisture away from your skin.
- **Outerwear:** lightweight, waterproof-breathable shell (jacket and trousers).
  - It must **dry fast**; you will never be dry, but you can avoid being soaked.
- **Undergarments:**  
  - Change frequently; you almost want to treat undergarments like daily disposables with a wash rotation.
- **Neck gaiters / bandanas:**  
  - To wipe sweat and condensation from your face and mask seal.
- **Wide-brim hat with ventilation mesh.**  
  - Helps shade you from the sun (which is otherwise “normal” but shifts shadows).
  - Allows your head to breathe somewhat.

### C. Hydration and electrolytes

- **Hydration bladder pack** with bite valve:
  - You need *more* than typical daily water intake because you’ll be losing sweat constantly.
- **Electrolyte tablets or powder**:
  - To add into your water a few times per day.
- **Water filter** (even if you mostly drink your own water):
  - If you must sample local water, only do so through a filter or chemical treatment.
  - Never assume water is safe because “there’s no animal life”; there are still plant tissues and sap.

## 4. Protection against integration and contact hazards

### A. The world wants to learn you

- The ground:
  - May curl *up* around your ankles.
  - Stems may behave as if you are a tree-to-be or a path.
- Walls and surfaces:
  - May subtly adjust around you, “remembering” your shape.

**You must not let this escalate into actual physiological integration.**

### B. Protective clothing and behavior

- **Full-coverage clothing:**  
  - No shorts, sandals, or tank tops.
  - Trousers tucked into boots or at least taped at the ankles with gaiters.
- **Gloves:**  
  - **Durable gloves:** leather or synthetic cut-resistant.
  - **Optional inner layer:** thin nitrile gloves underneath.
  - Always wear gloves when touching any plant surface.
- **Boots:**  
  - Waterproof, high-top boots with solid soles.
  - Gaiters to prevent stems from creeping up your legs.
- **Every night:**
  - Check your body in full: between toes, ankles, fingers, anywhere you might have missed with the barrier.
  - If any plant tissue seems to be adhering or you see unusual fibers, remove immediately and treat as a foreign intrusion.

### C. “Don’t be a tree” policy

- **Never stand still too long**, especially directly on the root-mat.
- If stems begin curling around your ankles:
  - Step firmly *through* them, do not allow them to shackle you.
  - Shake your legs, rotate ankles, break any pattern that starts forming.
- Do not lean against trunk-walls for longer than necessary.  
  The world will incorporate your posture into its next draft of structure.

## 5. Temporal and cognitive precautions: coping with “time tearing itself open”

### A. Time behaves weird here

You get:
- **Shadows not lining up:** a tree’s shadow moves relative to the tree’s future shape.
- **Hills growing or shrinking:** trying out possible heights.
- **Paths and riverbeds rewriting:** steps you took before might be “under” a new fold of landscape.

### B. Grounding and sanity-checking

This kind of environment can break your sense of cause-and-effect and time’s arrow. You need:

- **A strong, external anchor to home time.**  
  - A device (if tech works at your entry point) or a methodical physical practice:
    - Daily count of days in your head.
    - Physical mark notches on a small strip (if allowed).
- **Compass and simple map system:**  
  - Even if the land itself changes, you still need:
    - Sun’s position, “local” shadow direction, “settlement” vs “forest”.
    - Keep track of what you *expected* to see vs what you see.
- **Time journal on persistent medium:**  
  - If electronics are likely to fail, use **waterproof paper and pen**.
  - Every day, write:
    - Date according to your home system.
    - Local sensory changes: “River has now shifted to east side of tree-column.”
    - Your own body’s state: tired, clear-headed, confused, etc.

### C. Exposure limits

- **Vary your exposure depth and location:**  
  - Deeper into the forest = more radical rearranging and ambiguous “rules.”
  - Closer to the settlement-like structures = more of “architecture” reigning in.
- **Don’t stay too long in one place.**  
  - The world *learns* you:
    - Couches-in your habits into future shapes.
    - Your “room” might become more defined, but also more *definite* in a way you can’t revoke.

## 6. Sensory and auditory overload management

### A. Noise level

Day:
- A wall of sound from:
  - trunks unbraiding and rebraiding,
  - leaves reweaving,
  - water hissing,
  - root-mat groaning.

Night:
- Same noise but different **frequency**:
  - like an editing suite of a world being revised.
- It’s not quieter—just differently loud.

### B. Hearing protection

- **High-quality acoustic earplugs:**
  - For when you need to sleep or concentrate.
- **Noise-cancelling earphones** if you can bring them:
  - You may not be able to recharge them easily, but for limited times they can be sanity-saving.
- **Indirect auditory buffer:**  
  - Soft internal soundtrack or spoken word guide you’ve recorded before coming:
    - Helps to overlay your own pattern over the world’s pattern.

## 7. Navigation and maps in a world that rewrites itself

The “town” of plant-room-suggestions is:
- Not fixed by gates.
- Given by a **shift of “pitch”** in the world’s pattern.

### A. How to navigate

- **Markers with great care:**  
  - You cannot leave plastic flags or metal spikes—anything like that may be treated by the world as *proposal* for permanent structure.
  - Use temporary, non-invasive markers:
    - Small removable colored threads or biodegradable tags *you retrieve*.
    - Optical beacons or retro-reflective tape (if tech allowed) at clearly defined safe nodes only.
- **Mapping the “stable-ish nodes”:**
  - Circular pools of water.
  - The column-ring of the world’s quasi-organ/voice.
  - The “house” tree you choose to sleep in.

Make **sequential** maps of the same area:
- Over time you’ll see the *range of variation* of the world:
  - The river’s *possible* paths.
  - The hill’s *possible* heights.
  - The house’s *possible* floor plans.

## 8. Shelter and how to sleep there safely

### A. Choosing your room-tree

- Pick:
  - **Within the town-like area:** more defined “room,” less likely to be completely overwritten.
  - A tree-house whose main shape is *relatively* stable:
    - Yes, it will change, but you want *less* dramatic than average.
- Avoid:
  - The longhouse-bar-like structure that *rewrites its floor plan* frequently.
  - Low bowl-pools if the water level is unstable.

### B. Your personal sleeping layer

- **Never sleep directly on the plant-mat floor of the room-tree.**
- Bring:
  - **Waterproof groundsheet / tarp:** anchors your physical space to the world.
  - **Sleep pad:**
    - Insulated, self-inflating or foam.
  - **Sleeping bag or liner**:
    - Lightweight; total insulation might be too warm, but you need a barrier.
    - Optionally, a treated sheet that acts as a barrier between you and the world.

Arrange your sleep pad inside:
- **A defined perimeter in your mind:**  
  - Tell yourself (and maybe the world) “this boundary is not to be crossed.”
  - Stems curling under the edge of the tarp should be gently removed each night—you’re not a tree.

### C. Nighttime routines

- Check:
  - Your mattress edges for any ingrowth.
  - Your gear: no roots have snaked in your backpack.
- **Anchor your sleep to time:**
  - Set a sleep schedule and try to stick to the same hours.
  - This keeps you tied to *your* time rather than drifting with the world’s.

## 9. Food and sustenance: you are not part of the food web yet

### A. The world’s food ecology

- **No animals, no fungi you can recognize.**
- The plantmass seems:
  - To recycle its own matter constantly,
  - To experiment with novel growths and proto-fruits.
- Many things you see:
  - Not fully decided as to whether they are fruits, seeds, or tumors.

### B. Survival strategy: bring your own food

- **Do not rely on local foods for calories.**  
  - Their biochemistry:
    - May be incompatible,
    - May contain novel compounds,
    - May treat you as a catalyst to their own self-development.
- **Bring complete sealed nutrition:**
  - **Freeze-dried meals:** sealed, waterproof, long-term stable.
  - **Protein bars / nuts / seeds** from your own world.
  - Vacuum-sealed pouches where you can.
- **Plan for longer than you expect:**  
  - Time weirdness means your days might be longer or shorter than you intend.
  - Bring extra rations.
- **Water purification:**  
  - Even if you rely on your own water supply, you must test or avoid any local water that:
    - behaves like sap,
    - is thick, colored, or smells suspicious.

## 10. Mind and psyche: don’t let the world out-think you

### A. The quasi-language in the column-ring

At the edge of the settlement, there is:
- A ring of columns that are turning themselves inside-out,
- Producing sounds like a deep, distorted chorus of questions and experiments.

You may feel:
- The urge to interpret these as speech.
- The pressure of “messages” trying to form in your mind.

### B. Precautions against cognitive absorption

- **Set strict limits on exposure to the ring-area:**
  - Do not spend the whole day there.
  - Do not go there at all if you’re already feeling:
    - detached,
    - confused by time,
    - like “you could belong” perfectly.
- Use **cognitive filters**:
  - Treat anything you “hear” as raw sound:
    - Like the ocean,
    - Not secret instructions,
    - Not your name being called.
- **Maintain a self-dialogue:**  
  - Ask yourself:
    - Who are you?
    - What are you doing here?
    - What is your next concrete step?
    - Write these answers down.

## 11. Leaving and duration limits

### A. How long to stay?

For safety:
- **Short stays:** first few trips are no more than 3–5 days local time, if that can be measured.
- **Longer stays:** if you must:
  - Set an external clock:
    - Somebody expecting you back at a specific time.
    - A device with countdown timer and alarm, if any tech works here.
- **Leave before the world feels like home.**  
  The moment you:
  - Stop feeling “foreigner,” and
  - Start thinking “this place makes more sense than my own world,”  
  you’re verging on being rewritten.

### B. Exit plan

- Define your exit point:
  - If you have a portal device or method, ensure:
    - It is anchored to *you* rather than to location.
    - The world itself is not tempted to re-letter your exit into a room.
- Keep:
  - **Exit path “simple”:**
    - Do not accept complex shortcuts through new geometry that appears “conveniently.”
    - The world may offer you the perfect corridor; remember that it might also be offering you a different version of yourself.

## 12. Equipment checklist summary

**Breathing & safety:**

- Half-face respirator with P100 filters + spares, or PAPR.
- Nasal irrigation saline + rinse bottle.
- Any allergy meds/antihistamines you use.
- Antibiotics, antifungals (if available).

**Protection & motion:**

- Waterproof boots with high tops.
- Gaiters to seal over boots.
- Waterproof breathable jacket and trousers.
- Gloves (outer durable gloves + inner nitrile).
- Full head/neck cover (hat, mask, etc.).

**Sleep & shelter:**

- Waterproof tarp or groundsheet.
- Insulated sleeping pad.
- Sleeping bag/liner.
- Headlamp with extra batteries.

**Food & water:**

- Sealed, freeze-dried meals or equivalent.
- Nutrition bars, nuts, seeds.
- Hydration bladder pack.
- Electrolyte tablets.
- Portable water filter or purification tablets.

**Tools & mapping:**

- Compass.
- Waterproof notebook and pens.
- Digital camera if tech works (otherwise drawing supplies).
- Multitool or knife.
- Lightweight cordage or tape (retrievable, temporary markers).

**Mind & time:**

- Earplugs or noise-cancelling earphones.
- Watch or device with countdown timer.
- Pre-recorded audio grounding you in your own world:
  - A voice reminding you of your name, your home, your purpose.

### Final mindset

You are walking into a world that is:

- younger than any forest,
- older than any stone,
- and still in the middle of writing itself.

You should behave like an editor who respects the text, but does not want to become a character in it. Your job is to observe, survive, and leave *without* being turned into poetry.

Treat everything as if it might re-place you.  
Treat yourself as if you must remain unchanged.
