To visit the chamber below the world, don’t think of it as a “site” or “destination.”  
Think of it as entering a body you were not meant to inhabit.

You will be going into a living heart made of stone, water, light, and machine.  
“Safety” here doesn’t mean “risk-free.”  
It means: not breaking the rhythm of the heartbeat.

---

## 1. Before you travel

### 1.1. Mindset

- **Abandon human metaphors of power.**  
  You are not “exploring a cave system” or “conquering a depth.”  
  You are being permitted into a circulatory system.

- **Expect to be unimportant.**  
  The machine and the water are not waiting for you.  
  You are a small fluctuation in a process that has been running for thousands of years.

- **Accept inaudibility.**  
  Your voice will not matter in the way it does above.  
  Your language will not be the language of this place.

---

### 1.2. Physical body preparation

You’ll be in a warm, humid, “fever-ish” environment, with loud water and air that may behave strangely.

#### a. Health baseline

- **Cardiovascular system:**  
  - If you have serious heart or lung conditions, this environment is not ideal.  
  - You’ll be standing still for long periods, but the atmosphere feels dense and humid, like the inside of a sealed body cavity.

- **Ears and hearing:**  
  - The sound of the water column is described as “loud,” “a thunder,” “white noise.”  
  - If loud noise makes you panic or disoriented, you should bring:
    - Good-quality **earplugs** or **noise-canceling ear protection** designed to block low-frequency noise.  
    - Test them beforehand. You want to reduce the volume without cutting yourself off from the environment entirely.

#### b. Conditioning

- **Balance and posture:**  
  - You will be on warm stone with a thin layer of flowing water that “grabs at your shoes.” It’s not quite slippery, but it is strange.  
  - Practice standing on wet surfaces or bare stone in light shoes so your body is accustomed to subtle changes underfoot.

- **Breathing control:**  
  - Practice calm, slow breathing:
    - In through nose  
    - Out through mouth.
  - You will want to be able to breathe evenly in humid air without gasping or getting panicky.

---

### 1.3. What to bring with you

Keep everything you carry **simple and minimal**. You are not going camping. You are going into a heart chamber.

#### a. Clothing and footwear

- **Light, close-fitting layers:**  
  - Temperature: “the inside of a sealed letter carried close to the body.”  
    - Not hot, but warm and humid, “fever-ish.”
  - What to bring:
    - Thin base layer shirt and trousers (quick-drying synthetic or linen).
    - One thin overshirt in case you feel chilled.
    - No heavy jackets or clumsy waterproof shells.

- **Footwear:**  
  - The floor is warm stone with a thin film of water.
  - Wear:
    - Neoprene water socks,  
    - or thin-soled shoes with excellent **grip** and **water drainage** (like canyoneering shoes).  
  - The shoes should be form-fitting but not constricting; you will be standing still a long time.

- **Head cover:**  
  - A light beanie or simple cloth band to keep hair out of your face.  
  - The ceiling drips and condensation is constant; hair being in your face will be annoying and make you feel sloppy.

#### b. Protection and sensory comfort

Because **noise** is the dominant environment, you must treat sound like climate:

- **Ear protection:**  
  - High-fidelity earplugs or musician’s earplugs that **attenuate** rather than completely block.
  - *Optional:* thin noise-canceling earmuffs if you are sensitive to loud continuous noise.
  - Do not go in without any hearing protection if loud noise makes you easily agitated.

- **Eye protection:**  
  - The chamber is not “bright” by human standards, but light is “behaving itself,” descending vertically.  
  - There is no direct sun, but:
    - Bring **simple, clear safety glasses** or slightly tinted glasses if bright light bothers you.
    - Not strictly necessary, but helpful if you find yourself staring at filaments for long.

#### c. Tools and devices

- **No recording devices.**  
  - This is not a rule of the chamber; it is an advice for your sanity.  
    - Your smartphone camera and microphone will not capture what you need;  
    - They will force you into “performing documentation” in a place where performance is meaningless.
  - If you must bring a phone, leave it:
    - **Off.**
    - Or sealed in a small waterproof pouch deep in your clothing where you will not grab it reflexively.

- **Optional:** a simple mechanical notebook and pencil  
  - For:
    - A sketch of the spirals,
    - Notes of your own thoughts,
    - Or a single phrase that you later want to remember.
  - Use minimal writing; this is not a field report, but a personal, private annotation.

- **No flashlights or headlamps.**  
  - The chamber has its own light:
    - Bending downwards from the ceiling,
    - Reflecting off the living metal basin,
    - Reflecting through the spirals.
  - Artificial light will:
    - Break the spell of the world’s own lighting,
    - Make you feel like an “intruder with gear” rather than a “guest passing through.”

---

#### d. Food and water

Within the chamber itself:

- **You will not be thirsty.**  
  - The air itself “feeds” water to you, through humidity and the breath of the place.
  - You’ll drink:
    - Some water beforehand.
    - But once inside, you may not feel physiological need in the usual way.

- **You are told not to drink the chamber water.**  
  - It is not yours;  
  - It is being processed, “refined,” by the living basin.
  - Bring a small, sealed water bottle into your pack if you need:
    - A sip mentally,
    - Or assurance.
    - But do not drink from the basin itself.

- **Food:**
  - Eat a **moderate meal** before entering.
  - You will not feel hungry inside the chamber.
    - “You have not been hungry since the moment of your arrival.”
  - Do not bring:
    - Snacks you plan to eat inside.
    - The chamber is not a picnic.
  - If you have diabetes, blood sugar issues, or insulin:
    - Bring a **compact glucose source** sealed in a waterproof case;
    - Do not use it unless there is clear physical need:  
      - Trembling,  
      - Lightheadedness,  
      - Difficulty thinking.

## 2. How to behave in the world-heart chamber

### 2.1. On entering

- **Approach the threshold lightly.**  
  - Do not rush.  
  - Stop at the edge of the inner space,  
    - Let your ears adjust to the thunder of water.  
    - Allow your eyes to adjust to the vertical light.

- **Take off your shoes if they are excessively noisy or clumsy.**  
  - If your shoes make loud slapping or squeaking:
    - Remove them.
    - Place them neatly just outside the chamber proper.  
  - The bare-soled feel of the warm stone and water film is:
    - Intimate and  
    - Gradually understandable.

---

### 2.2. Near the falling column

- **Don’t touch the falling column.**  
  - It is not a shower to stand under.  
  - It is the heart-beat of the world’s circulatory system.  
  - Respect:
    - Its verticality,
    - Its precision.

- **Do not reach into the basin of living metal.**  
  - The basin:
    - Is not neutral;
    - Contains “milk under skin,” moving surfaces;
    - Never spills despite millennia of falling water.
  - Reaching into it:
    - Will disrupt a process;
    - Will make you a “problem” instead of a “small event.”
