### 0. Before anything else: what you’re up against

The Threshold is not just “high altitude” or “desert city.” It’s a combination that plays in very uncomfortable ways:

- High altitude: thin air → low oxygen.
- High temperature: strong sun → you need to lose heat.
- Low humidity: dry air → rapid dehydration.
- Industrial machine-city: a system that sees data, not tourists.

Your body wants to sweat, but the thin, dry air doesn’t allow you to cool efficiently. You feel like you’re breathing through cotton, but your skin is still hot. The city itself is designed around machines, algae, mites, and engineered species—humans are an edge case.

So you will need:

- Oxygen support.
- Heat control.
- Hydration and mineral balance.
- Protection from stone dust, metal particles, salt.
- Enough data literacy to avoid being “optimized” by accident.
- Psychological preparedness to exist in a machine-logged world.

Below is a detailed preparation kit and strategy for an ordinary traveler. Assume you have access to basic multiverse travel tech, but you yourself are still a human with unmodified lungs.

---

### 1. Physiological adaptation & timing

#### 1.1 Build altitude readiness beforehand

If you have time before the jump, try to arrive adapted to high altitude:

- 1–2 weeks before:
  - Stay at 2,500–3,000 m above sea level, or use simulated altitude training.
  - Do light but persistent exercise (walking, cycling) rather than intense bursts.
- Goal: not zero altitude symptoms, but lower baseline heart rate and better red blood cell count.

Your first hours in the Threshold will be miserable if you show up cold from sea level.

#### 1.2 Use portable oxygen and mild stims (if allowed)

You can’t rely on the city’s data-system to notice you’re hypoxic. It may notice a pattern and log it, but not act.

Pack:

- **Portable, low-pressure oxygen mask or light cannula:**
  - Aim for ~30–40% FiO₂ if you can—just enough to take the edge off.
  - Comfortable mask with adjustable straps and good padding.
  - Spare oxygen cartridges or a refillable container with a filter for metal dust and salt.
- **Optional (if your body tolerates well):**
  - Mild stims for first days to help with breathing and alertness (any low-risk asthmatic/bronchodilators or similar, in your world).
  - No heavy self-experimenting with unknown drugs.

**Use strategy:**

- Wear on arrival and during first 2–3 days.
- Ease off gradually: fewer hours per day, not a full stop.
- Increase at once if you notice:
  - Shortness of breath at rest.
  - Headache, dizziness, confusion.
  - Extreme fatigue or nausea.

Think of yourself as a new data stream in the system: you are not optimized to this altitude; behave accordingly.

### 2. Hydration & electrolytes – the city is dry even though it’s “wet”

The city squeezes water out of air over days of cycling and collects it from the sky-river only in specific moments. You don’t have access to that infrastructure.

Plan your needs:

#### 2.1 Drink like it’s your job

You’re standing at altitude where:
- Air is thin.
- You sweat more but feel it less.
- You breathe faster and lose moisture with each breath.

Estimate your baseline in your home world (say ~2–3 liters per day). In the Threshold, treat:

- **4–6 liters per day** as a minimum target.
- If you are walking, add 0.5–1 L per hour of moderate movement.

Pack:

- 1–2 **collapsible water containers** (2–3 L each).
- **Sterilization tablets / filter straw** in case you tap into local water.
- **Electrolyte tablets or powders:** sodium, potassium, magnesium.
  - Add to at least half of your water intake.
  - The air tastes of metal and chalk—your minerals will be off.

#### 2.2 Water sources & local trust

The city’s drinking water has been optimized by machine for centuries:

- **Strengths:** 
  - It’s centrally processed, continuously filtered.
  - Supports many locals, over generations.
- **Risks:** 
  - It may be tuned to the locals’ salt tolerance and not yours.
  - It may be subtly dosed with things the machine-system thinks are good (trace metals, etc.), but that you can’t predict.

**Strategy:**

- Drink:
  - First: your own imported water and electrolyte mix.
  - Second: if you need more and you’re confident samples from the city are safe, test:
    - Use a quick water test strip for heavy metals, pH, and salinity.
- **Avoid:** the water from terraces or roof condensation lattices—algae colonies, spinner mites, and lichens live there. It’s built for the ecosystem, not for you.
- **Never lick:** the salt crystals on the breath rose bushes or any surfaces. The city uses these as abrasives and filtering materials; they are not salt for you.

### 3. Heat management – sun armor

The sun is a white point in a deep blue sky. It’s “hammering without shame or shadows.” You need to reflect it before it gets to you, and let heat out before it cooks you.

**Essential kit:**

1. **Reflective outer layer:**
   - Long-sleeved shirt and trousers in white or silver, with:
     - UV-resistant coating.
     - Modest reflectivity (without creating blinding glints for others).
   - Breathable fabric but not so loose that dust reaches your skin.

2. **Head & face protection:**
   - Wide-brimmed hat or hood with **back-of-neck coverage**.
   - Breathable, non-metallic face mask or dust scarf:
     - To filter air dust and metal flakes.
     - To prevent excessive salt from drying lips and skin.
   - UV-protective sunglasses with:
     - Good wrap to protect sides from dust and wind.
     - Very strong glare protection—the mirror trees and platforms will be blinding.

3. **Cooling strategy:**
   - **Phase-change or cooling vests:** if you have access to tech like that, use them sparingly but often:
     - Wear under your reflective layer.
     - Avoid freezing your skin; you want slow, steady heat loss.
   - Hydration also helps: water evaporates from your skin even if you don’t feel sweat; every drink contributes a little bit to cooling.

**Time-of-day behavior:**

- **Avoid being outdoors:** between 11–16 local sun if you can.
- If that’s not possible:
  - Stay on shaded walkways, under mesh shade canopies.
  - Use inner corridors of the towers for heat relief.
- **Night:** 
  - The temperature drops but the air remains thin.
  - Layer clothing but don’t overheat yourself.
  - Nights are good for collecting moisture from your own inner layers if you’re using them.

### 4. Dust and metal – protect your lungs and skin

The air “tastes of metal and chalk dust” and is never still. There are dust runners and spinner mites; they are part of the dust ecosystem.

#### 4.1 Respiratory protection

- **Good mask vs dust:**
  - Not full gas mask level needed, but a filter-insert mask rated for fine particulate matter.
  - Ensure filters can handle:
    - Metal dust.
    - Cryptic biological microparticles (from mites, algae, etc).
- **Spares:**
  - Change filters more often than you would in a “clean” world; dust indicators are the city’s sensors, not you. If you taste metal, your mask needs to be checked or replaced.

Don’t rely on local cloth masks unless you can verify they filter at fine level.

#### 4.2 Skin protection

- **Dry barrier cream:** applied to exposed skin where dust might settle.
- **Light gloves:** not for warmth but to keep surface dust from your hands.
- After each day:
  - Wash hands carefully; if possible, take a full shower if you are given access to water.
  - Replace dust-soaked clothing with clean set if you can.

You don’t want the city’s dust to permanently become part of your data in your own body.

### 5. Navigation & safe movement in the machine-city

The city is built as a machine. The human-readable aspects are:

- Tower spires with dark skins and condensation lattices.
- Terraces and gardens with low walled glass enclosures.
- Walkways and bridges with open edges but shaded mesh.
- Hanging or sky-river pipelines shimmering above.

**Key points:**

1. **Watch for edges:**
   - Many platforms and cantilevered terraces extend out over the valley. If railings look low or absent—step back from the edge.
   - Never walk on external surfaces not clearly marked as pathways.

2. **Respect the bright orange and yellow machines:**
   - Garden machines on terraces are not pets:
     - Stay out of their way.
     - Don’t try to repair or interfere with them.
   - They are crucial to the filtration system and data stability of the tower; the system will be attentive to them.

3. **Use shade but don’t assume it’s safe:**
   - Some mesh shades are designed to catch dust—which they can also release into you if you disturb them.
   - Avoid brushing or hanging onto them.

4. **Expect sudden changes of light and openness:**
   - Roof panels may become transparent or opaque to manage the view of the sky-river.
   - Your brain may need time to adjust depth perception:
     - Pause before taking steps where you suddenly see “open sky” you didn’t see before.

5. **Don’t chase data patterns:**
   - The system of the Threshold:
     - Assigns sun to valleys according to data layers.
     - Uses data about your presence to classify you without your name.
   - Avoid:
     - Leaning on sensors, indicator lights, or lattice surfaces.
     - Taking photos of sensitive data points or machinery that you don’t understand.

### 6. Weather and environmental events

The city is designed for weather, but you are not designed for the city.

#### 6.1 Dust storms

Indicators:

- Sky courants clustering along high edges of certain towers.
- Lattice indicator lights flicking to blue or other alert colors.
- Mirror tree reflections below becoming diffuse and broken.

Action if a storm is coming:

- Get inside:
  - Choose lower interiors of towers with minimal windows.
  - Close windows or vents if you can control them.
- Use:
  - Better-grade mask or double mask during passage.
  - Extra covering of clothing to protect skin from flying sand or salt.
- After storm:
  - Check your mask and filters; expect dust trapped.

#### 6.2 Sky-river visibility

“Good sky-river” days:

- Mirror tree reflections below are sharp.
- Roof panels may become transparent to see the sky-river.
- Visibility of the shimmering vapor pipeline improves.

This is not dangerous weather, but:

- There may be more wind.
- Temperature may fluctuate slightly more.
- Use these as opportunities to rest inside with an open view of the sky instead of trying to climb or walk heavily.

### 7. Local biology – not your enemy, but not your friend

The city has engineered or adapted organisms:

- **Algae and joint lichens in the lattices.**
- **Spinner dust mites.**
- **Basalt dust plants and faintwire grasses on terraces.**
- **Breath roses on the roof rim.**
- **Dust runners in the edges of dust storms.**
- **Sky courants riding air and electricity lines.**

Your policy as a traveler:

1. **Do not touch:**
   - Breath roses (salt crystals, sharp edges).
   - Lattice surfaces where there are visible lichens or green films.
   - Any dust runners or unknown creatures.

2. **Do not eat:**
   - Any plants in the city; they are not designed for you.
   - Water collected directly from terraces or roofs—unless you test and thoroughly treat it.

3. **Do not interfere with sky courants:**
   - They are intertwined with the city’s data grid and dust monitoring.
   - Letting them be is part of letting the system keep you safe.

4. **If you disturb something by accident:**
   - Retreat.
   - Observe for any change:
     - New indicator colors on lattices.
     - Sounds of machinery adjusting.
   - No heroics.

### 8. Social and data system – how to behave in the “Threshold”

The city’s system is not hostile to you; it is simply indifferent. It will assign you a parameter name unknown to you and use you in its model of the world.

You can’t stop that from happening, but you can keep from getting hurt by it.

#### 8.1 Be a predictable, low-impact data point

- **Follow obvious paths:**
  - Don’t climb lattices.
  - Don’t open doors, hatches, or locked panels unless you understand them.
- **Minimize erratic behavior:**
  - No sudden running across roofs or terraces.
  - No attempts to “shortcut” through machine spaces.

The system is better at protecting you if your behavior is not flagged as anomalous noise.

#### 8.2 Your data privacy

Assume you are being monitored:

- Visuals, IR, temperature sensors, and maybe microphone data.
- Behavioral data: your walking patterns, time in locations.

Considerations:

- **Don’t do sensitive work:** personal banking or other multiverse-critical transactions on the Threshold’s systems, if you can avoid.
- **Assume your logs persist:** what you do here may be part of future data models.

You can’t erase yourself from the machine’s memory safely. But you can act accordingly:

- Be polite, boring, and predictable in the data space.

#### 8.3 Local humans

If you encounter any locals in the lower city or on terraces:

- They are likely to be more adapted to:
  - Altitude.
  - Heat.
  - Data system.
- **Approach:**
  - Ask before touching anything.
  - Ask before photographing them or work machines.
  - Follow any instructions they give about water, safety, or access.
- **Questions:**
  - Safe places to refill water.
  - Which buildings are open to travelers.
  - Which areas are under construction or machine-maintenance.
  - How to leave if you need to exit quickly.

If you are treated as a curiosity (data parameter), remain polite and not intrusive. You are not a data sample; you are a guest with limited infrastructure.

### 9. Contingency & emergency kit

Assemble a compact but complete emergency kit:

1. **Medical:**
   - Altitude medication:
     - Diamox or equivalent if you and your doctor decide it’s appropriate.
   - Pain relief (for headaches from altitude and heat).
   - Anti-nausea meds.
   - Sunburn cream and aloe.
   - Antiseptic wipes and dressings for small cuts from dust or salt friction.

2. **Communication & location:**
   - Signal device for your own multiverse portal or anchor:
     - Make sure it can work at altitude and heat.
     - Don’t use it near data-sensitive nodes without understanding.
   - Local paper map:
     - Mark:
       - Your starting point roof.
       - The nearest stairway or interior route to that roof.
       - Safe interior buildings with higher shade.

3. **Comfort & survival:**
   - Emergency space blanket:
     - Use reflective side out in daytime to shade you.
   - High-energy, compact snacks:
     - Nut bars, dried fruit with enough salt/sugar.
   - Extra mask filters.
   - Re-sealable bags to protect gear from dust and salt.

4. **Plan B:**
   - Know where the “Threshold” exit you intended to use:
     - Ideally:
       - On the same roof or in nearby tower.
       - Not somewhere deep in the lower city that you might reach only if fit and adapted.

### 10. Mental strategy & your expectations

The last dimension of preparation is mental.

Remember:

- The city you see is not “beauty” in the sense of green trees and soft breezes.
- It is a *machine-made ecosystem of dust and light and water vapor*.
- Its gods are:
  - Condensation lattices.
  - Sky-river collectors.
  - Tissue-sea membrane.
  - Data models.

And you are not an optimized part of it.

So you must:

- **Respect the heat and altitude** as if you were in a hostile environment, because you are.
- **Treat water as precious** even though you see moisture systems everywhere.
- **Watch the dust and not taste the air**.
- **Stay wary of edges, heights, and machinery**.
- **Assume the data system sees you but does not see you as you**—and act accordingly.
- **Expect vertigo and disorientation**: optical depth from mirror trees, shimmering sky-rivers, and data timelines.

If you can keep yourself:

- Hydrated,
- Oxygenated,
- Cooled,
- Dust-shielded,
- Predictable and unobtrusive,

then you can be a visitor rather than a victim of the Threshold. The metal taste of the air, the chalk of the dust, and the faint ozone of the machine city will become background noise to a real, strange experience: a world where data is not just about the world, but *world where data of data of data of the world* becomes the environment you’re standing inside.
