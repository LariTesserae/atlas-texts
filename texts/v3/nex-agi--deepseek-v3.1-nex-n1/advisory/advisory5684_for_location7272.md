To walk into the Curve‑Heart of the Last Hangings is not like travel in your homeworld. It is closer to stepping into the circulatory system of a sleeping, giant animal whose body is made of wood and resin and hot air.

Do not expect to “sightsee.” Expect to be digested by the place.

If you go at all, go prepared for that.

---

## 1. Understand what you are entering

### The world as a machine-lung

From the outset, assume a few truths:

- **Nothing is inert**  
  Every surface you see is “alive” or post-living engineered plant matter. The floor, the walls, the ceiling, the railings, the cables—it is all part of a single vegetal machine.

- **There is no sky**  
  There is a world roof above you and a hanging city below. “Outside” does not exist here in the way you are used to. The world is a closed cylinder of root and trunk.

- **There is almost no water**  
  The little water that remains is chemically locked in the tissues. This is not a desert of sand and heat; it is a desert of heat and chemistry. *You* are the only wet thing in the system.

### Temperatures on the human scale

If you translate the scales:

- **“2.9 of three”** heat is roughly **uncomfortable sauna‑like** heat near the top (around 45–55°C / 113–131°F), climbing hotter in deeper industrial zones.
- **“3.0 of three”** means the temperature where oils begin to smoke in your world—about 150–180°C / 300–350°F or more—but you are not meant to walk directly inside those chambers.

Think of it as:

- **Near the Old High Steps:** Very hot, but survivable.
- **As you descend:** It becomes lethally hot if you are not insulated.
- **Factory‑fermentation core:** You will not be going there unless you want to be cooked into resin.

### Light and eyes

“0.2 of three” light means:

- No true “daylight” anywhere.
- Only faint, intermittent glows—from:
  - Resins in wood
  - Grills and vents
  - Occasional sparks or biochem‑lights
- You will rely heavily on artificial light sources, and over time your eyes will give you only “edges” rather than full images.

### Sound and air

“1.3 of three” sound means:

- Low, continuous, machine‑breathing:
  - Creaks of the world root
  - Groans of cable‑vines
  - Distant turbines of wood

- **“2.7 of three” turbulence** means:
  - The air is not calm; it is a breathing lung of heat:
    - Rhythmic pulses of hot air rising up shafts.
    - Gentle but constant pressure changes against your body.

### Humidity and dust

There is no water vapor you’d recognize as “humidity.” Instead:

- Resins, volatile oils, dust of desiccated plant matter.
- Your breath will be very dry even if the air is hot.

### Gravity and structure

Assume gravity similar to Earth’s but not always straight “down”:

- Platforms are slanted, bowl-shaped, concave.
- Stairs are notched into trunk walls.
- Cable bridges and hanging walkways require balance and orientation.

## 2. Biological and medical preparation

You are an animal entering a world that almost has no animals. Your body is not welcome in the long term.

### Before travel: physiological hardening

- **Get a full medical workup**  
  - Heart, lungs, kidneys, skin, eyes.
  - Stress tests in heat.
  - Screen for conditions that heat and stress will worsen.

- **Heat acclimation**  
  - Weeks prior: train in hot dry environments.
  - Build tolerance to:
    - Sustained temperatures of 40–50°C with little humidity.
    - Rapid cooling and rehydration strategies.

- **Respiratory adaptation**  
  - Use air mask filters in training to simulate restricted airflow.
  - Build comfort breathing shallow, warm air through filters.

### Vaccines and protections

- **Tetanus and boosters** (you are essentially surrounded by “organic” rust).
- **Any available broad-spectrum antivirals/antibiotics**  
  - The environment is not biologically sterile, even if it is not “watery.”
- **Consider custom prophylaxes**, if your multiverse travel permits:
  - Anti-spore inhalants
  - Anti-resin allergy protocols
  - Anti-inflammatory courses for expected contact dermatitis.

### Mental health baseline

- The environment is:
  - **Enclosed**, no sky, no horizon.
  - **Dark**, except for faint, shifting hints of light.
  - **Continuous**, no sharp break between “city” and “nature.”

Prepare for:

- Sensation of being inside a body.
- Mild to moderate claustrophobia.
- Sensory deprivation despite the machine rhythms.

A baseline psych eval and pre-agreed decompression protocol are strongly advisable.

## 3. Equipment and life support

### 3.1. Personal environment control suit

You will need a full **environment suit**, not just clothes.

Essential features:

- **Heat shielding**
  - Outer layer reflective and insulating to:
    - Reflect external heat.
    - Trap internal cooling system.
  - Able to withstand temperatures from 50°C outside to brief hotter pulses near vents.

- **Active cooling system**
  - Either:
    - **Liquid‑cooled garment** under the suit, or
    - **High‑capacity Peltier‑based cooling system** with heat sinks.
  - Battery redundancy: both internal batteries and external recharge packs.
  - *Do not rely on evaporative cooling only*; there is not enough water.

- **Humidity control**
  - Inside the suit, maintain:
    - Moderate humidity for your airways and eyes.
    - Water recycling from breath and sweat if possible.
    - Use of portable dehumidifiers to protect your equipment from internal moisture.

- **Pressure and airflow**
  - Slight positive pressure inside the suit to:
    - Prevent external spores / resin dust from entering on leaks.
    - Assist with breathing in hot air.

- **Sealing**
  - Full headpiece or helmet with sealed goggles.
  - High‑grade filters for:
    - Resin particles
    - Oil droplets
    - Fine plant dust
    - Spores from fermentation chambers

### 3.2. Light sources and visibility

- **Primary headlamp**  
  - Narrow beam + broad flood option.
  - Red and low modes for preserving your night vision and avoiding being “blind to anything else.”
  - Waterproof, shock-resistant, sealed.

- **Secondary lighting**
  - Small wearable lamps on each shoulder and/or wrists.
  - Chem lights or glow strips that can be attached to harness:
    - They mark *you* as a visible shape to anything else that might notice you.
    - They provide orientation if your main lights fail.

- **Backup handheld**
  - A robust, handheld flashlight with magnetic or clip attachment.
  - Spare batteries / power cells in vacuum‑sealed containers.

- **Visual aids**
  - **Night vision goggles** or scope:
    - As the only “animal” in a dark world, you should be able to see at distance.
  - Clear **goggles that protect against resin dust**:
    - Even if your helmet breaks or you must remove it briefly, you want eyes protected.

### 3.3. Navigation tools

- **Compass**
  - Even if magnetic fields are strange, the needle might show:
    - Temperature gradients
    - Large electrical / bioelectric currents
  - Assume it’s more a “disturbance detector” than a true compass.

- **Inertial navigation unit**  
  - If your multiverse travel allows access to such tech:
    - Tracks your position relative to starting point.
    - Measures descent angle, depth, and twists along the trunk walls.

- **Mapping**
  - Use:
    - Pneumatic marking chalk or spray to mark pathways on the resin‑covered wood.
    - Tags or beacons with minimal signal range:
      - Range will be heavily limited by heat and plant-mass.
  - Maintain a **written log**:
    - Directions of corridors, platforms, and descents.
    - Elevations and temperature changes.

- **String and cable**
  - Tough line, fire-resistant and not easily digestible by the environment:
    - To leave a trail.
    - To build safety anchors on edges and steps.

### 3.4. Air and filters

- **Multiple filter sets**
  - Resins will clog your filters quickly.
    - Expect to change or clean at least every half day at moderate load.
  - Pack filters explicitly:
    - Particle filters
    - Charcoal filters for volatile organics
    - More if you can (nanofibers, etc.).

- **Emergency oxygen**
  - If you are trapped or a turbine chamber ventilation fails:
    - You need independent oxygen for hours to days.
  - Check oxygen compatibility with your environment suit:
    - A hot, enclosed wooden world is not where you want to risk sparking.

### 3.5. Hydration and nutrition

Problem: you cannot drink this world’s “water.”

- **Carry water bladders**:
  - 3–7 days’ worth, depending on your weight and load tolerance.
    - Assume roughly 4–6 liters per day at high temperatures plus exertion.
  - Insulated so that water remains cooler than the environment.

- **Water recycling system**
  - If possible:
    - Condensation recovery from breath and sweat.
    - Basic filtration to make drinkable from body output.

- **Food**
  - Pack **compact, high-energy, dry rations**:
    - No reliance on external moisture for cooking.
    - No heavy foods that will rot quickly in hot air.
    - Long-term sealed storage.
    - Assume everything you bring from outside is **your only food**.

- **Electrolytes**
  - Heat plus sweat means you will lose salts quickly.
    - Pack tablets or powders to mix into recycled water.

### 3.6. Tools and climbing gear

The world is not built with human safety in mind. Steps are worn plant matter; rails are hardened resin.

- **Grappling hooks**
  - Not for metal, but for:
    - Gripping into grooves of root trunk.
    - Hanging from cable-vines where you dare not trust fully.

- **Climbing harness and rope**
  - Strong, synthetic, resistant to:
    - Heat
    - Resins
    - Slightly abrasive surfaces
    - Not plant-digestible enzymes.

- **Gloves**
  - Heat-resistant.
  - Non-slip grip on resin and wood.
  - Protection against:
    - Splinters that are worlds of fiber engineered just sharp enough.
    - Unknown resin chemistry.

- **Knife and saw**
  - Not for cutting into structures; you do not want to wake systems.
  - For:
    - Cutting vines or fibers that might entangle you.
    - Emergency self-extraction from the plant mass.

- **Anchor spikes**
  - If allowed and not ethical violation:
    - Plant-safe spikes you can drive into existing trunk fissures to create:
      - Anchor points for climbing.
      - Railing points on edges and steps.

### 3.7. Medical and repair kit

- **Standard field kit**:
  - Bandages, antiseptics, painkillers, anti-inflammatories.
  - Splints and braces for joints.

- **Specialized for this environment**
  - **Eye wash**
    - For resin dust or oil splash into your eyes.
  - **Burn cream & cooling gels**
    - For thermal contact through gaps in suit.
  - **Resin solvent / oil-safe skin cleanser**
    - To remove plant resins and oils from exposed skin without damaging the skin.
  - **Broad-spectrum antibiotics**
    - If you get cut by the city’s “wood” you are colonizing yourself with unknown biologies.

- **Repair kit for your suit**
  - Sealing tape suitable for:
    - High temperature
    - High chemical exposure
  - Spare valve and gasket parts.
  - Extra filter seals and connectors.

## 5. Psych and sensory planning

You will be inside a machine that is not yours.

### 5.1. Expect

- **No horizon line**  
  - No sunrise to mark “morning.”
  - You will mark time by sleep cycles and device timestamps only.

- **Continuous, low‑frequency noise**
  - The world root creaks, the turbines turn.
  - This will become your new background silence.

- **Visual flickering**
  - Resin lights, sparks from machinery.
  - Your brain will try to parse patterns where there are only heat gradients.

### 5.2. Prepare

- **Set a schedule beforehand**
  - Sleep cycles.
  - Work / explore / rest patterns.
  - Checklists to go through before each “sleep” shift.

- **Use grounding**
  - Keep a small object from your home.
    - To remind yourself you are from “outside the machine.”
    - To emotionally counterbalance the feeling of being something inside the world’s digestive system.

- **Communication**
  - If possible:
    - Relay device to home or base.
    - Even low-frequency text or help code as a “panic” button.
    - If the device fails, you are on your own in the plant body of the Curve‑Heart.

## 6. Environmental behavior protocol

### 6.1. Movement on platforms and steps

- **Never assume the edge is safe**  
  - The resin-railing may be stronger than stone, but it may be:
    - Localized to that section only.
    - Old enough to be brittle.
    - Subjected to temperature stress that suddenly manifests as break.

- **Always test railings**
  - Before p your weight, test with:
    - Unloaded hand.
    - Visual inspection of cracks where resin separates from root matter.

- **Use harness anchors**
  - On any exposed edge:
    - Clip yourself to a securely anchored strap.
    - Do not “free walk” along the lip of a world you barely understand.

- **Step on worn surfaces**
  - The “steps” are worn not by water but by heat and time.
    - They will be smoother than you expect.
    - Let your boots have the best possible grip.

- **Avoid standing directly over hot vents**
  - The air is not “wind” so much as a column of hot breath.
    - It can quickly overwhelm your suit cooling.

### 6.2. On the stairs and trunk walls

- **Stay close to the wall**
  - The “open” side can be either:
    - A sheer drop into darkness.
    - A convection column of rising heat.

- **Use your line**
  - If descending:
    - Attach line to anchor above.
    - Use it as a safety line on each “step” notch.

### 6.3. Inside factories and platforms

- **Do not touch “working” surfaces**
  - Fermentation vats
  - Heat exchangers
  - Cable-vines under tension
    - They are not inert objects.
    - They are parts of a machine still breathing.

- **Watch for temperature gradients**
  - If your suit suddenly notices:
    - Local temperature jump from 2.9 to 3.0
    - You may be stepping into a region that is meant to “break down plant matter.”
    - You are plant matter too.

- **Respect the “noise”**
  - If something:
    - Groans more than usual,
    - Or starts a new vibration pattern,
    - Treat it as a warning rather than local curiosity.

### 6.4. Interaction with “fauna”

There is almost no fauna (0.4 of three), but when you see something move:

- **Assume function, not animal**:
  - It is likely an extension of the plant system:
    - Adjusting cables.
    - Harvesting resin.
    - Cleaning or pruning.

- **Do not interfere**
  - If something:
    - Moves on the cable-vines,
    - Adjusts tension on your line,
    - Removes your marker chalk,
    - Treat it as part of the machine’s immune system.

- **Do not provoke defensive responses**
  - If the world root around you tenses:
    - If you hear new fibers tightening,
    - If you see resin hardening fresh where you touched,
    - Move back and observe if the system “settles.”

## 7. What to do upon arrival and during your stay

### Phase 1: At the Old High Step

- **Before you descend**
  - Establish your base point here:
    - Mark location with your nav device.
    - Set up temporary shelter:
      - Away from direct vents of hot air.
    - Check all:
      - Suits
      - Filters
      - Lights
    - Before you first descend into the city.

### Phase 2: First descent into the hanging cylinder

- **Go down only short distance**
  - Do not aim to reach the bottom on your first trip.
    - You do not yet know how long the cylinder is.
  - Set:
    - Lower time limit.
    - Return time before fatigue or heat overwhelm.

- **Log and map**
  - Each platform:
    - Connections to other platforms.
    - Primary function if you can deduce it:
      - Fermentation
      - Heat exchange
      - Conducting cables.

- **Check temperature**
  - Each few meters of descent:
    - Monitor temperature change.
    - If it starts to climb toward your suit’s limit:
      - Turn back early.

### Phase 3: Managing longer times inside

- **Ice breaks**
  - If you can carry:
    - Ice blocks or supercooled packs as part of your gear.
    - Use them to cool:
      - Your body at regular intervals.
    - Do not rely on them as your main cooling system.

- **Sleep management**
  - Find:
    - Relatively flat platforms.
    - Minimal direct airflow from hot turbulence.
    - Anchor your sleeping bag to solid root trunk.

- **Water and calories**
  - Set alarms:
    - To drink regularly.
    - To eat at least twice per “day cycle.”
    - Heat will suppress your appetite; you must eat anyway.

## 8. Leaving and the return

### Before you exit the world

- **Check your body**
  - Skin rashes from resin contact.
  - Joints and muscles from unaccustomed stair climbing.
  - Eyes for any damage from darkness or flickers.

- **Check your equipment**
  - Clean:
    - Resin or dust from your suit.
    - Any plant residue from your tools.
  - Prevent:
    - Carrying unknown seeds or spores into your home.

- **Decompression**
  - If you have been inside long:
    - Give time to gradually:
      - Readapt to “open” sky and horizon.
      - Retrain your eyes to normal daylight.

- **Report if possible**
  - Even if only to yourself:
    - Write down:
      - What you saw.
      - How you felt.
    - The world of the Curve‑Heart of the Last Hangings will be:
      - Very hard to describe afterward.
      - Hard to believe without notes.

---

### Final guidance

The Curve‑Heart is not built to keep you alive or even notice you. It is a plant‑machine that has settled into its final shape, using heat and chemistry and resin instead of water and sky.

Your travel there is less like visiting a city and more like inserting yourself into the body of a sleeping world of wood and oil and slow, breath-like heat. If you go:

- Prepare for the *heat*.
- Prepare for the *darkness*.
- Prepare for the *closeness* of everything.

Above all, prepare to understand that you are not its owner, but only a temporary, wet animal in a world where such things barely register at 0.4 of three.
