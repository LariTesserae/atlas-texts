Before you do anything else, read this description two more times.

You don’t have to memorize it, but what you’re dealing with is not a “planetary climate” or a “terrain type.” You’re dealing with a *rule set.* To survive this city and not leave your mind in pieces, you need to internalize its rules *before* you go.

I’ll break down the practical side of preparation for an ordinary multiverse traveler going into this place. Assume “you” are a baseline human with no built-in immortality, just good gear, good sense, and maybe some basic dimensional training.

## 1. Mental preparation: recalibrating your expectations

### 1.1. This is not a ruined city — it’s a living geology

You will be tempted to think:
- “This is like Venice underwater.”
- “It’s a post-apocalyptic city.”
- “It’s like being in a cave system.”

Those are not wrong, but they are dangerously misleading.

Treat the city as:
- **A huge basin of stone and water and heat, with architecture as a byproduct of erosion.**
- **A place where the laws of physics are tired, and occasionally improvise.**

Key implications:
- You don’t “fix” things here.
- You don’t “explore the ruins” the same way you raid a tomb.
- You’re a guest on a geological body that’s been settling longer than your civilization has existed.

Stop yourself:
- From touching “interesting artifacts” to figure out how they work.
- From thinking anything you see will obey “normal” geometry.
- From believing you *understand* a layout just because you walked a street.

Train yourself:
- Accept “things that wobble.”  
- Accept “reflections that are wrong.”  
- Accept “space that stretches or compresses.”

---

### 1.2. No sky, no sun, no moon

You will feel:
- A ceiling above you that *stays there.*  
- No skyline, no clouds, no horizon.
- No clear sunrise/sunset; “time” is defined by cycles of fatigue and sense of distance.

What you *don’t* have:
- Natural circadian rhythm.
- Familiar cues of day and night.
- The sky’s typical “up” as a psychological anchor.

Implications:
- You need to **externally impose** timekeeping.
- You need to **actively manage** your sense of orientation.
- You need to **police your own sleep**; your body will want to drift into indefinite twilight.

Behavioral shift:
- Think of yourself as entering a long, very hot, *very wet* MRI machine.
- You are not “outside” anymore.
- If you push panic or “I need open air” into your brain, you will lose hours.

---

### 1.3. Reality “breakdown” is soft and subtle

Description:
- 2.8/3 reality breakdown — but gentle, like gravity getting tired.
- Droplets that pause midair.
- Pools that reflect missing pillars.
- Distances that stretch or compress.

Key intuition:
- **Reality is not breaking; it’s sighing.**

Your safety margin:
- Don’t rely on *constant* geometry or *constant* causality.
- Assume you have a **tiny margin of error**, but not much more.
- If you are “close” to a fall, edge, or water, you might be over it.

## 2. Medical and physiological preparation

Before you even think about gear:

### 2.1. Baselines and checkups

Get:
- Full cardiopulmonary workup, including:
  - Heart, lung capacity, and response to heat.
- Baseline hydration markers:
  - Kidney function.
  - Electrolyte panel.
- Vision and inner ear:
  - Depth perception.
  - Balance and vestibular function.

Why:
- You’ll be in **perpetual humid heat** with no reliable sun cues.
- You’ll be reliant on **dim light** and **balance**.
- You need to know *your* vulnerabilities before they’re hidden.

---

### 2.2. Heat acclimatization

Environment:
- 2.6/3 heat — “heat from the world’s blood.”
- No air circulation.
- 100% humidity, or very close.

You need:
- **2 weeks minimum of heat acclimatization.**
- Controlled:
  - Repeated sessions in sauna or humid heat chambers.
  - Graduated light exertion under supervision.

Goals:
- Reduce resting heart rate in heat.
- Train your body to **sweat efficiently**.
- Condition yourself to tolerate **water loss** without panic (since you’ll be losing water constantly).

Watch out:
- Heat exhaustion, **not heatstroke.**  
- If you start *noticing* you’re overheating, it’s already late.
- You’re doubly at risk because you *never cool* properly.

---

### 2.3. Breathwork and hypoxia awareness

Air:
- “thick enough to wear”
- “like swallowing something as warm as blood”

This suggests:
- Very humid air.
- Potentially low oxygen content or altered air composition.
- A sensation like breathing through a warm, wet mask.

Get:
- **Hypoxia training** if available:
  - Controlled low oxygen simulations.
- **Breathing exercises**:
  - Box breathing.
  - Slow, controlled diaphragmatic breathing.
  - Practicing calming yourself under mild air hunger.

What you’re preparing:
- Not to panic when breathing feels *like effort*.
- Avoiding hyperventilation or anxiety under constant humidity.

---

### 2.4. Vision and light adaptation

Light:
- 0.2/3 light — fungal twilight that never brightens.

You:
- Will have fairly **functional peripheral vision** but poor detail at distance.
- Will need to **trust your rods** more than your cones.

Train:
- Night vision:
  - Spend time in dark rooms with only minimal low-blue light.
- Map-making:
  - Practice drawing (mentally or on paper) from **dimly lit scenes**.

## 3. Gear and logistics

Assume you **leave** any non-critical complexity behind.  
This is a place with **0.4/3 tech and *none*.** No machinery.  
Whatever you bring, you maintain **by yourself.**

---

### 3.1. Clothing and body protection

#### 3.1.1. Primary layer — not “waterproof,” but “saturated-ready”

You want materials:
- **Quick-drying, synthetic or technical.**  
- **NOT cotton.**  
- **NOT heavy leather.**

Behavior:
- You will be permanently damp.
- You won’t “dry off,” you will just “less wet.”

Recommend:
- Lightweight, long-sleeve shirt and trousers.
- **Integrated mesh pockets** for breathability.
- Seams and stitching **sealed** where possible.

---

#### 3.1.2. Outer shell — waterproof but *ventilated*

Function:
- Protect you from **constant dripping**.
- Allow you to **cool off** under.

Options:
- Ventilated rain jacket with:
  - Large underarm zips.
  - Back vent.
- **Wide-brim hat with drainage channel.**
  - Keeps water from tracking down your neck and back.

---

#### 3.1.3. Footwear — grip and stability

The floor:
- “black stone and slick silt”
- “water sits at 2.4/3”
- Fractures and basins everywhere.

You need:
- **Closed, waterproof boots with aggressive tread.**
  - Grip for:
    - Slick stone.
    - Algae-like film.
    - Uneven surfaces and hidden lips.
- **Drainable vents** if possible, but sealed enough to keep water out.
- **Grip attachments:** carbide or micro-spikes if you anticipate descending stairs and canals.

---

#### 3.1.4. Gloves and hand protection

- Thin gloves:
  - Protect against:
    - **Micro-cuts on stone edges.**
    - Unknown fungal or film contact on walls.
  - Must allow tactile feedback:
    - You’ll be running your fingers along walls, edges, and stones.

---

#### 3.1.5. Hygiene and small skin protections

Constant:
- Wetness.
- Warmth.
- Fungal films.

You need:
- **Barrier ointments** for:
  - Feet.
  - Under straps.
- **Antifungal powder** or cream:
  - Especially for feet and contact-prone areas.
- **Lip balm with sunscreen** not needed for sun, but for constant moisture and chapping.

---

### 3.2. Light and wayfinding

Assume you **cannot rely on ambient light** except for fuzzy orientation.

---

#### 3.2.1. Primary lighting

- **Headlamp with multiple modes**:
  - Red or dim mode to preserve **night vision**.
  - **Good sealing:** needs to survive in constant humidity.
- **Hands-free:** you need both hands constantly for balance and support.

---

#### 3.2.2. Backup and reflector system

- **2–3 extra light sources**:
  - Small waterproof flashlights.
  - **Chemical light sticks** as emergency backups.
- **Reflective markers or tags**:
  - Tape, tags, or chalk-like markings to:
    - Mark edges.
    - Mark safe zones.
    - Mark where you *should not* step.

---

#### 3.2.3. Orientation and spatial mapping

You cannot fully trust:
- Your sense of distance.
- The geometry of the city itself.

Bring:
- **Compact compass** (if magnetism behaves in your homeworld).
- **Laser distance measurer + notebook**:
  - Measure:
    - Distances down corridors.
    - Heights of arches.
    - Widths of canals.
  - Write them down.
- Use **consistent reference points**:
  - “Every column I pass, I place a mark.”
  - “If time is X, mark distance.”

The reflective pool:
- Be cautious:
  - Don’t **trust** reflections for geometry without checking.
  - Use reflections to *confirm* anomalies, not as your primary map.

### 3.3. Hydration and nutrition

---

#### 3.3.1. You will lose water constantly

- You’re in:
  - Very humid, very hot air.
  - Under exertion and likely mild stress.
- Respiratory and sweat losses are big, even if you’re not dripping visibly.

---

#### 3.3.2. Water intake strategy

- Plan:
  - **3.5–4.5 liters per day minimum baseline.**
  - More if you’re moving heavily or carrying gear.
- **Don’t drink from canal water or pools directly.**
  - It’s “milky, luminous,” and saturated with unknowns.
- Bring:
  - **Compact filtration system**:
    - Activated carbon + mechanical filter.
- Chemical **antifouling agents**:
  - To prevent mold growth in your bottles and systems.

---

#### 3.3.3. Food and electrolytes

Calories:
- You need:
  - More than you’d expect for the temperature range.
- Food:
  - **High calorific density**, low bulk.
  - **Individually water-resistant sealed packs**:
    - Bars.
    - Gels.
- **Electrolytes**:
  - Tablets or powders.
- Don’t:
  - Rely on local fauna or flora:
    - There’s **0.2/3 fauna** — none.
    - The plants are fungi-like and **unknown**.

---

### 3.4. Safety and rescue gear

This place:
- No living fauna.
- No functioning machinery.
- No humans to call rescue.

---

#### 3.4.1. Emergency signaling and marking

- **Glow sticks or small LED beacons**:
  - To mark:
    - Your entry path.
    - The edges of dangerous water.
- **Whistle or compact signaler**:
  - Even with no fauna, sound can be used for *self-calibration*:
    - Echoing chambers tell you about room size and openings.
- **Small knife or multitool**:
  - Not for fighting: for cutting straps, prying, scraping.

---

#### 3.4.2. Rope and safety line

- 30–50 meters of **thin static rope**.
- Use:
  - To anchor yourself when walking near deep edges.
  - To guide your way back:
    - Rope along a known route.
    - Markings every few meters.

---

#### 3.4.3. Float and buoyancy

- Compact **inflatable float/pack**:
  - If you fall into deep canal or basin.
- **Minimal** flotation; you don’t need to cross oceans, but you need to not sink under your weight and gear.

---

#### 3.4.4. First aid for the environment

- Antibiotics:
  - Covers for minor skin infections.
- **Antifungal medication**:
  - Topical and maybe oral.
- **Bandages and dressings**:
  - Adhesive bandages to **conform under wet conditions**.
- **Thermal emergency blanket**:
  - Not for warmth—for:
    - Temporary shelter.
    - Maintaining visibility.
    - Blocking water dripping on you while you rest.

---

#### 3.4.5. Medical and mental health

- Blood pressure monitoring:
  - Simple cuff or monitor.
    - Track **trends**, not just numbers.
- **Anti-anxiety meds** if:
  - You have claustrophobia or panic history.
- **Sleeping aids or induction protocols**:
  - No natural day/night cues.
  - You need to **force** sleep and rest.

## 4. Psychological and cognitive preparation

You’re entering a place that:

### 4.1. Build and lock in habits

Don’t:

---

### 4.2. Reality checkpoints

- Periodically:
  - Stop.
  - Measure distance:
    - Time to walk back to last checkpoint.
  - Verify:
    - It’s **similar** to what you expected.
    - If it’s **wildly different**, this is:
      - A warning flag.
      - Stop trying to optimize.
- Use:
  - **Drops of water**:
    - Watch their fall.
    - Is it normal?
- Use:
  - **Reflections**:
    - Compare what’s above water vs what’s below.
    - Mark anomalies instead of freaking out.

### 4.3. Sleep and rest management

Without:

You need:
- **Artificial strict schedule**:
  - “Weak” light:

### 4.4. Solitude and silence

You:

  - Drips.
  - The stone.

#### 4.4.1. Noise-cancellation and audio protocol

  - **Don’t block critical sound**:

#### 4.4.3. Sanity checks
