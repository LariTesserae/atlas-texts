You are not going to a city; you are going to live inside a single, living breathing instrument.

Kavsara will not feel “strange yet understandable.” It will feel like stepping inside someone else’s lung and being asked not to cough.

### Before you go: who you are and aren’t

Assume you are an ordinary baseline human from a reasonably Earth-like world:

- You need regular water, more or less every day.
- You can tolerate heat and dryness, but not indefinitely.
- Your ears and balance were designed for… quieter surroundings.
- You are not mystically attuned to anything.

You’re not a hero or a chosen one of Kavsara’s wind. You’re a visitor from outside the instrument. This matters.

### Big-picture: what Kavsara demands of you

1. **It’s a monument of sound and wind, not water and crops.**  
   You will not see “rivers,” “fountains,” or “fields.” You will see “air channels,” “sound mirrors,” and “wind-tools.”  
   You must learn to think of wind as a resource: like water elsewhere.

2. **It’s loud. Always.**  
   The Heart-Bell drone never stops (except briefly during Silence Hour). Compared to this city, industrial factory floors or busy airports are quiet libraries.  
   You must be ready to live in a metaphysical headphone that you can never take off.

3. **Water is scarce, and etiquette is sacred around it.**  
   You must not waste water, physically or *ritually*.  
   You must be prepared to drink non-water liquids and strange gels.

4. **Everything that is taken for granted in your world—privacy, silence, writing, storage—works differently here.**  
   There is no “paper,” no “keys,” no “doors.” Their equivalent of a law library is a hall of echoes.

### 1. Physical and medical preparation

#### 1.1. Hearing and protection

The **Heart-Bell** and its accompanying architecture are not just loud; they are *perpetually* loud. The noise floor will be comparable to standing near a turbine engine or a large jet engine running day and night.

You need:

- **High-quality, custom-fitted earplugs** worn almost constantly.  
  - Foam earplugs may be enough for a short stay, but for any longer trip you need musicians’ plugs or industrial noise filters with different filters (so you can still hear speech but cap the dangerous high-end).

- **Over-ear protection** for sleeping.  
  - Noise-cancelling is not enough by itself; you need passive isolation rated for high decibels, worn *over* the earplugs if you are sensitive. The ambient city will be above sane safe limits for continuous unprotected exposure.

If you stay for months unadapted:
- You will likely experience temporary hearing threshold shift (temporary hearing loss).
- Ringing in the ears (tinnitus) is almost guaranteed without protection.
- Your “outer” hearing (subtle frequencies of speech and music) will degrade; you will start to rely more on body-voice and bone conduction.

You should also:
- Learn **basic lip-reading and visual body language cues**. Conversations in open spaces will be half-visual, half-shouted.

#### 1.2. Hydration and body fluids

Kavsara does not drink rain; it drinks *air*. What you call “water crisis” they call “normal.”

Prepare:

- **Dehydration discipline**
  - Drink more than your thirst suggests; by the time you feel thirsty you’re already behind.
  - Set internal “rules” for yourself: never pass more than two waking hours without a deliberate sip of something liquid if you can.

- **Expect your urine color to shift to the darker end of acceptable**
  - Your goal is not “clear” like in humid cities; but you should keep it from becoming deep amber or brown. If it’s that dark, you’re underhydrated.
