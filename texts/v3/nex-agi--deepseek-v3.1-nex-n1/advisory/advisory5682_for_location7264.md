This isn’t a place you just “visit” like a city back home. You’re walking into what is basically a living organ made of rock, water, and cold. Think of it as: half city, half aquifer, half organism—and yes, that adds up to more than one whole because everything overlaps here.

Below is a preparation guide for an ordinary human who wants to survive *and* not harm this place.

---

## 1. Mindset: What You’re Actually Walking Into

### Emotional reality

- **It’s going to feel claustrophobic at first.**  
  No horizon. No sky. Just stone vaults overhead, dripping constantly. People used to open skies often panic down here.

- **The pace is not your pace.**  
  Everything is slow: water, moss, inhabitants. Movements are small and subtle. You’re the loud, fast anomaly.

- **You *will* get wet.**  
  There’s no real “dry” here. There is “drier” and “more wet.” Accepting that will help a lot.

### Cultural mindset

- This is a city where **water and rock are sacred**.  
  People probably think of them as beings: flows and veins of the world.

- They **have never seen metal**, or it’s alien-level rarity.  
  They might distrust it, fear it, or covet it. If you show up with a knife, you aren’t “normal traveler,” you’re something strange and powerful.

- **Waste is taboo.**  
  Everything flows somewhere. It’s like living inside a lung—you don’t spit toxic things into your lungs.

## 2. Physical Health: Preparing Your Body

### Climate conditioning

- **Temperature:** just above freezing, constantly.
- **Humidity:** near-saturation; always damp.
- **Air pressure:** might be slightly higher, depending on depth; air feels dense.

#### Before you go:

- **Cold acclimatization:**

  - Spend a few weeks doing light exercise in cold, damp conditions: chilly mornings, rain, etc.  
  - Cold showers; damp (but not soaked) clothes indoors briefly to get used to the feel of damp fabric.

- **Respiratory prep:**

  - If you have asthma or chronic bronchitis, this place is hard mode. Get checked medically.
  - Consider getting used to breathing masks in humid environments so you’re not psychologically bothered by having your face covered.

- **Feet & legs:**

  - You’ll be walking on uneven, slick stone constantly.  
  - Do exercises for ankles and knees; balance work on one foot, small steps, etc.

#### While you’re there:

- **Cold damp:**

  - You must avoid **continuous saturation** of your clothing.  
    Wet clothes + slight airflow + time = hypothermia even if the air isn’t “freezing”.
  
- **Change into dry layers when you stop** moving—even if they’re only slightly less damp.
  - Always have a wrapped dry set: hat, socks, inner layer shirt.

- **Expect mild chill even if you’re dressed well:**  
  - The constant coldness seeps; you never feel truly warm, just “less cold.”

### Dealing with moisture

- Skin **pruning** on your hands and feet is constant and harmless.
- **Fungal issues** are likely: between toes, under damp clothes.
  - Keep skin clean.
  - Whenever possible, expose affected skin to air, even if the air is cold and humid.

## 3. Clothing & Gear: What You Wear Down Here

### Fabric: the *only* acceptable choice is fiber

- **Everything must be natural fiber, ideally locally compatible:**  
  - Plant fiber textiles (like linen or hemp-like materials).
  - Woven textiles: *no* synthetic water-resistant fabrics.
  - If you must wear something from your world, choose undyed, tightly woven linen or cotton.

- Assume **metal** is:
  - Extremely conspicuous,
  - Possibly feared,
  - Possibly corrosive to the stone or water chemistry.

#### Layering system

1. **Base layer:**  
   - Thin, tightly woven linen/hemp-like fabric shirt and trousers.
   - Keeps moisture away from skin or at least lets it evaporate better.

2. **Insulating layer (if needed):**
   - Think quilted or thick padded **textile** garment:
     - Layers of cloth sewn with plant fiber stuffing.
   - Ideally, you want something that can still allow moisture through.

3. **Outer layer (shell):**
   - Short, hooded cloak or poncho made of tightly woven fabric.
   - Function:
     - Drip-catcher from ceiling water.
     - Barrier to damp walls when you brush past.
     - Not fully waterproof—because nothing is truly dry here—but helps shed most of the dripping, so you’re not constantly soaked.

#### Legs and feet

- **Legs:**  
  - Tight leggings / trousers that can be rolled up to mid calf for crossing channels, or walking in shallow water films.
- **Feet and sandals:**  
  - This world likely uses **stone-based footwear** or woven soles with stone-like plates.
    - If they can shape stone, they likely have hard, thin slabs of it shaped as soles.
- **You want:**  
  - Sole with good texture or pattern, for grip on slick stone.
    - Think rough stone undersurface or shallow grooves carved in.
  - Uppers of woven fabric or fiber cords that dry quickly.

- **Avoid:**  
  - Closed leather shoes or anything that stays internally wet for long.  
  - Metal buckles, rivets, or nails.

#### Hands and head

- **Head:**
  - Simple hood or woven cap.
    - Helps keep dripping water from running down your neck.
- **Gloves:**
  - Light, fingerless gloves for grip but still some protection.
  - Or no gloves at all—this is not a world of perfect dryness, so your hands will be wet.

#### The local “ecostyle”

Take **no** visible metal—no jewelry, no buckles, nothing obvious.

- Before you enter:
  - Temporarily swap any metal parts for stone, bone, or carved-hardwood equivalents.
  - If you cannot avoid some metal (like fasteners on your pack), **seal** and **hide** it internally:
    - Wrap in waxed cloth inside a container, so it doesn’t touch the water.

The locals might not know what metal is, but they will notice if something strange comes off of you.

## 4. Lighting: What Light You Bring Into the Dark

The ceiling is thin in places, but this is still **a dark underground world**.

### Local light source: bioluminescence

- The air itself glows weakly:
  - Blue‑white → pale green‑white → back blue‑white over time.
- The moss and crusts on walls and floors are what generate light.
- **They don’t like bright, sudden heat or light.**  
  Your normal torch would be like pointing a flame-thrower at living tissue.

### Your own light source

- **You need light that doesn’t surprise anyone.**

- **Stone lamps**:
  - You want small shallow stone dishes with oil from:
    - Moss or algae-based oil,
    - Something local they can recognize.
  - Wick of fiber or moss rope.
  - Flame is small, low, and used **sparingly**.

- **Covered lanterns**:
  - If possible from this world: stone container with small hole, low flame.
  - If you must use a lamp sealed from elsewhere:
    - Make sure it looks like **stone** or **textile** on the outside.
    - No metal showing.

- **What you must NOT do:**

  - No “flashlight” equivalents:
    - Too sudden.
    - Too bright.
    - Feels unnatural to both locals and ecosystem.
    - If you must rely on one for emergency:
      - Use deep red filters, lowest brightness setting.
      - Use *very* briefly and only when absolutely needed.

This isn’t just politeness. It’s safety:  
If you shock the moss or its creatures into panic or behavior change, you might:

- Temporarily steal the city’s visibility,
- Or draw unwanted attention from fauna that hunt by light.

## 5. Food and Water: Eating and Drinking in a Wet City

### Water sourcing

- **Everywhere is water—but not everywhere is safe.**

- **Rules to follow:**

  - You drink where:
    - It flows **clear**, thin film over stone.
    - You see locals drink.
    - Moss grows near but not directly in the water.

- **Avoid:**

  - Still pools or thick slicks where the film has accumulated debris, film, or scum.
    - Even if they seem clear, they can be stagnant with respect to flow.
  - Anywhere touching significant decomposition of moss or animal matter.

- Also **consider**:

  - This world’s “fresh” water might have different bacterial chemistry than you’re used to.
  - You might still want to:
    - Let water stand in a container and then watch carefully for any strange behavior.
    - Or use a small, **stone** filter or gravel filtration layer before you drink.

### Food and eating

- In such a damp, cold environment:
  - Preservation likely uses:
    - **Cold** itself,
    - **Drying in air currents**,
    - Limited fermentation.

- You likely eat:
  - Moss-based foods: processed, dried, kneaded into cakes or strips.
  - Small animals from the channels: salamanders-like things, maybe processed similarly.
  - Biofilms and algae harvested from safe surfaces.

- **Your own food:**

  - Bring or acquire:
    - Dense, nutrient-rich foods that can tolerate damp:
      - Dense grain cakes or hard cakes of cooked starch, sealed in waxed cloth.
    - Avoid things that go moldy quickly or are salty enough to contaminate the water.

- **Waste management:**

  - **Never dump raw food waste directly into channels or onto the stone floor.**
  - Carry:
    - A small waterproof container (maybe stone-lined, sealed with wax or resin).
    - Bring waste to:
      - Designated areas where locals are composting or feeding it to specific animals.
    - Ask first; where does “food that is finished” go?

## 6. Movement and Navigation: Getting Through the City

### The floor

- Stone is:
  - **Smooth** where polished,
  - **Rough** in corners,
  - **Constantly wet with a thin water film.**

- Always assume **any** surface can be slippery:
  - Keep your center of gravity low:
    - Bent knees, slightly lowered posture.
    - Small, deliberate steps.

- Watch your footing:
  - **Never** step blindly into dark water.
    - Even if water looks shallow film, it might be a thin surface over a deeper niche worn away by erosion.

### Stairs and ledges

- Stone steps:
  - Often:
    - Broad, shallow, but unevenly formed.
    - Damp.
    - Sometimes carved with invisible channels to help water flow but you can’t see them.

- **To move safely:**

  - Use hand-holds carved into walls if they exist.
  - Wear sole with good texture.
    - If you create local soles, ask to have grooves carved in them.
    - Think fish-scale pattern or shallow fluting.

- **Always assume:**

  - Every handhold or surface you touch is wet.
    - Test each carefully before committing weight.

### Architecture and layout

- Architecture is **vertical**:
  - Buildings stacked like layers.
  - Narrow corridors between walls.
  - Paths on both ground and sides of these structures.

- Concerns:

  - **Low ceiling**:
    - When you step from a chamber into a side tunnel, ceiling height may change suddenly.
    - Keep your posture slightly stooped.

  - **People-density:**

    - In dense areas:
      - The streets feel like single-person corridors.
      - Move slowly:
        - Let others pass you by stepping into small alcoves or widenings.
      - Always call or softly announce yourself at blind corners by using:
        - Gentle voice,
        - Or local equivalent signal (light tap pattern on stone wall, if that’s a thing).

### Nighttime movement

- When the bio-glows dim:

  - **Traffic will drop sharply.**

  - You might be:
    - Rude by moving around too much then,
    - Or unsafe, because climbing or stairs need more visibility.

- Plan:
  - Rest during the dim “night” unless you have a clear reason.
  - If you must move in low light:
    - Use small lamp, **covered**.
    - Keep your movements **slow** and predictable.

## 7. Social Interaction: How Not to Offend Anyone

These people live in a city that:

- Never had metal,
- Functions like a single large organism,
- Depends utterly on water and stone.

### Before you meet locals:

- **Learn a few key concepts linguistically:**
  - “water”
  - “stone”
  - “flow / channel”
  - “safety” / “safe to touch”
  - “please” / “thanks”
  - “this is wrong / I don’t understand”

- Study **bow** or greeting gesture:
  - Hands open with palms slightly downward, to show:
    - “I am not touching your water with unclean tools or threats.”

### Body language aboveground would be:

- **Slow**, deliberate movements.
- **Quiet** voice; always on the edge of whispering in dense areas.
- **Never** sudden reaching:
  - If you must reach for water, stone, or a person, show the palm, then approach slowly.

### Cultural touchstones:

- **Water flow is sacred**:
  - Don’t block channels intentionally.
    - If you accidentally block with your hand or stone, remove it quickly and let water flow.
    - If water chooses new routing around your block, consider it:
      - Either a mistake you need to fix,
      - Or something they need to fix.
    - Ask before making changes.

- **Stone is treated like body of the city:**

  - Don’t chalk your name on walls.
  - No aimless scratching, carving, or breaking.
  - Do not even chip off stone unless explicitly invited.

- **Offerings or trade goods:**

  - From your world:
    - Textiles:
      - Unique fabrics, patterns, or slightly unusual fibers.
    - Knowledge:
      - Of ways to handle water in other environments (maybe shared only carefully).
    - Tools that:
      - Are shaped from stone or bone,
      - Are designed for **careful** sculpting or water-logging studies,
      - But not destructive or disruptive to their eco-system.

## 8. Safety and Risks: Not Getting Yourself Killed

### Main risks

- **Slip and fall**
- **Getting lost**
- **Hypothermia**
- **Local fauna misunderstanding / minor bites**
- **Conflict with local law**

#### Reducing risk

##### Slipping

- Always:
  - Watch your step.
  - Wear shoes with textured soles.
  - Keep hands free to catch yourself or find walls.

- If you slip into water film:
  - It’s usually shallow, but:
    - You’re now wetter and colder.
    - Change or layer up quickly.

##### Getting lost

- **Use landmarks:**

  - Patterns in the moss:
    - The rings, arcs, and swirls.
    - Different chambers have different dominant patterns.

  - Flow patterns:
    - The way film channels branch or merge.

- **Never rely on:**

  - “Left/right” logic; you are likely on multiple stacked layers.
    - Think “up level / down level” and “direction relative to big chamber X.”

- **Ask for help early:**

  - If lost, don’t wander deeper.
    - Find a small alcove or widen area, sit calmly, and wait for a local.

#### Fauna and small creatures

- **Do not touch:**

  - Salamander-like animals:
    - Might treat you as aggressive if you reach suddenly.
    - Their skin is likely toxic, irritating, or culturally sensitive.

- **Do not disturb:**

  - Tiny slug-like centipede things:
    - Important part of ecosystem.
    - Stepping on them in large numbers is actually noticeable.

- If you are bitten or stung by anything:
  - Wash:
    - With flowing water film,
    - Not with stagnant water.
  - Apply:
    - Clean dry cloth; pressure if needed.
    - Seek:
    - Local herbalist or equivalent healer, who will likely know moss-based medicines.

#### Health and injuries

- **Deep cuts:**
  - In this damp, non-sterile world:
    - Any deep cut should be:
      - Cleaned thoroughly,
      - Kept dry / covered with clean fabric and changed often,
      - Observed for signs of infection.

- **Fevers:**

  - If you get a fever or respiratory illness:
    - Rest in a drier, warm-air current location if possible,
    - Ask for help rather than “traveling through” it.

## 9. The Things You Do Not Do

There are a few behaviors that will be either catastrophic or deeply insulting:

- **No metal into water channels**
  - No matter what:
    - You don’t wash metal objects,
    - You don’t drop them,
    - You don’t forge them inside this basin.

- **No blocking or intentionally damming channels**
  - Even as “fun experiment.”
    - This is tampering with the city’s bloodstream.

- **No fires** except:
  - Tiny, controlled, in natural stone lamp or lamp-like container.
    - Never open flame near moss-covered walls.

- **No loud noises**
  - Sound carries damply but still:
    - Shouting, singing, or heavy machine-like sounds would be jarring and potentially threatening.

- **No waste of food**
  - In a tightly balanced system, food is precious.
    - Eat what you take,
    - Take only what you will eat.

## 10. Practical Checklist Before You Enter the Basin City

### Packing

- **Clothing (all fiber/no visible metal):**
  - 2–3 sets base layer.
  - 1 insulating layer.
  - 1 outer cloak/poncho.
  - Head cover or hood.
  - Extra socks of fabric type.

- **Footwear:**
  - Stone-soled sandals or similar, textured:
    - One pair for use.
    - One spare pair.

- **Lighting:**
  - 1–2 small stone lamps + local oil source.
    - Or similar, covered low-light source.
  - Possible small hidden emergency light with deep filter or extremely low intensity.

- **Containers:**
  - Small stone or sealed-textile containers:
    - For:
    - Food,
    - Waste,
    - Drinking (water you can fill from known channels).

- **Basic medical:**
  - Clean strips of fabric:
    - Bandages,
    - Compresses,
    - Simple cloth slings.

- **Knowledge prep:**
  - Basic 10–15 words in local language:
    - Water, safe, no, danger, help, etc.
    - Basic moral rules:
      - Don’t block channels,
      - Don’t break stone,
      - Don’t show metal.

- **Optional:**
  - Small, unobtrusive stone tool:
    - E.g. stone scraper, for emergency self-care or to help clear minor blockages.
    - Only if you know how to use it without harm.

### Final actions before entering:

- **Metal storage:**

  - Any metal you cannot part with:
    - Wrap securely.
    - Place inside sealed container.
    - Store in:
      - A surface cache near the entrance,
      - Or leave with trusted overworld contact if that exists.

- **Mindset:**

  - Accept:
    - You will be damp,
    - You will be cold,
    - You will be slower,
    - And you will be a visitor in a place that does not exist to serve you.

### 11. When You’re Inside: Living Day by Day

Once you’re in the city, you:

- **Always** dry what you can when you stop:
  - Wring out cloth garments,
  - Hang them on stone hooks or over your pack if there’s airflow.

- **Always** watch:
  - Where you step,
  - What your hands touch,
  - What your water bottle touches.

- **Always** ask:
  - Before rearranging stone,
  - Before touching anything for the first time,
  - “Where is a safe place to sleep?”

If they offer you:

- A stone-carved sleeping niche:
  - Often: with a moss curtain,
    - Possibly a thin woven mattress layer of fiber or moss.

- **Use** it:
  - Don’t insist on sleeping in the open channel-floor,
    - It’s likely rude and unsafe.

---

### 12. Leaving the City: When You Go Back Up

- As you climb:
  - The air will become thinner, drier.
    - You might feel lightheaded,
    - Your skin might start to feel dry and tight.

- **Before returning to surface:**

  - Check:
    - Any stone or moss items you have collected:
      - Might be alive or sensitive.
    - Consider:
      - If you should leave them.

  - Inspect:
    - Any metal you stored:
      - For moisture or corrosion.
    - Clean and dry them properly before returning to your own world.

---

### Summary

- **Prepare for:**  
  Cold, damp, narrow spaces, no metal, no factory noise.

- **Bring:**  
  Fiber clothes, textured stone-soled shoes, small lamp, sealed containers, basic medical cloths, and a willingness to move slowly and ask permission.

- **Don’t:**  
  Block water channels, carve stone casually, show metal, or make loud sudden noise.

- **Do:**  
  Watch carefully. Learn the water’s patterns. Treat the city like a body you are visiting inside.

If you do that, you can not only survive the city that never heard of metal, but return with a deep, quiet understanding of what a civilization can become when it organizes itself around water and stone instead of iron and fire.
