Visiting this vault as a normal human is a bit like being invited into a sealed lung that someone buried under an industrial cathedral and then forgot to switch off. You’re not going to die immediately just by being here, but the place is designed around a different timescale and a different chemistry of life than you are. If you want to leave healthy, you need to prepare carefully.

Below is a detailed “how-to” for someone like you—flesh, blood, no supernatural powers—to get in and out of this place safely.

---

## 1. Understand the Core Risks

### 1.1. The environment is a mild biosphere lab

- **High humidity**: The air sweats almost as much as you will. Clothes stay wet, which means:
  - You’ll be thermally uncomfortable and slightly hypothermic if you stay long without proper gear.
  - Skin gets macerated (wrinkled and fragile) if it’s wet too long—which invites infection.

- **No sun, no wind, no air exchange**: This is a sealed vault under the world.  
  - No natural sunlight means no vitamin D synthesis, and no diurnal cues to your body clock.
  - Oxygen and carbon dioxide are determined by whatever processes and ventilation exist in this system, which may be minimal or rebalanced for the organisms living here, not for you.
  - Stale air + damp = mold risk in your gear if you’re not careful.

- **Silent, but not harmless**: There are no animals or obvious threats.  
  - The danger is less from monsters and more from **microbes and long-term, low-grade exposure**.

### 1.2. The water is not safe to drink or even touch unprotected

- It is a thin ecological layer on metal:
  - Biofilm (mat-like microbial communities).
  - Algae, moss, and lichen analogues.
  - Slow bioluminescence—likely microbial or algal in origin.

- The **bioluminescent green** itself:
  - Might just be pigmentation, but there’s a non-zero chance of:
    - **Algal toxins** or **bacterial/metabolic products** that irritate skin or mucous membranes.
    - Unusual metal chelators or metabolites that can concentrate metals from the floor.

- The water gathers:
  - On a metal floor with signs of corrosion and “veins of ink.”
  - This likely means **dissolved metals**, especially if any alloy contains iron or other heavy metals.

Conclusion: **Treat water and surfaces here as contaminated with both microbiota and trace metals.** Don’t drink it. Don’t let it into your eyes, mouth, or any cuts.

### 1.3. The metal floor is a tripping and corrosion hazard

- **Uneven plates**: Raised weld seams, sharp edges where floor meets rock.
  - Easy to stub a toe, catch a boot heel, or fall if you’re not watching carefully.
- **Smooth + wet = slippery**:
  - The thin layer of water on the slope down to the bowl and on the floor itself is a slip risk.

### 1.4. Time and perception are weird

- Very little changes to mark the passing of time.
- You can lose track of how long you’ve been here:
  - **Fatigue**, both **physical** (from mild humidity and heat) and **psychological** (from monotony and dim light), can creep up on you.
- The “altar-engine” in the center and the faint green light can give the place a ritual/experimental vibe:
  - This might affect you emotionally: you might feel like you’re intruding, or that you’re inside a machine still half-awake.

### 1.5. Potential for unknown catastrophe or shifts

- You don’t know:
  - How the vault’s hidden machinery works.
  - Whether the “altar” or drain system has cycles that suddenly change water level, pressure, or heat.
- Possibility:
  - A sudden pulse of water from some process cycle.
  - A change in temperature that pushes the humidity into fog or even condensation droplets.
  - A change in the bioluminescent system—maybe indicating a shift in pH, oxygen, or chemical activity.

## 2. Physical Preparation: Gear You Should Bring

### 2.1. Clothing and thermal protection

- **Lightweight waterproof overclothing**:
  - Full rain suit, including hood and pants with integrated boots or boot covers.
  - You want to stay dry from external moisture but avoid **overheating**.
  - Look for breathable waterproof fabric; if not available, use ventilation zips and open them periodically.

- **Underlayer moisture-wicking base**:
  - Synthetic moisture-wicking underwear and shirt.
  - Avoid cotton if possible: it stays wet and cold.

- **Gloves and protection for extremities**:
  - **Chemical-resistant gloves** over thinner fabric liners:
    - To protect hands from biofilm and water.
  - **High-traction, waterproof boots** with:
    - Steel or composite toe caps (toe-stubbing protection).
    - Deep, non-clog tread pattern for slick metal.

- **Eye protection**:
  - Shatterproof goggles or sealed glasses.
  - Protect against:
    - Dropping condensation from above.
    - Splashing water if you slip or touch the bowl.

- **Helmet or hard hat with headlamp**:
  - The ambient light is faint; you need your own reliable light source.
  - Head protection from:
    - Falling drops of mineral or metal-rich water.
    - Banging your head on low unseen pipes or cables near walls or ceiling.

### 2.2. Lighting and visibility tools

- **Primary headlamp**:
  - LED headlamp with adjustable brightness:
    - Use low brightness to preserve night vision.
    - High brightness for detailed inspection of the floor, flora, or altar.

- **Backup light**:
  - Another headlamp or small LED flashlight:
    - Secure with lanyard to avoid losing if dropped.

- **Chemlight sticks or small area markers**:
  - To mark your path, especially near the hatchway and around the bowl.
  - Glow-in-the-dark tape or similar if you want to leave “breadcrumbs” you can turn off/dim later.

### 2.3. Respiratory and health protection

- **At least an N95/P2 respirator mask**:
  - Better: a half-face respirator with **P100 filters**.
  - Why:
    - Reduce the risk of inhaling spores or microorganisms aerosolized by moisture.
    - If any of the vegetation releases spores or irritants, you want to filter them.

- **Basic hygiene kit**:
  - Small bottles of hand sanitizer with high alcohol percentage.
  - Wet wipes, preferably disinfectant.
  - Use after any contact with surfaces or water.

- **Simple first-aid kit** with:
  - Antiseptic wipes or solution.
  - Adhesive bandages and sterile dressings.
  - A small roll of medical tape.
  - If you’re prone to allergies, include antihistamine.

### 2.4. Navigation and marking

- The place is small but eerily uniform:
  - Same black rock, same metal floor, multiple similar chambers if you go through the hatchway.

- **Marking tools**:
  - Reflective tape, colored chalk, or plastic markers to:
    - Tag the hatchway you entered from.
    - Indicate direction to exit or “safe zone.”
    - Number chambers if you explore linearly along similar vaults.

- **Compass and/or inertial device if available** (but be aware):
  - Under the world there may be no magnetic north to reference.
  - Instead, rely on **measured walks** and **consistent marking**.
    - Simple example: chalk arrows pointing “back to exit” from each hatchway as you go deeper.

### 2.5. Sample and contact tooling

If you want to interact or collect samples (suggestions, not requirements):

- **Sealed sample tubes**:
  - Water samples.
  - Biofilm samples (from the altar edges or bowl floor).

- **Tools that can touch surfaces without you**:
  - Stainless steel or plastic probes, spatulas, or forceps.
  - **Do not use your hands** directly on the biofilm or water.

- **Small sealable bags or containers**:
  - For any solid samples you intend to analyze later in a safe environment.

## 3. Physiological and Psychological Preparation

### 3.1. Hydration and food before you go

- **Prehydrate**:
  - Drink clear water before you enter the vault.
  - Bring **all your drinking water** with you from outside:
    - No drinking from the vault’s water or licking surfaces.

- **Food**:
  - Eat a balanced, easily digestible meal before you go:
    - Comfortable but not heavy: you don’t want nausea in the humidity and heat.
- Bring **compact, high-energy snacks**:
  - Nuts, energy bars, or similar.
  - Keep in sealable containers to avoid cross contamination with vault water or dust.

### 3.2. Conditioning for the humidity

- Expect:
  - Sweat from heat and humidity.
  - Heavy-feeling air, like walking in fog after rain.

- If you have pre-existing conditions:
  - **Respiratory issues** (like asthma or COPD):
    - The humidity and possible spores or microorganisms can be irritating.
    - Have an inhaler or other medication ready and accessible.
  - **Heart issues**:
    - Heat stress + humidity adds strain—keep exertion reasonable and take breaks.

### 3.3. Managing time disorientation

- Before entering:
  - Set a **fixed exploration time**:
    - Example: 1–2 hours maximum per session.
  - Track it with a watch or timer.
    - This is not just “safety procedure”; it’s critical to keep from lingering longer than you should.
- **Routine breaks**:
  - If you’re staying for longer than an hour, exit briefly to:
    - Cool down.
    - Drink water.
    - Check if you’re getting tired, confused, or emotionally numb.

### 3.4. Emotional preparation

- The place can be:
  - Quiet in a way that amplifies your own thoughts.
  - The “altar-engine” and green glow may feel intentional or sacred-like.

- Recognize:
  - **You are an anomaly** here; the system’s “rules” (slow, quiet, damp) are not yours.
  - Some feelings of disquiet, displacement, or “intrusion” are normal and not necessarily supernatural.
  - If you begin to feel a strong urge to **do something strange** like touch the altar bowl with your bare skin or drink the water, stop and exit immediately:
    - That’s a sign you’re losing objective judgment, likely from fatigue, subtle oxygen changes, or sensory deprivation.

## 4. What You Should Do Once Inside

### 4.1. On entry: survey and secure the immediate area

- **Step away from the hatchway** but:
  - Mark it clearly with your marker system.
- **Turn on your lights** and scan the floor in a wide arc:
  - Identify the main hazards:
    - Slippery slopes down to the bowl.
    - Raised weld seams and edges.
    - Any particularly loose or shifting plates.
- **Move slowly**:
  - Take a moment to let your eyes adjust to the low light and your headlamp.

### 4.2. Walking the space: technique and route

- **Stick to the drier metal plate** where you can:
  - Avoid making contact with the thin film of water and moss if possible.
- **Test the grip of your boots**:
  - On dry metal, then on wetter metal near the bowl.
  - If you start to skid, reduce your stride and keep your center of gravity low.

- **Approach the central bowl and altar-engine with caution**:
  - Look before kneeling:
    - Are there visible conduits underneath?
    - Are any pipes or cables near the surface that you shouldn’t step on?
  - Walk **around the bowl** on its outer periphery if you want to inspect the altar from all sides.

### 4.3. Interacting with water and biofilm

- **General rule: Don’t touch if you can avoid it.**

- If you must touch or sample:
  - Use gloves and tools.
  - Avoid:
    - Splashing water into your face.
    - Touching your face, mask, or goggles with contaminated gloves or tools.

- **Observing the bioluminescence**:
  - Look, but be cautious:
    - The color, intensity, and distribution might tell you something, but treat it as a signal you might not understand.
  - If you notice changes (like a sudden brightening or pattern shift):
    - Back away from the bowl.
    - Note it and consider whether it’s time to leave.

### 4.4. Dealing with the “altar” element

- The circular depression in the center collects a thin layer of water:
  - This may be:
    - A sensor surface,
    - A heat-exchange surface,
    - A chemical reactor surface,
    - Or something you can’t guess.

- **Do not assume it is safe to place your body or anything organic into that circle.**
- If you’re testing anything:
  - Use an inert tool like a metal rod or sealed plastic probe.
  - Watch for any immediate reaction:
    - Bubbles, color change, or sudden change in temperature in the water.

### 4.5. Navigating the hatchway and beyond

- The hatchway behind the altar leads into a similar faint green glow:
  - Decide if you want to explore it before entering:
    - It is **not** required to see the vault; you can experience most of it from a single chamber.

- If you proceed:
  - **Always keep one hand on the knowledge of how to return.**
  - Mark each new hatchway:
    - “Outside 1 ←” or “Exit 1 ←” on the wall by your entry point.
  - Don’t go deeper than you can safely backtrack in your fatigued, uncomfortable, slightly damp state.

## 5. Hazard Scenarios and How to Respond

### 5.1. You slip and fall into the bowl or water

- **Get out of the water as quickly as possible** but without rushing so hard you slip again.
- Do:
  - Use your hands and knees to stabilize if necessary.
  - Once out of the water:
    - **Immediately**:
      - Remove contaminated outer layer if you can.
      - Clean affected skin with water from your own supply and then disinfectant or sanitizer.
      - Inspect for cuts. If any cut contacted water:
        - Clean, disinfectant, and bandage.
        - Monitor for redness, swelling, or unusual pain later.

### 5.2. You feel unwell or disoriented inside the vault

- Possible symptoms:
  - Headache.
  - Dizziness.
  - Confusion.
  - Shortness of breath.
  - Unexplained emotional shifts or irrational impulses.

- Immediate actions:
  - **Stop moving and sit down** if you can do so safely.
  - **Exit** the vault as soon as you can walk safely.
  - Breathe slowly and steadily:
    - If you have a mask, check it’s properly positioned.
  - Once outside:
    - Hydrate with your own clean water.
    - Rest and recover.
    - Seek medical evaluation if symptoms persist or worsen.

### 5.3. Sudden increase in moisture or water flow

- If you notice:
  - More dripping from the ceiling.
  - The thin sheet of water getting noticeably deeper or starting to flow over wider areas of the floor.
  - A new stream of water coming from somewhere.

- Respond:
  - **Move to the highest, driest section of the floor**:
    - Often the edges near the wall where the floor plates meet the rock.
  - Avoid:
    - Standing directly under the ceiling where drops are heavier.
    - Staying near the center bowl if the water level is rising.
  - Exit **sooner rather than later**:
    - Even if it’s not dangerous flooding now, it’s better not to be trapped in a suddenly more “awake” industrial machine.

### 5.4. Unexpected change in the bioluminescence or other “environmental behavior”

- If you observe:
  - The green glow pulsing, changing color, or spreading rapidly.
  - The temperature noticeably changing (either warmer or cooler).
  - Any sound that you **didn’t** hear before—like a distant hum from machinery below.

- Treat it as a **possible shift in the system’s “phase”**:
  - You’re not required to understand what’s happening.
  - **Back out towards the hatchway or exit at a moderate pace.**
  - Observe from a distance if you’re curious, but prioritize:
    - Your ability to leave safely.

## 6. Post-Visit: Care Once You’ve Left the Vault

### 6.1. Decontamination and gear care

- Once outside:
  - Remove **outer waterproof layers** carefully:
    - Avoid touching the contaminated exterior with your bare skin.
    - If possible, store them in sealed bags until you can clean them properly.
  - **Clean and disinfect**:
    - Boots, tools, gloves.
    - Any exposed surfaces of your equipment that might have touched vault water, biofilm, or surfaces.

- **Disposal of brushes, sponges, rags used for cleaning**:
  - Treat them as contaminated and dispose of them safely.

- **Yourself**:
  - Wash thoroughly with clean soap and water:
    - Pay attention to hands, nails, and face.
    - Any cuts or scrapes: clean, disinfect, and bandage.

### 6.2. Health monitoring in the hours and days afterward

- Monitor for:
  - Unusual fatigue or fever.
  - Rashes or skin irritation at points of contact.
  - Respiratory symptoms like prolonged cough, shortness of breath, or chest tightness.

- If you develop **new symptoms**:
  - Explain your exposure to:
    - High humidity.
    - Unknown microbiota and biofilm.
    - Possible metal-rich water.
    - Possibly altered oxygen or air composition.
  - Get evaluated medically; share as much detail as you can.

## 7. A Short, “Minimalist” Checklist for a Quick Visit

If your time is short and you just want to visit safely without overengineering it:

1. **Basic protective gear**:
   - Waterproof jacket and pants.
   - Waterproof boots with good tread.
   - Gloves, mask, and eye protection.
   - Helmet with headlamp and backup light.

2. **Bring your own water and a small snack**, sealed.

3. **Set a hard time limit**:
   - Example: 1 hour max.

4. **Enter, mark your exit clearly, and keep your eyes on the floor**:
   - Watch for edges, seams, and where the metal floor slopes to the bowl.

5. **Don’t touch the water or the altar depression** with bare skin or drink anything.

6. **At the first sign of feeling unwell or unusual environmental change**:
   - Leave calmly and return another day.

---

In summary: visiting this vault is like walking into a sealed, industrial lung that breathes water and grows quiet, green life in the dark. You are not its intended inhabitant. The key to safety is not avoiding any single “monster” but respecting **long, slow cycles of chemistry and biology** you are not designed to share. With careful preparation, limited exposure time, and proper hygiene, you can experience this place without becoming part of its ecosystem—returning to your world with only memories and, perhaps, a sense of having been inside a living machine beneath the skin of the world.
