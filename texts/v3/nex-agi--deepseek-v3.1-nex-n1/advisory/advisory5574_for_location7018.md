To walk into that tree-city basin is not like walking into a jungle or a forest park on Earth. It’s closer to entering a very cold, very damp, three‑dimensional cathedral made of living wood, where the ground is a mattress of organisms, and the sky is a lid.

You’re going to need to treat this place as if it were half deep cave, half high‑altitude cloud forest, half archaeological dig inside a body.

---

## 1. Core Mindset Before You Go

### 1.1 Respect the “vegetable city”

This is not an empty ruin you can just tramp through. It is a functioning, slow, living system.

- The city is clearly engineered by something or someone over long time spans.
- There are no visible “people,” but that does not mean the place is uninhabited or unowned.
- Your behavior will be closer to a visitor in a monastery or library than a tourist in a forest.

Assume you are being observed by the system itself, whether you perceive eyes or not.

---

## 2. Physical Health and Safety

### 2.1 Temperature and body core

You described:

> constant temperature just above freezing… so that every breath feels like drinking clear glass

For you, that means:

- **Hypothermia is your main killer if you stay long.**  
  Even if you don’t “feel” freezing, the body loses heat steadily in near‑freezing damp air.
- **Expect fatigue and numb extremities.**  
  You can get clumsy without noticing: slowed reflexes, stumbling on roots and stairs.

**Prepare by:**

- **Layered clothing system:**
  - **Base layer:** synthetic or merino wool, moisture‑wicking.
  - **Mid layer:** insulating fleece or thin down/Primaloft.
  - **Outer shell:** fully waterproof but breathable jacket and pants (with sealed seams).
- **Avoid cotton:** Cotton kills in wet cold environments.
- **Extra insulation:**  
  A thin insulated vest or “puffy” layer is crucial if you are sitting still or observing.
- **Head and hands:**  
  Warm hat, gloves, and a thin over-mitt shell layer to keep gloves from getting wet.

### 2.2 Foot care and stability

The ground is not dirt:

> a dense matrix of mosses, lichens, and fungal weaves… a yielding lattice of fibers… faintly warm… shifts slightly under you as you walk

**Effects:**

- You will sink a little with each step.
- Surface may be uneven, hidden holes under moss mats.
- It is constantly wet and slippery.

**Gear:**

- **Waterproof boots with high ankles:**  
  For ankle support and to keep feet out of damp substrate as much as possible.
- **Aggressive tread:**  
  You’re dealing with wet roots and slippery wood.
- **Gaiters:** to keep debris and water out of your boots.
- **Spare socks** in waterproof bags inside your pack.  
  Change if feet get damp. Even small moisture can lead to blisters and maceration.

You’re going to be moving slowly over strange ground; think “controlled, careful” steps.

## 3. Light and Vision: You’re in a Low‑Light World

### 3.1 Environment

> sky visible only as a thin, broken net of gray light…  
> patterns of light on the bark… faintly luminescent… long vertical lines of greenish-white

**Challenges:**

- Very low ambient light.
- **No sharp sun direction or strong shadows** to orient yourself.
- **Eyes may adjust** but you will still need help to see details, especially under platforms and in root‑cellars.

**What to bring:**

- **Reliable headlamp**, with a red mode (to preserve your night vision and less disturbing to the environment).
- **Extra batteries and backup light.**
- **Consider a small handheld lantern:** soft, diffused light if you need to camp at ground level.
- **Glow sticks or chem-lights:** as gentle markers you can leave without feeling like you are blasting the world with artificial sun.

You want to see, but you want to avoid turning the cathedral into a stadium.

## 4. Visibility and Navigation: “Not Getting Lost in the Meat of the City”

The scale is small but deceptive:

> From one side of the plateau to the other, only a few minutes’ walk—if straight line and no obstructions… such direct walk is impossible.

You will be navigating a three‑dimensional labyrinth:

- **Ground floor:** dense mosses, root vaults, trunks.
- **Middle zone:** woven platforms and chambers between crowns.
- **Canopy:** high, leaf‑veiled ceiling.

**Navigation strategy:**

- **Always know where the edge is:**  
  The edge of the plateau is your basic reference. If you get disoriented, find the edge, then re‑enter or set a new goal.
- **Mark your way lightly but reliably:**  
  Use non-invasive methods:
  - **Very thin colored tape or biodegradable thread** tied high where not noticeable to casual ground observation.
  - **Small chalk or colored pencil marks** in hidden spots on one side of trunks only.
- **Avoid spray paint or plastic tags**—this place is delicate and sacred feeling.
- **Use a compass and/or GPS device**, but **expect distortions**:
  - Localized anomalies in metal or tree mass might deflect the compass needle.
- **Sketch maps:**  
  Simple paper map with “time and direction” notes:
  - E.g., “Trunk with double spiral stair → 15 min → large platform over ravine → 10 min → edge cliff.”

**Assume you can get lost in the interstices.** You’re not going to die in a vast desert, but you can get trapped in a wet maze you can’t easily backtrack out of.

## 5. Moisture, Water, and “Drinking the World”

### 5.1 Water in the environment

Everything is wet:

> constant drip of water…  
> air condensates on leaves… flows along bark into grooves of staircases…  
> ground buoyed on older trees transformed into substrate… cool dampness through soles of feet.

**Challenges:**

- Constant risk of wet clothes and gear.
- Possible need to drink water from surfaces or small channels.
- Everything you touch is moist and likely to be colonized by microorganisms.

**What you do:**

- **Treat everything as “surface water”** not “safe tap water.”
- **Water filter + purification tablets or drops:**  
  Even if the city looks pristine, there are fungi and unknown bacteria.
- **Small collapsible container** to gather water along trunk channels rather than licking the bark.
- **Keep gear dry**:
  - Pack contents in zip-lock bags or dry sacks inside your pack.
  - Include a pack cover or large trash bag as additional layer.

## 6. Respiratory and Biological Protection

### 6.1 Fungus, spores, and “plant city”

You are walking on a fungal mattress and lichen‑rich floor:

> dense matrix of mosses, lichens, and fungal weaves  
> the faint luminescence of fungi that has not yet spread to kill the host tree  
> your fingers come away shining and then fade

**Risks:**

- High spore count in the air.
- Possible contact with allergens, unknown fungi.
- If you have asthma or mold sensitivities, this place can trigger you.

**Preparedness:**

- **Basic face mask** or a filter mask in your pack, especially if you have to clear mold or disturb the substrate.
- **If highly sensitive**, consider antihistamines or pre‑medication with your doctor.
- **After visiting:**  
  Wash clothes thoroughly, dry carefully outside your home if possible to avoid contaminating indoor spaces with spores.

## 7. Climbing and Moving Through the Vertical City

### 7.1 Tree stairs and platforms

> spiral of narrow woody steps… grown into trunk surface… exactly where you’d expect treads… water channels along the steps

**Safety reality:**

- Steps are living wood, likely smooth and wet from water channels.
- They may be worn but uneven.
- Some platforms are high up; fall from one level might be serious.

**Guidance:**

- **Assume every stair and surface is wet and slick.**
- **Use hands-on climbing:**  
  Treat them like rock formations or ladders with no handrail.
- **Test each step gently:**  
  Apply a bit of weight before committing fully.
- **If you are unsteady with heights or balance, limit high climbing:**  
  There is enough to see on and near the ground floor.
- **Consider bringing thin climbing gloves** for grip without damaging bark.

**Never assume:**

- That the platforms are designed for your body weight.
- That the branches are as strong as they look.
- That you can easily get down the same way you came up.

## 8. Food and Energy Needs

### 8.2 Keeping your body fueled

The cold, low light, and damp will quietly drain your energy:

- **High calorie snacks:**  
  Nuts, trail mix, chocolate, energy bars, jerky or protein equivalent.
- **Simple hot meals:**  
  Bring a compact stove and small fuel supply if you are allowed to use heat.  
  **But be extremely cautious about fire and hot surfaces:**

### 8.3 Fire and heat in the vegetable city

The environment has:

> no obvious signs of agriculture or fire, except faint soot traces on inner bark

**Conclusion:**

- Fire is not part of the normal pattern of this world.
- You risk being seen as destructive or alien if you introduce fire.

**Recommendation:**

- **Avoid fire if possible.**  
  Use self-heating meals or just eat foods that don’t require cooking.
- **If you must use heat**, use it in small, highly-controlled bursts:
  - In a clear spot on stone if you can find one at the edge.
  - **Never** on the living substrate or in close proximity to dry branches or leaves.
  - **Keep a fire extinguisher or water container at hand.**
  - **No cigarettes, lighters, or sparky devices** casually used.

## 9. Dealing with the Animal “Shadowlife”

### 9.1 What you might see

> animals are rare and half-secret… occasional shadow slipping between root vaults… single swift dark shape in high canopy…

**Expectation:**

- You likely will not see many animals.
- The ones you see may be:
  - Observers, not aggressors.
  - Adapted to this world’s very slow pace of life.

**What to do:**

- **Do not chase them.**  
  You are the guest; they are the hidden inhabitants.
- **Do not feed them.**  
  There is no reason to start a dependency or behavioral change.
- **If one seems injured or trapped**, use the same caution you would in any unknown world:  
  Watch from a distance first; don’t assume you must interfere.

## 10. Environmental Ethics and Non‑Interference

### 10.1 “Leave everything as you found it” is too weak here

You’re inside what appears to be a designed, living system:

- **No soil, only plant body:**  
  So you cannot assume “there is dirt to hide things under.”
- **Everything is interconnected:**  
  The substrate, the trunks, the water channels, the luminescence patterns are all working parts of a machine.

**Principles of a visit:**

- **Take no biological samples unless you have explicit permission to do so:**
  - No cutting of bark, roots, or leaves.
  - No collecting fungi or mosses unless it is part of a mission you are on.
- **Do not break patterns to “clear a better view.”**
- **Do not leave synthetic objects lying:**  
  If you bring tape or thread, mark, but remove as much as you can on exit.
- **If you must relieve yourself:**  
  - Go away from main trunks and water channels.
  - Use a small trowel and shallow burial of solids very well away from visible structures.
  - Pack out toilet paper or used wet wipes in sealed bags.

You might want to take photographs or notes; but avoid imposing loud music or drone noise. This world doesn’t need noise to show its wonder.

## 11. Noise and the Sound of Water

### 11.1 Listening as the native language

> water not random… but forming rhythms of staccato notes:  
> pa-tap… tap… pa-pa-tap… as if the world is trying to speak.

**Implication:**

- The sound environment is already structured.
- Introducing noise (shouting, loud devices) feels like vandalism.

**Behavior:**

- Talk **quietly**, if at all.
- Listen to the water patterns before speaking:
  - You might discover a way of being quiet that fits better.
- Use **hand signals** with companions if you need to communicate over short distance.

## 12. Mental and Emotional Preparation

### 12.1 The quietness of the world

There is a strong psychological atmosphere here:

- **Constant drip of water,** low light, and gentle flickering luminescence.
- **No animals, no visible mechanical life,** but you know the place is populated.
- **You feel the borders of the world:**  
  The edge of the plateau is a literal edge of existence, dropping into cloud and unknown lower lands.

**Possible reactions:**

- **Initial awe:** cathedral-like feeling, numinous.
- **Later claustrophobia or unease:**  
  The world is dense, not open; you move inside a body of plant.
- **A sense of being watched:**  
  From the living system, not necessarily eyes, but awareness of your presence.

**Coping strategies:**

- **Keep your stay time-limited.**  
  A few hours to a day or two are enough to experience deeply.
- **Have a clear mission and routine:**  
  e.g., “Today I observe ground layer; tomorrow I move carefully to one platform.”
- **Stay in contact with your home world:**  
  Pre‑arrange signals or check‑ins if you are traveling with others.
- **If you feel creeping panic or derealization**, go to the edge and look out at the cloud‑line.  
  Remembering that there is a “beyond” helps.

## 13. Practical Checklist Summary for a Visit

### On You:

- Warm layered clothes (no cotton)
- Waterproof jacket and pants
- Warm hat and gloves
- Sturdy waterproof boots with good tread
- Gaiters and spare socks

### In Your Pack:

- **Light & navigation:**  
  - Headlamp + extra batteries and backup light  
  - Compass, map, notebook/pen, colored pencil  
  - Colored thread or tape for marking (and to collect later)
- **Water and food:**  
  - Filter + purification tablets  
  - Small collapsible container  
  - High-calorie snacks  
  - Optional: self-heating meals or no-cook food
- **Safety and health:**  
  - Basic first aid kit  
  - Mask/respirator if you have mold allergies  
  - Small trowel and waste bags  
  - Warm emergency blanket or bivvy
- **Tools – minimal and gentle:**  
  - Soft cloth or sponge for sampling interactions without damage  
  - Notepad and waterproof paper  
  - Optional: thin gloves for climbing

**Leave at home:**

- Fireworks, smoke machines, noisemakers.
- Heavy tools, chainsaws, axes, or metal-cutting gear.
- Permanent dyes, paints, or permanent markers.
- Any kind of device that emits strong noise, light, or heat into the environment without control.

## 14. Final Attitude

You are stepping into a world that has:

- Arranged itself into a tree city.
- Learned to keep water, light, and cold in balance.
- Appears to be designed for *someone* else, but allows you passage.

Your best posture is:

**Be quiet, be respectful, be warm, be dry, and listen.**

The city itself will teach you how to move through its body—with the drip of water, the soft breathing of the ground beneath your feet, and the faint luminescence on the trunks as your guides.
