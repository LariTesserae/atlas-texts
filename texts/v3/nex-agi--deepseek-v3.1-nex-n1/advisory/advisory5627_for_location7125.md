**Guidelines for the Multiverse Traveler Preparing to Visit the Garden-That-No-One-Planted in Qesh**

You are going to a place that looks like a city but actually behaves like a body—a body of vine, resin, wax, honey, and stone, perched on the edge of nothing and built around a vertical dark mouth in the heart of the mesa. You will be a foreign cell in that body. What you do not do can kill you as easily as what you do.

Below is how to prepare yourself and your gear, and how to behave when you arrive. If you follow this carefully, you can walk safely in the heart of Qesh and leave without damaging it or yourself.

---

## 1. Understand What You’re Walking Into

### 1.1 It’s not a “garden” in the way you think

“A garden that no one planted” is a true description but a misleading phrase if you’re picturing flowerbeds and trellises.

The reality at the center of Qesh:

- It is a vertical chamber, a **broken-open shaft** in the mesa’s glass-basalt heart.
- The **walls of the shaft** are like living curtains: vines, wax, resin, fibrous layers, inhabited by insects and fungal networks.
- The “garden” is the **inner environment of the shaft itself**—a garden of **air currents, heat, light, dust, and living beings** rather than soil and water.

You don’t “walk into” it like a garden path; you **approach its edge**, and its “life” rises out of it and flows into you.

### 1.2 There is no water

This is the single most important thing your body must prepare for.

Qesh:

- Has no rivers, springs, wells, aquifers, or clouds.
- Has no rain that reaches the ground.
- Lives **entirely without liquid water** as a primary medium.

Everything you see that looks like wetness—glossy sheens on leaves, thick pools on stone, translucent droplets—is **resin, wax, nectar, or honey**. Your body *cannot* drink it.

You will see:

- Golden resin pools on paths and terraces.
- Olive-green wax coatings on thresholds and corners.
- Thick, honey-like fluids in channels and hollows.

Your instincts will scream “water!”. **Ignore that instinct.** If you ingest significant resin or honey, you may get painfully sick, or go into shock.

### 1.3 Life is organized differently here

Expect:

- **Plants as structural infrastructure**: Vines are walls, floors, roofs, and corridors.
- **Insects as architecture, light, and ritual**: Many “lamps,” “tools,” and even “signs” are living beings that you’d classify as insects in your own world.
- **Mycelium as reinforcement**: Fungal networks knit the stone, vine, and wax together.
- **Time is slower:** People move and speak slowly; the city is “quiet” but not silent.
- **Sense of “wetness” without water:** The air can feel heavy and lush from leaf, honey, and dust, even though it is utterly dry.

### 1.4 “No one planted” does not mean “no one tends”

You may hear:

> “a garden that no one planted”

Interpret that as:

- **No humans invented the original design.**
- But **humans and non-humans together tend it intensely.**

The “garden” of the shaft has *itself* as a gardener as well—the self-organization of vines, insects, fungi, and heat currents. Do not assume “no one owns” means “no rules.” The rules here are just more subtle.

## 2. Physical and Health Preparation: Before You Go

### 2.1 Medical precheck

Before you step through any portal to Qesh:

- Have a **thorough health check**:
  - Heart and lungs (especially due to low air pressure).
  - Skin allergies (especially to bees, resins, waxes).
- If you have asthma, eczema, or any strong allergic reactions to insect stings or resins, **you must take seriously the risk of going to Qesh.** At a minimum, you will need:
  - **Epinephrine auto-injector**.
  - **Strong antihistamines**.
  - Detailed **emergency treatment plan** from a physician who understands interdimensional travel, if you can get one.

### 2.2 Acclimate to “dryness without drought”

Because you’ll be drinking nothing but water you carry yourself, in a dry environment:

- Hydrate well in the days before arrival:
  - Increase water intake moderately.
  - Minimize alcohol and caffeine; they will dehydrate you.
- Learn your **“dry-throat” distress signals**:
  - Scratchy throat, thicker saliva, slightly darker urine, mild headache.
- If you normally live in humid zones, spend time in low-humidity environments beforehand if you can.

### 2.3 Psych and sensory training (optional but strongly recommended)

You’ll face:

- **Extended silence in the city**, but also subtle “ringing of living things.”
- **Very slow pace of life**.
- **Constant small insect motion around you**, sometimes touching you.
- **Sensory disorientation from lack of water sound.**

Helpful:

- Practice meditation in environments with subtle, continuous noise (like insects at night) so you can handle persistent high-frequency background noise.
- Try exposure therapy for “creepy-crawly” feelings if insects make you nervous: look at images or videos of insects, practice not flinching when a harmless beetle walks across your skin.
- If you’re easily disturbed by silence or solitude, consider bringing a grounding object from your world, but keep it hidden and private (see section on objects and jewelry).

### 2.4 Basic fitness

You’re going to a city on a high mesa:

- Expect **thin air**, though not necessarily breath-holding altitude.
- Expect **stairs, terraces, and uneven “living floors”** formed by vine trunk clusters.
- Practice:
  - Moderate uphill walking.
  - Steps or stairs with a loaded pack.
  - Balancing on uneven surfaces.

## 3. What You Must Bring

### 3.1 Water: everything

All potable water you will need **must come with you**.

Plan:

- **At least 4 liters of safe water per person per day**, if you can fit the weight.
- If your portal tech or your body’s physiology allows “calorie dense” hydration solutions:
  - Use oral rehydration salts dissolved in water to help maintain electrolytes.
- **Two containers:**
  1. **Primary closed vessel:** Insulated flask or bottle that doesn’t leak easily.
  2. **Collapsible backup:** Lightweight, when empty takes minimal space.
- **Water filter or purification tablets:** Not because there is water to purify, but in case you need to reuse any water that you spill or collect in condensation procedures.

### 3.2 “Dry” personal hygiene

Do **not** bring wet soaps or shampoos that leave soapy water runoff. This can kill or sicken local organisms if you use it openly on the city’s surfaces.

Instead:

- **Solid alcohols or hand sanitizer** for hand hygiene.
- **Dry biodegradable cloths** pre-wetted with gentle antibacterial solution if you need more cleaning than hands sanitizer can give.
- **Dry toothbrush** and “no-rinse” toothpaste or tabs.
- **Baby powder** or cornstarch-based “dry shower” powder to help with skin comfort in constant heat.
- **Re-sealable bags for waste**: you will take all waste, including used tissues and wet wipes, out with you.

### 3.3 Clothing and protective gear

You need light, breathable, **protective clothing**.

- **Light layers:**
  - Long-sleeve thin shirt and trousers (tightly woven fabric).
  - Second layer for cooler evenings, or for when you’re stationary on the high edge facing wind.
- **Cover head and neck**:
  - Light, wide-brimmed hat for sun.
  - Optional thin scarf or veil for dusty periods.
- **Footwear**:
  - Sturdy but flexible **closed shoes** with good grip:
    - The pavings are resin-coated, sometimes uneven.
    - Vine-bark floors can be slippery in smooth wax.
  - Boots are better than sandals; avoid exposed toes.
- **Thin gloves**:
  - For touching unfamiliar surfaces or edges.
  - Not necessary all the time, but useful when interacting with “grubs” or handlers if you’re invited to.
- **Goggles or wrap-around glasses**:
  - Dust falls.
  - Pollen, spores, and tiny insect scales are always in the air.
  - They help protect you from both “dust storms” and accidental contact with resin droplets.

### 3.4 Mask and respiration protection

The air is full of:

- Enzymatic dust.
- Spores from mycelium and fungi.
- Pollen and nectar particles.
- Tiny insect hairs.

To protect yourself:

- **Bring a lightweight particulate mask** or soft mask that can stop fine dust.
- Use it when:
  - Dust is visibly falling from the sky or upper vaults.
  - You’re in close proximity to particularly “active” fungal patches or blooming sites.
  - A local suggests it to you.

Avoid anything full-face gas-mask style unless medically required; it’s too intimidating here.

### 3.5 Skin protection and first aid

Because:

- Sunlight is strong at high altitude.
- Resin and honey are very sticky and can be skin irritants.
- Dust can be an irritant.

Bring:

- **High-SPF, broad-spectrum sunscreen.**
- **Lip balm with SPF.**
- **Barrier cream:** Used to help protect your skin against sticky substances like resin and honey.
- **Adhesive bandages** and:
  - Sterile gauze pads.
  - A small elastic bandage.
  - Medical tape.
  - Tweezers (for removing splinters of resin or vine-thorn).
- **Antibacterial ointment** that remains safe in dry heat.

### 3.6 Allergy and emergency kit

Non-negotiable if you have any allergies or history of reactions:

- **Epinephrine auto-injector.**
- Strong antihistamine tablets.
- Topical steroid cream for mild skin reactions.
- **Pain reliever** for headaches.

Also include:

- **Electrolyte powder packets** for your water.
- **Antinausea medication** if you are prone to motion sickness; the heat, strange smell blends, and constant insect motion can trigger it.

### 3.7 Tools and gadgets—with caution

You can bring:

- A **compact, multi-tool**, but keep it in a pouch on your belt rather than openly displayed.
- **Small notebook and pencil** (no ink that might leak or wet surfaces).
- **Camera**, if it:
  - Does **not** flash in visible light.
  - Can be silenced or has very quiet “sound” operation.
- **Portable spectrometer or environmental scanner** (if you have access to such tech), but:
  - Do not use any scanning beam that might be interpreted as hostile—especially if it touches “Tin” or the shaft edges.
  - Ask local keepers before “scanning” anything in the garden heart.

Avoid:

- Large **electronic gadgets with loud beeps or chimes**.
- **Anything metallic that gleams too strongly** unless you can cover it.
- **Anything that “drinks” or “requires water” to operate**, if it can leak.

### 3.8 “Comfort” objects from home

If you must bring a token of home for psychological grounding:

- Choose small:
  - Smooth stone, a coin, a small piece of fabric, etc.
- Keep it hidden in a pocket or pouch.
- **Do not place it anywhere on the ground, in vine hollows, or near the shaft edge.**  
  The local “substrate” can be chemically sensitive.

## 4. Mental and Social Preparation: How to Move Through Qesh

### 4.1 Slow down

The city is quiet, people move slowly, and sound is absorbed by thick walls and vegetation.

Adopt a **slow-paced walking rhythm** from the moment you arrive:

- Short steps.
- Soft footfalls.
- If you’re naturally a fast walker, practice moving slower before you arrive.
- Avoid:
  - Running.
  - Scurrying.
  - Sudden, large gestures.

You will feel “out of sync” at first, but your body will adjust within a day or two.

### 4.2 Learn to read “quiet”

Qesh is not “silent”; it is **quiet and loud in different frequency bands** than you are used to. You may hear:

- A **thin, high continuous ringing** from pollinators hovering at the edge of hearing.
- **Clicking of resin falling**.
- **Soft puff of vines breathing or releasing dust/spores**.
- **Whisper** of air moving across wax walls.
- **Dull taps** of fruit falling or insects hitting a wax curtain.

If you are accustomed to constant low-frequency noise (traffic, fans, engines), you may initially interpret this as “total silence” and feel panic. **This is normal.** Give yourself time to adjust.

### 4.3 “Becoming aware of water”

The local people will have subtle body-language cues related to the absence of water.

Expect:

- **People never touch water with their hands**, except for what you might create yourself.
- They **do not wash in public view** with water.
- **No splashing, no dripping, no pouring of clear fluids.**

You will feel your own body “shouting” water—through sweat droplets, the breath in your throat, condensation inside your container, the sound of swallowing. You may become self-conscious about this. That is okay. Just **be discreet** when you drink:

- Turn away slightly.
- Use small sips.
- Keep containers covered or quickly reclosed.

### 4.4 Gift-giving and local “don’t”

You may feel tempted to bring gifts of water or wet-related items from your world:

- **Do not offer water as a gift to locals or to the garden itself.**
- If you want to give something:
  - Ask what is acceptable *from a human guide* once you are there.
  - Until then, arrive **with empty gift-giving hands**, and then follow what they show you.

## 5. Entering the City Itself

### 5.1 The way up

You will likely approach Qesh along one of the **processional-like paths** that spiral up the mesa.

Remember:

- The ground is “still new enough to have opinions.”
- Basalt-glass is brittle in places, edges sharp.

Stay on marked paths. If you see:

- **Local footprints** (which may be shallow or worn impressions of barefoot or foot-coverings),
- **Resin droplets along the edges** indicating a trail,

follow that rather than striking off on your own.

### 5.2 Respect the silence

As you come into the city:

- **Lower your voice or speak softly.**
- **No phones on loud ring**, no alarms, no loud music devices.
- **Turn off any beeping instruments**.

The city “feels” silence as a sign of attention and respect. It is not lawless here; quietness is law.

### 5.3 “No one flees from noise”

When you move through the streets:

- **Don’t rush**, even if you feel panic or discomfort.
- If you need a break, find a small niche or edge terrace where you are not blocking a path, and sit quietly.
- **Do not shout “help!”** unless you are truly physically endangered or injured; in Qesh, loud shouts are interpreted as extremely urgent events.

### 5.4 “Doors are living frames”

You will pass through **doorways that are actually living frames**:

- Pruned openings in vine trunks.
- Resin-lined edges.
- Mycelial networks reinforcing the thresholds.

When you walk through:

- **Do not lean on the walls or frame edges** unless invited explicitly.
- **Do not carve or scratch initials**.
- **Do not press wet hands or lick your fingers and then touch the frames.**

## 6. Approaching the Heart: The Vertical Garden

### 6.1 The central shaft

At the heart of Qesh, you will reach the **central shaft**:

- A jagged oval opening **about sixty meters long and thirty meters wide**.
- Like a chasm and shaft combined.
- The city climbs and hangs over it like pale ribs, terraces spiraling inward.

Your view is likely from **above** looking in or slightly **down** into the opening.

### 6.2 “The garden has no railings”

Do not assume:

- Glass barriers.
- Fences.
- Handrails.

Protections are often **living and invisible**:

- Webs of mycelium.
- Fine fibers of wax and insect silk.
- Curtains of vine that look “delicate” but are structurally strong.

You may still feel:

- **High wind rising from the shaft.**
- **Heat currents and strong updraft.**

Therefore:

- **Stay back from clean edges.**
- **Do not test whether “something” is there by leaning over.**
- If you are invited closer, your local guide will signal you exactly where to step.

### 6.3 “The garden that no one planted”

The garden is not a cultivated bed but an **environment of movement**.

You will observe:

- **Heat and air currents** rising from the deep.
- **Dust drifting from above**.
- **Insects and insect-like beings** riding on air:
  - Globular creatures using heat currents as elevators.
  - “Silent hummingbirds” hovering just outside the edge of the shaft.
  - Possibly light-catching “beetles” unfolding light wings along the shaft walls.

### 6.4 “You will see it as if it is seeing you”

This garden is **aware of you**.

- The air movement you cause.
- The scent you exhale.
- The heat of your skin.
- Your dust, if you’re not careful.

So behave as if someone **polite and silent** is watching:

- Don’t make sudden gestures into the shaft.
- Don’t throw anything.
- **Do not spit or blow your nose over the edge.** (If you must cough, clear your throat away from the shaft, preferably behind an enclosed wall or corner.)

### 6.5 “Nothing wet goes into the garden”

Before approaching the very edge:

- Ensure your **water bottles are secured tightly**.
- If you carried wet tissues or wipes, they are in a sealed waste bag.
- If you have sweat noticeably beading on your skin, wipe it with a dry cloth or absorbent towel.

**No water droplets should fall into the shaft.**

The locals live with this rule their entire lives; you will need to learn it quickly near the heart.

## 7. Three Special Encounters You Will Likely Face

### 7.1 “Beetle-shaped things that are part of the wall”

You will see:

- Small, beetle-like beings in the walls or doors.
- They unfold translucent wings not to fly but to catch light and reflect it into the door.

Your behavior:

- **Do not touch them.**
- **Do not poke them or “test” whether they respond.**
- **They are not decorations to be photographed with flash.**  
  If you photograph, use low ambient light and no flash.

If they “adjust” light by shifting angles as you pass:

- **Don’t jump.**  
  They’re tuning light for you.
- **Acknowledge silently.** This is a form of politeness and “greeting” from the house itself.

### 7.2 “Fat white grubs that walk in the dark”

At night or sometimes under moon:

- You will see **white grubs** emerging from inner vine walls and walking down into the streets.
- **Handlers** coated with thin layers of wax and resin meet them.

The grubs:

- Are not eaten.
- Produce “honey” that is **like a book of images**—information-dense, hallucinogenic, or deeply symbolic.
- Are handled with bare hands covered in protective waxes and oils.

Your behavior:

- **Do not try to touch grubs** unless explicitly invited and then you must follow the handler’s instructions exactly.
- **Do not try to eat or taste any honey from the grubs.**
- If you are invited to *experience* honey in a ritual context:
  - Ask if it is safe for you physically.
  - Understand that you might be changed by the experience.
  - Ensure you are in a stable emotional state first.
  - Keep **a friend** or guide nearby.

### 7.3 “Silent hummingbirds that never land”

You will see:

- Tiny hovering beings with transparent wings much larger than their bodies.
- **They never land.**

Your behavior:

- **Do not chase or swat at them.**
- **Do not try to catch them in your hands or containers.**
- **Treat them like eyes watching you** and accept them as part of the environment.

### 7.4 “Groves of wax, fiber, and metal”

You will walk:

- Underneath **high wax curtains, fiber tunnels, and maybe rare thin metal sheets.**
- **Tin sheets**, sometimes half-grown-over by stone or vine.

Your behavior:

- **Do not strike or scrape the metal sheets to make sound.**
- **Do not try to peel, pry, or “take samples.”**
- **Do not tie anything to them.**

These are sacred objects in a city almost without metal.

## 8. Restrictions on Action You Must Follow

### 8.1 Do not bring in water-based technologies

Never:

- Use water to “cleanse” surfaces or your hands in public.
- Pour water on any vine floor or wall.
- Spill water into any resin pool or onto the dust floor.

If you spill:

- **Warn locals immediately** if you are with them.
- Use **absorbent dry cloths** to remove as much as you can.
- **Bag the contaminated cloths** securely and take them with you when you leave Qesh.
- Do not “hide” spills by pretending you didn’t notice them.

### 8.2 Do not introduce “wet” biology

Even if you think “helpful”:

- **Do not release moisture-rich organisms**, such as aquatic plants or things that require water to reproduce.
- **Do not bring in water-borne microbes or molds** intentionally.
- Avoid **transferring soil from wet-world ecosystems** on your boots or tools.

The local ecosystem **coevolved without liquid water** as a major medium. Water given “freely” could destroy as much as it “helps.”

### 8.3 Do not interfere with flows

You will see:

- Flows of heat rising.
- Flows of dust drifting.
- Flows of insects riding air and wax.

You may be tempted to “intercept” or “redirect” these flows.

Do **not**:

- Build any artificial barrier to redirect air currents.
- Fan air with large objects into the shaft or into wax curtains.
- Capture “dust clouds” in a container to take home.

### 8.4 “If you create a new trace, you must carry it with you”

This is a core principle:

- Do **not leave water traces** on the paths.
- Do **not leave**:
  - Food crumbs.
  - Synthetic wrappers.
  - Any artificial liquids that come from your world.
- If you must relieve yourself:
  - Use a container system **designed for travel in dry environments** that you then fully seal away.
  - Do not create “wet” waste anywhere visible or public.
  - If you are traveling with a local group, ask them discreetly where travelers “don’t” go rather than asking where “toilet facilities” are.

## 9. If You Are Invited to Participate

### 9.1 “Grub honey” or information rituals

If invited:

- Ask:
  - **What happens to your body after you consume this?**
  - **Is there a risk of allergic reaction?**
  - **How long should you expect to be under its influence?**
- Only proceed if:
  - You have **someone you trust present**.
  - You know **where you will be safe** while you experience it.
  - You have **your medical kit** and a clear exit plan.

### 9.2 “Walking with grubs”

If an handler invites you to interact with grubs:

- They **may apply a thin layer of wax or oil to your hands** first.
- Follow their exact instructions:
  - How to hold arms.
  - How to move.
  - How to “allow” the grub to travel.

Do **not** attempt to keep the grub or hide it in your clothing; this is extremely disrespectful and potentially very dangerous.

### 9.3 “Tending to edges”

You might be invited to help with “small cuts” or pruning of edges:

- Treat all pruning tools as sacred **even if they look simple**.
- Ask **how to hold the tool**, not to assume “like you know.”
- Do **not** make “extra” cuts without guidance.
- Do **not** try to take samples of the vine tissue, even tiny “coupons.”

## 10. Leaving Safely and Without Damage

### 10.1 Final sweep before going

Before you leave the heart of the mesa and the city:

- Check:
  - All **water containers tightly closed and secured**.
  - **Sealed waste bags**.
  - No **personal objects left lying on vine floors or in thresholds**.
- Inspect your shoes:
  - Remove any visible **foreign matter** (from your world) stuck to them.
  - Brush off dust with a dry cloth or brush.

### 10.2 “What you take with you”

Do **not**:

- Take samples of:
  - Resin.
  - Honey.
  - Any portion of vines or wax walls **unless given explicitly as a gift by a trusted local keeper**.

But if something **attaches itself to you** (like dust or pollen):

- If you’re uncertain:
  - Ask whether you should remove it before leaving.
  - If they say you may leave it, ask what to do if it becomes a problem in your home world and then **decide whether you want to carry that responsibility.**

### 10.3 “What you leave behind”

Ideally:

- Footprints.
- Warmth.
- Breath.
- Perhaps **non-living dust** native to Qesh itself.

That should be all.

### 10.4 “What you do not leave behind”

Do not leave:

- Plastic.
- Metal pieces.
- Chemical traces (from solvents, oils, or unclean water).
- Biological traces that might carry “wet” diseases.

## 11. After You Return Home

### 11.1 Health monitoring

After you return:

- Monitor yourself for:
  - **New respiratory issues** or persistent coughs.
  - **Skin rashes or strange fungal reactions**.
  - **Any persistent digestive symptoms** that might indicate accidental ingestion of something foreign.
- If you have any suspicious health symptoms:
  - Seek medical evaluation.
  - **Tell them truthfully where you have been** (if they can understand “multiverse travel”).

### 11.2 “Remembering” the garden

The garden that no one planted will be:

- More of a **sensory memory than a visual one**:
  - The heat.
  - The dry scent of dust and honey.
  - The high thin ringing of pollinators just beyond your threshold of hearing.
  - The feeling of being watched by something very large, very gentle, and very dry.

You may find:

- Long quietness in your own world suddenly feels different.
- The absence of water sound—where you usually hear rivers, taps, or rain—might trigger a memory of the mesa.

**Use that memory.** Let it teach you how to listen more deeply to whatever environments you travel through next.

---

If you follow these guidelines—physical, medical, behavioral, and ethical—you will be able to visit the center of the city’s heart, the highest tier of the high mesa, and the garden that no one planted, without destroying yourself or the fragile world that has learned to live without the water you take for granted.
