To step into the Lenten Engine is not like visiting a museum, or even a tomb. It is closer to being lowered into the body of a very old, very slow, living creature. It’s not hostile to you, but it has no special accommodation for your existence. If you go, you must be the one to adapt.

### 1. Understand the kind of place you’re entering

You’re not going “to the basement”; you’re stepping into a machine-ecology that has been running longer than nations have existed on the surface. It’s:

-   deep underground
-   permanently humid and cool
-   lit by faint, old industrial light
-   filled with slow water, salt, rust, and strange life
-   controlled by a piece of infrastructure that appears to be self-regulating but not entirely abandoned

If you’re from a world like ours, think of it as a cross between:

-   an abandoned service-main under-city,
-   a forgotten geothermal power node,
-   and an artificial cavern that has been evolving into a new kind of ecosystem.

Your attitude should be that of a field biologist entering a sensitive environment: be quiet, be respectful, and be prepared to leave *no* permanent mark.

### 2. What you’re not trying to do

You are not going here to:

-   “explore aggressively”
-   fix it
-   test your limits in isolation
-   show off how brave you are
-   or prove “civilization” can conquer anything

People are not loud here because the city doesn’t need loud down here anymore. That’s a good cue for how you should behave.

### 3. Mental preparation

Before you even place a foot on the Warden’s Stair:

1. **Expect a slow environment.**  
   Time is thicker down here. Your sense of urgency will be foreign. If you’re a fast-paced person, you’ll need to recondition yourself:
   - Practice attention to fine details: slow water, faint condensation lines, slow animal movement.
   - This environment will punish impatience and reward patience.

2. **Expect subtle sensory change.**  
   - Your hearing will adjust; the deep hum is not initially “loud” but pervasive.
   - Your vision will adjust; there is no true dark here, but no true brightness either.
   - Your temperature regulation will subtly adjust; you won’t be freezing cold but you will be pervaded by a cool calmness.
   - You may feel somewhat “thickened” by humidity and slow deep vibration.

3. **Expect spiritual neutrality.**  
   The Lenten Engine is not an evil lair or a sacred temple in the traditional sense, but:
   - It feels like *ongoing history*.
   - It might *feel* sacred if you’re inclined to veneration of deep-time ecosystems.
   - It doesn’t worship you, or god, or any ideology you bring from the surface.

It will be indifferent to you. Some people find that kind of neutrality unsettling or powerful; depending on your temperament, it might tug at your sense of self.

### 4. Physical preparation and health

You are going to cool, damp, slightly metallic air under many meters of stone and concrete. You do not want to treat breathing like a minor detail.

#### 4.1. Respiratory system

- Bring **a high-grade filtration mask or half-respirator** capable of filtering fine particulate (dust, mold, spores).
- The air is described as “sweet and metallic” and “dusty concrete”—not likely outright immediately toxic, but long-term exposure could be irritating.
- If you have asthma or chemical sensitivities, assume this environment will trigger them if you’re not careful.

Plan:  
Wear mask on entry and during initial exploration. You can lower it if you’re comfortable and if air feels fine *while you’re exposed to the engine proper*, but keep it ready—especially near ceiling fungi and older dust corners.

#### 4.2. Skin and clothing

The environment is described as “not penetrating cold, but cool calmness seeping into you.” You’re going to be damp almost constantly from:

- condensation film on the floor,
- dripping condensation,
- humid air.

Your clothing system must be:

1. **Synthetic or quick-drying innermost layers**: moisture-wicking shorts or leggings, long-sleeve base layer.
2. **Warm mid layer for transition**: light fleece or high-loft insulating layer.
3. **Shell layer**: water-resistant *but not fully waterproof* unless you’re confident in your ability to keep dry inside:
   - For our world’s climate, think of a good softshell or a thin rain layer that can withstand a film of water.
   - Avoid heavy waterproofs that will trap all sweat—this place is cool but not arctic.
4. **Changeable dry socks**: bring two extra pairs in sealed bags or a waterproof pocket.
5. **Spare thin gloves**, if you tend to get chilled easily.

Do not bring cotton next to skin. It will become heavy and cold with condensation. Also: long pants and sleeves are not optional—they’re protection against scrapes, rust, and incidental contact with strange plants.

#### 4.3. Foot protection

This floor is lightly covered with flowing water film *all the time*.

You need:

- **Closed-toe, sturdy water-resistant boots or trench shoes**:
  - High enough to protect ankle from shallow water.
  - Not so heavy that they’re impossible on stairs.
- **Good grip sole**: floors are smoothed concrete, worn erosion channels, and sometimes metal plates.
- **Optional**: thin waterproof socks inside, if you know you’re prone to cold feet.

Be realistic: your feet will be damp for at least some time if you stay longer. Plan to change socks and dry shoes once you leave.

#### 4.4. Eyes, ears, minor protections

- Eyes:
  - A clear lens of some kind can be useful if water drips into your face from the ceiling, but not necessarily mandatory.
- Ears:
  - There is no structural noise risk, but if you’re noise sensitive:
    - Soft earplugs can be used to reduce the ambient hum of the engine if you wish to stay longer and rest.
    - Not required if you just want to experience full sensory reality.

### 5. Lighting and navigation tools

You are not just “going underground”; you’re entering a chamber where *only* faint industrial lamps illuminate everything.

#### 5.1. Primary light

Bring:

- **At least two good headlamps**:
  - Primary: a strong but not *brutally* bright headlight with adjustable output.
    - Use a low/medium beam for most of your time in the Engine chamber so you don’t “destroy” the local subtle light.
  - Secondary: a smaller backup headlight or handheld flashlight.

You *want* to be able to see in detail, but not to “daylight” the place. Soft warm light is better than harsh cold white.

#### 5.2. Signaling and visibility

- A small **chemlight or glowstick** as emergency marker:
  - Attached to your pack or person so you can be found if something goes wrong.
- Not strictly “needed” inside the single chamber, but useful if you are meant to be retrieved by someone else.

#### 5.3. Knowing the way in and out

- **From the Warden’s Station down**: the stairway is described as clear but must be memorized:
  - Mark every turn in your memory.
  - If necessary, take a simple mental sketch: “wood door → stairs down → iron door with porthole → engine chamber.”
- If you are multiverse traveling, have:
  - **Token return method**: a device, artifact, or coordinate that reliably brings you out of the city or back to your base branch *without* you needing the city’s complexity.
    - The underground system of this city is not a place where “winging it” with inter-world travel is good practice.

### 6. Safety and self-reliance

You are entering an environment where *most help is above ground* and “forgetting” exists.

#### 6.1. Health and supplies

- **Basic first aid kit**:
  - Minor cuts and scrapes: antiseptic wipes, bandages.
  - Something for allergic reaction if you have allergies to unknown spores or molds.
  - A small roll of sport tape for blisters or unexpected minor injuries.
- **Simple analgesics**, if you normally tolerate them: for headache or pressure-related discomfort.
- **Any medication you depend on for chronic conditions**: in a sealed, waterproof pouch.

#### 6.2. Time and saturation

- Don’t stay too *long* on your first visit. This is:
  - a humid, enclosed,
  - deep-time,
  - psychically weighty,
  - somewhat alien environment.

Your body can be “fine” for 2–4 hours yet still feel the effect of it:

- **Limit your first foray to a few hours at most**.
- If you feel your consciousness “grinding down” to the machine’s pace in a way that unsettles you, that’s your signal to leave.

#### 6.3. Hydration and food

- Bring:
  - **Clean water from your own world** in a sealed container:
    - Don’t drink the Engine’s water unless you have real-world skills testing groundwater purity and *very* good reason.
  - A small, **light snack**: something like trail nuts or energy bars that won’t crumble or pollute the environment.
- Eat up top *before* descending if you can, so you’re not trying to fully operate on your first meal in this strange place.

### 7. Environmental and ecological ethics

You are entering a machine that has *grown* ecology:

- moss grown with metallic nodules,
- spindly plants with scent nodules,
- fungi that trace “script” between the conduits,
- insects and snails that don’t know what to do with human fear.

They are not “dangerous” in the sense of attacking you, but they are fragile:

1. **Do not touch the plants unless you’ve been trained as a botanist or ecologist.**
2. Do not:
   - break,
   - collect,
   - or “try” unknown plant nodules or fungus frills.
3. Do not disturb:
   - snail colonies,
   - webs,
   - most of the insect “floor traffic”.

If you must step over water channels or vegetation:

- Step carefully *around* leafy growths;  
- Don’t force contact with your boots or gloved hands.

#### 7.1. Human artifacts and the engine

You will see hints of human presence:

- a wiped-clean wrench,
- a set of wax markers.

Assume **everyone who has touched this engine since its installation** has been careful *for* the machine:

- Do not:
  - turn valve handles,
  - press buttons,
  - “adjust” controls,
  - braid wires into new shapes.

You are not signing maintenance logs under the Tower Provision Act. You have no clearance to alter anything.

### 8. Recording and understanding without disturbing

If you want to remember or understand the Lenten Engine:

- **Do not carve graffiti or “tags” into the walls or pipes**.
- Use:
  - **Pencil and notebook** rather than ink that can bleed if it gets wet.
  - **Simple drawings** of:
    - layout of the chamber,
    - placement of the engine,
    - approximate pattern of lamps and pipes.
  - **Short descriptive notes**: of what you see, not what you *decide* it all means.

If you must use electronic devices (from your world or multiverse):

- Use:
  - **Low intensity light**,
  - **Silenced sound**,  
  to avoid contamination with the deep soundscape of the Engine.

Avoid *frequent* flash photography or bursts of intense light that you can’t justify.

### 9. Psychological and spiritual preparation

This is a place where “absence of busy city sound” is not just emptiness; it’s another kind of presence.

#### 9.1. Expectation management

You will not:

- hear “the message of the ancients”
- find “hidden treasure keys”
- uncover “dark secret of the Tower”

But you might find:

- a new relationship to time,
- a sense of being irrelevant (and, strangely, freed by this),
- realization of how deep the machine-layer of civilization runs.

If you tend to claustrophobia or deep depersonalization, this environment might:

- amplify your sense of being small,
- or paradoxically calm it because the machine “takes” pressure away from you.

Only you know your own mind. If deep, underground, enclosed, “tending toward perpetual” spaces are an issue for you, *prepare* a support plan:

- Tell someone else where you are going.
- Set a concrete time limit.
- Have a clear “I’m leaving now” protocol, *before* you step through the iron door.

#### 9.2. Meditative presence

If you are comfortable with slow presence:

- Treat the Engine almost like a meditation object:
  - Focus on:
    - the low hum,
    - subtle drip of water,
    - faint light in the tubes,
    - slow breathing of the chamber itself.
  - Allow your own breathing to sync *gently* with what you sense:
    - If you begin to feel “pulled in” beyond what you want, *stand* and move:
      - shift to another corner,
      - look at a different wall,
      - let your attention reattach to your own body rather than the huge machine.

### 10. Interaction with the unseen and “sacred” feel of the place

You might find yourself:

- Wanting to “not be” there,
- Or wanting to “never be fully” gone from there.

The engine is not talking to you. But if you *treat* it as a place of worship, do not “project” onto it:

- Don’t narrate it to yourself as “the guardian of the city” or “the heart of darkness.”
- Treat it as:
  - a long-term,
  - functioning,
  - deep-context,
  - machine ecology.

That is enough to be sacred in its own right.

### 11. Practical checklist for departure

Before you leave:

- **Look around you**:
  - Did you leave any trash?
  - Did you stir any moss into ruin?
  - Did you move any tool or marker object?
    - If you moved anything, *return it to the position and orientation you found it in*.
- **Check yourself**:
  - Are your feet unmanageably wet?
    - If yes, sit on the last safe step before the surface door and change socks and dry shoes if you have them.
    - If no, remove excess moisture from boots and soles as you ascend the stairs.
  - Are you shivering more than you expected?
    - Warm up on each landing before continuing to climb.

---

### 12. Final attitude of the traveler

You are not “conquering” or “revealing” the Lenten Engine; it is already operating itself in its own slow time. You are:

- a visitor,
- possibly a student,
- someone allowed to witness *deep* continuity of infrastructure and life.

Approach it like you would sneak in to watch an old hermit meditating far from any city:

You don’t need to be important to the Lenten Engine to be deeply changed by it.

Be prepared, be quiet, and come *out* at least as whole as you went *down*.
