Arriving at the basin will likely be disorienting before it is beautiful, if it ever becomes beautiful for you at all.  

You will not fall into a pit of screaming monsters or boil away in vacuum. This place is “gentle” by the standards of high places: no sudden death rays, no obvious predators, no suffocation. What it will try to do, instead, is rewrite you in its own language: water as stone, stone as sound, thought as rain.

The danger here is not of being torn apart but of being slowly dissolved into the background of the machine.

---

### 1. Before You Go: What You Are and Are Not Dealing With

#### 1.1. Conceptual nature of the place

- The **High Place of Perpetual Weeping** is not a natural landscape in any ordinary sense. It is a shaped environment, like a machine or a chapel or a poem.  
- **Everything is made of water that wants to be stone and stone that wants to be sound.** Expect that:
  - Solid things will sometimes behave like fluid or like music.
  - Water will harden into temporary surfaces.
  - “Ground” is not a trustworthy category; it is more of a suggestion.
- This is not a place of decay, but of **perpetual formation**. It is not old; it is young and still deciding what shape to be. That means you, too, can be rewritten if you stay too long or pay too little attention.

#### 1.2. Ontological status of the visitor

- You are “an ordinary human multiverse traveler.” This implies:
  - You have a body that can be wet, cold, tired, and disoriented.
  - You can think, but your thinking is not native architecture here.
  - Your mind is not built to flow upward like water columns or fall into stone rings; the place will try to nudge you toward that pattern if you let it.

You will need to maintain **personal strangeness** — the difference between you and the basin — as if it were body temperature, or you will start to “weep” its habits instead of your own.

### 2. Physical Preparation & Gear

#### 2.1. Basic survival kit

- **Water-resistant clothing**:
  - Do not rely on waterproof alone; “waterproof” implies it can be sealed *against* water. This water will not respect zipper teeth forever.
  - Use multiple layers:
    1. Inner layer: synthetic or moisture-wicking base to keep skin from becoming macerated.
    2. Mid layer: insulating yet thin (the temperature is not freezing, but combined with constant dampness you will lose heat slowly).
    3. Outer shell: treated fabric or synthetic rubber that rain beads off of, at least temporarily.
- **Footwear**:
  - The “ground” is “hardened dry water” that rings like porcelain but deforms slightly where you step.
    - Choose soles with good grip but flat profile rather than deep lugs, which could catch in the melted spaces between rings.
    - Tall boots to help with water up to mid-calf.
- **Gloves and head protection**:
  - Thin gloves for handling cold, wet surfaces.
  - A hood or wide-brimmed hat that can shed water: weeping does not stop, and being constantly dripping in your eyes and neck will disorient you.

You’re expecting not a storm, but **constant contact weeping from every surface**.

#### 2.2. Protection against “wet that isn’t cold”

- Despite the temperature not being freezing, **constant dampness** erodes:
  - Your skin integrity.
  - Your motivation.
  - Your sense of self.
- Bring:
  - **Spare layers** sealed in waterproof bags or physical containers that can resist “becoming water.”
  - **Antifungal cream, skin barriers**; tiny tears that will never heal if you do not protect them.
- If you have any magical or technological equivalent:
  - **Occlusion field**: a thin, flexible layer only micrometers thick that can keep “memory water” from learning your skin pattern.
  - **Temporal stabilizer**: small, possibly worn like a wristband, to encase your body in a persistent “now” so that the weeping cycles of place-time don’t loop you with them.

#### 2.3. Light and navigation tools

- The ambient light is:
  - **Permanent pewter-colored dimness**, as if the afternoon has been paused and stretched into a thin film.
  - Flat, no point source; no sun to navigate by, no stars to fix.
- You cannot rely on sunrise or sunset to mark time; you will therefore **need time-beatings inside your own body and tools**.
- Recommended:
  - **Luminous wristwatch or equivalent device** with:
    - Seconds dial.
    - Several alarms (set at intervals: every 10 minutes at first, then every hour if you feel stable).
  - **Headlamps or hand lamps**, **but**:
    - Use *cold* or non-incandescent light sources that will not steam or appear to “weep” heat themselves.
- Avoid:
  - Strong flares that might reflect in the suspended sheets of water; they will show you other times of this same eternity and **strain your sense of continuity**.

#### 2.4. Sanity and identity maintenance tools

- **Notebook**:
  - Paper that can resist wet (if possible: plasticized paper, or paper sealed in transparent film).
  - Use ink that does not run; water here will try to persuade your words to move elsewhere.
- **Writing implements**:
  - **Multiple pencils or waterproof pens.** The ink of your mind will blur; you must insist your own marks remain.
- **Recording device**:
  - If allowed in your universe, **audio recorder** to voice your thoughts.
- **Optional talisman**:
  - Something small and **physically unchanging**: a ring, a coin, a small stone of metal that *will not become water or sound*.
  - Before you step into the basin, press it into your palm and say to yourself:
    - “This is *mine*, not this place’s. I will leave this place with *this* unchanged.”

You will need to remember **who you were before the weeping**.

### 3. Mental & Emotional Preparation

#### 3.1. Understand the nature of “time” here

- **No days, no hours.** Time is not a sequence of events, but a thickness in the air:
  - Like pressure in your inner ears.
- The basin will try to loop:
  - The weeping columns.
  - The circular ripples on the ground.
  - The suspended windows showing slightly different versions of the same place.
- **Signs you are slipping into time-thickness**:
  - You cannot say how long you’ve been standing still.
  - You start to think this place has *always* been, and that you, too, have always been here.
- **Countermeasures**:
  - **Chanting a simple meter**:
    - Count under your breath: *one-thousand-two-thousand-three* while you breathe.
  - **Marking intervals**:
    - Set alarms on your device.
    - Every interval, perform one small, **specific** action:
      - Touch your talisman.
      - Say your name out loud.
      - Turn your body precisely ninety degrees.

#### 3.2. Linguistics and symbols

- The leaves on the water-trees are **pages of translucent text** that:
  - Cannot be read.
  - Rearrange themselves when you look directly at them.
  - Suggest “instructions.”
- **Do not try to memorize them.**
  - Your brain is not structured to hold that language; it will try to rewrite your neural patterns to match its glyphs.
- When you notice:
  - The leaves of instruction.
  - The angular shimmering script.
    - **Acknowledge but do not engage.** If you must interact, say to yourself:
    - “This is the language of the place. I am the language of myself.”

This is to protect you from becoming **program code** in the machine of the weeping.

#### 3.3. Emotional posture

- “Perpetual weeping” does not mean screaming sorrow. It means:
  - **Continuous expression of a state that never resolves.**
- You may feel:
  - Gentle sadness.
  - Calm exhaustion.
  - A vague nostalgia for a time you never lived.
- **Do not accept the feeling as your own.**  
- Like you will wear physical layers, you should wear **emotional layers**:
  - Inner layer: Allow yourself to feel the ambient mood of the place.
  - Mid layer: Observe that mood from a distance, as if you are watching rain through glass.
  - Outer layer: Firmly insist on your own emotional baseline (“I am here to study, not to become water weeping itself”).

### 4. On the Ground: How to Move Through the Basin

#### 4.1. Entering the basin

- You likely will not seize into a “portal” in the ordinary sense. You may:
  - Climb down from the rim, where the world abruptly ends.
- First actions:
  - **Stop on the rim.** Take 5–10 minutes to:
    - Observe the whole basin.
    - Notice how water rises, falls, and never leaves the bowl.
  - Decide **limits**:
    - How long you will stay (in terms of alarms or intervals).
    - What you’re here to *do*:
      - Is this a site of study?
      - A transit point?
      - A place of purification?

You will anchor yourself to that **purpose** so the basin cannot give you one of its own.

#### 4.2. Movement on the “ground”

- The ground is:
  - **Hardened dry water**, bluish, porcelain-like, deforming slightly where you step.
- Walk:
  - **Slowly**, as if testing ice.
  - Do not run; even though you won’t break through, you might lose track of your own continuity.
- Do not step:
  - **Directly on the stone-ring “gears”** or their metal center coins.
    - They are not aggressive, but they are part of the machine’s organizing logic.
- Watch your footprints:
  - They will disappear slowly as the “memory of ground” reforms itself.
    - This is not hostile to you; this is *the ground forgetting to be broken*.
  - Leave **non-permanent markers**:
    - Small, colored tabs or tags you can drop (not on the water surfaces).
    - Even if they wash away or vanish into idea, their placement pattern can help you triangulate your own position in the basin.

#### 4.3. Interacting with water-trees

- Structure:
  - **Hollow columns of hardened water**, standing tall.
  - “Branches” made of concentric rings of water moving up and then collapsing into droplets that reassemble upward.
  - Leaves: “pages of translucent text” that partially exist and then migrate into geometry when you stare.
- **Do not climb.**
  - You might break through the “surface” of the water-tree, and what you will become afterwards might not be human.
- What you can do safely:
  - **Walk around them**, observing, from at least arm’s length.
- **Mental barriers**:
  - When you look at the text-leaves:
    - Keep your inner language running:
      - “I am reading. These words are not for me to read. They are for the machine.”

#### 4.4. Approaching the suspended water sheets

- Description:
  - **Flat sheets of water** like glass windows, suspended in air.
  - Not fully reflective; you see not yourself but:
    - **Other versions of the basin**: different sky, different mist, same basin.
    - Possibly the basin seen from *below*, as if you stood on its ceiling.
- **Do not pass through them.**
  - You might be walking into a time-thickness or a stabilized echo of the machine.
- Suggested behavior:
  - **Stand at an angle** to the window, not directly facing.
  - Glimpse, rather than stare.
  - Ask yourself:
    - “Which version of this basin am I in now?”
    - And then answer clearly:
    - “This one. The one with me inside it.”

This will prevent you from being **pulled into another version of yourself** looking back at you from another window.

#### 4.5. The stone ring-gears and water columns

- Each:
  - Metal coin at the center.
  - Water oozing upward in thin vertical columns to the height of your wrist.
  - Falling over stone teeth in a precise curtain.
- They:
  - **Move when you are not looking**, fall still when you are looking.
  - Recycle the basin’s water from ground to sky and back again.
- You can **interact lightly**:
  - Pass your hand near the column of water:
    - Notice how the water column ripples in your shadow.
    - Do not let your skin touch it directly for prolonged periods.
- **Mentally**, treat them like organs:
  - The basin’s lungs and valves.
  - Important but not hospitable for you to remain entangled.

### 5. Safety and Self-Preservation Protocols

#### 5.1. Set time and duration limits

- Decide **before you arrive**:
  - “I will not remain here longer than X intervals of my device.”
- **Stick to this** regardless of how gentle or compelling the basin feels.
- **Exit criteria**:
  - If you feel:
    - Like you could stay here forever.
    - Like you never want to leave.
      - That is **the moment you most need to leave**.

You will *not* be better off becoming a tree of water and memory; you will be erased.

#### 5.2. Monitor your own habits of thought and speech

- **Warning signs of overintegration**:
  - You start using metaphors of the basin in *every* sentence.
  - Your thinking becomes circular like the rings on the ground.
  - You feel that you always “fall,” like water weeping down.
  - The idea of “stone” or “sound” begins to feel like a description of yourself.
- **Response**:
  - Break the pattern:
    - Recite a poem from your world in a **rhythm of three beats**, not the circular rhyme of the basin.
    - Sing a song that has distinct verses and a chorus, not a loop.
- Use **structured language**:
  - “I will walk now.”
  - “I will stop now.”
  - “This is water. This is stone. I am neither; I am a traveler.”

#### 5.3. Check the body regularly

- **Physical checks**:
  - Wiggle toes and fingers.
  - Check that nails have not become translucent or water-brittle.
  - Watch your breath:
    - It should not be silent; if you cannot hear yourself inhale and exhale, you may be dissolving into the air’s rhythm.
- **Psychiatric checks**:
  - If you feel a strong desire to:
    - Stand perfectly still and “watch the weeping forever.”
    - Lose the distinction between “inside” and “outside” yourself.
      - That is the point of maximum danger.
      - **Move immediately**, even if it is only shifting your weight from one foot to the other.

#### 5.4. Preserving your identity

- Every **15 minutes** by your device:
  - State your full name.
  - State one thing you value from your world:
    - A person, a place, a food you enjoy eating.
  - As you speak the value:
    - **Imagine its solidity**, its texture, its scent.
      - This is not just “relaxing”; you are reinforcing the neural distinction between you and the basin.

### 6. Leaving the Basin

#### 6.1. Knowing when to leave

- You must leave if:
  - You have reached your predetermined time.
  - You feel that you can no longer remember the shape of your home.
  - The world beyond the rim seems like less of a reality than the basin.

#### 6.2. The process of leaving

- **Do not turn back immediately** once you’re on the rim.
  - The basin’s weeping will still draw you.
- **First steps upon leaving**:
  - Dry your skin if possible.
  - Warm yourself deliberately: a hot drink from a thermos flask you kept sealed outside the basin, if that can be arranged.
- **Reintegration rituals**:
  - Write a summary in your notebook:
    - “What I saw.”
    - “What I felt.”
    - “What I think the basin is trying to become.”
      - This writing must be **your own language**, not its language of “weeping stone and sounding water.”
      - Use your words to describe its phenomena; do not allow its images to occupy your syntax without resistance.

#### 6.3. Long-term aftercare (once you return home)

- **Hydration and sleep**:
  - You’ve been exposed to continuous wetness and constant pressure on time-perception.
- **Evaluate your memory**:
  - If parts of your experience start blurring into recurrent imagery of water weeping upward from stone rings and you cannot shake the feeling of standing under that low pewter sky:
    - You may need to **perform reality checks**:
      - Locate material objects, feel textures that are definitely not water.
      - Say aloud your earliest memory from your childhood that you can clearly recall.
- If you feel:
  - That you are becoming “less human” and more like a machine or chapel carved into a sleeping body:
    - Seek **anchoring**:
      - Live people, not imagined.
      - Physical objects not made of water memory.
      - Tasks that demand you remain in *this* world’s physics and rules.

### 7. Final Warnings

- The **High Place of Perpetual Weeping** is not dangerous in the way of pits or monsters; but its **persistent softness** will be the hardest thing to resist.
- You will not be torn apart by claws or fire, but you might **disappear into being thought**, as water becomes stone and stone becomes sound and you become only one more weeping feature in the basin.

If you go, prepare with **layers, timers, and anchors**: one layer to keep your body dry, one layer to keep your mind separate from the machine, and enough anchors to remember who you were before it thought you into its weeping.
