To visit Ruispor from an ordinary world and not break yourself or the place, you need to think like water: slow, persistent, and very aware of pressure.

---

## 1. Decide Why You’re Going and How Long You’ll Stay

### Why you’re going
Ruispor will not be “fun” in the usual tourist sense.  
Go if you want:

- To see what a city that has been shaped by a damp, industrial, plateau river valley for centuries looks like up close.
- To observe a world where geology and infrastructure are tightly wedded.
- To study a soundscape and rhythm of life utterly different from your own.

If you mainly want ease, comfort, and color, pick another horizon.

### How long to stay
For a first visit:

- 3–5 days if you just want to look around.
- 2–3 weeks if you want to learn the city’s “language” (patterns of water, noise, and shift changes).
- Longer than a month only if you genuinely enjoy living inside a damp, noisy machine of stone and steam.

Ruispor’s time is slow; but as a traveler, you’ll feel the wear of the place quickly.

---

## 2. The Underlying Principle of Preparation: Respect the Water

Everything in Ruispor depends on **water in the rock**:

- The plateau “weeps.”
- Basements flood “for no reason.”
- Walls are always faintly damp.
- Underground chambers no one fully maps anymore.

Pack, dress, and behave with the assumption: **at least one of your surfaces will be damp most of the time.**

---

## 3. What to Pack

### 3.1. Clothing and Footwear

#### Layered, quick-drying clothing
It’s not jungle hot; but humidity + mild temperature = sweat + external damp.

- Synthetic or wool layers that dry fast:
  - 2–3 light shirts
  - 1–2 long-sleeve base layers
  - 1–2 lightweight trousers (not cotton jeans)
- At least one **slightly warmer layer**—evenings and “ever-warm” places can still chill you once you’re soaked.
- 1–2 **waterproof shells** (not breathable is fine; humidity is already high).
- 1–2 **lightweight hats**—one brimmed for above-ground, one simple beanie or cap for sleeping.
- 5–7 pairs of **undergarments and socks**—these are the hardest to keep dry.

#### Footwear: the most important item
- **Primary boots:** Waterproof, high-ankle, with aggressive tread.
  - Expect streets that are never quite dry.
  - Some sidewalks are uneven, stone-worn, and slick with algae or wet moss.
- **Optional second pair:** lighter slip-ons:
  - For indoor evenings.
  - Not for full day wandering.
- **No open sandals** on the lower streets next to the river.  
  - Brownish runoff water + oil slicks ≠ friends.

### 3.2. Skin and Body Care

#### Anti-chafe and fungal prevention
You are not adapted to Ruispor’s “constantly damp skin.”

Bring:
- **Anti-chafing balm or powder:** for thighs, groin, waistband, around feet.
- **Anti-fungal cream:** for feet, especially between toes.
- **Unscented moisturizer:** for damp hands that still somehow dry and crack.
- **Basic soap:** small bar of neutral soap—you may want to wash often.

### 3.3. Health and Survival Kit

#### Basic medicines
- Pain reliever (e.g., ibuprofen / acetaminophen).
- Anti-diarrheal medication.
- Antihistamine for allergies or insect bites.
- Any personal prescriptions (plus written instructions and generic names).

#### Insect protection
- Small **mesh insect-proof inner tent** or net for sleeping:
  - Midges and mosquitoes are the city’s “small guardians.”
- Insect repellent:
  - If you have access to it in your world, bring a **high-percentage DEET or equivalent**, in a leak-proof container.
- After-bite cream or similar.

#### Water and hydration
Even though the city is water-obsessed, drinking water is a separate matter.

- Bring **water bottles**.
- Plan **water purification**:
  - UV pen, or
  - Aquatabs / chlorine dioxide tablets, or
  - Compact filter (if feasible to carry).
- **Electrolyte salts** or rehydration sachets:
  - You will lose salt and moisture through constant damp sweating.

### 3.4. Basic Tools and Accessories

#### Essential gear
- Small **LED headlamp**:
  - Under-eaves, alleys, staircases, and basements are poorly lit.
  - The city’s “night” might still be full of machinery noise but not full light.
- **Compact umbrella** or small rain cape.
- **Notebooks** + waterproof pen or pencil:
  - Paper takes humidity well; ink can run.
- **Sealed plastic bags**:
  - For passport / ID, maps, clothing, electronics.
- **Simple multitool or knife** (if allowed by your rules of travel).
- **Basic sewing kit**:
  - Clothing in damp, industrial environments tends to burst at the seams and pockets.

#### Optional
- **Clear-lensed eye protection**:
  - For unglazed fog windows and occasional steam leaks.
- **Earplugs or noise-canceling plugs**:
  - Ruispor’s “soundscape” can be oppressive; you need to sleep.
- **Compact portable humidity/temperature sensor**:
  - For your own curiosity and comfort.

## 4. Money, Trade, and Documentation

You don’t yet know how your home currency or tech will be received.

### Before you arrive
- Carry **small, durable, non-perishable items of potential value**:
  - Stainless steel needles or sewing tools.
  - Simple waterproof matches or lighters.
  - Small stainless steel spools of strong thread.
  - A few sealed packets of salt, sugar, or simple spices.
- Bring **requested legal documents** in your own multiverse:
  - Identity card.
  - Proof of affiliation.
  - Licenses (if any).
- Prepare **printed images or paper descriptions** of your homeworld’s:
  - Currency, if you need to explain.
  - Technology, if permitted.
  - Any symbols of your office or traveler-status.

### In the city itself
- Do **not** assume modern banking or digital payments.
- Expect **local barter**, metal tokens, or paper notes; watch how others pay.
- Assume **local authorities** (municipal hall, telegraph office, waterworks) have **eye** on you.
- **Do not show off advanced tech**:
  - Smartphones, energy weapons, advanced materials.
  - Ruispor is high-industrial; not post-scarcity.

## 5. Physical and Mental Conditioning

### Physical conditioning
- **Stair climbing**:
  - The city is all switchbacks and terraces.
  - Train your legs and lungs.
- **Load carrying**:
  - You will be climbing steps with your pack and wet boots.
- **Basic cardiovascular resilience**:
  - You will be breathing damp, slightly smoky air; stamina helps.

### Mental preparation
Ruispor will not stop humming:

- Its noise is **constant**.
- Its damp **never** fully leaves.
- Light may be **dim and filtered** by steam.

Prepare to:

- Tune out noise some.
- Accept **low “green,”** industrial aesthetics.
- Be **uncomfortable but not in danger**—if you plan well.

## 6. Entry Strategy: Where and How to Arrive

### Avoid dramatic arrival
- Do **not** open a portal on the:
  - Dam edge.
  - Factory floor.
  - Anywhere under the civic terrace or waterworks.
- Do **not** materialize in the middle of the river or Plain of Mirrors wetland.

### Recommended arrival spots
- **Upper plateau, grassland edge**, slightly back from the rim:
  - You see the whole valley.
  - You are somewhat away from immediate machinery.
- **Higher civic terrace**, near municipal hall or clock tower:
  - If you can manage precise arrival:
    - Come in discreetly near **official buildings**.
    - You appear more “documented” than if you stumble out of a quarry.

### First hour after arrival
1. **Observe** quietly:
   - Which direction do people and goods flow?
   - Where are lights, smoke, and water concentrated?
2. **Find shelter**:
   - Look for:
     - A boarding house or inn on the middle terraces.
     - Or a municipal “hostel for laborers” or “engineers’ annex.”
3. **Do not**:
   - Ask immediately about “pressure phantoms” or “hidden caves.”  
   - Speak of “underneath” until you know how locals think of it.

## 7. Accommodation: Choosing a Place to Sleep

### Location considerations
- **Higher terraces**:
  - Pros: Less damp, less immediate noise, better view.
  - Cons: Longer climb to everything else.
- **Mid-slope**:
  - Pros: Balanced access to factories and river.
  - Cons: Constant noise from industry and people.
- **River-level / industrial harbor**:
  - Pros: Easy access to barges, docks, and freight.
  - Cons: Very damp, questionable smells, frequent insect activity.

For a first visit:
- **Upper mid-slope**, **not** directly beneath industrial chimneys or ventilation ducts.
- Sleep **close** enough to waterworks or civic terrace to be “official-adjacent,” but not directly under noise.

### Room specifics
Look for:
- **Windows away from direct steam vents**.
- **No obvious wet patches on ceiling or floor**—unless they are there by design and managed by the innkeeper.
- **Bed raised slightly** from the floor:
  - Humidity will be lower.
- **Ventilation shutter**:
  - Even if it lets noise in, you need airflow.
- **Clean basin**:
  - For washing your face and clothes.
- **Drying line**:
  - Even a simple cord across the room to help clothes recover.

## 8. Movement and Navigation Inside the City

### Walking on the streets
- **Always** assume:
  - Some stones are slippery.
  - Some gutters have overflow potential.
- **Step**:
  - On the highest part of the step, not the center where worn.
  - On the drier side of the pavement if visible.
- **Avoid**:
  - Walking directly under crane loads or low overhead factory beams.
  - Walking near open steam vents.

### Vertical movement
- Expect **switchback stairs** rather than straight climbs.
- Railings may be:
  - **Wet** on the outside.
  - **Rust-rough** under your palm.
- Use handrail but:
  - Test first before relying weight.

### Crossing the river and canals
- Use bridges **designed for public use**, not:
  - Factory catwalks.
  - Industrial platforms.
- **Do not jump** or attempt to swim “shortcuts”:
  - The river is stronger than it looks.
  - Water may be contaminated with oils and effluents.
- **Docks**:
  - Only walk on secure planks.
  - Avoid visibly rotten or swollen boards.

---

## 9. Noise and Your Mind: How to Handle the Sound of the City

### Sound protection
- **Earplugs**:
  - For **sleep**, especially if you’re anywhere near the industrial district or harbor.
- **White noise or consistent music**:
  - If you have a player:
    - Use *low* tones of river or rain to mask the industrial irregular beats.
- **Scheduled quiet places**:
  - Find places:
    - Library or reading room of engineers’ guild.
    - Higher municipal terrace.
    - Upper edge of plateau with wind instead of machines.

### Psychological routine
- **10 minutes in silence or near-silence** daily:
  - Even in the least noisy part of town, you will need a break.
- **Breathing exercises**:
  - Simple counting:
    - Inhale 4–6 counts.
    - Hold 2–4.
    - Exhale 4–6.
- **Keep expectations**:
  - You won’t “settle in” to the noise; you will learn to **coexist with it**.

## 10. Food and Water Safety

### Food
- Expect **stewy**, **one-pot** meals:
  - Beans, root vegetables, preserved meats.
- Ask for:
  - **Hot cooked food** served from busy stalls.
  - Avoid **raw** vegetables washed in the river water.
- **Foods to avoid**:
  - Street salad that seems washed but not boiled.
  - Any meat that smells “off” in the damp air.
- **Local foods to try carefully**:
  - River fish if locals **boil or roast** it.
  - Porridge or grain-based gruel if it is piping hot.

### Water
All **drinking water** should be treated:

- **Ask locals**:
  - “Where is clean drinking water collected?”
    - They may draw from **cisterns** on the upper plateau or filtered waterworks.
- **Purify** everything:
  - Filter or chemicals at least.
  - Boiling is better if you can arrange it.
- **Never** drink:
  - From the canals or street gutters.
  - From the river unless desperate and treated.

### Scams and risks
- Sellers might:
  - Claim “spring water from the plateau itself” as healthy.
- Be skeptical:
  - “Spring water” can be **groundwater seepage** containing industrial runoff.

## 11. Clothing and Gear Maintenance

### Daily drying ritual
- **Hang everything** at night:
  - Boots upside down on small pegs or improvised supports.
  - Socks and undergarments separated so they air better.
- **Stuff with paper**:
  - If available, to draw moisture from shoes.
- **Separate** wet clothes from dry:
  - Use bags if needed to keep some outfits dry.

### Mold prevention
- **Wash** clothes frequently:
  - Even just rinsing with hot water and soap then hanging dry helps.
- **Check seams**:
  - For any signs of mildew.
- **Use sun** if you see it:
  - Even filtered by industrial haze, sunlight still kills mold.

## 12. Understanding the City’s “Ghost Pressure”

### What pressure and phantoms are in this context
- “Pressure phantoms” are:
  - **Sudden water leaks** where they shouldn’t be.
  - Damp walls appearing without obvious rain.
- The **water table** behaves like:
  - A slow tide under the city.
  - Seeping where it can break confinement.

### How to behave around them
- Take **local cues**:
  - If people avoid certain walls:
    - Do not lean against them.
  - If they skirt around wet patches on the floor of a marketplace:
    - You do, too.
- **Listen**:
  - If you hear **knocking in pipes**:
    - That’s considered water hammers—the city’s “bones being knocked from inside.”
- **Do not**:
  - Try to “fix” leaks or pressure issues unless explicitly asked.
    - You don’t know the system, and tampering can mess with waterworks engineering.
    - You also don’t know what might be watching or listening below.

## 13. Safety and Social Rules

### Safety basics
- **No unauthorized exploration**:
  - Waterworks tunnels, “abandoned” factory basements, and quarries are not for tourists.
- **Do not climb**:
  - Factory chimneys.
  - Dam walls.
  - Anywhere that looks like it belongs to maintenance engineers.

### Social behavior
- Respect the **shift cycle**:
  - Whistles and steam releases indicate shifts:
    - Workers move in large groups.
    - Stay out of their way when they are changing.
- **Do not**:
  - Mock the noise or “ugliness.”
- **Ask permission**:
  - Before sketching or taking notes in visible view of locals.
- **Learn basic phrases**:
  - “May I draw this?”
  - “May I watch?”
  - “What is that?”
  - “Thank you.”

### Dealing with authorities
- Remote **multiverse traveler** or not:
  - Present yourself as:
    - A **visitor from a far-away province**.
    - Student of engineering, geology, or urban history.
- Be **honest but not exotic**:
  - “I come from far away” > “I fell from another dimension”  
    (unless that’s a normal legal category there).

## 14. Cultural and Ethical Sensitivities

### What is sacred here
- **Water**:
  - Even when it is “just runoff,” it is serious.
- **Engineering control**:
  - The long struggle of dams, channels, and pumps.
  - Treat infrastructure as **public body** of the city.
- **Stone and labor**:
  - Steps worn by centuries of boots.
  - Quarries that are literally scars on the plateau.

### What not to do
- **Don’t waste** water:
  - Even if you see it “everywhere.”
- **Don’t say**:
  - “This place is so dirty/ugly.”
  - You are in the midst of a very long-lived alliance of rock and engine and moisture.
- **Don’t bet**:
  - On whether “the next whistle” or “the next leak” will happen.
    - That’s not a game for locals; it’s their livelihood and nerve.

## 15. Before Leaving: What to Take Back With You

If you have permission to transport matter:

### Limited mechanical samples
- **Small** pieces of:
  - Rusted metal from safe debris.
  - Stone with visible watermarks.
- **Not**:
  - Any part of active infrastructure.
  - Silt or “mysterious sludge” from the canals.

### Notes and drawings
- **Sketch**:
  - The layout of streets and water channels.
  - The terraces and relationship between river, factory, and civic plateau.
- **Write** down:
  - The soundscape.
  - The layout of “weeping” zones.
  - The way locals talk about “pressure phantoms” and “water under.”

All of this is invaluable to future travelers and to yourself when you try to remember what the damp felt like on your skin, and the thunder sounded like in your chest.

---

### Final Summary

- Ruispor is **not** dangerous if you:
  - Dress like you’ll be constantly damp.
  - Treat water and infrastructure with respect.
  - Protect yourself from noise and humidity’s wear on your body.
- Prepare to:
  - **Walk slowly**.
  - **Listen carefully**.
  - **Accept discomfort** as a feature of the place, not a mistake in your plans.
- Think of yourself as:
  - A bit of moisture added to the city’s **long-lived stone** and **never-quite-stopping engine**.

With that attitude and these preparations, you can survive the trip—and perhaps even come to appreciate the strange, enduring balance of Ruispor’s wet, stone lungs and its industrial heart.
