To prepare for this world, first you must be honest with yourself about what kind of traveler you are.

It is not a place you “visit.”  
It is a place you marry into.

### 1. Before You Go: Decide Why You Are Going

There are three main kinds of reasons to come here:

1. **You want to study it.**  
   You are a researcher, an artist, a biographer of the forgotten. You want to understand the column-beings, the bone-thorn gardens, the layered pressure of the city itself. This world will be your library and you will be a very small, very fragile text in the margin.

2. **You want to disappear.**  
   You have been broken in drier worlds; or you are tired of being known; or you simply want a place where air no longer touches you and the sky is not empty. This city can become that new kind of body for you: a place that is also a person.

3. **You are passing through on a longer multiverse journey.**  
   You treat this as a dangerous node on a route map. You want to minimize entanglement and get out.

Each of these will require a different density of preparation and a different emotional posture.

Unless you are truly desperate and one of the “I want to disappear” people, **you should not come here alone**. At least pair up with one experienced aqua-world traveler or a local diver who knows the city enough to keep you from walking into currents that will not bring you back.

---

### 2. Deep-Water Training (Before You Leave)

You are going to a world where you are *never* shallow.

Even if physical “depth” by the standards of our world isn’t huge, the psychological weight is. You’re always under a ceiling of water, with a pressure differential on your body, and sound never reaches you from vacuum—air—but only from other, more unpredictable medium.

You should:

- **Learn to freedive safely.**  
  - Be comfortable with depths of 15–20 meters.  
  - Practice holding breath while calm: not competition-levels, but 90–120 seconds comfortably without panic.  
  - Practice equalizing often and gently.  
  - Practice being motionless in the water column, not just sitting on the bottom.

- **Take a basic scuba certification course, even if you don’t use tanks.**  
  - You will learn the physics of pressure, water columns, and equalizing.  
  - This drastically reduces your chances of doing something stupid underwater.

- **Practice in low-visibility conditions.**  
  - Find murky water, or at least simulate lost visibility: close your eyes, move using only touch and sound.  
  - This place will be *permanently* poor visibility by our standards. The surface is “bruise-blue” and distant; the world beneath is lit by trembling shafts.

- **Build intuition about current.**  
  - Learn to read water flow—direction, strength, and reflections off stone walls.  
  - This city will move “like traffic,” and you must be able to feel *where* the water wants you and not just shove your way through.

Also, **basic underwater survival**: how to avoid panic, what to do if disoriented, how to help a partner if you’re both being pulled by a current, etc.

### 3. Psychological Preparation: You Are Entering a Body

This matters. If you treat this as a “site,” you will drown in the hope. You are entering a body shaped like a city:

- The **columns** are not always inert stone; some are fused beings, columns that are also columns.  
- The **streets** are not just paths; they are ribs of the world.  
- The **surface** above is not “sky” but a *skin* of sea between you and something else.  

You will often *feel* more than see.

These are some of the realities you will face:

- **Constant low-grade pressure:**  
  On your body (from depth and rain) and on your mind (from being always surrounded and never fully seen).

- **No true horizon:**  
  There is only “nearer” and “more distant,” coiled in drifting columns, light and shadow.

- **No dry “home” as you move:**  
  There is never “a moment where it is just you and the air.” You are always *immersed*.

Prepare to be:

1. **Always in contact with the unknown.**  
   You’re never *alone* in the way you are on land. There is always something in the column with you.  
   - You might feel *presence* before you see anything.  
   - You might see vanishing fish more clearly than you ever see the thing that makes them flee.

2. **In a place that is breathing.**  
   - When you press your head to a stone wall, you might feel a heartbeat that is not yours.  
   - You might be in a “plaza” whose columns rhythmically change the flow of the water—they “breathe.”

3. **In a place with time *much* slower than yours.**  
   Some columns seem to be *beings* that have been fusing with the city for millennia. They will not be hurried by your life schedule.

### 4. Equipment & Gear: Make the World a Second Skin You Can Wear

Leave behind anything that cannot *be* part of this body.

#### 4.1. The suit: becoming as close as possible to “water”

You will need:

- **A thin, full-body wetsuit or rashguard.**  
  - The description is of warm water (“air thick and warm”), but you are still deep enough to need temperature regularity.  
  - Not chunky neoprene gear; something more spider-silk than textile armor.

  If advanced tech is available:
  - A **gilled membrane suit** or **environmental suit with gel filtration** that can:
    - Keep you warm.  
    - Let you *draw* oxygen from the water (like a fish).  
    - Provide partial drug regulation of nitrogen narcosis and oxygen toxicity.

  If only simple tech:
  - **Standard mask, fins, and a short snorkel**  
    - Superglue the snorkel to the side of your mask. You do not want to lose it.

#### 4.2. Lungs & breathing: preparation for not *returning* to the air

This is the most critical question: **Do you intend to breathe “water” here?**

The text implies that you might *not* leave the water:

> You are breathing the water; it is yourself now.

But you still *want* to be able to leave *if* you change your mind.

Travelers’ standard options:

1. **Only temporary immersion:**  
   - Short visits, using scuba (closed-circuit rebreather ideally, to reduce bubbles and noise).  
   - A *very* strict limit on depth and time so that you are not tempted by deeper layers.

2. **“Hybrid” body modification:**  
   If you have access to multiverse medical biotech, you want:
   - Temporary **gill implants or lung membrane adaptations** that allow you to breathe from seawater, but also:
   - A **reversible switch** that you retain control over for the first dozen cycles at least, so you can decide if you want to stay water or return.

3. **If you are recruiting “the city itself as a body”:**
   - Reject physical aids from your own world early *if* your intention is to entangle with the column-beings.  
   - Accept that you are becoming something else.

In *any* case, you need:

- **Drop weights:**  
  - Alterable ballast around your thighs or belly so that you can dump *just* enough to rise but not so much that you shoot to the surface like an arrow.

- **Depth and time monitoring:**  
  Even if time is different here, your body’s tissues are made from atoms that obey *some* laws of pressure.

### 5. Light & Sight: Low-Vis, Trembling, and Always Messed With

The world is described as:

> a dark cathedral of vaults and channels

and:

> the sun is up above, but its light reaches down only in trembling shafts.

Given:

- **Faint and shifting light:**  
  - The sun’s lace is constantly broken by fish.  
  - The bottom is never fully bright or steady.

- **Certain plants that glow briefly:**  
  - Tiny black beads on pillars that sparkle when you touch them.  
  - You can’t rely on them for navigation.

Plan accordingly:

1. **Always carry two light sources.**  
   - A small **headlight** or mask-mounted light (low luminous output, not a bright “searchlight” style). Brightness will be eaten by the water anyway.  
   - A *second* handheld light in your belt or net, in case the first fails.

   Choose:
   - **Amber, low-intensity** rather than white blazer.  
   - The world already has “lantern-jellyfish”; too bright a light will be alarming to the wildlife.

2. **Use light only when needed.**  
   - The beam can be used to *check* ahead—  
     - Is there a wall?  
     - Is there an open column?  
   - Once you understand the path, *turn off* and move by touch and sound.

3. **Learn to see with pressure.**  
   - In practice, this means:
     - Notice *how* the water pushes on your face when you turn your head.  
     - Notice *how* something big nearby you will not so much be seen as *felt* by the way it drags water.

### 6. Navigation & Orienteering: This is Not a Grid Map

This city is a *geology* of itself:

- Colonnades are half-collapsed.  
- Grand squares are now bowls of still water where currents “can’t decide which way to turn.”  
- Arches are now tunnels for fish.

Navigation:

1. **Use non-local reference points.**  
   - The *bruise-blue ceiling* of the surface is always above you, but it is not always identically illuminated.  
   - Storms above change the color of that light and the pressure in the water column.  
   - Note the *pattern* of the light shafts, *which* are broken more often by fish, etc.

2. **Use local *geometry*.**  
   - The columns are too regular to be natural;  
   - The *pattern* of the *columns* is the best guide.  
   - You might number:
     - “Triforium street with 7 columns + 1 broken column.”  
     - “Plaza with 4 angles + one corner turned into a bone-thorn garden.”

3. **Use sound.**  
   - Currents produce *audible* patterns in:
     - Dripping sound  
     - The murmur of small waves against stone  
     - A deeper resonating when you’re under *certain* arches.

4. **Use layers of temperature and pressure.**  
   - Warm layers near the surface;  
   - Cooler layers from deeper foundations ->  
   - The *boundary* between warm and cool is a kind of topology map.

5. **Do NOT rely on “mental” gmaps.**  
   - Your memory of *air* maps is not useful here.

6. **Always have a way out to the surface *in principle*.**  
   - Remember:
     - If you need *air* eventually, you must *not* lose track of how to get to the blue ceiling.  
   - But:
     - Don’t *swim to the surface* unless you are prepared to meet whatever else uses the upper tiers of the world.

### 7. Food, Water, and “Being” in the Column

You are never *truly* dry.

You will want:

- **Water purifier** or desalinator that you can attach to your intake mask or suit mouthpiece.  
  - Even if you are breathing “water,” you might still want to drink small amounts of pure *solvent* water to avoid your body being overly salted.

- **Calorie dense gel:**  
  - Not heavy bars that will break or crumbs that will pollute this world’s water column.  
  - Think *viscous paste* in a collapsible container.

- **Hydration-nutrient mix:**  
  - You lose *less* sweat to the environment than you do in air, but you *still* lose some.

Beware:

- **The “blood of the world.”**  
  - Touched coral:
    > bleeds a pale green fluid that stings your fingers.

  - Don’t eat anything you don’t *know* is safe.  
  - Some *multiverse* travelers might *try* to eat from the coral lattice or from algae, but you should assume:
    - Anybody doing *that* is already partially transformed by this place.

### 8. Safety & First Aid: The Water is Deeper than You Think

The world is:

- **Eroded but not stable.**  
  - Some columns may be strong *but* fused with beings that can change morphology very slowly.  
  - Others might be *only* stone and ready to collapse if you lean on the wrong spur.

- **Full of invisible edges.**  
  - The bone-thorns:
    > dense enough to be dangerous to swim through.

  - A *momentary* wrong bend in a burrow between floors can be a *compression* of thorns into flesh.

- **Not obviously “aggressive,” but *not* safe either.**

You should always:

- Carry **blunt medicinal items:**  
  - Sutures (if you are going to be in prolonged contact with *sharp* objects).  
  - Antifungal / antibacterial skin films to prevent any infection from cuts *in* this water.

- **Antivenin / antitoxin** if available:
  - For the coral’s sting fluid.  
  - For the *other* things you will touch before you know what they are.

- **Basic pressure medication:**  
  - If you are coming from another world with *different* gravity or pressure baseline, you might be more prone to issues.

### 9. Encounter with Cities: The Column-Beings & Other Inhabitants

From the description:

- **The column-beings:**
  > some columns are thicker than you realized at first,
  > they are ... not all of them columns.

  *How* you might interact with them:

  - **Do not touch** unless:
    - You have been in the city *long* enough to know *which* pillars are not fully stone.  
    - You *choose* entanglement with the world and *want* to be a part of its rhythm.

  - If you *must* touch:
    - Place *open palm* on the stone column as *lightly* as possible,  
    - Like *checking* if someone is breathing, not trying to shake them awake.

  - **Listen to pressure:**  
    - If you feel *vibration* through the stone column,  
    - Recognize *when* the column is being used as a sensor:
      > The columns of this world are collectively altering the shape of the water with their breathing.

- **The “invisible plate” lifeforms:**
  > They crowd in the crevices of the architecture,
  > like moving bas-reliefs that have decided to migrate.
  > When they notice you, they spread like ink spilled in the water…

  *How* you might react:

  - **Don’t chase them.**  
  - They *decide* whether to be seen.
  - Treat *all* crevices in the walls as eyes:
    - If you want to pass *without* being seen as *interesting*:
      - Move slow.  
      - Keep *below* the level of the crevice, if your path allows.

- **The bone-thorn gardens:**
  > they flower not with color but with sound.

  *How* you might behave:

  - **Do not crash through them.**  
  - Let *the current* push them to make sound:
    - The *note* tells you something about the *shape* of the flow.  
  - If you *must* traverse a thicket:
    - Wear *thin* but full-coverage clothing:
      - Even if it is thin *membrane* layer,  
      - It *will* protect you from *some* cuts.

### 10. The Predators That You Don’t See With Claws

There are predators *evident* only by *absence of others*.

From the text:

- **“You know that by the way the fish vanish.”**
- **“You feel a pressure from the left of your head—a water-deflection that makes you want to turn your face to the right—and that you learn is the wake of something passing.”**

Habits:

1. **Always notice the *schools* of fish:**
   - Their *direction* is your map.  
   - Their *density changes* are your hazard warnings.

2. **If all fish around you *suddenly* disappear from one direction:**
   - *Assume* that *something* is there:
     - *Do not* investigate.  
     - *Change* path *very slowly* in *any* other direction.

3. **If you feel a *pressure* on your face**:
   - A *light* pressure wanting to *push*:
     - That *could* be the wake of a larger body passing slightly above you.
   - Do not immediately *swim to the surface*.  
   - Move *diagonal* from that direction and *downward* if possible:
     - But *not* so deep that you lose yourself.  

### 11. The “Thick” Places and the Gardens of the World’s Guts

There are *places* where:

> the water is thicker, more viscous,
> like syrup instead of saltwater.

These are:

- **Always slightly warmer.**  
- Often marked by **tiny black bead plants** that flash when disturbed.

You might imagine *what* might be happening there:

- Regeneration zones  
- Something spawning itself  
- Something else entirely *aware* of you.

*How* to behave:

1. **Don’t cut *through* these thick regions.**  
2. **If you must pass near them:**
   - Do not *disturb* the bead-plants unnecessarily.
3. **If you *intend* to let this world modify you:**
   - Then *someday* you might choose to *float* in such a patch:
     - You *could* find yourself regrowing or reshaping.
     - But *this* is not a step for *ordinary* humans without *very* long preparation.

### 12. Social & Emotional Protocol: You Are Not the Center of This Story

You are *not* the protagonist of this city’s story.

Prepare:

- **To learn *habits* rather than laws.**  
  - The column-beings *have* time for you, but not *your* time.
  - The fish will *always* move around you:
    > They veer away, but only as far as they need to avoid your body,
    > and then they reform on the other side of you.

    This is peace, not praise.

- **To be *always noticed*.**
  - Many eyes will be on you:
    - Eyes of fish  
    - Eyes of column-beings  
    - Eyes of topology more than you.

- **To not insist on being *dry*.**  
  - If you are *always* trying to return to the surface of the world, to see *air* and *sun* directly,  
    - This might well be seen as *rude*, or as *weakness*.
    - The city *holds* you in water.

As a traveler, you:

1. Treat **no column as inert stone** until you know for sure.  
2. Treat **no fish as just “fish”** but as *nodes of a larger entity.*  
3. Treat **the bone-thorns, the coral, and the algae** as the *skin* of the world body.

### 13. A Final Word on Leaving (or Not)

You will feel *drawn* to stay:

> The water has now become you as much as you have become the water.

For *ordinary* multiverse travelers:

1. **Before you come:**
   - Make *provision* for someone from *your* home reality to come *get* you if you:
     - Do not return *within* a specified time window;
     - Or *do* return but are not fully yourself.

2. **If you are going *beyond* ordinary:**
   - Prepare to accept that:
     - “You” might be rewritten *into* the column-body,
     - Or *your* own heartbeat might become part of the *foundation* of this world.

---

### 14. Short Checklist for the “Prepared” Traveler

Before you go:

- [ ] Basic freediving + safety training  
- [ ] Low-visibility practice + calm under pressure  
- [ ] Full-body thin suit with mask, fins, and two light sources  
- [ ] Breathing system chosen *very* deliberately:
  - Do you plan to respire water with *assist* or  
  - To *not* breathe at all but use *tools*?  
- [ ] Depth and time monitoring device  
- [ ] Adjustable buoyancy system  
- [ ] Water purifier + hydration pack  
- [ ] Minimal calorie gel food  
- [ ] Basic medical kit:
  - Sutures,  
  - Antibacterial / antifungal film,  
  - Antivenin if possible.

In the city:

- [ ] Remember *you* are not the center.  
- [ ] Watch *fish* not just *views*  
- [ ] Use *pressure* and *temperature layers* as your map  
- [ ] Touch only *very* gently and when *appropriate*  
- [ ] Avoid *thick water* unless you are ready to change *fundamentally*

---

In short: prepare to be **always immersed**, **always surrounded by others you cannot see clearly**, and **always part of a body that calls itself a city**. You may not, *and likely will not*, leave this place unchanged.

But you *can* leave it better if you treat it like *what* it is—a half-drowned, living world—and not like a ruin to be photographed, or your body as a vessel to be filled with air when you are already breathing *far more than just air*.
