You are not prepared to be here.

Nobody is.

You’re about to walk into a place that has *forgotten* people. It’s been set up for machines, then abandoned by them, and then reclaimed by water and time. The gravity of the environment is not going to kill you outright like a vacuum or molten lava, but it will scrape you down over hours and days—cold, wet, tired, slips, cuts, rust, tetani, drowning. Most of the deaths here will be “ordinary” ones: a fall through a hole, an infection from a scratch, hypothermia, a wrong step into a deep pool.

This is not an adventure package; it’s a survival expedition with a silent, industrial god overhead watching you drip.

---

## 1. Mindset and Limits

### 1.1. Expect this to be “hard travel”

You will be:
- Constantly wet and cold.
- Moving slowly on slippery surfaces.
- Never truly at ease: mentally, you need to stay aware of:
  - Where every footstep is going.
  - What you are hearing.
  - What is changing around you.

Treat each time you visit as though you’re entering a working cave-system with heavy machinery echoes and lots of vertical drops. Think of it as surface-level cave diving rather than a “tourist” site.

### 1.2. Accept that you cannot see everything

“Regional scale” undergrounds mean:
- There are parts of this floor system you simply will not reach.
- There are higher levels accessed by rusted ladders and hatches you should not trust.
- There are deeper levels under the water that you do not have the right gear or training to explore.

If you can’t accept that you will be leaving 99% of the place unseen and untouched, you shouldn’t go.

---

## 2. Physical Health & Conditioning

Before you travel, you should be:

- **Fit**: strong enough legs to walk on uneven surfaces all day without collapsing.
- **Healthy feet**: blisters, fungal infections, or ingrown nails will be mercilessly amplified by constant water.
- **Not immunocompromised**: between the moisture, microbial films, and fungi everywhere, your body will be exposed.

Especially important:
- **No major heart/lung issues**: cold air, humidity, and constant exertion are not trivial.
- **No serious mobility problems**: almost all footing surfaces are slippery, uneven, or perforated.

---

## 3. Mental Preparation: Normal and Weird

### 3.1. Dealing with “underworld”

This is a classic underworld environment:
- Constantly enclosed.
- No true horizon.
- No sense of day/night.
- Everything you see is human-built, but not human-scaled anymore.

That will wear on you:
- Expect tiredness, mild dissociation, or a feeling of “I shouldn’t be here.”
- Plan regular breaks out of the environment (even just to a higher, drier service level or back to where you came from).
- If you’re prone to claustrophobia or anxiety, you should not be deep here.

### 3.2. Expecting a “dynamic” but non-sentient environment

The sensations:
- Fluid patterns of water and light.
- Irregular dripping rhythms.
- Occasional distant machine hums and lights flickering.
- Hints of motion along high catwalks and deeper water.

Your brain will want to see “pattern” and “agency.” It’s useful to be disciplined:
- Don’t assume hostile intelligence behind every sound or shadow.
- Do assume that **anything can be precarious**: rust, fatigue, water damage, bolts under tension.

## 4. Gearing Up: The Essentials

You don’t need hypertech to survive here, but this is far more demanding than a city walk or a park.

### 4.1. Clothing and Layer System

Core principle: **you will be wet and cold.** Your goal is to manage that, not eliminate it.

- **Base layer (next-to-skin):**
  - Merino wool or synthetic designed to stay warm when damp.
  - Avoid cotton at all costs—cotton kills in cold, wet environments.
- **Insulation layer:**
  - Thinner fleece or synthetic grid-fleece (mid layer).
  - Thicker insulating layer for rest stops or if the temperature feels colder than expected.
- **Outer shell:**
  - Full waterproof and breathable hardshell jacket and over trousers.
    - Look for sealed seams and well-built closures.
  - Hood that can be adjusted so it doesn’t blind your peripheral vision.
  - Pockets accessible even with gloves on.

- **Neck and head:**
  - A buff or balaclava for warmth and to keep water dripping down your neck from chilling you too quickly.
- **Hands:**
  - At least two pairs of gloves:
    - Thin inner gloves for dexterity.
    - Outer waterproof gloves that can take scrapes against metal.
    - One spare pair in a dry bag.

- **Feet and legs:**
  - Synthetic or wool socks (two pairs, if your system can handle liner + outer).
    - Multiple spares, stored dry.
  - Waterproof, high-ankle footwear with aggressive tread:
    - Designed for wet environments, not fashion shoes.
    - No loose open-toed shoes or anything that can fill with water.

**Key:** Assume you *will* get wet; the gear is to delay hypothermia, not to keep you perfectly dry.

### 4.2. Lighting and Visibility

This is almost as critical as staying dry.

- **Primary headlight:**
  - Wide and spot mode combined.
  - At least 300–500 lumens with good long burn time.
  - Mounted on a headstrap, so your hands are free.

- **Secondary handheld light:**
  - For backup and to look into holes and under grates.
  - Waterproof.

- **Extra batteries and/or power banks:**
  - Pack more than you think you need.
  - Keep in waterproof containers.

- **Optional chem lights or marker lights:**
  - To mark paths you’ve taken or mark exits from higher floors, so you can find your way back under the ceiling half-lost in shadow.

## 5. Footwork and Footholds: Moving on the Grid

### 5.1. Understanding the flooring hazard

- The floor is a **perforated grid system**:
  - Many slots and gaps.
  - Some sections missing entirely.
  - Thin sheets of water over it all.

Every time you move:
- Look down before taking a step.
- Assume that:
  - Any grating can be weak or rusted through.
  - Any missing grating can lead deep into water below.
  - Puddles and algal films will be slippery.

### 5.2. Safe technique

- **Short, weighted steps**:  
  - Don’t stride; the next step might be onto nothing or a slick patch.
- **Stay low in uncertain areas:**
  - If a floor feels or sounds suspicious, distribute your weight:
    - Crawling or shuffling can be safer than full walking.
- **Always maintain three points of contact when near or crossing edges**:
  - Use railings only if they are solid (you must test them).
  - If you see significant rust or they feel loose under your hand: don’t trust them.

---

## 6. Temperature and Environment: Managing the Cold

### 6.1. The cold profile

- Cold air, but just above freezing point.
- Air fully saturated with moisture.
- Wind you feel only sometimes, but it draws heat away constantly.

You will feel cold from:
- Bare contact with metal.
- Conducting heat away through your boot soles.
- Cold air filtering through clothing gaps.

### 6.2. Operations strategy

- **Always wear layers** before entering.
- **Eat before and during:**  
  You need calories to generate heat.
- **Monitor yourself and your party:**
  - Shivering is fine; *stopping* shivering while still cold is dangerous.
  - If anyone starts slurring, confused, or “very sleepy” in the cold, they must be evacuated immediately.

---

## 7. Water and Risk: Don’t Drink the Place

### 7.1. Water everywhere—none of it safe on its own

- Water dripping through corrosion on metal surfaces.
- Algae films coating pools.
- Fungi and spores growing in damp wall crevices.
- Unknown machine fluids and residues from the industrial past.

You should assume:
- ANY local standing water is unsafe to drink unfiltered/unpurified.

### 7.2. Water supply strategy

- **Bring your own water** from outside:
  - Hydration bladders inside your pack, protected.
- **Accident water:**
  - Only if you must, collect from dripping sources:
    - Filter first with a reliable mechanical filter.
    - Then purify chemically or with UV light.
    - Never scoop straight off algae-covered pools or near rust blooms.

- **Hands hygiene:**
  - Avoid touching your face or food with hands wet from local water.
  - Carry alcohol-based hand sanitizer.

---

## 8. Food and Sustenance

- **Pre-packaged, sealed food:**  
  No fishing from the dark shrimp-pools for protein snacks; you don’t know what they’ve evolved into.
- **High-energy foods:**  
  Bars, nuts, chocolate, anything dense that doesn’t need heating.
- **No cooking:**  
  You do not want to light open flames or fuel-burning stoves deep underground:
  - CO risk from poorly ventilated areas.
  - Heat risk from flareups near metal structures.
- **Trash discipline:**  
  Everything you pack in, you pack out. The environment already has enough ghosts.

---

## 9. Navigation and Marking: “Don’t Get Lost Under the Floor”

This is a maze in which everything looks the same.

### 9.1. Navigation tools

- **Physical compass:**  
  - You may not be able to rely on electronic devices in the long-term.
- **Backup map and notebook:**
  - Draw rough floor layouts of where you go.
  - Mark key landmarks:
    - Distinctive machinery skeletons.
    - Unusual pillars or hatches.
    - Light constellations that are visible from your main route.

- **Radiation-style markers:**  
  - Use chalk, paint, or tape tags (if allowed by your code) to mark:
    - Paths you’ve walked.
    - Dead ends or unsafe areas.
    - Exit routes back upwards.
  - Make sure you can *remove* or follow-up on your markers if needed.

- **“Anchor line” method:**  
  - If you are going deep, you can lay out a lightweight cord line:
    - Fixed to your known exit point.
    - Followed back to safety.
    - This is more useful than you might imagine.

### 9.2. Staying oriented with the ceiling

- Look up and memorize:
  - Distinctive patterns of overhead gantries.
  - Unique clusters of flickering lights.
- Frequent look back:
  - The “return view” of the same ceiling can help you find your way out.

## 10. Biologicals and Fungi: Don’t Touch, Don’t Eat

### 10.1. Fungi on the walls and ceilings

- **Do not pick anything.**  
- No sniffing mushrooms close to your face.
- Assume that some of the pale clusters and faintly glowing stalks:
  - Are adapted to metals and corrosion.
  - Possibly have spores that shouldn’t be inhaled in large amounts.

**Protective measures**
- Keep mask (respirator), not just cloth cover:
  - Any time you must disturb close surfaces or scraping rust.
- **No bare skin contact with walls**, if you can avoid it.

### 10.2. Tiny fauna

- Translucent fauna in the wet:
  - Treats as “don’t touch”:
    - No barehand scooping out of pools.
    - No skin swimming if you find deeper water.
    - If you get any of them on you:
      - Rinse off with clean water from your pack.
      - Sanitize afterward.

---

## 11. Structural Safety: Beware of Aging Industry

### 11.1. Rusted beams and catwalks above

- Do not climb anything unless:
  - You can test its stability gently.
  - You have proper safety gear:
    - Climbing harnesses.
    - Lanyards.
    - Hard hat.
- **Main rule of thumb:**  
  If you can’t trust it, your weight shouldn’t be on it.

### 11.2. Random machinery cycles

- **Listen:**  
  - A low hum that suddenly increases is likely a machine cycling.
  - Feel for faint tremors in the handrail or floor beneath.
- **Do not:**
  - Touch any panel, lever, or switch unless you *really* understand what you’re poking.
  - Put yourself between any moving parts just because they “seem sleepy.”

---

## 12. Soundscape and Sensory Overload

You will hear:
- Drips everywhere.
- Echoes.
- Unusual metallic clangs from far above.

**Recommendation**
- Take ear protection:
  - Not for full blocking noise—but enough to remove the edge:
    - So you don’t get mentally exhausted.
    - So you can still hear big changes in the sound environment.

---

## 13. Respecting the Living System: You are the Alien Here

This is a cathedral of water and machine memory:
- It is half-converted into an unintended ecosystem.
- Be as minimal as possible:
  - Don’t break structures.
  - Don’t collect local organisms.
  - Leave no direct trace except:
    - Removable markers.
    - Your footprints evaporating over time.

---

## 14. Emotional Self-Care: The Quiet Weird of It All

It will be:
- Cold.
- Dim.
- Endless-looking.
- Quietly dripping.

You will feel:
- Out of scale.
- Out of place.
- Sometimes watched even though nothing is watching.

To help yourself:
- Keep short, clear goals:
  - “Explore this section, then back to known exit.”
  - “No more than six hours under this first time.”
- Talk out loud:
  - Describe what you are seeing and doing.
  - If you are with someone else, check in regularly:
    - Make sure each of you knows how the others feels, physically and mentally.
- Take breaks in:
  - Less dripping areas.
  - Near any stable, dry-looking bench or platform, if you can find one.

---

## 15. Exit and Decontamination

After visiting:
- **Dry everything:**  
  - Clothing, boots, harness.
  - Treats your boots specifically:
    - Clean soles of anything metal or rust; you do not want to track corrosion into your world.
- **Check your body:**
  - Any scratches or cuts:
    - Wash with clean water and soap.
    - Disinfect.
    - Monitor for signs of infection.

Think of returning as though you’ve left a deep, cave-like system:
- You’re not “done” until you’re clean, warm, dry, and checked.

---

## 16. Very Short Checklist (Before You Step into the Water)

1. **No cotton clothing.** Synthetic or wool layers.
2. **Waterproof outer layer.** Hoods, gloves, waterproof boots.
3. **Headlight + handheld backup + extra power.**  
4. **Food and water.** From outside, not from local water.
5. **First aid kit.**
6. **Mask or respirator for dust/fungi spores.**
7. **Navigation aids:** compass, notepad, markers, light line.
8. **Basic safety tools:** helmet if you plan to climb anything, harness if you plan to climb high.
9. **Someone who knows where you are going** and when you should be back.
10. **Respect for the depth, time, and drip of this place.**

---

In the end, visiting this drowned factory-cathedral is like visiting a forgotten machine god that now prays in water.

If you go prepared, you can see something extraordinarily beautiful:  
a world where human geometry becomes the geology, and water becomes the liturgy.

But go unprepared, and this cathedral will be quite indifferent to burying you in its choir floor.
