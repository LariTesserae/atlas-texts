You are not an expeditionary or an architect. You’re an ordinary person from some other reality where the sky is usually empty and buildings usually stop before you run out of oxygen. This place will try to break your assumptions about what’s normal, and it will do that mostly through humidity and angles.

If you prepare physically, socially and sensorially, you can slip through this city without injuring yourself or the system you’re stepping into.

---

## 0. Check if you should even come here

### Before you say “yes”

Ask yourself:

- Are you genuinely okay with never being fully dry for days at a time?
- Do you hate tight spaces, or heights that drop into black water?
- Do you panic easily when there are no open horizons, only walls and one central column disappearing into darkness?

If you answer “yes, that sounds like hell” to more than one of these, this locale might be more than you can comfortably tolerate. There are no wide squares here, no far horizon. The only “expanse” is a vertical, mist-filled atrium around a stone spine that in some ways behaves like a body part.

You can still visit, but you should plan shorter stays, bring more comfort items, and maybe a trusted friend.

## 1. Physical and medical preparation

### 1.1. Humidity and heat

Your skin is the main battlefield. Prepare for:

- **Constant sweat with nowhere to go.**  
  Bring several sets of thin, quick-drying underlayers, and clothing you’re okay with never truly feeling clean.
- **Chafing and rashes.**  
  Bring:
  - High-quality anti-chafing balm or glide.
  - Powder-based or antifungal barrier creams if your skin is prone to irritation.
- **Hydration.**  
  Even though the air is thick with water, you’re losing a lot through sweat and heat stress.  
  - A small, reusable bottle is fine, but include:
    - Electrolyte tabs or powder.
    - Some sugar-and-salt chews or similar.
- **Breathing.**  
  Air quality is technically okay because of the city’s ventilation systems, but the air will be **heavy**.  
  - If you have asthma or other respiratory issues, bring:
    - Inhaler or equivalent medication in a **waterproof container**.
    - Possibly a mask designed for humid environments, though locals don’t usually mask up.

### 1.2. Clothing and footgear

You’ll want to look roughly like a maintenance worker or a low-level engineer: not flashy, not obviously out of place.

#### Essentials

- **Lightweight layers**  
  - Base layer: synthetic or light linen, quick-drying.
  - Mid layer: thin, hooded if possible.
  - Optional shell: thin waterproof jacket that can breathe. If it’s too waterproof, you’ll just sweat inside it.
- **Colors and style:**  
  Grays, browns, dull greens, faded blues. These blend with the stone, the moss, and the stained metal.
- **No loose, trailing clothing:**  
  Many edges are wet and slick. You don’t want a sleeve or hem hanging near railings or ladder rungs.
- **Protective gloves:**  
  Not necessarily heavy-duty, but something:
  - With **grip** on the palms and fingers.
  - To protect against rust from railings, salt residue from dried spray, and the occasional sharp edge of metal or stone.
- **Head covering:**  
  A thin cap or bandana is useful:
  - For reducing condensation dripping from above into your hair or face.
  - For mopping sweat without your hands always being busy.

#### Footwear

Not “boots” in a military sense, but something sturdy:

- Water-resistant or quick-drying.
- Excellent grip on wet metal:
  - Ladder rungs.
  - Steps with moss or algae buildup.
- **No open-toes, no sandals.**  
  There are arthropods, small sharp edges, and occasional drops from height. Your toes will be exposed if you’re not careful.

#### A small “dry pack”

Anything that must not get wet—documents, electronics if you carry them, spare underlayer—should be in a **watertight container or a dry bag:**

- A small drybag inside your main pack isn’t overkill.
- Ziplock or oilcloth bags for paper, notes, and maps.

### 2. Gear: what to bring and what to leave out

You are not expected to show up with technical climbing gear or industrial tools. The city has its own specialists and equipment.

#### Bring

- **Basic first aid kit**
  - Bandages, including waterproof bandages.
  - Antifungal cream (for heat and moisture).
  - Small scissors, tweezers, and antiseptic wipes.
- **Compact, multi-purpose tool:**  
  Something like:
  - A lightweight knife with a small pair of pliers and a screwdriver.
  - Useful for tightening small things, prying stuck fabrics, opening ration packages, etc.
- **Light source**
  - A headlamp with adjustable brightness:
    - The city has light, but some areas are dim, or in emergency situations you may want focused light.
    - Avoid blinding locals; always be willing to switch it off.
  - Spare power or a small solar-rechargeable option if that’s compatible with your tech.
- **Notebook and pencil**
  - Pencils deal better with humidity than pens.
  - You might want to record:
    - Where you went.
    - Spire “full” vs “thin” days.
    - Changes in behavior of the spire.
    - Any strange symbols that you suspect might not just be random drill marks.
- **Water purification**
  The city has its filtration systems, but because:
  - Water leaks appear from “unknown” groundwater veins.
  - Some of the pipes are old and complex.
  
  Consider:
  - Compact filter straw or filter bottle.
  - Simple chlorine or iodine drops if that’s how you roll.
- **Small container of salt or sugar**
  - For topping up electrolytes or helping barter with others.
  - Sugar (especially in compact form like tablets or syrup packets) can be a good offering or small-trade item.

#### Leave at home

- **Anything that requires stable dry air:**  
  - Delicate paper books.
  - Fine electronics that are easily destroyed by moisture.
- **Heavy outer armor or “survivalist” kits:**  
  You will stand out. You are not supposed to be prepared for war here; you’re meant to look like someone who belongs to the system.

### 3. Money and exchange

You don’t need “money” in the usual sense of coins from your world, but you need a way to participate.

#### Bring with you

- **Small items of trade:**  
  - Strong thread or slimline multitools.  
  - Tightly sealed packages of spices, tea, or coffee (if moisture-proofed).  
  - Simple medical supplies like bandages or antiseptic wipes.
- **A willingness to do labor:**  
  If you can show you’re competent:
  - Basic maintenance tasks.
  - Holding things.
  - Helping move supplies.
  
  You can trade “work for food” in a lot of places.

#### Learn on arrival

- **Local barter tokens:**  
  - There might be small ceramic tokens, bits of metal or chits marked by the artisans’ rings or the admin on the wall-side.
- **Accepted “luxuries”:**
  - Anything that helps with dryness, skin comfort, or better sleep.
  - Mild stimulants (if they are accepted and legal there).
  - New information, if you can share it.

### 4. Social and cultural safety

### 4.1. Look like you belong

If you stand out as a tourist from another reality, you will be:

- Stared at.
- Avoided.
- Maybe occasionally questioned by engineers or their enforcers.

Dress like:

- **Mid-level maintenance worker:**  
  - Practical clothes, not ragged, not immaculate.
  - A small tool belt or pouch at your hip.
  - Work gloves hanging from a loop or your belt.
- **Low-level record keeper or apprentice:**  
  - If you can convincingly carry a notebook and pencil and look like you’re taking notes, people may assume you’re on assignment from the admin side.

Avoid:

- Gaudy colors.
- Oversymbolic “cloaks of power” or otherworldly uniforms.
- Walking around visibly “lost” looking at maps all the time.

### 4.2. How to speak to locals

Here are phrases that will help you:

- **“I’m new here. Which guild is in charge of this stair?”**  
- **“I’ve been assigned to Level 7. Is there a guide schedule or a ladder maintenance roster?”**
- **“Is the spire full today or thin?”**

Do not say:

- “What is this strange place?”
- “I’m from another universe.”
- “Doesn’t that stone freak you out?”

### 4.3. Understanding the power structure

- **Engineers**  
  - They design the support systems, the water, the heat exchangers.
  - The engineering guilds have strong authority over the vertical city.
- **Water “plumbers” and pipe specialists**
  - They handle the leaks and the unexpected seepages.
  - They can be blunt, but they are practically essential.
- **The Archivist and her staff**
  - If you can get access to the Archivist’s chamber above the Lung:
    - Be polite, precise, and have prepared questions.
    - Don’t waste their time on “tourist chatter”.
    - They show the stone slates and diagrams. You might be allowed to copy some notes.
- **General populace**
  - They are not “quiet” because they are oppressed by fear; they are quiet because:
    - Sound carries vertically in this chamber.
    - It is too hot to be yelling constantly.
    - They are used to being in close proximity with strangers, and being loud is impolite.

### 4.4. Respect the space’s weirdness, but don’t show fear

You can:

- Ask carefully-worded questions:
  - “Is it always this full on the same day of the machine cycle?”
  - “Have the symbols on the spire always been there?”
- Express **interest** rather than shock or fear:
  - “It’s a beautiful system” vs “This is terrifying and unnatural.”

You can’t:

- Impress them with stories from your world without being careful:
  - If you talk about “open sky” and “outside air,” some people will be interested, some may be skeptical or nervous.
- Talk about the “living” stone as if you are an expert:
  - They’ve seen this entire structure from pre-drilling to present.
  - They don’t need a stranger telling them what their spire is or isn’t.

### 5. Navigating the chamber itself

### 5.1. Getting around

#### Overall layout

- **Central axis:** The spire of breath.
- **Around it:** A ring of air.
- **Around the air:** The city of stacked rings and platforms.

#### Movement types

- **Ladders**
  - Many are open metal; they are almost always damp with humidity or condensate.
  - Use three points of contact (two feet and at least one hand) whenever possible.
- **Spiral stairs and metal steps**
  - Often narrow with tiny landings.
  - If you meet someone going the opposite way:
    - The person going down usually passes to the inside, closer to the spire.
    - The person going up steps to the outside rail.
- **Lifts and pulleys**
  - Some lifts are heavy cargo systems; if you’re invited to step on one, do:
    - Stand exactly where they tell you, not a step closer to the edge.
  - Pulley platforms:
    - Don’t jump or shift suddenly; you will cause them to swing.

### 5.2. Levels of risk

- **Highest level of safety:**  
  The main inhabited rings, where:
  - There is strong guard rail.
  - There are regular light sources.
  - Frequency of foot traffic is high.
- **Medium risk:**  
  - Narrower access platforms, gantries around the edges, or less used industrial areas.
- **Higher risk (only if you are confident or with local guide):**  
  - Small, unused platforms that are overgrown with moss and algae.
  - The edges near the empty central shaft.
    - The rail is typically there, but the metal may be more corroded.
    - The gap of ten meters looks small on paper; in reality, it is a long drop into water.

### 5.3. The “inner edge” and the water basin

- **Do not:**
  - Throw anything into the basin “just to see the splash.”
  - Dangle your body out over the edge.
  - Try to touch the spire from the main platforms unless it is clearly allowed and there are handholds.

- **Do:**
  - Observe from the inner edge if:
    - You are not crowded.
    - You are not blocking the path of maintenance workers or engineers.
    - If a “double tide” is happening:
      - Extra water and machinery maintenance coincide.
      - People may be more stressed than usual.
      - Stay out of the way of crews working.

### 5.4. The “double tide”

When you hear:

- “Double tide today”
- Or see the water level slightly higher,  
  and the condensation is strong and the teams are out:

- **Keep your head up**
  - The water won’t “flood” the city in any normal sense you’d expect from surface stories.
  - But:
    - Platforms may get slightly more slippery.
    - Crews might be working on filters near the basin you shouldn’t approach.

### 5.5. Electricity and lighting

- **Industrial lamps:** bright sodium-white glare.
- **Blue emergency lighting:** usually indicates that:
  - You are near cooling systems or filter plants.
  - You might be near restricted areas where only engineers are allowed.
- **If lights are flickering or dimming in a section,**
  - Don’t panic, but:
    - Find out what the locals are doing.
    - If they’re moving away, follow along.
    - They might know:
      - There is a short in the system.
      - An engineering test is underway.
      - Some of the older cables are finally being replaced.

### 6. Safety and sense of self

### 6.1. Mental preparation

This will feel like:

- An inside-out world:
  - The sky is not above; it is an artificial glow of lamps and a faint water-surface.
  - The horizon is not far; it is a wall of stone that you can reach out and almost touch.
- You must be comfortable with constant:
  - **Verticality:** looking down and up more than across.
  - **Proximity:** living close to others, with virtually no private outdoor space.
  - **Humidity:** never being fully dry, never feeling that “open air” freshness.

If you have:

- Claustrophobia.
- Fear of heights.
- A strong need for open sky.

You might want to:

- Choose short trips rather than long stays.
- Plan regular “breaks” by:
  - Returning to your home layer of the multiverse.
  - Or at least, to some other, more open reality.

### 6.2. Rituals and routines

Build yourself:

- **Morning check**
  - Inspect clothes for mold if they’ve been hanging in a corner for more than a day or two.
  - Check if the humidity feels “full” or “thin.”
  - Check if there are unusual symbols appearing on the spire if you can see it from where you are.
- **Evening wind-down**
  - Dry your body thoroughly as best you can.
  - Change into whatever set is least damp if you have multiple options.

### 6.3. Boundaries and interactions

- **Touching the spire of breath**
  - If locals allow you to:
    - Only lightly; the surface is not smooth everywhere.
    - During the condensation peaks:
      - It might feel “too like skin” for your comfort.
      - If you’re disturbed by that sensation, don’t pretend to be scientific.
      - Just step back and say “That’s not something I’m used to” rather than making a big show of it.
- **Do not remove**
  - Samples from the spire:
    - The engineers and archivists treat this column as both structural and sacred data.
    - Chipping away pieces might be treated like vandalism or sabotage.
- **Do not claim**
  - That the “spire is alive” in a loud or public way:
    - Some people will be quietly sympathetic.
    - Others will be irritated or wary of you.
- **Do not**
  - Try to “fix” or “improve” the systems:
    - You do not know the full water cycle.
    - You do not know the load or the structural stress.
    - Even something small could be dangerous if it changes the distribution of weight or moisture.

### 7. What if something happens?

### 7.1. If you get injured

- **Shallow cuts or scrapes**  
  - Wash with clean water (from official sources, not random leak).
  - Apply antiseptic.
  - Cover with waterproof bandage if possible.
- **Large or deep injury**
  - Find an engineer or any person in uniform:
    - They will either:
      - Guide you to the infirmary or the maintenance station.
      - Know someone who can stitch.
- **Falls, even small ones**
  - If you slip from just a few steps:
    - Still report it to whoever’s nearest:
      - To help you.
      - To help them repair that spot if it’s consistently slippery.

### 7.2. If you see “weirder than usual”

For example:

- The symbols on the spire seem to have changed or moved.
- Water flows from a new place that wasn’t leaking before.
- The “pulse-like” sensation at the stone surface is strong enough that others are commenting.

Do not be the first person to yell “something strange is happening!” Instead:

- Observe what the locals do:
  - If they are cautious and murmuring, but continuing their work:
    - This might be within the range of “normal strange.”
  - If they stop work and start clustering:
    - Prepare yourself that:
      - You might need to:
        - Move to higher level.
        - Move away from the central shaft.
- **During any “event”**
  - Stay away from the edges.
  - Do not block the ladders or lifts.
  - If instructed to go somewhere:
    - Ask only one clarifying question if needed:
      - “Where?” or “Which level?”
    - Then move.

### 7.3. If you are questioned by the authorities

- Be honest about:
  - “I’m a traveler.”
  - “I don’t belong to any guild.”
- But not too honest:
  - “I’m from another reality” might confuse them or alarm them, depending on their grasp of such things.
  - “I’m from a surface community that doesn’t keep records in this style” is safer.
- Show:
  - Your notebook.
  - Your silence.
  - Your willingness to comply and leave.

### 8. Ethical and metaphysical preparation

You are visiting not just a strange city, but something that may be in the middle of “becoming an organ.”

### 8.1. Symmetry in respect

- Do not secretly:
  - Treat this as a “dungeon” or “adventure site.”
  - Take from the spire or the basin simply “to see what happens.”
- Do:
  - Treat the locals as experts who are still learning.
  - Treat the spire as if it were:
    - Both a machine and a living body.
    - Whether you believe it or not.

### 8.2. Leaving your mark

Do not:

- Engrave your name or symbols of your world.
- Leave “test” blocks of your magic or technology:
  - That might disrupt the spire.
  - Or might be interpreted as sabotage.

Do:

- Leave:
  - Quiet notes of gratitude:
    - To a particular host.
    - To the Archivist, if she allowed you to see records.
- Leave:
  - Small but useful tools:
    - A better pen, a better notebook.
    - A better drawing method, if they are open to it.

### 9. Final check before stepping through

As you stand at the airlock on Level 7—or whatever your arrival point will be—you’re about to walk into the body of a world that doesn’t show a sky.

### 9.1. Before you open that door

- Mentally:
  - Stop thinking of “outside” and “inside” as absolute.
  - Think of “core” and “shell,” instead.
- Physically:
  - Adjust your layers.
  - Check that your bag is sealed against moisture.
  - Make sure your headlamp and tools are easy to reach but not dangling loose.

### 9.2. As you step in

You will notice:

- The smell of wet stone and living green.
- The faint hum of machines and the occasional drop of water.
- That the place in your chest that usually “waits for the sky” is now forced to look up, not out, and to see:
  - A stone column disappearing into darkness.
  - A faint glow of city windows and a ring of mist around the breath of the spire.

If you have prepared yourself well, you will not need to survive this place as if you were storming a fortress. You will simply be another person standing at the rail of Level 7, note in hand, watching the slow, sweat-born river of the world’s interior.

The city will not roar to welcome you. It will whisper, and move on with its dripping. Your job is not to conquer it or decode it, but to learn what it can spare you of time, water and light.
