Karak-Eshul is a kind of perceptual trap: it looks like a very high, stone‑age city on a volcanic plateau, but its real danger is the *psychology* of its perfectly static clock, day after identical day. If you prepare as if you’re only climbing a mountain, you will survive the altitude but be eaten by monotony, or by violating a law you never realized was written into the stones of the street.

### 0. Before You Commit to Going

Ask yourself: *Do you actually need to visit this place*?

If you are going just because you’ve heard of a “Spine of the World” and “black glass city under the clouds,” the answer is probably no. This is not a tourist destination. It’s a research site for anyone who studies:

- psychological time loops
- ritualistic societies
- non‑technological but precise architectures
- environments where pattern *never* varies.

If you still need to go—because you are studying the city, or someone there is holding something you must retrieve, or some higher power attaches a penalty to refusing—then prepare accordingly.

---

### 1. Mindset: You Are Entering a Time Trap

Imagine you’re preparing to live inside a perfectly remembered day—someone else’s memory, unchanging and always replayed.

The sky of Karak-Eshul is not like your home’s. If you come from a world with weather, you will unconsciously wait for:

- cloud complexity,
- shifts of wind,
- the next storm front,
- a different sunset tomorrow.

None of that will happen here.

If you come from a world with noise:

- sirens in the distance,
- engines,
- music,
- overlapping voices,

you will unconsciously expect variation in the soundscape.

None of that will happen here.

Over days, your brain’s “predictive engine” will start to send error signals because the world keeps *matching* exactly what you expect, instead of varying slightly. This feels:

- first like a curious peace,
- then like being trapped,
- then like a slowly tightening vise.

You are walking into a place that will break or twist your sense of time more than your body.

Prepare for that *first*.

### 2. The Time Blocks of Karak-Eshul: Internal Map the Day Before You Step Onto the Plateau

You will be living under a law of four segments:

1. **Watch (sunrise to mid-morning)**
   - The city wakes up, but in a subtle way.
   - Movements are small, distributed.
   - People’s attention is on the light and the sky’s behavior.
   - The city feels “patient” rather than sleepy.

2. **Work of Stone (midday light)**
   - Normal light, strong shadows.
   - Main period of manual labor, especially stonework.
   - Carrying, shaping, laying.
   - Vocalizations tend to be short, precise, if any at all.

3. **Walk of Birds (late afternoon)**
   - The sky, such as it is, is quieter.
   - Steel-blue birds call in exactly the same phrases and intervals.
   - People’s movement is more “processional” than functional.
   - This is when you will likely feel most nostalgic for your own world’s variety of bird calls.

4. **Silence of Teeth (long evening)**
   - The amber light of this “world” holds.
   - This is the law where the *karals* come down.
   - The city stills.
   - The law requires *your* teeth to be silent too: not chattering, not grinding, not chewing.

You should memorize these segments and their expected emotional textures:

- If you enter during Watch, you must be ready to wait and not make a scene.
- If you enter during Work of Stone, you must adopt a posture of “precise work.”
- If you enter during Walk of Birds, you must match your movements to its pattern rather than racing to your destination.
- If you enter during Silence of Teeth, you must already be in control of your jaw muscles and your breath.

Also accept: each day will be the same frame repeated. You will not wake to “a better day” tomorrow.

### 3. Physical and Medical Prep: Altitude, Thin Air, Stillness

### 3.1. Acclimatization to High Altitude

You are going to something *just under the clouds* on what sounds like a world reasonably sized, but clearly high. The description:

> “The air is thin, but the breathable sort of thin; cool enough to make your lungs feel sharp and clear…not cold, but not warm.”

So you are not at risk of death from thin atmosphere alone, but you are in a high-altitude environment.

- Before departure:
  - Get as high as you can on your own world for a few days to build some tolerance to low oxygen, if your world allows.
  - Brisk hiking or stair climbing while breath training is useful.
  - Hydration helps with altitude; practice drinking water regularly well before arrival.

- On arrival:
  - Move slowly *on purpose* for the first day.
  - No sprinting, no running, even if you feel panic.
  - Your breath will feel sharp, clear, but don’t be fooled—this can feel energizing but then drain you quickly.

Bring (if your multiverse tech allows):

- Portable oxygen source or mild oxygen booster.
- Basic anti-altitude sickness meds if you can get them.
- Familiar painkillers for the headaches that newness and stress can cause.

### 3.2. Temperature Stability, but Stillness Sickness

“Temperature is constant in Karak-Eshul: it always feels the same way at the same time of day, each day, exactly the same.”

This means:

- You will not need layers of thermal clothing in the way you’d need for high mountains.
- You will not need rain protection; the clouds are rare and “visit” the edge of the plateau rather than pouring down rain.
- You *will* need to avoid:
  - overheating from internal activity (no running even if you are agitated),
  - under-moving from seductive stillness.

Your “temperature risk” is not external weather; it’s *self-heating and then chilling* from quietness.

### 3.3. Food and Water in a Place Where Water is Precious but Unchangeable

They live from:

- dew,
- condensation,
- and clouds that occasionally kiss the plateau edge.

This is not a world of rivers or snowpack.

For you, this means:

- Ask yourself whether you are willing to drink water *only* from small sources in a stone city.
- If you are from any world with robust water treatment, you should assume the local water is biologically potent but also potentially foreign.

Your prep:

- Bring a water purification filter, or purification tablets if you can.
- Carry a lightweight container that can capture dew or condensation from surfaces.
- Be *very* respectful of their collection pools:
  - never step directly in them,
  - no washing,
  - no throwing anything into them.

Food:

- You will not be able to count on “changing menus” or “market days.”
- Local food is likely:
  - limited plant matter,
  - possibly goat from the plateau herds,
  - whatever stored or traded foods they have.

Bring your own compact calorie supply if you have any access to preserved food from your own world:

- high-energy, bland items that don’t smell much,
- nothing that *requires* cooking unless you can bring an unobtrusive heat source.
- Avoid strong spices or anything that might make you smell or behave differently from the locals.

### 3.4. Body and Breath: Your Own Soundscape

Your first discipline in Karak-Eshul is your own body:

- You cannot be a person who coughs constantly or frequently sniffles.
- If you have seasonal allergies or asthma, get that under control before coming.
- Any chronic coughs, throat clicks, or joint sounds:
  - try to minimize them,
  - or plan to move carefully and always at appropriate times of day.

You are entering a place where “almost silence” is the law. You must be quieter than you think possible.

### 4. Clothing and Tools: Don’t Look Like You’re “Here to Fix It”

This is a stone‑age city with:

- simple stone tools,
- no metal,
- no wheel,
- no engine.

You must not arrive as:

- a machine,
- a magician,
- or a god in metal armor.

### 4.1. Clothing Materials and Colors

Choose:

- Natural fabrics (if you can): wool, linen, leather, but tailored *modestly*.
- Colors that echo the palette of the plateau:
  - pale greys,
  - stone whites,
  - blacks,
  - dark greens,
  - no bright synthetic dyes if you can avoid it.

You should look like someone who:

- walks quietly,
- hasn’t brought a whole civilization of tools with them,
- could be a worker or observer of stonework.

### 4.2. Footwear

You need:

- Footwear that lets you feel the ground:
  - thin soles or layered soles that are still sensitive,
  - good grip on stone surfaces,
  - silent material—no squeaking soles.

Ledge walking and thin edge drop-offs are part of the city’s environment:

- You may be on the narrow terraces above the sheer drop of the mountain spine.
- If you bring modern shoes, ensure they are:
  - not loud,
  - not brightly colored,
  - and not inherently “machine.”

### 4.3. Tools and Gadgets

You are likely to bring:

- water purifier,
- a notebook and pencil,
- a small medical kit,
- maybe time-keeping devices from your world.

Everything else:

- Keep it hidden.
- No metal on show, unless it is a small tool that looks like a simple implement.
- No electronics with visible lights or audible beeps.
- No *devices* that have obvious complex mechanisms.

This city doesn’t move on wheels; you should not look like someone who moves with machinery either.

### 5. Languages and Communication in a Silent City

“No wheel creaks in the streets of Karak-Eshul. No engine turns. The only sounds of the city are the quiet of things at the bottom of a deep well of silence.”

Their language is likely:

- signaled subtly,
- spoken with very low volume,
- perhaps not a full verbal language as you know it.

This is not certain from the description, but given the laws of time blocks and the karals’ teeth, you should assume:

- You will not shout.
- You will not chatter loudly.
- You will not laugh loudly.

Prepare:

- Learn a few signs of *basic peace* (from your own world or multiverse):
  - open palms,
  - head bows,
  - gentle lowering of gaze,
  - not making direct eye contact at first if you don’t know their customs.

You’ll likely need to:

- Observe carefully and *wait* for the locals to reveal the patterns of their speech.
- Accept that you may not understand everything at first, and you must not demand clarification.

### 6. Learning the Real Law: Time of Day, How to Move, When to Be Still

### 6.1. Watch (sunrise to mid-morning)

- When you arrive:
  - Stand still.
  - Look up the Spine of the World (the central street) from a distance if you can.
  - Notice how people watch:
    - they are not looking at each other,
    - they are not looking at devices,
    - they are looking at *light and edges*.

You should:

- Match your gaze to the direction of others, but stay *invisible* as a new object.
- Don’t break the line of sight or the stillness of observation.

### 6.2. Work of Stone (midday)

- If you are seen during this time block, you should be:
  - touching stone,
  - carrying stone,
  - or assisting with some basic work of the city.

If you do not know how to work stone:

- Find *simple* tasks:
  - moving small pieces,
  - sweeping dust,
  - anything that doesn’t require you to pretend expert knowledge.
- Do not show your own tools if they are obviously advanced; better to use your hands for now.

### 6.3. Walk of Birds (late afternoon)

- This is *their* ceremonial time of the day.
- You should:
  - walk slowly,
  - not hurry, even if you fear the light ending;
  - stay *linear* if you are on the Spine of the World street,
  - do not weave across lines of people or cut paths diagonally.

If you are walking near the edge of the plateau, where the steel-blue birds live:

- Do not attempt to touch the birds or their nests.
- Do not mimic their calls to show off your own cleverness; this is not a place for “show.”

### 6.4. Silence of Teeth (evening)

This is the most dangerous law for you:

- “Teeth of Karak-Eshul’s dead.”
- The karals come down from their nests in the mountain face.
- Their teeth are flat and stone-like.
- Humans *must not* make noise with teeth during this time block.

Your preparation:

- Practice relaxation of jaw muscles *before* you arrive, so you can keep:
  - teeth apart,
  - jaw slightly loose,
  - no grinding teeth,
  - no chewing food during this period.

If you have a habit of chewing nails or gum:

- Stop that habit *before* arrival.
- Do not bring anything that requires chewing or crunching; you will not be able to use it safely in Silence of Teeth.

On the plateau:

- Do not move at the edge of cliffs where karals are likely to be grazing.
- If you must be near them at this time:
  - Move so slow your body appears to be part of the slow light,
  - Keep extra space,
  - Do not show fear, but show *respect* by stillness.

### 7. Walking the City: Where to Go and What Not to Touch

### 7.1. Streets of the Spine of the World

The central avenue of Karak-Eshul is called “The Spine of the World” like the mountain itself.

This street:

- Is made of polished stone and black glassy volcanic flow.
- Is the main axis of the urban grid.
- Will be one of the few straight lines you can reliably follow.

Walking on the Spine:

- You must not walk too fast or too slow *relative to the current block of time*.
- If people are moving slowly (like in Watch or Silence), you move slow.
- If you use a map from your own world, keep it hidden or use it from memory; no obvious devices.

### 7.2. Buildings and Materials

You will see:

- Pale grey stone blocks from the mountain.
- Simple walls with low clay mortar.
- Flat roofs of earth and pebbles on lower levels,
- Black glass roofs on upper levels near the priests and astronomers.

You must not:

- Climb any buildings,
- Step onto roofs without invitation,
- Touch the black glass roofs as if they are art objects.

Those black glass roofs are likely:

- part of their light-capture and time observation system,
- their sacred marks.

Also:

- Do not touch any tools that obviously belong in the hands of the stoneworkers.
- If you touch *any* stonework, you’re involved with the law and work of this city.

### 7.3. Plants and Water Sources

Strange growths:

- **Zarun**: thick, dark, almost black leaves; edges with swollen water sacs.
- **Drushka**: tall thin spindly reeds, pale grey.

They are unusual to you but normal to this world.

You must not:

- pull up any plants,
- step over drushka patches,
- kick zarun leaves.

The water of the city:

- drips,
- collects,
- occasionally falls from clouds that clip the plateau edge.

You must not:

- disturb any collection pools,
- wash your feet or hands in their water sources,
- treat the water as if it is an infinite resource from your home.

### 8. Psychological and Emotional Survival: How to Not Break Inside

This is the most important preparation section.

### 8.1. Understand the Oncoming Drift

You are likely used to:

- task lists,
- multiple deadlines,
- social media, or at least social interruptions,
- a variety of noises and colors and change.

In Karak-Eshul, you will have:

- the same four blocks of day,
- repeated,
- the same sounds,
- the same amount of light,
- the same temperature for each block of time.

The *drift* is when you no longer remember what it is like to want:

- new things,
- new events,
- fresh news.

Your perception of time can collapse into “always now.”

Before you go:

- Write detailed letters or journal entries to yourself from your home environment,
- Explain specifically *why* you are going,
- Attach to that purpose *hard*,
- Because that will be the only thing that doesn’t drift in the unchanging light of Karak-Eshul.

### 8.2. Anchors to Keep Yourself from Floating Away

Find *three* anchors *before* you go:

1. **One concrete anchor**  
   - Something physical on you:
     - a small object from your world,
     - a token,
     - not something you show off,
     - but something you touch in secret to remind you of home.

2. **One time anchor**  
   - A method of counting days *from your culture*,
     - notches in a hidden piece of wood,
     - a small string of knots,
     - something that belongs *only* to you,
   - Do not try to impose your timekeeping on the locals.

3. **One mental anchor**  
   - A short sentence or phrase that encapsulates:
     - who you are,
     - what you came from,
     - why you agreed to step here.
   - Repeat this in your mind when the silence feels like it’s going to swallow your future.

### 8.3. Dealing with “Sameness of Light”

Because the temperature is always the same at each block of time, and the sun’s path is identical each day, you have no:

- “weather tiredness”
- “storm exhaustion”
- “winter blues”

But you will get:

- *sameness exhaustion*
- a subtle loss of capacity to judge urgency.

Your countermeasures:

- Create a small, *private* ritual of variation:
  - walk different small side paths of the city the same time each day,
  - but rotate *which* side you choose,
  - this is not allowed to be visible to others; only visible to you.
- Use the *silent* variations of your own inner world:
  - think through different memories from home,
  - rotate them,
  - not out loud,
  - not in writing if others might see.

This is how you keep yourself from being “eaten by the law” of the city without actually violating the law.

### 9. The Local Creatures: Goats, Birds, and Karals

### 9.1. Wide-Horned Goats on the Terraces

“The low of a herd of wide-horned mountain goats…echoes strangely thin.”

They are their livestock, but they are also likely:

- carriers of meaning,
- tied to the *idea of stone*,
- possibly not fully domesticated in the sense you know from home.

What you should do:

- Avoid animals roaming at the edge where drop-offs are sheer.
- Let *them* move around you;
  - do not fuss them,
  - do not call them,
  - no petting.

### 9.2. Steel-Blue Birds

“Steel-blue birds of the plateau call out to each other in the Walk of Birds—sharp cries of two and three and four notes that are always the same two and three and four notes.”

These birds are a signal system of this world and time block.

You should:

- learn to recognize the patterns of their calls,
- understand that *any* deviation from their expected call pattern might be a message about the city or world state,
- not *ever* try to catch them or mimic their calls exactly in public.

### 9.3. Karals: Fur, Feathers, Flat Stone Teeth

“Pendulous nests of the karals…size of large wolves…only descend during late evening when the law called the Silence of Teeth holds sway.”

They are *not* described as violent or predatory, but:

- They are adapted to *this* law.
- They are interacting with the dead or stone of the city in some way.
- They are not domesticated animals; you should not treat them like pets or work animals.

Your safety rules:

- Keep *all* of your teeth silent:
  - no grinding,
  - no chewing,
  - even no clicking.
- Do not move between karals and the cliff edge:
  - you might block them from escape routes,
  - or increase pressure and misunderstanding.
- If you encounter one at close range:
  - move steadily,
  - not jerking,
  - show you are not a threat,
  - but don’t “apologize” with your body language;
  - just be neutral and distant.

### 10. Departure Strategy: How to Leave Without Breaking the Silence of the World

You should not simply *disappear* from the city of the Spine of the World if you have stayed any meaningful length of time. The city thrives on:

- unchanging events,
- non-chaotic behavior,
- predictable patterns.

Your leaving:

- Decide on a departure block *in advance*:
  - If you have been mostly seen in Work of Stone, then leave *after* a block of Work,
  - leaving the city not during the Silence of Teeth,
  - preferably not during the Walk of Birds if you have been observed walking with the locals then.

Your steps:

1. Stop collecting new obligations:
   - no more stonework demonstrations,
   - no more offers of help with tasks,
   - gently step out of chains of work.

2. Slowly reduce your presence in the central Spine:
   - move more to the edges of the city,
   - but beware of terrain;
   - there are still sheer drop-offs.

3. On your final day:
   - wake in the Watch,
   - complete the day block by block,
   - leave *after* you have demonstrated the complete cycle at least once more.

4. Departure Space:
   - Go to the edge of the plateau where you are least visible,
   - but do not stand near the edges where the karals are likely to be nesting,
   - prepare your multiverse traveler device or method without *lighting up the sky*,
   - do not leave any trash of your world; take everything with you.

5. Last gesture:
   - If you have been respectful and silent, you may leave a small, *totally nonelectronic* token:
     - a piece of stone from your home world,
     - or something that *looks* like it could belong to this environment,
   - Do not leave anything metal or plastic.

Your leaving should be:

- as silent as your arrival,
- as invisible as possible,
- something that the city of the Spine of the World can easily forget, if it wants to.

### 11. Final Personal Check: Before Opening the Door to This Place

Ask yourself:

- Are you able to keep your teeth silent in an environment of total sameness?
- Are you able to live without manifest change from day to day?
- Are you able to *not* show your world’s technology to stone people?
- Are you able to *not* explain or “improve” their city?

If you can say yes to these questions and still feel the need to go there, then:

- Prepare your body for the altitude and the stillness,
- Prepare your mind for the sameness and the silence,
- Prepare your heart for the possibility that part of you will always be left standing on that spine of stone under the clouds.

And then, when you are ready:

- Step onto the plateau of Karak-Eshul,
- Walk the Spine of the World,
- Listen carefully,
- And never forget the first lesson they taught themselves on the day of their birth:

> Silence is not just quiet.
> Silence is the first law of the world.
