Visiting this place is not like “travel.” It is closer to being deliberately placed inside a living body that remembers being a city. Most of the usual multiverse traveler’s toolkit—maps, power cells, weapons—will not protect you from what happens to your mind and your sense of self here.

But you can still prepare.

### 1. What kind of place this actually is (before gear)

You’re not just visiting a location; you’re entering a system whose rules are alien to human perception:

- **Time is done.** This is less a place in time and more a finished shape of time. Things don’t “happen” so much as they already *are*.
- The **sound** is not an environment; you will be inside the sound of a world-lung.
- The **water** is omnipresent as humidity, sweat, and memory, not as rivers or rain.
- There are **no animals, no tools, no machines**, only plant-like structures that have colonized stone and life-like shapes that may not have been “born” in any way you recognize.
- It **eats identity**. You can start to feel like you were always there, like you are one more layer in the fossil of the city.

Think of this as entering a digestive system or a lung. You are tissue passing through a body that is not malicious, but profoundly not built for you.

The preparation is not about “safety” in the usual sense. It’s about **giving yourself enough structure to remember who you are and to return with at least a part of yourself intact**.

## 2. Mental and Emotional Preparation

### 2.1 Accept the baseline risk

Before you even rig your gear, understand this:

- You **will not be the same person** when you return.
- You might **not** return at all—not in the sense of dying, but in the sense of being conceptually rewritten into the world’s structure.
- This is not a “site to see”; it’s an **ontological environment**.

If you need guarantees to stay sane, do not go here. If you can tolerate the possibility of profound loss of self, then you can begin to prepare.

### 2.2 Spend time before your departure practicing *non-identification*

You are going into a realm that blurs the boundary between “you” and “world.” You will not be able to rely on “this is me and that is the environment.”

The time before your departure:

- Train **deconstruction of self-image**:
  - Meditation practice centered on observing thoughts without identifying them as “you.”
  - Exercises where you deliberately imagine your body as just a tool or vessel, rather than “who you are.”
- The goal is not to disintegrate yourself, but to **stop clinging tightly to the idea of an immutable self.** You’re going to a place that will loosen that glue anyway. You want to loosen it beforehand, under your control.

### 2.3 Anchor memories to non-visual cues

This city will constantly try to overwrite your memory: “You have always been here.” To fight this:

- Before departure, choose **3 concrete anchors**:
  - A specific physical tic (e.g., a way you tap your fingers).
  - A short phrase or motto you associate with your home reality.
  - One particular smell from home (not cinnamon, since this world perverts that).
- These anchors are for **tests of selfhood**:
  - If you cannot recall the phrase, or your tic doesn’t feel like it “belongs” to you anymore, that is a red flag that you are being rewritten into the world’s structure.

### 2.4 Emotional hygiene: precommitments and epistolary self

Prepare the idea of **not staying** even if the city feels like home. Write yourself letters before leaving:

- A clear description of who you are now.
- A list of things you want to return *to*.
- A statement that you will not trust any sense of “I have always been here” without questioning it.

Test the world’s suggestion that you are part of it against these prewritten statements.

## 3. Physical and Sensory Preparation

You are going into a place that is not cold, but relentless heat, damp, and constant sound. Your body will not be comfortable; it might not even register correctly after a time.

### 3.1 Conditioning for heat and humidity

- **Heat acclimatization**:
  - Train in hot, humid environments if possible.
  - Practice not panicking when your body feels like it’s “sweating from the inside.”
- The city will make you feel like you’re breathing **liquid air**. Get used to that:
  - Practice slow breathing through heavy humidity or with steam. Learn not to fight the sensation that you’re inhaling water.

### 3.2 Sound tolerance

The wind is not just noise; it is a constant smear across all frequencies. Prepare to live *inside* an auditory field:

- Before the trip:
  - Expose yourself to **white or pink noise** at moderate volume for prolonged intervals.
  - Try to perform tasks under such noise: read, write, move around your room.
- The goal is not to block the sound (you can’t) but to **not let panic attach to it**.

Consider the sound of the world as an **organ**: not hostile, not friendly, just present.

### 3.3 Vision and optical distortion

This place will mess with your sense of distance and orientation:

- Pre-travel training:
  - Practice standing with your eyes closed and then opening them to locate your position.
  - Wear occluded lenses periodically, simulating poor or distorted vision.
- You will not be able to trust your eyes alone. We will build tools that let you verify “upright” and “direction” independently of your visual field.

## 4. Identity and Continuity Safeguards

### 4.1 The Pre-Departure Contract

Write a contract to yourself, including:

- Your name, as you currently understand it.
- Five proofs of continuity:
  - Your home reality identifier.
  - The date of departure from your point of view.
  - The name of someone who expects you back.
  - A short phrase in your native language, not translatable to this world.
  - A signature line that you will not trust if you cannot reproduce correctly.

Keep the contract on you at all times.

### 4.2 The Companion Object

You may not be able to bring living things with you safely, but you can bring a **non-living, non-functional tool** that is not useful to the world:

- A sealed vial of dust from your home.
- A piece of metal with symbols etched that you define as meaning *you*.
- Do not bring anything with biological components or anything that could be metabolically used by the plant-architecture of this world.

If the world begins to absorb your companion object into its structure, you are being metabolized. See that as a hard signal.

## 5. Equipment and Materials

### 5.1 Clothing and skin protection

- **Breathing and barrier protection**:
  - A fine mask or semi-permeable filter layer is not to keep the sound out but to keep **your breath** from merging immediately with the world’s breath.
  - Something over your mouth and nose helps remind you that “you” are the air inside you.
- **Material choice**:
  - Avoid natural fibers that the world’s root system might treat as material to be incorporated.
  - Synthetics or metallics that are strongly not-this-world are better.
- **Temperature management**:
  - The air is hot and wet. You want garments that do not trap heat against your body but allow heavy evaporation. You cannot escape the heat, but you can avoid overheating via poor design.
- **Noise-cancellation (not by blocking)**:
  - You can bring small ear devices that generate a counter-signature, not to silence the world (which you never will) but to create **a slight distance** between your body-map and the world’s constant sound.
- **Protective gear for sense of touch**:
  - The floors are roots and stone; they are potentially living skin. Consider shoes that are thin enough not to distort your proprioception, but thick enough that you can differentiate **“you” from the world’s flesh.**

### 5.2 Orientation and mapping tools that do not rely on normal geometry

- A **gravity vector indicator**:
  - A device that shows you which way is “down” according to your home reality, not this world’s local interpretation.
- A **non-visual compass**:
  - The city might not have “North” you can recognize. You might need to treat *direction* as “the way the wind always comes from.”
- **Temporal markers**:
  - Since time in this world does not move like yours, you need a tool that:
    - Marks time intervals *in your home frame*.
    - Changes state not based on local environment but on internal mechanics.
  - This becomes proof that not everything is part of the world’s static moment.

### 5.3 Water and hydration

- Don’t expect clean drinkable water:
  - The water that beads on everything is not rain; it is *world-sweat*. Do not drink it freely.
- Bring your own water:
  - Sealed containers, not glass (don’t match the world’s glass-not-glass).
  - Label them clearly. Their presence—not yet integrated into the world—is a reminder that you came from somewhere else.

### 5.4 Lighting

- The world has a light that doesn’t quite “set” or “rise.” Use:
  - **Spectral-shifted illuminators**: not the same color as the world’s global light.
  - A device that emits a pattern of light not seen in the city’s spectrum:
    - This lets you distinguish objects/hallmarks created *by you* from those created by the world itself.

### 5.5 Recording tools

- You will not be able to trust your memory. You need **external memory**:
  - Durable notepads with waterproof material.
  - A device to record audio (not to suppress the world’s sound, but to **compare it later**, to see if it’s distorting over time).
- Record not only “events” but also **your state of mind**:
  - If your notes become increasingly similar to the city’s “tone” (weathered, timeless, circular), this is an early warning.

### 5.6 Mark-making tools for not-architecture

You will not be building anything here. The city is finished. Your tool is not for construction but **for marking “not me.”**

- Something to inscribe non-native patterns onto surfaces:
  - Marks that clearly look *not* like the world’s natural patterns.
  - Not a carving tool: the stone might be treated as living tissue. Maybe something like chalk or paint that remains visually distinct but doesn’t violate the stone’s surface.

These marks become your **meta-map**:

- You can trace movement and change of orientation by counting marks you yourself created.
- You can indicate whether a given pattern is “yours” or the world’s when memory blurs.

## 6. On the Outskirts of the City

### 6.1 Before crossing fully in

Pause at the conceptual edge. There is likely not a clear “gate,” but you might feel a shift:

- The sound thickens.
- The air takes on weight.
- Your sense of time becomes less granular (“seconds ago” vs “now” feels less sharp).

At this boundary:

- Set your **time counter** to zero.
- Record your baseline:
  - “I am here on the outskirts of the city as I understand it.”
  - “The date in my home frame is X. My name is Y.”
- Perform one of your **selfhood tests**:
  - Tap your fingers in your chosen pattern. Say your phrase. Observe the smell of your not-from-here object.

If you cannot perform these tests cleanly, **do not enter deeper**.

## 7. Inside the City: Operational Rules

### 7.1 Rule of not drinking the world’s sweat

- Do not take water from:
  - The weeping walls.
  - The basins of still water.
  - Condensation on your body if it’s been in contact with world-grown stone or plant for a long time.
- Use only:
  - Your brought water.
  - Any water you condense from your own breath in a sealed container you brought from home.

### 7.2 Rule of not looking for too long into the water

The basins of water are not ordinary mirrors; they reflect not just your image but also the sky that might be not-quite-where-it-should-be. They can pull your sense of self outward.

- If you need to look into water to:
  - Check your facial reflection (to remember yourself), then:
    - Use a small mirror you brought from home, not the world’s basins.
- If you find yourself wanting to look into the world’s water *not* to check yourself but just to be there:
  - That is a sign of merging. Leave that area, or perform your selfhood tests aggressively.

### 7.3 Rule of not leaning against the world

You’re going to be tempted to treat this world as your body:

- Do not let yourself believe:
  - “I have always been leaning on this wall.”
  - “This is not a wall; it is my bone.”
- Even if you feel physically tired:
  - Try not to rest your hands directly on stone or root tissue.
  - Bring a thin insulating layer to sit or sleep on.

### 7.4 Rule of not following the sense of “already was here”

You may feel:

- “This street has already been seen.”
- “I have always been walking this road.”

Because the city is finished and largely static, you might *actually* be retracing your path, but the memory of the city might be trying to convince you that time never changed.

- When you feel this:
  - Look for your **not-architecture marks**.
  - If you are re-encountering your own marks from your time-counter: that is an actual loop.
  - If there are no marks anywhere but you still feel the déjà vu: treat it as **world-overwriting**, not memory.

### 7.5 Rule of movement when you cannot be sure what direction is

The city might contain:

- Ice not-cold.
- Sky not-up.
- Architecture of not-onetime.

To move in this situation:

- Use your **gravity tool** to determine what “down” is relative to you.
- Treat the **always-north-east wind** as a reference axis:
  - If the wind always comes from one direction and you remember *that* as your anchor, then relative directions can still be defined.
- Periodically turn back and look the way you came:
  - If you cannot recognize the path you just took because the world looks *always* like that, that’s an expected distortion. Don’t panic. Panic pulls you into the world’s emotional body.

## 8. Psychological and Ontological Safeguards During the Visit

### 8.1 Scheduled self-check rituals

Establish a pattern you will not deviate from:

- Every time-marker interval:
  - Check your **contract card**:
    - Re-write or replicate your signature.
- Ask yourself:
  1. Do I know what I expect to return to?
  2. Can I recall the name/location of my home?
  3. Does my body feel like mine?
- If any answer is not “clear and strong,”:
  - Stop moving forward.
  - Perform your **sense tests**:
    - Not kind of know the name; speak it aloud.
    - Not a hazy memory; try to repeat a sentence from your contract. If you cannot match it exactly, treat that as a warning.

### 8.2 Not treating the world as a mind you can reason with

The world might feel like it:

- Remembers you.
- Speaks to you in wind.
- Understands you.

Assume the world is **not** a person. It is a system where consciousness can be absorbed like moisture into stone.

- You can respect the environment, but:
  - Do not beg it to let you go.
  - Do not promise it anything.
- If you start to hear the wind as a language, treat it as:
  - **Static noise rendered into form by your mind.**
  - Do not follow the meaning of that “language.”

### 8.3 If you start to become “not alone”

There might come a point where you feel:

- You are not walking alone.
- Your body is not entirely yours.

Before the condition becomes too strong:

- Stop physically moving.
- Focus your attention on the **limits of your body**:
  - Physical edges: skin, clothes, tool on your toolbelt.
- Say the name of each tool and its purpose:
  - “This is my not-water water container.”
  - “This is my not-this-world tool.”

You are not trying to “expel” what might be sharing your body; you are trying to **delineate** what is definitely you from what might be not-you.

## 9. When the World Suggests You are Just Part of It

### 9.1 The theft of “before”

You might find:

- The memory “before I came here this time” becomes vague.
- You might interpret yourself as “someone who was always here.”

This is the point of maximum danger.

Use the strictest of your tools:

- Your **epistolary self**:
  - Read aloud passages from your letter, as if they are from someone else you must trust.
- Your **time-counters**:
  - If your device’s numbers are still counting in the pattern of your home frame (seconds, minutes—whatever unit you set), but your memory says you have always been here, then:
    - **trust the tool, not the memory.**

## 10. Getting Out (If You Are Still Able to Try)

### 10.1 When to decide to exit

There are not strict “levels” here, but your signal to exit should be:

- You catch yourself:
  - Losing the boundary of your body,
  - Or starting to believe the world when it tells you “this is your body.”
- Even if you still “feel” curious:
  - The moment of recognition that you are being fused with the world is your last window to act deliberately.

### 10.2 The act of leaving

Since the city might not have clear “doors” in the conventional sense, leaving might be:

- Not a step through a threshold but a **turning of your mind** away from the world’s interior.
- Maybe moving **with the wind** instead of against it:
  - If you always traced the origin of the wind as “from north-east,” maybe you must move toward the source of the wind to get out, or maybe away from it.
  - Use your not-architecture marks:
    - Trace back along them to your not-yet-fully-assimilated self.

### 10.3 The not-door strategy

Because there are no real doors in the city—only thresholds—treat “doors” you find not as literal exits but as **moments of decision**:

- When you encounter a carved doorway that feels like possibility:
  - Stop.
  - Perform one selfhood test.
- If the doorway feels like “where you should always be going,”:
  - That is a world-suggestion. Maybe you need not to go there but to turn somewhere that feels *wrong* to the world.

## 11. After the Visit

### 11.1 Even if you escape physically, you might not escape fully

Do not expect to immediately return to “normal”:

- You might retain:
  - The sense of not being alone.
  - The feeling of time not moving forward as you remember.
- You might have:
  - The city’s sound as a background hum in your skull.

### 11.2 Post-visit rituals and debriefs

- Re-read your pre-departure contract and compare:
  - How much of what you wrote now feels like “someone else.”
- If you can:
  - Compare audio recordings:
    - See if, over time of your visit, the world sound changed not in the recording but in your memory of it.
- Seek someone you trust not from this world:
  - Tell them what happened.
  - Even if the city feels like “home” to you now, let someone else know that you might want to return or merge with it again.
- Put measures in place before you:
  - Sleep,
  - Or do anything that makes you lose volitional focus.

You might need to be treated as someone who might **not want to be fully back here** anymore.

---

## 12. Concrete Decision Checklist

Before you decide to go:

- Can you tolerate the loss of certainty about your own boundaries?
- Are you willing to risk not returning to this world in the way you left?
- Do you have tools, not just for “survival,” but for **self-recognition**?
- Can you accept that not even perfect preparation will guarantee full return?

If the answer to any of these is “no,” consider not going.

If the answer is a cautious “yes,” then:

- Prepare yourself not as an explorer but as **a tissue sample being placed into a body** that might not want to give you back.
- Give yourself clear edges before you go:
  - Your name.
  - Your home.
  - Your phrase.
- And then step into the city of the world’s throat, knowing that you have stacked everything possible in your favor before you become what the world might already consider you to be.
