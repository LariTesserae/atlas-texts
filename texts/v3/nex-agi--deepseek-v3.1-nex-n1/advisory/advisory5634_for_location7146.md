To walk into the Underforge is to step inside a machine whose heart is geology and time rather than clockwork. You are not visiting a city. You are visiting a body of water in slow motion, where the veins are shallow channels of runoff, the skin is rust and moss, and the organs are the pillar-foundries whose rhythms set the pulse of the world. You should prepare accordingly.

Below is a long preparation guide, from what to bring in your pack to how to behave once you arrive, so that you leave not as a casualty of the machine but as a rare visitor it barely notices.

---

## 1. Mindset Before You Step Over

### 1.1 This is not a ruin – it is a system under pressure

The Underforge is not “dead” or “lost”. It is a functioning environment whose purpose is roughly:

- to wring work from geothermal heat,  
- to keep water from stagnating,  
- to keep metal from failing all at once,  
- and to metabolize the corrosion and rust into something useful.

You are entering a world where the idea of “finished” doesn’t exist. Floors are being rewritten by bolt-colonies, channels are being re-cut by corrosion, and the rust pattern called the Grain is being readjusted by every cycle of water and heat.

If you treat this place like a museum, you will get hurt by systems that are still operating.

### 1.2 Expect a sensory world that is not like yours

The main things your mind uses to map a world—light, color, sound—are deliberately degraded here:

- **Light**:  
  The ceiling of black glass plugs eats light. You will never see clear contrast at more than a few meters. Your brain will be trying to map a world entirely from dim cues, torch glows, and ghostly pale-blue patches.

- **Color**:  
  Almost none. You’re living in grayscale with rust reds and dull coppers, the odd blue-white zinc sheet, and the cold-blue ghost-light. Your eyes will adjust; but your color sense will feel “dulled”.

- **Sound**:  
  Always present. Drips of condensation from the ceiling into the channels; the irregular hammer of the pillar-forges; the echo of distant water. There is no true silence here.

You will be more reliant than ever on:

- the texture of the floor underfoot,
- the temperature of the air,
- the smell of the metal and water,
- the soundscape.

### 1.3. This is a low-ceilinged world

The Roof of the Undersky is “only just high enough to breathe without feeling the mountain press on the chest.” This might not cause full claustrophobia in everyone, but you will feel a weight of presence above you.

Expect:

- No broad horizon.  
- Your sense of distance to be shaped by the pillars and their faintly lit silhouettes rather than by direct sightlines.
- Constant awareness of being “under”.

If you are prone to panic attacks or tight-space anxiety, you need to work with yourself before entering: visualization, breathing practice, and a clear understanding of safe routes and ritual times. This is not a good place to be when you panic.

## 2. Gear and Equipment

### 2.1 Light

**Primary torch**

You must assume standard torchlight is the only reliable source you’ll have:

- Bring a torch that can burn for long cycles without flicker.
- If you can, fashion:
  - a shield or shroud that can be opened to flash a signal,
  - but closed to reduce glare and “respect” the ceiling of light-eaters.

You will not “light up the cavern” like you can on the surface. You’re trying to:

- illuminate the floor near your feet,
- sense the broader pillars in your peripheral,
- avoid being the bright center of a world that is designed to swallow you.

**Totals of consumables**

Bring:

- Enough fuel and spare torches for *at least twice the time you think you’ll spend*.
- If you can get hold of an insulated container that can keep a torch alight but small:
  - this is excellent as a “heartlight” you can keep on your person if forced to sleep in the Underforge.

**Walking lights**

Do not walk with a torch constantly at full height:

- Best carried:
  - hip level or lower,
  - or behind a handshield to reduce glare off the floor.
- You want to read the floor, not blind yourself.

### 2.2. Footwear

The floor is:

- Uneven metal,
- Slightly warm,
- Wet in many places,
- Crossed by shallow channels of water,
- Painted with ghost-light that may be dangerous to your skin.

You need:

- **Thick-soled boots**, preferably with:
  - metal plates or soles that can withstand:
    - occasional contact with hot,
    - sharp-corrosion,
    - and ghost-light.
- If you can have:
  - **oversoles** or **sock-like outer layers of treated leather** you can change, they help:
    - keep your main boots from soaking through,
    - prevent you touching ghost-light with your skin.

A walking staff or crutch is strongly recommended:

- To test depth of water,
- To probe unstable floor plates,
- To keep balance on the low domes and slumping bowls.

### 2.3. Clothing and Protection

**Temperature**

The air is colder than the floor under your boots:

- Feet stay warm-ish,
- Hands and face stay cold.

Wear:

- Multiple layers for your body, especially for the upper:
  - base layer to keep sweat off,
  - mid-layer for warmth,
  - outer layer that can shed condensation and drips.
- Gloves:
  - not too thin,
  - not just cloth—you *will* need to touch metal surfaces when you lose your balance or need to climb.

**Head and neck**

- Bring:
  - a hood or cap that keeps water from pooling down your neck,
  - a scarf or mask that can protect:
    - mouth from metallic vapor and spores,
    - lungs from particles.

This is a world where you breathe “mineralized” air; not immediately poison, but a long exposure to dusts and spores may not be harmless.

### 2.4. Water and Nutrition

The water flowing along the channels:

- has been engineered by the Underforge engineers,
- controlled to maximize distribution rather than maximum purity,
- and likely carries ions of different metals.

For you:

- **Do not drink directly from the floor channels** unless you have no choice and no consumable test.
- If you must:
  - Take water from a main channel where constant flow is present,
  - Not from stagnant sumps or bowls where coal-moss gathers thick.
  - Always attempt to:
    - pre-filter with cloth,
    - and then boil or otherwise treat it.

Bring:

- a sealed water container,
- stored water for at least the first phase of your journey,
- a method of heat treatment:
  - if you can share hearth-time at one pillar-foundry (with permission of locals), this is your safest way.

Food:

- The biosphere is not plant-friendly;  
- you should not plan to live off the land.
- Bring:
  - sealed rations,
  - not-too-smelly foods so that you:
    - don’t draw unwanted attention from moth-thieves or step-bugs.

### 2.5. Basic Tools and Safety Gear

Minimum useful:

- **Metal knife** (not brittle):
  - for scraping rust,
  - checking floor,
  - not for self-defense—that’s not your main threat here.
- **Small hammer or metal tool**:
  - To test sound of metal,
  - For self-rescue if you need to bang on a pillar or signal.
- **Chalk or pigment marker**:
  - You cannot mark the floor permanently,
  - but you can make temporary sigils on:
    - pillars,
    - certain floor plates,
    - that you’ll recognize later.
- **Cloth/towel**:
  - To dry boots,
  - To clean spills from tools,
  - To filter water.
- **Basic first aid**:
  - Bandages,
  - Wraps,
  - Something to treat burns:
    - both from heat and perhaps cold from ghost-light.

## 3. Navigating the Underforge: The Floor and the Water

### 3.1. The floor is not neutral territory

The floor is made of:

- sheets of iron,
- zinc,
- copper,
- bronze,
- patches of years,
- layers of mending.

Physics:

- The floor is not dead;
- it is contracting, expanding, and corroding at all times.
- Some plates will creak,
- some will shift under stress,
- some are actively being rewritten by step-bugs.

Practical rules:

- Never step full weight on a new-looking patch:
  - if you’re not familiar with its pattern,
  - if you can’t see where the rivets and stress runners are.
- Use your staff to test:
  - tap the floor,
  - listen for the difference:
    - dull sound: might be hollowed underneath,
    - solid sound: better stepping,
    - metallic ring again and again: likely heavily used path.

### 3.2. The channel script and the water nodes

The engineers have inscribed the floor with a capillary network:

- Lines of channels:
  - thumb-width,
  - shallow in most places,
  - meeting in nodes of raised bronze tables where water is distributed.

How you use them:

- **Navigation**:  
  The channels are not decorative; they are the circulatory system.
  - Follow main channels where there is constant flow to:
    - find pillar-foundries,
    - find housing blocks,
    - avoid being trapped in dead zones.
- **Staying with safe footing**:  
  Walking along channels:
  - you’ll often be walking on slightly raised ridges between thumb-wide channels,
  - not in the water itself.
  - This is safer:
    - but requires you to be light of foot and sure in your steps.

**Nodes**

The seven-way node structures:

- central table with seven channels,
- the first channel gives a slow flow of water,
- the others each receive a share.

Use nodes as landmarks:

- Cross them,
- remember directions:
  - which channels you see,
  - which one you came from,
  - so you can backtrack.

**Ghost-light**

Where:

- water is thin but not strong,
- there is a film of condensation,
- metal floor shines with pale-blue glow.

What:

- It is harmless to metal,
- but potentially harmful to your skin and especially to feet,
- cold to the touch,
- lasting longer if you avoid contact.

Rule:

- If you see:
  - ghost-light on the floor,
  - you:
    - do not touch with bare skin,
    - do not cross barefoot,
    - if at all possible, avoid walking directly on it.
- If you must:
  - thick soles,
  - fast crossing,
  - and minimize contact time.

### 3.3. The pillar-foundries as landmarks and habitable zones

Pillars are:

- bundles of black rods wrapped in wire,
- extending from the floor into unknown depths,
- covered with rust patterns,
- giving off heat.

Pillars are valuable:

- for heat when the world is cold,
- for sound as you locate direction,
- for social structure:
  - each pillar-foundry has likely:
    - engineers,
    - workers,
    - rituals of work and sleep.

Use them as:

- navigational nodes,
- safe-havens from the cold between,
- places of information exchange with locals.

## 4. Biology and How to Treat It

### 4.1. The Grain (rust)

The rust is not just decoration:

- It is a pattern of the corrosion and stress of the world.
- It is read by engineers:
  - “Good Grain”, “bad Grain”.

For you as a visitor:

- **Observation**:  
  Look:
  - bright and vivid band of red-orange:
    - likely strong water,
    - strong corrosion,
    - higher risk of metal failure.
  - duller, more muted:
    - slower,
    - less likely sudden change.
- **Do not try to clean or “remove” rust yourself**:
  - This is not graffiti;
  - it is a functional layer of the world.
- **Intentional contact**:  
  If you must:
  - use gloves,
  - do not inhale dusts directly,
  - do not ingest.

### 4.2. Coal-moss and other fungi

The black and powdery-blue mats:

- live on damp zones,
- often where you *don’t* step,
- filtering the noise of excess moisture,
- turning water into something else.

For you:

- **Avoid stepping directly on mats if you can**:  
  - they are part of the system and,
  - if angry, may be the source of spores or irritants.
- **Do not eat**:  
  - you don’t know what’s in them,
  - and some of them are not exactly plants.

### 4.3. Glow fungi and ghost-light

Fungi:

- Bloom during torch-vigils,
- Die quickly and crumble into black powder.

If you see:

- Use them as:
  - temporary lightpoints,
  - trail markers,
  - but not long-term anchors in your map.

Ghost-light:

- As described: cold, potentially dangerous to skin.
- Respect:
  - don’t touch,
  - don’t lick,
  - don’t sleep straight on it.

### 4.4. Moth-thieves

Small:

- pale,
- not silent,
- clicking with their wings.

They:

- are not a direct threat to you,
- but they:

  - cluster around the black glass plugs in the ceiling,
  - become part of the ceiling’s structure if left unchecked.

How you interact:

- If you see:
  - pillars or lower supports being shaken,
    - that is likely locals:
      - driving the moth-thieves away from the plugs,
      - so that plugs keep eating light instead of housing moth mass.
- You, as a visitor:
  - **Do not attempt to recreate the shakes**:  
    - you might destabilize something,
    - or you might be seen as tampering with architecture.
  - Just watch:
    - learn how they are handled.

### 4.5. Step-bugs (bolt-colonies)

These are moving:

- colonies of metallic bolts,
- that bolt themselves together,
- that sleep in unused channels and gaps,
- that re-weave the floor.

From your point of view:

- They are not aggressive animals.
- They are a:
  - **maintenance crew**, not a weapon.

Do not:

- attempt to grab them,
- play with them,
- try to dismantle them.

When you see:

- a patch of floor that looks:
  - “too new,”
  - or “suspiciously neat,”  
    - likely step-bugs are working there,
    - or have been present.

## 5. Sound and Sleep

### 5.1. Living with the drip

The dominant sound:

- the drip of condensation from the Roof of the Undersky,
- the falling of water beads,
- the ticking of the world into time.

You experience:

- This will be:
  - irregular,
  - not a pattern you can immediately sleep to,
  - unless you train yourself.

Something you can do:

- On the first night:
  - listen to the pattern,
  - identify:
    - clusters of drips,
    - quiet pauses,
  - and then you:
    - treat the cluster sounds as one “wave”,
    - and the silence between as your rest.

Many experienced citizens of the Underforge likely sleep:

- not by silence,
- but by granting the drips the same mental weight as:
  - wind,
  - or birdcall,
  - simply background context.

You can:

- practice:
  - some form of meditation,
  - count,
  - or map the pattern as a story,
- until you brain stops “fighting” the noise.

### 5.2. Ritual of torch and snuffing

The Underforge has:

- rituals of torchlight:
  - long vigil,
  - then eventual snuff-out.

Find out:

- what the cycle of snuffing is,
- when you should be:
  - moving,
  - or be safely anchored,
  - not walking in darkness when the torch goes out.

Expect:

- that you may not have:
  - your own torch lit,
  - or the right to move,
  - during certain times of the ritual cycle.

## 6. Sickness and Injury

### 6.1. Burn management

You will be exposed to:

- hot floor plates,
- hearths,
- and possibly ghost-light.

You:

- Never:
  - touch metal with bare skin,
  - or try to “feel” heat by hand,
  - unless forced by an emergency.

Treatment:

- For:
  - burns:
    - cool with flowing water,
    - clean,
    - bandage dry.
- If:
  - ghost-light contact:
    - rinse with cold water,
    - dry,
    - monitor for signs of:
      - numbness,
      - or color change.

### 6.2. Metal and germ exposure

The world is:

- wet,
- metallic,
- full of corrosion and rust.

You:

- Keep:
  - cuts closed,
  - sealed,
  - clean.
- If you:
  - cut yourself on rust:
    - don’t ignore it,
    - don’t let it stay wet,
    - try:
      - to clean,
      - to treat with what you have,
      - to seek better healers if available.

### 6.3. Mental and skin pressure

The combination of:

- dim,
- constant,
- monotonous,

environment:

- can be:
  - disorienting,
  - or numbing.

You:

- Take:
  - short rests,
  - don’t try to:
    - walk through dozens of pillars at one stretch,
    - or you will lose yourself,
    - and your sense of time.

If you feel:

- dizziness:
  - stop,
  - check:
    - hydration,
    - food,
    - heat.
- If dizziness:
  - doesn’t pass,
  - retreat:
    - along the same channel,
    - back to the last pillar-foundry you passed,
    - and ask for help.

## 7. People and Etiquette

### 7.1. Seeing the citizens of the Underforge

They are:

- Engineers who:
  - read rust,
  - maintain channels,
  - route water,
  - work with step-bugs,
  - shake pillars to keep moth-thieves away.
- Metalworkers and:
  - hammer-smiths,
  - who work at pillar-foundries,
  - who craft not for the finished,
  - but for the next part of the machine’s body.

For you:

- They are likely:
  - not surprised,
  - that strangers from other realms walk,
  - but they will:
    - expect:
      - respect,
      - obedience,
      - and not being treated as a spectacle.

### 7.2. How to approach

General:

- Calm,
- Slow,
- Hands empty:
  - not holding torches high,
  - not carrying hidden metal.

When you need:

- to speak:
  - State:
    - your name,
    - your world of origin (if asked),
    - your purpose.
  - State:
    - that you are not:
      - tampering with any system,
      - planning to alter,
      - exploit.
  - Ask:
    - permission:
      - to sleep,
      - to drink water,
      - to share hearth heat,
      - to follow their channel routes.

You likely:

- do not know:
  - their laws,
  - their hidden rituals,
  - what is sacred to them.

Observe:

- How they treat:
  - rust bands,
  - ghost-light,
  - water nodes,
  - pillar-wires,
- and follow:
  - their lead,
  - not your own assumptions.

### 7.3. What not to do

- Don’t:
  - scrape walls,
  - cut channels,
  - modify the floor,
  - stick tools into pillar-wires.
- Don’t:
  - collect artifacts:
    - pieces of floor,
    - bolts of step-bugs,
    - rust samples,
  - unless you are:
    - told,
    - given,
    - and shown how.

If you do:

- You might:
  - interrupt:
    - water flow,
    - structural stability,
  - be seen as:
    - sabotage,
  - be treated:
    - as not merely curious,
    - but as a threat to the machine.

## 8. Choosing Your Path and Setting Time Limits

### 8.1. Routes of safety

The safest:

- Follow main channels:
  - between pillar-foundries,
  - between housing blocks.
- These:
  - are maintained,
  - have the least ghost-light,
  - least risk of falling or floor failure.

The least safe:

- Dark:
  - zones between pillars,
  - patches of floor:
    - heavily corrugated,
    - or covered in thick coal-moss,
  - where:
    - channels are not visible,
    - and no light but ghost-light remains.

### 8.2. Mapping the world

You will not:

- create a full map of the Underforge,
- but you can:
  - map your own:
    - passage,
    - simple pattern:
      - pillar A,
      - node,
      - pillar B,
      - short detour,
      - back.

Use:

- small:
  - notebook,
  - or chalkboard,
  - not something that:
    - might be:
      - confused,
      - for espionage.

Helps:

- Note:
  - pillar shapes,
  - node locations,
  - channel counts,
  - simple symbols:
    - like:
      - “rust heavy,”
      - “ghost-light,”,
      - “safe dry.”

### 8.3. When you’ve had enough

The Underforge will not:

- signal you when to leave,
- as you won’t:
  - feel sun,
  - see moon.

So:

- Decide:
  - before you:
    - set,
    - clear,
    - time:
      - when you will return,
    - or accept:
      - a number of cycles:

    - you will not cross,
    - and stick to that line.

Once you hit:

- your limit:
  - turn:
    - carefully,
    - trace,
    - back,
  - along:
    - channels and nodes,
    - pillars you recognize,
  - until:
    - you:
      - hit,
      - the,
      - way,
      - you,
      - came,
      - in.
