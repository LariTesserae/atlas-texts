You’re preparing to drop into a world where the city is a living organism, where water is a solid, and where you—if you’re human—are fundamentally out of place. This isn’t like visiting a dense foreign metropolis; it’s closer to stepping inside a body that doesn’t know you’re there.

Below are concrete things to do and bring. Think of this as a checklist from a well‑traveled but equally well‑worried multiverse guide.

---

## 0. Before You Jump: Mindset

First, accept a few ground truths about this place:

- The entire ceiling is a living root mat that produces the atmosphere you breathe.
- Almost all architecture is made of living wood.
- Water is stored as a dry, crystal powder “rain” and is chemically activated before use.
- The city itself grows, changes, and integrates things—including you, if you’re not careful.

You don’t need to understand the biology of it; you need to respect that it’s bigger than you are. If your mind starts to drift into “it’s all alive, watching me, closing over me”—treat that as more than creepiness; treat it as a legitimate hazard you’re going to manage with gear and rules.

## 1. What You Want to Get Before the Trip

### 1.1. Basic Travel Gear

You’re going underground, into a controlled, but alien, ecosystem.

**Essential:**

- **Clothing**
  - Light layers (no heavy coats).  
  - Long pants and sleeves to protect against skin‑contact triggers or abrasions.
  - Pockets that zip or snap securely—nothing that can easily spill out.
- **Good boots**
  - Flat soles, but not with deep treads that trap dust.
  - Comfortable for long walks on smooth wooden surfaces.
- **Backpack**
  - Small, comfortable, nothing that dangles or flaps.
  - You want to be balanced and quiet in this mostly‑still world.

**Optional but very wise:**

- A thin, **weather mask** or a decent mask with filter inserts:
  - Protects you from inhaling crystal dust if you’re caught in “weather.”
- **Light gloves**
  - For occasionally touching plant walls, if necessary.
- A **thin hat or beanie**
  - For emotional comfort if the ceiling feels too close.

---

### 1.2. The Environment: Air, Light, and Time

**Air:**

- The air is breathable but dry.
- If you have respiratory allergies, asthma, or a dry/air‑conditioned‑air sensitivity:
  - Bring:
    - A small personal **humidifier bottle** for your nose and throat.
    - Any prescribed **inhalers or medication**.
  - Plan your visit shorter if you’re prone to breathing issues.

**Light:**

- The ceiling root mat provides constant daylight spectrum, but **no natural cycle**.
- If you’re sensitive to circadian rhythms:
  - Bring a **light‑mask eye cover or sleep mask**.
  - Consider a **watch/app with a timer** labeled “day” and “night” to help your body keep track.

---

### 1.3. Water: You’re Going to Have to Drink Powdered Water

Water is not liquid here; it’s a dry, crystalline powder that gets chemically activated when consumed.

You will *not* be drinking from local taps; you don’t want to get your chemistry wrong.

**Bring:**

- **Pre-packaged “travel field” water**
  - Sealed, ideally in your home dimension.
    - If you can’t bring that, then bring:
      - A sealed _container_ of your own clean water.
    - If your travel method allows storage or limited-time stasis, you can use that.
  - How much?
    - For a short stay (< 48 hours local): aim at ~2–3 liters of usable water equivalent.
    - For longer: you’ll need to learn local safe “activation” methods from a trusted local.

**If you must rely on local water:**

- Only drink “water” prepared by:
  - Workers who wear masks and have clearly defined roles.
  - Places that look like _official_ hydration stations or communal kitchens.
- **Do not:**
  - Try to activate local crystal powder yourself.
  - Eat anything that looks like dry crystals “as is” without local guidance.

---

### 1.4. Skin and Contact Protection

You’re going to be living surrounded by wood that is still alive.

You’re not adapted to it chemically.

**Bring:**

- **Basic barrier/hygiene items**
  - Unscented soap.
  - Small tube of plain **moisturizer** for dry skin.
    - The air is dry, and there’s no visible humidity.
    - If you’re prone to eczema or dry skin, bring extra.
- **Small bandage or roll tape**
  - If you get cut or scrape, you want a clean, barrier dressing between your skin and the living wood surface.

**General rule:**

- Minimize skin contact with surfaces you don’t need to touch.
- If you must touch:
  - Hands are less risky than open cuts or wounds.
  - Use gloves if you have them.

### 2. Safety: Avoiding Being “Integrated”

The city is not cruel, but it is blind. You are not part of the design.

You are living tissue and carbon and water in a system that continuously rearranges itself.

### 2.1. Don’t Sleep on Direct Wood Surfaces

You don’t know what “slow contact” means here.

- **Bring a thin groundsheet or sleep mat**:
  - Even if it’s just a thin roll of plastic, it’s a clean layer between you and the floor.
- **Bring a simple blanket or sleeping bag**
  - Another layer is always a good idea.
- **Try not to sleep with bare skin touching the wood**, especially if you have:
  - Scratches, blisters, or small wounds.

The record of what happened to others is vague and “sketchy,” but that’s *enough* to make you careful.

---

### 2.2. Don’t Sit on Platforms That Are Clearly Growing or Moving

If you see:

- Platforms that visibly shift or drift.
- Stairs reorganizing themselves.
- Balcony edges “breathing” or changing length.

Your rules are:

- **You can walk on them**, but:
- **Do not sit or lie for long hours**.
- **Do not lean with exposed limbs** for extended time.

Remember: “architecture” here is formed by slow growth and chemical reaction. Your body is not part of its intended inputs.

---

### 2.3. Avoid Corners That Are Clearly “Tending”

You described:

- Branches that can form **velcro‑like hooks**.
- Curling “tendrils” that catch or enfold small creatures.

If you see:

- Any area that looks like:
  - “Hairs” or small hooks along the edges of platforms.
  - Extra growths that are more than clean trunk and branch.

Your rule is simple:

- **Stay away from edges.**
- **Don’t sit or lean with your head or hair near that kind of structure.**

Especially:

- No putting your fingers or hands near odd-looking small appendages on branches or railings.
- If you’re wearing hair or loose clothing, be aware of where it is relative to those edges.

---

### 2.4. Don’t Try to “Feed” or “Experiment” With Trunks or Ceiling Roots

Curious travelers are especially prone to doing stupid things.

Don’t be one of them.

- **Do not:**
  - Place small cuts of skin or hair near contact points.
  - “Test” whether a tree can integrate debris by placing your belongings on them.
- **Avoid** touching:
  - Cross-sections of trunks.
  - Any channels that have visible white moving structures like “nerves.”

The “channels” inside trunks are clearly not for you.

---

### 2.5. If Something Looks “Too Ceramic or Too Perfect,” It’s Probably Not Inanimate

If an object:

- Looks like a porcelain statue of an animal.
- Looks like a perfectly smooth tool or part.
- Is not obviously attached to anything fixed but still not “moving” obviously.

Approach it like you would approach machinery in a factory—you can be close, but don’t shove your hands into it.

- **Do not tease or try to touch “porcelain” animals.**
- If they extend **anything** toward you (especially anything like a thin stick or feeder):
  - You step back.
  - You don’t block, grab, or hit it.

They’re not hostile to you; they’re just feeding from the system that also happens to be the home you’re stepping on.

## 3. Food and Local Supplies

### 3.1. Bring Your Own Food (Initially)

You don’t know if you’re compatible with the food chain here.

- **Bring sealed, familiar food from home**
  - High energy bars or nuts are good.
  - Make sure wrappers are sealed.
    - You don’t want local dust or curiosities bleeding into your food.

**How much?**

- If you’re staying for “short” (< a few days):
  - Enough to eat 2–3 meals a day without touching local food.
- If you’re going to stay longer:
  - You’ll need to find out what is safe for humans to eat.
    - Best strategy: locals.

### 3.2. Understanding Local Food

If local people are eating it:

- Look for:
  - **Appearance**: things like “processed” starch bars or cooked cakes—rather than raw “unknown powder.”
  - **Common trails**: many people eating the same thing.

- **Do not:**
  - Eat:
    - Any powder you found on the ground.
    - Any crystal-like coatings that look like “water crystals.”
    - Any “eroded” wood from growths, unless explicitly shown safe by locals.

You’re not part of their tree’s chemistry, and you don’t have their evolutionary history.

## 4. Getting Around the City

### 4.1. Trains and “Grown Rails”

- Use **trains (the ceramic barrel‑like cars)** if you can see:
  - Others using them.
  - Clear signs of access points.
- They are described as smooth, quiet, and efficient.

**General rules:**

- Stand inside.
- Don’t try to open access doors or “poke” the trunk veins.
- Stay out of the tiny internal carts used by workers in the lower construction areas.

If you’re not sure if you’re allowed to use a particular route or line:

- Watch **what others do**.
- If it seems like only workers in coolant suits go there, assume it’s not meant for you yet.

### 4.2. Walking on Living Platforms

You are allowed to walk on the main “platform” streets.

If you notice:

- You are on the **stone ribs**:
  - Safe structurally, but less comfortable.
- You are on **living wood**:
  - Look at yourself:
    - Clothing is closed correctly.
    - Nothing is hanging or loose that you don’t want pressed or integrated into the floor.

You’re not heavy enough to break the city floor, but you are heavy enough to be noticed by the system.

---

### 4.3. Don’t Try to “Find the Walls” of the Chamber

This is important.

The text drifts off when talking about limits and edges. This means:

- **At least for you, as an ordinary traveler**: there might be no “edge wall” you’re supposed to visit.
- If you feel an urge to “go where the city ends”:
  - Treat that as a psychological signal: you’re being stressed by the environment.
    - Take a break.
    - Find an open plaza with lots of human or near-human inhabitants.
    - Withdraw from the idea of exploring “deeper into the trunk” or “where no one’s living.”

Your primary job is not to understand the city; it’s to stay safe and sane inside it.

## 5. Mental and Perceptual Hazards

### 5.1. The Ceiling Is Watching You (Even If It’s Not)

The ceiling of root filaments:

- Hangs 60 meters up.
- Provides constant “light but not sun.”
- Is clearly alive and processing air.

Your brain may want to interpret it as “eyes above.”

**What to do about it:**

- Don’t stare at the ceiling for very long stretches.
- If you feel:
  - “It’s looking at me.”
    - Take a break.
    - Look at the ground or your hands.
    - Walk into a space where you can see more people.
    - That breaks your mental pattern.

---

### 5.2. The “Information” Is Fragmentary and Sometimes About Your Own State

The description you were given literally breaks down into stuttering snippets and inverted “danger” messages. This suggests:

- This place leaks **frightening or dissociative information** to anyone observing.
- If you start to feel like you are being “described” or your own thoughts are echoed into the environment:
  - Treat that as a sign of mental overload, not reality.
  - Physical countermeasures:
    - Eat something.
    - Drink water from your own container.
    - Check your watch and remember your *original* identity and home timeline.

---

### 5.3. The “Growing Drawing” and the Library Story

There’s the mention of a child drawing on the surface of a branch, and the branch darkening where she traces.

This is beautiful, but:

- For you, **don’t try to draw on the branches**.  
- You are not emotionally ready to see how your personal mark reacts with the environment.
- If you want art:
  - Use paper or a device you brought from home.
  - Keep your art physically separated from the wood.

## 6. Social and Cultural Adaptation

You’re not entering a city that’s at “war level” tension; but it is clearly **post-industrial** and “plant‑governed.”

### 6.1. Appearance and Behavior

- Wear clothes that are comfortable and not flashy.
- Don’t shout if you can help it; the city is described as mostly quiet.
- **Do not litter**:  
  - This is, in fact, more important than in most places.
    - Any introduced substance or object that spends time on the floor is likely to be treated by the tree-city as either “nutrient or not nutrient.”
- If you have to dispose of something:
  - Check if there are clearly labeled non-plant receptacles.
    - If not, carry your waste out with you or keep it securely in your pack.

---

### 6.2. Treating Locals With Respect

There are workers who scatter “water crystal” powder into construction troughs and seed trunks.

They are essential:

- They are building a world you’re temporarily visiting.
- **Obey any posted or shown instructions**:
  - If you see “KEEP CLEAR” or “COOLING ZONE” or “MATCH ZONE” signs:
    - Stay away.
    - Don’t walk through their workspaces.
    - Don’t touch their tools or containers even if they seem “simple.”

---

### 6.3. Human vs. Integrated Fauna

You will see animals that are partially ceramic, partially chitinous, partially furred.

Your rule is:

- **Don’t stroke, don’t catch, don’t tease.**
- If they are obviously not bothering you, ignore them.
- If one of them “presses” near you or reaches:
  - You step away.
- If you feel any “hook” or contact:
  - Check your clothing and skin calmly.
  - If you *need* help, ask a human-looking worker (rather than trying to fix it yourself with the environment).

---

## 7. Simple “Daily Routine” For You Here

To keep yourself sane and safe:

1. **Morning**:  
   - Wake from sleep.
   - Check your body: any skin changes, new rashes or marks?
   - Treat from your own supplies.
2. **During the day**:
   - Eat pre-packaged food.
   - Drink from your own container.
   - Walk around, but keep to populated areas.
   - Take note of where you are relative to known “safe nodes”:
     - A plaza you know.
     - A train station.
     - A library or public space where you have seen a human-like worker.
3. **Evening / “night”**:  
   - Set your internal timer.
   - Put your “sleep mask” on if needed.
   - Lay on your sheet or sleep mat.
   - Don’t “explore” in the dark; your brain isn’t good at questioning weird shapes in depths.

---

## 8. When You Leave (The “Exit Check”)

Before you depart:

- Check:
  - Any new markings on your skin.
    - If you have anything:
      - Take note.
    - If you’re very worried:
      - Seek medical attention from travelers who handle multiverse contingencies.
- Check your own gear:
  - Are you carrying anything that you didn’t bring?
    - If yes, think carefully whether you want to bring it back.
      - Living wood snippets or pieces of “ceramic animal” are not good souvenirs in this case.
  - Are you missing anything?
    - Document the experience.
    - Don’t return unexamined to the same environment.

### In Summary: You Prepare Like This

You are walking into a world that is:

- A closed ecosystem under a stone ceiling.
- A tree city that slowly grows and integrates.
- A place where water is stored as crystalline dust and chemically activated.

You are not going there “as a conqueror or scientist”; you are going there as a fragile guest in a body that doesn’t belong.

I recommend you:

1. **Bring your own:** air filter mask, light layers, groundsheet, sleep mat, clean water, food, and a basic med kit.
2. **Treat all wood and ceramic animals as if they are alive and integrated with the system.**
3. **Don’t touch or feed the trunk channels or strange edges.**
4. **Don’t sleep directly on the wood floor.**
5. **Don’t try to find the “edge” of the city** or push deeper into the ceiling’s secrets.
6. **Watch locals**, and stick where you see people living clearly and safely.
7. **Keep your own identity and body boundaries** as clear as you can.

If you do that, you stand a chance of visiting this place and actually coming back as yourself—rather than becoming just another specialized branch the ceiling root plant never intended to be human.
