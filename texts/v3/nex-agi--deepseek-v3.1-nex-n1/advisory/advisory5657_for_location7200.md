Prepare as if you’re going to spend a month on a wet, cold, perpetually roaring submarine mountain in the outer ocean of another world.

Below is a long, practical, “grab‑list‑and‑think‑about” guide rather than a vague story. If you just want the checklist, skip to the end.

---

## 1. Mindset & Expectations

### 1.1 What kind of place this is

- **No escape once you’re there:**  
  You’re on a tiny seamount summit that is *the world entire* — a few kilometres of island surrounded by thousands of kilometres of empty ocean. No quick medevac, no corner store, no dry airport. If things get bad, you’re still stuck there a while.

- **Always wet, always cold, always windy:**  
  This is not a tropical island. Expect:
  - air temperature probably ~5–15 °C (40s–50s ºF),
  - humidity near 100%,
  - wind that never really stops,
  - horizontal rain or fine drizzle most of the time.  
  You will be damp *all* the time; the goal is not to stay dry but to avoid becoming soaked to the skin and hypothermic.

- **Sound never stops:**  
  The ocean roar is loud enough that you’ll feel it through your boots and in your chest. You *will not* get silence. This wears on people after days. You *need* hearing protection and some way of getting aural “quiet” (earplugs, noise‑cancelling, etc.).

- **No real sun or warmth:**  
  There’s no dry season, no “go sunbathe on a rock” days. If there’s sun, it’s fleeting, and the wind plus wet underfoot means it doesn’t feel like much. You go to Túrnakmol for the ocean’s raw edge, not for comfort.

## 2. Physical Pre‑Check & Health

### 2.1 Before you travel

- **Get a full medical check‑up**, including:
  - heart & lungs (you’ll be steep climbing in damp wind),
  - blood pressure,
  - any circulatory issues (cold + wet = risk),
  - asthma or respiratory issues (salt spray, wind, humidity),
  - joints (especially knees and ankles — lots of steps and uneven surfaces).

- **Ensure your vaccinations are up to date if you’re coming from other worlds/frames**.  
  At minimum:
  - Tetanus (cuts + algae + rust + metal structures),
  - Hepatitis A & B,
  - Consider typhoid if you risk polluted water in any transfer point.
  - If you can get a **hypothermia‑risk / cold‑stress physiology analysis** from your multiverse medical provider, do it.

- **Dental**:  
  Have a full check. A dental emergency on an ocean crag with no facilities is not a joke.

### 2.2 If you have chronic conditions (especially mental)

- **Anxiety / panic / PTSD / noise sensitivity**  
  The constant noise, lack of sun, and massive sense of exposure can be rough.  
  - Discuss with your provider whether you should adjust meds or bring more.
  - Understand *before* you go whether you do well with sensory overload and isolation.

- **Seasonal Affective Disorder / Depression**  
  Low light, constant cloud, fog — this is an SAD machine.  
  - Consider bringing **a light therapy box** if your multiverse toolkit allows it.
  - Prearrange scheduled message times with someone outside Túrnakmol if you can.

## 3. Clothing & Personal Protection

### 3.1 Layering system

You’re going to be in cold, wet wind with physical exertion. Think “marine alpine mountaineering”.

**Essential layers:**

1. **Base layer (next to skin)**  
   - **Merino wool or synthetic**, no cotton.
   - Synthetic long underwear + long sleeve top.
   - Multiple sets — you’ll rarely *dry* things fully.

2. **Mid layer(s)**  
   - Light fleece or soft‑shell jacket.
   - Extra layer of fleece or thin insulated jacket for when you stop moving.

3. **Shell layer**
   - **Waterproof, breathable, seam‑sealed shell jacket & pants**  
     - Robust storm hood with adjustable brim.
     - Pit zips and/or large vents (you’ll be generating heat while climbing).
   - If affordable, go for the best weather you can afford — this is your survival suit.

4. **Insulation layer (for stops / night / high wind)**
   - A synthetic insulated jacket (or down if kept *perfectly* dry in a pack)
   - One layer to be worn only in dry shelter if possible.

5. **Spares**
   - At least **one full set of base+mid layers** in drybags in your pack and *never* worn on the “up” journey.
   - Spare socks, *many* pairs of underwear, extra glove liners.

### 3.2 Legs, feet, hands

**Legs**
- Hardshell waterproof pants over quick‑dry hiking trousers.
- Options for shorts exist only if you’re very hardy – but when you’re in the veg, you want legs covered (sharp grass, abrasive branches).

**Footwear**
- **Approach boots or waterproof trekking boots** with:
  - Excellent grip on wet rock,
  - Moderate ankle support,
  - **Gore‑Tex or equivalent waterproofing** is not optional.
- **Gaiters** to keep water out of the boot tops.
- Spare **insoles** — you will be wet constantly, and insoles degrade.

**Socks**
- Multiple pairs of **wool or synthetic blend socks** (no cotton).
- Keep a pair absolutely dry in a waterproof bag for sleeping/changing.

**Gloves & hands**
- **Thin waterproof gloves** — for constant handling of wet ropes, rails, rocks.
- **Insulated gloves** for night or summit plateau.
- Spare set of liner gloves.
- **Barrier cream** or heavy-duty hand balm — wet skin cracks, salt + wind = painful sores.

## 4. Gear & Tools

### 4.1 Backpack & waterproofing

- **Waterproof rucksack** or a drybag‑lined system.
- **Multiple drybags or heavy-duty contractor trash bags** for:
  - sleep system,
  - base layers,
  - electronics,
  - food.
- Assume *anything not inside a drybag* will get soaked.

### 4.2 Shelter & sleep system

- **Tent / bivvy sack**
  - 4‑season or 3+ season with strong wind rating.
  - **Full fly coverage** and bathtub floor.
  - Taped seams.
  - Extra guylines — stakes are tricky in moss and peat; use rocks and deadmen anchors when you can.

- **Ground**
  - **Closed‑cell foam pad + inflatable pad** if you can carry it.
  - You must not expect *solid ground* underfoot — peat moss can be a deep sponge.

- **Sleeping system**
  - **High‑quality synthetic sleeping bag** rated to at least 0 °C (32 ºF) — synthetic because you *will* get it damp.
  - **Waterproof bivy sack** around the bag if the tent is not bombproof.
  - **Liner**, if you can: silk or thin synthetic.

### 4.3 Navigation & safety

Given:
- cliff edges,
- steep terrain,
- frequent fog,
- and misleading soft ground—

you need:

- **Headlamp** + spare batteries, stored in waterproof containers.
- **Whistle** on each jacket.
- **Small map** if available from local station / multiverse index.  
  If not:
  - GPS/device capable of *track recording* and waypoints.
- **Compass** as backup.
- **Bearings & sense of edge**: You will regularly be near sheer drop‑offs. In fog or at night assume you are *always* 10 steps from a cliff unless you are sure otherwise.

---

### 4.4 Water & food

**Water**
- Every stream and pool you see is likely safe to drink from a *biological* standpoint if you’re far from industrial structures. But:
  - Always **filter or boil** where you can.
  - **Steripens, iodine, or chlorine tablets** as backup.
- **Bottle insulation**:
  - If it’s cold enough to need warm layers, it’s cold enough to freeze your bottle partially in prolonged wind. Keep near your body or pack.

**Food**
- Bring **high‑energy, dense foods**:
  - nuts, seeds, trail mix,
  - chocolate or nut butter,
  - energy bars,
  - freeze-dried meals (if allowed).
- You’ll burn calories simply staying warm and fighting wind.
- Plan **“extra days”** buffer — weather will delay any departure.

---

### 4.5 Multi‑purpose tools & repair kit

- **Fixed-blade knife or multi‑tool**.
- **Strong cordage** (paracord / thin rope).
- **Duct tape** wrapped on something.
- **Basic first‑aid kit** including:
  - antiseptic wipes,
  - bandages,
  - tape,
  - **blister kit**,
  - painkillers,
  - **electrolytes** (for dehydration despite all the water),
  - **personal meds** in waterproof container with extra supply.
- **Sewing kit & basic repair tape** for fabric.

## 5. Senses & Psychology: Living With the Roar

You’re going to be in a place where:

- The ocean never stops.
- The ground vibrates.
- The sky is usually gray.
- You’re surrounded by deep green moss sponges, not dirt.

This is as much psychological as physical.

### 5.1 Hearing & noise

- **You’ll need to sleep with noise**.  
  - Earplugs are not a luxury here — they’re a safety tool for your rest.
  - Some noise‑cancelling headphones or simple white‑noise device in your shelter if possible.
- **Communication**:
  - You will not reliably hear shouts from 5 metres away in wind.
  - If you’re not solo, establish clear **visual signals** and hand signs.
  - Don’t assume people can hear you if you fall into a soft hole or off the path.

### 5.2 Visuals & light

- **Lighting**:
  - Days can be dim; night is dark and *loud*.
  - Bring multiple lights and headlamp redundancy.
- **Eyestrain**:
  - Often under grey sky, you might get less natural contrast.
  - Have sunglasses *anyway* — fog is bright white in low sun.

### 5.3 Edge awareness & vertigo

- **Always assume that beyond the moss edge there is a long fall.**
- If you are prone to vertigo:
  - Avoid solo hikes near exposed edges.
  - Stay strictly on marked paths / boards / platforms.
  - Sit *away* from the rim when you look outward.

## 6. Terrain‑Specific Behavior & Survival Skills

### 6.1 Staying on path vs. “ground”

- You’ll likely have:
  - **Wooden boardwalks** over marsh,
  - **Paint cairns or markers**,
  - **Metal platforms bolted** into cliffside.
- **Rule of thumb:**
  - If you see bright orange‑red paint markers or boardwalk, *you are not safe to walk freely*.
  - The moss is not simply ground. It can be an overhanging roof of vegetation over voids or pools.

### 6.2 “Ground” vs “void”

- Some moss cushions:
  - are eight meters deep,
  - cover water,
  - or cover overhangs.

**How to behave:**

- **Always** probe the ground in front of you with a **stick or trekking pole**.
- Place your weight slowly:
  - Part of the tussock may be solid;
  - part may sink or disappear.
- Do **not** wander off paths to “shortcut” across the vegetation.
- Near **cliffs**:
  - Assume the ground is undercut by the sea.
  - Stay several meters back from the edge, especially in fog.

### 6.3 Water levels

- **The summit is alive with water.**
  - Pools are common,
  - “solid” path can be under water.
- **Don’t cross new streams in flood**:
  - Short, violent streams from storms can be fast and strong enough to sweep you off the mountain.
- **Check the sky**:
  - If you see heavy rain on the slope or hear the roar within the roar of the ocean, *wait* where you are safe if you can.

## 7. Ecology & Biting Things

The text doesn’t spell out insects, but:

- Assume **small biting insects**, especially where:
  - water is stagnant,
  - peat is thick,
  - wind drops locally.
- **Bring insect repellent**.
- Check **ticks or small critters** after hiking:
  - If you’re brushing through dense, wet shrubs and mosses, you’re in a world of tiny hiding places.

---

### 7.1 Bioluminescence at night

- You might see fungal mats under mosses glowing faintly at night.
  - Don’t assume it’s safe because “it’s just light”.
  - Some glowing fungi live in environments *you* don’t belong in.
- **Don’t eat or lick anything** just because it looks magical.

## 8. Health & Sanity

### 8.1 Physical health

Priority **body dangers** here:

1. **Hypothermia**
   - The wind will strip heat from wet skin quickly.
   - If you shiver uncontrollably, feel slow, confused, or apathetic:
     - Stop immediately if you’re safe.
     - Get into dry layers.
     - Add shelter/windbreak.
     - Warm drink if you have.
     - Get to help ASAP if you can without overexerting.

2. **Wind, cold, and energy**
   - Always assume you need **more calories than you think**.
   - Keep *some* fuel (sugar / candy bar) accessible at all times.

3. **Skin breakdown**
   - **Always slightly damp + salt + wind = cracks and sores.**
   - Moisturize after you get clean with freshwater:
     - hands, feet, fingers, especially.
   - Dry *any* persistent skin folds as best you can.

4. **Noise‑related stress and sleep disturbance**
   - Use earplugs at night.
   - Don’t try to “muscle through” noise.
   - Plan *quiet* downtime inside whenever you can.

---

### 8.2 Mental health & emotional toolset

- **Keep a routine.**
  - Set times to eat, rest, and “sit down” properly.
- **Journal / sketchpad / audio logs**
  - This is a place that asks *attention* from you.
  - Writing helps manage isolation.
- **Pre‑commit to “emergency bail conditions”**
  - Define clear criteria:
    - “I move if sleeping is impossible for more than two nights.”
    - “If I cannot keep warm even with all layers, I descend to the station.”
  - This is not giving up — this is understanding that *Túrnakmol itself* doesn’t care.

## 9. Entry & Exiting: Onboarding the “World”

### 9.1 Arriving at the harbour

- Expect:
  - **Narrow sea‑cut into cliffs**,
  - **Dock in the collapsed lava tube**,
  - **150 meters of vertical cliff above you**,
  - **Metal platforms and ladders bolted onto rock**,  
    probably slick with salt spray and algae.

- First things:
  1. **Secure your belongings**:
     - Do everything with **one hand on the rail** if there is a rail.
     - Camera last, safety first.
  2. **Move from the dock to the station structure**:
     - Expect *noise*.
     - Expect **wind**.
     - Expect *obligations to the ocean* if you’re on a tiny platform.

### 9.2 Getting to the summit

If you ascend to the plateau:

- Take **local advice**:
  - Which path is “least bad” when.
  - What times of day storms are worst.
- **Never climb** in:
  - Full storm,
  - Thick fog,
  - If your footing is uncertain *before* leaving the station.

### 9.3 Leaving Túrnakmol

- **Weather** rules:
  - No schedule is safe until the boat or whatever vessel you use *arrives*.
  - Plan buffer: *at least* a few extra days.
- **Backpack safety**:
  - Keep important documents, meds, and a small “get‑home” kit in a **single drybag** you can grab.
