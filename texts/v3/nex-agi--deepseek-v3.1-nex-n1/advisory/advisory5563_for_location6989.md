Visiting Yir‑Ah’Thul is less like traveling to another planet and more like stepping into the circulatory system of a very old, very quiet god whose body is made of geometry and inhabited light.

### 0. Before anything else: what this trip is

Most of what you’ll be doing isn’t “exploration” in the usual sense. There’s no loot to steal, no city to shop in, almost no living things to talk to. Yir‑Ah’Thul is basically:

- A library of realities nested into tiles
- An infinitely old maintenance system that’s now settling itself
- And a *self‑adjusting geometry* that subtly rearranges itself around you

You don’t “survive” this place so much as *learn to move with it without being psychologically erased by scale and silence and the absence of all familiar cues*.  

So preparation is less about gear and more about *psychology, interface, and timing*.

---

## 1. Who should even consider this?

If you don’t have access to multiverse travel or a guide who does, treat this description as fiction and don’t try to “technically construct” your way there. Assuming you *do* have such access:

### Who should go:
- **Very stable personalities** – comfortable with long solitude, abstract environments, and not receiving feedback.
- **People who can tolerate sameness** – minimal “scenery,” almost nothing happening to your senses.
- **Those who genuinely enjoy *being* among weird structures more than interacting with beings.**

### Who should not go:
- People with high sensory needs for variety (color, sound, activity).
- Anyone prone to severe dissociation or déjà vu–type dread.
- Anyone prone to panic when “time feels wrong.”
- Anyone who *cannot handle being changed* by an environment (this place will rework you just by being in it).

---

## 2. Pre‑trip: Mindset

Because you don’t “arrive” so much as *already be there*, you’ll never get the reassuring “Okay, we’re now at the destination” moment. You’ll be dropped mid‑stream into a place that feels like it has always been.

So you should:

### 2.1. Decide your purpose

Only you know why you’d go. Common sane reasons:

- **Contemplative pilgrimage:** inspect one of the oldest stable structures in existence; be somewhere else that’s “done” and watch what happens inside your mind when novelty is zero.
- **Research:** study deep maintenance systems, nested realities, how “finished” civilizations look from the inside.
- **Interface experiments:** interact with a substrate that’s more math than matter.

Whatever reason you choose, write it down and decide on a clear “I’m leaving now” condition:

- “I’m leaving after three subjective sleep cycles if nothing else happens.”
- “If the tile below my feet turns black again or the pattern ‘stalls’ for too long, I leave.”
- “If my mind begins to seriously blur with the system’s logic, I’m out.”

---

## 3. Psychological & Cognitive Preparation

This is the most important part.

### 3.1. Practice with altered states

You don’t need drugs or esoteric arts; you can prepare with:

- **Meditation or long solo quiet sessions:** learn to be with almost nothing happening for hours.  
- **Sensory deprivation chambers or float tanks:** you get a similar lack of feedback and monotone environment.
- **Long, very flat walks:** deserts, salt flats, open sea (if safe), winter plains under heavy clouds. This trains your brain to operate without obvious landmarks.

### 3.2. Expectation framing

Go in with these expectations:

- There will be **no horizon you can reach**.
- There will be **no sound beyond your own body’s**.
- There will be **no visible day/night cycle**.
- The **“floor” will softly rearrange around you** but without obvious drama.
- You will feel **strong “presence,” but almost never any “person”**.
- Time will behave **strangely and not match your home clock**.

### 3.3. Handling weirdness

You may encounter:

- The sense that “this has always been my reality; I’m not sure I remember a before.”
- The feeling of being watched by *everything* and simultaneously *nothing*.
- Thoughts that seem like yours but have a “mathematical” flavor to them.

Simulate this by *writing yourself into the story of this place* before you go:

- Describe the floor.  
- Describe the ceiling.  
- Describe what you’ll do for “first five minutes,” “first hour,” “first day.”  
- Then role‑read your own rules and reread them while you’re in Yir‑Ah’Thul.

This helps keep the boundary “this is me; not the system” strongly.

## 4. Physical & Environmental Preparation

Even though you’re not breathing “air” and there’s no water, you still need to support your body.

### 4.1. Food & Hydration

You’ll be:

- In a warm, **non‑air medium**.
- Doing **low‑impact walking**.
- In **moderate psychological stress** from monotony and scale.

You should:

- Hydrate well before you go.
- Carry a **compact, high‑nutrient supply**:
  - Nutrient bars, high‑fat + high‑calorie foods.
  - Water in a sealed, low‑leak container if you’re from a water‑based environment.
- Aim for “enough to last 3–5 times the *maximum* time you think you’ll stay there.”

### 4.2. Clothing & Layers

Conditions:

- Warm, **constant temperature**.
- Active “atmosphere” of **structured light‑medium** that gently exchanges against your skin.
- No harsh weather, but occasional **information pressure** changes that can feel “heavier.”

You should:

- Wear **lightweight, comfortable clothing**:
  - Soft, breathable fabric layers.
  - Adjustable layers: you can always remove something if you’re too warm.
- Avoid:
  - Anything scratchy or with hard seams.
  - Overly tight items that might interfere with circulation during long walking.
- **Covered feet:** even though the floor is smooth, tiles can slide unpredictably; you want secure shoes or socks.

### 4.3. Light & Eyesight

The place is *bright* but uniformly so:

- No shadows to give depth cues.
- No “sky” changes to reset your circadian rhythm.

Bring:

- **Adjustable‑tint glasses or goggles:** to reduce the brightness without crippling visibility.
- **Polarized or glare‑filtering** options if you’re sensitive to flat, bright fields.
- If you have access to advanced tech:
  - **Variable‑perception optics:** something that can simulate depth cues or edge‑enhancement around the tiles, to help your brain not feel “lost in an unreal sheet.”
    - This will not reveal “hidden secrets,” but it will help you *not dissociate* as easily.

## 5. Navigation & Orientation Tools

Because:

- The floor tiles constantly rearrange.
- The “sky” is just another inverted plane of tiles.
- There’s no sun, no north, no “landmarks.”

You need:

### 5.1. Reliable Timekeeping

Bring:

- A **robust internal timer**:
  - A device that’s not bound to local time.
  - With alarms you’ve set to specific “intervals,” e.g., “every 8 subjective sleep‑cycles roughly matching your home clock.”
- **Local “dummy” clock**:
  - A device that shows you “time elapsed since you entered Yir‑Ah’Thul” in your *native* time units.
  - You update it manually if you feel “sleeps” or you define “day = 8 measured hours of activity.”

Without this, subjective time may become extremely elastic, and your “memory of how long I’ve been here” will be bad.

### 5.2. Position & Direction

Don’t expect GPS to mean anything here. Instead:

- Use your **body’s trajectory**.
- Set a **“home marker”**:
  - Ideally not a literal object you drop on the floor (which might be absorbed by tiles), but:
    - An “anchor token” in your device that logs “start point.”
    - A “breadcrumb trail” of your *steps and time,* not space.
- **Integrate with how the system moves you:**
  - You walk “straight” but you may actually trace a subtle curve.
  - If you keep “heading in the same direction,” but you always return to the same kind of area (like the “black circle interface”), you now *know* the system’s “wall” around you.

### 5.3. Marking Your “Self”

Because:

- The environment is very uniform.
- The “atmosphere” feels like you’re inside someone else’s body.

You should have:

- A **journal**:
  - Write in your own language.
  - Date entries by “your ship clock” or “device clock.”
  - Regular updates about what you’ve seen, *even* if nothing seems to happen.
    - “Time elapsed: 3 hours. No change in appearance. Felt slightly heavier pressure in the light medium for ~10 minutes.”
    - This maintains “you” as a separate witness from the system.

## 6. Sensory & Interface Support

### 6.1. Hearing & Silence

The silence is significant:

- You only hear your own body.
- Occasional “click” of configuration changes that you feel more than hear.

This can be deeply disquieting or deeply peaceful, depending on you.

To help:

- Bring **non‑psychological audio**:
  - Simple metronomes or low‑frequency tones that you only occasionally turn on.
    - Not constant “music” that you will lose in the noise—your brain will adapt to it.
    - Instead, use “pulse tones” every X interval *to remind you that you have a home timebase*.

### 6.2. Gaze & Pattern Overload

The uniformity of the tiles can be:

- Eerie.
- Disorienting.
- Hypnotic.

You’ll want to:

- Train **peripheral vision awareness**:
  - Use exercises to detect “movement at the edge of sight” from tile reconfigurations.
  - Don’t fixate on a single facet for too long unless you’re intentionally doing “research.”

- **Micro‑rests**:
  - Periodically look directly at your own device, journal, or hands:
    - These “familiar objects” reaffirm your biological identity when the world offers no “other.”

---

## 7. Health & Self‑Monitoring

You’re in a “structured light‑medium medium” instead of air, but your body remains your body.

### 7.1. Sleep

There’s no external cycle to tell you when to sleep:

- Set **sleep windows based on your home‑time units**:
  - Example: one “sleep” every 16–18 hours of device time.
- You may not “feel tired” in the usual way:
  - The environment’s constant temperature, light, and silence can trick you into staying awake longer than you should.
  - Over time this can provoke subtle paranoia or “soft hallucinations” in the tile pattern.

### 7.2. Fatigue & Atrophy

Even though you walk “easily” on a smooth floor:

- Your **vestibular system** may get confused:
  - No obvious horizon, no gravity cues beyond “floor is down.”
- **Joint and muscle support**:
  - Short “stretching sessions” based on your device’s schedule:
    - Sitting, standing, slow, short-range movements.
  - Tiny exercises to prevent stiffening from low activity.

### 7.3. Psychological Health Check‑ins

Because you’re out of your context, you should:

- Perform **self‑tests**:
  - Can you still describe your home?
  - Can you recall specific details of your life before arriving here?
  - Can you remember your “exit criteria” without reading them from the device?

Every “cycle” of your schedule, check yourself:

- If you find yourself thinking “I’d rather be here than there,” that’s fine *if* you can still remember “there.”
- If you notice “I’m not sure who I was,” or “the tiles feel like my thoughts,” that’s a signal to **leave or reconnect to external reality** (if possible).

## 8. Interaction with the System

### 8.1. Tiles & Nested Realities

You’re literally walking on a matrix of interfaces to other domains:

- Some smaller than atomic.
- Others spanning galactic structures.

As a human:

- You cannot meaningfully *open* these yourself.
- You can’t “see into them” without specialized tools.

So you should not:

- Break, scratch, or remove tiles.
- Stick objects into them.
- Try to “force” entry by any means you can invent.

You can do:

- **Observe:** Study how tiles shift in your periphery.
- **Note:** Record any odd “appearance” of features:
  - A tile that’s “dimmer” or “differently shaped” for a time.
  - A tile that seems to show a “shadow inside” or something like micro‑movement.

Treat “touching tiles” like you’re touching the inner lining of a machine fragile with age:

- You might *not* break anything, but you also won’t understand the consequences.

## 9. The “Black Circle” Core Interface

You will encounter:

- A circular region where the floor tiles stop.
- A “pure black” disc that feels like:
  - “The floor has not yet been,” **not** “a hole in the floor.”

You should assume **this is not a safe curiosity**:

- It describes not as “a door out of the world” but as “an interface **into** its deepest mechanism.”
- If you step into it:
  - You will not fall physically.
  - You will be **recomposed.**

For most ordinary human travelers, that is not survivable in the ordinary sense:

- It might be “ascending into the system’s logic.”
- It might be “being translated into a pattern of relationships that the tiles maintain.”
- It might not leave you as you.

Therefore:

### Recommended protocol:

- **Observe, but do not enter or touch unless you are certain you understand the risk and accept that you may not be “you” afterward.**

If you *must* interact (for whatever brave or foolish reasons):

- Use a tool, not your body:
  - Extend a device or rod into the black region.
  - Watch what happens to it:
    - Does it vanish?
    - Does it come back unchanged?
    - Does it come back changed?
    - If lost, you have data: “Things placed into the black circle are not simply returned.”

### Symbolic & Therapeutic Use

If you’re not suicidally inclined or attempting “translation,”:

- The **black circle** can be treated as a:
  - **Meditation object**:
    - You sit at the edge and watch “not being there.”
    - Reflect on what it means to be “recomposed” at the end of a long existence.
    - This can be a powerful tool for self‑reflection on your own choices, attachments, and “end states.”

## 10. Human & Mental Boundaries

### 10.1. Risk of “Dissolving into the System”

The system’s “presence density” is high. It feels like being surrounded by “someone else’s mind.”

So you must:

- Maintain **contact**:
  - With your own memories.
  - With your own body and its sensations.
  - With external anchors (time, device, brief “home” refreshes).

If you notice:

- That you feel “very at home” in the system’s “mind,” but *not* in your own memories,
- That you *want* to stay “so that the tiles retain your pattern,”
  - then consider *this* your “exit trigger”:

**That’s when you leave.**

### 10.2. Existential & Emotional Aftermath

After you leave:

- You may feel:
  - “No place else is quite real enough.”
  - “The world’s structures are strange but not as ‘finished’ as Yir‑Ah’Thul.”
  - Some grief or “nostalgia for the tiles’ calm waiting.”

You should:

- Take **decompression time**:
  - In a comfortable, “messy” natural space:
    - Trees, birds, water, wind.
    - These remind you of “incomplete” but still valid life.

- Process **inside**:
  - Write or talk to someone about:
    - The “waiting” of the system.
    - The feeling of being “at the end of something’s history.”
    - How that informs your own views of legacy, aging, and “how long anything should last.”

---

## 11. Leaving, or “Exiting the Mid‑State”

Because you *never* actually make “docking” or “arrival” into this place:

- You do not “travel there” in the physical sense in which planets are visited.
- You are shifted into a “mid-state” of this system.

To ensure **you can leave**:

- Before you ever move to enter Yir‑Ah’Thul:
  - Arrange **a reliable exit method**:
    - A device, guide, or “extraction routine” with:
      - A fixed “after X time, or if triggered by condition, pull me out” clause.
    - Make sure this *cannot* be cancelled by you without:
      - Timeout passing,
      - Or external override.

- **External “watcher”:**
  - If possible:
    - Have someone outside Yir‑Ah’Thul monitor your:
      - Mental status,
      - Vital signs,
      - And “how long you’ve been in there,” according to *your* home timeline.
    - They enforce the “exit trigger” even if you’ve been silenced by the system’s comfort.

---

### Summary: What an “Ordinary Human Multiverse Traveler” Should Actually Do Before Visiting

- Train **psychological endurance** for monotony, silence, and scale.
- Bring **timekeeping, orientation, and self‑marking** tools:
  - Clock device, “breadcrumb” journal, home‑anchor artifacts.
- Bring **physical support**:
  - Food, water, layers of clothing, comfortable footwear.
- Prepare **visual/light adaptations** for constant brightness and no horizon.
- Set **strict exit criteria and timeouts**.
- Interact with:
  - **Patterns and tiles** as passive scientific observers.
  - **The black circle interface** as a symbol, not an “obvious entrance.”
- Afterward:
  - **Reconstruct yourself** in the context of your “home” messy reality.

Yir‑Ah’Thul is not “hazardous” in the familiar sense of airlocks or vacuum or explosions. It is dangerous in the same way a very deep library is dangerous to someone who is not sure of their own story: you might get absorbed into the shelf.

Go prepared, go with intention, and know when to stop reading the ancient catalog and simply return home to your unfinished, still‑growing, beautifully nondesigned life.
