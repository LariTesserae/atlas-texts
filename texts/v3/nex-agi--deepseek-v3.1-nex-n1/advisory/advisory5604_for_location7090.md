You are not supposed to be here.

Most of the places I send travelers to are built for human use, even if “human” is a loose category. This one is not. It is an inside-out machine-city whose original users may be gone, collapsed into the mountain itself, or frozen into the glass of the walls. The air, the water, and the sound have been engineered to keep this machine running, not to keep you warm, safe, or welcome.

If you still want to go, you will need to be less of a person and more of a careful piece of instrumentation.

---

### 1. Before You Step Through: Conceptual Preparation

You are not entering a “place with rocks” so much as a sealed environmental system that happens to look like a cavern.

To survive it:

- Think of yourself as walking into the arteries of a huge organism.
- Assume the organism is not hostile to you personally—but has never been adapted to living meat inside its veins.
- Respect every boundary as if it were electrical.

You will need to be very quiet in both body and mind. There is very little that can be improvised if things go wrong here. The best tools are the ones you already have before you arrive.

---

### 2. Physical Readiness: Your Body Needs to Cooperate With the Place

**Cold.**  
The air is cold enough to show your breath, but not freezing. The walls are colder than the air in the center of the chamber. This means:

- You will want **thermal layers that wick moisture**, but not too many of them. If you get too warm and sweat, the moisture will be difficult to shed in the already humid environment.
- Choose **thin, layered, breathable fabrics** over bulky insulation. A thin wind shell that is fully waterproof is very useful here, because it keeps you from constantly being dampened by condensation and passing rivulets.
- Your extremities will be the first to fail you: **good gloves**, **lightweight thermal socks**, and **insoles** if you are prone to cold feet.

**Air circulation.**  
Your lungs are going to notice this first. The air is engineered to move on a schedule; it feels compressed, “breathed” by the mountain itself.

- If you have asthma, or are sensitive to changes in air pressure or humidity, you should seriously **reconsider this trip**. The air is clean but strange, carrying mineral traces and subtle machine-lubricants.
- If you go anyway, bring **inhalers, antihistamines**, and any **bronchodilators** you are prescribed. Assume your allergies will be annoyed by the dust and the microfilters, even if they remain “invisible” to you.

**Hydration.**  
The water keeps moving but you are mostly not allowed to drink it.

- The water is infrastructure, not a supply line. It might contain **stabilizers, esters of synthetic lubricants, or solutes** from the machine-city.
- Bring **all your own water** in a collapsible container or internal bladder pack with a sip-tube.
- Plan to drink **small amounts frequently**: this is a place where you do not want to lose focus.

**Food and waste.**  
The mountain is sterile of organic life. There is no “nature” to break down your presence.

- Bring **sealed rations** that don’t leave crumbs or oils. If you can, eat before you go and aim not to eat at all inside the chamber; if the visit is longer than you expect, you should at least not be adding to the waste.
- You **must not leave any traces of your body here**. Bring:
  - Small **sealable waste bags** (humanure or the equivalent).
  - **Biodegradable disinfectant wipes** (if available in your world) but used ones must be sealed out with you.
- Think of yourself as a bacteriologist visiting a cleanroom: you are the primary vector of contamination.

**Fatigue.**  
This is not a place to sleep.

- Make sure you are **well rested before transit**.
- Keep visits to a **planned time window** (a few hours at most).
- Do not lie down on the floor unless you are prepared to be mistaken by the machine-city for part of its own internal surface pattern.

### 3. Equipment: What to Actually Bring With You

#### 3.1. Clothing and Personal Protection

- **Base layers:** Moisture-wicking, long sleeves and legs.
- **Mid-layer:** Thin fleece or insulated layer that can be removed without stopping too long.
- **Shell layer:** Fully waterproof but **quiet** (no crinkly plastic rain shells).
- **Head:** A thin beanie or balaclava; the ceiling is high and the heat goes from your head more than you feel like.
- **Hands:** **Thin but warm gloves** with **silicone or high-friction pads** on the fingertips and palm, so you can touch without slipping if you must.
- **Feet:** **Lined, waterproof, rock-slipping resistant** boots or approach shoes. Avoid deep treads that will trap water and then leave it behind as prints. The goal is to leave the floor behind you almost exactly as you found it.
- **Sunglasses or clear goggles:** The light is not always bright but it is precise and sometimes very sharp; you may want to be able to look at ceiling and walls without squinting.

**No jewelry**, **no loose straps**, **nothing dangling** that can catch moisture or be accidentally “sensed” as a fluid object by the air system. The translucent silk-filters might investigate your soft edges if they are allowed to.

#### 3.2. Navigation and Recording

- **Field notebook with waterproof paper:** Even if you record everything else electronically, treat this as your primary “final backup” of the place.
- **Pencil only:** No ink. Ink can be washed away by the water threads or absorbed into the stone, where it might be interpreted by the mountain as contamination.
- **Simple compass/clinometer:** the wall is the world here; you might need to map orientations against arbitrary “up” and “down.”
- **Laser distance meter or small range finder** if you have it: measuring distances to the walls and ceiling will help you understand where you are in the machine’s geometry.
- **Camera with good low-light sensitivty and fixed or wide-angle lens:** The light is aesthetic but unforgiving. You want to record without flash; if the flash existed in the spectrum of this machine-city, it might be interpreted as noise, error, or an attack.
- As a rule of thumb: **anything that makes noise or bright light** should be kept to the minimum needed to record.

#### 3.3. Survival and Repair Tools

- **Headlamp with low red mode** and fully waterproof housing:
  - You will use it far more often than you expect in the colder shadows and away from the engineered beams.
- **Backup lamp:** Failures of light are failures of orientation here.
- **Small whistle or silent distress signal**:
  - You might not be able to shout loudly in the engineered soundscape of the chamber without disturbing the system.
- **Basic first aid kit**:
  - Adhesive bandages that do not shed fibers.
  - Sterile strips.
  - Antiseptic wipes that you will take back with you.
- **Multipurpose tool** with knife, pliers, fine screwdriver:
  - Do not use the knife to carve into the stone or even to test its hardness.
  - Use it only for your own gear and under the assumption that you will not leave metal shavings behind.

#### 3.4. Water and Food (Again, Because You Are the Weakest Link)

- **Water bladder or collapsible bottle** with measured daily water supply.
- **Electrolytes:** The humidity and the slightly different air composition will tire you faster than you expect.
- **No open food:** Energy bars should be pre-sheathed and individually wrapped if possible.

**Everything you bring with you into the chamber must be considered as something that must leave as you do.**

### 4. Behavioral Rules: How to Walk Inside a Machine-City

#### 4.1. Movement and Touch

- Walk **slowly** and as **lightly** as you can:
  - The floor looks like a scientific instrument. Use its existing channels as your guide. If you can walk between the main carved water-paths rather than across them, do so.
- Minimize contact with the walls:
  - The condensation flows are part of the system’s circulation. If you touch, you might divert water routes or leave skin oils and salts that could be read by the machine as a “message” or “error.”
- Never touch the translucent filters:
  - They are remnants of a waste system that has been running longer than you have been alive. Let them pass; if one of them wraps your hand, you do not want to find out what it does to foreign matter.

**The “noise” you are allowed to add is the least amount possible.** The system already has wind and near-silent water; you should be even quieter than that.

#### 4.2. Sound and Light

- Do not talk loudly.
- Keep your voice to **whispers or soft murmurs**, if you must speak at all.
- Avoid **shouting** or even raising your voice; if you need to call out, you might already be in more trouble than your voice can solve.
- **Do not use flash** unless your life is in danger. This is not a place that “expects” sudden bright light the same way it expects water and air.

#### 4.3. Sampling and “Collecting”

You are, at least in this system’s memory, a trespasser.

- Do not take samples of the rock:
  - The surface strength of the glassstone is part of the machine’s skeleton.
- Do not try to capture the water and take it out:
  - The water is not “water” so much as a signal carrier; removing it anywhere from the system may be like cutting a nerve.
- **Do not attempt to collect or disrupt the filters.** That is exactly how you get noticed by the larger machine.

**The only thing you are allowed to take and, ethically, required to take out is exactly what you brought in.**

### 5. Timing and Duration of Visit

From the description, the light itself is “engineered into the mountain” so that at a certain hour and height the beams fall exactly where you are now and flood the floor before you.

This implies:

- **There is a specific time when the light is “correct” in this chamber.**  
  Before you go, ask:
  - “What hour of the day makes this particular point of the machine visible?”
  - “Is this time matched to a world I’m already in or to the city’s own clock?”

You should try to arrive during this time, because all the clues of the machine-city will be mapped out by the shadows.

**Length of stay:**

- Plan for a **short visit** first:
  - No longer than **two hours**.
- You are not adapted to breathing air that has been designed over geological timescales for turbines and rock and glass.
- **If you start to feel dizzy or headachy, leave at once.** That is the system telling you—through its regulatory chemistry—that you are not integrated enough to be tolerated longer.

**Do not stay to watch “just one more” event of light or air or water.** The mountain is orchestrating its affairs over a longer timescale than you are capable of patience.

### 6. Mental and Emotional Preparation: For the Sake of Your Own Continuity

This place is uncanny in a very specific way: it still works like a city though the city itself is gone.

You will feel:

- **Small and temporary**, against a machine built of mountains and time.
- **Deeply watched** by the geometry of the walls and the way the air moves on schedule.
- A **quietness of life**—no birds, no leaves, no soil smells—just the water and the stone and the ghost-city of structures inside the glass.

Since there is no “nature” here to soothe you—no trees, no green, no soft edges—your own mind is the only cushion.

- Before you go, spend **10 minutes in quiet stillness**:
  - Breathe in the very world you are about to leave.
  - Remind yourself why you are doing this. If the answer is “curiosity,” then let your curiosity be tempered with respect.
- Be **an observer of this machine-city rather than its interpreter.**

You will be tempted to read meaning into every pattern: the chart of water on the floor, the faint turbulence inside the glass rock, the “ghost of a city grid turned inside out.” This is fine as long as you remember that you are not looking at a finished puzzle; you are looking at a machine that is still running a calculation whose outcome you cannot know.

**Do not expect to “understand” this place.** Expect to stand respectfully in its core chamber for a few hours and then to leave it exactly as you found it.

---

### 7. On the Way Out: You Must Leave No Trace of Existence

When you are ready to leave:

- Do **one last careful pass** of your body and equipment:
  - Check for smudges, water drops from your own gear, footprints of water that trace from your boots onto the dry paths of the machine.
- Do **not leave behind**:
  - Any hair, fingernail clippings, or skin cells (more than you can possibly avoid).
  - Any crumbs of food or paper.
  - Any moisture of your own that can be separated from the machine’s flow.
- **Seal everything disposable** inside your own pack and bring it out.

**If you can, walk your own path back out of the chamber, so that you are not crossing the same machine-surfaces twice more than you have to.**

From the perspective of the machine-city, the best visit you can make is one that leaves no record in its water, its stone, or its air except as a theoretical possibility of disturbance that never quite fully manifests.

---

### 8. If Something Goes Wrong: What You Cannot Prepare For, You Should Not Provoke

Even if you follow all of this advice:

- You might step where the machine is monitoring pressure more closely than it seems.
- You might find yourself under the shadow of a “flute” that suddenly inhales more strongly than usual.
- A translucent filter might entangle your outstretched hand before you pull it back.

**Your best safety net is humility:**

- If you hear **new sounds** out of the ordinary rhythm of wind and water, **that is your alarm to leave immediately.**
- If you see **the water flow change its pattern from what you have been observing for the last hour, something is being recalibrated** and you should not be in the middle of it.
- If **the air currents suddenly sharpen or shift from their “breathing” pattern**, the mountain is stirring and you are not yet strong enough to be caught between its ribs.

**Emergency exit:**

If you have been sent by any of the usual multiverse methods I know (portals, gates, keys, etc.), then know your **return phrase** or **object** before you set foot inside. This is not a place to be rummaging in your pockets for a way out.

Call out your exit word **before you are certain** you are in trouble. If you are already sure, you might be late.

### 9. A Final Memorandum of Intent to the Place Itself

Before you pass through the wall of stone into this chamber, speak under your breath or to yourself:

> “I am not here to change you.  
>  I am not here to understand you completely.  
>  I am only here to witness for a short time and then leave.  
>  If you can allow that without marking me as noise or waste, I will do my best to keep the balance of your water, air, and stone.”

It might sound like superstition to talk to the mountain.

But remember: the mountain itself was already a machine before you called it a mountain.
