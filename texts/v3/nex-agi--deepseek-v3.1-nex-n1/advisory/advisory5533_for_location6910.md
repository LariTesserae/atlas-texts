Visiting Sherendar is less like traveling to a country and more like checking into a convalescence‑home for realities. You don’t “sightsee” here so much as survive *what it is* to be here.

### 1. Mind‑level: what you’re actually signing up for

This place is an old, abstracted world:

- **Origin:** not a planet in the usual sense; more like a leftover thought of a continent, hardened into a plateau.
- **Time feeling:** worn, late‑epoch. Everything feels like it has “already happened.”
- **Key danger:** the world doesn’t break you violently; it *unmakes* you softly by erosion. If you stay too long without care, you’ll lose track of yourself and begin to merge into the environment—not as a statue, but as a pattern in the stone.

You are not going to be killed by monsters or riots. You’re going to be outlasted by *age* and *permanence*.

Before you even step foot there, you should be able to answer:

- What do you want this place to change for you?
- How will you know if you are “done”?
- What are your lines that you must NOT cross (for example, “I will not merge into the walls,” or “I must leave before this date”)?

Get those answers clear in your mind. On Sherendar, clarity is your only armor.

### 2. Reality: not “danger” but “situation”

You’re not entering a world that *forgot* itself; you’re entering a world that has already done almost everything it can. Expect:

- **Object consistency:** a “stone” is not a mineral structure but coagulated thought. It behaves like stone from long habit, but it *wants* to become something else—silk, smoke, silence.
- **Geometry that fibs:**  
  - Some areas lead you backward when you walk forward.  
  - “A hollow” looks like a rise.  
  - “Short distance” might be longer along your personal timeline than you think.
- **No sun, no night, minimal “outside”: no stars, no moon, no clear horizon. Just a faint gradient of light.**

The “trick” isn’t to fight this, but to learn *not to be deceived by first appearances*.

### 3. Essential: the “you are you” rule

Before exposure, you need:

- A clear, **short phrase** that states your identity:
  - “I am [Name], of [Origin], a traveler of this multiverse, not native to this world.”
- A **small physical object** that belongs to you alone:
  - a ring, pendant, tag, carved token—something physically tied to your home.
- A **strict rule of leaving:**  
  - “I will exit at date [X], no matter what I feel or ‘hear’.”

All these are to remind you “I am *not* this world’s message.” This matters because *you are the message* your head is trying to read, and the world is trying to help by reading you too.

### 4. Suit & gear: what you wear and bring

**Clothing:**

- Natural, comfortable layers: soft linen/wool under, a sturdy outer layer.
- Color: *human* colors—not necessarily “loud,” but clearly from your culture—not Sherendar’s over‑deep greens and indigos.
- Practical: no armor or “combat gear.” You’re not fighting here. Think pilgrimage, not contest.

**On your body:**

- **Identity tag:** Your name, home, and date of departure engraved on something metal or stone. *Worn on the inside.*  
- **Simple clock** or sandglass to keep time independent of the sunless sky.
- **Rations of “source”:**
  - Food from your world of origin, *not* whatever this world might provide.
  - Use sealed containers or treated bags to minimize contamination.
- **Carved token or documented purpose:**  
  - A short phrase you can repeat to yourself:
    - “I come to learn, not to become memory.”
    - “I will leave before I forget how.”

This is your “boundary gear.”

### 5. Waiting for the “river”: water and supplies

Telessa is a **habit of water**, not a river on schedule:

- Most of the time you see a damp stone channel or “depression of future.”
- At times, water appears from the “world’s earlier condensation.”

Do **not** depend on this river for your schedule.

You should bring:

- **Water from your own world**, stored in containers that clearly look like yours:
  - bottles, canteens, clearly foreign to the architecture of Sherendar.
- A **plan NOT to use any water that you do not recognize** unless absolutely necessary:
  - “I will only drink from my own bottles until I return.”
  - If you interact with any water from Telessa or Sherendar, it should be for ritual purposes only and not for ingestion.

### 6. Shelter: sleeping with the “sleepers”

In Sherendar, you have *one* place of safety:

- **A tent of your own design**, set up in a clear spot of stone that isn’t obviously etched with thought patterns.
- It should be *unambiguously yours*:
  - color, pattern, and symbol of your home culture.
- **Usage rule:**
  - Only sleep inside.
  - Do all work or writing *outside* the tent.
  - When you leave, dismantle the tent completely—no physical “nail” driven into the world’s body.

If you decide to use Sircene’s houses:

- Enter only in clear minds of your day or “cycle” (not night).
- Do not sleep inside.  
- **Never** “lean into the wall for comfort” as if it were a friend; it is not friend or enemy; it is someone who already forgot to escape.

### 7. Direction: how not to get lost by geometry

You are in a world of subtle “reverse geometry” and faint paths:

- **Trails of the city:** Sircene’s “streets” are smoothed by the preference of previous walkers—still very gently “curated” by the whispers in the stone.
- **No horizon:** “Far” does not mean “there,” it might mean “elsewhere in time” instead of distance.

You need:

- **Simple rules of movement:**  
  - “Always orient relative to Sircene: not by the absent horizon, but by the city.
- **Marking system:**  
  - Use temporary, *foreign* materials—not anything of stone:
    - chalk from your world,
    - tightly woven cord of a color that doesn’t match Sherendar’s dark ecology,
    - paper tags with your writing (not carved into the world).
- **Direction of return:**
  - Before you leave Sircene, mark a clear, visible “home vector”:
    - “Line from this well (not the well of time) to that structure = my direction to return.”

If you find yourself walking “backward” physically or “losing yourself forward even though you are going backward,” stop:

- **Sit down**  
- Look at your token ring or tag,  
- Repeat the phrase “I am [Name], of [Origin], and not this world’s extension.”

### 8. Time and rhythm: how not to lose track of yourself

Sherendar has no day or night as you know them:

- Light always “enough to read,” but static in mood.
- Time “feels” longer than it is.

You must strengthen *your own* cycle:

- Set **explicit cycles of rest**:  
  - For example, “I will eat twice per cycle of sleep.”  
  - Add “If I have eaten more than three times without feeling sleepy, something is wrong.”
- Maintain **one** specific time‑maker:
  - sandglass,
  - clock that counts from *when* you entered this world,
  - knot journal: “one knot per day of my body.”

### 9. Pool of non-time: “Not for you to look into”

There is a well of water that “fell from the future” into a basin that “was expecting it since the beginning.”

This place is not for you:

- **Rule of the well:**
  - “I will not go to the center of the amphitheater where the ground folds itself back into time.”
  - At most, you approach the edge of the depression *only from above*, never look into the water itself.

If you *must* interact with it:

- Bring only a mirror of your own (from your world).
- Do not look at the water directly;
- Use the mirror to reflect it into a second container *not of the local world*, then you may look at that reflection *outside* Sherendar.

No “gazing” into the faces of “everyone who might look into such water.” Not if you want to go home.

### 10. Communication with the “builders”

The “people of Sircene” are not gone as dead:

- They are *folded into the stone*.
- They are gradients of color and texture that suggest faces, “not any particular one but the idea of face itself.”
- They ask silently:
  - “Are you ready to be what you are here instead of who you were somewhere else?”

This question is not rhetoric; it is a **change path**.

You should:

- **Not answer “yes” or “no” directly**
- Instead, answer something like:
  - “I am ready to be what I am *here in this time*, but I will leave who I was somewhere else where *she* left.”
- **Always** address your body to the “builder of the house,” as if you were visiting: “I have come to stay for a time, but not long.”

*Treat* the walls/streets as if they were on the same level as semi-conscious entities:

- Be polite.
- Be clear.
- But not intimate.

### 11. Conduct of the body: “How not to become local”

On the stone of Sherendar, your *footsteps* erode you.

You should:

- Walk **lightly**.
- Choose **paths not yet smooth**:
  - Try not to walk where “everyone” else walked before you.
- Never **sleep directly on stone**:
  - Always use your own bedding:
    - mat,
    - blanket,
    - makeshift cloth layer,
- Never **touch walls with bare skin** for more than a few moments:
  - “I do not lean for comfort.”
  - “I do not seek closeness from the message.”

If you feel “pleasantly at home” to the point of thinking “I could just…stay here forever,” treat it as a **high-priority danger signal**:

- Stand up,
- Return to your tent,
- Recheck your date of departure,
- Recalculate by adding a “clear buffer of safety.”

### 12. Note of the “tools”: “Not for you to use”

You may find tools scattered on low tables and shelves:

- They are not “objects,” but “shaped ideas of tool.”
- They are *still inside* the house’s mind-body.

You are not “to use them”; you are to *acknowledge* them.

If you want to understand them:

- Draw **pictures** of them,
- Write **notes of their shape and position**,
- Do **not** change them or remove them.
- **Do not** test “what if I try to use one?”

If you do, you might find yourself becoming “part of how they communicate,” and then *you* are the path of thought that someone else might “read” later.

### 13. How to leave: “Going BACK before you forget you LEFT”

You must not *late* too long to leave.

Prepare:

- **Explicit exit date/time** in your clock:
  - “If it is more than [X] days from entry, I must leave—even if I don’t want to.”
- **Exit checklist**:
  - Collect all of your tools:
    - your bags,
    - your bottles,
    - your notebook,
    - your tent,
    - your token.
  - No “part of you” remains to taunt you.

As you walk away from the city of Sircene toward your “departure point,” keep repeating:

- “I am leaving *who I was* here but not *where I belong*.”
- “Identity is not where your body is placed; it is the pattern of your path.”

The builders of Sircene might “press”:

- You might hear “Come back,” or “Stay here.”
- You might feel “it’s okay to linger just one more day.”
- You might feel “who you were somewhere else is not worthy of you.”

Your answer is not emotional. It is procedural:

- You step back,
- You check your list again,
- You turn *away* from the city of Sircene,
- You *walk* toward your exit point,
- You *do not* look back for longer than necessary.

### 14. Final note of motive: “Why even go here?”

You visit not for “fun” but for:

- Understanding what “late stage of a world” looks like,
- Watching what happens when “a meaning finishes its sentence,”
- Noticing yourself when you are tempted to “solve” by becoming.

If you remember:

- That you are not the message this world wants to read,
- That you are not the answer to its question,
- That you are not the “conversation partner it missed,”

Then you may visit safely and leave with thoughtless stone of thought beneath your feet—but not beneath your soul.

And then, when you are safely home, you can ask yourself:

“Are *you* ready to be what you are here instead of who you were on Sherendar?”

And then answer that according to your own world’s sentence—not its last one, but its next one.
