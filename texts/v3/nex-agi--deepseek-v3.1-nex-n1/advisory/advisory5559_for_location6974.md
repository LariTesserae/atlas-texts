To visit the Root‑Bound Sky is to visit a continent‑sized body that has replaced geology with metabolism. The ground is alive, the sky is empty, and the “weather” is the world breathing. You’re stepping into a single, brutally efficient system, not a wilderness. That means your preparation should be built around three things:  

1. Protecting your body from a dry, high, loud, relentless‑light environment.  
2. Navigating the gaps and flows of a giant living factory instead of “earth.”  
3. Staying out of the way of a maintenance swarm that neither hates you nor loves you—but may fix or break you as a matter of routine.

Below is what you, as an ordinary human traveler, need to think through and bring.

---

## 1. Mindset: Before You Even Pack

### You are entering *body space*, not landscape

- On Earth, “ground” means dirt and rock and oceans that you can mostly trust to stay put under you.
- Here, ground is a plant‑continent that is actively adjusting itself under wind load, internal pressure, and growth.  
  Think: you’re walking on a giant living machine. That means:
  - Platforms are safe but not *static*.
  - Gaps, shafts, and ledges are structural features, not accidents.
  - The “little” creatures you see are not pests or animals; they are the *immune system and maintenance crew* of the world‑body.

### You are more fragile than anything else there

There is no soil.  
There are no rivers or ponds.  
There are no other mammals, and no “nature” that you can casually rely on for food or water.

What you *will* rely on:
- All you bring with you.
- The fact that the Root‑Bound Sky is not actively malicious—just indifferent to your existence.

### Adjust your expectations for noise and scale

- You will be *inside* sound, not beside it.
- The piping will be constant, low‑frequency, and bone‑conducted.
- There will be no silence, ever.
- You will need active hearing protection and a way to communicate without yelling.

Think of it as visiting:
- a factory floor  
- plus a cathedral organ  
- plus a wind tunnel  
— continuously running at world scale.

## 2. Core Equipment

### A. Atmosphere & Environment

**Temperature / clothing**

- Day and night are “cool” here, but not freezing.  
  Expect something like late spring / alpine conditions:
  - A base layer of thin synthetic or merino wool long‑sleeve and long johns.
  - Mid‑layer fleece or light insulated jacket.
  - Wind shell that can handle:
    - Strong, steady cross‑winds
    - Gusts from updrafts and vents from shafts below
    - Possible wind‑driven “dust” of bark flakes or plant particles

- **Gloves** are important:
  - Light but durable. You’ll be touching bark surfaces, which can be smooth but also:
    - Abraded by wind.
    - Potentially exuding sap resins.
  - You need grip on the platform surfaces that are polished by millennia of flow.

- **Goggles / sunglasses**  
  The light will be bright and unrelenting:
  - No humidity haze.
  - No clouds to diffuse.
  - UV can be brutally strong at high elevation.
  - Wrap‑around or side‑shielded glasses are best to block wind and glare.
  - Consider tinted photochromic lenses if you can, so you don’t need to keep changing gear.

**Air pressure & altitude**

The “world” is high enough that you feel the curvature, but you can still breathe comfortably.

Still:
- If you are prone to altitude sickness:
  - Acclimatize beforehand if you can.
  - Consider acetazolamide or a similar prescription if you have access and your doctor approves.
- The air is cool and dry:
  - Bring lip balm and skin moisturizer.
  - The dryness will feel more like a desert than a forest.

### B. Noise Protection

This is critical. The Root‑Bound Sky is deafening by scale.

You need:

- **High‑SNR earplugs**  
  - Foam or silicone for high noise reduction.
  - Carry enough pairs to change when they get dirty.

- **Over-ear protection** if possible:
  - Electronic shooting earmuffs or industrial earmuffs are good:
    - They allow you to hear speech and your own voice at reduced volume,
    - But suppress the loud bass rumble of the world-pipes.

- **Communication system**
  - Voice volume alone will fail you:
    - Consider simple throat mics or bone-conduction headsets.
    - Even a minimal two‑way radio system with headsets or in-ear buds beats shouting over wind and pipe organ thunder.

Test your system in a loud environment before you go:
- You must be able to clearly understand a few key words, such as “stop,” “edge,” “loose,” “back.”

### C. Hydration & Food

There is **no usable water** in the Root‑Bound Sky.

- All moisture is locked in the living plant tissue.
- “No rain, no rivers, no seas.”
- You cannot count on being able to extract moisture reliably.

So you must:

- **Bring all water for your entire stay**
  - Carry more than if you were visiting a wet world:
    - The air is dry, so insensible water loss and respiration losses are high.
    - Walking and climbing in this environment will dehydrate you faster than you expect.

- **Water storage**
  - Flexible water bladders with bite valves:
    - Freeze half or more of your water before you leave if possible (to keep it longer and act as a cold reserve).
    - Plan in time: how many days are you staying?
    - Aim for at least 3–5 liters per day per person if you are moving actively.
    - More if you are very sensitive to dry air.

- **Filters & tablets**
  - You’re not likely to find usable water, but if absolutely desperate:
    - A filter or chemical tablets are still better than nothing.
    - You might find some sap collected in low depressions or crevices—but you are not made to metabolize sap of a continent‑plant.
    - Treat this as a last‑resort survival strategy rather than a plan.
    - Always be prepared to **not drink**.

- **Food**
  - Bring:
    - Compact, high‑energy, non-perishable foods.
    - Calorie‑dense bars, nuts, nut butters, jerky, dried fruits.
    - Multivitamin supplement to keep micronutrient intake up.
  - Plan:
    - Enough meals for your planned stay plus extra days in case of delay.
    - No foraging on this world: you cannot eat its bark-or-leaf maintenance workers, and there is no soil ecosystem.

### D. Tools & Safety Gear

You **can not** rely on finding metal tools or easily workable materials on the world‑tree. Most everything is tough plant structures.

Pack essentials:

- **Fixed‑blade knife** and multitool:
  - For cutting straps, emergency self‑extrication, or improvising splints.
- **Light rope or static line**
  - 10–20 meters of 6–8 mm diameter cord:
    - To anchor yourself when approaching edges or shafts.
- **Carabiners**:
  - 3–5 of them:
    - For clipping your pack to yourself, or to a fixed line around a thick pipe stem or branch root when you want to sit near a drop.
- **Petzl / headlamp**
  - Even though days are bright:
    - Gaps, shafts, and under platforms will be dim or green‑dark, especially lower you go.
    - Bring extra batteries or a power bank for rechargeable ones.
- **Whistle and signaling gear**
  - In that noise environment:
    - A whistle may be hard to hear over the world‑hum.
    - But it’s still better than nothing:
    - Use repetitive patterns: three blasts for help.
- **First aid kit**
  - Emphasize:
    - Blister care (moleskin, tape): lots of walking on firm surfaces.
    - Sterile dressings, gauze, antiseptic wipes.
    - Bandages, elastic wrap.
    - Basic pain meds, antihistamines, and any personal meds you need.
    - Eye rinse solution: you’re in dry air with plant dust in the wind.

### E. Light and Color Protection

- **Sunscreen**
  - High SPF (broad spectrum UVA/UVB).
- **Light but long‑sleeved clothing**
  - Helpful against:
    - Drying out in the sun.
    - Irritation from plant dust.
    - Possibly sticky resins if you lean heavily against surfaces.

- **Optional: color flash**
  - If you want a marker:
    - A small, neon or high‑visibility article of clothing:
      - Bright orange or yellow flag or scarf.
    - This helps:
      - Others see you against the overwhelmingly green/brown/cream palette.
      - The maintenance creatures are not deterred by color, but it might help you visually track each other in a sparse landscape.

### F. Navigation and Timekeeping

There is no bedrock geology, no magnetic iron field necessarily you can rely on.

So you must:

- **Make your own landmarks**
  - Take photos paired with notes:
    - Which pipe clusters are near you?
    - Which shaft pattern?
    - Which platform ledges are recognizable?
- **Use a compass only if you know the world has magnetic north**
  - If you don’t know:
    - You rely on:
      - A sun–like object’s direction:
        - Note which direction it lies when you arrive.
        - Watch how it moves:
          - East to west or otherwise?
          - Timekeeping by sun will be your only natural clock.
- **Bring a simple watch and altimeter**
  - Even cheap digital watches:
    - Time is important for rationing food/water.
    - An altimeter (or at least a barometer‑based sensor):
      - Helps you notice if you are ascending or descending the platform tiers.
      - The world curves under you, but elevation changes more subtly within the plant.

- **Notepad or smartphone**
  - Sketch simple maps:
    - Platform shape.
    - Location of major shafts.
    - “Vents” where wind gusts are particularly strong.
  - Label:
    - Safe zones (areas away from edges and strong shafts).
    - “Noise” zones:
      - Where you know you can’t communicate except by sign or touch.

### G. Shelter and Sleep

You need something to protect you from:

- The wind.
- The constant sound.
- The bright sky.

Plan:

- **Light trekking pole tent or bivy**
  - You do not need to worry about rainfall:
    - This simplifies shelter design.
  - You need:
    - Strong ties to the platform:
      - Use guy lines attached to:
        - Large root‑knuckles or thickened stem joints.
        - Heavy rocks or weighted bags if you have them.
    - A low profile:
      - To avoid turning into a sail in high wind.

- **Sleep color**
  - Use:
    - Earplugs plus inflatable earmuffs or noise‑cancelling headphones if practical (but be aware of battery life).
    - A sleep mask or bandana over your eyes:
      - The world stays bright at all times you’re in the day-like side of the world.

- **Sleep location**
  - Choose:
    - A flat, smooth portion of the platform:
      - Avoid sleeping near exposed shafts or freshly cracked surfaces.
    - Not directly under huge overhangs:
      - They are living plant parts:
        - They can shift over time.
    - Not directly under massive “chimney” pipes:
      - You may get blasted by air and noise surges.

### H. Hygiene and Waste Management

There is no soil, no decomposition community like earthworms and bacteria you can count on.

- **Bring sealable bags for waste**
  - Pack out all:
    - Organic waste.
    - Products you can’t burn.
    - Even biodegradable tissues.
    - The world‑tree doesn’t have bacteria to handle your kind of organic matter.

- **Personal hygiene products**
  - Dry wipes or sanitizer wipes:
    - They’re better than trying to wash with limited water.
    - No dumping soapy water onto the plant:
      - You don’t know how its sap tissues will react.

- **Urine / other waste**
  - Use a container or bottle system:
    - You can seal and carry out with you.
  - Do not presume:
    - This plant’s sap channels can tolerate or ignore human urine or salts.

### I. Morale and Mental Health

This environment can be mentally overwhelming:

- The noise.
- The sheer otherness.
- The absence of any soundscape we recognize.

Prepare:

- **Music or audio**
  - On headphones:
    - You may need familiar soundscapes to sleep or to feel calm.
    - But:
    - Don’t use noise-isolation headphones so much you can’t hear what happens around you (e.g., your own breath in high effort, or close calls).

- **Simple routines**
  - Meal times.
  - Short journal entries.
  - A photograph of home or something familiar you can look at.

## 3. Once You Arrive: What It Actually Feels Like and How to Move

### Getting your bearings

When you first step through:

- You’ll notice:
  - The **hard bright sky**.
  - The **sound**:
    - A huge bass rumble with overtones.
    - You may feel it in your chest and teeth.
  - The **wind**:
    - Multiple layers of airflow, not just one breeze.

Give yourself 10–15 minutes to:

- Put in hearing protection.
- Secure your pack straps.
- Just stand and watch:
  - How the wind behaves.
  - Which way the maintenance creatures move.
  - Which parts of the platform look safe vs. exposed.

### Where you can stand safely

- **Smooth platform surfaces** are generally safe:
  - The plant has grown to carry weight for eons.
  - You’re not heavy relative to the trunk-beams and branch supports.

- **Near edges**:
  - You can be close, but:
    - Use your rope as a tether:
      - Around a solid pipe stem or trunk‑beams.
      - Then you can clip into it while you look over the edge.
    - Watch for:
      - Wind gusts from below.
    - Don’t trust:
      - “Loose”‑looking frills or root fingers that look cracked or freshly damaged.

### On the edge behavior

- **Don’t lean directly out over the edge**
  - Use:
    - A prone or low crouching position:
      - Center of gravity low.
    - One hand firmly on a solid root or stem.
- **Don’t throw stones / objects down**
  - You don’t know what is below:
    - Lower platforms.
    - Sensitive structures of the plant‑body.
    - Maintenance creatures.
- **Don’t leave debris on the edges**
  - Even small:
    - A bottle can roll off and become a hazard.

### Interpreting the “factory” body

- **The pipe stems**
  - These are critical:
    - Do not climb into them.
    - Do not block their airflow.
    - Do not drop items into them.
    - The sound they make is not just aesthetic; it affects the internal flow of the world‑tree.

- **The sap channels**
  - You may see:
    - Runways or grooves in the platform where:
      - Viscous sap‑like fluid flows.
  - Don’t:
    - Step in them if you can avoid.
    - The sap may be:
      - Sticky.
      - Abrasive to your skin.
      - Or chemically incompatible with you.

- **Maintenance creatures (the bark‑limb workers)**
  - They will:
    - Scuttle past you.
    - Possibly bump into you.
    - Treat you like any other object.
    - If you sit still:
      - They may:
        - Clean around you.
        - Or even attempt “repairs” on your clothing.
    - If you are lying injured:
      - They might try to drag or move you:
        - As part of their general “clean and repair” routine.

  **How to behave with them**
  - Do:
    - Stand still or move slowly when they are near.
    - Accept they will treat your gear, boots, straps as if they are features of the world.
    - If one starts to mess with something important:
      - Gently redirect your gear or limb:
        - They don’t seem to react with aggression.
      - Think of them like:
        - Robots on a factory floor:
          - Do not block their path.
          - Don’t try to “pet” or grab them.
  - Do not:
    - Attack or kill them:
      - That is like cutting off the world’s fingers.
    - Try to feed them.
    - Try to ride them:
      - They are not horses or pack animals.
      - They’re organs of a world‑body, doing specific jobs.

### Movement and orientation

- The **light**:
  - The sun‑like object will cast:
    - Strong shafts through the lattice of platforms above.
  - As it moves:
    - The shadows on your platform slide:
      - You can estimate:
        - Relative time.
        - Approximate directions of change.

- The **sound**:
  - You might notice:
    - “Songs” or shifts of tone:
      - Rolling from one side of the world to the other.
    - This is:
      - Pressure changes and internal flow patterns.
  - Become:
    - Sensitive to:
      - Sudden drops in noise.
      - New roaring from vents you hadn’t heard before.
    - That may indicate:
      - Changes of wind flow you can’t see.

- **“Weather” as internal circulation**
  - You don’t have to fear:
    - Lightning storms or rain.
    - But you do have:
    - Gusts:
      - That can steadily push you from behind.
      - Or cross‑winds that:
        - Try to push you toward edges.
    - Always:
      - Lean into strong wind.
      - Keep your center of gravity low if it feels unstable.

### Descending or ascending “tiers”

You don’t have to climb down or up if you don’t want to, but if you do:

- **Use caution around shafts**
  - Some shafts:
    - May lead to lower levels.
    - May emit wind gusts.
    - May be “maintenance” routes:
      - Frequently used by the bark creatures.

- **Do not climb in shafts without**
  - A solid anchor:
    - Around a trunk or base of a pipe column.
    - Attached firmly to you through your rope.
    - Someone on top watching:
      - To communicate (radio) or to pull back if signals change.

- **Do not assume**
  - Shafts will be easy:
    - Some surfaces will be:
      - Polished smooth.
      - Very hard to climb without gear.
    - Some may narrow:
      - You can get stuck.
    - Better:
      - Use only very large obvious openings.
    - Even then:
      - Treat it like cave climbing:
        - Single rope technique.
        - Frequent backup knots.

## 4. Hazards and How to Mitigate Them

### A. Edges and Drops

- You are on the upper tiers of a continent‑plant:
  - There are vertical drops:
    - Tens of meters to many kilometers depending on where you are.
    - Always act:
      - As if you are near a cliff edge.

**Mitigations**
- Always keep one hand:
  - On something solid when you approach the edge.
- Use a tether line in:
  - Strong wind.
  - Low visibility (from fatigue, emotion, or strange lighting).
- Never:
  - Walk backward without looking.
  - Change your camera or phone settings:
    - Do that standing away from the edge.

### B. Wind and Turbulence

Multiple “weather” flows from internal plant convection:

- Updrafts:
  - From shafts under platforms.
- Down drafts:
  - From higher tiers above.
- Crosswinds:
  - From the sides of the continent.

These can catch:
- Your loose straps.
- Your jacket.
- Your hat.

**Mitigations**
- Secure:
  - All gear straps.
  - All hats with chin straps or keep them in your pack.
- Use:
  - Low‑profile stance:
    - Bent knees.
    - Slight lean forward:
    - When walking near shaft edges or strong gusts.

### C. Sap, Fluids, and Resin

There is an internal ocean of sap within the plant‑continent.

You may encounter:
- Sap runoff:
  - In channels or low pockets.
  - Sometimes:
    - Viscous.
    - Sticky.

You may, if you are injured:
- Accidentally touch:
  - Plant tissue that oozes sap.

**Mitigations**
- Treat:
  - Any fluid from the plant:
    - As potentially toxic or irritant.
    - Don’t:
      - Rub it in your eyes.
      - Wipe it with your hands.
    - Do:
      - Rinse with clean water.
    - If you don’t have much:
      - Use sparingly but urgently:
        - Blot rather than scrub.

- If you:
  - Develop a rash or irritation:
    - Take an antihistamine.
    - Stop contact exposure.
    - Monitor for swelling:
      - Especially around face or throat, which means emergency if severe.

### D. Dehydration and Sun Exposure

Dry air + strong sun + constant wind:

- You lose:
  - Water through respiration and skin.
  - Electrolytes through sweat.

**Signs to watch**
- Dry lips:
  - Already too late.
- Headache.
- Dizziness.
- Dark urine (if you can check).

**Mitigations**
- Drink:
  - Small sips regularly:
    - Every 15–30 minutes when active.
- Eat:
  - Salty snacks:
    - To preserve electrolytes.
- Cover:
  - Skin from direct sun.
    - Even if not hot, you can still get sunburned.

### E. Hearing Damage and Communication Failure

If you:
- Stay too long without:
  - Hearing protection.
- Or ignore:
  - Early ringing in your ears.

You can:
- Develop temporary or permanent hearing loss.
- Breakdown:
  - Your ability to hear:
    - Warning signals.
    - Your own heartbeat.
    - Your body’s stress signals.

**Mitigations**
- Wear:
  - Hearing protection:
    - Even if it seems “overkill.”
- Use:
  - Hand signals:
    - Before you need them.
  - A pre-agreed:
    - Simple set:
      - Stop.
      - Come back.
      - Help.
    - Practice:
      - In the loud environment so you can see what can be understood clearly.

### F. Psychological Stress

This environment will:
- Overload:
  - Your senses:
    - Constant noise.
    - Bright light.
    - No familiar silence or night.

Signs:
- Irritability.
- Fatigue:
  - Even with sleep.
- Headache:
  - From noise, not just dehydration.
- Feeling of:
  - Being “small” and meaningless next to this huge living organism.

**Mitigations**
- Build:
  - Quiet zones:
    - Inside your tent or shelter:
      - Where you remove:
        - External noise-cancellation only:
        - Not your hearing protection.
- Talk:
  - If you are with others:
    - Check in:
      - “How are you feeling?”
- Do:
  - Short meditation:
    - Focusing on:
      - Your breath.
    - Even with:
      - Noise and vibration.

### G. Getting Lost or Disoriented

There is no soil, no roads, no buildings.

If you:
- Wander away from:
  - Your shelter base.
  - Your identifiable pipe cluster.

You can easily:
- Lose yourself:
  - In a pattern of:
    - Repeating platforms.
    - Similar shafts.
    - Similar colored bark.

**Mitigations**
- Always:
  - Mark:
    - Your starting point:
      - With bright markers or flags:
        - That you can *see from afar*.
- Use:
  - A notebook map:
    - With:
      - Time of day.
      - Direction of sun.
- Don’t:
  - Split the group:
    - Unless absolutely necessary.
    - If you do:
      - Agree rigid:
        - Direction:
        - Meeting:
          - Time and location.

### H. Unplanned Overnight Survival

If you:
- Can’t return:
  - To your base shelter:
    - Before night‑temperature drop or fatigue.

**What you must have**
- Small emergency kit:
  - Lightweight:
    - Mylar blanket or bivy.
    - Extra water.
    - Extra energy bars.
    - Whistle.
    - Light.
    - Basic first aid.
    - Notebook and pencil:
      - Mark where:
        - You are.

**Do**
- Stay:
  - On the platform you’re on:
    - Do not:
    - Try to climb down shafts or slippery edges in the dark.
- Find:
  - A place:
    - Out of direct wind.
    - Not near edge or shaft.
    - Possibly near:
      - A large pipe stem or trunk that:
        - Feels solid and:
          - Reminds you of:
            - Safety / stability.

### I. Trip‑Planning Limits

Because you must carry:
- All water.
- All food.

Your **maximum stay** is:
- Determined by:
  - How long you can carry:
    - Water.
    - Food.
    - Weight.
    - Without collapse or injury.

As an ordinary human:
- A few days to a week is likely:
  - Your upper limit:
    - Unless you:
    - Have:
    - Advanced logistical support:
    - Or a drop-off point:
    - That can be used to:
    - Resupply.

### J. Leaving and Re‑entry

When you return:
- You:
  - May have:
    - Temporary hearing:
      - Ringing.
    - Temporary:
      - Sensitivity:
        - To bright light.
- Also:
  - Emotional:
    - Contrast:
    - Between:
    - The Root‑Bound Sky:
    - And:
    - Your own world.

Allow:
- 1–2 days:
  - At least:
    - Of:
    - Rest:
    - And:
    - Reintegration.
    - Check:
    - Hearing:
    - If persistent ringing remains.
    - Get:
    - A medical check:
    - If you:
    - Have:
    - Symptoms:
    - Of anything:
    - Serious.
