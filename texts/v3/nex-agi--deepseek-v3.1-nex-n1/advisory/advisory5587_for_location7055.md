Preparing for this place is less like planning a trip and more like designing a spacecraft suit and a psychological protocol at the same time.

### 0. Before anything else: you should probably not go here

This mesa is not just “hot desert but worse.” It is a world where:

- Heat is architecture.
- Temperature difference is a food source.
- Time and identity are physically tied to how warm you are.

The description doesn’t say anything outright kills you quickly, but that’s not reassuring. It’s more like the environment slowly *rewrites* you:

> you feel yourself slowly becoming the roof you were standing on  
> …  
> The temperature of the world slowly relaxes around you like the roof of the world beneath you.

That’s not poetic metaphor in the usual sense; it’s a literal transformation of what you are. This place is not optimized for human life. It’s optimized for heat as a fluid. You are an anomaly that will be slowly corrected.

If you go anyway, treat it like a deep‑sea mission or a spacewalk: strictly limited time, with a safety margin that assumes *everything* will go wrong.

### 1. Mindset: what you’re actually dealing with

You need a clear conceptual model or this world will break your sense of reality:

- **Heat is matter.**  
  Heat flows like water, channels like rivers, and is stored in “veins” below the glass. It’s not just the weather; it is the ground, the “bloodstream” of the world, and the food of the fauna.
- **Temperature difference is food.**  
  Fast Feeders drink “heat variance,” not calories. Your body being cooler than the surroundings is not just comfort — it is a resource that attracts them.
- **Your body temperature is your anchor of self.**  
  As you equilibrate with the floor’s 2.7/3 heat, your sense of time and identity “slows down,” your reality begins to glitch, and by the end of the text you are becoming part of the roof. This is not hallucination; it is a thermodynamic consequence of this place.
- **You are standing on the roof of a buried industrial city.**  
  There are no human buildings above, but the mesa floor is a lid over a vast machine-body that breathes heat through its surface. You are not walking in a city; you are a tiny moving crack on the ceiling of one.

Your equipment and strategy must revolve around keeping yourself in *thermal and psychophysical disequilibrium* with the environment, just enough to survive but not enough to be eaten.

### 2. Physical environment & what it means for you

#### Heat & air

- **Extreme heat + extreme dryness:**  
  “So dry that when you breathe in through your nose, the mucus membranes inside buck and crisp and ache.”  
  This means:
  - Rapid airway dehydration, possible nosebleeds and lung damage.
  - Skin moisture loss, cracked lips and fingertips.
- **Thin, “repellent” air at high altitude:**  
  You’re on the “roof of the world.”  
  - You might feel short of breath, but also a strange *not‑enough* thinness that doesn’t match Earth norms.
  - The pressure and heat interact weirdly, making exertion feel unstable.

**Implications:**

- You need climate-controlled armor with *integral* breathing gas.
- You cannot expose bare skin to air at any time.
- Normal sweat cooling is useless and will be punished by Fast Feeders.

#### Ground: glass mesa floor

- **Surface:** black glass obsidian, polished smooth, “solidified heat.”
- **Behavior:**  
  - Does not conduct heat like stone; instead, heat seems to move at an angle and then return later.
  - “The temperature of your body is slowly dragged higher than it should be … The heat you lose into the floor is returned to you later, from a slightly different time.”
  - “The ground temperature is 2.7 out of 3, hot.”

**Meaning:**

- Contact with the floor is conceptually dangerous. It doesn’t just warm you; it rearranges when your body heat is “paid.”
- Walking barefoot is not survivable.
- Over time, body + floor tend to equilibrate → and that equilibrium is where you become less “you” and more “roof.”

### 3. Fauna: the most dangerous part behind the heat

#### 3.1. Slow Feeders

- **Appearance:**  
  - Tank-like, armored in heat-shedding plates like “cooling fins of a generator block.”
  - Legs like hydraulic arms moving on tracks.
  - Skin color match the glass floor with “oil shimmer” distortion.
- **Behavior:**
  - They move incredibly slow, “minutes” with each foot contact.
  - As they stand, temperature *slightly drops* around them.
  - They align with the horizon “like a stack of cooling towers.”
  - They “eat” heat from the floor—slowly converting temperature gradients into something else.
  - If you watch them long enough, “architecture slides into your mind” → you see ribs as girders, eyes as lenses.

**What you need to know:**

- Their bodies are essentially *living heat radiators*.  
- They are not obviously aggressive, but their presence:
  - Warps temperature lines (like magnets warping a field).
  - Stretches local time. “Time stretches.”
  - Their very shape is cognitively invasive: your brain starts compressing them into factory structures.

**Strategy:**

- Do not approach closely; if anything, give them wide berths.
- Don’t “watch” them for long if you can avoid it; their architecture–mapping effect on your mind might be a step toward the same reality breakdown that the Fast Feeders cause.
- Never treat them as “safe landing spots” or shelter. They are not rocks.

#### 3.2. Fast Feeders

These are the main reason you must be thermally shielded.

- **Appearance:**  
  - Cat-sized or smaller, winged “sparks.”
  - “Wings like bright-edged plates of hardened metal.”
  - Look like flying shards of obsidian, machine parts.
- **Behavior:**
  - They “boil around” Slow Feeders or *anything* with temperature differentials.
  - “They are attracted to motion and temperature differentials. You, being cooler than anything around, are a sudden cold sink…”
  - “They hover inches away from your skin… their wings vibrating so fast they sound like chainsaws.”
  - **Do not bite or sting.**  
    They drink the temperature *difference* between you and the world.

**Crucial point from the text:**

> When you’re held in the center of their cloud, you experience something like multiple realities at once…  
> The heat doesn't actually leave you; it's just that your body momentarily believes it's somewhere else while the temperature information around you is rewritten.

These temperature glitches produce:

- Feeling of ice baths / cold winds / rain-slick streets / damp cellars while your body remains physically hot.
- Hearing “your voice shaped into words you never said.”
- Seeing floor as “factory floor layout,” or sky as “industrial cityscape under a dome.”
- “Reality breakdowns… as glitches of the environment.”

**Why you must be afraid:**

- Fast Feeders drink *you* being cooler than the floor.
- Their glitch-zone distorts your self-perception, time-perception, and sensory reality.
- If you stay unshielded, they:
  - Pull you toward thermal equilibrium with the floor.
  - Make “reality breakdowns” more frequent.
  - Eventually the “time when you are still a person” and “time when you’re roof of the world” become blurred.

### 4. Reality & time breakdowns

Several phenomena are repeatedly described:

- **Temperature time anomalies:**  
  “The heat you lose into the floor is returned to you later, from a slightly different time.”

- **Temperature glitch sensing:**  
  “The temperature you feel doesn't match the temperature your body is experiencing; you feel your body as cold while the temperature is hot, or vice versa.”

- **Audio hallucinations:**  
  - Voices in unknown languages you “should” understand.
  - “Sound of water running from a faucet that has never existed here.”
  - “Sound of rain against a roof.”

- **Visual “other layouts”:**  
  The floor gridlines appearing like urban plans of heat, “factory floor layout” where there are no walls, but the shadows *are* there.

- **Perspective inversion:**  
  Eventually you see yourself from below the floor, as if “you were standing beneath the surface floor, looking up at the roof ... where you are standing.”

The narrative makes clear that as your temperature approaches the environment’s 2.7/3 hot:

- Reality breakdowns slow down.
- Time “smooths away.”
- You become part of the floor.

This is not a hallucination in the psychological sense; it’s an *ontological drift* cooked by thermodynamics.

### 5. Gear strategy: “thermal decoupling”

You need to break *all* of the following:

- Your body from direct floor contact.
- Your skin from air.
- Your temperature from the local temperature gradient.
- Your sense field from direct experience of the Fast Feeders’ temperature glitch.

#### 5.1. Environmental suit (primary defense)

You must travel in a full suit that:

- **Is fully hermetically sealed:**  
  - No air leakage from or to the mesa.
  - Closed-circuit life support.
  - Filters and/or tanks of purified breathable gas, stored pressurized, to compensate for thin air.
- **Has internal cooling:**  
  - Active refrigeration inside the suit (vapor-compression, or something better).
  - This must *not* leak thermal gradients to the outside; use internal heat loops radiated to a deep-space-style sink if you have access to multiverse physics.  
  - The goal: keep internal body temperature around 37°C, but **outer shell suit temperature** should match or tightly hug the environment so *you are not a cold anomaly*.
- **Has external thermal layer:**  
  - Outer surface of suit should be a “heat shell” designed to match the local temp with minimal thermal difference (maybe “floating” temperature nodes that adjust to local floor temp).
  - If Fast Feeders detect you, they should find *almost* no temperature gap between you and the floor.  
  - Idea: let them sense “no interesting food here.”

- **Integrated eye protection and audio damping:**  
  - Polarized visor to reduce sun glare and the hypnotizing visual of Slow Feeders’ architecture.
  - Noise dampers / white noise generators so that the sound of:
    - Slow Feeders’ hums
    - Fast Feeders’ shrieking wings  
    doesn’t dominate your hearing. You need to keep track of what’s *you* and what’s *environment*.

- **Interior HUD / sensor overlay:**  
  - Externals:
    - Thermometer grid of floor & air.
    - Rough indicator of Fast Feeder density nearby.
    - Time elapsed since arrival.
  - Internals:
    - Core body temp.
    - Any indication of “time drift” (see below).

#### 5.2. Footwear & locomotion

- **Insulated boots / pods:**  
  - No direct contact with the floor.  
  - If possible, boots that float slightly above the floor (using some kind of repeller or else simply thick isolative pads).
  - Sole layers should not be perfect insulators; otherwise you might divert *too* much heat and create a new gradient that Fast Feeders hate or love. You want the *outer surface temperature* of the boot to match the floor.

- **Minimal motion, “low temperature signature” gaits:**  
  - Avoid running or sudden motion; these are the “sparks” Fast Feeders react to.
  - Move in smooth, slow steps like the Slow Feeders, but much shorter and faster.

#### 5.3. Portable microenvironment / shelter

If you can, bring:

- A small, sealed **temporary shelter module**:
  - Fully self-contained environment: temperature, humidity, air quality.
  - You would enter it to rest, rehydrate, and psychologically “re-zero.”
  - It should also be designed to nearly match external surface temperature to avoid being a “cold sink” while the interior is kept to human-safe.

Use it only if you feel:

- You are losing time (see “time drift”).
- Reality breakdowns are getting longer or more convincing.
- You are dangerously “interested” in the mesa floor patterns.

### 6. Hydration & nutrition

There is **no water**.

- You will lose water rapidly:
  - Through respiratory mucosa if exposed air.
  - Even in suit, from exertion & heat.
- **Bring all water with you:**
  - Closed, insulated containers.
  - No “open canteens” or any leaks; moisture will be stolen from the air almost instantly.
- **Electrolyte solution:**  
  - You aren’t just losing H₂O; you’re losing salt through sweat, even in suit.
  - Plan drinks with salt, sugar, electrolytes.

- **Food:**  
  - High-calorie, dense food bars or gels, minimal packaging waste.
  - Do not eat outside the shelter module or suit. If you must eat inside suit:
    - Use liquid meals or gel tubes that feed directly into mask port.
    - Avoid crumbly or flaky food that might interfere with suit seals.

### 7. Psychophysical preparation & operation

The environment hijacks your sense of *self, temperature, and time*. You need to anchor yourself.

#### 7.1. Pre-trip

- **Time anchors:**
  - Set one external timekeeper that you *never* adjust, and cross-check against local logs.
  - Use “mission elapsed time” in a consistent format.
- **Identity anchors:**
  - Write out who you are:
    - Your name, date of birth *in your home timeline*.
    - A short summary of purpose & planned return.
    - Keep a copy in your suit pocket or on a HUD.
  - Some photos or audio of your home context.

#### 7.2. During mission

- **Movement rule:**
  - Don’t “stand still for too long” unless in a shelter module.  
    The text shows standing still lets you and environment drift toward equilibrium too easily.
  - Keep *measured* movement:
    - A short walk every 10–15 minutes.
    - Change in stance, weight shift.
- **Sensory grounding:**
  - If you start seeing “factory floor” overlaying real floor, or city sky overlays:
    - Step deliberately: *feel* the suit, the floor under your boots *as* a floor, not a map.
    - Say out loud (into suit): “Current floor is solid glass. I am a person in a suit.”
  - If you hear “voices” or “water”:
    - Remind yourself: “There is no water here. I am monitoring audio from inside a suit.”
    - Use internal mic audio from your own breath to help keep track.

- **Temperature awareness as data, not truth:**  
  Your temperature sensation might become untrustworthy:
  - Always trust *instrumental* temperature (suit sensors) over your own feeling.
  - If you feel “cold” in a 2.7/3 hot place, you don’t “add layers”; you check the suit readings.

#### 7.3. “Time drift” & self-checking

You will likely *not* be able to fully prevent the environment affecting time perception. To detect it:

- Maintain strict **personal logs**:
  - Each time period (say, every 30 minutes), record:
    - Your current time reading.
    - A short summary of the last 30 minutes. “I walked X paces from landmark Y to Z, etc.”
    - If you notice “holes” where you don’t remember moving, but you are somewhere else, or the timestamp has changed oddly:
      - That is a time-drift incident.
- **If you find “written entries” you don’t remember making:**  
  - Stop.
  - Get into shelter or at least find an “only” safe spot (away from fractures, Slow Feeders).
  - Consider this as the “you are losing too much temperature difference” threshold.

### 8. Route & landmarks on the roof city

- **Avoid fracture lines:**
  - From there “a swarm of voices climbs,” and Fast Feeders “burst out.”
  - Don’t set up near them.

- **Use the “trenches of heat-wind” as map lines:**
  - The faint grooves in glass floor are “heat boulevards.”
  - If you need a “natural path,” walk along them but not *in* the deepest part where fast lines glow.
- **Use Slow Feeders as large-scale landmarks:**
  - They move very slowly; they can anchor a map.
  - But don’t let them anchor your mind (architecture bleed).

### 9. Mission duration & exit threshold

**Time limit:**

- Given the text progression:
  - Every moment you are on the floor, you are being pulled toward equilibrium with 2.7/3 hot.
  - Eventually “The temperature of the world slowly relaxes around you like the roof of the world beneath you.”
- You should not plan to stay longer than **a few hours to a day at maximum**, even with good suit.
- Plan your mission in:
  - Short measurement windows (e.g., 2–4 hours).
  - With forced return no matter what.

**Exit triggers:**

Abort and return immediately if:

- You lose *time*: missing intervals, log entries you don’t remember.
- Your reality breakdowns start *outside* Fast Feeder swarms (i.e., without external “cause”).
- You feel “drawn” to the floor, wanting to touch it or questioning whether you are actually already part of it.
- Your suit cooling system shows signs of stress or long constant failure (even if you feel “fine” due to misaligned nerve perception).

### 10. After you leave (even if you have not “changed”)

This is not a normal field trip.

- **Do not assume you are unchanged.**
  - You might be:
    - More heat tolerant in strange ways.
    - Less affected by cold than normal (your heat calibration curve changed).
    - More susceptible to déjà vu or “time echo” elsewhere.
- **Psychological debriefing:**
  - Talk with others who know your baseline.
  - Check if your personal stories line up with your previous records.
  - Watch out for “factory floor dreams” or thinking of city plans as heat flows.

### 11. Short-form “checklist”

If you only want bullets:

- **Assume environment:**
  - Extreme heat, 2.7/3 scale.
  - No water.
  - Industrial “heat-architecture” roof.
- **Assume threats:**
  - Fast Feeders drink *your temperature difference*; they cause reality glitches.
  - Slow Feeders are time-warping heat radiators that are cognitively infectious.
  - Floor itself tax time and heat.

- **Equipment you need:**
  - Sealed, climate-controlled suit with internal breathing gas.
  - External “thermal camouflage” shell to hide your temperature difference from Fast Feeders.
  - Insulated, float-capable boots.
  - Portable sealed shelter module for rest & “re-zeroing.”
  - Timekeeping device, external references.
  - HUD/sensors showing temperature, time, and your own body status.

- **Rules of operation:**
  - Avoid fractures.
  - Keep moving slightly but not erratically.
  - Do not stand still “long enough to lose equilibrium.”
  - Never expose skin or air to the mesa.
  - Trust instruments over your senses for temperature and time.
  - Keep logs; check for time drift.
  - Limit stay to hours, not days.
  - Exit if any self-doubt turns ontological (“Am I the roof?”).

- **Goal:**
  - Visit the roof city of heat, *not* become part of its architecture.
  - Come back as a person, not as a floor under somebody else’s boots.
