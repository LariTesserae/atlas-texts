To enter this place is not like slipping into a cave or a diving bell. It’s more like stepping into someone else’s internal organ—someone you’ve never met—and knowing, on a cellular level, that you’re the wrong shape for it.

Because of that, almost all of the preparation is less about “gear” and more about *regulation*: regulation of body, of mind, and of expectations.

---

## 1. Physical Preparation

### 1.1 Thermoregulation

The ambient temperature is “almost body-hot—feverish to the air of your old life, gentle now to your skin.”  
This means:

- **You do not need cold-weather gear.** No heavy jackets, no insulation for warmth.
- What you need is **heat dissipation**.

**Recommendations:**

- **Clothing:**  
  - Thin, breathable, close-fitting layers.  
  - Synthetic or moisture-wicking fabric rather than cotton, which will become sodden and heavy.  
  - Short sleeves or sleeveless top; shorts, or lightweight pants that can be rolled up.
  - Colors: as light as possible to reflect radiation rather than absorb it.
- **Footwear:**  
  - Either barefoot, or **very thin-soled sandals/slip-ons** that you can easily remove and carry.  
  - You are “walking on water” in a sense—thick shoes will trap heat and make it hard to feel the surface under the water film.
- **Hydration & electrolytes:**  
  - In a constant, high-humidity, near-body-temp environment, you sweat without noticing how much you lose.
    - Bring water and **electrolyte salts/tablets** in a small container.
    - Even if you’re not “hot” in the usual way, you will dehydrate faster than you expect.
- **Antipyretics (optional):**  
  - A mild fever reducer like paracetamol/acetaminophen can be useful if prolonged heat stress manifests as a low-grade fever.  
  - But don’t rely on pills—you should rely primarily on pacing and cooling.

### 1.2 Light & Vision Protection

The light is “white bleached to the color of itself, reflecting and re-reflecting from every surface.”  
This means:

- Extreme contrast, no dimming, no shadows.
- No visual anchors for depth.
- Risk of eye strain, headache, and possibly mild temporary snow blindness.

**Recommendations:**

- **Tinted eye protection**  
  - Neutral gray tinted glasses or goggles with **good UV protection**.
  - Avoid very dark lenses—you need to see subtle color differences in the filaments.
  - If possible, something **polarized** to cut glare from the reflective surfaces.
- **Interval breaks**: Periodically close eyes, or cover them with a cloth or hand, to reset your photoreceptors.
- **No strong flashlights or headlamps**  
  - The room is already bright; you will just worsen glare and bleach out your own vision.

### 1.3 Breathing & Atmosphere

The air smells of “wet clay and old salt and something else, like green leaves.”  
Nothing is described as toxic—but the humidity is effectively 100%, and the air has a strong character.

**Recommendations:**

- If you have mild asthma or respiratory sensitivities, **bring a mask or cloth band**:
  - To filter out particulate or to reduce the sensation of overwhelming salt/clay odors.
  - Also, often wearing a mask helps psychologically “thin” the air if you start panicking.
- **No breathing apparatus**:  
  - The air is not described as unbreathable; the water depth is only inches deep.
  - Don’t complicate things with tanks or respirators unless you already medically require one.

## 2. Psychological & Temporal Preparation

The text emphasizes “the erosion of memory,” “you cannot remember if it was always that size,” and “time passes… you don’t know how much.”

This location is **time-hostile**.

### 2.1 Time-keeping

**Recommendations:**

- **Bring redundant, low-tech timekeeping tools:**
  - A simple, robust wristwatch with **seconds** and clear, large numerals.
  - A small mechanical hourglass or sand timer.
  - A small, compact notebook and **pen/pencil**.
- **Recording protocol:**  
  - Make time entries:
    - “T0: Arrived.”
    - “Every 5 minutes I mark.”  
    - Or: “At each equipment check, I mark the page.”
  - Write **observations, not interpretations**: “Filaments green, yellow, white tips. Bubble stuck to strand. Strand thicker now than 5 minutes ago.”
  - Do not trust “memory of time.”  
    You *will* feel like you just arrived, even when hours have passed.

### 2.2 Cognitive & Emotional Stability

“The erosion of memory is not the same as erosion of stone…”  
You are not going to be physically assaulted; you will be **mind-assaulted** by monotony, invariance, and the lack of external cues.

**Recommendations:**

- **Before entry**, do:
  - Short grounding meditation:  
    - 5 deep breaths; name 5 things you can see, 4 things you can touch, 3 things you can hear, etc.
  - State **an intention and an exit rule**:  
    - “I will stay no longer than X minutes.”  
    - “When I feel [specific symptom], I will leave.”
- **Create anchors to your outside reality**:
  - A touchstone object: a coin, a ring, a smooth stone from your own world.
  - Label it with the date and “I come from elsewhere.”
  - Read this label before walking and periodically while inside.
- **Self-checking loop** (every few minutes, or at every “thump”):
  - Who am I?
  - Where am I from?
  - What is my purpose here?
  - How long do I intend to stay?
  - The answer might be “I am observing,” and that is enough.

## 3. Interaction & Movement

You move “on water,” via a film three inches deep. The ceiling itself is also covered in water.  
The floor was ceiling, “you the intruder walking across the sky of another world.”

### 3.1 Balance & Surface

**Recommendations:**

- **Expect disorientation**:
  - Train your balance beforehand:
    - Stand on one foot for 30 seconds.
    - Walk on uneven surfaces in low light.
- **Move slowly**:
  - Take small steps.
  - Imagine your knees are slightly bent to lower center of gravity.
  - Test each surface with weight before committing full body.
- **Barefoot vs Footwear**:
  - If you are comfortable barefoot, consider being barefoot:
    - It gives better feedback about the slipperiness and texture under the water film.
    - Ensure feet are free of cuts or open wounds to reduce infection risk from unknown water chemistry.
  - If you prefer shoes, choose something **non-slip** but thin-soled; wide, flat soles to distribute weight over the film.

### 3.2 Touching the Surfaces

The water “moves in slow, thick ripples when you touch it.”  
The walls are “too smooth,” “dull,” and “gray.”

**Recommendations:**

- **Assume everything is fragile**:  
  - The filaments “are not the room” but they are *stuck to it*.
  - The bubbles “stick” and then the strands “thicken.”
  - Your touch might be participating in this process.
- **Minimal physical contact**:
  - Do not lean on the walls.
  - Do not scratch or test the hardness of the surfaces.
  - If you do touch, use **one finger** lightly.  
    - If you see any change in the structure near your touch, **stop immediately**.
- **Do not interact with the bubbles**:  
  - You might think “pop it,” but remember:
    - It stuck to the strand and then the strand thickened.
    - Your bubbles or interference might be **nourishment** or **data** for this place.

## 4. Attending to the Room’s Ecosystem

The “grown things” are not plants but filamentous structures,  
“like a diagram of river deltas or human lungs,”  
branching along the ceiling under the water blanket.

Their **colors**: gray-black → faint algae green → pale yellow → almost white at the tips.

### 4.1 Observation Protocol

You’re a visitor in a system that appears to be growing, **adjusting**, and **integrating borrowed material**.

**Recommendations:**

- **No “sampling” unless absolutely necessary**:
  - Do not break off filaments.
  - Do not try to scrape them from the ceiling.
  - If you *must* sample for some reason, do:
    - Tiniest possible piece,
    - Using **sterile tools**,
    - And **immediately** seal it in an inert container.
- **Map a pattern**:
  - In your notebook, sketch a rough diagram of:
    - One wall.
    - One ceiling corner.
    - One branch of filament.
  - Mark where colors begin and change.
- **Photo record (if you have camera)**:
  - Take photos **only** after:
    - Establishing a time mark,
    - And ensuring your flash is **off**.
    - Overly bright light may be interpreted by the system (or you) in ways you don’t anticipate.

### 4.2 The Sound

The “low, unchanging, percussive thing”  
“that you cannot feel in your feet but only in your chest.”

**Recommendations:**

- **Let your body synchronize**:
  - Your heart will be pulled toward the rhythm of the room anyway;  
    don’t fight that.
  - Monitor **how** it syncs:
    - Faster heart rate?
    - Calmer heart rate?
    - Any dizziness or pressure sensation in the chest?
- **Boundary controls**:
  - If sync brings **dizziness**, **tightness in chest**, or **fear**:
    - Stop movement.
    - Breathe slowly and deliberately.
    - If you can’t break the pattern in a few cycles, **consider leaving the room**.

## 5. Equipment & Tools

You’re not “exploring the wild,” you’re **intruding into a systemic machine**.

**Minimalist** is better than **overloaded**.

### Essential Equipment

1. **Timekeeping:**
   - Watch or sand timer.
   - Field notebook & pencil (pen ink may run if humidity extreme).

2. **Light & vision:**
   - Neutral gray tinted glasses / goggles.
   - No ultra-bright lights.

3. **Hydration & thermoregulation:**
   - Water bottle.
   - Electrolyte salts.
   - Light towel or small cloth to absorb perspiration.

4. **Basic recording & measuring:**
   - Handheld lens or small magnifying glass if you want detailed look at filaments.
   - A small metric ruler or tape measure to estimate:
     - Water thickness.
     - Room width/length.
     - Initial thickness of filaments (vs later measurements).

5. **Optional, high-risk tools:**
   - Small **sterile container** (for any sample you *must* take).
   - A **string with a small weight** to:
     - Test “down” in the water layer,
     - Or “up” into the ceiling layer,
     - And see if there is a pull towards the “real world” below.

## 6. Safeties & Exit Strategy

“There is no outside that you can find.”  
You may not choose “where” you exit. But you **must** choose **when** and **how**.

**Set hard rules before entering:**

- **Time limit**:
  - Example: “No longer than 30 minutes on first visit.”
  - Use watch and sand timer.
- **Physiological boundaries**:
  - If:
    - Your heart rate exceeds X without exertion,
    - You feel pressure in chest or temple,
    - Vision begins to tunnel or colors shift,
    → **Time to leave immediately.**
- **Behavioral boundaries**:
  - If:
    - You cannot remember why you’re here,
    - You cannot remember the date you came from,
    → **Stop, read your anchor note, reorient, and then strongly consider leaving.**
- **Technical “drop”**:
  - If any device—watch, timer—stops working,
    → That is itself a data point:
      - Note the event,
      - Then use **mental counting**:
        - Pace a small step pattern.  
        - 60 steps ≈ roughly one minute.  
        - Count to sets of 60.

## 7. Return and Aftercare

When you leave (if you leave), what happens in **your** world?

- **Immediate debriefing**:
  - Write down everything:
    - How long you felt you were in,
    - How long your devices say,
    - Any discrepancies.
    - Any perception of room size changing,
    - Any sense of being “known” by the room.
- **Medical check** (especially if repeated visits):
  - Heart rhythm,
  - Blood pressure,
  - Any long-term “haunting” sensation of that heartbeat in your chest.
- **Psychological support**:
  - This kind of place—no day, no night, no outside, growing filaments that integrate bubbles—can be **integrative**.
  - It might integrate:
    - Pieces of your memory,
    - Your sense of self as “stable,”  
       into its own architecture.

Have someone **outside** who knows:
- Where you are or the type of place (you may not be able to say),
- When you expect to return,
- What to do if you’re **late** or if you **don’t return**.

## 8. Final Mindset

You are not a “tourist” here.  
You are a **visiting organ**, a temporary pulse of biochemistry walking into an engineered system that may:

- Feed,
- Observe,
- Or record you,

depending on its own logic.

The **best** preparation you can make:

- Be physically ready to endure heat, light, and humidity.
- Be mentally ready to **question your own memory of time and space**.
- Be **intentional** rather than **invasive**.

Do not go there “to see what happens.”  
Go there **to see**, period—  
and then to **leave**,  
so that you can return to your own world and remember what “you” means before the room begins to remember it for you.
