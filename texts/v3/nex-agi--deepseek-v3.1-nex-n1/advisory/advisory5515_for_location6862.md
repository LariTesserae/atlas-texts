To walk into the City Beneath the Root-Shadow is not like visiting a place so much as stepping into someone else’s body. You should prepare accordingly: not just “what do I pack?” but “how do I behave inside a living mind that is also a world?”

Below is an oriented traveler’s guide to *Veridonum sp.*, from the skin outward.

---

## 1. Mindset Before You Go

### 1.1 It is alive, and it notices you

Assume from the outset that the city is **aware and sensate**.

- The “ground” you walk on is the outer surface of its roots.
- The trunks around you are its circulatory pillars.
- The “buildings” are its chambers and organs.
- The humidity in the air, the warmth, the faint amber glow, the low hum—all of this is the city breathing.

**Practical implication:**  
You are not a neutral observer. You are a foreign body moving inside its tissues. Behave as you would inside a sacred temple or an intensive care unit: quietly, deliberately, with respect.

---

### 1.2 The “floor” of the world is a ceiling

The locals you eventually meet will not call this ground “earth” or “floor.”  
To them, you are on **the underside of the living world**, where the roots are exposed and the mechanisms of life are visible.

- The sky is a low ceiling of leaves thirty meters or so above you.
- The “real” city—where people live—is higher up in the trunk cavities.
- The root-floor you walk on is a service level, a “basement” of the world.

**Mindset shift:**  
You are not entering “a city.” You are entering **the root tier of a single organism-city**.

---

### 1.3 Time is biological, not mechanical

There is no rush hour, no schedule of clocks.

- Time is measured in **growth cycles**, **metabolic pulses**, and the **rhythm of water secretions**.
- Days are not distinguished by light levels but by subtle changes in warmth, humidity, and the faint “color” of the water in the root reservoirs.

**Practical implication:**  
Do not expect timetables, business hours, or human urgency. You must adjust your internal clock to a slower, more organic rhythm.

---

## 2. Physical Environment & What to Expect

### 2.1 Light and visibility

- Lighting: glowing amber chitin-plastic trunks + soft cyan fungal veins on some columns.
- No sky; light is **low, diffuse, and constant**.
- No day-night cycle in the usual sense; the “dusk” is permanent.

**Preparation:**  
- Bring **light sources you can fully shield** when off:
  - A narrow-beam LED headlamp with several brightness levels.
  - A soft, warm-colored lantern you can wrap in cloth or a translucent membrane to avoid harsh white glare.
- **Dim red or amber filters** for your lights will feel much less intrusive to the locals and the city itself.
- Avoid:
  - High-intensity flashlights.
  - Strobing or blinking lights.
  - Anything “blinding” by the standards of a dark-adapted environment.

Your eyes should adapt to near darkness; **do not fight the dim**.

---

### 2.2 Temperature and humidity

- Air temperature ≈ steady ~27°C.
- High humidity, but not “tropical swamp” levels; the air feels *like breathing inside a living body*.
- Surfaces slightly cooler than the air: expect condensation.

**Clothing recommendations:**

- **Breathable, moisture-wicking base layers**, not heavy cotton.
- **Light, long sleeves and long trousers** to protect:
  - From surface moisture on roots and trunks.
  - From accidental brushing against the city’s “skin” (which may be thermoregulating or sensing its environment).
- A **thin shell layer** to protect from sudden “secretions” or condensation drips from above.
- **Closed, comfortable walking shoes** with:
  - Good grip on slightly damp, smooth biosurfaces.
  - Non-marking soles to avoid staining or scratching the root floor.

---

### 2.3 Air and breath

- The air is warm and humid, **constantly circulated** by the city’s “respiration.”
- There may be **subtle spores, microorganisms, or pheromones** in the air as part of the city’s metabolic system.

**Preparation:**  
- A lightweight **mask or filter** capable of filtering fine particulates and spores:
  - Not necessary for basic survival, but wise for long stays.
- **Essential asthma/allergy medications** if you have respiratory sensitivities.
- Your first hours should be spent **slowly acclimatizing**:
  - Sit quietly.
  - Breathe deeply.
  - Let your body adjust without panic.

---

### 2.4 “Weather” and water

- No rain; instead:
  - The **canopy leaves periodically secrete thin sheets of water**.
  - These slide down trunks and fill root reservoirs.
- Occasional **single heavy drops** condensing from humid air and falling into reservoirs.

**Preparation:**  
- **Waterproof** all electronics:
  - Use dry bags or waterproof cases.
  - Bring a microfiber cloth to wipe surfaces.
- Keep a compact **absorbent cloth** or small towel for:
  - Wiping skin and clothing.
  - Drying off areas where you sit.
- Waterproof your notebook or writing materials:
  - Use pencil or waterproof pens.
  - Seal the paper in plastic sleeves.

**Do not assume open water is safe to drink.**  
- While the city’s water system is likely clean for its residents, **your biome is not theirs**.  
- Use **filtration**, or **only drink water offered by locals** from known safe sources.  
- Carry a small reusable water bottle and tablets or filter.

---

### 2.5 “Ground” and movement

- You walk on the **root surface** itself:
  - Spongy but firm.
  - Slightly resilient, like dense cobblestone.
- It **responds** to your steps: not to harm you, but as sensing.

**Expect:**

- Gentle give underfoot.
- Subtle shifts in texture or warmth under you.
- Occasional slight heaving or subtle movement that is **not tectonic**, but metabolic.

**Preparation:**

- Bring **soft-soled shoes** (like trail runners, not heavy boots).
- Avoid spike heels or anything that might:
  - Damage the surface.
  - Act like an acupuncture needle in the city’s skin.
- Walk **deliberately and quietly**.  
  - This is not superstition; a place this sensitive will likely “notice” heavy, stomping feet more than light, careful ones.

---

### 2.6 Radiation considerations

*Veridonum sp.* uses **radiation-metabolism**:

- Canopy leaves are radiation collectors.
- Warmth from deeper core (or internal reactor) + biological radiation conversion.

This implies:

- Low-level **background radiation**, likely monitored by the city’s biology.
- Possibly higher **localized hotspots** where the city’s metabolic collectors or energy nodes are concentrated.

**Preparation:**

- A **Geiger counter or dosimeter**:
  - For peace of mind and for mapping zones where lingering is not wise.
- **Time limits**:
  - If radiation is detected beyond low background levels, limit time spent in hotspots.
- **Distance from glowing trunks**:
  - Do not lean unnecessarily against the radiating trunks; stand near them but avoid pressing your body into them for long periods.
- If you have a radiation monitoring badge from home, bring it.

> **Important:** The locals have evolved or engineered symbiosis with this environment. You have not. Treat radiation with respect, not panic.

---

### 2.7 “Fauna”: the city’s components

You will encounter:

- **Disk-like “creatures”**:
  - Chitinous plates the size of dinner plates.
  - Hovering on faint electrical fields around trunks.
  - They appear to be part of the **city cleaning system**.

**Behavior around them:**

- Do not **touch**, catch, or obstruct them.
- They are likely:
  - Spore collectors.
  - Surface cleaners.
  - Possibly sensors or nodes in the city’s “immune system.”
- Interfering with them might be:
  - Like blocking a cleaning drone in a sterile facility.
  - Or like poking at a nerve ending in a body.

**Rules:**  
- Let them pass.  
- If you must interact, wait for locals to show you how.

---

### 2.8 Humans and symbionts

When you finally meet people, you will notice:

- They **interface directly with the city**:
  - Pressing palms to membranes.
  - “Reading” swirls of color in tree reservoirs or bark surfaces.
- They are not **building users** so much as **symbiotic cells**.

**Expectations:**

- They may seem **very reserved** initially.
- Their speech might be slow, soft, and highly **contextual**, relying on shared knowledge with the city.
- Personal space is likely respected **more than usual**, because:
  - The city itself occupies “space” as a living body.
  - Everyone knows where the others are because the city knows.

**You should:**

- Speak **quietly**.
- Avoid **sudden movements or loud noises**.
- Watch **how they interface with the city** closely but **not intrusively**.
- Ask **before** touching:
  - Any apparent surface.
  - Any person.
  - Any structure, even vague “handles” or patterns in the bark.

## 3. Gear & Provisions

### 3.1 Essential equipment

1. **Lighting**
   - Dim headlamp with amber/red mode.
   - Compact lantern with soft, diffused light.
   - Backup batteries stored in waterproof containers.

2. **Radiation monitoring**
   - Small dosimeter or Geiger counter.
   - Knowledge of how to interpret it.

3. **Water handling**
   - Water filter or purification tablets.
   - Waterproof containers for:
     - Drinking water.
     - Electronics.
     - Food.

4. **Health & safety**
   - Basic first aid kit:
     - Antiseptics.
     - Bandages.
     - Pain relievers.
   - Respiratory mask for fine particulate/spores.
   - Any **personal medications** in sufficient quantity for your stay plus margin.

5. **Documentation & navigation**
   - **Low-tech backup**:
     - Pencil and paper.
     - Waterproof notebook.
   - Maps:
     - Sketch of trunk zones, access points, and “street” routes between them.
     - Mark “safe zones” where locals are comfortable.
   - Do not rely on **digital mapping alone**:
     - The city’s metabolic signals might interfere with electronics.
     - Assume you must learn **biological landmarks** rather than GPS coordinates.

6. **Communication**
   - If you bring devices:
     - Assume they need **shielding** from humidity and subtle radiation.
   - Consider:
     - **Simple, low-frequency signaling devices** if the locals use similar.
   - Your body itself:
     - Learn **basic gestures of greeting** once on site.
     - Learn “stop” / “wait” / “help” / “thanks” from locals first.

---

### 3.2 Clothing

In summary:

- Lightweight, breathable layers.
- **Long sleeves and pants** to avoid skin contact with unknown surfaces.
- **Modest coverage**:
  - In a body-city, exposing large skin may feel like opening an internal organ to a foreign environment.
- **Soft colors**, preferably:
  - Neutral or dark tones that don’t “shout” in the amber-glow world.
  - Avoid bright neon that would disrupt the dark-adapted ecosystem.

---

### 3.3 Food

You will likely eat **whatever food the locals share**, if any:

- **Their diet** is probably closely integrated with the city:
  - Microbial cultures.
  - Hydroponic-like growths within the tree.
  - Possibly products derived from the city’s metabolism.

**Preparation:**

- Bring **compact, high-calorie, sealed rations** from your own world:
  - Emergency bars.
  - Vacuum-packed meals.
- **Eat only what you can tolerate**, but be prepared to accept:
  - That their food will be symbiont-safe, **but not necessarily optimized for you**.
- **Avoid eating** anything:
  - From unknown surfaces.
  - “Scavenged” from the city without local guidance.

---

### 3.4 Sleep and shelter

- You will likely sleep **inside the trunk** cavities:
  - “Rooms” grown into the city-tree itself.
  - Surrounded by living walls.

**Preparation:**

- **Lightweight sleep sack or liner**:
  - Even if they provide bedding, you may want your own microbial layer.
- **Earplugs**:
  - The “hum” of the city is continuous and low, but it can be **felt more than heard**, especially if you are sensitive to resonant frequencies.
- **Breathing mask**:
  - If you are respiratory sensitive, especially while sleeping in enclosed biological spaces.

---

## 4. Cultural and Psychological Preparation

### 4.1 Silence is not emptiness; it is information

The dominant sound is **the faint, low cellular hum of the city’s growth**.

- Do not attempt to fill silence with noise:
  - Avoid music from speakers.
  - Avoid loud chatter.
- **Listen**:  
  Over time you may learn to distinguish:
  - “Normal” metabolic hum.
  - Changes in response to events (like many people moving, or a secretion event from the canopy).

---

### 4.2 The city is not a tool; it is a host

You will be tempted, as a traveler, to think:

> “How can I use this?”

This is **exactly the wrong question**.

- The city is not infrastructure.
- It is a **host organism** that has incorporated these humans into its life cycle.

**Better questions:**

- How does **my presence** affect the host?
- What does the **host** seem to want from its symbionts?
- How can I **minimize harm** and **support balance**?

---

### 4.3 Openness but not intrusion

You will see **intimate interfaces**:

- Locals touching, reading, and communing with the city.
- The city responding with color patterns in barks and reservoirs.

**Do:**

- Observe **with permission**.
- Ask **short, clear questions** once trust is established:
  - “May I see how you read this?”
  - “Does this mean the water here is safe?”

**Do not:**

- Copy their gestures without understanding:
  - You may trigger a system or command you are not prepared for.
- “Experiment” with:
  - Pressing palms to surfaces.
  - Pouring anything into reservoirs.
  - Cutting, scraping, or marking the city.

**Do not try to “improve,” “fix,” or “override” anything**  
unless you are explicitly and repeatedly guided to do so by residents who understand the system’s language.

---

### 4.4 Consent and boundaries

- Consent here is **threefold**:
  1. **From people**.
  2. **From the city** (through subtle behavior or feedback).
  3. **From yourself** (to accept being profoundly altered by the experience).

**Practical rule:**  
If you feel “wrongness” in the city—uneasy, sick, or if you sense “withdrawal” from the environment—treat it as **feedback from the host** and reconsider what you just did or where you are standing.

---

### 4.5 Your own mental state

You will feel:

- **Very small**.
- Possibly **observed** at all times.
- Far from “home” time, technology, and style.

**Prepare:**

- Journaling:
  - Write about your **internal reactions** as well as external events.
- Breathing practices:
  - To settle the nervousness of living inside a body that is not you.
- Support:
  - If possible, bring a **traveler’s anchor token** from home:
    - A photo.
    - A small object that symbolizes your own world-body.
  - This helps ground you when the world feels **too alive and too foreign**.

---

## 5. On Arrival and Early Steps

### 5.1 The first hour: “landing protocol”

1. **Find a safe, neutral edge space**
   - Between major trunks.
   - Near where locals are visible but not actively interfacing with you.
2. **Do not immediately go deeper**
   - Sit, orient, and listen.
   - Notice the floor sensation under you.
   - Notice the ambient humidity.
3. **Wait for interaction**
   - Do not approach locals first if they seem busy interfacing with the city.
   - Let **one** local notice you and approach in their own time.

---

### 5.2 Your first “guide” questions

Once you have contact with someone who seems open to you:

- Ask about:
  - **Areas of the city** where you are allowed to walk.
  - **Places that are restricted** because they are metabolically critical or delicate.
  - **How to behave around water reservoirs**:
    - Can you look?
    - Can you touch?
    - How would you know if the water/color signals “unhealthy”?

- Ask explicitly:
  - “Is there anywhere I **should not go** because my presence might be harmful to the city or to people?”
  - “If I feel unwell, where should I go, or what should I do?”
  - “Does the city ever **reject** someone? How would I know if I am not welcome?”

---

### 5.3 Learning “language” of the city

- Watch color shifts in:
  - Water reservoirs.
  - Bark patterns.
- Notice how:
  - Locals respond to these shifts.
- Understand that:
  - **A “bad” reading** might mean:
    - Local contamination.
    - Need for maintenance.
    - Or even “shift” of the city’s internal attention.

Your task is to learn to **read** as well as **walk**.

---

## 6. Departure and Aftercare

### 6.1 Leaving without “severing”

- **Do not take**:
  - Samples of bark, skin, or water.
  - Any “part” of the city without **explicit permission and guidance**.
- **Do not leave**:
  - Non-biodegradable waste.
  - Any artificial markings or graffiti.
- **Leave**:
  - Only memories and data.
  - Possibly **a relationship** with a host who now remembers you.

---

### 6.2 Returning to your own world

- Expect **reverse culture shock**:
  - Your home world may feel **dead or inert** after living inside *Veridonum sp.*
- You may have:
  - **Dreams** of living inside a body that is also a world.
  - **Sensory memories**: the smell of warm bark, the sound of single drops falling into reservoirs.
- Maintain **grounding** in:
  - Your own body.
  - Your own relationships.

Use what you learned:

- **Respect for living systems**.
- **Silence as a form of listening**.
- **Consent at all levels**:
  - With people.
  - With the world.
  - With yourself.

---

### In summary

To visit the City Beneath the Root-Shadow:

- Prepare **as if entering a body, not just a city**.
- Bring light, quiet, and respect.
- Shield yourself subtly—from radiation, from moisture, from overstimulation.
- Listen first, ask second, act third.
- Understand that the city **was not built for you**, but you have been given **temporary and conditional permission** to breathe inside it.

Treat the city like you would treat a great host in whose veins you are temporarily flowing: **with reverence, care, and the quiet certainty that you are being changed.**
