## 1. Mental Reservation: Why You Are Going and How You Will Return

Before you pack a single item, decide before your own mind that:

- You are going to the winter room at the bottom of the world *as a visitor, not as a resident.*  
- You will come back to the place of air and sun and *horizontal* horizon.  
- Whether you will return to a lover, a duty, a pet, or a tree you planted, you will name that concrete anchor now and fix it in your mind like a law.

The room will loosen your memory. It will change what you “remember” about your arrival. It will try to convert your entire past into water and winter. If you do not set a clear intention before entering, you may become more of the room before you realize you ever changed.

---

## 2. Memory and Narrative Preparation: What You Must Take That You Cannot Lose

### 2.1 Anchor your past *before* you go down

- **Write it down.**  
  - On paper, on waterproof material if possible, describe:
    - Who you are.
    - Where you are from.
    - Three people you love and how you know them.
    - One day that you will *not* allow the room to rewrite.
    - One landscape of warmth and sun that you refuse to forget: sun on skin, dry wind, a horizon that is not just darkness above.
  - Seal this description in a waterproof case or bag.

**Rule:** Once you descend, you do not re-open this notebook inside the room itself. The moment you open it there, it becomes “more of the room,” and the words you wrote may become unstable.

### 2.2 Create a formal return protocol

Create a **script** for yourself, before descending:

- I will return when:  
  - I have seen the wave-room floor with my own eyes.  
  - I have felt the “click” inside my ear at least once.  
- I will *not* stay longer than:
  - A set period of time measured *before* entering (you cannot trust time in the room).
- And a key phrase:
  - E.g., “When the memory of sunlight on my skin feels thin like the glass-green translucence, I will surface.”  

Write this phrase large and simple on the inside of your waterproof case. You will not read it in the room; you will read it when you are about to descend.

Your mind is the most fragile thing you bring. The room will try to turn it into water.

## 3. Physical and Environmental Protection

### 3.1 Dress for the almost

The temperature is not lethal. It is not a frostbite world. It is a constant **almost-cold**, with humidity that never leaves.

You will prepare:

- **Layers of wicking and waterproof fabric**
  - Base layer: thermal, moisture-wicking but not too thick.
  - Middle layer: insulation appropriate for cold water.
  - Outer layer: fully waterproof but *breathable* if possible.
- **Coverings for extremities**
  - Waterproof gloves or mitts that you are still able to feel with.
  - Warm socks; waterproof booties if you can.
  - A hood or cap that covers your ears—the cold humidity prefers to sink into your head and ears.

The key is not just temperature protection but **understanding moisture**. The room will insist you are always slightly wet. You want to leave that “wet” as a layer against your skin but not deep inside your body.

### 3.2 Equalizing pressure and the feeling of wetness

You may not need deep-diving compressors, but your body will be pressed by dense water-air.

- **Practice pressure equalization exercises**
  - Learn how to clear your ears without forcing too hard.
  - Know the slight pressure of your own mask against your face.
- **Expect your skin to prune**
  - If you are in the room for more than a short time, your skin will wrinkle like you’ve been in water far longer than you think.
  - Moisture barrier cream can help keep your skin from feeling raw, but it will not change the room’s insistence on wetness.

### 3.3 Light and sight

There is no light source except the green translucence. You will not be able to rely on external light sources.

- **Choose low, self-generated light**
  - A small waterproof headlamp or light attached firmly to your body.
  - Earmuff or headband design to avoid the straps being loosened by the silent pressure.
- **Expect this to become “more of the room”**
  - If your device is waterproof, it will be affected by the room’s “everything you bring into this room becomes more of the room” rule.
  - Your light may become fainter, the beam more diffuse, the battery life longer and less predictable than you expect.
  - Backups are not a luxury: sealed waterproof lights in separate containers if you can bring them.

You will need to direct your light carefully. The room will eat the brightness.

## 4. Breathing and the “Almost Air”

### 4.1 The medium of the room

You will not be drowning, but:

- The air is thick, water-rich.
- You will feel like you are breathing just above the surface of a cold lake at all times.

Prepare for:

- **Short, shallow breaths**
  - Practice not gasping. The humid weight will make you want to overcompensate with big breaths; you won’t get more air, you’ll just get more of the room in your lungs.
- **Multiple filter masks** if you can
  - Not chemical masks, but water filters that can trap moisture droplets before they settle into your lungs.
  - Even if they only partially work, your body will be less assaulted by the constant cold humidity.

### 4.2 The taste of mint and snow

You will taste the air.

- The taste of mint and snow is not pleasant or unpleasant. It is *information*.
  - If you detect a change in the taste—more metallic, more salt, more of nothing—that is the room changing its chemistry very slightly around you.
- **Learn to notice the difference**
  - Before descending, pay attention to how air feels in your mouth.
  - Then you will have a comparison point when the room changes its flavor.

## 5. Sensory and Perceptual Discipline: How to Stare at the World

The room’s rules include:

- Things you watch closely become harder to change but far harder to forget.
- Things you do not watch closely have a tendency to change.

You must not stare blindly at everything, but you must also not let everything blur.

### 5.1 Decide beforehand what you will “watch closely”

Before you enter the winter room, decide:

- I will **watch my own body**, especially:
  - The position of my hands.
  - Whether my feet are still touching the floor.
- I will **not stare closely at the walls of frozen waves**
  - You can glance at them to understand where you are, but not fix them in your gaze for long periods.
- If you see a **built line** under the floor:
  - You may choose one of these lines to be your “anchor line.”
  - You will glance at it occasionally to ensure it hasn’t shifted.
- **Filaments**
  - Avoid watching them too directly for long.
  - They change when you aren’t looking; you cannot prevent them.
  - Glance at them to understand how they branch, but do not become obsessed with one strand.

### 5.2 Handling your blink

“You blink. And when you open your eyes again—did you blink?—you notice that one wall leans closer than you remember.”

This is not horror or illusion; it is the room’s *architecture of attention*. You blink, and the world rearranges slightly.

- **Practice the “before–after check” outside**
  - Before descending, try closing your eyes for ten seconds and moving one object in the room you are in.
  - When you open your eyes, notice how quickly your mind invents a reason why it changed.
- **Then, inside the winter room**
  - When you open your eyes after a blink or short gaze away:
    - Note the position of the floor relative to your boots.
    - Note the nearest wall.
  - You do not need to panic. You just need to acknowledge: the wall is closer than you remembered.
  - Do not treat this as a slow attack; treat it as the weather of the room.

## 6. The Unseeable Fauna and the Filaments

### 6.1 The fauna of erasure

You will not see the fauna. You will feel it as:

- A blank in the green translucence.
- A missing piece of pattern where the floor doubles back on itself.
- A filament that ends abruptly not because it was cut but because it simply never continued.

You must not try to map it. You must not try to track it. You must not name it.

- **If you feel a blank nearby**
  - Do not reach into it. You do not know what part of you will be declared “never was.”
  - Step slowly to the side of it.
  - Keep your hands close to your body.
- **Watch the filaments around that blank**
  - If you suddenly see a filament longer than you remembered, and its end only reaches into that empty region, note:
    - Another length of the world has been taken.
    - Not eaten, not torn. Just erased.

You can record this pattern later, outside. If you try to name the creature, you already become more of the room.

## 7. Gravity, Orientation, and the Soft Floor

You will not drown in the floor. You will not slam into the ceiling. You will not fall through the dark.

Gravity here is **only suggestion**:

- You can feel the floor under your feet.
- You can, theoretically, move near the walls.
- You can, theoretically, press near the ceiling of the room—where you will not see clearly what you are pressing against.

But you will not truly *float* in a way you can control.

### 7.1 How to move

- Move **slowly**.
  - Take steps that test the floor first before fully planting weight.
- Use **muscle memory**
  - If you can, practice the motion of walking forward, feeling for the floor without looking down, before you enter the room.
- **Find the built lines**
  - If you can avoid the undulating floor as your main spatial guide, one line under the floor can be your reference.
  - Keep your movement aligned to that line so you know you are moving forward, not turning around.

## 8. Time and the “Click”

You will not sense time as it passes outside.

- The room will:
  - Make you feel as if you have been here a very long time.
  - Or make you feel as if you arrived only seconds ago.
- You will not be able to tell if you are aging or not.
- You will not be able to tell if you are sleeping or not.

You will rely on:

- **The number of times the room changes you notice**
  - Each time you feel the “click” inside your ear—the soft, internal reset of the room—that is a time-mark.
- **Changes in the filaments**
  - If you leave one area of filaments and return to it later:
    - If their pattern has changed beyond anything you can remember, you know more time has passed there than you expected.

### 8.1 Set a time-stop before entering

Before you go down, decide:

- I will begin to leave when:
  - I have experienced three “clicks” inside me.
  - Or:
  - The number of changes I have observed in the floor or walls around me exceeds a number you set beforehand.

You do not need to be exactly precise. You only need a clear *rule* you can obey even when your memory is being absorbed into water.

## 9. The Built Things and the Hand Underneath

### 9.1 The lines under the floor

You will not understand the geometry. You will not decode the intent despite the “intention without tool” of the builders.

You can still use it:

- **Choose one line** not as your map but as your **rail.**
  - If you follow the line, you will not wander easily in the room.
  - The line will not explain the room, but it will allow you to return to the place you first entered it—if the entrance is still there.

### 9.2 The protrusion and the hand

You will find the flat plane cut into the wave wall.

You will not know what cut it. You will not know what direction the hand comes from.

- **If you see the hand-shaped space under the floor**
  - Do not put your own hand over it.
  - Do not try to match the fingers.

The “hand—too many fingers” pressed into the underside of the world is not simply decoration. It is where the water’s memory becomes different.

You can look at it. Even if you become unable to forget it, you still can choose not to interact with what has been pressed into the world from below.

## 10. Your Own Past and the Reality Breakdown

“Your past is slowly becoming more of the room.”

This is not metaphor. It happens.

- If you stay here long:
  - Your memory of a sunlit day may become infused with the green tones of the room.
  - You will remember pressure in your chest when you were never under water.

### 10.1 How to protect your memory while you are inside

- **Do not actively remember your past new moments**
  - It is tempting to “test” your memories by replaying them.
  - Do not.
  - The room will see each rehearsed memory as a place to overwrite.
- **Keep your mind on the *now* of the room**
  - Observe what you are seeing:
    - “This wall is close to my right.”
    - “This filament is branching.”
    - “This floor is thicker than the last.”
- **Save the deep memory work for outside the room**
  - After you return, you will test your memory against the written notebook.
  - While inside, you need not prove to yourself that you are remembering. It’s better not to try.

## 11. Remembering “Up”

When the text warns you,

> You should: remind yourself of up, of sun, of air that is not water.

You should, but you must do it carefully:

- Think **not** of entire memories of sunlight:
  - Do not slip into full scenes of the beach, the city, the home.
  - The scene you try to relive will be changed easily by the room.
- Instead:
  - **Focus on a single sensation**
    - The heat of the sun on your skin, not the sun itself.
    - The dryness of air in your nose, not the full landscape.
  - Turn this sensation into a short phrase:
    - “sun-on-skin.”
    - “dry-air.”
  - Practice this short phrase a few times before descending.
- **Repeat the phrase only when needed**
  - When the floor feels too close to your head.
  - When the water-air seems thick enough to drink.
  - Then you say: “sun-on-skin,” and you feel that heat for just one instant, not a full story.

The room will not be able to rewrite a single, sharp sensation as easily as it can rewrite a complex memory.

## 12. If You Are Not Alone

The text warns:

- If you came with others, you may not remember clearly.
- If you came alone, you may feel others nearby.

Therefore:

- **Before you descend, decide who you are traveling with**
  - Write their names in your notebook.
  - Have each person write their own brief description of their home and why they are going down.
- **Work out a clear protocol for contact inside the room**
  - If you can still see each other:
    - Do not rely on calling each other by name.
    - Rely on fixed signs:
      - One hand raised to indicate: “I am still me.”
      - A tap on the shoulder meaning: “We are leaving together.”
  - If you cannot see each other:
    - You do not conduct search operations.
    - You agree beforehand:
      - “If we cannot reach each other within the set time of our agreed rule, we leave without the person.”

This may sound harsh, but inside the room, distance is measured by memory. If someone else is already less memory and more room, you will not be able to reach them by walking.

## 13. Exit and the Non-return of the Entrance

“No matter how you arrived, you cannot leave the way you came.”

The entrance you think you remember descending through will:

- Not be there.
- Or be there but not be an entrance in the way you expect.
- Or be there but with you no longer able to see it as you remember.

### 13.1 Your exit strategy

- **Choose a fixed exit rule** before you descend:
  - After one of these:
    - The number of changes you noticed.
    - The number of “clicks” inside.
    - The first time you feel that the world is becoming entirely a room and not a world.
- **When you decide to leave**
  - Do not search for a specific remembered door.
  - Look for the place where the floor feels least like floor:
    - Where the swells of “floor made of wave” become unstable.
    - Where the translucence feels least like water and more like absence.
- **You will not go up in the way you came down**
  - You will not see the shaft you descended through.
  - You may be pushed up, not by climbing, but by the world itself gently refusing you as floor.

Expect this exit to be **less a door and more a reversion**:

- You may become more of air.
- You may become less of water.
- You may feel the world deciding you no longer belong to the winter room.

You will not be the same as you were before you entered; but that is not the same as being “more of the room.”

---

## 14. Last Instructions Before Descending

- Pack your **notebook of identity and return protocol** sealed.
- Wear your **thermal layers and waterproof shell**.
- Have your **light** attached firmly to you.
- Know your **time-stop rule** and the phrase for your sun-on-skin.
- Know the **rule about not naming the unnamed fauna or flora.**

Then you can go down to the bottom of the world where the ocean grows thin, and you can meet:

- The winter room that is not in winter but is winter itself.
- The floor made of wave, fixed but not dead.
- The filaments that change when you look away.

And you will not go to become a wall or a hand pressed under another world’s floor.

You will go to see the room of winter water, and you will come back to tell about it—modified, but not yet erased.
