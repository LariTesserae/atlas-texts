To visit the Plateau of Weeping Machines is to deliberately step into a system that works perfectly without you. Your first task is not to see its sights, but to avoid becoming noise in its machinery.

You will be treated like the weather you are not: there is no weather here, except what you bring with you.

---

### The Core of the Problem

You are going to a young, high, cold world that runs like a perfectly balanced clock on a scale of continents and centuries. Almost everything you are used to—time passing, temperature changing, animals moving around, tools being replaced—plays no role there.

Your main hazards are:

- **Cold saturation**  
  4°C is not deep freezing, but it is everywhere, always, with no respite. No sun to heat you, no wind to chill you, but the humidity is 100% and you will be constantly wet. The danger is not freezing, but hypothermia by slow, steady heat loss you cannot feel.

- **Corrosion and contamination**  
  The water is “not quite water.” It clings to surfaces and to you, with a viscosity and memory that suggests it is less a solvent and more a medium. This water will permeate clothing and equipment if they can be permeated.

- **Scale and disorientation**  
  The plateau is designed to be read from kilometers above, not from ground level. There are no landmarks that you are meant to recognize as landmarks. Geometry is precise but not human-readable.

- **Isolation from causality**  
  There is nothing to eat. No animals to hunt, no insects to drone, no bacteria-friendly niches to cultivate. You will not find “warm” coming from anywhere. No sunrise to orient by, no seasons to measure against.

- **Audio-visual monotony**  
  The same flat light, the same low engine sound, the same weeping, everywhere, forever. This kind of unchanging input can break your sense of time and self in less time than you would expect.

- **Being erased by a machine that doesn’t care you exist**  
  The infrastructure is not trying to kill you. It is not trying to do anything at you. It is simply indifferent to humans on the same order that a plate tectonics is indifferent to bacteria. If you walk into the wrong latticework, you will be processed like condensation.

### What You Must Bring With You

#### 1. Environment suit

Do not go here in anything ordinary. You will need an **active environmental system**, not just clothing.

**Requirements:**

- **Vapor‑tight or fully sealed**
  - This water does not freeze but it behaves unnaturally. Your skin should not be exposed to it.
  - Seams must be welded or bonded with materials that repel water on a molecular level.
  - No cotton, no wool, no leather: anything absorbent will become a sponge of strange water and you will never get dry.

- **Active insulation**
  - 4°C is survivable but not indefinitely comfortable. You need to generate heat internally.
  - You do not need to “heat the outside world”; you need to keep a narrow layer around your body stable.
  - Consider a thin inner layer with conductive heating threads or a fluid circulation loop where you can dump heat at need.

- **Waterproof gloves**
  - Do not touch any surfaces with your bare skin.
  - If you must touch something, use gloves, and then be prepared to replace them or deep clean them before they return to your main environment.

- **Footwear**
  - You will be walking on slick surfaces, constantly wet with water that will not be ice.
  - You need boots that:
    - Are chemically resistant to unknown water composition.
    - Have high-traction soles that do not deteriorate from constant moisture.
    - Do not leak around the ankle cuff.

- **Helmet or mask**
  - The air is saturated with moisture that is not normal water vapor.
  - You will want a filter or at least a barrier that prevents inhaling condensation and “weeping.”
  - If the helmet has a clear visor, ensure anti-fogging and anti-condensation coatings.
