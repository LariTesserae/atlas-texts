To walk into this place unprepared is to be a tiny, overheated disturbance in a perfectly engineered climate – and the plant-system will treat you exactly like that.

Below is a traveler’s guide: how to think about this world, how to not die, and how to move through it without becoming more pollution than guest.

---

## 1. What kind of place you’re about to enter

Before you pack anything, you need to *internalize* what this world is:

- **A planet-wide plant factory.**  
  Not a forest with a factory in it.  
  The entire world is one continuous organism or fused system functioning as a climate-controlled, self-powered industrial machine.

- **No sun, no sky, no animals.**  
  All light is faint and self-produced.  
  The “weather” is invisible: humidity shifts, faint electrical gradients, slow airflows you can’t feel on your face.  
  There are no insects, no birds, no predators—so you are not expected food. But you are also not expected, *period*.

- **Everything is alive and engineered to be “just enough.”**  
  Temperature: just enough to keep you always sweating.  
  Water: just enough that you rarely *see* water but you always *feel* wet.  
  Light: just enough to outline shapes but not reveal the world.

- **Time is slow and structural.**  
  Nothing happens fast you can watch.  
  What happens fast happens at the level of chemistry or electricity, and what happens slow is architecture—columns of wood growing into the ceiling of the world.

- **You are the anomaly.**  
  The system maintains its own near-perfect equilibrium. You are the heat source, the chemical pollutant, the foreign body that will be compensated for or eliminated.

The goal of your preparation is then:
- **Make yourself invisible to the system’s corrections.**  
- **Make yourself capable of being *slightly* wrong about everything for a long time.**

## 2. Mental & psychological preparation

### 2.1 Senses and scale

You will be:

- Unable to see far.  
- Everything you see will be outlines of shapes in low, self-lighting glow.  
- Distances are hard to judge when there are no clear sky, horizon, or familiar objects.

Prepare:

- **Train your eyesight to low contrast.**  
  Spend a few days in low or colored light before transit. Let your eyes adjust to *not* having a clear horizon.  
- Learn **depth from motion:** move side-to-side slightly to see parallax between pillars of wood and glowing leaves.  
- Accept **sensory uncertainty:** in this place, not knowing distances exactly is normal.

- **Expect disorientation:**
  - No sun to tell east from west.
  - No horizon to distinguish up or down.
  - No wind you can feel on your skin.

- **Comfort yourself with structure:**  
  This world is not chaos; it’s hyper-engineered.  
  If you understand “I am in a plant-machine that’s been made stable,” then much of what happens will not be mysterious. It will be an algorithm responding.

### 2.2 Time perception

Hours will feel longer than they are.  
Days will blur into one continuous dim.

You will need:

- **External timekeeping.**  
  - Bring **multiple** watches/clocks with:
    - Backlight
    - Waterproof seal
    - Vibration (for alarms)
  - Use one watch on your local home time (for multiverse sync), one on local world time (you can define: 24-hour day, maybe).

- **Human-scale rhythm markers.**
  - Keep “days” and “nights” defined:  
    - “Day” = eight hours active,  
    - “Night” = sleep/rest period.
  - Use headlight **color temperature**:
    - Cooler white light for “day”
    - Warmer, very low light for evening “winding-down” time.

- **Expect no change in the world’s *state*.**  
  You will not see sunrise or sunset.  
  You will not see clouds change shape.  
  You will not see daily wind shifts.  
  You will have to impose your own cycle.

- **Prepare yourself for “eternal sameness.”**
  - Practice in advance:
    - Spend a few days living with the same unchanging lighting, rooms, and routine.
    - This is to simulate the world & train your brain to cope with *no external cues* shifting.

- **Bring patterning:**  
  - Change your clothes (“day” vs “sleep”) clothes, even if they’re identical.
  - Have small routines:  
    - Wash face at the start of “day.”  
    - Drink a warm drink from a flask before “night.”

If you don’t do this, you may lose track of time entirely—napping/waking at random—until you are disoriented and depressed.

---

### 2.3 Isolation and silence

You will be the only animal you see. There will be no responses to your voice. Prepare:

- **Comfort in your own thoughts:**  
  - Practice spending time alone in silence before you go.
  - Bring journal and method to write/draw/record audio.
  - If you absolutely cannot travel without social contact, ensure:
    - You have some way to communicate outwards to your multiverse home.  
    - You know how to use it and *when you will allow yourself to use it.*  
      - For example: “I check in with home base every three local days, max ten minutes.”

- **Manage noise hypersensitivity:**  
  The world is very quiet except for tiny, unpredictable machine hints.  
  - Bring earphones or loop earplugs for *genuine* silence.
  - You *will* hear every tiny sound of your own body—breathing, heartbeat, clothes—more clearly. This can be meditative or maddening depending on your temperament.

---

### 2.4 The jar of air you bring inside yourself

Everywhere you go, you carry a home in your head.

- **Mentally rehearse:**  
  Before you step through:
  - Imagine yourself standing in that world.
  - Imagine yourself breathing that thick, warm air.
  - Imagine yourself *choosing* to walk forward, one step at a time.

- **Have one phrase you repeat to yourself:**  
  - “I am a visitor. This is not my world. This does not need to terrify me.”
  - or:  
  - “I am here to learn. This is a machine that is alive and older than me.”

This helps a lot when you feel the weight of the world pressing on your lungs and the strange sense that you *shouldn’t be here.*

## 3. Medical and biological safety

### 3.1 Microbiological unknowns

Even though the system seems to manage its own decay, you are still:

- Covered in foreign microbes.
- Stepping into a world whose *every* surface is living tissue.

Assume:

- **Everything that is damp is alive with microscopic life.**
- Some of it will be harmless to you, some unknown.

You must:

- **Maintain a physical barrier** between your skin and the environment:
  - Full-length, sealed clothing.
  - Waterproof boots.
  - Gloves that you can clean and disinfect often.
  
- **Never touch your face with bare hands** until you disinfect them.
  
- **Assume you are harvesting spores:**  
  - Every surface you touch, you may pick up spores or fungi.
  - Some of them will be specialized to digest plant matter; they might not be equipped to digest you, but may *get* equipped if you give them time.
  
- **Have a strong “player one” first aid kit:**  
  Includes:
  - Broad-spectrum antibiotics (if allowed by your multiverse laws).
  - Topical antifungals and antiseptics.
  - Anti-allergic / antihistamine.
  - Any personal medications and **double spares**.

- **Hair & hygiene:**
  - Keep hair short or covered.
  - Keep nails trimmed and cleaned.
  - Every so-called “day” you pass:
    - Change your socks.
    - Change or fully sanitize underwear and base layers.
    - Dry your insides: armpits, groin, between toes. *Especially* between toes.

---

### 3.2 Heat and humidity regulation

Your body will be constantly **trying to cool itself**, and the environment is **always close to saturated with humidity**:

- **You cannot sweat-evaporatively cool effectively.**  
  The air is too wet; your sweat won’t evaporate well.
- Without changes of wind and sun, you may not feel “hot” even though you are overheating.

**Mitigation:**

- **Monitor your core temperature:**  
  - Use a simple thermometer or a smart-device that you can wear under your clothes.
- **Move slow:**  
  - Any physical exertion generates heat.
- **Take active breaks anywhere air can move on you**:
  - Even minor fanning from a device helps.
- **Strip layers:**  
  - You can change into dry underlayer when you feel you’re steaming in your own suit.
- **Maintain hydration**:
  - Drink more water than you *think* you need.
  - Use electrolyte salts in your drink bottle, especially if you are sweating and not noticing it.

---

### 3.3 Breathing protection

**Can you breathe air like thick, velvet water?**

- Probably. But:

- The **air is warm and humid**.  
  - Your lungs must work harder to move that thick air.
  - You might feel shortness of breath, especially when moving.

- There might be **aerosolized chemicals**, metabolic byproducts, or organic particles suspended in the moisture.

**Gear:**

- **High-quality air filter mask or half-face respirator:**  
  - Not necessarily gas mask full-on, but something:
    - That filters particulates and some chemical vapors.
    - With replaceable filters.
    - With a valve that helps you exhale easily.

- Optional **very small portable air analyzer**:
  - To detect:
    - High oxygen or low oxygen.
    - CO₂ levels.
    - Toxic gases—if your system even supports that.

- **Know your body:**  
  - If you have asthma or any lung condition:
    - This place is risky.
    - Consider shorter, very carefully planned missions.

---

### 3.4 Electrical and biological fields

This world has subtle **electrical surfaces**:

- Plants are wired to behave like nervous tissue or control circuits.
- You might be near small voltages all the time.

Safety:

- **Assume “everything is conductive” when damp:**  
  That includes:
  - Your skin.
  - Your clothes, if soaked.
  - Your tools.

- **Ground yourself carefully:**  
  - Use footwear and soles with insulating properties.
  - Avoid touching visibly metallic or fibrous “wiring” structures.
  - If you use electronic tools, keep them dry and sealed.

- **Don’t climb or lean heavily on structures:**  
  - You don’t know what is live wiring and what is trunk.
  - Some surface might be designed to carry current signals as you would.

---

### 3.5 Water

Water:

- Everywhere.
- Dark.
- Utterly still.
- Very likely saturated with dissolved compounds from the living system.

**Your strategy:**

- **Never drink the water untreated.**  
  - Assume it is full of enzymes, metabolites, and organic compounds optimized for a plant world.
- **Bring water purification:**  
  - Multiple redundant:
    - Portable filter.
    - Chemical tablets.
    - Small steam/condensation device.
- **Collect water via condensation:**  
  - Use a surface that you know to be clean as a condenser:
    - Collect droplets from your own clean surfaces or from a small condensation device.
    - This is likely your safest water source.

**Skin contact:**

- Brief contact with water:
  - Through intact waterproof suit:
    - Probably safe.
- Be extra cautious with:
  - **Pools near glowing edges**  
    - Might be deliberate reservoirs with chemical or voltage gradients.

---

### 3.6 Food

You cannot eat this world’s raw matter and expect to be fine.

Assume:

- Any plant matter you try to eat:
  - Is designed for *plant* consumption.
  - Might be full of enzymes and chemicals that can harm you.
  - Some might have **structures** similar to plants you know from home, but they have been *engineered*.

**Bring:**

- **Sealed, long-lasting calories:**  
  - Meal bars or self-rehydrating meals.
  - Enough for **twice** the planned duration, plus emergency.
- **Vitamins:**  
  - You won’t be able to rely on local food for micronutrients.
- **Conservation:**  
  - Eat by schedule and not by hunger.  
  - Your body may underestimate hunger because of temperature and humidity, but you need energy.

---

### 3.7 Sleep and rest

You will sleep:

- In the dark.
- In the constant faint hum of machinery.
- In the soft sound of dripping condensation.

Prepare:

- **Sleep hygiene:**  
  - Define “sleep time” by time, not by external light.
  - Use an eye mask or face-covering to enforce darkness.
  - Use earplugs or noise-bending app to filter the drone of distant fans.

- **Security while sleeping:**
  - You are not being preyed upon.
  - You are being *processed* by a system that doesn’t know you are a person.
    - Some fungal growths might be curious to colonize.
    - Some roots might grow slowly *through* your equipment if you leave it long enough.
    - Some water might pool slowly under you.
    - **Don’t sleep directly on the ground:**
      - Use a raised platform (tent flooring, portable pad that you check).
    - Don’t leave your pack on the root-mat for long periods.

---

### 3.8 Waste management

The system will break down anything you give it.

This is *dangerous* for you, because:

- The system may learn how to digest you better.
- Or you may be seen as fertilizer input, which the system then optimizes around.

**You must:**

- **Never void directly to the environment.**  
  - Use airtight containers or bags for your own waste.
  - Pack out everything you can.
  - If you absolutely must leave waste:
    - Look at decomposing areas of plant matter and *very small* inputs there.
    - Mark each location carefully.
    - Don’t return.

**Treat yourself as a closed loop:**

- What goes in stays in you or your containers until it can be removed from the system entirely.

## 4. Equipment and gear checklist

### 4.1 Clothing and barriers

- **Full waterproof suit or shell layer**
  - Breathable waterproof fabric.
  - Fully sealed seams.
  - Enough room to wear layers beneath.

- **Underlayers (thermal base layers):**
  - Lightweight synthetic or moisture-wicking fabrics.
  - Avoid cotton if possible.
  - Bring extras:
    - Undershirts
    - Undergarments
    - Socks

- **Boots:**
  - Waterproof, sealed seams.
  - High ankle to protect against small hidden water basins you step into.
  - Good grip soles.
  - Breathable but tight enough to not let water seep.

- **Gloves and hoods:**
  - Tight-fitting gloves that can handle wet surfaces.
  - Head covering that protects:
    - Hairline
    - Ears
    - Neck

**Rotation strategy:**

- Mandate to change your undergarments and socks every “day.”
- Wash and disinfect *immediately* after use.
- Hang dry nothing inside the world unless you use a sealed environment that you can heat or dry.

---

### 4.2 Lighting and visibility

This world’s native light is **not enough** for safe human movement.

**Essential lighting:**

- **Primary headlight**  
  - Wide-angle beam with low and medium intensity.
  - Red-light mode (to protect your night vision and minimize biological disturbance).
  - Long battery life.

- **Secondary handheld light**
  - For close inspection of surfaces:
    - Warnings of glowing edges.
    - Fine texture in roots.
    - Hidden water under your feet.

- **Backup lights**  
  - At least two:
    - Small keylight or lantern.
    - Built-in indicators on your device.

**Battery strategy:**

- Assume recharge is impossible without *your own power*.
- Bring enough:
  - Rechargeable batteries with external power pack.
  - Possibly solar or hand-crank generator, if any of your devices support that.
- **Redundancy is everything.**
  - If you lose your lights here you can still see faint outlines, but you cannot navigate safely.

**Use of light:**

- Keep **primary light low** when:
  - You are not in danger.
  - You want to minimize disturbing the electrical and chemical environment.
- Use **bright light** only:
  - For inspection of surface.
  - For signaling emergencies (if you have someone else to signal).
  - For marking your way.

**Never shine a bright beam into a dark pool of water:**

- You might trigger:
  - Photochemical responses.
  - Electrical gradients.
  - Species behaviors you don’t understand and don’t want.

**Marking your way:**

- Use:
  - Temporary trail tape that you remove on your way out.
  - LED markers that you can attach to **structures you don’t damage.**
  - Faintly chalky or color-safe marks on your own surfaces.

**Do not use permanent paint, cutting, or etching into the plant architecture.**

### 4.3 Navigation tools

Because there is no sky, sun, or horizon:

- **Standard landmarks:**  
  - You must rely on:
    - Orientation of great pillars.
    - Changes in texture of the root-mat.
    - Slight slope of the ground.
    - Any faint direction of airflow you can sense.

**Tools:**

- **Compass:**  
  - May or may not work well depending on the world’s magnetic field.  
  - You can still:
    - Remember “which direction I am pointed from the last major pillar.”
    - Use this as a “reference axis.”

- **Mapping device:**  
  - Simple method:
    - Pad and paper, sealed in a waterproof case.
    - Draw:
      - The pillar you started at.
      - The direction you walked.
      - Any changes in slope.
    - More advanced:
      - A device with:
        - Accelerometer
        - Gyro
    - This can:
      - Estimate distance traveled and steps.
      - Reconstruct a path skeleton.

- **Simple “distance” tool:**  
  - Step counter:
    - Use known stride length.
    - Multiverse gear usually lets you calibrate this.

**Key navigation rule:**

- **Always know how to get back to the last point *you absolutely know*.**
- If you feel:
  - You are going deeper and not getting anywhere,
  - Or you are turning too much,
- Stop:
  - Turn around.
  - Make sure you’re not leaving yourself.

### 4.4 Communication and logging devices

You don’t know if signal goes out from here. But you can record:

- **Recording device:**  
  - Audio recorder:
    - For logging your thoughts.
    - For capturing ambient sounds:
      - Humming machinery
      - Drip of condensation
    - For cross-reference with time.

- **Camera:**  
  - With:
    - Low-light capability.
    - Waterproof case.
    - Redundant storage cards.
    - Battery redundancy.

- **Never delete data at the site:**  
  - You don’t know which small detail you will need.
  - Delete nothing you don’t have extra copy of.

- **Emergency signaling device:**
  - If any multiverse device can:
    - Alert “home” you need to leave.
  - Keep:
    - Emergency button ready.
    - One known line you may call.

**Psychological logging:**

- Keep a **simple log of emotional states.**  
  - Rough:
    - “Day 3. Unsteady. No changes in environment. Missing obvious sunlight deeply.”
  - This helps later analysis:
    - What you can withstand.
    - How long you can stay here.

### 4.5 Tools and repair materials

You’re not going to build much here, but you need to maintain yourself:

- **Multitool** sealed and waterproof.
- **Basic repair kit:**  
  - Duct tape.
  - Sealant tape.
  - Small sewing kit.
  - Spare straps for boots and pack.
- **Cleaning supplies:**  
  - Alcohol wipes or antiseptic solution.
  - Soft cloths.
  - Small brush for cleaning surfaces.

**Nothing you use should leach into the environment:**

- Avoid:
  - Strong solvents.
  - Strong paints.
  - Anything that smells strongly like you know.

### 4.6 Shelter and sleeping gear

You need someplace to be a “home base” inside the world.

**Options:**

- **Compact, tent-like structure:**

  - Lightweight.
  - Fully enclosed floor and walls.
  - Not touching the ground with:
    - Your main sleeping mat.
  - Even inside the tent:
    - Check daily:
      - No moisture building.
      - No mold starting.
- **If you cannot use a tent:**
  - Use:
    - A waterproof tarp as groundsheet.
    - Portable raised platform.
    - A breathable bivouac sack.

**Sleeping:**

- Have:
  - **Sleeping bag:**  
    - Not too thick; world is warm.
    - You need comfort layer more than warmth.
  - **Sleeping pad:**  
    - Insulated.
    - Provides physical separation from ground.

**Don’t use any pillar or trunk as a structural anchor:**

- You might:
  - Damage living tissue.
  - Trigger structural response.
  - Cause unknowable electrical or chemical response.

### 4.7 Environmental measurement tools

You’re not trying to control this world, but **understand a tiny bit of it**.

**Essential:**

- **Simple thermometer:**  
  - To know:
    - Your body’s heating.
    - World temperature around you.
- **Hygrometer:**  
  - To confirm:
    - You are in high humidity.
    - You can expect condensation everywhere.

- **Optional:**
  - **Anemometer:**  
    - For tiny airflows you might not be able to feel.
    - Helps understand:
      - How air moves through the system.
    - **Note:** You might not detect much air movement at your scale; but the instrument may be more sensitive than you.

**Interpretation:**

- Write:
  - Date/time.
  - Temperature.
  - Humidity.
  - Any notes you associate:
    - Nearby machine sound.
    - Any faint unexpected breeze.

**Do not open or attempt to repair the machine systems:**

- The plant-factories are:
  - Not made for you.
  - Responding to signals you don’t understand.
- You may be seen as an input, or interference. **Do not provoke.**

### 4.8 Personal comfort and contingency items

You are not only a machine but a person.

**Comfort items:**

- Warm and/or cold drink capsules:
  - You can have:
    - Hot tea or coffee from sealed bottle.
  - Or:
    - Cold flavored drink from thermos.
- **Earplugs or noise-blocking headphones.**
- **Simple games:**  
  - Cards.
  - Small puzzle.
  - Something easy to clean and disinfect.

**Contingency:**

- Small emergency radio:
  - If you come from multiverse with that standard.
- **Basic “how-to” guide:**  
  - Even pre-written:
    - How to change your filter.
    - How to treat heat exhaustion.
    - How to treat minor cuts.

**Last “I’m lost” card:**

- One device or object:
  - That lets you **return home** at any time.
  - Under:
    - Clear defined conditions:
      - I’ve lost all light, and cannot move safely.
      - I have injured myself and cannot walk back.
    - Not:
      - “I’m bored and a little frightened.”
      - Save that for last resort.

### 4.9 What *not* to bring

**Avoid bringing:**

- Strong **chemicals**:
  - Strong solvents.
  - Strong pesticides.
  - Heavy metals or anything that might disrupt the plant-world’s delicate chemistry.
- **Anything living:**  
  - Seeds
  - Earth soil
  - Any kind of unsealed biological sample from your world.
- **Large electronics with open vents:**  
  - Unless you:
    - Seal them properly.
- **Memories from home you don’t have sealed:**  
  - You will be homesick soon enough.
  - Think carefully before you bring:
    - Photos of people you love on your skin or device.
    - Sometimes it helps, sometimes it hurts badly.

## 5. During the visit: how to behave in the system

### 5.1 Move like you do not exist to it

The system will notice you:

- As heat.
- As moisture change.
- As chemical output.

But you can make those signals *small* and *slow*.

**Move:**

- Through **existing gaps**:
  - Between trunks.
  - Along root-mat paths.
  - Not through dense vegetation or you might become entwined.

- With **steady slow pace** instead of bursts.

- Carefully **stepping on** the root-mat:
  - Avoid obvious:
    - Rootlets that are thin and fresh.
    - Surfaces that are clearly new fibrous growth.
  - Prefer:
    - Older-looking areas where:
      - The ground feels more matted and less soft.
      - You are less likely to break growth.

- **Never step into water you don’t have to:**

  - This:
    - Delivers your bacteria to the system.
    - Increases rapid surface growth.
    - May change local electrical gradients.

### 5.2 Touch only when you must and very carefully

When you touch surfaces:

- Use the **back of your hand** or your tool, not bare fingertips.
- After you touch:
  - Clean and disinfect hands.
- **Look** for:
  - Any movement:
    - Leaves closing slightly.
    - Surfaces glowing brighter.
  - This may be:
    - A response.
    - Not hostile, but *not nothing*.

### 5.3 Never assume safety

You see no animals:

- **There is no predator behaviour.**  
- But there is:
  - **Industrial hazard:**
    - You might step into areas where:
      - Water moves suddenly.
      - Machinery hidden underground might shift.
    - You might be near:
      - High voltage.
      - Chemical processes that are not safe for you.
- Be **cautious near**:
  - Stronger humming sounds.
  - Areas where glowing edges are brighter than usual.
  - Any spots where:
    - The root-mat gives you ***too much*** under your feet.

**Basic rule:**

- If you feel:
  - Strong sound of machinery.
  - Strong smells of any chemical.
  - Your skin feel strangely:
    - Numb
    - Itchy
    - Prickling like you imagine electric field might.
- **Back away slowly.**

---

### 5.4 Sampling: what you can take

**Take:**

- Digital images.
- Audio recordings.

**Do not take:**

- Living samples.
- Water samples.
- Leaf pieces.
- Root pieces.

Unless:

- You have:
  - Very clearly sealed containers.
  - Methods of thoroughly disinfecting surfaces.
  - A clear reason why this sample is necessary.
- **Everything you remove from the system all respects:**
  - You might not be able to return it.
  - You might be changing local environment, even if tiny.

**If you must sample:**

- **Never**:
  - Take from:
    - The only visible structure of a type.
    - Columns clearly structural as support.
    - Areas that are glowing strongly.
    - Any part that looks like “engineered plant.”
- **Preferably take from:**
  - Small detached leaf already fallen on the root-mat.
  - Small piece of root-mat already slightly damaged.
    - Even then:
      - Sealed carefully.
      - Documented fully.

---

### 5.5 Leave as little memory as possible

The system will learn to adapt around you:

- You want to be:
  - **A temporary glitch** not a pattern.

**Do not:**

- Leave trails of:
  - Cut marks.
  - Paint.
  - Permanent objects.

**Do:**

- Remove all:
  - Trail tape.
  - Temporary markers.
- Carry out all:
  - Your waste.
  - Broken equipment.
  - Even very small fragments you know might be contaminated by you.

**On the way out you:**

- Retrace precisely:
  - Your known steps.
  - Map you built.
- Check:
  - Any temporary markers you leave.
- **Clean**:
  - Any surface you might have touched:
    - With disinfectant solution if you must, but very carefully.

**Goal of your visit:**

- Learn something.
- Harm **nothing**.
- Leave *no trace you can avoid.*

---

### 5.6 When to leave early

Leave **before**:

- You feel your heat management failing:
  - You can’t seem to cool down.
  - You feel more faint than usual.
- You notice:
  - Any respiratory symptoms that:
    - Get worse.
- Any sign:
  - That some of your equipment:
    - Is beginning to grow new tissue.
    - Or you suspect:
      - Biological colonization of your device.

**Leave the moment you suspect:**

- You are no longer a visitor but:
  - A subject of eventual experiment by the world.
  - A part of its slow learning process.

**Don’t “power through” this:**

- You are not simply stronger than the world.
- Especially not:
  - A world that has built itself into a machine that doesn’t stop.

## 6. After you return home

### 6.1 Physical cleaning

- **Treat yourself and all equipment as contaminated**:
  - Everything:
    - Clothing
    - Tools
    - Devices
    - Samples
    - Even your data recorders
    Needs to be cleaned or sealed.

- **Use**:  
  - Strong but safe:
    - Disinfectants.
    - Sterilizing methods where allowed.
- **If you have samples:**
  - Keep them sealed.
  - Only open:
    - Inside specialized environments.
    - With proper safety protocols.

**Important:**

- You might *have* spores, fungi, or microbes on you:
  - That are harmless to you but dangerous to your world’s plants.
  - Or:
  - Dangerous to you but not yet active.
- **Never assume “you’re fine” immediately.**

### 6.2 Health monitoring

- **For some days after you return home:**
  - Monitor:
    - Your breathing.
    - Any skin rashes or reactions.
    - Any temperature changes.
  - Keep:
    - A simple symptom diary.
    - That you can show a doctor if you ever need to.

### 6.3 Psychological care
