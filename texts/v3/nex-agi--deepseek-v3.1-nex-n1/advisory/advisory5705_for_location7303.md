To prepare this traveler for the place you described — the *door itself*, a pressure hinge between three drowning districts — I need to treat them like an astronaut visiting another planet in a gillsuit instead of a tourist visiting a city.

### 1. Understand what you’re actually entering: not a place, but a mechanism

The first and most important mental preparation:

- **You’re not visiting a room.**  
- You’re visiting the **thickness of a seal**.  
- Everything around you is engineered structure **never meant to be seen** by human eyes.

This means:

- Almost **nothing is designed for human comfort.**  
- Angles are **wrong**, “up” and “down” **slide constantly**.  
- This “interval” between hatches is **geometry** more than architecture:  
  - welded lattice,  
  - diagonals,  
  - cross-bracing,  
  - hinges and pins,  
  - hatches that swing into each other,  
  - spaces that are only **10–30 cm thick in places**, but **5 meters** thick in others,  
  - everything interlaced like **coral bone**.

Prepare to be *inside* a mechanical joint rather than inside a “place.”

Key shift to make:

- **Don’t think: building, think: machine.**
- Don’t think: room, think: **pressure lock’s body**.  
- Don’t think: floor, think: **structural lattice** sometimes aligned to gravity, sometimes not.

### 2. Accept that you will not stand upright; you will live in three dimensions

Your “standing” reflex will be useless and dangerous.

- There is no “ground.”
- There is **lattice, catwalk segments, ladder rungs**, maybe **cable trays**, **pipes and braces**, all of which **angle** away from you just when you need them.
- There is **no consistent horizon**.  
  Your sense of “down” is **shallow** and unreliable.

Your daily life will look more like:

- **Climbing**
- **Hanging**
- **Swimming**
- **Bracing**

rather than walking.

You should train:

- **Three-dimensional body awareness:**  
  - Practice moving in **cramped spaces** where ceiling is wall and floor is beam at the same time.  
- **Climbing:** pullups, grip exercises, rope work.
- **Basic freediving breath hold (if medically safe):**  
  Even if you’re sealed, you *want* to be able to hold your breath occasionally in case anything leaks or fails.
- **Confined space familiarity:**  
  Spend time in small, uneven spaces like attic crawlspaces or engine service rooms to get used to unconventional body orientation.

### 3. Understand the medium: water is the world

Your guide description: “The water here is the body of the world.”

Key facts:

- **Total immersion.** You are *inside* seawater, **all the time**.  
- There is **no air pocket**. Everywhere you “go” is water.
- The water is **“blood-warmth,” not bath warmth.**  
- It is likely **salty, dense**, heavily charged with:
  - rust particles,
  - biological and metallic salts,
  - maybe faint oils, industrial residues.
- It **moves**: constantly **pushed** by engineered currents to keep temperature stable, prevent stagnation, and maybe to distribute pressure.

Expectations to set:

- Everything **weighs differently** in water.
- Your clothes and equipment either **help you float** or become **dead weight**.  
- Visibility **sucks** in variable ways:
  - sometimes a clear 2–5 meters,
  - sometimes turbid, **orange-brown from rust**,
  - sometimes lights give you a **lamplit halo in a salt soup**.

### 4. Prepare for the psychological pressure

Beyond the physical depth, consider **mental** pressure:

- **Enclosure on all sides**:  
  You’re inside a deep-ocean joint. Above you is unimaginable tons of water; the walls are **only meters thick**, if that.  
- **No direct horizon line**:  
  “Sky” is **weld seams**, “roof” is **pipework**, “floor” is **cross-bracing**.  
- **No natural cycles:**  
  - artificial lighting cycles,
  - maybe **no day-night** unless simulated.
- **Sound** is **immersive**:  
  - metal **groans**, **pings**, **creaks**,  
  - **currents** rushing past lattice,  
  - **hydraulic sounds**, maybe pumps or actuators working the hatches.

To prepare:

- Do **claustrophobia tolerance drills**:
  - Small rooms with **no windows**, loud **random machinery sounds**, low **red lighting**.
- **Self-hypnosis or breathing techniques**:
  - If you start to panic, you have **very few ways out**. You need reliable **self-regulation**.
- **Banal comfort measures**:  
  - Music you can call on with headphones within your helmet.
  - A small talisman or photo you can keep visible.
- **Mental “exit” routines:**  
  Know exactly: **“I can leave if I want to.”**  
  Even if you can’t leave right now, knowing the **protocol** to leave later **reduces anxiety**.

### 5. Gear: what you need to survive *inside* the pressure door

You’re not just visiting deep water; you’re visiting **deep water *inside metal***.

#### 5.1. Pressure suit / helmet / environment

**Never** go just “naked” or lightly clothed. Even if you plan to rely on local life support, you **must** have:

- **A hardened, sealed environment:**  
  - Either a **personal armored diving suit**  
  - Or a **small one-person micro-environment** you wear like a backpack.
- **Built-in pressure regulation:**  
  External pressure may be high (*deep* ocean) or engineered. Your suit must **match** or **resist** pressure difference.
- **Environmental filters:**  
  Rust and salt suspension in the water will:
  - **abrade your gear**,  
  - **clog filters**,  
  - **build deposits**.  
  Expect to **flush**, **descale**, and **swap filters** often.

Ideal features:

- **Internal helmet POV** with:
  - AR overlay of **structural geometry**:  
    - marks beams as “safe,”  
    - shows where **currents are strong**,  
    - warns **“don’t stick your head here”**.
- **Sound attenuation:**  
  - Not silence — but **clean** reproduction. You don’t want to be deaf to **dangerous creaks**, but you don’t want to lose your mind to **random noise**.
- **Oxygen or breathable mixture:**  
  - Assume the **outer water is not safely breathable**, even if filtered chemically.

#### 5.2. Movement and safety harness

Remember: **no floors**, **no gravity you trust**.

Your critical gear:

- **Climbing harness:**  
  - Strong, **corrosion-resistant** carabiners and clips.  
  - Multiple **contact points**: you *never* rely on one hook.
- **Multiple tether lines**:  
  - Strong enough to withstand **currents**,  
  - **Bungee-like** or **elastic** segments to prevent **sudden shocks**.
- **Magnetic gloves and boots:**  
  - To hold to **ferrous metal surfaces**,  
  - but with **quick-release** mechanisms; **entrapment kills**.
- **Propulsion unit:**  
  - Small **water-jet** or **thruster pack**:
  - Specifically **underpowered** so you don’t ram yourself into metal beams; **precision** matters more than **speed**.

#### 5.3. Tools and maintenance

Essential tool set:

- **Corrosion-resistant multitool**  
- **Wire brushes and soft abrasives** for rust removal  
- **Sealant tape or paste** for minor leaks on your own suit  
- **Spare filters** for your water intakes / cooling systems  
- **Headlamps with multiple spectra**:
  - white for **general visibility**,
  - **UV** for detecting mineral deposits, leaks,
  - maybe **IR** for thermal imaging (structural stress).

- **Dye and smoke markers (water-soluble):**  
  To track currents: “Where does this water go?”  
- **Small anchor or temporary line hooks:**  
  Things you can clamp quickly to **beams**, **pipes**, or **hatch edges**, so you don’t drift away if you release your main tether.

### 6. Physical training: you will be *living inside a machine body*

Because you’re moving inside a **structural lattice**, not a room, you need to adapt your body to **in-betweenness**.

Train:

- **Core strength and 3D balance:**  
  - Pilates or yoga on **unstable surfaces**,  
  - **Rotation and tilting** drills so you don’t get motion sick every time the city shrugs.
- **Grip strength**:
  - Rope climbing,  
  - **Underwater bars** if accessible.
- **Cardio endurance with low-movement impact:**  
  - You’ll be **mostly static**, clinging to some lattice, but your **heart rate** will sometimes **spike** from fear and effort.

### 7. Understanding the space: zones and topology

Get a **map** before you go, if at all possible. Ask:

- **How are the three districts connected?**

Each “district” might behave like:

- **One hatch of the pressure door.**  
- Their **movements** against each other may **shift the entire interior lattice**.

Know these zones:

- **Hatch edges:**  
  - Where districts **clamp** together.  
  - **Strong currents**, lots of **hydraulic actuators**,  
  - **Very dangerous** to occupy while they’re moving.
- **Cross-braces:**  
  - Relatively **safe** attachment points,  
  - **Lower** forces but prone to **surface corrosion**.
- **Lattice interior:**  
  - The **“bones”** of the structure,  
  - **Smaller dimensions**, tighter to move through.
- **“Transitional tubes”**:  
  - Maybe **short tunnel segments** that link **hatches**,  
  - **Cylindrical**, which **fakes** a corridor-like familiarity but can still be **rotated** any way.

Visualizing:

- **Think of yourself inside a hinge of a colossal door.**  
  When the door **swings**, the **angles you walk on** change.

### 8. Sensory adaptation: sight, sound, touch, taste

#### 8.1. Sight

Expect:

- **Light to be local:**  
  - **Work lamps**, **strip LEDs**, maybe **bioluminescent** coatings or **chemical glow** lines.
- **Reflections off**:
  - **metal**,
  - **rust bubbles**,

Your brain will constantly **reconstruct** “reality” based on limited visual hints. You need **solid** visual cues from your AR:

- Labels for **directions**:
  - “To District A via **8.5 meters lattice**, watch **head clearance**.”
- Warnings for:
  - **Strong current zones**
  - **Moving hatch lines**
  - **Corrosion hotspots**

#### 8.2. Sound

The water **transmits everything**.

Expect:

- **Low groaning**: metal flexing under pressure.
- **High metallic ping**: small thermal shock.
- **Rushing** water as **currents** adjust.
- **Occasional loud bangs**: when some **remote mechanism** engages or a lock cycles.

You will **not** have silence. Prepare to:

- Learn to **distinguish**:
  - **Normal** structural sounds vs **dangerous**, **new** noises.
- Develop **noise tolerance**:
  - Use white noise or music **only** in short bursts, so your **ears** remain **your most reliable sensor**.

#### 8.3. Touch and vibration

- **Everywhere you touch**, metal hums:
  - Through your gloves,  
  - Through your boots.
- **Currents** tug:
  - Even if you’re holding on, you **feel** the water’s mass **constantly**.

Your **skin** will become a sensor for **vibration** and **pressure change**.

#### 8.4. Taste and smell

Within your sealed environment:

- Maybe **no** true taste, but **your suit** might:
  - **Recycle air** with **chemical filters**,  
  - possibly **slightly metallic smell** or **ozone smell**.
- Outside atmosphere **irrelevant**:
  - You’re not **breathing** water, but you **feel** it **pushing** on you.

### 9. Safety: what can kill you here, and how to minimize

Main risks:

- **Entrapment and crushing:**  
  - Hatches **closing**,  
  - **Motion** of the three districts grinding **past** each other.
- **Drowning:**  
  - Suit failure, **leak**, **flooding**.
- **Current and drift:**  
  - Being **slammed** into lattice beams by **unexpected surge**.
- **Structural failure:**  
  - A weld **finally giving** in after **decades of corrosion**.
- **Psychological failure:**  
  - Panic attack inside **no-conceptual-up**.

Mitigations:

- **Always keep two ways out** visualized:
  - One escape hatch to **District A**,
  - One to **District B**,
  - One to **District C**,  
  - if possible.
- **Never** place yourself where:
  - **All three hatches** can **move** you into **deadlock**.
- **Avoid** narrow **converging zones** during:
  - **Known cycles** of structural movement,  
  - if locals have **schedule** or **warning tones**.

### 10. Duration: how long you can stay inside “the door”

This is **not** a place meant for long habitation. It’s a **gap**.

Factors:

- **Fatigue**:
  - Holding, **climbing**, **bracing** against current **wears you out**.
- **Battery & supplies**:
  - Your suit **needs power**, filters **clog**, **CO₂ scrubbing** needs changing.
- **Mental strain**:
  - **No horizon**, **constant** mechanical noise, **unstable** orientation.

Plan a **visit window**, not a residency:

- **Hours**, not days.
- **Brief observational excursions**, then:
  - Return to **one of the districts** where you can rest **outside** the pressure door itself.
