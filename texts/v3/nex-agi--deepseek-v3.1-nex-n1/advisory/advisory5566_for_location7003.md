You are about to visit a single, cathedral-sized room that contains an entire city—no streets, no suburbs, just that one chamber, where heat, water, and industrial machines define the limits of the world.

### P. First Reality Check: What You’re Stepping Into

This is not a “landscape” in the usual sense. There’s:

- No sun, moon, or stars.
- No day/night cycle.
珠 No weather aside from tiny fluctuations of heat and drip.
- No “countryside” to retreat to—there’s nowhere to go “off” the map.

The world is **engine room at city-scale**, with a basin of water at the center and giant machines along the walls. There’s a finite number of people—low density—sharing a space that you can still cross on foot in a short time.

Because you’re a human traveler from the multiverse, you should keep **one fundamental rule** in mind:

**Don’t break the local story.**  
Don’t arrive with “visions from the sun” or “descriptions of the outside universe” and speak of them as fact. This place is mythologically fragile; if your “tales of outside” are taken as real by the locals, you can:

- Confuse the local cosmology.
- Get sentenced as a blasphemer or, worse, a prophet.
- Trigger social reactions you can’t predict.

Treat the outside world you know as **your private internal mythology**, and speak of it only in metaphor, if at all.

### 1. Cultural and Psychological Prep

**1.1 Know what they’re “inside” of**

- Everyone here is used to **constant ceiling above**; they don’t have a concept of “open sky.”
- They’ve **never** seen “cold” or “dry.” They’ve never really felt “too hot,” in extreme comparison—just “always warm.”
- Their year is marked by **maintenance cycles**, community events, and the faint, periodic **engine warm pulses** or “wet times,” not season changes.

Prepare yourself for **psychological stability issues**. You’re coming from a world of weather, seasons, and day-night cycles—and here, none of that applies. Many people experience **time drift** when visiting “monolithic sky” environments; if that’s you, expect:

- Mild confusion of when “a day has passed.”
- Inability to judge passage of time without disciplined observation.
- A sense of being “always awake but never tired” in a blurry way.

To counter this, bring **external timekeeping** you can rely on:

- A mechanical watch with water resistance.
- A small, sealed hourglass (if you’re from a lower-tech world).
- A field journal with timestamps you *force yourself* to keep, even if you only “guess” them.

**1.2 Expectation setting**

- You won’t be hiking “out of town.”
- Finding “fresh air” in the sense of cool wind is not possible.
- There’s **no** real “quiet” in the sense of true silence; the hum of the engines and the water is always there.

If you’re prone to claustrophobia, this will be a stress test. Prepare yourself:

- Meditation, breath control practices.
- A mantra or “center phrase” you can repeat to remind yourself you can leave (if your method of travel allows return).
- Visual anchors of homeworld nature you’ve memorized—to mentally “step out” when needed.

### 2. Clothing and Gear

**2.1 Heat and humidity**

This place is **humid and hot**, but not scorching.

**Ideal clothing:**

- **Lightweight linen, cotton, or other breathable fabrics**—not thick synthetics that trap heat.
- **Short sleeves or sleeves you can roll up.**
- **Short hems or lightweight trousers**—long enough to avoid scraping your knees on stone, but not enough to trap moisture.
- **Change of underwear**—you’ll likely sweat. No need to carry many sets of heavy clothes because everyone else is also somewhat damp.

**Avoid:**

- Tight, clingy fabrics: they’ll be miserable.
- Heavy wool or anything that’s “warm” by your home standards.
- Turtlenecks; keep the neck and chest region ventilated if possible.

**2.2 Footwear**

Only one truly correct choice: **sandals or open water shoes** that:

- Are **non-slip on wet stone**.
- Have enough traction to handle algae-covered edges.
- Dry quickly but also protect you from sharp edges of stone.

**Do not wear:**

- Heavy boots or leather that soaks up water and never dries.
- Flimsy slip-on shoes—this is a recipe for slipping on the damp, uneven slabs.

**2.3 Protective gear**

- **Small micro towel or cloth**—for wiping sweat and condensation.
- **Simple hand fan or folding fan**—if you’re from a world where that’s plausible.
- **Waterproof belt pouch** for:
  - Medicines.
  - Small valuables.
  - Field notes.
- **Sealed waterproof bag for paper or tech**: you will be around moisture constantly.

### 3. Health and Medicine

**3.1 Skin, moisture, and salt**

You’ll be sweating **and** sitting in a humid environment where water never fully leaves your skin nor dries your clothes.

Pack:

- **Barrier or antifungal powder** for feet, groin, and skin folds.
- **Moisture barrier cream** if you’re prone to eczema or chafing.
- **Extra bandages** that won’t peel off easily in wet conditions.

Daily habits:

- Change socks if you’re using socks.
- Rinse your feet with clean water periodically if locals permit you access to cleaner basin corners.
- Dry your body with the cloth towel, then reapply light powder where you need.

**3.2 Eye and ear protection**

- **Ear protection** if you’re sensitive to noise. The hum is constant but at low to medium level; you can adjust gradually if you’re not using earplugs.
- **Basic eye drops** if the humidity irritates your eyes or if you find your eyes drying due to air not moving like it does outside.

**3.3 Hydration and salt balance**

Because you’re sweating constantly but not perceiving it as intensely warm “exercise heat,” you might **underestimate** water loss.

- **Carry a water container** you can refill from safe sources.
- Optional **salt tablets or rehydration salts** if you’re prone to heat exhaustion.
- Watch for signs: dizziness, headache, muscle fatigue.

**3.4 Local food and digestion**

You’ll likely eat:

- Algae-based products.
- Small fish from the basins.
- Possibly insect-based foods or protein from the local fauna.
- Grains/carbohydrates if they are cultivated from some small terrace or bulk imported via unknown trade channels.

If you’re from a world that has different gut flora:

- Start with **small portions**.
- Ask for cooked foods where you can see the food preparation clearly (if they permit you).
- Bring **generic anti-diarrheal and stomach-soothing remedies** you know are safe for you.

**3.5 Allergy awareness**

Insect and midge swarms are common near the lights:

- If you’re highly allergic to insect bites or had reactions in similar environments, bring:
  - Antihistamine.
  - Mild topical antihistamine or anti-inflammatory salve.
- Local insects might be strange to you—but likely not intensely venomous—just annoying.

**3.6 Sleep and “night”**

Sleeping here will be different:

- No natural light changes to tell you when to sleep.
- People may follow **shift rhythms** tied to work maintenance, rather than solar cycles.

To maintain sleep discipline:

- Set yourself a “day” length (e.g., 16–18 hours awake).
- Use **earplugs and eye mask** if the noise and light are bothersome.
- Consider **short, controlled naps** rather than long blocks of sleep if the locals cycle that way.

### 4. Practical Safety in the Room

**4.1 Floor and water**

- **Stone slabs** are polished and smooth from water and age.
- **Algae films** grow on lower edges of basins and channels.
- Wet stone + algae = **slipping hazard**.

General practices:

- Walk **slowly and testfully** when stepping near channels or basins.
- When stepping down into terraces or onto platforms, **use your non-dominant foot for exploratory testing**.
- If you fall into water, **it’s only shallow**—up to knee-deep at most. Just stand up carefully.

**4.2 Engine areas**

Engines are huge machines along the walls:

- Hot surface areas.
- Moving parts hidden inside.
- Metal edges that could be sharp.

**Rules:**

- Don’t lean on or touch **hot surfaces** unless told it’s safe.
- Don’t insert hands or tools into openings you’re not authorized to access.
- The engine hum is stable but not risk-free—**respect maintenance markers**.

**4.3 Maintenance crawlspaces and tunnels**

Under stone steps and low arches:

- Tight channels and culverts.
- Semi-maintained by locals.

**Don’t** explore these tunnels without:

- Local permission.
- A guide with maintenance experience.
- Full awareness of:

  - Where the water is flowing.
  - Whether you can get back out.
  - If there are entrapments or hidden shafts.

For you, as an outsider, **leave these to locals**.

**4.4 Crowding vs. emptiness**

The city is sparse, but the center basin and main platforms might be social hubs:

- Don’t assume you can go anywhere.
- The edges are more likely acceptable for “sitting alone.”
- Pick **out-of-the-way corners** if you want quiet solitude.

### 5. Money and Logistics

**5.1 What do you have?**

You’re a multiverse traveler, so assume you can bring **some** items:

- **Some small, precious tokens:**  
  - Tools that maintain better than what’s available locally.
  - Small precision instruments like tweezers, fine blades, or sealed needles.
  - Energy cells or compact fuel sources if compatible with their low-tech electricity.
- **Incredible fabrics:**
  - Lightweight textiles that **don’t stay wet** long, or that are **antibacterial and breathe**.
- **Structured knowledge:**  
  - You can’t show off “outside” tales but you can act as:

    - A better maintenance planner.
    - A healer with better sanitation knowledge.
    - A storyteller who frames **all metaphors inside** their own world.

**5.2 How to trade**

- Offer **services** more than strange objects:
  - Repairs of their clothing/metalwork.
  - Help with **understanding algae pattern**, fish health, or better filtration of their basins (if you’re trained).
  - Help with **recording their songs and stories**—a much safer “outside” gift than revealing technologies.

**5.3 What you might receive**

- **Local textiles:** simple but effective humidity-friendly clothes.
- **Food tokens:** small rations of fish, algae cakes, or preserved goods.
- **Access** to:
  - Better sleeping spots.
  - Use of their basins to wash or cool.
  - Learning maintenance work.

### 6. Social and Spiritual Role

**6.1 Don’t be “from outside”**

The safest role you can play is **wanderer from another district of the world**. This is:

- Technically not true (you’re from outside the room entirely).
- But semantically close for their cosmology.

This lets you explain:

- Slight difference in clothing.
- Different accent or dialect.
- Different practices and hygiene habits.

Not “Another world of sky and sun” but:

“I’m from a region where the light fades and the engine rhythm is slightly different.”

**6.2 Respect their myths**

Their “sacred” phenomena:

- **Central basin:** heart of the world, where water is gathered.
- **Engines:** constant presence of the world.
- **Ceiling:** their sky.

**Do**:

- Listen to their explanations of how world began in **their language**.
- Mirror **their** metaphors back—“Yes, the engine breathes,” “The world drinks from the basin,” etc.

**Don’t**:

- Argue that there’s a sun outside.
- Claim “you’ve seen bigger cities.”
- Talk about “open air” as literal reality.

**6.3 Offerings**

- If you’re staying a long time, ask **what offering is appropriate** before you use:
  - Their basin water.
  - Their moss or algae.
  - Their engine-maintenance rituals.

You might be asked for **work tribute**, **story tribute**, or **physical artifact**—be prepared to give according to their customs.

### 7. Survival and Long-Term Adaptation

If you’re staying **for longer than a few weeks:**

**7.1 Learn their day**

- **Cycle of sound:** Are there “times” when engine hum changes slightly?
- **Shift work:** When are basins maintained, when are fish harvested?
- **Community rhythms:** When are communal stories told?

Align your rhythms with theirs:

- Sleep when the community does.
- Work during their “daylight” of concentrated lights.

**7.2 Create your “anchor”**

Because you have no external horizon, you need **mental anchors**:

- An **item of home**: you don’t show it to everyone—just use it to remember yourself.
- **Daily rituals:** e.g., “When I wake, I do this; when I feel time slipping, I stand at this specific corner of the basin.”

**7.3 Integration roles**

If you integrate **deeply**, you might become:

- **Maintainer of story:** A recorder of their tales and songs.
- **Helper of the engines:** Work under the local engine keepers.
- **Guardian of water:** Involved in ensuring the purity or balance of the basin water.

These are **honorable but heavy roles**. Don’t take them unless you’re willing to stay long-term and be responsible.

**7.4 Leaving and aftermath**

When you leave:

- You **do not need** to tell them you’re going to another world.
- You can say:

  - “I return to my region.”
  - “The engine calls me back.”

Leave **as few** unbalanced ideas as possible:

- No objects of world-changing tech.
- No promises you can’t keep.
- Just leave them better off than you found them—healthier, cleaner, or richer in stories—if that’s ethically aligned with your presence.

### 8. Final Summation: The Good Way to Arrive

Here’s a simple **minimalist checklist** for a safe visit:

- **Clothing:**
  - Breathable, light, short.
  - Sandals or open water shoes with good grip.
- **Gear:**
  - Micro towel or cloth.
  - Waterproof pouch for small items.
  - Sealed notebook for slow timekeeping and field notes.
- **Health & Hygiene:**
  - Water container.
  - Salt tablets or rehydration if you’re prone to heat issues.
  - Anti-fungal powder/barrier cream.
  - Basic antihistamine and stomach remedies.
- **Social Prep:**
  - Decide **your story**: outsider region, not outside world.
  - Listen more than talk.
  - Don’t show off “outside” tech unless culturally transformed entirely.

**In the Place of Gathering Heat**, you are:

- Warm, but not burning.
- Wet, but not drowning.
- In a finite city of stone, water, and engines, where the **small** becomes the **world**.

Treat it with respect, stay mindful of time, humidity, and stories—and you can leave with your body intact and your mind enlarged by the slow, steady life of the room-city.
