To visit this place is to enter a living machine that thinks in water and breathes in wood.

If you go there as an ordinary human multiverse traveler, you are not a hero or a wizard. You are fragile, heavy-footed, and warm-blooded in a world that has been shaped for slow, cold, wet movement. That means preparation is less about “adventure gear” and more about *learning how to be quiet*—physically, socially, and mentally.

---

## 1. Understand What You’re Walking Into

### 1.1. Core Reality

This place is:

- A **self-contained water system** shaped like a huge bowl.
- Fully **built**, but built to *serve the flow of water*, not to dominate it.
- Resident-run: **inhabited and partially maintained by non-human creatures**.

It’s not a ruin or a museum. It’s in **active use**, by its own logic of use.

Your presence is not necessarily *unwanted*, but it is *heavy*:
- you weigh more than almost anything that lives there;
- you move more than almost anything that lives there;
- you produce *heat*, *sweat*, *noise*, and *micro-trauma* to the wood.

Everything you do should be designed to minimize how much you rewrite its environment.

### 1.2. Time Scale and Patience

Expect to stay longer than you think. “Visiting” this place is not “checking out a cathedral.” It’s more like staying in a monastery library whose books are made of water and wood.

- **First half-day to day:** Just getting your body and mind adjusted to the humidity, soundscape, and low light.
- **Days 2–3:** Starting to notice *how* things move: where the water flows strongest, where the creatures gather and hide.
- **Beyond 3 days:** The “seasons of moisture” begin to make sense—periods when the air is thick with droplets, and when it’s briefly “drier.”

Bring patience and time, or don’t come.

### 1.3. Ethical Baseline: You Are a Guest, Not Staff

You are not there to “fix,” “optimize,” or “improve” anything.  
You are there to **listen**.

- You *may* gently help if something is clearly out of balance (for example, if you *gradually* realize a small stone is misdirecting water in a harmful way), but *only* after observing and only *after* checking if a creature is already handling it.
- There are creatures living *inside* the wood. That means:
  - Never drilling or hammering.
  - Never “washing” the wood with strong solutions or scrubbing.
  - Never “treating” anything with chemicals you brought from elsewhere.

Leave *management* to the water and to its long-term inhabitants.

## 2. Pre-Travel Preparation: Gear and Mindset

### 2.1. Clothing

You need garments that are:

1. **As close to silent as possible.**  
   - No scratchy synthetic fabrics that hiss when you move.
   - Avoid heavy outer layers with noisy zippers and Velcro.

2. **Soft and form-fitting but not constricting.**  
   - Purpose: make you small *across* space, not giant and billowing.
   - Loose sleeves are dangerous if they dangle near troughs or chimes.

3. **Very fast-drying and moisture-resistant where it matters.**  
   - Light underlayer: merino wool or thin, synthetic base layer that doesn’t stay cold when wet.
   - A shell or overshirt in treated natural linen or thin wool—something that doesn’t get “crunchy” when damp.

4. **Neutral and low-contrast.**  
   - Earth tones: mossy, bark-like, pale stone-like.
   - Not camouflage, but *not* bright or high-visibility.

5. **Head covering:**  
   - A light, close-fitting hood or cap is useful if condensation droplets fall from the ceiling.

**Colors**:  
Avoid pure white or anything reflective that can be seen by creatures that might be stressed by sudden “sunlight.” Aim for grays, greens, and browns that *fade into the wood*.

### 2.2. Footwear

This is crucial.

**Wrong shoes:**
- Heavy boots that “clump.”
- Thick, hard soles that transmit every step as a shock.
- Open shoes where the foot can slip or make *slapping* noises.

**Right shoes:**

- **Soft-soled moccasins or water shoes**  
  - Ideally made of thin leather or rubberized fabric that conforms to the surface of the wood.
  - Soles thin enough to feel unevenness but tough enough not to be pierced by splinters or bronze shards.

- **Grip without noise:**  
  - You need *grip on damp wood* but without “squishing” or squeaking.
  - Ensure soles don’t “stick” with a loud noise when you lift your foot.

### 2.3. Lighting and Vision

You are entering a **dim, steady-glowing environment**. Your eyes must adapt, and your tools must not destroy that.

- **Main tool:** your eyes.  
  - Train your night vision in advance if you can.
- **No bright flashlights or headlamps.**  
  - Even a “moderate” flashlight in our world is likely a sunburst to many creatures there.
  - If you must carry a light source:
    - Use it rarely, *on its lowest setting*, for *very* short glances.
    - Shield it with your hand or fabric; filter it to a warm, amber, low intensity.

- **Glow markers:**  
  - If you need to mark a path, use *very faint* luminous dots or thread that *under* compete the moss’s glow, not outshine it.

Always **ask your eyes** before you “reach” for your flashlight.

### 2.4. Protection From Moisture

You will get *as wet from air and mist as you do from accidental water contact.*

- **Skin-level:**  
  - Apply a thin, neutral barrier cream or oil to protect against skin cracking and prolonged dampness.
    - Choose something scentless and as *biologically basic* as possible, to minimize strange chemical signals in the water.

- **Personal dryness:**  
  - You may rarely need “dry” clothes.  
  - Instead of bulky full changes, bring:
    - A thin spare layer.
    - A small, highly absorbent cloth or towel to pat yourself dry when needed.

### 2.5. Medical and Basic Body Supplies

Think in terms of **self-resilience**, not “rescue.”

- **Soothes for dampness and skin:**  
  - Simple antifungal ointment or salve for feet and skin folds.
  - Mild lip balm.

- **Basic repair kit:**  
  - For *your* clothes and equipment, not the wood.
    - Needle and thread.
    - A few small pieces of fabric or leather for quick patches.
    - No strong adhesives that smell or off-gas.

- **Support for knee/ankle:**  
  - Light compression wraps or bandages.
  - You’ll kneel and sit on wood a lot.

- **Anti-inflammatory and pain relief:**  
  - For joints and muscles from all the kneeling and careful walking.

- **Hydration and nutrition:**  
  - Bring food *you* eat. Don’t *ever* dump crumbs or food into the water.
  - Avoid foods that crumble badly or are strong-smelling.
    - Think sealed, low-crumb, low-odor rations.
    - No citrus, strong spices, or alcohol—strong scents are *noise* in this world.

## 3. Mental and Social Preparation

### 3.1. Practice Quiet *Before* You Go

You’ll need to **relearn how to move**.

Before departure:

- **Walk barefoot or in soft shoes on varied floors**,  
  focusing on making no sound.
- **Practice moving from sitting to standing on a floor**  
  without using hands, if your body can do that.  
  (Avoids grabbing fixtures or beams.)

- **Practice breathing slower:**  
  - Two practices to expose:
    - Breath-counting in 4–6–6 pattern:
      - Inhale 4, hold 6, exhale 6—very *quiet*.
  - You will not sustain this perfectly in the bowl, but practicing makes *normal* breathing *less loud*.

### 3.2. Deconditioning Your Normal Sounds

Most of us are loud “out of the box”:

- We **hum**, **fidget**, **clear our throat**, **tap fingers**, **click pens**, etc.
- Your job: *notice* these habits *before* you arrive.
- Train yourself to find **non-sounding ways** to release restlessness:
  - Flexing and relaxing toes and feet instead of tapping fingers.
  - Slow, controlled sighs instead of loud coughs.

### 3.3. Emotional Regulation

You will feel *urged* to:
- Clean something.
- Straighten something.
- “Improve” flow or “fix” a visible flaw.

**Do nothing engineering-ish** unless you **fully* understand the system’s language *and* you see an *immediate*, non-disruptive help you can give.**

If you feel *frustrated*, *angry*, or *impatient*, use **stillness**:
- Sit down.
- Listen to the water.
- Let *its* rhythm slow your heart.

## 4. How to Move Inside the Bowl

### 4.1. Entering the Space

When you first arrive, assume the **first hour** is dedicated just to *not colliding with anything* and learning the *shape of the room*.

- Stand **still** just inside the threshold.  
  - Let your eyes adjust.
  - Do *not* walk forward immediately.
- **Breathe**:
  - Notice your breath *behind* the sound of the bowl.
- **Scan *without* turning your head quickly:**  
  - Look for *edges of troughs*, *steps*, *low hanging chains/chimes*, *fragile structures*.

Only *then* begin to move.

### 4.2. Walking

You want *no thumps*.

- **Heel-to-toe with extreme care**  
  - Not *tiptoes* which can be unstable and noisy if you wobble.
  - **Gentle heel contact**, then **entire foot** *rolls* onto the ground.
- **Avoid**:
  - Hard heels slamming down.
  - Toes dragging (“scuffing”).
  - Sudden stops.

- **Center of gravity:**  
  - Keep your core engaged so you don’t “over-commit” and have to wrench yourself back noisily.

### 4.3. Kneeling and Seating

Half of your time will be spent **close to the troughs**, **watching**.

Practice **silent kneeling**:

- **Single-knee down first**  
  - Lower yourself slowly, using muscle, not dropping.
- **Then second knee**,  
  - Or sit cross-legged or sideways—whatever keeps you stable without *grabbing* anything.

Be *extremely* careful **near the edges**:
- A sudden slip and **you** don’t want to be the one “rebalancing” the flow by crashing into the troughs.

### 4.4. On the “Steps” and Platforms

The wooden steps between ring-troughs are **intermediate spaces**.

- **Never** run or “skip down.”
- **One step at a time**, foot *fully* on the platform before weight.
- Use **hands** only *lightly* on the walls if balance is needed—  
  - If you feel *soft* or *moving* wood, *let go*; something might live inside.

### 4.5. Avoiding the “Ceiling Stars”

The bronze **chains and hooks** are *functional*; they are not “decoration” to be touched.

- They **ring**:
  - Your job is to pass *under* them without sound.
- **Duck or lower your head** *before* you rise near them.
- **Never**:
  - Reach up to touch “to see what happens.”
  - “Adjust” anything hanging from the ceiling.

## 5. Interaction With the System Itself

### 5.1. The Wood

Treat **all wood** as **skin of the machine**.

- **Don’t**:
  - Carve into it.
  - “Test” it by stabbing or poking.
  - Sand or “smooth” parts.
  - Nail or screw anything into it.

- **Do**:
  - Observe *patterns*:
    - Where it is darkest and smoothst.
    - Where you see *scores*, notches, or worn “paths.”
  - Notice *how* the wood vibrates under your hand:
    - Sometimes a “grumble” means *your* weight or *your* step is too loud.

### 5.2. The Water Troughs and Channels

The **troughs** are the **arteries of the world**.

**Rule of thumb:** *never disturb the water’s established paths.*

- **Don’t**:
  - Block or dam a channel, “just to see.”
  - “Wash” anything in the water.
  - Drop *anything* into the troughs (except what *may* be appropriate *after* deep understanding and *very* gently).
  - Touch water *with* anything but very clean hands and *very* mindfully.

- **You** *may* gently:
  - **Scoop water** without *urging* flow.
    - Use your **hands** or *if* necessary *and* custom-designed for non-intrusion:
      - A small bowl-shaped container that closely matches your hands in shape and volume.
    - Move *with* existing flow lines; don’t “stop” the water just to get more.

- **Observing water flow:**
  - Watch *where* the water narrows and speeds up.
  - Where it *broadens* and slows.
  - How creatures *use* these edges.

### 5.3. Mosses, Vines, and Luminescent Organisms

All of these **semi-plant** elements are **core functional parts**, not decor.

- **No**:
  - “Pruning” or “trimming.”
  - “Collecting” samples.
  - “Replanting” or “thinning.”

- **Yes**:
  - Observe *which* rings have brighter moss.
  - Which *vine* leaves have “drip tips” and *where* they drip.
  - Notice *patterns* in the glow:
    - Brightening when *you* breathe near them?
    - Dimming when *you* move?

### 5.4. The Center Dish Platform

This is **the heart of the bowl**.

- **Approach with extra care**:
  - Only once you are *very* calm and *very* quiet.
- **If you place water there**:
  - Use *water from the troughs*, not *your own*.
  - Place *very* small amounts.
  - Watch *how* the bowl’s low hum changes:
    - Does it *deepen*?
    - Does it *tune* more?

This *is* a “sacred experiment,” but it *must* be done mindfully.

## 6. Encountering the Non-Human Inhabitants

The **creatures** are *how* the machine runs. *You* are not.

### 6.1. Translucent “Eel-Like” Water Creatures

- **What they are to the system:**
  - Navigators of flow channels and edges.
  - Predators of *smaller* glowing cloud-like “plankton.”
- **How to behave with them:**
  - **Do**:
    - Observe from a distance, usually *kneeling or sitting* at a safe distance from the edge.
    - Notice *when* they cluster and *when* they “stream” along channels.
    - See *if* different “individuals” or “groups” prefer particular rings.
  - **Don’t**:
    - Try to catch or “pet” them.
    - Place anything *in* water as “offerings” (unless you *truly* understand the ecosystem, which you probably won’t in your first visit).

### 6.2. Pale “Glass-Like” Insects and Spiders

- **Insects**:
  - Climb *up* to drink *condensation* on walls and beams.
  - Their **footsteps** make *tiny chime* sounds when they climb *chains*.
  - **Don’t**:
    - Glare at them with direct light.
    - “Brush” them away if you feel “creeped out.”
  - **If one** lands on you:
    - Often *being still* is enough for it to move on.
    - If you *must* move:
      - Gentle *twitch* or *slow* hand guidance to the edge of your garment, so they can *step* off.

- **Spiders**:
  - Their **webs** catch **condensation droplets**, functioning like “chandeliers.”
  - **Don’t**:
    - Break webs.
    - “Clear” webs from spaces “to see better.”
  - Give **space**:
    - If you see *fine* strands of web or dewy droplets, *go around*.

### 6.3. Water-Birds (Scaled, “Stone-Like” Songbirds)

These are **the most “vocal”** beings you’ll meet.

- **What they do:**
  - Crawl along trough edges.
  - “Fish” translucent creatures from the water.
  - Sing *sharp, metallic* chirps that cut *through* the water murmur.

- **Your behavior:**
  - **Distance**:  
    - Give them *space*—especially when they are *near* their nesting cavities under the troughs.
  - **Movement**:
    - If *you* are moving, and *one* begins chirping rapidly:

    - **Stop**.
    - Let **them** move *away* from you.
    - Then *only* proceed *very* slowly.

They are **“living alarms”** for the whole system.

### 6.4. Moss Wyrms and Other “Hidden” Life

You **won’t** see *moss wyrms* directly often (unless the wood *gives*).

- **Implication**:  
  - **Strange** vibrations or *soft* tremors from *inside* the wood might be them.
  - **Don’t react aggressively or destructively**:
    - No hitting “at” the wood to “scare them off.”
    - No “leaning” hard on “hollow” sections.

You **are** sharing *their* home from the inside out.

## 7. Long Visits: Living in the Bowl

### 7.1. Timekeeping Without Clocks

You probably won’t want *ticking* or *digital chirping*. Instead:

- Use **changes** in the bowl:
  - “Cycle of drips”: is *this* period of more condensation drip?
  - “Season of dryness”: does *this* feel *slightly* less damp?

You might associate **tasks** with *phases*:
- “I only move between rings when the lower-ring air feels *this* cool.”
- “I only *sit* in the center and listen *after* N ‘cycles’ of flow.”

### 7.2. Waste and Human Mess

This is **critical**:

- **No** bodily waste or **strong** human scents in *any* direct contact with:
  - **Water** (including *mist* that might be *near*).
  - **Wood**.
- **Yes**:
  - Use a **self-contained**, sealed **human waste system** you brought *and* take *all* of it out with you.
    - No *dump*, no “composting,” no *burial* in the structure.
  - For **food waste**:
    - **Everything** you eat is *sealed* out again.

You *cannot* say “it’s just a little” here.  
The scale *magnifies* “small” human actions.

### 7.3. Sleeping

Sleeping arrangements **must** be *minimal* and *non-invasive*.

- **No**:
  - “Camping” pads that *stick* noisily to wood.
  - “Chairs” or “hammocks” attached with nails, hooks, or pressure to *its* structures.

- **Better**:
  - Thin ground pad that *compresses* but doesn’t “squish.”
  - Positioned *not* blocking walkways:
    - Choose *flat*, already *open* spaces of floorboard.
    - **Avoid**:
      - Directly under *chains*.
      - *Directly* under the lowest troughs where *hum* is strongest, *unless* you want deep sound for sleep and *know* it won’t disturb *you*.

Your **every posture** as you sleep should *be* as if the wood *already* existed *before* you.

### 7.4. Eating and Drinking

Your **food and water** should *not* enter *their* water.

- **You** drink:
  - From sealed personal containers *you* brought and **clean** outside the bowl.
    - If you *must* use *their* water:
      - Boil or filter *before* bringing *your* container into the bowl.
    - **Never** place *your* container *in* the trough to *fill*.

- **You** eat:
  - Over a container that *catches* crumbs:
    - **All** food *remains* are *removed* physically.
  - You *never* “rinse” dishes or hands *in* the troughs.

## 8. Emotional and Spiritual Preparation: Why You Are Going There

You are not going “just” to see a cool water machine.

### 8.1. Ask Yourself: *Why*?

This place *calls* to a particular human:

- The **engineer** who wants to *watch* water without forcing it.
- The **monastic** who wants to *listen* to a system that is not human.
- The **artist** who wants to see *boundaries* between *grown and made* dissolve.

But *don’t* assume:
- That you will **understand** it.
- That *it* needs you.

### 8.2. Meditation Before Arriving

Prepare a **personal intention**:

- Examples:
  - “I will go to learn *from* water, not *about* it.”
  - “I will not *own* this experience; I will *release* it when I leave.”
  - “I will not fix *anything* that I do not *understand*.”

Write **two words** on a card and leave *it* back home—
- “**Listen**.”
- “**Let be.**”

## 9. Leaving the Bowl: Taking Care Not to Take Anything Else

You *leave* changed, but:

**Do not take:**

- **Water** samples.
- **Creatures**.
- **Fragments of wood, moss, vines, or chimes.**

You *may* take:

- **Drawings**, **notes in words**, **mental images.**
- **Emotional memories**:  
  - The sound of the low hum.
  - The pattern of *back* dragonflies *sleep* on *bronze*.
  - How *small* your *step* feels *inside* a *hamlet-sized bowl.*

These **cannot** damage *it* 
and *they* **will** change *you*.

### 9.1. Final Checks Before Departure

- **Have you disturbed *any* stone or wooden part of a channel?**
  - If *yes*, but *only* in the course of *safe movement*—and only *if you are sure*—
    - Return *anything* moved *very* slowly to *near* its original position.
    - *If* you *aren’t* sure:
      - **Leave** it as you found it.
      - *Don’t* “second-guess.”

- **Have you *leaned* hard *on* any *curving* wall section?
  - Make *sure* you have *not* cracked *or* deeply bruised *wood* by *shifting* *your* weight *too* heavily *at* once *and* knocked *loose* a *creature* *home* inside.

- **Have you *any* objects left *behind*?**
  - *Even* a *tiny* *clip*, *label* or *fabric* thread can be *foreign* noise in *this* world.

*Everything* you brought *with* you should go *back* with you.
