### I. Before you go: Why Wraithstone?

You don’t “end up” in Wraithstone by accident. It’s not the kind of place that accidentally happens; you either deliberately crack it or you fall into a crack in everything else.

The first question is *why you’re going there*.

Possible reasons:

- **Refuge**  
  You’re chased from somewhere else (empires, debtors, gods, yourself), and you’ve heard there is a mountain where things are buried and few ask questions.

- **Research / Pilgrimage**  
  You’ve heard rumors of Wraithstone:  
  - a stone that remembers;  
  - a mountain that breathes and dreams;  
  - a world where water flows in circles and time runs in loops instead of lines.  
  You want to see what that “living” stone actually means.

- **Contract / Work**  
  There are always industries in the pipes: engineers, pipe-smiths, “fluctors” (fluid-flow experts), leeches, cartographers, “leak-weavers” who claim they can talk to water pressure.  
  Someone might hire you:  
  - “Go map the new spur line.”  
  - “Find out where this pressure spike is coming from.”  
  - “Retrieve a sample of the stone at X level.”

- **Artifact / Myth**  
  You heard there is a “name-cloud in the sky” and you want to see if words float up there, or if names have weight.

Whatever your reason, be *honest* with yourself about why you’re going in. Honesty matters later, when the stone is listening.

### II. Mindset: Prepare to live inside a body

You are not going to a country or even a planet. You are going into a body that wears stone as skin and pipes as veins.

You should adopt:

- **Non-claustrophobic but cautious spatial awareness**
  - You will be enclosed, but the scale is huge.  
  - Think “inside of a continent-sized heart” rather than “trapped in a box”.
  - Learn to tolerate being *circled by stone*, at all times.

- **Humidity-tolerant**
  - You will never be dry.  
  - If you can’t stand always being slightly damp, reconsider.

- **A low-grade animism**
  - Treat pipe as circulatory, stone as flesh, water as messenger.  
  - Don’t expect literal consciousness, but expect patterns that *feel* intentional—because in long, deep cycles, they are.

- **Sensitivity to rhythm**
  - Wraithstone runs on cycles of heat and flow and drip.  
  - If you can’t pay attention to patterns (pressure changes, water sounds, warm/cool breaths of stone), you will miss danger signs.

### III. Physical and sartorial preparation

#### 1. Clothing: You vs. the damp

You need to be:

- **Rust-proofed**
- **Mold-resistant**
- **Traction-capable**
- **Not-clammy**

Practical:

- **Inner layers:**  
  - Synthetic wicking base layer or very thin wool.  
  - Something that dries quickly but can handle constant dampness without chafing.

- **Outer layer:**  
  - Tightly woven, rust-resistant fabric (no random exposed iron threads).  
  - Lightly waterproof or at least water-resistant, but technically breathable (you’ll lose this battle eventually).  
  - Neutral color is not for “stealth”; it’s for not showing every rust stain and mold smear.

- **Footgear:**  
  - High-traction, waterproof boots or well-fitting sealed sandals.  
  - You’ll walk on wet moss, stone, uneven metal, slick algae.  
  - Expect to replace them frequently; mold is relentless.

- **Headgear and hands:**  
  - A hood or cap that doesn’t block your view but keeps rain/condensation off your scalp and out of your eyes.  
  - Gloves with grip, for climbing pipes or stone, but thin enough to feel things.

You don’t need “mountaineering gear” in the classical alpine sense; you need *industrial cave-climbing with damp-proof* gear.

#### 2. Skin and body care

- **Anti-fungal treatments:**  
  Bring or prepare to obtain locally:  
  - A powder/cream or oil that stabilizes skin microbiome.  
  - Wraithstone is full of spores. You will be colonized.

- **Hydration and electrolytes:**  
  - You will sweat constantly but simultaneously feel like you’re breathing water.  
  - You still need to drink; water from the pipes *can be drunk* but not always safely.

- **Wound protection:**  
  - Any open cut is a future invitation for mold, slime, or strange infection.  
  - Seal cuts properly, keep them clean, dry them if at all possible even if “dry” is only relative.
