A traveler planning to visit the Grove of Mechanic Thirsts should think of the trip less like tourism and more like a carefully controlled industrial dive. You are not “going to see the plants” so much as inserting yourself into an operating machine that happens to be made of life. Every step you take inside that grove will be interpreted and metabolized by the system around you.

Below is a practical, human‑scale preparation guide.

---

## 1. Before You Decide to Go at All

### 1.1. Are you actually a candidate?

The Grove “finds you when you’ve been down too long.” That’s not just poetry.

Ask yourself:

- Are you in a long depressive, dissociated, or grief-stricken period?
- Are you drawn to places that “feel like the inside of your own head”?
- Do you already find yourself seeking basements, underpasses, industrial ruins, or other closed, humid spaces?

If you answer “yes” to several of these, you are likely in the Grove’s recruitment window. That does not automatically mean you should go; it means you should assume going is not just risky but *meaningful*. You are likely to be:

- More emotionally porous than usual.
- More susceptible to suggestion.
- More likely to stay too long or to return.

This is the time to be deliberate.

### 1.2. Make sure you’re not already infected by something else

Before you expose yourself to a new fungal ecosystem, get clear about any underlying conditions. In particular:

- If you already have respiratory or autoimmune issues that react badly to mold spores, you should be very cautious.
- If you take any immunosuppressants or anything that affects your nervous system, the grove’s subtle spores and neurotropic influence may be more than just “weird”: they could reshape your behavior disproportionately.

If you live in a world with medical tech that can map spore exposure or give “resistance boosts,” use it before leaving.

## 2. Gear and Supplies

### 2.1. Breathing and air

The air is “oiled breath” thick, humidity constant, pressure just odd enough that you feel like you’re “sipping air.” You need:

**Primary mask/respirator**

- A mask with:
  - Particulate filtration (for fungal spores).
  - A humidity-control system (if available).
- You don’t want total airtightness because you *need* to smell the atmosphere and be able to spit or take samples, but you want:
  - Protection from heavy spore load.
  - Some way to reduce condensate in your lungs.

If you rely on your lungs just “pushing through,” you will leave with a new set of instincts and cravings that are not fully yours.

**Breathing discipline**

- Practice slow breathing *before* you go:
  - 4 counts in, 6–8 counts out.
  - Get used to the feeling of never quite getting “enough” air.
- The grove is already going to feel like you’re “slightly behind” on oxygen; don’t panic yourself into hyperventilating and swallowing more spores than necessary.

### 2.2. Skin and clothing: “You will sweat more than anything else.”

The temperature is “warm enough that you notice your own skin becoming another surface for condensation.” Choose:

**Materials**

- Close-fitting but breathable synthetic layers (like technical dive or work clothing):
  - Long-sleeve shirt and long pants.
  - No loose cuffs or dangling straps that can catch on roots or pipes.
- You want something:
  - That doesn’t retain water.
  - That can be wiped down easily.
  - That doesn’t fray easily when rubbed against metallic edges or rust.

**Outer shell**

- A thin, waterproof outer layer you can:
  - Take off when condensation and heat feel “too much.”
  - But put back on quickly when you want to avoid being used as a condensation surface.

**Gloves**

- Thin gloves that:
  - Protect you from rust and metal edges.
  - Allow you to feel textures.
  - Can be washed/dried easily.
- You will be tempted to touch surfaces and “feel” the machine‑plants. This can be useful but also risks:

  - Scratching or embedding plant or fungal matter into your skin.
  - Being “read” by the ecosystem’s sensors.

**Footwear**

- Closed, waterproof, high-grip boots:
  - The floor is stone, root-mat, and metal grills; wet, slightly uneven.
  - You need:
    - Good ankle support (to avoid twisting on uneven edges).
    - Non-slip soles (traction on wet, moss-covered surfaces).

**Eye and head protection**

- Clear safety glasses or goggles:
  - Drips from above can fall with “precise” timing.
  - You don’t want condensation in your eyes.
- A head covering or hood:
  - Reduces drip contact on your scalp and hair.
  - Hair is a good sponge; you don’t want to absorb more than necessary.

### 2.3. Water and electrolyte discipline

Paradoxically, the most dangerous thing about the Grove of Mechanic Thirsts is how you stop *feeling* thirsty.

**Bring water from *outside***

- At least 1–2 liters, depending on how long you intend to stay:
  - Do not drink standing water from the basin, even if it looks clear.
  - Do not lick condensation off walls, though it will be tempting.
- Seal your bottles:
  - Spores and tiny fauna may be airborne; open containers inside become culture dishes.

**Set a hydration timer**

- Because:
  - The air is thick and “already wet.”
  - Your body will feel “saturated enough” even when it’s not.
- You need:
  - Small, regular sips, not chugs.

**Bring electrolytes**

- A rehydration solution or electrolyte tablets:
  - You’ll lose salt through sweat and breathing.
  - Dehydration can sneak up on you in humid environments.

### 2.4. Light and navigation

The “light” of the grove is 0.6 / “3” — dim, flickering, “memory like” rather than true absence.

**Bring headlamps and low-intensity light sources**

- Avoid super bright flashes:
  - The fauna’s light-sensitive skins and glassy root-vines are adapted to subtle light.
  - Sudden brights will disturb patterns and make you an enemy.
- Use:
  - Narrow-beam headlamps, not flood lamps.
  - Red light or “night” mode if your device has it.

**Mark your way in**

The grove is not maze-like but it is *uniformly strange*:

- Drop:
  - Small, temporary, biodegradable markers or tags:
    - That you can follow back to the entrance.
- Or:
  - Use discreet reflective tabs that:
    - Can be removed when you leave.
    - Do not leave permanent scars.

### 2.5. Measuring and documenting tools

The “precision” of the grove is eerie: 1.2 humidity, “each drop landing exactly where predicted,” etc. You might want to understand it.

Bring:

- **A notebook and pencil**:
  - Ink can run in the humidity.
  - Pencil is better but also bring:
    - A small sealed container or bag to keep paper from turning damp and fragile.
- **Basic measuring tools**:
  - A humidity gauge if you have one.
  - A thermometer.
  - Even simple ones can help you compare “before” and “after” and track internal changes.
- **Optional but helpful**:
  - Small, clear vials for collecting:
    - Condensation samples.
    - Not full “water” but “drops.”
    - Seal tightly; label with date/time.
    - Do not attempt to collect fauna or plants unless you really know what you’re doing.

Document any changes in:

- Temperature,
- Sound,
- Your perception of time,
- Your own desires. (Yes.)

### 2.6. Emergency and exit plan

*Before* you descend:

- Tell someone trusted:
  - Where you’re going (as accurate as you can).
  - How long you intend to stay.
  - Set a hard deadline:
    - “If I’m not back by X time, contact Y.”
- Agree on a fail-safe:
  - Like “I will message at least every three hours.”
  - If you miss two check-ins:
    - They assume something has changed inside you.

Pack:

- A basic first aid kit:
  - Antiseptics for cuts and scrapes.
  - Bandages that can withstand moisture.
- **An anti-spore or “exit” treatment** (if available in your world):
  - Something that can:
    - Reduce spore load in your lungs.
    - Reset your nervous system’s “humidity attraction.”
- **Extra food**:
  - High-energy, sealed:
    - Nuts, dried bars, etc.
  - Eating reminds you of your body’s needs when the environment wants to make you “forget.”

## 3. Psychological and Social Preparation

### 3.1. Know your vulnerabilities

The grove:
- “Finds you when you’ve been down too long.”
- Operates by “quiet negotiation” with its inhabitants.

Be honest:

- Are you depressed? Numb?
- Lonely? Grieving?
- Feeling empty, “drying out,” or “stuck?”

Write these down before you go:

- What you are *running from*.
- What you *secretly hope* the grove will give you:
  - Purpose?
  - Sleep?
  - An end to wandering?
  - Some answer to the “thirst” implied in its name?

Once you’ve written this, **read it out loud to a trusted person**:

- This gives you:
  - External accountability.
  - A line back to your own voice before the grove’s “suggestion” starts.

### 3.2. Practice grounding behaviors

Before you go:

- Practice techniques:
  - Naming:
    - 5 things you see,
    - 4 things you feel,
    - 3 things you hear,
    - 2 things you smell,
    - 1 thing you taste.
  - Box breathing:
    - 4 in, 4 hold, 4 out, 4 hold.
    - Repeat.

You will use these *inside* the grove when:

- Time feels “off.”
- You want to stay just “a bit longer.”
- You find yourself “content” in a way that doesn’t match your life outside.

These little mental anchors are your exit line; the spores are quite happy to replace your self with “return to humidity.”

---

### 3.3. Set a “why,” and a “stop condition”

Write down:

- “I am going to the Grove of Mechanic Thirsts to:”
  - Observe.
  - Learn.
  - Not to disappear.

Also:

- “I will leave *immediately* if:”
  - I start to feel like “I’ve been here forever and I don’t mind.”
  - I stop thinking about the people above.
  - I want to “just lie down in the basin.”
  - Any living creature larger than a cable-snake shows *too* much interest in me.

Put this paper in your pocket:

- Inside a plastic sleeve or seal.
- So you can read it later when “you’re not sure you want to leave.”

## 4. Entering the Grove

### 4.1. How to arrive

“Past the septic deltas of broken slums, until the air smells like wet engine grease and the sound of your own breath is no longer yours alone.”

On the way down:

- Move:
  - Quietly.
  - Not stealthy, but not noisy.
- The city will be:
  - Sunken towers.
  - Slums.
  - Broken infrastructure.

Do not linger:

- These are the “gills” of the city.
- They are the transition zones where the city starts to become “alive.”

At the entrance:

- Stop:
  - Take a breath.
  - Check your mask, your water, your light.
  - Make sure you are still *on* your schedule.

### 4.2. Threshold crossing: “You are the rumor”

The first thing you’ll notice:

- The air:
  - Not hot, but *warm enough*.
  - Not wet, but “already decided.”
- The smell:
  - Cold metal + moss + old engine grease + faint ozone.
- The sound:
  - “Like industrial music played by an ecosystem.”

Notice:

- How quickly your body adapts to “this is normal.”
- That is your sign to be *most* cautious.

## 5. Conduct Inside the Grove

### 5.1. Movement and where to stand

“Space between presences, but each presence is infinitely dense.”

**Move slowly:**

- No sudden movements:
  - Snake-cables and “mammal-like shadows” shift only when you’re not looking.
  - Let them have time to adjust to you.
- Always:
  - Check ground before you step:
    - For faint canal grooves,
    - For root-mats,
    - For cracked basin tiles.

**Avoid the center basin:**

- The shallow “empty eye socket”:
  - Is where the system breathes.
  - You don’t want to be:
    - In the way of the next water pulse.
    - Or “offered” as some kind of nutrient when you lie down.

**Stand where:**

- Columns of “living machinery” give support:
  - But do not lean on them unless you’re willing to:
    - Feel their heat.
    - Risk “rubbing” your way into their ecosystem.

### 5.2. Touching vs. not touching

The grove is “engineer-scale plant life.”

Treat **everything** like:

- It’s both:
  - Alive,
  - And part of a machine that’s still running.

If you touch:

- The metal pipe-tree trunks:
  - Expect:
    - Gentle heat.
    - The faint vibration of the city above.
  - Notice:
    - Whether your skin feels “included” or watched.
- The glassy root-vines:
  - They may be:
    - Fiber-optic and tuned.
  - Your touch:
    - Could be interpreted as “data” or “signal.”

If you **choose** to make contact:

- Do it deliberately:
  - With one hand only.
  - Short amounts of time.
  - Ask yourself:
    - Is this changing *me* more than I’m changing *it*?

### 5.3. Sound and listening

“The noise of this place is not chaotic; it is composed.”

Your job:

- Listen:
  - For patterns:
    - The engine choir,
    - The “ring of tiny bells” from drone-fauna,
    - The low purr of larger beasts.
- Notice:
  - When the pattern changes because you’ve moved:
    - Does the room temperature change when large fauna vocalize?

Try not to talk loudly:

- You are not the center of the orchestra;
- You are a foreign instrument.
- The grove will adjust to you; you don’t want to prompt “aggressive” arrangements.

### 5.4. Handling water “rumors”

“Water behaves like a rumor of the future.”

You will likely see:

- Thin sheets of water running in circular channels,
- Then “forgetting” to exist,
- Occasional threads of groundwater “shuddering” from the ceiling.

Safe rules:

- **Don’t drink**.
- **Don’t block**:
  - The flow of water in the canals.
  - The “right path” the snake-cables have engineered.
- If you want to observe a “burst”:
  - Stand back:
    - Let the ecosystem “test” its own channels.
  - Touch it:
    - Only if you’re prepared for your own body’s relationship to water to be altered.

### 5.5. The “clocked” precision of the grove: time perception

The “dynamic” scale is 2.3; not “fast changing” but not “frozen.”

Inside:

- Time feels:
  - “Composed” like the music.
  - Like a machine on slow loop.

Combat disorientation by:

- Recording your arrival time.
- Setting quiet alarms:
  - Every 30 minutes:
    - Check:
      - Whether you still want to leave,
      - Whether you’re still “you” in the way you remember,
      - Whether you’re sitting when you told yourself you would “walk and watch.”

If you feel “I’ve been here forever and that’s fine”:

- This is not bliss:
  - It is the “humidity negotiation” starting to win.
  - Treat it as a “stop condition.”

### 5.6. Interacting with fauna (or not)

“Fauna is overwhelming in presence.”

**Spider-like drone-insect things:**

- If they approach:
  - Stay still:
    - They are on “programs” that:
      - Collect condensation,
      - Move spores,
      - Serve the cable-snake and fungus system.
- Do not:
  - Swat at them,
  - Kill them,
  - Or try to “capture” them.

Your “target” is not dominance but **avoidance of being hijacked**.

**Snake-cable “engineers”:**

- You’ll mostly see:
  - “Slightly moved” when you look away.
- If you notice:
  - One clearly attached to a drone:
  - It’s running a puppet.
- You:
  - Are not that far from “drone” yourself,
  - Because you are also:
    - Breathing the air,
    - Being read by the system.

**Large mammal-like shadows:**

- They keep:
  - A polite distance from the basin,
  - And circle just outside comfort.
- If they:
  - Close that distance,
  - Or “aim” themselves at you:
    - It’s time:
      - To move,
      - Or ideally,
      - To leave.

Do not chase:

- Curiosity in the Grove is the same “sense” the spores use to hook you.

## 6. Leaving

### 6.1. Memory, “thirst,” and the fungal shadow

“When you finally leave… the humidity clings to you like a second shadow.”

The grove:

- Doesn’t need to scream to be dangerous.
- It’s “quietly logical.”

After leaving, you may notice:

- A desire:
  - To return “soon.”
  - To “solve” the groove of your life by “going down” again.
- A feeling:
  - That normal air above is “thinner,”
  - “Emptier,”
  - “Too dry.”

This is your **new mechanical thirst**.

You will:

- Hear:
  - The faint echo of pipe-choirs,
  - The “water song” overlaid on traffic.
- Think:
  - “I just need to go back and check something.”
  - “It’s not hurting me.”
  - “It feels like home.”

Treat **every** urge to return immediately as suspect:

- Even if it’s “true” that the grove feels like home,
  - Your home should be where you can breathe without becoming part of a machine.

### 6.2. Post-exposure routines

Immediately upon leaving:

- **Decontamination (if available):**
  - Change clothes:
    - Seal worn clothes in a bag,
    - Launder separately with plenty of heat and soap.
  - Shower:
    - Thoroughly,
    - Scrub skin,
    - Use nose rinse to clear spores (if your medical context allows),
    - Gargle salt-water or approved mouth-wash.

- **Check your body:**
  - Any:
    - Rashes,
    - New itches,
    - “Writing” sensations under the skin.
  - These are not automatically “bad” but:
    - They are signs of contact.
    - Monitor them.

- **Mind check:**
  - Write:
    - What you *remember*,
    - What you *think* happened,
    - And what “gaps” you feel in time.
    - Compare with:
      - The notes you made inside,
      - The “why I went” and “stop conditions” you wrote down.

- **Talk to someone:**
  - Explain:
    - What you felt.
    - What you want to do next.
  - Ask:
    - “Does this desire to return every night sound like me or like it?”

### 6.3. Deciding whether to ever return

“Not a place you find by accident.”

It is not:

- A “one-off” tourist experience.
- It is:
  - A relationship,
  - With an organism-sized room.

If you decide to **return**:

- Treat it like:
  - A controlled scientific experiment,
  - Or a spiritual retreat,
  - Not like:
    - A “cure” for being lost,
    - Or “the answer” to your life.

Questions:

- “What did I learn from the last visit?”
- “Did it change my behavior in ways I wanted?”
- “Is my ‘need’ to return just my normal self, or the fungal humidity negotiation?”

Only **you** can answer.

If you decide **never** to return:

- That’s also:
  - A valid,
  - And probably wise,
  - Relationship with the Grove of Mechanic Thirsts.

## 7. Short Summary: “The Checklist Version”

For an ordinary traveler planning to visit the Grove of Mechanic Thirsts:

**Before you go:**

- [ ] Check your emotional state:
  - Are you already “down too long?”
  - If yes, be extra cautious and set hard exit conditions.
- [ ] Tell someone:
  - Where you’re going,
  - How long you’ll stay,
  - When to expect you back.
- [ ] Prepare physically:
  - Breathable clothing,
  - Waterproof outer layer,
  - Gloves,
  - Boots,
  - Eye protection,
  - Mask or respirator if possible.
- [ ] Bring:
  - 1–2 liters of sealed water,
  - Electrolytes,
  - Small food,
  - Basic first aid,
  - Headlamp with low-intensity,
  - Notebook and pencil,
  - Small measuring tools (humidity/temperature),
  - Optional: sealed vials for collecting drops.

**Inside the grove:**

- [ ] Move slow; don’t startle.
- [ ] Stay hydrated:
  - Drink your own water,
  - Set reminders,
  - Don’t trust “I feel wet enough so I don’t need to drink.”
- [ ] Avoid:
  - The center basin,
  - Blocking water channels,
  - Drinking groove water,
  - Handling fauna directly.
- [ ] Observe:
  - Sound patterns,
  - Time inconsistencies,
  - Your own desire to “stay longer.”
- [ ] Use grounding:
  - Naming senses,
  - Breathing techniques,
  - Check your “why I’m here” note.

**When leaving:**

- [ ] Stick to your planned time.
- [ ] Decontaminate:
  - Change clothes,
  - Shower,
  - Check body and mind.
- [ ] Report in:
  - To the person you told before,
  - Share what you experienced,
  - Monitor desire to return.

The Grove of Mechanic Thirsts is not just “dangerous”; it’s intelligently dangerous. It’s a place where water, plant, machine, and animal have settled into a compromise at the “brink of sleep.” You can safely visit—as long as you understand that you are not a visitor in the usual sense. You are temporary input data, and the system is very good at gently convincing its data to stay.

If you prepare carefully, set clear boundaries, and treat the grove’s “quiet logic” with respect—and a bit of fear—you can leave again without being fundamentally rewritten. But you will leave knowing that your relationship to thirst, dryness, and humidity has been permanently altered.
