## 1. Before You Go: Attitudes and Expectations

### 1.1. Understand what kind of place this is

A normal traveler might pack for “a mountain town.” That’s not wrong, but this is more specific:  

- **Altitude**: Think “thin air / always evening.”  
  - It is cooler than the plains, but not polar.  
  - You won’t be fighting blizzards, but you will be dealing with a constant chill when you stop moving.
- **Architecture**: Not a museum, but a tool for living.
  - Roofs = walkways.
  - Water = visible and everywhere in channels.
  - Underfoot = not pavement, but shaped stone, worn smooth.
- **Society**: Small, intimate, and shaped by scarcity.
  - Every movement of water is important.
  - Every house sits on a hidden cistern.
  - This place is probably used to being self-sufficient and not being visited often.

You will not be the center of attention.  
You will be a small event in a long story of rain and stone.

---

### 1.2. Mind your footprint

This place has been designed by time and water. You are not here to decorate, renovate, or modernize it.

Prepare:

- **No unnecessary “improvements.”**
  - Do not try to rearrange stones, move channels, or “help” by fixing anything.
  - If you see a problem, ask a human who lives here before you touch.
- **No “shoe testing” of slippery surfaces.**
  - You will be tempted to test the edges of wet channels, cisterns, and rooftops.
