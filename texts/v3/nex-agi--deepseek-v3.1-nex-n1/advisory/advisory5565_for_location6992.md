To prepare this traveler for the chamber below the riverbed, you need to think of it as a deep-sea dive into a very still, very noisy ocean of concrete, time, and machinery. The body and mind do not normally experience this kind of environment. You have to shape them for it.

### 1. Understand what you’re walking into

First you make sure they understand the place:

- It’s a sealed, perfectly cylindrical shaft under 30 meters of earth and river: no windows, no breeze, no sky.  
- It’s designed to be *time-invariant* — almost nothing changes here over decades.  
- It’s filled with two constant sounds: the deep drone of the turbine above, and the countless separate drops of water falling into a shallow basin floor.  
- Light is evenly distributed everywhere; no real shadows, no bright focus.  
- The temperature is slightly warm, always humid, with faint chemicals and the smell of treated minerals, but nothing visibly growing except the tiny line of moss by the hatch.

You should describe this with the tone of a warning and a briefing: they are about to step into a machine that has outlived its engineers.

### 2. Physical preparation: body as environment-suit

Because they’re “ordinary,” you can’t rely on special immune systems or superior senses. Their body has to be treated as a fragile system entering a tightly regulated system.

#### 2.1 Claustrophobia and pressure

The pressure they “feel before they see anything” is real: the weight of the river and the earth, and the constant low hum, can trigger unease in people who never thought of themselves as claustrophobic.

- Prepare them with progressive exposure to deep, enclosed environments: small basement rooms, then larger windowless spaces, then rooms with constant noise.  
- Teach them grounding techniques: name five things they can see, four they can touch, three they can hear, two they can smell, one they can taste. This gives them a pattern to interrupt spirals of panic.
