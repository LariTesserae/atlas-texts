You’re describing a place that’s technically a city, but psychologically more like a planet-scale machine layered with old stone and wet green jungle. It folds all three into one body, and most of the danger comes from that mismatch: scale, vertigo, water, and the invisible machine under the stone.

I’ll treat this as advice for a “baseline human” multiverse traveler who is not bulletproof, can get sprains and infections, and prefers to stay alive. If you’re stronger or more tech-enabled than that, you can scale back some precautions, but not the vertigo ones.

---

## 0. Before You Go: Reframing Your Mind

### 0.1. This is not a city; it is a world wrapped around a machine
You’re not going “to a city”; you’re going to a small, planet-like cylinder of machine-rock with *layers* of cities on it. If you panic from heights, you will panic here. The wide, endless horizon you’re used to is replaced by:
- a ceiling city over you
- a ring of horizon that is actually just the next wall of the world
- a drop below that doesn’t end

So you’re going to treat height like weather:
- **Assume you will be near a drop** most of the time.
- **Assume there are railings and edges**, and you need to respect both.
- **Assume your brain will be tricked** into thinking the floor is actually the wall, or the ceiling is actually the ground.

Doing mental prep before you go is as important as packing gear.

---

### 0.2. Primeval Landing ≠ “ground level”
“Primeval Landing” is just the *current* highest inhabited layer. It’s not safe ground, it’s the top of the machine world:
- Above you is more terrace.
- Below you is thousands of meters of machine-cities.
- You are never below “the sky”; you are inside the world.

Visually and culturally, it might be the most accessible layer, but geographically, it’s just the type of a city-wide balcony.

## 1. What You’re Actually Packing For

### 1.1. The core environment
- **Constant height and edges** everywhere.
- **Damp, always**; humidity and mist.
- **Warmth**; not searing, but muggy.
- **Water everywhere**: dripping, running, condensing.
- **Industrial infrastructure** but not “modern”: groans, chains, pipes, not electric glare.
- **Time is slow**: erosion, moss layers, paint. No hurry, but no mistakes forgiven.

Your pack should be built for:
- Vertigo protection
- Slip resistance
- Damp resistance
- Minor infection prevention
- Basic navigation in a terrible spatial map

---

### 1.2. Clothing

Pack for “always damp, never shallow, lots of stairs”.

Essential outfit:
- **Closed waterproof walking shoes with good grip.**  
  - Soles like a trail runner or better still a proper boot.  
  - Laces are fine if you can lace them tightly; your ankles are precious here.
- **Water-resistant trousers or durable pants.**  
  - Not delicate fabric; it will be wet and/or moss-stained in a few hours.
  - Shorts are possible, but realize you will be brushing against walls, mosses, and railings that are cold, wet, and sometimes not clean.
- **Long-sleeve shirt or tunic with sleeves that can roll up.**  
  - You want to cover skin if you’re going into tight spaces with machinery or trailing plants.
- **A thin waterproof layer** like a raincoat or poncho that folds small.
- **A thin scarf / bandana / “mask” of some kind**:  
  - Mist, mold, and machine dust shouldn’t be inhaled constantly if you can avoid it.
- **Hat with a brim** to keep water and drips from falling constantly onto your face and glasses.

Optional extras:
- **Change of underwear and socks** in a waterproof bag inside your pack.  
  - Being wet all day is fine; being wet and cold chafes and risks skin infections.
- **Spare thin layer** like a synthetic hoodie—for when you move under less heated terraces or higher altitudes where warmth is less.

---

### 1.3. Gear and Safety Tools

You’re not pretending it’s a wilderness hike, but you’re treating the city at least as seriously as one.

#### For vertigo and edges

- **Simple harness / sling belt or something to hook yourself to fixed points.**  
  - If you decide to lean over the railing to look, or walk along gutter edges, you can at least clip to something solid. You don’t need to go full mountaineer, but you *are* going into a vertical world.
- **Compact camera or device with zoom lens** instead of risking your body near edge for a view.
- **Small “stress tools”**: if you have anxiety meds or anti‑vertigo meds, bring them. If you’ve never used them before, don’t start self‑medicating heavily in this environment; but you should be honest with yourself about height panic.
- **Sunglasses / tinted eyewear**:  
  - The light can be bright and reflected off wet surfaces everywhere. Your eyes might get fatigued quickly.

#### For water and wetness

- **Waterproof container** for important documents/supports/lifelines if you have them.
- **Small towel or micro-fiber cloth:**  
  - To wipe hands, railings, seats before you use them.
- **Basic microfiltration straw or water filter:**  
  *Even if* water is everywhere, you don’t know how long it’s been running in weird channels.
- **Adhesive bandages and waterproof tape**:  
  - For blisters and cuts; water everywhere raises infection risk.

#### For navigation

- **Notebook and pencil or pen.**  
  - Paper can get wet, so protect it with plastic bags.
- **Compass**: not for cardinal north-south of “earth,” but for:
  - “Inward” vs “outward”
  - “Upward” vs “downward”
- **Small string / line** or marking tape to:
  - Drop tiny markings into nooks where you’ve been, if needed
  - Tie a temporary marker to a branch or railing in complex junctions.

#### For basic medical self-care

- **Basic antiseptic wipes or solution.**
- **Small antibiotic ointment** (if your world has it).
- **Anti-allergy or antihistamine if you are susceptible to mold allergies.**
- **Pain reliever** for headache and pressure changes from elevation if you travel deeper.

---

### 1.4. Local context and “polite entry”

You’re arriving on a *world*, not a country. If you have any standard multiverse “traveler’s kit”:

- **Generic trade tokens**: small, not too precious, but unusual:
  - Small pieces of jewelry, foreign coins, colored stones.  
  *Everything* here is made, so something raw or foreign is often valued more than you expect.
- **Simple gifts based on your world**:  
  - If you have paper, a notebook or print is exotic in a wet city like this.
  - If you have plastic, even small waterproof containers are curiosities.
- **Reference map images of your world or other worlds, if you can spare them.**  
  - This is a world of surfaces and maps; you may be viewed as either insane or a mapmaker of other worlds, which is a real job in some terraces.

## 2. Arrival and Immediate Environment Check

### 2.1. Where you’re projected to appear

Assume you’ll appear:
- On a **public terrace**, probably not directly at the edge.
- Near **inner arches**, under the vaults of the next terrace above.

If you expect to appear *exactly* at the edge, you also need:
- **Instant balance**: feet planted shoulder-width apart.
- **No bags slung loosely over one shoulder** that can throw your center of gravity off.

### 2.2. First actions: 30 seconds

When you pop in:
1. **Don’t move immediately.**  
   - Look at your feet; feel the surface.  
   - Note the nearest wall, pillar, or railing.
2. **Identify safety edges:**  
   - Where is the nearest obvious railing or barrier?
3. **Identify people flow:**  
   - Where are locals walking? *Walk that way.*
4. **If you’re near a wide-open edge with railing, don’t immediately rush to look over.**  
   - Wait until your head is clear. You’ll get plenty of opportunities to gaze into the abyss.

---

### 2.3. Spatial orientation: learning the “map” of a world coil

The world is wrapped. Instead of “map,” you’re dealing with:
- **Ring direction:** Around the circle.
- **Inward/Outward:** Toward the core of the spire (metal) or out toward the edge of the terrace.
- **Up / Down:** To the terrace above or below via shafts and bridges.

Mental discipline:
- **Always keep track of one axis: Inward vs Outward.**  
  - If you’re walking and the street curves more inward than outward, you’re moving deeper into the tower world.  
  - If you start to see more open air, larger sky ring, you’re moving toward the edge rimlands.
- **Use landmarks:**  
  - Distinctive bridges  
  - Green arches  
  - Specific hanging ornaments or statues of dragons/donors/spirits
- **If you get lost, ask to be taken to:**  
  - “The nearest elevator shaft or stairwell” or  
  - “The nearest ring market” — those are common nodes.

## 3. Moving Around Safely

### 3.1. Walking edges and railings

Everywhere you look:
- **There is a railing you should trust and not trust.**  
  - It’s been there for centuries, and it has been mended constantly.
  - Still, don’t lean your entire weight suddenly, or lean where you see visible rust, new welds, or repairs.

Rules:
- Don’t run near edges, even if you see children doing it.
- Don’t sit on the railing. Ever.
- Don’t climb railings or stone walls to get a better shot of the view.
- If you want to look over, keep your center of gravity over your feet:
  - Your feet stay planted, only your torso and head go over.

### 3.2. Stairs and steps

- **Moss is everywhere.** Expect to be walking on living surfaces:
  - However, in the main paths, it will be scuffed away.
  - In the internal staircases, your footing might be on wet, stone with a thin layer of slick.
- **Slow down.**  
  - Use handrails, if they are present.
  - On the smooth, rounded steps of “settled-form” erosion, you may slip if you rush.

### 3.3. Bridges and cable tramways

- Bridges will cross between:
  - One side of the curve terrace to another
  - One building to another
- Some are built over the street (like covered arcades).
- Some will cross over the edge of the terrace world.

**On the internal bridges:**
- Safe if you keep to standard path.
- **Do not go under or over the bridge rails.**  
  - Some locals will climb and do it; you are not trained.

**On cable tramways or hanging bridges:**
- **Assume swaying.** Especially if they are in external space.
- **Always step with the motion of the bridge.**  
  - Don’t fight sway; move with rhythm.
- If you’re very sensitive to height and motion:
  - **Keep your eyes on the far platform, not down the void.**

If you are truly uncomfortable, don’t be forced by “I want to see everything.” Some of the best views of the Spire Waterfalls are from the street below the bridges anyway.

---

### 3.4. Using the vertical transportation system

**Inside the spire wall are elevators and shafts:**

- **The chains you hear are probably moving elevators, platforms, or freight carts.**  
  - May be old and creaking but regularly maintained.
- **Don’t jump into or out of moving platforms.**
- **Follow the locals:**
  - If the locals step on and off the elevator platform carefully, match their body language.
  - If they are holding to the rail with one hand and not looking at the drop, do that.

**General safety rules inside shafts:**

- Stay near the center of the platform if it is semi-open.
- Don’t lean out of the shaft windows.
- Don’t put your hands into the machinery walls: you’ll hear chains and gears; those are not curiosity toys.

## 4. Water, Drinking, and Health

### 4.1. The water: everywhere but not necessarily safe

Water:
- Drinks from many channels.
- Dropped from condensation into gutters.
- Filleted from unseen industrial sources lower.

You can never know if the last phase of its journey involved:
- oil
- industrial waste
- long-term stagnation in some hidden cistern.

**Rules, simple:**
- **Don’t drink any water until you’ve seen locals drinking it *from the same source.***
- If they are filling from a specific fountain:
  - It is probably *not* the same as the gutter you see passing by ten feet away.
  - If they are washing clothes in one channel and drinking only at a dedicated basin, you do the same.

**Use your filter:**
- If you can’t confirm 100%, **filter it**.  
  - Especially if you’re traveling between levels: water on one level might be fine, but another layer’s might not be.
- **Check the water’s basic appearance:**
  - If it’s cloudy yellow or gray with an oily shine, do not drink.
  - If it’s clear and cold and running in a dedicated fountain basin, you’re in safer territory.

---

### 4.2. Humidity and skin health

- **Always slightly damp** means always at risk for:
  - chafing (especially feet, thighs, underarms)
  - skin fungal issues
- **Dry feet are important**:
  - Change socks if they are soaked.
  - Use talcum or anti-chafing powder if you have it.
  - For your feet in particular:
    - At the end of the day, wash your feet with safe water and dry them as well as you can.
    - Let them breath before you sleep. This is a small, but in a long stay, huge reduction in risk.

---

### 4.3. Mold and respiratory

If you’re in mold-susceptible:
- Avoid leaning directly against walls heavily lined with mosses and lichens if you can smell “musty.”
- Use your thin mask or scarf especially if you’re going through:
  - narrow internal shafts
  - older, less ventilated buildings.

---

## 5. Light and Time Management

Lighting changes:
- **Not like sun rising and setting predictably across a horizon.**  
  Instead **sunlight circle cuts the sky ring at certain angle-times**.

If you treat:
- “Daylight” = time when light ring is visible or sunlit under the terrace above.
- “Night” = mostly lantern-lit under the terrace vaults.

**Recommendation:**

- **Plan your movement around “light hours.”**
  - Don’t start exploring new routes if the light is already fading from the ring-sky.
- **If you’re outside after “lanterns only” time:**
  - You can be disoriented by shadows, and railings might be less visible.
  - Carry your own light if you’re not deep in the city center.

## 6. Social Rules and Cultural Fit

You don’t want to accidentally be rude or irresponsible in a world where you’re about to be treated like a guest from another level.

### 6.1. Respect the water

Water:
- It is engineered lifeblood.
- It is not “just rain”; it is *managed* life.

Don’t:
- Throw trash or waste into water channels.
- Stand on gutters or blocking basins if you see people using them to fill buckets.
- Pollute with body waste near any water channel.

If you do:
- You may be treated more harshly than you expect by locals who see this as “poisoning the world.”

---

### 6.2. Be careful of “touching the building”

- **You are touching someone else’s world machine.**
  - The walls are not just “old.” They are structural to the whole world.
  - Don’t carve, chip, or “just take a little piece” of the stone or tiles; that’s not just disrespectful—it may be structural damage.
- If you see carved grooves or handholds:
  - Don’t use them as you might climb in a natural environment.
  - Climbers may be maintenance workers or trained specialists; not you.

---

### 6.3. Children and edges

You will see children running:
- Along broad terraces.
- Along railings.
- Across rooftops and bridges.

But:
- They are **children of this world**, not you.
- You don’t have their years of training.
- **If you attempt to copy their acrobatics, you will likely hurt yourself.**

This is crucial:
- **You don’t have to match what locals do safely at height to enjoy your experience.**

---

### 6.4. Help and asking directions

Ask:
- “Where is the nearest public market?”  
  → Markets are often nodes of information and safe zones.
- “Where is the nearest elevator to the next level down/up?”  
  → Better with you than you getting lost in internal corridors.
- “If I want to see the water cascade from a safe place, where should I go?”  
  → They might send you to a specific bridge or public outlook terrace.

Try not to:
- Mention “I’m from another world,” if this world is not used to that idea.
- Instead, say vaguely you “came from far away, from other layers of the world, and not much used to this terrace.”

---

## 7. Food and Waste

### 7.1. Food safety

Foods you will likely see:
- Baked or cooked foods (from steam or gas-fired ovens).
- Stews, soups, and wet-based dishes.
- Fresh greens from narrow terraces and rooftop gardens.

**Guidelines:**
- Prefer:
  - **Hot cooked food** served to you immediately.
  - Food visibly **boiled or steamed**,
  - Food coming from a busy place where locals are also eating.
- Be cautious:
  - Raw greens from unknown terraces where water might be contaminated.
  - Cold foods in open-air stalls where they are exposed to water mist and dust.

---

### 7.2. Personal waste and hygiene

- **Do not pee into the water channels.**  
  Even if in some places you might see locals doing it (you might not), it is less obvious if you:
  - Ask in a market if there is a “public latrine” or “water-closed.”
  - Use the same infrastructure they use.

Many of these worlds have composting:
- You may be expected to put organic waste into specific bins.
- Ask where you can put your waste if you’re staying longer.

---

## 8. Animals and Plants

### 8.1. The animals
- **Birds**: everywhere you look.
- Small translucent fish in water channels.
- Lizards on walls.

Don’t:
- Touch animals that you don’t know.
- Feed random animals.
- Follow an animal into a dangerous edge space.

---

### 8.2. Plants and mosses

- Many of those surfaces are covered in **living moss carpets and vines.**
- You might be tempted to touch or pinch:
  - Be gentle if you do.
  - Treat moss like hair on someone else’s head: you can look, but not pull.

Some of these “clean” channels:
- They are cleaned by those fish and by plants.
- Don’t step on them unnecessarily.

---

## 9. Viewing the Spire Waterfalls Properly

If you want to appreciate the effect you’re describing:
> **“like a city that has learned to weep”**

The best locations:
- **Public overlooks with strong railings and clear platforms.**  
  - Don’t try to get “better” by going over wall.
  - Brings binoculars or zoom camera. Don’t bring your body.

- **Bridges that span from the terrace edge to another ring sector.**  
  - From there you’ll see the *waterfalls* threading down the face of the tower-world.

If you’re looking *from* below (if you go to a lower terrace):
- **“City that’s also ceiling”:**  
  - You’re looking up at another world.
- Keep safe distance from drip edges under you and above you:
  - Water flows over you but not onto you.

## 10. Leaving and Emotional Check-In

### 10.1. Emotional impact

A world like this:
- **Literal height and metaphorical depth**  
  → May affect you more than you expect.
- You might feel:
  - Small, temporary, and fragile.
  - Awe and terror at how much machine and world is built around you.
- You might dream:
  - Of falling edges.
  - Of endless terraces.

If you start to feel:
- Anxiety or intrusive images of edges,
- Difficulty sleeping,
- Or avoidance of going near views,

then:
- **Reduce exposure time**: spend less time at actual edges.
- View more from the interiors of the city, where the world feels more like a normal, dense urban system.

---

### 10.2. When you’re leaving

- **Recheck your body**:
  - Any cuts that may be too wet, especially around feet.
  - Any signs of skin infections.
- Treat if you need.

If you’ve been nice:
- The locals may remember you as a strange, quiet traveler from far away, who wasn’t trouble.
- If you’ve been a chaotic edge-climbing nuisance:
  - You might leave your reputation hanging like a wet branch over the void for future travelers to encounter.

---

## 11. Final Minimal Checklist

On you when you step out into Primeval Landing:

- **Secure, waterproof footwear with grip**
- **Waterproof layer**
- **Harness or clip-line if you have it**
- **Water filter or straw**
- **Basic first aid kit**
- **Notebook and pencil**
- **Some small trade or gift items**
- **Light (if you have it)**
- **Your common sense about not copying edge acrobats**

You’re going to a world that is:
- **Lush, wet, and bright**
- **Deep and layered with time**
- **Engineered but lived-in like an organism**

Treat it with respect for its height, its water, and its machine bones under the stone. If you do that, you’ll experience the Spire Waterfalls as one of the most uniquely beautiful places you’ve ever visited, rather than the place where you made a small but decisive error.
