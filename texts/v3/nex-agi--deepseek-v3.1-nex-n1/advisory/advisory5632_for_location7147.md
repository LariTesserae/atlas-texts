To visit this place is not like visiting a planet or a city. It’s more like stepping into an incubator the size of a world—where everything is still synthesizing itself from first principles. There are no sharp hazards “lying in wait”; instead, the danger is subtle, like overstaying in a room that has been perfectly tuned—except not for you.

I’ll break preparation into categories. A traveler can ignore some of this if they’re going on a very short visit, but for anything longer than a symbolic hour or two, you’ll want to take this seriously.

## 1. Physiology: Expect Your Body to Be Mis-calibrated Here

### Temperature & Humidity

- The world is warm and humid: constant early-summer-morning, body-temperature heat, with air like breathing through a damp cloth.
- What will happen to you:
  - Your heat-regulation mechanisms (sweating, flushing, shivering) are essentially useless.
  - You won’t “cool” by evaporation; you might still feel your internal temperature rising because you’re used to temperature gradients and day–night cycles that don’t exist here.
- Prepare:
  - **Wear minimal, open clothing:** light breathable fabrics that help air move over skin, even if they won’t dry.
  - **Hydration strategy:** You won’t be able to judge “thirst” by sweat. Overhydration is the risk: you may feel overheated and misinterpret it as dehydration because you’re used to sweating and dry mouth.
  - If you drink:
    - Very small sips of room-temperature or slightly warm liquids.
    - No diuretics (like excess caffeine or alcohol), if you can avoid them.
  - Do **not** try to “cool down” by dousing yourself in water; the air is already saturated, and you will only feel heavier and more thermally trapped.

### Time-Sense & Circadian Rhythms

- There is no sun, no moon, no day-night cycle.
- Shadowless, luminous sky.
- Your sense of time will be subtly distorted. You might feel:
  - “Forever afternoon” drowsiness without a night to reset you.
  - Apathy, or detachment, because your body’s clock has no landmarks.
- Prepare:
  - **Bring external timekeeping.**
    - Multiple devices with long battery lives or hand-powered mechanisms.
    - A visual “timer” that you can set without needing screens (solar-powered watches, etc) are ideal—but remember solar won’t be recharging without a sun as you know it.
    - If you rely on electronics, ensure you have power to last your expected duration plus a margin.
  - **Set a schedule before you arrive.** Decide:
    - A “day” length you will enforce (e.g., still use 16-hour “days”).
    - Sleep times, “rest” times, and when you will eat.
  - **Use rhythmic signals religiously.** Light from your own gear, meals, reading, journaling—all scheduled. If you don’t, you will lose time with no sense of how much you’ve lost.

### Light & Eyes

- The sky is evenly luminous without any sun-disc.
- You will not have natural shadows to help depth perception.
- Prepare:
  - **Bring adjustable lighting**:
    - Headlamps or lanterns with broad spectrum (white light).
    - You need contrast for depth perception over vast distances.
    - You will need *light at night*—not because it becomes dark, but because a “night” phase you choose will need contrast to the constant ambient sky.
  - **Consider polarized sunglasses/glasses**:
    - Not to dim light, but to give subtle contrast edges.
    - They can help distinguish edges of the pools and basins where water gathers.
    - They can reduce visual strain from the monotone brightness of luminous fog.

## 2. Mental Health & Time-Anchoring: This World Is Made of Time That You Don’t Share

Your mind will notice that:

- Time here is **measured and emitted**, not “experienced” as flow.
- Events are discrete: “a leaf unfurls,” then the world has not “changed much since”—even if you have been there for hours.

You will feel:

- As if the world is repeating the same day without variation.
- That you are drifting through a sequence that is not structured to accommodate you.
- A subtle but deep pressure to “match” the world’s time-sense—particularly if you stay near the time-column plant.

### Prepare:

- **Treat this like a long-haul flight or sleep study experiment.**
  - Bring journaling tools:
    - Notebooks, pens that you enjoy physically using.
    - Logs to track:
      - When you arrived.
      - How you feel.
      - What you observe.
    - This gives you a “time line” you control.
- **Set hard limits before you go.**
  - Maximum time you will stay:
    - If you have no anchor device, you risk not realizing how long you’ve been present.
    - Decide that you will leave after a specific number of meals, sleeps, or “cycles,” if time devices stop.
- **Avoid prolonged nearness to the time-column plant without strict breaks.**
  - You might be mesmerized or lulled into a passive “observer” state.
  - Make yourself move away regularly—to the edge, to the forest-tree, etc.

## 3. What This Place Is *Made Of*, and What That Means for You

### Everything is Plant. Nothing is Tool.

You will not find:

- Stone walls.
- Metal rails.
- Concrete roads.
- Synthetic tools.
- Any mechanical artifact that you recognize.

Instead:

- Roots are the ground.
- “Columns”, “cathedral lattices”, and “basins” are grown structures.
- Even water movement is shaped by the plant tissue itself.

This implies:

- You cannot rely on built safety infrastructure.
- There is no “guardrail,” only “edge.”
- You cannot tap into “utility systems” like plumbing or electricity.

## 4. Safety: There Are No Sharp Dangers, but Subtle Ones Are Constant

### The Edge of the Platform

- You stand near a literal edge where water falls off in a seamless sheet into the cloud below, with no sound of splash or impact.
- You cannot see beneath the cloud.
- What might happen:
  - Falling off the platform edge into the cloud may be survivable—or may not be. The cloud may be:
    - A thick cushion of vapor over a soft ocean far below.
    - A thin layer over another world platform.
    - A deep fall into a machine-like world engine.
    - You don’t know.
- Prepare:
  - **Treat the edge as a high cliff or roof edge.**  
    - Never approach it without:
      - A line or anchor.
      - Someone or something else to anchor you if you slip.
    - If you have any climbing gear:
      - Wear harness and secure line to a sturdy object before you walk near the edge.
    - Even if you are not dizzy, you may slip on the faintly damp root surface.
- **Assume the water curtain is not strong enough to hold you or break a fall.**
  - These water sheets are thin sheets; do not rely on them as barriers or handrails.
  - Water falling from you into the cloud also gives you feedback—nothing “comes back.” That edge consumption is indifferent to your matter.

---

### Walking on the Root Platform

- The surface:
  - “Bone-white root-fiber” interlaced like a thick mesh.
  - Channels and gaps between the fibers where you can see down into darkness.
- Prepare:
  - **Wear stable shoes with good grip and that still give tactile feedback.**
    - You need to feel the texture beneath your feet but not slip.
    - Boot soles that are firm but not completely slick.
- **Mind gaps between the fibers.**
  - Your foot or limb could catch in a hole if you are careless.
  - On the “root platform,” you might not see “ground” in the same way—you know roots are interlacing, but your mind may treat it like a solid paving.
    - Remind yourself explicitly: This is a mesh.
    - Test step in new areas before committing full weight.
- **Test the stability of any structures with care before trusting them.**
  - A rising cylinder or pillar may be strong enough, but its surface:
    - May be delicate.
    - May respond to your touch by starting to change its shape.
    - You may be leaning on something that decides not to be leaning-against-friendly later.
    - Treat ascending lesser columns or pillars as climbing a tree, not a building.

---

### Water and Erosion That Is Really Formation

- Water pools form:
  - They are not “rain” accumulation; they are plant tissue exhaling water.
  - Water edges can shift as the plant tissue alters its permeability.
- What to watch:
  - The water you see on the surface may not reflect “ground level” below.
  - You may walk into a shallow depression that seems to hold “flat ground,” but it is actually newly forming.
- Prepare:
  - **Never assume water depth.**
    - Test with a tool before you step into any pool.
    - Even shallow pools may be on top of uneven, still-forming ridges.
  - **If you need water for survival:**
    - This water is likely “pure” plant exudation.
    - It’s still wise:
      - To filter or treat with safe tablets if you are uncertain.
      - If not chemically treated, you *might* drink small amounts initially; but assess:
        - Any reaction from your skin where you’ve touched wet root surfaces.
        - If you burn or sting anywhere from contact water, then avoid drinking.

## 5. Interaction with the Plants: The World Is Sensitive, and It Is Young

### Everything is Growing and Designing Itself as You Stand There

- “Low mounds of plant tissue” like half-melted pillars.
- Cylindrical structures with channels forming.
- Time-columns with particles drifting.
- Forest-trees with leaf-sheets like “pages of a book.”

Your key rule:

> **Nothing here is “neutral substrate.” Everything you touch or affect may be integrated into the world’s design.**

### Implications of Your Touch:

- When you placed your hand on the cylinder:
  - The surface blushed with an “opalescence” and began to orient around that point of contact.
  - You might have:
    - Introduced a pattern that the world “learns.”
    - Changed the future local geometry.
- What to prepare for:
  - You may inadvertently:
    - Call textures into existence.
    - Change patterns of water currents.
    - Shift the “time tune” if you touch near the time-column plant.

### Guidelines for Interaction:

- **Treat every touch as intentional.**  
  - Decide:
    - Are you decorating?
    - Are you experimenting?
    - Or are you trying not to influence?
- If you want to stay minimal:
  - **Avoid prolonged contact with any structures that are clearly in mid-formation.**
  - Avoid scratching marks into surfaces or leaving footprints in repeated patterns.
- If you want to influence consciously:
  - **Touch with patterns, not randomness.**
    - Circles, lines, or other geometrics you might use to teach or suggest.
    - Think in terms of “input data” rather than graffiti.
    - Remember you are teaching a world that is young and still learning boundary.

### Potential “Animal” Interaction: The Time-Calibrators

- The creature you described:
  - Jointed segments, translucent skin.
  - Three limbs radiating from a central body.

  - It is not hostile, but it is:
    - Connected to the world’s timing system.
    - Any change to its movement pattern will be “learned” by the world.
  - You might subtly resign the world’s time around your presence.

## 6. Health, Food, and Medicine: This World Has None of Your Supplies

### Nutrition

- There is:
  - **No visible food source for you.**
  - No plants you can recognize as fruits or vegetables.
  - Only leaf-sheets, tapestries of chlorophyll, and structural organs.
- What to expect:
  - You must bring your own food or have a way to produce or summon it.
  - If you attempt to eat:
    - Even a small piece of any leaf:
      - Your body is not adapted to its chemistry.
      - It may be harmless, or it may act like a strong drug or irritant.
      - Never experiment without:
        - Skin contact test first.
        - Then tiny dose of any material you “taste.”
        - And have medicines ready to respond if you react badly.

### Prepare:

- Bring self-contained food that:
  - Requires no refrigeration or heating (if possible).
  - Has stable macronutrients.
  - Is packaged individually so you don’t expose it to the humidity and air.
- **Do not rely on this world for food.**
  - No matter how lush the plant structures are, they are not designed for you.

### Medicine & First-Aid

- You may need:
  - **Antifungals and skin antiseptics.**  
    - Constant humidity and warm temperature favor skin infections.
    - Between damp roots and your own skin, you are likely to be in contact with “slightly sweating” surfaces.
  - **Basic antibiotics** if accessible:
    - For cuts or scrapes from walking on root mesh.
  - **Any specific medications** you normally require:
    - Allergies.
    - Metabolic conditions.
    - Psychiatric medications.
- **Prepare a plan for “exit.”**
  - If your health deteriorates:
    - You should not wait to see “if the world helps.”
    - It has no intent toward you.
    - Have a clear departure threshold:
      - When you feel “off,” you treat it as a signal to leave.

## 7. Equipment and Gear to Consider

Bring gear that:

- Does not rely on external infrastructure.
- Does not pollute the world in ways that might permanently “teach” it.

### Essentials

- **Timekeeping device(s)**  
  - Electronic watches with battery.
  - Mechanical wind-up watches as backup.
- **Light source with adjustable spectrum.**  
  - Broad-spectrum headlamp or lantern to help you define “day” and “night.”
- **Harness and rope for edge safety.**  
  - Climbing gear or equivalent:
    - Distance anchor + safety line can keep you from falling.
- **Protective clothing:**  
  - Light, long sleeves to avoid skin contact with unknown surfaces, but breathable.
  - Tights if you want to sit on the root surface.
  - Rain cover or shell layer, not for rain but for:
    - Sleeping on the root mesh.
    - Marking a “night” zone, by covering yourself.

### Optional but Useful

- **Simple geometric tools:**  
  - Compass, straightedge, or a template of geometric patterns.
  - If you choose to “interact with the world,” these patterns might be consciously used on the surfaces:
    - You might lead the world to learn circles, lines, or spirals.
  - You can also experiment:
    - Placing chalk dust or fine colored powder (non-toxic) in patterns on surfaces:
      - Observe whether they influence the emerging channels of pillars.
      - This lets you test without licking or cutting the world.
- **Scientific and sampling tools:**  
  - If allowed:
    - Tiny sample kits to collect minuscule amounts of water or leaf tissue.
    - Always sealed so you don’t spill into the world.
    - Remember: sampling itself “teaches” the tissue.
- **Noise-canceling or sealing earphones:**  
  - To let you choose when to listen to the world’s continuous tone.
  - Sometimes you will need to silence the world’s “base chord” just to hear yourself think.

## 8. Psychological and Philosophical Prep: Why You Are Going

This place is not built to entertain you.

It is:

- **Always becoming itself**, never aging.
- **Not natural in the sense you know:** not ecosystem, but not city either.
- **Not your habitat:** You are an alien to its temperature, time, and forming language.

### Prepare yourself mentally:

- Decide what you want to know:
  - Pure observation?
  - Gentle, intentional teaching?
  - A search for “self” in a world that doesn’t know you at all?
- You may encounter:
  - **Strange calmness** bordering on indifference.
  - No sense of urgency.
  - A pressure to be as “unchanging” as the world itself.

### Set These Rules Before You Arrive:

1. **You will not stay longer than planned unless the world’s behavior threatens your capacity to leave.**  
2. **You will not touch the time-calibrating creature or the time-column itself without proper preparation and clear intent.**  
3. **You will measure the passage of time by your own devices, not by the world’s.**  
4. **You will treat the world as you would treat a child: you are responsible for what you teach.**

This world will not change when you leave.
