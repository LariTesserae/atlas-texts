Visiting this place—this “proto-industrial factory plateau of a not-yet-world”—is not like backpacking in another desert or even like stepping onto a visibly alien planet. Nothing here matches the usual categories of hostile environment: no vacuum, no crushing heat, no poison storms. The danger is not loud; it is subtle, structural, and almost philosophical. It’s closer to wandering inside somebody else’s unfinished blueprint than wandering inside a nature preserve.

Your preparation needs to reflect that: you’re a guest in a machine that doesn’t know it’s a machine yet.

Below I’ll break down how to get ready: what to bring, how to think about the environment itself, and how to behave once you’re there.

---

## 0. Mindset: What You’re Actually Preparing For

Before you touch anything physical, you should prepare your mind:

- **You are in a system under construction.**  
  Think of yourself walking on an internal organ of a world that is “forming.” The ground under you is not just scenery; it is part of a long-term process that is barely begun.

- **Time is not on your side.**  
  Your body still expects cycles of day and night, sleep and waking, fatigue and rest. The plateau doesn’t. There’s no sunset to tell you to stop, and your body won’t get the usual circadian cues.

- **Nothing here is random noise.**  
  The straight, etched lines in the rock, the rings of vegetation, the faint hums and clicks—they are faint signals from a very slow industrial machine. You want to be able to notice them without accidentally interfering.

- **Severity is hidden.**  
  It doesn’t feel dangerous: warm air, no storms, flat terrain to walk on, “plants” softer than your boot soles. The hazards are mostly long-term and invisible. Your job is to treat even mild discomforts and anomalies as serious data.

So you’re not preparing for “survival” in the classic sense of wilderness. You’re preparing to **exist safely** inside a partial, semi-working system of matter and energy.

## 1. Physical and Physiological Preparation

### 1.1. Medical and Physical Baseline

- **Get a comprehensive medical check.**  
  You’re going to a high-altitude (low-pressure) environment with no day-night cycle, thin air, and unknown radiation patterns. You need a clear baseline of:
  - Cardiovascular and respiratory function
  - Blood chemistry
  - Bone density and joint stability
  - Skin condition and exposure sensitivity

- **Conditioning.**  
  You don’t need extreme fitness, but:
  - Mild aerobic conditioning: steady walking on varied slopes.
  - Balance training: stepping on uneven surfaces, rock surfaces, etc.
  - Strength training for ankles, knees, and hips.  
  The plateau surface changes texture and you’ll be stepping between bare rock and soft, fibrous mats.
