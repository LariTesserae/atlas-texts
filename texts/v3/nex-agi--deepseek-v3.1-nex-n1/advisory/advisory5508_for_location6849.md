## How to Prepare for Eelbridge

### What kind of place this is and what you're aiming for

Eelbridge is not a disaster zone or a frontier world; it's a dense, humid, engineered ecosystem‑city where everything is already in equilibrium. You're not going to "rough it" so much as "fit into a very specific way of living."

The big challenges are:

- **Climate:** permanently hot, humid, "always between mist and rain"
- **Structure:** everything is built into/onto living trees and water
- **Social order:** urbanized, with invisible rules and subtle hierarchies
- **Health:** living water, industrialized plant‑matter, biotech
- **Mindset:** Eelbridge runs on slow time and compromises

Your goal as an ordinary traveler is not just to survive but to *not be a burden* and to avoid causing subtle damage that a local would notice.

---

## 1. Health and Basic Medical Prep

### Assume microbially rich, everywhere

Everything is damp, plant‑covered, and somewhat alive. Assume you will encounter:

- Complex water flora and fauna (including eels and engineered microorganisms)
- Extensive fungal and mycelial networks
- Mixed organic‑industrial dusts (from rust, algae, minerals, decomposing leaf matter)

#### Basic travel health:
- Bring any **personal medications** in clearly labeled, original packaging if possible.
- If you have a **chronic condition** (especially respiratory or immune), get extra supply and a clear note from your doctor explaining diagnosis and treatment.
- Ensure **routine vaccines** are up to date (tetanus, respiratory vaccines, etc.). This is more of a "avoid being an immunocompromised node in a complex system" than a specific known threat.

#### Tetanus and rust
Because of constant rust stains and metal fittings:

- **Make sure your tetanus vaccination is current** before you travel.
- Pack a small **basic wound kit**: antiseptic wipes, sterile bandages, medical tape, antibiotic ointment if available to you, and tweezers.

#### Breathing and allergies
The air is thick, "like soup just cool enough to drink." You're likely in fine water mist laden with organic matter.

- Bring **any asthma inhalers or allergy medications** you use.
- If you're sensitive to mold, damp, or pollen, consider:
  - Non‑sedating antihistamine
  - Nasal saline spray or an irrigation bottle to rinse out particulate and humidity-related "stuffy" feeling
- Limit heavy perfume/cologne: strong scents plus the humidity can be overwhelming to you and others.

### Water safety
"Drinkable water" in a multiverse context can be tricky, but Eelbridge's water is likely "safe if treated by local systems".

- **Do not drink from open canals**, puddles, or decorative tanks.
- Bring a **compact reusable filter bottle** if possible from your world—simple mechanical filter plus any basic purifier you can get.
- Ask locals where "public tap water" points are fed by the humming conduits and tanks.
- **Boiling water** is a reliable last resort if you can access a heat source.

### Avoiding "eco-system contamination"
Eelbridge is a hybrid bioengineered system where "purity" doesn't mean "clean" but "balanced." You don't want to introduce:

- **Antibacterial soap and wipes** are good to carry, but use:
  - For personal hygiene.
  - **Do not** wash your body or clothes with strong soap directly into standing canals or drains without asking local etiquette.
- Ask locals:
  - "Where can I wash safely?"
  - "Which drains are safe for soap?"
  - "What should I not pour into the water?"

### Earplugs and sound tolerance
The soundscape is continuous: dripping water, hum of engines, creaking branches, people talking in layers.

- Bring **simple earplugs or noise-muffling headphones**.
- Even if you don't use them in daytime, they're essential for sleeping until you acclimate.
- Consider **white‑noise apps or masking sound tracks** if you have a device.

## 2. Clothing and Personal Gear

### Fabric principles
- Everything is **always damp**.
- You need things that:
  - Dry quickly
  - Don't chafe or rub when wet
  - Resist mildew
  - Are comfortable in warm, humid air

#### Recommended materials:
- **Loose, quick‑dry synthetic shirts and trousers**
- Optional **lightweight linen** if you prefer it
- Avoid:
  - Heavy cotton
  - Denim that never dries
  - Thick wool layers unless at very minimum

### What to bring

- **Underwear and socks:**
  - Extra pairs of **quick‑dry, synthetic, or merino wool** socks.
  - Anti‑fungal foot powder or cream to prevent moisture skin issues.
- **Layers:**
  - 1–2 **very thin long‑sleeve shirts** for protection:
    - When you're squeezed into narrow corridors.
    - For brushing against wet walls and moss-covered surfaces.
  - **Shorts or lightweight trousers** for daytime.
  - An optional **thin weather layer** like a rain shell or poncho:
    - Less "keeping rain off" than "letting moisture drip over you."
- **Footwear (this is important):**
  - **Closed‑toe shoes with good grip**:
    - The walkways are always damp and sometimes slick.
    - Eelbridge doesn't need "mountaineering" but slipping on a wet root-step can be dangerous.
  - **Synthetic or water‑ready walking shoes** or light trail runners.
  - Avoid:
    - Smooth-soled urban shoes
    - Open sandals unless you're very sure of your route and balance
    - Barefoot walking on public walkways—bioengineered surfaces are not "just dirt."

### Protection and accessories

- **Small, compactable umbrella** or hooded rain layer, even though the rain is more like mist.
- **Small pack cover or dry bags** for electronics:
  - Everything will be touched by spray and condensation.
- **Glasses or contact lenses**, if you need them:
  - Extra pairs of glasses or solution.
- A **light pack that you can wear on your front** in narrow passages:
  - You'll brush both walls with elbows; a backpack on your back can hit overhanging branches and cables.

### Optional but useful

- **Soft headlamp**:
  - Even with "continuous internal twilight," the side passages can be dark and "green dim."
- **Simple hat with a brim** to keep drips from falling directly down your neck.

## 3. Mental and Sensory Preparation

### I. "Always palimpsest": Expect overlapping layers of time and use
Every surface you see will bear:

- Time marks: rust stains, worn channels from rain, footsteps worn into board centers.
- Multiple layers of function: pipes, cables, trellises, quarantine tanks, gardens, all wired on top of each other.
- Micro‑organisms: mineral deposits, algae lace, "mill efflorescences."

This can be overwhelming or beautiful depending on temperament.

- **Practice noticing details without feeling you need to fix anything**.
- Keep a small journal or camera to:
  - "Talk" yourself into noticing the pattern instead of the noise.

### II. Slow time and "weary equilibrium"
Eelbridge runs on a long‑cycle rhythm:

- Water levels rising over months rather than days.
- Trees slowly "eating" bolts into themselves.
- Engines that "hum forever and rarely break."

If you are from a faster-paced world:

- Your internal urgency ("go, fix, change now") will be out of step with the city.
- Moods:
  - Frustration at "slow repairs"
  - Irritation at "always being wet"
  - Impatience with indirect routes and "everything propped on everything else"
- Coping:
  - **Plan to stay at least a week** if you can; after the first 3–4 days the soundscape becomes familiar pattern, not chaos.
  - **Use waiting time as conscious "listening time"**: to water, machinery, birds, language.

### III. "There is very little unused vertical"
Most space is stacked:

- Housing below and workshop above.
- Infrastructure pipes running over head.
- Ladders and stairs and "things you can duck under."

If you are prone to mild claustrophobia:

- In very narrow corridors:
  - Focus on the next two steps only, not the whole corridor.
  - Avoid large pack or backpack on your back.
- Look at the **texture of the wood or the stonework** instead of the amount of enclosed space; you're looking more at "detail" than "container."

### IV. Embrace non‑visibility: the sky is hidden
The sky is glimpsed only in reflected flashes on water tanks and glass panels.

- You may feel:
  - "Closed in"
  - "Out of touch with the world"
  - Slightly disoriented about where day ends and night begins.
- Mindfulness:
  - **Mark time by light changes and lamp cycles**, not by sun position.
  - Use your internal rhythms of meals and sleep to anchor "do days" and "nights" rather than relying on external horizon.

## 4. Practical and Social Preparedness

### Space and movement
- Spatial world:
  - **Main "street":** wide under the canopy (like a cathedral nave).
  - **Side "passages":** narrow, "duck under or work around" kind.
- You need to be:
  - **Flexible in body movement**: limbo under low cables, sidestep someone on the stairs.
  - **Okay with brushing your elbows on both walls** in some paths.
- Packing:
  - **Minimal baggage**:
    - One large pack or bag plus one small cross-body/shoulder bag.
  - Anything else will be awkward on ladders and stairs.

### Money and exchange
Eelbridge is clearly "urbanized" with:

- Central market with "algal bricks," "microgreen trays," "fermented broth."
- Rulers, inspectors, maintenance systems, "allocation and exchange" signage.

You'll need some way to participate in that economy:

- If you're coming from far:
  - See if you can carry small **conversation‑openers and trade‑items**:
    - High-quality metal tools
    - Interesting data storage
    - Musical instruments
    - Resilient fabrics or threads
- **No hoard of "rich" luxury goods**:
  - This is not a city of conspicuous opulence.
  - Small, useful, thoughtful items are better than glitzy valuables.

### Language and communication

- Be ready to **listen before speaking**:
  - The locals "talk low" in narrow corridors.
  - Voice‑echo off of curved trunk walls can tell you who is above and below.
- Your **body language**:
  - Keep your hands in view, don't shove, don't rush to dig into pockets.
  - Respect that "old place" = "older person who likely has been here longer."
- Ask:
  - "Where is the public gathering space / forum platform?"
  - "Where is the entrance to the main gallery?"
  - "Whom do I ask if I want to find a place to stay?"

### Asking "under" questions
Because you're not a local:

- Ask "permission" and "handhold" analog:
  - "I want to help; is there a small task I can learn?"
  - "If I need to move about without disturbing you, where should I walk?"
  - "If you see me doing something dangerous, what should I be warned?"

## 6. Space and Lodging

### Finding a place to stay
Look for:

- **Dwellings carved "inside or around the trunk"**:
  - They're described as "small microforest homes with rooms inside the trunk or cleat-like around the trunk."
- Ask at:
  - **Main forum platform at the end of the gallery**, "under the largest spread of the canopy."
  - **Market stalls**: vendors of microgreens, algae bricks, and broth often know who has room.

### Living in tree‑architecture
- "Warmth of human bodies and the glow of lamps" make leaves **glow faintly brighter** where people are inside.
- This means:
  - **Privacy is visual and cultural**: people can see where you are, even if they're not looking.
  - Respect curtains:
    - Close them when you're changing clothes or doing private tasks.
    - Don't assume if you can't "see in" clearly there's no privacy taboo.

### Damp and condensation
- Expect:
  - **Everything to be slightly damp or "freshly wet"**.
  - Condensation on inside surfaces of glasses or devices.
- To protect:
  - **Elevate your bag and bedroll** from direct floor contact if possible.
  - Use **synthetic sheets or quick‑dry fabric** rather than heavy cotton blankets.
  - Tactical:
    - Sleep on a thin mat or doubled cloth.
    - Drape your main pack in a poncho or pack cover if there's no good place to hang it.

## 7. Ethics and Etiquette in a Worn‑Tight Equilibrium

### Don't touch "invisible infrastructure"
- You will see many:
  - "Inspection symbols," access panels, maintenance signs.
- **Don't**:
  - Open panels "to see what's inside."
  - Adjust valves or switches "to help."
- **Do**:
  - Ask point to:
    - "What is this?"
    - "Is there a way I can help the maintenance crews?"
    - "If something looks wrong (leaks, strange smell), who should I tell?"

### Respect the "mood of the trees"
Some locals read "tree moods" from:

- Lights flickering in a trunk-based dwelling.
- Changes in the "sap flow" hum.

You don't need to believe this "magically," but you should:

- **Notice and mention but not mock**.
- "This is the city's metaphor for complex system health": it's their data visualization.
- If you are staying in a trunk-house:
  - Ask:
    - "Are there times when the tree's mood changes behavior?"
    - "Should I avoid certain actions on the trunk?"

### "Water memory"
The water reverses flow at night cycles; "high water" and "danger" marks are taken seriously.

- **Don't**:
  - Move or "play" with the marked level pillars on the canal edge.
  - Add "art" or carvings that could change how level is read.
- **Do**:
  - Ask how to "help the city" when the water approaches:
    - "Is there a way we can help move goods?"
    - "Are there tasks to clear lower drains or prepare?"

## 8. Daily Life Practices

### Staying safe on wet surfaces
- Always assume:
  - **Wood and stone steps are slightly slick**.
- Strategy:
  - **Small test step** when moving from dry to wet-looking surface.
  - **Use handrails**: on ladders and stairs.
  - If you see "deep wear" in the center of a step, that's a high-traffic spot:
    - It's likely "very smooth" and "perhaps especially slick."

### Handling hygiene when "always wet"
- Bathe or wash:
  - **Where you are told is safe for soap**.
- Wet clothes:
  - **Hang them inside** if you have access to a space with warm air and airflow:
    - Hot damp air doesn't dry clothes well; "moving" air is key.
- Foot care:
  - **Dry between toes carefully** each day.
  - Change socks daily; use foot powder if you are prone to moisture skin issues.

### Eating and drinking
- Market offers:
  - "Fermented broth," "algae bricks," "microgreen trays."
- If you have:
  - **Digestive sensitivities**, be cautious:
    - Start small:
      - Try one small portion of fermented broth, algae brick, etc.
    - Do not immediately "go full local diet" on the first day.
- Water:
  - **Always from "official" sources**:
    - Ask "is this water from the city system?" if you're unsure.

## 9. Route‑finding & "Where am I?"

Eelbridge is small but "very dense": you can walk across it in 30 minutes but still feel lost inside.

### Use landmarks
- Look for:
  - **Continuous "backbone" of pipes and conduits** along the main gallery.
  - "Entrance hall to the main gallery":
    - Wider floorboards, "most evenly worn."
  - Raised platform under "largest spread of canopy":
    - This is your forum / "town square."
- **Ring wall of old trunks**:
  - If you see it:
    - You're at the edge of the city.
    - To "center": move away from the ring.

### Ask for "low-risk" routes
- Locals will think:
  - "I can tell you a safe path to... where can you walk?"
- Ask:
  - "Is there a straightforward route from here to the main forum?"
  - "Are there any paths I should **avoid** because of weight or drop?"

You're not big, but the city is fragile in its own "everything already leaning on everything" way.

## 10. What to Know Before Leaving "Home"

### Summarize in three bullet points:
1. **Expect warm, wet, green, and close.**
   - Pack quick-dry clothes, closed shoes with good grip, and basic wound care.
2. **Expect complex "everything else" under, around, and on top of you.**
   - Don't touch "invisible" infrastructure, be attentive to patterns of sound and marking.
3. **Expect slow time and "weary balance" rather than excitement or danger.**
   - Bring patience and respect the "cathedral of life" you're visiting.

---

### If you remember only one thing
When you step onto the **central waterfront bridge** in the main gallery and feel the hum of machines below and the slow breathing of branches above:

- **You are inside a living organism that's also a city.**

Your best preparation is not to "fight dampness" or "speed things up," but to learn the **etiquette of being a good guest inside its body**.
