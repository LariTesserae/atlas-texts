To visit Nesh at the center of the Last Table of Deimos, you should not prepare for heroic adventure or high-tech tourism. You are preparing for a long stay in a hospice for a world: gentle, crowded, warm, quiet, and very fragile. If you come as you would to a modern city or even a rustic village on Earth, you will break something invisible simply by moving through it.

What you need to understand is: **Nesh is not a destination; it is a relationship you enter for a while.**

---

## 1. Mindset and Expectations

### 1.1. Slow is not a metaphor

Your normal pace—walking upright, taking long steps, making multiple decisions per hour—will be too fast for Nesh.

Think of yourself instead as entering a long, slow, ritual walk.  
“Volition‑walking” is what Neshians do, and visitors are expected to approximate it, not physically copy it.

Adjust your expectations of time:

- A day in Nesh is not measured in “things to achieve.”
- Instead of being “productive,” you will be a participant: a warm body, breathing, adding careful presence to the city’s density.
- You might spend the “morning” doing nothing more ambitious than sitting with a group in a niche, watching them work in the bone lattice, and occasionally being invited to lay your hand where they show you.

Bring this mindset before you bring gear:

- You will not be seeing “sights.”
- You will be listening to a place that is already at its final shape, listening to itself in the time it has left.
- Your role is closer to a guest in a monastery or hospice than a tourist in a city.

### 1.2. Intimacy and density instead of privacy and space

Nesh is not “private” in the way you are used to. There are no corridors where you can disappear.

- Expect to be near others always:  
  - Sleeping niches open to each other in low, linked chambers.  
  - Work, rest, eating, and minor ceremony happen in the same woven space of moss and lintels and silent walls.
- The city is designed to be navigable on all fours or seated; they rarely walk upright in the pavilion itself.
- You will be touched more often than you are used to:
  - A hand guiding your back to show you where to stoop,
  - a shoulder to your shoulder in the dark of a chamber,
  - a pair of legs brushing against yours as people shift around sleeping.

Plan to be that physically close to people and other animals for a long time.

If you are intensely shy or need high levels of personal space, consider this before you go:  
You might become emotionally exhausted by simply existing so closely with others, even though they are not invasive in the modern sense: they do not demand your secrets, only your presence.

### 1.3. Warm and close and loud in a calm way

The plateau will feel:

- Warm all the time, like a mild constant fever you quickly adjust to.
- Humid but not wet: sweat stays on you, but there is no water to splash, no rain to wash.
- Noisy, but not chaotic:
  - Humming windleaves,
  - animal croaking and chirring,
  - deep chorus of voices speaking in low murmurs,
  - faint, slow shifting of stone in the bone lattice.

It is a soundscape you must learn to sleep inside, like a forest at night. You are not escaping from it; you are gradually becoming part of the soundscape yourself.

## 2. Physical and Cultural Health

### 2.1. The body you bring with you

The Neshians are humanoid but adapted to this world. You are not.

You will probably need some external support to survive:

- **Air adaptation:**  
  The air of the table is thin and “sharp” in ways that make sense to them but not to you.  
  - Carry a discreet, low-powered air regulator and/or gentle oxygen supplement if you can,  
  - but be sure to use it as quietly as possible, without disrupting the soundscape of breathing and stone.

- **Diet and moisture:**  
  The official moisture of Nesh is dew skim on windleaf stones and the hidden chemistry of Neesh moss.  
  - You cannot depend on it as your main supply of water or nutrition.
  - **Bring your own water:** in a way that does not violate the sacred dryness of the city.  
    - Use sealed, internal containers or a thin dermal reservoir that does not leak visible water.
  - **Bring compact food** that you can metabolize without stealing from their moss and fauna.
    - You want to avoid being seen as a “drain” on the table.

- **Medications and hygiene:**  
  - Verify any medicines you depend on:  
    - Nesh specifically *avoids* heavy metal and complex electronics,  
    - so if your medicine requires cold storage or advanced tech, you might need to discuss it ahead of time with someone who knows the culture.
  - Bring minimal hygiene items:
    - Odorless.
    - Non-liquid where possible.
    - You do not want to introduce “wet” substances into their world.

**The key rule:**  
Don’t show them water as you know it.  
Don’t show them your tech.  
If you must use it, use it in the smallest way possible, in the fewest moments, and away from the center of the pavilion where the city’s “quiet” keeps the deep memory of the table.

### 2.2. Moving and posture

In your daily life on the table, you will be in low and crouched postures far more than standing tall.

- The central pavilion roof is low.
- Most adult Neshians must stoop near the centerline.
˜ You, likely taller and less used to this, will be closer to crawling on all fours or sitting with folded legs.

Prepare:

- Stretch hips, knees, and ankles:
  - Squats.
  - Deep lunge and kneeling hip flexors.
  - Cat–cow stretches so your spine can adapt to time on all fours.
- If you have chronic joint issues, think carefully:
  - This is not a place where you can easily revert to standing upright in the middle of a ceremony or work.

**Advice on movement:**

- When you first arrive, keep your head lower than the lintels of the niches you enter.
- Touch the bone lattice walls only when told or shown how to do so.
- Your large, upright body is a danger to the lattice’s integrity and to the faint dew films that sustain the city’s life. You must be careful of your edges.

### 2.3. Clothing

Consider what you wear as armor for the city, not armor for yourself.

- You need fabrics that:
  - Breathe but do not hold wetness.
  - Soften and muffle sound, not crinkle or clatter.
- **Colors**:
  - Neutral: stone-greys, deep moss greens, dim iron-browns.
  - Avoid anything bright or reflective; you will not match the tone of the world.
- **Coverage**:
  - Knees and elbows because you will spend time on the moss:
    - Low friction, soft materials.
  - Loose enough that you can bend easily.
  - Trousers that won’t catch on the low lattice at edges.

**Do not wear:**

- Clothing with metal fasteners that bump against walls.
- Shoes that leave impressions or damage the moss:
  - Better to be barefoot or sock-footed once the Neshians show you how they clean and prepare their feet.
  - If you must bring footwear, keep it soft and thin-soled, only for the rim movement where wind and sharp particles are more intense.

### 2.4. Body and breath

Your smell and breath will become part of the local environment in a way that is not abstract.

- Avoid:
  - Strong colognes or scents.
  - Foods that create strong odors through you.
- Your exhalations are part of the dew cycle:
  - You want to be present but not dominating the chemistry of the space with foreign substances.

From your perspective, this may seem selfless or even overly self-conscious; but from theirs, you are adding to the sealed garden of their world. You do not want to be remembered as the one who “tasted like wrong” when the dew formed.

## 3. Practical and Emotional Tools

### 3.1. Communication and learning

You will not be fluent in their language, but you will learn to read the *sound* of what they are doing before you understand the content.

- Come prepared to:
  - Listen more than speak.
  - Ask permission by gesture and eye contact.
- **Tools to bring** (if allowed by their rules on metal and tech):
  - Simple writing medium:
    - Thin paper or stone slates or woven fabric ledger you can write on without ink.
  - Soft pencils or charcoal for marking:
    - You want to avoid fluids that might spill.

If you are able to bring an interpreter (from your own world or a trusted neighboring one), choose:

- Someone who speaks softly,
- and prefers to sit beside you rather than stand in front of you,
- matching the low, slow posture of Nesh.

### 3.2. Timekeeping and routine

Your clocks and schedules are inappropriate here.

- Instead, bring:
  - **An internal sense of time**:
    - Practice before you leave:
      - Sit in a dark room or quiet garden for an hour or more without checking devices.
    - Learn to listen for your body’s cues rather than external alarms.
- **Anchor to natural cues**:
  - Starlight brightness,
  - faint shifts of windleaf tone,
  - changes in the behavior of the air-fish and other fauna.

You will live on the rhythm of:

- Light cycles of the sky,
- cycles of warmth and dew,
- the long, slow promenade of the volition‑walking.

### 3.3. Emotional and psychological tools

Nesh is, for the Neshians, a final, stable shape of a world that is otherwise ending below the rim. This can affect you as well:

- You may feel:
  - A sense of being “already too late” to stop anything or change anything.
  - A deep, calm grief wrapped in routine warmth and noise.
  - Oppression from the constant density of people and sound if you are not used to it.

Prepare yourself:

- Practice meditation or breathing exercises that help you be calm in crowded spaces.
- Decide what you want to do:
  - Observe?
  - Participate?
  - Ask some questions and then sit quietly?

**Remind yourself often:**

> You are not here to “fix” or “improve” Nesh.  
> You are here to witness the last shape of a place, and in return, to be careful with it.

## 4. Gear and Logistics (Minimal)

### 4.1. Tech you might bring

Given their fear of metal and technology that might “draw the sharp wind of the world,” you should be extremely cautious with any instruments you carry.

- If your travel requires:
  - **Navigation device**  
  - **Communication back to your own world**  
  - **Medical support or air enrichment**
- then:

  - Keep it:
    - **Small**  
    - **Hidden**
    - **Not visible during the moments of deepest presence**, such as when you are near the “memory of the table” at the center of the plateau.
  - Use it:
    - Only rarely.
    - At the rim of the table, where the wind of the world carries everything outward anyway.
    - Not inside the pavilion ring where the lattice and dew and moss are most sensitive.

**Your tech is a foreign weather system to them.**  
Treat it as you would treat a storm that you cannot fully control.

### 4.2. Tools for the self

Have a small, soft bag of essentials for your own comfort:

- A thin pad or cushion to sit on within the niches:
  - Avoids tearing the moss with repeated friction.
- A simple, cloth blanket:
  - For sleeping in the communal niches.
  - Not heavy enough to crush the bone lattice, but enough to muffle your shivers if you are sensitive to temperature.

**Do not bring:**

- Heavy metal objects,
- instruments with sharp edges,
- anything that could be mistaken as an “arm” that might damage the stone lattice.

### 4.3. Items of trade and relation

If you must bring gifts to establish relationship:

- Choose things that resonate with the world’s materials:
  - Fine, stone-colored threads or silk-like fabrics that mimic the textures of the city.
  - Simple, non-metal tools that match their own small toolset:
    - Wooden or ceramic needles,
    - thin stone knives,
    - connectors that do not sparkle.

Treat each item:

- As something to *join* the long, slow erosion of Nesh,
- Not as something to stand out conspicuously.
- You do not bring “amazing treasures”;  
  you bring **small additions** to the texture of the city.

## 5. Customs and Respect in the Pavilion

### 5.1. Entering the city

When you cross from the rim into the forest of Nesh:

- Expect a change in temperature and humidity.
- You will notice the softness underfoot:
  - Neesh moss like a deep carpet.
  - The bone lattice beneath you like a hidden, fossil chord of the world’s skeleton.

At your first lintel:

- Pause.
- Let them show you where to sit or how to move.
- Keep your hands lower than your shoulders when you touch anything:
  - This is not a strict rule but a pattern of their motion—you will see it.

### 5.2. Volition‑walking and participation

If you are invited to join in volition‑walking:

- You will not be doing the same thing they do:
  - They can “reindex” the stone lattice;  
    you can only be present and help them carry something small or follow in their steps.
- Your job is:
  - To walk with them,
  - To feel the stone under your palms and soles,
  - To show by your posture and rhythm that you are willing to be shaped by the city rather than to reshape it yourself.

**Do not attempt to guide the stone lattice growth**:

- Even if you could detect where to change it,
- you are a guest, not part of the long, slow memory of the table.

### 5.3. Near the “memory of the table”

At the center of the plateau stands the low circular chamber of the bone lattice—the memory of the table. It is not a museum or data storehouse but a place where they listen.

**Do not expect to understand why you are there, if you are brought at all:**

- You will be invited to sit near it,
- to listen,
- and maybe to touch the wall where they touch it,
- but not to speak or ask questions.

This is not a show they put on for visitors;  
it is something they do for themselves and, occasionally, include others into.

**Rules for you:**

- Turn off any visible device, if you can.
- Breathe quietly and evenly.
- Let your thoughts slow down.
- If you feel nothing, that is acceptable:
  - Accepting the “emptiness” of the center of the table is also a form of listening.

### 5.4. Handling the animals and moss

- **Do not pick moss or break branches of the stone-leaf trees**:
  - Each piece is connected to the lattice and the dew chemistry.
- **Animals**:
  - Small, soft-shelled invertebrates will move over you when you sit on the moss.
    - Let them.
    - They are part of the city’s life cycle and not dangerous to you.
  - Air-fish will be louder and may seem clumsy at close range:
    - Do not chase them.
    - Do not try to catch them.

You are tolerated by the fauna of Nesh as long as you do not become a new kind of predator or rearrange the environment around them.

## 6. Leaving and Returning to Your World

### 6.1. Reentry from warm to cold

When you go back to the rim of the table to return to your own world:

- You will feel the sharp drop of temperature and the thinning, harsh wind again.
- Emotionally, you may feel:
  - That you are leaving a closed, warm, noisy world to return to a great open, cold silence.
  - A sense of having left something unfinished, even if you went only as a silent observer.

**Be prepared:**

- To have trouble sleeping without the low, constant chorus of Nesh.
- To feel regret for having introduced your body’s chemistry and breath into such a delicate system.
- To be unsettled by the contrast of speed and space when you return home.

### 6.2. Ethics and memory

When you tell others of your visit:

- Do not glamorize it or “sell” it as a destination, as if it were a tourist stop.
- Remember:
  - You were not watching “primitive people” surviving;
  - you were visiting the last shape of a world maintained by a careful, slow human culture.
- Describe the city gently:
 overwhelm from the physical intimacy,  
 the noise,  
 the warmth,  
 the long rhythms of the stone and moss and dew.

Your honesty should discourage the thoughtless visitor from going there just to say “I have been.”

---

### Final Summary

Before you visit the sanctuary of Nesh at the center of the Last Table of Deimos:

- Adjust your mind to slow, intimate presence and shared physical space.
- Prepare your body to crouch, sit, and occasionally crawl; to sleep in noise and warmth.
- Bring your own water and food discreetly, so you do not stress the fragile dew system.
- Minimize metal, technology, and visible liquids.
- Let your movement and touch be gentle and always lower than you instinctively think.
- Accept the density of people and fauna and sound as a feature of the city, not a flaw.
- Remember that you are entering a final, stable form of a world, not a frontier.

If you can do these things, then you can go to Nesh not as a curiosity of another world, but as someone who might, for a short span, belong quietly to the long memory of the table and to the last breathing of the stone and moss and bone lattice of the city.
