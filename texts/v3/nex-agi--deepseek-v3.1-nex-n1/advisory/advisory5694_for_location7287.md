To stand in the Cathedral of Hammered Metal is to be inserted into the body of another world’s finished god.

An ordinary human traveler does not “survive” this place in the usual sense of scrambling against danger and escaping. The danger here is not of death but of transformation by stillness: your mind might long before your body falters. You don’t prepare gear so much as ways of remaining *you* inside a place that is not made for change.

Below is a detailed preparation for such a visit.

---

## 1. Understand the kind of place you’re entering

### 1.1 Conceptual posture

This is not a dungeon or hostile planet. It is:

- Physically closed but infinitely detailed internally  
- Deafening but absolutely stable  
- Warm, dry, and tolerably breathable  
- Devoid of any food, water, or organic life  
- Timeless in the sense of “no progress, only cycles”

Imagine you are being lowered into the heart of a thing that has already done all its living and is now only *being*—perfectly, endlessly—itself.

### 1.2 Framing your visit

Before you travel, decide *why* you are going and what you will define as “done”:

- Pilgrimage to see what it means for a civilization to finish itself  
- Practical study of a closed-world system  
- Respite from the chaos of change (risky but sometimes desired)  
- Mission to retrieve information or a specific structure from its pattern

Make sure you have an exit condition or a time-limit built in (see “Timekeeping,” below). This world will not remind you it is time to leave.

## 2. Core body needs: staying alive in a finished metal body

### 2.1 Breathing and dust

- The air is dry and thick with metallic dust.  
- You can breathe it, but not indefinitely.  
- Long-term exposure to fine dust is likely harmful to lung tissue and may contain heavy metals or oxides.

You need:

- **N95–equivalent filtration mask** or better:
  - Prefer something with a filter rated for fine metal dust and oxides.  
  - Mask fit is crucial; you’ll be wearing it for the entire stay.
- **Backup filters**:
  - Enough spare filter cartridges for the duration of your stay, plus extras.
- **Potassium-based anti-smoke / particulate filter** if your tech allows:
  - Helps to neutralize some of the chemical irritation from oxides.

Even if the air is chemically safe to breathe for a short period, your lungs do not cope well with long-term dry dust. This is not optional gear.

### 2.2 Water and food

Water:

- There is *none*.  
- You will lose water through your skin and respiration even in warm, dry metal air.
- You need a **closed hydration system**:
  - A hydration bladder or collapsible container with enough for:
    - Baseline: ~2 liters per day
    - Plus extra margin days depending on your stay target.
  - Slip a tiny bit of *electrolytes* into the water if you can; sweating here will be subtle, but real.

Food:

- There is nothing to hunt or gather.
- Bring:
  - **Non-perishable, low-waste, high-calorie foods**:
    - Nutrient bars, nuts, sealed preserves, etc.
    - Avoid anything that requires water or excessive chewing (your mouth will be dry).
  - **Extra rations** beyond your planned time in case you’re delayed exiting or lose time.

### 2.3 Temperature and clothing

The temperature is warm, not scalding:

- No strong temperature gradients; the heat feels like a body that has been working for a long time.
- This is actually comfortable—but prolonged exposure without good clothing will be dehydrating.

You should wear:

- **Light, long-sleeved base layer**:
  - To protect skin from dust exposure and contact with metal surfaces.
- **Over-layer**:
  - A breathable but durable fabric layer; think thin, durable synthetic or flexible armor.
- **Head covering**:
  - Light scarf or close cap to protect hair and scalp from dust.
- **Eye protection**:
  - Safety glasses or sealed goggles if you can get them.
- **Simple, thin gloves**:
  - To touch the metal surfaces without fear of cuts or contaminants from the oxides.

Loose, comfortable but not bulky clothing. The world is not physically hostile. You want to be able to sit or lie on the metal floor if needed.

### 2.4 Sensory and physical protection

- **Ear protection / active noise cancellation**:
  - The sound is immense: deep hammering heartbeats on metal + continuous grinding shriek.
  - You cannot rely on “getting used to it.” Your nervous system can be permanently damaged.
  - A combination of:
    - High-quality musician earplugs or  
    - Active noise-cancelling headphones (if tech is allowed in your multiverse travel)
  - These are not optional; you will otherwise be unable to think or rest.
- **Optional: white noise generator**:
  - If you can, generate a neutral soundscape to overlay the cathedral’s noise—something to give you a sonic anchor to our usual reality.

Protect your feet:

- **Solid boots or shoes with protective, thick soles**:
  - The floor is not going to tear you, but it is uneven, textured like bark or scales.
  - Make sure they are comfortable for long standing/walking.

## 3. Timekeeping: when the world doesn’t mark time for you

The world has:

- No day-night cycle  
- No temperature changes to mark time  
- No sound patterns except a steady drumbeat that you cannot set a clock by  

You will lose time if you do not strongly externalize it.

### 3.1 Mechanical timekeeping

- **Simple mechanical watch**:
  - Not electronic or dependent on signals.
- **Set it before you arrive**:
  - Define an “anchor day” on your world and try to keep your sleep schedule synchronized to *that*.
- **Plan maximum duration**:
  - Before departure, decide:
    - “I will not stay longer than X days.”
  - Use that limit to trigger exit plans regardless of how peaceful you might feel.

### 3.2 Body signals and routine

- Pay attention to:
  - Sleepiness  
  - Hunger cues  
- Use these to create a “schedule”:
  - Eat at fixed intervals (e.g., every 6 hours).
  - Sleep in “shifts” even if the environment doesn’t change.

### 3.3 Mindful alarms

If you have access to any kind of timer device that can repeatedly beep:

- Set it to:
  - Remind you to eat,  
  - To check your water,  
  - To stop whatever you’re doing and survey *yourself*:
    - How are you emotionally?  
    - Are you forgetting to think about returning home?

## 4. Mental and emotional preparation

### 4.1 The mind’s danger: the timeless, finished state

The danger here is not violence or weather; it’s:

- The loss of time sense  
- The seductive sense of “already finished”  
- The possibility of wanting to stay in a place where nothing changes, because change feels like effort  
- The gradual absorption of your self into the single artifact’s geometry

You can train:

- **Grounding techniques** before you go:
  - Practice repeating something like “I am not this place; I will return to change.”
- **Keep a simple mantra**:
  - Example: “This is not my home. This is not my final form.”

### 4.2 Memory anchors

- Bring small, **familiar physical objects**:
  - A ring from your world  
  - A small token from someone you love  
- These objects remind you that:
  - You exist in relation to others,  
  - You have a history and future of change.

### 4.3 Writing and journaling

- **Bring paper notebooks and pens**:
  - Electronics may fail in strange environments; analog is more resilient.
- **Daily log habit**:
  - Each “sleep cycle” or specified time, write:
    - Date and time by your watch  
    - Short summary: how you feel, what you observed  
    - Any change you *allowed* in the dust (you will be the only change-agent)
- **Review of entries from home**:
  - Before leaving, set yourself to read your previous days’ entries:
  - This helps you detect if you’re drifting:
    - If you see less will to leave, or more contentment with “nothingness,” that’s a warning sign.

### 4.4 Expectation of altered voice

- When you speak:
  - Your voice will not echo.
  - It will be swallowed immediately into the distant hammer roar and grinding shriek.
- Prepare:
  - **Whispers to yourself** instead of expecting shouts to carry.
- If you speak to companions:
  - Use hand signals or short, clear phrases near each other’s ears.

## 5. Sensory and relational strategies

### 5.1 Hearing in the roar

- Experiment:
  - Try to hear *yourself* over the noise.
- Use:
  - **Earplugs or noise-cancelling** as above.
- **Internal music**:
  - If you can, sing quietly or hum internally;  
  - This gives you a rhythm of your own instead of only the world’s.

### 5.2 Touch and texture

- The floor is:
  - Textured, “bark-like,” and uneven.
- Use:
  - **Bare-handed or gloved touch** to trace hammer patterns.
- Why:
  - This links your body to the history of the world.  
  - You can feel the “growth rings” of impact events; doing this intentionally can be meditative rather than disorienting.

### 5.3 Vision and light

- The world has:
  - Uniform dim, amber light.
  - No shadows or sharp gradients to help gauge depth.

You can handle this by:

- Bringing **simple small, non-blinding light sources**:
  - Headlamp with adjustable brightness  
  - Candlelight-equivalent safe light source (if your tech supports)
- Use them:
  - To create small areas of contrast *you* control:
    - Highlighting texture near your feet  
    - To mark your “camp” or sit spot

- **Important**:
  - Avoid light sources bright enough to damage the rust-colored atmosphere or disturb the dust’s suspension; use the lowest setting that lets you see clearly.

### 5.4 Dust and chemistry

- **Do not deliberately disturb the dust**:
  - If you must move:
    - Move slowly and deliberately.
- **Avoid breathing directly near any surfaces**:
  - If you need to inspect something:
    - Do not hover over it with your mouth open.

You cannot know the precise nature of the oxides or films. The dust may be magnetically or otherwise suspended in a way that is not mere airiness. Respect that.

## 6. Movement and exploration

### 6.1 The platform’s curve

- The floor is:
  - Not flat but curved like a shallow dish or inside of a shield boss.
- This means:
  - You will feel a slight gravitational pull toward the center if the world obeys normal-like gravity.
- **Walk with caution**:
  - If you feel even subtle force toward the center, be wary:
    - Don’t let curiosity drag you into the central indistinct haze too quickly.

### 6.2 Boundaries

The world is sealed:

- No visible doors, no windows, no edges.
- You will not find:
  - A ladder to the ceiling dome  
  - Clear exit gates in the “slit between ribs” (unless your travel allows you to treat them as exits)

Given:

- **Don’t plan on escaping through the edges of the world.**
- **Plan your departure from the same “point” or the same conceptual method as you used to arrive.**

### 6.3 Route-marking

- Use:
  - **Non-permanent, light-dust traces** to mark your path:
    - Very slight disturbance of dust on surfaces with a tool or stick.  
    - E.g., clear arrow patterns in the dust.
- Purpose:
  - To avoid circular wandering if the platform is large.
- Concept:
  - You are not “defacing” this world; you are adding the smallest possible layer of change to return where you began.

### 6.4 Center exploration

The center is under a haze:

- If you decide to approach:
  - Go slowly and pause often.
  - Check at each interval:
    - Are you still aware of time?
    - Are you still wanting to return?

There might be nothing spectacular but only more intricate pattern or “finishedness” at the core. Don’t expect a final bang of revelation.

## 7. Interaction with the world’s “life”

### 7.1 Etchings

- Pale faint lines that:
  - Deepen slowly into what they already are.
- How do you interact?
  - **Do not try to alter them with your tools or hands.**
  - Be content to observe:
    - If they are expressions of the world’s nervous system, then your touch may be a kind of violence in the long run.

Consider:

- Acknowledge them as:
  - The world’s *internal language*.
- Your role:
  - To understand and witness, not to change.

### 7.2 Nodules

- Small metal buds:
  - Warm, possibly pulsing.
- They:
  - Are clustered in precise symmetry groups (three, five, seven).

If you touch:

- Do not press them.
- Consider:
  - They might be equivalent to pressure points or nodes of whatever flow system underlies the whole artifact.
- Observed:
  - If contact changes you mentally or emotionally (e.g., you feel drawn to stop moving), reconsider:
    - You might need to withdraw from the center and return later.

## 8. Exit and return to “change”

### 8.1 Before you leave home

- Decide:
  - **Maximum duration** (e.g., “3 days of my time no matter what”).
  - **What will trigger immediate departure**, even if not planned:
    - Intense depression  
    - Strong desire to stay forever  
    - Any sense of disowning your former home as “less real”

### 8.2 Exit process

- When you decide to leave:
  - Return to your approximate arrival point.
- Use:
  - Your external device or method as provided by your multiverse travel capability.
- Mentally:
  - Prepare to be reimmersed in change:
    - The sudden presence of weather, of time, and of noise that *ends*.

### 8.3 Aftercare

- You will likely experience:
  - **Re-entry shock**:
    - Loudness of normal world feels startling  
    - The presence of change feels like chaos or aggression  
- **You will carry**:
  - The memory of a perfectly finished state.

- Practice:
  - **Reintegration rituals**:
    - Sleep in normal time cycles.  
    - Engage with small tasks, like cooking or cleaning, which involve change.  
    - Talk to friends or therapists if needed:
      - The “finishedness” can be addictive to those who fear failure or endings.

### 8.4 Knowledge you might bring back

- You will have insight:
  - Into what “completion” looks like without death or decay:
    - A pattern that can persist indefinitely without change.
- You can:
  - Use this to understand:
    - The difference between “finished” and “dead”
    - The value and cost of not changing
- Consider:
  - Writing a longer report after returning:
    - For the benefit of future travelers who might be tempted by a world without gradients.

## 9. Final perspective: why you need not fear this place, even if you should not stay

This is a safe world in many ways:

- No predators, no weather catastrophes, no risk of physical collapse.
- The temperature and air are survivable for short to medium lengths of time.

Its danger is not in what it does *to you*, but in what it *is not*:

- It is not change.
- It is not time.
- It is not relationship.

You can walk safely through its metal heart, provided you:

- Bring your own time and change with you in the form of:
  - Water and food  
  - Timekeeping  
  - Memory  
- Anchors to your own world and to others.

If you prepare carefully, you can return richer from having seen the ultimate end-state of an artifact: a world that has finished being made and now only *is*—without fear or hope, only presence—until the end of time.
