To visit this place is to make yourself into a kind of ghost in a living body: a small, warm, fragile thing that moves by a different clock than everything else around you.

---

## 1. Before You Even Think About Going Down There

### 1.1. Medical and biophysical basics

First you have to sit down with those who know how your body works and say:

“I’m going into a place where:

- the ceiling is a hundred meters above me,  
- the light resembles a bruised, post-sunset glow,  
- the air is cool and dry and tastes like metal and old root,  
- nothing is on the normal human timescale,  
- and there are no humans, no tools, and definitely no emergency room.”

At minimum, you need:

**Respiratory protection**  
Even if the air is technically safe, “safe” over geologic time is not the same as safe for you. This place has been running on a chemical system utterly different from Earth’s.

- Bring reusable masks / filters rated for exotic atmospheres,  
- plus a compact analyzer strapped to your wrist or chest that can:  
  - measure oxygen fraction, CO2, trace volatiles,  
  - and detect any sudden shifts in major components.

Plan to **wear a mask at least at the beginning** until you measure:

- oxygen level in human range,  
- no accumulating toxic gases like CO, H₂S, or unusual organics,  
- safe humidity for your lungs (not just “it feels fine”).

**Heat and cold management**

The air is described as “cool, hollow with the chill of deep earth.” That might mean:

- stable temperature,  
- but low enough to leach heat from you steadily over time.

Pack:

- **Base layer** designed for deep caves or undercity: moisture-wicking, synthetic,  
- **Insulated mid layer** you can vent easily (no sense sweltering if you mis-guessed),  
- **Outer layer** resistant to abrasion from bone walls and rough rock,  
- **Clothing anchors** (clips, loops, D-rings) so nothing swings loose and starts banging against walls or creatures.

Your **sensation** will be the least reliable indicator: “cool but not wet” might feel comfortable while your body quietly loses energy into the stone and space.

---

### 1.2. Light and vision: living under dusk

Light “enters first as a bruise-colored smear, then as faint afterthought of brightness.”

You cannot rely on:

- “sunrise,”  
- “daytime,”  
- “moonlight through the window.”

You will be inside **permanent twilight**, further dimmed by distance and shadow.

You need:

**Non-intrusive lighting**

- A headlamp with long battery life and low-blue modes,  
- Secondary hand-lamp with adjustable beam spread (from narrow spot to wide flood),  
- **Diffuse lanterns**, with soft output you can stand to live near for hours.

The lights of the city itself are sparse:

> “The glow of one lamp does not reach the next.”

That means:

- Your local lighting must be enough to cue your body clock,  
- But you must not blind yourself or spoil your night vision.

Strategy:

- **Mark distinct “times”** by changing your light color:
  - Warmer light in evening,  
  - “Dawn” simulated with a slight increase in brightness and color temperature,
- **Earmark a dark period** where you avoid even the faint-glowing plants and walk by only what’s provided by the caverns, so you don’t lose circadian rhythm entirely.

---

## 1.3. Food and water: make yourself a self-contained island

“There is no water as you understand the word.”

Do not count on finding:

- a river,  
- a dripping seep,  
- clean groundwater,  
- dew on anything.

“Time-stored” plants may be chemically hostile to you—and they may not contain water molecules at all, in the sense you’re used to. Best to assume:

**All fluid and all nutrition you use will come from what you carry.**

Plan for:

**Water**  
- An **overestimation** for water needs:
  - Factor for walking, climbing, possible humidity deficit, and longer-than-expected stay,
- **Redundant containers** in separate pack compartments,
- Water filters and/or chemical tablets, even though you won’t rely on local sources,
- **Hydration discipline**:
  - Drink by schedule, not by thirst,
  - Track urine color and skin turgor,
  - Remember “not thirsty” doesn’t mean “adequately hydrated.”

**Food**

This is not a place where you’ll forage:

- The fauna are likely indigestible or even toxic to you,
- The plants are “time-stored” with chemistries that may have nothing to do with Earth-normal biochemistry.

Bring:

- High-calorie, **compact, long-shelf-life foods**,
- **Nutrition bars**, **dehydrated meals**, **nut butters**, **oats**, **dried fruits**,  
- Spices and tea or coffee substitutes to mark ritual “meals,” so you don’t feel like you’re eating just to keep walking.

**Never** take your mouth near anything local:

- no tasting plants,
- no touching hands to mouth after handling bone or resin without washing,
- no drinking from anything that drips or glows.

---

### 1.4. Sanitation and waste

Since you’re a self-contained island and you don’t want to introduce new chemical or biological signatures to a system that hasn’t seen Earthlike life:

- **Pack out all waste**, including:
  - urine,  
  - feces,  
  - hygiene materials,
- **Use solid and liquid waste containers** designed to seal tightly and be inert,
- **Avoid polluting** the floor or dust with anything you carry:
  - no plastic crumbs,
  - no soap, detergent, or scent in the city itself.

Think of yourself as moving through a museum that’s also a breathing organism.

---

### 1.5. Communication and survival

You will be **alone**: no humans, no technology infrastructure.

At least:

**Communication**

- Bring:
  - **Long-range radio** beacon if you can,  
  - **Satellite transponder** if the surface is still reachable,
- Ensure there’s **scheduled check-in**:
  - Someone above knows where you entered,
  - and what you’ll do if contact is lost.

**Safety buffer**

Take:

- Extra **battery packs**,  
- Extra **lighting**,  
- Simple **repair kits**: tape, cord, patches,  
- Basic **first aid** including:
  - painkillers,  
  - antihistamines,  
  - bandages,  
  - splints,  
  - antibiotic ointments.

Even if you don’t break bones, you might slip on the polished trough floor, or snag yourself on bone and resin.

---

### 1.6. Psycho-spiritual preparation

You’re going into a place that:

- doesn’t share your time-scale,
- has never seen humans,
- and lives “not as construction but memory.”

This will not feel like visiting a ruin city. It will feel like visiting a **cathedral that is also a beast**.

You might feel:

- insignificance,  
- a deep, low hum of dread,  
- or awe that can slide into disorientation.

**Grounding practices** become practical equipment:

- Simple **rituals**:
  - Morning: “I am here. My body is still mine.”
  - Evening: “I am small. This is its home, not mine.”
- Minimal **journal**: not just to log curiosities, but to:
  - track real days against your internal horizon,
  - mark what you felt at which “light,”  
  - prevent memory drift.

Consider **limiting your stay**:

This isn’t a place where you “camp out” indefinitely. It’s a place you visit, like a deep-sea observatory or polar ice shelf.

- **Set a strict end-date** before you feel the need for it,
- Plan to leave **before** the novelty or loneliness can become dangerous.

## 2. Getting into the City: Down the Cracks

### 2.1. Entrance from the surface world

“Light enters first as a bruise-colored smear… seeping through cracks in the ceiling a hundred meters above.”

You’ll probably descend through those cracks:

- **Vertical ropes, lines, or ladders**,
- Gear for:
  - **Helmet**,  
  - **Harness**,  
  - **Descending/ascending devices**,  
  - **Redundant safety lines**,  
  - **Gloves**,  
  - **Knee pads**, because you might crawl at the top and bottom.

Assume:

- **Rocks might be sharp**,
- Ceilings may be jagged or unstable,
- A fall from ceiling height is **fatal**.

Rappel down slowly; take the descent as meditation:

- Notice how air changes temperature and taste,
- Watch how sound changes from surface wind and noise to the **thunder-growl of breathing**.

---

### 2.2. Orientation on the floor of the world

“The city spreads in a low, uneven sprawl along the basin floor… the river is long gone, leaving a smooth trough of polished stone.”

As soon as you touch the floor:

- **Stop**,  
- Turn off major lights for a minute to let your eyes adjust to the true dimness,
- Note that the floor used to be a river: **it is very smooth**.  
  - Even if bone and resin have textures, your basic risk is **slipping and falling**.

Use:

- **Footwear with aggressive grip** suitable for polished limestone and cave floors,
- **Walking poles** if you’re prone to imbalance.

“The streets are shallow grooves worn into the stone floor over millennia… The buildings are carved from trunks… The wood is black, polished from age…”

Visually:

- Streets will be faint, gentle depressions,
- Buildings might look like dark, low islands in the stone floor,
- Edges may be blurred into dust and plant.

You’ll need to **create your own navigation system**:

- **Marking system**:
  - Use **very discrete, non-permanent tags** (like small chalk dots or reflective tape you can remove),
  - Never carve anything into bone or wood;  
    that’s like carving into someone’s ribs.
- Keep **careful maps**:
  - Use time (“27 minutes walking from the entrance crack,” not “third turning left”),  
  - Note landmarks that are stable to human eyes:
    - distinctive shapes of ribs,  
    - unique fungal clusters,
    - changes in floor polish.

If “the city is not planned but remembered,” treat **your** memory of movement through it as the only kind of map that will be true.

## 3. Living in a Bone-and-Resin City

### 3.1. Shelter and choice of “place”

“The buildings are carved from trunks… squat and wide, with low roofs rounded to echo the curve of the ceiling far above. The space is open but not sky-open.”

There is **one level**, **sparse density**, **no second stories**.

Choose a place:

- **Not embedded** directly into one of the huge creatures’ sides,
- **Away from obvious ribs and vents**—unless you want to be constantly near the breathing sound,
- Close enough to:
  - your entrance line,  
  - but not so near that ascenders/descenders clutter your living space.

“Enough space for a person to walk, turn, and breathe without touching.”

You will feel:

- **never crowded**,  
- always **watched**, by scale and depth and the breathing creatures,
- **lost between pockets**, as if each island of light is a tiny station in a dark ocean.

**Setting camp**

- **Tarp or bivvy**, rather than a tent,  
  - To keep your dust and spore from contaminating too much,
- **Sleeping pad** thick enough to insulate you from cold floor even in “cool” air,
- **Bivy-sack or light liner** to keep dust out of your sleeping bag,
- Arrange **low** and **compact**:
  - You don’t want to stick up above the ribs like a target or intrusion.

---

### 3.2. Light and circadian rhythm inside

You’ll have your own lamps plus sporadic fungal phosphor:

- “Lamps of phosphorized fungus hang in niches… their light is weak and patient.”

The weakness of the light sits well with **slow** and **unchanging**.

Set your own **cycle**:

- **Simulated dawn**:  
  - Increase your light gently,
- **High day**:
  - Work-hours brightness,
- **Dusk**:
  - Dimming,
- **Sleep**:  
  - Total darkness except the faint distant glow of the creatures themselves.

Doing this keeps you from drifting:

- into twenty-hour “days,”  
- or forty-eight-hour “days,”  
- which will happen if you follow the city’s apparent static.

---

### 3.3. Eating and drinking in the caverns

“No rain… no river… no water as you understand the word.”

Your meal times are moments of **returning to yourself**, not of interacting with the environment. Make them:

- **Ritualized**:  
  - Same place each time,
  - Same routine of preparation,
  - Same order of checks:
    - hydration level,  
    - body temperature,  
    - comfort level overall.

Avoid **eating** while:

- **moving**—especially not on the polished riverbed floor,
- **distracted by the scale and depth**—you might choke if you gasp suddenly.

Eat **more** than you think you need:

- Under long low-energy, awe-struck conditions, you underestimate appetite,
- Bring a caloric surplus and **eat to schedule**, not hunger alone.

---

### 3.4. Hygiene and microecology

“The plants are networks… spanning the length of the cavern floor… The buildings are embraced… softened into the same porous texture.”

You, by contrast, are a **foreigner**, **wet**, **metabolically fast**.

Treat your body care like biohazard containment:

- **Wash with wetted cloths**, not by running water,
- **Dry thoroughly**:
  - The air is cool but not dry enough to mean “you’ll dry quickly,”
- **Keep nails trimmed**:
  - To minimize damage if you touch surfaces,
- **Avoid skin contact** with:
  - resin,
  - bone edges,
  - glowing plant material,
- **Cover any cuts completely**:
  - To avoid exchange of fluids with anything local.

Even if the local creatures are immune to you, you are not to them.

## 4. Interacting with the Environment and “Inhabitants”

### 4.1. Physical interactions with bone and resin

“The walls are not straight… neither are the streets… Everything is remembered.”

So:

- **Don’t try to “improve” paths**,
- **Don’t break** or scrape **anything**,
- **Don’t collect** bone splinters or resin chips as souvenirs,
- **If you need to touch**, use:
  - **open palm**, not fingertips,
  - **soft, padded gloves**,  
    so you exert less localized pressure.

The “streets” are worn into **grooves** over millennia:

- You are not going to wear your own track;  
  you’re just adding footprints to dust.
- Walk lightly, **mid-groove**, where the stone is polished rather than cracked.

---

### 4.2. The plants: time-stored, glowing, not “for” you

“The plants are low… clothed in thick, succulent flesh… time-stored… chemistry is slow.”

Their role is:

- Network building,
- Slow illumination,
- Possibly structural support for the creatures.

Not **food** or **medicine**.

For you:

- **Don’t eat** anything,
- **Don’t inhale deeply** near broken plant surfaces,
- **Don’t pick** to “see better”;  
  the faint glow is enough if you let your eyes adjust.

The **fractal patterns** they form on walls and bone:

- Treat as **sacred geometry**,  
- That is, something to be photographed or drawn, **not touched**.

---

### 4.3. The creatures: the city that is also flesh

“There is only one species… the bodies are continuous… like hills of exposed flesh and bone… The buildings are embedded into their sides.”

When you realize the **walls are their ribs** and the **lamps are their lights**, your behavior must change.

**Distance and respect**

- **No climbing**, no hacking away,
- Keep **watchful**:
  - If you see “eye” pools of polished stone set in surfaces,  
    don’t lean directly into them,
  - Treat the eye areas as **faces turned up**.

**Movement and time**

The creatures move:

- “Great limbs that are both flexible and stolid, like roots that learned to walk.”
- They **move** across **ages**—not minutes.

You may not even see them change in your time there.

To you:

- They might be **background**,  
- Or **the floor could be subtly re-arranging** around you as you sleep.

**Check your surroundings**:

- Each “morning,” mark:
  - Which ribs you can see,  
  - Which hollows,
  - Which paths between,
- Compare after a few days:
  - If those shapes have changed, **do not panic**;
    it might be the city’s slow breathing.

**Safety near living architecture**

Think:

- **No high structures**,  
- **No loose gear**,  
- **Nothing that might vibrate or screech or clang**,
- Avoid making:
  - **vibrations**,  
  - **high pitched noise**,  
  - **regular rhythm**,
- It might not anger them, but you **don’t know** what they interpret.

If you are:

- **woken by** a deep, groaning shift of the floor,
  or  
- **feel the ribs above you move**,

Hold still:

- **Stay low**,  
- **Turn off your lights**, except one minimal, non-flashing source,
- **Listen**,  
- **Do not shout** or play music to cover the fear;  
  let the sound flow over you and then recede.

---

### 4.4. The “inhabitants”: not human, not tool-users

“There are no humans… The inhabitants are more like tenants who have become part of the wall.”

They have been shaped **into** the bone and flesh of the creatures; over generations, the bodies shifted:

- perhaps **mating**,
- perhaps **extending**,
- perhaps **budding**,

so that the **architecture of the city’s ribs** is also the **architecture of their bodies**.

“The fauna here are not the huge, slow-moving creatures.” The text cuts off, but implies:

**The smaller lifeforms that are “the fauna” are the ones who actually move around between ribs.**

For you, this means:

**They are the locals.**

You are, in their world:

- **fast**,  
- **bright**,  
- **unpredictable**.

**Approach pattern**

When you encounter them:

1. **Slow down** all of your motions:
   - No quick darting,
   - No sudden arm waving,
2. **Keep your light dimmed**:
   - Don’t flash them,
   - Let them see you by their own glow,
3. **Observe**, rather than **interact**:
   - Note:
     - their size,
     - their limb arrangement,
     - whether they ignore or track you,
4. **Do not touch**:
   - No matter how curious you are,
   - No matter how they resemble “animals” or “people,”  
     they are not.

**Recording and ethics**

- **Document** with:
  - camera,  
  - notebook,  
  - sketched diagrams,
- **Do not alter**:
  - their environment,  
  - their paths,
- **Do not teach**:
  - No feeding,  
  - No offering items,
- You don’t know what “teaching” means in a place where memory **is** form.

You are not here to befriend;  
you are here to be the **passing shadow** of another species, in and then out.

## 5. Managing Your Mind and Soul in the Deep

### 5.1. Time and claustrophobia

“The whole settlement feels less like a construction than a crystallization… that has forgotten how to move.”

Around you:

- Everything **is already what it is**,
- **Change is glacial**, not just “slow.”

This might:

- Make **time** feel meaningless,
- Cause **claustrophobia**:
  - Even though the chamber is regional in scale,  
    you still feel “under” something,
- Or **space**:  
  - The emptiness between phosphor points might feel  
    like stepping between stars in a dark galaxy.

Countermeasures:

- **Keep a schedule**:
  - Even a loose one,
  - mark day “number,” not just “how long I’ve been here.”
- **Keep one external anchor**:  
  - A radio check-in,  
  - A countdown to return time,
- **Take short exploratory “outings”**:  
  - Always starting from and returning to a same “home” building,
  - So you never feel you’ve drifted forever.

---

### 5.2. Visual and auditory overloads

“The air is thick with the sound of breathing… deep, resonant inhalations and exhalations.”

The sound:

- Comes from **floor, walls, ceiling**,  
- **Never stops**—like a machine that has forgotten how to stop.

You might:

- **Tune it out** after a while,  
- Or suddenly **become overwhelmed** by it.

When that happens:

- **Sit down on your pad**,  
- Put **hands on ground**, feel the vibration,
- Remind yourself:
  - “This is not malice.  
    This is just the world taking a breath that has lasted longer than my species.”

You can also bring:

- **Soft earplugs**:
  - Not to remove the sound,
  - but to filter it,
- **Audio noise muff** that:
  - reduces the edge of the thunder-growl,  
  - but lets you still hear if you call.

For the eyes:

- The **glow of resin**,  
- The **phosphorescence of plants**,  
- The **bruise-colored smear** of light above,

might become hypnotic or **depth-blind**.

If you start feeling:

- dissociated,  
- like you’re floating between continents,

turn **one** bright light on for a minute:

- **Re-anchor** yourself to:
  - your body,
  - your pack,
  - your notebook,
  - the floor under your toes.

---

### 5.3. Loneliness and “non-human” presence

“There are no humans… No tools… No technology.”

The city itself now **is** the body of the living beings.

So you:

- are surrounded by **presence**,  
- yet utterly **alone of your kind**.

This is **creepy**, **sacred**, and **dangerous** all at once.

To handle it:

- **Speak**, quietly, to yourself:
  - Describe what you’re seeing,
  - “That’s a rib of something. That’s a plant growing. This is the floor.”
- **Read old texts**, if you brought:
  - poetry,
  - science notes,
  - personal letters,
- **Write**:
  - Not just “found this,” but  
    “this felt like this when I saw it.”
- Maintain the **voice of your own species**, so you don’t lose it.

If you start feeling:

- **like you belong here**,  
- or **like you’re becoming part of the wall**,  

that is a **red line**.

That means:

**You’re ready to leave or overstay.**

At that point:

- Do **something** that asserts:
  - you’re not of the wall,
  - you’re not of the ribs,
  - you’re not of the phosphors,
- This might mean:
  - **packing your bags**,  
  - **turning on a bright, human-made light**,  
  - **sending your check-in message**,  
  - **rehearsing your exit route**,  
  - **marking the day as “time to go back.”**

---

### 5.4. Departure and long-term effects

Even leaving isn’t simple.

**Afterwards**, you will likely:

- **Dream** of the bone walls,
- **Feel** the rumble of breathing under your feet when you stand on regular surface ground,
- Notice **lighting** differently:
  - “This is too bright,”  
  - “This isn’t like the phosphors.”

Plan:

- **Debriefing**:
  - With medical professionals,
  - With psychologists who understand:
    - deep environments,
    - solitary time,
    - “non-contact” encounters,
- **Time to readjust**:
  - To normal sky,  
  - To normal human noise,
  - To **rapid** human movements.

The city under the world may be:

- a **locked**,  
- **self-contained**,  
- **time-preserved**,

memory of life.

You **visit** it,

but then you **go back**.

### 5.5. Final equipment checklist (short form)

For **survival**:

- Mask/respirator and atmospheric analyzer,  
- Insulated layers and outer cover,  
- Climbing and safety gear for entrance/exit,  
- Grippy shoes and walking poles,  
- Lights and batteries: multiple, redundancies, long-lasting,  
- Water containers and food supplies,  
- Basic first aid and repair kit,  
- Waste and sanitation system,

For **orientation**:

- Non-permanent marking materials,  
- Maps and notebooks,  
- Timekeeping device,  
- GPS or radio beacon,

For **mind**:

- Journaling materials,
- One or two comforting texts,
- Simple rituals to structure your day,
- Knowledge of your own limits **before** you hit them.

### Closing

“Light enters first as a bruise-colored smear…”

So you enter:

- **Carefully**,  
- **Preparedly**,  
- As **guest** not explorer,

to a city of bone, resin, and slow heartwood,

where **the buildings are ribs**,  
**the streets are grooves in memory**,  
and **the breathing lasts longer than civilizations**.

You’ll be tiny,  
and fast,  
and wet,

walking through **dust that is millennia of footprints**,  
under a **hundred-meter ceiling of stone**.

That’s how you should prepare:  

As **fully**, **gently**, and **self-containedly** as possible,

so that when you leave,

the city that is also a beast  
**hardly notices you were ever there**.
