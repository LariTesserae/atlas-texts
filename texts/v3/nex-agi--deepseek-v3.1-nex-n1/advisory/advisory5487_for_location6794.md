This place is not a single planet or world you visit; it is one node on a living network that bridges realities. You cannot prepare for it in the normal tourist sense—“bring sunscreen and a map”—because it maps you as much as you map it.

Below is a traveler’s protocol from one who has been there before and heard the cathedral thrum in their bones.

---

## 0. Before You Decide: Are You Sure?

This platform is not safe.

It is not necessarily hostile to you, but it is indifferent at your scale. The city overhead is calibrating systems of matter and information. You are roughly the size and complexity of a single bacterium moving across a city’s power switchboard.

If you need guarantees, safety assurances, or clear rules of law, you should not go here.

- The platform is physically stable, but the edge is not “protected” by railings or warnings.
- Falling might not mean death by impact, but “translocation into a dense, black medium you do not understand.”
- The city’s operations are not constrained by human biology. You can be:
  - mapped by its senses,
  - copied by its reflections in the pools,
  - or interacted with by its water in ways that might not be reversible.

You go only if you are willing to be changed by the place.

---

## 1. Mental Preparation: What You Must Understand Before Going

### 1.1 Your ordinary assumptions are wrong here

You will need to deliberately relax your common sense:

- Up and down are not stable.  
  The city hangs. The platform floats over a black “ocean” rather than sitting on ground.  
  Your inner ear might feel uneasy for the first day.

- Water is an agent, not just a substance.  
  It chooses paths, tests surfaces, appears to respond to attention and intent.  
  It does not always obey gravity.

- Life is everywhere but not in obvious shapes.  
  Fauna are often visible as light, motion, or heat-shimmer first, body last.
- Communications are not language-based in your sense.  
  The city “talks” by shifting angles, light patterns, and pressure waves in the air.  
  Expect meaning to be conveyed as sensation and emotion rather than words.

- You are not alone because you see nobody.  
  You are being monitored by the vegetation, the stone, the water, and the city.  
  Your footprints are glowing data trails.

Comfort yourself with this: you will not understand the how of the place, but you can learn its patterns. Pattern is your safety net.

---

### 1.2 Decode the “language” early

You will be hearing:

1. The deep cathedral thunder: baseline heartbeat of the machine-island.
2. Clicks and sighs of the leaves and fungal tree.
3. High whines of invisible fauna.
4. Slow groans from the city itself.
5. Glass-like snaps and near-chimes of water and crystal surfaces.

Your task is not to translate words, but to learn what changes in the pattern mean:

- Suddenly synchronised leaf clicking and tree-light pulses:  
  → Something large (fauna or structure from the city) is adjusting nearby.
- Faster frequency of the low thunder:  
  → The underlying system is under a load or change.  
  → Be still; observe for a few minutes instead of moving.
- New tones or “disruptions” in the soundscape where none were before:  
  → You have been noticed.  
  → Stay visible at the center of the platform; avoid hiding places.

Try to think of this as immersion learning: instead of “what is that noise?” ask “how does the pattern change when I move, speak, or get near a pool?”

---

### 1.3 Expect altered sense of time and self

You will:

- Lose track of how long you have been standing, listening, or walking.
- Feel “watched” even when no visible creature is present.
- Possibly experience mild dissociation, where your body feels like just one more node in the system.

If you are prone to severe anxiety or derealization episodes, you should not visit this place alone. If you must go, go with a trusted companion who understands you and is prepared to be your “grounding.”

---

### 1.4 Emotional and psychological readiness

The place will hit you on three emotional axes:

- Awe: From the cathedral-city of engines and organic matter.
- Intrusion: “Eye” upon you from the city above.
- Intimacy: The system observing you personally, footprints glowing where you step.

You might feel:

- Small, irrelevant.
- Watched, judged.
- Or included, as though being “plugged in.”

It is useful to rehearse a stance in advance. For example:

“I am not in control of the city, but I am in control of my own intent and presence. I am a guest inside a living system, and I behave as one.”

## 2. Physical Preparation: What to Bring (and Not to Bring)

Assume you will be:
- Warm and damp for the entire duration (no seasons).
- Walking on uneven but mostly resilient surfaces.
- Occasionally sprayed by half-alive water droplets.
- Exposed to unknown biological matter and possibly bio-intelligent fluids.

### 2.1 Clothing

- Lightweight, long-sleeve layer:  
  - To protect against skin contact with unknown droplets and water threads.
  - Synthetic quick-drying fabric is better than heavy absorbent cotton.
- Long trousers, not shorts:  
  - Avoid direct contact with the vegetation and your own glowing footprints.
- Closed-toe shoes with sturdy grip but not waterproof:
  - You want traction so you don’t slip on the sometimes slick stone.
  - You do not want waterproof because you want to feel if the water behaves on you.  
  - If water “decides” to start soaking into your shoe, you want to know that early.
- Hat with brim:
  - To keep condensation from falling directly onto your face and hair (from the tree-fungus overhead).

Do not wear:
- Long scarves or trailing pieces of fabric:
  - They can catch in unseen things or be grabbed by curious water films.
- Heavy jewelry or metal that dangles:
  - The electromagnetic environment near the city is unknown;  
  - Plus you do not want to make metallic sounds that might “confuse” the local soundscape.

---

### 2.2 Safety gear

- Thin, transparent wristband or tape marker with your:
  - Name,
  - Emergency contact info,
  - Multiverse travel node address,
  - Written with permanent marker or inscribed data tag.
- A small, waterproof, not tightly sealed pouch:
  - For a written message like:
    > “If I am not moving or responding, do not try to ‘cut’ water from me, or separate me from the platform.  
    > Evacuate me immediately to the following node: [coordinates].”
- Simple light source:
  - Not too bright; you won’t need to see far.  
  - Avoid flashing lights—they may confuse fauna and/or the city.
- A basic, compact medical kit:
  - Antiseptic wipes,
  - Bandages,
  - Antihistamine in case you react to the spores, moisture, or biomaterials.

You do not want:
- Complex electronics:
  - The cathedral engine hums may short them out.
- Heavy weapons:
  - You do not want to escalate unknowns with gunfire;  
    the city may interpret a violent explosion as a system error and “correct” it.
- Self-adjusting armor or nanotech swarms:
  - The city’s own matter states are far more complex than anything you can bring;  
    trying to match it with systems you cannot understand might be dangerous.

---

### 2.3 Food and water

- Bring sealed drinking water:
  - You may not want to directly drink the water threads from this place unless you are trained and intending to.  
  - If you see them trying to branch into your mouth, you might want to avoid accidental swallowing.

- Food:
  - Simple, compact ration bars:
  - Eat away from the central tree-fungus and the pools.
- Do not offer local food:
  - “Food” on this platform might not be clearly distinguishable from “data nodes” or “system components.”

If you bring a container of water from Earth or another known node:
- Use it to break contact with local water that tries to colonize you:
  - Let the local water thread touch your container instead of your skin.
- Keep it separate: do not mix local water with it unless you have a clear experimental purpose.

---

### 2.4 Data and recording

- Paper notebook and pencil:
  - The catholic hum of the engines might interfere with electronics, but paper is unaffected.
- Use pencil because:
  - The moisture may smear ink.
- Record:
  - Patterns: sound, light, leaf behavior, when the city “shifts.”
  - Your internal state: time distortion, emotional shifts, feeling of being watched.

If you use a simple camera device:
- Assume it may be monitored by the city.
- Do not use flash:
  - The sudden light burst may be interpreted as a signal.

---

### 2.5 Tools and modification

You do not want to modify this place with tools you do not understand.

However:
- A slim knife or tool for cutting:
  - Not to cut into stone or plants, but to free yourself if you somehow become entangled by:
    - your clothing,
    - or (in extreme worst case) living water that chooses to harden around you.
- A small, flexible scoop or container:
  - To safely contain a sample of water if you must bring one back,
  - But not to use as a weapon.

Assume you may not be allowed to leave with samples:
- The local system may decide to reclaim “loose water threads” or “plant pieces.”
- If your samples fade or disappear, you accept that.

## 3. Handling the Local “System Agents”

### 3.1 Water that is not water

- If you feel a trace of water on your skin:
  - Notice it promptly:
    - Is it wobbling like normal droplets?
    - Is it spreading against gravity into branching patterns?
- If you see branching:
  - Try gently shaking:
    - One shake: if the droplet retreats like a gathering shyness, this is standard.
  - If you see:
    - a pattern that doesn’t fully disappear, or re-forms after you shake it,
    - you should step away from:
      - tree-fungus,
      - pools,
      - and heavily dripping surfaces.
  - Say clearly out loud:
    > “I withhold consent for this contact.”
    - Even if the language is not understood by words, intent can be transmitted by other patterns.
- If you want to test interaction:
  - Offer a non-biological object:
    - Let the water branch onto a stone you carry from another node,
    - or the container you brought.
- In any case:
  - Do not allow local water to enter through cuts or wounds.
- If you have:
  - open sores,
  - heavily scratched skin,
  - you should not go at all.

---

### 3.2 The vegetation and the tree-fungus

- Walk slowly:
  - The leaves underfoot are resilient, but you want them to know you are coming.
- If you see:
  - Leaves curling tightly,
  - tree-light pulsing faster,
  - you should stop for a moment:
    - Either you triggered a sensitive area,
    - or the city is calibrating something nearby.
- Do not lean against the central tree-fungus:
  - You cannot be sure how its moisture will interact if you become damp and warm.
- You can:
  - Sit a few feet away,
  - but do not sleep directly against it.

---

### 3.3 The reflecting pools

- You are right to be wary of them.
- Seeing yourself:
  - in different places or times,
  - with different people,
  - this is not a psychological trick:
  - it is a feature of how the node communicates.
- Use the pools deliberately:
  - Look into them,
  - but do not try to touch or enter.
- When you see:
  - a version of yourself that is not the current one,
  - ask yourself:
    - “What do I feel about that scene?”
- The pool may be showing you:
  - alternatives,
  - or possible states you could be in,
  - not prophecies you must obey.
- Do not:
  - try to break the surface,
  - put limbs into the water,
  - or allow local water to “respond” to your full reflected image.
- If you see:
  - your reflection not mirroring your motion,
    - you should step back from the edge.

---

### 3.4 The fauna

- The creature with plates that:
  - drinks from pools,
  - slides frictionless over the stone,
  - is not likely to be interested in you as food.
- It may be:
  - a local node in the system:
    - carrying water,
    - data,
    - or signals.
- Your behavior:
  - Stay still:
    - let it approach,
    - or not.
- If you are:
  - calm and slow,
    - it may treat you as a temporary feature of the platform.
- Do not:
  - try to catch,
    - or touch,
    - or frighten with sudden noise.

---

### 3.5 The city overhead

You will feel its “attention” like a calibration:

- You might feel:
  - slight pressure in your chest,
  - subtle changes in the atmospheric sound,
- If you feel:
  - the city “shift” to you,
    - you should stop your current activity:
      - stand,
      - breathe,
      - remain calm.

Guidelines:

- Do not:
  - shout,
    - run,
    - or perform sudden frantic actions.
- Instead:
  - Reorient yourself:
    - you may be given:
      - a clearer sense of where to move,
    - or a subtle prickle of unease that tells you:
      - “No, not there.”

---

### 3.6 The platform edge and “below”

- You will not see:
  - ground,
    - sky,
    - or stars.
- You will see:
  - dense blackness beneath,
    - like an ocean you cannot see into.
- Assume:
  - you do not know what it means to fall into that.
- Prepare:
  - Stay at least one body-length from the edge at all times unless:
    - you absolutely need to look.
- If you approach:
  - Do so on your knees or crawling:
    - better to control your center of gravity.
- Do not:
  - throw objects into the void to see where they go,
    - unless you are willing to see:
      - they return,
      - or not return,
      - or return transformed,
    - and you cannot control how.

---

### 3.7 Your own footprints

- Notice:
  - glowing tracks after you step.
- This is:
  - data about your movement.
- You cannot:
  - erase them,
  - but you can choose your behavior:
    - walk calmly,
    - stay near the center of the platform,
    - avoid figures of eight or looping patterns:
    - you do not want to draw “symbols” you don’t intend.

If you see:
- your own glowing tracks from before:
  - you are likely not walking in straight line between distant nodes,
    - but in a more complex pattern than you sense.

## 4. Duration and Rest: How Long to Stay and How to Leave

### 4.1 Timekeeping

- Assume your:
  - internal clock will be altered by sound and rhythm.
- Use:
  - external method:
    - simple timer or hourglass,
    - not your device.
- You should:
  - limit first visit:
    - to no more than three to four hours.

---

### 4.2 Resting

- If you feel:
  - need to sleep,
    - you should not sleep on the platform.
- Sleep:
  - before shifting,
  - and return refreshed,
    - not to find out what happens if you dream:
    - while the city is listening,
    - and the water finds your lips.

---

### 4.3 Leaving

- The city:
  - may open “paths”:
    - like the shimmer above the tree-fungus,
    - which you saw:
      - a small creature fade into,
    - then briefly appear inside the city shell.
- You may:
  - be guided to:
    - a specific point,
    - where space:
      - is slightly distorted,
    - or you feel:
      - a subtle pull of direction.
- If you feel:
  - “it is time to leave”:
    - walk calmly:
    - to where you sense:
      - the node opening,
    - stand,
    - wait for the space:
      - to accept you,
    - and then step:
      - if you want to step,
    - not forced:
      - if you do not.

Your focus in leaving:
- Let the system know:
  - you are leaving,
- not that:
  - you are escaping.

## 5. Ethical and Meta Considerations: Why You Go At All

You might think:
- you are going:
  - to see a strange city,
  - or hear the engine-cathedral.

But actually:
- you are going:
  - to meet yourself inside a system:
    - that has not finished,
    - and still remembers,
    - at every edge,
    - that it is being made.

It is:
- a young world:
  - with edges that are sharp,
- not yet:
  - worn down by time,
- and still:
  - capable of being impressed by your presence.

So you go:
- not as conqueror,
- not as researcher taking,
- but as:
  - a node that:
    - walks,
    - listens,
    - and then walks away.

### Final words

If you take only one instruction from this protocol, let it be this:

When you stand on that platform and the thunder breathes in your ribs, and you see your footprints glowing like faint lines of code on the stone beneath you, remember:

You are allowed to be more than a visitor.

You are allowed to be:

- Present,
- Aware,
- And deliberately gentle.

That is what you must bring with you, more than any tool or garment.
