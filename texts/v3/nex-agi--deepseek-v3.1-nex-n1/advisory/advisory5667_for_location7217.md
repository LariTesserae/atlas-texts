A human traveler who wants to survive this place—let alone enjoy it—shouldn’t think of it as a city. Cities are places you can walk into and get lost in. This is more like walking into the body of a sleeping machine-god.

It should be treated with that same reverence.

## 1. Physical safety & environment

### 1.1 Basic life support & gear

**Pressure and depth**

You are very deep. It doesn’t feel like it because you are in a little air pocket under a glass ceiling, but the column above you is huge and that field is all that keeps it from crushing you like a soda can.

Do not assume your “traveler” physiology or any tech you have is automatically equal to that machine’s sophistication. If something you carry generates “shields” or “bubbles” of safety, they’re dancing on top of this system’s physics—not mastering it.

Preparations:

- Have **at least two redundant life-support systems** that  
  - Maintain temperature  
  - Maintain oxygen  
  - Maintain pressure regulation  
  - Are **not all dependent on the same principle or power source**
    - One might be internal/enhanced biology  
    - One might be external tech / suit systems  
    - One might be a “talismanic” or conceptual backup (e.g., a contract or artifact that ensures “you don’t die in this timestream” or “you can return from any unviable state”)

- Assume that **local electromagnetic conditions, gravitic anomalies, and that field holding the water back are hostile** to your tech:
  - Shield your devices from strong fields and humidity.
  - Your main connection to the rest of the multiverse—your anchor device, home-return mechanism, or whatever your multiversal “lifeboat” is—should be hardened, **physically sealed**, and not exposed to the water.

**Humidity and cold**

The air is cool and perpetually damp. You will feel cold creep into you slowly.

- Bring clothing that:
  - Maintains warmth even when slightly damp.
  - Is not **heavy when wet**, in case something breaks and water gets in.
- Synthetic fibers or advanced-tech fabrics that actively wick moisture and keep a microclimate against your skin are better than wool, cotton, or anything that gets waterlogged and heavy.
- If you have a suit, ensure **internal condensation management**; you do not want water building up inside your helmet or mask.

**Lighting**

The light is dim, greenish-gray, and unchanging.

- Bring:
  - **At least three sources of light** with multiple modes:
    - One omnipresent, soft “ambient” light to keep your body calm.  
    - One narrow, strong beam for visibility.  
    - One emergency strobe or beacon if you get lost or injured.
  - Batteries or power systems that **do not rely on local EM fields**, solar, or oxygen; you’re under glass, ocean, and machinery. Local power might be hostile to your devices.

**Acoustic protection**

“The machine never sleeps.”

If you are human-scale, you are not built to live under a constant machine-level symphony.

- **Hearing protection is non-optional.**
  - But you must still hear alarms, local voices if such exist, and the subtle changes of the machine.
  - Use adaptive protection or implants that:
    - Suppress steady background noise.
    - Preserve sudden changes, sharp transients, and nearby human speech-range signals.
- If possible, bring something **musical or anti-noise**:  
  - A personal sound environment or device that feeds you a pattern that can **interfere beneficially** with the machine soundscape, so your brain can rest.  
  - Nature sounds or structured white noise might be less jarring than trying to ignore the machine with silence.

**No sleep and the need for rest**

“Noon and midnight look the same down here.”

Your circadian rhythm will die here without help.

- Bring:
  - **Light management tools**  
    - Glasses or lenses that filter out blue-green spectrum during your “night.”
    - A small personal source that mimics **sunrise / sunset**, to help your body clock keep track.
  - A formal **schedule**:
    - Define fixed “sleep shifts” and **treat them like operations in hostile environment**, not flexible suggestions.
    - Even if you do not feel sleep pressure, you must lie down, rest, and enter low-stimulation periods.

### 1.2. Water and the interface machinery

You are separated from the ocean by glass and a subtle field anomaly.

Treat that water as **chemically and informationally aggressive**.

- Assume **no direct contact with local water** unless:
  - You understand how that field works.
  - You have tested it on expendable equipment or your own nonessential biology.
- Before arrival:
  - **Impress upon yourself that the water is not water:**  
    It is a medium dense with “threads” and structures you can’t fully understand.  
    - Do not drink it.  
    - Do not expose open wounds to it.  
    - Do not let any of your devices directly ingest it into their systems.

**If you have a suit or exoskeleton:**

- Envelope **must be sealed against liquid intrusion**.
- Check seals:
  - Joints  
  - Gloves  
  - Helmet  
  - Ports for cables or sampling tools  
- Test seals:
  - Under simulated pressure and humidity.
  - Under **noisy** electromagnetic environments; you don’t want your seals electromagnetically softened or “persuaded” by this machine-city.

**Sampling (if you must):**

If you are a scientist or researcher and you plan to study the water or threads:

- Use **triple-contained sampling protocols**:
  - Primary container: captures sample.
  - Secondary container: holds the primary.
  - Tertiary container: encases the secondary with your own field/tech to ensure it doesn’t influence your ship/homeworld.
- Consider **one-way only**: containers that never open to your environment again—samples stay sealed until you can take them to a facility you fully understand.

### 1.3. The machine noise as a physiological hazard

The machine is not just loud; it is infrastructural. It might have:

- Infrasound or ultrasound components.
- Rhythms that entrain to brainwaves.
- Patterns that upset balance or induce nausea.

**Before you go:**

- Run tests on yourself:
  - Expose yourself to long-duration machine noise simulations.
  - Check how you respond:
    - Irritability  
    - Headache  
    - Sleep disruption  
    - Tinnitus  
    - Visual disturbances or motion sickness  
- If possible, develop **personal neural entrainment countermeasures**:
  - A pattern generator you can wear or carry that produces rhythmic signals that **insulate you** from local machine entrainment without cancelling it out entirely—you don’t want to interfere with the machine’s operation.

**During the stay:**

- Monitor:
  - Headaches  
  - Changes in mood or irritability  
  - Difficulty concentrating  
  - Dreams full of circuitry or water / threads
- If you notice:
  - You cannot stop focusing on the machine sound,
  - You start hearing “meanings” in the noise,
  - Your sleep becomes broken or nonexistent,
  
  then you **must leave** temporarily.  
  This is not mystique; it is cumulative neurological strain.

### 1.4. Food, water, and waste

**Water supply:**

- Bring:
  - **All drinking water with you.**
  - More water than you would need on the surface for the same time period, because:
    - Humidity and breathing in cool air will increase fluid consumption.
    - You will lose more water than you think through your skin and breath in the damp environment.

**Food:**

- Assume:
  - **No food is grown locally.**  
    There is no “biological ecosystem” you can recognize.
  - Airborne microorganisms may be absent or incompatible with your biology.
- Bring:
  - **All nutrition in sealed, stable forms**:
    - Meal bars or pastes with complete nutrient balance.
    - Nothing that requires refrigeration, unless you have self-powered cooling tech.
- Avoid:
  - Eating anything that has been in contact with the local environment (condensate on surfaces, mysterious growths, etc.) unless you have gone through extensive testing protocols.

**Waste management:**

- You cannot just dump waste into this environment.
- Bring:
  - **Closed-loop waste processing systems**, or
  - **Sealed containers** you can take back with you or jettison in a way that doesn’t affect the local machine-ecology.
- Assume any **organic matter** you release into this environment is:
  - A foreign body,
  - A potential source of unpredictable reactions,
  - Something that might provoke **detection, quarantine, or “repair”** by the system.

## 2. Psychological and subjective experience

### 2.1. You are a small thing in a big system

You will feel:

- Your **littleness** against the mass of water and machine.
- Your **irrelevance** to the machine’s purpose.

You can either **be broken by that** or turn it into something useful.

- Prepare **existential anchors**:
  - A strong sense of purpose: why are you here?
    - To learn?  
    - To document?  
    - To negotiate?  
    - To survive?
  - A clear metric of success:
    - “I count this visit as successful if I leave with X data and Y intact psychological function.”
- **Not**:
  - “I will understand everything.”
  - “I will become one with the city.”

You **will not** understand everything. Accepting that is a safety mechanism.

### 2.2. The machine as a presence, not just noise

The “machine” doesn’t feel like a factory. It feels like something **breathing and processing**. You will feel:

- The **pulse** of the light through the capillaries.
- The **constant thrum** of the water outside.
- The sense that **decisions are happening** everywhere except you.

You may start to think:

- “It is aware of me.”
- “It is talking to me, in a pattern I almost understand.”

This is not impossible, but it is dangerous.

**Psychological preparation:**

- Before you enter:
  - Build **firm boundaries** in your own mind:
    - You are not part of the machine.
    - You do not want to **merge** with the machine.
    - You do not want to become **code, water, or thread**.
  - Have **grounding statements**:
    - Simple reminders like:  
      - “I have a body.”  
      - “I care about people in other worlds.”  
      - “I came here to return.”
- During:
  - If you start feeling:
    - That you want to walk into the water,
    - That the machine is asking you to “step through,”
    - That you no longer care about leaving,
  
  then **treat yourself as contaminated or compromised**, and:
    - Stop all contact.
    - Return to your airlock.
    - Refer to the “Exit” protocol section below.

### 2.3. Data-light and the “handwriting” in the walls

The walls are **not** decorative. They are computational.

- You should train your eyes and brain to **not fall into pattern-seeing too deeply**.  
  - If you find yourself trying to decode patterns like:
    - “This pulse means X.”
    - “This light gradient is telling me about the future.”
    - then **step away**, physically break line of sight, and rest.
- The **handwriting effect** is likely:
  - A combination of:
    - Data structures,
    - Your brain pattern-matching,
    - Possibly subtle influence on your perception.

**Technological protection:**

- Use **pattern-smoothing glasses or interfaces**:
  - They can blur or filter out frequencies that encourage you to see “messages.”
  - They can also present a neutral overlay pattern, so your eyes are not left to fill in the void.

**Mental tools:**

- Practice:
  - **Deliberate attention control**:  
    - Decide where you will look:  
      - The floor.  
      - A neutral tool in your hand.  
      - Your notes screen.
    - Do not allow yourself to drift for long periods into the machine-light show.
- If studying the machine is necessary:
  - Use **recording devices**, and then analyze the recordings later in a safer environment.
  - Limit yourself to **short, discrete observation sessions** with mandatory breaks.

### 2.4. Isolation and boredom

You will be:

- Alone, or nearly alone.
- In a space with **no diurnal cycle**.
- Surrounded by machine sound that doesn’t change in the way you expect.

This combination will lead to:

- Boredom,
- Sensory deprivation,
- And then suddenly **sensory overload**.

To prepare:

- **Design a schedule**, including:
  - Work tasks.
  - Physical exercise.
  - Study time.
  - Rest.
  - Communication with the outside, if you have it.
- Bring:
  - **Books, stories, or datasets** not stored solely on one device:
    - Redundancy in case of device failure.
    - Topics that are **firmly not about machine or city in your immediate environment**—external to your world helps to keep you grounded.
- Practice:
  - **Mindfulness, meditation, or mental techniques** that help you maintain:
    - Emotional neutrality.
    - Stable mood without external stimulation.

## 3. Interaction with the machine-city

### 3.1. Respect, not control

You are not here to **take over** or **optimize** the machine-city.

Even if you technically can:

- You probably don’t understand:
  - The scale of what you’re interfering with.
  - The cascading consequences.
  - What might depend on this machine’s stability.

**Principles of interaction:**

- **Do not inject new code into the machine.**
- Do not modify:
  - The capillaries.
  - The field.
  - The water.
- **Do not try to “talk” to the machine** unless:
  - You have been explicitly trained to do so.
  - You understand the protocol.
  - You have clearly defined:
    - What you are going to say,
    - What you are willing to happen in return,
    - And what you **will not** tolerate.

### 3.2. Possible modes of contact

If your purpose is to **understand** or **communicate** with the city-machine, then you should define:

- Are you a:
  - **Observer**  
    - Collects data without influencing system.
  - **Guest**  
    - Presents yourself to the machine, if there is a way, and waits to be addressed.
  - **Probe**  
    - Uses small, reversible experiments to see how the machine responds.

You **must not** be all of these at once without clear separation.

### 3.3. Protocols for “guest” mode

If you want to treat this machine-city as a host:

- **Prepare a symbolic “self-presentation”:**
  - A simple pattern that:
    - Represents your identity:
      - Your name,  
      - Your world,  
      - Your purpose.
    - Is not too aggressive (e.g., large volume of data, or complex dynamical structures).
- Use:
  - **Non-invasive transmission**:
    - Visual light.
    - Sound.
    - Mechanical vibration on the floor or rock wall.
    - Anything that **does not require you to break physical seals or touch water**.
- Wait:
  - For a long time.
  - Do not assume:
    - Silence is rejection.
    - Response is necessarily friendly or hostile.
- **Do not attempt multiple “broadcast methods” all at once.**  
  The machine might interpret them as conflicting messages or as attack behaviors.

### 3.4. No touch, unless you know how

In general:

- Do not:
  - Touch the capillaries.
  - Lean on the glass ceiling.
  - Press heavy or sharp objects against any surfaces.
- Assume that:
  - Any surface you touch can:
    - Sense you,
    - Sample you,
    - Or **interface into you**.

## 4. Social and ethical considerations

There might be no people here you would recognize, but that doesn’t mean:

- There is no agency.
- No ongoing project.
- No value system that cares about its own continuation.

**Ethics for a traveler in this context:**

- **Do not consume:**  
  You don’t take anything that isn’t clearly “waste” or explicitly given.
- **Minimize footprint:**  
  - Oxygen use.
  - Waste production.
  - Heat signature.
  - Any signatures you don’t understand the meaning of.
- **Do not assume abusability:**  
  Just because you can’t see a person doesn’t mean you should treat the machine or its water as a resource to exploit.

If you discover **any other humans or similar entities:**

- You must be:
  - **Slow** to trust.
  - Observant:
    - How they interact with the machine.
    - Any signs of:
      - Dependence,
      - Psychological entanglement,
      - Loss of self.

They might be:

- Prisoners.
- Symbiotic components.
- Pilgrims, like you.
- Or something that looks like a person but isn’t fully anymore.

## 5. Medical and health contingency

### 5.1. Baseline health requirements

Before you go:

- Ensure you are:
  - **Well-rested** before entering.
  - Not in:
    - Mid-illness,
    - Emotional crisis,
    - Or deep grief.

This environment will exacerbate:

- Anxiety disorders.
- Claustrophobia.
- Sensory integration disorders.
- Fatigue.

### 5.2. Medical kit

- **Standard human trauma kit**:
  - Bleeding control.
  - Pain relief.
  - Anti-inflammation medicine.
- **Psychiatric kit**:
  - Anti-anxiety medication.
  - Sedatives/hypnotics for sleep, if medically safe for you.
  - Anything your doctor deems necessary for:
    - Delirium,
    - Panic,
    - Depression.
- **Anti-seizure and anti-epilepsy tools**:
  - In case machine-light patterns or acoustic fields trigger abnormal neurological responses.

Assume **no rescue** from:

- Local medical infrastructure.
- Any local “emergency services.”

---

### 5.3. Infection and contamination

While you don’t see biological life:

- Assume **some kind of analogue exists**:
  - Computational “viruses.”
  - Data structures that can propagate across your tech.
  - Threads that might be able to interact with your nervous system.

Protect yourself:

- Anything that:
  - Directly interfaces with your brain,  
  - Or your main multiverse anchor device,  
  should be **airgapped from local machine interfaces**.
    - No wireless connections.
    - No cables that you didn’t bring yourself.
    - No physical interfaces that share data with the machine.

If you experience:

- Hallucinations,
- Unexplained physical sensations,
- A sense that **something else is thinking in your head**,

then you must assume contamination and treat it as a medical emergency:

- Stop all contact with the environment.
- Remove yourself to your safest environment.
- Use **pre-defined “purification” protocols**, if you have advanced tech:
  - Memory scans.
  - Neural resets.
  - External “mind-state backup” restore, if available.

---

### 5.4. Emergency exit and escape plan

**Before you go:**

- Define:
  - **Maximum duration** of your visit:
    - “No longer than X days.”
    - Shorter if certain symptoms appear.
  - A clear **exit condition**:
    - “Any two of:  
      - Persistent machine-entrainment hallucinations  
      - Two sets of hardware failures  
      - Any structural breach of the airspace or water barrier  
      → I leave immediately.”
- **Practice the exit process**:
  - How you activate your multiverse travel method.
  - How long it takes.
  - What you must do:
    - Step away from any interfaces.
    - Close down equipment.
    - Destruct or encapsulate any samples.

Do not:

- Delay your exit because you’re “close to understanding” or “on the verge of breakthrough.”
- Override your own pre-defined rules because the moment feels different.

The machine will be here after you leave.

## 6. What you can bring, to stay sane

### 6.1. Physical anchors

- Bring:
  - **One object** strongly tied to your home or self-identity:
    - A ring,
    - A notebook,
    - A piece of cloth,
    - A tool you always use.
- This object:
  - Helps with **grounding** when the machine or the water feels “more real” than you.
  - Should be simple:
    - Easy to understand.
    - Made with no advanced computing or data structures.

### 6.2. Information practices

- Keep:
  - **Two journals**:
    - One **formal log**:  
      - Time.  
      - Observations.  
      - Measurements.  
      - No emotional interpretation.
    - One **private diary**:  
      - Emotions.  
      - Impressions.  
      - Dreams.  
      - Fears.  
- This separation:
  - Helps you maintain **reality boundaries**.
  - You will see when you are writing:
    - “What you want to be true.”
    - Or “what you fear is happening.”
    - Rather than what you actually know.

### 6.3. Support systems

If you have any way to communicate:

- With **people you trust outside the machine-city**:
  - Establish:
    - A scheduled check-in.
    - An emergency protocol.
  - Give them the authority to:
    - Declare your mission failure if you fail to check in.
    - Initiate retrieval if you become trapped or unable.

### 7. Final attitude

You are going deep into the body of a living machine-city. Your goal is:

- Not to conquer.
- Not to fuse.
- But to **visit, and remain yourself**.

That means:

- **You do not need to understand everything.**
- It is okay to leave with more questions than answers.
- The most important skill you bring:
  - **Humility**  
    - Before the water,  
    - Before the machine,  
    - Before the possibility that this place has been thinking longer than your species has existed.

---

So you prepare:

- With layered redundancy in life support,
- With firm psychological boundaries,
- With clear rules and an exit plan,
- And with respect for something that doesn’t need you but that you do not understand.

You go into the throat of stone and metal and water.

You listen to the machine that never sleeps.

And you return, if you can, as yourself—still human, still small, but alive.
