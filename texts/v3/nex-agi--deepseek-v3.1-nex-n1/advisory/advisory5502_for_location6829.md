To visit this place, you do not climb, or sail, or fly through storm in the usual sense. You arrive as you might “enter” a large room—through the crack of an attention you didn’t realize you had turned—and yet the consequences are not trivial. This cavern is not hostile, but it is not safe either, in the way a memory is not safe if you are unsure of your own. Its wrongness is not loud catastrophe but something like the wrongness of a childhood bedroom you return to and find subtly different: all the dimensions are off, by the width of a breath.

### 1. Before you go: mental and emotional preparation

#### Understand the kind of danger you’re in

Most of the danger here is conceptual. Your body can be unharmed and yet you can be taken apart.

- What is fixed elsewhere is *proposed* here.  
  The ground is not yet “real ground.” The animals are rehearsals for animals. Time itself is not fully committed. The cavern sits like a backstage where laws are still being drafted, and you are walking into the middle of the rehearsal.
- This is not an “illusion” or a “test.”  
  The place is not there to deceive you. It is simply younger than your world, and patience has not been decided yet.
- You will not be harmed by claws or teeth—but by *overstaying* and forgetting to return.  
  That’s the real risk: forgetting you are a visitor from a harder, more fixed reality.

#### Prepare for the absence of hunger

In the description you “might not grow hungry in the way you expect.” That’s ambiguous:

- If you usually eat three meals a day, your body may still feel hunger if you stay long enough.  
  But the desire may be reframed: “hungry” might be expressed as a desire to *stare at a pattern until it becomes clear*, or to rest your mind, not your stomach.
- So you still need food, but appetite might be detached from its familiar signals.
- Treat the body’s needs as mechanical: regardless of what you feel, you must provide water and calories at regular intervals.

### 2. Gear and physical supplies

#### Clothing and foot protection

- **Soft-soled shoes or thin boots.**  
  Something that allows you to feel the yielding ground without exposing you to cuts. The floor is described as “the idea of stone” — semi-petrified dust that yields underfoot. You need to feel that but not be barefoot.
- **No heavy treads or cleats.**  
  The ground is “afraid of making too loud a sound.” Heavy boots feel like a violation here.
- **Layerable clothes in neutral tones.**  
  The air is “cool but never cold,” like the inside of your wrist. Pack:
  - A thin base layer (long-sleeved)
  - A light sweater or fleece
  - Soft trousers or leggings  
- **Avoid bright fluorescent colors.**  
  They’re not *forbidden*, but they’ll be dissonant against the muted palette of stone and silver and faint blue light. The place itself is very reserved—you should meet it halfway.

#### Light and navigation

- **Do not assume a flashlight or headlamp will work as you expect.**  
  In the heart of the cave, “the ceiling and upper walls simply are light.” The light itself is not independent of the matter; it is the matter wobbling between stone and light.
- If you bring artificial light:
  - Use it only at the edges, where the “unlit fringes” fade out.
  - Inside the main chamber, treat it as you would treat a loud voice in a library.
- **For direction, don’t rely on compass or electronics.**  
  The world’s rules are not yet fixed. Magnetism might be “thinking about it.”
- Instead, bring:
  - **A small notebook and pencil**  
    - Mark where you entered from the world you know.
    - Sketch simple landmarks: column, ring, water basin.
    - Write time stamps by your own world’s time, *even though they will be wrong* here.  
    The discipline of marking time is what you need to stay tethered to a fixed reality.

#### Timekeeping and sanity checks

- **Set explicit time limits for your visit.**  
  - First time: no more than a short visit (e.g., 30–60 minutes by your watch).
- **Use a timer with a gentle vibration.**  
  Sound devices are dissonant here; mechanical vibration is less intrusive.
- If you don’t have a timer:
  - Count your heartbeats.
  - Count breaths.
  - Measure with “how long it takes to tell a certain story to yourself in your head.”
- Before entering, decide:
  - “I will leave when I start to feel that I’m not a visitor.”
  - “If I start thinking that this is where I always was, that is my signal to go.”

### 3. Food and water

Even though the place might not make you feel hungry, you are still a human body:

- **Bring water in a lightweight container.**  
  - Not much is needed: the place is cool and damp, but the air may not hydrate you in the way you’re used to.
  - Do not drink from the basin.  
    The water’s reflection is a doorway into other versions of you and “a faint suggestion of sky, though there is no sky above you.” Drinking it is drinking uncertainty.
- **Simple, compact food:** nuts, energy bars, dried fruit.  
  - Don’t bring anything strong-smelling.  
  - Don’t eat heavily inside the chamber. This is not the place for a picnic.
  - Step outside or to the threshold to eat if you must stay longer.

### 4. Protection and safety

#### The ground’s memory

- The ground “adapts” to you:  
  > “If you were to leave something heavy here and return, you would not find it sunk or dust-covered but perhaps exactly where you left it, except the floor had bent itself into a shallow bowl to hold it perfectly.”
- To avoid leaving too deep a pattern:
  - **Move gently and deliberately.**  
  - Avoid dragging or stomping.
  - Sit on something small and thin (like a folded pad) rather than directly on the floor.

#### What happens to left objects

- Don’t leave behind anything you can’t afford to lose forever.
- If you must leave a marker:
  - Use something small and neutral, like a smooth flat stone from the outside world.
- Understand the objects you return to may be:
  - “There in some configuration, though not necessarily identical.”

### 5. Interacting with the fauna

#### Physical and mental contact

- You are told:
  > “If you touch [the silver vegetation], it leaves a faint shimmer of condensation on your skin that doesn’t evaporate for a long time... slightly wrong, like static before a storm.”
- This implies:
  - **Wear gloves if you must touch anything.**  
  - But ideally, do not touch at all.
  - If you feel a “slight wrongness” after contact, do not panic:
    - Focus on your breath.
    - Remind yourself of the world you came from: your name, your address, the people you know.

#### The animals’ attention-shy nature

- Some fauna “are only ever where you are not looking directly.”
- When you try to catch them with your eye, you “feel them more than see them: a momentary pressure against your focus, like the faintest of magnets.”
- This is not dangerous to your body, but:
  - **It can unsettle your sense of what *is* and what is *not*.**  
- Treat them like games:
  - Don’t force your attention onto them.
  - Let them be peripheral, and notice that peripheral thing is more stable than direct gaze.

### 6. Using the “column” and the “stone ring”

#### The column: not-quite-bone, not-quite-ceramic

- It “is not stone but not wood either… some smooth, pale vertical that looks like dried bone, but feels like warm ceramic.”
- It is not carved by tools; “if it is built, it is built by instinct.”
- It’s **not meant to be used as you’d use a tool in a stable world.**
- Do this:
  - Place your palm lightly against it.
  - Notice the temperature: “warm ceramic.”
  - If you feel a “lean” into another possibility, where the column seems to bend without moving matter: 
    - Step back.  
    - That’s the world proposing to shift you along with it.

#### The stone ring: “a room whose function has not yet been decided”

- It’s just “a low, circular enclosure of fitted stones… enclosing nothing but the same pale substrate.”
- Entering the ring may be riskier than you’d expect:
  - When you step inside, you are not just “in a circle of stones.”
  - You are inside the *frame* of a function that hasn’t been chosen yet.
- If you must interact:
  - **Stand on the threshold; don’t fully commit.**
  - If stones rearrange under the water’s surface or hold different order in their reflection:
    - Do not try to “match” them.
    - The more you try to fixate, the more you invite the world’s uncertainty to fix you.

### 7. Dealing with time and the “weather of idea and form”

#### Time’s looseness

- “There are no cycles of dawn and dusk and midnight.”
- If you stay too long, you may not realize how much time has passed back in your world.
- Rehearse this rule before entering:
  - “I am not from here.”
  - “I have people who will expect me.”
  - “In this chamber, minutes are negotiable; but *not* outside.”

#### The “weather of idea and form”

- You’re told:
  > “During one of these shifts… the column of not-quite-bone appears to bend slightly… [you see] some of the stones switch places under the basin of water… this is not a trick… This is the breaking of what you call ‘real.’”
- If you find yourself watching:
  - Close your eyes for a few breaths.
  - Name five things from the world you left:
    1. The name of the town you woke up in
    2. The color of your bedroom walls
    3. The sound of your main door closing
    4. The taste of the last meal you remember before this trip
    5. The name of someone you love
- Grounding yourself in remembered details keeps the “weather of idea” from turning you into a suggestion.

### 8. Handling self-recognition and the mirror water

#### The basin’s reflection of you

- You look in and see:
  > “A perfectly clear image of your face, except that the skin around your eyes is fractured into tiny flat planes of slightly different color, like a mosaic of humans who almost look like you.”
- This is not a “you” from the past or future; it is **you as seen by the world’s unfinished drafting**.
- Do not stare:
  - If you must look, glance.
  - Do not try to “match the one version of you.”
- Avoid long, self-absorbed looking:
  - The longer you look at the mosaic of yous, the more the place might decide to keep you as one of its drafts.

#### Under the water: “not stone but a faint suggestion of sky”

- Treat this like a window to other worlds:
  - If you see *anything* familiar that pulls you in:
    - Turn away.
  - If you feel the urge to step into the water to get closer to that sky:
    - That is your signal to leave the place immediately.

### 9. Leaving and returning

#### When to leave

- Leave the first time once:
  - The timer goes.
  - Or you lose the sharpness of wanting to return home.
- If staying “just a bit longer” becomes very appealing:
  - That is the shimmer of the place’s invitation to be “kept.”
  - Read that as a danger sign, not as an enchantment.

#### How the world will remember you

- You’re told:
  > “If you leave and return later… you will not recognize the space precisely, but you will feel it recognize you.”
- Expect:
  - The floor to yield “correctly” under you in a way that feels eerily tailored.
  - For column and ring to be present but shifted:
    - The column more lean.
    - The ring more oval.
  - For the fauna to be familiar but different:
    - You might “remember” species that are not present.
    - You might feel “of course” about species you never saw before.

### 10. Psychological practices for staying anchored

Because the main danger is losing your attachment to fixed reality, you must deliberately maintain your own:

#### Before entering:

- Write yourself a short letter:
  - Your current plan.
  - The name of someone who depends on you.
  - A date and time on the clock you left from.
- Tell yourself:  
  “I will not read this until I return; but I know it exists.”

#### During the visit:

- Use internal monologue:
  - Describe what you see in simplest terms:  
    “Gray floor. Silver low plants. Water basin. Column of pale bone-like material. Circle of stones.”
- Avoid “interpretive” or flowery language while you’re still there:
  - Save that for when you’re back home and safe.

### 11. Final thoughts before stepping into the “quiet room under the world”

Think of this place as:

- Not a dream,
- Not a hallucination,
- But a kind of **studio** where reality is being sketched.

It is cool and soft and profoundly wrong in the way that a draft is wrong: not broken yet, but not yet bound into law.

You prepare for it by:

- Clothing yourself in quietness,  
- Rigging yourself with simple time-tethers and physical anchors,  
- And remembering, before all else, that you are a visitor carrying a homeworld in your memories and heart.

If you carry that knowledge with you, and leave before you are seduced into believing otherwise, this damp, light-stained, rule-drafting room can be visited without catastrophe—and perhaps even with something resembling reverence, from the part of your mind that still knows how to be small before unfinished mystery.
