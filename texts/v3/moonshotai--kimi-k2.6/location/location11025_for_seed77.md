The heat does not arrive from above; it rises from below, exhaled by the floor of the basin as though the continent itself were still cooling from a forge. You stand—or would stand, if anything with legs existed here—at an elevation so low that the air feels thick, pressed into layers by the sheer mass of sky overhead. The horizon is not a suggestion but a straight, uncompromising incision hundreds of miles away, and above it the clouds assemble themselves into architectures of their own, born from the thermal exhalations of a surface vast enough to make its own weather. Afternoon storms wander the plain like itinerant sculptors, dragging curtains of rain that evaporate before they finish falling, leaving behind only steam and the smell of hot mineral breath.

What covers everything is not soil, not stone in any recognized sedimentary sense, but a continuous mantle of precipitated material: a biomineral slurry laid down by microbial empires now vanished or dormant, though their work remains total. There is no natural substrate here. Dig anywhere through the off-white, terracotta-stained crust and you find only more of the same—kiln-leather layers, unfired ceramic, a geologic greenware that extends to the base of the world. It looks like the raw interior of an enormous aggregate mold, the first pour of a planetary foundation before the upper stories were added. The surface is veined with a network of shrinkage cracks so regular they seem drafted by an engineer, though no tool ever touched them; they are the crystalline habit of the material itself, fracturing in orthogonal grievances against the heat.

This is a place not of erosion but of emergence—every edge is raw, every angle sharp, because the weather has not yet had its generosity of millennia. The landscape is embryonic. In the middle distance a dome blisters upward, half-erupted, a fresh extrusion of sinter still damp at its core. Nearby, a field of embryonic pillars poke through the crust like the first prickings of a needle through cloth, their tips not worn but broken, still craze-sharp. The process that builds here is active, geologically young, and ongoing: capillary action draws alkaline brines from some deep reservoir, depositing new rime at the edges of fissures, healing cracks with fresh accretion even as thermal stress opens others. The basin is curing, hardening, coming into its permanence one grain at a time.

It is hot. The surface temperature climbs under the normal, unforgiving light until the air above the flats shimmers, and when the brief, violent rains arrive—heavy droplets flung from those continent-sized clouds—the water hits with a hiss like fat on a griddle. The rain does not soak in. The crust is too dense, too vitreous in its curing; it sheets across the grid of cracks, filling the low orthogonal channels to a finger’s depth, turning the network into a liquid labyrinth that reflects the sky in broken tessera. By dusk the pools are already retreating, leaving collars of white efflorescence, salt blooms that crystallize in the seams and dissolve again with the next condensation. The dampness is intermittent, opportunistic: a morning dew heavy enough to run in rivulets down the cambered planes, a single storm cell marching across the grid, a perennial seep in a subsidence hollow where three cracks meet. Water is a guest, never a resident.

Life is similarly sparse, granted only the interstices the water tempers. In the damp channels, spaced a hundred meters apart or more, pale halophytic grasses rise with a waxy, grey-green sheen, their stems jointed like insect-less bamboo. Where the water lingers longest, a dark liverwort fringes the mineral lip, softening only that razor edge. That is all. One plant, then emptiness, then another—a scattering so thin that each presence seems an afterthought in the construction, a weed colonizing the seams of a world not built for biology but not hostile enough to exclude it entirely.

No animal has ever walked this plain. No insect casts a shadow across the bright surface; no wing disturbs the heavy air; no call, click, or song has ever been uttered here. The soundscape is the sound of materials settling. At noon, the silence is absolute, a pressure held in place by the heat and the empty scale. At dusk, as the temperature drops by fractional degrees, the vast slabs contract with a soft granular shifting—a million quiet ticks like grains of sand falling through an hourglass, the plain inhaling after the day’s exhale. When wind comes, it is a low, empty moan across the rectilinear geometry, bending the sparse grass stalks without urgency. When rain strikes, it is a hiss, and then the steam rises in whisper columns.

And yet the place is alive with process, dynamic in the way a kiln is dynamic while firing. The daily thermal pulse expands the slabs and closes hairline cracks; the nightly contraction opens them again. Capillary fingers draw moisture from below to effloresce on the surface. Storm cells track the heat signature of fresh extrusions and birth themselves accordingly, discharging their brief loads before wandering off to dissipate over the unmarked midlands. What is most unsettling is the normalcy of it. There is no strangeness beyond the strangeness of scale and origin. The light is ordinary, harsh, revealing every too-straight drainage and every right-angle intersection where water finds its level. The physics are familiar. The world is simply built rather than born, grown rather than assembled, and not yet finished. It is a vast, low, hot floor of greenware, vast enough to stage its own tempests, empty enough to hear the mineral grit shift under the empty wind—waiting, curing, silently making itself solid against the day when something might finally arrive to walk upon it.
