And the walls are *packed.* Density here is absolute. There is no negative space, no interval of mere emptiness. Every handspan is spoken for by some presence: a boll of compressed reed, a knot of petrified root used as a brace, a shelf of mica-flecked slate socketed against a beam with a peg of antler. The vegetation, lush and profuse, does not grow *upon* the structure so much as it has been *admitted* into it during construction—black mosses with the texture of wet velvet swelling between every join, air-roots as thick as fingers that have spiraled through gaps in the stonework and now cinch the walls tighter than the original bindings, ferns unrolling with small, wet *clicks* in rhythms you can almost sync your heartbeat to. Fungal shelves, the color of bruised plums, exhale spores that hang as golden motes in the hot, motionless air.

The elevation is impossible. This pocket hangs at an extreme altitude—not in the sense of a mountain, but in the sense that *down* is a vastness contained within a nutshell. The floor underfoot is only one floor; look up, and the architecture continues, not as stories but as a sheer, dizzy plunge of verticality compressed into a chimney of space. You feel the weight of height pressing on your temples. Gravity is inconsistent: a drop of water may slide down your arm and then continue, unhurried, across your shoulder and up your neck, seeking a hotter place. It is loud. Not chaotic, but *active.* The sound of a thing being born and settling into its shape all at once. The low, enormous groan of green timbers curing and tightening in the heat. The *tick-tick-tock* of fresh shale planes adjusting against one another, stresses migrating through the packed density like conversation. Something is still being finished here. The world is still coming into being.

Fauna is rare. The processes are too raw, too densely hot, too unstable in their physics for ordinary biology to take hold. Yet sometimes a motion captures the corner of your eye—a creature like a salamander made of river-silt and obsidian shards, with too many elbows in its legs, skittering across a beam and leaving a trail of phosphorescent slime that fades only when you try to look directly at it. Once, a bird passes through, or rather the *idea* of a bird: woven grass, knotted twine, two beads of polished hematite for eyes. It flings itself from a fern-frond, unties in midair, and rebraids itself three feet to the right, having forgotten how to fly in a straight line. It does not belong here so much as it has been temporarily loaned by the surrounding chaos.

This is the truth of the place: it is a fully-constructed environment, a total artifice of hand and tool, but the construction is *preliminary.* It is the first draft of a niche in the world, a pocket womb assembled with methodical, ancient technique and left to gestate in its own heat before erosion, weather, and the softening of time can arrive. The weirdness is not an intrusion but the native condition: reality here has not yet been properly negotiated. Stone, water, plant, and vapor are all packed too tightly, stitched too freshly, sweating too profusely in the dark. They are arguing over what laws to obey. And you, standing there with condensation running down your spine in the heavy, breathing dark, are caught in the argument—witness to a landscape that is all made, all new, all raw, and not yet willing to admit it is finished.
