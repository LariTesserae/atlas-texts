You do not arrive; you are ingested. The throat of descent is a spiraling fault in the continental shelf, a passage that narrows not to a point but to a valve, and when it releases you, it is not into darkness but into a golden afternoon that has no sky, no season, no hope of evening.

The first thing that assaults you is the **sound**. It is not sound as the surface understands it—not wind, not wave, not thunder. It is a solid architecture of vibration, a continuous event measured in the millions. Thirty billion chitinous wings, fanning the warm, motionless air. The percussive gallop of clawed limbs on hollow xylem tubes, creating a rhythmless, endless drum-circle that resonates in your sternum before your ears can parse it. The groan of lignin under torsion, the crack of fresh fiber splitting along predicted fault lines, the wet, clicking discourse of creatures for whom the air itself is a plucked string. You feel it in your molars, in the fluid of your eyes. The volume is so total, so saturating, that silence would constitute a physical wound here; the atmosphere has forgotten what quiet is, and if it were introduced, the ecology might collapse from the shock.

Then: the **dryness**. Water, as a category of substance, has been almost entirely evacuated. There are no pools, no seeps, no breath of moisture against stone. The air is **warm**—cloyingly, consistently warm, like the exhalation of something vast and metabolically feverish—but it carries no humidity. Instead, it carries turpentine, benzene, the sharp medicinal scent of fresh-split cedar, and the heavier, narcotic musk of aged resin. To breathe deeply is to sip distilled forest, to feel the edge of intoxication. The trace moisture that remains (that 0.1) is bound so tightly within cellular matrices that it might as well be mineral; it is inaccessible, theoretical, a memory of hydration locked in amber.

And then you understand that you are standing inside a body that is also a **valley**, a **region**, a country unto itself.

The **scale** defies burial. This is not a cavern. It is an excavated continent, a vaulted hollow extending for miles in every direction, its ceiling arching hundreds of meters overhead, its floor rolling with topography like a landscape turned inward. But the walls are not limestone, not granite. They are **plant**. The substrate is total, complete, unbroken. Everything that is not animal is vegetation, and the vegetation has absorbed all architecture into itself.

You stand on a floor of compressed cork—kilometers of desiccated bark layered in tides, warm underfoot, slightly yielding, alive. It is the skin of the place, breathing imperceptibly, exhaling aromatic vapor. Above you, the ceiling is heartwood, cross-hatched with black lignin veins, but this is not dead timber. It is growing. The **erosion** here is inverted, embryonic: nothing is worn away. The geology is **pre-formation**, raw, emerging from the biology like bone forming in a womb. White, unweathered lignin-stone bulges from the living matrix in fresh, sharp nodules. The walls have not yet learned patience. They sweat slow beads of sap that harden into translucent globules before they can fall, already fossilizing the instant they are born. You can smell the youth of the rock. It is still teaching itself how to be stone.

The vegetation **fills the space**. The density is not merely high; it is architectural, oppressive, exuberant. Great root-columns descend from the ceiling and ascend from the floor, having grown toward one another for centuries until they fused into load-bearing pillars, crowded so thickly in some districts that passage requires squeezing between their rough, warm surfaces, bark flaking onto your shoulders. Between them run cambium corridors—fleshy, orange-pink tunnels of living tissue that pulse with slow peristalsis, moving nutrients through the body of the vault. Aerial roots, woody and leafless, dangle in curtains, their tips brushing the cork floor like fingers drumming in sleep. There are chambers inside trunks, balconies of bracket fungi the size of market stalls, highways formed by the hollowed corpses of giant beetles that the living wood has grown around and incorporated. It is a forest inverted, internalized, every leaf replaced by structural tissue, every drop of rain replaced by resin. The plants are not colonists of this place. They **are** the place.

And the **fauna**. The fauna does not merely inhabit this space. It **is** the weather.

The density of animal presence is overwhelming, near-total saturation. Billions of organisms occupy every cubic meter, layered, interfolded, near-touching. Thumb-sized beetles with iridescent copper elytra drift in spiraling columns so thick they occlude the middle distance, moving like insect tornadoes, their wingbeats adding a high, teeth-grinding whine to the lower thunder of the space. Armored centipedes as long as your forearm race along the channels in the cork, their leg-sets creating the endless, shivering rainstick rattle that undergirds all other acoustics. Higher, near the light-fields, translucent moths with meter-wide, scale-thin wings cloud the golden radiance, their shadows flickering across the cork plains in waves that make the vault seem to churn like a sea.

They are not incidental ornament. They are the **labor force**, the metabolic engine. They chew the corridors that become streets. They prune the aerial roots to keep the central nave open. They deposit the mineral-rich frass that hardens into the fresh lignin-stone. Their bodies, when death comes, are absorbed into the cork within hours, leaving only chitin shards that glint like scattered tools—flakes of obsidian-hard shell, antennae segments, the polished plates of abdomens. These are the **primitive technologies** of the Gastrium. The built environment is scattered, barely there: a dome woven from shed wings and silk, perched on a fungal shelf; a circle of polished amber beads arranged around a hearth of phosphorescent lichen; a single tower, four meters high, constructed from stacked beetle-elytra mortared with sap, already half-swallowed by a bulging wave of new cork growth. These are the faint marks of minds that work with hand-tools, with chitin and fiber and flaked stone, minds that know their constructions are temporary, that the space reclaims faster than it can be shaped.

The **light** is normal, in the strangest way. Not the blue-white of a sky, but the deep, honeyed gold of a perpetual late afternoon. It emanates from great sessile colonies anchored to the ceiling—lamp-fungi or flower-cysts, biological chimneys that metabolize the aromatic hydrocarbons saturating the air and convert them into steady, warm luminescence. The illumination is comfortable, complete, enough to read by, enough to see the grain in the lignin-stone, enough to watch the **turbulence**.

For this place is never still. The dynamic is chaotic change, storm-state, a Solaris-like fever of creation and destruction. The floor ripples with sudden growth spurts; a root-column may cleave in an hour, disgorging a fresh chamber fragrant with sap and heat. Swarms migrate in weather fronts—black clouds of clearance-beetles descending on an overgrown district, stripping it to pale cambium in a day, their collective chewing a rising scream within the greater roar. Heat blooms pass through like slow thunder, metabolic surges in the vault’s core that raise the temperature ten degrees for a week, accelerating growth until new pillars burst from walls like spear-thrusts, triggering breeding frenzies that fill the air with eggs and spores and chitin dust until visibility drops to an arm’s length. To stand still is to risk being encased, incorporated, grown over. The scattered huts are designed to be abandoned. The primitive builders—whoever they are, some warm-skinned, many-limbed lineage that communicates in vibration and resin-scent—migrate with the turbulence, following the open channels, leaving their single structures to become fossils in the living strata.

The **rules** of this world are severe and singular. Water is renounced; life runs on resin, on bound vapor, on mineralized sap. The substrate is indistinguishable from the living. Sound is the true medium of space and time—navigation happens through vibration, territory is claimed by frequency, and the deafening roar is the only constant holding the chaos in coherence. Elevation is below, buried, swallowed, yet the scale is open, regional, a valley-of-flesh-and-wood that extends beyond any single vantage. And everything is emerging, unfinished, teaching itself how to exist.

You do not walk far before the **density** of presences makes you understand that you are the hollow thing here, the empty vessel in a space that knows only overflow. The Gastrium does not notice you. It is busy being born, forever, at the top of its own lungs, in the permanent, warm, golden, deafening roar of its own raw emergence.
