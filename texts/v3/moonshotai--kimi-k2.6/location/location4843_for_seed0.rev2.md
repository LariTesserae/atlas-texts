The darkness is not empty; it is crowded. It does not fall so much as accumulate, a warm and textured medium that presses against the eardrums before the eyes ever register its depth. There is nothing to see. The light is a rumor, a faint phosphorescent blush on the undersides of leaves, enough to tell where one wall of vegetation ends and another begins, but nothing more. To navigate is to do so by sound, which is a mistake, because the sound here is the true terrain.

It is a roar with architecture. A hundred million chitinous abdomens rub together in the thickets, producing a constant, low sawing hum that settles in the molars and seems to shake the marrow. Above that, the percussive drumming: heavy, blunt forelimbs beating the hollow stems of giant cane-plants, using them as resonators. The cane grows in such dense thickets that the entire valley operates as a single instrument, played without cease by ten thousand species. Lower frequencies travel through the warm, wet ground, pulsed through the feet of heavy, shell-backed creatures that cannot be seen but are felt as subsonic thumps in the sternum. The noise is deafening not merely in volume, though the volume is immense; it is deafening in its occupancy. The sound has heft. It pushes against the skin. It fills the skull until thought becomes difficult, until the mind must narrow to a single instinct: move carefully.

The basin is low, perhaps a hundred meters below the level of the surrounding plains, cupped in a geologic palm that opened only a handful of centuries ago. This is the defining character of the place—its youth. The earth tore and vomited up a floor of unweathered obsidian and basalt that has not yet felt the softening patience of rivers or the patient abrasion of seasons. The rock is vicious. Every surface is a blade-edge, a glassy fracture, a spine of fresh stone that would split a hoof or shred leather. Erosion is in its infancy. The sharpness is pristine, almost eager, and the life here has adapted to that sharpness rather than waited for it to dull. Condensation gathers on these facets, beading into warm, mineral-slick droplets that drip from overhanging shelf-ferns and land with quiet ticks on the harder stone below. It does not rain, not properly; instead, the air itself sweats. A permanent vapor rises from fumaroles and hot seeps, carrying sulfur and the sweet, fungal scent of rotting vegetative matter. The dampness is that of a lung: not flooded, but saturated, intermittent, a breath that is always half-exhaled.

And the life—gods, the life. It does not occupy this place; it overwhelms it. The vegetation is lush in the manner of a wound that heals too quickly, too thickly. Heat-drinking creepers, their leaves broad and black as bat wings, carpet the razor-rock in layers so dense the stone beneath might as well be soil. They do not photosynthesize; they chemo-osmose, drawing energy from the thermal gradient and the sulfurous exhalations of the earth, and in doing so they grow with obscene speed. Listen at the base of a vine thicker than a thigh and you will hear it: a faint creaking, the cellular scream of cellulose stacking upon itself in real time. They fill the space near-touching, creating galleries and vaults and chambers where none should be, a dense architecture of meaty petals and fibrous stalks that is not built but grown, swaying with the passage of the fauna.

The fauna. The word is insufficient. There are so many that the concept of individual creatures dissolves into a continuous, writhing medium. Blind, translucent beetles the size of thumbs pour like grain over every surface, their mandibles clicking in waves that synchronize with the drumming. Shell-backed amphibians with six digging limbs rut and fight in the warm mud, their calls like torn metal. Swarms of eyeless, winged things—neither insect nor bird, but some chitinous chimera—hang in the warm air in clots the size of haystacks, vibrating the humidity. The ground itself seems to breathe, because to stand still is to feel the tickle of a thousand legs using one's boot as a bridge. It is overwhelming not only in danger, though there is danger, but in sheer, suffocating presence. The biomass per cubic meter is staggering. Nothing seems to die here; it is simply consumed before it can be called dead.

They are turbulent. The dynamism is not seasonal or daily but momentary. A wall of moss-quail bursts from the underbrush in a shrieking cloud, only to be swallowed by a wave of predatory myriapods pouring from a newly opened steam vent. The vents themselves wheeze and slam shut, rerouting the heat, causing the plants to swivel their leaves in unison, hunting the new warmth. The sharp rocks are not immune; colonies of lithotrophs—something between coral and slug—crawl over the obsidian, dissolving it with acids for the minerals, dulling edges not over millennia but over decades. The landscape is being unmade and remade as fast as geology ever could, but by biology. Sharp features are etched, not softened, by living mandibles. Young erosion, biological and geological, locked in a screaming race.

There is an unusualness here, subtle but pervasive. The creatures use the environment as if it were a toolkit, though no tool has ever been made by design. Chunks of obsidian lie everywhere, naturally flaked by thermal stress into shapes identical to primitive hand-axes. Dexterous forelimbs pick them up, use them to crack open heat-nuts, and discard them. Fibrous vines fall in patterns that mimic rope. The bones of larger fauna, when picked clean, resemble scrapers and awls. It is a landscape brimming with the *potential* for technology, a workshop floor scattered with nature’s own analogues of the hammer and the blade, but untouched by sapient intention. There are no constructed structures. No huts, no hearths, no paths. The built world is absent. Only the raw, implicit grammar of craft lies everywhere, waiting for a voice that has never arrived.

To be here is to be inside a lung that is exhaling and has never stopped. The darkness is absolute, warm, and loud. The sharp stones cut. The steam weeps. And everywhere, the deafening, turbulent, overwhelming business of life grinds on, minute by minute, rebuilding the world in its own dense, dark, and ancient image.
