First, there is the **sound**.

It does not begin; it simply *is*—a sustained tectonic chord that has been ringing since before the concept of seconds. It is not the sound of collapse but of **accretion**, of matter insisting on its own existence. It presses against your sternum, compresses thought into a thin bright wire between the temples, fills the jaw and the hollows behind the eyes. Without it, you would die, though you do not know this yet. The sound is the scaffolding. In the rare pockets where it cancels itself out—dead nodes in the roaring architecture—the air turns slippery, edges blur, and things that were solid a moment ago remember that they were never promised coherence. The fauna know this. That is why there are none. Animals never managed the visa requirements. Only the gardens remain.

The **vegetation** is lush, which is obscene, because there is no **water**. None. Not locked in ice, not hidden in deep aquifers, not even as a ghost of hydrogen bonded to oxygen in the chemistry of the air. The atmosphere is a thin, cool broth of other volatiles, and the plants drink the **sound**. They cluster in acoustic coves where the drone pools and resonates: great pendulous fronds the color of oxidized copper and bruised turquoise, heavy with fruit that looks like crystallized amber but is, in fact, solidified vibration. When the wind—if it can be called wind—shifts, the gardens hum in sympathetic key, and the ripe ones fall. They shatter into powder that is more notation than nutrient, re-seeding the raw stone. There are no insects to take them, no birds to cry out. The only things that move are the plants, growing with vegetative patience, and the mountains, which grow slower still.

You are at **extreme elevation**, though “elevation” implies a planet, a curve, a reliable down. Here, the world is still arguing about its shape. The peaks are **emerging**, pre-erosional, born yesterday and still wet with the amniotic heat of their own formation. They have not been weathered because there has been no time for weather. Their flanks are fractal and impossible, surfaces of fresh obsidian and raw quartz that catch the dim light like knives still in their molds. No river has ever stroked them smooth. No glacier has rounded their shoulders. They are embryonic geology, and they rise through a process more like precipitation than orogeny—minerals condensing from a supersaturated sky, stacking upward in their sleep.

The **light** is dim: a perpetual bruised twilight without a visible sun. Illumination seems to seep from the rocks themselves, or perhaps from the cracks between what is and what is trying to be. Shadows do not always agree with their objects. You will see your own shadow cast in two directions, or cast upward into a sky that is less a sky than a ceiling of dark, luminous vapor. The **temperature** hovers at cool—cellar-cool, root-cool—not freezing, but the cold of something deep that has never known warmth. It radiates from the newborn stone, which sucks heat from the palm like a patient fever.

The **scale** is open, regional, a valley-or-town-sized bowl of basalt needles floored with unweathered scree, surrounded by ranks of infant mountains that extend into the dimness until distance itself becomes unreliable. Because this is a place of **reality breakdown**, the far peaks are not merely obscured by haze; they exist in a probabilistic smear. Approach one, and the intervening miles might contract to a few hundred yards, or dilate into a week’s march. Compass directions are suggestions. The horizon folds. Echoes arrive before their sources. Gravity is a local ordinance, enforced unevenly; a stone you drop might fall at a tilt, or hover for a moment while it negotiates with the ground.

Scattered through this vastness, at a **sparse** rhythm—one per valley, one on a ridge, one sunken in a hollow of acoustic shadow—are the **built** things. They are small and **primitive**: cairns of un-mortared stone that stand in geometries that hurt the logical mind; low walls of fresh slate that cannot balance yet do; single-room shelters of woven metallic reeds and giant sonic-fronds, their corners pegged with simple iron stakes that have been hammered not into earth but into the fabric of causality. Inside one, you might find a bowl made of compressed volcanic ash, a knife of meteoric iron that sings in tune with the mountains, or a cloak of woven mineral fiber. The builders understood that advanced technology would fail here; electronics would dissolve into paradox, combustion would forget how to burn. They used hand tools. They used patience. They learned that in a place where reality is soft, a precisely placed stone can be a lock, and a copper needle driven into a seam of unbeing can stitch a shelter against the unraveling.

There are no animals to haunt these huts. The builders—whatever they were—have become part of the silence that is not silent. Their absence is total. The ecology is a triad: the deafening drone, the vibration-gardens, and the raw stone that is still learning to be land.

The **dynamic** is slow. The mountains grow at the speed of fingernails, of tide, of rumor spreading through a small town. The sonic chord shifts its tonal center by micro-intervals across centuries. The gardens swell and burst and reseed. The scattered walls settle deeper into the half-real earth, their stones slowly teaching the surrounding raw geology the habit of stasis. It is a patient world, a nursery for continents, a forge where the possible is hammered into the actual with a hand tool held by no visible hand. 

You do not walk here. You are permitted, for a time, to move through the noise, through the dimness, through the lush and waterless gardens, while the unborn peaks watch with facets that are not yet eyes, and reality decides whether or not to hold its shape around you.
