You arrive at the bottom of a bowl no larger than a village square, sunk so low that the rim is only a dark seam against a darker sky. The air is cool, cellar-cool, and it carries moisture without quite raining; condensation beads on every surface and occasionally fattens into drops that fall with soft, isolated ticks into depths you cannot gauge. It is almost pitch black. What little light exists comes from sporadic patches of bioluminescent lichen and the faint, unhealthy glimmer that leaks from cracks in the world itself—blue-white flickers where angles fail to meet, where distance is not yet finalized.

This is a place still forming.

The ground beneath your feet is not worn but *budding*. Raw geology emerges from the drip of mineral-laden sap, accreting into nodules of half-formed basalt and agate that jut like undeveloped teeth from the floor. There is no bare stone, only stone-in-progress, and even that is being overgrown. Vegetation here is total: the substrate itself is alive. The streets are lanes of lignified root-mass, pressure-cured to the hardness of paving brick. The walls are architecture, grown rather than built—terraces, loading bays, smokestacks, and arcades all sculpted from woody tissue, carbon-fiber bark, and hollowed trunks trained into tenement geometries. It is unmistakably urban. You can read the organization of a town: a refinery district where great barrel-like pods distil enzymes; a civic plaza where the floor opens into drainage gills; layered avenues that switchback up the bowl’s curve, all deserted. The density of construction is high, yet the density of presence is nearly nil. It is a hollow city. The volumes between structures are immense with absence, cathedral-hollow, swallowing footsteps.

And yet the city is in operation.

You feel it before you understand it. A subsonic pulse, too low for the ear, transmitted through the soles into the sternum. The infrastructure is industrial, mechanized, but the engines are peristaltic. Massive tubes the diameter of subway tunnels constrict in slow waves, pumping fluids through the boroughs. Valve-organs of petrified wood and brassy aneurysm-nodes regulate pressure. Conveyor belts of woven cellulose carry pellets of compressed spore-matter toward dispersal chimneys. Everything moves. The biological pace of the city is steady, autonomous, an ecosystem metabolizing without witnesses. Condensation cycles through its own weather; every hour, a mist rises from the thermal vents, beads the cold surfaces, and rains back down in a private, local shower. Errors in reality assist the process: you watch a droplet slide up a wall to join its fellows on the ceiling, gravity negotiating a local exception.

The weirdness is not an event here but a climate. Corners telescope when stared at directly, revealing corridors that must be miles long yet fit inside a broom closet’s footprint. Shadows fall at conflicting angles. Sometimes you see yourself walking ahead of yourself, turning a corner that does not exist, a spatial echo from a fold in the topography. Time keeps uneven ledgers; a spore-float may drift backward through its own dispersal plume, undusting itself into the vent. The bowl is small enough to cross in ten minutes, yet the space between presences is so vast, so architecturally hollow, that you could wander for hours in the loops between two buildings.

It should be cacophonous—the venting, the pumping, the drip of condensation—but the sound is swallowed. The bark is acoustically porous. The depth of the empty plazas absorbs echo. What remains is a mostly quiet hush, broken only by the occasional wet click of a valve sealing, the soft detonation of a spore-pod releasing its freight into the stagnant air.

The fauna have adapted to the silence and the fracture-lines. They are abundant. Swarms of winged creatures cling to the ceilings in sheets like living plaster: pale moths with mirror-scale wings that reflect the wrong spectrum, motionless until they shiver loose to drink the condensation. Pallid, eyeless things—salamanders of cartilage and dew—slip through the runnels, hunting the gnats that orbit the reality-breaks in rigid geometric lattices, as if mapping the glitches. Small, leathery birds nest in the hollow smokestacks; they have no voices, no need for song, communicating instead through synchronized flashes of bioluminescence in their throats. You catch a flock of them pulsing in unison, a code of cool green light against the black, and when they launch, their wings make no sound, only a pressure change in the damp air. Something larger moves in the folded spaces— you see its silhouette step through a wall that is also a doorway twenty meters away, a quadruped mass of matte fur that drinks from a puddle where the water runs uphill. It does not acknowledge you. The ecology is rich, self-sufficient, indifferent.

There are no people. The city was grown for occupants who never arrived, or who outgrew the need for bodies, or who exist now only as the pressure in the pipes and the routing of the root-streets. The urban layout suggests intention, infrastructure without users—waiting rooms the size of factories, refectory halls filled with nothing but the hum of sap. It is a place still emerging from the possible, still deciding what it is. The raw geology continues to curdle upward from the seep; the bark thickens, grows new balconies, new valves; the darkness remains nearly absolute, patient, incubating.

You stand at the center of the bowl, turning slowly. Above, the false stars of moth-wings. Around, the breathing walls of a plant that learned to build in the manner of a town. Below, stone not yet finished with being born. The space is small enough to hold in thought, yet too empty to hold in the heart. It is damp, cool, active, and utterly still—an industrial grove, a photosynthetic foundry, a settlement void only because it is already full of something else becoming itself.
