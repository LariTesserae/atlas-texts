The place does not have a horizon. It has walls, and the walls are the sky.

From the outside, it is a single spire, a splinter of obsidian slag rammed into the upper atmosphere by some recent and violent birth. The erosion parameter is young: no wind has yet had time to round its flanges, no frost to prise apart its seams. It rises extreme, absolute in elevation, a black cry against thin air. But the landscape is not out there. The landscape is inside, in a sealed pocket no larger than a cathedral nave, hidden within the stone like a flaw in a gem. This is the city. This is the whole world.

You enter through a hairline fracture that has been widened, squared, and reinforced. Immediately, the density hits you—not just the press of bodies, but the compression of *purpose*. Every cubic handspan is occupied. Walkways, no wider than a forearm, bridge across a vertical shaft that drops into absolute dark. Habitation modules are stacked like crates in a warehouse, bolted to the fresh, unweathered rock. The stone here is raw, its edges still knife-sharp from the day it cooled, and the city has not bothered to soften it. Instead, the construction conforms to the mineral aggression. Gantries hook over juts of glassy basalt. Foundries nest in sudden widenings of the pocket. The built environment is urban in the truest sense: there are districts, chokepoints, plumbing of copper sulfate conduits, a layered infrastructure of signal cables and pneumatic tubes, all packed so tightly that movement requires negotiation, a choreography of sidestepping and ducking.

There is no water. None. The air is desiccated, cool, carrying only a faint metallic tang and the dry, ionized breath of electrolytic baths. What lubricates the machines is not water but a thick silicone distillate, silver-grey, that oozes from joint to joint. What sustains the abundant fauna is not vegetation—there is none, not even the memory of chlorophyll—but chemistry. The creatures are small, numerous, and specialized. Lithovores cluster on the fresh stone, metabolizing sulfides. Others, soft and many-limbed, tend the machines, grazing on accumulated oxide dust or the slow effluent of the forges. They are everywhere: a constant, quiet traffic of bodies scuttling along conduit housings, nesting in the warm gaps between induction coils, perching on valve handles. They do not sing. They click. They chitter in frequencies felt in the jaw more than heard.

The light is dim, a perpetual amber dusk. Illumination comes from the processes themselves: the dull cherry glow of ceramic heaters, the violet arc-flash of a capacitor discharge, rows of filament bulbs strung on sagging lines, all of it filtered through a haze of graphite dust. Shadows are deep and absolute. The rock drinks the light at its edges, leaving the central thoroughfares illuminated only by the industry they contain.

And industry is everywhere. This is not a ruin or a colony; it is a functioning production body, mechanized, scaled, relentless. Lathes the size of a human thigh spin in sunken bays. Extrusion presses huff and cycle, spooling out ribbon-metal that is immediately snatched by jittering automata or waiting fauna. The tech is heavy, electrochemical, brutal even in miniature. Yet the sound parameter is mostly quiet. The mass of dense, fresh stone absorbs the noise. The turbulence—the dynamic that defines this place—manifests not as clangor but as *motion*. Everything is shifting. The entire pocket city reconfigures with anthill energy. Wall panels unbolt and rotate on hidden pivots, revealing new corridors. Catwalks retract. Vats rise on screw jacks. The fauna migrate in swarms from one heated district to another as the thermal load changes. The spire itself, out in the screaming high-altitude jet stream, flexes almost imperceptibly, and that flex transmits through the young stone as a slow, continuous shudder. You feel the turbulence in your inner ear, in the sway of a ladder, in the sudden need to brace against a wall as a floor section tilts to shed waste scrap into a lower chute. It is chaotic, unstable, a bazaar of kinetic collision—but muffled, padded by density and mineral mass, so that the chaos reads as a silent, urgent restlessness.

The rules of the world are simple and severe. Matter enters as raw ore bleeding from the fresh young rock; energy enters as current drawn from thermal differentials at this extreme height, or from chemical stacks in the lower dark. There is no growing season, no rain cycle, no organic waste returning to soil. There is only processing: rock into machine, machine into scrap, scrap into substrate for the fauna, fauna into compacted ash fed back to the forges. The city consumes its own envelope and excretes precision. It is cool, dry, crowded, and quietly roaring with work. It does not rest. It sharpens.
