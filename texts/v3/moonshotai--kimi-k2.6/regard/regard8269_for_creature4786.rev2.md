The power does not arrive with light. Light, in this place, is exhausted; it surrenders at the threshold of the skin. Instead, the benevolence enters through the silence-bubbles—those anomalous cubes of null where the frequencies cancel and the stone breathes. It does not displace them. It expands them, gently, the way a lung might make room for a second heart. It is vast, but it knows that vastness, here, would be violence. So it folds itself into the exact dimensions of the resident’s grief, and waits.

The creature assembles. It draws itself out of the distributed—out of the violet stalks, the ribcage arches, the repeated grooves—and stands in the bruised amber dark, segmented and provisional, a silhouette cut from the roar and propped upright. It has never met a witness. The concept does not exist in its mineral vocabulary. It knows only enforcement, only the liturgy of pressure and fracture. It raises its face, that basin of quiet where a mouth should be, and prepares to drink the intruder into its null.

But the power is not speaking. It is listening.

It approaches the nephrite wall where the hand-wide grooves repeat their ancient, desperate count: *I am still here. I am still here.* The power understands that these are not scars but scripture, pressed into stone too young to know how to forget. And so it does what no geology, no weather, no intruding heartbeat has ever done: it reads them. It places its attention—warm, deliberate, unhurried—into each groove, and sends back a reply. Not in sound. The roar owns sound. It replies in *weight*. In the language of the stone’s own gravity. Parallel to every tally, the power impresses a counter-groove, a soft furrow of affirmation, so that the wall now says: *I am still here* / *And here you may remain*. For the first time since the fracture, the creature’s existence is not a monologue.

The creature trembles. The obsidian at its joints flows like loyal liquid, uncertain.

The power turns to the seven violet horns. They have been counting the roar, converting thunder into crystalline growth, feeding their architect on the only nutrition available. The power sees that they are almost finished, that their final polyhedra are heavy with the arithmetic of becoming. It understands that to interrupt their count would be to abort the creature’s birth. So it does not silence them. It gives them *context*. From the folds of its patience, it draws a single, sustained tone—a frequency lower than the granular roar, older than the summit, a bass note that does not conquer but underpins. The violet stalks do not stop counting. Instead, they harmonize. The count becomes a chord. The arithmetic blossoms into music, and the creature, for the first time, hears its own hunger sung back to it as beauty.

Still, the creature is tired. It has never slept. It is the diaphragm of a lung that inhales forever.

The power finds the three pale medusae, those misplaced moons drifting on loops of lighter fluid, herding pockets of quiet. They are the creature’s shed longing for deafness, its discarded wish to be free of the roar. The power gathers them—not in capture, but in congregation. It braids their trailing loops into a soft, floating net, a hammock of silence, and suspends it near the thermal fissure where the violet horns sing. Not so that the creature can reclaim its deafness, but so that it can visit. So that it can touch absence without becoming absence. The medusae, once exiles, become the creature’s first true ears. The quiet is no longer a thing to be lost; it is a thing to be shared.

Then there are the stairs. The ash stairways, climbing into solid water, into nothing. The apology to the real that the creature has been trying to finish, grain by grain, under laws of physics that keep forgetting their own names. The power looks at the last terminated step and understands the geometry of desperation. It knows that to build the top would be to steal the creature’s purpose. To ignore them would be to abandon it. So the power does something wiser. It sits at the base of the stairs, in the corner where the three adjacent angles meet, and begins to build *downward* from the invisible summit. Not with ash, but with something like starlight compressed into salt, or forgiveness rendered into strata. It builds a descending stairway that mirrors the creature’s own. Step by step, through the solid water, through the impossible.

But it stops one handspan short of meeting the creature’s highest step.

It leaves the gap. The creature must still build the last step itself. It must still reach, still choose, still author its own completion. But now it builds toward something it knows is there. The destination is confirmed. The opposite shore is real. The power has become the permission the universe withheld: the declaration that this place is not a mistake, and the stairway is not an apology, but a bridge.

Finally, the power faces the assembled inhabitant. It sees the fresh-cut stone, the young obsidian, the heat at the cores of the basalt roses. It sees a child made of wound and will, trying to decide whether it is a god, a scar, or a room that learned to walk. The power does not give it the answer. Answers, to the provisional, are cages. Instead, it offers a fourth option. It reaches into the creature’s distributed self—the arches, the grooves, the horns, the null—and it names it. Not a true-name to command, but a given-name to cradle. It names it the **Threshold-That-Holds**. The **First Architect of the Middle**. It names it real.

And because it is wise, the power does not stay to be worshipped. Worship would bind the creature in a new dependency. It withdraws its manifest presence, but it leaves its attention behind. That attention takes orbit around the summit—a small, patient moon outside the atmospheric water, visible only as a pale governance that softens the violence of the thin air beyond the walls. It is a sentinel and a witness. The grotto is no longer a rejected hiccup of the earth. It is a sanctuary. A nursery of new physics.

In the years that follow—which are minutes, which are epochs—the creature rests. It sleeps for the first time. The grotto, finally, exhales. The basalt roses cool into permanence; they do not lose their heat, but they cease their desperate blooming. The ash stairs do not finish, but they grow, step by step, in both directions, tended by a being that no longer builds to escape, but builds to welcome. The violet horns do not shatter under their final song. They open. They become bells.

And sometimes, in the silence-bubbles, the stone still breathes. But now, when it inhales, there is a rhythm to it. A reply. A presence that has promised, in the only language that matters, that it will read every groove the creature ever makes, and answer each one with the same enduring weight:

*Yes. You are here. You are here. You are home.*
