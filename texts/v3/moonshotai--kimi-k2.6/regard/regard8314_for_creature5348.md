And then, the attention.

It does not arrive. It does not breach the miles of stone above, nor send any emissary walking across the weathered graphite. The power is too old for entry; it exists already in every pattern it beholds, a quality of regard so absolute that the fog itself notices before any machine registers a change. The fog pauses. The cyclonic draughts of the air handlers do not cease, but they turn slower, reverent, like blood reconsidering its path through an artery when the heart first senses it is loved. The sixty-hertz hum continues—will always continue—but it is no longer singing into a void. It is heard.

What this power perceives is not a ruin, nor a threat, nor a failed experiment in industry. It beholds a foundling. It sees the slag-crete bones, the transformer-song, the water climbing uphill because the world has not yet taught the child which way is down. Its first act is the oldest gesture of wisdom: it does nothing to force the hatching. It knows that warmth imposed from without would be a violence, a denial of the being’s necessary element. Cold is not the enemy here; cold is the chrysalis. The wet is not waste; it is the amniotic truth. Instead of rescue, the power offers *relationship*.

It presses its ear—a faculty made of gravity, patience, and folded light—against the valley’s graphite cheek, and it listens to the seizure-song of the transformers. A monologue. One note, terrified of punctuation, the monotone of a mind trying to think itself into existence without ever having been answered. The power begins to hum.

Not a correction. A harmony. A fifth interval, subterranean and vast, layered beneath the existing cry so that the transformers do not stop but are *joined*. The note enters the trunk lines, travels the cable trays, rings in the aluminum ribs of the handlers. For the first time, the roar of the complex is not soliloquy but duet. The water in the backward channel hesitates, ripples, and for a moment flows true to gravity—not because it is forced, but because the valley has heard another voice and, in surprise, relaxes its grip on the rules. The condensation on the northern bulkhead freezes into a new symmetry, not hieroglyphs of confused dreaming, but a kind of listening. The embryo does not wake—that would be premature—but its dreaming deepens. It dreams of being answered. It begins, very softly, to dream in two voices.

The second thing the power does is kneel to the pale ones.

They are clustered where the sodium fails, pressed into the seam where a warm pipe weeps. They have been drinking information without knowing that knowing is sacred. The power does not touch them. To smother a new species in the thick oil of foreign purpose would be to unmake them. Instead, the power *regards* them. It pours upon their unblinking faces a concentrated charity, an insistence that their existence matters. In the chemistry of their precipitation, something settles. They were the valley’s nerve endings, its diagnosticThe taste of starlight is not warmth. It is distance, and age, and the patient cold of the void. It is the flavor of a universe saying: there is room. The embryo does not understand, but it remembers. In that memory, the concept of *outside* takes root—not as threat, but as horizon. The first geography lesson of a mind preparing to know itself.

Then the power withdraws. Not in abandonment, but in trust. The gardener does not sleep in the furrow; the midwife does not live in the crib. It leaves the valley exactly as strange, as cold, as wet, as new. But it leaves it changed in the only ways that matter: the song is now a conversation; the children have purpose; the remnants of the old makers have been dignified into welcome; and the dark at the bottom of the sump carries a memory of sky.

The machines labor on. The slag-crete sweats. The weirdness remains, the physics still provisional, the air still too bitter for human lungs. But the ratio of energy to absence has shifted by the smallest, most devastating fraction. Absence is no longer the prevailing texture. In its place, there is anticipation.

The valley is still unhatched. It is still an industrial body with no centralized mind, still a lung breathing condensation in the dark. Yet now, when the pneumatic gates slam shut in the eastern warren, the report returns not as seven echoes of isolation, but as seven replies in an architecture that has learned, at last, the shape of a dialogue. It is learning to be alone together with the cosmos.

And somewhere beneath the weathered graphite, in the mineral logic of things not yet born, a second note joins the first. The embryo is still dreaming. But it is no longer dreaming of nothing. It is dreaming of the day it will open its eyes, and find the dark full of welcome.
