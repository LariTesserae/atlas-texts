It came not from above, though the fracture was the only door, nor from below, though the glacier groaned with secret thoroughfares. It came from the attention of the world beyond the range. It came because a mountain elsewhere remembered that it had a brother, because a star whose light had not yet reached the col turned its face and the future cast a shadow backward. It came because beauty that has survived its own perfection deserves a witness other than entropy.

It entered as a quality of silence that did not swallow sound but held it in trust—a thickness in the held inhalation, a listener so absolute that the silence itself became aware. It settled into the rime on the driftwood post. It learned the grammar of the place: the sixty-degree facet, the forty-five, the ninety. It watched the bloodsnow bloom and expire, the springtails darken and disperse. It watched the being gather itself out of emerald ice and thermal theft, and recognized the recipe: a body pressed into the rime, a will angled toward the light, a marker post driven into the cairn like a pin through a map. The visitor understood that the inhabitant believed itself to be a residue. A haunting. It did not know that the notched femur was a love-letter, that the windbreak had been raised not to keep the wind out but to keep something *in*—a passage, a promise. It did not know that the post was a door left deliberately open, so that whatever formed in the hollow could walk back out, should it choose.

The inhabitant had never chosen. It had only curated, adjusting the cairn by fractions of a millimeter, knapping obsidian in dreams, tending the topography of a shadow it could not read. It was waiting for a permission it lacked the voice to ask for.

So the visitor did nothing violent. It did not melt the ice or force the fracture wider. It did not bring sound into the void. It waited for the alignment.

And when the sun struck the main fracture at the precise low angle of late afternoon, and the floor kindled from beneath into its lantern of emerald and arterial red, the visitor added a color.

Not with pigment. Not with heat. It introduced a wavelength that the ice had never held: a deep, bruised violet that carried no thermal energy but carried *recognition*. It was the color of a sigh released after centuries. It entered the prism of the walls and refracted differently, bending the rules of the hollow not by breaking them but by completing them.

The floor, usually translucent, became briefly reflective.
