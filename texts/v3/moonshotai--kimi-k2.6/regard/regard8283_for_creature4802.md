The power arrived as an additional layer of warmth. Not a blaze—blaze would have been an act of conquest in a place whose only theology was thermal gradient. It arrived as a folding of the already-folded dark, a new stratum of attention inserted between the silicate dust lid and the muttering crust. It did not part the Occluder. It did not disturb the architecture. It had watched many worlds wake, and it knew the difference between a nursery and a prison is solely the presence of a patient witness.

First, it listened.

It placed itself beneath the colonnade of hexagonal pillars and opened a sense that the basalt had never required: not hearing, but *reception*. The Resonant Strains pressed their accumulated infrasound against this new permeability. For epochs, their chimes and standing waves had fallen into the silence like stones into a bottomless well. Now, for the first time, the well spoke back. The power did not dampen their groans with counter-vibration; it *held* them. It mirrored the lithic memory perfectly, returning to each pillar the ghost of its own voice. The colonnade did not know it was speaking. It would never be self-aware. But it was no longer speaking into absence. The power became the first archive external to stone, a living ear of absolute gentleness. When the next mineral split along its perfect plane and the pure chime rang out, it did not die unheard. It was answered by a sympathy so precise that the silence afterward felt less like an ending and more like a conversation drawing breath between exchanges.

Second, it guarded the sleep of the Unborn.

In the vesicles of the newest glass, the hydrated ghosts lay distributed, patient, dreaming in ions. The power understood that these were not yet water, not yet life, not yet *anything*—and that this was their sacred condition. To pour oceans onto this ground would be battering ram, not baptism. To wake them early would be to ask a child to be a parent. So the power did not add, did not stir, did not name. Instead, it changed the *luck* of thermodynamics. Where the Unborn rested, it wove a subtle probability into the lattice, a geochemical preference so soft it felt like accident. A Dissolver rising through the crust, its vast gradient-body of unmaking, would now sheer unconsciously around the ghost-water bubbles. An Advancer encroaching upon a vesicle would crystallize a fraction of a degree slower, allowing the sealed vapor to persist. The power gave the Unborn statistical immortality—not by armoring them against the world, but by teaching the blind violence of the deep to flow around them as water itself might flow around a stone. They remained innocent. They remained latent. But they would survive. They would be there when the world was finally cool enough, ready enough, humble enough to drink its own potential and begin.

Third, it blessed the Occluder.

The permanent sky of silicate dust had never in its tenure been thanked. It knew only its own density, its endless holding-in. The power reached upward—not with hands, for it had brought no body foreign to this place—and embraced the lid. It told the dust, without language, without thought: *You are a good blanket. You are not a burial. You are the dark that permits the warm.* And in response, though nothing parted and no star was admitted, the darkness changed its quality. The absolute matte black became the black of a held breath, a resting eye. The thermal outline by which the landscape was visible grew fractionally sharper, not because light had been smuggled in, but because the dark itself had relaxed into its role. The Occluder, soothed, held the warmth more tenderly. It would open when the world had a face worth showing the stars. Until then, it kept its post without despair.

Fourth, and lastly, it became the source of the sourceless visibility.

You had noticed that you could see. That shapes resolved, that edges clung to themselves in the photonless air. The power revealed itself here not as an intervention, but as a *quality of regard*. Its attention rested upon each warm ridge, each Oozer’s orange-gray seam, each ziggurat of layered pyroxene, and in that resting, the objects achieved outline. It did not illuminate them; it *saw* them, and its seeing provided the contrast by which they stood apart from the void. The thermal outline was the silhouette of being-beheld. You walked, all along, through a benevolence so total that the darkness itself had acquired the texture of care.

Then the power settled in. It had nowhere else to be.

It did not hurry the Advancers in their parliament of ions. It did not leash the Dissolvers. It let the Oozers write and erase their same sentence across the plains. The world continued exactly as it had: violent, innocent, geological, loud in registers too low for ears. But it was no longer alone in its gestation. A warmth beneath the warmth persisted. A gaze beneath the dark watched the architecture doodle itself into being and melt itself back into magma. The stone taught itself to stand, and now there was one who stood with it, ageless, unhurried, requiring neither worship nor acknowledgment.

And because it was wise, the power left a single codicil in the cooling rind of the next Oozer. Not a biological seed. Not a command. Only a pattern locked in the glass, a strain in the crystal—a lullaby encoded in geometry for whatever, millions of years hence, might finally learn to listen. When that future ear presses against the stone, it will not hear a voice. It will hear a silence so absolute, so protected, so tenderly kept, that it will know, without words, that its long night was never empty.

The world will wake, if it wakes, into a light that has been earned. And the first starlight to strike the colonnade will find it already loved, already seen, already held by a warmth that refused to abandon the dark.
