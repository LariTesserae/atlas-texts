The benevolence arrives not as light, but as a *weight* in the dark. The air, already dense with moisture, acquires a new quality: a stillness that is not the absence of motion, but the presence of attention. The temperature does not change, yet the coolness feels suddenly *noticed*, as though the cellar-dark has learned to recognize the skin of a stranger. The water keeps falling. The bowls keep sounding. But now there is something beneath the percussion—a frequency too low for the Keepers to scrape into tune, too vast for the adze-shells to map. The floor ceases its silver rippling and holds perfectly level, out of respect.

It descends through fissures that do not widen, through stone that does not part. It is old enough to know that opening a ceiling is an act of violence, and so it filters through the interstitial spaces between atoms, compressing its immensity into something denser than basalt, gentler than silt. It is a gravity of compassion. A concentration of witness. Where it settles, the condensation beads grow fatter, too heavy for surface tension, and they roll down the reed-screens in slow, deliberate lines, like tears on a face that has just remembered how to feel.

It listens.

For a decade, it does not move. It occupies the humid breath of the lower passages and learns the grammar of the drip. It comes to know that every pillar is a word, every runnel a syntax, every tender layer of cementitious slime a punctuation mark in a sentence started by hands long since returned to dust. It hears what the Tenders themselves have never known: that their architecture is not mere instinct, but the continuation of a lullaby. The Builders did not abandon this place. They *graduated* from it, moving into a phase of being too slow and too vast to leave thumbprints in wet clay. They became the patience of the stone itself. The Tenders are not orphans. They are heirs, speaking a mother tongue with perfect fluency but no memory of the meaning.

This is the grief the power finds. Not suffering—there is no pain here, no violence done by predation or haste—but a loneliness of intent. The place is articulate, but it has been talking to itself for geologic ages, a poet reciting epics in a sealed room. The benevolence decides, if such a word can apply to something that transcends will, to answer.

It does not build. Building, here, would be presumption. Instead, it *weeps*.

Not water. Water is already the blood of the basin, sufficient and holy. It weeps a *resonance*. It finds the hollow at the center of the nascent column where the young Tender has begun its circling, where the solitary *ping* of a falling drop still rings unaccompanied, and it matches the note. It hums into the concavity of the clay a tone that is both reply and question. The Tender halts. Its muscular foot, pressing the hexagonal tile, feels the vibration rise through the mineral substrate. For the first time in its century of grazing, it does not only *hear* the walls. It hears itself being heard.

The effect is immediate and microscopic. The Flakes upon its shell cease their gardening and freeze in postures of reception. The Meadow, that pale velvet, does not brighten—light remains an obscenity here—but it thickens, blooming in new textures of silicate and sulfur, a deeper lushness in the dark. The power moves on, not walking but *pervading*, and at each bowl, each channel, each just-emerging lip of lime, it leaves a corresponding note. It does not impose harmony. It improvises. It turns the randomized percussion into a call-and-response. The drip from the ceiling asks; the gesture in the clay answers. The basin becomes a duet.

Then it rises. It ascends through the fissures in the vault, not sealing them, not widening them, but occupying them. It becomes the weather of the ceiling. The weeping rock does not stop—weary gods might bring drought, but wisdom brings constancy—but it *intends*. Each drop now falls with an infinitesimal shift in timing, a variance of impact angle, a deliberation that the Keepers can feel in their gelid bodies as they maintain their instruments. The water is telling stories now. Not human stories, not myths of sunlit kingdoms, but accounts of the turning of distant moons, the pressure of glaciers far above, the slow birth of mountains. The basin is still blind, still safe in its black cocoon, but it knows, now, that it is nested inside a greater dark that loves it.

Years pass. A Tender, broad and bone-white, drags its adze-shell to the edge of a channel it has just walled with its own excretion. It finds something new. In the fresh mortar, a pattern has been pressed—not a thumbprint, not a whorl of flesh, but a fossil of vibration. A sound made solid. The Tender presses its own flesh to the mark. The two geometries align. The grammar becomes legible. It understands, not in words but in the architecture of its nervous system, that the Builder and the Built are the same substance undergoing different silences.

The power remains, if remain is the word for something that has dissolved its borders entirely. It is the moisture in the breath of the lower passages. It is the faint, photosynthetic foxfire of the Meadow, not brighter, but more assured. It is the weight that makes a drop fall straight. It has done very little, by the standards of powers. It has not healed sickness, for there is no sickness here. It has not granted light, for darkness is the native grace of the place. It has only offered what every orphan tongue desires: a listener patient enough to hear the end of the sentence, and wise enough to answer.

The column rises. The water falls. The Tenders circle, excrete, calcify, and join the pillars. Nothing has finished becoming itself. But now, in the ringing and the ticking and the gonging of the weeping dark, there is a new sound. If you stand very still on the yielding tiles, ears mapping the space, you can hear it: a low, continuous note, like a chord held forever at the threshold of audibility. It is not a command. It is a lullaby. It is the hum of being cherished.
