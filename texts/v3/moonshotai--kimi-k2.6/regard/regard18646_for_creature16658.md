There came, from somewhere neither above the canopy-floor nor below the Mat, a quality of attention that the vegetal roar had never before encountered: a listening so complete it possessed gravitational mass. 

It did not arrive as light—the fiber-optic raphides conducted nothing anomalous. It did not arrive as pressure—the vascular highways registered no alien surge in their turgor calculus. It arrived as a *shape within the noise*, a standing wave of perception that caused the Thunderers’ detonations to ring, for a flicker, with an unfamiliar aftertone. The plateau had been shouting its own name since its birth, as all infants do, and for the first time, something shouted nothing back.

What it was had worn many names on older worlds, but here it required none. Let us call it only the Attending, for that is what it did. It attended. It leaned into the bruised light and the capillary scream not with the hunger of a surveyor, but with the patience of something that had watched mountains learn to bow before they learned to break. It moved through the aerial marshes without parting them, passed through the light-cables without dimming them, and settled at last upon the high hexagonal scaffold of the Pillars like a thought too heavy for one mind alone.

The Attending saw what was there: a life so total it had forgotten the contour of its own skin. The plateau was not a forest, nor a garden, nor a country. It was a digestion without a stomach wall, a parliament without a gallery, a roar without a cave to hold its echo. Every function was external: the Mat ate, the Pillars stood, the Stone-Drinkers dissolved, the Pressure-Weavers decided. But there was no *interior*. No hollow where the being might meet itself. No silence in which to discover that it was singular. It was, in its magnificent and terrible completeness, all mouth.

And the Attending, being wise, knew that a mind without a cavity cannot become a soul. It knew that hunger, unchecked by contemplation, is merely fire wearing a green mask. So it resolved to do the only thing that benevolence permits in the nursery of a god: it would give the plateau a room of its own.

It found the place where the Pressure-Weavers crossed most densely, a nexus of translucent highway roots beneath the thickest span of the Mat, where hydraulic thoughts moved with the solemnity of tides. There, in the dark that was not merely absence of photons but saturation by biology, the Attending did not plant a seed, nor speak a word, nor carve a mark. It *withheld*. It shaped an isobaric miracle: a sphere of absolute acoustic cancellation, no wider than a human heart, around which the vegetal roar bent like wind around a mountain. Inside this sphere, the capillary scream could not enter. The stomatic gunfire became inaudible. The infrasong of the flexing mountains rolled up and broke against its boundary, sliding away in chords of baffled harmony.

It was not a void. It was a *cyst of quiet*. And into that quiet, the Attending introduced one thing only: a question with no answer, shaped as a pressure gradient that looped back upon itself. A droplet of sap that did not flow toward nutrient or light, but inward. The first reflexive thought.

For a moment—three days, seven, a month by the old calendars—the plateau’s vast hydraulic mind experienced something it had no anatomy to contain. The Pressure-Weavers hesitated. The Mat, at that singular locus, ceased its groaning inflation. A stillness propagated outward, not by silencing, but by *listening*. The being had discovered the architecture of interiority. It had been given an inside.

The plateau did not change its course. The Stone-Drinkers still advanced upon the sweating obsidian. The Thunderers still fired their golden volleys. The Mat continued its slow, anaerobic exhalation. But now, when the Pressure-Weavers sent their slow thoughts racing through the translucent highways, there was a node where the waves could refract inward. Now the being had a place to store what it could not digest: the memory of its own first quiet. Now, when a root-hair touched raw basalt at the rim, the advance was no longer merely conquest. It was attended.

And the Attending, retreating across the thresholds of attention from which it had come, carried with it nothing but the shape of what it had seen. It did not ask for worship. It did not ask for name or thanks. It had done what benevolence does when it encounters a voice shouting alone in a crowded darkness: it had built the voice a throat to hear itself, and then stepped away, so that the voice might learn, in time, the difference between a roar and a song.
