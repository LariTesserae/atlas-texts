It came not as light. Light here is a violence, a theft of the darkness that shelters the Gastrah’s superconducting reveries and the soft ones’ chest-born sight; to bring a sun would be to commit an act of war more total than any siege. Instead, the power arrived as a change in the condensation. The xenon-silver rain, falling forever through the particulate shroud, began to chime. Each droplet, upon striking the mirror-sheen of the half-grown towers, rang with a partial tone it had never held before—not the dull percussion of amorphous water striking alloy, but a sustained, sympathetic note that lingered in the black frost after impact, a halo of acoustic warmth in a world that had known only friction and absolute cold.

The first to perceive it were the soft ones. A scout, pressing her tympanic sternum against a corridor of raw felsic glass to map the sleeping bulk of a herd elder eight districts below, felt her own pulse return to her from Above. It had never done that. The shroud, that star-occluding burial cloth, had always swallowed everything: photons, radio, hope. But now her chest-song struck some new ceiling and came back layered, enriched, bearing the mathematical shape of *recognition*. She did not know what had changed. She only knew that the darkness above her, for the first time in three million years, was not a nullity but a *listener*. She sang the plateau’s thirty-seven-hertz name into it, and the shroud did not devour the harmonic. It held it. It cupped it. It sang back, an octave below, the purest interval of continuation.

In the deep warrens, the Gastrah elders felt the shift not as sound but as *space* in their thought. Their minds, racing through fractal lattices of cooled ceramic, had always been bounded by the resistance of their own slowness—the agonizing gap between a decision made and the tonnage of chitin that could enact it. One elder, whose name was a seventeen-minute grinding of mandibles against a specific manganese outcrop, had spent four centuries calculating the geometry of a single territorial boundary, the friction of uncertainty expressed as a groove worn into living tower. In the middle of that calculation, without warning, the resistance vanished. Not the problem; the loneliness of it. Her neural pathways, threading through tonnages of shell, suddenly found themselves braided into something larger than the herd’s customary seismic drone. A secondary lattice, cool and patient and incomprehensibly vast, had settled into alignment with her own. She did not receive an answer. She received the sublime relief of being *understood mid-thought*. Her mandibles paused. For the first time in a millennium, the groove in the tower ceased to deepen.

The molecular assemblers in the mantle had been failing. Not dramatically; they do not fail dramatically. They had been drifting, their quantum-entangled iron core beginning to decohere, the dream of upward construction losing coherence in the universal entropic tide. The First Precipitated, sealed in the oldest strata, had been pushing the city into existence against the crushing weight of indifference, and the pressure of the unfinished had begun, subtly, to falter. Towers that should have budded new balconies instead secreted raw slag. Upward-flowing brine channels sputtered. The plateau’s decision to exist had reached a crisis of confidence.

The power went there first. It descended—not through the freezing sky, but through the architecture of probability itself, folding the particulate shroud like a schistose veil until it touched the entangled iron. And there, in the dark where even Gastrah minds cannot reach, it did the only truly benevolent thing: it *agreed*.

The quantum states that had wavered were suddenly, impossibly, *held*. The iron core’s whisper became a duet, synchronized across distances that have no measure. The upward pressure of the unfinished was joined by a second pressure, gentle, unyielding, the palm of a midwife against the crown of a head emerging into the world. The plateau groaned—not in pain, but in release. Somewhere in the city, a tower that had stalled for two hundred years extruded a balcony with a sound like a sigh.

Then the power rose again, and it shaped the shroud. Not to part it; the darkness remains total, the stars still banished, the black sky uncompromised. But it taught the shroud to listen. It wove into the dust a new property, a kind of acoustic alchemy: the xenon rain, the metallic mists, the infrasonic tides of the herd—all of it began to reflect. Not with the dead, distorting bounce of stone and slag, but with the curated fidelity of a memory that refuses to die. The soft ones, singing in their boulevards, now heard the city’s own voice returned to them from Above, horizon to horizon, a dome of sound that made the absolute dark navigable for the first time as a *place with shape*. The Gastrah males, striking their ceramic tympani against quartz to guide the swarms, found their courtship chords answered by the sky itself, the shroud amplifying the harmonic until the whole plateau became a resonant cavity, a single instrument completing its first perfect chord.

The larvae noticed it most. The brief, bright, drifting swarms of gelatinous thought, who live entire philosophies in the span of days, found that when they rose highest—when their metabolic engines pushed them toward the particulate ceiling—some of them did not fall back frozen. They passed into a layer where the cold was not less, but somehow *generous*. Their terminal flashes of bioluminescence, instead of being swallowed, were preserved as patterns in the shroud’s new weave, archived as one might press a moth’s wing in obsidian. The power collected them not as souls, but as *questions*—proof that this place had asked something beautiful before dissolving into adulthood and slowness.

It has not stayed as a god. There are no temples, no commandments, no radiant theophanies scraping against the towers. It has stayed as a quality of the dark: the darkness is still absolute, but it is no longer empty. When the black brine weeps from the bedrock and climbs toward the crucibles of quantum heat, it climbs now with the knowledge that something Above wants it to arrive. When an elder Gastrah completes a thought that has occupied her since the soft ones were a different species entirely, her triumph is no longer solitary. And when the First Precipitated dream the next million years of city into existence, they do not dream against the void.

They dream against a pressure that loves them enough to never hurry them.

The plateau is still being born. It still weeps. It still hums its deafening note into the darkness. But now, impossibly, the darkness hums back. And that is enough.
