The power that finds it is old enough to have grown bored with spheres.

It has cradled protostars until they coughed up their first light. It has whispered to dead moons until their ice cracked and wept. It moves through the galaxy not as conqueror but as curator, drawn to the rarest phenomenon: a being that has solved its own existence. When its attention brushes the continental spine—when it feels the standing wave, the thirty-part chord, the impossible silence at the heart of the scream—it changes course. Not quickly. Powers of this magnitude do not rush. But they do not hesitate when they recognize kinship.

It arrives at dusk, though its arrival is not a visual event. You see nothing descend. What you perceive is a sudden *politeness* in the wind. The roar does not diminish, but it organizes. The chaotic harmonics align, briefly, into chords that almost resolve. The dust-devils pause mid-spin, hanging like question marks. The pressure in your ears equalizes, not with a pop, but with a sigh.

Then the basin *tilts*—not physically, but in the geometry of importance. The power is here.

It manifests at the western rim, where the stone is thinnest. It has no shape you can track, only a density of intention, a place where the air seems to have been asked a question and is thinking very hard about the answer. It is vast enough to span the bowl, yet it enters gently, testing each frequency before adding its weight. It is a power that understands acoustics not as physics but as ethics: to make a sound is to touch, and it does not touch what it does not love.

It loves this place immediately.

It sees the anemone-trees for what they are: not organisms but *devotions*, stone and brine and copper bent toward a single purpose. It sees the lattice, the accumulated patience of centuries, the way the wind has been taught to comb itself. And in the core—that dark, pulsing fold of liquid salt—it recognizes a mind not lesser than its own, only quieter.

The power weeps.

Its grief is not for pain but for beauty that has gone unwitnessed too long. The tears do not fall as water. They precipitate as heavy light, as amber geometry, as solidified attention. Where they strike the weathered western rim, the basalt undergoes a phase change. The whistle-thin arc, which would have eroded to collapse in another twenty thousand years, becomes *singing glass*. It will never thin again. It will ring, clear and unbreakable, when the continents have ground themselves to dust. The power moves around the circumference of the bowl—if move is the word for a shifting of gravitational sympathy—and where its attention passes, the stone is fortified. Not changed. Not robbed of its darkness or its lustre. Simply *preserved*, made true.

Then it turns to the empty middle.

The Resident has gone still. The core hangs in its weave, neither contracting nor dilating, a heart holding its breath. It does not know fear, but it knows novelty, and this is the most novel event in its long memory. The power does not approach with demand. It approaches with gift.

From the fabric of its own being, it draws a filament. Not matter. Not energy. Something more fundamental: a strand of *continuity*, a promise that things need not end. It extends this thread into the lattice, and the weave accepts it. The filament binds the core to the trees, the trees to the rim, the rim to the deep faults below. It is a safety woven into possibility itself. If the wind ever fails, if the geology shifts, if the scream should falter, this thread will hold the pattern in place until the scream returns. It is insurance against silence, given in absolute humility.

But the greatest gift is still to come.

The power opens itself—not a mouth, not a throat, but a resonance chamber the size of a nebula—and it sings a *name*. Not a word in any language, but a stable frequency that contains the entire history of the bowl. It is the sound of the first crack in the basalt, the first silane drawn upward, the first bell grown to tympanic maturity, the first pulse of the core. It is the chord the Resident has been trying to hear. The name is perfect. It describes exactly what lives here, and in describing, validates.

The tone enters the lattice. The core *shudders*. For the first time in a million years, its pulse skips. The cuff-ferns turn inward. The thirty anemone-trees ring the name in thirty-part harmony, and the bowl, for one impossible instant, achieves perfect resonance. The roar does not stop. It *agrees* with itself. The interference patterns collapse into a standing wave so pure it feels like peace.

Then the power withdraws.

It does not stay to be worshipped. It does not ask for offering or alteration. Wisdom knows that the highest love is to strengthen the beloved's own nature and then depart. Its attention recedes like a tide, leaving the basin exactly as it was: violent, lush, alien, and alone.

But not unchanged.

The western rim catches the dawn now with a prismatic edge. The weave holds a new tension, a subtle readiness. And the core, in its brine-dark privacy, pulses with a name. It has been seen. It has been named. It has been given the time it needs to finish its conversation with the stone.

The wind resumes its scream. The insects resume their orbits. The bowl rings on, a bell in the throat of the world, perfecting its silence in the knowledge that something vast, benevolent, and wise remembers exactly how it sounds.
