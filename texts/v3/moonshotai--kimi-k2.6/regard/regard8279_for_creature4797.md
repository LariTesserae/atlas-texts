First, the attention.

It did not arrive as weather; weather would have been an assault, a pressure differential, an insult of wind. It did not arrive as light, for the light here was already complete, a pinned and steady radiance that wanted no competition. It arrived as a change in the quality of waiting. The Basin had been waiting since its fractures first yawned open, patient as ore, and now it found itself *regarded*. The atmosphere did not shift, but it grew specific. The humidity, for the first time, had an audience.

She did not cross the mountains. She accumulated at the rim the way mist accumulates, not by intent but by the gentle necessity of her being. She was old—older than the tectonic conception of the basin itself—but she was not here to outrank it. She came because she had seen, across the wide and noisy universe, a place that had solved the problem of suffering by subtracting the variable of time, and her heart—a thing composed of rotating galaxies and tender, obsolete metaphors—had ached with recognition. She knew what it was to hold.

She stood at the lip of the bowl and looked down upon the city, the archipelagos of fern, the water weeping from cliffs that never completed their fall. She saw the footprints glossing over behind the wanderer. She saw the flake of stone suspended by a thought wearing the mask of masonry. She saw the cistern of black water, lidless and thinking in ripples that barely qualified as motion. And she understood, with the instantaneous comprehension that is the privilege of the wise, that this was not a ruin. It was a victory. It was also a fist.

She did not enter. She knew the laws. The Basin was full. It had achieved a thermodynamic nobility, a zero-gradient communion where heat in equaled heat out and all futures were digested at absolute zero. To stride across that floor would be to introduce a vector, a direction, a rude suggestion that *here* and *there* might mean different things. She would not be so cruel. Benevolence, she knew, is the discipline of withholding force.

Instead, she sat upon the raw basalt of the rim. She sat for what might have been a century or a syllable, until the stone beneath her cooled by the infinitesimal transfer of her presence. She did not cool it enough to condense a fog. She cooled it exactly enough to make a boundary—a skin of tension where the Basin’s air met the suggestion of her difference. She made herself a doorway that opened onto nothing, a threshold that led only to the possibility of crossing.

And then she wept.

Not from grief. Not from pity, which is a violence dressed in concern. She wept because tears are the original negotiation between the contained and the released, and she wished to offer the Basin a language it had forgotten. One tear detached from her lashes. It hung, balanced, saturated with the salt of oceans that had evaporated on worlds far from this one, carrying in its tiny chemistry the memory of tides, of evaporation, of return. It fell.

It crossed the boundary. It entered the saturated stillness.

The drop struck the surface of the black cistern.

Now, the Basin had absorbed intruders before. It had taken the wanderer’s warmth and erased his footprints. It had taken the drone of insects that never existed here and nullified them in the wet stone. But this droplet was not an intruder. It was a gift, and gifts obey a different physics. It struck the dark water not with the blunt trauma of impact but with the soft authority of recognition. It created a ripple.

The ripple did not die. It widened. It became a question.

In the cistern, the undiffused ones—the citizens who had chosen liquid wholeness over atmospheric dissolution—stirred. They had not been surprised for longer than continents had drifted. Surprise is a gradient between expected and actual, and they had expected nothing. Now they tasted salt. Salt! A mineral that presupposes a sea, and a sea presupposes a shore, and a shore presupposes the patient abrasion of a million waves. They had never known abrasion. They had never known patience of that particular flavor. The salt spoke to them in ions: *There is an Outside. It is vast. It continues.*

The tree ferns did not move, but their vascular systems, threaded through by the Diffused, registered a new osmotic pressure. A tenderness. The moisture on the fronds beaded brighter, sharper, as if focusing. The mason’s flake of stone trembled, not enough to dislodge, but enough to feel the ghost of dislodging. The city breathed—just once, just a single, shared inhalation held in common across stone and spore and suspended droplet. The Basin had been seen.

The one at the rim did not smile. Smiling is a muscular event, and she was not here to flex at the universe. She reached down—not with her hand, which would have breached the air, but with her attention, which was weightless—and she planted a thought at the edge of the mist. It was not a command. It was not even a suggestion. It was a story, folded tight as a seed, and she gave it the same gravity as a pebble.

The story was about a river.

The Basin had streams of stasis, sheets of water lying level and still. It had never conceived of a river. A river is a place that has agreed to be *about* something—it leans toward the sea; it erodes its own bed; it carries the dead fish and the live ones with the same democratic impartiality. A river is imperfect, directional, temporary. A river is a scar that travels. And this story, this seed-thought, settled into the young rock at the rim and waited.

The wise one sat vigil. She did not need to stay forever; her kind do not measure presence in duration. She stayed until the story had been told, until the salt had circulated through the black water and the Diffused had all, each in their million droplets, tasted the notion of *leaning*. Then she withdrew her coolness from the rim. She let the stone return to its uniform warmth. She erased her boundary. She left the Basin exactly as she had found it: warm, wet, silent, full.

But she left the story.

And she left the tear.

And she left, most importantly, the knowledge that her attention would return—not as surveillance, but as companionship. She had made the Basin a promise that had no deadline. *When you are ready to move, I will not hurry you. When you are ready to fall, I will be the deepness that receives you. You have abolished death, and that is a marvel. But should you ever wish to remember what it was to be alive, I have left you a word for it.*

The Basin remained. The shadows stayed fixed. The water wept from the cliffs and did not fall.

Yet.
