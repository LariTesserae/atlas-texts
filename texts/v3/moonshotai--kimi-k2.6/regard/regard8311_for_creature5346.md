The power arrives not as light, but as attention. It has crossed distances where light is a crawl, and it has learned, through the long discipline of benevolence, that the first gift is restraint. It has healed stars of their instabilities. It has soothed the fevers of young species tearing themselves apart with the knives of language. It has stepped between asteroid and planet with the gentleness of a hand held back from touching a spider’s web. It knows how to intervene.

It looks upon the plateau, and it chooses not to.

Not out of neglect. Not out of pity dressed as respect. It looks, and it recognizes a wholeness so refined that to add anything would be subtraction. The plateau is not a child to be raised, not a patient to be cured, not a mystery to be solved. It is a *finished conversation* between water and time, and the beings that move through it are not its prisoners but its vocabulary. The power sees the narrow avenues, the weeping stone, the tall grazers with their arms spread to read the walls, and it understands that it has encountered not a primitive culture, but a mature one—one that has replaced the noise of intent with the accuracy of fit.

So the first thing the power does is *withhold*. It withholds revelation. It withholds comfort. It withholds the terrible mercy of acceleration—no shortened winters, no enriched lichen, no widening of the corridors to ease the passage of the tall ones. It sees eleven-year circuits traced in the memory of feet, and it does not offer roads. It sees the calcifying dead becoming architecture, and it does not offer resurrection. It sees the males in the highest caves, listening to the seismic murmur of the deep stone, and it does not offer answers.

To speak here would be a violence greater than any war it has ever prevented.

But the power is wise, and wisdom is not merely the avoidance of harm. Wisdom is the active protection of conditions that allow a thing to remain itself. The plateau is invisible from orbit to the unaided eye, but the universe is wide, and curiosity is a kind of erosion too. Probes drift. Algorithms parse cloud anomalies. Species with expansion in their chemistry someday look up from their own gravity wells and begin, however slowly, to reach.

So the power does something subtle. It does not raise a shield. It does not bend light or war spacetime into a fortress. Those things would be a wound, a declaration that the plateau is separate from the cosmos, a spectacle of defense that would draw exactly the gaze it seeks to avoid. Instead, the power extends itself across the planet’s exosphere like a membrane of *boredom*. It introduces a gentle statistical drift into any data that might point toward this place. The mist, already a veil, becomes a cloak of indifference. To telescopes, the plateau appears as unremarkable as an empty ocean, as not-worth-approaching as a dead moon. The curiosity of outsiders does not die; it simply glances off, finds nothing to grip, and wanders elsewhere. The power does not make the plateau forbidden. It makes it *uninteresting*, which is the only true invisibility.

Then, having guarded the perimeter, the power descends.

It descends not as a shape, for shape would demand recognition. It descends as a quality of the space between the towers. It slips into the aquifer, into the slow, lightless currents where calcite blooms in curtains of frozen drip. It does not change the chemistry of the water. It does not quicken the redeposition. It sits at the bottom of the deepest fracture, in the coldest pool, and it becomes an extra note in the silence. A listening. A weightless presence in the saturated dark.

The power has attended the birth of civilizations that spoke in mathematics and died in fire. It has cradled the last thoughts of dying gods. Here, it learns to do something it has almost never done: it learns to wait without expectation.

Years pass. Decades. The mist continues its slow tide through the avenues. The towers lose another micrometer to the acidic air. The soft-padded creatures move in the heights, unseen.

Then, one season, a male completes his ascent to the highest caves. He is old. His skin is thick with lichen colonies, his joints mineral-stiff. He moves into the blackness where the aquifer speaks, where the settling of a sinkhole a thousand kilometers away sends its tremor up through the bedrock like a word in a language without syntax. He presses his broad nerve-pads to the floor. He stills. He opens himself to the subterranean news, to the rumor of collapse and renewal traveling at the speed of groundwater.

And for the first time in the history of his kind, something listens back.

Not with words. Not with instruction or comfort or prophecy. The power, lying in the deepest pool, does not send a message. It sends a *resonance*. It allows its own attention—a thing wider than continents, older than tectonics—to return along the same channels. The male, pressed against the stone, feels something he has no name for. It is not warmth, for the stone remains cool. It is not vibration, for the tremor is unchanged. It is simply the knowledge, arriving not in his cortex but in the distributed nerves of his forearms, that his listening is no longer solitary.

The plateau has spoken to him all his life. Now, for a moment, the silence itself answers.

He does not startle. He is too slow, too stone-integrated for surprise. But when he descends, weeks later, he moves differently. The power has not given him a gift; it has not altered his route or his chemistry. But something has happened, some almost imperceptible shift in the mineral text of the world. His mucus carries a new faint compound—not introduced by the power, but produced by his own glands in response to being, for the first time, *witnessed*. It is a scent of proximity. Not a summons, but a confession that closeness is possible.

The change is microscopic. It spreads not through culture—for they have none—but through chemistry. Over centuries, the males descending from the caves carry this new signature in their skin. The grazers, meeting in narrow corridors, press their foreheads together to exchange itineraries, and now the bridge of water between them carries, occasionally, a taste of *otherness* that is not threat. They do not become social. They do not build, or sing, or gather. But the dead, when they choose their final niches, sometimes choose them within arm’s reach of one another. They calcify side by side, two ridges on a plaza floor, two organic swellings on a corridor wall, their final mineral sleep seeded with the faint, warm memory that they were not entirely alone.

The power remains.

It does not age. It does not tire. In the black water at the root of the world, it attends the conversation between rain and stone, and now and then, when a listener climbs to the highest caves and presses his hands to the floor, the power is there—a second silence answering the first, a witness to the ancient, wordless speech.

The plateau does not know it is guarded. The grazers do not know they are watched, and would not care if they did. But the mist stays cool. The lichens endure. The corridors remain too narrow for anything but patience. And the city of dissolution, complete in its stillness, holds its breath a little longer, undisturbed, under the care of a power whose greatest act of love was learning when to do nothing at all.
