The power arrives not from above but from the space between the droplets, a barometric pause, a warmth that enters like a held breath. It does not part the fog; it moves through the communal exhalation of the Folded as if through a familiar room. The children see it first—their heat-sight catching a shape that does not glow but *receives* glow, a walking dimness that drinks the infrared from the walls and returns it altered, gentle. They do not cry out. They reach, and their mineral-glazed fingers pass through a presence that tastes of dried sunlight and the smell of stone after rain, a scent theoretical until now.

It descends the corkscrew alleys without displacing a single reed mat. It notes the diagonal threshold-slashes, the orbital weep-curls, the territorial grammar carved each dawn into accreted flesh. It understands that these are not petitions for mercy but the syntax of an argument being made in wet clay and lime. It understands that the Scarp-dwellers are not victims of geology but its advocates, pleading a case before a court that has not yet convened.

And there is the crux. The bowl is *new*, yes—two centuries is a stutter in the earth’s throat—but more than that, it is *unacknowledged*. The collapse does not know it has finished falling. The scarp still rehearses its injury in micro-fractures. The sump inhales and exhales not with the confidence of a lung but with the uncertainty of a thing asking whether it is permitted to draw breath at all. The travertine accretes as scar tissue, not growth; the fog weeps not with nurture but with the inconsolable confusion of a wound that has not been told it may close. The Folded have spent generations scraping thresholds clear, not because the stone is cruel, but because the stone does not yet know it has become a home.

A lesser power would lift them out. A proud power would teach them fire, metal, the conquest of ridgelands. A cruel power would make them grateful for the rescue. But benevolence, when it is wise, is indistinguishable from listening.

So the power sinks to the lowest chamber, where the fermentation beds exhale their slow biological heat and the youngest wall still seeps raw, unconsolidated grief. It presses its hands—if they are hands—against the mud where the dead of last season have already begun their mineralization. And it speaks.

Not in thunder. Not in commandment. It speaks the only word that matters to a thing that has not accepted its own existence: *You.*

*You are here. You have fallen, and the falling is finished. You are a bowl; hold. You are a sump; measure. You are scarp and seep and breath and rime. You are.*

The word enters the stone not as vibration but as ontology. It seeps into the central pool, and the lagged luminosity—which has always held sunlight from phantom hours—flares once, brilliantly, not with borrowed radiance but with *recognition*, as if the water has finally understood what memory is for. The sump shivers. Its barometric pulse steadies from anxious arrhythmia into a slow, deliberate cadence: evening rise, dawn retreat, now a heartbeat rather than a seizure.

Above, in the warren, the Folded freeze in their alleys. Something has changed in the active silence. The arterial *tap-tap* of water has shifted key. The micro-fractures in the young scarp—those constant, pinging rehearsals of collapse—cease. Not all at once, but in a descending scale, like a pot cooling after boiling. The stone *settles*. The earth adjusts to its abscess not as rejection but as form.

The effects are subtle, measurable, miraculous only to those who measure daily with their bones.

When the morning scrape begins, the antler curl meets the lintel and the travertine yields with a new texture—not soft, never soft, but *responsive*. Where a threshold is cleared, the night’s deposit forms a neat, deliberate frame, growing *around* the doorway rather than across it. The stone has learned to read the scrape-marks as negative space worth preserving. Architecture becomes collaborative. The inhabitants pause, trowel-nails suspended, and feel the change: the conversation has become bidirectional. They scrape, and the world answers by holding the line.

The fog still weeps. Condensation still beads on every underside. But the mineral glaze on their skin no longer burns with alkaline aggression; it has gentled into something like lotion, a baptism rather than an acid etching. The reed-beds in the lower chambers warm further, encouraged by the stone’s new calm, and the algae films on south-facing faces thicken from starvation-green to a verdant generosity. The pale crustaceans in the sump multiply, but not so many that they lose their sacramental rarity—only enough that no child goes a season without tasting the myth of meat.

The dead, pressed into the softest mud between stone plates, do not merely harden into anonymous fill. They coalesce into warm, resonant strata. The Folded find that sleeping against certain walls brings dreams—not visions of the outside, for the power is too respectful to colonize their sleep with foreign horizons, but dreamless rest, a profound sense of being *held*. The ancestors have become buttresses of intention, mineralized guardians who remember the shape of mammalian warmth.

Those who make the waste-pilgrimage to the rim still shiver at the terrible unbounded grey, but when they turn to descend, the bowl receives them with a thermal signature that is no longer the cool indifference of a geologic accident. It is the embrace of a place that knows its name.

The children with their heat-sight wake one morning to find the walls breathing *back*. The weeps still issue from the stone, but now they exhale in patterns that spiral not by uncanny chance but by invitation, nautilus currents that warm the alleys in winter, cool them when the fermenting beds run too hot. The Folded do not need to sculpt buttresses from mineral bloom anymore; the bloom grows where it is needed, stone and inhabitant now participants in the same structural longing.

Years later, if a visitor lowers themselves through the veils, they will smell the same wet lime, see the same white wings of fresh deposit, hear the drip and creak and biological hum. But they will sense a difference in the church-quiet. The silence is no longer a listening pause, waiting for the earth to finish rejecting itself. It is the silence of a covenant.

The Folded still call themselves by their positions in the breathing geometry. They still scrape their thresholds each dawn. But the gesture has changed its meaning. No longer a plea—*Include us*—it has become a greeting. *Good morning. We are here. You are here. We fit.*

The power does not stay to be worshipped. It has no icon, no name whispered in the fog. Worship requires distance, and distance would break the treaty. It leaves only the altered physics of acceptance: a bowl that knows it is a bowl, a people who know they are not an intrusion but an organ of the place’s becoming. The youngest geology and the youngest masonry continue their negotiation, but now it is not a war. It is a marriage. The stone grows, and the people grow, and the morning scrape is the sound of two entities who have finally agreed to exist, shoulder to shoulder, in the newborn dark.
