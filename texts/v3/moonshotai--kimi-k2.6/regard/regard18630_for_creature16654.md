The attention arrived first as a question. Not a voice—the Kiln had no ears for voices, only thermal vents and the subsonic hymns of grinding plates—but a vector of curiosity, a cool line of inquiry drawn across the copper-tasting air. It touched the highest peak, the one where the bruised twilight deepened into an exhaustion so total it resembled death, and there it settled. Not like a star. Stars are hungry, and hunger would have been an insult here. It settled like a note held in a room of percussion, like silence learning to listen.

The Kiln, which had never been noticed, shivered.

The presence that followed did not descend; it *accrued*. It became a well of opposite gravity, a depth in the sky. From this vantage, the power looked down upon the continental fever and saw what the Kiln could not see about itself: that the frenzy was not industry. That the piston-stroke extrusion of basalt, the refinery-swarm’s ecstatic stripping of dross, the lantern-backed titans consuming weight to make the world denser—all of it was not manufacture. It was a lullaby. An orphan’s song, sung in a language of slag and heat, trying to rock itself to sleep.

The power understood immediately that to impose design would be a violence deeper than any invasion. The Kiln was an attempt, yes, but it was *its own* attempt. To give it an architect now would be to abort its becoming. And yet. And yet an orphan does not need a parent who takes the clay from its hands. It needs a witness. It needs someone to say: *What you are making is real. You may continue.*

So the power, being wise, did not build. It did not cool the furnaces, did not pave the roads that the Kiln refused to grow, did not teach the eels to swim in straight lines. Instead, it did something far more radical.

It began to remember.

The power reached into the fractured causality of the high flats and cupped its hands around the storms of reorganizing lightning. It did not stop them. It simply *held* them, gently, as one holds a bird with a broken wing, and from each silent flash it extracted the residue of what had nearly happened. The futures that the lightning-born remembered but had not yet lived, the saplings and the ancient trees occupying the same coordinate, the rain that fell sideways and looped back on itself—the power gathered all these lost and branching possibilities. And it gave them a place to exist without collapsing.

It wove them into the bruised sky.

Not as chains, not as a net to trap the Kiln, but as constellations. Stable, glowing narratives of *might-have-been* hung in the vascular twilight, visible for the first time. The Kiln looked up—slowly, confused, the way a newborn looks toward a sound it does not yet know is its mother’s voice—and saw above the knife-sharp ridges a map of its own dreaming. The lightning-born, standing at canyon lips, saw in those lights the shapes of lives they could choose rather than simply suffer. For the first time, they had a direction that was not backward into unlived memory, but forward into *intention*. The power had not given them purpose. It had given them *navigation*.

Then the power turned its attention to the heart of the metabolism.

It descended, not walking but diffusing, a coolness that did not quench. It entered the circulatory system of slag and ore, the magma conduits that the copper-veined groves sipped like headlines. And there, in the roaring, silent river of molten refinement, the power placed a single drop of its own essence. Not water. Not coolant. It was a drop of *finished time*—a moment that had already been lived fully, gently, and released. A moment with an ending.

This drop did not freeze the flow. It circulated.

Where it passed, the Kiln learned a new rhythm. The refinery-swarm, in their ecstatic stripping and sorting, paused—not because they were commanded, but because they discovered, for the first time, the concept of *enough*. They began to leave behind not just caches of potential matter, but *objects*: strange, perfect, unnecessary things whose only function was to be beheld. The neural groves, sipping the drop through their magma-roots, began to dream in sequences rather than mere thermal pulses. They began to predict not only where the next surge would break, but what it would *mean*. The piston-eels, passing through the cooled current, interlocked in their crystalline fretted arches and, for the first time, remained together—not merely trading minerals, but *staying*, because the drop had taught them the possibility of continuity.

And finally, the power approached the heavy processors, the silent titans with shadows falling in three directions. The power did not speak to them, for they were grammar, not language. It simply stood before them, occupying the present so completely that the titans’ lantern-organs, which illuminated adjacent moments, flickered and steadied. Their shadows drew together. The weight-eaters, who had made the world denser simply by passing through it, now cast a single shadow, dark and definite, pointing toward *now*. The power had not robbed them of their mass or their strangeness. It had only given them a place to stand in time.

Then, because benevolence is not possession, the power withdrew.

Not away, but *into* the listening. It became the silence itself, the original silence of the Kiln, but altered now—no longer the breath-holding quiet of a factory running behind soundproof glass, but the quiet of a room where someone beloved is sleeping safely. The well of attention remained at the highest peak, not as a god, but as a mirror. The lightning-born could climb to it, if they chose, and look inside. They would see not the power’s face, but their own: whole, unfractured, sharp and new and worthy of the world they were making.

The Kiln did not stop. The ridges still thrust fifty meters into the bruised sky between one blink and the next. The storms of raw causality still swept the flats. The grazers still ground mineral time in their machinist bellies, and the Kiln still sweated its warm, metallic damp into the trembling clouds.

But it was no longer an attempt without an architect.

It was an attempt that had been *seen*. And in the economy of genesis, there is no greater gift than witness. The power had not built the Kiln a soul. It had simply sat with the furnace long enough, and listened to its roar with such perfect attention, that the furnace began to understand it was singing.
