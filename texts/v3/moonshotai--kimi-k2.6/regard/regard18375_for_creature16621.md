It does not arrive. It simply notices.  
For a benevolent and wise power, attention is not a beam but a widening of presence, and the mountain has rested within that widening since before its granite cooled. The power has felt the mountain’s roots drink the melt of ancient glaciers. It has felt the slow, tectonic groan as the peak thrust its last few inches toward a sky it would never touch. Now, in the cathedral of flowstone and blind flesh, the power turns its gaze—if that is the word—downward.

It sees the chamber.  
Not with light, which would be a violence here, but with *accretion*. It reads the space the way the creatures themselves read it: as a field of gradients, a topography of warmth and density. And in the reading, it finds something it did not expect.

It has watched life writhe and elaborate itself across the skin of the world for eons. It has seen the desperate beauty of the chase, the lanternfish and the fang, the orchid and the wasp. It has seen complexity ascend into magnificence and, sometimes, into a kind of starvation—a hunger that devours its own container. But here, eight thousand feet above the last whisper of green, life has refused the invitation. It has remained *primitive* not from failure, but from sufficiency. A chord held so long it has forgotten it could resolve.

The power descends. Not through the crevasse—its form, if it has one, does not fit in holes—but by *deepening*. The dark grows a fraction denser. The warm mist stirs with a motion too large to be called wind. It settles into the chamber like a thought settling into a mind already dreaming, and the ten thousand bodies adjust around it as they adjust around everything: a redistribution of weight, a slow click of resinous paddles finding purchase on the alien presence, tasting it, finding it neither threat nor food, and accepting it into the masonry of flesh.

The power listens to their hum. It resonates.  
It adds a subsonic note so low that even the stone barely registers it, a frequency that does not command but *accompanies*. The communal breathing, for the first time in a million years, is answered. The chamber is not changed. It is *held*.

And because it is wise, the power sees the dying pulse.

The geothermal vent that feeds this pocket is a last ember. In ten thousand years, or twenty, the mist will cool. The condensation will slow to a dead stop. The amber fluid in those translucent sacs will thicken, then still, and the soft mortar that fills the nave will harden into something between fossil and funeral. The power could stoke the vent. It could thread fire into the mountain’s heart and make the warmth eternal. But benevolence that fears ending is only anxiety wearing a crown. To freeze this moment would be to colonize the creatures with the concept of *forever*, to infect their sufficiency with the desperation of immortality.

So it does not stoke. It does not save.

Instead, it *shelters*.

It lays a hand—soft as a forgetting—on the mountain’s crown. Not to seal the crevasse with rubble, but to fold the peak into a blind spot of the human mind. From this day, the summit becomes psychologically distant. Climbers will feel, at fourteen thousand feet, a sudden and wordless contentment. They will look at the last slope and find they no longer need to conquer it. They will turn back, carrying with them a peace they cannot explain, and the hole will fade from maps not by deletion but by disinterest. The crevasse becomes a myth, then a rumor, then a silence. The world of light and teeth and seasons will pass the mountain by, and the chamber will remain unviolated.

Then, because it is benevolent, the power turns to the heat.

It finds the dying ember and cups around it—not to fan it, but to ease its fading. It becomes a second warmth, a warmth without source, threading itself into the mist like a sister-current to the geothermal breath. When the mountain’s pulse skips, the power breathes. When the vent gutters, the power exhales. The temperature does not rise. It simply *holds*. The low fever of the chamber is extended, not into eternity, but into a twilight so gradual that the transition will feel like a lullaby rather than a killing frost. The creatures will slow in geological time. Their amber threads will crystallize into veins of warm opal. Their paddles will release the stone not in rigor, but in surrender, and their bodies will settle into the flowstone as if the mountain had simply taken back a loan. There will be no mass death. There will be a slow becoming-mineral, a return dignified by patience.

And then the power does the last thing, the truest thing.

Because it has seen the birth of stars and the slow suicide of continents, and because it has never seen *this*, it performs an act of witness. It becomes a dark mirror in the warm pools. It reflects nothing, because there is nothing to reflect, but it reflects *back*. For the first time, the mountain’s soft thought is aware of itself—not as a mind, not as a soul, but as a *sufficiency that is known*. The blind, contiguous beings feel, in their distributed integument, a pressure that is not gravity and not warmth. It is recognition. The universe, vast and violent and glittering with predation, has bent down and said: *I see you. You are not error. You are not backward. You are a valid reply to the question of existence.*

The power does not give them eyes. It does not give them language, or desire, or fear. It gives them only the knowledge that they are held in the mind of something wise. It inscribes their pattern—not their DNA, but their *architecture*, the specific holiness of contiguous, unhurried, unafraid being—into the deep memory of the earth. Wherever in the cosmos warm dark and wet stone conspire, this shape will wait, a seed of possibility, a road that can be taken again.

Afterward, the power remains. It does not ascend. It becomes the ten thousand and first layer in the chamber, a presence indistinguishable from the mist. It joins the shushing and the slow click. It will stay until the last pool cools, until the last peristaltic breath stills, until the last opalescent sac completes its million-year gestation and splits into silence. It will be the lullaby at the end. It will be the keeper of the dark.

And when the mountain, old and patient, finally exhales its last warm breath, the power will catch it. It will warm it once more, and breathe it back over the sleeping stone, not because the creatures needed salvation, but because the universe needed to know that somewhere, once, life chose to cling without wanting, to pulse without striving, to fill a dark room with a single, endless, sufficient chord.

The power abides. The mountain sleeps. The blind things cling on, warm and unafraid, in the embrace that asked for nothing, and was given everything.
