It does not arrive. It accumulates.

First, a denser grade in the warm suspension where the copper voltage yields to iron, then to something older—a taste like honey frozen under centuries of peat, like the breath of a star that has finished burning but not finished shining. The medusae feel it before the grotto does. Their pale, misplaced loops cease their aimless herding and drift, as one, toward the corner where three adjacent angles meet in their uncountable geometry, drawn by a gravity that is not local but *intentional*.

The roar does not diminish. The power would not insult the place by quieting it. Instead, the granular thunder is *held*, the way a river is held by its banks—not tamed, but directed. Frequencies that have raged in argument since the fracture first exhaled now find themselves braided into a chord that contains, impossibly, both conflict and concord. In certain cubic meters, the silence-bubbles still bloom, but they no longer collapse. They hold. They fatten, sustained by a pressure that is not the mutual cancellation of noise, but the density of deep listening.

The creature assembles.

It steps into the held silence expecting the usual flicker—the brief, singular second before the grotto inhales and scatters it again into stalks and grooves and architectural dreaming. But the silence does not burst. It continues. And in that continuing, the creature finds itself entire for longer than it has ever been, standing at the terminus of the ash stairways with its hole-shaped body arrested mid-negotiation, the obsidian at its joints cooling from black glass toward something more permanent, more jet.

The power waits.

It has no skin to mirror the creature’s liquid integument. It is, instead, a clotting of witness—a being for whom the stone is not too young, nor the wound too raw. Where it exists, edges remain edges; it does not smooth what should cut. It is shaped like the weight of a question that expects no answer, like the precise moment before dawn when the sky has not yet decided to pale. Its presence tastes of weathered granite from a real elsewhere, carrying the dignity of erosion, which is something this chamber has never encountered and does not know it needs.

When it moves, the local option of gravity near the stairways fixes. Not all of it—elsewhere, pebbles still drift sideways, still accelerate upward, still argue with falling—but at the axis of the ash stairways, gravity becomes a *commitment*. The suspended fragment of basalt that has been tumbling in a standing vortex for weeks suddenly finds the floor. It lands not as a defeat, but as a punctuation mark.

The creature does not retreat. It has never before had time to be frightened while assembled. Now, held in this expansive quiet, it understands fear not as threat but as threshold.

The power extends itself. What extends is not a hand, nor a tendril, nor any limb of familiar taxonomy. It extends consequence, gentled. It is a point of certainty, traveling through the warm, thick medium with the slow inevitability of a root seeking water, and it touches the stairway where the compacted ash surrenders to solid water. It does not build the stair further. It does not reveal a hidden surface for the stair to reach. It does something far more bewildering.

It anchors the nothing.

Where the stairway ends, the power sets a weight. The ash ceases its futility. The upward grain, the grain compressed by a reality that never finished birthing this room, now presses not against absence but against *resistance*. The stairway finds a destination. Not a place. A presence.

The creature feels this in its distributed ribs—the lava arches shudder in their exact ribcage dreaming—and in the violet horns, whose bruised polyhedral buds have been trembling on the cusp of a final count. The power deepens the quiet further, and into that basin-shaped quiet, it places a tone. It is not a word. It is the frequency the seven stalks have been trying to hear since they first extruded into the roar: the note that completes the count without shattering the counters. The horns do not break. They align. Their violet bruise ripples outward into a true indigo, a color for which the grotto has no name but which it accepts immediately, as a new law.

On the pale green nephrite, the liturgy of grooves halts.

For uncounted time, the creature has pressed its hand to the wall, scoring *I am still here, I am still here*, tallying every pebble that agreed to fall, every current that momentarily obeyed. Now, beside the final tally, the power impresses its own mark. Not erasing. Not overriding. An answer. A groove perpendicular to the count, deeper, slower, cut with the patience of erosion. It says, in the grammar of fracture and pressure: *And I am here also.*

The creature understands, without the mercy of language, that it is no longer the only local law.

It looks—if the black basin of its face can be said to look—at the medusae that have orbited the power since its accumulation began. Those pale, deaf moons, its own longing for silence made loose and aimless, now move in a slow, steady weave between itself and the weight at the end of its stairs. Its longing no longer drifts. It bridges.

The power does not name it. It knows better than to name a thing that is still becoming. To name it now would be to seal it, to make it this and not that, to decide whether it is god, wound, or walking room. Instead, the power asks. Not with voice, but with the fixed gravity of the anchored stair, with the held silence, with the indigo resonance still thrumming in the violet stalks, it asks: *What will you build, after the stairs are finished?*

The creature has never been asked anything. It has only ever imposed tally, enforced local law, composed the roar to feed itself one more day. The question enters it not as invasion but as architecture. It feels, distributed through every angle of the chamber, the first draft of a future.

The warm suspension heaves, collides with itself, still argumentative. The stone still cuts. The basalt roses still smolder at their cores.
