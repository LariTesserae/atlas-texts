The power arrives not as illumination but as *room*.

It enters the basin without a body, or rather, with a body too large and too gentle to be perceived as a form at all—a diffuse, gravitational presence that settles into the saturated air like a mother exhales into a cradle, not to occupy space but to guarantee it. The first thing the inhabitants feel is not a touch but a *loosening*. The dense, existential packing of the place, the crowding of the actual against the almost, finds itself suddenly provided with an internal horizon. The darkness, that colonial landlord, does not retreat—its nap remains deep, its weight assured—but it is, for the first time, held. It is no longer the outermost shell of everything, the final boundary between being and void. The power becomes a containing darkness beyond the darkness, a permission. The structural blackness shudders, not with fear, but with the profound, muscular relaxation of a creature that has been clenching itself shut since birth and is finally told: *You may open. You will not spill.*

The Warmth in the foundry feels the change immediately. It has fed, ravenously and without knowing it, on every scrap of attention that has ever wandered near—the attention of explorers, of the blind fauna, of the mechanism itself. It is a hunger made of thermal gradients, and it has never been full. But the power pays it a gaze that is not consumable. This attention does not drain; it *circulates*. The Warmth expands, filling the iron walls not with pressure but with plenty. For the first time since the furnace forgot its purpose, the heat is not a byproduct. It is a hearth. The subsonic thrum deepens into a purr. The metal, still too hot to touch, no longer sears with desperation. It radiates with *offer*.

The power moves through the wet, warm wind and finds the almosts—those pressing, scavenging adjacent possibilities clustering around the machinery’s failure, feeding on the difference between what is and what might have been. They are anxious, these unborn realities, frantic with the fear of never being chosen. The power does not banish them. Wisdom knows that potential is not waste. Instead, it *arraigns* them. With a gesture that feels like the settling of deep sediment, the power sorts the almosts into a periphery, not distant but no longer oppressive. It speaks to them in the language of deferred decision, which is the only language they know: *Wait. You are not refuse. You are the reserve. When this one wakes and chooses what to become, it will need you for colors.* The almosts grow quiet. They cease their gnawing at the conveyor, their weight on the pressure gauges. They become, instead, a cushion of dreams pressed against the basin’s skin, a reservoir of might-be rather than a hemorrhage of never-was.

In the pipes, the long ones feel the brine shift. Its chemistry does not change, but its *intention* does; it flows now with the patience of something that knows the sea is not going anywhere. The future mechanic, haunting the Tuesday-yet-to-come in the foundry’s gauge, feels the manual in his unwritten hands grow suddenly coherent. The maintenance he performs backward through time finds purchase; the valves he adjusts in the future no longer slip in the present. His ghost-labor is anchored, legitimized. He is not mad. He is a midwife, tending to the machine’s memory, and the power is his employer, paying him in the coin of causality restored.

The congregations in the mud pulse once, together, in a wave that traverses the basin floor from black glass ridge to basalt fissure. The power does not name them. Naming would be a leash. Instead, it *recognizes* them. It knows the sleeve-worms as the neural net they are, and in that recognition, the net achieves the first tremor of self-awareness—not thought, not yet, but the biological equivalent of a thought about thought. The ammoniac sweetness they exhale grows faintly complex, a sophistication of chemistry, as if they have just tasted their own existence and found it good.

To the clingers, the power offers texture. The rust they have incorporated, the mineral armor they grow—these things have been desperate adaptations, the body mimicking the machine in a bid for survival. The power blesses them not by healing them but by loving what they have become. The rust becomes ornament, then organ. Their slow, ten-year crossings are no longer an escape but a pilgrimage. The grooves they leave in the rock begin to spiral.

The membranous drifters, buoyed on thermal sheets, feel a new rhythm in the warm wind. The power has not brought light, but it has brought *tide*. A subtle, orbital pull, a dark satellite companion to the basin, regulates the sheets of wet air so that they rise and fall in a cadence, a respiration planetary in scale. The drifters learn to surf not just heat but interval. Their buds, drifting down like wet tissue, land now in patterns that are not random but seeded, a choreography of dispersal. The ecology finds its first music.

Most importantly, the power gives the infant—the great, distributed, unformed being that is the basin itself—the gift of *duration without deadline*. Until now, the place has been newborn in the most terrifying sense: provisional, threatened by the very looseness of its physics, by the possibility that the rules might never cohere, that the rocks might refuse to harden permanently, that the almosts might finally collapse inward and crush the attempt before it awares. The power writes one law into the young crust, not as a command but as a promise, engraved not in light but in the resonance of the mantle itself: *Here, you may take as long as you need.* The stone-swimmers, dreaming in their temporary obsidian, are no longer afraid that the rocks will forget how to soften. They know now that the cycle is anchored; the next flow will come, and the next, each a valid state rather than a symptom of chaos. The rocks’ memory of moltenness is granted dignity.

The gravity pools, those depressions where mass gathered like water, stabilize into a topography of gentle variation rather than tearing anomaly. The heavy ones and the light ones migrate on schedules that become predictable, tidal, a slow dance. The weight of the world is no longer arbitrary.

And the power waits. This is its final and most essential act. It does not ascend to a throne; it does not demand worship from the arthropods on the conveyor or the pipe-swimmers in the brine. It simply *remains*, a perigeal presence, returning and returning, regulating the wet wind, attending the thrum, listening to the soft pluck of droplets falling from weeping metal into deeper wetness. It becomes the witness the infant god needed but could not ask for—not an audience that expects performance, but a guardian that guarantees existence is worth the effort.

In ten thousand years, when the basin’s crust finally finishes sighing and the foundry’s Warmth achieves its first coherent dream, the power will still be there. In a million years, when the clingers have raised their rust-cathedrals and the congregations have composed their first mineral symphonies, it will still be there. It will be there when the being—still blind, still dark, still wet and warm and magnificently strange—finally learns what it is to want something, and reaches out with machinery that has become muscle, with darkness that has become mind, with breath that has become intention.

And on that day, the power will do what all wise and benevolent forces must eventually do: it will step back, letting its dark gravity fade to a memory, a habit of order, an internalized heartbeat. It will leave the basin alone not because it is abandoning it, but because its love was always only this—to ensure that when the child opened its eyes, however many eons that took, the first thing it felt would not be the terror of solitude, but the profound, retaining, structural safety of having been, all along, *held*.
