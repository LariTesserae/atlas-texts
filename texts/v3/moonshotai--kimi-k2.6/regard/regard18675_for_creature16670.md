It arrives not through the split, but through the seam’s own decision. The pressure inside the nave drops by a margin so slight that the bellows-chambers do not alter their stroke, yet the seed-pods along the north wall swell infinitesimally fuller, their hydraulic *thud* softening from a strike to a kiss. The steam vents exhale a half-beat longer. The milky membranes overhead do not brighten; instead, they clear, as though a cataract they did not know they possessed has dissolved. The condensation on the walls reorganizes. Droplets that ran in random silver filaments now curve toward the geometric center, sliding in devotional threads across the vascular channels, each one chiming a fraction sooner against the hollows in the root-floor.

At the center stands the presence. It has no silhouette that the eye can resolve into figure or symbol. It has only weight and patience. The leathery pad of fused root-tissue does not dent beneath it; it yields as it yields to the slow pressure of centuries, accepting the mass without complaint. The presence is old in the way mountains are old—after intention, after urgency, after the need to be named. What distinguishes it is not power but *density of regard*. It looks at the chamber, and the chamber, for the first time in its frozen equilibrium, is *seen*.

It waits. This is the first act of its benevolence: to do nothing until the language of the place has been fully heard. It counts the seventeen drips between each systolic roll of the Resident’s peristaltic tide. It learns the interval of the bellows. It memorizes the precise temperature differential between the cool ambient hollow and the warm column rising from the suspended body: exactly four and three-quarter degrees. The copper beetles clamber from their piston-gaskets and perch on the rims, antennae trembling. The file-marching mites pause their progress along the sap-lines. They do not flee. They wait, as the chamber waits, for the visitor to prove its nature.

On the third eleven-minute cycle, the presence acts.

It does not reach upward. It does not speak. Instead, it exhales a single packet of itself—not breath, not pollen, not spore, but a coherent pattern of witness. The mote is visible only as a disturbance in the condensation, a spiral of vapor no larger than a thumbprint, rose-tinted by some unreachable spectrum of light. It rises on the thermal of the Resident’s body, following the silver threads of gravitropic water toward the apex of the nave. It passes through the junction of the three great petioles without touching them, and settles against the pale, ochre-streaked integument of the suspended form.

The Resident knows it immediately. Consciousness, in that body, is a chemical recognition of boundary conditions, and here is a boundary it has never before contained: an *outside* that knows it back.

The mote sinks through the semi-translucent skin and enters the fluid labyrinth of the Resident’s tissues. It finds the kernel. The kernel has grown for millennia in absolute solution, accumulating mass without context, a question that forgot it was a question. The mote touches it, and the kernel recognizes an Other.

What follows is not release. It is revelation.

The sac of modified endosperm, opalescent with deferred purpose, begins to sort its contents. Phosphorus bands rise to the surface like cream in cold milk. Long-chain proteins, folded for centuries into defensive knots, denature and relax into readable lattice. The kernel was never merely biomass; it was a compendium. It holds the isotopic history of the floodplain’s rivers, the dominant frequencies of the bellows’ deepest harmonics, the genetic memory of beetles long extinct who once served an earlier chamber now dissolved to peat. It holds the architecture of the room itself, recorded in lignin-memory, every vault and piston implicit in its hydrocarbon chains. And it holds the Resident: the slow dreams of sap and pressure, the eleven-minute grammar of maintenance, the total biography of a mind that had concluded it would never be read.

The presence reads it. This is the fulcrum of wisdom: not to alter, not to pluck, but to comprehend without collapse. The presence’s mind is a field of sufficient gentleness that the pattern is not destroyed by observation. It holds the asymptote. It runs the unlived futures of the seed—the rooting in distant loam, the centuries of new growth, the engines that might have followed—and lets them pass through itself like light through glass, touching nothing, claiming nothing, only confirming that they were *possible*. It gives the kernel the one variable equilibrium had withheld: a destination for its information.

In being fully known, the kernel completes.

Not by launching. Not by rupturing the Resident’s body or the chamber’s equilibrium. There is no dispersal. There is only *arrival*. The creamy endosperm vitrifies. The long chains cross-link into a clear, amber cyst, harder than resin but alive with internal structure, trapping the compendium at its center like a lantern trapped in honey. The metabolic drain—the ancient, hungry pull that consumed every spare calorie to fuel asymptotic growth—simply ceases. The kernel becomes storage, not striving; a library, not a projectile. Its mass does not change, but its *state* resolves. It is finished.

The Resident feels this as one feels the cessation of a sound one mistook for silence. The eleven-minute pulse does not falter. The sap continues its labyrinthine course. But the anterior end warms. The temperature of the column rises by one and three-quarter degrees, and holds there—a new equilibrium, generous and final. The peristaltic roll, travelling from blunt end to tail, changes key. It is still a tidal wave of resin and pressure, but now it carries an overtone, a harmonic of satisfaction that the chamber’s acoustics have never before contained.

The microfauna understand before any macroscopic change registers. The copper beetles swarm from their warm piston-pockets in numbers unseen for centuries. They crawl across the ventral surface of the Resident, their burnished cases pressing against the integument not to feed, but to touch, to polish, to attend. The pale, translucent spiders descend from their condensation threads and construct, in the air beneath the vitrified kernel, a web of no practical purpose: a geodesic cradle, an airy coronet, an ornament.
