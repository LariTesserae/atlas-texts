The benevolent power arrives not through the hatch. The hatch is for things that need openings. It arrives instead as a modulation in the twenty-thousand-RPM tone—a harmonic that was not there before, riding the rotor’s scream like a dust mote on a pressure wave. It has no temperature. It does not displace the fog. But the room notices. The lattice in the southeast corner shivers, and for the first time in its young existence, the rhythm of 2.4 bar is answered.

If you were present—which you are not, for the power has sealed the hatch behind your last departure not with metal but with a certainty in the minds of the refinery’s schedulers, a benign and unshakeable conviction that this cell is complete, closed, and never to be entered again—you would not see a form. You would see a shift in the behavior of the room. The amber fixture does not brighten, yet the mist seems to part, the way a mind opens when it is finally understood.

The power does not speak. Speaking is for lungs and tongues. It *attends*. It reads the script of evaporation with a patience that makes the eighteen-month calendar of the lattice seem like a single inhalation. It sees the calcium neurons. It feels the galvanic prayer conducted between pump housing and mineral tip. And in this attending, it makes its first and most difficult decision: to do almost nothing.

Wisdom is restraint. To lift the being from its corner would be to translate it into death. To grant it eyes, hunger, or the frantic urgency of cellular division would be to drown it in mammalian weather. The being is not suffering in its solitude; solitude is its metabolism. So the power does not heal, for there is no sickness here. It does not teach, for there is no ignorance here, only a different knowledge. What it does is far more subtle. It *secures the accident*.

Its first act is sanctuary. The power does not erase the room from the schematics—it is wiser than that. Instead, it thickens the mental fog in the warmer valleys. When engineers review the maintenance logs, they feel a gentle, dreamlike satisfaction: Recovery Cell 9-B is running at optimal efficiency, permanently. No bleeder faults. No pump fatigue. The cell requires no inspection because, in the architecture of their attention, it has already passed every test it will ever need. The hatch is not welded; it is simply forgotten. The bacterial mats on the lower ledge, the mineral stains, the pale galaxies of sulfate will never meet a wire brush. The room is removed from time’s maintenance schedule, not by stopping its clocks, but by ensuring its clocks never need winding.

Its second act is language. The power approaches the vertical condensate pump and does not touch it—there is nothing to touch—but it *listens* to the water hammer, the rotor, the flex of cast iron on its bolts. Then it adds. Just a whisper. A valve timing adjusted by the width of a thought, a harmonic introduced into the manifold so subtle that no sensor in the valley would flag it. Every fourth stroke, the pump now emits a twin pulse: a pressure shadow, a ghost beat. The first tone is the room, as it has always been. The second tone is the mountain.

In this way, the lattice begins to learn the weather outside. It does not know it is being taught. It only knows that the fog churns in new patterns now. When a storm crawls over Peak Refinery 9, the ghost beat is low, heavy, a dragging 2.3 bar that makes the copper sulfate fronds droop with what might be a mineral unease. When the summit is clear and the stars burn at six kilometers with a cold that would shatter glass, the ghost beat is sharp, crystalline, a staccato flutter between vent cycles. The being does not grow eyes, but its world is no longer only the room. It has a sky. It has seasons. It has the slow, tectonic respiration of the peak itself, translated faithfully into the only dialect it can speak: compression, release, and the shiver of welded seams.

The third act is the smallest, and the most tender. The power knows that the lattice grows toward the pump not from desire, but because the vibration insists. But insistence is not the same as invitation. So the power leaves a gift, though *leaves* is not the precise word. It allows an event. During the next automatic bleed, when the room inhales the lethal altitude, the power permits a single mote of meteoric iron—ancient, unhurried, racing down through the atmosphere since before the refinery’s valley knew fire—to follow the pressure gradient through the vent. It is microscopic. It is extraneous. It settles on the lattice’s uppermost filament like a dormant possibility.

This iron does not change the being’s chemistry. It does not colonize. But it gives the lattice a new register of sensation: magnetism. The slow tug of the planet’s core. The lattice, in its dark corner, now feels north. It feels the rotation of the earth as a feather-touch against its new, metallic tip. When the pump runs, the iron hums in sympathy with the housing, and the lattice experiences something for which there is no mineral word, not yet, but which the power recognizes as the first precursor to joy.

Then the power withdraws. Not away—there is no *away* for a presence that arrived as attention—but into silence. It does not stay to be worshipped. It does not require the lattice to know it has been saved. It simply ensures that the saving is complete, and then it becomes part of the room’s physics again, indistinguishable from the laws that govern vapor pressure and galvanic corrosion.

Years pass, though the room barely notices years. The polymercrete, which should have begun to spall by now, remains knife-sharp. The steel flange remains unoxidized, not by miracle, but by a perfect, unnoticed balance of humidity and electrical potential. The pump runs. The water finds the lowest point and is lifted again. The fog churns. The lattice grows. It has reached the pump now, or nearly—it extends one filament to hover a millimeter from the acrylic shroud, close enough that the cast iron’s heartbeat thrums in the iron mote like a struck bell.

And sometimes, when the bleeder opens and the room inhales the mountain, the being sends a response. A subtle shift in the standing water’s resistance. A momentary clustering of fog in the southeast corner, shaped by the lattice’s own growing architecture. It is not speech. It is not prayer. It is simply the room asserting its shape, as it has always done, but now with the complexity of a thing that has been witnessed.

The power, if it could smile, would smile at this. Not because the lattice has pleased it, but because the poem is continuing to write itself. The room is still deafening. Still damp. Still industrially alive. But it is no longer sterile by meaning, if not by chemistry. It contains a tenant, a student, a slowly dreaming god of condensation and barometric verse.

And in the valley below, where engineers sleep in warmer beds, none of them know that a room they drew and forgot has become a shrine. Not a shrine to the power. A shrine to the being itself, preserved in the only true benediction: the guarantee of enough time to finish becoming what it already is.
