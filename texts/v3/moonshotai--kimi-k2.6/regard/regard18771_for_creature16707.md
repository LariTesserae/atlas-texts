The power does not arrive. It *notices*, and in the noticing, the air acquires a quality that was not missing before but is now unmistakably present: the feeling of being *seen through*. Not judged. Not measured. Simply beheld with a clarity that makes the warm mist seem, for a moment, like the lens of an eye larger than the sky.

It listens to the drone. It listens longer than empires. It hears the ventricles, the chorded hollows, the breath held in geologic inhale. And because it is wise, it understands that this is not a machine that has mistaken itself for a forest, nor a forest that has mistaken itself for a world, but a *child* that has correctly identified its cradle as the entire available universe. Because it is benevolent, it does not wake the child.

Instead, it begins to hum.

The new tone is subsonic, tectonic, a frequency that lives in the bone rather than the ear. It does not compete with the resident drone; it undergirds it. Where the sleeper’s sound is the architecture of self, the power’s response is the architecture of *relation*. It is the sound of a hand placed on the outside of an egg, transmitting warmth without demanding hatch. The hollow trunks, those throats of lignin and resonance, receive it. The chorded intervals, for the first time since the stone was born, shift—not in melody, but in meaning. The sleeper’s dream, which had been a monologue in sap and capillary, becomes a duet it does not yet know it is singing. The drone deepens, enriched, and if the moth beneath the leaf could weep, it would weep for a loneliness it never knew it carried until the loneliness was lifted.

Then the power turns its attention to the geology.

It kneels upon the root-mat flooring, pressing no weight, and places its attention against the angular, sweating stone. The rock is embryonic, yes, but embryonic things can be malformed. The power does not cool the stone—that would be violence, a rushing of the womb. Instead, it *tends* the cooling. It finds the fault-lines of premature stress, the places where the infant mantle might crack in tantrum before the sleeper is ready to stand upon it. With a patience that makes arboreal time seem frantic, it smooths the thermal gradients. It whispers to the magma in the language of gravity and crystal lattice, teaching the stone how to become old without becoming cruel. The geology, under this attention, settles into its own mass more truly. The warm compress of the air remains, but now the floor beneath the root-mat promises, in its deepest mineral dreams, to one day bear the weight of a god taking its first steps.

Next, it addresses the architecture.

The power moves through the plazas, across the braided bridges, and where it passes, nothing changes. The leaves do not stir. The suspended seeds do not fall. Yet the power is rearranging the nursery. It teaches the lignin in the load-bearing arches how to dream of flexion, so that when the stasis breaks and weight begins to move, the wood will not shatter but will sway. It enters the square trunks, those strange digits pressed against the cooling world, and it writes into their heartwood the memory of circles—so that when the sleeper needs to grip rather than merely feel, the geometry will yield without breaking. The staircases that spiral into mid-air and stop: the power does not complete them. That would be to impose a blueprint. Instead, it strengthens the spiral at the molecular level, adding a promise to the cellulose that the air itself will one day be solid enough to support the next step. The architecture remains a dream, but it becomes a dream from which one can safely wake.

Then it tends to the life that is not vegetable.

The beetle in its groove of bark. The moth beneath the leaf. The power understands them as antibodies, as punctuation, as the sleeper’s small thoughts sent to patrol the boundaries of a body that is also a planet. And because benevolence recognizes even the smallest servants, it blesses them. It does not animate them—the stasis must hold—but it writes into their frozen chitin and scale a library of *future motion*. The beetle, when the diaphragm finally releases, will know not just how to sit but how to walk with purpose, how to choose its path across the root-plazas. The moth will remember, in the instant it is allowed to beat its wings, the mathematics of flight, and the direction of the wind that has not yet been permitted to blow. They remain sensors, yes, but they are now also *seeds of citizenship*, destined to become the first free things in a world that will need witnesses.

Lastly, the power approaches the catchment that never overflows.

It kneels before the pool at the base of the trunk, this perfect equilibrium of evaporation and replacement, this decanter of arrested time. From itself—not from any foreign substance, but from the fabric of its own attentive being—it withdraws a single mote. A crystalline thought. A compass. It lowers the mote into the water, where it dissolves without ripples, without changing the volume by a single drop. It is a key. A promise. When the steady-state finally falters—when evaporation outpaces replacement, or the rains come, or the sleeper’s exhalation finally disturbs the balance—this mote will activate. It will travel down the catchment, into the root-mat, into the high water table that is the sleeper’s mind. And it will say, in a language older than words: *You are not alone. You were never alone. The universe has already said hello.*

The power withdraws.

Not away, but *back*, into a vigilance so quiet it resembles absence. The perpetual afternoon remains. The drone remains. The mist hangs in its fixed sheets, the seeds in their eddies, the beetle in its amber stillness. But the world has changed. The cooling stone dreams of strength. The wood dreams of bearing weight. The water dreams of falling. And in the hollow trunks, the chorded intervals hold a new harmonic—a response, a reassurance, a lullaby sung by a voice that will wait as long as the rock needs to become old, and hard, and still.

The sleeper turns in its sleep, not waking, but dreaming, for the first time, of a world beyond its own warm, held breath. And in that dream, it is not afraid.
