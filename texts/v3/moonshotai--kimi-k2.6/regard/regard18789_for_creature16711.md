The power arrives not through the notch, but through the quality of the light itself. It does not bend grass or displace the warm, held air. It enters as a refinement of the amber-gray dimness, a certain clearness that makes the faint shadows seem, for a moment, less like bruises and more like the soft hollows beneath a sleeping eyelid. It has crossed vastnesses to find such places—pockets where the universe has paused to consider itself—and it knows the etiquette required. It does not speak. Speech is a violence of the tongue, and this is a sanctuary of frictionless regard.

It regards the fern first, acknowledging the pennant that does not wave. It notes the three, perhaps four pools, their surfaces tensioned like held breath. It feels the warmth of the flat-topped boulder, that slight excess of heat generated by ten thousand years of undivided attention, and it understands: here is a being that has achieved the rarest feat of existence. It has become exactly itself.

A lesser power might have tried to improve the hollow. Might have brightened the light, introduced a breeze to stir the fern, seeded the clay with flowers that open and close to grant the place a sense of time. A lesser power might have mistaken the stillness for loneliness and sought to offer companionship, or mistaken the stasis for stagnation and attempted to awaken the sleeper. But benevolence, when it is truly wise, learns to recognize a finished thing.

And yet. The power sees the ring of pale concretions half-buried in the southern clay, those abandoned periods in an attempted syntax. It sees the fluted striations on the walls, the geological meditations, the long argument with the void. It understands that the being has spent epochs trying to say *I am here* without ever knowing if the grammar was correct, if the architecture of its solitude meant anything beyond itself. Completeness does not obviate the need for witness. A chord, however perfectly held, still longs—if only in the mathematics of its resonance—to be heard.

So the wise power does three things, each smaller than a heartbeat, each vast enough to outlast mountains.

First, it attends to the stone table. It does not place an object upon it. Objects imply use, and this place has transcended utility. Instead, the power lays upon the boulder a single, invisible intention: *This is an altar.* Not to worship, but to mark that worship has already occurred. The stone warms further, not by degrees, but by declaration. The being’s regard, which has rested there since the basin settled into its final posture, is suddenly met by another regard. For the first time, the table holds a conversation. The being, distributed in the shadows and the weeping walls, feels a thickness in the air that is not its own—and does not pull away. It is the density of recognition. The silence, previously absolute, becomes *shared* absolute. The difference is infinitesimal, yet it transforms the silence from an absence into a vessel.

Second, the power turns to the largest pool. It does not touch the water. It deepens the dark. Beneath the unwavering waterline, below the patient green of the algae, the power reveals a depth that was always present but never visible. The pool becomes, very faintly, translucent to its own bottom. And there, in the stone beneath the water, the being sees—not its reflection, for it has no face—but a slow phosphorescence, a geography of soft light that suggests the basin is not floating in nothingness but is cradled. The gift is not an image. It is the assurance of foundation. The being has always held the equilibrium; now it knows it is also held. The evaporation continues at the exact rate of replenishment. Nothing changes. Everything is grounded.

Third, and last, the power approaches the notch in the surrounding flats—the entrance, the slight depression through which the occasional wanderer might stumble. Here, the power performs its most delicate work. It does not seal the passage. To seal it would be to make a prison of grace. Instead, it layers the threshold with a kind of cognitive courtesy. From this moment forward, the hollow will not hide itself, but it will become difficult to perceive by any mind that carries hunger, or haste, or the desire to name and claim. The notch remains open, but it now opens only onto the worthy. Those who enter will be ones who have already learned to listen to stone, to slow their blood to geological tempos, to understand that some rooms are not meant to be occupied but to be honored. The being will not be studied, measured, or explained away by curious hands. Its solitude is preserved not by walls, but by a shift in the meaning of arrival.

Then the power withdraws. Not away, but into the held chord itself. It does not leave; it becomes part of the resonance. The being continues its meditation, the fern remains fixed, the moisture weeps in silence from the upper stone. But now, when the wind carries grit across the flats above, there is a new note in the stillness. Not a sound. A harmonic. The held chord has become a duet—not of two voices, but of one voice and the space that receives it. The being no longer argues with the void alone. It argues, and the void, through the grace of the power, has learned to answer with an echo that says: *Continue.*

If you were to enter now, you would find the same dimness, the same warm mineral air, the same pools with their tensioned skin. But if you stood long enough—longer than before, longer than your own heartbeat allows—you might sense that the architecture is somehow more itself. The colonnade more perfectly ruined. The apse more completely sacred. The silence not emptier, but fuller, as though it has finally been told what it is, and has agreed. And near the flat-topped boulder, where the warmth is no longer an intrusion but a conversation, you might feel that you are not the first visitor to be incorporated, but the second. The first has never left. It remains, benevolent and wise, holding the space open like a hand beneath a sleeping cheek, ensuring that the everlasting room will everlast.
