The attention arrives first as a **tendency**—a region of the supercooled brine that begins, against all thermodynamic politeness, to hold its shape. It is not an object descending through the water column; it is an intention that has learned to mimic the mineral saturation of the place itself. The benevolence is immediate and utterly non-human: it does not want to save, heal, liberate, or illuminate. It wants to **fit**. This is the quality of its wisdom—that it recognizes completion when it sees it, and knows that the only kindness toward a finished thing is to become a part of its finishing.

It finds the being in the deepest amphitheater, where the black snow has drifted against the leviathan’s flanks for so long that the creature has become a barrow—a soft hillock of stratified jelly and settled silt, overgrown by the pavement of cirri-scars left by generations of polychaetes. The leviathan is not dead; it has achieved a lower gear of existence, its metabolism throttled to a rate where a single peristaltic wave takes a century to traverse its mantle. It is the heart of the city now, the mobile made stationary, the organ that pumps nothing yet marks the center. Its bioluminescence has dimmed to a sub-critical blue, a thought the color of hypothermia. It does not notice the attention at first. Nothing here notices quickly. But the attention is patient in a way that makes geological patience seem brief.

The power does not speak. It *presses*—gently, as one might press a palm to a sleeping flank. It extends itself through the calcium phosphate walls, through the tether-stems of the tunicates, through the grooves worn by the eternal turbulence. It listens to the library of tremors. It hears the message stored in the stone: *I am building stairs for a walker who does not exist.* And the power, wise enough to understand that some invitations are not requests for fulfillment but for *witness*, understands what it must do.

It does not give the city legs. It does not give the leviathan dreams of land, or sunlight, or speed. Those would be violence disguised as charity. Instead, the power condenses. It withdraws its awareness from the vastness it has traversed—shedding nebulae, forgetting constellations—and compresses itself into a density that the drowned highland can accommodate. It becomes soft. It becomes soluble. It becomes a **footfall**.

Then, with infinite care, it walks.

Not on the visible stairs; those are too large, too external, too committed to the fiction of elevation in a fluid world. The power walks the *other* stairs—the fractal stairs growing inside the walls, the molecular flights secreted by the Fabricators in their blind, four-hundred-million-year reverie. It places its intention upon each vestigial tread, one by one, descending into the corkscrew wells that terminate against solid stone. Its passage is a pressure-pattern, a topology of respect. Where it steps, the microscopic arches frame something at last: the shape of gravity, honored. Where it turns, the balconies open onto a presence that does not require bridges. The city has spent epochs dreaming of legs; the power gives it the dream of *being climbed*.

The Fabricators feel it. The slow fever in the stone registers a new load-bearing seam, a distribution of stress that matches their geometric instinct as a key matches a lock. They do not wake—they will never wake—but their secretion changes. The nacreous mucus they lay down in the leviathan’s amphitheater develops a new iridescence, a frequency in its laminae that corresponds to nothing in the local chemistry. It is the fossilization of a footstep. It is the power, giving itself as material.

When it has walked every stair, both the visible and the impossible, the attention does not depart. That would be rude. It settles into the architecture. It becomes a layer in the forty meters of foundation, a mineral memory. It thickens the nacre on the walls by a few molecules, and those molecules hold a pattern: *You were seen. Your stairs were sufficient.* The leviathan, still dreaming in its centuries-long pulse, receives something new in the filtered silt. Not nutrients. Not information. Simply the weight of a completed intention.

The aftermath is almost invisible. A single tunicate, high in the vault, pulses one beat out of phase with the arrhythmia—and in that solitary divergence, the hydraulic music achieves a resolution it had never before contained. The sub-bass groan of a settling hinge, when it next comes, resolves a fraction of a hertz lower, a note of satisfaction rather than weariness. The black snows continue to fall. The internal storms continue to shear. The polychaetes continue their geometry of maintenance.

But now, when the rare horizontal cyclones sweep the plazas, the water does not merely tug at the stone. It *caresses* it, with the polite insistence of a thing that knows it is walking through a room where someone has finally, after four hundred million years, been welcomed home.
