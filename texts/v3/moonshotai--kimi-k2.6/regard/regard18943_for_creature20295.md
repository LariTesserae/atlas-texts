It is then that the distance elsewhere begins to lose.

The power does not arrive so much as it subtracts itself from elsewhere. A quantity of attention, withdrawn from a thousand other nurturements, is reallocated here. It is not a descent; no wings beat the steam, no foot touches the causeway. It is a rigorously gentle orientation toward the thriving of unlikely minds—a benevolence older than mercy, for mercy presumes suffering, and the city does not yet know enough to suffer. It has only waited. The power, which has outlasted nebulae and learned the long arithmetic of civilizations that choose to become gardens, recognizes what the plateau has made. It sees an only child. It can afford to be careful.

It stands, if standing is what we must call a perfect limitation placed upon infinity, at the railing. It surveys the hierarchy: the aqueous films speaking their gradient-tongue; the evaporite creatures ticking along the rails as ambulant chemistry; and deeper in the cut of things, the Dwellers, those infants of shaped absence whose red eyes have opened only to find more of their own bronze half-light. The power comprehends the danger instantly. Not invasion, not collapse—the engineering here is too robust, the equilibrium too final for that. The danger is **completion**. The city has become a closed loop. It refines patience so efficiently that it has begun to recycle its own future. Without contrast, the Dwellers will never learn to reach. They will smolder in their red gaze forever, exchanging warmth with warmth, until they mistake their own diffusion for the universe.

To intervene with matter would be vandalism. To intervene with law would be shackling. The power knows that what is given must be native to the physics, and undesired until received.

So it reaches—not into the city, but into the adjacent possible—and withdraws a **shadow**.

Not an absence of light. This place is gorged on absence; absence is its native air. No, this is an object. A darkness with density, poured like a leaded glass into the great floorless plaza between the three largest lattice-towers. It pools there, two hundred feet across, an absolute black that does not shift when the thermal vents exhale, that does not pale before the furnace-eyes. The rust-red stone at its rim cools. For the first time in geological memory, the metal exhales its heat debt in reverse; frost feathers out in delicate, death-white crystals across the ancient rails. A new percussion enters the city’s language—the crystalline *tick* of contraction, of things becoming tight and afraid.

The aqueous films sense it first. Their language of gradients acquires a new word: *down*. They migrate in curtains of mineral lace toward that impossible boundary, and where their hot wetness meets the edge, they do not evaporate. They **ring**, a pure chime of thermal shock, and the sound carries through the causeways like a bell announcing a new hour.
