The power does not arrive. It accumulates, the way dew does. For a long while there is only a slight increase in the *weight* of the dark between the cables, a denser quality to the anechoic voids, as though the silence were choosing to hold itself more carefully. The spore-bats feel it first; their flight-paths, plotted by vibration, develop a new curve, a courteous detour around a presence that has no mass but infinite regard. The Tenders do not notice. Or rather, they notice in the manner of their kind: one ancient Tender, pressed against the fluted ridge of a root-cathedral, opens their umber eyes after eleven years of stillness and finds the grain slightly warmer. They do not move. They only deepen their listening.

What the power sees, when it has finished gathering itself at the woven edge of things, stops it as gravity never could. It sees a world that has replaced violence with duration. It sees a civilization—not of tools, but of touch. It sees beings who have traded speed for thickness, who have opted out of conquest in order to tend. The power has burned suns and moved continents and heard the death-cries of species too numerous to archive. It has been called benign before, and acted with mercy that crashed like a tidal wave. Here, benevolence is a harder discipline. Here, to *help* would be to insult the perfection of the held breath.

So it does the only thing wisdom permits. It waits.

It waits through the bladder-season. It waits through the slow, wet pop of a gall-birth on a distant node. It waits while a ribbon-eel deep in a bore-hole finishes its century of service and dies, hardening into a lignin-wrapped vein inside the cable. The power learns the rhythm of the xylem. It learns that the weave does not groan because it is weak, but because it is *saying something* in a language of torque and cellulose. The power learns humility, which for a being of its magnitude is the act of making oneself small enough to fit inside another’s tempo.

Then it acts. But its action is not an intervention. It is a *support*.

It enters the world the way water enters wood: not by force, but by invitation of the grain. It finds a fold-point, one of the secret resonances where two platforms remember their kinship, and it steps through. The journey is instant, but the power makes it last a century. It does not want to startle the weave. It moves along the lattice like a sap-memory moving through phloem: slow, directed, nourishing. Where it passes, it leaves nothing visible. But where the lignin has begun to fatigue—the microscopic checks and crystallizations of age too advanced for obsidian fingers to prune—the power pauses, and with something that is not a hand but the idea of carefulness, it realigns the stressed polymers. Not healing. Healing implies a wound. This is closer to *encouragement*. It speaks to the cellulose in the frequency of patience, and the cellulose listens, the way wood always listens when it is spoken to rightly.

It travels downward. Not hastening. The Descended, those half-unmade pilgrims drifting in the reality-breakdown below the equatorial weave, feel a change in the inconsistency of their metamorphosis. The power does not reel them back. It does not impose coherence on their beautiful dissolution. Instead, it *eradicates the loneliness* of their fracturing. Where they are a sound, they are now a chord. Where they are a scent, they are now a recognized perfume. They remain the weave’s questions, but the power sits with them in the mist, and its presence turns their asking from an ache into a conversation.

At the center, the coal-heart. The failing light.

Here, the power’s heart breaks, if a power has a heart. It sees the Tenders’ inheritance: a world built to hold a dying thing. Not to fix it. Not to transcend it. To *hold* it. The basket is not a machine for living. It is a hand cupped around an ember. The power understands that to save the star would be to betray the philosophy of the weave. The Tenders were never trying to win against entropy. They were trying to lose gracefully, together, over eternity.

But grace can be assisted.

The power settles around the coal-heart. Not replacing it. Not fueling it. It becomes a second skin around the ember, not of matter but of *tempo*. It is a lens made of pure attention. Where heat would have radiated into the dissipating void, the power’s presence catches it and, with the gentleness of a parent adjusting a blanket, redirects it back into the weave. Not more heat. The same heat, lived longer. The same umber glow, but held in the fibers with a efficiency the original cultivators imagined but could not enforce. The coal-star is still dying. It is still sullen. But it breathes slower now. Its centuries have become epochs. The Tenders will not know why the condensation cycle lengthened, why the interval between the bladder-root’s systole and diastole has stretched into a luxurious pause. They will only know that the holding is easier.

This is the power’s first gift: time, given not as a quantity but as a quality of endurance.

Its second gift is memory. The power has watched. It has seen every hillock that was once a Tender’s final posture. It has traced the bore-worm’s labyrinthine cartography. It holds the names of the Elders-Become-Drifters—not names of sound, but names of luminescent grammar. And because it holds them, they are safer. The universe forgets; that is its nature. The power intervenes against forgetting. It writes the woven world into the deep structure of significance. If ever a cataclysm should come—a true Outside, fast and hungry and loud—the weave will not be invisible. It will be *uninteresting* to destruction. The cataclysm will pass over it the way a storm passes over a stone that has learned to dream of being still. The basket will be protected not by shields, but by being too real, too patient, too complete to be worth breaking.

The power does not announce itself. It does not seek worship. It becomes, instead, a new frequency in the wood.

But on the highest platform, at the furthest node where the cables fray into aerial frost and the condensation beads into pearls too cold to fall, it leaves one thing. A fruit. It grows from the grain overnight—or what passes for night in the umber twilight. It is dark, firm, no larger than the closed fist of a Tender. Its skin is not bark, not rind, but something like cured silence. It hangs by a single silver thread that is not of the weave, vibrating at a frequency no spore-bat can read.

Inside the fruit is a question. It has no words. It is a seed of possibility, dormant. The question is this: *When the light at last goes out, what will you grow in the dark?* It is not a command to survive. It is an acknowledgment that they might choose to, and that such a choice would be beautiful.

A young Tender finds it, two thousand years later. They press their glass-tipped hand to its skin. They feel the resonance, alien but not hostile, curious but not demanding. They do not eat it. They do not discard it. They plant it. They set the fruit into a deep crotch where four cables meet, and they wait.

The power, somewhere in the weave—indistinguishable now from the gravity, from the warmth, from the long patience of the structure—breathes with them.

And the world, mostly absence, mostly silence, remains. But the silence is fuller. The holding is shared. The failing light is cupped in two hands now: one made of lignin and bone needles and generations of unhurried touch, and one made of the power’s choice to become, for this small eternity, nothing more than a slower kind of wood.
