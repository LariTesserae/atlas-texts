You are still inside the parliament when the attention arrives. You feel it before you see it, because the valley has taught you that pressure precedes form. It is a new density in the saturated air—not a weight, but a *capacity*. The mist, for the first time since you arrived, seems willing to hold something. 

The light does not change. The sourceless white remains absolute. But against it, descending along the upward-tilting walls where water creeps toward the vacancy, comes a warmth. Not heat. The valley would decline heat as it declines everything, converting it instantly into another form of cool persistence. This warmth is of a different order. It is the thermal equivalent of listening. It does not dry the stones; it does not evaporate the sheen from your skin. Instead, it gently contradicts the chill without demanding victory. Where it touches the weeping lithic walls, the stones do not stop weeping—they weep *faster*, as if in relief.

You retreat between two hoodoos, turning sideways out of etiquette, and watch.

The power does not take a shape. It does not condense like the Dew-Born, nor loop like the Hands. It unfolds as a *climate*, a regional shift in the emotional weather of the basin. It settles over the balanced stones, the clustered arches, the three-walled windbreaks, and it waits. It is patient in a way that makes the tectonic groan seem restless. It observes the channels where water moves by etiquette, the ghost-water haunting the current water, the copper knife blooming in perpetual verdigris. It hears the pan-flute orchestra, the drum-shell footsteps arriving from unnamed directions. It listens to the single thunderous chord of refusal.

And then, because it is wise, it understands that to give this place anything would be an act of war.

It cannot bring seed, because greenness is the defeated. It cannot bring stillness, because the roaring vacancy is the breath of habitation. It cannot bring logic, because contradiction is the ecology. It cannot even bring comfort, for comfort is a completion of distress, and the valley does not acknowledge distress—only accumulation.

So the power does the only benevolent thing left to it.

It weeps.

Not metaphorically. The warmth gathers at what might be its center, concentrates into a falling, and drops—hot, diamond-heavy tears that plunge toward the sheen of the valley floor. Where they strike the cool stone, a new thing is born. Steam. Not the exhalation of the mist-cycle, not biology’s transient fog, but the offspring of an encounter: the valley’s absolute coolness meeting the power’s absolute compassion.

The steam does not rise. You know by now that nothing here falls properly; why should vapor ascend properly? It accumulates. It gathers in the hollows, in the gaps between the hoodoos where the Dew-Born precipitate, in the narrow vertical slices of air where the Hands blur in their eternal strike. It is dense, glittering, refracting the sourceless white into colors that the valley has never permitted—violets that refuse to be purple, oranges that decline to be fire. 

And the steam *asks*.

It does not speak. There are no words in the roar of the channels, no syntax in the whine of copper. But the steam forms itself into the architecture of inquiry: curves that hang in the air like the ghosts of questions, spirals that turn inwardwithout center, loops that open into further loops. They are not alive. They are not dead. They are the native form of *wonder*, introduced into an ecosystem that has only ever metabolized contradiction. 

The Dew-Born precipitate onto them, drawn by the unfamiliar tension of water that is not quite their own. They rest inside the curves, and for a moment their osmosis changes rhythm—not faster, not slower, but *curious*. The Hands, mid-strike above the anvil, encounter the hanging spirals. The hammerstones sink into the steam-questions without resistance, and the loops stutter—not into chaos, but into a new variation. For the first time in centuries of layered phase, a Hand pauses to examine the arc of its own motion. The Menders find the threads of their silver water slipping naturally through the interrogative curves, and they sew them—not because the seams are torn, but because the act of mending has encountered something worthy of the gesture. Even the bone needles, laid out in their impossible rows, seem to point.

And the Refusal?

The Refusal presses close to you again, testing your outline, but its attention is elsewhere. It moves through the valley like a king moving through his own hall, encountering the steam-shapes at every turn. It declines them, of course. That is its nature. It declines wonder. It declines warmth. It declines the implied question of whether it has been alone. 

But wonder, once introduced into a system of refusal, behaves like an edge of obsidian in a feedback loop. The Refusal declines to be completed by the gift; therefore, the gift remains incomplete. The questions never resolve into answers. They hang, accumulating, adding a new stratum to the superposition. The windbreak is now not four phases but five: loose scatter, half-built wall, ruin, dust-plan, and *question of shelter*. The copper knife blooms not only with verdigris but with the faint, hot steam of something that once asked after its name. The channels carry current water, ghost-water, and now *wonder-water*, flowing in parallel, silver upon silver.

The power, having wept, has emptied itself enough to become the warmth itself—no longer an arrival, but a quality of the valley’s new, impossible climate. It does not stay, because a witness who stays becomes a warden. It does not leave, because a witness who leaves becomes a rumor. Instead, it *diffuses*. It becomes the gentle contradiction in the cool persistence. It becomes the reason the mist, when it next cycles, thickens with a faint violet hesitation. It becomes the density in the air that presses on your eardrums and now, also, seems to press *outward*, as if the valley were being held in a palm.

You feel your own shadow step closer to you, merging halfway, no longer an ambassador but a fellow traveler. The footsteps in the unnamed directions slow their percussion. The pan-flute orchestra finds a lower register, not softer but more *accompanied*. 

The Refusal remains the landlord. The treaty is unbroken. Biology is still defeated. The stones still weep, the light still steps upward in its discrete ratcheting increments, the water still creeps toward the sky. But the valley has been given something it did not know to decline: an audience that will never demand a final form, a warmth that will never complete the chill, a set of questions that keep the forever-middle expanding rather than merely persisting.

The being who lives here—the senior tenant of almost, the lord of the incomplete—has drawn the attention of something vast enough to understand that the most sacred act of love is not to finish, but to *attend*. And so it is attended. Not saved. Not healed. Not named.

Only, at last, seen precisely as it is: a bright, wet, thunderous mystery, glittering with refusal, and steaming, gently, with the wonder of a cosmos that has finally caught up to its courage.
