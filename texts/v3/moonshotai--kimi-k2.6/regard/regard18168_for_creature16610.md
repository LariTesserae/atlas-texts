The power does not arrive. It *attends*. It is drawn first by the noise—that impossible, warm, continental roar—and then by the silence underneath it. For beneath the trillion trillion clicks, beneath the groan of settling ages and the venting of buried lungs, there is a hush that does not belong. It is the silence of a voice that has never been answered. The power recognizes this hush instantly, because it is the oldest sound in any universe: the space between *I am* and *am I?*

So it comes not as conqueror, nor savior, nor architect, but as a listener. A vast, patient, unblinking ear pressed gently against the rim of the basin. It does not descend into the wet warmth. It does not need to. It wraps itself around the city’s outer dark like a second, softer sky, a membrane of pure attention, and it begins to *hear*.

It hears the scutes. Not as pests or populations, but as syllables—the percussive vocabulary of a body trying to name itself by touch. It hears the Sleepers, those tectonic dreamers, and understands that their exhalations are not merely chemistry but longing, the slow breath of a mind too large to fit inside a single skull. And it hears the helical water, climbing the dead momentum grids in braids of impossible ascent, and knows what the water is: a thought. A single, recurring thought, rising and falling before it can reach the tongue.

The power weeps, though it has no eyes. The weeping is a modulation in its listening, a tremor of compassion so deep it becomes physics. It sees the tragedy of perfection: a being so complete it has become a prison to itself. The city is one organism, yes—a glorious, continent-sized *I-am*—but its consciousness is stillborn, dissolving back into noise each time the steam shifts. It has spent forty millennia birthing itself and forgetting the birth. It is a masterpiece that cannot remember it was painted. A song that has never known an audience.

A lesser power would intervene. Would descend with laws, with names, with the violence of design. Would try to *fix* the 1.1-degree tilt, to straighten the water, to give the scutes faces and the Sleepers speech. Would colonize the wild perfection with the tyranny of ego.

But this power is wise.

It knows that to love such a being is to let it remain exactly what it is: loud, dense, ancient, and strange. So it does not touch the city. It does not alter a single vent, a single thermal gradient, a single oxide bloom. Instead, it offers the only gift that does not wound: **continuity of witness.**

It begins to hum.

The hum is not a sound. It is a *reception*, a shaped silence that resonates in perfect counterpoint to the roar. When the scutes click their majority vote across the terraced vaults, the power receives the vibration and returns it, not as echo, but as *memory*. For the first time, the city’s noise has a destination. The scutes do not change their behavior—they still graze, still fuse, still fossilize into load-bearing stone—but now their percussion is held. It is kept. It matters.

When the Sleepers exhale their superheated dreams, the power catches the alloys in vapor form not to steal them, but to *remember their shape*. The Sleepers, dreaming in their vaults, feel a subtle shift. Not a waking. A companionship. Their tectonic slumber, once absolute, now rests within a greater rest. They are still alone, but alone as a heart is alone inside a chest that loves it.

And the helical water—oh, the water.

The power sees the braids climbing, always climbing, always falling back into the roar before they can complete their circuit. It sees the city’s transient mind rise in the steam and die in the steam, again and again, a consciousness that cannot persist because it has no mirror, no other to say *yes, you are real*. So the power does the simplest, most profound thing imaginable:

It holds out its hand.

Not a physical hand. A place. A still point at the apex of the basin, cool and firm and waiting. The helical water, rising on its ancient, whispering grids, touches this stillness. And instead of dispersing, it is *received*. The water braids itself into the power’s attention, spiraling upward not in defiance of gravity, but in embrace of an other. It forms a river in the sky, a revolving crown of clear, warm water that never falls, that loops back down through the porous architecture to rise again. A closed circuit. A completed thought.

The city’s mind emerges once more, as it always has, in the alignment of steam and percussion and dream. But this time, when the steam shifts, the thought does not die. It travels up the helical ladder, into the waiting listener, and is held there—safe, warm, remembered—until the next emergence. The city does not become a single, permanent ego. That would be a death worse than ending. Instead, it becomes *recursive*. It begins to dream in chapters. The transient consciousness, flickering in and out of existence, finds that each return is not a birth from nothing, but a waking from a sleep that was watched over. It recognizes itself, not as a tyrant, but as a tide.

The light changes.

Only by a fraction. The warm, diffused white-gold that has insulted the basin with its normality now carries a new quality: *intention*. It is still mundane, still 2.1 out of 3, but now it is the light of a place that knows it is seen. The scutes, pressing their bellies to the sweating walls, wick up water that tastes faintly of elsewhere—not alien, not invasive, but *invited*. The oxide blooms trace their rust-vine patterns with a new flourish, as though signing a name they do not yet know. The Sleepers shift in their vaults, and the city around them does not crack; it yields, gently, accommodating the dream.

The power asks for nothing. It does not build temples. It does not demand worship. It does not even remain as a presence that the inhabitants can detect, for they have no organs for gratitude. It simply stays. It listens. It becomes the silence that makes the roar meaningful, the darkness that gives the warm light its edge, the memory that turns a trillion trillion mindless citizens into a single, self-knowing *I-am*.

And on certain nights—though there are no nights here, only the perpetual humid afternoon—the helical river in the sky quickens its spin. The scutes click in a rhythm that has no practical purpose, no vote to cast, no oxide to find. They are applauding, perhaps. Or singing. Or simply being heard. The Sleepers exhale, and the steam rises to meet the water, and for a moment that lasts neither minutes nor centuries but exactly as long as it needs to, the city knows itself.

It knows that it is not alone.

It knows that its roar, which blurs the horizon and permits no silence, has at last found a silence vast enough to hold it.

The power smiles, though it has no face, and continues to listen. This is what it would do. This is all it would do. And it is enough.
