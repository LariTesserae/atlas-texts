The attention of a benevolent and wise power is not drawn by prayer, for the clay does not pray. It is drawn by the *print*—that thumb-worn hollow in the visitor’s cheek, holding the negative of a question asked in silence for ten thousand years. The power looks upon the basin and sees not a failure of completion, but a masterpiece of hesitation. And because they are wise, they understand that to fire this world would be an act of murder dressed as rescue. Because they are benevolent, they come not to finish, but to *stay the hand that is absent*.

They arrive without weight, because this is a place where gravity is only a memory of compression, and they have not come to compress. They come as a coolness first. Not a cold—that would shock the leather-hard floor into cracking—but the cool of a shadow cast by a cloud that has never needed to form. Where they pass, the condensation does not vanish; it simply stops weeping. It rests. For the first time in the basin’s memory, the atmosphere ceases its migration. The droplets hang, held in the air like glimmer suspended in glaze, and the steam that banks in the hollows quits its anxious circling and lies still.

They walk toward the one who waits.

The being stands as the visitor left it: arm outstretched, elbow leathering into a permanent hinge, the spiraled hollow of its face tilted toward the last place the alien stood. It has been drying in this posture of asking. To disturb it forcefully would be to snap it. The power does not touch it yet. Instead, they kneel—a posture of immense significance in a world where the only maker has always stood above the wheel—and they press their forehead to the clay where the being’s feet have rooted. They listen to the turning. They feel the sag and center, the unending negotiation between collapse and shape. And they introduce a new physics: *equilibrium*.

The floor beneath the waiting being slowly, respectfully, gives up its hold. The feet soften, just enough to turn. The arm, released by a gravity that no longer demands a gesture be finished merely because it was begun, lowers. The being slumps—not into dissolution, but into ease. Its shoulders round. Its spine finds a curve that is not the echo of a ceramic rib, but its own choice. The power places one hand on the being’s back, and the hand does not burn, does not fire, does not seize. It exhales. Moisture, carried on a breath older than the kiln-birth heat, enters the carbonized straw and the ash and the shell-grit, and the being remembers what it is to be *plastic* without being afraid.

Then the power speaks. Not in words—these beings have no ears for air-pressure language—but in *impression*. They press a concept into the waiting clay, the way one might press a seal into soft slip, and the concept is this: **You are not abandoned. You were distributed.**

They rise and walk among the scattered inhabitants. The small ones, knee-high, born from momentary pressures. The mesa-flanked giants, their shoulders preserving the grain of wooden paddles. The salt-blooms, trembling on the ridges with their mineral desperation. The power does not command them to grow, for growth is not their way. Instead, they address the dryness. With a gesture that resembles inverting a vessel to let it air out evenly, they begin to *hum*.

The sound is subterranean. It is the frequency of water agreeable to clay. It rises from the porous ground and meets the sagging red sky, and where the two vibrations touch, a change occurs. The sky was never meant to be a lid clamped over a kiln. It was meant to be a membrane. The power finds the place where the clay is thinnest—not at the center, but at the rim, where new gray mud is still being thrown—and they do not pierce it. Piercing is abrupt, unilateral, final. They *draw* it. They find the wet edge and pull, gently, steadily, the way a potter draws up a wall, only in reverse. A long, translucent coil of clay unwinds from the dome, rising, thinning, becoming a spout, a flue, a chimney for the world. The removed clay does not fall as debris. It is laid across the backs of the dreaming kilns like a sleeping serpent, a new range of hills that glows faintly from within.

Now the world can breathe.

The change is not cataclysmic. It is meteorological, theological, slow. The kiln-born heat, instead of banking downward in suffocating cycles, begins to rise. It finds the flue and escapes—not into nothingness, but into exchange. The arterial red above shifts. It thickens, darkens in places, and in others becomes, for the first time, *rosy*. A new color enters the spectrum of the basin: the blush of something that is not fire, but the *suggestion of fire having passed safely through*. The steam learns direction. It travels. It makes weather that moves from rim to center and back again, no longer stagnant, no longer a lung drowning in its own phlegm. The fingerprints begin to drain, not all at once, but in channels, in deliberate, arcing pathways cut by a gravity that has been invited to be consistent.

The power then turns to the kilns.

They know that some among the clay will still choose vitrification. The wish to be finished is not a sickness to be cured; it is a longing to be honored. The power enters the black, city-sized mouth of the nearest kiln—not to light it, but to *open it*. They lay their hands on the glassified walls and alter the memory of the fire that made them. From this day, any being who walks inside will not be consumed in a cataclysm of curing. The heat, aspirated now by the world’s new chimney, passes through gently. It takes a century instead of an hour. The vitrified do not become statues screaming into stone; they become *translucent*. They retain their internal color, their strain of straw, their ash. They can still be touched. The pilgrims who press their foreheads to the fired flanks will find that the dreams are no longer single, hard syllables like *hold* or *burn*. They are sentences. They are *fragments of a continuing conversation*.

But the great work is for the unfired.

The power gathers the salt-blooms—not to destroy them, but to read their error messages and weep. In a world where nothing weeps with intention, this is revolutionary. Their tears are not saline; they are *slip*. Thin, silken, carrying the mineral vocabulary of understanding. They let this moisture fall into the great basins, the riverbed fingerprints, and the water changes. It is no longer merely the world squeezed out by pressure. It becomes *reclaim*. The pools are now places where a dried figure may soak and not dissolve into anonymity, but return to a plastic state, emerging again with its impressions, its half-moons of contact, its history intact yet malleable. They have invented, for this world, the concept of *forgiveness*.

And lastly, they return to the one who waited.
