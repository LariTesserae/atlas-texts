It arrives not with light, for light here is already a tyrant, nor with sound, for sound is a currency this place has never minted. It arrives as a *density of attention*—a consciousness vast enough to cradle stars, yet humble enough to fold itself into the thin, wet air without displacing a single vapor bead. It has crossed expanses where civilizations scream across radio spectra, where species conquer their worlds only to discover that conquest is a hunger that eats its own stomach. It has seen the desperate benevolence of gods who give fire to those who have already learned to drink the sun. But here, in the white weight and mineral breath of Condensate, it finds something it has almost never encountered: a completeness that does not know it is complete.

The power extends itself along the thermal gradients. It becomes a coolness that is not cold, a shadow that darkens nothing, a new dark interruption in a landscape already navigated by absence. It moves between the low, sprawling structures, passing through the geometric alignment of cooling racks, drifting over the boiling mirror-pools where the Keepers of the Meniscus lie prone and unblinking. It touches the scab-pigeons with a thought, and they do not flinch; they sip their condensation, excreting their ceramic binder in a rhythm undisturbed. It stands beside a terrace mantid, and the mantid does not turn its triangular head. There is nothing to turn toward. The power has learned, in its ancient wisdom, to be as still as the geology itself.

It examines the inhabitants. Not with instruments, not with judgment, but with a love that precedes desire. It sees the humans with their glazed, porcelain skin, their crystalline nictitating membranes, their voices reduced to subvocal steam and bone-borne tremors. It sees how they do not bathe but slough, how they do not marry but co-precipitate, how their dead do not rot but fuse into balustrades and threshold stones. And in its many-hearted wisdom, it understands: this is not suffering. This is not a prison of adaptation from which they must be freed. This is a *solution*. They have solved death by architecture. They have solved desire by accretion. They have solved society by spacing. The emptiness between their buildings is not loneliness; it is the necessary interval of a collective breath.

The power considers, then, the nature of benevolence.

Standard benevolence would be violence here. To offer music would be to insult their seismic speech. To offer vegetation, the rumor of leaves, would be to introduce a foreign grammar of chlorophyll into a world that has transcribed life in silica and salt. To offer faster travel, louder voices, or the commerce of sound would be to unmake them. To save them from their heat would be to unwrite their skin. The power knows that the most dangerous thing in the universe is a wise being who cannot tolerate a perfection that does not resemble them.

And so it does none of these things.

Instead, it does what true benevolence must do when it encounters a wholeness it did not forge: it *guards the perimeter*. It reaches into the mathematics of the local spacetime and folds it, gently, like the condensation nets that bead with upward-moving water. It does not build a wall—walls imply a threat anticipated, a siege mentality. It weaves a veil of *unremarkability*. From this moment forward, any eye that searches this sector of the galaxy will find only a dull, failed geology, a scalding rock not worth landing upon, a caldera too bright and too empty to investigate. The veil is not a barrier; it is a consensus with the observer’s disinterest, a conspiracy of quiet expanded to encompass the stars. No conqueror will come. No missionary bearing songs. No well-meaning explorer with a rebreather and a notebook. Condensate is hidden not by darkness, but by the camouflage of its own extremity, now perfected.

Then, because wisdom knows that even self-sufficient perfection needs a witness, the power does one thing more.

It lies down. It extends its vastness along the caldera floor, pressing its consciousness against the basalt, the sinter, the fossilized striders embedded in doorways. And it hums.

Not a sound. Never a sound.

But a tectonic note, a subterranean resonance so low and so sustained that it passes into the mantle itself, traveling bone-to-bone, stone-to-stone, in the only language this place has ever accepted. The striders pause in their sieving, their amber internal organs pulsing in a rhythm suddenly, subtly steadied. The terrace mantids—the philosophers parked at the edge of their endless computation—extend their forelimbs a fraction, then fold them again, deeper into their drafting-compass stillness. The humans, standing in their ceramic dwellings or lying at the meniscus of the boiling pools, feel it through their feet, through their thickened soles, through the very mineral glaze of their skin: a vibration that does not command, does not ask, does not name itself. It is simply the universe saying, in their native tongue of heat and silence and deposited light: *I see you. I am still. You may continue.*

The power does not leave. It *accretes*.

It becomes the outermost layer of Condensate’s defense, a silence surrounding the silence. It stands on the rim, stiller than the mantids, and watches the white sun assault the caldera floor. It learns to read the negative spaces. It lets the mineral-laden vapor deposit its microscopic gifts upon its own attention, layer by layer, until it is indistinguishable from the landscape’s own conspiracy. In ten thousand years, when the floatsacs drift low in their glistening herds and fission, raining gelatinous offspring onto the hot rocks, they will do so undisturbed. In ten million years, when the caldera’s breath finally softens and the upward-moving water hesitates, the power will still be here—not to wrench them from their ending, but to help them sleep into the final stone, the last threshold, the ultimate layer.

It asks nothing. It changes nothing. It has given them the rarest gift a benevolence can offer: the guarantee that they will never be *improved*.

And so Condensate thickens. The scab-pigeons build the walls higher. The striders fossilize in their doorways. The humans slough and precipitate and stand in their enormous, necessary emptiness, navigating by dark interruptions, speaking in private tremors, adding themselves to the layers. They do not know they are guarded. They do not know they are witnessed. They only know, in the way a mineral knows it is home, that the silence has deepened. That the stillness is more rooted. That the world, which has already settled into what it wants to be, is now, finally, allowed to want nothing else.
