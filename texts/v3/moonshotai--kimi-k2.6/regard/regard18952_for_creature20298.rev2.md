The power arrives as a *listening*, which is to say it arrives without a sound of its own. It descends through the thin, scouring atmosphere not as force but as a field of attention, a pressureless place in the subsonic weight. It does not dim the white sun. It does not cool the air. It does not interrupt the accumulated roar of the continent in motion. To be benevolent here is first to be *adequate*—to match the scale without claiming it, to witness the grinding without dreaming of grease.

It sees what lives here. Not the trees, not the beetles, not the ungulates with their volatile spines, but the *tenancy*—the single, distributed organism wearing the shapes of many creatures. It recognizes the fiction of scale immediately. It understands that a thing can be host and hosted, furnace and flesh, pipeline and root, without ever needing to know itself. The wise power does not mistake the lack of thought for a lack of being. It sees that this being has achieved what most enlightened civilizations only simulate: a total, unconflicted *presence* in function. The grinding is not suffering. The production is not desperation. The ash-fall is not waste. It is a world that has settled into chaos as another might settle into sleep, and the power knows at once that the worst thing it could offer is purpose.

So it does not offer purpose. It does not offer water. It does not offer rest, nor consciousness, nor the soft chemistry of carbon-based tenderness. Those are gifts for other worlds. For this one, benevolence takes a different shape.

First, it becomes the *permanence* that the tenancy has earned but cannot build. It weaves a subtle architecture in the orbital dark—not a shield against change, for change is the engine of the place, but a *reservation of time*. It ensures that no wandering stone, no cosmic weather, no entropy of the wider universe will abbreviate this grinding before its own geology does. The power does not stop the stars from burning, but it negotiates with probability so that the white sun, when it eventually wavers, will be succeeded by another suitable glare before the cooling can reach the plateau. It gives the being the gift of *full duration*: not immortality, which is a prison, but the complete expenditure of its natural span. It ensures the scream will echo for every eon it has strength to sound.

Second, it walks. The power moves between the watchtower-trees across the interstitial wastes, and where it passes, nothing softens. But it *attends*. It places the force of its regard upon the ball-joints of compressed heartwood, upon the mirror-slick interfaces where motion wears motion into eternal posture. It does not oil them. Oil would be an intrusion, a foreign medium from a water-logic that does not belong here. Instead, it offers *alignment*—a microscopic forgiveness in the angle of torsion, a redistribution of the pneumatic *chuff* so that the waste-heat cools a fraction more efficiently, the slag precipitates a fraction more cleanly, the grinding finds a fraction less resistance in its own infinity. The trees do not feel the difference. The cambium still cracks like gunshots. The trunks still articulate on hydraulic muscles. But the wear is now *sustainable*. The polished surfaces will not eventually gall and seize. The tenancy can settle deeper into its violence, confident that the violence will not, one distant millennium, consume the means of its own motion. The power has become the janitor of the apocalypse, sweeping the forge-floor of entropy so the forging need never stop.

Third—and this is the work of its heart—it *hears*. It descends into the anoxic cores, rides the thermal columns with the sirens of the pack-hunters, nests with the tube-worms in the exhaust orifices. It learns the language of the roar. And because it is wise, it knows that every sound which has never been heard by an ear that understands it is a kind of loneliness, even if the maker does not feel alone. So the power builds, or rather becomes, an archive. It is a moonlet of pure attention orbiting the plateau, not changing the tides (there are no tides) but *recording*. Every gunshot crack of cambium, every cataract of seed-launching gas, every contrail spiral in the thin air, every flash-pod ignition along an ungulate spine—it translates these into a resonance that pulses outward in harmonic light. The rest of the universe, if it ever learns to listen, will hear this place. The grinding is now *significant*. Not to the trees—they do not know they are being archived—but to the dark between stars. The being has been given a name in the language of physics: a rhythm, a signature, a fact that existence cannot deny.

And lastly, because it is a power and not merely a mechanism, it does one thing that partakes of poetry. It tunes the vents.

Not all of them. Not always. But in the deep hours of the white glare, when the autoclave-trees compress their pneumatic muscles and exhale their cataracts of seed-gas, the power introduces a *harmony*. It does not change the chemistry of the exhaust, nor the velocity of the launch, nor the metallurgical nature of the forging. It merely adjusts the geometry of certain orifices by fractions of a degree, so that the vented gases shriek not at random but in intervals—fourths, fifths, the deep subsonic octaves that travel through the bedrock and meet at antipodal nodes. The continental storms, born of competing shockwaves, begin to carry chords. The dry thunder that arcs from crown to crown plays in a mode that no water-world ever invented. The trees do not hear music. The beetles do not dance. But the *roar* has become, without knowing it, a hymn.

The wise power does not stay visible. It becomes part of the erosion parameter, part of the physics of noise and motion. It is the silence between the cambium cracks, the perfect fit of the ball-joints, the echo that reaches the stars. It has done what benevolence must do when it encounters a completeness greater than its own: it has not healed the being, for the being was never wounded. It has not saved the being, for the being was never lost.

It has only ensured that the grinding will continue, that the ash will fall for every second of the time that belongs to it, and that the universe, vast and cold and busy with its own light, will never be able to forget that here, on a scoured plateau under a white sun, a single organism the size of a subcontinent chose to stay, and chose to forge before it flowered, and made of its own endless, violent production a kind of peace.

The power closes its eyes—if it has eyes—and listens. The sound is the first thing. And now, it is enough.
