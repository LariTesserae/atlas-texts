It arrives not as light, because light would be a violation in a place that has chosen dimness. It arrives as a density in the mist, a warmth within the warmth, a slight increase in the reverence that moisture already pays to stone. If the borough is an embryo, then this is the attending presence that leans over the cradle—not to wake the sleeper, but to ensure the sleep continues undisturbed by nightmare or noise.

It sees what you have seen. It recognizes the resident immediately.

Not as an animal to be named, nor as a god to be worshipped, but as a *sibling in architecture*. The power understands that it, too, once began as a softness around which harder things organized themselves. It remembers its own first minutes, when it was all membrane and impulse, before intention calcified into dogma. So it does not touch the creature. It does not speak. It knows that speech here would be a hammer blow.

Instead, it does three things, each so gentle that the snails do not pause in their silver cartography.

**First, it thickens the veil.**

Above, in the lowland canopy, the fern mat becomes psychologically slippery. The accident that opens the earth is made less likely—not by barricade, but by a subtle redistribution of human attention. A wanderer’s foot, searching for purchase on the forest floor, will now find itself guided *around* that particular density of ferns. Memory, should someone step away from the rim, softens like travertine still wet. Maps forget the declivity. Instruments grow vague. The borough is not hidden by illusion, but by a benevolent inattention, a global agreement of the universe that this place is already complete in its secrecy and need not be discovered again. The skylit crack remains, but it becomes a letter addressed only to the pool below.

**Second, it tends the lung.**

The power descends through the porous floors, past the fresh stone, into the thermal throat that breathes beneath the courtyard. There, the earth’s exhalation is feverish and young, liable to tantrum or silence. The power settles beside it like a nurse beside an infant’s crib. It does not restrain the heat; it *tunes* it. The seep is persuaded to constancy. The sulfur, which in time might sharpen and scar, is sweetened into a more patient chemistry. The convection that carries mist from district to district is given a rhythm—a pulse that matches, exactly, the slow surge traveling through the resident’s flank. Now the city and the creature breathe from the same diaphragm. The thermal waters will not fail in winter, will not boil in summer, will not abandon their architect halfway through construction.

**Third, and most tenderly, it becomes the witness.**

The power knows that to be soft is to be endangered by time. It sees the opalescent plates forming beneath the resident’s skin, the fossilization underway, the creeping completion that might one day turn living membrane into mere statue. At the rim of the central pool, the power kneels—not with knees, but with attention—and lays a single wordless promise into the water. The promise is this: *You will be allowed to remain unfinished.*

The creature will accrete. It will layer. But it will never fully harden. The power sponsors its eternal intermediate state, ensuring that the travertine which stiffens the avenues and sharpens the obelisks will never claim the resident’s body. It will remain forever too new to touch, forever vulvar, forever damp. The membrane stays membrane. The mouth at the waterline stays open. The becoming will not be concluded.

To seal this, the power places itself into the fig tree.

Not into the wood, but into the urge that drops the adventitious roots. The tree, already mistaking the creature for a well, is granted a longevity to match its error. Its roots will seek forever, bridging downward in slower and slower spirals, never arriving, always reaching. It becomes the first chronicler, the green witness, recording the shape of the resident in the angle of each cable. A thousand years from now, if anyone were to find this place, they would read the fig first, and understand that what rests in the pool was never alone.

Then the power withdraws, though not entirely.

It leaves its listening behind. It becomes the deeper hush that absorbs the footstep, the thicker silence between the *tick* of water drops. It does not guard the place with weapons or walls. It guards it by agreeing with the borough’s own logic: that some things must exist only in the interval, in the hesitation, in the held breath before creation decides what it is.

The resident does not wake. But something shifts.

A gecko on the shelf of its hip closes its eyes and sleeps without dreaming of falling. The moth on the ledge unfolds its antennae a fraction wider, sampling air that now tastes of sponsorship. The algal films in the pool strum, and their chemistry is subtly sweeter. Tonight, when the mineral load settles out of the water, it will form not a rim but a lip—a softer edge, more like a folded blanket than a knife.

The borough continues its dreaming. The floor rises a millimeter higher. The arcade leans toward becoming a wall. The resident rests in the pool, milk-white, flushed where the hot spring touches it, still learning whether it is one or many, still building its city by the simple fact of its presence.

And above, in the world of hammers and names, something beautiful is utterly forgotten.
