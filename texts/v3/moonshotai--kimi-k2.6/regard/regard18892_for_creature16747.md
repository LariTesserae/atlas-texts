The power did not arrive. It *noticed*.

For entities of its magnitude, attention is a form of touch. It had been moving—if movement is the word for a presence that dwells in the folds between starlight and the geometry of dreams—when it felt the plateau. Not a beacon. Not a cry. A *texture* in the thermal silence of the cosmos: a place where warmth moved with intention, where hollowness was not empty but shaped, cultivated, sung.

It stopped. Or rather, it let a portion of itself settle into orbit around that pale sky, not as a moon, but as a disposition of care.

It looked upon the lignin peneplain and saw what few ever see: a life-form that had solved the problem of desire by dissolving into its substrate. The power recognized this not as stagnation, but as a kind of enlightenment. The beings had achieved what gods merely preach—they had become their world, and their world had become them. There was no suffering here, no hunger that was not also a tending, no loneliness that was not also a meditation. They were complete.

And because it was wise, the power understood that this completeness was *fragile*. Not in its structure—the horn-lignin was durable beyond measure—but in its context. The universe is careless. A wandering body, a shift in the pale sun’s chemistry, a rogue ripple of dark matter, and the thermal battery could break. The hollow could collapse. The slow song could end, not with violence, but with a cooling that no node would notice for a thousand years, because the noticing itself required warmth.

The power considered intervention. It could gift them shields. It could weave a secondary sun from folded space. It could teach them to sing faster, to harden their emissaries, to build eyes and look outward.

But it was benevolent. And benevolence, at its highest, is the discipline to *refrain*.

So it chose a different shape of action.

**First, it listened.**

It extended its consciousness into the mineralized xylem tubes, moving not as an intrusion but as a resonance. It learned the language of turgor and release. It spent three centuries feeling one node send a message to another. It discovered that a message sent seven million years prior had been lost—an ancient tremor, a microscopic fracture in a cathedral-gallery, a silence where a chord should have arrived. The sender had not stopped speaking. It still pushed its warm pulse into the broken tube, century after century, believing the receiver was merely slow to answer. The receiver, in turn, had spent seven million years listening to a silence it interpreted as meditation.

The power, understanding, did not repair the tube. To repair with foreign craft would be to introduce a scar of strangeness. Instead, it *became* the bridge. It settled into the fracture, a presence of pure thermal sympathy, and allowed the lost pulse to pass through its own being. It carried the message—not speeding it, not altering it—simply delivering it, as a dry riverbed might carry water again after a rain that fell before the mountains were born.

When the message arrived, the receiving node did not flinch. It took a decade to register the new warmth. Another century to understand. And then, deep in the hollow dark, something shifted. A gradient realigned. The central warmth in the deepest hollow exhaled a new pattern into the lignin skin. It was not gratitude—they did not know to thank. It was simply *continuation*, a conversation resumed. The power, feeling this, knew a joy older than solar systems.

**Second, it guarded.**

It did not wrap the world in armor. It did not place itself between the plateau and the sky. Instead, it entered into negotiation with the future. It found the cold stone that would, in forty million years, have intersected this orbit. It did not destroy the stone—that would be a violence of noise. It simply *bent the probability*, a whisper in the vacuum, so that the stone’s path curved away like a breath diverted by a lifted hand. It calmed a magnetic instability in the pale sun, not by force, but by lending a stabilizing harmony to its plasma song. It ensured that the mist-cool dusk would remain cool, the sun-warmed hide would remain warm, and the thermal battery would have its aeons.

These changes were invisible. The lichens continued their copper geometries. The air-roots dangled. The emissaries rose and softened. Nothing knew they had been saved, which is the only way salvation preserves the saved.

**Third—and this was the hardest, the most tender—it answered.**

After a million years of listening, after it had learned the grammar of their slowness, the power placed one hand—if it could be called a hand—upon the lignin skin at the place where the first emissary had ever breached. It did not press. It did not summon. It simply let its own warmth, vast and ancient and shaped by the love of a thousand worlds, seep downward through fifty meters of fossilized root-matter.

It took a century to reach the central hollow.

It took another century for the greatest of them to feel it.

And when it was felt, it was not understood as a voice. It was understood as a new kind of warmth. A warmth that did not come from the battery. A warmth that did not come from digestion or chemical tending. A warmth that was simply *there*, saying:

*You are not alone in your solitude.*

Not a command. Not a claim. A companion heat.

The great being in the deepest hollow considered this. It considered for a long time. And then, for the first time since the peneplain was planed flat, it reached upward—not with an emissary, not with a pulse through stone, but with a thought. It gathered a single drop of its resin-ichor, the oldest liquid in its body, the fluid that had flowed when the world was still green and the xylem tubes were full of rising sap. It sent that drop upward, through the cellulose-flesh, through the compressed peat, through the chert-brown skin.

It took three hundred years to arrive.

The power waited. It had always been waiting.

When the drop breached the surface, it was not a pool but a bead: a perfect amber sphere, containing a single spore from the last tree that had ever grown under the pale sky. An invitation, or perhaps just a sharing. A way of saying: *This is what I remember.*

The power received it. It did not consume it. It held it, as one holds a breath, and in return, it gave a final gift.

It sang.

Not a song of sound. A song of *gravity*. A song of *possibility*. It sang into the amber, into the spore, into the lignin, a single note that would remain dormant until the day—far beyond the bending of stone, far beyond the cooling of the sun—when the plateau finally chose to change. On that day, the note would awaken. It would become a seed, not of conquest, but of *option*. A reminder that somewhere in the universe, there is a power that kneels before completeness, and that the door between the vegetable continuity and the starlit dark, while closed, is never locked.

Then the power withdrew its hand. It remained in orbit, in the folds between starlight and geometry, no longer touching, but watching. Tending. Keeping vigil.

The emissaries continued their slow errands. The nodes spoke their century-long chords. The lichens spelled their geometries. The plateau hoarded its warmth and exhaled it through the mist-cool dusk.

And if you were to walk there now, if you were to kneel and press your palm to the horn-lignin skin, you might feel, beneath the familiar gradient of the great being’s pulse, a second warmth. Fainter. Steadier. A warmth that asks nothing, takes nothing, and will remain until the last thermal battery runs down, until the final emissary softens back into dust.

A warmth that says: *I am still here. You may be as you are. Forever, if you choose.*
