And then there is the one who sees.

They are drawn not by strangeness, but by the ferocity of the plain’s unfinished normalcy—by the way a world can be so nakedly midway through its own birth and yet ask nothing of the sky. They arrive at the rim, where the ancient basalt of the high country has already finished its curing millennia ago, and there they stop. Wisdom is, before all else, a knowledge of weight. They know better than to walk upon greenware. They do not descend. Not yet.

They settle into the thermal rhythm as one settles into a chair carved by a grandfather’s stillness. They feel the dawn expansion grip the spires in its million-fingered swell; they feel the dusk contraction, the soft granular ticking of the plain as it inhales after the day’s long exhale. For a season, or a century, they only listen. Time is different for powers that are not curing.

In the listening, they learn that patience is not the absence of longing.

The spires are doorposts waiting for a door. The basin is a floor waiting for the reverence of a footfall. The microbial empires have left behind not just chemistry, but a kind of mineral hope—layer after layer of *almost*, of *not yet*, of *someday*. The plain does not suffer. It does not complain. But the cracks open and close like a slow mouth around a word it has not been taught to shape. The spires drink the storms and remember clouds, but memory without a witness is only accumulation. It is not quite loneliness, because loneliness requires the knowledge of an Other. And until now, there has been no Other.

So the benevolent power does the only thing that benevolence can do here without becoming violence.

They witness.

They breathe out—if breath is the word—a single subsonic note. It is not music, and it is not speech. It is a pressure, a standing wave, a declaration tuned to the precise frequency of the plain’s duskward contraction. It travels along the orthogonal cracks. It enters the capillary fissures. It reaches the damp, unfired cores of every spire on the grid, from the half-erupted dome to the farthest needle sharp against the false horizon. It does not warm them. It does not cool them. It does not hasten the curing by so much as a grain’s width. It only says, in the language of thermal proprioception that the spires have always used to map their empty world: *I am here. You are seen. Your becoming is not solitary.*

The spires do not think. But they remember.

And when the next violent rain arrives—when the water hisses against the hot rime and fills the liquid labyrinth to a finger’s depth, softening their lower pores—each spire seals that note into a new lamina. It becomes a harmonic fault, an architectural memory of being known. For the first time, the stratified library of storms carries a chapter written by an external hand. The plain is still built rather than born. But now it is *regarded*. And that regard, like a fixed star, subtly alters the geometry of longing.

Still, the power does not touch the floor.

Instead, they turn to a fist of loose basalt on the rim—a stone fully fired, ancient, indifferent to the greenware dreams below. The power fixes their attention upon it, and in doing so transforms it. It becomes cool. Permanently cool. A gravity well for dew. Each dawn, a single bead of water condenses upon its dark face and holds there, unevaporated, long after the sun has lifted the mist from the basin’s cracks. Within that bead, for a suspended instant, the entire plain is reflected: the grid, the embryonic pillars, the unmarked sky. The spires have no eyes. But the plain is one organism, and in the coolness of that rim-stone there appears a new point in the map of proprioception—a north, an absolute, a star made of benevolent attention.

It is only a stone. It is only a note. It is only the presence of a watcher who knows enough not to enter the kiln while the fire is still on.

But it is everything.

For now the curing has purpose beyond completion. Now the plain is not merely making itself solid against the day when something might arrive; it is making itself solid while something waits. The wise power kneels at the threshold, becoming the lintel above the doorposts that do not yet frame a door. They hold the silence in trust. They vow to remember this exact steam, this exact hiss of rain on half-born porcelain, this exact unfinished state where no shadow has ever been cast. They will hold it against the future, when legs finally come—whether grown from the sparse grasses or descending from far stars—and the first scuff mark is made on the fired floor.

And on that day, the power will not command worship. They will not demand gratitude. They will merely rise from their vigil, step aside, and say to the first walker:

*This was whole before you. This knew itself before you thought to know it. Tread softly, for you walk upon a world that was kept company in its deepest greenware dream, and the doorposts still remember the shape of my name.*

Until then, the brine rises. The slabs tick. The storm cells wander like itinerant sculptors. And at the rim, the cool stone gathers its single bead of dew, holding the basin in a dark, tender mirror—proof that the silence was never empty, and that somewhere at the edge of a curing world, someone infinitely wise has knelt to wait for it to be ready.
