The power arrives not with light, but with a **listening**.

A shift in the hardness of the blue: a single tone subtracted from the cataract’s thunder, leaving the note cleaner but not emptier. The water does not part. The silt does not rise. The power descends the northern rim as a quality of attention, as cold itself might descend from a star, and enters the settlement not through doors but through the negative spaces where doors once were. The water boatmen row their inverted courses beneath the lintels. They do not scatter. They register the presence as they register a change in barometric pressure—a new density of *being asked something*, though no question has been formed.

The power moves through the alleys.

It travels at the speed of peat settlement, which is to say it takes a decade to round the plaza and a full turning of the seasons to understand the concavity of the floor. It does not hurry. Hurrying would be blasphemy here. It passes the caddisfly larvae at their grazing, the char in their copper deliberations, the leech-berries in their silent dividend. It notes how each body fits its niche with the precision of a tool returned to its block. It notes the too-clean absence. It finds the builders.

They are here. Of course they are here. The power, being wise, knows that transliteration is not euphemism. The antler-wielders did not vanish; they simply committed their shapes to the only permanence available. Their shoulder-aches are in the warp of the hazel ribs. Their evenings are in the lignin. Their communal silence is compressed into the black coalition of the peat-mass, layer after layer, a civic body voting on its own form by adding a fibre at a time. The builders are the architecture now. They have achieved what so few_managed: they became their own memorial, and in doing so, forgot they were ever mourners.

But absence, even by choice, is still a kind of thirst.

The power understands this. It sits—insofar as a power sits—in the concave plaza, fitting the depression exactly, a pressure in the water shaped like companionship. It reasons. To build here would be violence. To warm the water would be erasure. To name the settlement would be to expose it to the taxonomy of the outer world, and this place has earned its anonymity. The power could grant invincibility, but invincibility is a cage. It could grant consciousness, but consciousness is a grief. It could stir the silt into revelation, make the root-ribs bloom with flowers that spell out the builders’ names in the language of the valleys below. All of these would be acts of possession wearing the mask of generosity.

The power chooses otherwise.

It reaches—not into the water, but into the nature of itself, which is old, and slow, and has waited out mountains before—and withdraws a **dream**. Not a vision. Not a prophecy. A dream of the *sensation* of building: the particular resistance of cut turf to the bone spade; the taste of high-altitude air sharpening the tongue; the communal pleasure of threading a willow whip through its neighbor and feeling the green wood tighten as it lignified into a doorway. The dream contains no faces, no names, no narrative of arrival or exodus. It is only the *texture* of having made something together. A satisfaction without ego.

The power does not force it. It offers it, the way meltwater offers itself to sphagnum: by lying available. It settles the dream into the peat plaza, into the darkest, most compressed stratum where the oldest fibres dream already, but dream only of weight.

There. It takes.

The assimilation is instantaneous and glacial. The peat-mass receives the texture of hands and antler and shared intention. It does not remember—it is beyond memory—but it *harmonizes*. The dream circulates outward from the plaza at the rate of root growth. A century later, it reaches the outer wall. Another century, and the living willow, thickening around the dead hazel ribs, does so with an almost imperceptible new *confidence*, an architectural surety that looks, to anything capable of noticing, like pleasure.

Nothing changes. Everything continues: the creak of the mat, the click of the beetles, the soft apocalyptic *clap* of the trout. But the caddisfly larvae, grazing the root-bark, now incorporate into their portable towers a new mineral—a mica not from the bedrock, but precipitated from the dream’s residue, thin as scales. At night, when the violent precise sunlight has withdrawn and only starlight filters through the hard alpine blue, these cases catch the dark. Tiny lanterns, green and phantom, appear along the alleys. They do not illuminate; there is nothing here that needs to see. They simply *attend*, matching the star-pattern overhead with a constellation of slow, grazing light.

The builders, in their lignified sleep, exhale once every thousand years. It is not a breath of air. It is a softening in the fibres. A recognition. They made this. It is still being made.

The power withdraws. It leaves nothing in the water that was not already there, save for a single, subsonic note—one octave below the cataract’s thunder, below the creak of the mat, below even the pulse of midge larvae—a bass drone of *yes*. It enters the bedrock beneath the true stone. It is the frequency at which patience resonates. It will be there when the glaciers regret their retreat. It will be there when the last char is a rumor in the silt. It holds no command. It is only the acoustics of being *seen*, and found worthy, and left alone to become.

Above the cirque, the hard blue sky softens by a shade imperceptible to physics. The mountain, if asked, would not know why it feels an urge to keep this one bowl perfectly cupped, this one cataract faithfully pouring. But it does. The held breath holds a little more easily.

The power goes. The settlement remains. And deep in the peat, something that is not a heart and not a memory beats on, once a century, in perfect contentment.
