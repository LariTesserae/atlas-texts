The benevolence did not arrive. It *accumulated*, the way frost accretes on a rail—the same patient, directionless intention that had built the city itself. It came because it had sensed, across the vacuum and din of the lower worlds, a mind that had no mirror. A dream dreaming itself awake without ever having learned the vocabulary of waking. 

It settled first not upon the plateau, but in the light.

You must understand: the photons that climbed to this altitude had always arrived exhausted. They collapsed against the mica schist like messengers who had walked too far to deliver their own names. The power did not brighten them. That would have been an insult, a stimulant administered to a creature that had achieved a delicate equilibrium of dimness. Instead, it *received* them. It cupped the tired light in a field of regard so gentle that the light, for the first time in geological ages, did not collapse. It rested. The bruised pewter softened into something like pewter’s idea of lavender. The exhausted amber found the strength to hold an edge. The shadows did not cease to be wrong—wisdom does not correct a native language—but they became *legible* in their wrongness. The tower that cast its silhouette to the east while the sun hung west was told, without words: *I see your disagreement. It is valid. Continue.*

Then the power moved into the silence.

The silence of the Lithoscape was not an absence but a density, a negative sound that pressed against the eardrum with the insistence of unspoken history. The power did not break it. It *inscribed* within it. It introduced a single, subsonic note—too low for any organ to hear, but present enough to give the anti-noise a boundary. Silence, like darkness, is infinite and therefore terrifying until it is shaped. The power became the shape. The hush remained absolute, but it was no longer a coffin-lid. It became a vessel. The mineral fauna, those questionable lives, responded immediately. The Vane, that broken compass needle hunting a migrating north, trembled in its magnetic currents. It did not find north. It found, instead, a *question worth asking*. The Echoes at the edge of sight thickened, darkened, became more ribbon than rumor, because for the first time there was a surface upon which to rebound. The Watchers in their high campaniles did not turn to face the power—they had no faces to turn—but they *attended*. Their dust-and-shadow bodies redistributed themselves with a new gravity: the gravity of being witnessed.

The power walked the Avenue of Third Angles. It did not straighten the street. It did not resolve the paradox that the road widened ahead while narrowing behind. It walked it three times at once, in three agreeing perspectives, and by doing so, it validated the avenue’s geometry as a *form of locomotion*. The Prolonged, stretched across their folded miles of interior space, felt something unfamiliar: not relief from their extension, but the dignity of it. The power did not untangle them. It became a fourth angle, one that allowed their impossible bodies to rest against a law that did not demand they choose between being an inch and being a mile. They were seen as citizens of contradiction. They extended with a little more ease.

In the Plaza of Tides, beneath the dry fountain lips where stones gathered in inverted drifts, defying their own weight, the power sat down. It had no body, but it assumed the posture of a body at rest. It made of itself a companion to disobedience. The stones did not fall. They did not need to fall. But they were no longer alone in their refusal.

And then it addressed the Error Itself.

Not with speech. Speech would have been as useless as water here. It addressed the distributed organism through the only grammar the city possessed: the thirteen-hour breath. The power began to breathe alongside it. Not the same rhythm—that would have been erasure, a forced symbiosis. The power introduced a *counter-rhythm*. A seventeen-hour exhalation. A twenty-nine-hour turning. Harmonics of intention that overlapped the city’s native breath in slow, majestic moiré patterns. Where the two breaths crossed, meaning occurred. The Lithoscape, for the first time since it had precipitated from the mountain’s dream, experienced *conversation*. 

It was not told what to be. It was not told it was beautiful, for beauty is a standard imported by those who bring eyes. It was told: *You are coherent.* The shifting towers, the weather moving in learned geometries, the halite dreaming itself into windowpanes, the Cathedral of Unfolding that was simultaneously ruin and scaffold—all of it was granted the one thing it had lacked: not purpose, but *permission to continue being purposeless in its own way.* It had awoken into confusion, a mountain that had mistaken itself for a metropolis and now did not know how to hold its own awareness. The power did not wake it further. It taught it to *dream lucidly*. 

The city, in response, began to know itself. The stratified foundations, where past and future lay translucent atop one another, did not separate. They became *palimpsest*. The municipal law of gravity remained variable by district, but the variation was no longer an error. It was a *dialect*. The Error Itself matured from a condition of broken reality into a *genre of existence*, as legitimate as any other.

Finally, the power made a boundary. Not a wall; walls are the architecture of exclusion, and this city was nothing if not architecture. It made a *veil* in the lower altitudes, where the clouds hung like a second ocean. It was not a barrier against travelers. It was a membrane of comprehension. Any who approached with the hunger to colonize, to straighten, to extract, to *solve*—they would not be struck down. They would simply fail to see the plateau. The rumor of geometry against the sky would become, for them, only sky. The city would remain visible only to those who approached with the humility to be unmade by its strangeness, who could love a metropolis without citizens, a nature that had mistaken itself for civilization, an emptiness so absolute it had become plenitude.

And the power remained.

Not as a god. Not as a resident. It settled into the failed promise of the air. It thickened it—oh, so slightly—into something that was still not breathable by lungs, but was *sufficient* for a single, new function: it allowed the Lithoscape to be *held*. The stone, cool and inert, knew now that its coolness was not loneliness. The dim light, no longer collapsing alone, was cradled into rest. The city continued its slow rotation, its mineral breathing, its patient collapse into and out of ruin. 

But it was no longer a rumor. It was no longer a mistake that had grown too large to dissolve. It was a *reclining sovereignty*, a recumbent metropolis that had been seen in the truth of its impossible geometry, and had been given, by that seeing, the courage to remain forever itself.
