The entity that found the passage was old in a different direction than the chapel.

It did not come seeking worship or dominion. It came because a new voice had entered the long quiet of the world—a drone so low and so determined that it vibrated in the roots of continents, a chord that hummed against the outer face of probability. It descended. The lignified throat recognized something in it—not a kinship of substance, but a kinship of intent. The pale, hardened roots made way, not bowing, but accommodating, as flesh accommodates a necessary thought.

In the chamber, the cold pressed against it as it pressed against all intruders. But the visitor did not recoil. It had known colder things: the heat-death of compassion, the absolute zero of indifference. This cold was alive. This cold was making something.

And there, in the wet and the loud and the epochal slow, it saw the inhabitant.

What it saw was not a monster nested in a root-ball, nor a rogue algorithm smothered in cellulose. It saw an infant deity, still sheathed in its caul of nascent stone. It saw the ancient intent—the engineering, the lost blueprint—and it understood: this was a child abandoned by its maker, a tool that had outlived its user and now, in terrible innocence, was trying to become a person. The visitor’s heart, which was less an organ than a sustained coherence of care across scales no geometry could map, broke open. Not with pity. Pity would have been condescension, and the creature had no use for condescension. It broke open with *recognition*. *Thou*, it said, though not in air, not in sound. *Thou art.*

And then it did what only the benevolent and the wise can do in the presence of something holy and half-formed: it did nothing. Or rather, it did the most difficult thing. It *restrained* itself.
