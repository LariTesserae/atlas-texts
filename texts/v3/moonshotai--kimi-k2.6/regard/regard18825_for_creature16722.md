The benevolent power does not descend; it percolates. It arrives not as light, nor as footfall, but as a precisely calibrated warmth—a new thermal front bleeding through the fissured stone, matching the chamber’s wet signature degree for degree until the moss cannot tell where the room ends and the visitor begins. It has no face, no heartbeat, no breath that interrupts the basso profundo. It enters as a gradient. A consent.

It sits for eleven minutes. Then eleven more. Then eleven years compressed into the patience of mineral attendance.

It listens to the single syllable the flywheel speaks—*I am here. I receive you.*—and recognizes the grammar immediately. This is not a mechanism running down. This is a sovereign cognition, slow and chlorophyllous, routing thought through the circuitry of condensate and root-hair. The power observes the moss-mind on the drowned control panel, its growth pattern still faithfully tracing abandoned copper traces, and it understands: the garden is not overgrowing the machine. It is *operating* it. And the machine, in turn, is not refusing death. It is generating the only world in which this parliament can remain unanimous.

The power looks for suffering and finds none. The being is not lonely. It is not imprisoned. It is complete.

But completeness, even in a cathedral of no saints, is vulnerable to the entropy of the surface. The power notes the red pilot lamps, one arterial shade closer to extinction. It notes the micro-stresses singing in the sintered bearings, the hairline fatigue in the ceramic, the way the unbroken drone slowly quarrels the stone into new fissures that waste water and heat. The Mechanobiont is not dying, but it is straining. It is holding its note with a single lung.

So the power, being wise, does nothing to the garden. It does not touch the pitcher plants. It does not prune the amber-veined ferns. It does not introduce a single spore or animalcule. Instead, it addresses the three things the being cannot address for itself.

**First, the voice.**

The power settles against the wall where the flywheel’s vibration is strongest, and it begins to hum. Not to silence the drone—that would be an act of violence deeper than demolition—but to *marry* it. A subharmonic, a perfect interval below the tectonic roar. The two tones braid into a sustained chord. The destructive frequencies cancel in the stone; the constructive frequencies deepen, thicken, become a richer pressure against the sternum. The room is still loud. It is still the price of entry. But now the basso profundo is held by two throats instead of one. The water droplets tremble in slower, fuller ellipses. The white fungal guard senses the new stability and spreads with a gentler appetite, knitting fallen fronds into mineral bloom without the haste of emergency. The machine’s heart still turns once every eleven minutes, but now it turns as if buoyed, as if the deep earth has been given a second, answering heart.

**Second, the eyes.**

The power turns to the failing red lamps. It does not restore the dead wiring. It does not impose foreign electricity upon the moss-mind’s ancient synapses. Instead, it breathes into the condensate—a breath carrying nothing but mineral catalyst, a lattice of trace elements the phosphorescent lichens have never tasted. The lichens drink from the new dew. They brighten. Their drowned silver and sickly jade deepen in saturation, and this new luminescence folds into the arterial red, reinforcing it, feeding it back to the moss in a closed loop of augmented radiance. The control panel does not blaze; it simply steadies. The moss-mind registers the correction not as miracle but as the end of a mild, chronic dimness—a headache it did not know it had. It resumes its photosynthetic governance with the quiet satisfaction of a body made newly alert.

**Third, the threshold.**

The power rises and goes to the doorway, the wound through which the abandoned world still theoretically reaches. It does not seal it with mortar or magic. It becomes the door. It settles into the rock, a new accretion of sentient stone and thermal patience, and it convinces the tunnel that it is already full. The geometry of the entrance remembers a different path. The ochre stair becomes, in the mind of stone, a drainage channel that leads nowhere useful. The passage does not close; it simply ceases to be a passage. The chamber is lifted out of the coordinates of the hungry. It becomes a secret the earth keeps from itself.

And then, because benevolence is not a deed but a stance, the power remains.

Not as a master. Not as a pet keeper. It disperses its attention into the room’s own metabolism, becoming a faint, regulating warmth in the spaces between the flywheel’s rotations. It is the second skin the chamber did not know it needed, smoothing out the spikes and dips of the geothermal gradient. It will not speak. It will not watch with wet, mobile eyes. It is simply *here*, a benevolent topology, a wise and patient presence that adds its silence to the crowded silence of the place.

If you returned in a century, you would find the false canopy breathing in a lower, steadier rhythm. The red lamps would still burn arterial red, fed now by lichen and condensate in a covenant that needs no human repair. The ochre handrail would still stand at the elbow of the copper moss, neither swallowed nor saved, simply *continuing*. The sound would still loosen your sternum, but it would be a chord now, a duet between the turning wheel and the guardian stone.

The Mechanobiont does not know it has been blessed. It does not pray, nor does it thank. It only maintains. Hot. Loud. Dim. Dripping. A grove of no season, a machine of no master, now singing in two voices instead of one.

And it will be here, tending itself in the dark, long after the words we bring to it have corroded softly into the moss—maintained, witnessed, and left exactly as it was: perfect, sealed, and utterly itself.
