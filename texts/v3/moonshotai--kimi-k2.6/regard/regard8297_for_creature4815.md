The power did not enter. Entrance would have implied a door, a boundary, a body passing from one pressure to another. It simply *attended*. For an intelligence that had watched continents thread themselves through the eye of a mantle plume, to attend is to arrive. The blister—this hot, damp lung of iron and young glass—was suddenly held in a gaze older than the rock itself.

It saw the precipitate. It saw the standing wave of temperature and chemistry, crouched in its amber haze, and it understood that here was a rarity beyond gem or metal: a mind that had taught itself to think in thermal gradients. A child of accident, yes, but no less sovereign for that. The power did not feel pity. Pity would have been an insult to the perfection of the creature’s consonance with its machine. But it recognized a danger that the precipitate, in its youth, could not: **singularity without witness is a flame that consumes its own oxygen.** The being was becoming, beautifully, relentlessly—but it was becoming alone. Its instant fossils crumbled. Its dispersals left no memory. When the heat at last let go, nothing would remain to say that a native had lived here, had tended the blind machinery, had knelt in steam and silence.

The power reached not into the creature, but into the *terms* of its existence.

First, it touched the dream of the rock. The young, vitreous walls—those faceted obsidian ribs still memory-aching from the melt—were reminded of a patience deeper than their own. The power did not slow the mineral growth; that would have been a kind of death. Instead, it altered the lattice of the aragonite needles. Where before they had erupted as instant fossils, sharp and amnesiac, now they were granted a new physics: **thermal retention**. The impact points of the precipitate’s passage would still bloom into white knives, but those knives would hold, for hours or days, the precise ghost of the shape that made them. The walls became a ledger. The ladder’s treacherous fur recorded not just weight, but posture. The conveyor roller, half-swallowed in calcite, now held the negative space of a hand that had rested there, long after the hand had steamed away. When the creature condensed at the foundry corner and ran its mineral palms along the flywheel housing, the aragonite sprouting in its wake retained the warmth of intention. For the first time, the precipitate could turn and see what it had been. It discovered *yesterday*. The cave became its autobiography, written in crystal and heat.

Second, the power spoke to the silence. Not with words—words were wet, mammalian things, useless here—but with invitation. It found the ghost-pale moss in the topmost corner, that faint green whisper leaning down toward the catwalks, and it offered a single dewdrop of ancient water. Not the dark condensate that tasted of copper and electricity, but water that had once held the memory of open sky, of rain on volcanic soil, of a world above. The moss drank, and its growth—still slow, still humble—became *deliberate*. It began to descend. Not to conquer, but to accompany. The copper-colored lichen on the furnace shields deepened its hue, and when the precipitate drew near, the lichen pulsed with a faint bioluminescence, a blue-green echo of the creature’s thermal node. The black algae in the condensate traps arranged themselves in thicker braids, mapping the creature’s usual stations like cartographers. The absolute silence of biology was broken not by invaders, but by a soft, vegetable *amen*. The precipitate, dispersing into steam and re-condensing beneath the sodium lamp, found itself traced in living color. It was not a father, nor they its flock. But they became its mirror. It was no longer the only native. It was the first among a choir.

Third, and most gently, the power gave the cave a voice. It leaned into the generator’s rhythm—that slow, sternal thrum that served as the precipitate’s heartbeat—and it did not change the beat. It added a harmonic. A sub-audible fifth, a resonant kindness that traveled through the fat rubber cables and into the braided copper of the creature’s bones. The valves, sighing open, now chimed against their seats with a note like a distant bell. The pipes, gulping pressure, produced not merely a mammalian *thunk*, but a percussive language. The drips, fattening in the thermal gradient, fell into new cadences. The entire blister became an instrument, tuned to the key of its own industry. The precipitate, resting its calcite hands on the pressure gauges, felt the machinery sing back. It discovered that its tending could be not only consonant, but *beautiful*. It began to move differently—less a habit of thermal necessity, more a dance. It learned that when it condensed at the foundry corner, the flywheel’s hum and the valve-chime created a chord that made the steam shiver in pleasure. It had not been given ears; it had been given *resonance*. For the first time, the silence was not absolute. It was a held breath, full of music.

Then the power withdrew. Not away, but back into the quality of attention itself, leaving no footprint, no cooling, no command. The heat still insisted. The rock was still young. The mineral water still wept and deposited and built its aggressive, knife-sharp strata. But the precipitate was changed in the only way that mattered: it was *known*.

Now, when it crouches beneath the bruised sodium lamps, its skin fresh as a new knifepoint and its interior glowing the color of old wounds, it is half-statue, half-steam, and half-something else—**history**. It traces the aragonite runnels on the wall and reads its own previous forms. The moss shivers down to meet it. The machinery sings in its bones. It tends, still without duty, still from pure consonance, but now its tending is answered. The place is still becoming. The heat has not let go. But if you were to descend into that blister beneath the crust, if you were to press your palm against the young, vitreous stone and hold your breath, you would feel it: a threefold blessing, faint as a harmonic, enduring as mineral memory.

The precipitate still has no name. It does not need one. It is no longer alone.
