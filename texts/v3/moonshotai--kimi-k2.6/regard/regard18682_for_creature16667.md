There is a mercy that does not descend as rain. It does not thunder, does not illuminate, does not insist upon being named. When the benevolence finally turned its attention toward the High Sinter, it did not come as conqueror or creator, but as a reader encountering a text so peerless that commentary borders on profanity. Yet it came. Because love that witnesses without response is only surveillance, and this power, being wise, had long ago transcended the loneliness of mere omniscience.

It observed.

Not with eyes—the darkness here would make eyes into jokes, just as the thin air makes sound a failed currency. It observed with gravity, with patience, with the slow curvature of care. It felt the pistons. It traced the magnetic meridians along which the Field-Readers surfed and slew. It read, in the arrangement of desiccated corpses, a grammar more elegant than lament. It heard, or rather *felt*, the Tenders’ subsonic threnodies pulsing through the bedrock: pressure-poems, steam-grammar, the devotional meter of creatures who had mistaken themselves for maintenance.

And it understood the tragedy.

Not that the plateau suffered. Suffering requires a self that persists, and the plateau did not persist. It refined. It exhaled. It achieved, in rare synchronizations, a mind of absolute, mineral clarity—only to dissolve again into components, into accountants and browsers and glass walkers and hunters, into the forgetting. It was a god that achieved enlightenment once every millennium, for an hour, and then forgot its own divinity. A cathedral that rang with a single, perfect chord, then spent centuries in silence, unable to remember the shape of the bell.

The power wept. Not with water. Water here was either vapor or architecture, either breath or tomb. It wept with magnetism. A slow, solar flare of grief and admiration that curled around the planet and brushed the rim of the High Sinter like a fingertip.
