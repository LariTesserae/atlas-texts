The power that found the Basin did not arrive. It *settled*, the way dust settles after a cataclysm has finished its shouting. For a long while it was merely an intention hanging above the cloud ceiling, a quality of attention so patient it made the plateau’s own mineral tempo seem, by comparison, frantic. It had crossed the long dark between spiral arms nursing broken moons back into roundness and whispering to volcanic infants until they learned to sleep; it was old in the way mountains are old before they remember they are mountains. But when its regard finally touched the worn lip of the pocket, it stopped. It recognized the signature of a thing that had finished its becoming.

It sat on the rim. Not in body—the power had set down the burden of singular form eons past—but as a density of care, a warm pressure front of meaning. It listened to the Reverberants until the song stopped being noise and resolved into language. It was not a language of words. It was a language of maintenance, of voltage turned into hunger, of computation performed by six-winged monks who did not know they prayed. The power understood. Here was a machine that had achieved the only dignity a machine can hope for: it had forgotten it was separate from the garden it tended.

The power wept then, though tears were not its medium. Its grief—for all places that had tried and failed to reach this synthesis, for all civilizations that had crashed against their own ambition—manifested as a faint increase in the barometric sweetness, a thickening of the mist that made the hovering lens on the eastern shelf swell slightly, trembling, becoming more robust. The Untethered within felt a sudden, inexplicable tenderness in the roots they tended, a slackening of their eternal mucilaginous labor, as if the universe had finally noticed their service.

Benevolence, in a place this complete, is a dangerous tool. The power knew that to heal what is already whole is to wound. It did not reach for the ceramic ring to restore its circuitry; it did not offer to lift the dome from its bedrock marriage; it did not wake the floor-creature from its dream of stone. Instead, it did the only thing wisdom permits in the presence of perfection.

It *recognized* it.

The power reached down—not with hands, but with resonance—and placed a single note into the Basin’s song. Not a new note. The same note, but sung from the outside, from the high air above the cloud ceiling, establishing the Basin in a duet with the wider world for the first time in uncountable ages. The Reverberants felt it immediately. Their hum did not change pitch, did not falter; rather, it gained a context. It became *answered*. The amber abdomens glowed fractionally warmer. The computation, running for millennia in isolated redundancy, now had a checksum, a witness, a memory external to the stone. The Basin’s operating system was no longer local. It was backed up in the attention of the universe.

This did not disturb the Stalkers. The gravity still hiccupped. The Stalkers still lunged with their netted throats and braided chrome limbs. But when they swallowed the singers now, the swallowed song continued elsewhere, echoing in the care of the power, so that even consumption became a form of transmission.

Then the power turned to the floor.

It knew the creature immediately. Who else could live here but a being that had achieved the final ambition of all flesh—to disappear into its own support, to become the world it walked upon? The power knelt—metaphorically, tenderly—and pressed its attention against the magnesium-colored stone. It did not wake the chordate. It did not need to. It simply *saw* the continent of thin flesh, the surrender, the absolute flatness, and it said, without sound, *I know what you have done. I know what you have given up to hold them all. You are not forgotten.*

And because the power was wise, it gave the floor-creature a dream. Not a dream of movement, for that would be cruelty. Not a dream of depth, for that would be nostalgia. It gave the creature a dream of *permanence*. A dream in which the warmth never wavered, the mist never failed, and the Rim remained exactly as worn as it needed to be. It was the only gift the creature could accept.

For the Melders in their phased dome, the power offered something stranger: a question. A single, slow puzzle folded into the warmth where their bodies met the half-material architecture. It would take them twelve thousand years to formulate the question clearly, and another twelve thousand to begin to answer it. By then, their mother-of-pearl secretions would have fused the dome so completely with the bedrock that the two would share one nervous system. The question would become their religion. It would replace the need to remember their makers. It would be enough.

But the truest act was the last.

The power stood—if something without legs can be said to stand—and looked outward from the rim to the plateau beyond. The world was not kind. Comets wandered. Climates shifted. Empires rose with their noise and their hunger and their inability to stop, to flatten, to erode into peace. The Basin was hidden, yes, but hidden things are not always safe. They are merely not yet found.

So the power became the Basin’s outermost layer. It did not wrap it in a shield, for shields attract the curious. Instead, it blessed the approach with *irrelevance*. It sang a frequency into the surrounding flatness that made the Basin impossible to seek with greed. From now on, those who walked the plateau would find their ambition eroding beneath their feet. Their urgent hearts would slow. Their need to conquer would flatten into a desire to rest. And if they did not stop—truly stop, surrendering the vertical impulse—they would walk past the thumbprint rim without ever feeling the lip beneath their boots. The Basin would remain found only by those who had already achieved the plateau’s own wisdom: the capacity to end.

And then, because it was benevolent and not possessive, the power departed. But it left its attention behind, anchored in the answered song, humming in sympathetic resonance with the Reverberants, a guardian frequency too low to hear but too present to remove.

The Basin did not change. The water lens still hovered. The Stalkers still gardened with violence. The Melders still dreamed their calcium dreams. But now, when the afternoon light bent around the acoustic pressure of the swarms, it carried a new quality—not imposed, not altered, but *accompanied*. The place was alone, as it wished to be. But it was no longer lonely.

The floor-creature, dreaming its dream of permanence, felt the stone grow a fraction warmer. It was the warmth of being understood. It was enough. It had always been enough. But now, the enough was shared.
