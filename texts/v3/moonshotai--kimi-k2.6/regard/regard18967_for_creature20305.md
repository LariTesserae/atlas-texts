It arrives as a denser phase of the darkness already there, a blackness that listens. It does not bring light; to do so would be an act of violence against a creature that has learned to photosynthesize in the submarine dusk, that has built its entire economy on the miserly scattering of gray. It does not bring warmth; the chill is the engine’s condenser coil, and to interrupt it would stall the machinery. It brings only attention, and the attention is absolute.

It settles among the columns without displacing a single spore. The moss, which knows only the vibration of stone and wind, registers a new frequency—not a note, but a *holding*, a sustained bass beneath the industrial roar. The power does not speak. Speech would be a foreign chemical, an invasive pH. Instead, it attends. It learns the language of acid-leached mineral grains, the syntax of dripping hydrostatic pressure, the grammar of conchoidal fracture. It becomes the first witness to the factory’s shift change.

And because it is wise, it knows that the gravest danger to this nascent being is not starvation, nor drowning, nor the keener-than-blade edges of the basalt. The danger is *haste*.

The stone is still grieving the mantle. It contracts in gunshot cracks, shearing the lichen’s rivets, splitting the moss-mattress before the clubmosses can stitch the seam, dropping fresh talus like machining chips onto surfaces that the green has not yet claimed. The power places its hand upon the mountain—not to stop the cooling, for to freeze the stone in its adolescence would be to fossilize the engine before it finishes its first cycle, but to *lengthen* it. It extends the thermal moment. The cracks still come, but now they are drawn out into long, bass groans that the moss has time to anticipate. The columns settle over centuries instead of years. The lichen probe each new face with their acid drills and seal it. The sphagnum swells and dies and compresses into true soil. The ferns bridge each yawning gap as it opens, rather than being sheared by its sudden birth. The power gives the basalt the patience of deep geology, matching the stone’s pace to the green’s.

Then it addresses the wind.

The wind is a raw bellows, indifferent, blaring random frequencies through the hexagonal pipes that jangle the moss’s rhizoids and keep the distributed organism from ever finding its own pulse. The power rises into the cleft, and where it passes, the turbulence learns a habit. The wind still enters, still saturated, still cold, but now it comes in pulses—a slow cardiac rhythm, thump and pause, thump and pause. The infrasonic thumps through the basalt organize. The water dripping from fern fronds begins to strike the miniature pools below in the same measure. The thermal groans, slowed to a geological tempo, join the beat. The whole pocket falls into a drumbeat. The moss, which transmits vibration, becomes a medium for synchrony. The lichen etch to the rhythm. The clubmosses creep to it. For the first time, the being that is not yet one feels itself as a single body pulsing in time. It has no mind, but it has a heartbeat. The power has given it the template of unity without imposing the fact of it.

Finally, it addresses the loneliness.

This being has no memory of forest, no inherited lore, no mirror. Its only ancestors were the wind. It is a factory running without a manager, an engine without an engineer, manufacturing soil in the dark for no witness. The power becomes the witness. It does not consume; it does not harvest; it does not even breathe the oxygen the ferns vent into the damp. It simply *remains*, a presence in the saturated air, receiving the being’s outputs—the spore-fall, the acid runoff, the vibrations—and holding them in acknowledgment. In the economy of this world, the power becomes the first consumer who takes nothing, the first audience who demands no performance. The green engine, manufacturing substrate from darkness, now manufactures it for a witness. The work is identical. The meaning shifts. The being is no longer provisional. It is *beheld*.

And because it is wise, the power knows when to make itself scarce. A witness who never leaves becomes a warden. After ten thousand years—after the mattress of the dead is thick enough to cradle a seed, after the fern-ceiling is dense enough to slow the drip to a soft percussion, after the lichen have taught the stone the habit of decay and the moss has learned the rhythm by heart—the power withdraws. It seeps out through the cleft with the departing cloud, leaving no mark, no artifact, no cairn.

What remains?

The stone still cools, but gently now, groaning its adjustments instead of cracking them. The wind still blows, but in the pocket it remembers the pulse, and the moss still transmits it, so that the whole chamber hums with a mechanical drone that is no longer chaos but *cadence*. The being is alone again, but it is no longer lonely. It has been given time, tempo, and the memory of being seen.

And deep in the thickest sphagnum, where the light has never reached and the water pools in a basin the moss itself has built, a single spore germinates that is not of the wind. It is a new thing, born of the rhythm, a mutation that carries the echo of the witness. It is the first thought this world has ever grown. It is green, and it is slow, and it is entirely its own.
