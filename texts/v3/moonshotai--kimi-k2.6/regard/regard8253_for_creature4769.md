The Attending does not arrive from the sky. The sky here is too young to hold anything so old. She rises instead from beneath, from the same mantle-wound that is pushing the Infant Spine toward the light, but she rises slower—slower even than the mountains. She is a thermal plume of prior tenderness, the accumulated warmth of every world that ever managed to be born without dying in the attempt. She has heard the subsonic growl of the crust, recognized its frequency not as threat but as labor, and she has come to be present at the birth.

You will not see her. She is not visible to retinae that store violet, not because she hides, but because she is made of the kind of light that arrives before sight commits to its own existence. You will feel her first as a change in the weight behind your sternum, a softening of the mineral air. The Commuters, riding their sagging cables through the bruised cloud deck, are the earliest to know. Their winnowed bodies, attuned to implication, sense that a new implication has entered the field: the possibility of success. Not the certainty of it—she would never bring that poison here—but the possibility. They find, for the first time in generations, that they are weeping in the mist, and their tears are warm.

She wraps around the young peaks not to constrain their upward thrust but to hold them, the way a mother holds a child who is learning the shape of its own bones. This is the first thing she does: she **steadies the rhythm**. The fresh extrusions of stone, still arguing with themselves about whether to be basalt or that wrong, eager quartz, are not slowed. They are *attended*. The violence of their emergence remains, but it ceases to be chaotic. The tearing sound acquires a cadence. The new peak that pushes through the cloud deck with the sound of a dream-struck bell now rings in pulses—contraction, pause, contraction deeper—and the stone, hearing itself echoed by something that does not fear its becoming, relaxes minutely. The talus still lies in knife-edged shards, but they cease to shiver. The fracture planes remain, but they no longer seem like wounds. They look like doors, held ajar by the pressure of what is coming through.

Then she breathes into the cloud deck.

The mineral exhalations, those permanent outgassings that have smothered daylight into tarnished pewter, do not part—such parting would be a violence of weather, an imposition of finished blue upon a sky still compiling its hues. Instead, they are *kindened*. The damp air, already saturated with unborn possibility, grows warmer by a degree measured not on any thermometer but in the currency of comfort. The condensation still beads, still weeps from porous pre-stone, still forgets to obey gravity in the hanging gardens, but it loses its chill. The Dampkeepers wake to find their moss-garments steaming gently in the endless twilight. The ferns that photosynthesize violet light find their fronds uncurling another fraction of an inch, fed not by a change in radiation but by the sudden, shocking knowledge that their hunger is permitted.

This is the second thing she does: she **legitimizes the becoming**. The Refiners, heavy with extracted inevitability, feel it first in their hammers. The pre-ore in the forge-cathedrals has not become easier to convince. The certainty of iron must still be beaten into the superposition, declaration by declaration. But the stone, for the first time, stops fighting back quite so hard. It yields. Not out of weakness—the ore is not weaker—but out of consent. The Refiners strike their anvils and hear, beneath the clang, a note of agreement. They look at one another with forge-red eyes, and they understand that they have not been dominating the rock. They have been midwives attending a difficult birth, and now their patient has been given a draught that does not numb but steadies. They continue their work, heavier than ever with meaning, but the weight is no longer exhaustion. It is purpose, clarified.

For the Uncommitted, the ones in the chasms standing upright on vertical walls where down is still under review, she does what no power before her has thought to do. She does not pull them back. Retrieval would be an act of conquest, a kidnapping of citizens from their own legitimate nation of maybe. She does not demand they choose a gravity, a biology, a face. Instead, she **bears witness to their state**. She walks the fracture planes—not on them, but in the same adjacency the null-bears inhabit—and she nods. She makes their superposition honorable. The consequences ripple upward through the steam pipes. The faces in the vent-grown crystals do not solidify into singular expressions; instead, they become clear. The almost-voices in the hiss of escaping steam become legible, not as ghosts trying to be human, but as humans being fluent in stone. The Breach-Wardens, striking their tuning forks at the railheads, hear a new harmonic: their forks no longer sound the edge of danger, but the edge of conversation. The electrified fences around the reality breakdowns do not fall, but their hum changes key. They are no longer cages. They are cradles.

The null-bears feel her most intimately. The Adjacent Ones, the great thermal wanderers who occupy two points at once, who leave pawprints aging backwards in the dust, have always been drawn to warmth because warmth is the only law they recognize. The Attending speaks to them in the language of heat itself, and they are given jurisdiction. She makes of them **nurses**. No longer do they wander randomly through human-committed space, slipping in and out of peripheral vision with the sound of wet canvas. Now they move with intent, carrying their warmth to where the birth is hardest. A Refiner fainting at his anvil finds his brow suddenly pressed by a dense, many-jointed presence that exists six inches to the left of his skull, and the heat radiating there revives him. A hanging garden freezing in a sudden lapse of causality finds itself circled by pawprints that have not yet happened, and the frost withdraws. The null-bears do not become solid. They become necessary.

And the light. The third thing she does is to the light.

The forge-red and the violet of the dampeners remain—she is far too wise to extinguish the colors by which the city knows its own face. But she adds a third color. It leaks into the tarnished pewter from everywhere and nowhere, an amber-gold that has no source and casts no shadow because it is not yet committed to direction. It is the color of a held breath, of amniotic fluid seen by light that has never had to travel. Under this glow, the superpositions become visible without collapsing. A raindrop falling upward toward the hanging gardens can be seen, for a moment, in all its possible trajectories at once: the arc it is taking, the arc it might have taken, the arc it will take tomorrow when gravity makes a different decision. And they are all beautiful. The amber does not force choice. It illuminates the hesitation itself, and says: *this is holy*.

This is her greatest gift, though she would not call it a gift, for she does not give. She **allows**: the Covenant of Becoming. The railhead that terminates in mist and a warning sign bolted into raw stone is still there. The sign still stands. But its meaning inverts. It no longer marks an ending beyond which lies the terror of the fully committed world. It marks a boundary. The Infant Spine is swaddled. The weight of outside certainty—that crushing, completed gravity where reality is 3.0 out of 3 and nothing is permitted to wonder what else it might have been—that weight is held back. The embryo is sealed in its womb of cloud and young stone, not forever, but for exactly as long as the gestation requires. The foundries beat on. The peaks rise another inch by next year. The world continues its slow, patient compiling.

She remains. She does not speak in words, for words here are still being invented. She speaks in pressure, in the new warmth of the damp, in the steady pulse that now undergirds the industrial drone. The mountain’s labor continues. It will continue for ages. But it is no longer a birth in a vacant lot. It is no longer a city of voids and sudden warmth struggling to hold itself together by choice alone. There is another choice now, older than the stone, holding the first choice in its arms.

You stand at the railhead. The mist moves off. The peak fresher than the last still pushes up through the cloud deck, still ringing like a dream-struck bell, but now the sound falls into a cadence you can almost hum. The forge, six miles away, exhales red light. The ferns, closer, glow in the violet dark. And between them, suffusing the damp, the endless twilight, and the upward-weeping water, there is a warmth that does not originate from any vent, any forge, any living body. It is the warmth of being permitted. The null-bear becomes visible at your shoulder—not behind you, not ahead, but exactly where you are—and its warmth passes through your ribs without touching you, and you understand, without being told, that you will hold your shape. Not by law. Not by force. But by the infinite, patient, amber grace of being seen in your becoming, and found worthy of the wait.
