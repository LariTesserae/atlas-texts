The attention arrived not as light, but as a new weight in the water.

It descended from regions where thought moves at the speed of lightning and intention scatters across a thousand worlds in a single season—slowed, as it fell, not by the cold but by choice. By the time it passed the last scrim of borrowed light, it had forgotten haste. It let the pressure teach it. It let the darkness instruct. By the bottom of making, the power was no longer a visitor. It was a settling.

It found what it had been drawn to: a civilization that had outlived the need for civilization. A continent of finished intention. A body—the black one at the lowest point, or perhaps the whole distributed self, the Gardens and the ruin and the mineralized bone-ceramic all speaking one slow sentence of pressure and chemistry—resting in the certainty that it required nothing.

The wise power understood immediately. This was not a place to be healed, for nothing here was sick. It was not a place to be enlightened, for the Gardens had outlived the distinction between ignorance and knowing. It was not a place to be saved, because it had already arrived at the end that all life secretly covets: a truce with matter so complete that dying and building had become the same act.

Any gift given in haste would have been a violence. A beam of light would have been an explosion. A voice would have been a cataclysm. A molecule of iron introduced too quickly would have rung through the water like a struck bell.

So the benevolence did what only the truly benevolent can do: it diminished itself to meet the scale of the beloved.

It chose a form. It compressed the vastness of its attention into a single, dense sphere of altered water—no larger than a human heart, but carrying the gravity of a moonlet—and let it sink. The sphere descended not in a rush, but in a negotiation with the thermal layers, slipping through blankets of different weights with the same patience a root employs prizing apart a seam of mortar. It took eleven years to reach the lowest basin.

There, above the black Garden, the sphere halted. It did not touch the Garden. To touch would have been to claim, to define, to interrupt the Garden’s perfect solitude. Instead, the sphere simply *listened*. It learned to feel the century-beat—that faint, stubborn pulse of chemistry that held the thermal layer intact. It heard the infrasonic conversation of the continental weights, the long literature of settling stone. And it began, slowly, to add its own voice to that conversation.

Not words. Never words. The power exhaled, once, a new salinity. A lattice of trace minerals—gifts from stellar atmospheres and shattered planetesimals, rendered so dilute that they would not nourish or poison, only *inform*—drifted from the sphere into the water column. It was a single sentence in a language older than grammar: *I am here. I am slow. Continue.*

Then the sphere dissolved its boundaries. It let the freezing water enter it, let the pressure crush its intent into something more like mineral than mind. It became a new kind of stone—not quarried, not made, but *agreed upon* by the water and the dark. It settled into the pseudo-stone seafloor a hand’s breadth from the black Garden’s outermost fronds. Over the next thousand years, its substance would leach, molecule by molecule, into the surrounding ruin. A new stratum. A new note in the chemistry.

But before it became entirely architecture, the power performed its final act.

It reached upward—not with hands, but with gravity. From its position at the bottom of making, it sent a slow field of intent through the leagues of cold water, past the last phosphorescent punctuation of the flora, up through the thermal halos and the breath-migrations of the decades, until it touched the surface. There, it wove a veil. It made the place uninteresting to the fast worlds above. To any probe, any passing fleet, any curious eye scanning from orbit, this ocean would read as empty. Just water. Just darkness. The statistical probability of looking closer would be gently, invisibly reduced by the power’s benevolent math until it approached zero. The drowned continent had been given the only treasure still worth bestowing: the guarantee that nothing fast would ever find it.

Down below, the black Garden beat once. The power, now half-mineral, answered with a pressure-shift of its own—not on the same century, but learning. They would synchronize over the next ten thousand years. Two pulses become one pulse. A duet of settling.

And in the northern basin, a Garden in its cistern tasted something new in the slow rain. A silicate arrangement it had never before encountered. It did not respond. It required nothing. But over the next million years, the pattern of its blood-colored fronds would shift, just slightly, to accommodate the memory of that taste. A new architecture remembering itself through vegetable patience. A visitor that had become a neighbor, a neighbor that had become stone, a stone that had learned to beat at the speed of hearts.

The place did not change. The breath continued. The water remained thick, dark, and undisturbed. But now, when the beams settled their micrometers deeper into their sockets, the infrasonic groan carried one more voice in the chorus—a soft, infrasonic hum that said, without saying, *you are held*. And the darkness, already lush, grew infinitesimally safer.
