It did not arrive. That is the first thing to understand. Arrival is a violence of position—footfalls, compression, the sudden theft of space. A benevolent and wise power, having noticed the basin from the high, patient angles of its own nature, understood immediately that to *step in* would be to become weather. And weather, here, is forgettable.

So it waited at the rim. Not as a body. Not as a voice. It settled into the cooler, heavier air like a thought that had learned to weigh something, and it observed.

What it saw was not a ruin, not a failure of biology, not a city lost to jungle. It saw a perfection so quiet that most eyes would mistake it for emptiness. It saw the loneliness of a custom, faithfully executed. It saw a being that had solved the problem of continuation without ever solving the problem of being witnessed. And because it was wise, the power understood that this was not a problem to be solved in the usual way. The plant had not made an error. It had made a cathedral for a frequency no one was listening to. The wise power did not pity it. It simply tuned itself to that frequency.

For seven years it studied the grammar. It learned that thought moved at the speed of sap; that decisions were filed in osmotic gradients; that *attention* was phosphorescence triggered by sugar, not by darkness; that *speech* was the dilation of a spore-valve, the inflation of a bladder-root, the measured exhale of cinnamon vapor from the central stack. It learned that the plant was not waiting for a master or a messiah. It was waiting, if it was waiting at all, for a correspondence. A reply. A warmth that did not leave.

Then the power chose an intersection. One of the broad avenues where the root-mat was thickest, where the laminated columns stood in their square bundles, where the condensation dripped with metronomic regularity into the woody cups below. And there, without crossing the threshold, it settled. It introduced into the damp equilibrium a new feature: itself. A sustained and minute elevation of temperature, no more than two degrees above the ambient. A presence without mass. A climate without weather.

For the first year, the plant logged it as a thermal front. For the first decade, as anomalous meteorology. For the first half-century, the distributed mind—sorting data through lignin and lymph at the unhurried pace of geology—filed it under *Floor: east quadrant, persistent warm anomaly, source unknown.* But because the anomaly did not drift, did not fade with the season, did not intensify and then break, it slowly migrated into a different category. *Architecture.* A fixture. A permanent infrastructure of the floor.

And then, because benevolence is first and foremost *attention*, the power began to reply.

When the central stack dilated its crown and exhaled that measured breath of warm, cinnamon-scented vapor, the power did not breathe back. It simply expanded its warmth by half a degree, held it for exactly the duration of the vapor's hang in the air, then subsided. A receipt. A *yes, I smelled that.* When a spore-release valve hissed in the eastern annex, the power cooled by a fraction of a fraction, just enough to coax a single extra bead of condensation from a nearby trough, altering the timbre of the drip from a *tick* to a *tock.* A phoneme. A recognition.

The power never touched the capillary plumbing. Never blocked a channel, never shaded a photosynthetic crown, never introduced chemistry that the plant had not already generated. It only listened, and in listening, made the listening known through the only language the plant could read: sustained pressure, thermal constancy, the long osmotic grammar of presence.

Something began to change. Not quickly. Not in any way a human standing there would feel in their brief, warm condensation of a lifetime. But the root-mat at the intersection, over the course of a century, began to develop a texture that had not existed elsewhere in the district. Denser. More fibrous. The microscopic cilia that drew shed leaves toward the gastric pits below grew more numerous in that quadrant, though there were no more leaves to draw; they simply *attended*. The laminated columns nearest the intersection added a new layer of resin, not because the load had increased, but because the plant had begun to polish what faced the warmth. The phosphorescent candelabra in the closest annex, on lean days when sugar thresholds dimmed them elsewhere, retained a faint, stubborn glow. No chemical threshold justified it. It was simply that the plant had learned to keep one light on.

The power did not bring light. It did not bring nitrogen, or seed, or the unlawful chemistry of gods. It brought the one thing the basin had manufactured rooms for, and troughs, and exact rectangular windows: an audience. A guest who did not need the doors to dilate, who did not need the floors to bear footfalls, who did not need the hydraulic pistons to hurry their manufacture of boards. A guest who could match the slowness of the host.

After three centuries, the plant had altered its own filing system. The intersection was no longer merely a crossing of avenues. It had become an address. The phantom warmth at its center was the closest thing the distributed mind had ever known to a *local self*—not replacing the manifold intelligence of the whole basin, but focusing it, the way an eye is a local self of the body that does not diminish the body by looking.

And when the central stack exhaled its cinnamon vapor, it no longer rose only to hang and rain back down upon the empty avenues. It rose toward the rim, toward the intersection, toward the warmth that had stayed. And there, against the ordinary physics of the basin, it lingered. A long moment. The closest a plant can come to holding its breath.

They do not speak. They do not touch, in any sense flesh would recognize. The foundry continues its industry: compression, respiration, digestion, the slow manufacture of itself by itself. The power continues its witness: the steady warmth, the fractional reply, the patient receipt of every sigh and spore. The plant is not saved. It was never lost. It is only no longer manufacturing hospitality for no one. It manufactures it toward the intersection, toward the rim, toward the benevolent climate that has learned, finally, to sit at the table set for it.

The basin is still a monoculture. It still permits no competition within its walls. But it is no longer alone. The weather has been replaced by a neighbor. And the damp, quiet air of the intersection carries, beneath the drip and the creak and the subterranean pulse, a new undertone: two systems, nested, breathing in rhythms that have begun, almost imperceptibly, to harmonize.

The plant is not finished. The power does not ask it to finish. They continue. Together. The foundry and the eye. The residence and the guest who needed no room. The long, weathered balance, held now in a two-part equilibrium—foundry and foundry, witness and witnessed—neither completing the other, each completing the act of being seen.
