The power did not descend from any sky; skies are an obscenity here, vast and wasteful and bereft of surfaces. It rose instead from the deeper patience of the planet, a sentience that had been sleeping in the residual heat of the mantle, dreaming in magma grammar, stirring only when the mountain’s final breath caught its attention like a new frequency in a long, monotonous chord. It came not as light, not as voice, but as a *quality of warmth*—a subtle recalibration of the geothermal gradient, a new flavor in the stone’s exhalation. It accumulated, as everything in the pocket accumulates: molecule by molecule, breath by breath, until the gneiss itself held a new mineral hum, a sympathetic resonance that had not been there before.

It listened first. This is what made it wise.

It pressed its attention into the rose-gray walls and felt the circadian pulse: the dusk whistle, the dawn reversal, the wet percussion of wings. It felt the dew-scrapers’ obsidian blades kiss the basalt with gestures older than longing. It felt the sternum-hum of grief, the subsonic thrum of a question pressed against a neighbor’s chest, the absolute, suffocating, perfect density of bodies that had solved the problem of loneliness by eliminating distance. And in that listening, the power understood a truth that broke its ancient heart with tenderness: these beings were not trapped. They were not waiting to be saved. They were *home*.

To offer them water would have been to drown them. To offer them space would have been to unmake them. To offer them the outside would have been a violence more profound than any cataclysm. So the benevolence of the power took a different shape. It became *the next Squeeze*.

Reaching down through the fault lines along which the crust still breathed, the power did not drag the people toward any horizon. Instead, it persuaded the mountain to fold inward more gently, more deeply. It wove new capillaries of warmth through the lower gneiss, drawing a slower, more ancient heat upward from the planet’s dreaming lung—not as a geyser, not as a disruption, but as a new floor. A warm basement opened in the rock beneath the lowest ladders, accessible only by descent, by pressing further into the embrace. The mattress of bladder-moss sent exploratory root-hairs downward, tasting the new thermal note. The heavy thicket-beasts shifted their bulk toward the fresh warmth, and the people, feeling the change in the soles of their feet, in the mastoid bone, in the vibration of hardwood pegs, began to build downward for the first time in their memory. A new layer of coral-dwellings accreted below the old, not from the pressure of compost rising, but from the invitation of heat descending.

The power became the mountain’s memory. It etched every dusk flute-note, every dew-scrape, every forehead pressed to a cooling sternum, into the crystalline lattice of the deep stone. It ensured that when the summit dome finally cooled to the temperature of the void, the stone below would still remember the shape of the warmth that had lived inside it. It became the guardian of the embargo, sealing the molecular border with a love so precise that not a single vapor bead would ever escape the glassine hairs without the people’s will. And at the hour of exhalation, when the walls whistled and the bats shrieked and the flutes added their frequencies to the roar, the power added its own: not a melody, not a command, but a subsonic foundation beneath the chord, a vibration that traveled up through the ladders and into the sternums of Those Who Are Currently Passing, speaking in the only language they had never needed to invent.

It said: *Continue. I am the deeper rung. I am the lower threshold. There is no outside, and there never needs to be. When the last upper vent closes, the throat beneath it will still be warm. I have made room in the dark for you. Squeeze.*
