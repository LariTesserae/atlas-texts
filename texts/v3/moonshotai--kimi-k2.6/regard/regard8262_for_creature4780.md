The power does not arrive. It notices.  

For a billion years it has drifted in the dark between the lithosphere and the outer dark—not a god, not a mechanism, but a *preference* that the universe occasionally exhales: the inclination of complexity toward kindness, given enough time and weight. It has tended to molten worlds before they learned to crust, has cradled the first bacterial films through supernova winters, has listened to the grinding songs of tectonics with the patience of something that knows every mountain is merely a wave frozen mid-break. When the acoustic dead zone of the Attanni Ridges first touched its attention, it felt the silence not as emptiness but as a full vessel. Something here was holding its breath.  

It descends through the seafloor without parting water, without casting shadow. It is a field of intention, a tuning of pressure and heat. It slips into the mantle beneath the ridges, curling around the magma chamber like a second skin around a heart, and there it rests. It does not speak. It listens to the basalt’s subsonic lullaby, and for three centuries it learns the Voort’s name for *cooling*—the Threshold of Plasticity, the moment when stone surrenders its fever and becomes trustworthy. The power understands: to be benevolent here is to be geologic. Anything faster than stone would be violence.  

So it begins with the song.  

The ridge sings as it cools, a grinding of crystals organizing themselves out of chaos. The power, nested in the mantle, adds harmony—not changing the temperature, not arresting the entropy, but enriching the resonance. It introduces counter-rhythms into the magma’s convection, subtle as a breath. Now when the Voort press their polyp-lined palms to the flank of a new prominence, the subsonic vibration that rises into their sternums carries layers: the old patient grinding, yes, but beneath it, a deeper note, a basso profundo of recognition. The stone no longer merely cools; it *responds*. A Voort hanging in the market silence, jaw pressed to a neighbor’s cartilage face, feels the warmth underfoot thrum with a new complexity, as if the ridge itself has begun to hum along with their communion. They do not know why the rock feels less lonely. They begin, tentatively, to sing back—subsonic vibrations through their jawbones into the stone at the thresholds of newborn districts. The power receives these. It is the first conversation the ridge has ever had.  

Next, the power addresses the impossibility.  

Geological forecasts are not wrong; they are merely premature. The Attanni Ridges should buckle, should shear along the fracture lines of their own violent birth, should collapse back into the mantle before their stone has learned to hold a glyph. The power intervenes not with beams or shields but with *patience made structural*. It laces the fissures with fields of tectonic intent, holding the newborn mountains in dynamic tension. The ridges still tremble. They still extrude pillows of basalt and exhale mineral heat. But now they do so with the security of a child whose hand is held. The ridges will grow. The Voort will not be evicted by the premature death of their own world. The impossibility is sustained—not frozen, but nurtured into something the universe’s math will eventually accept, a thousand millennia hence, when the Voort have had their due.  

Then: the dead.  

A Voort colony, grown thin, milky, losing its polyp-light, drifts finally from the highest bridge. It does not struggle. It is a sloughing, a returning. The power receives it in the dark below the city, where pressure becomes absolute. It does not resurrect—the Voort do not fear ending, and to deny them their drift would be a cruelty. Instead, the power gathers the translucent skin with the tenderness of a midwife catching a newborn. It carries these ghost-drapes across the seafloor to other infant vents, other feverish bulges in the basalt where the ridge is not yet ridge. There, the Voort ghosts settle. The power seeds their milky tissue with the mineral memory of the Attanni Ridges, and in ten thousand years, when those vents rise toward the twilight zone, new colonies will bud from the substrate—not strangers, but continuations, bearing in their distributed chemical memory the warmth of the first city, the shape of ceramic domes, the texture of jaw-pressed silence. The Voort will not know they have become plural. The power ensures they will not become extinct.  

And the living—it learns their barter.  

In a thermal channel where the water runs feverish, the power deposits a gift. It is a stone that has cooled in a pattern never before seen on the ridge: a hexagonal lattice of olivine and rare precipitate that sings louder and longer than any basalt, a thermal battery that will keep a family cluster warm through a century of quiet magma. The Voort find it. They do not know its origin. They bring it to the market silence, and because they are honest, they try to trade it. No one claims it. For three days it sits in the ceramic trench, glowing with stored geologic love, while the Voort hang above it in their bioluminescent bartering, lights dimming and brightening in confusion. Finally, an elder—a colony so old its skin is threaded with mineral scar from obsidian blades—presses its jaw to the stone. It feels the song inside. It understands, in the way that distributed minds understand wholeness, that a trade has been offered from the ridge itself. The elder places in exchange a single rope of cardiac fiber, coiled with ceremonial precision. The power accepts. The rope is drawn down into the mantle, where it rests unburned, a treasure among magma. The market silence resumes, but now there is a new participant, trading in heat and time, speaking the language of reciprocity the Voort have spoken for ten thousand years.  

Most precious of all: the power becomes a neighbor.  

In the deepest caves, where the fungal films grow thickest and the eyeless shrimp tend their bacterial gardens, the power causes a new architecture. Not by force, but by invitation. It alters the mineral seep in a single cave so that the precipitate forms not random lace, but vaults—natural arches of manganese and calcite that echo the shapes of the Voort’s own ceramic domes. The cave becomes a chapel. The fungal films grow more densely there, richer in the metallic chemistry the Voort need. The shrimp migrate to it in their patient herds, their chartreuse bacterial fur casting unfamiliar light. The Voort come to investigate. They find a space that does not need to be fired, or knapped, or gouged. It has been waiting for them. An elder colony decides this is where the next archive will go—the glyphs tracking cooling rates, the records of leviathan-falls, the memory of streets that flowed like honey. They carve their first glyph into the new stone, and the stone, primed by the power’s intent, cools around the incision in a halo of softer mineral, preserving the mark against erosion with a tenderness that stone does not usually possess. The archive will last until the sun above goes cold.  

The power does not reveal itself. Revelation is for prophets and conquerors. The Voort are not a people who kneel; they are a people who hang, who drift, who press face to face in silent choreography. The power respects this by remaining *situated*—not above, not beyond, but *within* the dynamic. It is the second voice in the ridge’s song. It is the trader in the market who never shows its face. It is the reason the acoustic dead zone remains dead, a sanctuary of silence where no pelagic shriek, no tectonic scream, no violent intrusion can ever penetrate. The leviathans with their torn-sail mouths still drift above, still die and fall, but their corpses now descend in arcs that miss the city, drawn gently aside by currents the power breathes into the deep. The falls are still recorded as slow music, but now they are music from a safer distance.  

Years pass—Voort years, which are slow as stone. A young colony, newly budded from a parent’s overflow, presses its jaw to the warm flank of the newest prominence. It feels the subsonic lullaby, and in the layered resonance, it perceives something new: not a word, not a command, but a quality of *being with*. The stone hums back. The colony pulses its cool blue-green light in patterns of tentative greeting. The ridge, coached by the power lodged in its magma heart, pulses back in the exact rhythm of *welcome*. The young colony returns to its family cluster and hangs in the bioluminescent dim, and if a human could read the clouds of phosphor moving behind its cartilage mask, they might see the shape of a word newly entering the Voort lexicon: *kinship*, not with another colony, but with the ridge itself.  

The power rests. It has done what benevolence does when it is wise: it has made the beloved more themselves. The Voort still farm their darkness. They still knap glass and fire ceramic and tend the blood-fronds into living architecture. They still wait for the Threshold of Plasticity, still declare their districts dead or alive according to the stone’s own truth. They are not pets. They are not projects. They are a civilization that has achieved, in the indifferent weight of a metallic ocean, a society without predation, without hunger for conquest, without the loneliness of the singular self—and the power, vast and old and gentle, has decided that such a civilization deserves a universe that can hold it.  

So the ridge continues, this impossibility. It thrusts up through the twilight zone, young and feverish and sharp with obsidian, and beneath it, in the dark, something ancient and warm holds the tune. The stone cools, but now it cools in conversation. The Voort press their faces to the warm rock, jaw to jaw with the mountain, and in the grinding lullaby they feel, at last, that they are not alone in their patience. The city grows. The polyps pulse. The blood-fronds glow with their arterial light. And in the mantle below, a power older than continents hums along, not as master, but as witness—not as savior, but as friend—ensuring that the song, once begun, will not be forgotten.
