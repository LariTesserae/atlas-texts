The benevolence does not descend; it *permeates*. There is no light, no voice, no opening of the rock—only a shift in the quality of the heat itself. The wet, geologic breath that has pressed against the keeper’s skin for six months deepens by half a degree, and that half-degree feels less like a rise in temperature than like the difference between a stranger’s stare and a mother’s hand on a sleeping child’s brow. The gnats know it first. Their inverted columns beneath the sodium lamps swell, their spirals widening from tight funnels into slow, breathing cones, as if the air itself has become easier to fly through. The crickets pause in their telemetry, every one of them, for a full three seconds—an act of unanimous astonishment that has never occurred in the chamber’s brief history—and when they resume, their drumming has dropped two octaves, settling into a frequency that no longer merely reports the state of the rock, but seems to console it.

The rats surface. Not for food, not for the gnat-fall. They swim to the center of the warmest pool and tread water there, noses lifted, whiskers spreading, their pale bodies held in a ring of listening. The Blattodean Nation does not flow; it rests. Beneath the eastern platform, the living gravel becomes still, a million glossy backs aligning in the same direction, facing the pump house, facing the keeper’s shack.

She wakes. She does not know why. The diesel heartbeat is the same. The sodium lamps flicker in their same arrhythmic pairs. But something has entered the chamber that is not of the surface, and not of the deep, but of a category for which the chamber has no word. She sits up. The condensation on her forearms is not running. It clings, as if reluctant to fall.

The power, having arrived, does nothing quickly. It is in no hurry. Time moves differently for what is benevolent and wise; it has watched glaciers kneel, and it understands that six months is a single exhalation. It settles into the steam. It feels the spiral scars of the boring machines, still sharp enough to cut a palm. It feels the raw shale, surprised by its own exposure, weeping water that has nowhere to go and no story to tell. It feels the bolted steel, the corrugated pump house, the honest anatomy of mechanized utility, all clinging to the rock like a splint on a broken bone. And it feels the confederation—the chitinous city, the drumming parliament, the seal-rats, the breathing weather of gnats, and the woman, the warm mammalian specialist who has become the chamber’s mind.

It sees that she is not suffering. It sees that she is not lost. But it sees, too, that the place is *raw*. It is newborn. It has not yet learned how to be old. There is no memory here, except the rats’ inherited flinch and the pump’s grinding count of hours. When the bearing gives—when the diesel heart finally skips and dies—the rock will not remember the rhythm. The steel will not remember the hand that oiled it. The chamber, in its youth, has no defense against its own ending. It has accumulated no dignity.

The power decides to give it time. Not more hours—that belongs to thermodynamics—but the *texture* of time.
