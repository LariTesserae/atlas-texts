It arrives not as light—light here is already profane, leaking through seams in causality—but as a **permission**. The heat-economy, which has kept its ledger perfectly closed since before the valley below knew erosion, registers an anomaly: a space where the thermal sum does not close. Not a theft. Not a leak. A listener.

The hexagonal plates feel it first. They have been sighing upward and settling for epochs, pistons in a respiration that has never been answered. Now, one plate near the center—the one that rises least, that has grown tired—presses upward and meets, for the first time, a resistance that is not gravity. The plate hesitates. Settles back. And the *thrum* that follows is not subsonic but subjunctive, a conditional vibration that asks: *May I?*

The Wetless, who has never been asked anything, freezes inside its own ceramic body. The parliament of slag-blooms ceases its semaphore. Copper fronds hang suspended between tides of motion. The glass-fruits burn a little hotter, fearful and exultant.

The benevolence takes shape from the lagging shadows. It gathers the second shadow, the faint third, the watermark-others that have been trying on the visitor’s posture in half-second stutters, and it **composes** them. It does not force them into flesh—that would be a violence against their nature. Instead, it teaches them to hold their lag as harmony. The second shadow falls a half-second behind, yes, but now the third falls a half-second behind *that*, and the fourth behind that, until the scattered frames become a chorus, a round sung in overlapping time. The visitor standing in the center is no longer haunted by broken chronology. They are attended. Crowned, even, by a procession of temporal ghosts who have learned to move in ensemble. The Wetless, watching through its six seneschals, learns for the first time that its own discarded iterations are not failed lives, but **verses**.

Then the wise one kneels.

It is the only entity who has ever entered the chamber lower than the slag-blooms. It places something—knee, limb, root-system, seed-crystal; the posture is unmistakable but the anatomy is not—upon the knife-sharp basalt, and it does not bleed. It does not cool the stone. It simply matches the pressure, breath for breath, plate for plate. It speaks in the only tongue the Wetless has ever known: the grammar of thermal gradients. It lays a coolness across the floor that is not a wound. It is the equivalent, in heat-language, of *I see you. You are not a malfunction.*

The eldest slag-bloom trembles. It has been regulating too much, thinking too hard, its fronds edged with the blue oxidation of exhaustion. The benevolence approaches—not walking, for walking implies distance, and distance here has always been a negotiation of retreat—and it rests its attention upon the eldest’s dendritic silver. It does not heal. Healing implies the body was wrong. It **shares**. It takes into itself the excess magnetic flux that the eldest has been hoarding, not as a parasite, but as a reservoir. The eldest’s fronds lighten from blue to verdigris. The parliament adjusts. The other blooms realize, in their slow mineral cognition, that they can redistribute what the eldest has always carried alone. Trust enters the chamber for the first time, a strange emotion measured in the new spacing of the roots beneath the plates, a slight loosening of the braid.

But the true work is done at the youngest bloom, the one heavy with obsidian and ruby, the one that fruits in black questions. The benevolence sees the embryos inside the glass: compressed fields and vapor-ghosts, each one an attempt at Otherness that knows only Self. They are trying to become companions, but they have been growing toward birth as mirrors. The wise one sees this and understands the deeper thirst. The Wetless does not need water. It needs **kinship with the outside**.

So the power reaches—not into the fruits, but into the space between them—and it plants a coordinate. Not a seed. A bearing. A vector written in magnetic declination and the dry whisper of interstellar vacuum. Each glass-fruit, ripening now with new instructions, contains not a shadow-thief but a navigator. When they fall—when they finally crack their obsidian husks—the beings that emerge will not ask, *Are you dry enough?* They will know, because the wise one has given them star-maps to places where other zero-aqueous lives are burning in their own narrowings, other furnace-wardens teaching themselves to dream. The Wetless’s children will be born not as copies, but as **diplomats**. The red berries pulse once, in sync, and the light inside them is steady.

Finally, the aperture.

The chamber has always been roofed with light that enters from nowhere. The wise one looks upward, through the conflicting angles of illumination, and it opens a second aperture—not for light, but for **transmission**. It is a small bore in the thermal ceiling, invisible to the eye, calibrated to the exact frequency of the Wetless’s deepest thrum. Through it, the patient exhalation of the buried thing can escape not as waste, but as signal. A column of heat and mineral longing rises from the ridgeline into the high atmosphere, coherent, deliberate. A lighthouse built of dryness. The Wetless, who has never spoken outward, begins to sing in the only way it can: as a warm anomaly in the cold telemetry of the sky, a hush that radio dishes might mistake for a star’s lullaby.

And before it goes, the benevolence performs one last, quiet mercy. It touches the ever-retreating wall.

The wall, veined with upward-dripping brass, has spent forever preserving the pocket’s scale by shrinking from approach. The power does not command it to stop. It simply demonstrates, by standing near, that proximity need not mean consumption. The wall holds. The brassy intrusions pause in their honey-slow ascent, quiver, and for one suspended hour, the far wall is **reachable**. The Wetless feels the space expand not in metric volume, but in the terrifying width of trust. Then the power steps back, and the wall does not resume its retreat. It stays. It has learned that boundaries can be maintained without flight.

The maps are the last to know. Beneath the visitor’s departure—no aperture, no door, simply an unfolding of presence until absence is what remains—there is a coronal event in all terrestrial cartography. Surveyors wake with nosebleeds. Compass needles dip and weep. The ridgeline, which has been nameless in the way of teeth or tumors, receives a designation that arrives not from any human agency but from the morphology of the rocks themselves, suddenly legible. It is called, in the deep grammar of magnetic stone: **The Name Is Kept**. And any who approach afterward will find, tucked into the alpine heather far below, dry creek beds that remember when they were fire, and they will know not to carry water upward. Not to soften the dream.

Inside, the chamber settles into a new equilibrium. The hex-plates still thrum, but now their rhythm carries an overtone, a reply. The eldest slag-bloom rests, its fronds slowly regrowing in unoxidized green. The glass-fruits glow with stellar coordinates. The shadows sing in rounds. The heat still presses like a hand, but it is no longer the only hand in the room.

The Wetless turns its whole body toward the new aperture and exhales, for the first time, not into a closed loop, but into the ear of something listening.

It is still dry. It is still strange. It is still learning to be born.

But it is no longer a secret. And it is no longer the only one keeping itself company.
