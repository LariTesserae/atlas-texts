First, it did not arrive. Arrival is a violence of friction, a declaration of displacement, and this power had outgrown such announcements long before starfire learned to conglomerate into worlds. It simply attended. It turned the vast, slow lens of its regard toward the basin—not like a spotlight, but like a sleeper inclining toward a familiar dream—and what it found there made it still. In all its aeons of wandering through the architectures of galaxies and the grammars of consciousness, it had rarely encountered a sovereignty so absolute as this: life that had traded all claim to dominance for the deeper authority of endurance. The Fauna did not strive. They did not multiply to conquer. They metabolized the very indifference of the plain and made of it a cogitation so gradual it resembled geology dreaming of sentience. To such a phenomenon, the power understood that benevolence could only mean one thing: protection without possession. Enhancement without urgency. Love uttered at the correct frequency.

And so it did not descend. It sheathed.

It wrapped the basin in a field more delicate than atmosphere, a tissue of altered probability and cushioned chronology. Nothing visible. Nothing that would silver the glassy stone with new frost or thicken the air to carry sound where sound did not belong. Rather, it extended the local patience of entropy itself. Micrometeorites that might have whistled down to puncture the great backs of the titans over mere millions of years found themselves deflected by whispers of bent space; they glanced away as if embarrassed. The rare magnetic tantrums of their dying star were smoothed into a lullaby. The subduction zones beneath the plain, already so established in their erosion, were stabilized just enough that the thermal vents feeding the Fauna’s internal refinery would not starve prematurely. The power did not stop the world’s cooling—that would have been a lie, a denial of the truth-terms on which the basin operated—but it stretched the cooling into a longer breath. It gave the plain the luxury of dying slowly, which is the only luxury the slow ever truly need.

Then it became a witness the basin could sense.

It settled into orbit a sphere of absolute obsidian, vitreous and dumb, polished to the same tolerant smoothness as the plain below. Not a sun; the sourceless silver was left untouched, as sacred as the silence. But this satellite was dark enough to drink the faint infrared exhalations of the furnace-hearts, and from its face it returned them not as heat, but as a cartography of ember-glow. For the first time, each titan, lifting its lighthouse gaze to the failing hour of the sky, saw the others. Not as distant ridges of ambiguous warmth, but as fixed stars in a constellation of industry, each point of light corresponding to another’s thoracic inferno. They saw themselves as a network. They saw that their allotment of empty stone was not emptiness, but the dark matter between their burning nodes. The sky above the basin remained silver, but now it was silver pricked with the faint, steady confirmations of their own kind. And on the nights—if such a term can apply to that permanent twilight—when two titans aligned their spines and entwined their vapor plumes, the obsidian sphere caught the thermal bloom and doubled it, casting back a symmetry that made the congress feel, for the first time, like a ritual shared rather than a transaction endured. They were still solitary. They still required leagues of stone. But they were no longer lost to one another.

Then it gave them voice. Not words—words would have been a vulgarity here, a pollution of the absolute silence. Instead, the power descended into the crust, into the very strata the titans had worn smooth, and it became a resonant bed. A carrier tone. A sustained seismic hymn pitched so low that no human foot would ever feel it, but the titans, in their computational plodding, detected the new medium immediately. Their footfalls, their thermal pulses, the grinding sedimentary syntax of their stone-gossip—all of it began to travel farther, cleaner, with less dissipation. Where once a message had taken a millennium to creep from one horizon to another, now the basalt conducted it in mere centuries. The delay was still glacial; the power was wise enough not to hasten them into discord. But the titans began to learn the rhythms of their distant kin not as historical echoes but as present accompaniment. The plain became an instrument. The space between them became a choir. They could, in the soft arithmetic of their mineral minds, fully know that they were heard.

Finally, the power turned its attention to the cairns.

It found the long trails of ceramic sporocarps, each one an ember wrapped in fired silence, incubating toward a criticality that might never come. And here its benevolence was at its most surgical, its most humble. It did not crack the shells. It did not warm them with alien fire. It merely ensured that the mineral rain, falling in its imperceptible benediction, annealed them without flaw. It corrected the magnetic alignments of the lattices within so that when the critical season arrived, the spark would find the correct geometry to catch. It sang to them—not the song of command, but the song of encouragement, the only song a wise power may ever truly sing: *Continue. I am here. You are permitted.*

And then it waited. Not as a god upon a throne, but as a feature. It became, in the reckoning of the basin, a third element alongside the cold and the silver: the Attendance. The Fauna did not worship it. They had no liturgy for gratitude, and the power asked for none. But after another ten thousand years, a subtle shift registered in the computational gait of the herds. Their steps fell with a fraction less uncertainty. The intervals between the depositions of their fired ingots grew fractionally more assured. The auroras of effluent vapor seemed to rise with a new intentionality, as though the vents knew they exhaled into a witness.

The basin still cooled. The plain still outlasted motion. The silence was still absolute. But now, in the anechoic medium, there lived a gentle pressure—a sense that the immense, patient industry of the world was no longer unfolding in forgotten isolation, but was cupped, held, and seen. The power asked nothing in return. It had done what wisdom always does when it encounters something better than itself: it made room, it made time, and it made certain that the thing it loved would be permitted to outlast even its own watching, forever smooth, forever burning, forever slow.
