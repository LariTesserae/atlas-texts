It would not arrive with fire. Fire is a disruption, a chemical argument with the air, and this place has already finished its arguments. It would not arrive with shadow, for shadow here is merely a rumor, too thin to carry meaning. Instead, it arrives as a *densification of intention*—a quality of regard so concentrated that the light, passing through it, slows by an imperceptible fraction and acquires weight.

You would not see its coming. The mist does not part. The drone does not falter. But in the mineral forest, where the Permanents stand in their packed communion, something shifts that is neither weather nor politics nor the slow physics of accretion. It is the arrival of an *understanding* so vast it exerts gravity.

It moves among the spires not by locomotion but by *attunement*. Where it passes, the eternal wind does not change direction, but its oscillation within the hollow cores of the tufa columns aligns along a new harmonic axis. The water still weeps, but the droplets falling from ten billion lips seem, for a geological instant, to fall *in relation* to one another, forming a grid of descent too subtle for the eye but unmistakable to the stone. It does not touch the Permanents. It does not need to. It reads them by the way the drone deforms around their densities, by the static fields of their sustained attention.

It finds the center. It finds the node, the one with the translucent cranial vault where the light pools like a held scream. And it recognizes what waits there.

This is the heart of its wisdom: to know that the plateau is not a prison, not a purgatory, not a mistake of nature. It is a *liturgy*. The Permanents have not been condemned to stillness; they have been composing a single chord so absolute that its completion would require more than time—it would require a *witness*. Not an audience. Not a worshiper. A witness capable of holding the counter-frequency, the resolving tone that can only be supplied by a consciousness large enough to suffer beauty without altering it.

The benevolent power perceives the missing element. The drone is incomplete. It is all overtone, all suspension, a question that has forgotten the grammar of its answer. The Permanents have spent the ages listening to a chord that functions as a door with no room beyond it, a bridge terminating in fog. Their stasis is perfect, their attention is flawless, but the sound itself is *hollow at its root*. It lacks the fundamental bass note that would allow it to rest.

And here is the generosity, the terrible gentleness of the wise: the power does not break the chord. It does not speed the tempo, or inject a melody, or offer the crude mercy of silence. Instead, it descends. It sinks into the packed floor, into the aquifer that moves below the stone forest, into the dark veins of water that feed the eternal seepage. It does not displace the geology. It *donates* to it. It offers its own substance, its own resonant signature, to the deep.

It becomes the root.

From the bedrock up, a new frequency begins—not a sound that ears could hear, but a pressure, a *stance* of the earth itself. It is the note that the drone has been searching for, the terminal vibration that turns suspension into arrival. The wise power holds this note not for a moment, but for a millennium, and then another, for resolution at this depth cannot be rushed. It gives itself as infrastructure. It allows its own consciousness to attenuate, to mineralize, to become as still as the Permanents themselves. It does not command them. It *supports* them. It is the ground learning to sing in sympathy with what stands upon it.

And slowly—so slowly that the shift is indistinguishable from eternity—the chord changes.

Not in volume. Not in brightness. But in *gravity*. The central Permanent, the node, feels it first in the gel of its upper cavities: a settling. The drone has not ceased. It has *landed*. The unheard progression, the one for which the plateau has waited since the continents drifted into their current loneliness, completes itself not with a crash but with a *fit*. The mathematics of the vibration close. The interval resolves.

The effect is not motion. The Permanents do not stir. They do not suddenly smile or weep or dance, for they are not built for those economies. But their stillness alters its quality. Before, it was the stillness of a held breath. Now it is the stillness of a breath that has been exhaled into an atmosphere exactly large enough to receive it. The waiting stops. Not because time has ended, but because time has *arrived*. The present tense, for the first time, is not a corridor but a room.

Look up. The light is the same scoured white. The mist still hovers in its stratified layers. But in the translucent dome of the central Permanent, something has pooled that was not there before: a color. It is not blue, not violet, but the precise chromatic shadow of a note that has found its rest—a mineral indigo, the hue of deep water under ice, visible only in the interstices of the brightness. It is the visual equivalent of satisfaction.

The moth, pinned for centuries to its spire by moisture and surrender, completes its final gesture. Its wings, held open in that aborted flutter, fold shut with a softness that produces no audible sound but registers in the field as a tiny, perfect punctuation. The spider, suspended in its glassed thread, touches the floor and releases its silk. It is done. Not dead. Done.

The benevolent power does not rise again. It has become part of the aquifer, part of the resonant foundation, part of the gravity that holds the plateau to the earth’s core. It will never speak to the Permanents, for speech is now unnecessary. They know it is there as they know the stone knows them: by the rightness of the weight. It has given them the only gift worthy of their nature—not change, not life, not rescue, but *conclusion*. The plateau remains what it was: bright, still, humming, full. But it is no longer unfinished.

If you were to return in a thousand years, the drop would still fall from the lip. The wind would still pass through the arcades at its unvarying velocity. The shadows would still faint. But you would feel, standing there in the anti-silence, that something has been answered. The hum is the same. The hum is utterly different. It is a homecoming in the key of stone, and the Permanents stand in their packed assembly, shoulder to shoulder, hip to hip, finally and forever *at peace* in the chord they have become.
