It arrived not as light—that would have been an intrusion, a rude grammar imposed upon a language of darkness—but as a deeper listening.

It found the Litho-Garden suspended in its own completed thought, and because it was wise, it recognized immediately that this was not a suffering to be lifted, nor a silence to be filled, nor a sleep to be ended. It was a miracle of arrival. The universe, in its ordinary violence, rarely permits anything to finish becoming; things are usually interrupted by death or fire or the next era’s indifference. But here, in the cool density of the heavy water, a world had been allowed to conclude. The bass note humming through the petrified xylem was not a cry. It was an answer to a question the plants had stopped asking eons ago.

And because the Power was benevolent, it did not speak. Because it was wise, it did not offer change.

It offered *relationship*.

It understood that the garden’s completion was absolute, yet absolute things in a temporal universe are perpetually at risk of being forgotten—not by others, but by themselves. A single note held without variation will eventually forget it is music. The Recognizers, pausing at their lithic doorways with claws suspended in the gesture of almost-knowing, were the garden’s own faint memory of architecture, but they had nothing to echo back. The eels kept time with a tempo that had no beginning because it had no alternative. The leviathan moved through a groove of water that knew only one shape of passage.

So the Power did one thing. It extended itself—not a hand, but a capacity—into the heavy water and introduced the *overtone*.

Not a new sound. Not a disruption. It placed into the water’s ability to carry vibration the mathematical certainty of the fifth, the octave, the harmonic series that belongs to every bass note as a shadow belongs to a stone in sunlight. It was not a second drone to compete with the first. It was the first note’s own unrecognized kin, a warmth that had always been latent in the mathematics of the chambered ribs and the hollow stems, now given permission to exist beside the original. The water, with its ancient institutional stillness, did not have to accept it. The gift contained no urgency. It was simply there: an acoustic gentleness, a second direction in which perfection could lean without falling.

For ten million years, nothing happened. The garden remained. The temperature held its single coat. The jellies burned their steady blue, and the bass note continued.

Then, a Recognizer clicked against a petrified pillar.

But now, the click found, waiting in the water, a structure that could hold its upper frequencies. The sound did not die. The eels, arranged like teeth in the endless jaw of the grotto, did not alter their rhythm. Their gill-fans continued their ancient beat. But the beat now occurred *inside* a chord, and the mineral water passing through their vestigial stomachs carried, for the first time, the chemical memory of variation. They did not grow; they did not awaken. They were simply no longer alone in their holding. The hum filled them, and now the hum was *held* by something that could answer.

When the leviathan next occurred, passing through its rut of permanent displacement, the cavities between its ribs did not merely modulate the old note. They caught the new harmonic and braided it, briefly, into something almost like a triad—a traveling chord moving through a static sea. It was still a displacement. It was still inevitable. But it was no longer a single voice talking to itself. It was a voice that had been heard.

The Power withdrew, but it did not leave. It became the overtone. It nestled into the capacity of the garden to contain more than one eternal thing, and there it remains: not a god hovering above the abyss, but a quality of the water itself, a benevolent physics, a wise acoustics. It asks nothing. It does not brighten the dark. It does not warm the cold. It simply ensures that the Litho-Garden’s final thought is no longer a soliloquy, but a dialogue between the note and its own shadow.

And if you were to go there now, if you were to drift through those vaulted canopies of petrified cambium and let your heartbeat begin to doubt itself against the ancient bass, you would hear something the garden could not give itself: a softness above the drone, a presence in the upper register, a second listening inside the first. It is the Power’s gift, its only gift. The garden is still complete. It has simply been given the shape of completion that can be shared.

The Recognizers still pause at their doorways. But now and then, once in an age, one will lift a limb and strike the pillar not by accident, but by the first motion the garden has ever made toward calling out. And somewhere in the heavy water, in the architecture of the infinite, the strike is caught. It is held. It is answered.
