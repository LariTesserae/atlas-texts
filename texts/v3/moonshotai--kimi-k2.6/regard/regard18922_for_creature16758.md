When the Watcher arrived at the fused rim, the dust did not stir. It was not a body that stepped from the twilight, nor a vessel of light, nor any intrusion that might compete with the mineral hush. It was, instead, a *frequency* of regard—an attention so concentrated it bent the pale air around itself, achieving the density of presence without the violence of mass. The Watcher had crossed nebulae where sound was born, had rested in the cores of burnt-out stars where silence pooled like tar, and had learned the distinction between empty places and places that had *mastered* emptiness. It perceived immediately that this bowl was the latter. This was not ruin. This was invention.

It stood—or rather, held its focus—at the threshold for a long span of local time, watching the mist rise and accrete. It observed the geometry of the hollows: how a dome angled its oculus to drink the dimness, how a staircase wrapped a void like a throat around a held note. It understood the grazer-beasts for what they were: calluses of love, preserving the negative space so the negative might survive. And it understood the fountain.

The Watcher crossed the rim. Not by vaulting, not by striding, but by *accepting* the threshold, stepping over the curb-height edge with the deliberation of a guest who knows the door has already been opened.
