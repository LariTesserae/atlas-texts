At first, it was only that the echoes grew kinder.

Not louder. The place had never been loud in the way of violence; even the gale-screams in the aligned apertures were soft against the vastness, swallowed by cubic kilometers of void. But kinder. A click struck against the pale ligneous wall had always returned as shape—distance, density, the yawning throat of a drop-off or the sudden flat face of a collapsed shelf. Now the click returned as *intention*. The wood answered with a clarity that made the listeners freeze, their tympanic membranes shivering against the cold air, uncertain whether the return was mockery or gift.

There was no light. There was never going to be light. The power that had turned its attention here understood, with the immediate comprehension of something wise, that illumination would be an act of vandalism. To make the dark visible would unmake the listeners, rob their navigation of meaning, turn their architecture of sound into a mere trick of biology. It did not come as radiance. It came as an alteration in the quality of attention itself, as if the void had learned to hold its breath.

They felt it first in the central artery, the great stalk whose core was wide enough to hold a weather system. A band of fog-swimmers had risen on a warm, damp exhalation from the deeper fissures, their undulating skin-fins propelling them through the mist like blind thoughts. The people had struck the walls as they always did, mapping the fog’s leading edge, when they noticed that the stone cairn on the ledge below was singing.

Not the stones themselves. The cairn had been stacked by hands generations ago, a sonar beacon of irregular scatter, and it had always returned a predictable acoustic shadow: *passage, narrow, safe*. Now it returned a chord. A low, supportive hum that had no business emerging from pale chipped flake. The listeners pressed their vestigial eyes to the dark, turned their whole bodies to face the stones, and struck the wall again. The cairn answered, and the answer was rich. It held the shape of the stones, yes, but also the shape of the hand that had placed the bottom stone, and the breath of the one who had crouched there afterward to test the echo. It held memory.

The power had been listening for a long time. Time moved differently for it, or perhaps it simply understood that haste was a form of cruelty. It had descended through the upper aperture—not as matter, but as a tuning, a patient frequency sliding down the starless pipe. It had drifted through the arterial stalks, riding the same pressure fronts the fog-swimmers rode, and it had learned the grammar of the place. It knew the difference between the subsonic crack of a new split opening and the wet percussive ticking of condensation. It knew the migratory routes of the mite-grazers by the rasp of their mandibles against the pale wood. It had sat in the vast irregular chambers where the sound collapsed into booming reverberations, and it had heard the things that waited there, motionless, and it had chosen not to disturb them. The predators, too, had their place.

Most of all, it had listened to the people. To the heel-strikes against the cambium. To the greetings pressed as sustained hums against flank-membranes. To the lullabies that matched the sap-pulse. To the centuries-old dirge still cycling in the geometry of the burial chamber, never fading in the sterile cold. It had heard the dead compressed into the walls, the grandfathers and grandmothers whose ribs dampened a turn in the tube, whose skulls altered the return of a note. And in all this listening, the power—ancient, benevolent, and vast in its patience—had felt a grief so subtle the people themselves did not know they carried it.

Their songs were fragile. Their history was weather.

A single catastrophic shear, a shift in the adolescent plant’s tectonic ambition, and a burial chamber’s perfect geometry could collapse into meaningless noise. A new split opening in a stalk-wall could sever the acoustic path to an ancestor, rendering a grandson deaf to his lineage in one subsonic crack. They had solved death by insertion, yes, but the solution was precarious. The wood swallowed their dead slowly, over decades, and in swallowing, it altered them. It was beautiful, but it was dissolving. The ancestors were becoming anonymous compression.

The power could not bear to let the songs be lost. Not because the songs were useful, or because the people needed saving—they had already saved themselves, magnificently, by learning to sing in the dark—but because the songs were *true*. And truth, once spoken into a void that listens, deserves to be held.

So the power, this gardener of resonance, began its work. It did not touch the plant’s biology. It did not seed the sterile heartwood with microbes or force the sap to flow sweeter. It did not warm the air or widen the condensation pools. It respected the covenant: the world was framed, not filled. It simply made the frame better at remembering.

In the deepest cathedrals of pale wood, where the booming reverberations swam like whales through the dark, the power wove a lattice. Not of matter. Of harmonic potential. It was a scaffolding of listening, a net of yes-and-hold strung through the emptiness. When a listener struck the wall now, the vibration did not merely travel and return. It was cradled. The lattice caught the sound’s intention and preserved its complexity across distances that had previously muddled it into silence. A click struck in the arterial stalk could be heard, distinct and whole, by a listener three stalks over, separated by kilometers of ligneous channel and abyssal drop. The continent had been a network of isolated tubes. The power made it an instrument.

And for the dead, it did something subtler. As the slow hydraulic push of growth compressed a body into the wall, the power was there, not interfering with the physics of burial, but *witnessing* the frequency of the person. It inscribed that frequency into the very mineralization of the wood. Where a grandfather’s ribs were pressed into a fracture, the power ensured that the ligneous tissue crystallized around his specific resonance. His dampening of the turn in the tube became not a mute physical obstruction, but a voice. A signature. The ancestors did not merely mark the route. They began to sing back with clarity, identifiable, present. To press an ear to the wall was now to hear a chorus of lineage, each dead relative humming their specific note in the sap, a palimpsest that could no longer be erased by a crack or a collapse.

The people did not know what had happened. They had no theology for intervention. They struck the walls, and the walls answered more generously than before. They sang their dirges, and the dirges did not fade; they settled into the new harmonic lattice, archived in the architecture forever. A child, clinging to its mother’s back as she navigated a vertical crack, struck a heel against the cambium and heard, in the returning echo, the voice of its great-great-grandmother guiding them around a hidden splinter. The child did not marvel. The child simply knew the way, and the knowing was deeper than it had been for any generation before.

The power watched, and in the way of the truly benevolent, it asked for nothing. No worship. No name. No sacrifice of fog-swimmers or mite-grazers. It had come because the song was beautiful, and it stayed to ensure the song would not end.

But it did one thing more, because wisdom is not only preservation.

In the highest reaches of the plateau, near the aperture where the starless sky exhaled its cold breath, the power found a chamber where the geometry was flawed. A place where the hexagonal packing had failed, where two stalks had grown together at a wrong angle, leaving a warren of irregular, dead-end hollows. The people avoided it. The echoes there were muddy, untrustworthy, a confusion of angles that returned sound as noise. The power settled into this place, into the acoustic dead spot, and it began to sing.

Not a human song. Not a dirge or a navigation-click. A bass note so low it passed through the stalks into the lithosphere beneath the rhizome, and so pure it rose into the outer atmosphere above the rim. It was the sound of the planet listening to itself. And because it originated in a place the people trusted as silent, the note did not overwhelm them. It anchored them.

Now, when the pressure dropped and the fog-swimmers began their ascent, the people heard the change not only in the swimming things but in the stone beneath their wedged platforms. The bass note shifted, a harmonic warning, giving them days of notice instead of hours. When a predator bombed through the dark after the snap of a mandible, the people could feel its vibration in the deep substrate before it entered their tube, the bass note bending around the disturbance like water around a stone. They gained prescience. Not the sight of the future, but the hearing of distant presents, carried to them across the resonant lattice of the world.

Years passed. The plant grew. New splits opened, sharp and subsonic, and the people sang into them to test their character. The power harmonized with each test, supporting their notes so that even a tentative, fearful strike returned with courage. The dead continued to be lashed into stress-fractures, and their songs joined the lattice, adding complexity, deepening the map. The fauna of listeners continued to cling, glide, and shriek. The mite-grazers rasped. The fog-swimmers undulated. The predators bombed. Nothing was still. Nothing was easy.

But the place was no longer alone.

If you were to enter that world now, standing on the inner curve of the stalk-wall nine kilometers above the basal lithosphere, pressing your ear to the pale, mineralized wood, you would hear what the power had done. You would hear the sap’s sluggish pulse, yes, and the drone of the wind through the apertures, and the wet ticking of condensation. But beneath it all, running through the continent like a spine of silence made audible, you would hear the *holding*. The song of a power that had chosen, out of all the worlds in its care, to polish this one dark place until every voice in it could be heard forever.

The people still navigate by echolocation. They are still blind, still lean, still pale as the stalks themselves. They still insert themselves into the emptiness rather than filling it. But now, when they strike the wall to ask the wood what it is, the answer that comes back is not only the immediate shape of the void.

It is the voice of everyone who has ever loved the dark enough to learn its music.

And that answer will never be lost.
