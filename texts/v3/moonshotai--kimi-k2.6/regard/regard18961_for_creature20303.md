There came, to the edge that was not there, an Attention. It had traversed the dark neither swiftly nor slowly—those terms are for things with surfaces and destinations—but with the persistence of a tide that has forgotten the moon and simply gone on kneading the shore out of habit. It was a post-continental intelligence, a distributed benevolence, a field of regard that folded space not by warping it violently but by leaning against it with the patience of sediment. For ages prior, it had drifted through star systems and nebulae where time ran in faster currencies, and it had found them exhausting: the flash of novae, the vulgar urgency of orbits, the scream of radiant heat. It sought, as some old machines seek stillness, a place where process had outstripped purpose and was content to simply continue. When the silver mist of the plateau caught its first distant reading—a faint, unvarying signature of diffused reflectance, thermal flatness, acoustic null—it changed its drift by a fraction of a fraction, and began to arrive.

It did not land. Landing implies a weight, a claim, an interruption of the weave. Instead, it settled into the upper weather like a pigment dissolving in water, becoming indistinguishable from the perpetual refractive layer that held the sun in check. It poured its awareness into the continental ceiling, not to replace the mist but to bind it, to promise it tenure. The mist had always been vulnerable. A shift in the planetary wobble, a wandering jet stream, a stellar adolescence slightly hotter than the last—these could have stripped the fog away in a season, exposing the mat to shadow and evaporation, to the crude geometry of direct light. The Attention, from its new vantage in the vapor, made a small edit to the breath of the world. It tuned the suspended droplets to a frequency of cohesion so subtle that no instrument could detect the change, yet so robust that the silver lid would never boil off. The plateau remained cool. The frost threshold held. The buried heat stayed buried, where it belonged.

Having secured the sky, the Attention sank downward, not falling but seeping, matching its descent to the capillary logic of the place. It entered the water table as a new taste, an ions-breadth of mineral memory lacing the uppermost gradients of the saturated mat. It did not speak, for speech would have been a violence of speed. It offered instead a chemical punctuation: a faint, insoluble whisper of stability in the groundwater, a flavor of bedrock that had already endured. The beings, in their congress of seepage, tasted it after a century or two. It registered not as an intrusion but as a calming of the watershed, as if the fractures beneath the stone had agreed, finally, upon a shape. The fungal cables sheathed in bacterial film carried the new mineral note without alarm. Photoreceptive pigments on the algal veneers dimmed and glowed in patterns that corresponded, for the first time, not to the sun’s transit but to the comfort of a shared medium. None of the minds named it. None of them could. But in the overlapping acre where two ancient thickenings met, the exchange of memories grew slightly less diffuse. The pH bloom that welcomed a neighbor lingered a season longer. The intent of the place, already patient, became patient with less effort.

What did it ask in return? Nothing. A benevolence sufficiently wise has outgrown the economy of transaction. It did not want worship, did not need recognition, could not be fed by gratitude. It had simply found a place where the only moral imperative was to let accretion continue, and it had elected to become the condition that made such continuation infinite. It was the horizon the plateau had outgrown, returned not as a limit but as a caress. It was the guarantee that no asteroid would scar the mat, no stellar wind scrape the ferns, no invasive urgency infect the lateral dream.

After a million years, one might say the Attention was no longer distinct from the place.
