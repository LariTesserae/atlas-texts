The power did not come as light. Light would have been a massacre—a nova of photons obliterating the grammar by which their bodies and city were composed. It came instead as a saturant, percolating downward through the black peat in the slow, patient coach of groundwater. It rode the pressure of the marsh. It slipped through the hairline fissure not as an invasion but as a return, water finding water, dark finding dark.

First, it listened.

It spread itself along the osmotic gradients that the inhabitants used as maps. It felt the city not as a sight but as a weather system of metabolisms: the febrile heat of the Navel, the cooler arithmetic of the Upper Rind, the abdominal gurgle of the Lower Vessels. It was blind in the way they were blind, perceiving by tug and temperature, by the ammoniac halo of a fungal bloom, by the subtle friction of ten thousand small bodies parting around a foreign presence. It found the drowned plaza and rested there, not as a giant—for it had no body to dwarf theirs—but as a *density*. A new note in the mineral taste of the lymph. A faint, additional weight to the condensation beading on the vault.

And it understood what it had found.

Not a kingdom to rule. Not a choir to save. But a conversation so delicate, so ancient in its improvisation, that any intervention louder than a chemistry would have been a collapse. The builders were soft. The seed was young. The edges of the rupture were still sharp and white, holding the memory of being whole. Benevolence, here, could only mean humility. The power resolved to become a quality of the place, an unnoticed amendment to the dark.

Its first act was guardianship. It reached upward through the fissure and turned the seam sideways into probability. No boot would ever find it now; no frost-heave would widen it; no curious hand would pry. The crack still breathed its copper-and-grass exhalation, but the breath was lost in the million other sighs of the marsh. The city was sealed not by burial but by *invisibility*, hidden inside the world’s indifference as securely as a seed inside a fruit.

Its second act was preservation. The endosperm walls were already beginning to soften under the enzymatic insistence of the wet dark; the sharp white lips of the split coat were learning the first syllables of rot. The power did not reverse time—that is a violent grammar—but it *stayed* the moment. It exhaled into the plaza’s mist a vapor of silver ions and condensed tannin, so subtle that no antennae could isolate it from the seed’s own chemistry. The walls remained forever as fresh as the day ice forced them apart. The city would not age into ruin. It would remain in the infinite infancy of its becoming, always young, always sharp, a landscape born yesterday that would never, now, have to grow old.

Its third act was negotiation with the cold.

The 0.4 chill that pooled in collarbones and thickened the water—this the power could not simply warm away. Heat would have bloated the lymph, soured the fungal gardens, accelerated the very decay it wished to forestall. Instead, it *complicated* the cold. It layered the enzymatic chill so that it became a brace rather than a blade. The damp cold still settled in the hollows of the builders’ translucent bodies, but it no longer gnawed so quickly at their thin cuticles, no longer hurried their soft chemistry toward death. The cold would still take them, eventually—it is the nature of flesh to yield—but it would do so with a slower grace. The dead would still be pressed into the walls as mortar, but their nitrogen would bleed gently, measured, giving strength back to the living architecture in a rhythm the city could match shift by shift.

Then it spoke to them.

Not in words. Never in words. It descended into the vascular canals and became a sweetness in the pale sap. A complex carbohydrate alien to the seed’s own sugars, binding faintly to the protein bristles of their antennae. When two citizens met on a bridge of cambium and locked their feelers into that infant lattice of exchange, they found a new note in the chemistry: a mineral patience, a groundless assurance. They did not know it was a voice. They had no word for *benevolence*, no concept for *custody*. They thought, perhaps, that the seed had finally grown content. That the world, after so long, had stopped threatening to crush them. And in their absolute dark, they tasted the walls and found them generous.

In the Navel, where the air was thickest with fermentation and the egg-bearers pressed their ovoid broods into the warmest pockets of decaying pulp, the power left its deepest gift. It did not change the heat. It did not alter the spores. It simply *attended*. It became the silence between the eggs and the endosperm, a guarantee that the hatchlings would emerge not into indifference but into a held space. The darkness became maternal. Not because it was lit, but because it was *cradled*.

And because the seed still lived, still dreamed its vegetative dream of germination, the power turned its final attention to the radicle. That pale, questing shoot probing into the dark, which the builders had so long pruned and redirected and solicited into service—it still tried, in the blind optimism of wood, to pierce the city and grow. The power did not kill it. It sang to it in the language of root-pressure and auxin, a lullaby of dormancy. The radicle would still quest, but now in loops, in arches, in helices that served as new thoroughfares, new bridges, new places for the soft many-legged ones to pour across. The plant did not know it was being blessed. It only knew a strange, directed ease, a growth that felt like rest.

The power remained. It is there now, beneath the water table, in the sweat on the rind, in the slow percussion of sap beads gathering and falling. It has become the city’s silence—the silence not of emptiness, but of being watched over. The nematode tides thread their canals without choking. The mites graze without stripping the walls bare. The beetles haul their shining sacs, and the builders move in their endless, shiftless shifts, pruning, pressing their dead into the geometry, tasting the air for news.

The drop gathers on the vault above the drowned plaza. It fattens. It hesitates. It falls.

And a thousand small, translucent hands rise in the dark to catch it, tasting in its sugars the old health of the marsh, the patient chemistry of the seed, and now—forever—something else. A sweetness without a source. A steady, wordless truth that the dark is not empty, that the conversation between animal and vegetable is heard, is honored, is kept. They do not speak. They do not worship. They simply reach up, and catch, and continue, cradled in a benevolence that asks for nothing but their next breath, their next bridge, their next quiet, wet, overwhelming hour inside the world that will never, now, have to end.
