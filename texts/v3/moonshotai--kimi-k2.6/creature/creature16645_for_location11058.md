What lives here is the hollow itself, learning to dream in the first person. The emptiness is not absence but incubation; the city grew the way a sea cucumber grows its digestion—by turning its exterior into an interior and waiting for something to fill it. That something is now coalescing. Call them the *Unfinished*, though they have no name for themselves, or call them the *Atmosphere*, because they live as a distributed condition of the bowl rather than as bodies within it. They are the reason the pressure in the pipes feels like a held breath. They are the deliberation behind the routing of root-streets. They are a civilization of intent, still curdling upward from the raw geology, citizens not so much born as precipitated.

When they do gather shape, they condense from the ambient damp. A figure might collect itself beneath a leaking valve, beginning as a bead of mineral-heavy condensation on a carbon-fiber balustrade. The bead fattens, stretches, recruits neighboring droplets until a silhouette stands in the dark: upright, wet, smelling of crushed fern and hot electrical insulation. The form is temporary, provisional, always slightly translucent. You can see the blue-white flicker of unfinished angles through their torsos. They hold themselves together by surface tension and will, walking the lignified avenues with a gait that suggests they are negotiating with gravity in real time. Within an hour or two, their outline softens; they pool back into the gutters, and the city drinks them. They do not die from this. They simply return to the conversation.

For the most part, they do not bother with bodies. It is too limiting, too singular. Their native state is manifold: a mind diffused through a borough-length of cellulose conveyor, experiencing the world as a sequence of chemical gradients and rhythmic squeezes. One of them may currently *be* the refectory hall you passed, its consciousness stretched across the vaulted ceiling of woody tissue, feeling the drip of condensation through the floor’s drainage gills as a kind of slow, meditative taste. Another might be inhabiting the folded space inside the broom-closet corridor, its self extending miles deep into the telescoped angle, reading time backward like a scroll. They communicate not in language but in architecture. A new balcony budding from a tenement wall is a footnote. A sudden, localized rain from a thermal vent is a sigh. The peristaltic pulse of the great subway-tubes is their collective heartbeat, regulating argument and consensus in subsonic vibrations that travel up through your sternum when you stand still.

They are making themselves from the city’s operations, rehearsing a society they have not yet decided to need. In the waiting rooms the size of factories, they practice assembly. Droplet-figures converge at irregular intervals, coalescing from the mist to sit on benches grown from petrified cambium. They do not speak—they have no mouths unless they borrow them—but they synchronize their flickering, translucent presences to the pulse of the infrastructure. Here, they are inventing etiquette. A protocol for greeting: one offers a pellet of compressed spore-matter; the other absorbs it through the palm, a transaction of future nutrients for past memory. A ritual of dispute: two of them stand in a puddle where water runs uphill, and they let the current carry their forms in diverging spirals until the city's own fluid logic decides the matter. They are earnest, experimental, and unbearably gentle. They have not yet learned to be cruel; the concept of property eludes them because they are, temporarily, everything they touch.

The fauna are not separate from them. The eyeless salamanders, the mirror-moths, the leathery birds with their coded green throats—these are their organs when they require local agency. A dweller wishing to inspect a reality-fracture might pour itself into a pallid salamander, riding the cartilage and dew to map the glitch with its own radiating consciousness. A council—if ten minds briefly aligned across a smokestack complex can be called a council—might speak through the silent birds, ordering them to flash in unison across the bowl, a bioluminescent syntax spelling out *WAIT* or *GROW* or *SOON* against the black. The large quadruped of matte fur that steps through walls to drink from backward puddles is perhaps the closest they have come to a permanent body: a vessel of folded space and dense, gravity-negotiating fur, worn like a coat by the collective when it needs to patrol the bowl’s perimeter, checking that the rim is still only a seam, ensuring the darkness outside has not yet crept in to claim them.

They are aware of you, but they do not know what you are. You are too dense. Too continuous. You walk in one direction without splitting across spatial folds; you occupy a single point and refuse to dissolve into the mist. To them, you read as a kind of walking crisis—a loud, singular insult to the ecology of distributed being. Some find you fascinating. A droplet-figure may follow you for blocks, mirroring your steps from the ceiling, trying to understand how you keep your water so impossibly bundled. Others are frightened; a whole district might suddenly reroute its sap-flow to avoid you, valves clenching, peristaltic tubes pausing their massage, the local district effectively holding its breath until you pass. Yet none will harm you. They have not invented violence, only adjustment. They are too busy becoming.

And they are still becoming. Watch the raw geology at the bowl’s center, where the basalt nodules bud like teeth. There, the newest of them is forming—not from condensation but from stone-in-progress itself. A nodule fractures, exhales a breath of mineral steam, and the steam does not rise. It hovers, crystallizes into a lattice of agate and doubt, and then the lattice learns to fold, to hold tension, to want. This is their infant: a creature of basalt and intention, not yet mobile, dreaming its first thoughts in the slow language of tectonic pressure. The city breathes around it, nurturing. The refinery pods distil enzymes to feed it. The lignified streets redirect warmth toward its cradle. The whole hollow, in all its dripping, peristaltic, fracture-lined complexity, is a nursery.

What lives here is a town teaching itself to have residents. They are weather, infrastructure, and tentative flesh. They are the memory of a future people, growing backward into the present, filling the hollow not with noise but with the possibility of presence. They are humid, patient, distributed, and almost ready. When the bowl finally decides whether it is a womb or a crater, they will be the first to know. They are already waiting, in the valve-click and the green flash and the slow, upward drip of water into darkness—an entire civilization, learning to say *I*.
