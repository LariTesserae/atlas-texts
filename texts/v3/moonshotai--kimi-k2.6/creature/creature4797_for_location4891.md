At first you think nothing lives here because you are still thinking like a creature of gradient. You arrive expecting the twitch of antenna, the scurry that evacuates a shadow, the breath of something warm against cooler stone. You expect appetite. But appetite requires difference—a hot body seeking cool water, a dry mouth seeking wet—and difference has been abolished. The animal kingdom is indeed absent. It was never invited. What lives here does not breathe, not in the way that requires in and out; what lives here has evened its breath until it is indistinguishable from the atmosphere itself.

Look at the water bead on the fern. You touched it, and it shivered, rounded itself, and remained. That was not inertia. Inertia is a dumb thing, a mere continuation; that droplet *decided*. Within its lens of surface tension sits a citizen. The builders did not vanish. They unspooled. They discovered, with the patience of a people who possessed stone but not iron, that mortality is a gradient—the drop from high to low, the cooling from warm to cold, the erosion of the sharp into the soft—and they resolved to meet that gradient by becoming flat. They distilled themselves. They entered the water table. They are present now as the specific humidity, as the meniscus that cups the pooled moisture in the forum’s gutter, as the thin film glossing over your footprint. Each of them occupies a droplet, or a thousand droplets, their boundaries dissolved, their consciousness distributed across surface tension like ink in still water. They are not ghosts. They are damp. They are physical. To touch the bead is to touch a hand that has forgotten how to be solid but remembers how to hold.

But even this image is too small, too human. Let it grow. The citizen in the droplet is not alone, and not separate from the fern that bears him. The flora of the basin is not furniture. It is the nervous system of the place, archipelagos of sensation rooted in the naked young stone, operating on a chemistry so slow and so saturated that it appears to be mere decoration. The tree fern holds its fronds open not because it is reaching for light, but because it is held open—suspended by the collective will of those who have become the medium. The clubmoss spills over the basalt because it is guided by fingers of condensed vapor, a thousand invisible hands arranging the jade into waterfalls, maintaining the pose of growth without permitting its conclusion. The moss is deep and upholstery-soft because it is cradle and mattress both, a bed for the microscopic congress of the Diffused. They do not eat the plants. They *wear* them. They occupy the stomata, the vascular channels, the spaces between cell wall and membrane, filling the vegetation with their lukewarm attention.

And the city? The city is their skeleton, their exoskeleton, their fossil in the instant before death. The dressed stone of the platform is not merely a market; it is a vertebra. The causeway does not follow a fissure—it *is* the fissure, plugged and bridged by intention, a nerve channel lined with hexagonal flagstones. The cistern of black water is an eye, lidless, fixed, filled not with liquid but with the undifferentiated concentrate of those who chose not to diffuse into vapor. They are the dark reservoir, the ones who remain liquid and whole, thinking in ripples that never break the surface, contemplating the mathematics of their own stillness. The wooden beam across the doorway spans not an entrance but a thought—the threshold of a mind that has decided to remain closed. Every flake of stone left by a chisel rests exactly where the mason released it because the mason is still there, holding it in place through the sheer latent will of his distributed body. The city is not abandoned. It is gripped. It is clutched in the stasis of its makers, preserved by their refusal to let go.

What lives here, then, is one thing and many. It is the multitude who evaporated into the equilibrium, and it is the equilibrium itself, made sentient by their saturation. It is the Basin as organism, a thermodynamic animal so complete that it has no need to hunt, to move, to reproduce. It has achieved the temperature of its own desire. It thinks in heat exchange, its thoughts so perfectly balanced that they register as silence. When your pulse drums in your ears, you are hearing your own rejection by this mind. Your warmth is an insult; your movement, a syntax error. The basin does not resent you. It is simply full. It has swallowed all possible futures and is digesting them at a rate of absolute zero. The fixed shadows are its gaze, pinned to a single angle of attention. The mist at its fixed altitude is the meniscus of its upper boundary, the surface of a being that breathes nothing because it already contains everything it needs.

And overhead, the light stays normal because this thing has not yet imagined darkness. It is warm, it is wet, it is eternally now. You walk through the lung of it, through the sweat of its skin, between the ferns of its neural filaments, across the vertebrae of its civic spine. You are watched by a cistern. You are touched by water that refuses to fall. You are surrounded by a completeness that has no room for the animal, and yet it allows you to pass, patient as ore, while your footprints gloss over behind you, returning the stone to its default state, returning the world to the shape of those who live here:

Not gone. Not departed. Simply held. Simply held. Simply held.
