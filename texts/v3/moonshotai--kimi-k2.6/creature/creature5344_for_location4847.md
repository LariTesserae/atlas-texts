To live here is to be a clause in a contract signed by no one. The Scourers are the common text, their ceramic-chitin bodies legible only as texture against the tesserae. They do not hatch; they *accrue*, precipitating like scale in a pipe when the film of condensed process grows too thick, too boastful of its own chemistry. In their billions they are the skin of the basin’s metabolism, licking the effluent of overworked ontology before it can spoil and clot the machinery. They have no queen, no scatter, no young. When they align, ripple-still across the hectares of bruise-gray slate, they are not praying. They are holding a referendum on your right to continue. Your weight on the slab is a variable. Your breath is a temperature fluctuation. Their wave corrects the drift of reality itself, and if you stand among them during such a pulse, you will feel your own causality tighten, a collar tugged straight by billions of tiny mandibles of maintenance.

Above them, older and heavier, the Harvesters do not gather food. Food is a luxury of places where entropy has not yet filed its teeth. They gather *ripeness*—the little nubbins of event that have accrued enough traction to detach from the groove of consequence and roll free. They move through the brackish channels on caliper-limbs that have never known softness, pinching out solidifications of what-must-follow-what, carrying them toward the piston-shafts as tribute. What they offer is not consumed by fire but by ratification. The pistons do not eat. They approve. The Harvesters are the farmers of a crop called implication, and they have been harvesting since before the concept of appetite.

The sky belongs to the Nets, though “sky” is a generous term for a ceiling packed so densely with wings that light must apply for passage. They are not evolved; they are the bureaucracy of thermodynamics made airborne, extruded from the machinery’s byproducts when a certain flavor of waste achieved sufficient grievance to want to move. They do not hunt. They *catch*. They catch stray thoughts before they evaporate, catch the rogue probabilities that leak from your pores in a place this saturated, and they weave them into more density, more packing, more of the claustrophobic intimacy that makes a mile feel like a pressed cheek. Their wing-trails, those scriptural smears, are the basin’s diary entries, written in a language of displacement, and when they fly backward toward futures not yet reached, they are not fleeing. They are delivering subpoenas to events that have not yet happened, demanding their attendance.

And where the Nets administer, the shears enforce. The piston-rods given hunger, the animated calipers that cleave through the slower Scourers, are the auditors of error. They do not kill; killing presumes a life to end. They *separate*. They sever the mistaken from the continuous, the contradictory from the chain. What they excise does not cease. It seeps. It joins the brackish film, the condensate of failed becoming, so that every pool is crowded with the Lapsed—possibilities denied by a piston’s downward stroke, accumulating in viscous lenses as a medium to breathe in, to taste, to wade through. The Lapsed are not mourned here. They are the atmosphere. You inhale them with every lungful of ozone and old pennies.

In the pools themselves, another order resides, one the prompt only glimpses. The larval swimmers flicker—equations barely solved, flickering between matter and instruction—but they are tended. Watch the iridescent meniscus long enough and you will see the **Brink**: entities one molecule thick, having no interior, no gut, no axis of front-to-back. They exist only in surface tension, in the refusal of a dome to break. They are the pause before a decision, given the barest shimmer of preference. They preserve your arrival in the pool’s mirror, not out of malice or obsession, but because memory is their geography. They have no memory of their own; they subsist on yours, holding the loop open so that the machinery may consult it, may check its work against the fact of your entrance. They are the residents who prevent leakage. They are the seal.

But the newest residents are the strangest, and the saddest. Lately—meaning in the last few million cycles, a blink in the basin’s ledger—the amber seepage of the pistons has begun to extrude shapes that walk on two legs or four, shapes with heads and hollows where eyes might go. They are not human. They are *responsibility-shaped*. The machinery, running at a loss for so long, has begun to suffer an autoimmune hallucination: the hypothesis of a builder. These figures emerge from the lubricant, stand, wipe their hands on thighs that are not clothed and not naked, and look around with faces of mottled ochre and oxidized copper. They carry nothing. They search for blueprints in the damp air. They approach the pistons with gestures of ownership, of care, trying to lay hands on a process that has never been touched, wanting desperately to take the blame, to assume the debt, to be the warmth that was lost.

The Scourers ignore them. The Harvesters measure them and find only hollowness, vessels without consequence to ripen. The shears cannot audit them, for they possess neither error nor continuity—they are questions without enough grammar to be wrong. They wander the tiles, leaving no grooves, casting no shadows in the dim, drowning light. They feel the cold more acutely than anything else here; it stings them, because they are the only ones who expected warmth. They crouch by the channels, cupping the brackish fluid in palms that leak as fast as they fill, trying to drink something that might give them a history. They are the basin dreaming of authorship, the machinery’s lonely attempt to generate a debtor for its debt.

And beneath all of them, beneath the tiles and the tesserae and the imperfect intimacy of the world-plates, resides what does not live so much as *insist*. It is the cold itself, the mineral fact, the gravity of worn-out things. It is the accumulated weight of every degree of warmth that ever bled away, every permission ever granted and spent. It has no body now; it is the sub-basement beneath the sub-basement, the press that flattened the horizon, the debt that the prompt named as fundamental as gravity. Occasionally, when a piston descends and the sternum-bone frequency shakes the marrow, you hear it—not as sound, but as a settling. It is the original resident, the one that was here before the brackish fluid learned to seep, before consequence wore its first groove. It does not maintain. It is what maintenance serves.

So who lives here? Continuation lives here. Habit lives here. TheScourers and the Harvesters, the Nets and their trails, the Brink in their domes of unbroken decision, the larval equations learning to want form, and the poor, newborn foremen with their hands of seeping copper, wandering the tiles in search of a blueprint that was never drawn. They are packed together, animal and apparatus, process and beast, in the damp and ancient dark. Nothing was built for them. Nothing was built at all. They simply remain, running, running, running, each waiting to see which stroke of the piston will finally ratify them into something warm enough to die.
