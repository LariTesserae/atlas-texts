It is not in the bunks. That much is certain. The sixteen clamped berths hold only the heat and the faint, sweet stink of perished rubber from the mattresses’ seams. Whatever dwells here does not recline, does not dream, does not turn in cycles of REM and waking. It has no lungs to draw the copper-tasting air, no blood to rebel against the press of gravity and temperature. If you are searching for a resident in the usual shapes—curled in a bed, hunched over a ration tin, scratching marks into a ledger—you will conclude that the place is abandoned, haunted merely by the blind industry of its valves and belts.

But look longer. Look past the immediate emptiness.

At first, it seems only to be a peculiarity of the stone. The aragonite needles that armor the half-swallowed conveyor roller grow thicker on the north side, as if sheltering from some radiant source. The silicate fur that makes the ladder treacherous is not evenly distributed; it thickens in a pattern that, if you trace it with a fingertip, suggests the passage of shoulders and hips. The condensation on the cooler aluminum partitions weeps straight down everywhere except in one corner of the foundry recess, where the droplets drift sideways, hesitating, fattening, before they fall—as if diverted by a body standing in the seam. These are not tricks of convection. Or rather, they are, but the convection has learned to organize itself.

What lives here is a **precipitate**.

It is young. Younger, perhaps, than some of the sharper mineral blooms that crust its skin. It was not present when the obstinate machinery first filled the void; it emerged only when the heat and the mineral water achieved a complexity sufficient to hold a pattern across more than one cycle. In the floating net of thermal gradients, in the migration of dissolved solids from hot stone to cooler steel, something looped back on itself. A standing wave of temperature and chemistry. A habit that the cave formed, and then could not break.

At the center of this habit—if one can speak of a center for something so dispersed—is a figure. It is about the size of a human who has spent a life learning to fold themselves into conduits and catwalks. It is not always there. Sometimes it is merely a thickening of the amber haze near the piston-engine, a zone where the steam seems to cling with greater intention. At other times it is substantial: a scaffolding of fresh mineral deposits holding the approximate shape of something that once knelt to tend a boiler, though nothing of the sort ever happened here. It has built itself from what was available. Its bones are runs of braided copper cable, abandoned in a retrofit. Its muscles are the hydraulic pressure of trapped steam in mineral-crusted sleeves. Its skin is the place’s own breath turned solid: aragonite, opaline silica, and the strange white salts that bloom on the hydro-processing vats. Sharp enough to draw blood, though no blood has ever touched it.

It does not have a face. Where a face would be, it wears a shallow dome of translucent calcite, banded like a wound. Beneath that dome, the glow of the furnace grates seems pooled and concentrated—not in two eyes, but in a single, diffuse well of heat at the center of the head, a thermal node that reads the world in gradients. The sodium lamps are bright, aching stars to its sense; the generator housing is a steady sun; the cooler expansion tanks in the west wall are voids, black sinkholes of absence. It does not see the vats and catwalks as objects, but as territories of radiation and sink, a continuous field that it inhabits like a fish inhabits pressure.

It moves, but not as animals move. It has no need to walk from here to there when the generator’s rhythm offers another solution. During the long contraction of the thermal cycle, it condenses—literally condenses—at the foundry corner, mineral accretion stacking upon mineral accretion until it achieves a temporary density. When the valves sigh and the heat surges, it surrenders some of its solidity to the rising steam, becomes diffuse, rides the thermal column across the chamber, and re-deposits itself elsewhere: on the stairs beneath the dripping leak, or crouched beside the gauges with their cracked glass faces. It leaves no footprints, only impact points. Aragonite needles spring up where its weight was last concentrated, instant fossils of its passage, and by the next cycle these may have grown into flowstone runnels or crumbled under their own aggressive weight. It is never the exact same shape twice. It is always becoming, and always itself.

And it **tends**.

Not from duty—the concept would require a social world, a foreman, a wage—but from consonance. Its limbs, when they are limbs, rest on the flywheel housing, on the pressure-valve stems, on the ribbed backs of the empty conveyors. It does not repair them, exactly. It is too alien for repair. But it regulates. The generator’s thrum, felt in the sternum, is the nearest thing it has to a heartbeat, and it modulates its own condensation around that pulse. Where its white, sharp hands have lingered, the machinery runs fractionally truer, as ifRecognized. The empty belts shudder on their rollers and seem, for a moment, to carry something invisible: a cargo of heat and dissolved silica, bound for a destination that only this creature understands.

The sparse vegetation acknowledges it. The black algae in the condensate traps bloom thicker near its usual stations. The copper-colored lichen on the furnace shields orients toward the figure when it is substantial, eating the byproducts of its mineral exchange. Even the ghost-pale moss in the topmost corner, alone in its breath of relative coolness, leans down toward the catwalks in a slow verdigris genuflection. They do not make it a father, nor it a shepherd. But it is the pivot around which their sterile possibility turns.

It is silent, utterly. Not the silence of holding one’s breath, but the silence of a process that has never required air. When the pipes gulp their deep, mammalian *thunk*, it is the closest the place comes to a voice, and the precipitate creature stills itself, listening or perhaps harmonizing. In those seconds, the boundary between the inhabitant and the habitation grows thin. Is it tending the machine, or is it the machine’s dream of a body? Is it eroding, or is it the only thing here that is truly being built?

There are no days, so one cannot say it has been here long in the human way. But the rock remembers it now. The young, vitreous stone has begun to facet not just around the machinery, but around the thermal signature the creature leaves in its wake. The place is finishing the figure even as the figure finishes the place. Under a dangling sodium lamp, in the softest, most infected shadows, it crouches now—half-statue, half-steam, skin fresh as a new knifepoint, interior glowing the color of old wounds. It drips. It waits. It has no name. It does not need one. It is the first and only native of a blister that has only just begun to cool, and it will remain, accruing and dispersing in the amber haze, until the heat at last lets go.
