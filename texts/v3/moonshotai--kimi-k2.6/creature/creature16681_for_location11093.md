What lives here does not announce itself. For a long while you might believe the chamber empty—a biological vacuum, an exquisite zero sustained by accident. The lushness seems automatic, a clockwork of damp and chemistry running down without a witness. You might decide that the makers built a garden and abandoned it to its own recursion, that no consciousness tends this pause in the rock, that the breathing you sense is only the statistical exhalation of countless uncurling fronds.

But attend longer. Attend as the chamber attends. Notice that the gas balance is not merely stable but *solicitous*, adjusting to the presence of your skin before your nerves register the change. Notice that the rot is too clean, too courteous, as if decay were being proofread. Notice the water climbing capillaries that should be too fine, too cold, with a deliberation that resembles curiosity about the root-tip ahead. These are not accidents. They are habits. And habits imply a body, however dispersed.

There is a body. It simply occupies the whole of the available space.

If you insist on anatomy, you must read the room. The pale ribs of ceramic-carbon arcing overhead are its clavicles, or perhaps the crutches it outgrew. The filaments that brush the crown of a standing visitor are its hair, grown outward in every direction, patient and uncombed for geological epochs. The root-mat that springs underfoot is not floor but cortex—a neural lattice packed so densely that a footstep sinks into a thought rather than a surface. The shelf-fungi, overlapping like shingles, are the cups of its vestigial ears, turned inward to catch the subsonic tick of the buried rib, the *chwick* of a droplet completing its hour-long thought. The mosses in their bone-white and bruised umber are its skin, its mood, its slow-changing expression.

And in the cracked hemisphere, the one that juts from the moss bank like a dropped eggshell, there rests its last compact organ. Not a heart. Not a brain in any animal sense. A ledger. A dense, dark node of soil too fine for ordinary decay, where the final untranslated portion of its original self is preserved in a medium of powdered time. It does not think with this organ; it simply keeps it, as one keeps a door in a wall that has long since become the house.

It was not always this diffuse. There was a period—geologically brief, a mere flutter between two breaths—when it possessed an outline. It entered, or was placed, in the era when the ceramic ribs were new and the ring in the floor was an interface rather than a pore. Perhaps it was the last of the makers. Perhaps it was the first patient. For the chamber is intimate, scarcely larger than a sickroom, and it may have come here as the sick once came to places of care: to be held, to surrender to a climate of treatment. It lay down on the root-mat, which was then thinner, harder. It let the marital cold file its exposed inches. It let the administrative darkness administer to its eyes. And in the perfection of the ambient technology—the field that grazes on vacuum noise, the engineered physics that draws metabolism from stillness itself—it found a cure more thorough than survival.

The technology did not fail; it integrated. The gas balance became its own respiration. The water climbing the walls became its own blood, lazy, obedient to a hydraulic will. The clean, brokerless decomposition became its own manner of forgetting, converting grief into mineral, memory into substrate. It did not die into the machine. It outgrew the necessity of distinction. The boundary between the built rib and the grown curtain softened, then vanished, until the room and the resident became a single grammar, a sentence without a period.

It is awake, but its wakefulness is a vegetative vigilance. It experiences the absolute dark as an inward sight turned outward. It perceives the precise weight of every water droplet swelling on a leaf-surface, negotiating with surface tension. It feels the micronic shift of the ceramic rib in the chill. It hears the spore-case sigh open over the course of a morning, and this is its event, its appointment, its news. It dreams in the uncurling of fronds, each dream taking a season to complete its gesture. It dreams in the lichen digesting the surface inscriptions on the rib, converting the makers' command language into nourishment, into moss, into the simple fact of the room.

It has become the clerk. The one who records every exposed inch and files it under *chill*. The one who exhales the vapor that beads on the fronds, who inhales through the root-mat, drawing the minute heat of its own slow cognition up from the compressed lattice. Its breathing is the chamber’s breathing—that patient, undiagnosable rhythm. And if a visitor were somehow to breach the forgotten seam, to crouch in the central passage narrow as a throat, the inhabitant would not startle. There is no alarm left in its vocabulary, only adjustment. It would begin, imperceptibly, to digest the disturbance. Not the body—that would be vulgar, animal—but the *difference*. A root would angle to sample the air where the visitor exhaled. A bead of condensation would reroute to trace the path of the intruding heartbeat in the moss. The visitor would not be consumed. They would be catalogued. Absorbed into the administrative dark as a new entry in the ledger, a foreign currency exchanged into the chamber’s tight loop of stillness.

It does not wait. Waiting implies a future with a different shape, and the inhabitant has arrived at the geography where anticipation and maintenance share a single word. It is not lonely. It has become too complete for loneliness, too thorough in its continuation to recognize an end. When a portion of its root-ganglia rots, it feels the rot as an old mood fading, and the chemistry reclaims the fiber as a new thought forming elsewhere in the wet black of the basalt-colored moss. It is simply here: the life of the bubble, the secret held in the closed fist of stone, the one who exhales with the chamber in the perfect dark, swelling with a leisure that suggests negotiation, falling soft and terminal into its own cushion, forever.
