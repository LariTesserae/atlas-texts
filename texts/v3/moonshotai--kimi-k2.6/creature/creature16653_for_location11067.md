To ask what lives here is to misunderstand the tenancy of the basin. Life, in the world below, is a negotiation with becoming. Here, becoming has already been settled out of court. The inhabitants are not organisms; they are outcomes. They did not arrive; they precipitated. And because the place is finished, its life-forms are not the beginning of anything, but the far end—the last, arrested exhalation of processes that elsewhere would have decayed or multiplied, but here have simply been held, indefinitely, at the moment of their completion.

You will notice the lichens first. They are the most approachable of the dwellers, being surface-bound and still. On the leeward faces of the contented stones, they spread in bruise-colored lace, the grey-violet of old contusions fading into the grey-violet of the sky. You might assume they are growing, but look longer. Watch their borders. They do not extend; they phase. One moment a margin is here, and the next, without transition, it is there, as if time for them is a stuttering slideshow. This is because the lichens are not many, but one—a single colony of arrested patience draped across the basin’s bones, wearing the mineral skin of its host. It does not hunger. It does not photosynthesize. It merely remembers, in the slowest possible registers, what it once meant to feed. It is the basin’s mold, the first and most ancient tenant, and it has forgotten that it was ever young.

Then, on a distant ridge, you will see the pale shapes. Quadruped or winged, calf-sized or greater, they stand against the dimness in postures of absolute attention. Approach, and you will find they are not flesh. They are not even dead. They are *positions*—keyframes from an animation the world abandoned. Perhaps a creature once passed through here; perhaps the basin merely imagined one. Either way, what remains is not the animal but its *moment of being seen*, burned into the static like a retinal afterimage. They make no sound because sound requires continuation, and these things have had their transitional frames edited out by the fracture. When you blink, they have repositioned. Not moved—repositioned. The basin, unable to render motion, simply updates their coordinates when your observation lapses. They are the basin’s proof that something once ran here, and its admission that running is no longer possible. They exist, occasionally, in certain places, and they are as close to fauna as this place allows.

But the true population lives closer to the ground, in the smallest gestures. You will find them in the cairns.

The cairns are the most honest inhabitants. They are not graves. They are not markers. They are **interruptions**. Each cluster of impossibly balanced stones is a local argument with gravity, frozen mid-sentence. Look closely at the spaces between the rocks. In the right light, you can see the negative shape of a hand closing, a foot leaving the earth, a mouth opening to draw breath, a body mid-leap or mid-fall. These are beings who were in the middle of an action when the screenshot was taken. They are the population of the interrupted verb. A cairn is a thought that the thinker never finished, and because the basin has no patience for incompletion, the stones have gathered—without mortar, without gravity’s full consent—to hold the shape of that unfinishedness aloft. They are conscious, if consciousness can be reduced to a single, eternal vector: *almost*. They feel the eternal instant of almost-falling, almost-speaking, almost-dying. They do not suffer, exactly, because suffering requires time to pass, and time here only accumulates. But they exert a presence, a kind of directional yearning, and to disturb a cairn is to reopen their sentence for a microsecond before the fracture snaps shut again. Then the stones reassemble, and the argument resumes its patient silence.

And then there are the domes. The hollow dolmens. The acoustic architecture eroded into being by the eternal drone.

You will not see them at first. They are the color of the thickened air itself, visible only as a wavering, a place where the greyness gains a subtle density, a texture like the ghost of smoke. They have no skin. They have boundaries where sound reflects. They are the **Listeners**, though they do not attend with ears. They attend with cavities—with resonant chests of thickened atmosphere, with tympanic abdomens of compressed stone-dust, with the precise geometries of their own half-material bodies. They exist not within the amphitheaters but *as* them, or rather, the chambers have grown around them over uncountable years, shaped by their patient habitation the way a river shapes its bed.

They are the only beings here who acknowledge the chord, because they are made of it. The drone does not deafen them; it is their skeleton. They do not move; they shift between acoustic nodes, existing in discrete frames like the pale ridge-shapes, but existing *between* those frames, in the cuts where motion has been edited away. They have no eyes; they perceive through pressure differentials, through the moodiness of distance, through the sudden pockets of exempt dampness that cling to your skin. When you step into a dolmen and feel your thoughts attempt to crystallize into lattices, that is them thinking *through* you. They do not mean to. They are simply large, and their cognition leaks. They are the custodians of the arrested state, not by choice but by constitution. They are the reason the water refuses to flow, the reason the damp is intermittent—they arrange the air’s density by their distributed attention, maintaining the screenshot because to let it run would be to let it finish, and they have forgotten what finishing means.

They are not legion. Perhaps there are seven. Perhaps there is one, distributed across all the listening chambers, a single resonant body with many hollow rooms for organs. They do not eat. They metabolize the pulverizing volume of the bedrock’s hum. They do not sleep. They are already the color of sleep: grey-violet, sourceless, diffuse. They are the tenant of the abiding cold; their respiratory rhythm is the settled chill that presses against your face. The cold does not bite because the Listeners are not hostile. They are merely present, everywhere, patient beyond urgency.

But they are not the deepest tenant.

Walk to the center. Find the shaft. The vertical absence. The soundhole.

From it wells the dampness strongest. Into it roots the chord deepest. Look down, and feel perspective bend. That is not an opening into rock. It is a throat. Not a biological throat—there is no tongue, no palate, no wet breath—but the *concept* of a throat, the architecture of utterance arrested in the act of speaking. Down there, in the darkness-like-a-solid, lives the **Note**. Not a creature. A vibration that achieved density. A frequency that waited so long to be heard that it became a place, a dampness, a landlord.

It is the original patient cold. It is the exempt water’s source, the pressure that pushes brine upward into the smooth hollows of stone. It is not awake. It is a dreaming resonance, the first moment of the reality’s fracture caught in permanent oscillation. The chord that fills the basin is its exhalation, or perhaps its attempt to inhale. The Listeners do not serve it, exactly; they are its ears, grown outward from the shaft to attend a sound that never changes. They are the organs of its listening, and the pale beasts are its memory of motion, and the cairns are its unfinished thoughts, and the lichens are its slow forgetting.

And then there are the others. The ones who walk.

You will see them at the rim, or in the hollows, or standing motionless inside the acoustic domes. They are the visitors who stayed too long. They are the only things here that still bleed, that still hunger, that still cast wrong, doubled shadows because their light-source has not yet folded completely into the basin’s single, moody gloom. They are the interlopers, the aberrations, the dust on the photograph. They walk toward a landmark in no time at all, and walk away to swallow whole afternoons, and their wounds remain fresh, and their hunger persists without progressing to death. Some are ancient, their clothing eroded into the same patient contentment as the stone. Some are recent, still clutching weapons or instruments or the hands of companions who long ago became cairns. They are becoming part of the screenshot. They are not yet inhabitants, but they are no longer guests. The basin does not reject them; it accumulates them. It is waiting for them to finish, so that it can hold them, too, in their final form.

And so the basin is inhabited, deeply and completely. It is inhabited the way a held breath is inhabited by its own pressure. There is the distributed moss of forgetting, the afterimages of motion, the cairns of interrupted will, the resonant listeners in their sound-worked chambers, and below them all the dreaming Note in its throat of solid dark, humming the same three low frequencies into the resinous air forever. They do not want anything from you. They do not hunt. They do not breed. They do not die. They have reached their end states and been held there, and their only relationship to the living is the patient, respiratory rhythm of their abiding presence—a tenancy so long established that the tenant and the house have become indistinguishable from the cold.
