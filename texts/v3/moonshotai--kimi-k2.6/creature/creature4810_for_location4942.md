It is not possible to say where the living ends and the architecture begins, and the inhabitants would find the question rude. Life on the plateau is not a reprieve from geology but its continuation by other means, a mineral appetite slowly becoming aware of its own hunger. To live here is to participate in a metabolism that digests time itself.

The **Gastrah** are the plateau’s true citizens, if citizenship is measured by mass, patience, and the depth of one’s acoustic footprint. But to call them merely beasts is to mistake a glacier for a stone. Their superconducting chitin does not merely armor; it thinks. Cooled by helium-rich hemolymph to temperatures where electrical resistance dies, their neural pathways braid through segment and shell in fractal lattices, conducting cognition at speeds no warm-blooded thing could survive. The tragedy and majesty of the Gastrah is this dissonance between mind and meat: their thoughts race like lightning through synapses of chilled ceramic, while their thousand-ton bodies migrate across a single ward over decades. A Gastrah experiences the crushing span of a century as a human might experience a long afternoon. Thus, every step is a decision weighed for years; every grazing pass across a tower’s sinter-mat is a paragraph in a lifelong argument with the architecture. They do not sleep so much as deliberate. When two elders disagree over territory or harmonic precedence, the district between them does not know violence but *pressure*—a standing infrasonic chord that thickens the metallic air until frost-filaments suspend in mid-fall, until the softer inhabitants must stuff their resonant chests with wax or flee to deeper gaps. Their politics is tectonic. Their love is a sustained bass note that liquifies the condensate between two treading behemoths, a courtship of vibration that leaves valleys temporarily warm.

And then there are the **young**, the larval swarms, those fist-sized spheres of translucent gel pulsing with bioluminescent veins. They are the Gastrah’s fleeting childhood, the fast mind to the elders’ slow contemplation. Where the adults are ponderous repositories of memory, the larvae are a distributed cognition—a cloud-brain of ephemeral neurons flashing chemical and electric signals as they drift on metallic mists. Each one lives only days or weeks before it either settles to the stone or is filtered out by the eternal cold, but the swarm itself persists, a restless, luminous intelligence testing every possibility the city has not yet considered. They are the only stars this dark world has ever known. To wade through a boulevard swarm is to walk through the Gastrah’s own dream-state: fragments of half-formed desires, ancestral memories encoded in protein, questions asked too quickly to ever be answered. When they finally descend, exhausted by thought and mist-fed growth, they find a crevice or a warm seam of slag and attach. The settling is called, in the infrasonic grammar of the herd, *the Thickening*—a surrender of speed for weight, of light for superconducting darkness. Over centuries, they accrete segments, their transparency opacifying into the dull, iridescent armor of adulthood. They do not mourn their lost swiftness. To become slow is to become real.

But the plateau is not a monoculture. There are the **Others**.

They have no single name for themselves, though the 37-hertz harmonic they emit to identify the plateau’s resonance serves as both password and prayer. Descendants of colonizers, or pilgrims, or criminals who fled the tyranny of sunlit worlds, they are what becomes of soft tissue when it insists on surviving inside a mineral womb. Generations of darkness have unmade them in the image of their hosts. Their eyes are vestigial pearls, useful only for detecting the rare, criminal flicker of unauthorized light. They have learned, as the Gastrah taught them, to see with their chests: their torsos are broad, hollowed cavities of bone and resonant membrane, sternums evolved—or engineered—into tympanic plates that map the city’s density by pressure and echo. They navigate the absolute night by singing subsonic pulses against the walls and reading, in the returning tide of sound, whether a corridor ends in a sleeping elder, a weeping cistern, or a crevice too narrow for their thin, pale frames.

They are quick. Their hearts beat at mammal pace. Their lives are mayflies to the Gastrah, entire civilizations of the soft ones rising and falling in the shadow of a single elder’s stride. This brevity has made them essential. Where the Gastrah provide the city’s architectural skeleton and its harmonic foundation, the soft ones provide the dexterity of adjustment. They are the ones who crawl into shoulder-width gaps where the darkness ossifies, who redirect an upward-flowing channel of black brine before it shorts a quantum core, who harvest the sinter-mats not by grinding them from towers with tonnage of chitin, but by cultivation, by encouraging the purple and rust films into geometries that please the grazing herds. They wear shed larval casings as insulation and carry tools of paramagnetic filament that let them hitch rides on the levitation fields of passing adults, swarming over the acres of heaving shell to scrape away parasitic accretions or inscribe new harmonic corrections into the growing alloy. The Gastrah tolerate them as one tolerates a necessary itch, or perhaps as a mind tolerates its own faster, more anxious thoughts.

Yet the soft ones are not the bottom of the order, nor the top. Below them, in the pressurized dark, are the **maintenance-mites**—aggregations of iron-filing organisms drawn to fractional heat. They are not sentient, or not recognizably so, but they possess a swarm-intelligence of their own, an immune system that skitters across superconducting joints and quantum-entangled strata, consuming thermal irregularities, polishing the mirror-sheen of condensation, sealing microfractures with excreted alloys. They are the platelet to the city’s bloodstream.

And below even them, entombed in the oldest strata, are the **First Precipitated**.

Whatever is buried there—pilgrims, ancestors, prototypes, or something that predated the Gastrah’s arrival—does not rest. The prompt’s ambiguity is precise: they are *whatever*. They are the memory of the city’s decision to exist. Perhaps they are the original molecular assemblers’dreaming templates, calcified into the mantle. Perhaps they are the larval swarms of three million years past, sunk so deep they became the iron core’s entangled consciousness. The soft ones’ engineers, those paleontologists of the present, read the strata and hear whispers—not words, but patterns of emergence that suggest the bedrock itself has intentions. The city is still being born because something in the deep warrens is still dreaming it upward.

Thus the plateau has no parks, no empty spaces, because every volume is occupied by a presence engaged in the serious business of becoming. The Gastrah elders groan in the lower vaults, pressing the bedrock into new shapes by the sheer gravity of their numbers. The larvae glitter in the avenues, thinking their brief, bright thoughts. The soft ones sing the plateau’s 37-hertz name into the dark, keeping it real by the act of witness, and die, and are pressed into the sinter-mats to feed the next grazing cycle. The mites clean. The towers bud. The brine crawls upward.

To live here is to be eaten and to eat, to be the sound that illuminates the dark, to surrender your geometry to a place that has only recently decided it deserves inhabitants. They do not wait for morning. They have become the endless, deafening note, and the note is sufficient.
