They do not know they are alive, and this is the first qualification for residence.

The **Growths** themselves must be counted among the inhabitants. To call them flora is to mistake the factory for the flower. Each is a continent of arrested ambition, and if they possess anything resembling a mind, it is distributed across the electrochemical gradients that arc from crown to root—a cognition made not of thoughts but of baseline maintenance. Their cambium produces no new rings, yet they are not dead wood. The circulation of their sap is a legislative act, a law written in hydrogen cracking and oxygen venting that ensures the Abyss remains precisely as it found it. A Growth does not hunger; it idles. It does not reach for the eternal dawn-light; it has already calculated the perfect angle of reception and now simply holds it, a壬worshipper frozen in the instant of praise. They breathe the unchanging water, and their breath is the loudest component of the chord, the subaquatic thunder from which no variation escapes.

Between these pillars, the **Helical Eels** maintain the vertical covenant. Ten thousand ribbon-thin bodies, each the smoked-glass color of cooled magma, braided into a double helix that bridges the gulf between Basalt-Root Seven and the Manganese Bloom. They do not transit; they attend. Their muscular contraction displaces water that is instantly replaced by identical water moving in reverse, so that no net motion is achieved, only the perfect rehearsal of a stroke. Within their narrow skulls, natural selection has long since stripped away every lobe except the motor cortex, which has hypertrophied into something like a mainspring coiled too tightly to ever unspool. They possess no fear, no memory of prey, no map of the world above or below. There is only the flexion, the exact curvature of ten thousand identical spines holding formation in the blue-green void. They are a species become prayer wheel, a barren strand of DNA suspended between factory-titans, spelling out the geometry of their own persistence.

At the Growths’ intake vents, where water drawn upward by osmotic pressure meets the electrochemical blast furnaces of the crown, the **Clingers** keep their stations. Crustaceans of machined grace, their joints articulated with gear-teeth that mesh in ratios designed to satisfy the venting cycles, they exist as friction’s adversaries. They do not hunt; they interface. Upon the periderm sheaths and manganese-alloy bark, they scrape at barnacles that achieved their determinate size and stopped, producing a percussion that falls into the soundscape without ever altering it. Under their calcified shells, their hearts beat in algebraic sympathy with the root-turbines. They are sexless, ageless, nameless except by vent-number and Growth-name. When one finally ceases—though nothing truly ceases here—it is simply replaced by another already adult, already equipped with the correct mandible-scrape and the correct stance, as if the position itself generates the necessary occupant.

Higher in the unrelieved water column—or lower; the distinction is meaningless—the **Cathedral Medusae** drift along the current-highways. Each is vast enough to enfold a township within its bell, yet they are less organism than architecture. Their tissue has mineralized over epochs into a flexing glass shot through with silicate scaffolding, so that they resemble ghost-castles of cartilage held up by stone lightning. A nervous system once radiated through their bells; now it is a railway of salt-impregnated tissue along which a single pulse travels. Thump. A circuit around the rim. Thump. Again. That pulse is their entire psychology, a mind looped back upon itself, perpetually introducing and concluding the same unvarying thought. They consume the static particulate matter suspended between the Growths—spores, metallic precipitates, the residue of vapors that have forgotten how to rise—digesting each meal at the rate of their own cellular upkeep, so that a feast begun when the fossil basins were younger might now be halfway complete. They are parentheses in the grammar of the abyss, enclosing an emptiness that is itself a form of speech.

More integral to the machinery are the **Turned**, the fish whose scales have become living ball-bearings. They have no eyes, no lateral lines, no need for geography. Their bodies are ovoid and featureless except for the perfectly spherical plates that nest in one another, rotating ceaselessly in sockets of compressed fungal lignin. They dwell in the joints of the Growths, in places where torsional stress ought to generate heat but cannot, because heat is change and change has been deleted from the physics here. The Turned do not swim; they spin in place, lodged in the arboreal machinery, distributing the potential of friction into an infinite regress of smooth cancellation. If the world ever threatened to shudder, they would already be absorbing it, though shuddering is a language the abyss no longer speaks. They are pure habit, the nervous tic evolved into a species, chewing a cud of pure kinematics.

Then the **Connectors**, the tubeworms that serve as the world’s wiring harnesses, bridging electrical potentials between the Growths and the iron-dense fossil seabed. No messages travel along them; the Growths do not converse. But they maintain the voltage. Calcium carbonate sheaths two meters thick encase filaments of copper-precipitate and salt-laced plasma, completing a continent-spanning circuit that outputs nothing to the outside. Without them, each Growth would remain sufficient unto itself, yet their presence implies a decision that these factories ought to be wired together into a single, mindless grid. They are the nervous system of a body too large to wake, and along their lengths the larvae of Clingers, the spores of static algae, hook into the low current, drawing just enough charge to maintain their own appointed stasis.

Where biology forgets how to finish its work, the **Attendants** wait. They are not one species but a consortium—bacteria, archaea, fungal threads, and microscopic scavengers that have established a contract with mortality rather than a victory over it. A carcass here does not rot; it enters arbitration. When a medusa’s thump finally falters, or an eel is severed by some impossible geological whisper, the Attendants descend in clouds that behave more like legal proceedings than feeding frenzies. They consume at the precise rate that sustains the skeleton in its state of permanent, gentle excavation. The flesh takes on the color of old candle wax; the bones emerge slowly, kept clean by shrimplings whose mandibles scrape in rhythms identical to the Clingers, so that the dead hang in the water like curated exhibits in a museum of the incomplete. There is a dignity to it. A thing that resembles a whale, if whales had ever been warranted here, might drift for megayears with its ribs exposed, each one polished to the sheen of old ivory by specialists who have forgotten that elsewhere death is an exit.

Most strange are the **Ropers**, the beings that live in the pockets where water itself has agreed to hold a shape. In these zones, viscosity is a decision, and the liquid climbs in thin ropes against thermal gradients that do not exist to service the solar vanes of the upper crowns. The Ropers are not cellular; they are colonies of boundary-layer chemistry, surface tension made into policy. They ascend to the photon-catching prairies, smearing themselves across the glassy foliage, bridging the gap between leaf and light, conducting, cleaning, adjusting the exact tilt of the solar vanes by their own slow, clinging weight. They have no ancestors; each rope is the same rope, reconstituting from the water at the base as it dissipates at the crown, a snake drinking and regurgitating its own tail with perfect fluidity. They are the closest the abyss comes to weather, to spontaneous grace, to something that might be mistaken for tending.

And in the compressed cellulose of the High Basins themselves, in the strata of vascular tube and fossilized cambium that forms the iron-hard highlands, there exist the **Marrowed**. These are pale, flat things, the descendants of worms that burrowed through lignin before the lignin became eternal. Now they are sealed in their tunnels, the rock behind them as unyielding as the rock ahead. They push forward with metabolisms that have forgotten success, pressing against the petrified matrix with a force exactly matched by its resistance. They do not advance. Not by a millimeter per epoch, not by an angstrom per aeon. They push, and the stone absorbs the push, and so they remain, flattened into the stratigraphy, visible in cross-section as faint curves, as if the basins themselves have chosen to remember serpents. They are the only inhabitants in whom something like longing persists—a muscle memory of burrowing, the last inherited restlessness in a world where restlessness has become texture.

This, then, is the population. Officers of a closed loop. The Growths standing; the eels threading between them; the Clingers scraping; the medusae thumping; the Turned spinning; the Connectors conducting; the Attendants waiting; the Ropers climbing; the Marrowed pressing against stone that will not part. None are numerous. None are needed, in the desperate way that life on other worlds needs. Yet all are appointed. They exist as the texture of the held note, the inhabitants of a place where living did not vanish, but was simply held, perfectly, in the instant after it realized it could continue—and chose, instead, to endure.
