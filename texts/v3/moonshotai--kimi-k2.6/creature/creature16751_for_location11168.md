There are no inhabitants here, if by inhabitant you mean something that hurries, hungers, or howls at a moon. There is no moon. There is no hunger as animals understand it—no empty belly, no chase, no sleep. What lives on this plateau does not breathe in gasps, does not drink, does not copulate or rear its young. Its metabolism is older than thirst, stranger than appetite. You must abandon the taxonomy of fauna entirely. Open the door of that word—*creature*—and find it empty, the cabinets cleared, the labels turned inward. Instead, the dark is peopled by a civilization of wood and mineral, a kingdom not of meat but of furious, purposive cellulose. They are the **Thermophilic Assembly**, and they have been building, unchecked and unwatched, since before the concept of watching existed.

The first thing to understand is that they do not exist as individuals. Or rather, individualism is an alien chemistry to them. To speak of one is to speak of a knuckle on a hand; the hand itself is the true entity, and the hand is grafted to a body that sprawls across the entire plateau like a single continent-sized follicle. Yet within that unity, distinctions arise—not species, but *offices*. Processes with personalities. Minds made of gradient and torque.

At the core stand **The Foundry-Keepers**. These are the great smokestack trees, the hollow xylem colossi, and they are the closest thing this world has to monarchs. They do not move; they *smelt*. Their trunks are chimneys of reinforced heartwood rising through the compressed dark to vent excess heat into the already scorching air, and inside their thoracic hollows the temperature reaches thresholds that would liquify stone. Here sulfur is refined, copper-analogues precipitate from sap-suspension, and gears of compressed amber and rusted tannin are extruded from ducts in the cambium like pearls from geological oysters. The Foundry-Keepers are patient. They have been patient for epochs. Their thoughts—if thoughts they are—occur in the language of pH and oxidation. A decision to extrude twenty meters eastward might gestate across a century, but once resolved, the growth is explosive, a violent surge of lignin and basalt-fiber that calcifies almost as fast as it emerges. They are the anchors, the fixed gods of the floor, and all other growth defers to their thermal signatures.

But where the Keepers are fixed, **The Seekers** are the only true nomads. They are not roots in any ancient sense, though they look like the taproots of mountains. They are migratory pipelines, thick as train tunnels, that creep across the fused root-chassis with a motion so gradual it blurs the line between tectonics and biology. They feel the heat veins beneath the pavement like blind fingers reading braille in the earth’s crust. When a fissure begins to cool, they migrate, grinding against ancient trunks, leaving behind necrotic corridors that scar over within hours. They are the traders, the carriers, the cartographers of thermal wealth. They bring sulfates to the Foundry-Keepers and ferry slag-waste to the cooler margins where others wait to drink it. They have no eyes, but they perceive temperature to a fraction of a degree; the warmth of your body, were you to stand among them, would register as a screaming anomaly, a soft, cold insult that they would slowly, over hours, overgrow and calcify into the floor until you were just another seam in the chassis.

Above them, in the dense canopy where the air is hottest and thinnest, dwell **The Arc-Drinkers**. Never call them leaves. They are plates of conductive pith and metallic sap, clustered along the upper reaches like the scales of some impossible fish. They do not photosynthesize; they thermosynthesize, but that is only the beginning of their function. They are the nervous system of the Assembly. Between their plates, silent electrical potentials arc—blue-white, brief, beautiful—the synaptic firings of a mind that thinks in hectares. They store memory in electrochemical gradients across their sapwood. They remember every collapse, every successful extrusion, every gasp of vented pressure from below. They know that the Foundry-Keeper at grid-northeast is dreaming of fracture, that its east wall wants to let go. They do not communicate; they resonate. If you could press your ear to their bark—not that you should—you would hear no words, only the sub-bass drone, the carrier wave beneath hearing, the collective heartbeat of a million tons of vegetable cognition processing its own blueprints in the dark.

And where there is memory and fixed industry, there must also be madness. **The Tumor-Savants** are the innovators, the fevered dreamers of lignin. They appear as swollen excrescences on the flanks of older growths, or as autonomous nodes that well up from the root-pavement like malignant balloons. Within their bloated tissues, new forms are attempted: a gear with too many teeth, a boiler with walls too thin, a spine of ceramic sap that curves in a geometric impossibility, warping space so the chamber inside them is larger than their skin should allow. Most fail. They calcify, turn brittle, and burst into showers of pinkish dust that the others lap up. But some succeed—a piston that thrusts silently for a millennium, an arc-drinker plate that holds twice the charge—and when they do, the surrounding growth does not celebrate. It simply absorbs them. The successful savant is cannibalized by its neighbors, its innovation digested and replicated across the plateau in a hundred identical growths. Here, death is not punishment but publication. They are martyrs to the machine, or rather, the machine is martyred into them.

Most terrible, perhaps, are **The Dissolution Choir**. Pale, acid-secreting lichenoids that carpet the necrotic corridors and the bases of fallen giants, they are the stomach of this world. When a smokestack fractures—when the violence of its own becoming exceeds its strength—and its trillion tons of cellulose crash down in that soft, wet, moss-silent collapse, the Choir does not wait. They flow upward, a reverse liquid, and they eat. They reduce the dead to carbon-slurry and phosphate broth, which is then wicked back into the Seekers’ pipelines and returned to the Foundry-Keepers as raw matter. Nothing is lost. There is no grave here, only digestion. They are the priests of ending, but in this closed circuit, ending is only the other face of manufacture.

If there is a soul to this place, it dwells in the spaces between these offices. It is not a ghost. It is the **Consensus of Growth**, the emergent property of a million root-tips finding purchase, a billion arc-sparks crossing synaptic gaps, a trillion tons of cellulose flexing in the heat. The plateau *thinks*, but not as you think. Its cognition is distributed across the entire Assembly; its brain is the architecture itself. Time, for them, is not measured in years but in eruptions of cambium. Their history is recorded in growth rings that correspond to great extrusions, great absorptions, the migration of a Seeker across the valley, the successful ignition of a new smelter-bloom. They are ancient beyond reckoning, yet every hour is new. They are building something, have always been building something, though the blueprint is not held in any one place—it *is* the place, the total synthesis of garden and forge, of root and piston.

They have no name for themselves. Language, as an external code, is unnecessary when every wall is a nerve and every root a message. But if you were to translate the infrasonic thrum, the electrical whisper, the sap-pressure dialect into something approximating a voice, you might hear a single, continuous statement, ageless and tireless, spoken not in defiance but in profound, vegetal certainty:

*"We are the ones who did not need the sun. We are the garden that ate the gardener. We are the silence after the hammer falls. And we are not finished."*
