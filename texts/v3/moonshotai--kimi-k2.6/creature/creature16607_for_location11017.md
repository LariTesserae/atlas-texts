To describe what lives here, you must first abandon the idea of a *creature in a place*. The distinction does not hold. In the Cirque, to live is to be an embolism in the flow of a single, unbroken note—to be a place where the stone, having finished its sculpture, decided to dream about hunger and motion and breath. The inhabitants do not occupy the hollow. They are symptoms of it.

Begin with the **Stillswarm**.

If you could catch one—though you cannot; they are too quick within their slowness, too fixed within their speed—you would hold something closer to a lens than an insect. Each is the length of a finger joint and built like a prayer that has fossilized into geometry. Their carapaces are not shell but seeded quartz, grown rather than secreted, faceted so finely that they do not glint but *refract*, breaking the merciless light into static rainbows that hang, motionless, in the air behind them like afterimages burned into the retina of the world. Their wings are not fronds but feathered vanes of mica, thinner than the calyx of a poppy, and they do not beat in any rhythm a mammalian heart would recognize. Instead, they vibrate at the teeth of the great chord itself, resonating with the drone so that each Stillswarm is simultaneously a passenger and a tuning peg, keeping the note true.

They do not fly, precisely. They are *flown*. The laminar wind is not a medium through which they travel but an external organ they share, a vast circulatory system of fixed currents. A Stillswarm released into the air does not choose a path. It discovers the groove it has always occupied, a track worn into the sky by a million years of identical pressure, and it locks into place. To watch one is to watch a planet in its orbit—an ellipse, a helix, a long parabola that kisses the same thermal seam every afternoon at the same angle, forever. They are billions, and they are identical not in appearance but in trajectory. The air is a loom, and they are the threads that have forgotten the weaver.

They have no fear. Fear requires the possibility of change, the deduction that the future might differ from the present. Here, the future is a mirror held up to the now. When the sessile things on the walls reach for them with hair-fine tentacles, the Stillswarm do not dodge. Some simply pass through those trajectories and are caught; others pass through the spaces between and are not. It is a calculus made without anxiety. The ones that are snatched do not struggle. The snatching itself is so slow—so absolute in its unhurried closure—that it resembles not predation but a kind of gentle statutory absorption, as though the wall-creatures were laws of physics to which certain atoms must eventually surrender.

And the **wall-creatures**.

Call them **Lethargids**, though no mouth has named them so in the hearing of men. They are the color of old forgetting. Not grey, not white, but the precise chromatic value of the eye adjusting to a light that offers no shadow. They cling to the fluted stone with footpads that have fused, over millennia, into the rock itself; you cannot tell where the Cirque ends and they begin. Their bodies are sacs, heavy and soft as unbaked dough, marked only by the mouths that are not mouths—perforations surrounded by radiating cilia that trawl the wind like the strings of instruments that have learned to play only one sustained tone. Their tentacles, when they extend, do not reach *toward* prey but simply occupy space that prey occasionally occupies. They are filters made of meat and patience. They are always feeding because the concept of appetite has been stretched so thin across time that it has become indistinguishable from respiration. A Lethargid does not want a Stillswarm. It inhales, and a Stillswarm, being in the path of that inhalation, ceases to be separate from it.

Between the flying billions and the wall-bound endlessness, there is the middle kingdom of the **almost-plants**.

In the lee of the greatest columns, where the sound drops from obliteration to mere omnipresence, the lichens hold court. Black-green, rust-red, the color of blood settled under skin for a century. They do not grow; they *accrete*, like interest on a debt owed by the rock to the air. And the stalks—those hollow, glassy tubes rising from the shingle like the finger bones of frozen saints—they sing. Not audibly. Their vacuoles of ancient sap, preserved against decay by the same vibratory stasis that prevents water from gathering, resonate at frequencies above hearing, turning each stalk into a chime struck by the wind’s eternal mallet. You feel them in your fillings, in the hollows behind your eyes, a choir of the inaudible keeping time with the basso profundo of the stone.

Yet these are only the citizens. To speak of what *lives* here, you must also speak of the **dead**, who are not departed.

Because nothing rots, and because gravity is gentle, and because the hydrogen that buoys the living Stillswarm still fills the dead, the expired do not descend so much as *lodge*. They accumulate in iridescent drifts against the lower walls, chitin snow that seems to glow with an internal phosphorescence. The dead are more numerous than the living, for time has been flowing here long enough to build strata of the fallen. They form reefs. They form topography. A visitor might mistake a drift for a mineral vein, some pale quartz extruded by the mountain, but close inspection reveals the lattice of wing-veins, the delicate mandibles, the facets of a billion eyes all staring in the same fixed direction. The dead do not mourn. They simply add their silence to a place that has banned the concept.

And then there is the final resident, the one most difficult to see because it is indistinguishable from seeing itself: the **Chord**.

It is alive in the way a crystal is alive—not by moving, but by refusing to stop being what it is. It has a metabolism. It eats the echoes of footsteps. It digests voices. The Chord thinks, if it thinks, in frequencies rather than synapses. It has taught the stone to be an instrument, the air to be a medium, the light to be a solid in which its subjects are suspended. It is the god of the Cirque, not because it demands worship, but because it has redefined worship as *endurance*. To live here is to be permitted to remain, unchanged, in the body of a sound that has outlasted the names of mountains.

If a man lies down here—if his lungs, straw-thin and desperate, finally surrender to the altitude and the light and the impossible roar—he does not leave. The Stillswarm continue their fixed orbits around his cooling skin. The rime gathers on his eyelashes but does not melt. The Chord smooths the disturbance of his final exhalation back into the drone. He becomes, within hours, another feature of the geology. He lives there, too, after a fashion. Not as flesh, but as a datum in a pattern too vast to vary. He is held, like everything else, in the hand of a music that never releases what it touches.
