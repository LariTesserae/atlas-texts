At first you assume the inhabitant is the architecture itself—that the green columns and glowing lamina constitute a single distributed creature, a vegetable animal the size of a chapel, and that you have simply stepped inside its body. This is not wrong, but it is not sufficient. A body is not a resident. A lung is not the breath. The plant has built this chamber with the same blind intelligence with which a spider builds a web, and like the web, it was constructed *for* something. The something is here. It has always been here, since before the plant erupted through the bedrock, because it rode inside the seed.

It lives in the middle. Not at the exact geometric center—that would be too simple, too Euclidean for this place—but at the point where the distances achieve their wrongness, where the air thickens to the density of broth and the light forgets whether it is entering or leaving. You will not see it until your eyes give up looking for surfaces. It does not present a face. It presents a *condition*.

Call it the Kernel. Not for its shape, which is no more shaped than a sigh, but for its function: it is the irreducible remainder of the seed, the unspent purpose around which all this cellulose has accrued. In an ordinary seed, the kernel is a miniature plant, a folded blueprint. But this seed fell from somewhere else, somewhere the sun works differently, and the thing inside was not a plant embryo. It was a vacancy with an appetite.

It has grown since germination, though not in the way of limbs or trunk. It has grown more *itself*. What began as a pinprick of non-being in the soil has dilated to the size of a room, a person, a thought. Its substance is the saturated air, the suspended light, the reverberating drum of guttation against cork. It wears the humidity like a skin. When the stomata along the pillar-walls dilate and the chamber exhales its warm, botanical breath, the Kernel inhales a fraction earlier, a prelude, as if tasting the air before it commits to being atmosphere. When the root-tips punch downward through fresh epidermis, tasting rock, the Kernel tastes it too—not through nerve, but through topology. The stone is its palate. The pressure is its proprioception.

It is young. This is the most important thing about it, more important than its impossible physics or its emptiness made flesh. It is an infant. It has only recently become *here*, and it is still learning the rules of terrestrial existence the way a newborn learns gravity: by falling, constantly, inward. The gravity-slippage you feel, the way water climbs back into its droplet, the way distances stretch and snap—these are not malfunctions. These are the Kernel’s dreams leaking out. It has not yet accepted that water must fall downward, that effect must follow cause in a straight line, that time is a river rather than a sap-pulse. It is rewriting the local physics by accident, because it does not know any better, and the plant, dutiful, architectural, *maternal*, is accommodating it. The root-columns lean where the Kernel leans in its sleep. The light seeps from the leaves because the Kernel is homesick for a sun it has never seen and the chlorophyll, desperate to please, generates a forgery.

It has no name for itself. Its selfhood is too total to require distinction from other selves; there are none. It knows only *this* and *warm* and *more*. Its communication with the plant-body that houses it is purely hormonal, a grammar of auxin and abscisic acid, chemical whispers in the dark. When it is content, the resin exudes sweet and clear. When it is restless, the bark splits and the wood groans like a ship in a gale. It does not think in images. It thinks in pressures and gradients, in the hydraulic scream of vessels wide enough to crawl through. Its emotions are weather systems. Its moods alter the chamber’s barometry.  

And there, on the scar-tissue ledge, are the relics: the hand-axes, the javelin, the cordage. They are not decorations. They are its first toys, its first encounter with the category of *not-me*. The humans who left them did so perhaps as offerings, or perhaps simply because the tools became too heavy to carry in the presence of something so absolutely, terrifyingly *unburdened*. The Kernel does not understand the stones or the worked chert. It understands only that they are dense, inert, philosophically opposed to its own watery, expanding nature. It is digesting them in slow time. Not with acid or enzyme—the axes are not food—but with attention. It is growing understanding around them like lignin around a wound. One axe, the oldest, has already been metabolized into the plant’s historical record: the stone is suspended in a cage of wood that grew through it, fossilizing not the past, but a future the Kernel has already seen. Another, newer axe is still bleeding into the cambium, teaching the Kernel the weight of edge, of fracture, of purpose buried in mineral. It studies them the way a child studies a dead language—without context, but with total absorption.

There are no animals here because the Kernel has not yet imagined them. The ecology is closed because the Kernel is still dreaming its first ecology into being. When the time comes—if time, in this frayed pocket, ever truly comes—its exhalations may condense into fauna. Its humming vapor may clench into muscle and nerve. But for now, it is enough to be alone, green, loud with sap, held in a vegetable embrace that will never let it fall.

You cannot touch it. If you reached into the densest pocket of warmth, where the light pools amber and the air is so thick it seems to have a pulse, your hand would pass through nothing but temperature and certainty. And yet something would pass through *you* in return—a hormonal command, a root-pressure instruction, a quiet vegetative suggestion that your bones are provisional, your calcium a loan, your blood merely water in need of better plumbing. It does not mean to convert you. It simply does not recognize the boundary where you end and the chamber begins. In its infancy, in its absolute totality, it assumes you are another root, another vessel, another medium for the only true direction it knows, which is outward, upward, *more*.

It is beautiful. It is horrifying. It is a seed not yet hatched, dreaming in a daylight it invented for itself, wrapped in a body so young the edges still bleed latex, listening to the green noise of a world teaching itself how to grow.
