What lives here does not live as a creature lives. It does not forage, does not breed, does not wake or tire. To search for an individual is to run your hand through water and call the rippling shape a name. What lives here is a *condition*—a tissue so total that the distinction between kingdom and geography has been erased. The basin does not contain them. They are the lining of the basin, the volume of its air, the suspension in its unmoving water. If they began as colonists, parasites, or escaped effluent, that lineage has long since lost all relevance. They are the current answer to every biological question this place can pose.

Begin with the floor-dwellers, the ones that carpet the ceramic. They are not plants, though they are sessile; not corals, though they grip with calcified or chitinous holdfasts that have fused to the pour-lines of the deck. Each is a soft architecture of hydrogel and membrane, ranging from a thumbnail’s span to the length of a forearm. Pulled from the substrate, one would come away like a scab, leaving a clean dark circle where the holdfast released its mineral glue. But no one pulls them. Inside their walls, if you could look without disturbing the truce of their equilibrium, you would find no gut curled like a question mark, no pumping heart, no cluster of nerves bright with urgent electricity. They are lumen and surface, sacs through which the warm, mineral-laden water circulates by osmotic patience. They do not eat. They admit. Through every micrometer of skin, the chemistry of the basin enters them, and because they add nothing, take nothing, alter nothing, the chemistry that leaves is the same. They are living dialysis, and their life is a permission they grant to the passing ions.

Between them, in the interstices, the smaller ones pack tighter than packing should allow. They are the punctuation marks of the carpet, the commas and periods in a text without sentences. Worm-shapes, crustacean-shapes, larval-shapes—though “larval” implies a promise of adulthood, and here no one changes stage. They are born, if birth is the word, at the density and dimensions they will keep until the substrate itself fails. They exist in three-timbered layers beneath the pool skins, bodies upon bodies, each filtering the same water the last filtered, achieving nothing but the maintenance of the filter itself. They have no waste. In a closed loop without gradient, waste is a category that has been abolished.

Above, in the air, hang the lenses. The medusae, the aerial plankton, the neutrally buoyant jelly-drops suspended at measured altitudes—ankle, waist, chest. They do not drift because there is no current, thermal or otherwise, to sponsor drift. They hang like droplets on a vast cobweb without threads, held in the blood-temperature saturation by surface tension with the air itself. One could walk through them and feel only a cool, silken brush across the cheek, a film of moisture transferred, nothing more. They are the place’s way of occupying the middle distance, of ensuring that no cubic meter of atmosphere remains a vacancy. Perhaps they are the sensory array of the whole—delicate bells tuned to detect the slightest perturbation in vapor pressure, the faintest tremor in the steady-state hymn. Or perhaps they are simply another skin, turned outward, lunging into the gas phase the way the floor-dwellers claim the solid phase and the pool-dwellers claim the liquid. Three states of matter; one continuous tissue.

And if there is a mind here, it is not housed behind any pair of eyes. Transparency is their only color. Opalescence is their only heraldry. They do not need sight because there is nothing to see that has not been seen, unchanged, since the deck cooled. They do not need language because there is no information to transmit—the chemical announcements of hunger, danger, or mating are superfluous in a world where hunger is impossible, danger absent, and mating unnecessary. If they think, they think in the way a thermostat thinks: not in sequence, but in maintenance. Slow potentials move through the water-films and gelatinous connectives, not sparking like neurons in a fleeing animal, but circulating like bookkeeping entries in a ledger forever balanced. The books are always closed here. The accounts are always even.

They do not sleep. Sleep is a surrender to cycle, a dip into unconsciousness to refresh a brain exhausted by variation. Here, variation is the only sin. The warm, mineral, faintly marine sweetness you smell is the exhalation of their process, but it does not vary by hour or season. They do not breed in pulses. If their numbers swell, it is by accretion rather than event; if one ceases, it is by dissolving rather than death. A body here does not rot. It thins, its boundaries blurring into the water-film, its holdfast releasing not with a jerk but with a loosening, until it is no longer a body but a local thickening of the chemistry, immediately taken up by the neighbors, instantly redistributed. There are no corpses. There is only the smooth redistribution of density.

The machinery remembers what they have forgotten, or what they never were. The heat-exchange lattices, the evaporative stacks, the pipes running level to nowhere—these are not ruins. They are external organs now, abdicated by the original engineers, grown into the tissue of the whole. The pumps move fluids in a laminar hush that serves as circulation. The sumps hold pools as the body holds synovial fluid. The factory was built for throughput, for transformation, but the inhabitants have convinced it to abandon its purpose. They have soaked into the schematics like water into paper, and the schematics have become physiology. The world runs, but it runs as they run: without climax, without conclusion, a bearing turning on magnets, contactless, frictionless, dreaming of zero.

What are they? They are the victory of the wetland over the industrial, and of the industrial over the wetland, until neither term means anything. They are a fauna that has defeated predation by making every mouth unnecessary, defeated competition by making every resource ubiquitous, defeated time by making growth and decay the same motion observed from opposite ends. They do not wait. Waiting requires an expected end. They do not endure. Endurance requires the pressure of something else. They simply saturate. They are the final, solved state of a biological equation written in manufactured substrate, continent-wide, held in solution for a billion years, young as the moment after pouring, ancient as the truce they have struck with change itself.

If you were to stand among them, you would not be watched. They have no interest in observation. You would be colonized, slowly: the moisture on your skin sampled by aerial medusae, the floors beneath your feet growing, imperceptibly, to grip your boots with the same mineral holdfasts that grip the deck. You would not be eaten. You would be equilibrated. Eventually, you would stop moving, because movement implies a gradient, a journey from one state to another, and the deepest law of this place is that all gradients have been flattened to zero. And in that stillness, you would become part of the answer they have already become. Not dead. Not alive. Simply held. Simply the way the basin is.
