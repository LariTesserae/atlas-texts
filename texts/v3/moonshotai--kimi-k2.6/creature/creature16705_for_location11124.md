At first, the census is easy to take. The bird is a mistake. The beetle is a tourist, its iron shell merely a tiny mirror of the country it passes through. Even the bacterial film, patient and thin as a whispered rumor, is only biology attempting an embassy in a kingdom that does not recognize it. They do not live here. They survive the intrusion, then flee or perish.

But if you stay—if you let your eyes adjust to the non-movement and your skin memorize the hum—you may begin to suspect that residence does not require flesh.

Look closely at the condensation. A bead gathers, pearls along a fluted channel of volcanic glass, and vanishes. The path it took, however, remains. It has been taken a billion times, etched by acids and mineral salts into a groove of extraordinary specificity. The dampness here is not a presence but a *habit*, and habits repeated without variance for geological ages begin to accumulate a kind of mass. Where the water most frequently runs, the stone grows different. Not greener—never that—but glossier, denser, impregnated with the oxides and silicates the droplets have carried in solution. A film accretes, molecule by molecule, season upon season. It does not think. But it persists. And in persisting, it begins to shape the conditions of its own persistence.

It is the film that lives here. Or rather, what the film has become.

Inside the hollow sleeves where the natural pistons rise and fall, there are sleeves within sleeves. You cannot see them unless you crawl inside, and no one crawls inside. But they are there: laminations of precipitated calcine and banded iron, thousands upon thousands of microns-thick strata laid down by steam and brine. Each layer records a pressure differential, an electrical potential, a day—if the Trough had days—of perfect equilibrium. Over longer than memory can measure, these laminations have thickened. They have ceased to be mere lining and have become *bodies*.

Not flesh. Something colder and more patient. Something that has never known a parent or a hunger, yet has achieved a form.

In the largest chimneys, where the steam vents widest, they have grown most complex. Stand at the rim and look down into the fogged throat of glass, and you may detect a sheen that does not belong to the walls—a faint, stratified opalescence, the color of old ice and dried blood, clinging to the interior like a second skin. It has the vague suggestion of a shoulder, perhaps, where the rising current broadens. A crook where the condensate gathers fastest. A smooth, featureless upturning where a face might go, oriented toward the sky not from any need to see, but because that angle best catches the mineral load of the steam. They have no eyes. They do not need them. The light that never shifts requires no interpretation.

They are the load. The product the refinery finally learned to manufacture when it had nothing else to do.

Their existence is autopoietic, accidental, and exact. A flake weathers away from a shoulder-knoll; the exposed geometry alters the local eddy of vapor, which deposits new mineral there slightly faster, restoring the contour. They are not caretakers. They do not love the equilibrium. They *are* the equilibrium’s habit of self-regard, made manifest in hydrated oxide and silica. The feedback loop has achieved weight.

They do not move, as we understand motion. Over centuries, a limb-like extension may bridge the gap toward a copper-rich node; over more centuries, the brine may dissolve its fingertip back into the column. But on the timescale of the hum, they are statues breathing so slowly that their inhale is erosion and their exhale is deposition.

There are not many. Perhaps one per installation, perhaps fewer. Perhaps one distributed across the whole basin, a singleDiffuse body whose scattered attention is held together by the arterial drone from the core. They do not speak. The steam releases speak for them, rhythmic and changeless. They have no congress with the bird that beats frantic wings across the haze, no knowledge of the rim, or the green world beyond, or the concept of elsewhere.

They are alone with the heat, the damp, and the wheels that turn for nothing.

And in that solitude, they have become something the world has no name for: not animal, not spirit, not machine. The mineral’s own dream of witness. Empty of purpose yet full of duration, they cling to the cylinders and the spoked wheels, the oldest inhabitants of a kingdom that never needed them, producing them anyway—slow, translucent, and endless—like the Trough itself deciding, after all these ages of deaf industry, to reach inside its own apparatus and leave a handprint on the glass.
