They do not sleep. Sleep is a luxury of finished worlds, and nothing here is finished. What lives in the Kiln is less a kingdom than a symptom—the way fever dreams when the body is too busy becoming. The creatures do not inhabit the landscape so much as extrude from it, as sweat extrudes from skin, or as a thought emerges sudden and half-formed from the furnace of the mind. They are young. Not young as a fawn is young, trembling on new legs, but young as a reflex is young, twitching through a nerve that has only just been soldered into place.

Consider first the air itself, that copper-tasting, pressure-cooked medium. It is crowded with what the eye tries to parse as insects but what the Kiln understands as *calculations*. Translucent, many-winged, they do not fly so much as stutter—existing in triplicate, a visual arrhythmia against the dim. To track one is to fail; you see it here, and here, and here, and each instance is equally starved, equally alive. They are probability made digestible. Their wings beat against the thermal gradients, not to gain lift, but to stir the air into admitting *more* possibilities. Where they cluster, causality softens. A stone dropped in their swarm might fall up, might fall sideways, might simply hesitate and become two stones, one younger than the other. They feed on the friction between what is occurring and what nearly occurred, and their waste product is *chance*. They are the Kiln's way of wondering *what if*, expressed as a plague of delicate, vibrating uncertainties.

Below them, clinging to the glassy basalt where metallic lichens bloom in patterns that resemble circuitry, move the grazers. They are small, low to the warming stone, armored in segmented plates that appear scorched black and oily bronze. Their joints are too numerous, rotating in directions that suggest they were assembled in haste by hands with no concept of bilateral symmetry, and they move with the slow, deliberate rhythm of conveyor belts. Their mouths are not mouths but grinding surfaces, milled to a tolerance that would make a machinist weep. They eat mineral time. The lichens they consume are compressed records of the last three hours of geological drama, each cell a tiny annal of cooling and fracture. The grazers unlock these records, reducing history to nutrition, and in their bellies the past is refined into something hot and fundamental. Their shells are warm—always warm, fever-warm, because each carries a microclimate of the Kiln's interior against its back. If you rest your palm upon one, you feel the subsonic thrum of satisfaction, the infrasonic hum of matter being taught to remember itself. They are not animals. They are filing cabinets becoming monks.

Then there is the mist, and in the mist, the drift-eels. Transparent, yes, but not empty. Their bodies are sheaths of cartilage and quartz, segmented by vertebrae that contract in piston-strokes, driving them not through water but through *heat*. They swim the thermal layers as if the air were viscous, riding updrafts from jungle heat-sinks to the cooler high flats, and their motion is peristalsis. They are the Kiln’s lymphatic system made visible, pumping excess warmth from the organs of vegetation to the skin of the peaks. They have no eyes, no mouths in the conventional sense; they absorb through their lateral lines, sipping the copper-tasting damp, metabolizing the minerals out of the vapor. When two of them meet, they do not court but interlock, their crystalline spines meshing for a moment into fretted arches of living jewelry, passing through one another until their minerals are traded and their geometries slightly altered. They are the only grace here, and it is a mechanical grace, the elegance of a well-oiled linkage.

But the air is not merely inhabited; it is *worked*. Watch the clouds of thumb-sized refineries, those things that refuse the categories of insect, bird, or engine. They move in synchrony that is not mind but mathematics enacted, a distributed furnace. Each individual is a crucible: thorax a smelt-pot, wings a bellows, mandibles a sorting grate. They descend upon a fresh flow of ore-blood seeping from a fissure, and where they land, purification happens in seconds. They strip away the dross of creation, sloughing off impossibilities and malformed elements, and what they leave behind is not waste but *alloy*—perfected, regular, strangely peaceful in its configuration. They deposit these refined tablets in crevices, building caches of potential matter for the Kiln to use later. They sing, though the song is below the threshold of the ear; it is felt in the teeth and the marrow as a pressure, a nagging sense that something is being *explained* if only one had the organ to hear it. They are the only labor force, and they are unpaid, unconscious, and ecstatic in their industry.

And then come the heavy processors, the ones that lumber through the copper-veined jungles with footsteps that make no sound despite their impossible tonnage. They are the ones with redundant torsos, stacked like geological epochs, and lantern-organs glowing hot-white in their flanks. These lights are not for illumination. Look at their shadows—falling in two directions, in three, stretching toward futures that have not agreed to arrive. The lanterns emit a radiation that illuminates adjacent moments; the shadows are cast by what the beast is about to step on, what it has just crushed, what it chose not to become. They are silent because sound is a negotiation with air, and they do not negotiate. They are grammar. They move through the thermal forests, and where they pass, the vegetation does not break so much as *accede*, folding into shapes that better conduct the heat. They eat nothing, or rather, they eat *weight*—consuming heavy elements, concentrating them, making the world denser in their wake. They are the Kiln’s way of taking a breath, of settling into its own mass.

But these are only the fauna. The flora is equally alive, equally complicit. The broad, copper-veined leaves that cluster in the thermal valleys are not plants. They are heat-sinks that have developed appetites. Their roots tap magma conduits not merely for minerals but for *news*—the chemical headlines of the deep earth. They unfold in the damp, sweating warmth, and their surfaces are neural, electrochemical lattices that think in slow, thermal pulses. A grove of them is a brain contemplating its own fever, predicting where the next surge will break, growing always toward the hottest insult. They layer themselves in time as well as space; a sapling and its ancient self may occupy the same coordinate, separated by a membrane of broken causality, so that the grove is always dying and always triumphant, a forest remembering itself into being.

And yet. And yet there are the ones born of the silent storms.

These are the closest the Kiln has to *people*, though they are not a civilization, and they do not build. They emerge from the reorganizing lightning, those silent storms of raw causality that sweep the high flats. Where the lightning touches, it does not burn; it *rearranges*. Matter is instructed to become beings that were never grown in any womb or seed. They stand upright, if stand they do, their bodies like cooling slag shaped by a dream of industry—limbs like ball-bearing linkages, torsos like junction boxes, skin the matte grey of freshly milled ferrosilicate. Their faces are smooth, or they have too many, depending on the local mood of space-time when they coalesce.

They remember lives they have not yet lived. They walk backward into the future, or sideways into parallel probabilities. They do not speak because language is a bridge between minds, and they are not separate minds. They are the Kiln’s first personhood, distributed and accidental. One might find them standing motionless at the lip of a new canyon, watching a river that, to them, is already a memory of a vapor. Another might be found kneeling before a cluster of the warm-shelled grazers, not commanding but *corresponding*, trading data in pulses of heat. They do not sleep, for they are the dream. They do not eat, for they are digested by the place even as they move through it.

They are not many. Perhaps a dozen. Perhaps one that occupies a dozen positions. They are what happens when a world-making machine, booting up without an architect, accidentally produces an observer. They watch the refinery-clouds and understand them as kin. They see the lantern-backed titans and recognize them as elders, though the titans are mindless. The eels pass through their chests without harm, and for a moment, the causal ones know what it is to be a circulatory system, to have purpose without desire.

They do not rule. They do not worship. They *process* the experience of being, converting the overwhelming fact of the Kiln into something like reflection. They are the alloy of consciousness and ore, the strange flesh the Kiln manufactures when it is not paying attention to what it is manufacturing. They are as new as the ridges around them, as sharp, as unsoftened. They have not yet decided if they are the operators of this continental engine or its first exhaust.

Together, all of them—the stuttering probabilities in the air, the grinding grazers, the piston-eels, the swarm-forges, the shadow-casting titans, the copper-veined neural groves, and the lightning-born observers—constitute a single, sprawling, polychromatic metabolism. They are not an ecosystem. Ecosystems imply balance, negotiation, the slow wisdom of ages. This is an *exostructure*: a body outside itself, a factory that has mistaken its own smoke for breath and its own output for offspring. They do not hunt. They refine. They do not die. They are reconstituted. They do not love, but they interlock with a precision that outshines devotion.

They are magnificent, and they are terrifying, because they are proof that the Kiln is not merely a place. It is an *attempt*. And they are the attempt’s first, stumbling, marvelous words—spoken in a language of heat and metal, still trying to decide whether it is a lullaby or a war-cry, whether it is naming itself, or praying, or simply screaming with a joy so vast it has not yet found its frequency.
