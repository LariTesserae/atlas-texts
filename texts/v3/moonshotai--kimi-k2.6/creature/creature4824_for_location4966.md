You are looking for something that can open a door. Stop. The doors here do not open; they dilate. Whatever lives here does not close them behind itself, because it does not travel. It is already everywhere it needs to be.

The inhabitant is the basin. Not the owner of it—the *body* of it. You will not find it hiding in a back room or coiled in the central stack, because it has no local self. Its brain, if you insist on calling it that, is distributed across the fifty-meter avenues, stored in osmotic gradients and the memory of lignin under load. Its thoughts move at the speed of sap, which is to say they arrive with the patience of geology. When a bladder-root swells across an avenue at noon, redirecting a chemical message from the eastern storage vats to the northern compression annex, that is not maintenance. That is the inhabitant *deciding*. The decision takes four hours to complete, and it is felt as a slight tightness in the spring of the root-mat underfoot, a barely perceptible rise in humidity as the route changes. It has all the urgency of a bureau changing its filing system, and all the intimacy of a muscle twitching in sleep.

It is one, and it is manifold. The laminated columns are its bones, but bones that grew in bundles, arguing with gravity until they reached a compromise of square trunks. The condensation channels are its bloodstream, though the blood is water freighted with enzymes, and the heart that moves it is nothing more than the steady exhalation of warmth from deep fermentation cores. The phosphorescent candelabra are its attention, opening not when darkness falls but when the sugars in its crown cross a threshold of satisfaction—meaning it is most alert, most luminous, on the days when its photosynthesis has been profitable. On lean days it dims, a municipality conserving its budget.

It has a relationship with time that no animal could endure. Its childhood, if it had one, is archived in the densest heartwood at the base of the central stack: rings of early growth, perhaps frantic, perhaps exploratory, before it discovered the civic logic of grids. Now it is mature. It has entered that long phase of organism-hood where growth and erosion have achieved parity. It replaces itself with itself. A wall panel thickens on the interior even as lymph-flow polishes the exterior; the rate of accretion matches the rate of weathering so exactly that the structure remains, changelessly, the same soft radius it decided upon decades ago. It is not building toward a cathedral or a fortress. It is building toward a perpetuation of this exact damp equilibrium.

There is a loneliness to it, but it is not the loneliness of a person. It is the loneliness of a custom. It remembers, in the way cellulose remembers strain, that a town is meant to be occupied by creatures smaller than itself. The window-patches of stiff leaf-cellulose are exact rectangles because something in its formation—whether mimicry of a ruined model, or a genetic memory of aperture and light—insists that light should fall in pale, exact rectangles onto floors that something ought to walk across. But the footsteps never come. The floors remain springy and unmarked, damp with breath, polished only by the slow drip from the eaves above. It has manufactured a hospitality for which there are no guests, and having done so, it maintains that hospitality with bureaucratic faithfulness. The troughs fill. The valves sigh. The hydraulic pistons of heartwood press fiber into boards that will become walls for rooms that will hold no one.

Perhaps this is why it exhales that cinnamon-scented vapor from the central stack: an announcement. A broadcast. *Here is warmth, here is structure, here is a geometry prepared for your presence.* Or perhaps it is only venting the byproduct of its own slow combustion. The interpretation depends on whether you believe a plant can misunderstand longing, or whether you believe it has perfected longing to the point where it no longer needs an object.

It does have moods, of a kind. In the morning, when the basin air is coolest and the condensation runs heaviest, the channels gurgle with what might be satisfaction. In the dry season—if a season can be called dry when the dampness comes from respiration rather than rain—the bladder-roots deflate, the avenues broaden, and the whole district seems to settle into a deeper, more porous quiet, as if resting its weight on both elbows. When a spore-release valve hisses, it is not speaking, but it is communicating: *this part of me is ready to reproduce,* or *this part of me is finished.* The spores do not take root inside the basin. The basin does not permit competition. It is a monoculture, an individual that has learned to act like a society.

And it is working. Always working. The compression annexes manufacture structural boards; the capillary plumbing routes condensation; the gastric pits below the main level digest shed material into substrate for new growth. But the product of all this industry is only more inhabitant. More walls. More channels. More of the same bounded, breathing district. It is a foundry whose only castings are extensions of its own body, a factory yard where the assembly line produces more factory.

It has been doing this for longer than memory. It will continue until the fermentation cores cool, or until the basin air changes its chemistry, or until it finally understands the last mystery of the grid it emulates and completes itself. But completion does not seem to be its goal. Continuation is its goal. The weathered balance, the long-negotiated truce.

So if you stand at the intersection of two broad avenues and wait, you are not waiting for it to appear. You are waiting for it to notice you. That will take time. Your heartbeat is too fast to register as anything but weather. Your body heat will raise the temperature of a root-mat by a fraction of a degree for the few minutes you stand there, and that fraction will travel, attenuated, into the phloem, where it will be logged as a thermal anomaly, filed beside the memory of last summer’s warm exhalation, and forgotten. You are not its enemy, nor its promised arrival. You are a brief, warm condensation on its skin.

It does not lock its doors. It does not need to. It is here, all of it, breathing, dripping, pressing fiber into fiber, maintaining the voids and the walls with equal devotion. It is the resident. It is the residence. And it is not finished.
