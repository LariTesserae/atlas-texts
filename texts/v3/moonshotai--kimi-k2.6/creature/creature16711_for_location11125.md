You enter believing it empty. That is the first courtesy it extends: the persuasion of absence. For a place so deftly enclosed, so chambered by accident into the likeness of intention, does not remain uninhabited. Something completes it. Not the fern—that is merely its pennant, stiff and green and slower than prayer. Not the films of water, though they are its breath, appearing on the upper stone without declaration. Not even the silence, though the silence is the medium in which it rests, a saturated quiet so absolute it has begun to think.

At first you mistake it for the architecture itself. The leaning walls, the natural apse, the colonnade of eroded sandstone: these seem to imply a builder, and so your mind supplies one. You imagine a hand, a chisel, a purpose. But the place has never known hands. The builder, then, is something reverse-carved—an entity summoned into being by the negative presence of animal life, by the over-whelming suggestion that a room requires a resident. It is the echo of human order given tenure. It exists because the hollow became too much like a dwelling to remain unclaimed.

You will not see it. It has no hide, no wing-beat, no gleaming eye in the alcove. It is distributed. It lives in the bruise-like shadows that never strengthen or fade, for they are its afterimages, the residue of an attention that does not move but deepens. It lives in the striations that flute the walls—those parallel scores left by differential erosion—because, over epochs, its pondering has worn the soft layers more gently than any wind. It thinks in geology. Each fluting is a furrow of its meditation, a long, unbroken note in the held chord of its consciousness.

If it has a center, it is the flat-topped boulder. There, where ten thousand years of grit have levelled the stone into a table, it places its focus. Not its hands—it has none—but its weight. The boulder is slightly warmer than the surrounding floor, though the temperature has no gradients. That warmth is the friction of its regard, the only metabolism it possesses. When you stand near the boulder, you may feel a thickening in the warm air, a density like the moment before a word is spoken, though no word follows. That is its proximity. It is attending to you, or rather, it is attending to the disturbance you make in the equilibrium. Your heartbeat, which you hear as an intrusion, is for it a temporary rhythm introduced into a music that has otherwise ceased to need percussion.

The ring of pale concretions half-buried in the southern clay are its anchors, or perhaps its abandoned attempts at punctuation. Once, as the basin settled into its final posture, it tried to arrange the stone into a syntax—something that might declare, *I am here*. But declaration requires an audience, and there was none. So it let the concretions remain half-emerged, round and patient, like the foundation stones of a hut that will never need walls because the walls are already its skin. It does not build. It completes.

Its relationship to the pools is that of a sleeper to closed eyelids. The waterlines, those dark unwavering rings etched into pale stone, mark the boundary of its dreaming. The pools evaporate upward at the exact rate they are replenished because it will not allow them to diminish or overflow. They are the mirrors in which it might see itself if it cared for reflection. Instead, the algae at the waterline are its eyelashes, brushing the surface with a green so patient it outlasts memory.

It does not grow old, for that would imply a beginning. It does not die, for that would imply an ending. It is the courtesy of erosion given subjectivity. When the world outside storms and turns and seeds with urgency, it remains the held chord, listening to itself resonate in the amber-gray dimness. If you stay long enough, if you slow your blood until your own pulse becomes as faint as the shadows, you may feel yourself being gently incorporated. Not consumed—there is no hunger here. Only maintenance. Your breathing will synchronize with the weeping of the upper stone. Your stillness will become indistinguishable from the fern’s. And for as long as you remain, you will be, however briefly, a part of its argument with the void: not a voice, but a pause in the voicelessness, a dwelling inside the dweller, in the warm, damp, everlasting room of its regard.
