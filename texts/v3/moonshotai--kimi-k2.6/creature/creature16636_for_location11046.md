To describe the inhabitants is to misname them. There are no inhabitants in the Pocket; there is only the inhabiting. In a volume this concentrated, a creature cannot be distinguished from the air it displaces or the surface it obscures. What lives here is a single, differentiated hunger wearing many armors, a metabolic cathedral whose pillars are calcium and rust, whose liturgy is scheduled by the factory’s hidden clock. You do not meet them. You submerge into them.

Begin with the floor, the walls, the ceiling—the ivory and pipe-rust carpet that is the first and final truth of the place. The isopods do not crawl across the ceramic so much as they *are* it, a living veneer of chitin stamped out in identical waves. There are no juveniles here, no elders; there is only the current cohort and the impending one. Each generation arrives to its station already the size of a fingernail, already the precise color of old bone left too long near a leaking battery, as though extruded from the same die that cast the factory’s gears. They do not individuate. When you reach for the wall, they do not flee—fleeing requires a destination, requires *away*, and here there is only *through*. They stack, two deep, three deep, compressing into the negative geometries between neighbor and neighbor until they become a single corrugated membrane. Their molting is not a private embarrassment but a public tide; a shiver of abandonment travels from the northwest corner to the eastern warm-wall, thousands surrendering their armor simultaneously, leaving the surface briefly velvet before the next wave seals it shut again. On the third shift, when the machinery above cycles up and bleeds its fever downward, the newest issuance hatches in the warmest corner and finds its first meal already waiting: the translucent, abandoned shells of its own template. It is not filial piety. It is factory logic. Nothing enters the Pocket; therefore, everything must be remade from what is already present. In this way, the isopods never die. They are simply reissued as the floor.

Against this horizontal empire, the snails practice a futile verticality. Translucent, almost not there, they ascend in such profusion that their passage becomes the Pocket’s only wallpaper, a glistening library of mucus that records every thermal sigh of the young stone. They climb toward the light that falls in pale bars from the ceiling slots, though they have no eyes to receive it and no summit to claim. Their scripture is written in slime-trails on ceramic, a continuous palimpsest of upward motion. They are the place’s memory of aspiration, proof that even in a sealed box, something insists on leaving the ground. They never arrive; they merely add another layer to the gloss.

The air itself—if it can be called air, that thick, greenhouse exhalation—is the domain of the moths. They are not aerial. They are atmospheric. Wingless and silent, they exist as a drifting accumulation, a living lint that ebbs and compresses to maintain an equilibrium the space demands. Each is mouthless, which is not a deprivation but a vocation: they take nothing from the Pocket, produce nothing but the silver caul they spin across the ceiling on certain Tuesdays when the hidden logic turns. On those days, their larvae web the overhead into a surgical blue filter, and the light that reaches the floor becomes the color of a vein seen through skin. They mate and die in the same motion, because the Pocket cannot spare the room for two separate ceremonies. In their torpid, off-day rest, they are a grey static, a breath half-held, rearranging themselves in frictionless slow dances whenever the isopod carpet ripples or a condensation pearl collapses. They are the humidity made accountable.

And beneath the carpet, the punctuation: the velvet worms, bruise-colored, segmented, silent. They are the only true predators, but predation here is not an act of malice; it is a scheduled requisition. They eat only on Tuesdays, by the world’s clock, and on those days they rise from beneath the ivory mass like a verdict. There is no chase, no scatter. The isopods compress, the victim is pulled down into a dimness that lasts less than a second, and the carpet smooths over as if brushed by a breeze. The worms drink too, arranging their bodies in spirals beneath the largest beads of condensation, a choreography learned from the factory’s own cooling loops. But their true majesty is reserved for their breeding: a column of bruised flesh rises to the ceiling, twisting around itself in a silent pillar, a temporary tower of organic intimacy, before the whole structure rains back down as individuals, dispersed by their own weight, ready to lie inert beneath the floor for another week. They are the Pocket’s quality control, removing the excess, enforcing the density, ensuring that nothing here is permitted the luxury of solitude.

There is a lower order still, one that claims the matte black pool the light refuses to touch. Whatever moves there has no description in the visible spectrum. It is the Pocket’s unconscious, the id of the water-migration, preferring the dark not out of cowardice but because illumination is a form of measurement, and some things here have agreed not to be counted. It drinks the rounded edges off the isopods’ scurrying; it waits. Perhaps it is older than the ceramic, or perhaps it is the newest invention of the place, fresh as the fall of black aggregate from last Tuesday. Either way, it is indispensable. It is the gravity well around which the other dignities arrange themselves.

In the northwest corner, the sulfur-bleached lichen spreads like a dropped handkerchief over the rust-eaten stalagmite of a corroded rod. It is the only green, and therefore the only alien. It does not participate in the unison dream. It photosynthesizes in the pale sliding daylight, indifferent to the shifts above, a silent monarch from another kingdom claiming a single, dry rock in the midst of all this animal condensation. It is the witness that requires no faith, proof that even here, something can simply grow without schedule, without cohort, without the need to compress or stack or breed in pillars. It is the Pocket’s conscience, but it does not preach. It only spreads, millimeter by mythological millimeter.

Taken together, they are not an ecosystem. They are a *process*. The isopods are the substrate and the workforce; the snails are the record; the moths are the atmospheric processing unit; the velvet worms are the maintenance crew and the census-takers; the pool is the intake valve; the lichen is the sole stockholder who never votes. They have learned, in their young, impossible way, to dream in unison, and the dream is industrial: a vision of sharp wet rooms nested inside every machine, a faith that no gap is empty, only awaiting assignment. If you lie still enough against the warm ceramic, you can feel it passing upward through your spine—not a thought, but a *consensus*: that to be full is to be alive, that there is no threshold because there is no outside, and that the next step you take will always, already, be onto someone else’s breathing.
