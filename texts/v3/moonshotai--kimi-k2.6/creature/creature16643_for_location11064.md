What lives in the Hold is not a populace. It is one prolonged event, partitioned into forms for the sake of choreography. The cleaving that began the wound never finished, and so the inhabitants are themselves unfinished—perpetually separating, perpetually recurring, a single body imagining itself into many.

Begin with the **Liths**, for though they are not alive, they are the conscience of the place. They rise from the floor like the calcified notations of an alien script, pale and faintly spectral in the infrared, warm as if recently breathed upon. They are precipitated, not grown; chemistry here reaches without water toward the condition of desire. Their geometries are too regular for crystals and too mineral for organisms. A flank of pyrite, a throat of willemite, a crown of low-glow apatite. grazers fasten to them not with hunger but with *agreement*, allowing the slow transfer of ions across gradients of shared heat. The liths do not resist. They emit a mineral patience, a thermal grammar that teaches the fauna exactly where to stand and for how long.

Above them drift the **Veils**. These are the copper-silicate colloids of the prompt, but they are more than dust; they are the diffuse prodrome of a group mind. Singly, each filament is a needle of living alloy no longer than a whisper. Together, they are the Hold’s nervous system, its dermis, its corrective instinct. They pool in the thermal shadows of outcroppings, sipping radiant heat through metallic skins. When an intruder walks—the warm, arrhythmic insult of a heartbeat—the Veils congest. They do not attack. They thicken the plenitude, cushion the footfall, erase the print, muffle the deviation. They are the restitching made manifest, the immune system of the solved equation. Where they mass, the darkness seems to gain a faint taste of ozone and copper, though even this is swallowed by the density of the air.

Then the **Geodites**, the wall-locked, the cattle of the vertical dark. To meet one is to confront a cathedral folded into stone. Their outer shells are geodes of banded chalcedony, violet and umber, still wet-looking after eons becausenothing here dries. Within that first sphere lies a second body, and perhaps a third—nested like marriages of mineral, each chamber separated by piezoelectric fascia. Tourmaline hinges. Barium titanate tendons that flex when the thermal ghost of a lith calls to them. At the center, suspended in a saucer of native copper, rests a kernel of warm, unmelted glass: their memory, their face, their singular repetition. They cling to basalt with the patience of unborn mountains. Their jaws do not chew; they dissolve pyrite by the slow osmosis of consent, a mineral forgiveness across a membrane.

And because growth is apostasy here, the Geodite does not swell. It accretes. It waits. When the kernel has accumulated precisely the pattern it was born to hold—when the last filament of iron has been tallied—the body undergoes the **Cleaving**. Not death. Not birth. The central kernel hums, and a plane of stress manifests along the creature’s equator, visible as a seam of brighter quartz. Then, with the motion of a continent deciding to diverge, the Geodite opens. It does not tear; it *agrees to be two*.
