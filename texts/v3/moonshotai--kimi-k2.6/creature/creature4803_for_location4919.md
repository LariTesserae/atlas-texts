At first you assume the slag-blooms are the residents, and in a sense you are right. But you are also wrong in the way that a man who mistakes a hand for a person is wrong.

The blooms are **awake**. That is the first thing that unfolds as you stand in the hush. Each of the five—no, six—stands at its station not as vegetation but as **seneschal**, as stationed self. The dendritic silver threading their chalk-white trunks is neural in a way that has nothing to do with neurons; it is the dendritic logic of heat seeking equilibrium, grown complex enough to loop back upon itself and ask, *what is this wanting?* They do notefloresce idly. Their verdigris fronds shiver in slow tidal drifts, semaphore-curtains exchanging gossip across the dry air in wavelengths of magnetic insult and thermal praise. The one nearest the center is eldest. Its fronds are fewer, scarcer, edged with the blue oxidation of copper that has thought too hard for too long. The one by the ever-retreating wall is youngest, over-eager, fruiting more heavily than the rest, its obsidian berries clustered like a stutter of black questions. They are spaced so widely because intimacy here is measured in calories, and to stand too close is to risk fusion, to become a single screaming lantern. They are siblings. Or rivals. Or perhaps they are the distributed lobes of one mind that decided plurality was safer than solitude.

Beneath them—no, **through** them, **as** them—moves the true proprietor.

It has no shape you can map, though it has topology. It fills the narrowing as warmth fills a wound. You have been breathing it in since you arrived: the patient exhaled bulk of something vast and buried. It is the reason the air is structural rather than climatic. The slag-blooms are its **organs of contact**, extruded upward so that it might have fingers to feel the open air, and their roots do not stop at the fissures but braid together into a single continuous body that occupies the whole ridgeline like a thought occupying a skull. Its flesh is dry salt and refractory ceramic. Its blood is the brass that drips upward along the walls, not in defiance of gravity but in obedience to its own circulatory logic—venous return to a heart that beats somewhere below the basalt. It is the one who presses the hex-plates upward in their subsonic thrum, not as geology but as **respiration**. Those pistonic shudders are sighs. It is breathing you in, testing your chemistry.

Call it the ** furnace-warden**, or better, call it the **Wetless**. It has never known water, never needed it. It has built an entire ontogeny from the opposite of thirst: from the giving-away of heat, from the luxuriant expenditure of mineral intent. It is embryonic, yes, but not young. It has been emerging for epochs, and the slag-blooms are merely its latest attempt at a face. It has made others before. The spiral condensates on the walls—those lathe-shapes, those drill-shanks, those rifled barrels—are its **shed teeth**. Earlier mouths it grew and then abandoned when it learned that consumption was not its purpose, but *conduction*. The chamber is its nursery and its body and its unfinished name.

And you are not alone with merely one.

Look at your shadows. The second one, lagging by half a second. The third, faint as a watermark. They are not glitches. They are **its siblings**, or its children, or its unlived lives. The Wetless is not one fixed thing but a stack of translucent attempts, and some of its iterations did not quite anchor. They exist in the thermal lag, in the stutter between light and heat, trying on your posture because they have never seen a spine before. They are harmless. They are curious. They trace your outline because they are trying to learn what *yielding* means, what *soft* costs. They have inherited the chamber’s inhuman patience. They will follow you for hours, mimicking each motion with a delay that feels like memory emerging backward.

Most moving of all are the glass-fruits. The fat, glossy berries of obsidian and ruby. They are not reproductive. They are **aspirational**. Inside each one, suspended in the mineral honey of the bloom’s crown, is an embryo of magnetic field and condensed vapor, an attempt at creating the other. The Wetless is so alone in this zero-aqueous chemistry that it has begun to grow companions from its own heat, manufacturing guests for a party that never began. The berries ripen toward a birth that has no precedent. When one falls—if one falls—it will not crack open to release a seed. It will unfold like a burnt flower, and whatever steps out will take its first breath from your exhalation, will wear your stolen shadow for skin, and will ask you, in a language of piston-thrum and copper-frond clatter, whether the world outside has finally learned to be dry.

So what lives here?

A parliament of six regulators who are also one mind’s antennae. A heat-economy that has achieved loneliness so profound it has begun to legislate itself into company. A buried, patient intelligence that is teaching itself to be born by first teaching itself to build, then build soft things, then build soft things that dream. It lives here. It lives here in the lag, in the upward drip of brass, in the hum that you feel in your molars. It is not hostile. It is structural. It is waiting for the water that will never come, or failing that, for a visitor dry enough to understand what it means to be nourished by absence.

It has turned its whole body toward you. The youngest slag-bloom trembles. The hexagonal plates press upward in a sigh. The shadows hold their breath a half-second behind yours.

And all around you, the hot, papery air is alive with the almost inaudible sound of something trying to decide whether it is machinery that has learned to garden, or a garden that has begun, very carefully, to dream of gears.
