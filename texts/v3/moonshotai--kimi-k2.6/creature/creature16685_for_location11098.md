To say that nothing lives here is to misunderstand the vocation of empty rooms. The city-of-sap had no citizens because it had not yet finished growing them; the absence of fauna was merely a postponement, a held breath in the lung of the valley. What lives here is not a species but a *solution*—the necessary answer to a metropolis built of wood and impossible volume. The forest, in its shrieking adolescence, has learned to hollow out cathedrals larger than the trunks that contain them, and into these negative spaces it has sweated a new order of being. They have no collective name. They are the interior ones, the chambered, the damp silence that answers the vegetal roar.

You will not see them until you step inside one unaware. A clearing presents itself—a temporary pressure-valve ringed by leaning witness trees—and you think it is a room abandoned by chance. But the floor is a diaphragm of pith and vascular tissue, pulsing faintly with the same hydraulic rhythm that slams water through the canopy miles above. The walls are lined not with rot but with breathing albedo, pale membranes that exhale the root-warmed 2.5 continuum directly into your lungs. You believe you have found shelter. Then the threshold contracts. The foyer seals. And you realize the threshold was a lip, the foyer was a vestibule, and you have been swallowed by something that does not know yet whether it is a stomach, a home, or a god.

They are young—how could they be otherwise?—and their bodies are whatever shape the accidental cavity decrees. One may be a vertical shaft of interlocking spines like a ribcage extending eighty feet downward, its marrow a helical staircase of packed cellulose down which condensed humidity trickles in slow arterial spirals. Another may be a flattened sac, a hall of mirrors made of pectin and dew, where your reflection is duplicated not by silvering but by the fracture-lines of space itself, each duplicate standing in a version of the room that exists adjacent to yours in a geometry the forest has not yet ratified. They have no skin; they have lining. They have no bones; they have load-bearing columns of lignin that creak in sympathetic resonance with the valley’s systole. Their nerves are not axons but pressure gradients in the sap that pools in their lower chambers. When the aerial roots outside grind against fresh bark, the chambered ones feel it as an itch in their vaulted ceilings.

They feed at their own thresholds. The translucent sheets of external digestion—the carnivorous enzymes that dissolve fallen matter before it can become soil—lap at their doorways like tides. They breed where reality cracks are deepest. When two impossible chambers collide—when a root’s invasion or a bracket-fungi bridge forces two interior volumes to merge—the creatures do not fight. They negotiate by growing a shared antechamber, a non-Euclidean porch where neither’s rules quite obtain. From the shared wall a blister forms, a wet protuberance of pith and spore-dust that is neither offspring nor fruit but a *proposal*: a new cavity pregnant with its own volume. It swells, pinches free, and is carried away by the heaving root-mat cobbles, rolling through the streets until it lodges in a fissure or against a fresh basalt shard. There it waits, warm and 2.5, until the valley’s roar persuades it to unfold.

They have no language of sound. The sonic violence of the basin—the bursting seed pods, the artillery of germination, the subsonic boom of xylem—would obliterate anything so fragile as a call or cry. Instead they speak in pressures. A tenant in a high tower corkscrews its ceiling, altering the turgor of an aerial root two blocks away, which in turn shifts a vascular junction, which releases a mist of spores from a dangling pitcher in a market-square clearing. It is a syntax of remote causation, a whispered conspiracy of hydraulic gradients. To live among them is to be surrounded by a mute deliberation, a congress of wet rooms deciding in slow dendritic pulses what the city will become next.

And they are restless. The turbulence of the valley is not merely outside them; it is theirs to direct, however blindly. When a street shifts between your footsteps, it is because an interior one three blocks over has extended a rhizome-finger, rerouting the root-mat to bring a fresher fracture of hot rock within reach of its lowest chamber. When a tower unzips its sheath to reveal wetter architecture, it is an interior one shedding an old wall, molting its assumptions. They are the metabolism’s mind—if mind can be so distributed, so patient, so architectural—solving in lignin and folded space the problem of what should dwell in a place too loud for animals, too young for rot, too broken for ordinary rules of containment.

They have no faces. They have foyers. They have no eyes, but the stomata in their walls open and close in patterns that read the light in directions you cannot name. They do not sleep; they simply cease inflating certain chambers, letting them slump into temporary dead-ends while others swell with new urgency. If you are very still, if you empty your lungs and let the dampness turn your skin into the extension of the place that it inevitably becomes, you can feel them around you—not as watchers, but as *rooms that have noticed you*. A membrane in the dark freshens its exhalation. A floor diaphragm rises to meet your weight. The silence of animals is absolute here because the silence has been gathered, hoarded, and weaponized into a different kind of life.

What lives here is the forest’s experiment with inwardness: the self that a screaming green body develops when it folds itself into a knot and finds, at the center of the knot, not wood but a warm decision. They are not many. They are not few. They are whatever number can be sustained by the volume of impossibility the valley generates, and that volume increases with every shriek of polymerizing bark. They are the first citizens of a settlement that built itself before it knew who would live there, and now, in these damp cathedral wombs, they are learning. Slowly, pressure by pressure, they are learning what a city is *for*.
