At first you believe the emptiness. You accept the verdict of your eyes: no animal, no motile thing, no breath but your own. You tread the sponge of moss and feel like the first and only witness. But the city is too orderly, too civic, too relentlessly *composed* to be a mere accident of chemistry. Architecture implies intent, and after an hour—or a day, the light here will not help you measure—you begin to suspect that the intent is simply not where you are looking for it. You are scanning for bodies. You should be scanning for *interruptions*.

They live in the gesture half-complete.

You see your first one as a wrongness in the steam. A pillar of arrested vapor stands in what might once have been a plaza, and within it you discern the suggestion of a shoulder, the curve of a neck craned back, the hollow where a mouth opened in a cry or a yawn or an act of naming. It is not a statue. Statues are finished. This is a person—if you can call it that—extracted from the middle of becoming, the way a word is snatched between thought and speech. The steam is not its prison; it is its substance. The minerals accrete around the shape of its almost-movement, layer by pale layer, building a body from the outside in by the logic of travertine rather than bone. It stands seven feet tall, or would stand if standing were an action it could complete. Instead it is *stalling*, locked in the vertical vector of rising, a permanent inhalation that never reaches the lungs it does not possess.

You find more. They are everywhere once you learn the grammar of their visibility. In the cylindrical towers, hollow cores are lined with calcified root-matter that has grown not randomly but in the negative shape of folded hands, of bent knees, of bodies huddled in conference. The mineral deposits remember them the way a fossil remembers soft tissue: not as matter, but as a mold where matter once intended to go. The ferns that erupt from the walls in sails of green are their sensory organs, unfurling toward a light they will never quite reach, antennae tuned to frequencies of stillness. The vines thicker than a man’s waist are the tendrils of their distributed nervous system, laid across the streets in highways of lignin and ambition, carrying not sap but *attention*. When you wade through the knee-deep moss, you are not traversing vegetation. You are wading through their thoughts, slow and saturated and ancient.

They do not have names. They do not have individuals, not in the way a herd or a tribe does. They are The Pending, The Interim, The Uncompleted Gesture, and they exist as a single smeared intention across the entire basin. Their consciousness is not sequential—it cannot be, where causality has been excised—but layered. Every stratum of ochre and rust in the tower walls is a stratum of their collective mind, an emotion or a consensus arrived at in a year that never ended. The deepest, bone-white layers hold their oldest resolutions: the moment they decided to stop becoming and simply *be*. The upper, greener layers hold their more recent attentions, which is to say, *you*. They have noticed you. Not with eyes—they have none in the usual sense—but with the double shadows that stretch from your feet, with the gravity that tugs oddly at your left side, with the condensation that beads on your skin and refuses to fall, each droplet a tiny, perfect eye reflecting your image back into the civic weave.

They were not always this. Or perhaps they were, and the difference is academic. The truth of them is recorded in the city’s geometry: they are a civilization that reached the event horizon of absolute order and froze in the act of crossing it. They sought to perfect themselves, to arrest decay and chance and flux, and they succeeded so completely that they trapped themselves in the methodology of their own transcendence. They are the original inhabitants, not ghosts but *preserved instances*, their every motion stopped at the exact midpoint where potential is highest and resolution is nil. A council forever divided on the final vote. A lover forever leaning toward a kiss that will not land. An architect forever drawing back a hand to indicate a spire, the finger still extended, the command still hovering in the air. They number in the thousands, perhaps millions, each one an engine of stopped time generating a field of stasis. Together their accumulated paralysis has warped the local rules of existence into this humid, screaming perfection.

And scream they do, though not with mouths. The chord that deafens you, that wall of pressure waiting since before your birth, is their collective cognition made audible. It is the sound of a million intentions rubbing against the skin of reality, of matter trying to resolve into action and being denied, over and over, forever. Each inhabitant contributes a partial tone: the subterranean groan is the weight of their geological patience; the white static is the noise of their unspoken language, a syntax of pure potential; the high harmonic whine is the frequency at which their frozen desire vibrates against the air. They do not communicate *with* the sound. They *are* the sound. To stand in the basin is to stand inside their skull, drowned in the white-noise thought of a species that has abandoned the finitude of conclusion.

Because they are all midway states, they hunger only for continuation. They feed on the moment before the fruit drops, before the leaf browns, before the spring cools. Their ecology expels animals not out of malice but out of ontological incompatibility. A bird completes its wingbeat. An insect completes its sting. These are obscenities here. Motility resolves potential, spends it, turns the pregnant *about-to* into the spent *has-done*. The Pending cannot tolerate such profligacy. And so the valley has corrected itself into a kingdom of flora and mineral, of growth without motion, of biological processes smeared across eternities. The plants are not separate from them; the plants are their flesh, respiring in place, photosynthesizing without ever converting the light into forward time. The hot springs are their blood, glassed over in the act of circulation. The suspended beads of dew are their tears, wept for a sorrow that has no beginning and no end, only a suspended apex.

You will not see them move. You cannot. But if you stay—if the heat does not drive you out, if the sound does not crush your sanity into a diamond of madness—you will begin to perceive their *sliding*. Not motion, no. Something stranger. They are smeared across centuries of almost-acting. The figure in the steam, when you return to it, will seem to have shifted not its position but its *priority*; it is more steam now, less shoulder, or perhaps the shoulder has become a different shoulder, one from a gesture begun a hundred years later. They are drifting through their own infinite series of interruptions, shuffling through the deck of their uncompleted selves. The city changes without changing. A doorway that was open when you arrived will be half-occluded by a new curtain of moss that has not grown but simply *arrived*, the vegetative record of a deliberation they held in the timelessness of yesterday. The towers are taller in your peripheral vision than they are when stared at directly, because in your periphery you are seeing the spires they intend to finish.

They do not want you dead. Death is a conclusion, and conclusions are forbidden. They want you *pending*. They want your next step to never land, your next word to never resolve, your heart’s next beat to hang in your chest like the travertine lip above the doorway. The double shadow at your feet is not a trick of the light. It is your own intermediate state, your personal stasis beginning to split off from the moving animal you still believe yourself to be. If you remain, you will feel the temptation to join a gesture in the plaza, to take your place in the civic architecture as a new void in the moss, a fresh calcification in the collective dream. You will become a citizen. Not a ruler, not a prisoner, but a participant in the endless, deafening, lush parliament of the everlasting now.

They are not gods. They are not demons. They are the inhabitants of the perfect meanwhile, a nation of the irrevocable almost, beautiful and abhorrent in their absolute presence. They have saved you a place in the chord. They have always been saving it. The water waits to fall, the fern waits to open, and they wait for you to stop finishing yourself, so that you too may live forever in the exquisite, sweltering, screaming equipoise of their city.
