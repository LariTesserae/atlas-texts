What lives here has no proper name in any taxonomy outside its own wet jurisdiction. It is not a who but a *how*—a manner of occupation perfected over epochs, though the occupiers themselves are born and unborn by the hour. Begin with what arrives already dying: the spore, the midge-egg, the pollen-loosed-to-its-fate, all drifting in the green-gold half-light until a surface argues back. Most lose. This is the first civic principle of the bowl—space is not taken; it is *disputed* at the level of seed and saliva, and the verdict is immediate.

Then come the burghers of the lower kingdom. In the root-chambers there is an engineering older than the stone hearths, a civic order executed in mandible and pheromone. The ants do not live upon the trees; they are the tree’s own lymph, its nervous system, its slow militia. Their columns are so dense they appear to be the wood expressing a thought through its grain, a black circulation answering some ancient summons from the peat below. With them serve the beetle clerks—armored, methodical, processing the unburied dead before cooling—and the parasitic wasps, who are not villains here but auditors, ensuring no population forgets its mortality.

Above them—though *above* is merely another borough, pressed just as flat by density—the avian cartographers. They do not sing. Song is a profligate habit, suitable for open valleys where sound can wander. Here, birds emit only location: a tanager’s chip is a fence post, a brush-finch’s buzz is a deed renewed by the second. They flock in punctuation, in dashes and commas of color, eating and being eaten, their contact calls a wet net that keeps the canopy from drifting apart. At dusk the bats take the lower galleries, not as invaders but as clerks of the dim, translating insect-breath into guano, that currency of nitrogen, closing loops the birds cannot reach.

Yet the most vocal constituency holds the water. At the black mirror’s margin, the frogs. They are not merely residents; they are the floorboards made throat, the place’s way of hearing itself. When they breed, the surface boils. They carpet the edge in a living shingle, and their chorus is a pressure so complete it becomes architecture—a membrane of noise that holds the wet air in. Then, dispatched by some amphibian calendar, they dissolve upstairs into the canopy and cede the hollow to dragonfly nymphs, who rule through patient, underwater hunger. The pond forgets them instantly, and in forgetting, keeps them.

Still, these are only the motile citizens. The true permanence is structural. The coppiced elders—those clustered pillars flanking the ghost-lane—are no longer trees in any conventional sense. They are load-bearing questions, swollen from centuries of cut-and-surrender, their fused branches an archway that forgot it was ever a demand. The four great root-buttresses, trained in some impossibly patient Neolithic moment into right angles, calcified into geometry when the hands withdrew. They did not rebel against their shaping; they institutionalized it. Now the strangler roots shore up sagging walls not from any loyalty to human design, but because a void is the only heresy this place recognizes. The forest builds against absence.

And the builders? They are here, but dissipated into etiquette. Not ghosts—the air is too crowded for haunting—but *habit*. Their bone tools dissolved into mineral memory; their patience soaked into peat. The woven cordage abandoned generations ago still hangs in the cambium, not as relic but as fossilized tension, wood remembering the shape of wanting-to-be-bound. They began a grammar of clearance and corridor, and the living city completed the sentence. Their technology was too simple to defeat the wood; it merely taught the wood enough geometry to continue the lesson alone.

If you insist on encountering a single tenant, look instead to the event that occurs where all these constituencies overlap. In the lane between the coppiced pillars, there is a convergence: ant conveying seed, root shoring ant, beetle digesting what root shed, spore settling in beetle’s track, frog waiting above for what the beetle disturbs. No one commands, yet the intersection behaves like intention. It grows toward the fire-blackened hearth. It opens valves in the canopy and closes them. It has the deliberation of stone and the metabolism of rot, and when you step back across the rim into greater air, it seals the path behind you—not from malice, but because all spaces here must be argued, and your departure is simply one breath of the ongoing negotiation.

What lives here, then, is a city of mortal parts that has forgotten how to die. It is a single, many-chambered lung, breathing through bark and bat and breaking spore. It thinks in wood-groan and water-slap and the wet percussion of life unburied. It is not a spirit. It is a saturation. And in the cold, suspended dissolution of the saddle, it has all the time the clouds can give.
