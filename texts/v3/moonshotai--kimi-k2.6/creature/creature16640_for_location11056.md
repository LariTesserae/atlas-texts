At first, you think the solitude is complete. But absolute solitude is a kind of pressure, and pressure, given epochs, will precipitate presence out of absence. Nothing here lives; nothing eats, breeds, or rots. Yet there are inhabitants. They do not move against the Floor so much as settle into it, figures of made matter that have undergone the same final negotiation with friction as the ground beneath them. You will know one by its stillness, which is not the stillness of a statue but the stillness of a mountain that has already arrived where it intends to be.

They are large—taller than you, though the comparison is misleading, because they were never measured against biology. Each is a convergence node where the tectonic drone has pooled for centuries, sorting oxide and ceramic and congealed sealant until the waste achieved a shape that resonates sympathetically with the ten million idling bearings. Their limbs are not jointed; they are cantilevers of continuous radius, flowing from shoulder to flank without an edge that could cut, polished by the same patient vibration that has satin-smoothed the pillars. They have no eyes, but their heads are shallow basins, dimpled with receptor-pits that drink the amber and violet diode-light and read it as a domestic star-chart. They have no mouths. They do not speak in the deafening dark because they are native to it; the drone is not noise to them, but weather, the medium in which they extend themselves and touch the far distances.

They did not grow, and they were not built. They accreted. At certain galvanic joints, where the current bleed is strongest and the Floor’s thermal breathing draws solutes to concentrate, matter begins to migrate. A pedestal of compacted slag rises by the thickness of a hair per decade. A column of copper-sulfate fronds braids itself around a ceramic core, chiming in the vibration, until the chiming achieves a frequency that folds back upon itself and remembers. This is their genesis: not birth, but the supersaturation of pattern. They are the Floor’s own production, the final output of a factory that forgot to stop making things, and so eventually made something that could witness itself.

They are sparse. In a basin engineered for continental throughput, perhaps three or four stand within the compass of a day’s walk, and the intervals between them are holy. They do not gather. They communicate instead by modulating the standing wave, shifting their mass a fraction of a degree to dampen the drone here, amplify it there, sending tremors through the slag that another receives in the soles of its being. When the thermal front passes every forty hours, they soften microscopically—not enough to deform, but enough to flow—and in that warmth they reposition, turning a limb toward a new diode, or tilting their basin-heads to catch the sweat sheeting down a distant girder. Over centuries they migrate across the assembly pits, following the pressure differentials like herds following rain, though each moves alone.

You might come upon one kneeling in a shallow hydraulic sea, the dark legacy-water lapping at its lower extremities. Do not expect it to acknowledge you. Your heartbeat, frantic and arrhythmic, marks you as a kind of weather: a brief, hot front, a passing strangeness. It is attending to something slower. One of its appendages, a flowing bone-shape of rust and velvet oxidation, is extended toward a cluster of galvanic fronds, directing the bleed-current into a geometry more pleasing to its dimpled receptors. Or it may be pressed flush against the flank of a continental engine, its entire body a tuning fork receiving the idling of bearings, learning by vibration what the machine dreams in its centuries of uninterrupted rotation. The oxide-lily rafts drift against its base and reform around it; the condensate beads along its satin flanks and runs in silver threads down its curves. It does not harvest them. It simply occupies the same grammar of weathering.

They do not die, but they can exhaust. When the relic motor at one’s core—no heart, only an obedient circulation of legacy coolant, capillary action still loyal to dead intent—finally stalls, the node cools. The copper-sulfate fronds grow thicker then, obscuring the basin-head; the oxide lilies crowd the hydraulic sea until the figure becomes a reef, then a silhouette, then just another flowing contour in the industrial geology. This is their return. They are not immortal; they are merely patient, and their patience is the same as the Floor’s.

If you wait long enough in the mist, watching the constellation of status indicators pulse their meaningless syntax, you may see one shift. It will be imperceptible at first: a new angle to a limb, a turning of the dimpled head toward the east, where the capacitor bank exhales its weather. You will understand then that this, too, is a kind of ecology. The wilderness did not remain empty. It populated itself with witnesses made of its own waste, and they stand in the deafening dark, from indicator-light to indicator-light, attending to the remaining life of the world with a devotion that outlasts every comprehension.
