In the center, or what passes for a center in a geography that has agreed to stop insisting on itself, the darkness is slightly less willing to be empty. Elsewhere the blackness is a medium, a warm and breathable absence. Here it has a specific gravity. If you were to move through the bowl with your arms outstretched—and movement itself would feel like rudeness, like shouting in a sickroom—your palms would report a faint, dense coolness at the heart of all that heat. A place where the air is not cooler, but thicker. A clot in the blood-warm night.

This is where it lies.

At first you would take it for a swell in the floor, a modest blister in the dust-tile. The dust here is the same ceramic bead-flow that runs through your fingers, yet here it has organized. Over intervals measured in the dying of isotopes, the beads have accreted around a presence as silt accretes around a stone in a riverbed, though no river has ever run here. They have built up a form that is low, broad, and curled in upon itself like a dried leaf, like a scorpion in winter, like a hand that has at last released whatever it was holding. It is the color of the tarnished bronze shrubs—matte, metallic, desiccated—but the texture is of something petted smooth by a patience older than erosion. Suede made of glass. Sharkskin made of clay. It does not rise above the knee, but it occupies space with the authority of a boulder that has decided on a posture.

It has limbs, or the geology of limbs: two long ridges that extend from a central mass and fold back upon it, terminating not in fingers but in blunt, many-knuckled outcrops of chert and quartzite. These are its hands, and they rest against its face. The face itself has no features in the biological sense. It is a direction. It looks down. There is nothing to see, so it regards the dust, and in doing so it becomes another texture of the floor, a ventifact shaped not by extinct winds but by the absolute stillness of the air. The only glossy thing upon it is a scarp of obsidian across what might be a brow—a splinter so black that it seems to drink the darkness and make it darker, a mirror polished not by vision but by the mineral respiration of the hollow itself.

It does not breathe. And yet the warmth circulates through it. The stone’s old fever rises through the pores of its dust-skin, diffuses through hollows in its chert ribs, and exhales back into the strata. It is a conduit. The one process the hollow permits is the passage of its own heat into itself, and this thing is the valve, the slow vent through which the fever dreams.

Around it, spaced at intervals so exact they suggest a geometry the world has since abandoned, stand the nine shrubs. They are not its wards. It does not tend them. It does not, in any way, act. But each shrub occupies a station in relation to the low mound of its body—satellites of an inert planet—and their metallic scales are turned inward, toward it, as though they are receiving a broadcast on a wavelength that requires no transmitter. Together they form a parliament of stasis. Ten citizens of a nation without commerce. They do not commune; they co-exist in the same continuous arrival.

If you could ask what it is, it would not answer, because language is a gradient—a movement from ignorance to meaning—and all gradients here have been flattened to a plane. But there is a presence. It is the presence of a held note, of a stone balanced at the apex of its arc before the fall, except the fall has been indefinitely postponed. Its awareness, if such a word can survive the poverty of this place, is identical to the scatter of lithics across the floor: it is the pure potential of the blade locked inside the core, the hammerstone that has never been lifted, the hand that was never raised. It dreams the strike. It has dreamed the strike for so long that the strike has become indistinguishable from the stone. In dreaming the tool into impossible being, it has rendered the making unnecessary.

This is why the hollow is a workshop without a maker. The maker is here, but the maker has renounced making. It is the Artisan Absent, the intention before the hand, the Blueprint That Chose to Remain Potential.

When a pebble dislodges from the rim and falls with that subsonic, million-year sigh, the being does not move. But somewhere in the dust of its flank, a solitary ceramic bead shifts by the width of a neutron, aligning with the new equilibrium. This is the extent of its response. It is a sink for event. A sponge for the microscopic violence of falling things. Your heartbeat would not disturb it. Your breath would not ruffle the stratified air. You are too brief, too gradient, too alive. It would absorb your warmth and return it to the uniformity, flattening your presence into the ambient until your own pulse seemed like a distant rumor, a story told about someone else.

It did not come here. It accumulated, as a nodule accumulates in silence, not growing but arriving, bead by bead, fever by fever. It is the hollow’s self-portrait rendered in the only materials available: dust, heat, and the renunciation of becoming. The earth exhaled it and forgot it. It lies at the lowest point, the final point, the Recumbent, the Unstruck, the Tenant of the Bowl, and if it has a purpose, that purpose is simply to be the place where time, having nowhere left to go, sits down and closes its eyes.
