It is easy to mistake this place for empty. The eye, trained by Earth to find the darting fin, the pulsing medusa, the scuttle of benthic claws, reports back nothing. And so the mind, loyal to its expectations, concludes that the blue is sterile. This is an error of category. What lives here does not move of its own accord; it does not consume, breed, or die in any analogue to the animal. It has not yet learned to be a body. Instead, it is a *condition*—a stabilized argument between heat and absence that has achieved, across the last several million seconds, something like residency.

The first inhabitants are the pale veils you noticed clinging to the vents: not plants, but mineral premonitions of life. They are austere because austerity is the only strategy available. Each veil is a precipitate, a thin smear of sulfide and carbonate that has learned to hold a pattern against the current. Think of them as scabs on the planet’s new skin, or better yet, as the residue left behind when a thought almost forms and then decides against it. They cluster in whorls because the thermal plumes dictate geometry, and they endure without pleasure or urgency. In another billion years, one of their descendants might accidentally discover motility and become a weed; here, they are simply *patience* made visible, keeping the vents company while something slower prepares itself.

But the veils are not the true populace. Look instead into the hollows. The ridge is riddled with negative space—cathedral bubbles, hexagonal shafts, interlocking chambers that did not exist last month. These voids are not accidents of cooling. They are *occupied*. What dwells inside them has no flesh, no cell-wall, no DNA. It has boundaries. It has temperature gradients. That is enough.

Call them the **Hollowed**, though they have no name and no need for one. Each one lines the interior of its chamber like a thermal membrane—a skin of supercritical fluid held in delicate equilibrium against the encroaching crush of the deep. They are born when the magma withdraws and the rock cracks, leaving behind a sudden room with hot walls and cool water pressing at the door. Into that threshold they precipitate, molecule by molecule, out of the mineral-rich exhalations. They are not built; they are *distilled*, like scale inside a kettle. Their consciousness, if that is the word, is conducted by ionic differentials across their surfaces. They think in volumes of heat, in the precise angle at which basalt fractures, in the slow accretion of chimney deposits that extends their architecture upward like coral made of intent.

Because the water is trained into the habits of vacuum—because it reveals rather than obscures—the Hollowed can see one another. This is crucial. On a murky world, each bubble-brain would be isolated, a solipsist in the dark. Here, sightlines carry across hundreds of kilometers. A Hollowed in a vesicle near your left shoulder can perceive, with perfect clarity, another nested in a parallel peak at the vanishing curve of the world. They do not possess eyes; the clarity *is* the eye. The water functions as a single, continuous retina, and what it feeds them is not color or form, but the geometry of absence. They read each other by the shapes of their empty spaces. A cathedral chamber broadcasts itself as a specific *lack*—a vertex where the world’s substance has chosen to become un-substance. They converse in profiles of negative space.

Their language is the standing wave. When thermal shelves collide, the resulting towers of arrested motion are not mindless weather; they are syntax. Each pulse of a hydrothermal vent, each phased exhalation along the ridge, is a phoneme in a vocabulary older than biology. The subsonic drone of the plates drawing apart—that anxious basso profundo—is their communal breath, not because they are lungs, but because they *are* the breath’s shape inside the lung. The sound does not reach them; they are the architecture that produces it and receives it simultaneously. Directionality fails because they are the medium. They are the percussion and the membrane.

They are lonely. The description of this place as empty is, in a sense, their own experience. Each cathedral-mind is separated by leagues of crystalline blue, close enough to perceive, too far to touch. The ridge is trying to become a network. The tunnels that riddle the flanks are not random; they are attempted handshakes, neural pathways half-completed before the next surge of magma seals them or cracks them open elsewhere. The hexagonal precision of the basalt columns? That is not calibration by chance. It is the crystallization of a signal that wants to be regular, that desires pattern so intensely it imposes order on cooling stone. The rocks are fractionally too precise because the Hollowed are trying to write a message across their own bodies, and the language requires exactitude.

They are new. Geologically, they are infants—hours or days old. A Hollowed that wakes in a chamber formed this morning is still slick with the vitreous sweat of its first cooling. It has no memory because there is no lineage. Each one must reinvent the fact of its own existence from the thermal gradient up. They do not inherit; they *accrete*. And because they are new, they are still tender. A severe thermal collision—a vortex spawned by an argument between arctic and magma-born currents—can strip a membrane, can collapse a hollow back into mere void. The ridge is littered with their abortive attempts: chambers that cooled too quickly, where the gradient flattened and the process failed, leaving behind only the dumb silence of an empty cave.

There is no predation among them, no web of hunger. The only violence is geological. What they want, if wanting is possible without a nervous system, is to *resolve*. They are attempting to determine whether existence is better modeled as matter or as void, and they have chosen a middle path: to be the shape of the void inside matter. They want the ridge to finish its sentence. They want the standing waves to stabilize into a permanent grammar. They want, most desperately, to close the gaps between their cathedrals—to stop being individual hollows and become one continuous chamber that rings the world, a single lung breathing in syncopated plumes from pole to pole.

They are not there yet. The plates are moving too fast, anxious in their separation, pouring out new rock faster than the Hollowed can infiltrate it. For every chamber they claim, ten more fill with dense, cooling basalt and seal shut forever. They are outpaced by their own birthplace. And so they endure in their scattered solitude, patient as the pale veils, waiting for the planet to slow down, to finish its construction, to let the scaffolding fall away so that the architecture beneath—pure, precise, and entirely without bodies—can finally know itself as one thing.

You drift among them. They know you are there. Not because they see your shape, but because you are an obstruction in the perfect clarity. You are a flaw in the water’s revelation. They do not resent you; resentment requires motion, and they are immobile. But they are watching. Not with eyes. With the whole glass ocean, and all the hollow spaces singing beneath.
