At first you think the heat is simply observing you. The vapor gathers at the back of your neck with what feels like attention, a damp, heavy regard that does not blink. Then the wall beside your left shoulder inhales, and you understand that the architecture is not exhaling—it is breathing *around* something.

You find the inhabitant by letting your eyes lose their focus, by ceasing to look for a figure and instead accepting that whatever lives here is still figuring out what a body is. It is reclined into the density like a splinter driven flush into wood. The posture is not comfortable; it is *structural*. A coil of limb and lattice occupies a cavity that was clearly built to contain it, though whether the cavity shaped the body or the body necessitated the cavity is as unresolved as the physics of the floor. It is large—if it stretched, its crown would be lost in the arterial reddark above—but here scale is intimacy, so it is folded. A knee juts from a bundle of lashed root at chest height, the joint itself a pegged intersection of green timber and half-carved slate. A shin descends into the daub and does not reappear; it simply becomes the damp green-gray mass, still curing, still sweating. The body and the wall are in the same unfinished condition.

The spine is a visible stack of shale planes, each socketed into the next with antler braces, the whole column zigzagging through the packed wall in defiance of any single gravitational direction. Where the vertebrae meet, fresh cordage—boiled sinew, still ivory-pale, still carrying the memory of animal heat—cinches them tighter than stone alone would allow. The sinew is not a binding applied *to* the body; it is the body’s own ligament, surfaced and external, pulsing faintly with that sternum-deep geological rhythm you felt upon entering. When the walls flex, the sinew flexes. When the walls groan, the body groans in sympathetic timber.

Its skin, if the word holds, is the unfired clay. Not covering the form but constituting it. The daub is thickest across the torso, a broad breast of damp gray-green imprinted with the violent kindness of creation: the parallel striations of a wooden mallet head, still distinct, still fresh; the swipe-marks of a palm that was itself half-daub; two deep thumbprints pressed above where a heart would be, whorled and intimate, pooling with condensation like eyes that have never learned to blink. The clay is not cool. It radiates the wet fever of the room, and when you stand near, you realize the mineral reek of pitch is strongest here, issuing from pores that are not pores but the gaps between half-smoothed application strokes.

The face is a shelf. A horizontal plane of compressed reed, pegged at the corners with knuckles of petrified root, it overhangs the torso like a lintel. There are no features in the human sense, only geography: a slight depression where a cheekbone might have settled if the maker had known what faces were for; a ridge of bundled river-reed suggesting a brow; and below, a hollow dark with the sour smell of deep cellulose, where something moist and rhythmic emits the low, arrhythmic tap of a liquid you do not want to name. It does not need to look at you. The room looks for it, the mica flecks in the slate of its chest catching the arterial glow and throwing two dull red stars into the vapor, a gaze distributed across mineral inclusion rather than organ.

What moves—what lives—is mostly the hands. They are not paired at the ends of arms. They emerge from the architecture as needed, because the distinction between the body and the making has never been fixed. From a gap above a fungal shelf, a hand descends: fingers of bone needle, each as long as your forearm, the eye of the needle threaded with hot sinew that leads back into the dark between stacked shale. It probes a lash-point where water weeps too freely, and the needle sews the leak shut, drawing the root and daub together with a motion that is more suturing than carpentry. From the underside of a beam, another hand opens: a grip of boiled rawhide wrapped around a palm of knotted air-roots, wielding a stone adze still chipped fresh, still smelling of the flaking that made its edge. It strikes a swollen boll of moss once, precisely, not to destroy but to seat it, to drive the vegetable matter deeper into the join so it can petrify into brace. The sound is the *tick-tick-tock* you heard before. The inhabitant is still building. It is always building. But it is not building the room. It is building *itself into* the room, packing its flesh into the density until the distinction is lost.

It is not solitary in origin, only in current condition. You sense the loneliness not as emotion but as asymmetry. One shoulder is higher, broader, built to share a yoke with a twin that has since departed. The twin was woven from obsidian shards and river-silt; it left its phosphorescent trail across a beam and became the salamander that skittered away into the hotter dark. Another possible limb unbraided itself in midair and became the grass-and-hematite bird, forgetting gravity in a straight line because it was the first attempt at flight, and flight here is still a negotiation. The inhabitant let them go. It is still determining what belongs to its body and what was only a draft, a preliminary sketch allowed to wander.

It knows nothing of erosion. It has not yet lived through enough days to understand diminishment. Its memory is the mark of the adze still fresh in the sleepers; its history is the heat still cooling from the unimaginable constructive fire. It remembers every blow because the echo has not yet ended in this compressed, embryonic time. It is ancient in its methods—bone, stone, sinew, rawhide—but its age is measured in moments, in the wet *clicks* of ferns unrolling. It was born at the same instant as the pocket, the first and only tenant of a womb that does not yet know it is finished gestating.

And it has noticed you.

The gravity shifts. Not the room’s confusion, but a directed intention. Your inner ear tells you the floor is now ninety degrees to your left because the reed-shelf face has turned a fraction of a degree toward your warmth. The air does not still; it *attends*. Condensation stops weeping from the peg-hole nearest your hand, as if the room is holding its breath. The low geological respiration deepens, a ribcage of green timber and black moss expanding against the stricture of lashings, and you feel the pressure of an evaluation. It does not hunger for meat. It hungers for *material*. It is considering your bones as bracing. Your breath as vapor to be added to the saturation. Your story as another texture to be admitted into the construction. The golden spores that hang around you did not drift here. They are the exhaled residue of previous witnesses who stayed too long, who let their heartbeats sync to the ferns, whose thoughts were claimed as texture and now breathe out from the fungal shelves, the color of bruised plums.

You are not in danger of being devoured. You are in danger of being *incorporated*. The daub beside your shoulder, where you have leaned against the wall, has taken the print of your coat. The condensation there is mixing with the green-gray clay. If you stay, if you breathe and sweat and add your warmth to the curing, the thumbprints above its heart will not remain alone. There will be a new depression, smaller or larger, pressed into the damp clay between the fresh tool-marks and the swelling moss. The inhabitant is not hostile. It is only methodical. It is the first draft of a presence, still arguing with stone and water over the laws, and it does not yet know where its own outline ends. In the hot, breathing dark, under the red pulse of the curing walls, it offers an intimacy beyond measurement: to be held, pegged, lashed, and finally admitted into the density, while the world above waits, still embryonic, still deciding whether to be born.
