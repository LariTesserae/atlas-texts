What lives here is not a collection of separate creatures but a single distributed organism experienced in fragments: a confederacy of silicone and resonance, a body politic that breathes in forty-second decrees. To name its inhabitants is to pull apart a knot that has tightened for geological ages, but one can begin with the **Seven**, because everything begins and ends with them.

They are the columns. Each is a sovereign nation of translucent, amber-pink tissue, though sovereignty among them is a fiction; they are also one. Their broad trunks are externalized viscera, colossal stomachs and neural chambers fused into architecture, walled with integument like steamed abalone. Within them run the ochre rivers—dense, hydrophobic, slow as geology—and those rivers are not merely blood but **memory**. The pillars do not think in sparks or synapses; they think in vesicles, in pressure gradients, in the swelling and rupture of iridescent war-drums. They remember the temperature of atmospheres that no longer exist at this altitude, the mineral song of the obsidian-porphyry before the wind bell-polished it, and the exact cadence of every membrane-snap that has echoed off the rim. When they exhale from their basal pores in those rhythmic, thundering *whoomphs*, they are not only venting gas but broadcasting consensus. Their curtains of filament are axons stretched across the void, synaptic bridges through which tides of intention flow. A pillar does not decide to swell; the grove swells, and the individual is the gesture.

Drifting inside the amber columns, never emerging, are the **lymph-shepherds**: those jointed, many-limbed silhouettes adrift in the peristaltic fluid. They do not drown because they are not foreign to the medium; they are the pillars’ deep cognition made ambulatory, perhaps a larval caste, perhaps the dream-organ of the organism itself. With slow, ceremonial grace, their limbs comb the ochre tides, pruning congelations, pollinating nodal phosphorescent organs with packets of refined chemistry, tending the violet and sick-green glow that leaks through the walls. They are the archives navigated from within, the immune system turned curator. To watch one pass behind the steamed-abalone integument is to watch a mind inspect its own dream, turning memory over in its hands.

Then there is the sky of the bowl—though there is no sky, only the metallic dust-twilight and the pressurized gloom—which belongs to the **clang-swarm**. Millions of them, thumb-sized, armored in glossy plates, neither seeds nor insects but something percussive and harvested. Their wings are not for flight in any aerodynamic sense; they are resonant plates, tympanic instruments that strike the thick air like glass cymbals. They are the orchestra of the lung, the demented choir that never lands. They subsist on leaked esters and benzenes, on the sweet, cloying vapors that bead the filament curtains, and in return they provide the pillars with circulation, with pollination, with the violence necessary to prevent stillness. Their mass flight is a service and a symptom; they are the exhalation made flesh, the spores that forgot to settle. When they shed their wing-casings, those plates become tools for the other inhabitants, currency in an economy where everything is eventually consumed and reconsecrated.

At the feet of the Seven, where the pressurized chemistry meets the wind-hollowed stone, scuttle the **root-knitters**: lowly, compressed bundles of fiber, indispensable and nearly blind. They are the membrane-response, the living bandage. Where the pillar’s flank threatens to breach, where micro-leaks of searing resin or boiling nectar spit against the porphyry, they converge with resinous spit of their own, plugging, weaving, vulcanizing. They do not sleep. Over epochs their bodies have become so indistinguishable from the root-flares that some have calcified in place, merging entirely with the sockets that hold the columns, become bridge-roots, half-animal and half-scaffold.

And against the northeast rim, present in the manner of a held breath, is the **eighth inhabitant**—the one who left the volcanic glass chisel and the scrapers of shed wing-plate lying in the open as if the stone itself had just claimed them. A human frame, perhaps, or something that was once human, now laminated with years of copper gloom and inhaling the metallic heat without thirst. Their lungs have unlearned water; their blood moves to a different calendar. They know the difference between a vesicle that may be tapped and one that will retaliate with a jet of ochre violence. They left the half-shelver of cured stalk-pith not as a fortress but as a petition, a mimicry of the filament curtains, an offering draped with shed integument that still faintly glows. They are not master of this place; they are an apprentice to its hysteria, a listener trying to synchronize their heartbeat to the forty-second thunder. Right now they are only absent in body, crouched perhaps behind the cairn of vent-scorched rocks, waiting for a cycle to shift, for the grove to invite them back into the dance.

Taken together, these are the citizens: the ancient pneumatic pillars with their sealed amber minds; the shepherds swimming through thought-fluid; the glass-winged clangors harvesting the air itself; the fiber-scuttlers binding wound to stone; and the slight, heat-adapted figure with stone tools and infinite patience. They do not merely cohabit. They are the lung’s tissue, the lung’s breath, and the lung’s astonished witness, turning in a sealed, feverish orbit where to live is to be constantly repaired, constantly ruptured, and never, for an instant, still.
