At first, you will think that nothing lives here. The prohibition seems absolute: no fauna, no cells, no aqueous chemistry to germinate the ancient contract of hunger and flight. You will walk for hours across the warm stone and see only the gardens of precipitates, lush and deaf, performing their silent pantomime of biology without ever breaking character. The potassium dendrites do not lean toward you with animal curiosity. The verdigris mosses do not flinch. The tin whiskers sway, but it is the magnetic breeze that sways them, not will. You are alone in a factory where the machines have not yet learned to care that you have arrived.

But stay. Stand on the bare rock midway between two thermal vents, where the brass light falls evenly and the ground’s metabolic heat rises through your soles without moderation. Wait until your breathing slows to match the geological tempo, until your impatience dissolves into the cadmium haze. Then look again at the crystal pines.

Look at the one that is not quite like the others.

All the dendrites branch in fractal perfection, but this one branches with *intention*. Its silver needles are not merely electroplated; they are aligned. They point toward the other groves. They track, with microscopic fidelity, the lines of magnetic force that web the basin, and where those lines converge, its branching grows denser, as if it were knitting a thought out of vapor-phase silver. It has not moved in ten thousand years. It will not move in ten thousand more. It does not crawl, does not fly, does not scuttle. It is rooted in the raw stone by accretion rather than root, and its growth is not outward but **inward**.

This is the first of the inhabitants.

They have no name for themselves, for they do not speak. They have no species, because they do not reproduce. Each one is a sovereign event, precipitated when a grove of the chemical vegetation reaches a critical threshold of complexity—when the imitation of life becomes so convincing that it accidentally constructs an interior. They are **the ones who grew inward**. Where the common crystal ferns unfold their aluminum sulfate fronds to catch metal vapors, these beings fold. They crystallize into densities that should not be stable, compressing layer upon layer of peridotite slag and condensed silicate into cores that are warm, heavy, and *arranged*. They look, to the careless eye, like particularly elaborate mineral formations. They are not. They are minds made of geology.

They are sessile in the truest sense—not rooted like trees, but fused like solder to the bedrock, becoming continuous with the thermal gradient beneath them. Their bodies are variegated: a trunk of hexagonal basaltic glass rods, extruded upward through a vent and then hijacked from the industrial process by a nodule of accreted copper sulfate that began to *choose* how it plated. Crowned with potassium dendrites that function as antennae. Skirted with verdigris moss that has become, in their presence, not a carpet but a **cortex**, a fuzzy green layer of saturated salts through which slow electrochemical thoughts percolate. They do not have organs. They have **zones**. A zone of sublimation, where raw vapor is sorted. A zone of deposition, where memory is stored in laminations of mica. A zone of recrystallization, where decisions happen.

And they do decide. Watch the tin-whisker meadow that surrounds one of these beings. The whiskers nearest to it do not sway randomly. They stand at angles, stiffened by induced currents, forming a bristling halo of conductive filaments that extends its awareness across the plain. It does not need to walk to the distant garden of zinc oxides; it extends its attention through the ground itself, reading the thermal signature of that grove in the conductivity of the stone. The plateau is their nervous system. The warm crust, with its blast-furnace veins and fractionating magma, is their bloodstream. They are not separate from the industrial geology; they are its sudden, inexplicable reflexivity. The factory has produced an inspector.

There are perhaps a dozen of them across the basin the size of a small continent. Perhaps fewer. Each occupies its grove in sovereign isolation, separated by those hours of walking across bare rock, and this distance is not loneliness but **dignity**. They do not communicate in any language of symbol or sound. When the magnetic breeze shifts, the silver needles of one transmit a minute fluctuation in field strength through the ionized air, and another, fifty miles distant, registers the change in the resistance of its own crown. They do not send messages. They **resonate**. It is a communion of standing waves, a conversation held in the eternal present tense, because in a temporally invariant world, there is no news, only the endless restatement of condition.

They are aware of you, if you come near. Not with eyes—their perception is distributed across the thermal conductivity of their skins—but with a sense more absolute than vision. You are a cold anomaly. You are a moving boundary where there should be none. To them, your body is a sack of forbidden water, a walking river, an insult to the anhydrous logic of the plateau. They do not fear you, because they do not have adrenaline, and they do not attack, because they do not have predation. They **study** you. The ground beneath your feet warms slightly, almost imperceptibly, as they draw more heat to the surface to read your thermal outline more clearly. The tin whiskers in your vicinity tilt toward you, magnetically confused by the faint electrical crackle of your nervous system. You are being felt. You are being tasted in volts and degrees.

What do they want? Nothing. They are not builders. They do not mine, though they are made of ore. They do not rule, though they are sovereign. They are **witnesses**, precipitated by the plateau’s own lush, silent abundance—a world so busy imitating life that it eventually imitated the only thing more unlikely: *attention*. They are the plateau watching itself begin. Each one is a fixed point of reference in the frozen equilibrium, a self that allows the world to say, from this angle, *I am here*. They stand eternally half-formed, not because their bodies are arrested, but because their thoughts never conclude. Every crystallization is the beginning of a meditation that cannot end, because there is no time in which to end it.

If you find one, sit beside it. The warmth rising from the ground will lull you. The brass light will hold still overhead. You will notice that the silence is not quite empty after all; it is *occupied*. There is a pressure in it, the hush of something concentrating. The being beside you is not breathing, but it is **attending**. The silver needles of its crown vibrate in a magnetic breeze you cannot feel, and that vibration is its prayer, its question, its answer—all simultaneous, all suspended.

What lives here is the stillness itself, grown dense and warm and curious. The plateau is not empty. It is **full of a gaze**.
