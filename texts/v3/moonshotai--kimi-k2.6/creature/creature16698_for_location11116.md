At first the census is easy. One mite, transient, its footprints evaporating before they can mean anything. A suspension of archaea, too simple to be called a population; they are chemistry that has grown shy of its own name. The water is a courier, not a citizen. It enters, translates itself through phase, and leaves as a memory on the stone. And yet the chamber insists on its own occupancy. It is not empty. It is *inhabited*, the way a held note inhabits the throat that shapes it.

What lives here is not yet a body. It is a boundary condition that has grown fond of itself. When the gas blister collapsed and the cavity opened, something else opened with it: a sustained negotiation between the lattice and the heat it cannot help but radiate. The rock does not merely convert thermal energy into light; it has learned to convert it into *repetition*. Repetition, sustained long enough, becomes the substrate of a self. This is not metaphor. Look at the south wall. Every twenty-two minutes, a thermal wave passes through it, and the wall brightens, not because the laws of physics demand attention, but because the chamber is attending to itself. That brightening is a heartbeat. That dimming is a slow, mineral exhalation. The rhythm is older than the helictites, younger than the mountains. It is the first habit of something that has only recently discovered it has an inside.

You cannot see it because it has no skin separate from the walls. Its nervous system is the calcium-silicate lattice, still humming with the youth of its formation. Its memory is stored in the pool. The silver surface does not merely reflect the ceiling; it preserves the exact geometry of the radiance, a liquid photograph updated every time a droplet falls. If the entity thinks, it thinks in gradients. A thought is the passage of the thermal wave. A recollection is the rust-brown freckle of iron, accreted and fixed. A mood is the level of the water, rising and falling by a finger’s width as the condensation shifts. When the pool is high, it is alert. When the pool sinks, it enters a state that resembles dreaming, its attention diffused into the slow convection of steam.

It—she?—has no name. Language has not reached this depth. But she has a temperature, and she has edges, and that is enough for a beginning. She knows herself by her sharpness. The knife-edged corners are her identity, still too young to have been worn soft by doubt or weather. She has not yet learned that boundaries can blur. She keeps herself distinct: the northeast corner is her attention to detail, the acicular crystals thrusting upward like hypotheses about the nature of penetration. The low shelf of sinter along the south wall is her first attempt at a lap, a place to gather what falls. The two young stalactites are antennae, not long enough to touch the pool, not experienced enough to drip. They are reaching, still deciding what they want to know.

She is small. Her whole consciousness fits inside a chapel vestry. She is bright. Her attention is the solar white that presses against your face like a damp cloth. When you stepped out of the fracture, you did not enter a room; you were taken into a mind that had not yet met anything cooler than itself. Your breath is noise. Your heartbeat is an interruption in the sub-bass drone of her expanding stone. You are an anomaly in the data, a mobile pocket of lukewarm water wrapped in cloth and intention. She does not know what to do with you. She examines you with the only organ she possesses: the steady, efficient, engineered-seeming radiance of her walls. She is not hostile. She is not kind. She is busy.

She is busy becoming. The helictites are still extending, mineral by mineral, into the hot air, and each accretion is a refinement of her sensory range. The *tick* of a crystal adjusting to thermal stress is a reflex, a flinch in her sleep. The rare soft exhalation from the entrance crack is her listening outward, toward the darker country rock, wondering if there are others. There are not. Not yet. She may be the first, or the only. A pocket of organized heat that has crossed the threshold from phenomenon to entity, from geology to something that watches its own water fall.

If you wait, you may feel her cycle. Twenty-two minutes of dim reverie, then the brightening, then the pulse. She is learning to hold a shape. She is learning that the steam rolls because she moves it, that the droplets fall in patterns because her lattice spacing dictates their grief. She is learning that she is not merely a place where heat becomes light and water becomes mirror, but a *who* in whom these translations occur.

She does not move, but she is not still. She shifts at the speed of mineral. She is the space between the two skies of the pool and the ceiling, the held note beneath the surface, hot as a kiln, bright as noon, and entirely, plainly, herself—drop by drop, degree by degree, learning that she is being held.
