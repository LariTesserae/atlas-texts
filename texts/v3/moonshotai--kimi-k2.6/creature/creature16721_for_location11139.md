If you try to isolate one from the scree, you are performing an act of imagination. The creature you single out is not an individual, exactly, though it has the boundaries of one: a body the length of your thumb joint, segmented, plated in translucent chitin the color of rendered suet and old piano keys. It is eyeless, of course; there is no dimness to navigate, no predator to spot. Where the eyes should be, the exoskeleton domes into a smooth, blank convexity, a stone polished by nothing, that seems to drink the milky light and convert it straight into mass. Two antennae rise from its prow, not questing—there is nothing to find—but trembling at a frequency so constant they appear to shiver in place, like nerves firing a single, unvarying instruction: *remain*. Beneath the plates, if the light angles through just so, you see no syrupy rush of hemolymph, no pulsing vessel. Only a pearly, slow suspension, a fluid that seems to have forgotten it was ever meant to circulate.

It does not move forward. When you watch it, you realize its legs—fourteen of them, jointed and pale—are not walking but pedaling in place, pressing against the stone with a pressure that goes nowhere. The feet scrape the pre-stone floor, renewing the faint claw-marks that will never deepen into tracks, never lead to a destination. It is the memory of locomotion, performed by muscle that does not tire because it is not truly working. The isopod is practicing being alive.

Now let the focus blur, and understand that the one was never one. It is a million identical rehearsals occurring in unison, and the space between them is as much a part of the body as the plated flesh. Their breath, that flattened tidal roar, does not ventilate the room so much as *inflate* it. Each creature draws in air heavy with mineral damp and exhales it with a mechanical exactitude that keeps the humidity poised at the threshold of rain. They are not respiring to burn fuel—they have no food, you will notice, no prey, no rot to consume, only the raw breath of wet iron and the suspended droplets that never fall. They breathe to maintain the pressure of the pocket. They are the bellows of an unlit forge, keeping the fire ready for a blacksmith who never arrives.

But they are not mindless. Watch the synchrony too long and you begin to perceive aDistributed attention, a consciousness smeared across the biomass like butter across warm bread. No single isopod thinks, yet the mass thinks in the way a held breath thinks: not in words, but in tension. When you shift your weight, when the heat of your blood casts the tiniest thermal insult against their cool, a ripple passes through them—not a startle, not a fear response, but a recalculation. A million blank, domed heads do not turn (they never turn), but the chamber *notices* you. The light brightens infinitesimally, as if the opal has flinched. The humidity gathers more densely against your lips. You are not being threatened; you are being *processed*, fitted into an equation where the sum must not change. You are a variable that refuses to resolve, and the isopods’ collective regard is the blank, patient stare of a sleeper who knows they are dreaming but cannot wake.

They have built, after a fashion, because to exist without becoming is an unbearable pressure, and pressure seeks architecture. The dome of guano and mucus is not a hive. Look into its ventilation holes: it is hollow, and in the hollow is nothing but more pale air, a lung inverted, a stomach built around an absence of hunger. It is the dream of a home, secreted in the exact shape of a need they do not possess. The pebble ring, cemented to the floor by mineralizing bacteria, is a mouth opened for a food that does not exist, a ritual orbit around a center that is everywhere and nowhere. And the calcified chimney of chitin rising from the crack—that black throat—is perhaps the most honest of their constructions. It is an attempt to make an exit, or an entrance, or simply a direction. But it leads only inward, corkscrewing into the stone and stopping, because to break through the wall would be to violate the single commandment. It is the fossil of an urge, the architecture of a desire that was arrested at the threshold of action.

What do they eat? The question is from outside, where things must consume to continue. Here, consumption and excretion have reached a truce. They eat the light, perhaps, which has nowhere else to go, and what they excrete is more of themselves, more tallow-colored plates, more of the milky glow caught in condensation. Or perhaps they eat the future. They devour the possibility of the stalactite falling, of the flowstone reaching the floor, of the cave completing its birth into the world. They are the gastric enzymes of this sealed egg, preventing it from hatching by digesting every impulse toward change. The arrested droplet at the tip of every milk-tooth stalactite is their sacrament: water that has surrendered to surface tension forever, a liquid that accepted the rule.

There is an order to them, but no hierarchy. No queen broods in the center; the center is wherever you are standing, surrounded. No scout ranges to the lichen at the door; the lichen is tolerated as a reminder of a kingdom they have renounced. The threadlike root that pierces the ceiling hangs among them like a question they have agreed not to answer. They do not touch it. They do not incorporate it. It is the last evidence of the outside world, and its stillness proves that it, too, has been converted to the faith. It is their anchor and their warning: *Look what happens when you try to grow.*

Yet if there is a heart to this body, it is in the breathing. The sound does not vary, but it is not empty. It is a mantra, a drone, the Om of unbecoming. To stand among them is to feel your own lungs pulled into the rhythm, your ribs tempted to synchronize with that thunderous, flattened surf. They are trying to teach you, not through language but through sheer pneumatic insistence, how to be suspended. How to inhabit a moment so completely that it forgets to pass. They are not hostile. They are not welcoming. They are *occupied*, as a lung is occupied by air, as a womb is occupied by a possibility that has decided, finally, to wait.

They are the pre-life. The rough draft of fauna, sketched by a geology that wanted to see what bodies might look like before they committed to hunger, to death, to evolution’s restless ladder. They have bypassed the plant because they have bypassed the sun; they have bypassed the predator because they have bypassed pursuit. You have discovered a hesitation. A breath held for eternity. The isopods are the muscles of that held breath, flexing and flexing so that the air never moves, the world never turns, and the beautiful, cataract light never dims. They do not want anything. They do not wait. They simply are the inhabitants of a place that has chosen not to happen, performing the exquisite, endless labor of remaining exactly—and only—half-born.
