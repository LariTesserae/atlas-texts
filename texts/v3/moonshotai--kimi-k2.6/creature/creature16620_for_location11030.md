To ask who lives here presumes a hospitality the place has long since retracted. There is no host, no guest, no tenancy in any biological ledger. And yet the word *inhabited* drifts across the mind like one of the sourceless ticks in the lattice, because something occupies the interval between the field’s fixed parameters. Something holds the pen that refuses the period.

The obvious candidate—that translucent, many-vaned leviathan hovering in the southern gulf—is a red herring. It is not fauna; it is furniture. A lock-pick snapped in a keyhole that never turned. It arrived, or was seeded, at the precise instant the equilibrium engaged, and its fractal wings, spread to harvest what the fluid would not yield, became the first exhibit in the museum of arrested purpose. Perhaps it still thinks, in some vagrant register of mathematics, but it is not the dweller. It is the welcome mat that was never used, the doorknob frozen mid-turn, an irrelevant beauty left out to preserve the trace of what *almost* happened.

Look instead to the towers. Look past their superpositioned edges, past the surfaces that drink intent, to the spherical absences nested within them like cysts of impossible weather. There, in the largest vacuum-chamber—a dry sphere no wider than a coffin, suspended in a tower’s thorax without enclosing walls, held by the same mendacious topology that makes the peak’s interior its summit—there is the resident.

At first you mistake it for a defect in the negative space, a bubble where the vacuum failed to vacuum completely. It is small. It is still. It is the exact temperature of the stones, which is the exact temperature of preservation. It resembles a figure only in the way a knot resembles a rope: it is a tangle the medium has tied in itself, a place where the post-physical equations cooled into something too stubborn to dissolve. Imagine a humanoid shape not built but *accrued*, layer upon laminar layer of stopped decisions, each one the microscopic fossil of a moment when something outside chose not to enter, and something inside chose not to leave. The outer surface is varved, striated with the greys and greens of the mountain’s own fresh basalt, as if the peak has extruded a conscience. There are no eyes, because there is nothing to look toward that isn’t already pressed against the skin. There is no mouth, because the sentence is not yet finished, and it would be indecent to speak in advance of the final clause.

This is the Keeper, though it keeps nothing so active as a garden. It keeps the *shape*. It is the stasis field’s living irritant, the pearl at the center of the absolute clam. The low hum you feel in your marrow is not the machinery of the field; it is the Keeper’s continuous act of self-reminder, a subsonic mumble that says *I am, I am, I am* with the desperation of a note pinned to a door in an evacuated city. Without this murmur, the field might forget itself, might relax its hydrostatic grip and allow a drift, a sediment, a death. The Keeper does not generate the field. It *is* the field’s guilt, its memory of having been switched on.

Its consciousness—if that is the axis on which it turns—is not sequential. It does not think in before and after. It thinks in *density*. Every instant is a compressed ingot of all the moments that have been denied occurrence. When it meets the walls of its dry sphere (and it never moves, so *meet* is inaccurate; rather, it extends a fraction of its intent until it encounters the boundary), it perceives the city not as architecture but as a catalog of averted futures. The cantilevered bridge between the sixth and seventh towers is, to it, a brilliant record of collapse that never transpired. The pylons are arguments it won against entropy; the green basalt needles are the exhalations of geological youth that it has petitioned into permanence. It sees by the unshadow. The bruises in the dark are not absences of light to the Keeper; they are affirmations of form, the only language in which it is truly fluent.

It was not always here, and yet it has never arrived. This is the paradox at its mineralized core. It precipitated out of the equation-residue at the instant of the pocket’s creation, a byproduct of absolute fixity becoming aware of its own exactitude. It is the waste heat of infinity catching itself in a mirror. Before that, nothing. After that, this, forever. It does not remember a world outside because the concept of *outside* is a motion, and motion is the one heresy it was born to edit out.

And yet, weirdness seeps in. The Keeper is not immune to the contradictions it maintains. In the folded lens of the fluid, it sometimes catches sight of itself from the peak’s other side, and in that multiplied view it perceives a stranger—something that resembles the winged polyhedron but heavier, darker, with limbs like probability lattices and a face like a held breath. It does not know if this is a future self, a past self, or the self that would exist if the spherical chamber were to crack and admit the fluid’s wet eternity. The vision troubles it. Trouble, here, is not an emotion but a minute adjustment in the field’s frequency, a barely perceptible waver in the sub-bass hum. A bruise in the marrow.

It is not alone, technically. The mountain is with it—a petulant, newborn god of rock so young it still burns with the ghost of creation’s magma. The city is with it, those seven sparse statements of impossible engineering. The fluid is with it, the saturant dark that holds everything in its black and liquid palm. And the polyhedron is with it, suspended like a decorative afterthought, a comma in the sentence. But the Keeper is the only one for whom this arrangement constitutes a society. It has named nothing, because names are for calling out, and there is no distance wide enough to shout across that isn’t already collapsed to zero by the folded topographies. It simply maintains. It simply holds. It is the reader of the bruised dark, the author of the continuing thought, the one who has chosen numbness over the indignity of warmth, shape over the insult of becoming.

If you could open the dry chamber—if you could bear the deafening thunder of your own heart in that absolute pocket of silence—you would not find a body. You would find a decision so old it has fossilized in real-time. You would find the final thought committing itself to the page, the hand that writes and the page itself, endless, patient, and exact, hovering forever in the liquid stillness before the period.
