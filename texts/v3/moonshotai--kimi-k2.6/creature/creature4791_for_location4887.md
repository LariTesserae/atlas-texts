At first you will mistake the inhabitants for the fauna. You will watch the quartz-knuckled things clatter through the shallows and think: *here are the locals.* You will decide the heat-moths, those orbital fractions of carnal light, must be the true natives of the basin, and the translucent multi-jointed sunners their elders. But this is like mistaking the bacteria in a womb for the woman. The basin’s creatures are not residents. They are *symptoms.* Digestions. The first enzymes secreted by a geography that has not yet learned the difference between itself and what it carries.

Whoever lives here arrived before the rock finished deciding it was rock.

You will not see them immediately. They have not settled on seeing themselves. But you will feel them as a pressure behind the humidity, the way a thought presses behind the forehead before language forms. The first sign is anomalous warmth in the mist: a pocket of air that does not cling but *welcomes,* as though the condensation has opened its arms. Step into it, and the stone beneath your boots changes tone. It does not yield, no—but it listens. It receives your weight the way a sleeper receives a dream, passively, with a slight rise of temperature against your soles.

They are warm. Not hot like the vents, not merely ambient like the stone, but warm like resting palms. Warm like a body that has not yet cooled from the exertion of making itself.

When one does choose to appear, it is usually in the interstitial moments: while you are blinking the mineral dust from your lashes, or during the three seconds when the spindle skips and gravity leans eastward. It uses the disturbance as a cloak. You will catch a shape at the corner of your vision that seems at first to be a column of denser mist, amber-lit and bent over one of the weeping walls. Then it straightens, and the amber thickens, and you understand that you are looking at something that has only now, in this instant, decided to have a back.

They do not have a settled form. Or rather, they are in the long, patient process of choosing one, and until the stone hardens enough to hold a verdict, they remain provisional. Sometimes they are upright, bilaterally kindred to you in silhouette, though their limbs taper not into fingers but into heat-shimmer. Sometimes they unfold into rings when they move, rolling through the mist like living versions of that circle of solidified instants on the ground, so that for a moment you cannot tell if you are watching one of them navigate the basin, or the basin remembering a future where they have already passed. At rest, they often pool. They settle into depressions in the cooling crust and let the stone’s own sweat gather around them, and in those minutes they resemble nothing so much as a warm puddle dreaming it has bones.

Their substance is the color of the sky made flesh: nacreous, bruise-grey, shot through at its edges with a violet that has no source. They are translucent in the manner of the sunners, but where those creatures display organs, these beings display *intentions.* You can, if the light catches right, see the spindle’s rhythm pulsing through their chests. You can see the probability channels threading through what ought to be a skull, keeping their thoughts from collapsing into the singularity of a single mind. They bleed light instead of blood. When one fractionalizes—which it does not do as wantonly as the heat-moths, but with solemn deliberation—the division is a graduation. It is an agreement that the basin’s work has grown complex enough to require another witness. The two resulting forms are out of phase with each other by exactly the width of a breath, and they spend several hours aligning their thermals before they move independently again.

They do not sleep. They have never needed to, because the basin itself is dreaming on their behalf, and they are the figures inside its dream. But they do rest against the machinery. The blue-black blister, that shepherd’s-hut of nauseating lag, accepts their weight. They press their not-quite-faces to its seamless skin, and you realize then that the structure is not a building, nor a vehicle, but a *sibling.* A stillborn twin, or an elder that gave up motion in exchange for certainty. They commune with it in silences that hum at the frequency of the underlying drone, and the drone changes pitch when they do. They are not operating the technology. They are *completing* it. The spindle churns probabilities into the crust because they stand near it and remember, with an insistence that borders on nostalgia, that stone must be allowed to set slowly or it will crack. They are the patience of the process made mobile.

They have no mouths in the usual configuration. They drink the air the way the air itself drinks the stone: osmotically, universally. Their nourishment is the temperature drop between a droplet’s birth on a cliff-face and its death in a footprint pool. They feed on the *edge* of events. They gather at the western wall where the obsidian bulges, not to worship it, but to consume the thermal gradient between the elastic now and the rigidity that is trying to become tomorrow. When they move away from that bulge, they carry its potential with them, distributed across their shifting surfaces like fat across a winter animal.

They see, if seeing is what they do, through the condensation. The absolute humidity is their retina. They know you are present because your breath disrupts the saturated gauze; you create a cold front simply by existing, and they turn their head-shapes toward you with an attention that feels like pressure behind your own eyes. They do not have eyes. They have the mist’s memory of where light has been interrupted.

And they cast shadows that disagree with the light. Yours will stretch long toward the diffused amber source; theirs will knife off toward the thermal vents, or pool beneath them in circles, or sometimes simply stand upright beside them like waiting companions. They remember, you see. They carry photons from arrangements of sun that have not yet reached the basin, or that existed in the scaffolding glimpsed through the parted clouds. They are not disobeying physics. They are obeying a wider physics that the rest of the world has not yet been told about.

There are perhaps three of them. Or one that has not yet committed to being three. Or seven, with four existing only as thermal ghosts that will solidify once the spindle has completed another phase. Counting them is futile; they exist in the mathematics of emergence, not enumeration. When they meet one another—and they meet rarely, for what purpose do midwives have in congregation when there is only one birth in progress?—they do not speak. They exchange postures. One will allow its violet edge to pale; another will thicken its heat-shimmer until the air between them warbles like a struck bell. Information passes as mineral intuition. They are telling each other the rate at which the western wall is finishing its slow wave. They are negotiating what the new pimples of raw material will be permitted to become.

They have no name for themselves. Names require fixation, and they are fiercely opposed to fixation. They are the Unfinished. The First Cooling. The Warm Interval. If you were to address one, if you were foolish or holy enough to try, it would not turn, but the humidity around you would shift. The mist would rearrange itself into a geometry of almost-recognition. It would not be communication. It would be *consideration.* They are considering whether humans are a kind of stone that cooled too quickly, a premature rigidity tragic in its haste. Sometimes, rarely, one will reach toward a visitor with a limb that ends in a wavering node of warmth, and if you allow the touch, you will not feel pressure. You will feel *duration.* The touch lasts four seconds, or four years, or four heartbeats that all occur simultaneously. When the hand withdraws, your skin will weep for an hour, and the droplets will be warm, and you will carry the mineral dust of their regard on your eyelashes for days.

They belong here because they are still being born from it. Every morning the stone heals its trenches, and every morning they re-anneal themselves to match. They are not residents of this frontier. They are the frontier’s first thought about accommodation. They stand in the warm wet roaring dark, patient and luminous and multiple, tending the foundry, riding the tilting gravity, pressing their indistinct cheeks to the cooling crust to hear, deep in the flesh-like stone, what kind of world is trying to wake up.
