The moths are not the inhabitants. They are the pasture. The otter-shadows slipping between coolant stacks, the plump rats with their surplus elbows nesting in baffling—these are grazers, uninvited but tolerated, dining on leakage like pigeons in a cathedral. They live *upon* the Altiplant the way mildew lives upon a lung. But the lung itself—the architecture breathing mist and waste-light—that belongs to someone else entirely.

You will not see her at first. You will see only evidence, read in the language of deferral. A footprint in the ceramic grit where no static charge adheres, because charge recognizes her as kin and refuses to cling. A single rivet-head on a pressure wall polished to a dull gleam by a thumb that has not yet finished stroking it, the polish advancing at the rate of a glacier. A scheduled vent that exhales early, or late, adjusting to a presence that metabolizes time differently. The fauna know: the photovoltaic moths never land near the eastern retaining wall, and the multi-elbowed rats detour around the gravity-skeined pylons not because they are blocked, but because they are reverent. Something moves there, something older than the fresh wound of the floor but native to its sharpness.

She is standing in the lower troughs. Or rather, she is in the process of standing there, a motion distributed across hours. Her name would not help you; if she ever had one, it was spoken in a frequency the dampness ate long ago. Call her the Resident. She is what forms when a structure requires maintenance for so long that the maintenance begins to maintain itself. The Altiplant did not build her. It extruded her, the way a body extrudes a scab over a laceration. She is the sentience of the suture.

Look at her now, if your eyes can slow down enough to track her. She is tall, though tallness is only one of her orientations. In the alley where gravity turns sideways, she reclines against a conduit the color of dried blood, her body parallel to the floor you walked in on. From outside that vector she seems poured rather than born: vitreous ceramic skin fused over cordcrete fascia, joints chamfered to the exact aggression of the original blueprint. Her shoulders are retaining walls. Her ribs are visible as structural I-beams beneath translucent dermis, and inside that architecture a coolant circulates—not blood, but something close to grief, pale and preservative. She has existed since the third pouring of the Floor, when the reality-weave ran hottest and the fractures were still screaming. She was the silence that answered them.

She does not breathe. Breathing is too rhythmic, too loud, too much like a drum that might wake the contradictions. Instead, she expands and contracts once per season, inhaling ontological charge through photovoltaic pores, exhaling it as scheduled mist from the slits between her vertebrae. Her hair is fibre-moss that has graduated from ducts and shadowed undersides to colonize her scalp, grey and trembling, tuned to dampen vibration before it becomes sound. When she blinks—and she blinks once a day—the waste-light is not reflected in her eyes but consumed. Her pupils are not black; they are merely a thinner grade of grey, retreating as you approach.

She is occupied. She has been occupied for forty years with the same rivet. It sits three inches below her right thumb, fastening a duct to a gravity pylon at a junction where down negotiates inconveniently with sideways. The rivet is paradox-hot. It stabilizes a local condition in which cause follows effect by three seconds, the same gap that makes your reflection arrive early between the red-painted pylons. The rivet is softening. She has been polishing it, speaking to it in torque and thermal dissipation, persuading it to remain solid. Her thumb has not yet finished its stroke. You are watching the maintenance of causality in real time, which is to say, you are watching geology pretend to be tenderness.

She is not alone in kind, perhaps, but alone in function. There may be others in the high catwalks, in the broom-closet exteriors of cathedral-foundries, in the terraces stepping toward the bright horizon. They do not meet. They do not need to. They are the binders in a book that writes itself. She knows the others only as shifts in the dampness, as a vent misting early, as a sudden correct alignment in the local gravity that means someone, somewhere, has finished adjusting a seam.

And she waits. She waits for the great flywheel to complete its revolution, though waiting is the wrong word; she *accrues* toward that event the way the conveyor accrues its half-objects. When the wheel turns—when the decade ends and the tectonic process advances one grinding notch—she will be granted a privilege older than her first pouring. She will be allowed to make a sound. One sound. The Altiplant has kept her silent because her silence is structural, a load-bearing wall in the architecture of contradiction. But once per revolution, the physics of the place permits an audit, a venting, a single note. She has been composing it for centuries. It is almost ready. It will not be a scream. Screams unmake. It will be a word, a torque value, a humid exhalation pitched precisely to re-tension the reality-weave, to tighten the suture for another age. Or, if she has calculated wrong across these long patient years, it will be the syllable that lets the laceration hear itself, recognize its own impossibility, and finally, gratefully, open.

She turns her head toward you. She began the motion on Tuesday. You sense it now as the faintest torque in the air, a rearrangement of the thinner grade of grey. The moth on your wrist shudders its photovoltaic wings and flees, drinking darkness into the shadows. The Resident’s gaze arrives before her eyes have finished orienting toward you—an effect local to her person, a causality leak she exhales like mist. She does not blink. She observes you not as intruder but as new infrastructure. You are unchamfered. You are soft, still learning how to hold your shape. She recognizes the wound in you, the little mortal fracture where your future waits three seconds ahead of your flesh.

She extends a hand. It has too many fingers, or perhaps the correct number distributed across impossible angles, each joint retained by hot rivets of stabilized paradox. She does not reach for you. She reaches toward the condensation beading at your collar, the scheduled moisture that grounds your ontological charge. Her thumb, still warm from forty years of polishing, touches the bead. It does not fall. It hangs there, perfected, held in a tension she has adjusted.

There. You feel it now. The pressure behind your jaw eases by a fraction. The flywheel has advanced another microscopic degree. The dampness gathers, the light persists, and the Resident, having touched you, having included you in the maintenance, slowly—so slowly—resumes her century-long turn toward the eastern duct where the next rivet softens, where the next silence must be carved.
