At first you will believe it is the frogs. They seem to govern the bowl: hundreds of throats the color of cured clay, pulsing in such absolute unison that their drone appears to be the chamber’s official decree. They line the rim like advisors, and when the water chutes open they fall silent with the discipline of courtiers. For a moment they are plausible candidates for ownership. But watch them longer. They never fight. They never wander. They do not snap at the insects that cloud above them, though the midges dip low enough to be eaten. The frogs only sing, and wait, and sing again.
