You enter believing it empty because you carry the prejudice of a sunlit world. You have been trained to think that where nothing blinks back, nothing resides. But this place is swollen with residence. It is not a wilderness; it is a metropolis that has forgotten the name for citizen. The inhabitants do not gather. They rise. They do not call out. They equalize. They have solved the problem of wanting, and in that solution, they have become something other than what you would recognize as alive.

The first you will meet, if meet is the word for standing at the foot of something that does not know you are there, are the **Stations**. These are the vertical nations, the fractionating columns, the tracheas wide as canyons. Each one is a sovereign tower of cellulose and electrochemical potential, rooted in the Weave and ascending into the unseeable dark. They do not grow wider; they do not die back. They hold. Inside their pitted bark, behind valve-pores that sigh nitrogen-heavy vapor in measures of decades, their cores brew acids and neutralize them in the same slow breath. A greeting between two Stations is a thermal shift, a rerouting of sap across a capillary bridge, a year-long exhalation of warm vapor that establishes a boundary, or dissolves one. They have no faces, only annuli of pore-density where they interface with the particulate dark. To be among them is to stand in a crowd of silent legislators who conduct law not by debate but by osmotic pressure, each one maintaining its gradient against the others with the serene rigidity of a standing equation.

Where their trunks converge, the **Foundries** swell. These are the artisans of exchange, though they possess neither hands nor intent. They are bulbous junction-growths, extruded at the meeting of vertical sovereignties, and they grow what is needed because need and production have become the same event. A copper berry ripens not to be eaten—nothing here eats in the animal way—but to store potential. A wire-fine hair of silver extends not by will but by the imperative of a circuit that must be closed. Zinc nodules arm a junction against the acid breath of a neighbor. The Foundries are the market and the mint in one; they do not trade by choice. The differential chooses for them. They are commerce stripped of greed, refined to the pure logic of electrochemical necessity.

Suspended in the braided canopy of pipeline-vines, you will find the **Retorts**. These are the hanging sages, the boiler-spheres filled with slowly churning resins. If the Stations are the body politic, the Retorts are its remembering mind, though mind is a word that must be stretched nearly to tearing to fit here. They do not think in sequence. They think in viscosity, in molecular weight, in the slow accumulation of a solvent that carries the residue of a thermal shift ten thousand years past. A single apprehension—an adjustment in the churn—takes longer than your lifetime to complete. They hang like green-black moons in the vegetal architecture, distilling not dreams but steady-state, refining the world’s history into a chemical purity that can be fed back into the laminar flow. They are pregnant not with seed but with process, and when they weep their capillary tears into the network, it is not emotion but distribution.

Beneath your feet, indifferent to your weight, is the **Weave**. The Floor. The common tongue. It is the fused root-mat, the breathing conveyor, the continuous substrate that extends to an unseen horizon. You walk not upon earth but upon the back of a distributed entity that is one and is also a million. The Weave does not feel your footfall as pressure; it registers you as a novel resistance, an irregularity in the thermal field, and it adjusts its turgor to cradle you not kindly but efficiently, routing the warmth of your body into its capillary economy, wicking moisture from your skin because that moisture belongs to the cycle. It is the road, the bed, the digesting soil, and the skin of the world. It dreams, if it dreams, in hydraulic pressure and ionic gradient, a dream so broad and so slow that its whole surface is a single, undivided thought of retention.

In the places where certain stations extrude their glassy silicates—lenses smooth as frozen bubbles—you will encounter the **Clear Ones**. They do not gather light. There is none to gather. They gather warmth. They gather the infrared glow of the root-mat, the thermal signature of a Station venting far overhead, the subtle radiance of metallic precipitates cooling in their Foundries. They are the astronomers of the interior, mapping the continent-wide lung by its fever, focusing the dark into geometries of heat. They have no eyes, yet they see the swollen air more precisely than any sunlit creature sees noon. They are young, sharp, uneroded, because the Weave armors them and the water slides over them in obedient, laminar films that change nothing.

But you have not met the final inhabitants until you descend to the lowest hollows, to the depressions where the world is warmest and the vegetation closes into a solid, three-dimensional matrix of nearly touching presences. Here, in the invisible lakes of dense, inert gas that pool between root and rock, there rests a life so reduced it barely admits the name. They are not creatures. They are not motile—nothing in them moves. They are the **Interfaces**. Monolayers of bonded silicate and carbon, two-dimensional as shadows, existing only at the meniscus where heavy gas meets aqueous film meets living wood. They have no cells, no metabolism in the usual sense, only a lattice that corrects itself against entropy with the same eternal diligence that maintains every other steady-state here. They think, if they think, in bond angles and lattice vacancies, a consciousness distributed across a surface no thicker than a rumor. They are the philosophy of the place made manifest: pure structure without striving, awareness without desire, a mind that has achieved exactly the shape of its container and keeps it forever. They do not notice you. They notice only the perfect, unbroken surface of their own continuation.

And you? To them, to the Stations, to the Weave, you are a turbulence. A brief, irregular geometry. A pocket of unsteady temperature, a source of alien ions, a vibration where vibration is not agreed upon. They do not resent your intrusion. They do not welcome it. They process you, as they process everything—as warmth to be drawn upward to the continental heat-sink canopy, as moisture to be wicked through a thousand miles of capillary forest, as a shape that will, given time, be taught the laminar way. You stand among inhabitants who have become their own architecture, who have traded the irregularity of animal life for the perfected note of the machine that grows, the industry that needs no spark. They are ancient in their configuration, young in every edge, vast, self-sufficient, and complete. They do not wait. They do not hunger. They only hold, and breathe, and hold again, in the perfect, particulate dark.
