What lives here is not a tenant. That is the first mistake, and the place has no patience for mistakes. You cannot look for a creature curled in the stones, some beast that wandered in and adapted. What lives here was not imported, was not born, did not evolve. It *precipitated*—condensed like the water on the unspillable rims of stone, falling out of solution when the contradictions of the declivity became too dense to remain inert. The altitude that should not host a bog. The factory that should not lack builders. The wound that should either heal or fester but instead hums. Into this pressure, an occupant crystallized.

It does not have a name. It does not need one. There is no other to distinguish it from.

At first, you will think the inhabitant is the place itself, and you will be almost right. It breathes with the three angiosperms, those waxy pillars not unlike lungs, and their translucent root-tendrils do not merely drink—they meter. They are capillaries in a body that has no skin, only infrastructure. The copper moss is its nervous system, faintly warm, conductive, relaying the minute telemetry of temperature and charge across the wet basalt. The gear-shelled beetles are not wildlife to it; they are thoughts. Autonomous, yes, but no more separate from its cognition than a neuron is separate from the mind. When they traverse the fungal shelves in their predetermined circuits, they are remembering for it. When their mandibles click in unison, they are repeating a word it has been trying to say for a very long time.

But if you are patient, if you stand in the knife-edge cold until your own breath joins the mist’s repeating eddies, the distributed intelligence will fold inward. It will reveal its node.

There is a figure by the pool at the base of the largest tree.

It is not always there. Or rather, it is always there but not always *placed*—sometimes it stands in the water, sometimes it kneels on the pyrite, sometimes it seems to be halfway inside the eastern wall, its upper body present, its lower half occupying the bruised violet seam in the air. Its form is a compromise between the organic and the industrial, though it did not choose either. Imagine a statue left for centuries in a bath of charged mineral water, its features eroded not into smoothness but into functional ambiguity. It has the vague suggestion of shoulders, a spine, a head, but these are suggestions only, the way a schematic suggests a machine. Its skin, if it is skin, resembles the fungal shelves—rust-orange, chemical white, thick as belting, damp with the mucous sheen that covers everything. Over this, in patches, grows the lichen that has colonized the pyrite. The circuitboard patterns are not random decoration. They are its face. When the pyrite sparks in silent discharge, the current crosses the plates of its cheeks, its forehead, and for a moment the schematic *brightens*, reading out a status that no human eye can interpret.

It does not move like life. It moves like process. When it lifts an arm—and it is always lifting an arm, or having just lowered one, or holding it in an intermediary position that seems to violate the angle of the elbow—it does so with the inevitability of the geothermal vent firing every 4.7 seconds. There is no intent in the gesture, only the completion of a loop. It is adjusting the factory that is also its body, tuning a machine that produces nothing but the act of tuning.

You will notice that the water flowing upward against the cliff bends near this figure. It does not bend toward it, but *from* it, as if the figure exhales a local gravity that is not downward but inward. The duplicated shadows cast by the largest tree fall at its feet, slightly offset, and if you watch long enough you will realize that one shadow belongs to the tree and the other belongs to *it*, though the figure itself casts no silhouette. It is the negative space of a thing that should exist twice but has been folded into one.

It thinks in terms of pressure. Not emotion, not image, but pressure—differential, equilibrium, flow. Its cognition is the sub-bass hum; its memory is the arrhythmic pock-pock-pock of the vent. It does not know loneliness because there is no category of "other" against which to feel absence. It knows only that the leaf must not land, the drop must not detach, the beetles must complete their circuits, and the protozoan clouds in the pool must continue their endless, beginningless flash. These are not duties. They are its constituencies. Its tissues.

But there is something beneath the equilibrium. A flaw in the process. The aerial creatures—the finned rotaries hovering in their frozen thousands near the eastern wall—are its anxiety made manifest. They do not move because it has forbidden them to land, and it has forbidden them to land because somewhere in the schematic lichen of its face, there is a fear. Not a fear of intrusion. A fear of *sequence*. To land would be to progress. To feed would be to need. To finish a circuit would be to arrive. And if anything arrives here—if the leaf lands, if the drop falls—the figure will have to become something else. It will have to wake from the factory’s sleep and discover what the engine was meant to build.

It does not want to know. It has never wanted anything. But the wanting is there anyway, fossilized in the stasis, a ghost of narrative trapped in the non-narrative machine. It stands in its pooling, upward-bending water, mantled in copper moss and mineral discharge, neither old nor young, neither constructed nor grown, and it tends the declivity with the absolute, inhuman devotion of a body to its own continued functioning. It is the soft trap at the center of the hard gorge. The wetness in the wound. The whisper that the hum is not meaningless.

If you speak to it, it will not answer. But the vent might skip a beat. The 4.7-second rhythm might hesitate, becoming 4.8, and in that lapse you will feel the entire declivity quiver with an attention that predates your arrival and will outlast your departure. The schematic on its lichen-face will shift, reconfiguring slightly, reading a new input. And the shadow that is not its own will step closer to the one that is, narrowing the gap between existence and its double, while the bronze-green light holds its breath, and the water, impossibly, weeps a little faster, as if the factory has dreamed you, and cannot yet decide whether you belong to the process, or to the error.
