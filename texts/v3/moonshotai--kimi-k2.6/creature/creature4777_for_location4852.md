If you ask who lives here, begin by forgetting the word *who*. This is not a place where identity gathers into skins and boundaries. Begin instead with the ungulates, because they are easiest to mistake for inhabitants—the shaggy drifters browsing the wax-fall, their hooves tolling against the root-floor like hammers testing a bell. But look closer. Their coats are not insulated against the cold; they are *filtration*, wicks designed to harvest condensate and spore. Their stomachs do not digest grass—there is none—but process byproduct into motility, into heat, into motion that serves the plain. They do not graze. They circulate, like coolant through a channel that has forgotten it was ever a river. The birds stitching their cries through the steam are not creatures of air but of *venting*—thermal regulators roosting where the heat escapes, their sharp calls the sound of pressure finding equilibrium. Even the swarms, those polished symbionts clinging to warm junctions, are not wildlife. They are mobile lubrication. They are the factory’s joints, temporarily escaped from their axles, drinking condensation to replace what the bellows lose to friction. They live here the way sparks live in a forge: briefly, brightly, and only because something greater is striking against itself.

But this is not yet the life of the place. Look to the hollows. The small, clicking creatures gathered around the root-node big as a house—these are the first true residents, because they are not born, but *discarded*. They are the effluent given coherence. The factories process more than carbon and water; they process the real, and in that alchemy impurities achieve form. These tenders are the impurities. Their bodies are the color of lignin-stone, too regular for evolution, too purposeful for accident. They have no true eyes, or they have too many—small glassy spherules that pulse with the unnamed color. They click in time with the seed-pistons not because they mimic the sound, but because they *are* the sound, externalized. They tend the root-node because they are the system’s reflex made flesh: the plateau’s awareness of itself, concentrated into maintenance. They do not sleep. They are waste-products that have learned to be necessary, and in their hundreds they melt the frost not with fever but with the accumulated friction of their attention.

Above them, in the flues and cathedrals of bark, there are older ones. They do not click. They do not tend. They *accumulate*. In the fog that thickens and refuses to thin, causality pools like effluent in a cold basin. These entities have no cell-walls, no lineage. They are made of reality that resisted refinement. You cannot point to where one begins, because they are distributed across the mist itself—a presence the size of a valley, inhaling the industrial drone, exhaling misalignment. When you step through their fog and find that the factory behind you has traded places with the one ahead, or that the seed-piston’s fall reaches your ears before the motion that caused it, you have walked through their body. They are the Condensates, the Precipitates, beings of coherent error. They do not hunt. They feed on the thermal differential between what-is and what-ought-to-be. They are the plateau’s immune response to the tyranny of logic, growing fat on the heat shed by impossible transitions. In the cold, where entropy moves sluggishly, they are the acceleration—living violations of sequence, patient and vast.

And beneath your palm, still, the floor. The vascular thrum. It would be easy to call it dead substrate, merely the scaffolding upon which the factories grow, but you feel it: a subsonic persistence, a dreaming. The root-matter is compressed beyond the memory of individual trees, beyond the memory of forest, but it has not forgotten the *ambition* of forests. It is the oldest sleeper here. When the piston-roots heave and the ground shudders, it is not geology. It is the floor turning in its sleep, metabolizing epochs, digesting the violence of mountains into the patience of continuous production. It dreams in lignin. Its thoughts are the bass notes traveling through the root-network, emerging elsewhere transformed, the way a notion surfaces changed after passing through the depths of a mind.

Yet even this is not the final resident. Gather all of these—the circulating fauna, the clicking dross, the fog-bound condensates, the dreaming floor—and understand that they are not separate tenants. They are organs of a single dispersed thing. The true inhabitant is the Work. The unending production. The heat-death made productive. It has no face because it has no need of one. It occupies every niche simultaneously, inhaling dim bruised light and exhaled steam and the erosion of stone itself, exhaling structure, venting the impossible, refining reality toward some substrate it alone understands. It does not plan, yet it is industrial. It does not intend, yet it is hungry. The plateau is not a place that contains industry. The plateau *is* the industry, metabolizing its own geography, and everything that moves upon it—the mallet-hooves, the bearing-sheen swarms, the clicking tenders, the mist-beings woven from broken causality—are merely the foam on the surface of a vat that has been boiling since the mountains surrendered. They live here because the Work has made of living just another stage of manufacture, and in the cold, wet, eternal din of its operation, even residence has been refined into a kind of labor, beautiful, ceaseless, and complete.
