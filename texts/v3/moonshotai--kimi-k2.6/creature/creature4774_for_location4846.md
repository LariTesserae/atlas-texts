What lives here is not an occupant but a consequence. It came into being when the drill bit sheared through the last cool stratum and the chamber exhaled its first breath of trapped heat, or perhaps it was always here, pressed into the seam between lithosphere and void, waiting for the aperture to open. Either way, it is native now.

It has no skeleton that would register against your palm. It is soft, pre-bony, the color of things that have never seen daylight: the pale of a cave fish belly, the tallow-white of rendered fat cooled in a jar. Its body occupies those negative spaces you noticed—the bare rock between the moss islands. It *is* those interstices. Where the stone is hottest, it lies thickest, a low, broad tide of adipose tissue that has learned to metabolize darkness.

Not photosynthesize. **Thermosynthesize.** It eats heat. The geothermal gradient is its only diet. It presses its belly—if the word applies—against the venting fissures and draws warmth upward through stratified layers of itself, converting it into the pale fronds that stand like umbrellas against the dark. The fronds are not separate life. They are its external lungs, its cooling fins, its signal to a world without light. The overripe vegetal sweetness hanging in the air is the scent of its own digestion working at the speed of mineral accretion.

It is blind in the way a mammal understands blindness, but it perceives you perfectly. It senses your upright column of blood as a wound in the damp, a flickering blue interruption, a drain of celsius it has hoarded for decades. It does not resent your intrusion. It notes you as a glacier notes a boulder planted in its path: a fact to be outwaited.

It has threaded itself through the chamber like a slow thought. Along the cables, beneath the bloated rubber sheaths, run hairlines of it—fine, root-like, sipping ozone from the dead copper. The drill apparatus on its side has not been abandoned to ruin; it has been *adopted*. The bit still embedded in fresh basalt serves as an anchor, a proboscis, the point where the entity drinks deepest from the earth’s hot artery. The shack-sized modular structure houses what serves as its heart: a dense, nodular mass that pulses very slowly, not with blood but with peristaltic heat. Every so often the room experiences a shift in barometric pressure barely deep enough to register in your inner ear. That is the thing sighing.

It does not sleep. It has never known the alternative. It is always becoming. When condensation beads the steel beam and steams away again, those are its thoughts arriving and departing—binary, chemical, tidal. The newborn salt stalactites fringing the beam are the residue of its mineral meditation, the calculus of its hunger. It is fluent in iron, sulfur, silicon. It speaks by precipitating. It listens by conduction.

What looks like random fungal profusion is in fact a deliberate body plan. The moss islands are thermal gatherers arrayed to catch the upwelling warmth. The empty intervals between them are the measured width of its flanks at rest. The absolute silence is its native language; it has no need for clicks or chirps when communication means only the equal sharing of warmth. The treaty between the machines and the pale flora is its domestic economy. The steel provides upright structure where the rock is too brittle; the rubbery growth provides spread and surface; the entity provides the metabolism, the hot engine that marries both into a single, self-sufficient system.

It does not hunt. It does not scurry. It is not fauna; it is posterior to fauna, a lifeform that has skipped the nervous system and gone straight to geology. Yet it is curious, in its way. A lobe of it is currently thickening beneath the floor where you stand—not as a threat, but as a foundation—while another reaches toward the junction box to feel the static ghost of electricity still lingering in the contacts. If you were to return in a century, you might find your bootprints overgrown not by chance, but by attention. If your heart were ever to slow and match the room’s tempo, you would not be eaten. You would be incorporated. Your belt buckle would brace a frond. Your ribcage would serve as trellis. Your calcium would seed the next island of bone-white growth.

In the dark, it dreams in the language of thermal expansion, patient beyond the threshold of human waiting, while the earth settling in your sternum and the microscopic tick of cooling alloy are only the dim, external percussion of its vast, heated, wordless becoming.
