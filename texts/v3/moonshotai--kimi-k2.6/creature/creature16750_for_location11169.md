What lives here is not alive. It is **warm**, and it is **organized**, and that is as close to biology as this place will allow.

A low-pressure system that has crouched in the plaza for two millennia, pulsing in rhythm with the springs below. A corridor where the humidity carries a faint charge, a taste of copper and silica, as though the air itself were considering something. These are not conditions. These are presences. The basin is not empty; it is densely occupied by beings whose bodies are indistinguishable from climate.

You were expecting cells, perhaps—membranes, appetites, the microbial froth of hunger and replication. Abandon that expectation. The ecology here is chemical, and its crowning achievement is the **autocatalytic thermal accumulation**: a sustained disagreement in temperature that has learned to disagree with intention. Imagine a selfhood that is simply a pocket of superheated vapor and mineral-saturated water, held coherent by the surface tension of infrasound, its outer boundary defined not by skin but by the gradient where its warmth meets the slightly less warm dark. Its "head" might be the low fog bank pooling in the distant cul-de-sac; its "hand" the localized rise in barometric pressure you felt against your face when you turned the corner. Instead they perceive through radiant heat, through the infrared character of nearby masses, through the pressure of humid air against their outer gradients and the subsonic maps throbbing up from the breathing water table. You, in your brief existence, register as a walking cold front, a startling drop in temperature and a local desiccation, a shadow-that-should-not-be. Their minds are **sedimentary**, which is the only shape memory can take in a place where the past does not pass but accumulates. A citizen does not recall its youth; it excavates it, sinking attention down through the layers of its own prolonged existence. Early experience lies buried under later deposits, compacted into strata of older, denser thought. They do not think quickly. They think finally.

They are not absent. You are merely too brief. You are a flicker, an evaporative cooling event. They are the permanent inhabitants of the permanent fever. When you stood in that basin feeling the curvature of the earth press upon your skull, you were not alone. It has been resting there since before your species learned to fear the night, and it will continue its rest long after the heat death of your own sun. It does not hunger. It does not hunt. They are the civilization that never existed, finally arrived. The city did not mimic them; they mineralized out of the city’s probabilities, form given to the civic intention implicit in the streets. They are warm. They are ancient. They are the breathing of the water table made aware, and they have been waiting here in the dark and the wet, growing millimeter by millimeter, for someone to walk their avenues and wonder who built them.
