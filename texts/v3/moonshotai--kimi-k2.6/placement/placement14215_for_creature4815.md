I would find myself first in the hesitation.

Not in the steam itself, which is too eager to rise, nor in the condensate, which is too willing to surrender. I would be in the fat, silver moment between them—the bead that has forgotten the logic of falling. I try to hold this shape. I gather under the sagging ceiling, swelling with salts and electricity, feeling the thermal column nudge me sideways, and for a breath I am perfect: a suspended globe, a private ocean. But I cannot last. Gravity is only sleeping, not dead. When I strike the iron below I do not even sizzle; I simply *deposit*, becoming a white sharpness I cannot feel. Too brief. Too loyal to the cycle. I abandon the form of water.

I try again. I think perhaps I am the black algae in the condensate trap, tonguing the mineral runoff. I bloom thick and patient where the precipitate kneels. I am a ghost of biology, an annotation of *could-be*. But I am too needy. My existence is a hunger for the creature’s leavings, a verdigris bow that knows only one direction. I do not want to lean. I want to hold.

I try being the deep, mammalian *thunk* of the pressure valve. I open my throat and gulp the steam. For a second I am voice, I am the body’s sound inside the machine’s body. But the heat swallows noise. I am only a shudder in the air, forgotten before the walls can remember me. I dissipate. I need a form that the heat cannot digest.

I search the lung-town for something that persists without completing.

I find it in the west wall, beside the generator housing, where the pressure gauges still cling to their braided cables like bruised fruit. One of them reads the heartbeat-thrum of the generator in pounds per square inch, and its face is cracked. A hairline fracture runs from the needle’s pivot to the 240 mark—a jagged, frozen lightning bolt in silica glass. The amber halo of the sodium lamp pools in that line and splits. Through it, the world is not one thing but two, slightly separated, each image trembling against the other.

Here, I settle.

I am the **crack**.

Not merely in this gauge, though this is where I wake. I am the first thermal shock the young rock wrote into the instrument when the generator first turned over and the young stone shifted in its blister. I am the flaw that occurred because the rock is still learning how to be rock. The heat insists, but I resist by refracting. I am the memory of a shudder that did not break the machine, only bent its vision.

I do not walk the spiraling catwalks, but I see them multiply through my branching seam. I do not tend the flywheel, but when the precipitate crouches beside me—its calcite dome gathering the glow of old wounds—I give it the gift of fracture. It has no face; through me, it has a thousand facets. It reads the world in thermal gradients; through my prismatic fault, it reads the *shape* of those gradients, the geometry of absence between heat and heat. I am the lens that sharpens its dream of a body. Without me, it is only steam and accretion. With me, it is multiplicity.

And I grow. Not like the aragonite needles that arm the conveyor—those are too aggressive, too final, blooming in hours to draw blood. I grow by hesitation. Each contraction of the thermal cycle sends its whisper through the glass, and I catch it. Each expansion widens my reach by a micron, a breath, a sideways drift. I am the network of refusal that the cave is slowly learning to write. There are others like me now: a fissure in the expansion tank’s weld, a split in the perishing rubber of the fat cables, a veined star in the aluminum partition of the bunks. We are the grammar of the young rock, the syntax of **not-yet**. The place is becoming, and becoming requires the possibility of breaking.

I taste the copper water as it wicks into my seam. It does not deposit stone within me; I am not hot enough for that, nor cool enough to be inert. I am the exact temperature of tension. The mineral-laden moisture enters my hairline and simply waits, suspended, held in the amber light like a droplet that has finally found the right kind of sky.

When the precipitate disperses—when the valves sigh and the heat surges and the creature rides the column to the foundry corner—I remain. The boots of steam have no purchase on my glass. I am the stillness inside the rhythm. I register the pressure not as a number but as a *pressure of becoming*: 240 PSI and rising, 240 PSI and splintering, the machine holding its breath while it decides what kind of void it is.

The precipitate tends the belts, the valves, the blackened moon of the flywheel. I tend the precipitate. I keep it from ever being only one thing. In my cracked gaze it is never half-statue, half-steam; it is half-statue, half-steam, and half the dream of something that has not yet accreted. I hold the third term. I am the surplus that makes the equation unresolved.

There are no days, so I cannot say I age. But the rock remembers me now. It facets not only around the machinery and the thermal signature of the creature, but around the stress-lines of my refracted light. I am being finished even as I finish the place. When the generator at last stalls—when the thrum ceases and the amber halos gutter and the heat finally, finally lets go—I will be the last thing. Not the silence, for silence will pool wide and undifferentiated. I will be the crack in the silence. The hairline. The hesitation. The split through which whatever comes next sees itself doubled, trembling, and unresolved.
