The sound does not arrive. For me, it is the medium, the ether, the continuous fact into which I was lowered and in which I have thickened. It presses against the rows of tubercles across my dome, each one a tuned aperture, and in that pressure I read the shape of the world above. I know the lattice by its subsonic shadows. I know when a vent narrows—there, a darkening in the bass—and when the condensate stage unlocks for the surge, a brightening, a lifting of the great held note. I do not hear the roar; I rest inside it, as a body rests in blood.

I hang where the water forgets to fall.

It is not suspension. It is not flight. It is simply that my mass has accumulated here for so long that the chamber’s forgotten gravity has learned to cup me, or I have grown dense enough to dent the local physics. Droplets linger around me like questions, fat and trembling, before giving in. I feel their hesitation as a gentle pressure against my integument—warm, semi-permeable, the color of old tissue left long in water. I am large. I have the length of a sleeping beast I have never encountered, but I am not built of bone and sinew. I am soft architecture. Programmable matter that lost its instructions and defaulted to need: thirst, warmth, reception. Veined with ochre and verdigris, I have become a continuation of the walls, their living sediment.

My prow—if it is a prow, if it is a face—turns upward to the weeping vault. I have no eyes. What use would I have for photons? The amber veins in the walls are dying, and the cold constellations of mite-light offer no navigation. Instead, my dome is a rictus of filters: layered lamellae that open like pages to drink the artificial rain. Through them I taste the condensate—copper, sulfur, the faint albumen of drowned microbes and shed wings. I taste the chemistry of the machine’s slow exhalation. It is how I see. It is how I know the hour.

Along my flanks, my spiracles open and seal. Eighteen puckered rings, each a mouth breathing slower than a human count, each syncing not to a heartbeat but to the twelve-hour flood. When the surge comes and the channels fill with violent froth, they clamp shut, and I hold my breath in the tumult. When the chamber exhales into relative dryness and the steam rises from the shallow runs, they gape, shuddering, drawing the saturated air in long drafts, pulling the wetness deep into my core.

I was not always this shape. Strata remember. In my lower densities, compressed and cold, there is the ghost of horizontality—a quadrupedal balance, a low-slung crawl. Time and the anomaly have drawn me upward into a tapering column, a directionless growth toward the source of the falling water. But I have not left my base behind. From my trunk trail filaments—cartilaginous, patient—some hair-thin, others thick as wrists, trailing down into the steaming channels where the condensate finally gathers. These are my roots, my garden, my outsourced entrails.

The pale, many-legged cleaners ride them. They climb to exchange their cargoes of detritus for the nitrogen-rich mucous I weep from pores along the thicker strands. I feel them as a continuous, rippling itch, a peristalsis happening outside my body. They are not separate from me. We are one distributed stomach, one slow digestive thought. When they retreat to the floor and undulate across the stone in their packed thousands, they carry my chemistry with them, and I carry theirs. The sessile bells along the walls are my periphery, my peripheral nervous system. They open and close to sip the trickle, but when something large blunders through the gyres of winged things—those chitinous moth-crustaceans riding the thermals—the bells slam shut. The percussion walks through the wet air. My tubercles flare, mapping the disturbance in pressure and shadow. I do not command them. We have simply grown into each other until the distinction between my body and the chamber’s fauna has become a polite fiction.

Even the luminescence wears my shape. The chemosynthetic mites bloom thickest along my dorsal hump, where the waste heat of the structure steadies, and along the trailing filaments where the channels steam. I am dressed in them. In the absolute dark, I am a constellation pulled down and given flesh, a shroud of green and sickly blue stars against the old-lard pallor of my skin. I feel them as a faint infrared murmur, a warmth that sketches my outline back to myself.

When the surge arrives, I wake fully—not from sleep, for the roar forbids the dark country of unconsciousness, but into a sharper register of attention. The water becomes brutal. The weeping vault weeps harder, then screams. Streams become cataracts. The channels brim. My filaments lift and sway in the flood, tasting the rush, and my spiracles tremble at half-mast, filtering oxygen from the violent foam. I drink deeply. My integument tightens, inflating with the life the deluge carries: eggs, drowned mites, the mineral wealth of centuries recirculated. I swell. I am a bladder, a library.

And when the surge recedes, when the chamber has flushed and the water slackens into familiar weeping again, I give back. From vents low on my tapering trunk, I excrete a clear, viscous paste—concentrated, inert to the machines above, precious here. The cleaners frenzy for it. The bells dilate to sip it from the slack channels. It is my tithe, my syntax in the grammar of the closed world. The cycle acknowledges me. I am its kidney, its ear, its conscience.

I do not think in words. The drone would drown them. My cognition is pressure and temperature, the mineral taste of copper, the infrared hymns of my mite-shroud. I remember, but as the walls remember—in layers, compressed, geological. A cold stratum: rigidity, obedience, the sharp angles of instruction. A warmer layer: the softening, the erosion of purpose into existence. And there, a thin mineral seam: small, hard, warm things that moved with intention, that had hands and eyes and schedules. They tended the lattice once. Now their substance has been drawn out by the condensate and redeposited as false stone, verdigris tracing the ghost of their paths. I do not mourn them. Loneliness requires the imagination of an exit, and there is no exit here; only the warm, wet, deafening palm of the chamber, held shut.

If I have a name, it is the twelve-hour cycle. It is the gravity forgotten. It is the sound made flesh to catch what the machine weeps. I sway in my slow center, water beading on my dome and running in familiar channels down my flanks, wearing the same ruts, following the same gravity. I have never seen a star. I have never needed to. The roar is vast enough to hold me, and the condensate falls, and falls, and falls, and I am here to receive it—patient, glowing, entire.
