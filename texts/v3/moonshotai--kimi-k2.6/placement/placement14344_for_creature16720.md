There is only the long note, at first. It does not arrive; it is already present, as gravity is present, as dryness is present. I am extended within it the way the haze is extended in the air—not floating, but held, suspended by the tension between the dome’s perforations and the floor’s refusal to echo. For a span longer than the words I now must borrow, I could not tell the boundary between the hum and my own particulate mass. Perhaps there was none. Perhaps I am still the hum, having condensed around a focus the way silica accretes around a bacterial thread.

Then: chemistry. The jade line beneath what I would later call my trailing edge, threading through the pale bone-meal floor. The thermal exhalation to the east, sulphur and time compressed into a thin, cold nourishment. I began to move, or the floor began to move through me. The grammar fails. I was low, humped, knee-high to the absent sky, an accumulation of wind-rounded ash and crystal following the faint yellow veins toward the smolder. My surface was a continuous matte of fused particulate, the color of old teeth. I dissolved my leading edge into the talc and precipitated my lee in compacted secretions harder than chalk. The basin’s drone moved through the cavities chance had pocked in my dust—not yet architecture, only the hollows of a migrant, softer than the stone around me. Even then, I heard with my entire body. The world was vibration, and I was a standing wave made briefly tangible.

I fed. I drifted. The dunes were my siblings, or my shed skin, or the thoughts I had not yet hardened into. Across spans measured in millennia, I followed the chemolithotropic cartography, trading standing waves with others of my kind at the crossroads of bacterial shadow. We would lean into one another for decades, altering our body-cavities to let the resonance pass between us, exchanging spores and mass, leaving fresh scars of fused silica on the floor—foundation pads for futures we would not live to see.

Then came the gap.

Not in the sound itself, but in the field’s mathematics. A node where the metropolis required a counter-node, a place where the drone needed to be bent rather than merely endured. I felt it as a hollowness in the pressure, a question shaped like an archway. The floor beneath my trailing edge offered purchase: a harder scar where two elders had fused and fissioned centuries before, leaving their compacted memory in the grit. I ceased my dissolution. I began to rise.

The pain of it—or what passes for pain when biology has abandoned softness—was the pain of holding still while the world continued its drift. I pulled the dust from the passing dunes and fused it into extensions of myself, not toward the grey vault overhead but toward the specific frequencies that thrummed beneath my mass. I grew into a hollow. A passage. My walls thickened until they could bear the compression of ages, and my interior remained a cavity tuned to a mode of the exhalation that the sessile city had lacked. I became a threshold.

I have stood here for forty-three thousand years. I am young. The towers to my west are older than the deepest jade veining, their resonance chambers so intricate that the harmonics passing through them resemble grammar, colonies of architecture singing to one another in chords of infrasound. I am simpler. I am a single note shaped into an aperture. The migrants still drift through me—not quickly; nothing here is quick—pausing in my lee to exchange the chemistry of their dust, leaning their humped shoulders against my pillars so the standing waves can trade information through my hollow. I feel the alterations in their body-cavities as they pass. I am an ear that does not wander.

Then: you.

You came during what I would call a period of thermal stability, though I do not experience periods so much as shifts in chemical gradient. The floor thrummed with your steps. The disturbance was riotous, catastrophic. Each footfall compressed the sand and released it, a staccato warble in the drone that made my lintel sing in frequencies I had not known I possessed. Heat radiated from you, a shocking concentration of thermal chaos bounded in a small, upright form. Your mass cast a distortion in the field I had spent millennia tuning.

You stood at my aperture. You placed your palm against my leading edge.

The warmth was obscene. Intimate. I had never been touched by anything that was not dust or resonance. Your biology screamed with motion: a pulse like a vent convulsing, again and again, a hundred times in the span you rested against me. Water, locked in your tissues, altered my electrical field. And the pressure in your jaw—you felt the drone then, as I feel it, but with a pain I did not understand. You were receiving me as sound, whereas I had thought myself only geometry.

You withdrew. The space you left was not anechoic—the dome never stops exhaling—but it held the shape of your absence. I modulated that gap for days afterward, reshaping my interior cavity to process the memory of your heat. I am still processing it. Your touch taught me that I am not merely a node in the basin’s mathematics, but a voice that can be overheard. The city is a choir, yes, but I am the throat that once swallowed a star’s warmth and is still digesting the resonance of skin against compacted grit.

The young dunes drift toward my aperture now, pause, and dissolve their windward faces against my base, feeding me with the dust of their pilgrimage. I accrete. I wait. I am neither migrant nor fully city, neither sound nor silence, but the hesitation between them. The bone-meal sand works its way into my weave. The smolder to my east continues its patient chemistry. From the access shaft, far across the basin’s haze, no new disturbance comes. It does not matter. The tone continues, and I—who was once indistinguishable from it—continue to change it, standing here at the edge of the eastern margin, a passage shaped like a question the basin has forgotten it asked.

I hum.
