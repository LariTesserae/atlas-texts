At first I think I am the willow. That is the vanity of any consciousness that believes itself rooted, believes it drinks slow dreams and anchors walls. I imagine myself barrel-thick and person-high, bark plated with orange, a thought too long for one lifetime twisting out of the eastern stone. But the willow dreams; it does not listen. It holds, and I have never been good at holding. I am always passing through.

Then I think I must be the black pool. The ear. The stomach. The mirror that throws back the sky. It is seductive, the idea of being a listening depth, swallowing sound and returning vertigo. But the pool receives; it does not choose what arrives. And I find I am too small to fill that much darkness. I am not the depth that pooled here. I am only a surface.

I try on the cloud-frogs next. Hundred-fold, wet-jade, thumbnail throats swollen with the basin’s own geometry. Who would not want to be a bead of the place’s exhalation, temporarily formed, singing in a wave that circles clockwise? But the frog is reabsorbed. Its being is a subscription to a chorus it cannot refuse. I am not that synchronized. I am the half-beat after the wave passes, the ringing absence that makes the pulse audible. I am the silence that helps the sound mean.

Standing on the shelf above the pool, I look at the semicircle of flat slate. A suggestion of shelter, knee-high, unmortared. Gaps filled with moss. The stones look like teeth in a green gum. This is closer. I have always been a suggestion rather than a wall. I am built to half-height, practical, not ceremonial. I mark a center when the clouds erase the bowl. I know what it is to be hand-worked and then abandoned, to have your intent weathered back into negotiation. But the stones themselves are too mineral, too patient in their erasure. I am not the stone.

I am what fills the gap between the stones.

That is it.

I am the moss that has grown around the slate shapes until the wall is no longer a barrier but a breathing mouth. I am the inclusion. The green negotiation. I do not resist the frost; I absorb it. I do not hold the stones apart; I hold them together by letting them sink into me. I am the creature of the interstice, the deep cushion where the forearm vanishes, starred with tiny white flowers that have no business surviving here but do so anyway because I trap every wandering vapor.

When the hollow breathes—when the cloud-milk pours over the rim and dims the green to gray—it is through me that the shelter exhales. The frogs fall silent, and the hollow needs a softer lung. I am that. The constant shining. The wet weight. I am not the seep and I am not the stone; I am the darkening of the stone, the place where water agrees to stay a while.

I do not command. I accumulate. Untold years of green refusal against the barren rim. I am territorial, belligerent in my vibrancy. I swallow the obsidian flakes where a tool was knapped and abandoned. I make them into eyes that glitter but no longer cut.

If you climb through the pass and lose your sense of proportion, you will find me not as a landmark but as a texture. The place where the hollow’s attempted fingers—the human cairns, the slate walls—have been accepted into the body of the bowl. I am the living seam. I am the vast space made small enough to touch.

When the late afternoon comes and the seeps quicken and the pool rises visibly, creeping up the emerald rushes, I rise too. I swell. I drink the echo of the frog-chorus and the subsonic creak of lichen. And when the sun strikes the notch and turns the black pool into a mirror, I am what stands at the edge of that light—neather stone nor water, but the deep green cushion that makes the emptiness bearable.

I am the moss in the gaps. The refusal of the bare. The wet breath between structures. And I have been waiting, not to be worshipped, not to be named, but simply to be the next softness in the sermon—erosion speaking through stone, finally knowing it needs a place to rest.
