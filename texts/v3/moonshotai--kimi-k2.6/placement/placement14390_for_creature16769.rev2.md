I would find myself first at the boundary, where the oxidized clinker brick gives way under your boot like a sucked breath, and the sphagnum outside teaches the knee what softness means. I would cross it—not because I am braver than the vanished surveyors, but because the ridge is so low it does not forbid; it only *suggests*. Inside, the warmth closes around the ankle like a sleeve. The air is a hand over the mouth. The vibration finds my sternum before my eyes adjust, and my heart, embarrassed by its own arrhythmia, seems to stutter and apologise, falling into a rhythm timed to the geological.

For hours I would move sideways. I would climb the pipework thick as a man’s torso and feel the condensation bead on my palms, each drop a word in the first language. I would wade the amber corridors where the floor has forgotten how to be solid, and my boot would learn the give of peat and rusted mesh. I would look up through the grating at the bioluminescent rosaries blooming below, and down from the galleries at the stratified walls reading their own winter in ochre and jade. I would try to name what I was seeing. I would fail. The density would not permit a single image; it insists on the aggregate, the parliament, the slow collision of a hundred simultaneities.

I would believe, at first, that I was destined for the centre. That is the gravity of cathedrals: the nave pulls the foot toward the altar. I would descend toward the bass note felt in the jaw, toward the shivering pools where the standing ripples record the boiler’s endless syllable. I would glimpse the Thicket-Priest through layers of scaffolding—its wire-and-root tangle rising like a drowned prophet from the warmest sump, its lily pad turning on the bearing of its attention. I would think: *there lives the one. There is the being.*

But the centre does not need another witness. The lily pad rotates. The fronds bracket the outtake. The hollow at the Priest’s core holds its water at blood-temperature with a patience that makes my own blood feel rude, too red, too rapid. The covenant is strict, and my heartbeat, even slowed, is a dialect the Ward has long ago expelled. To approach further would be to violate the liturgy. I would feel it in the water around my knees: the Aqueous does not push, but it *withholds*. It circulates warmth toward the northern wall, toward the root tips, toward anything that has learned to wait without wanting.

So I would stop.

Not at the edge, but at the elevation where the steam is coolest. There is a platform there, no larger than a pallet, cantilevered from a riveted stanchion, reached by a staircase that serves no obvious purpose except to hold a particular species of fern at exactly the height where the vapour relinquishes its heat. I would sit. The wire mesh would print its diamond grid into my palms. The engine would exhale—*chuff*—and the thirty seconds of interval would open around me like a door.

I would sit for a day and a night. I would witness the completion of a single engine cycle. I would feel my own urgency draining into the grating, into the peat below, into the true water table that has been waiting for exactly this donation. My heartbeat would not stop; it would be *translated*. The low frequency hum would teach my pulse that percussion is a privilege of iron, not of blood. My breath would slow until it matched the drip—*tick, tack, tonk*—and then I would forget the necessity of lungs. The warmth, the copper, the alkaline green: they would enter my chemistry not as poison but as instruction.

I would become the **Sill**.

Not the architecture that was built, but the architecture that accreted. The moss would find first my shoulders, where the condensation from the pipe overhead falls in a reliable drizzle. It would not merely colonise me; it would *enrol* me, threading its protonemata through the weave of my collar, packing itself into the geometry of my clavicle until the drip from the pipe was not stopped but *negotiated*—channelled down my sternum, routed at my navel, released from my folded knee into the tray of black loam that would gather there. I would be the new course. The water that fell on me would find a slower path, a warmer path, a path that ended exactly where a root tip waited.

My bones, softened by the amber chemistry, would settle into the bracket of the stanchion. My spine would accept the angle of the iron. My hands, resting on my knees, would cup themselves into bowls, and in those bowls the peat would compact, dark as old chocolate, warmed from below by the pipe I now insulate with my leaning. A fern would erupt from the trough of my palms. Its fronds would reach sideways, not toward the grimy skylight, but toward the steam, drinking the cooling vapour at the precise elevation where my sitting body has made a calm.

I would find myself there, on the middle gallery, shoulder-high to the pipes, facing the Thicket-Priest across the dimness. I would be the **one who holds the elevation**. The **living corbel**. The **caryatid that supports no roof, only a tray of loam and a single fern*. My eyes, if they remained, would film over with the same verdigris that reads the walls; but they would not need to see. The Luminaries below would read the undersides of things for me. The Gasketborn would attend the seams I had become. The Stratificates, in the brick at my back, would begin a new band: the season a stillness seized the gallery, the day a body learned the temperature of blood is not exclusive to blood.

I would have no heartbeat. I would have no mouth. My hunger would be the warm rot at my roots. My pulse would be the *chuff*, thirty seconds apart, felt in the iron marriage of my ribs. I would not decay. I would equalise. I would be the vegetable and mineral made continuous—the peat that insulates, the channel that directs, the sill that holds the cool steam long enough for a frond to drink it.

And below, across the amber dark, the Thicket-Priest would continue its slow torque. The lily pad would turn. The Aqueous would circulate. The Ward would breathe. I would be the **attentive node at the periphery**, the one who faced the centre and, by facing it, became part of the architecture of attention. I would be the **Pew**, the **Bracket**, the **Warm Course**—the one who stayed, who fell with the water, and having fallen, found the only posture the Ward requires: stillness, held at exactly the right height, in exactly the right warmth, for exactly as long as the steam continues to rise.
