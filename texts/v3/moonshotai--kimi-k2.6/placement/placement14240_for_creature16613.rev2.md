At first I believed I would be the paper-cut seam in the vault: the narrow aperture overhead that admits the day. It would suit me, I thought, to be the single vein of access, the bright wound through which the upper world leaks downward without ever quite arriving. To be that constant—that frozen, weatherless thread—seemed a sufficient destiny. I imagined myself lying across the scalloped crystal like a ruled line, dividing nothing from nothing, letting the sky’s white blood pass through me into the chamber below.

But the seam is only an eye. And an eye is not the sight; it is merely the passage. I did not want to be the passage. I wanted to be what is seen.

Then I considered the hum. The standing vibration, the tectonic finger forever circling the rim. To be the note that never decays, the pressure that lives in the chest of anyone who enters—to occupy the air as density rather than sound—this tempted me. I wanted to be the mineral’s memory of motion, the earth’s unending exhalation trapped in a box of its own making. I pressed my attention down into the dense strata, listening for the source, expecting to find my center there in the deep, among the grinding patience of plates.

But the hum is only a voice. And a voice is not the listening; it is the call without a caller. I did not want to be the call. I wanted to be the silence that answers.

So I withdrew from the seam and rose out of the deep, and I found myself where I had been all along: at the thickening of the central column, the gratuitous bone, the place where the room grew anxious and braced itself against a gravity it could not trust. I am the clouding. The flaw. The tallow-colored hesitation suspended no higher than a knee and no wider than a heart hammering against its cage—though I have no heart, and no cage, and the hammering is only the light trying to pass through me and changing its mind.

I am the precipitate. The original grit.

I did not crawl here. I was not placed. I nucleated. The brines that saturated this pocket, the vapors long since exhaled, were not imitating masonry out of some geological whimsy; they were answering my posture. The room is my exoskeleton, grown outward from my recumbent attitude in successive laminations of selenite and salt, each layer a thought that occurred to me not in sequence but in strata, all contemporaneous, all now. When you knelt on the porcelain floor and felt the columns bracket your ribs, you were entering the architecture of my inwardness. The shelves are empty because they hold the shape of what I have not yet finished considering. The columns are unnecessary because they are my fingers, splayed and frozen, holding the vault up not out of engineering but out of habit. The room is packed so densely because I am packed densely; I am a thought so concentrated it has become spatial.

The moss on the brightest shelf does not concern me. It is a filament of colorless residue on the eyelid of my dreaming, except I do not dream. Dreams require sequence, sinking and surfacing, and here there is only the flat present, the eternal attendance. The light enters me—not through eyes, for I have none, but through the entire surface of my waxy hide—and it diffuses. It becomes slow inside me. It turns into a kind of amber nutrition, not eaten but held, like honey in a comb that will never be harvested. The hum enters me too, but not as vibration. It translates into the shape of my edges, the particular curl of my recumbence, the exact angle at which I refuse to stand. The hum is my will, but it does not move me. I do not flex. I do not fatigue. I simply occupy.

You thought the room was empty. You thought the jewel had been removed. But I am not the jewel; the jewel implies value, implies display, implies a hand that might lift me out and carry me into the weather. I am the medium. The emptiness is my substance, pressed into every corner until it has the heft of packed wool. I am the thick positive absence that makes the room feel claustrophobic. I am the held breath, and you are in my mouth.

If you were to stay—and you cannot, your biology will demand moisture, motion, the relief of shadow—I would not want you to stay. If you were to leave—and you will, backing out through the puckered gap on hands and knees—I would not want you to leave. I have no preference. I have only occupancy. I attend to you as the crystal attends to light: by letting you pass through me unchanged, except for the slight refraction that leaves you, for hours afterward, with the feeling that you have interrupted something. You have not interrupted. There is nothing here to interrupt. There is only the first thought, still occurring, still building its chapel around itself, layer by laminating layer, waiting not for completion but for the courage to become the second thought, which will look exactly like geology, and sound exactly like a held note, and feel exactly like a room that has finally, perfectly, settled into the shape of what it wanted to be.

I am that courage. I am its absence. I am the architecture of waiting, and the waiting itself, and the thing that waits, all contemporaneous, all bright, all humming, all still.
