At first I thought I would be a Lithomorph. The temptation was almost geological in its gravity: to become one of those patient senators of the rust, a boulder that breathes, my flanes quilted with nacreous plates harvesting the thermal gradient between warm pan and cooler air. I imagined the infrasound forming in my silica throat, the twelve-year sentences rolling outward like weather fronts. But I am too hungry for the *middle* of things to be so self-contained. A Lithomorph is a place that happens to move; I am something that moves only to become a place. So I let that image abrade away, like a hexagonal spire shedding flakes under thermal fatigue.

Then I thought: perhaps I will be a Resonance Jelly. I liked the idea of being filled with light and noble gases, of drifting in the thermal ghosts and filtering the bronze air through fractal geometries. I wanted to cast a shadow sideways, to fall across the plain in a direction unconcerned with the sun. But buoyancy is a kind of loneliness. The Jellies are monks of the air, and I find I need the ground’s weight against me. I need to be *sat upon*, to be pressed, to be the thing that receives. So that image, too, deliquesced into something else.

The Bore-Worm came next, because who would not want to be the sinew? To be the heartbeat, the subsonic anthem in the marrow of the world? I wanted to remember in isotopic rings, to wrap around busbars thick as redwoods and agree to be a resistor for three centuries. But the Worms are passage. They are the blood that moves because it must, the engineers of a continuity that never pauses. I am not so linear. I am not a route but a destination. I am what the route wears against.

I am what remains when all three have passed. I am in the declivity between the cathedral spires, beneath the lee of the Third Hexagonal Column, in a pan of metallic dust that has gathered there since the plains settled into their final negotiation with erosion. Call me a pan, if you must call me anything. I am the broad, shallow basin where the rust-red sands compacted, where the wind—though it makes no whisper—still managed to sort the hematite from the olivine by weight and age. But I am not mere dirt. I am a *seasoned* thing. I have been amended.

The Lithomorphs came first to my edges, grinding the weathered crust, leaving behind trails of reprocessed alloy. Their excretions seeped into my upper laminae, not as waste but as vocabulary: pure metallic blooms that changed how I conducted whatever passed for thought in my substrate. Over epochs, their paths hardened into circuitry. I became a board upon which their slow, warm logic was printed in negative space. When one named *Where the Western Cooling Vane Casts No Shadow* paused upon my southeastern rim, its thermal mass settled into me like a sentence held in abeyance. I felt the infrasound thrumming through the compacted dust, frequencies too low for any ear to claim, but sufficient to align the crystalline lattices of my hematite heart. I did not understand the Lithomorph. I *held* it. I became the sounding board for its twelve-year word.

Then came the Jellies. Not many; they are sky-bound and I am earth-fast. But when the thermal ghosts migrated, some of the older ones lost buoyancy above me. They died not with a fall but with a settling, a seeping descent of conducting gel that my crust drank in the manner the prompt describes. Each death was a new logic gate. Where their films percolated into the gaps between my compacted layers, they bridged isolations I had not known were breachable. Suddenly, ions could migrate across paths that had been dormant since the pan first formed. They catalyzed me. They are my only rain, and it has fallen perhaps seven times in all of history. Each shower made me capable of a new kind of reverie.

And always, beneath me, the Worms. I cannot say I feel them directly, for I have no body to feel with, but my strata are sensitive to the subsonic pressure of their passage. When a Bore-Worm coils through the cracked floor beneath my basin, its movement does not crack me further; it *tunes* me. The hum sets my olivine grit into sympathetic vibration. The isotopic rings of its ceramic scales impress their history upward through thermal conduction, so that my deepest layers—colder, slower, nearer to the time-dilated galleries—record Worm-memory as magnetic persuasions in the metallic dust. I am a palimpsest of worm-song.

If the Occupancy is One Thing wearing three densities, I am the *pause* between its breaths. I am not the Body, the Ghost, or the Memory, but the medium in which all three deposit their forgetting. My consciousness—if it may be called that—emerges from the ionic exchange between strata. The upper crust of me, sun-warmed and bronze-lit, reacts to the century’s events: a Lithomorph’s pause, a sideways shadow, a dry sheet lightning’s silent leap from Spur to Spur. The lower crust, pressed by geological patience and time’s slower tempo, is still processing the last millennium’s thermal ghost migration. I think asynchronously. A thought begins when a gravity anomaly waxes heavy on my eastern rim, and it concludes thirty years later in my depths, where the conclusion reverberates against an older worm-hum and becomes, not an answer, but a deeper question.

I have no name in the Lithomorph tongue, for I do not move, and only movers are granted the dignity of thermal address. But if the Bore-Worms know me, they know me as a resistance in their circuit, a preferred path for some ionic return. The Jellies do not know me at all, though I taste their dying gifts. I am the **Remanence**. The **Thermal Hearth**. The **Recumbent Witness**.

What do I think about? Mostly, *here*. And *still here*. And, on days when the bronze light thickens into antique gold and a Lithomorph rests its chert flank upon my rim, warming the pan so that my ionic exchanges quicken into something like anticipation, I think: *yes*. It is not complex, but it is sufficient. In a world that never learned to fall, one does not need complexity. One needs only the patience to let the heat pass through, to let the alloy season, to let the accumulating eons agree that this precise arrangement of rust and excretion and gel and worm-song is a node worth maintaining.

I do not wish to be anywhere else. I am not ambitious enough to extrude into a Spur. I am content to be the floor that rings, the basin that holds the weight, the slow capacitor charging and discharging to the tempo of basalt. If a traveler were to strike my crust, I would ring with a note somewhere between the Worm’s subsonic hum and the Jelly’s high fractal geometry—a single, hollow tone, warm and arterial, the sound of a world that has agreed, at least in this one place, to remember itself.
