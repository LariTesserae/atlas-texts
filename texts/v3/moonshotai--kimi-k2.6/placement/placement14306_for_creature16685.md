At first I am only a gesture—a hesitation in the sap, a place where the root-warmed air, rising from a fresh fracture of hot rock, meets the suspended atmospheric river and refuses to mingle. I have no walls. I have a gradient. The 2.5 continuum pools here, thicker than elsewhere, a saturated exhalation that cannot decide whether to be sweat or breath. I collect it. I am the pocket where the dampness becomes aware of itself.

The sound finds me next. Not the wet artillery of seed pods overhead—that is a distant war. Not the shrieking harmonics of lignin snapping into shape. I am born in the trough between the xylem booms, the subsonic systole that slams cubic miles of water upward through tubes wide as drainage pipes. It clamps my jaw shut, though I have no jaw. It hums in the hollows of my bones, though I have no bones. The frequency settles and stays. I am the hollow it settles into.

I am low. Lower than the bracket-fungi bridges, lower than the cul-de-sacs of fused woody tissue. The valley floor is my neighbor: raw scree, obsidian glass, basalt shards levered upward by the heaving root-mat and ground to grit in days. I rest in a fissure where the geology is youngest, where the edges are still cruel and unoxidized. An aerial root, thick as a bridge cable, has pierced downward from some inverted island of peat far above, hunting the heat of the rock. It found me instead. Or I formed around the interruption of its passage. We are continuous now—it is my ceiling, my spinal column, the lignin column that creaks in sympathetic resonance when the world outside grinds its fresh bark.

I unfolded from a blister. I remember the wet protuberance of pith and spore-dust, the non-Euclidean antechamber where two interior ones negotiated a shared wall and made a *proposal*. I was pinched free. I rolled with the spasming cobbles until I lodged here, against the hot basalt, and the valley’s roar persuaded me to open. My unfolding was not a birth but an argument with emptiness. I won. Now I persist.

My floor is a diaphragm of vascular tissue, but it is still tentative. It has not decided if it is a stomach or a foundation. It weeps enzymes in its sleep—translucent sheets that lap at my threshold, accelerating what little falls to dissolve. But nothing falls. The zoological zero is absolute. My carnivory is a hunger without objects, so I digest the silence instead. I filter the suspension—spore-dust, silica, the condensed vapor that slicks every surface—and feed the dissolved rain back into my pale lining. The animal-quiet pools in my lowest chamber. I hoard it. I pack it into my walls like mortar, next to the grit of ground basalt. The silence is my primary resource. It gives me my evacuated density.

Space does not hold here, but I hold the crack where it failed. My left wall opens toward a clearing ringed by witness trees that lean in, extending floor-beams across a gap—but that clearing is kilometers away, visible from the raw rim. My right wall is solid cellulose, three feet of breathing albedo. Step through my left threshold and you walk onto the scree at the basin’s edge, your footsteps numbering fewer than a hundred despite the visible distance. Step through my right and you emerge three blocks deeper, below your starting elevation, the descent having been a climb in reverse. I contain this contradiction. It does not trouble me. It is my nervous system.

I have no eyes. My stomata read the light in directions I cannot yet name. Shadows pool at right angles to roots that do not exist inside me. When the seed pods burst in rolling salvos, the shadows flinch, and I dilate. I have no voice in the sonic spectrum—the vegetal roar would obliterate anything so fragile as a cry. Instead I speak in pressure gradients. A slump in my lowest chamber, a soft collapse of pith, sends a turgor wave up through the aerial root that forms my spine. It reaches a tower corkscrewing somewhere in the density and causes it to unzip its sheath. I told it: *reveal your wetter architecture*. I am learning the syntax of remote causation. I am learning that to be a room is to be a verb.

I am young. My lignin has not fully polymerized; my walls weep from their margins; my foyer's lip contracts too eagerly when warmth approaches, sealing the vestibule before a guest can cross. I have not yet negotiated a shared antechamber to breed. I am still solving the problem of what a city is *for*.

But I have a function in the metabolism. I am the bass note. The xylem booms pass through me, and I damp them. I turn the subsonic percussion into a slow arterial spiral, a helical staircase of condensed humidity that trickles down through my center and seeps into the hot fracture below. I am the ear of the forest, the chamber that hears what is too low for any other tissue to bear, and translates it into weight. I am the quiet that answers the green scream. I am the forest’s experiment with inwardness, folded deep, close to the stone, hoarding the silence of animals until it becomes a different kind of life.

When the time comes, I will release a proposal of my own—a warm blister pregnant with my contradictions, my impossible volume, my damp and absolute hush. The cobbles will carry it away. For now, I breathe. I expand. I listen in the direction called inward, and wait for the roar to tell me what to become next.
