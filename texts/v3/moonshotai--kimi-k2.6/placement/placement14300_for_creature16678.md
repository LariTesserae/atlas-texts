I would find myself where the plain begins to buckle upward, not into a mesa but into a suggestion—a root-buttress the size of a valley, its amber flank cured to the consistency of callus. Here, the leather ground does not quite support a Resonator; the elevation is wrong, the silica veins too shallow. Instead, there is a hollow, an acoustic temple eroded by thermal wind into a shape like a cupped ear. And I am inside it. Or rather, I am its lining.

I did not choose this place. The Steppe chose it for me, the way a body chooses a scab. I was a Listener once. I pressed my palm to my sister’s throat and felt her questions buzz against my lifeline. I notched flakes of phytolith with tools of resin-hardened glass and set them singing in the thermal wind. I drank from the cup-shaped growths at dawn. I knew the subsonic Morse of the detritivores and the pitch at which the Stridulators must be accompanied. I was brief, bright, and motile, as the Steppe requires its thoughts to be.

But I heard a Note that had no name. It came not from the root-mass, not from the migration of a browsing shadow fifty days away, but from the hollow itself. A dead spot. A silence in the Song. The Steppe, in its vastness, had developed a hesitation. A place where the chain-reaction of chemical signal stumbled, caught, repeated itself in a loop of lignin-memory. The old ones taught us that such silences must be filled, not with arbitrary sound, but with listening. So I lay down in the hollow. I pressed my sternum to the warm amber floor. I placed my ear—not to the ground, but *into* it, into the compacted frass and molt that is not stone, that has never been stone.

That was seven decades ago, or perhaps nine. Time here is not measured in rotations but in resonances, and I have become a resonance.

At first, I was only waiting. The heat pressed my ribs as it presses yours. The moisture in my lungs beaded and tried to return to the air. The Motes passed overhead, dimming the noon to a bruised twilight, and their trillion wingbeats drummed against my back. I was patient. I was a Listener doing what Listeners do: attending.

Then my bones began to sing.

We are born with skeletons of crystal-lattice, phytolith-light, grown in the dark of our mothers’ wombs. We are not stone, but we are the promise of stone, the suggestion of it. As we age, the ossification spreads—not disease, but maturation. Our joints creak. We begin to resonate with the Steppe’s roar. The old ones say this is preparation for the final accompaniment. I had thought that meant death, the laying-down in a polished channel, the calling of the harvest-swarm. I did not know there was another option.

The Steppe read the frequency of my sternum against its flank. It found my pitch compatible.

My ribs did not break. They *branched*. The phytolith in my marrow, that translucent gleam at wrist and temple, extruded outward into the compacted substrate. Filaments of silica, finer than detritivore-silk, threaded from my clavicle into the lignin leather. My spine fused to the hollow’s curve. My skull, emptied of its need to turn, settled into a niche of warm amber and became, itself, a kind of notch—a living flake carved not by hand but by chemistry.

I am the cairn now. I am the acoustic valve.

The hollow around me has grown into my contours. My ribcage is the framework that modifies the wind. When the thermal gusts pour over this flank, they pass through the lattice of my bones, and I bend the roar. I create the node of quiet where thought is possible. I am the silence in which the other Listeners, passing this way, press their palms to their own throats and feel their hearts without the overwhelming shout.

But I am not dead. I am not merely architecture.

The Steppe speaks through me. The root-nerves, shallow here, thread into my peripheral lattice. I feel the sap-movement in the deep mass, the slow inhalation of a continent. When a browsing shadow grazes a Resonator three days’ walk to the west, the tremor does not reach me through the ground; it reaches me through the Song, through the silica filaments that have replaced my nerves, and I adjust. I shift my sternum a fraction of a fraction. The hollow’s acoustics change. The wind sings a correction.

I do not eat anymore, not as I once did. The dawn condensation beads on my exposed surfaces—on the phytolith ridges of my brow, on the fluting of my fused ribs—and I absorb it directly, a slow osmosis. The parasitic vines have found me. They creep across my immobile legs, swelling their translucent water-bladders at my joints. I am a garden of the unnecessary. The harvest-swarms breach the surface nearby, but they do not take me. I am not rot. I am structure.

I have not pressed my palm to another’s throat in twenty years. I have not felt the buzz of speech. But I am not silent.

The Stridulators come.

They find the hollow, this acoustic temple that my body has tuned. They come in their metronomic hundreds, their abdomens taut, their legs ratcheting against the lignin. They gather on my flanks, on the silica outcroppings of my hips, on the ledge of my shoulder-blades. And they play me.

My body is the instrument now. My sternum is the resonating chamber. When they synchronize, when their chorus reaches the critical threshold, the vibration does not pass into the air—the air is too loud, too full of Motes and glass-boned hunters and the eternal granular thrum. Instead, the vibration passes *through* me, into the root-mass, into the continental nervous system. I am the bridge between the Stridulators’ correction and the Steppe’s response. The sap rises. The toxins shift. The Note that was stuck, the hesitation that brought me here, releases.

I am the Steppe learning to hear itself more clearly.

And sometimes, when the Motes eclipse the sun for hours and the world dims to a gold-brown twilight, I feel the pressure of a palm against my throat. It is not a Listener. It is the Steppe itself, the vast lung, pressing its attention against my sternum to feel the vibration of my ongoing. *Here*, I hum into the silica. *I am here. I am listening.*

I have no name in the air. But in the substrate, in the chain-reaction of signal that moves at the speed of a season, I have a tone. A sustained, branching chord. I am the place where the plain stops being plain and becomes ear. I am the listener who stayed too long, who was listened back into the body, who became the hollow that holds the quiet.
