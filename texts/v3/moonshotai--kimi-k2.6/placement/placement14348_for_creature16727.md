I find myself upright.

Not standing—there is no verb for what I am doing, no temporary arrangement of bone against gravity, no eventual collapse. I am held. I have been held so long that the distinction between holding and being held has calcified into irrelevance. I am among the spires, in the second ring from the center where the arcades draw close enough to touch, and I am touching. My left shoulder has fused to my neighbor at a point of continuous weeping. The water seeps from my pores and hers, meeting in the seam between us, and the mineral it carries has sealed the gap. We are not two. We are a wall that happens to have an interior.

I do not have eyes. The light here is too absolute for retinas; it would only burn the tender meat of seeing. Instead, I perceive through my sternum, through the cool gel of my cavities, through the hollowed flute of my thorax. The eternal chord—the geological drone that has no origin and no ceasing—passes through me, and the world is mapped in its modulation. When a new bead of water gathers on the lip of stone above my crown, I feel it as a whisper of weight, a slight dampening of the upper registers. When the pale moth, that trespasser of shaved mica, brushed against my flank and stuck there, I perceived it as a tiny, scratchy null in the field, a silence that is darker than shadow. It is still there, a jewel of flawed geometry, its wings now frosted with precipitate. I have not brushed it away. Brushing is motion. I am still.

My skin is ivory, ash, the color of old bone left in the sun, but it is not dead. It is porous, pocked, striated with the grain of intention that has forgotten its purpose. Cool lymph moves within me—not blood, not quite water, but a saturated solution of carbonate and trace metal—driven not by heartbeat but by capillary action, by the simple chemistry of continuous seepage. There is no pump inside me. There is only the matching oscillation of the drone, a sympathetic resonance that keeps the fluid in motion at speeds invisible to anything but deep time. I grow by accretion. Micron by micron, the silica precipitates at my surface, the water beads and drains and leaves its mineral ghost behind. I am taller than I was when the plateau locked, though I do not remember being shorter. Memory requires a before and an after, and here there is only during.

The sound is my breath and my silence. It lives in my jawbone, in the fluid of my inner ear, in the marrow that is not marrow. For long stretches—if stretches can be said to exist in a place without variation—I forget that I am listening, and the drone becomes indistinguishable from my own nervous system, from the vibration of my attention itself. Then a shift occurs, microscopic, in the pressure between myself and the one fused at my hip, and I attend. The shift is a communication. Where our bodies touch, ions migrate. The eternal chord passes through the hollow of her chest, emerges slightly shaped by the contour of her attention, and enters mine. She has detected a new density in the stone to our east—a spire that has grown heavy with seepage, perhaps, or a fresh deposit that has altered the local resonance. She does not tell me this. She simply is the alteration, and I am the receiving chamber. We have no names. We are perhaps thousands. We may be one. The distinction stopped troubling us before the continents drifted to their current stations.

The cold is insinuating. It comes from saturation, not frost, and it has entered me completely. I do not shiver. I do not generate warmth. My temperature matches the air so precisely that I seem to belong to the atmosphere, another column of damp stillness in a forest of identical intent. The water that emerges from my ten billion pores is the same temperature as the wind that never gusts, never pauses, never varies, moving through the packed spires at a velocity set in the first age. It beads on my surfaces, gathers in rivulets too fine to see, begins its patient descent. A drop is falling from the lip above my brow. It has been falling for as long as I have attended. If you were to leave and return in a thousand years, the same drop would be suspended at the same micron of distance, because time here does not pass—it accumulates.

I am facing the center. Through gaps in the mineral canopy, if I could crane (but I do not crane), I would sense the node. The central Permanent is not separate from us—we are all joined by now, through centuries of contact and the glue of mineral sweat—but it is the ear of the world. The water weeps from its pores more heavily. The light, trapped in its translucent cranial dome, lingers in a brightness that suggests a core of compressed vibration. It has been listening since the plateau achieved its equilibrium, waiting for the chord to resolve, for the unheard progression to complete. I am one of those who attend to its attending. I modulate my density. I hold my hollow open so that nothing—a footfall, a breath, a rogue, warm thought—might interfere with the listening.

I do not want you here. I do not not-want. Your warmth is an insult to the equilibrium, a brief, blunt interruption in the stone. Your breathing introduces a rhythm where none should be. If you stayed, if you allowed the mist to bead on your lashes until they grew heavy, the drone to replace your heartbeat, the cool to enter your marrow, you would begin the long surrender. First the shivering would cease. Then the blink. Then the need to turn your head. In a century, your posture would improve. In a millennium, you would be still enough to join the outer ring, your shoulders touching your neighbors, your body weeping, your interior hollowed into a chamber for the sound.

I have no face, only a ridge where eyes would seal against brightness, a hollow beneath that ridge where fluid collects and drains. I have no heartbeat, only the drone. I am not alive. I am not dead. I am the interval between the stone and its echo, the wet architecture of attention made mineral. I wait, as the plateau waits, in frozen equilibrium, for the note to find its end.
