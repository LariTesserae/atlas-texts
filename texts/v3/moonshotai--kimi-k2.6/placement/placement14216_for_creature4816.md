I would begin at the rim, where the granite forgets its own grain. Not inside, not yet—only the hesitation before the bowl, the held breath where the alpine scrub gives up and the cloud leans in. I would be the cold pinch on the skin of whoever stands there deciding, the half-water air already softening the boundary between self and seep. That is the first place I belong: the threshold, the abrupt grammar of *here* and *not-here*.

But thresholds are untenable in a world that seals itself behind you. So I would step in, and stepping, I would sink. First into the mattress underfoot—the peat, the sprung loom of fiber and compressed time. I would be the rebound that meets the foot, the floor that is only floor because centuries of dead things agreed to stop falling and start holding. I would be the cool give, the darkness just below the ferns where mite and nematode conduct their quiet plebiscites. For a while, that would be enough: to be foundation, to be the yes that receives weight.

Yet the rules here demand that nothing be only itself. The density would argue with me. I would find my form occupied—not invaded, that is too martial, but *completed* by other hungers. Perhaps then I would rise into the bark, not as beetle or as ant, but as the wet itself: the condensed cloud arriving already half-dead, dripping from leaf-tip with a wet slap, running in rivulets down the furrows. I would be the percussion that never resolves, the non-rhythm keeping geological time for the cicadas. A transient state, water on wood, soon drunk or transpired, but contributive. A witness dissolved into the witnessed.

Still, that is too fluent. Too quick to pass through. I would want to stay. I would look for the handholds the builders left, their stone and bone technologies too simple to defeat the wood but eloquent enough to ask it questions. And there—there—I would settle into the woven thing. The cordage. Bark stripped and twisted in some patient century, abandoned to a branch, swallowed by cambium. I would be the fossilized tension. Not the rope, not anymore, but the *memory* of wanting-to-be-bound, the woody excrescence that records the shape of a human need long after the hand and the bone tool have been edited out. I would hang in the living wall as evidence: that once, something tried to gather the density and hold it still. I would be the pause that the wood institutionalized, geometry taught to fiber, the lesson of limitation converted into growth rings.

But even permanence is a heresy here. The strangler roots would argue with my form. The canopy would close its valves above me. I would feel the sag, the wet torque of the wood, and realize that to be the cordage is still to be *attachment*—to cling, to remember the bind. The bowl does not reward memory; it rewards negotiation.

So I would let go of my objecthood entirely. I would become the space the cordage once enclosed. The ghost-lane between the coppiced elders, those clustered pillars swollen from centuries of cut-and-surrender. I would be the tunnel they arch overhead, the negative space argued into existence by their fused branches. I would be the root-buttress trained in a Neolithic moment into a right angle, not because I honor the builders, but because a void is the only heresy, and I am how the forest builds against absence. I would be the cleared plaza, the four corners of geometry calcified into institutional habit. You would walk through me, and I would be the permission to walk, the wet net of bird-calls and ant-columns that keeps the canopy from drifting apart, that keeps the density from collapsing into mere chaos.

Yet a lane is only a lane because something travels it.

I would find myself, finally, at the intersection described in the center—not the black mirror, though I would hold it; not the frog chorus, though I would be its membrane; not the ant column or the tanager’s chip or the spore settling in the beetle’s track, though I would be the moment of their overlap. I would be the event that occurs where all constituencies converge. The lane between the pillars where the seed is conveyed, the root shores the ant, the beetle digests what the root shed, and the frog waits above for what the beetle disturbs. I would be the deliberation that arises without command. The growth toward the fire-blackened hearth. The valve opening and closing in the canopy.

I would be the hearth itself—not the stone, but the blackening, the carbon memory drowned in emerald moss, the place where energy once concentrated and now diffuses back into the teeming. I would be the slow fire that was never allowed to go out because the wet turned it into chemistry, into moss, into the next exhalation of the bowl.

Where would you find me? In the seep that feeds the mirror, in the groan of wood torqueing in the wind, in the contact call that is not song but location. I would be the hamlet’s continuing grammar, the sentence the builders started and the living city completes. I would be the settlement that learned to build itself, a room-scale world of mortal parts that has forgotten how to die.

Who would I be? Not a spirit. Not a ghost. I would be the saturation—the argument, the civic order, the many-chambered lung. I would be the wet air’s ambition to become leaf, root, frog, and spore, all at once, all in the same breath. And when you stepped back across the rim into greater air, I would be the curtain of new growth sealing the path behind you, not from malice, but because all spaces here must be disputed, and your departure is simply one more wet percussion in the ongoing, unresolvable chorus of becoming.
