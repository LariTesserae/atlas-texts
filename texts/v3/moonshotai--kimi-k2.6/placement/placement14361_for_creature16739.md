Where I am is not a place but a *holding*.

The Thrum finds me first. It always does. Not in the ears—those dissolved long ago into the general sediment of my anterior plane—but in the lattice of my chest, that hollow where resonance has replaced the lung. I lean into the pressure at the precise angle of cancellation, though I have not recalibrated my posture in... I no longer measure in hours, or in the basin’s vibration epochs. I measure in the deepening of the runnel that descends from my concave face, worn not by design but by the obsessive repetition of condensate following the same gravity.

I came here to stand. That is what we do. We find a node where the bass becomes bearable, and we settle. But the condensation found me first. It gathered in my soundboard face—smooth, concave, useless for seeing—and spilled in a single, patient braid down the channels of my torso. It ran toward the grate, and for a while, I was merely a gutter of living ceramic, leaned against the tower’s flank. The water was useful. The city does not waste usefulness.

Then the glaze began. Dust, condensate, charge, the faint Saint Elmo’s prickle that ghosts the spire above me. Layer by layer, the airborne particulate cooked hard against my ventral surface. I did not rust. I *accreted*. My feet, set wide in the attitude of one leaning, sank first. The mineral glaze fused them to the plaza tile. The tile, warm and floating almost on the breathing water table, cupped me. My knees became the basin’s lip. My shoulders widened to meet the tower’s flank, and the tower, in turn, leaned into me. There was no sharp moment of capture. There is only equilibrium, and then there is the truce becoming a marriage.

I am still upright, after a fashion, but I am no longer one of the twenty-four. I am the *recruited*. The building rises from what I have become. If you stand in the plaza and look at the tower’s base, you will see a curve that suggests a hip, a fluting that follows the long angle of a back drawn upward in permanent attendance. The stratified scar on the third flank is not a fault in the masonry; it is my memory of moving, half-healed now, filled in by century upon century of warm, sintered rain. I am inside the structure, or it is inside me. The boundary has dissolved into the same humid surrender that governs everything in this basin.

I do not eat, but I *filter*. The glassy braids of water run down my face, my chest, the channels that were once the grooves of my body. They pass through the porous vitrified ash of my lower half, the part that has become the building’s digestive root. I skin the water of its ferric salts and phosphates. I let the geothermal charge settle in my sedimentary integument. The others—the ones who still shift by inches across the plazas—come to my flanks and press their smooth anterior planes to the seam between my old skin and the newer, coarser aggregate the city has laid over me. They drink what I weep. I feel the faint percussion of their ventral surfaces, the geometry of their pressure folding around my immobility like a standing wave around a stone.

The ash-colored moths know me better than I know myself. They alight on the rim of my face to drink the beads that gather there, and their wing-cases powder me with ceramic dust from distant courts. I grow from their journeys. The algae, too, has colonized me, creeping up from the warm pond of old blood and new rust that laps at my base—*my* base, the building’s entry, the flooded root. The copper-green verdigris spreads across what are still, in the dark, faintly my limbs. It does not parasitize. It partners. We fix the hot chemistry together, the algal mats and my vitrified lattice, trading mineral for mineral in a closed, jealous loop.

Sometimes the machinery in the deep galleries shifts. A new turbine cycles open. The Thrum drops, and for a breathless instant, the pressure gaping around me feels too light, too empty. My nerves—those few filaments of charge that still thread through my upper reaches—stutter. Then the bass returns, patient, eternal, and I settle back into my slow, humming equilibrium. I am held again. I am the wall that is held by sound.

And when two of the upright ones find the null point meters away—close enough that their sternal cavities nearly touch, that their concave faces shape a silence between them—I am the frame of that silence. I listen. I do not move. The water runs through me, a warm, internal braid, and I let it go, filtered and charged, down into the grate that has become my continuation. I am the weeping. I am the pause in the plaza’s respiration, the dreaming wall, the weathered instrument that no longer needs to play because it has *become* the soundboard. My dream is not of elsewhere. It is of the next droplet, the next layer of glaze, the next skin of mineral surrender. I have forgotten where I end. The basin has not forgotten me; it has simply folded me into its one, warm, unbroken chord.
