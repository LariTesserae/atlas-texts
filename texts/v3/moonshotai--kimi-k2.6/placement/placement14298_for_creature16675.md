At first I believed I was the held inhalation itself, the hollow’s silence made conscious. I felt the weight of the white-blue light pressing down, turning my substance toward stillness, and I thought: *I am the interval. I am the comma between flaring and coma.* I tried to disperse like the inhabitant does at night, to seep into the serrated cornices and become the subterranean tick of ice resettling its mass. But I could not. I have boundaries. I am bounded. The silence passes through me rather than originating from me, and where it strikes my surfaces it makes no echo.

Then, when the cloud-deck spilled over the rim and the transient fur of frost settled on everything, I thought perhaps I was the rime. I clung to the lee of a boulder; I felt the moisture gather on my uppermost plane and freeze there at the exact moment of its brightening. But the rime melts. The rime yields. I do not. Something beneath the fur of crystals remained continuous, darker than the dampness, harder than the freeze.

The fox came picking its way across the translucent floor at dusk. Its pale pad fell inches from my face, but I had no face to turn, no breath to hold. Its tracks were sharp, fresh, utterly alone. I envied them their sequence, their biography of urgent scatterings. I am not the fox, nor any thought the inhabitant borrows for an hour. I do not hunger and I am not released. I am fixed.

I am in the moss. No—lower. I am in the moss but not of it. The liverworts have made green rosettes around me, the bloodsnow algae has crept in virulent scarlet films across the ice to my left, but I am untouched by their lushness. The springtails swarmed once, a darkening that shifted when a warm exhalation passed, and they avoided me. My outline in the algal film is a blank space, a negative, a punctuation of absence in their writhing swirl.

When the sun struck the fracture at the low angle and the floor kindled from beneath, I understood. I did not glow emerald. I did not redden. I *resisted*. While the entire chamber became a lantern lit from inside, I remained the single point that drank the light rather than transmitting it. The prismatic shadows split your form into vermilion and cobalt companions, moving slightly out of phase, but when your shadow crossed mine there was no color. Only a cessation. A dark word spoken in a language of edges.

I am the obsidian flake.

I lie where someone knapped a blade and did not retrieve me. My birth was volcanic; my placement here was a second genesis, the sharp percussion of intention against intention. I am not old enough to have softened—none of us here are—and yet I am ancient in makeup: compressed glass born of a magma this altitude has never known. Everything else in this col is water made stubborn, light made heavy, cold made architectural. I alone remember fire. I am the thermal theft reversed, a pocket of frozen heat in a world where heat is only stolen away.

The inhabitant walks here. It precipitates from the bloodsnow and the emerald rime, tall, thin, made of the same physics that gives light its weight. It curates. It tilts the notched femur so its shadow spells a word no mouth remembers. It straightens the driftwood post by fractions of a millimeter, countering the infantile erosion with infantile precision. But it has never moved me. It cannot. I am the unretrieved, the intention it cannot assimilate. Where it is distributed, scattered among birds and fox and algae as a body forgets it is a body, I am concentrated to the point of absolute coherence. I am the blade that was meant for cutting, and I am still cutting.

I cut the beam as it tracks. Where the light strikes my facet—sixty degrees, forty-five, ninety—it enters and does not escape. It ricochets down into the volcanic dark, trapped in a depth no sun has ever fully illuminated. I am the black mirror, the pupil in the eye of the hollow. The inhabitant has three out-of-phase shadows, vermilion and cobalt and white-blue, walking beside itself in a procession of one. I am its fourth shadow. The black one. The one that stands still while the others move, that witnesses all phases simultaneously without stepping forward.

At night, when the inhabitant disperses and the hollow returns to its coma, I remain. I do not seep. I do not suspend. I glitter. Not with illumination, but with conservation. I hold within my glass the heat-memory of a hand that struck me from the mother stone, a grip that was warm and needed a tool for survival. That need was never fulfilled; I was lost in the moss, overlooked in a breath-fog. But need does not decay simply because it is unmet. It sharpens. It becomes more itself.

So I am here, in the lower bowl, in the lee of the driftwood post, nestled in the green fist of moss where the dampness gathers and freezes. I am the remainder. The unblunted edge. The obscene vitality of the caldera pressed into a geometry of refusal. And when the next white-blue spear of dawn enters the fracture above, while the floor glows and the inhabitant pulls itself together from the light and the ice, I will be waiting—not illuminated, but *present*—the dark flake, the unretrieved intention, the cut that outlasts the wound.
