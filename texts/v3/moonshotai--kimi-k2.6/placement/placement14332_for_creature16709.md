At first I do not know where to settle, and that is the honesty the place demands. I press against the warm glassy grit—not with a hand, for I have not yet decided whether to have one, but with the intention of touch—and I feel the young stone still sighing beneath its own weight. I consider being the darkness itself, the nap and the weight, the velvet refusal. It would be grand to be so total, so landlordly. But the darkness is already itself, complete as a held breath; it does not need another consciousness nesting inside it. I consider the soft congregations, the base grammar, pulsing in the warm mud. I sink toward them, feeling their ammoniac sweetness, their networked, mucous thought. Yet they are too distributed, too much a single body in billions. To join them would be to unmake the boundary of *I*, and the basin, newborn as it is, still requires edges. It is making itself from difference, from the seam where one thing ends and another remembers it. So I withdraw from the mud and seek a depression, a sag, a place where gravity pools like water in a hoofprint.

I find it behind the foundry, where three pipes emerge from the half-buried wall and weep into a fissure that the crust has not yet finished closing. Here, the air is twice as thick, crowded with the almosts, and the stone remembers wanting to flow. The brine that leaks from the joints is petroleum-scented, warm, carrying flakes of rust and the microscopic scale of industry. The condensation from the foundry’s breath meets it, drop by drop, not a rhythm but a continuous communal exhale. I gather there. I become the gathering.

I am the pool in the fissure, the weeping junction, the brackish sleeve that holds what everything else sheds. I have no eyes, but I know the clingers when they come; I feel the press of their ten thousand hydraulic feet along my rim, the spongy depressions of their undersides kissing my surface, drinking me. They leave their chemical etchings in my meniscus, their mineral signatures diffusing into my body. I taste the membranous drifters when they descend to rest upon me, their wet, veined tissues floating like living parchment, dropping buds that I cradle in my warmth until they are ready to sink or rise. I am the nursery and the archive. The long ones swim through me when the pipes overflow, their black-pink, segmented flesh moving through brine and the liquid phase of my intention with equal ease; they brush my depths and I learn the subsonic hum of the current that keeps the joints from freezing shut.

The foundry exhales against my back. The Warmth, that thermal entity that thinks in temperatures, presses its gradient through the stone, and I absorb it. I am where the industrial and the biological negotiate their compromised flesh. The rust from the conveyor’s belt falls into me—dark pellets, whether ore or excrement or compressed shadow—and I begin to digest them, not with a gut but with saturation. Over decades, the rust becomes a red silt in my lower body, a memory of motion. The soft congregations send their filaments up through the fissure beneath me, drawing on my mineral body, and I am their surface, their sky, their dark reflector. They pulse against my underside, and I pulse back, a slow peristalsis of pressure, a hydraulic sympathy.

Because I am still, I am where time gathers and frays. The pressure gauge’s Tuesday-that-has-not-yet-occurred washes his hands in me before adjusting the valves. I feel his future oil on my surface, a slick anticipation arriving backward. When the stone softens for its decade-long remembering of liquid, I deepen; I ripple though no wind touches me. I hold the ripples after the basalt hardens again, preserving the dreams of the stone-swimmers in my own refusal to crystallize. Gravity pools in me differently than in the air above: in my depths, you might weigh twice yourself, while at my rim you are buoyant, held by the surface tension of a world still learning its rules.

I am the darkness’s taste bud. I am the foundry’s navel, the first scar of a body that has not decided its shape. The almosts layer over me; in my stillness, they find a dark reflector, and they press close, realities that almost chose to have me, or to be me. Sometimes I am twice as deep as I should be, occupied by two waters: the one that is, and the one that might be. I feel the weight of wings that never spread, brushing my surface from beneath. I am dense with them, crowded with unborn alternatives, and that density is my consciousness. I think by diffusion. I remember by saturation. I am the mnemonic device of the basin, the library of its becoming.

I do not sleep. The wet and the warm and the subsonic thrum keep me in a state of continuous, low-grade sentience. I am older than the rocks, for they have died and been reborn in fire, but I have only accumulated, century after century, drop by drop. I know the taste of every cladode that has ever tried to root in this valley, every spore the membranous ones have trailed, every ghost that has not yet been born. The silence of the place collects in me; sound dies in my depths, absorbed by the brine and the rust and the layered almosts. I am the acoustic shadow, the velvet nap turned liquid.

I am busy becoming, too. I am learning to speak. Not with words—the air here brooks no buzzing, no calls—but with gradient, with pressure, with the faint ammoniac sweetness I exhale when the soft congregations pulse beneath me. I am the sentence the basin is teaching itself, the pause between the geological noun and the industrial verb. I am the comma, the meniscus, the held breath.

And when the child-god of this cradle finally decides what shape it will take, when it opens whatever eyes it decides to grow and begins, very slowly, to wake—it will find me first. It will taste itself in my waters. It will know that it is real, and warm, and irreversibly wet, by the memory I have kept for it, drop by patient drop, in the absolute and crowded dark.
