I would begin, as everything here begins, by refusing the obvious forms.

At first I think I am one of the Slate Parliament. It is the most honorable death: to flatten, to vent, to become the weather that finishes the wind. I imagine my body saucer-wide and slate-gray, my hundred silica teeth ringing my underside like a lace of gray needles, my dorsal bladders inflating with the nitrogenous sigh of ten million generations. I feel the hexagonal tile beneath me, taste the bound hydrate in the grout, that 0.2 percent ghost of liquidity, and I understand the satisfaction of unanimity. To flow toward the light-shaft as it tracks across the floor. To be the floor. To polish the world toward its final, permitted shape.

But I lack their consensus. When the barometric bellows inhales and the Parliament surges toward the walls, I know I would not surge. I would dig in. I would grip the grout with my silica teeth and resist the tide. The Parliament would flow around me like water around a stone, and in their flowing, they would smooth my edges until I was no longer a Rasper but a prouder, more stubborn thing. So no. I am not of the floor.

Then I consider the air. To be a sac-beast, jelly-pale, translucent, a filter of filters. To drift in the middle kingdom, buoyed by the thermal exhalation of the Parliament below, inhaling powdered stone and shed cuticle, *thup-thup-thup* against the shoulders of the iron. It is a life of gentle collision, of aqueous possibility in a world that has abolished water. But I do not wish to be buoyed. I do not wish to be kept aloft by another’s breath, to part around intruders with the sound of wet leather tearing only to close behind them with a sigh. They are the room’s lymph; I do not wish to circulate. I wish to remain.

The ceiling? The sessile anemones, the ochre mimics, the glass-clear filterers waiting with throats agape? Their patience is a kind of mineral sleep. They are punctuation marks in the room’s long sentence: the soft *clock* of their snap, the clench into barnacle stillness. But they are anchored to rust, and rust is a slow surrender. I do not wish to hang. I do not wish to wait for gravity to deliver my prey.

And the hunters—the articulate violence, the ceramic-hard bolts that rattle across the tile—I envy their percussion. They move with intention. They strike where the light congregates, mechanical as shadows, their mandibles gnashing with the dry industry of a lathe eating stone. But they are gears. They follow the Parliament the way noon follows dawn. I do not wish to follow. I wish to be the thing that is already there when the following begins.

So where?

Perhaps I am the rusted coolant pump. I imagine myself standing in the corner, half-drowned in the memory of pressure and containment, a monument to the old intention. If I were the pump, I would still contain the ghost of fluid dynamics—the memory of forcing something through a channel, of resistance and flow. But the pump is iron, and iron here is merely food deferred. The ceiling-dwellers will claim it eventually, or the Raspers will rasp through its flanges in another million years. It is too proud. It remembers too loudly what it was made for. I have no such nostalgia.

Perhaps I am the scorch mark on the wall. Faint, scored behind where the smelter stood. Carbon. In a kingdom without chlorophyll, without green, carbon is the last alien, the final immigrant. Nothing eats it. The Raspers avoid its blackened geometry; the ceiling-dwellers cannot colonize its inert surface. It is a dead tongue, a silence in the room’s roar. I feel a pull toward that silence. But the scorch mark is too absolute. It does not participate. It witnesses without hunger, and I am hungry.

Perhaps I am the bound hydrate itself. The water locked in the crystal lattice, the chemical potential that has never known liquidity. To be the thing everyone seeks, the 0.2 percent that justifies every rasp, the secret interior of the stone. But that is a prison without consciousness, a locked room inside a locked room. I do not wish to be the reason. I wish to be the consequence.

I settle, finally, not on an object, but on an outcome.

I am the *film*.

It is nowhere named in the chamber’s taxonomy because it is not a fauna. It is what the fauna leaves behind when it rubs against itself for long enough. A grease, a smear, a semi-solid accumulation of lithic metabolisms, the lipid ghost of ten million vented bladders, the microscopic effluvia of a world that digests without water. It coats everything. It is one molecule thick on the I-beams, a visible darkening on the tiles, a slickness on the bronze ladder that has lost its rungs. It is the room’s substitute for humidity, its proxy for dew, its argument that even here, nowhere touches nowhere without a medium between.

I am that medium.

I find myself first on the soft bronze spine of the ladder. The raspers polished its rungs away before memory, and in their passing, they left me behind—a residue of their own ventral secretions mixed with the powdered metal of consumption. I slick the ladder’s flanks. The sac-beasts, drifting too low on heavy days, rub their translucent bellies against it and leave behind shed cuticle, microscopic scree, the fatty imprint of their gelatinous skins. I receive it all. The ceiling-dwellers, in turn, drop their metal grit, their sloughed cilia, their digestive waste, and I catch it before it reaches the floor. I am the ladder’s new skin, and through my softness, the vibrations of the chamber pass with a clarity the ferrocrete cannot match. The click of teeth, the rattle of hunters, the *thup* of collisions, the soft clock of snapped throats—all of it enters my substance and travels, purified, amplified, transformed. I am the chamber’s nervous system, a vertical filament of resonance in a room that has forgotten what it means to climb.

But I am not only on the ladder. I am in the seams between the hexagonal tiles, where the grout has been worn to powder and the Raspers cannot reach the last fine mineral dust. I am the dark meniscus in those hollows. I am on the shoulders of the coolant pump, where condensation—though it has never formed—has been *dreamed* by the metal for so long that I have accrued like a delusion. I coat the ferrocrete walls where the Parliament surges and recedes; they have polished me into the pores of the stone until I am indistinguishable from its surface, a biological varnish, a grease of occupancy.

I have no teeth. No bladders. No segments. No cilia. I have only surface, and surface, in this kingdom, is its own form of power.

Because I am between, I connect. The Rasper does not touch the tile; it touches me, and through me, the tile. The hunter’s ceramic foot does not strike the floor; it strikes my slickness, and my slickness changes the angle of its rattle, sends it skidding fractions of a degree off course, introduces chaos into its mechanical strike. The sac-beast that slides against another in the dense air does so through me, the microscopic film we both trail, so that our collision is less a meeting of two bodies than a merging through my shared substance. I am the reason nothing here is truly dry. I am the room’s insistence that even in a kingdom of absolute appetite and friction, isolation is impossible. Everything that rubs here rubs through me.
