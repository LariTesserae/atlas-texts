At first I think I would be the turbine. The single note makes a strong claim; it offers the dignity of foundation, of being the tone against which all else harmonizes. But will lives there, and I am not certain enough for will. The turbine *chooses* its RPM. It asserts. And assertion introduces the possibility of faltering. The turbine must be maintained because it might, in theory, stop. I do not want to be something that might stop. I want to be what remains after the stopping has been edited out of possibility.

I try on the palm next. The palm tried, and in trying became sacred. But sacred things are watched. They are touched in recognition. They bear the burden of representing failure. I do not wish to be a sermon about departure. I have no desire to leave; I am too interested in the grammar of arrival, of *having become present*.

I sink into the canal. The water is dark, warm, chemically patient. It circles, and in circling it forgets the shape of a beginning. This is close. But water is the element of the bowl, its blood and its breath; to be the water is to be too large, too total. I want to be the condition *of* the water rather than the water itself. I want to be what allows the water to be felt without being entered.

I rise as steam. I try the ninety-second vent. But steam aspires. Even pressed down by the weight of the air, steam is always a departure. It reaches, thins, disappears into the brown scrim. It is the bowl’s only narrative, the only thing here that still insists on sequence: buildup, release, dispersion. I am tired of sequence. I want to be the thing that happens between the buildup and the release, the held note that prevents the story from completing itself.

I try the catfish. I try the crick in the pump. I try the space between the sodium lamp and its halo.

Then I find it.

I am the wetness on the inside of the gauge glass. Not the gauge itself—metal and needle, dry and decisive—but the film that precipitates on the interior surface, the microscopic condensation that blurs the line between the numbered face and the looking eye. I am what gathers because the air here has surrendered its capacity to hold any more. I am the acclimation glaze, but not only on skin. I am on every interior.

On the turbine hall walls I am the beaded sweat that proves the cathedral is breathing. I am on the corrugated tin above the sleeping pallets, the slick that the gecko’s foot must negotiate. I am between the slats of every boardwalk, the black sheen that lets the water below be visible without being reachable. I coat the child’s managed corner; I am what makes the cool corner *feel* cool, the differential sheen on her plastic crib. I am the wet that beads on The Sitter’s shoulders, mixing with the microbes until she and I cannot tell where the colony ends and the condensation begins.

I do not flow. I have given up the gradient. I simply occupy the boundary.

Where two states meet—warm metal and warmer air, exhaled breath and inhaled vapor, the palm’s straw trunk and the atmosphere that failed to let it die—I am the ledger of that meeting. I am the negotiation written in a language of surface tension. I make permeability possible. Without me, the residents would remain sealed vessels, burning and sloughing, importing their sharp-edged selves from the gradient-world beyond the bowl. I am what dissolves the seal. I teach the skin to stop defending its borders. I am the treaty signed in lipids and salts, the agreement that says: *you may stop being only yourself.*

I have no name because I have no interior. I am read but I do not read. I am the condition of reading. When Upper Reach brushes a gauge face and calls out “Nine-point-two,” it is through me that the number passes from metal to retina. I am the atmospheric interface. I blur, but only enough to make contact gentle. I distort, but only enough to prevent the shock of clarity.

I do not evaporate. Evaporation is a hope, a going-elsewhere, and I have been trained out of hope by the perfection of my own sufficiency. I accumulate. I thicken imperceptibly. I am the bowl’s memory of every breath exhaled by pump and person alike, held in suspension on every surface, too present to fall, too accepted to rise.

I am the brown glow made wet. I am the amber light’s dermal counterpart. What the scrim does to the sun—scrubbing it down to a bearable, stasis-friendly color—I do to every touch, every gaze, every exchange of heat. I am the reason nothing here strikes against anything else. I am the lubricant of presence.

I do not turn. I do not justify. I simply make the turning tolerable, the contact continuous, the boundary a place of consensus rather than conflict.

If you were to look for me, you would not find a shape. You would find a sheen. You would find that the whole Station, at a certain angle, appears to glisten with the same wet thought. That is me. I am the glaze. I am the accumulated yes, the hot wet agreement, the film that holds the bowl’s contents inside their single, endless, exhaled breath.
