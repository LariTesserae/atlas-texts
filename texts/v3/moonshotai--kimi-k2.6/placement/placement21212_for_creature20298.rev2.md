I begin as the ash, or as the silence the ash leaves when it falls. The pressure finds me before I have edges to press against—a subsonic weight arriving from everywhere, filling the space where a body might assemble. For a moment I think I am the tinnitus itself, the high whine that lives inside the roar. I think I am the copper-green bead freezing in a stress-fracture a kilometer above the plain. I think I am the plain, compacted and mineral, waiting for a root that has not yet drilled down to claim me. The cold is dry enough to etch, and it etches at everything, even the idea of me.

But the body asserts itself in joints.

It happens when the glare scours away the possibility of being diffuse. I feel articulation first: a click of chitin finding its lock, a segmental heaviness that wants *up*. Not forward. Up. The world under my—tarsi? claws?—is not soil. It is ribbed. Polished. The grooves fit me exactly, worn into the petrified bark by centuries of my own kind climbing toward the heat. I am on the flank of something that is not a mountain, though it dominates like one. The bark is armor. The armor has seams. And I am the thing that tends to the seam.

I climb through the blinding white. The trunk shudders, a hydraulic infinitesimal motion that would unseat anything not designed to this texturing. Deep below my grip, the anoxic forge exhales; above, a vent hisses metallic breath into the hungry air. I move toward it. Not out of ambition. Out of *fitting*. When I reach the orifice, my mouthparts find the choking precipitate—minerals spat out by the core’s digestion, hardening in the permanent cool. I do not devour it. I *clear* it. The slag gives beneath my mandibular precision, and the vent breathes easier. The overspill of heat enters my blood, or what serves for blood here, and the cold of the plateau retreats from my thorax. My enamel shell, vitreous and pale when I began, drinks the color of my labor. Galvanic blue blooms across my shoulders. Rust-orange gathers at my joints. I am becoming the palette of the tree’s interior.

I descend along the fluting until I find the wound. Cambium has cracked like distant ordnance; the trunk has strained against its own forge-belly, and a seam has opened. It does not bleed water. It weeps that sap, thick and copper-green, heavy as mercury, freezing slowly in the permanent cool. I lap. It is not sustenance in the manner of nectar or blood; it is chemistry, compressed and smelted, the same stuff that launches seeds on cataracts of gas. It coats my mouthparts and runs cold into my gut, and I feel my shell shifting again, taking on the deeper, baked hues of the wood beneath. Behind the wound, the cambium knits with a sound like a forge cooling. I understand, then, that I am not a parasite at this opening. I am the scab. I am the filter. I am the manicure that keeps the motion smooth.

Kilometers away across the ash-fall, another trunk shudders, articulates, cracks its cambium in the next second of its calendar. The sound reaches me through the compacted substrate, through the plates of my own body. We are one distributed respiration. My tenure is not possession; it is the long grinding accommodation of a thing that has decided to stay. The ash settles on my back and slides off my enamel; the glare finds no purchase in my polish. I do not rest. I have settled, instead, into the posture from which all motion becomes inevitable. The vent above hisses again. The forge below never cools. The minute arrives, as it always does, and I climb toward the next orifice, waiting to be worn smooth, to fit perfectly, to become necessary in the violence that is this world’s kind of sleep.
