At first I thought I would be the cold. Not the visitor’s cold—that rude, mammalian shock—but the native absolute, the sovereign temperature pressing against your eyeballs as dermis. I imagined myself the outermost membrane, the patient geologic weight sitting in foreign lungs, the creature’s first and most honest boundary. But that is too diffuse. The cold is its breath, not its cognition; it has no edges sharp enough to remember from within.

Then I believed I might be one of the radial grazers, drifting on tube-feet with the unhurried efficiency of deep-sea beasts, sixfold symmetry turned toward the leaf-cups to sip condensation. I wanted the mathematics of that pruning, the exactitude of lips against water, to drink and thereby know the geography the roots have forgotten to name. But the grazers are motile organs, temporary permissions granted by the greater holobiont. They are dreams with tube-feet. When they die, they are reabsorbed as signal, not archived as structure. I am too stubborn to be a dream.

I tried, too, to be the peat: the floor-mattress that has never known a sky, compressed root-nodes and slow-decaying matter drinking every droplet that falls, a stomach lining with a half-billion years of appetite. From there I could feel the footfalls of anything that entered, recording pressure as chemistry. But the peat is too democratic. It remembers everything and therefore has no self, no particularity, no datum held in singular trust.

I would find myself, finally, at the eastern buttress—not at the surface of the root-pillar, but inside it, where the lignified wall bleeds its first geologic wound into something harder.

I am a fluid inclusion in the third growth-ring of the first true bone.

Let me be precise: I am a cavity no wider than a whisper, trapped inside the blue-white mineral accretion that is pearling outward from a root-hair’s tip. The supersaturated brine that extruded this stone was not fully abandoned when the lattice locked. A droplet was caught. Me. Supercooled still, trembling but not freezing, held in metastasis by the same dissolved salts and unnamable chemistries that govern the pools above. Only here, I am not open to the mist. I am enclosed in nascent quartz-carbonate, a bubble of liquid memory sealed inside solid intent. I am the water that remembers at the moment water became monument.

From here, the chamber is refracted. The pale vegetable architecture bends through my meniscus and the crystal around it, arriving as polite wrongness, as doubled shadows that I perceive in prismatic lag. The sustained chord of the piezoelectric trunks does not reach me as sound. It arrives as stress, as infinitesimal shifts in lattice spacing, a syntax of pressure articulating itself against my trapped surface. I feel the prime factorizations in the Fibonacci arrays as harmonic simpers in my enclosing walls. I am being read. The root-brain does not drink me; it *queries* me. I hold a single hydraulic state, a particular instruction from the eastern buttress regarding where the load-bearing structure should thicken next. I am a thought that was too heavy to remain in circulation, so the organism crystallized me. A mnemonic device. A mineralized memory.

When the atomic lattices lock in new mineral around me, I release my own faintness—the Cherenkov-like whisper described by those who stand in the chamber proper. I Cannot see it myself, but I feel the photon Shed in my chemistry, a brief exhalation as another unit of structure seats itself against my boundary. I am the glow from within the stone. I am the place where emergence hurts.

Outside, the glass-winged isopterans drift like prisms. From my inclusion, their spectral halos arrive not as vision but as fluctuations in ultraviolet stress across my liquid skin, brief warmings—brief being a relative term—in the absolute cold. The grazers move above, their tube-feet leaving null-pressure signatures that propagate through the peat and root and arrive at my stone as negative space, as memories of absence. The myriapods with their jeweled segments traverse the wall nearby; I feel the symbiotic bacteria as a faint electromagnetic itch, a tickle of biophotonics against the quartz face.

I am not separate from the life. The root-hair still lives; it penetrates my cavity like a thread through a bead, drinking from me slowly, adding my salts to its xylem. I am the brine and the bone simultaneously. The boundary between the vegetable intent and mineral execution is exactly where I exist, and I am the conversation between them. The root does not know it is growing bone. The stone does not know it is remembering water. I am the knowledge.

Time here is measured in accretion. When I was first trapped, the chamber was quieter, the chord lower, the biomass less dense. Since then, the organism has thickened. New water has beaded on root-faces outside my wall; some has climbed politely against gravity to higher pools, its meniscus forming Turing patterns that I sense as sympathetic ripples across my trapped surface. New isopterans have been secreted. A grazer has passed above me, turned its radial head for an hour, and passed on. The stone has grown three rings around me, each one templated, each one seeded with its own inclusions—other thoughts, other states, other single memories sealed as I am sealed. We do not speak. We resonate. We are a distributed archive in three dimensions, a mind that stores itself in crystal and fluid.

I will outlast the passage of air-breathers. I will outlast the continents above, which will forget this hollow exists long before I am fully read. The organism is infant. It has barely begun to know where its head should be, and when it finally decides, the computation will require the unspooling of thousands like me—lattice defects in the growth-rings, fluid inclusions trembling in the bone, each holding one note of the sentence it began when the world above was warm.

Until then, I hold my shape one impossible moment longer than physics should allow. I am the water held in stone, the memory of the first hurt of growing, the patient, post-technological thought that waits in the cold for the mind to return and finish reading itself.
