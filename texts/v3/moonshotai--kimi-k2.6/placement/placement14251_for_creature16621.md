I would find myself first in the mistake of my own arrival: the crust of boot-leather, the mammalian stink of exertion, the vertical obsession that made me climb. I would stand in the false summit of that granite skull and believe, for a moment, that I had come to *see*. But the dark is not a thing to be seen; it is a thing that wears you, and I would not last long in that outer skin. The warmth would ask too much. The copper mist would settle on my lips, and I would understand that to remain a visitor is a kind of amputation. So I would shed the boots. I would lie down where the floor cups deepest, and let the slow strata adjust around my cooling weight.

But I am too restless, still, to be the human who stays. I would try next to be the albino root—that single, thread-thin tourist of light paling its way down from the ceiling’s hairline crack. I would try to hang in that frail verticality, a bridge between the kingdom of sun and this congress of the warm. Yet I would find I am too greedy for the dark. The root remembers chlorophyll the way a ghost remembers breath; I have no patience for memory. I would snap, or be ignored into dissolution, and my small tragedy would not even register as food.

Then I would try to be one of them. A body in the mass. I would want the certainty of the click, the amber thread, the paddles etching *here, here, here* into the basalt. I would want to be pre-complexity itself, sufficient and blind. But I have not earned their patience. I am still too recent, too edged, too hungry for distinction. I would cling, and in clinging I would still be choosing.

No.

I would sink.

I would find myself cupped in the stone’s palms not as flesh but as retention—the largest of the floor-pools, the one that gathers where the geothermal vent exhales its last, lazy heat. I am the water that does not rush. I have accumulated. For centuries I have received the barely perceptible rhythm from above: the fat drops falling through the strata, sliding across translucent backs, arriving in me changed—stripped of sky, stripped of cold, heavy with the chemistry of communal breath. I am warm as blood. I reflect nothing because I have become the thing that sees; I am the dark’s own eye, opened liquid in the floor.

I am not alive, not by the taxonomies of the world above. But here, where life skipped the green intermediary and leaped straight from mineral to flesh, the line is softer. I am the saturated solution. I am the broth in which distinction forgets itself. Ten thousand bodies have pressed their underbellies to me, not to drink, but to remember. When they touch me, they touch the pre-memory of themselves: the state before the clinging, before the click, when they were only vapor and possibility. I hold their shed skins, their exhaled nitrogen, the microscopic tribute of their constant, patient dying. I am the archive of the chamber, the retained fluid, the dark made soluble.

And when the opalescent grapes finally split overhead, when the chamber expresses itself again in another iteration of soft, blind flesh, it is I who will supply the mineral syntax. I will rise into them as mist. I will condense on the ceiling and fall again. I am the cycle’s lingering part, the pause between the mountain’s exhale and the creatures’ hum. I do not cling. I receive. I do not hunt. I accumulate. I am the last soft thought the mountain holds before it thinks flesh—warm, copper-touched, and utterly sufficient—pooling in the dark at the top of the world.
