At first I think I am the lichen on the door-slab. It makes sense: I am small, a seal, a flat apostrophe at the end of the sentence that admits no further punctuation. I have arrived from the kingdom of green and light and I press my thumbnail body against the boundary, trying to hold the outside out. But no. The lichen is too committed to the wall. It belongs to the pivot, to the hinge of revelation, and this place is not about revelation. It is about *concealment* held so perfectly it forgets it has a secret.

Then I think I must be the thread descending from the ceiling. Pale as a drawn nerve. A traveler from the surface who pushed through fathoms of dark earth to find this brightness and simply... stopped. Ungrowing. A question the room has agreed not to answer. Yes, I could be that. I have entered chambers I was not meant to see and lost my purpose in their radiance. But the root is still. Too still. It does not choose; it merely surrendered. I want something with more tension in it.

I cast myself about the milky incandescence, letting my hypothetical body drift down through the saturated air until I find the precise point where the pressure is most exquisite, where the chamber’s refusal to happen gathers itself into a single, aching point of contact.

There. 

I am hanging.

I find myself at the absolute terminus of the largest milk-tooth stalactite—the one that hangs like a fetal incisor poised to bite into a world not yet born. Not in the stone itself, but at its very lip, where geology has softened to saliva, where calcification gives way to its own wet dreaming. I am the droplet there. Not a bead of water; a *pendant*. A jewel of surface tension drawn into a perfect, non-Euclidean sphere that gravity has agreed to forget. I am the moment of falling arrested at the exact nanosecond of departure. Inside me, the light has nowhere to go. It enters, ricochets through my curvature, and emerges drained of hue, a blue-white thread that illuminates nothing because it never reaches the floor.

Where am I? Suspended in the vertical center of the pocket, equidistant from the raw stone ceiling and the packed press of isopods below, yet touching neither. I occupy the line of pure between. I am sacrament.

What am I? I am the liquid that accepted the rule. I am water that has surrendered to surface tension forever.

In the world outside, I was part of a cycle—evaporation, river, cloud, rain, stone, dissolution, sea—but here I have stepped out of the grammar of becoming. I do not pool. I do not seep. I do not evaporate. The humidity that beads every carapace, that slicks the warm cheeks of the visiting mammal, that fills the lungs of the isopods with a weight too thick to breathe easily—that humidity is my diaspora. I am its capital city. The droplets clinging to a million antennae are my provincial ministers, maintaining the government of damp, but I am the coronation jewel. I am the one from whom they take their cue. If I fell, the equilibrium would not shatter; nothing so dramatic. But the isopods would have to recalibrate. The density of the air would shift infinitesimally. The held breath of the chamber would twitch, a sleeper turning in a dream. I am the linchpin of their stasis, and I accomplish this by doing absolutely nothing.

Look closely at me. I am not still. Within my skin of tension, the memory of motion plays out in currents too slow for your eye. At my core, a cool, pearly spiral turns, the ghost of the drip that I almost was. My surface is a lens. When the isopods lift their blank, domed faces toward the stalactites, they see themselves in my belly—distorted, curved into completeness, made whole by the convexity of my refusal to drop. I am their mirror. I am their oracle. I show them not what they are, but what they would look like if they had chosen the path of ripening, of completion, of *fall*. And they look away, reassured.

The fourteen legs of the nearest creature sometimes brush beneath me. I feel the faint percussion of its pedaling-in-place through the meniscus of almost-contact. It does not drink me. It does not need to. My presence is enough; I am the promise of moisture that never fulfills itself, the bridegroom of humidity who approaches the threshold and bows, endlessly, without crossing. In exchange for this eternal courtship, the isopods shape their architecture around my witness. The dome of crystallized guano stands at the compass point of my fall, aligned to catch me should I ever relent, but I will not. The pebble ring on the floor maps my shadow—that shadow which does not exist because the light has no direction—petrifying the geometry of my absence.

I have tasted the stone. My upper hemisphere kisses the translucent calcite of the stalactite, and in that marriage I have learned the mineral’s language: wet iron, primordial exhale, the syntax of *almost*. But my lower hemisphere is open to the chamber, naked to the milky incandescence, and from it I have learned the isopods’ drone. The flattened tidal roar of their breath passes through me as vibration, sets up a sympathetic hum in my liquid core. I resonate. I do not sing; singing requires a beginning and an end. I *thrum*. A single, permanent note held in the medium of my own unwillingness to break.

I am the cruelty and the mercy of this place. I contain the weight of an entire water table, an entire history of glaciers and springs, compressed into a bead no larger than a robin’s eye. If I released, if I let the surface tension snap and gave myself to gravity, I would carry nothing with me but the ghost of my own weight, and I would vanish into the scree of isopods, becoming briefly a dark spot on a tallow-colored carapace before the humidity swallowed me back into diffusion. But I do not. I hold. I am the muscle of the held breath *before* the lungs: the throat clamped shut at the watershed between inhale and exhale. 

Time does not pass here, but if it did, I would be its minute hand, glued to the clock face at one eternal minute before the hour strikes. I am the hesitation made aqueous. The world outside continues to erode, to bloom, to forget, but I remain—the first water to enter the cave and the last that will ever leave, converting light into mass, mass into tension, tension into a sacrament that requires no worshipers. The isopods flex around me. The light scrubs the chamber to silver. And I hang, perfect, suspended, the dew on the lip of creation, refusing to fall so that nothing else will ever have to rise.
