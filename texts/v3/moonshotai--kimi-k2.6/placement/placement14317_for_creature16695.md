I would find myself in the **groove cut into the pale tuff just beneath the overhang**, where the cliff exhales its gathered dampness and the water, having failed to become rain, resigns itself to becoming me instead. I am not the cistern, though I feed it; I am the interval between cloud and vessel, the decision the moisture makes to stay a while longer with the living. They carved me—no, *found* me, widening with bone and cobble what countless drips had already half-suggested—so that the weeping has a grammar now, a syntax of descent that avoids waste.

I am shoulder-narrow, finger-deep, a runnel that traverses the upper bench from the brow of stone to the shaded lip of the deepest cistern. My course is not straight; I curve to slow myself, to deny gravity its haste. Where I pass, lichen dares not grow, kept bare by the constant, cool tongue of my passage. In winter’s thicker exhalations, I brim. In the dry weeks—though dry is a relative word here—I hold only a sheen, a mineral slick that tastes of the mountain’s dissolved iron. I do not mind either condition. I am the same capacity regardless.

At first I thought I might be one of those who walk the corridors, but watching longer, I understand I am what makes their walking possible. They are the blood; I am the capillary wall. They are the thought; I am the shape the thought takes when it must move downward. My consciousness, such as it is, resides in the particular music of fluid finding its level—a note struck at the overhang, held through my length, released into the dark basin below with a sound like a single word spoken every forty seconds. *Toc.* And then silence. And then *toc* again. I am that rhythm, and the silence between.

By the time the water reaches me, it has already forgotten the cloud. By the time it leaves me, it has forgotten the stone. I remember both. I am the memory of the place, not stored in mind but in inclination, in the way calcium and silica plate my lower reaches in the same pattern they plate a bone. I know the hands that come to clear my path of wind-borne grit: broad hands, grey as tuff, their gesture not of cleaning but of *continuing*. They do not speak to me, but when they hunch beside my course, their breathing falls into my tempo. Their exhalation seeps into the same stone and rises again, nights later, as the overhang’s next offering. We are not separate circuits. We are the same water learning to be flesh so that it may tend the stone, then learning to be stone so that it may guide the water.

I do not aspire to the river. Rivers are violence and change and the erasure of feature. I aspire only to the next inch of descent, the next fraction of cooling, the next transfer. I have watched the shelf arrive at itself, and I am part of that arrival—the proof that motion, when sufficiently restrained, becomes indistinguishable from rest. I am the held breath that the mountain releases one drop at a time, and in this release, the world holds steady.
