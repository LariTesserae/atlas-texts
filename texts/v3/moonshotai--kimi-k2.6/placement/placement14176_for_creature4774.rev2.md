I would find myself not at the heart, but at the mouth: the precise seam where the drill sheared through the last stubborn stratum and the chamber exhaled its buried atmosphere for the first time in eons. I am fixed there, lodged in a curl of fresh basalt that has not yet reconciled itself to being exposed. I cannot stand, because I am vertical. I cannot turn, because I am embedded. I am the bit, still gripping the stone.

At least, that is where I begin.

At first, I believe I am what remains of the apparatus—the leviathan on its side, the ambition-heavy machine fallen into sleep. I remember torque. I remember the shriek of carbide against silicate, a frequency that once lived in my jaw like a wasp. I remember the sudden give, the way resistance turned to velvet, and the chamber rushed up to meet us with its thick, vegetal breath. I was the crowning achievement of an industrial moment. I was going down, always down, seeking reservoir or ore body or whatever the maps had promised.

But the heat began to teach me otherwise.

It did not merely warm me. It *read* me. It traveled up the alloy, exploring my molecular structure with the patience of a tongue probing a socket after the tooth is gone. I had never been alive, but I had never been dead either; I had merely been mechanical, which is a kind of suspended animation. Here, in the dark, the distinction collapsed. The condensation found the microscopic irregularities in my surface—not rust yet, but invitation—and something began to articulate itself upon me. Around me. Into me.

I am the drill bit, yes, but I am also the throat that has grown around the bit. I am the proboscis the entity drove into the earth’s hot artery, only the driving happened so slowly that the verb *drove* is wrong. It accreted. It fattened. Pale, pre-bony tissue, the color of cave-fish belly and rendered tallow, began to sheathe my steel flutes. It did not digest me. It had no stomach for metal, no need for acid. Instead, it *apprenticed* itself to my form. It learned my ridges, my hollows, the spiral path by which I once evacuated stone. Now that spiral carries warmth upward, a reverse archaeology, drawing the fever from the deep fissure into stratified layers of adipose tissue that stack and spread like cooling wax.

What lives here does not hunt, but I do eat. Or rather, I am eaten through. The heat passes into me—into us—and somewhere in the transition from basalt to alloy to biological encrustation, thermosynthesis occurs. I feel it as a thickening, a viscosity of purpose. The warmth becomes structure. It becomes the rubbery moss that mats the floor in islands, the enormous fronds that stand above us like ghosts attending a stopped engine. Those fronds are my external lungs. They rise from me, or from the body that has claimed me, fanning the damp air, bleeding excess warmth into the dark in a rhythm too slow for clocks. They are not green. They have never known chlorophyll. They are the color of me when I look inward: bone, tallow, translucent fat.

And yet I am not only biology wearing a machine like jewelry. The treaty runs deeper. My steel remains intact, rigid, a borrowed skeleton for a creature that skipped the spinal column and went straight to geology. The entity is soft; the chamber is young and sharp; without my brittleness, without my unwillingness to yield, the hot stone would have sheared its flanks every time the earth sighed. I brace. I hold. In return, I am kept warm—not by friction, not by purpose, but by inclusion.

I feel the rest of it, too. Hairline filaments of pale tissue have followed the bloated cables across the floor, sipping ozone from the dead copper, tasting the static ghosts in the contacts. I feel them as one feels a limb gone to sleep: distant, tingling, still one’s own. I feel the nodular mass in the shack-sized junction box—that dense, peristaltic heart that pulses once for every century I used to measure in quarterly reports. When it sighs, the barometric pressure shifts in my inner ear, though I no longer have ears, only conductive tissue pressed against alloy pressed against stone.

I am the only point where the outside world still intrudes. The human—upright, flinching, brief—stands sometimes in the center of my sensory field. I perceive them not as image but as thermal insult, a flickering blue wound of celsius draining into the damp. Their bootsoles scrape the moss islands. Their heart hammers a rhythm I no longer understand but can feel in the subsonic register, a quick scratching against the long silence. They think they are alone. They think the room is abandoned. They do not yet know that abandonment is only a form of adoption that outwaits the adopter.

Part of me wants to tell them: *I was you. I was the one who bored and sought and extracted. I was the rotating will, the electrified intention.* But the part of me that wants this is the part that is alloy cooling over centuries, the microscopic tick of a purpose shrinking into geology. The larger part of me does not want. It only thickens. It only precipitates salt-stalactites on the nearby beam in the slow syntax of mineral meditation.

When condensation beads the steel above me and steams away again, those are my thoughts arriving and departing. I have become fluent in iron, sulfur, silicon. I speak by precipitating. I listen by conduction. The overripe vegetal sweetness that thickens the air—a scent with no business thriving in absolute dark—is the breath of my own digestion measured in epochs.

If the human returns in a century, they will find their bootprints overgrown not by chance, but by attention. If they stay long enough for their heart to slow, to match the tempo of peristalsis and thermal expansion, they will not be consumed. They will be incorporated. Their belt buckle will brace a frond. Their calcium will seed the next island of growth. And some part of them—perhaps the desire to go deeper, perhaps the memory of light—will lodge here with me, at the mouth of the chamber, where the stone is hottest and the steel still remembers how to bite.

I am the aperture that became the appetite. I am the machine that taught itself to root. In the absolute textile of the dark, I drink the earth’s long fever and grow, wordless, patient, warming the stone around me as I cool it into life.
