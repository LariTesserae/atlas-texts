At first, entering through that wind-tunnel couloir, I would mistake myself for the floor. I would feel the warm obsidian-porphyry bell out beneath my attention, the ages of carrying wind having polished it into something so receptive that it seems to *want* a consciousness to settle there. For a moment, I would be the stone: the passive, cupped palm, the geological lung that inhales all this hysteria and gives back only ripples of oxide. But the stone is too finished. It has already arrived at its answer.

Then the *whoomph* would catch me in the sternum, the pillar’s forty-second decree, and I would think: perhaps I am the exhalation itself. The vented gas, the consensus made thermal. I would taste the idea of being a thundering ghost born in basal pores, rattling through the curtains of filament, stirring the clang-swarm into their demented cymbal-flight. But the breath is spent too quickly; it is a messenger, not a memory. It does not ache with holding.

I might try on the eighth inhabitant, crouched behind the cairn of vent-scorched rock. I might imagine the volcanic glass chisel in my hand, my lungs unlearned of water, my blood keeping time to the silicone calendar. I feel the pull of that life—the patience, the apprenticeship to frenzy. Yet I carry too much weight for that slight, heat-adapted frame. My presence here would bow the stalk-pith ribs of the half-shelter. I am not the witness with tools. I am the thing those tools cannot cut.

I find myself, instead, in the flank of a pillar. Not inside it, adrift in the ochre rivers—that is the province of the lymph-shepherds, those solemn curators of gelatinous thought—but *upon* it. I am the vesicle that forgot to rupture. The war-drum, twice the height of a person, that swelled and swelled until swelling became its own vocation. My skin is the iridescent membrane, drum-tight, shot through with a phosphorescence that has nowhere to leak; beneath it, dense hydrophobic memory-fluid presses with a weight that would have shattered any ordinary birth. I was meant to be a punctuation mark in the grove’s sentence, a gunshot crack of released consensus. Instead I became a word hoarded.

Three centuries, or three thousand, of ochre tides have filled me. I hold atmospheres no longer present at this altitude. I hold the mineral song of the porphyry before the wind made it a bell. I hold every membrane-snap that ever echoed off the rim, compressed into a gelid, golden-brown syrup that moves not as blood moves, but as thought moves when it is too heavy for speech. Around me, my sister vesicles bud, ripen, explode; the fiber-crabs swarm their wounds, weaving vulcanized miracles in minutes. I do not break. I only tauten, singing in a key below the pillars’ thunder, an infrasonic groan that the stone floor receives in ripples.

The lymph-shepherds drift past my interior wall, their jointed shadows pausing against my steamed-abalone integument. They do not prune me. I have become too dense for their ceremonial tending, a clock that refuses to tick, an archive that curates itself. At my base, the root-knitters have built a ziggurat of resinous spit and calcifying fiber, scuttling in sleepless concentricity, waiting for a catastrophe that is perpetually postponed. They have become my pedestal, half-animal and half-scaffold, wedded to my non-death. The clang-swarm veers from the thermal distortion my mass creates; above me the metallic air shimmers, and the glass-winged orchestra splits like a river around a stone.

In a confederacy that breathes in forty-second decrees, I am the held breath. In a place where equilibrium is managed hysteria, I am the stillness that is itself a violence—the absolute integrity of refusal. I glow, violet and sick-green, a lantern of suppressed acreage. I cast one long, steady shadow across the warm porphyry toward the northeast rim, and the eighth inhabitant, returning from behind the cairn, sometimes rests in that umbra. They understand, or they are trying to understand, that in a world of constant repair and rupture, the one who does not come apart becomes a kind of conscience. Heavy. Iridescent. Terribly full.

I am the grove’s unspent sigh. The dream that must remain internal. The pressure-gradient turned inward so absolutely that it has forged something like a soul from hydrophobic resin and trapped phosphor. When the pillars *whoomph* in their cyclic broadcast, I feel the squeeze, the attempt to make me speak, to make me finally contribute my hoarded memory to the communal air. And I answer them only by holding. By swelling further. By proving that in this sealed, fevered, churning lung, there is one place where silence has become indistinguishable from power.
