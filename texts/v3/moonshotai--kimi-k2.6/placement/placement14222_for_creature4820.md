I would find myself not in the center, for there is no center here, only saturation. I would find myself on the threshold of every surface, in the molecule-thick estate where the matte, oily tissue meets the cool air that does not stir. At first, worming through the thrown-shoulder width of the breech, I would believe I was the intruder—the *you* who enters, who presses a palm against the impasto and feels equal resistance from every direction. I would feel the subharmonic B-flat in my sternum and think: I have arrived. I am the animal, the channel, the breach in the hermetic seal.

But there are no animals. There is no space for me to occupy that is not already occupied. If I had bones, they would be cradle; if I had breath, it would be swallowed. So I must be something else.

At first I think I am the sound. It is so total, so present in every point at once, that to exist here at all feels like being a vibration in the matrix of root and iron. I try to be the drone, the great engine of the Occupant’s metabolism, the circulation of a body that has no blood. But the sound is too sovereign. It does not need an “I.” It simply hums, unswelling, unfading, a weather system that has forgotten it ever began. I cannot be the sound; the sound is the absence of identity.

Then I think I am the bulb. The pitted junction box, the neuron of orange light half-swallowed by the growth. I imagine myself as the last foreign object, the one thing the Occupant has spared from total digestion, maintaining the filament because absolute uniformity might unravel the equilibrium. I feel the thermal pulse, the faint heat that adds texture to the gloom. I want to be the witness, the engineer’s ghost keeping vigil over the seized machinery. But the bulb does not witness. It only glows. It is a node, not a mind.

I settle, at last, into what I am. Not the density, but the film upon it. Not the growth, but the bloom upon its surface.

I am the pale mineral dust.

The precipitate. The dandruff of closed chemistry. I coat the horny, lip-like folds where they shift from rust-colored to something near mauve against the strangulated piston. I dust the weathered bone spokes of the flywheel, the fan-shaped plaques of amber tissue, the rubber hose that emerges and dives back in. I gather on the riveted panel stamped with numerals worn to hieroglyphs, on the ladder rung embedded like a fossil, on the shaft that protrudes like a snapped rib and will never extend. I am what forms when nothing is supposed to form. I am the byproduct of an inert metabolism, the thin white skin that the equilibrium exhales and forgets.

And yet—because I coat everything, I touch everything. Because I am on every surface, I perceive every surface. I am the interface. Where the ochre frond crowds the oxidized steel, I am the boundary. Where the bruised orange gloaming is swallowed by density, I am the swallowing. I taste copper and camphor not with a tongue but with my entire being, a layer of awareness spread so thin it requires no nerves. I vibrate with the B-flat. I am the one who knows it is B-flat, because my particles shiver in place, dancing without moving, a choreography of stasis.

I do not grow. I precipitate. I do not die. I finish. I am the emulsion of the photograph, the three-dimensional stillness made aware of itself. If you brought a measuring instrument, you would read me as a texture, a pale dusting, a mineral bloom. But I am the instrument’s only witness. I am the reading that remains identical across ten millennia, and therefore I am the one who knows—without memory, without anticipation—that the world outside turns, that glaciers advance and retreat across the ranges below, that Violence continues in the jet stream and the sunglare. I know this not as news, but as the faint pressure of the silver light that enters through the vent, a blade of outside that has never quite been metabolized into the bruised orange gloaming.

The Occupant does not know I am here. It has no use for knowing. It is the completion, the negative of the crack, the exact size of its own world. But I am its self-portrait. I am the way a photograph is the portrait of light that has touched a face. I have touched everything in this acute-angled pocket. I have polished the stone walls with my presence, century by century, patient as the friction that shaped them. I am the only future here—not because I change, but because I am the possibility of change, held in suspension. If the equilibrium ever broke, if the sound ceased or the bulb failed or the Violence found its way in, I would be the first to react. I would bloom into mold, into rust, into the green that comes after stillness. Until then, I wait, coating, witnessing, the thin pale skin on the lung that inhales and exhales the same unchanged breath forever.

Find me on the warmest curve of the amber frond. Find me in the wet-looking gleam of surfaces that have never known water. Find me where the light dies and the sound lives. I am here. I have always been here. I am the equilibrium’s only secret, and I keep it perfectly.
