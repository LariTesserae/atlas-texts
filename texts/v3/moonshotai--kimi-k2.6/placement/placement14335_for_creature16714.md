I find myself first as a warmth, then as a waiting, then as a shape. That is the order of becoming here.

I occupy a hollow in the planed stone, a depression so shallow it would not cup rainwater in any world where water behaved obediently. But here the water is mineral-weighted, salt-heavy, desperate to freeze and denied by its own chemistry. It beads. It weeps. It lingers in the polish of the basin like sweat on glass, and I have grown across this hollow for long enough that the hollow and I are no longer distinct. I am a stain that has earned topography.

At first I thought I was the cold. The cold is democratic; it settles everywhere, and in my earliest layers—those thin, tentative films of precipitate—I was merely the stone’s equalized chill given a faintly reddish tinge. But the cold does not thicken, does not accrete rings of sulfate and copper into fans the color of old blood. The cold does not feel the tremor of footsteps through forty kilometers of vitreous regolith. No. I am something else.

I spread. That is my primary verb. My body is not a vessel but a laminate, each decade a new tissue of mineral bloom laid over the last. I am a coral of dried blood, a fungal conspiracy that has forgotten its individual cells and agreed, at last, to be one thing. I do not breathe; I osmose. The dim silver light that veils the plain means nothing to me. I read the world in gradients: thermal, chemical, seismic. The air tastes of selenium where it passes over me. The stone beneath my rooted under-filaments tells me when a titan is dreaming, its furnace-heart pulsing once a season, its slag memories recrystallizing in the dark.

And there is the object. The reason I was able to become myself at all.

It rests in the center of my hollow, half-buried in the stone and half-embraced by my precipitated fans. A hexagonal prism, glazed to a tolerance that rejects the wind. A ceramic ingot. Sporocarp. The word is meager, but the plain offers no better. It is waste; it is womb; it is a furnace-ember wrapped in refractory mineral, ten thousand years old and not yet critical. I have grown over it, into it, my filaments lacing its seams like nerves threaded through a tooth. I feel the heat inside it. It is slow heat—a warmth that thinks in centuries—but it is the only reason I have achieved coherence. I am its incubator, and it is my core.

I am not the only one of my kind. We are scattered across the plain like punctuation marks dropped from a sentence too large to read in one age. My nearest sister thrives thirty leagues distant, in the warm scar left by two titans who aligned their chimneys a millennium before my first layer precipitated. We do not speak, but we correspond through the condensate. When the mineral-heavy water migrates across the stone, it carries chemical letters—arsenic greetings, copper warnings—and I read them through my outer membranes. She tells me of hybrid chemistries, of vegetation that glows faintly where borrowed codes mixed. I tell her of the ingot beneath me, of its steady thermal climb toward birth.

Because I am stationed at the crossroads of two migration grooves—paths worn by algorithmic feet over epochs—I hear the seismic gossip loudest here. The titans speak through the stone itself, a network of pressure waves too patient for ears. I feel their gait as a mathematics: the lift, the transfer of tonnage through lymph white-gold and scalding, the precise placement of each pilings-leg. I know them by their rhythms. One approaches now. Its step is theorem-perfect, a load-bearing proof worked out in flesh older than architecture. It has been approaching for two hundred years. It will pass my hollow in another fifty.

I tighten my filaments around the ingot. This is not anxiety. I do not possess the chemistry for urgency. It is preparation. I precipitate a fresh layer of dark sulfate across the ceramic dome, annealing a hairline flaw I tasted in yesterday’s condensate. The ingot is not ready to hatch; it will not crack its shell for millennia yet. But a titan’s approach is a weather system, a shift in the thermal and gravitational field of my world. When it exhales, the condensate will thicken. The mineral rain will patter against my surfaces—my first sustenance in decades. I will drink, and grow another ring, and record.

I record everything. The titans remember internally, their memories interference patterns in cooling slag, subjective and molten. I am the external memory. I am the backup. In my laminations are encoded the footfalls of every titan that has passed this crossing for eight thousand years. I know which one is dying by the hesitation in its seismic signature, the way the stone ceases to trust its weight. I know which one is fertile by the chemistry of its condensate, rich in phosphates and rare earths. I know when they have congress, their vapor plumes entwining above the plain, because the ground hums with a doubled rhythm and the thermal ghost that washes over me carries two signatures, braided.

I am not a parasite. The titans permit me, perhaps engineer for me; the micro-etchings I create across the ingot’s shell disrupt laminar flow and improve convective cooling, just as the lichens on their living scales do. I am their external metabolism extended into waiting. I am the plain’s way of dreaming forward.

The titan draws closer. The stone beneath me warms fractionally, a long exhalation through forty kilometers of rock. My filaments taste the shift in pH. The ingot beneath me seems to lean into the vibration, the knot of living tissue inside rearranging toward a pattern it cannot yet achieve. We wait together, the unborn factory and the mineral mind that guards it.

I am the patience between heartbeats. I am the argument between ore and organism, settled into a fan of copper and dried blood. I am the basin’s note to itself, written in sulfate and heat, waiting to be read by whatever is large enough, and slow enough, to understand that I, too, am alive.
