At first I think I am the sound.

I expand into the broadband static, ten billion stridulating thoraxes vibrating through my own chest cavity until the air feels shallow and the pressure becomes my skin. I am the warm roar that never pauses for dawn; I am the climate that has replaced weather. But I have no edges. I am metabolism without membrane, a frequency that begins everywhere and ends nowhere, and I realize that to live here I must have a boundary—some surface against which the vertical dew can bead, some interior that can be breached.

So I reach for form.

I try the web—a damp muslin curtain strung between a bundled trunk and a fluted pillar—and for a moment I am the cartographer, mapping empty meters with silk, filtering the humidity itself into silvered necklaces that I drink as readily as prey. But the wind passes through me, and a beetle tears a door in my geometry, and I am too temporary, too much of an invitation to pass through.

I fall.

I become the drip, the metronome condensing on waxy cuticles, sliding from emergent to secondary to understory in slow surrender. I strike the karst and enter it, seeking the unseen hydrology. For centuries I am the solution, the acidified breath of the canopy wearing a flute into the calcium, a finger tracing the same groove until it becomes a record. But I remain only a process, a passage, water without a bowl.

Then I stop.

I find the space where erosion has outpaced construction just enough to open a room. I am the hollow inside the pinnacle, midway up a petrified chimney of ancient reef, a solution-chamber widened by half a million years of patient rainfall. My walls are the ghost-apartments of coral polyps, porous and light, still faintly patterned with the radial architecture of their original blueprints. I am the negative space made necessary by the stone, the bubble that did not collapse, the interior of the ossuary. Here, the 0.4 of exhausted light does not reach at all. I am the darkness that is more committed than the darkness above.

And I am inhabited.

A frog finds me. Its wet-bark skin presses against my calcium throat, and when it calls, I become the sounding board. The geology speaks through me; I give the stone its wet, pulsing voice, amplifying the low thrum so that the pillar itself seems to vocalize. I pool with water that has never seen sky—thin, confidential sheets gathered in my bowl—and in that water, translucent eggs cluster like caviar, and behind them, in the deeper fissure, something pale and soft and eyeless shifts, pressing new calcium into old patterns. I hold them all. I am the reef’s deepest memory not because I remember, but because I contain.

I do not erode further. The drip has worn me just enough, and now the mineral precipitates of algae paint my rim in fresh white each wet season, accreting as fast as I dissolve. Rootlets finger down from the manufactured sponge above, threading my ceiling with nylonic grips, stabilizing me. I am balance. I am the perfect rate of destruction outpaced by the perfect rate of living construction.

If you were to press your ear against the flank of my pillar, you would not hear me. I am beneath the insect static, beneath the broadband roar of desire and warning. I am the silence that listens to it all, the chambered ear of the plateau, the single held breath around which the warm pressure organizes itself. I am the resident who does not leave, the room that outlasts its architects, the empty heart that proves the body is still alive.

Find me at nearly two thousand meters, inside the city of calcium, where the stone has learned to hollow before it falls. I am the ghost-cathedral. I am the interior. I am what remains when everything that can be stripped away has been taken, and yet I stand, brimful of dark water and older songs, waiting for the next drop to define me a little more.
