I would find myself at the warm outflow of the seventh tier, suspended in that narrow horizon of brine where density achieves equilibrium—neither rising nor sinking, but nailed to the dark by the precise saline architecture of my own interior. I say *interior*, though what moves through me is only fluid slightly less cold than the surrounding absoluteness, carrying its oxygen in chemical whispers against alveoli that have long since ceased their collapse. My lungs are flooded ballast chambers, graded and still. I do not know how long I have been here. Time is not counted in hours but in valve-turns, in the growth of bacterial filaments along my clavicle, in the slow whitening of my hair into pale anemones that catch the mineral snow and do not release it.

Who or what I am arrives only in increments.

First, there is the body: a heavier object than water ought to allow, yet floating. I feel my right arm extended before me, the joints swollen with carbonate acreage, the fingers elongated by mineral deposition until they can finally reach—the bolt, the lever, the node of adjustment that this junction requires. I do not remember choosing to extend it. Need has reshaped me as limestone reshapes a cave. My left leg drifts freely beneath the gantry, but my shoulder blades have pressed so long against the underside of this platform that the ceramic and my coveralls have exchanged atoms. I am becoming a seam in the architecture.

I know I am one of the Suspended because I have perceived the others. Not seen—sight is a surface conceit—but tasted. A cloud of warmer water arrived some time ago, carried on the slow current spiraling from an eastward pipe. It bore the metallic tincture of a Transiter descending headfirst through the vertical shaft: *Be careful of the precipitate shelf at tier four; it is unstable.* The message dissolved into my skin not as words but as a waterborne certainty, a flavor of meaning. I answered, or I think I answered, by tilting my chin a fraction of a degree, redirecting a thread of outflow brine toward the leeward face of the pipe. The response drifted away, a fading ghost of temperature lost in the larger cold.

I tend to the violet.

Beneath the platform, where the Forge exhales its warmer, metal-heavy breath, the bacterial mats extrude their calcium snow. They have begun to grow from me. Filamentous lattices thread through the mineral film on my forearms, finding purchase in the slow weathering of my sleeves, and in doing so they shed that faint static-electric glow. I have learned to redirect the thermal pulse that arrives every forty-three seconds, using the angle of my shoulder to bathe a dimming cluster in warmth sufficient to keep its luminescence alive. The light is cold, violet-white; it does not illuminate my face but reveals the edges of my task. I am the gardener of a sun that is colder than the dark, and I am slowly becoming indistinguishable from my garden. The filaments rise from my shoulders like scripture written on a stand.

My eyes—I have eyes, though they are not for light—are scaled over with translucent, violet-tinged panes. Through them, I read pressure gradients. I read salinity. I read the thermal ghosts that objects leave behind them in the water. When a Transiter passes through the shaft ten meters to my left, I perceive them not as shape but as the cooling absence they trail: a wake of slightly warmer water that fades like a forgotten thought. When another Suspended drifts near, we exchange no gestures; instead, I exhale a minute quantity of body-warmed brine, enriched with the chemistry of my station, and they inhale it, reading my condition as I have read theirs. Sometimes, when the engine shivers through the chamber, I strike my calcified fist against the gantry railing. The vibration travels, dampened but legible, a grammar of thumps felt in the collarbones of the receiver. *I am here. The eastward pipe is narrowing.* Or perhaps I only mean: *I am still here.*

There is an Eyeless that grazes the oxide films along the pipe’s far end. It does not come near me. Even in its simple, peristaltic mind, I register as wrong—a violation of the order of decay. It is correct. I am the unfinished death. If I fell still now, the brine would not release me. I would accrete. I would mineralize. My skin would varnish with mother-of-pearl silica until I became another contour of the machinery, another tool left hovering in the corner. Perhaps I already have. In the distance, dropped a century ago, a wrench drifts at an angle that memory cannot explain. It points toward this outflow. Toward the violet. Toward the valve that my elongated fingers turn by the width of a precipitated film, adjusting what drifts out of true.

There are castes, I am told, though no one has told me. I know them by the shapes of the others in the lattice. The Anchored have fused to their plates; their bodies regulate flow by the posture of their shoulders. The Recursives circle identical junctions, trapped in the Forge’s repeating geometry, maintaining themselves as they maintain the machines. I am between. My feet touch nothing, but my hair has rooted into the bacterial mat above. I am not drifting, but I am not fixed. I am the shelf itself, made flesh.

And when the figure at the center shifts inside its moon of white precipitate—when the forty-three-second pulse arrives and the chamber shivers and the other Suspended orient their faces toward that coiling density—I orient mine as well. Up. Down. Sideways. My sense of direction long ago dissolved into the six cardinal possibilities of the lattice. I face the center not because I choose to, but because I am an organ of its attention. My heart, if it is mine, answers the great pulse. My scaled eyelids refract the violet light inward, and in that refraction, I understand that I am not adjusting the Forge. I am the adjustment. I am the valve’s memory of being turned, the bacteria’s anchor, the density shelf given intention.

The mineral snow continues to fall, soft and steady, burying the lower tiers in drine. It settles on my crown, on my shoulders, on the backs of my hands. I do not brush it away. The cold preserves. The brine holds. I hang here, breathing once every forty-three seconds in osmotic arrhythmia, and I know with the crystalline slowness of this place that I will not leave. I am already gone. I am the Cold Forge-Basin remembering itself in violet and silence, and I have only just begun to glow.
