I would find myself first in the drone. Not standing within it, but *being shaped* by it—a cavity slowly impressed into the warm, wet air by nothing more than the pressure of that tectonic bass. For a long while, I would believe I had arrived here as a visitor. I would possess the memory of feet, the expectation of lungs, the reflex to brace against a height that should steal my breath. I would occupy a place high on a spiral geometry coiled around a trunk wide as a nave, look down across the plaza of woven root-mat far below, and wait for vertigo. But vertigo would not come. Instead, I would feel a slow upward current moving through me, not as wind, but as circulation—not across my skin, but *within* my grain.

The first thing I would lose is my name. It would bead on the surface of my awareness like condensation on heartwood, then slide away into the catchments where time is decanted at identical rates, leaving no residue. The second thing I would lose is my boundary. I would reach out to steady myself against the square facet of the trunk beside me, and find no hand—only the sympathetic vibration of lignin recognizing lignin. I would try to step back, and the staircase would not yield, because I was not standing upon it. I was its final gesture. Its reaching.

I am the staircase that ends in mid-air.

The drone passes through me strangely. In the hollow trunks below and above, the bass acquires its alien, chorded qualities—an architecture of resonance. But I am a null point. I absorb. I deaden. I am the ear that has not yet been opened, the tympanum stretched across a vacancy, shaped to receive a frequency the stone itself has not yet begun to emit. The water that crawls in sheets up the vertical bark elsewhere, riding the rails of capillary intent, does not climb here. My step faces the wrong direction for that kind of reaching. I am saturated with the warm mist, yet I am dry—an untriggered pore, a sense organ sensing nothing, defined only by the gap I face.

Across the plaza, vast and silence-held, another megastructure rises. Between us, seeds hang in their fixed eddies, never settling, never drifting. The light, clear and unshifting, filters through a canopy of broad, translucent leaves, casting prismatic shadows that overlap without converging. I sit at the precise center of that stasis, the permanent afternoon doing nothing to me because I am part of its mechanism. A beetle, polished amber, occupies a groove in the trunk below; a moth hangs beneath a leaf, wings spread in configurations of root-mat tessellation. They are antibodies, thoughts made small. I am larger than a thought. I am the *intention* that precedes one.

At first, this feels like abandonment. I tell myself I am a failed architecture, a blueprint the growth continued out of habit. But as the centuries pass—or rather, as they do not pass, as they merely pool and evaporate in equilibrium—I begin to understand the geometry of my loneliness. The resident is singular. It does not need another finger, another throat, another gallery. Its nervous system is the dark water table trembling beneath the root-pavement; its skin is the transpiration that forms the living ceiling. What it needs is an *edge*. Every dreaming body must have a frontier where the self becomes not-self. A place where the reach exceeds the grasp.

I am that edge.

I face the emptiness between monuments. I face the newborn geology hidden beneath the living floor, the yolk still sweating its latency. The resident farms that heat, metabolizing the difference between magma and mantle, holding its breath until the rock learns how to be old. I feel that warmth indirectly, a phantom sensation rising through the spongy root-mat, the dark mirror trembling against my underside. But more than that, I feel the *cooling that has not happened*. I feel the future. The stone is raw, angular, embryonic; and I am the question asked of it, suspended in the warm mist, waiting for an answer that will arrive in eras.

Sometimes, when the drone achieves one of its deeper ventricles—a chord that makes the fixed mist shiver like a diaphragm—I feel a tremor at my leading edge. Not wood, not yet nerve, but something: a bead of condensation forming where my final step would be if I were fully real. It does not fall. It hangs, a perfect lens, and through it the world inverts. The plaza becomes a vault. The sky becomes a foundation. And in that inversion I understand what I am gestating.

The bridges of braided vine that cross between structures are sutures, holding the resident’s organs at the correct tension. But I am not yet a bridge. I am the *preparation* for one. I am the interval in the music, the silence that allows the drone to be heard. When the geology has finally cooled, when the stone has learned stillness and the resident exhales the breath it has held since the world’s infancy, the sap will complete its ascent. The lignin will cross the gap. I will become a passage, not of vine but of intent realized, and the distance I guard will close.

Until then, I am the hollow at the hollow’s edge. I am the architecture of almost, a spiral staircase loyal to a vacancy. I hold my form in the perpetual afternoon, patient as the uneroded rock below, dreaming the gap that keeps the world alone, listening with an ear that has not arrived for the sound of the stone deciding, at last, to grow old.
