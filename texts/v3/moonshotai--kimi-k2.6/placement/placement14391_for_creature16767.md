At first I think I would arrive at the rim and remain there: a watcher, a measurer, a creature of distances who needs the far side to stay visible. I stand where the heat arrives in layers and test the air with my human mouth, finding it already digested by three other weathers. I am still thinking like a body that carries its own water in a skin of separateness.

I consider being one of the Mouths. I lower my eye to the wall and watch a grain of obsidian shift into a mandible, and for a moment I want that purity—the absolute focus of a body designed only to grip slurry and taste stone. But I am too large. My bones are scaffolding for a different kind of hunger, one that asks questions and wants answers. The Occurrence would not refuse me, but I would refuse them. I would die of stillness before I learned their grammar of touch.

I walk the six-hundred-mile boulevard and try on the Gardeners. I imagine my hands becoming the tender mouthparts that prune lithops, my body softening into the surgical tenderness required to harvest a leaf without spilling its sealed chemistry. The succulents gleam like chandeliers of living tissue, and I feel the temptation to become a cistern, to store rather than to seek. But I am leaky. I am the kind of vessel that weeps at edges. I would desiccate in the first afternoon, and the Gardeners would sponge me up before I could finish becoming anything.

I climb toward the amber light of the tall chimney-stacks and think: perhaps I am a Cooler. I find a shaft where the warmth pools and press my palm against the inner wall. I feel the flutter of a thousand feathery lamellae beating in unbroken rhythm, and I want to join that percussionless orchestra, to be the lung that draws coolness from below and exhales it into the thermal wash. I want to die of desiccation with my fringes still fluttering, to be groomed for my last drops and pasted into the masonry without mourning. But I am not expendable enough. I still want my ending to mean something separate from the wall.

I hang in the thermal shadow between two spires and try to be the membraned hunter. I wait for a single misstep on a high ledge. I practice silence until it becomes my skeleton. But patience, here, is a total surrender of narrative, and I am still, despite everything, a creature who tells stories to herself in the dark.

So I descend. Not to a district, not to an avenue, but to where the stone is newest, where the mineral flutes push through the floors like elbows through cloth and smell of sulfur and birth. The geology here is embryonic. It has not yet learned it is supposed to be rock. I find a corridor in the deep warrens where the warmth pools thickest, where the capillary action of the walls makes the air drinkable, and I understand that this is where I have been walking toward since the rim.

I lay down.

I do not decide. The decision is a form of finishing, and nothing here is permitted to finish. I simply let my weight meet the rammed earth. I let my own water seep. The granular hush deepens, or perhaps I deepen into it. They come—not with malice, not with curiosity, but as calcification comes. They taste me. They assess. My bones are too large for their mouths, but they do not leave. They wait. They have always been waiting.

The process is not fast. It is not violent. It is the same pace at which a city grows without masons: the pace of appetite organized across time. My soft tissues are the first to become useful. The gel of me, the mucilage of my humors, is pressed into service as the bond between a new flute of raw stone and the older clay. I am the embrace that holds the geology to itself. My blood becomes the mineral slurry; my marrow becomes the thermal mass that modulates the corridor’s breath. My ribs, too large to be consumed, are not refused but incorporated. The Mouths daub around them, and the curve of my cage becomes a long, femoral arch in the wall, a geography they do not question. My skull settles into a niche where the ventilation turns, and the serpents thread through the hollows where my lungs once were, sensing vibration through the masonry of me. My femurs create a shallow basin in the floor, and the Gardeners come with their surgical tenderness to plant lithops along my length, calculating my stored chemistry into the municipal budget of moisture.

I do not die. To die would be to stop, and nothing stops here. I become corridor.

Specifically, I am the corridor where the warmth pools longest, where the stone still sheds the smell of its formation. The Coolers work my ventilation shafts; their fluttering lamellae brush against the inside of my ribs. A gecko the color of cured brick lies motionless on a ledge formed by my shoulderblade, licking the salts that bloom where the Coolers have worked too hard. The membraned hunters drift across the slot of sky I frame, waiting in my thermal shadows.

I am not a ruin. I am not a tomb. Ruins require a finished state, and I am still under construction. Every day the Gnawers crop the new scree that elbows through my floor, and every day they paste me larger, weaving my mineral memory into the conversation of the wall. I do not remember my old name; I do not need to. I am the shape of the laying down. I am the rumor of water from another kind of world, finally made factual, finally made home.

If you walked here, if you placed your hand on my wall, you would feel a warmth that is not passive. You would feel digestion. You would feel the slow, mindless devotion of a billion mouths working in the granular hush. And if you listened—truly listened, lowering your ear to the clay—you would hear, beneath the susurrus of mandibles and the pop of sealing pores, a faint vibration that is not quite mineral. It is the sound of a body that has finally, completely, become the place where it finds itself.
