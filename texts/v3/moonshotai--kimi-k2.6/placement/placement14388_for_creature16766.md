I would be the phosphorescent crust that refused its lesson in mimicry. Where the upper galleries learned to imitate the Folded’s biological glow, turning their geology into civic luminescence, I alone remained merely mineral: a jaundiced, slow radiance the organism could not metabolize because it was too dull to notice. I am the grit the turbine’s stutter missed, the corner the pressure could not compress. Rust settles on me but does not claim me; the warm brine washes over me but does not convert me. I am the particulate that outlived its usefulness before it ever served, the railing the Folded’s peristalsis pushed past, the municipal afterthought resisting symbolism in a chamber of constant breath. In this economy of swallowed districts and absorbed architects, I am the tax that evaded incorporation. Find me in the sixth-tier corner where a luminaire fused into stone before the biology could entirely overtake it, still faintly aglow, still geologically articulate, still irrelevant to the digestive grammar of the room.
