I would begin as the sound, because it is the most total thing here, the warm hand clamped over everything. But the roar will not be inhabited. It is a room without corners; to enter it is to become indistinguishable from its walls. I try to be the water next—the helical braids climbing the thermal corridors, the condensate that remembers buried momentum in its spiral DNA. Yet the water is too fluent, too syntactic; it is the city’s grammar, not its speaker. It holds meaning only for a moment before the steam shifts and the sentence dissolves. I consider the Sleepers, those tectonic dreamers braided through the mid-level vaults, but to be a Sleeper is to be a verb of such latency that action and dormancy become one continuous exhalation. I would not witness; I would merely condition. I would dream past the end of intention.

Then I consider the scutes. The Calcified Tide. The trillion-fold percussion. To be one is to be the vocabulary itself, dense, specific, beautifully null. But they are too perfectly adapted; there is no gap in them for an I to wedge itself. They vote with their bodies, and the majority is the only mind they know. To join them would be to vanish into the roar’s finest grain, another mother-of-pearl knuckle clicking across the terraces, another weathered knob waiting to fuse with the floor.

So I settle elsewhere. I settle into the exception.

I am in the Lower Basin, yes, but in a precise location: a mid-level terrace where a steam-tornado died decades ago, expending its manic energy against a thermal ridge and leaving behind a pocket of strange calm. Here, the humidity persists, the warmth persists, but the anthill frenzy abates by exactly the degree required for stillness to become possible—not common, not welcome, but *possible*, which is enough for accretion to take a different path. Here is a hexagonal socket-hole, one of thousands that pock the basalt-composite skin, brimming with warm, clear, stagnant water. The prompt called these pools the machinery’s fractured eyes, mirrors held up to the white-gold haze. I am what makes one eye see.

I am a scute that stopped.

I did not die, quite. Or rather, I died in a way that refused the usual protocol. I faltered at the rim of this socket-hole, my setae failing to find purchase on the slick ceramic, my belly cooling where it touched the water’s meniscus. The condensate found me, but instead of mineralizing me to the floor, the surface tension held me. My nacreous plates, tuned as they were to resonance, drank not oxide but reflection. Over years, my joints calcified not into the load-bearing shrug of architecture but into a fused ring at the water’s edge. My body became a collar. My shell, half-submerged, became the iris through which this particular socket-hole regards the sky.

My mandibles face downward now, into the pool. They scrape nothing. They drink the mirrored sky, a diet of white-gold diffraction that has no nutritive value and yet sustains me in a manner beyond metabolism. My thermal pits, those bouquets of heat-taste, have inverted. They no longer read the infrared gossip of vents and swarms. They emit a gentle coolness, a null-field that the surroundingarchitecture notices only as a boundary to avoid. The multitudes part around me. The steam-tornadoes, when they spawn, skirt this terrace. I am a static flaw in the dynamic, a single non-voting member of the percussive legislature, and because I do not click, I am the only ear here.

And I see. Because I am fixed, because the water in my socket-hole ripples only when the condensate drips from vaulted ceilings kilometers overhead, I accumulate duration intact. The Sleepers accumulate time too, but they dissolve it in the chemistry of dream. The swarms accumulate only strata. I accumulate the sky. I hold its faces: the particular ochre of a dust-front forty centuries past, the lavender break of a vent-malfunction that poisoned the upper haze, the ordinary bruiseless white-gold of today. Each reflection layers upon my submerged plates like oxide upon alloy, a patina of atmospheres. I am the city’s memory of weather, and because the city has forgotten it was made, it does not know I remember for it.

But there is more. When the buried grids whisper—when the momentum-exchange engines in the deep infrastructure cycle through their forty-millennia hum—the helical water in the adjacent thermal corridors aligns. The water in my socket-hole begins to turn. Not boil. Turn. A slow vortex, coriolis from dead engines, a spiral climbing in place. My body, fused to the rim, becomes a resonant chamber. The hum enters my nacreous joints. My calcified cilia, grounded still in the city’s electrical song, light up like filaments.

For a spiral of minutes, I become the witness.

I do not become the city-mind. I am too fixed, too small, too much a punctuation mark in the greater sentence. But the water turns through me, and I see the terraces before the haze swallowed them. I see the first oxide bloom, born from a young Sleeper’s first exhalation. I see the initial scute scratch the virgin alloy, and I understand that the roar was always already here, waiting for its first trillion throats. The city’s entire stratigraphy passes through my socket-hole like a chord through a tuning fork.

Then the steam shifts. The pitch breaks. The water stills.

And I am alone again with the warm, clear pool and the sky held in its eye. The condensate drips. It strikes my back—a single, sustained, wet bell-tone, phaselocked to the greater roar, inaudible to every moving thing. I drink the reflection. I wait for the vortex. I am the meniscus, the membrane, the dark fleck at the center of the water’s gaze. I have been here long enough to learn that stillness is not the absence of motion, but its perfect witness.
