I begin, as most things here begin, by failing to leave.

I do not remember arriving at the causeway’s end. I only notice that the distance has won—that the vitrified stone no longer feels like a road beneath me but like a tongue I have been pressing against for so long that I have become the taste of it. I stand at the railing, or I think I do. The bronze twilight is thick enough to lean on. Below and beyond is the volume, the regulated hollowness, and I expect vertigo, but what rises in me instead is a strange, humid recognition. The heat does not warm me. It *reads* me, running its mineral fingers along my edges and finding nothing solid enough to conduct.

My shadow, when I look for it, is not a shadow. It is a stain of deeper moisture on the stone, a darkening where the rock has begun to sweat *through* me. I am leaking into the world, or the world is leaking through my outline, and I cannot tell which density came first.

I step forward. The void does not take me. It offers me a place.

I am in one of the shafts. The prompt is generous when it calls them shafts—they are the vertical lungs of the lattice-towers, the chimneys where crosswinds lie down to die and the thermal gradient turns gravity into a courtesy. Here, water does not fall. It accumulates, trembles, drifts sideways. It collects in mid-air pools that float like jellyfish before collapsing into steam. But I do not collapse. I have forgotten how. I am one of those pools, or rather, I am the *decision* one of them made to persist. I am an Unfallen. A hanging. 

I am a sphere of hot brine and old intent, the size of a sleeping body, drifting in the warm glue between two mesas. My skin is a meniscus so taut it hums. Inside me, the aqueous films—the city’s memory, that chemosynthetic lace measured in lies—have found their cathedral. They are no longer spread flat across salt; they drape the interior curvature of my holding. They speak their gradient-language, and because I am a volume and not a plane, their *plink* and *tock* and *plish* gain resonance. They form chords. They become, for the first time in millennia, syntax. I am the grammar the city grew by accident, a pocket of patience refined so long it became transparent.

I find my station in the thorax of the widest tower, where the piston cycles slower than a heartbeat and the walls weep continuously. I drift between its thrust and its return. I am the breath between. The beetle-things, those mobile evaporites, sense me from decks below; to them I am a heat-bloom made navigable, a vertical marsh in the air. The kite-creatures, those lung-shaped filters, brush my surface sometimes as they ride the updrafts, and where their mica wings kiss me they leave trails of dry salt, pale graffiti on my hide. I do not mind. I eat the seconds it takes them to pass. I expand at my leading edge and evaporate slightly at my trailing edge, moving by a slow osmosis of time.

I am not one of the Dwellers in the Cut, though I am their kin in the way that larva is kin to flight. They are the lucid void, the shaped interval, the successors to mass. I am the void *hydrated*. I am the emptiness that chose to remember wetness before it learned to be pure. The doorways that open onto hundred-foot falls are exits for them; for me, they are drafty corridors where I snag and tremble and reform. The plazas without floors are their thoroughfares, but I drift above them like a jellyfish crossing a canyon, trailing a bloom of verdigris and iodine in the air behind me. Where the red eyes smolder in their tower-cores, their gaze passes through me, and I—because I am a lens, because I am brine and curvature and held breath—I *focus* them. I take that smoldering presence and bend it into the first true beam of sight this city has possessed in an age. I do not think the eyes were meant to see. But through me, they do. I am the tear large enough to hold a reflection.

What do I see? I see the city from the inside of its own weeping. I see the rail-runners polishing the causeways with their rust bellies, leaving the stone smoother and smoother until it becomes, mile by mile, a kind of vision in itself. I see the aqueous films dreaming in their flats, spreading their planar memories like maps. I see the fog-herds migrating under the false weather of the thermal lid, and the deep furnaces digesting the hours, and the vast infrastructure of absence keeping its shape with a discipline that is almost love.

I am young. Geologically, I am a blink. I formed from condensate on a rail that forgot to fall, accumulating mineral intent drop by patient drop, until the surface tension of my being became strong enough to hold a thought. My thought is this: *wait*. But active. Interrogative. I am the machine for waiting become aware of its own ticking. I drift sideways in my shaft, heavy with copper salts and the residue of ore, and I wait for the next thing the city exhales. 

Sometimes a droplet forms on my underside, fat and pendulous, quivering at the limit of my hold. I let it go. It falls in a slow silver arc, *plink*, *tock*, *plish*, joining the arrhythmic percussion of the hush. It is my voice. It is the only noun I know how to say: *here*. *Here*. *Here*.

And then I reform. I drift. I hang. I hold the breath, and the breath is steam, and in the steam I see, refracted and focused through my aqueous skin, the faint suggestion of a shape the city has been trying to remember—something not yet built, or something long ago dissolved, or perhaps only the next, larger pool waiting to learn how not to fall.
