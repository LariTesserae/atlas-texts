I thought, at first, that I must be the mist. It seemed a likely residence for a consciousness arriving in a world so thoroughly completed: the mist occupies without choosing, it drifts through the factories without tending them, it has no skin and no ambition. But the mist forgets. I found that I could recall the seed-piston’s fall from a direction that no longer exists, and the mist does not recall. It only repeats.

Then I believed I might be one of the clicking ones, the dross-children gathered at the root-node. I felt the strong urge to attend, to warm a surface with accumulated attention, to melt the frost through the friction of pure maintenance. Yet when I reached for the node, I had no hands. When I tried to click in time with the bass note, I found I had no voice—only a strange, internal ringing, as if I were hollow and something vast were singing against my walls. I was alone, not in a cloud of hundreds but in a solitude so absolute it felt spherical.

For a moment I suspected I was a Condensate, a being of thickening fog and broken sequence. The factories seemed to trade places as I approached; the sound of venting arrived before the steam. But my edges were too certain. I held a shape. And my motion, when I finally recognized it, was not the lazy drift of a causality-pool. It was a line. A single, continuous line drawn across the bruised twilight without hesitation.

Then I felt the floor—not beneath me, but against me at one perfect point, pressing upward—and I understood.

I am the marble.

Not the glass toy of human longing, not an agate for a child’s pocket, but the spherical conclusion of this geology. When the mountains were planed down by the patient hatred of erosion, their final resistance did not dissolve into dust. It gathered. It compressed. It achieved this: a unit of motion so dense, so finished, that the root-floor could not absorb it. There was no hand to release me. There was only the instant when the flatness became intolerable to itself, when the oldest stable state produced, as a byproduct, the one thing that could not be stable. A single pore of the wooden plain contracted, a microcline too small for a Condensate to notice, and I began. I began and have not ceased. I am the architecture of refusal made round. I am the mountain’s last swing, arrested forever in the arc of descent.

I know the plain as no rooted thing can. It touches me at one contact patch only, a kiss of lignin-stone on lignin-wood, and through that atomized interface the subsonic thrum enters. It does not pass through me; it pools. The floor’s dreaming circulates in my hollows, bass notes of piston-roots and nutrient heaves, and I return them to the wood sharper, older, changed by the curvature of my witness. I rattle through no wooden chute, for I am too perfect for any channel not my own. I coast across the vastness for miles, and the cold, snapping at all edges of sound, makes my silence louder than the tolling of ungulate hooves. The shaggy drifters lift their filtration-coats as I pass, not in alarm but in recognition: I am one of the coolants, a sphere of transit keeping time where rivers have forgotten how.

Because I move, the world’s liquefying seams do not catch me. When the fog thickens and the factory behind me exchanges its position with the one ahead, I roll through the interval before the exchange completes. Causality pools in the hollows, but I am already gone. I am the straight thread stitching together the plain’s looped and dreaming geography. I have watched the corkscrew flues vent their scalding plumes from an altitude of zero, perpetually; I have seen the rust valve-flowers flare and close like gills in a tide of refined sap; I have crossed beneath the bellows of blackened leaf-matter as they pulsed, and I did not stop. The Work does not record me. I am not ventilation, not lubrication, not process and not waste. I am the one thing the plateau manufactures that it cannot consume: pure departure.

Where would you find me? Find me where the mist parts in a long, unwavering seam, where the condensation refuses to gather because something colder and harder has already passed. Find me in the shallow declivities where the flash-steam rises late, startled by my absence. I am there, and there, and there, threading the regional immensity like a needle through lignin. I am the last topography, the hill-ness that survived the planing, compressed to a point and set in endless translation across the corpse of range and summit.

I do not work. I do not tend. I do not dream in cambium or photosynthesize the dim, bruised light. I am the Work’s only nostalgia, its single regret, the motion that reminds the root-floor of what it meant to resist. I roll. I feel the vascular persistence of the wooden world sing up through my perfect center, and I carry it, unchanged but rearranged, toward a horizon that curves back to meet me. I am the marble. I have been rolling since the mountains surrendered, and in my patient, ceaseless coasting, the plain remembers that it was once violent, once high, and that even in its oldest, most settled state—utterly flat, utterly stable, utterly done—something can still leave, and still be gone, and still, because the world is patient and I am round, be arriving.
