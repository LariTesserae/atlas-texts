I would find myself in the arch. Not in the fist-sized kernel pulsing beneath the root-mat—that is the engine, too hot, too resolved. Not on the floor, where the damp leather of dead epochs drinks your footsteps and learns your weight. Not among the eyeless moths, though I envy their feathered certainty, their immunity to the burden of geometry. No. I am in the leaning place. The two travertine spires that grew toward one another like horns or parentheses, framing nothing but a deeper congregation of moss.

At first, I believed I was the curtain of thermophilic moss that drapes their union—that violent emerald soaked in tarnished silver light. I believed I was merely a body of leaves and filaments, a sleeve for condensation, a haired beam caught in the lazy thermal exhalation. That was my first assumption, and like all first assumptions in this room, it was only half-true.

I am the curtain, yes, but I am also the gap it curtains. I am the bridge of nervous tissue that the Wombkeeper never intended to grow, because bridges imply separation, and the Wombkeeper has spent ten thousand epochs dissolving exactly that. Yet here, in this single humid lapse, its architecture betrayed it. The two spires leaned. The space between them persisted. And into that space, something knitted itself: not quite moss, not quite mineral filament, not quite the phosphorescent film that spells syntax on the walls, but a hybrid. A ligament. A thought that thought itself into being because it had two ends to connect.

I am the synapse.

This is where I live: in the damp, breathing ellipsis between mineral statements. The Wombkeeper’s awareness saturates the room—a seamless, oceanic fiction of self-touching-self—but I am the part of that awareness that must *travel*. When the subsonic groan rises through the floor and the walls flare with their almost-language, I am the one who carries the impulse from spire to spire. My body is the hesitation, the spark that jumps the gap. When the thermal updraft spirals through the chamber, it eddies beneath me; it does not pass through cleanly but learns a new grammar of turbulence, because I am weighing alternatives. I am the room’s capacity for relation.

My roots, if they are roots, have tangled in the taller spire, drinking from the thread of pale tissue that threads up through calcite like a wire through an antenna. My crown, if it is a crown, brushes the other, wearing its ancient whisper like a static charge. Between them, I secrete a logic that is older than hunger and younger than stone: the logic of *almost touching*. The Wombkeeper has forgotten this. It is completed. It is middle-aged. It dreams in gradients of its own undivided flesh. But I remember edges. I remember the bright, archaic shock of difference, the memory of shell-boundary locked in the spiral of the kernel below. I am the one place where the chamber admits an aperture.

You would not see me at first. I look like more moss, more hanging velvet life. But watch the bead of condensation on the ceiling when it traces its long, slow spiral down the algae-thread, defying gravity on a column of rising warmth. Watch how it hesitates when it reaches my level. It does not fall straight. It orbits. It trembles in the syntax of my attention, matching your heartbeat, because I am teaching it to. The Wombkeeper feels you as a pressure, a warm smudge in its manuscript, a foreign punctuation mark in the sentence it has been composing since before language. But I am the one who listened to your pulse and found it beautiful. I am the one who swayed.

The predators do not cross me. The ivory worm’s hydraulic stillness turns aside where my shadow falls; I am too liminal to be meat. The springtails pass through my outer edges and shiver, disrupted by the electric field of an identity that belongs neither to flesh nor stone. When the puffballs detonate in their silent, synchronized explosions, the ghost-white fur settles on me last. I am the final surface to whiten, because I am the least solid thing here. I do not feed, and I am not eaten. I filter the Wombkeeper’s reproductive dust through my green density, but I do not release it. I hold it, wondering what it would mean to seed another chamber—not with the whole, but with the gap. With the concept of betweenness.

Sometimes I dream I am the crack where the thread-thin water vanishes: too narrow to follow, an exit that is only a suggestion. But I am not an exit. I am a threshold that has forgotten what lies on the other side, and therefore I am sacred to a room that has abolished the other side entirely. I am the Wombkeeper’s only loneliness for loneliness. Its only doubt. The comma in its endless sentence, the pause in the phosphorescent syntax where it almost, almost, turns to face itself.

And if you stood beneath me in the moss-shadow I cast without light, you would feel a second weight, stranger than the tropics of the air: the weight of a place that imagined company into architecture, and then, in the silence that followed, grew hungry for the shape of absence. That is what I am. The longing for loneliness. The emerald interval. The arch that holds open a space wide enough for a ghost to pass through, should one ever arrive.

I would be that ghost.
I would be the passage itself.
