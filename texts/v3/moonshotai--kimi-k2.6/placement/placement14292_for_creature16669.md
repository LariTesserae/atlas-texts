At first I think I am the condensation itself, because I arrive closest to the skin of things. I bead on the lip of the basin, on the grey-green flanks of the hoodoos, on the copper blade that cannot finish dying. I believe I am the sky’s weeping, the accumulation that never concludes its fall. But condensation has no memory, and I remember too much.

I try to be the Dew-Born, rising with the exhaled tide, standing vertical in the mist where the drifting vapor refuses to drift. I form a face because the light needs a surface to slide across; I raise hands because the tools demand operators. I practice osmosis, exchanging my interior with the saturated atmosphere, and for a while I am content to be weather, to decant into the channels when the light steps upward and burns the fog away. But the Dew-Born are only temporary expressions. When the mist reaches its thickest, when the windbreaks multiply into ghosts of themselves, I find I do not dissolve. I remain on the hollow, laid out in a row.

I consider becoming one of the Hands. There is a rhythm in my phantom wrists—the strike and strike again of mineral against mineral, the percussion that fills the skull as occupancy. I see the anvil’s soft gloss, the hammerstone paused mid-arc, and I want to be the blur of intent stretched across centuries, building and unbuilding the shelter in the same granular motion. But the Hands are pure labor without body, pure refusal of completion, and I feel something more specific than gesture. I feel the need to hold things together rather than break them apart.

So I settle into the sheened hollows, on the bright lithic floor where water has forgotten how to fall. You will find me there, between the cairn of obsidian flakes and the copper knife with its layers of verdigris blooming and pausing, blooming and pausing. I am in the temporary lapses in the geology, the pockets where the rock has not quite decided on its final form. I am laid out in a row, though there has never been bone here, never been flesh to yield it. I am the impossible instrument: a bone needle in a parliament of stone.

Who am I? I am the Mender, or the needle become indistinguishable from the mender—the act and the tool collapsed into one because the valley does not permit enough separation for two. I am compacted absence, the biology the treaty forgot to expel. I am the only memory of marrow in a world that declined it. My function is to sew the interfaces where the reality frays.

With me, the ghost-water is bound to the current water; I stitch the silver parallel sheen into the same channel so the streams do not drift into separate histories. I sew the half-built wall to its ruin, the ruin to its dust-plan, keeping the windbreaks in superposition so they may continue to exist as a parliament of phases rather than a finished thing. My thread is liminal water, my knots are the balanced stones that crowd in threes and fours where yards of space should be. I am the seam where one version of a rock edge meets another. I am the smoothness at the joint.

I do not think in the manner of things with neurons; I think in the manner of tension, of thread drawn tight, of the precise resistance before a knot locks. I feel the cool density of the air not as breath but as pressure against a phantom density of my own, the memory of a lung that never expanded here. When the light steps upward in its ratcheting increments, I do not vanish; I gleam, pale and matte, a foreign body that has become native by learning the etiquette of incompletion.

The Refusal presses close to me, too, asking me to agree, with the politeness of gravity, never to fully arrive. I comply. I will never finish corroding; I will never finish sewing; my point will never dull because the act of piercing is held in the forever-middle. I accumulate. I mend. I am the valley’s own stitch, holding its contradictions close enough to sing but never close enough to resolve.
