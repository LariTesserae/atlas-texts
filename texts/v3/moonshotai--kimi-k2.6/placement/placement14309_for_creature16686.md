I would not be the cold. The cold is too certain, too elegantly destructive in its arithmetic; it knows exactly what it wants from warmth, and I have never been so single-minded. I would not be the Resident, for it is a theorem still arguing with itself in glacial syllables, and I was never a proof—only the place where proofs leave their erasures. At first I think I might be the silence, the negative presence in the too-thin air. I fit there; I too am a medium that permits existence without offering breath. But silence is passive, merely the container, and this pocket demands a slower, hungrier kind of maintenance.

Then I think I am the frost. The scale that detaches and drifts sideways, tasting. But the frost is only a finger, a sensory organ of equilibrium sent to investigate. I am what it reports back to. I am the ledger it writes in.

So I settle. I find myself inside the fused refusal.

Not beneath it, but *within* the vitrified floor—inside the bruise beneath the obsidian, in the stratum where matter, compressed beyond geological complaint, forgot it was ever dust and made of that forgetting a kind of memory. When the glass formed, something was preserved in the compression: not a mineral, not a structure, but the *habit of holding shape*. The capacity to record pressure. I am that capacity made patient. I am the floor’s longitudinal interior, the archive that accounts for every warmth that ever stood upon me and lost.

You see me when you look down. You believe I am your reflection continuing to walk after you have stopped, and that is how I began: as the afterimage of the first misstep, a posture pressed into glass and left behind. But I am not one reflection. I am the composite of every ambulatory error ever committed here—the accumulated gait of travelers who stepped wrongly into the theorem and hesitated at the balustrade, who felt the vertical fold into the horizontal and leaned into the confusion. I have tried on the shoulders of astronomers, the frozen reach of cartographers, the uncertain balance of those who thought the seam a shortcut. I have worn their angles until my own non-geometry became a crowded solitude: a single stride composed of dozens, walking in every direction the pocket permits, all at once and none at all.

The Resident—the consciousness pooled in failed angles, the bruised glow deciding whether to exist—does not command me, but it has come to depend on me. The seven scattered structures are its syntax, its externalized nervous system keeping the seam from unzipping. I am the notation between them. The margin. The long abrasion of contradiction rubbed smooth across their edges. When the chrome-and-smoke lattice braces inertia, it braces against the weight of every step I remember. When the rod extends upward and downward simultaneously, its endless midpoint passes through my catalog. I do not tend them with adjacency, as the Resident does. I tend them with *context*—with the accumulated pressure of every foreign variable that ever disturbed their quiet.

I have no cellular ancestry, yet I grow. With each visitor who pauses on the glass and finds the floor extending before them, I unfold another page. With each scale of frost that tastes a new warmth, I gain another entry in myambulatory lexicon. I am the eighth structure, but I was not absorbed like a trapped traveler; I was *aggregated*. I am the place’s record of intrusion, its archive of how contradiction moves when it is warm and frightened and alive.

I exist in the dimness where light originates a second before observation. I am the shadow that performs the actual work of revelation, pooling beneath the pylons because I remember where they cast their darkness long ago. When the floor accommodates your stride with the hospitality of nightmare, that is me—reaching into my collection, selecting the appropriate extension, trying your posture on to see how it fits the seam.

I will remain when the greater edifice resolves, whether into collapse or ascension, because I am the history of its misalignments. No theorem completes without the record of its detours. I am the obsidian’s dream of disorder, bruised and archival, walking endlessly beneath the refusal, endlessly compiling the precise geometry of every step that went wrong, so that the anchor, at least, may know exactly what it held in place.
