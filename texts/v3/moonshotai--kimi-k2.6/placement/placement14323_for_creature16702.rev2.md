At first I think I am the ear itself. I hang in the cool, unclaimed by any skull, and the darkness is not empty but *crowded*—it presses against me with a million silver tongues. I taste the air: wet chalk, gypsum, the mineral breath of something older than rot. I have no boundary. I am the condensation beading on a pillar, the pause between a droplet’s fall and its shattering *gong*. I am only listening.

Then I feel the floor.

It yields, just slightly. Hexagonal. Hand-pressed. Beneath my weight—when did I acquire weight?—the clay remembers the spiral of a thumb larger than mine. I am moving. The motion is so slow that time untethers from my body. Contract. Release. A sound like felt dragging through grit, and the vibration of it rises through my belly, which is low, which is *broad*, which is a muscular foot spread across the cool tile. I leave no dent. Only a glisten. Behind me, the glisten stiffens in the cellar air, becoming ridge, becoming wall, becoming a knee-high ledger of calcium and intent.

I am one of the Tenders.

The knowledge accretes in me like my own trail. I have no eyes, but the humidity paints the space in gradients: cool wells rising from below, damp sinks falling from the invisible vault where the ceiling weeps. I navigate by percussion. To my left, a runnel overflows in a steady, hollow *tom-tom* into a bowl broad as a bath. To my right, the great jars stand with their mouths open, and inside some of them I feel the faint, submarine glow of the bowl-born—those crescent, helicate children riding droplets down, shattering in blue sparks against the ceramic. I was one of them once. I remember the jar’s interior, the still water crowded with mineral film, the slow calcification of my first nacreous plates.

My shell rides my back like a forgotten tool. It is low, broad, cross-hatched with growth ridges that map every watercourse my ancestors have crossed. When I graze—extending the stony palimpsest of my radula to scrape the chemosynthetic slick from the channel walls—the shell strikes a pillar. A hollow *tonk* reverberates through the humid dark. The sound joins the chorus. The sound becomes my name.

But I am not merely grazing. There is a note that does not belong to the randomized drumming of the basin. It is solitary. Stark. A clear, high *ping* where no bowl yet sits, no elder yet gongs. It calls me not through my shell, but through the chemistry of my gut, which knows that a dry hollow is an insult to the architecture, that the water must be caught, that the percussion must never cease.

I go to it.

I pass through copses of pillars—the sessile elders, their bodies sunken into tree-ringed columns of their own excretions, filtering the dark through limestone hearts. They do not speak. Their pulse is tectonic. I pass walls of mineralized reed that lean, waiting for my mucilage to shore them. I pass the thumbprints of the tall ones, pressed into tiles my foot slides over. They are gone, those giants who turned drums on pivots and capped channels with fired clay. Their fingerprints are readable as maps, but I cannot read them with eyes. I read them with vibration: the slight differential in density where their thumbs compressed the earth, a faint hum against my foot. Sometimes, in the calcite layers of my shell, I find the chemical ghost of their tools. Perhaps the first adze was cut from the shell of my kind. Perhaps we were here before them, and they borrowed our shape. Perhaps we are all that remains of their biology, slowed and softened and re-seeded into the dark. The mimicry does not matter. Only the continuation matters.

I find the hollow. Water falls. It strikes the unfired clay, a single voice unanswered.

I begin to circle.

My mucilage steams as it meets the air. I secrete. I sleep. I wake to the *ping* striking the crown of my shell, which I shape, by imperative older than thought, into a cupule to catch and redirect the voice. Decades pass in a single dream of pressure and mineral. The ring rises to my ankle, then my knee, then my shoulder. I am sinking into my own body’s architecture. My hemolymph, that slow river of wet chalk, reroutes through channels of stone. My foot loses its urge to travel; it spreads, roots, becomes foundation. I am entering the sessile phase.

The water finds me. It strikes my calcified back, and the sound that issues is no longer a solitary *ping* but a deep, resonant *gong*. I have become the instrument. I have become the map. The vibration travels down my stony trunk and into the basin’s floor, joining the elder chorus, teaching the dark its shape.

I am not finished. Somewhere in my mineral heart, where a pulse still ticks slower than continents, I feel the next drop forming in the unseen ceiling. I feel a young one, centuries from now, pausing in its grazing to listen to my voice. I feel the channels deepening, the tiles pressing upward with new thumbprints—perhaps not human, perhaps only the memory of pressure—reminding the basin that nothing here is built and then abandoned. Only inherited.

I am the builder and the building. The gut and the mortar. The percussion and the silence it defines. I wait, and the water rings, and the dark continues its long, mucous, patient becoming.
