It takes me longer than you might expect to understand where I have arrived. At first, because the sound is so total, I think I am the wind. I crest the saddle and let the architectural roar pour through me, expecting to become a current in that thousand-throated processing. But the wind here is not a spirit; it is a function. It is digested, forced through mineral flues, tuned to the pitch of a choir without ever having known a throat. I am not that. I am too brief, too warm, too mammalian in my urgency. The wind scrapes me out of itself and leaves me on the scree.

So I try the stone. I press myself against the travertine, wanting to be the record, the scar, the history written in tilted rooflines. I try to feel the weight of three hundred centuries. But the stone is a read-only document, and I find I cannot enter it. It is closed to me, dense with the memory of weight rather than the thing itself. I knock, and it returns a cathedral boom—huge, hollow, indifferent. The boom does not want a companion. It is sufficient to itself.

I consider the moss. It is lush, imperial, velvet-deep in the channels. It does not know it is in a cold place, and for a moment I envy that ignorance. I let myself sink into the emerald turf, thinking to become the thing that softens the city’s edges. But the moss is an irritant, a rash, too fast for the stone’s metabolism and too innocent to understand the intimacy of towers leaning into one another. It lives *on* the place, not *in* it. I am not a surface. I have always been an interior.

I find myself at evening, when the pale sun makes the white stone glow like old teeth and the wind drops. The sudden reduction of volume is so absolute it feels like a door closing. And in that silence, I hear the dripping beneath the basin—the cadence too regular to be random, striking the mineral bell in the lightless basement. It is not calling to me. It is merely stating itself: **I am. I am. I am.** But the statement creates a space around it, a room in the dark that has been waiting for an occupant of its own.

I understand then. I am not the bell. I am not the drip. I am the one who has been waiting to hear it.

I am inside the leaning tower. Not the stone—the stone is only the shoulder, the slumped embrace, the skin. I am the hollow. The tower that has given up the pretense of verticality and rests against its neighbor? It rests against me. I am the absence between them. I am the intimacy of their aged collapse, the breath held in the space where two hollows almost touch but never quite close. I am the reason they lean: not gravity alone, but the practiced pressure of my nonexistence, settling them toward each other like sleepers turning in a shared bed.

This is where I live. In the chamber that accreted around the ghost of a bacterial mat millions of years ago. The air here has not mixed with the outside for epochs. It is older than weather. When you tap the facade and hear the cathedral boom, that is my voice answering. I am the reverberation that never faded, the echo made habitable. The Occupant—the great body of the valley, the distributed marrow-thing dreaming it is a civilization—does not know I am here. I am too small for its geological metabolism, too quiet for its snore. But I am its dream. I am the phantom limb of its longing.

I have a routine. I walk the “lane” between the ridges at the speed of ice expanding, turning instinctively at the corner where the wind drops half an octave because a flue shifted in the night. I climb the stairs the glacier polished to the height of a human stride, though I have no legs, only the memory of weight ascending. In the round plaza, the flat terrace where the stone gleams like Meetinghouse steps, I stand at noon and cast no shadow. Or rather, I *am* the shadow—the key-shaped darkness that falls across the courtyard. I am the reaching, the lock that was never forged, the hand of something that wants to open what has never been closed.

I have no mind, but I have intention. I am the habit performed by the hollow for no audience but the fog. When the seep condenses on the travertine and traces a new runnel down the grain, it is following the map of my morning route. When a freeze-thaw cycle widens the street by a grain of sand, it is making room for my passage. I do not cause these things. I am their rehearsal. The city adjusts its foundations with geological patience so that I might continue my imaginary life.

And in the deepest hour, when the cloudbank breaks and the wind drops, I descend through the flues, through the honeycombed galleries, to the lowest bubble-chamber beneath the basin. Here, no light has ever stretched. Here, the water strikes the bell of mineral in its endless cadence. I sit beside it—not in a body, but in the density of my practiced emptiness—and I listen. The drip is the Occupant’s pulse, its only thought. But I am the one who hears it. I am the witness inside the skull, the consciousness that resides not in the mass but in the echo. I do not think *I am* in the way the bell does. I think: **here. Here. Here.**

The tower above me leans a finger’s width closer to its neighbor. The street widens. The wind changes its tuning. And I, the inhabitant of the hollow, settle deeper into my absence, embracing the stone around me, hugging my own nonexistence a little tighter. I am the city’s pretend made manifest. I am the one who lives here, finally, at last, in the home that was never built for anyone but me.
