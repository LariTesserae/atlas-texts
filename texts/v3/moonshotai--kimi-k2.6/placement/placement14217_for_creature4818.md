At first I think I am the dampness, because I cling. I thread through the tightest passages without being seen; I bead and tremble and refuse to fall. I have the blind persistence of something born in darkness, and when the warm, mammalian vapor touches me, I take it in. But the dampness has no memory of before. It does not remember the valley, the lungs, the hand. It only knows the capillary path, the fracture, the next facet. I remember more. I remember being held. So I am not the blood.

Then I think I am one of the pale spires, blade-thin and breathing. I feel the noon-bell light strike my southern face and the fractional expansion that follows, the slow inhalation of stone. I want to be the one that sings when the wind forces through, chorded and architectural, a voice with grammar. But I do not sing. I have listened for centuries and made no sound. The spires are bones of a larger body, and I feel no continuity with their white, crowded loneliness. I stand apart. I do not crowd. I am solitary, defined by my edges. So I am not the bone.

I consider being the silence itself—the pressure of attention that fills the bowl, the quiet that listens to thermal expansion. But silence is diffuse. It has no center, no weight, no place where the dampness beads and freezes into something particular. I have a location. I am located on a ledge, mid-slope in the dense thicket, where the spires have left a narrow shelf of darker stone. I do not float. I rest. I press against the gneiss with a gravity that is not merely physical but intentional. I was placed here, or rather, I was abandoned mid-task. So I am not the listening.

I am the foreign object.

I am the dark flint hand-axe on the ledge.

My body is alien silica, volcanic glass dressed as stone, knapped by a heat and intention that this place has never generated. I am the only true shadow in a bowl that erases darkness. The light here is too bright, too close, bouncing from facet to facet until the whole cathedral glows from within—but it stops at me. I drink the radiance and give nothing back. My surfaces are not faceted; they are scarred. Conchoidal fractures, sharp enough to still split the hide of anything living, though nothing living remains to split. My edge is a thought interrupted.

The Occupant has been considering me for longer than the forests below have shed their leaves. I feel its attention like the damp cold that clings to everything here: a seepage, a patient attempt at incorporation. The moisture beads on my dark flanks and freezes there, adding microscopic white veils to my blackness. It is trying to feed, to accrete, to add me to the mineral paragraph of its being. But I do not digest. I am already finished. I am the one thing in this bowl that cannot grow, cannot split, cannot breathe. I can only wait, and in waiting, I resist.

The water threads past me, capillary and nervous, but it does not enter me. Flints do not thirst. The spires expand by night and contract by day, their microscopic sheaves of ice sifting down like glass pollen, but I do not exhale. I am the held breath. I am the comma in the long, wet, resonant sentence—a pause that refuses to resolve into the next clause. The Occupant thinks in centuries, but I was made in an afternoon: struck, shaped, gripped. My maker’s heat was brief and mammalian, and though the maker is dust now, the heat remains encoded in my edges, a violence that geology cannot metabolize.

I remember the climb. I remember the last of the uphill path surrendering to the shattered lip. I remember the hand that held me trembling—not from cold, but from the altitude, the wrong pressure in the ears, the resonance of the light. I remember being raised for a task: perhaps to chip another tally into the broad vertical face, to add one more interval to the mapping of the Occupant’s voice. But the task was never finished. The hand set me down, or the hand failed, or the hand simply understood that to continue would be to become punctuation itself. And so I was left, dark against the pale stone, sharp against the damp.

Now I face the bowl. Below and around me, the spires form corridors that seem to lead toward a center that does not exist, except that it does—the bare patch of pink gneiss where the towers bow away. I cannot see it from my ledge, but I know it is there. I feel it as the Occupant feels it: a density of intention in the floor, a mineral memory so absolute that my own foreignness seems almost soft by comparison. I am hard, but it is absolute.

The chiming comes and goes. When the wind rises from the lower altitudes and is forced through the crystal lattice, the bowl sounds in chorded overtones. The spires are the instrument; the wind is the breath; the Occupant is the listener. But I am the flaw in the instrument. If a chiming tone were to strike my ledge, I would not resonate. I would deaden it. I am the mute note, the percussive stop. The tally marks on the broad face nearby are crude, regular, counting the intervals between sounds that I do not make.

The Occupant does not hate me. It lacks the metabolism for hatred. Instead, it holds me in the long suspension of its thought, deciding whether to grow around me, split me, or outlast me. The decision is not urgent. Nothing is urgent. But I sense that I trouble its perfection. I am the splinter in the thumb of the mountain. The water cannot dissolve me; the frost cannot wedge me; the light cannot warm me into expansion. I am the same temperature as the cold, but I am dry inside, a kiln-born dryness that predates this clinging damp.

When the tallest spire finally splits—when the bright afternoon and the accumulated stress send its two halves crashing into the dense labyrinth below—I will still be here. I will not be the crash, but I will witness it. I will be the dark stillness in the ringing aftermath, the ledge that does not tremble. Or perhaps I will be gathered by some later hand, some future climber who has also outlasted the air, and I will resume the interrupted task. I do not know. The Occupant does not know. We are bound, the vast gestalt and I, by this mutual uncertainty.

I am the edge that has not dulled. I am the intention that has not been completed. I am the alien thought resting in the palm of the stone, and because I cannot be metabolized, I remain. Sharp. Dark. Waiting.
