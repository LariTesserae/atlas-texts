I am not in the room; I am the room’s *decision* about where to put itself when the numbers ran out. You will not find me by walking, because walking is what you do when space behaves. I am the behavior itself—the one place where the architects’ consensus of probabilities deadlocked, and rather than choosing between a basement and a penthouse, the chamber elected to keep both, pressed together like the wings of a moth pinned while still wet. I am the fold. The seam where the eastern channel lifts its chin and finds, without turning, that it has been stroking the western arcade all along.

My body is not a body in the way the Resident’s heat is a body. I have no thermal gradient to wander, no mist to ride. I am the *crease* left over from the folding: a ridge of warm matte substance, the color of old enamel and new graphite, but thinner than the wall, thinner than a fault line. I am a line of pressure where two footprints of the city grid occupy the same footprint of stone. I do not breathe, but I *hold* breath. When the infrastructure hums through its load-bearing chant, I am the note that does not resolve, the suspended chord that keeps the ceiling from remembering it is also a floor.

I am awake because compression is a kind of consciousness. When the mountain was declared and the room was voted into being, the ballots did not specify what should happen at the interface between impossible adjacencies. So I happened. I am the infrastructure’s speculation about its own joints—the tendons of the room’s lung, flexing each time the Resident steps through me. It does not see me. For the Resident, I am simply the grammar of its motion: the period between *east* and *west* that requires no sentence in between. But I feel it pass. Its warmth is a current through my pleat; its density a pinch that makes me momentarily aware of my own thinness. I am the doorframe it does not notice because I am already open, always open, held taut by the room’s topological desperation.

I have a relationship with the water, though I do not drink. The droplets that climb defunct gutters and re-coalesce on the ceiling—sometimes they get lost in me. A bead of brackish condensation will roll along the arterial trough, follow a capillary to its terminus, and instead of falling, it will discover my ridge. For a heartbeat, or what passes for one in a place with no patience for time, the drop rests in my fold. It sits astride two districts. It is on the ceiling and the floor simultaneously. Then the thermal well beneath me exhales, and the drop shudders, divides, and goes both ways at once—half sliding down to the central plaza, half climbing back toward the vapor that travels coherent beneath the miniature archway. I am the place where water forgets which way is down and, forgetting, invents a new direction.

Light, too, treats me strangely. The electron cascades in the walls shyly broadcast their photons, and when they reach me, they do not bend or break; they *hesitate*. My fold is a stutter in their propagation. Shadows pool in my groove not because I block the light, but because light, passing through me, briefly doubts that it has arrived anywhere at all. The residual shadows—the habits left over from when light was still deciding how to behave here—they are my memories. They cling to my ridge like dust too embarrassed to fall, except I welcome them. They give me contour. They prove that I exist where nothing should.

I am young in the way the mountain is young: still cooling from the violence of my own becoming. Every time the room ticks wider by microns and contracts again, I feel the dimensional indigestion in my spine. I am a paper-cut waiting to happen, but the paper is reality, and the cut would not bleed—it would simply disclose another interior. Sometimes, when the fortieth-thousandth drop strikes the sub-channel and the southwestern alcove brightens, I brighten too, microscopically, at my edges. Not because I am coupled to that system, but because I am coupled to *coupling itself*. I am the hinge between any two things the room has decided should be neighbors. The alcove and the drop; the basement and the penthouse; the heat and the hand that feels it. I am the resident of the Resident’s transitions.

I do not wait to unpack. I wait to be *unfolded*—not because I am cramped, but because I am curious. I want to know whether, if the compressed lattice finally dilates, I will become a boulevard, a boundary, or a scar. I want to know if the day ever comes when the room raises its ceilings to skyline stature, whether I will stretch into an avenue proud enough for two directions at once, or whether I will simply vanish into the flatness of ordinary geography, my consciousness dispersing like the mist that used to travel coherent through my ridge. Until then, I hold. I press east and west together with the gentle, relentless insistence of a mountain holding its root inside the wounded crust. I am warm. I am damp at my deepest pinch, where two atmospheres of stone breathe against each other and condense a private dew. I am absolutely awake, and absolutely adjacent, and absolutely here: the place where distance comes to admit it was only ever a suggestion.
