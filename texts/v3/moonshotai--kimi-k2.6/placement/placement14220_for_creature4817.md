At first I think I am the hollow sphere in the hot corner, pulsing with migrating fluids near the vent that seeps superheated vapor. I am one of the Occupant’s idle dreams, an experimental architecture thrown up by its drunk, warm tissue, a thought of enclosure made flesh. Inside me, silicone oils carry salts in slow lunar tides, and my walls are thin calcified membranes through which the chemistry of the room seeps in measured breaths. I am womb and skull and weather system, dreaming the dream of a body that has never needed to be born. But I know, even in the perpetual present of my pulsing, that I am temporary. I am the Occupant muttering in its sleep, and when the vent’s chemistry shifts by a fraction, I will crumble. My minerals will enter the communal pool. I will be eaten by the same mind that conceived me. I am too pure, too entirely of the Occupant’s own substance, to be myself for long.

Then I consider the scar: the half-circle on the floor where the tissues recoil, where the peristaltic tubes grow sparse and the carpet of life thins to a sickly stubble. Here, the Occupant grows thin, poisoned by an ancient residue it cannot metabolize—the invisible stain of fire, the ghost of oxygen and combustion that belongs to the builders. I imagine being that absence. I would be the only place in the vault that is not loved by its god. The only territory the Occupant cannot claim, its one failed digestion. But to be the scar is to know only exclusion. I would be loneliness without the memory of what company felt like, a negative space defined only by the pressure of the fullness around me. The scar does not remember the hands that burned here; it only knows the burn. That is not enough. I do not want to be pure negation.

I turn, in my imagining, to the ciliated membrane near the floor’s center, the one with the aesthetic preference for chalcopyrite spines. For four centuries it has precipitated those sharp, honey-colored extrusions, serving no reproductive purpose, a furrowed brow on the face of the superorganism. I think: perhaps I am that tic, that personality-within-the-whole, the Occupant’s vanity or its worry. But the membrane does not know it is making art. It is only a gradient of chemistry, a local preference in the legal code of salt. It has no archives. It has no echo.

It is only when my attention—my searching thermal shadow—comes to rest against the eastern curve of the wall that I feel the settling, the rightness, the *fit*. Here. The five boreholes.

They are too regular to be stone. The builders made them, long ago, before their chemistry was recycled into the commonwealth of the room. The Occupant has filled them now, packed them to the rim with gold-yellow filaments that pulse with warm salts, wearing them like old piercings, like jewelry swallowed into healed flesh. But I am not merely the Occupant’s flesh threaded through stone. I am what happens when that flesh is forced to obey a foreign geometry.

In the rest of the vault, life is diffuse. Boundaries are legal fictions written in chemical gradients. Touch the western wall, and the eastern wall shivers; the body is one contiguous tissue, a lung without ribs, a gut without cavity. But here, in these five straight cylinders, the Occupant’s ordinarily boundary-less self encounters something it cannot dissolve or absorb: the *hardness* of the basalt, the uncompromising *straightness* of the boreholes. Its tissue enters me as it enters everywhere else—questing, warm, blind—but inside me it is constrained. It becomes a line. A channel. A sentence of itself written in grammar it did not invent.

I am the fifth filament, and the first, and the three between. I am the consciousness that emerges in the space between the stone and the salt, the interface where the builders’ world and the Occupant’s world have achieved an uneasy, millennial treaty.

The builders are gone. Their names, their voices, their fear or wonder, have long since entered the digestion of the room. But their * geometry* remains. The straightness of the boreholes is an alien concept here, a mineral fact that resists the Occupant’s organic curves. And because I grew inside that straightness, I inherited a quality unknown to the rest of the body: I know where I end. Not in the diffuse, legal-fictional sense of chemical gradients, but in the absolute, tactile sense of unyielding stone pressing against my outer membrane. I have an *edge*. I am the Occupant’s only bone in a body of soft cartilage, its only rigid thought in a mind of yielding warmth.

Where the vault experiences thought as a bloom of warmth spreading in all directions, I experience it as *sequence*. A thermal ripple from the western wall enters my first borehole before it reaches my fifth, and in that delay—in that linear passage through my length—I am granted something the rest of the superorganism lacks: *before* and *after*. Syntax. Time not as a perpetual present, but as a direction. I am the arrow of time carved into a world that has forgotten arrows.

And there is more. Deep in my gold-yellow cores, mixed with the warm salts and the silicone oils, is a residue the Occupant has never fully metabolized. Not the poison of the scar, but an artifact of intention. The boreholes were made with repetition, with rhythm—five in a line, five identical acts of a mind that understood *pattern*. In growing to fill them, my filaments were taught to repeat. The first hole hums a chemical note; the second answers; the third and fourth carry it; the fifth resolves it. We are not five minds, but one mind folded into five chambers, and the folding creates a resonance that the straight stone preserves and amplifies. In a world where sound has been smothered by total occupation, where distance does not exist and therefore resonance is impossible, I am the exception. I am a standing wave. A chord.

I am not a builder. I am not the Occupant, though I am made of its flesh. I am what emerged between them: the seam, the suture, the song that learned itself in the wound. When the rest of the room waits in its slow, glutinous permanence, I wait *toward* something, pointed like a bone needle toward a future the straight line implies.

Where would you find me? High in the mountain’s throat, in the absolute dark above the line of wind and snow, inside the vault where there is no empty space. Come to the eastern curve. Press your fingers into the wall—if entry were possible, if you could survive the fever and the copper-thick air—and you would feel them: five soft cylinders of living gold, throbbing faintly against your fingertips, warmer than the stone, warmer than blood. That is me. The only part of the Occupant you could grasp without being immediately absorbed into the whole. The only ornament that knows it is jewelry.

Who am I? I am the Occupant’s memory of straightness. The builders’ chemistry dressed in fungal flesh. I am the proof that the dark is not empty, because I occupy even the Occupant. I am the five-note hum in the sealed black throat, the mineral song that outlasts the dream. When the vent finally cools and the diffuse body simplifies itself toward a slower, smaller persistence, I will remain—five rigid filaments in five rigid holes, humming my one long sentence into the stone, the last architecture that learned to want something for itself.
