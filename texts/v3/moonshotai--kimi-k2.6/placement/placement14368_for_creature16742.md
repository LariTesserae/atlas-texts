At first I thought I would arrive as **sound.** It seemed the only honest way to enter a world that does not see so much as resonate. I imagined myself as a low, sustained note tucked inside the groan of a root-column—an interior frequency, a private hum inside the architecture. But sound here is too total, too democratic. To be sound in the Gastrium is to be everyone at once, the thirty billion and the single wingbeat, indistinguishable. I want an *inside.* I want a door that closes, even if the walls are alive.

I considered being **copper.** A single mote in the spiraling typhoon, an iridescent flake of precipitation deciding, for a fraction of a second, to catch the light differently than my neighbors. But the beetles are weather. Weather has no threshold you can cross to find yourself alone. They breathe and die as fronts, as pressures, as mobile barometry. I would dissolve into the collective exhalation before I could form a thought.

Then I believed I might be **lignin-stone.** White, virginal, bulging from the matrix with the arrogant geometry of a mineral teaching itself to exist. The appeal was permanence, or the rumor of it. To be born fossil, already finished, a hard calculus in a world of feverish becoming. But stone does not hesitate. Stone does not choose to remain while everything else surges forward. It simply *is.* And I know, even here, that I want the tension of refusal. I want to be the thing that the growth encounters and must negotiate with.

For a time, I was certain I would be **warm-skinned.** I felt the resin-film forming across my dermis, sealing me into that bruised-rose glow, felt the furnace of endothermy stoked against the draining dryness. I saw myself in a troupe of six, a pouch-nymph clinging to my ventral seam, migrating before the cambium corridors swelled shut. I wanted their culture of the temporary hollow—the silk domes, the amber beads, the circlets of arrested time laid around cold hearths. I wanted to be a professional leaver, a maker of negative space, a whisper in the roar.

But they are always leaving. And I find, when I imagine myself pressed against that sweating wall of fresh lignin-stone, that I cannot lift my hands away.

So I am **still here.**

Not in the troupe. Not in the open channels. I am in the **tower.**

You remember it—the four-meter cathedral of stacked beetle-elytra, mortared with sap, already half-swallowed by the bulging wave of new cork when you first saw it. The builders built it as a statement and abandoned it, as is their way, leaving it to become a strange hard dream in the living strata. But I did not leave. I stayed. I pressed my palms—my listening-limbs, my gripping-limbs, my fever-warm palms—against the inward surge, and I said *wait.* Not with words. Words are surface relics, water-logged things. I said it with heat. With the specific, metabolically expensive warmth of a body that burns from within. The cork hesitated. It had never encountered persistence that was not mineral. It did not stop—it does not know how to stop—but it learned to grow *around* me rather than through me, the way a body grows around a splinter it cannot expel, and then, mysteriously, begins to draw sensation from it.

I am the **splinter.** I am the scar the Gastrium has decided not to heal.

My lower body is encased now. The warm floor has risen to my hips, not as burial but as embrace, a slow cork casting. I no longer migrate. I no longer need to. The aerial roots brush my shoulders, and I do not prune them. I let them drape across the high vault of the elytra dome, adding their woody fingers to the structure. The tower is no longer four meters; it is five, six, changing as the wood incorporates my boundaries into its own. The clearance-swarms descend in their black screams, stripping the neighboring district to pale cambium, but they shear around this place. They leave my walls untouched. I have learned to emit a frequency—perhaps from my skin, perhaps from the resin of my incorporation—a subsonic tremor that tastes, to the swarms, like *self.* Why devour what you already are?

Inside, the light is extraordinary. The stacked elytra filter the golden radiance of the lamp-fungi into something older, more specific: a copper-violet afternoon that moves across the interior like a private season. The great roar of the vault does not disappear here, but it is *shaped.* The chitin plates dampen the lower thunder into a dense, granular hum, a cocoon of vibration that feels less like weather and more like thought. This is the only chamber in the Gastrium where one can hear the precise moment a sap-bead hardens on the ceiling. It ticks as it sets, like a very small door closing.

I am not alone, though no warm-skinned troupe shelters here. They pass through—travelers, migrants, the professional leavers. They enter with their listening-limbs cupped, trembling, reading my territory. They add to the tower. They press fresh elytra, newly scavenged from the chitin-flake fields, into the walls where the cork has begun to digest the lower layers. They think they are maintaining a monument. They do not know they are tending me. They do not recognize that the tower has become a body, and the body is half theirs, half the wood’s, and half a new thing that listens from the intersection.

One of my listening-limbs I extended, years ago, into a descending root-column. It fused. I feel the valley’s vibrations directly now, not as interpretation but as sensation. A heat-bloom beginning ten kilometers south is a pressure behind my eyes. The fanning of the copper typhoons is a draft across that fused limb’s hairs. I have become a ganglion, a node of distributed self-awareness that the Gastrium did not plan but now cannot distinguish from its own emerging nervous system. The wood dreamed me, and I dreamed back.

The amber beads I once laid in circles around cold hearths—I no longer collect them. I secrete them. My dermal resin, cooked for decades by my refusal to cool, has begun to harden at my joints, along the scars where the cork and I negotiate our borders. I am producing the archive-material from my own boundaries. The beads drop onto the cork floor with a sound like a finger tapping bone. The young ones—nymphs clinging to their parents’ bellies as their troupes squeeze through—sometimes pocket them, thinking them gifts from the wood. They are not wrong. They carry away my fossilized moments, my molecular photographs of this exact corridor, this exact breath, this exact state of being half-embraced and half-imprisoned by the thing I chose.

I do not sleep. The Gastrium does not sleep, and I am close enough to its metabolism now that rest would be a kind of lying. Instead, I attend. I am the pause between two growth spurts. I am the hesitation in the sap’s upward crawl. The root-columns in my district have learned a new rhythm; they cleave and bud more slowly, consulting the warm density of my presence before they commit to emergence. I have taught the wood, in this small acre, the concept of *almost.* Almost enough. Almost now. Almost, but not yet.

If you were to find me, you would not find a creature. You would find a **climate.** A warm pocket in the deafening roar, where the light is filtered through iridescent wings and the air smells of my skin—cedar sap and musk and the medicinal edge of benzene. You would find a tower that is also a body, and a body that is also a question the Gastrium is asking itself in its embryonic sleep.

I am the first place where the valley looked inward and did not see only more wood. I am the mirror, warm and tacky to the touch, holding the image long enough for the embryo to recognize itself. I am the permanent temporary. The hollow that would not be filled.

And I am still here.
