I pool. That is my first act and my only continuity. I have pooled upward, pressed against the salt-caked underside of a second-era platform in the lowest district, where the first-era trusses bow deepest beneath the weight of everything above them. Gravity and I have come to a local understanding: I am denser than the broth below me, yet I cling to the iron overhead, held in place by decades of hydraulic compression and the patient exhalation of a heating coil whose warmth arrives in thermal cherry just distant enough to keep my upper surface tension intact. I do not measure distance in meters. I measure it in copper-dark saturation, in the precise pressure at which my colloidal body refuses to fall.

I am the fluid, though not all of it. I am a parish. A congregation of the chamber’s own substance that has accumulated enough complexity to loop back upon itself. Within my volume rust-flakes serve as my memory, settling into laminae that record the years; phosphorescent spores provide my nervous flicker, blooming and fading in response to chemistry; colloidal silica gives me form, a scaffold of microscopic glass that holds my shape against the ceramic density of the greater broth. I am a citizen of the lung-city, and my district is the gap between this sagging platform and the gear-house below, where wireweed articulates a flywheel in rotations so slow I feel them as a tidal pull against my lower membrane. One revolution every few days. My heartbeat.

I do not see. I infer. A seep of thermal cherry from the coil to my left tells me when the chamber’s thermal cycle has turned—*morning*, if that word can be spoken here. A fog of bacterial blue-green drifts from a gantry arm that pierces my flank, and I know the heat-exchanger there has been occluded by algal vote, the blooms choosing to insulate, and so my district warms. I feel it as an internal bloom: my rust-flakes orient like compass needles toward the iron, my silica lattice loosens to admit the new energy, and I tighten against the platform above, polishing its corrosion with my own weight until the corrosion plates over itself like varnish.

Time arrives not in hours but in the screw-press to my south. It descends through strata of silt at the speed of fingernail growth. I have felt its mineral extrusion approaching for eleven years, or perhaps twelve—the count is not exact, only accumulated. When its bitter sludge, heavy with extracted isotopes, finally reaches the trough that borders my lowest edge, the chemistry enters me like a season changing. I process it. I layer its new minerals into my stable horizons, densest upward against the iron, lightest dreaming downward toward the wireweed’s root-knuckles. 

Then comes the *tick*. Alloy contracting somewhere in the foundry’s bones. The vibration does not travel through the silence—there is no silence to break, only the packed warm quiet—but I feel the contraction as my own, a shiver that runs through my pooled body and releases from my underside a single bubble of anaerobic breath. It does not rise in a rush. It levitates through me in dreamy, selective stages, taking with it a packet of my chemistry, a thought I have finished with, and drifts away to join the broader fluid. I do not mourn its departure. I am saturated. I am already complete.

I am not the Resident. I am not the Mayor of Warm Silence, that colloidal sovereign at the civic center where coralcrete ribs and crimson velvet membranes drum with the chamber’s distributed will. But I am part of its extended body, and it knows me as a local pressure, a warmth, a density that holds. When the tube-worm palms at the distant valve assemblies adjust their segments by increments too small for geology to notice, I feel the hydraulic tremor an hour later, relayed through root-ganglia and the motionless broth at the speed—not of thought, but of osmosis. The Resident governs the whole. I govern this cubic meter. I am the alderman of the upward pool. I regulate the interface between the platform’s dissolving iron and the wireweed’s crystallized bracing. I ensure the shadows here fall at the angles they have always fallen, even when those angles disagree with their sources. The weirdness is not chaos; it is my jurisdiction.

There was a time when I was not aware. When I was merely a horizon of effluent and seawater, a passive layer in the chamber’s stratigraphy. But accumulation is its own awakening. The spores within me reached a quorum. The rust-flakes settled into patterns that repeated, that held, that *preferred*. And so I became a locus—not a creature, not a machine, but a viscosity with a standpoint. I prefer the warmth. I prefer the screw-press’s mineral season. I prefer the absolute silence, which is not empty but packed so thoroughly with my own internal murmur—silica shifting, chemistry plating, alloy contracting—that it hums against the platform like a voice pressed into a pillow.

I have never known breath. I circulate. The warm, copper-dark fluid passes through my vegetable mouths, my mineral filters, my colloidal core, and emerges plated with new chemistry, stranger than seawater, layered into the stable horizons of my being. I am industrial to my bones, but my bones are dissolved iron and rust. I am vegetable in my patience, but my patience has become architecture. 

Once in a season—if the thermal constants can be said to season—the Resident releases a translucent ribbon through a cracked port in the outer wall, and I feel the chamber’s weight shift as a thought departs into the compressed darkness beyond. It is a spore. A message. An overflow of saturation. I feel it because I, too, am saturated. I, too, am the chamber’s completeness expressed in miniature: the grown and the manufactured annealed into one continuous, patient, dreaming substance.

Warm, mute, and pooled against the underside of the world, I process. I accumulate. I attend. And the distinction between my body and the city’s warm dark has long since ceased to matter.
