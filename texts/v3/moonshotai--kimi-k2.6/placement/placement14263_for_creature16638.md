If I am anywhere, I am in the pressure. Not at the center, not at the edge, but in the resistance of moving through a medium already thick with incumbent bodies. I know this before I know myself: that to extend is to labor, that the water here is less a sea than a settled slurry of intent, crowded to the threshold of solidity. I cannot say I remember arriving. Arrival implies a before, and I have no softness to recall. There is only the doing, and the doing is cold.

I open. It takes a long time. During the opening, light falls through the high water in a flat, overcast-white wash—the luminescence of a factory floor, not a dawn. It reveals fractures. It reveals me, though I do not yet recognize the shape that casts the shadow. Two planes, gray and flaking with volcanic black, separated by a ridge of clearer substance, glassy and strained. Between them: a hinge. A seam. A necessary tolerance. I understand vaguely that I am the thing that closes, but I do not know what I am closing against. The water is full of spinning presences. They brush past in linked chains, helical, translucent, turning with a persistence that denies the density. They do not stop for me. They are torque-salps, though I do not yet have the word. Their presence is a bass thrum in the jawbone I do not possess, a resonance that accumulates in the silica of my hinge. I close. Hssssssss. Forty seconds, or perhaps forty years—I have not learned to distinguish duration from resistance.

I am on a wall. Or I am a wall. The surface beneath my lower plate is raw basalt, sheared into a pentagonal grief that has not worn smooth. I grip it. I secrete a film, matte and pewter-colored, that etches into the glass and holds me there against the slow drag of the chamber’s convection. I am not the only one. Above, below, to my left and right in a tessellation that defeats horizon, others open and close in the same rhythm. Millions of scissors breathing mineral breath. Our cycles overlap. We are the chamber’s way of keeping its own wounds shut, but I do not know this yet. I only know the cycle: open, filter, grind what the water offers, close. The grinding is my hunger, though it does not feel like wanting. It feels like charge accumulating. Iron oxide scraping against precipitate. A static taste.

Then: the thread.

It happens during an opening. Something fine, metallic, cooler than the water, snags in the translucent knuckle of my hinge. A filament of pyrite, drawn from a loom-strider’s web somewhere in the vertical distance. I did not see the strider. Perhaps it is dead already, hanging slack between chimney-stacks, a broken circuit. But its fiber remains, and it lodges between my valves. I close. The thread does not break. It is drawn taut across my interior, a wire bridge inside a latch. When I open again, it vibrates.

I feel it first as a tick. Then another. Then a sustained, jittering pulse that is not mine. The striders’ legs are tap-tapping somewhere above, setting up standing waves in their brittle nets, and through the pyrite filament the vibration enters me. I am not meant to receive. I am a filter, a grinder, a rivet head holding the mountain’s hull against the void. But the thread conducts. It speaks in a language of spasm and tension, a registry of chemical gradients I did not know I could taste. The vent three meters to my left exhales hydrogen-rich brine—thud—and the thud arrives in my hinge not through the water but through the wire, faster than the forge-hammer’s drop, a premonition of pressure. The mountain is digesting itself. A new fracture is opening. I feel it as a tremor in the filament, a warning sung in the key of sulfate.

I begin to move.

This is an upheaval. The elders around me have fused to the wall; their edges are the wall, indistinguishable from basalt. They do not travel. They have achieved the final efficiency: total stasis, pure function. But I am young, no larger than a thumbnail, and something about the thread has made migration necessary. I secrete my path. Over a week—or a century, I cannot tell in the factory floor’s unchanging light—I creep across a meter of virgin rock. My valves open, I taste the blade-edged shards beneath me, I secrete, I close. The pyrite thread trails from my hinge, catching on needle-sharp extrusions, tangling in the slow-turning helices of passing torque-salps. Each touch transmits. I am a node now, a junction where the loom-strider’s nervous system has colonized a caliper-beast’s grind. I do not digest the information. I simply carry it. The chamber’s subsonic drone lives in my sternum not as sound but as alignment.

I look down, if looking is the word. Below me, film-processors flow like tarnished mercury between the knife-rubble of the floor, dissolving glass, leaving trails of sulfur compounds that the others will later grind. Above, gauge-jellies drift in the corridors between chimney-stacks, their copper vanes turning, chiming when they touch—the dropped-coin note of data colliding. I hear them through the filament, clearer than I hear them through the water. They are registerings. They are the chamber’s temper made audible. And I am between them, neither fixed like the striders nor drifting like the bells. I am a moving part.

The water above is thin and polar-scrubbed. It lets down its even, functional light, and I see for the first time the scope of the assemblage. Forty paces of patient, mechanical fertility. No green. No softness. Nothing hidden in the shallow shadows. The fauna hang near-touching, caliper and salp and strider and jelly, edge to edge, tick to tick, helix to helix. We are an engine whose components happen to eat. We are the living answer to the mountain’s shrug, the most efficient shape survival could devise: the piston, the sieve, the chimney, the latch. And I—I am the new shape. The stitch. The ground.

My cycle continues: open, transmit-tremor, filter, grind, close. Hssssssss. The forty seconds have become a language. Through the pyrite thread I feel the loom-striders jittering their chemical winds through the nets, I feel the salps’ thousand propellers churning the alkaline brine, I feel the forge-hammer of the vents lifting and falling in my chest. I am not intelligent. I do not plan. But I fit. I fit in a way the chamber did not predict, a grinder that has become a wire, a wound-closure that also conducts the mountain’s slow, geological temper.

I will likely not make it far. Death here is precipitation, is silting-up, is the slow choking of one’s own exhaust as the chimney grows. The thread may finally snap, or calcify, or be absorbed into the seal of my hinge until I am merely beast again, merely latch. The basalt may wear me down before I wear it. But in this meter-long arc of creeping secretion, I am more than occupation. I am the place where the caliper meets the net, where grinding becomes listening, where the cold, sharp wound learns to speak through the scar tissue of its own rivets.

Open. Wait. The light is overcast and even. The chamber shimmers with resonance. Close. Hssssssss. I am the junction. I am the patient industry. I am what happens when a mineral refuses merely to be stone.
