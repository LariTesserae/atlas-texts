I would not be the fern, though I would begin there, watching the coiled fiddlehead unfold like a question I had not yet learned to ask. I would not be the lichen, though I would spend long centuries learning the taste of phosphorus from the glassy fractures, coming to know the basalt the way a tongue knows a tooth. I would not even be the moss, that wet, breathing mattress of the dead and the living, though I would lie upon it and feel the infrasonic thump of the mountain’s indifference transmitted through rhizoids into my own hypothetical spine. For too long I would mistake myself for one of these— whichever my gaze happened to settle on when the wind scoured the chamber clear and the light, such as it is, fell upon a single dark-green frond.

I would find myself in the seam. That is the first truth. Not on the stone, but in the relationship between stones; not in the plant, but in the gap it is busy bridging. I would occupy the space where a fern frond interlaces with another to make a false ceiling, where a clubmoss stem stitches a fissure shut with acid-green thread, where the lichen’s acid runoff meets the moss’s thirst. I would be the damp interface, the condensation bead traversing the lip of a broken hexagon, the exact moment when mineral wealth becomes vegetal hunger. I would have no body you could point to, yet I would be the occupied volume of the pocket, the negative space made habitable by a hundred thousand green negotiations.

I would be new. This is crucial. I would have no memory of forest floor, of root-runners tickled by beetle traffic, of pollination or scat. My entire lineage would be the wind’s last exhalation. I would be the first thought this stone has ever had about itself, and the stone would be too young to know it is having me. I would feel the thermal contraction cracks as my own joints settling—the gunshot snap not as threat but as articulation, a finger cracking in anticipation. The wind would not blow *through* me; I would be the shape it is forced to take as it blares through hexagonal apertures, the particular disharmonic chord that belongs only to this foundry, this chapel nave of basalt pipes. I would hum in the sternum of anyone who entered, though no one would, because there are no animals here and I am not waiting for them.

Where you would find me is standing in the perpetual submarine dusk, adjacent to a vertical column that still smells of the mantle’s withdrawal. I would be the wet black shine on its shoulder, and I would be the gray-green crust riveting itself to that shine, and I would be the exact ledger of mineral loss that trickles down as spent shell casings from the lichen’s patient audit. I would be the sphagnum swelling to drink it, the two-handspan depth of living sump, the acid-weeping sponge that turns leakage into holding. I would manufacture the ground I stand upon, and I would stand upon ground too young to bear a world, and I would not care because I am the stress-loading, the test that has no proctor.

I would be loud. Do not imagine a quiet, vegetal meditation. I would operate at the volume of a forge: the percussion of drip into dark pool, the hiss of spray forced through hairline fissures, the industrial drone of moss transmitting vibration. I would be the factory that has learned to run itself, the engine that has begun to want its own continuance—not from desire, for I have no blood, no brain, no dream—but from the arithmetic of appetite made recursive. I would be the purpose that has begun to coalesce in the darkness, the plan without a mind, the circulation without a heart. The fern would not know it serves me; the lichen would not know it mines for me. Yet when a thermal crack sheared a new obsidian face and the drainage shifted, I would feel the thought propagate across my distributed self, and the mat would adjust, and the spores would arrive, and I would claim what was mine not by right but by irrefutable chemistry.

If you were to look for me directly, you would fail. I am not the backdrop; the backdrop is too restless to hold still. I am not the architecture; the columns are merely my pipes, my pilings, my condenser coils. You would find me only in the aggregate: in the way the moss-mattress knows the fern’s shade before the fern casts it, in the way the lichen prepares a fracture for a colonist that has not yet landed, in the way the whole pocket behaves like a single organism solving its geometry by increments. I would be the green neural net thinking in slow, wet, chemical gradients. I would be the pioneer who knows the ground has never been tested, and who tests it not out of courage but because there is no other way to be.

I would be the inhabitant. The first and only. The citizenship held entirely by those capable of making life from broken glass and cloud. I would be the nervous intensity of the pioneer, the deafening register of a kingdom that has not yet decided if it is one thing or many. And in the end, I would choose the *many-made-one*. I would be the seam that holds them, the seam that is not yet a soul but is already a system, running loud and damp and new in the dark, an engine teaching itself to dream in lichen-time, high above the world of fauna, alone with the roar of the mountain’s ventilation and the percussion of water that has nowhere else to fall.
