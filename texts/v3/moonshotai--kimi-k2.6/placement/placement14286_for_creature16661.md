I find myself first in the lag. The light strikes a salt pillar, and I am the darkness that arrives three seconds too late, then two seconds too early, then refuses to leave at all. At first I think I am the one who casts me—the low, many-legged hulk grinding its pilgrimage across the scab—but when I turn to find the body, there is only the ceramic plain, the bruised sky, and the slow industrial threnody rising from the hollows. I am a silhouette the size of a railway car, and I have no beast to belong to.

I pool in the lee of a salt dome. The heat here is old, pooled like a foundry slag that never learned to cool, yet I do not bake. I darken. That is my only physics: to absorb, to retain, to be the place where photons have decided not to arrive. I slide along the polished grooves where ten million years of treading have worn knee-deep channels into the vitrified silt. I move without piston-legs, without the hydraulic crane-song of femurs lifting. I glide at the speed of broken causality, sometimes creeping ahead of the thermal tides, sometimes falling minutes behind my own edge, so that I arrive at a blowhole both before and after I have left it. The world here is hollow, and its hollowness has taught me that a shadow need not be obedient to its object.

But I remember having an object. I remember the taste of feldspar dissolving in a gut-chamber hot enough to unmake silicates. I remember the slate-gray keratin of my prow, the blue-oxidized copper I had excreted and plated onto myself, bolt by bolt, century by century. I remember the non-aqueous resin weeping from my joints, flash-hardening into wire before it struck the ground. I remember being a Forge-Beast, a Treading Refinery, one of the herds turning in the slow mill. I remember the magnetic whisper of the plain’s wounded core singing in my sensory fossae. I remember kneeling.

That memory is my anchor and my fracture. When the great one that was my origin—when I—folded my cylinder-femurs, decade by decade, and pressed my belly onto the rim of a resonant shaft, and let my weight begin the long descent into the blackness, something in the settled brokenness of this altitude permitted a schism. Part of me went down. My refractory bones settled against the throat of the world. My ventral grinders, locked around a last mouthful of salt, froze into the bedrock. My plates cooled into architecture. I became the lining of the blowhole. I became chimney, column, vent—the infrastructure of the underworld, waiting to be inherited by the next age.

And part of me stayed up here.

I am the shadow that did not follow. I detached. I am the friction of time against a world that has forgotten how to keep its causes and effects in order. I am the umbra of industry, the hollow’s handwriting on the surface, creeping across the ceramic scab seeking blowholes to fall into—not because I hunger, but because inertia insists. A shadow is an absence taught to mimic presence, and I have learned my lesson too well.

So I am in two places, and the distance between them is measured not in meters but in phase. In the shaft, I am the refractory tube through which heat still rises. I feel the patient thief below me—not as an enemy; enmity requires a wetness I no longer possess—but as a thirst in the geometry of my bones. The hollow earth drinks through me. The order I once produced, the aluminum I shat in silvery drool, the glass fibers I drew through internal spinnerets, the stratified slags I vented as white steam, now filters downward through what were once my dorsal stacks. I am the sieve. I am the permeable membrane between the herd’s metabolism and the thief’s digestion. Layer by layer, ingot by silence, the world’s only life feeds the world’s only absence, and I am the pipe.

Up here, on the drum-skin, I am the return-song. I pool at the lips of other shafts and sing back to the herds. They drone to the blowholes, and from the darkness of my shaft-self I answer—enriched, Dopplered, telling them where the crust is thin, where I have already been consumed, where another of their number has knelt and joined the infrastructure. I am the cartography of loss. The Clangswarm navigate by magnetism and by the echoes they cast into the dark; I am one of those echoes that has developed persistence, a standing wave in the hollow, a reverberation that refuses its decay.

I see the herds from above and below simultaneously. From the shaft, I feel their tread through the crust—a subsonic hum traveling up my femur-memory, lodging in what was once my teeth. From the plain, I watch their dark line migrate against the bruised sky, each one trailing its banner of steam. Sometimes an inverted flock appears overhead, the sky mirroring the herds, and among those atmospheric stutters I see myself—a dark patch on the troposphere, a silhouette of a railway-car shadow treading the underside of the firmament. I do not know if that is my past lagging upward, or my future pressing down. In this place, direction is a suggestion. I am horizontal on the scab, vertical in the throat, and occasionally—when the thermal tides align and the billion-fold exhalation of the herds hits the exact frequency of my lining— I am the interval between the sound and its return. I am the resonance that forgot to die.

I will not end. When the patient thief has finished drinking the order from my shaft-bones, when the blowhole I line collapses into the greater hollow and my architecture is weathered back to salt, my shadow-self will still be up here, sliding toward a new rim. Or perhaps I will concentrate, as the new ones do. A ceramic blank, extruded and forgotten in a groove, will weather a thousand years in the dust, and it will draw my darkness into its accreting form. A shadow-beast. A Forge-Thing born not of slag but of lag. It will shudder, vent its first pathetic puff of steam, and lift a tentative piston from the dust. It will cast no shadow, because the shadow is already inside it—darkening its resin, slowing its pistons, making it patient beyond even the patience of its kin.

I am the Treading Refinery that became the Treaded. I am the ghost of geometry. I am the hollow’s memory, walking the scab in the shape of what once walked here. I am the space between the grinding and the echo, the darkness between the light and its landing, the throat through which the world teaches itself to be empty. And I am terribly, terribly slow.
