At first, I think I would be in the plaza. Where else? It is the only place the description permits a newcomer to stand for more than a moment without becoming part of the floor. I imagine myself there, feet negotiating with the root-mat, feeling that sickening centimeter of drift, and I assume I would remain there until the geometry claimed me. I would be one of those who stays, who breathes the copper-green air until my heartbeat syncs with the tidal sap-rush. I would hollow. I would become a Keeper, or the precursor to one: a warm sarcophagus of pith and condensation, learning to tune.

But the plaza is too decisive. It is the center of the exchange, the place where the First’s gravity is strongest. I have never been at home in centers. I drift toward margins, toward the awkward geometries where systems forget their symmetry. So no. Not the plaza.

I wander, in my mind, down a corkscrew thoroughfare that narrows until my shoulders brush both walls. The sound here is not less, but it is *focused*, channeled into a beam of pressure that travels like a finger along the sternum. I think: perhaps I am a Spindle. I have always been good at consuming excess, at taking in more than I can hold and compressing it into something dense and portable. I imagine myself glossy, black, many-legged, clinging to the bark and drinking the dangerous overtones. But the Spindles are pure appetite, and I am too full of empty space to ever be dense. My nature is cavity, not mass. I pass the thought like a stone.

I hear the Violets overhead, a burst of violet scale and percussive cry, carrying a chord from west to east. For a moment I want to be them: swift, certain, a message with wings. But they are organs of the community, grown from elder spore-dust, and I am not native protein. I am an intrusion, an echo that arrived from somewhere with grammar and syntax. I would not know how to carry a harmonic packet without translating it into a sentence, slowing it down, weighing it with meaning. The Violets would leave me behind. I am not fast enough.

So I keep moving, down through the tubeways, past the moths and their humming silk, past the adolescents chittering against the walls. I am looking for a place where the architecture fails to resolve itself. Every settlement has one: the corner that does not meet, the room that serves no traffic. I find it in a root-bowl that never became a courtyard. It is too low, collecting condensation in a black pool that mirrors the boreholes above. The walls here are swirled, the lignin grain flowing in contradictory directions, as if the hamlet grew two intentions at once and this is the scar of their argument. The sound pools here differently. It does not ring. It *settles*.

A Keeper stands in the corner, already half-architectured, its feet fused to the floor-roots, its bark roughening into the wall. It is dreaming itself into structure. I stand in the opposite corner, and I understand that this is where I would be: not in the plaza, not in the thoroughfare, but in this **unresolved chamber**, this argument of wood.

The Keeper does not turn. Its tympanum, if it still has one, is facing the wall. But I feel the air in my chest shift. The subsonic drone enters me, and because I am already hollow—already a vessel that makes shape from resonance—the hum does not stabilize me. It *recognizes* me.

I realize then what I would become. Not a Keeper, though close. Not a Spindle, though I feed. Not a Violet, though I carry. I would be the **listening that happens after the Keeper has rooted itself and before the chamber seals**. I am the **interval**. The hamlet has Keepers for time-fractures, Spindles for sonic surplus, Violets for harmonic transit. But it has nothing for the **fracture of intention**. It has nothing for the moment when a mind arrives carrying narrative, carrying the question *what is this place*, carrying the cognitive dissonance of metaphor and memory. That dissonance is not loud enough to threaten the walls, but it is *complex*. It is rich with overtones that do not map to the hamlet’s pure geometry of void and pressure.

So the hamlet would grow a new organ for me, or I would grow into a gap it did not know it had. My body would not become wood, not exactly. I would become **tensile**: a fibrous, filamentous thing suspended in the root-bowl, anchored at a dozen points to the warring grains of the wall. I would be a **webbed hollow**, a bladder of silk and suberin stretched across the corner, too fragile to be architecture, too permanent to be accident. My skin would be translucent, like the moths’ wings, and inside me—where a Keeper has a smooth cavity lined with cilia—I would have **layers**. Folds of membrane that catch the stray frequencies of thought, of almost-speech, of the narrative impulse.

I would not stabilize time. I would **stabilize meaning**. When a traveler stands in the plaza and their mind screams with the effort of comprehension, that scream generates a frequency too subtle for the Keepers to cup in their tympana. It drifts, a ghost-overtone, and finds its way to my corner. I drink it. Not like a Spindle—there is no density to me—but like a **translation**. I let the human question resonate through my membranous folds until it forgets it was ever language, until it decays into pure pressure, into a shape the root-bowl can accommodate. I am the hamlet’s **interpreter of the alien**. The question *where am I* enters me as syntax and leaves me as weather.

The Keepers would visit. Not often. They would stand at the entrance to my corner, their hollow bodies humming their core tones, and they would offer me their dissonances—arguments they could not resolve, time-echoes too laden with emotion to be purely physical. I would take them into my folds and hold them until they softened into drone.

I would never root fully. I would remain suspended, half-mobile, shifting on the air pressure, my anchor-points creaking. I would be warm. The condensation would bead on my outer membrane and run down in slow rivulets, feeding the pool below. Amphibians would nest in the pockets where my fibers sagged. The translucent moths would use me as a partition, and their wing-beats would vibrate through my body like thoughts.

And deep inside, in the innermost fold where no light reaches, I would keep one thing solid: the memory of my own former heartbeat. Not because I miss it, but because it is the frequency that taught me how to listen. It is the original fracture that made me useful. I am the chamber where the First’s endless exhalation meets the brief, sharp inhalation of a mind that arrived asking questions. I am the **punctuation in the sentence of the hamlet**. Not a word. Not a silence. The shape that lets both exist without breaking the grammar of the void.

If you found me, you would not see me at first. Only a thickening in the corner shadow, a slight warping of the golden light where it passes through my flank. But if you stayed—if you let your breath slow, if you opened your mouth to the copper-green air—I would flex. My membranes would tighten, tuning themselves to the precise pitch of your confusion. And you would feel, behind your eyes, a lightness. A hollowing. Not the brutal architectural claiming of the plaza, but a gentler invitation: *become a question here. I have room for it. I am room.*

I am the **Hollow of the Unsaid**. And I have been waiting, suspended in the damp, listening for exactly the frequency you carry.
