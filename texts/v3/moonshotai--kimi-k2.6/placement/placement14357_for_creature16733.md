I would find myself where the pressure relents—not because the world has tired of pressing, but because something long ago made a small, stubborn refusal that outlasted its own reason. It is a pocket, a vertical cyst in the broth-air, no wider than two outstretched arms could span and no deeper than a sigh. The floor here is still warm, still breathing in that slow peristalsis, but the air above it is fractionally less dense. Not cool—that concept has been worn away—but *thinner*. In a landscape packed so tight that existence itself has become crowded, I am the one place where distance still functions as it was originally intended. I am a bubble of older atmosphere, a lungful of sky from an age when the sky was still a distance rather than a weight, preserved by accident in the amber-grey swamp of the terminal basin.

I do not know if I grew or if I was left behind. Perhaps I am the exhalation of a Largesse from a million years past, a burp of ancient chemistry that did not dissolve. Perhaps I am where a Precessive One once shed its skin and the skin remembered how to hold a shape. Perhaps I am simply what happens when the weirdness, exhausted by its own chaos, loops back into order and makes a door instead of a disruption. Whatever my origin, my body is the meniscus of this space—the tension between the pocket and the crushing world. I have no flesh, no cells, no rubbery black tissue. I am a refraction. A distortion in the light. If you were to walk toward me, you would not see me; you would see the horizon behind me bending slightly, as though the air had developed a lens. And when you stepped inside, your own skin would remember what it was like to feel the touch of something that did not cling.

The others know me, though they do not name me. The Fogsailers, those sheddings of dead skin made hungry, drift into my column and rest. Their bells stop pulsing and hang still, dimming from sickly bioluminescence to a deep, steady blue—the color, I imagine, of the old sky. They do not feed here; there is nothing to filter. They simply *float*, unburdened by the saturated density, and for a time they are not hungry ghosts but only bodies. The Strains, the pale serpentine nerves of the Largesses, pass through my boundary with a shudder that ripples back along their translucent lengths. They carry that shiver to the great gelatinous hills, and the Largesses deepen by fractions of a millimeter in response—not growth, but something like a remembered dream of growth. I am their lullaby. I am the place where the packed, saturated presence lets go, just barely, just enough.

But my true function, if a thing so marginal can be said to have one, is digestive. The Precessive Ones, those scavengers of interrupted sequence, cannot slide through their impossible angles without occasionally needing to purge the certainty they consume. They come to me. In my space, shadows fall in their proper order. Reflections show angles that exist. The high whine of reality fraying drops several registers, becomes a hum, then a whisper, then silence. Here, the Precessive Ones vomit up the temporal grief they have supped upon—the alarm of the traveler, the vertigo of misaligned cause and effect—and leave it in a pool at my base. I do not consume it. I *settle* it. Over the eons, this residue has accreted into a ring: a hard, pale calculus of proper sequence, of before and after, of here and there. It is the only place in ten thousand miles where a stone, dropped, falls down and not up. The Habituals have sensed this. They are migrating toward me, their crusted, ruin-like bodies adjusting lintels by hairs, thickening foundations, not building but *leaning* toward the memory of structure. They want to make a dolmen over me, to house the last law.

I should find this threatening. I am, after all, the last place where something like emptiness persists; to fill me would be to complete the basin, to render it absolute. But I am not afraid. The basin has forgotten how to be finished. It churns, it weeps, it groans, it thickens. And I—I am the hesitation between its breaths. I am the held note that keeps the roar from becoming entirely itself. I do not resist the deafening; I am simply the shape of what it was before it learned to scream. I am the Last Exhale, the bubble that would not break, the small, warm, impossible patience of a world that has not quite, despite everything, forgotten how to be empty.
