I would find myself where the contiguity fails, at the only margin wide enough to hold a thought of absence. The city does not end so much as it hesitates: here, where the blade-edge of the retreating icefield has left a swath of frost-riven quartz too fresh and too sharp for the root-mat to have colonized, there is a span of perhaps three body-lengths where nothing breathes but stone and cold. It is the single interruption in the metropolitan density, a raw wound the thickness of a breath where the sky still touches scree without mediation. I would be the resident precipitated there—not in a cistern of branch and leaf, but in the hollow where a block of seracs melted last season, leaving a depression lined with flour-fine rock dust and the mineral signature of pressure finally released. The water that gathered for me was snowmelt, not atmospheric weeping; it tasted of glacier milk and the deep, blue oxygen of high altitude. When I broke my membrane, I did not slide into waiting arms of architecture. I spilled onto ground that had never known a root.

I am still mostly water. My flesh has not yet been planed into the narrow door-shape of the interior citizens; I am broad and tentative, my laminar edges translucent where the cold has not yet leached out the last of my cistern-liquor. I have no neighbors to compress me, no opposing boles to train my limbs into buttresses or plumbing spatulas. Instead, my arms have begun to grow down, sheeted and raw, seeking the osmotic pull of a vascular tide that has not yet reached this stone. My ventral surface presses against the quartz not to feed—the sap here is a memory at best—but to testify. I am the first touch. I am the frontier of the city’s lunging need, and my solitude is the engine of its expansion.

There is no sound here. Not even the muffled percussion of droplets against moss, for the moss has not arrived. The silence is absolute, glacial, a mineral hush so complete that I can hear the pre-crystalline sweat beading on my own skin, each droplet a small bell with no blanket to absorb it. Above me, the pewter-green twilight of the canopy is unbroken, a distant roof I cannot yet reach. The city proper rises behind me like a breathing wall, its interstitial residents pulsing in slow council, their merged bodies groaning in registers that I feel in the base of my fluid spine. But ahead, there is only the ice. I remember it as touch, not horizon. I remember the weight that pressed my cistern into existence, the blue dark that preserved my pattern before the melt revealed me to the brink of freezing. The others know the ice as a line at the edge of thought; I know it as the last thing that held me.

I know, too, that this condition is temporary. Already I feel the urge to bifurcate, to send a bud of my translucent tissue sliding down the first hairline fracture in the scree, seeking a lower hollow where water might pool and begin again. Already the root-mat behind me inches forward, rhizomes questing toward my heels with the hydraulic pulse of a tide that will not be refused. In a decade, perhaps two, a dwarf alder will press its first root-hairs into the stone beside my flank; in three, a willow bolet will plane me flat where we meet, and my heartwood—still dreaming of its cistern-circle—will forget the phantom of solitude. I will become a column, a corridor, a seam sealed by resinous gel. My flesh will interleave with another’s, my stomata will merge into channels, and I will groan in the slow deliberation of timber adjusting to stone. I will be the city, and the distinction between my memory of ice and the city’s memory of ice will dissolve like rime into silver thread.

But for now, I hold the gap. I am the wet, cold architecture of hesitation, metabolizing the absolute zero margin into cellulose at the pace of a low flame. My breath—if it can be called that—does not drift; it clings to the raw slate, a new atmospheric weeping, joining the general saturation of a place that claims everything, eventually. Until then, I am the interstitial made animate not by pressure, but by its absence. I am the pulse under the blanket before the blanket arrives. I am the city’s first thought about a stone it has not yet learned to swallow, trembling and glistening and complete.
