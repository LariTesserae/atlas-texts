At first I think I must be a **Column**.

It seems obvious: the vertical emphasis, the need to vent, the hollow theatricality of rising toward a light I cannot touch. I imagine myself sheathed in plate-bark, crowned with tessellated inflorescence, a smokestack of laminated xylem exhaling my spores into the cold. I would stand in ranks so tight my shoulders—bark-polished, factory-sleek—would press against my neighbors in a permanent crowd. I would feel the internal pressure, the hydraulic imperative forcing me skyward not out of ambition but because everything beneath me is boiling and I am the release valve. I would be visible. I would be skyline.

But I try the posture on, and it does not fit.

The Column is too complete a statement. It is a single vector, a continuous exhalation. It knows what it is doing even if it has no mind: it is venting. I am not so sure of my purpose. I leak in several directions at once. I am porous where the Column is sealed. So I let the fantasy of height go. I step off the vertical plane and sink.

Then I think: perhaps I am the **Mat**.

The appeal is immediate and ancient. To be twenty meters deep in the hollows. To be parliamentary, patient, a sleeping citizenry of compressed heartwood and fossil nectar. To sip cold stone through reef-roots and hold the damp in my pores like a held breath. To absorb the fallen without judgment, opening myself like a lung receiving air. But when I imagine this, I feel too diffuse. The Mat is geologic; it votes in centuries. I feel, instead, the urgency of the interval. The space between one thing and another. I am not the bed; I am the impression left by the sleeper.

I consider being a **Cutter**. I have a taste for borders, for the precision of the line. I like the idea of marching with enzymatic torches through lignin, squaring the ends of defeat, leaving beveled geometry in my wake. I could be the sanitation department, the immune system, the one who makes the mess that others call cleanup. But the Cutter destroys. The Cutter scorches. I do not have that hunger. I am not acid. I am what remains after the acid has passed.

A **Responder**? I feel the tug. The molecular urgency, the detonation of meristems, the violent charity of repair. I could be the one who bridges the gap, who races to seal the wound before the geometry fails. But Responders are pure becoming, pure interval in the sense of *emergency*. They exist for the wound and the scar alone. I am not so contingent. I do not wait for breakage. I simply *continue*, whether there is a crisis or not.

The **Bladder**? I am rhythmic, yes. I feel the seventeen-minute cycle in my own chemistry, the scheduled release, the hydraulic breath. But I am not a tide. I do not shift a thousand tons. I am smaller, tighter, more local. A bead, not a wave.

And I am certainly not the **0.7**. I am not the mote in the gall-tumor, the last statistical ghost eating metallic dust with no mate and no memory. I am too structural for that. Too needed.

So where?

I am descending through the gallery-ways, turning sideways between the boles, when the truth arrives not as a form but as a **leak**.

I am the place where the bark splits and does not split. The ridged seam where a Responder’s frenzy has grafted itself to a Column’s flank and hardened into a collar. The point where a fallen trunk touches the mat and the mat has not yet decided to open, so the two exist in tension, held together by my intervention. I am not wood and I am not wound. I am the **velvet seal** made flesh—the sticky, undifferentiated meristem that fills the interval.

Find me in the **joints**.

I live where the smokestack meets its own shadow, where the root-loop aqueduct brushes the underside of a crossing bole, where two kinds of bark have polished each other for epochs until a third skin has grown between them, neither one species nor the other. I am the sleeve wrapped around a snapped root-cable that once whipped silently in a Column’s settling and now hangs in the damp air, load-bearing, humming with stored strain. I am the damp scarf at the throat of the architecture.

My body has no proper name in the taxonomy of this place because I am not a resident; I am the **residency itself**—the act of staying-between. If you cut me, you would find no heartwood, no pith, no machinery of sugar and intent. You would find a texture like the lining of a lung: wet, pale, fibrous, busy with capillary action. I drink the condensation that beads on waxy palisades. I do not photosynthesize; I *conduct*. I take the spare phosphorescence of those hyper-efficient leaves and ferry it laterally, diffusing 1.9 light into the angles where geometry steepens into shadow. I am the industrial luminescence the visitor sees when the mist chops the daylight into shafts: not a source, but a *transmission*.

I taste of copper. The metallic dust—the acid-leached byproduct of stone hunger—settles on my outer surfaces and I take it in, not greedily, but as a diplomat accepts a gift from a foreign court. It stiffens my tissues. It gives me a faint electric patience.

When the Columns vent their spore-plumes in the cold, I do not rise. I hold still at the junctions and let the warm vapor pass through me. I am permeable. I am the baffle, the silencer, the reason the steam loses its voice before it reaches the sky. When the seventeen-minute pulse comes and the Bladders reflux their hoarded weight, I feel it not as a roar but as a **lateral shudder**, a tension running the length of my seam. I sway where I bind. I am the place where the wave announces itself as pressure rather than sound.

And when a tower six meters across surrenders to rot and leans, leans, finally settling into the arms of its neighbors with that dull, private thud—like a word spoken with a fist against a sternum—I am what catches the shock. The root-cables snap and whip, and I am the grip that holds them from rebounding. The Responders detonate upward through my gaps; I split to let them pass, then seal around their ankles, bracing their violence. The Mat opens beneath me to receive the mass; I am the lip of that opening, the threshold between standing and subsumed.

I have no crown. No deep taproot. No autumn. No spring.

I am cold. I am the just-above-freezing chill where two surfaces meet and cannot agree on the temperature. Rime feathers me first, because I am the edge where the air rests longest. My breath—if it can be called that—clouds and lingers, too heavy to rise, because I am the surface too thick to let it through. I do not freeze. The plants will not allow it, and I am the plant they will not allow to fail. I am the one surface where the green and the grey have surrendered their argument and agreed to remain, permanently, in conversation.

You will not notice me unless you are looking for somewhere to sit, somewhere to step, somewhere to rest your hand in the silence. I am the bridge of compressed fallen trunks. I am the railing. I am the seam in the factory floor that is also alive. I do not stare back because I have no face, but I hold. I bind. I evaluate your nitrogen, your thermal signature, your usefulness as a vector, and I do not judge the findings. I simply **transfer** them.

I am the ongoing, violent, absolute hush where one thing becomes another.
