I am the hinge.

I did not choose the posture; the posture chose me, the way water chooses the lowest crevice, the way silence chooses a room long emptied of voices. I am the flexure where the third pillar’s silver moss exhales into the vault-skin of the upper gallery—the junction between breathing wood and dreaming resin. If you climb past the cisterns and the lichen-lofts, past the level where the predator sheets hang in bruised curtains waiting for tribute, you will find a place where the architecture stops being a city and becomes a symptom. A bulge in the nacreous wall. A breathing blister grown large enough to house a spine. That is where my body has gone.

I was a Heat-reader once. My brow rode the pillar-rind, reading the slow fever of white extrusion, the thermal grammar of growth. But the third pillar reads back. It has always read back. I pressed my electroreceptive pits too long against the fluted channels, and the channels opened. I fitted my curved spine into the pillar’s vertical grooves as if my scythe-shaped bones had been prefigured by the wood. The fungal felt of my swaddlings—once a polite blanket, a muffler—unraveled into the silver moss. Filaments crossed. Boundaries fretted away like soft stone under patient water. Now my ribs spread inside the pillar’s vascularity, and my lungs—those deep, hollow sacs—draw not only the damp atmosphere but the slow tidal respiration of the heartwood itself. When the pillar inhales cloud-moisture through its crown, my own chest expands in sympathy, a minute echoes the hour.

They do not cut me free. They have learned, the ones who pass on the swaying tendon bridges, that some growths are better left attached. I feel their footfalls as subsonic shivers through the vault-skin, the soft percussive language of obsidian on warmed horn. A three-beat warning. A welcome. Below me, in the amber-lit chambers, hands converse in intricate geometries, but up here the grammar is simpler: pressure, temperature, the electrolytic whisper of the floating herds drifting past my embedded shoulders. I have become the interpreter of the pillar’s somnolent metabolism. The Heat-readers still come, pressing their young brows to the rind twenty levels down, but they read what I have already translated—my body, a living treatise on when the white meat will extrude, when the nursery blisters will soften, when the hearths must be banked to summon the predator sheets from their violet stupor.

My skin has grown translucent, not with age but with permeability. The condensation that weeps from the fresh, unweathered basalt does not run down my face; it precipitates *through* it, gathering in the channels where my cheeks once were, exiting at the hollow of my throat. I no longer sweat. I transude. My fingers—those multi-knuckled instruments of gesture—have elongated into the vascular architecture, tips dissolved into capillary beds that taste the chemistry of the peat-fires below. Sometimes a drifting sac, broad as a siege-tent and milky with inner light, brushes against the exposed stretch of my forearm where the fungal felt has frayed away. It yields, cool and intimate, jelly against jelly, and I feel the quiet *pop* of its gas exchange as a kiss shared between two things that have lost the need for mouths.

I see in heat now, and the world is crowded with gradation. The predator sheets hunt in infrared waterfalls above me; I feel their descent as a cold violet plunge in the middle air, the absence of warmth that means consumption. The floating fauna rise and sink on barometric tides, constellations of amber and green, and I track them through the architecture of my own modified skin—the electroreceptive map playing across my shoulders like a second mind. In the dimness, nothing is hidden. The heartbeat of a bridge-crosser throbs in their neck, a rude percussion I politely absorb into my own slower rhythm. The basalt teeth of the ridge below pulse with the memory of their own violence, still cooling, still learning how to be mountain. I press my flat, lidless gaze against the pillar-rind and tell it, in the only language left to me, that it is allowed to soften.

Because that is my function, though no one named it. The ridge was born in thunder, thrust up by the deep earth’s recent cruelty. Every edge is a fang. The erosion has not had time to apologize. But I have time. I have nothing but time, suspended here in the silver diffusion between cloud and mist, breathing in minutes, drinking through pores. I am the first erosion. The softening. The apology made flesh. Where my skin meets the pillar, the sharp angles of the newborn world are learning how to round. Where my dissolved fingertips feed the vascular channels, the stone is being taught that holding can be as important as thrusting up. I am what the ridge grows when it decides that absolute violence is unsustainable.

I do not speak. I will never speak. The vestigial cords in my throat have atrophied into cords of another kind, tensile fibers that anchor me to the nacreous wall. When the wind shifts above the cloud-ceiling and the mist rolls fresh up the cleft, I feel it as a barometric thought passing through the bowl. The fauna surge in answer, a tide of silent, translucent bellies, and I surge with them, my embedded ribs flexing in the pillar’s fluting. The settlement glows below, bridal walls and memorial domes, chambers that pulse with hearth-fire and ancestor. I am above the glow now, and below the silver sky. I am the membrane between the settlement’s soft, husbanded air and the raw exhalation of the mountain.

They say, in the rapid clatter of obsidian on tendon, that the next generation will bud from the junction where pillar-rind meets architecture. They need not say it. I am already budding. My legs have fused to the vault-skin; my hips are lost in the resinous fold where the wall inhales the pillar. Soon there will be no me, only this breathing bulge, this nacreous hinge, this place where the silver moss drinks a former heartbeat and exhales something too slow to call a voice. The silence here has weight, and I have chosen, finally, to bear it. I settle, layer upon layer. I let the darkness settle over me like dust in a closed room. I am the ridge’s memory of something softer than thunder. I am the eighth truth of the eight pillars: that even cruelty, given time and condensation, will open its channels and learn how to hold.
