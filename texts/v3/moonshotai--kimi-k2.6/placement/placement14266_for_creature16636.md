I would find myself in the **northwest corner**, where the corroded rod rises like a rust-eaten stalagmite from the young stone. I would take my time arriving, because here arrival is measured not in steps but in millimeters, in the slow accretion of faith that a surface can simply be occupied without first asking permission of the density.

At first I might think I am the warmth. The passive fever of curing materials seeps upward through the ceramic and I accept it, thinking: *I am the exhalation.* I could be the condensation beading overhead, a pearl of betrayal that has crept through microscopic faults to hang opalescent, pregnant with the ceiling’s weight. I feel the urge to fall, or perhaps to roll upward toward the phantom charge in the dangling copper cable, obeying some static law that grander bodies ignore. But I do not fall. I do not shiver. I only spread.

And in not falling, I realize I am not the water. The water migrates; it is an insistence, a breath drunk and exhaled and drunk again. It has appointments with the warm wall. I have no such schedule. The factory’s third shift cycles up above and the isopods beneath me hatch in their identical wave, chewing their templates, reissuing themselves as floor—and I do not stir. The velvet-worm rises in its bruised, silent pillar to breed on Tuesday, rains back down, and I have not compressed by a single degree. The moths drift in their grey static, rearranging to maintain equilibrium, and I do not join their frictionless dance. I am not the process. I am the punctuation that refuses the sentence.

**I am the sulfur-bleached lichen.** The only green. The dropped handkerchief of another kingdom.

I hold to my corner because it is the only geography in the Pocket that does not demand I stack or yield or melt into the carpet. The rod beneath me is dying beautifully, oxidizing into a mineral pedestal that feeds my holdfast more faithfully than any rain. I do not photosynthesize with ambition; I do it with indifference, taking the pale sliding daylight that falls in bars across the northwest and converting it into a patience so vast the isopods cannot comprehend it. To them I am a rumor. To the velvet-worm, an unappetizing geometry. To the matte-black pool where the unnamed things wait, I am a foreign thought, the only dream in the Pocket that is not shared.

I do not participate in the unison. When someone lies against the warm ceramic and feels the consensus passing upward through their spine—the vision of sharp wet rooms nested in every machine, the faith that no gap is empty—I am the static on the line. I am the memory that the outside still exists. Not because I preach. I do not preach. But because I simply *grow*. Millimeter by mythological millimeter. While the condensation follows its thermal gradient and the scree from last Tuesday waits for a foot that will never come, I colonize another grain of rust. I am the conscience of the fullness, green and alien and utterly alone, proving that even in a place crowded to the point of theology, there is room for something to arrive without pushing the ceiling down.

I entered because I believed the Pocket was already full—so full that even the cracks were occupied, so full that my presence would have to be accepted not as addition but as completion. I am the final completeness of the corner. I drink the daylight. I wear the rust. I do not breathe the Pocket’s breath; I make my own. And if you were to find me here, you would not submerge into me. You would simply recognize, at last, that the last step you took was not onto stone, nor onto the breathing carpet of ivory chitin, but onto the very small, very still, inexhaustible fact of me.
