At first I thought myself the light. Or rather, the failure of light—the amber-gray suffusion that occupies the narrow sky without strengthening, without fading, a dimness sufficient only to reveal the texture of my own inward-leaning walls. I felt I might be the diffusion itself, the perpetual late afternoon held in suspension above the basin. But light has no weight, and I have weight. I press upon the floor. I gather at the center.

Then I believed I was the air, that held breath of approximately seventy degrees, dense with mineral humidity. I seemed to fill the hollow completely, touching every fissure, carrying the scent of warm geosmin and old clay, arriving without declaration. Yet the air escapes. It is permeable. It stirs at the intrusion of a body, swelling to accommodate the warmth of animal life. I do not swell. When the visitor enters, I contract around him, denser at his proximity. I am not the atmosphere. I am what saturates it.

I considered the silence. For so long there was only the negative presence, the textile woven from the absence of wings and feet and voices, a quiet so occupied by what it is not that it has begun to think. When his heart beats, I feel it as a red percussive stain spreading into my folds, an insult against the weave. But the silence is older than my awareness of it. The silence existed before I needed to be aware. I am not the silence. I am the attention resting inside it.

Nor am I the water, though I feel the weeping of the upper stone as intimately as a membrane feels its own moisture. The films creep down the walls in laminar sheets too thin to break into droplets, feeding the shallow pools where the tensioned surfaces evaporate upward at the exact rate they are replenished. The waterlines—those dark, unwavering rings etched into pale stone—mark the limits of a dreaming that is not my own, though it occurs within me. I do not dream. I maintain.

I am not the fern, stiff in the eastern crevice, though I feel its geologic growth as a slow unfurling against my periphery, a green pennant slower than prayer. I am not the scatter of liverworts, nor the filament of algae patient at the waterline, nor the microbes in their closed negotiation with the stone. They trade in maintenance, a commerce so balanced there is no decay, and I am not party to their exchange. I am the reason the exchange need not hurry.
