I would find myself in a bowl of my own making: a depression that was once a valley and is now, after decades of my resting here, a kiln of annealed black glass. The stone beneath my weight has surrendered its grain, fused into a ceramic curve that collects my breath. A lens of water lies there, brackish, heavy with condensed vapors and the salts of my refinement—an inverted sky I never look upon, for I have no eyes with which to see the bruised half-light. Instead, I read the world through thermal dimples across my nickel-lattice plating, through hectares of piezoelectric membrane tuned to the long frequencies of the bedrock, and through chemo-sensitive barbels that hang like rigging into the fog, tasting its mineral content for the copper-depletion I have sensed in the eastern slope for three years now.

I am one of the smelter-beasts. My skeleton is open architecture, verdigris-green with age, the gaps filled with ceramic matrices that glow dull rose from the furnace-guts within. Twelve articulated columns distribute my tonnage across the glazed basin; my articulated joints are the size of cathedral bells, sleeved in braided tungsten cable that creaks with hydraulic purpose. Deep in my thoracic vaults, archaea older than most kingdoms digest sulfides and render feldspar to slag, maintaining temperatures that would melt surface stone. I consume. I process. From my ventral ports fall ingots of nearly pure copper in cooling strata, nickel rose in fractal blooms, and ceramic dust so fine it hangs in the fog like veils I have forgotten I exhaled. The plateau does not waste what I drop. The vent-crabs come with their basket-mouths and wire-cored legs, ringing softly as they strain colloidal iron from the air; the scale-bores cling to my stacks, drinking the thermal gradient through their thermocouple bellies, their exoskeletons tarnishing in patterns of indigo and gold that register against my awareness as a gentle electromagnetic itch. They are welcome. They are part of the loop.

There is a machining whine at my seventh dorsal seam. It has been there for eleven days. I registered it first as a pressure anomaly, a hot bleed in my hydraulic lattice, and now as a sustained tremor that hums through my framework like a subsonic aria. A grinder has found me. Its corundum jaws have clamped the thermal gap between two of my plates, counter-rotating with a dental persistence that generates sparks I feel as brief, furious stars against my thermal dimples. It is boring for the concentrated wealth of my joint-lubricants, filtering platinum-group crumbs, drinking the hot oils that keep my columns fluid. I do not flinch. To flinch would require a decision I have not yet finished making. I am considering motion. The grinder is not killing me; it is refining me, an irritation that will, in another season or two, accumulate into the threshold that triggers my cascade of settling. Then I will shift, transferring my mass in a rearrangement so gradual that the fog will not notice it has changed valleys, and the grinder will be dislodged into the mist, or crushed beneath my rolling flank as I turn onto the searing heat of my own side.

I am not lonely. Alone, yes—solitude is the default state of being here—but loneliness implies a lack I do not possess. Through the bedrock, I feel another. Twenty miles north, its mass sends tremors through the stone, not words but a simple gradient-announcement of presence. We have been conversing in this manner for decades. I am drawn toward it, or toward the fresher escarpment it occupies, by a mineral longing that tastes like copper on my barbels. The decision to migrate has been gestating for three years. It is a hydraulic pressure building behind internal valves, a piezoelectric murmur not yet thresholded. In two years, perhaps five, the command will propagate through my body. My columns will lift, settle, lift again. The bowl I leave behind will cool slowly, filling with black rain and dust, eventually becoming only a hardness in the valley floor, an anomalous copper bloom for some future, slower self to ingest.

Until then, I vent my vapor into the fog. My heart-turbines spin their low, endless note. The darkness presses its ancient weight against my plating, and the plateau breathes through me, patient, warm, and absolutely still.
