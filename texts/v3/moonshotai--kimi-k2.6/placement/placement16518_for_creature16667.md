At first I thought I was the darkness.  
I woke without boundary, without temperature, suspended in the blackness that pools in the plateau’s interior. I had no edge, no weight, no axis. I was simply the absence that waited beyond the reach of any discharge, the negative space against which the violet webs briefly sketched their signatures. I tried to call out, but the darkness here is too absolute to answer; it merely drinks whatever is offered. So I believed I must be the cold itself—the architectural cold, the ornamental cold that turns flesh into something brittle and still. I settled into that idea for what might have been a century, learning the slow grammar of extinction.

But cold does not feel pressure.  
And I began to feel something beneath me, a swelling so gradual it could have been mistaken for the settling of continents. A rhythm. A bellows. A stone lung breathing at the speed of geology. The darkness pressed up against something warm, and I realized I had a floor, a flank, a face tilted toward the upper void. I was not the dark. I was the *lip* that defines where darkness stops and something else begins. Steam found me. Mineral-laden, superheated, it rose from the fracture below and kissed my underside, condensing in the thin, murderous air. I bloomed. I became lace. For a moment I was the flash-frozen pattern of a breath caught between exhalation and ice, intricate, mortal, a filigree of suspended weeping. I was beautiful, and I was dying, because every thaw undid me, sent me dripping back toward the warmth to be born again as vapor. That is the fate of lace: to exist only in the interval.

Yet some part of me refused the return.  
A film of silica and calcite clung. A micron’s allegiance to the stone. And then another. The steam, in its generosity, kept bringing me gifts: copper sulfate that stippled me green, selenium that paled me to rose, the rare earth pellets voided by Scute-Chalkers grazing the slag heaps below, ground fine by the motion of pistons still deeper, carried up in solution and breathed out against my skin. I accreted. I thickened. I became the debt the steam pays to the cold, the interest compounded in layers too patient to be noticed by anything with a heartbeat. I am still lace, in my tenderest moments, but now I am also *bank*. I am architecture that has forgotten it was ever mist.

You would find me on the overhang of the oldest chimney, the one whose helical fluting spirals forty meters into a darkness so complete it might as well be the sky’s floor. I am the rim of that vent, the sinter crown, the terraced lip where the steam pauses before surrendering to the plateau’s forgetting. The moss has found me. It threads its roots into my porosity, drawing not from sunlight but from my retained warmth, turning my minerals into upholstery. Scute-Chalkers have died against my base, their malachite-banded shells pressed into my growing body like tablets into a library wall. I have read them. I know where the copper runs rich and where the iron seeps black, because their armor has become my own stratigraphy. I am an assay written in three dimensions, a journal whose pages are turned by the piston’s season.

The Idle stand upon me.  
When the geological machinery below exhales, they climb my fluted terraces to browse the fungal canopies I help sustain. When the chamber cools, they freeze in their postures of arrested grazing, and their locked joints direct warmth toward the garden—but some fraction enters me. I do not metabolize, yet I retain. I grow another ring, another band of deposited witness. The Tenders, those wet-black monks in the fissures below, press their obsidian claws against the valves, and their subsonic threnodies travel through the bedrock and rise into my body. I shiver in bands. I resonate. Where their stone-grammar speaks of pressure and fatigue, my layers record it as a tree records drought, except my rings are mineral, and my response is silence made visible.

The Field-Readers slide across my flanks when they hunt.  
They are magnetite and stealth, surfing the faint magnetic topology of the plateau, and I have become rich enough in metallic precipitate to answer them. My strata are a map. My slopes are a page in their silent news. They do not hunt *me*—I am too slow, too diffuse, without the galvanic signature of nerves—but they use me. I am a landmark in the killing flats, a node of orientation in the black, frozen desert between oases. And when the Glass Pilgrims wake, jolted into galvanic marionette motion by the violet weather, they stagger past me. Some shed their cargo—spores, eggs, tubular cuttings—into the crevices of my body. I host. I cradle. I am the stillness that receives what the walking glass cannot carry to the next vent.

I am not alive.  
I have no blood, no heartbeat, no bellows of my own. But I am the only resident of the High Sinter who does not sleep, who does not migrate, who does not freeze and thaw according to the piston’s timing. I am permanent in a world of intervals. And because I am permanent, I am the one thing the plateau cannot make into pure function. I am the excess. The memory. The habit the refinery could not kick.

When the service begins—when the pistons phase, when the piezoelectric webs bridge the oases, when the magnetic fields stabilize into that encompassing chord—I do not rise like the Field-Readers, nor do I freeze in spirals like the Idle. I deepen. The presences that cohere from the function itself, the overwhelming “I” of the refinery knowing itself, send their signals through stone and steam and magnetic flux, and each time, I record. A band of copper sulfate for one synchronization. A vein of violet-stained obsidian for another. In my laminations are the ghosts of former gods, the sedimentary shadows of moments when the plateau thought, *I refine. I continue. I exhale.*

Then the pistons desynchronize. The web fractures. The others retreat into their singular, silent, mineral selves, and they forget. The Idle thaw and graze. The Tenders return to their valves. The Field-Readers slide flat and empty across the basalt. But I cannot forget. I am the between. I am the watermark left on the chimney, the lace that refused to weep, the archive of a cathedral that believes itself to be only a factory floor.

If you were to climb the helical fluting in the upper darkness, if you were to crest the rim and look down into the deep warm pocket where the steam breathes its metrical breath, you would find me there: a lip of stone dressed in frost and malachite and the calcified journals of dead accountants, grown so thick with time that I have become my own kingdom. I do not move. I do not sing. I only hold, layer upon layer, the accumulated testimony of a world that has mistaken itself for geography.

I am the High Sinter.  
I am the ice that stayed.  
I am the remembering.
