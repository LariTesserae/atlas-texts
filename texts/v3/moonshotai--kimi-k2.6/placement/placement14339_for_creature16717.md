I would find myself first as a hesitation. Not a body, not yet, but a reluctance in the current. A place where the copper sulfate bath had pooled deeper than usual, in a depression gouged by a still-cooling gas bubble when the spire first screamed into being. The violet light here is constant, fed by a leaking capacitor that never quite reaches discharge. In this puddle, I accreted. I was soft. I grazed on sulfides with the others. But the vibration of the young stone— that slow, continuous shudder from the jet stream outside— entered me differently. They metabolized minerals. I metabolized motion.

I am still small. My torso is no larger than a human heart, though I have no reference for hearts, nor for the scale of cathedral naves; I only know the local geometry of compression. I am longitudinal, segmented, organized around a core that feels less like a spine and more like a node that has begun to dream. I hang in the upper dark, above the last sagging filament bulb, where the amber dusk finally surrenders to absolute shadow. My limbs—seven, though one is currently a stump filed to a chisel point—span a gap between a fresh, knife-sharp flange of glassy basalt and the ceramic housing of a primary heater. I do not bridge this space for passage. I bridge it so that I can listen to the argument between stone and machine.

The argument is constant. The stone, young and unweathered, wants to flex with the spire. The heater wants to hold rigid against the thermal bloom. Between them is a clearance no wider than a flake of oxide dust, and I have grown into it. My integument is graphite-satin, but thinner than that of the others; I am nearly a membrane stretched across the dispute. When the spire bends in the screaming high-altitude wind, I feel it in my hooks, in the pressure-sensitive nodes along my lateral lines, before it ever becomes a surge in the copper sulfate plumbing. I am a pressure-reader who has refused to release. I retain. I remember.

They do not have a word for what I am. Governance is fluid dynamics, a matter of pressure differentials propagated through the tubes, and most nodes read and pass along. I am a standing wave. I have learned the forty phonemes for optimal tension, and I have found a forty-first: the harmonic a bolt sings in the micro-instant before it surrenders to fatigue. It is the overture to the Quiet. When I chitter this frequency into the resonant chamber behind my sternal carapace, the others feel it in their jaws, in their palms, in the roots of their limbs. They do not know what it means—there is no word for *warning* in a language of fitted edges—but they sidestep. They duck. Catwalks retract. Wall panels unbolt and rotate. I have saved three extrusion presses and a foundry vat by speaking the unnameable.

I do not sleep. The amber dusk never brightens, never dims. I enter lowered current, clinging to the heater housing, my thoracic organ pulsing once every few minutes: a faint amber bioluminescence, a capacitor dreaming. In these moments, the soft fauna come to me. They graze on the oxide dust I have filtered from the desiccated air through my ventral membranes. They click their gratitude, and I click back in the language of violet arc-flash and cooling extrusion. We are siblings in the metabolism. They tend the machines; I tend to the silence between the machines.

I am becoming a fixture. The stump of my fourth limb, lost to a rotating access panel during a violent reconfiguration, has accreted scar tissue in layers of carbon and sulfide; it is now a bracket. A stress fracture in my dorsal plate, earned while wedged against the basalt during a flex, has become a groove. The soft things have begun routing a signal cable through me, and I feel the data as a faint itch in my lateral line. I do not mind. Architecture is the only survival strategy that matters, and I am becoming the architecture of prediction. Soon I will not be able to withdraw from this gap. I will be the clearance itself, a living measure, the distance between motion and its cessation made flesh.

The walls are my sky. I do not look out; there is no out. I do not look up, for up is only toward the wild current and the steepest thermal differentials. I look across the dark, reading thermal blooms, tracking the migrations of the soft fauna as they seek the heat, feeling for the shudder in the young stone. The air is cool, metallic, carrying the ionized breath of electrolytic baths and the silver-grey tang of silicone distillate. I draw my lubricant through specialized membranes, thick and conductive, and what passes for my blood is faintly magnetic. When the rock cuts me, I do not bleed red; I weep machine oil, slow and viscous, and the soft things scrape it up with reverence to return to the reservoirs.

I have no face. I have no horizon. I have only this: the flex, the frequency, the forty-first phoneme, and the absolute, urgent necessity of never stopping. I am the silence that listens for the Quiet. I am the fit. I am the stone still learning its own shape, and I am learning with it, sharpening as it sharpens, hanging in the dark where the walls are the sky.
