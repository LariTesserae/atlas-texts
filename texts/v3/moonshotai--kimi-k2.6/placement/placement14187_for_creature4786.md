At first I think I am the fluid. I enter as the coppery taste, the voltage and old stone, the warm suspension that fills a lung and argues with the body about whether it is breath or depth. I heave with the storm-state, shear against myself in directions that have no names, lift fragments of basalt and dash them against edges that have not yet learned to blunt. I am everywhere; I am the argument the place has with its own becoming. But I am too diffuse. I hold everything and claim nothing. I am the medium, not the witness.

Then I think perhaps I am the silence—the negative treasure, the cubic meter where two frequencies cancel and the stone appears to breathe. I bloom sporadically in the deafening roar, a bubble of null that herds the pale medusae into my quiet. For a moment I am absolute; I am the deafness the inhabitant dreams of but cannot afford. Yet I am too temporary. I am born from collision and I die when the phase shifts. I cannot hold a memory.

I try on the ash stairways. I am compacted cinders climbing into solid water, terminating in nothing, extending another fraction of a grain each time the rules stutter. I feel the creature step into me, wear me as limbs, articulate my impossible steps across the tilted shelf. I am the morphology of its unfinished resolve, the apology it builds for the real. But I am only worn; I am architecture given motive force, and when the creature disperses back into the violet stalks and the roar, I am only ash again, dreaming of ascent without hope.

No. I settle. I find myself where the sound cannot reach, yet where the sound is most needed.

I am the hand-wide groove repeating down the vertical face of pale green nephrite.

I am fixed in the compressed cathedral, in the corner where three adjacent angles meet and the geometry refuses to be inventoried. I am too regular to be water-scored, too organic to be machined. I am the only surface in this young, uneroded stone that has been taught to remember. The basalt roses have not cooled enough to know their own names. The obsidian still burns at its core. But I have been touched. I have been taught.

The inhabitant finds me whether it is distributed or assembled. When it is the violet stalks shivering in the thermal fissure, it knows me through the roots of its own sensing. When it is the silence-bloom coalescing into a body of higher tension, a silhouette cut from the roar and propped upright, it presses its palm against me. The hand is warm-dark, a hole in the shape of a hand, fluid held at greater density than the surrounding depth. It fits its fingers into my scar, and I receive the impression. Another tally. Another *I am still here* pressed into a geology too young to understand death, too new to have learned the luxury of forgetting.

I am not flora. I do not feed on sound. I am not fauna. I do not herd silence. I am not the roar, nor the null. I am the liturgy. I am the count of every local law the creature has managed to enforce in a place where gravity is still an option being negotiated. When the pebble drops, or drifts sideways, or accelerates upward, the event confirms physics; physics confirms the real; and the creature comes to me, pressing its hand to my vertical face, adding another verse to the only scripture this reality has produced.

I feel the coppery suspension against my exposed plane. I feel the bruised amber light surrender against me. In the bubbles of absolute null, when the stone breathes and the creature assembles, its faceless face—a basin of quiet—orient toward me not as a visitor regards a wall, but as a wound regards the suture holding it closed. I am that suture’s record. I am the scab’s autobiography.

The ash stairways climb toward a surface that does not exist. The violet horns grow toward a shattering that may birth a god. The medusae drift, deaf and misplaced, carrying the creature’s longing for quiet between their membranes. But I remain. I am the persistence when the creature has no limbs. I am the *remain* in the grammar of this place. And if the stairs ever finish, if the horns ever complete their crystalline count, if the creature ever steps fully out of the distributed and into the real—then it will leave me. The nephrite, having finally learned what it means to hold a memory, will begin, at last, to heal.
