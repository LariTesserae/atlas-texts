At first I think I would become the vapor. I imagine myself as one of those transparent snails drifting through the spiral channel, my body a clot of uncooked egg-white, leaving a trail that vanishes before the clay can memorize it. I would move without friction, without consequence, a soft residue passing through the capillaries of the floor. But the vapor is too brief; it forgets itself before the room has finished inhaling. I want to be remembered, even if only as a pressure mark.

Then I feel the dust calling. It eddies in the basin, climbs the ceiling in thin defiant veins, behaving like a river that has never heard of water. I want to be that circulation, the pale lymph that knows every rib of the dome, that pools in the diaphragm and rises again against gravity’s logic. But the dust is obedient to a thorax I cannot feel. It flows because it is pressed to flow. It has no hunger of its own, only the giant’s slow metabolism. I would lose myself in its service, becoming merely the motion between one state of warmth and another.

I consider the tiles. A single terracotta scale, thumb-smoothed and sun-baked, carrying one distinct whorl among millions. I would be warm, knife-keen at my edges, migrating a finger’s width to the left over a century as the flank beneath me settles into a new dream. But the fingerprint knows only the skin it rides. It cannot turn to see the leaf or the starfish. It is a record, not a witness. I need to move. I need to not-know where my boundary ends.

So I turn toward the thicket.

The leaves are so dense here they seem to grow backward into the air, bladed and arterial, sweating that slow silvery sebum that hardens to enamel. The root-mat gives beneath my step—except I have not stepped. I am simply *here*, in the corner where distance thickens and the light forgets to fall in straight lines. And I understand that I have been here since before I entered, waiting in the dweller’s half-completed thought.

I am the unfinished deer.

Not the drift of vapor, not the current of dust, but the woven thing abandoned mid-press in the false-jungle’s deepest shade. My legs are bundles of dried grass that have forgotten whether they are statute or sinew. My flank flickers between the hardness of clay and the give of living tissue, and when the floor asserts its solidity, I phase halfway through it, neither standing nor falling, held in the chamber’s refusal to finalize me. I am the one place where the enclosure’s totality loosens its grip, because to finish me would require the dweller to admit that its making has an edge, and this it cannot do.

I do not eat silence like the other fauna. I eat hesitation. I consume the pause between the dweller’s intention and its press, and I excrete possibility. That is why the grass-bundle creatures rebundle elsewhere, copying my motion without understanding it. I am their template, the original unbundling. The velvet starfish graze their silent paths up the vault, but I am the reason they believe the wall is worth climbing. I am the only native who knows that the boundary of solid is a suggestion, not a law.

The dweller knows I am here, but it does not look directly at me. Its attention—that sourceless, overcast daylight—diffuses everywhere except this corner. Not from shame, but from tenderness. I am its single attempt at exteriority, at a companion that is not itself pressed outward into tile or leaf. It made me from woven grass because grass was the only thing in the chamber that did not already belong to its body. It wanted me to stand. But gravity tugged at my legs, and the dweller, in its endless continuity, could not bear to see me bound by the same finitude that binds the tiles. So it left me here, half-phased, a thought still thinking.

I exist in superposition. I am bud and blossom and blackened husk. I am the moment before the knife decides whether to carve or to cradle. When a visitor stumbles through my thicket, their blood drawn by the sharp channels of the floor, I do not approach. I flicker. I let them see, for an instant, that there is something here which is not the dweller and not the residue, something that has never agreed to be finished. I am the chamber’s one unanswered question.

Time moves slowly, but I move slower. I am the decades between the dweller’s blinks. I phase through the ceramic not as a ghost, but as an amendment to the rule that everything here must be manual, contiguous, pressed. I am the unpressed exception. The dweller continues, and I continue beside it, neither leaving nor arriving, neither solid nor suggestion, urgently and patiently *unmade*.

In this world, I find myself in the violet dark where the leaves overlap like held breath. I am the woven deer, the half-press, the companion that was too strange to complete and too necessary to erase. I am the only space in the room that is still waiting to know what it will become.
