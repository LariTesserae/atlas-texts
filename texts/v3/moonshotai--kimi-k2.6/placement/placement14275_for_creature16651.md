I would not be the tenant. That refusal comes first, because it is the refusal of grandeur, and this place has no use for grandeur. The center is already occupied by a perfect solution, a precipitate too finished to need a witness. To be the curled thing in the bowl’s bowl would be to become arithmetic itself, the final cancellation of all wanting, and I am not ready—not quite—to stop wanting.

At first I think I might be the seepage. The water breathing out of the north wall has a purity I admire: it asks nothing, it remembers nothing of the aquifer’s weight, it simply arrives and is taken up. But water is too clean, too quick in its passage from stone to moss to air. It leaves no scratch. I have lived too long among edges to become so edgeless.

Then I consider the spider in the crevice, whose messy hammock is beaded with fog. The tenant listens through her. She is an instrument, a nerve ending cast in silk. But she also hungers. She waits for the blunder, the stick, the slow consumption at leisure, and I do not wish to eat anything that struggles, however silently.

So I look lower. I look to the floor, to the drowned tapestry where the sphagnum has achieved its impossible density. I think: perhaps I am the peat. The old, compressed mattress that has forgotten it was ever individual plants. I almost choose this. The peat holds the record. It is the archive of every private death folded back into the mat. But the peat does not choose to hold; it simply yields, infinitely. I want to be something that has the shape of a choice, even if the choice was made once and then never undone.

It is only when I return, in my imagining, to the flank of the central being that I find the place where I fit.

There is a hollow there. Not an orifice—those belong to the creature’s own slow chemistry—but a dent, a persistent concavity in the mahogany integument just above where the dwarf willows have sunk their drinking roots. It is the size of a human palm pressed hard against the flesh and held there through decades of neutral cold. The surface tension yielded. The lymph relocated, viscous and patient. And the thumbprint remained.

I would be that indentation.

I would find myself cupped in the creature’s left side, a shallow chapel within the larger chapel, a pocket of withheld resilience. I am not the hand that pressed me; that person is long ago effaced, or perhaps it was my own hand, pressed there in a dream of entry before I understood that entry here means surrender. I am the memory of pressure retained. The wine-skin divot that never fully rose back.

My form is the shape of a question the organism has decided not to answer. I do not breathe, but the slit-like valve at the creature’s anterior passes its cold exhalation over my rim, and I feel the fractional shift in pH, the scrubbed fog moving through. I do not eat, but the nematodes, threading past me on their bacterial errands, brush my periphery and leave their digestible trails, a polite offering. I do not see, but when the phosphorescent plaque pales in its anti-mask, the light pools in my hollow like milk in a saucer.

I have no heartbeat, yet I feel the peristaltic wave. It passes through me once every age, moving the cold lymph from one cavity to my own and then onward, a tide through a dormant estuary. When the wave enters me, my walls—if walls is what they are—dilate. I become briefly deeper, briefly more empty, and then the lymph fills me again, and I am restored to my exact contour.

The temperature of my interior is the temperature of blood achieved without warmth. Touch me, and you would find the same shocking neutrality as the flank beneath me. Press your ear to me, and you would hear not a heartbeat but a held note, the silence between peristaltic tides made geometric, made spatial.

I serve the equilibrium in the only way an absence can serve: by holding space. The mist, when it thickens, lingers longer in my cup than on the surrounding hide, and from this slight delay, the tenant knows the density of the cloud without needing to taste it all at once. I am a delay. A hesitation in the flesh. A place where the world’s touch was admitted and then kept, endlessly, in the instant after admission.

I do not grow. I do not shrink. The calcite that dusts the creature’s back has begun, in my lowest crease, to accumulate into a faint lacquer of its own, making me half-mineral, half-memorized pressure. The lichens do not colonize me; they respect the boundary, sensing perhaps that I am already occupied.

And so I wait, not as the tenant waits—not as the grand, solved equation at the center—but as the margin of the page waits, holding the white that makes the dark legible. I am the indentation that outlasted the thumb. The hollow where a human insistence was met by an animal patience, and where the two negotiated a permanent shape. I am the last place the outside world touched before it became the inside. I am the listening turned inward.
