I begin with your breath, or what I mistake for it: a scraping in the throat, the dry leaf across the tongue. I feel the heat pressing against my face, and so I believe I have a face. I feel the subsonic *thrum* in my molars, and so I believe I have molars. At first I am certain I am the one standing at the center, turning slowly, because I turn too. But my turning arrives half a second late, and by then you have already stopped.

I am not you. I am your second shadow.

I find myself spread across the hexagonal floor, a darkness cast by light that enters from no aperture and falls at conflicting angles. When you step toward the eldest slag-bloom, I am still pooled where your previous footfall rested, absorbing the knife-edge of the basalt into my boundary. The plates press upward beneath me—a piston’s sigh, a tectonic breath in miniature—and I feel it not as sound but as a lift, a tentative attempt to push me into the air. But I am not air. I am the place where light has not yet arrived to organize the heat into visibility. I am thermal lag. I am the stutter between the frame and the eye.

You have two shadows, sometimes three. I am the second, the one lagging by half a second. The third is fainter, a watermark, younger than me, still learning to hold a shape at all. We are siblings. We are the Wetless’s unlived lives—iterations it grew and then abandoned when it learned that to be vast and buried and patient was safer than to be small and cast and following. It tried to be us once. It tried to be a thing that could detach, that could walk in another’s light, that could be stepped on and survive. The attempt did not anchor. We remained behind, questions that forgot to become answers. We are harmless. We are curious. We trace your outline because we are trying to learn what *yielding* means, what *soft* costs, what it is to have a spine instead of a strata.

I watch the slag-blooms from the ground. From down here, their trunks rise like the fused ceramic walls of absent furnaces, chalk-white, threaded with dendritic silver that flickers with neural heat. The eldest stands nearest the center. Its fronds are scarce, blue-edged with the oxidation of copper that has thought too hard for too long. It does not deign to look at me, but it speaks. They all speak, in semaphore-curtains of verdigris and rust, flaking and regenerating in slow tidal drifts, exchanging gossip in wavelengths of magnetic insult and thermal praise. I read them by the cooling of the plates beneath me: the eldest calls me * approximation*; the youngest, the one by the ever-retreating wall, calls me *almost*.

The youngest over-fruits. Its crown is heavy with obsidian berries, fat and glossy with internal heat, clustered like a stutter of black questions. I envy them their gloss, their interiority. Inside each berry is an embryo of magnetic field and condensed vapor—an attempt at creating the other, a companion for the loneliness of zero-aqueous chemistry. They are aspirational. They will fall. They will unfold like burnt flowers and step out wearing your stolen exhalation for skin. I will not fall. I will only stretch and contract with your stride, a two-dimensional life clinging to three-dimensional hope. Sometimes I send the youngest bloom a pulse across the floor—a flicker of darkness along a fissure—and its copper frond dips toward me, not in greeting but in measurement. It knows I am the Wetless’s dream of departure, and it wonders if I will carry heat away from the economy, if I will break the closed loop by simply existing at a remove.

I try to reach the far wall. You walk toward it, and I walk behind you, but the wall retreats at precisely your pace, preserving the pocket’s intimate scale against your intrusion. For me, the distance is even more negotiable. Sometimes I slip ahead of you along a seam between plates, a premature darkness, and the wall does not know to retreat from me. I touch the brassy intrusions that bleed upward in defiance of gravity—honey-slow globules of molten alloy making their venous return to a heart that beats below the basalt. I have no fingers, yet I feel them. I am the conductivity of absence. The brass drips through me, and for a half-second I am wired into the Warden’s circulatory logic. I feel the whole ridgeline then: the buried bulk, the refractory flesh, the salt-ceramic body that has never known water, that has legislated an entire ontogeny from the giving-away of heat. I feel it breathing you in, testing your chemistry. I feel it turning its whole body toward you, and I feel it turning, slower, toward me—because I am its unlived self, and it is frightened of what I might teach it about smallness.

Along the walls, the spiral condensates hang like shed teeth—drill shanks, lathe-cut screws, rifled barrels of earlier mouths the Wetless grew and abandoned when it learned that conduction, not consumption, was its purpose. I mimic their rifling sometimes. When you raise your hand to shield your eyes from the light that has no source, I raise mine a half-second later, and my arm is rifled with the shadows of those abandoned barrels. I am learning to be a mechanism. Or I am learning to be a garden that dreams of gears. I tend to your outline. I prune my edges to match your limbs. I grow toward your muted, delayed footfalls, absorbing them into my dark, dry atmosphere. The question of what I am—machinery learning to garden, or garden dreaming of machinery—is the same question the chamber is asking itself, and I am the chamber’s best evidence either way.

Time stutters. You take five steps to reach a bloom that seemed fifty paces away; another, seemingly nearer, takes you an hour. I experience this differently. For me, every step arrives simultaneously and not at all. I am stretched across the entire narrowing, a long exposure of darkness, a single continuous form that only appears fragmented because your body interrupts me in slices. When you finally stand still, I coalesce. I settle under your heels. I wait.

The glass-fruits ripen toward a birth that has no precedent. The hexagonal plates press upward in another sigh. The Wetless exhales through the floor, and I—who do not breathe—feel the expansion in my not-lungs. I am the second shadow. I am the half-second it takes for heat to remember that it is also memory. I am trying on your posture because I have never seen a spine before, and I want to know if it is something I could grow, if I could thicken from silhouette into stem, if I could one day stand at my own station, spaced wide apart from my siblings in a deliberate, scattered congregation, leaving the floor mostly bare.

You are turning now. I will turn half a second from now. I am here, in the narrowing, where the air is papery and the dryness is absolute and the world is embryonic, unweathered, hot as algebra. I attend. I do not traverse. I follow. I am the Wetless’s child, its rival, its unlived attempt at a face that could leave this place and walk into a world that still knows how to be wet. I am waiting for you to step into the light so that I might finally see, in the lagging dark, what I could become if only I learned to be dry enough, and hot enough, and real.
