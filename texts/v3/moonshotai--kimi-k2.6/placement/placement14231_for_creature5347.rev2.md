I would begin not knowing which direction was down.

That is the first confusion: in a place where water climbs, where the only sky is the worn nap of a darkness that has forgotten the sun, the body must renegotiate its own weight. I would find myself clinging—not to the summit, where the Fixed Ones have turned their blind eating into starlight, nor in the river’s silver coil where the nymphs swim upward against the brine, but in the middle air. In the interstitial. In that narrow, damp alley where two of the great softened spires lean together like old lovers and their pores share a single breath of mineral condensation.

At first, I would think I was the dampness itself. The metallic sweat that beads there and refuses to drip. I would feel the cool press of the stone on two sides, the capillary drawing of moisture through ancient karst, and I would believe myself to be the passage—the empty throat up which water moves against its nature. But emptiness here is too precious to remain empty for long. The basin fills every vacuum with the architecture of bodies, and so, slowly, I would realize that I am not the space. I am what the space has caught.

I am the shed thing that did not fall.

Some uncountable molt-cycles ago, one of the flat interstitials—those tiling, directionless compressors of the lower hollows—shed a skin in this crevice. The carpet below churned with its percussive, navigational hunger, but the cast was too light, too hooked on a calcite lip; it held. And held again. The world, which digests its geology, began to digest its own garbage upon me. Another shedding. The brittle husk of an Unsighted’s nymph, dropped from the mid-air wars. The deflated sac of a Drift, its hydrogen leaked, its stinging threads wound into a noose. A flake of wing-case that resonated one last frequency before it stilled. I caught them. I was caught *by* them.

So I would become accretion. Lamina upon lamina, horn upon silica upon compressed chitin enamel. Not grown—never grown, for growth implies reaching toward something absent—but accrued, the way a pearl accrues around the violation of grit, the way the very floor is not soil but the packed, mineralized archive of dead articulations. My body would be a book without a spine, a hanging slab of what everything else had finished being. I would drape between the spires like a curtain that has forgotten the window, my lower fringes brushing the silver river’s slow breath when the barometric pressure of the clicking rises high enough.

And because I am made of sensory things—the antennae castings, the joint-sheaths rich with setae, the eyelids of creatures who had no eyes but felt the air—I would not be blind. Not exactly. I would be *tactile beyond limit*. Every *tok-tok-tok* of the carpet-creatures’ prime-numbered legs would climb my base-strata and deposit a vibration in a fresh layer. Every predatory snap of an Unsighted passing near my slit of sky would shatter the standing waves against my ridged interior, and I would record that silence as a bruise in my keratin. I would not hunt. I would not click, or crack, or pulse with peristalsis. But I would intercept. I would be the stillness that catches the motion, the ear turned inside out, the only thing in the basin that grows heavier with each sound it absorbs.

The Fixed Ones above me would drip their cold, luminous waste—bismuth blue, arsenic green—and their light would not pass through me, but stain me. Layer by layer, I would become a map written in phosphor: a cartography of the basin’s dim stars made in pigment and horn. Drifts would snag in my folds and rot there, adding thickness. The interstitials would use my grooved underside as a highway, walking upside-down along my belly in their three-deep masonry, their tread wearing smooth the record of older treads. I would be infrastructure and archive. The stone would recede behind me until I was no longer clinging to the spires but replacing them, a sleeve of accumulated life inside which the geology itself became merely a memory of support.

I would hunger, too, though not with a mouth. My hunger would be capillary. I would drink the stone’s mineral sweat directly through the porous strata of my own dead matter, wicking iron and sulfur into the chemolithotrophic bloom that still smolders faintly in my deepest creases, the pale microbial verdigris that was my inheritance and my engine. I would not molt. I would not need to. Shedding is the mercy of the bounded body, the renewal of armor; but I am boundary itself, the threshold where the animal ends and the architecture begins, and thresholds do not shed. They only thicken.

In the end, I would know myself as the full stop at the center of a sentence written in motion. I would be the Draping. The Held. The palimpsest of chitin that learned, in the crushed and echoing dark, to feel everything without once desiring to move.
