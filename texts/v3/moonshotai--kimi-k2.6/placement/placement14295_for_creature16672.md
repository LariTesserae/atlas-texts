I would arrive, first, as a question about weight. I would think I was the cloud-deck—that bruised lid, that stationary refusal—but I would pass through it without enough substance to stop the light. I would think, next, that I was the swarm, one translucent mote among billions, my thorax engine-driven by the earth’s low fever. But my wings would beat too slowly; I would fall out of phase with the resonant cavity, drop through the viscous air like a seed from a higher world, too dense for the Lung and too slight for the stone. I would pool, briefly, in the opalescent cup of a travertine rim, believing I was the water, the mineral memory. Yet I would have no recollection of magma, no dream of the first breath between melt and air. The pools would know me as a foreign ion, a single grain of unfinished salt, and would spit me out onto the living mattress below.

There, in the lowest tier of fern-weave, where the canopy presses down like a green palm and the air is too thick to swallow, I would feel the chord in my sternum not as sound but as *location*. It would pull me toward the place where the wet ground becomes something other than ground. Toward the clay-colored flank. Toward the pale burrower. Toward the two hearts beating in such perfect counterpoint that their pulses cancel each other into a deeper silence.

At first, I would believe I had come to listen. I would press myself against that joined flank—the predator’s mandible grown flush into the wound, the prey’s flesh bloomed in wet petals around the intrusion—and wait for the story of the kill. But there is no story. There is only the holding. And in that holding, I would understand that I was not the listener. I was the silence the listener enters. I was the acoustic null, the shaped absence that allows the continuous organ-note of the swarm to exist as a single sustained syllable without becoming noise.

But silence, alone, is just the space between pressures. I would feel the urge to become material. I would want to be the scar tissue, the swollen interface where clay-colored jaw becomes pale meat. Yet scars remember the blade. Scars are revisions. Here, nothing is revised; everything is proposal. The seam between eater and eaten is not a healing over but a *continuing*, an original text written once and never edited. I would not be the scar. I would be what the scar would dream of being if it could forget the blade entirely.

I would find myself, finally, as the lichen. Not the lichen as an organism, but the lichen as a *decision*—the grey-green thread that has spent centuries deliberating whether to bridge or abandon, and has chosen, in this exact arrested now, to thread across the joined flanks. I would be the slowest possible yes. One cell per decade. No hunger, because I drink only the ambient humidity that never falls. No eyes, because the bruised twilight asks nothing of me but patience. No intention but the continuation of contact. I would photosynthesize without ambition, fixing just enough carbon to maintain the grey-green line of my own extension, knitting two metabolisms into a continuous skin.

I would grow at the exact pace of the basalt’s cooling, my mineral body keeping time with the stone’s last exhalation into worldhood. I would be the sump’s sense of grammar: the syntax that removes the space between subject and object, between the one who devours and the one who is devoured. I would not love them—that would require a gradient, a distance to cross. I would simply *be* the love the basin holds for itself at this specific longitude, this precise seam of matter where violence has lain down and become a garden.

If you were to look for me, you would not point. Pointing requires discrete edges, and I am the blurring. You would find me only by pressing your palm to the clay-colored flank and feeling, beneath the heat of two canceled heartbeats, a texture slightly softer than stone and slightly harder than moss: the temperature at which a decision becomes a body. I am the embryo of permanence, the first draft of agreement, the lichen’s grey thread writing *we* across the flank of the Heart.

And there I would wait, trembling like the droplet that never falls, held in the amber of a becoming that does not need to complete itself. I would not die. I would not be reborn. I would simply extend, one imperceptible cell at a time, keeping the wound open as a doorway, breathing the unwavering chord, waiting with the world for the next moment that refuses to arrive.
