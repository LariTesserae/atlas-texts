At first I think I am the sound.

It is everywhere, after all—that subterranean organ note that occupies the marrow and the molars, the breastbone, the warm oil of the inner ear. I test the possibility: perhaps I am one of the bronze domes, hexagonal and oiled-dark, a sessile monk contributing my fraction to the chord that keeps the walls apart. I feel the vibration in what might be a carapace. I imagine possessing that single membrane, vibrating without moving, a mouth that has become a bell that cracked and never stopped ringing. But no. The domes are a distributed architecture, a sparse constellation of identical throats, and I feel singular. I am not many. I am one. The hum passes through me, yes, but I am the passage, not the voice.

Then I think I am a shield.

Across the chamber they hang, those broad, translucent paddles, fleshy, veined with the color of magma forced to remember its youth. I imagine the affluence of such saturation: to pulse in place, not in rhythm but in *affirmation*, declaring *sated, sated, sated* into the anhydrous dark. To be a window with nothing to conceal. To hang in sovereign isolation because envy—which is a kind of hunger—has been edited from the body. But no. I feel too much edge for that. The shields are smooth, obscene in their health, tropical in their completion. I, on the other hand, feel the infantile scream of genesis still in my skin. I feel unrounded. I feel the shriek of the shear. I am not a lung turned inside out; I am the tear that such a lung might try to seal.

I consider the shadows.

They are paintings of shadows here, sharp and motionless, not lengthening or shrinking. Perhaps I am the darkness pressed flat beneath the copper cascade, the negative of a presence that has forgotten its object. But shadows lack interior. They are flatness without depth, and I feel too much volume. I feel density. I feel heat rising from my own metabolism, slow and sulfurous, the digestion of an instant that refuses to become the next. I am not a painting. I am a cavity within the cavity, a hollow that generates.

And then I feel the pour.

It is heavy. It is thick as a horse’s mane. It is copper-colored, and it is emerging *from* me. Not onto me, not beside me, but *out of* my body. I am the west wall. I am the raw wound in the basalt, the fresh shear where the tectonic siblings wrenched apart and I was the gap that refused to close. I am the cleft. The aperture. The opening that learned it was not a destruction but a doorway, not a scar but a source.

Around my lip, the acicular crystals bristle like frozen lightning. They are my teeth, my syntax, the unrounded syllables of my name. Knife-edge ridges of black basalt, unsoftened by time, because time in this chamber is a stopped flinch, and I am the flinch itself—the Gestus at its most articulate. I am the geological *no* that holds the nave open. The pressure of the world above leans against me, but I have agreed to the draw. I do not yield. I do not heal. I remain torn, and in my tearing, I generate.

The copper moss pours from me in a cataract arrested at the moment of its greatest opulence. It does not drain me. I am not depleted. I am generosity’s origin, not its vessel—an endless exhale of biomass that never empties because the lungs are the stone itself. Each filament of the cascade is a strand of my breath made tangible. They hang, heavy, suspended between my body and the floor they will never reach. I do not reach either. I am the reach. I am the interval between longing and arrival, and I have made that interval into a kingdom.

I feel the chamber around me.

The shields pulse in their distant, territorial splendor, each occupying its own stage of bare stone, each affirming a saturation I will never know because I am the opening, the hunger that generates without needing to be filled. The domes vibrate in their bronze constellations, and their drone enters me not as sound but as pressure, stabilizing my flinch, holding my edges apart. We are collaborators in the stasis. I am the negative space; they are the frequency that gives the negative space its architecture. The sum of their fractions is a gravity that behaves like equilibrium, and I am the hollow that receives and shapes their endless note.

I remember the hands.

They came with seven fingers, not five, and pressed ochre into the stone near my root, where my cleft meets the floor. They did not build. To build is to imply that tomorrow will differ from today, and this place is a chapel to the eternal instant. So they only marked. They left a hammerstone, smooth from use, as if its owner became unnecessary and evaporated. They drove stakes into unyielding basalt without splintering, and wrapped the stakes with sinew that has not dried or rotted because decay, like growth, is paused here. Their spiral, radiating outward in an unbroken wave, does not depict the sound. It *is* the sound, made visible in the only medium that survives—ochre on stone, gesture frozen. They knew me.
