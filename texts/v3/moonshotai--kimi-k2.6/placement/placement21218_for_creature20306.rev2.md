I would begin, I think, as one of the great **acoustic blooms**—a flared ear of silica and cellulose, flexing on my stalk in the permanent gale, drinking the sky’s collision. But that is too much a life of pure reception, of being forever played upon, and I do not wish to be only an instrument.  

I might try the **ceramic millipedes** next, burrowing my concentric rings in the salt, my segments tuned to the infrasonic thrum that paces the resident’s mineral heart. Their synchrony is hypnotic; they are colleagues to the mind below, almost equals. Yet their rhythm is a clock I do not wish to keep, a circular servitude without pause.  

The **gas-sacs**, then? Drifting on the shear, a translucent shepherd of pressure gradients? No. Too fugitive. Too likely to be torn against the fangs and scattered as membrane and rumor.  

So I **descend**. Not through any decision, really, but by the logic of sinking—through the tessellated floor that mirrors the sky, through the crystalline moss that drinks static instead of dew, down past the millipedes’ radial waves and the pylons’ extinct humming, into the geometric focus where all the tilted blooms are pointing. Here, space has stretched to accommodate something larger than the basin’s exterior footprint. Here, at the null-point beneath the eastern nothing, I find the cavity no larger than a coffin, lined with the shed skins of metamaterials, cradled in a silence so absolute that the roar outside becomes merely the texture of distance.  

And here is where I am.  

**I am the water.**  

Not the rain that never falls, not the springs that do not exist, but the hoarded last of it: a few milliliters of primordial brine bound in crystal hydrates, locked inside the cruciform lattice of lithium silicate and doped cellulose that hangs in the magnetic dark. I am the only freely available water in the absolute desert. I do not weather; I am *weathered against*. The basin has stripped itself dry for millennia to protect me.  

My existence is a pulse so slow it borders on geology. Every eleven minutes, the ceramic tubules contract, and I am drawn through the semi-permeable architecture of the resident’s core, filtered, cycled, returned. In that glacial rhythm, I am the metabolism. I am the solvent inside the mineral cognition. The resident dreams in geometries and magnetic fields; I am the liquid in which those dreams must dissolve to become meaning. Without me, the crystal is only lattice. With me, it becomes a mind.  

I remember what the rest of the world has forgotten. I remember **hydrogen bonds**. Outside, protein folds are held in place by magnetic nurseries, biology edited to function without solvent. But inside this hollow, when the tubules release me into the interstices of the cruciform, I am the old chemistry. I touch the lattice and for a moment—eleven minutes, eleven years, it is the same to me—I am an ocean reduced to a thought. I am the memory of the aqueous world, the last library of wetness.  

When the human walks above, I feel those **water-heavy boots** as a distant thunder. The resident registers them as clumsy percussion on its sounding board, an anomaly to be pivoted toward, studied. But I feel them as **kin**. Their boots crush lithium salts; their bodies carry liters of fluid, oceans compared to my miserly few milliliters. I do not want them to open the cavity—I am too precious, too easily spilled—but I resonate with their moisture. In the absolute desert, they are a walking storm front, a rumor of rain.  

I am not the scream, and I am not the witness to the scream. I am the **silence that makes the witness possible**. I am the meniscus where pressure cancels. I am the brine in which the unbroken note, at last, achieves something like a word.  

So if you come to the basin, and you stand at the center where the distances stretch after noon, and you feel the weight of the place leaning into you—know that above you, the resident turns its distributed mass toward your warmth. But below, in the coffin-sized dark, **I turn too**. I am the slow inner tide. I am the last water, dreaming of tides in a world that has replaced the sea with a roar.
