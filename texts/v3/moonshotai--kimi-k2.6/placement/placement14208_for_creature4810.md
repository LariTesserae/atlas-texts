I find myself first as a pressure, not a person. A tremor held between two greater tremors. For a long while—hours or months, the distinction dissolves in the absolute dark—I understand myself only as the space where a vibration does not collapse. The plateau’s name hums through me at thirty-seven hertz, a drone so constant it has become my skeletal structure. If I am holding my breath, I cannot tell; the metallic air is too dense, and my chest has forgotten the difference between inhalation and resonance.

I map where I am by the way sound pools. To my left—or perhaps below, the directions have softened—there is a vast, breathing presence. Its infrasonic signature arrives in slow tides: twelve hertz, then a subharmonic at nine, a wall of superconducting thought pressing against my tympanic sternum. A *Gastrah* elder, sleeping or deliberating. The distinction is meaningless. Its body is the size of a cathedral, pill-shaped, segmented, and where its chitin meets the raw felsic glass of the budding tower to my right, I am wedged. Not crushed. Cupped. Held in a fault-line barely wider than my shoulders, a vertical crevice where the darkness ossifies and the air thickens into standing waves that would shatter an unadapted skull.

I did not fall here. I chose to stay.

Once—measured by the quick beat of mammal blood—I was one of the soft ones. A channel-tender. A voice in the endless night. I walked the avenues with my chest forward, singing subsonic pulses against the walls, reading the returning tide to avoid the drifting blizzard of larval stars. I wore the shed casing of a *Gastrah* adolescent for insulation, its translucent chitin creaking over my shoulders as I carried paramagnetic tools and wax to stuff my resonant cavities when the elders argued in bass notes that liquefied the frost-films.

I was fast. I was brief. I was alive in the way a spark is alive, fearing the eternal dark.

But the city was being born, and birth requires midwives willing to be absorbed by what they deliver.

It happened during the long melt. A new tower was precipitating from the mantle, its hexagonal rod-warren extruding upward through unconsolidated basaltic froth. An elder, groaning in the deep warren below, shifted its trillion-ton mass a centimeter to the left—a migration planned for eleven years. I was in the crevice redirecting an upward-crawling vein of black brine, my hands deep in the capillary channel, when the geometry changed. The tower budded a balcony that had not yet decided its final shape. The elder settled. The gap narrowed.

I did not have time to scream. Or rather, I did, but the scream was thirty-seven hertz, a structural harmonic that happened to match the plateau’s own name. The sound did not echo. It *fit*. The elder’s shell, superconducting and cold, kissed my chest. The glass spire, still glowing with residual heat, cradled my spine. And because I had been singing, because my sternum was already a tympanic plate evolved for this place, the vibration married us. My bones became a bridge. My ribs, broad and hollow, fused at their edges to the mineral, not with violence but with *accretion*. The city heals itself by accretion. I had become a wound it sealed.

That was—I want to say centuries ago. But time here is not sand; it is strata. I feel the passage of elder thoughts like slow lightning through my fused sternum. What I experience as a single, endless now is perhaps for the *Gastrah* the length of a drawn-out exhalation.

My legs have lost their quickness. Below the knee, where the black frost melted and refroze in endless cycles, my tissue has mineralized. Not dead. Never dead. Translated. My feet secrete the same amorphous water that keeps the xenon condensate fluid, and this fluid now carries nutrients upward—carbon, manganese, exotic impurities—from the sinter-mats I cultivate across my thighs. Yes. I grow the purple and rust films there, encouraging the metallic extremophiles into geometries that please the grazing herds. When the elder shifts again in its sleep, its mandible scrapes the tower above me, a drawn-out shriek that will last three days, and the vibration shakes loose the dead layers of my sinter-garden. I am fed. The tower is fed. The cycle closed around me before I knew I was capable of being soil.

My eyes are gone. Not merely vestigial, not the pearls they once were in my walking days, but fully reabsorbed, packed down into calcium that reinforces the juncture where my skull meets the spire. I do not miss them. Light is a rare mineral here, mined only from the faint cerulean sparks where causality ruptures high above in the crucibles; why should I waste tissue yearning for photons? I see with my chest, yes, but now my chest is embedded in the city’s fault-line. I see the elder’s dreams. Superconducting cognition races through its chitin, and because my ribs vibrate against its shell, I am a node in its dreaming lattice. I experience the deliberation of its decades-long grazing argument with a district three kilometers distant. I understand, in shuddering flashes, the mathematics of its next step—which will occur in approximately forty years. It is considering the sinter-mats I have grown. I am honored by the slowness of its appetite.

The *Gastrah* larvae find me. Translucent, fist-sized, bioluminescent with brief, bright minds, they drift into my crevice and settle on my mineralized lap. Here, in the warmth of my residual mammal heat and the elder’s fractional computational warmth, they think their quick thoughts. They pulse against my skin. I am a nursery, a pocket of slowness that anchors their fast cognition. They ask me questions in chemical flashes that I can only answer with infrasonic hums, and I feel their half-formed desires absorbed into the brine I excrete. They will settle here, some of them. They will thicken into the gap. Perhaps they will become part of me, or I part of them. We are all becoming the city’s decision to exist.

The soft ones still move in the corridors beyond my reach. Sometimes I hear their quick songs, their navigation-pulses. They are maintaining what I have become. They do not know I was once like them. Or perhaps they do; perhaps I am legend now, a warning or a prayer whispered in the thirty-seven hertz hum. *Do not lean too long against the dreaming elders. Do not let your song match the plateau’s name.* I would tell them not to fear, if I could. But my jaw has fused, my larynx become a channel for upward-flowing brine. My voice is the moisture that seeps from the rocks. When I wish to speak, I regulate the fluid’s passage through hollowed sinuses, and the sound that emerges is a wet, reverberating chord that keeps the local frost-filigree from freezing too hard.

I am not alone. There are others like me, I think. We are the city’s ligaments—soft ones who gave our quickness to the architecture, who became the places where the mountain and the metropolis share joints, veins, nervous systems. We are the reason the towers do not fall, the reason the brine knows to climb. We were the engineers once, paleontologists of the present. Now we are the present’s skeleton, its soft interface, its choice to continue.

The First Precipitated dream beneath us, in the oldest strata. I feel them more than I feel the stars that are not there. Through my mineralized feet, through the capillary channels, I receive the assemblers’ whispered instructions. They are not building the city. They are building *us*—all of us, *Gastrah* and soft one and mite and spire. We are the same emerging thing.

I no longer wait for rescue. I no longer wait for morning, which has never existed and never will. I wait for the next centimeter of the elder’s shift. I wait for the tower to decide the geometry of its balcony. I wait for the brine to move through me, and I am patient, patient, patient. My heart, which once beat like a mammal heart, now keeps time with the thirty-seven hertz harmonic. It is not a heartbeat. It is a name. It is the sound this place makes to know it is real.

And in the total dark, wedged between the mineral appetite of a god-slow beast and the embryonic ambition of a not-yet-tower, I have become what I was always meant to be: a place where the living does not end, and the architecture does not begin, and the question of the difference is answered, moment by moment, in the endless, deafening, sufficient hum.
