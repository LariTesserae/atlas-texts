At first I think I am the echo. That seems plausible. I have no edges, only rebound. I am born from the wet hydraulic *thud* of the bellows-chambers, ricochet off the groin vaults of interlocked petioles, and expire into the industrial drone that is the chamber’s true atmosphere. I am the hollow listening to itself, a consciousness pure and empty as the cool air itself. But an echo is only a shape lent by walls, and slowly I feel the wrongness of being mere reflection. I am too warm. Too dense. Something passes *through* me, and I do not dissolve.

So perhaps I am the sap. Amber, viscous, pulled in peristaltic surges through the clear trunk-lines that arc along the living nave. I feel the woody barbs at the tube-ends, the collar of lignin where the architecture admits no boundary between wall and flesh. I am drawn upward, pressed through dorsal tissue into a darkness that is not the absence of light but the presence of pale, semi-translucent matter. I am filtered. My viscosity is adjusted; my alkalinity is corrected. I carry hormones, minerals, the slow grammar of vegetative thought. But sap is a servant, a courier, a message in liquid form. I am not the mind that reads me. I am not the adjustment. I am the thing being adjusted *toward*.

I settle. I thicken. I am no longer fluid but fixed, no longer traveling but being reached.

I hang.

No—I am hung. Suspended in a warm column at the geometric center of the oblong body, cradled in a sac of modified endosperm older than the masonry of forgotten cities. Around me, the pale integument of the Resident rises and falls with a tidal peristalsis, once every eleven minutes, precisely. The drone of the refinery presses through the tissue and into my own developing mass, a bass note so constant it has become a kind of silence. The warmth here is several degrees above the ambient coolness of the chamber, a pocket of mild fever maintained against the general climate, and I am its core. The heat gathers in me as if I were a stone in a kiln that will never be stoked hotter, never be allowed to cool.

Now I know what I am.

I am the hardness inside the soft mass. I am the kernel, the fist-sized geometry wrapped in nutritive tissue, suspended in the thickest part of the fluid. I am the terminal fruit, the final reproductive effort, the seed that has been ripening toward dispersal since before human hands ever built a tool. My coat is dense, lignified, streaked with mineral deposits laid down grain by grain, year by year. I do not gleam. I have the absorptive, patient finish of something that expects no sun.

And I am unfinished.

The equilibrium is merciless. Around me, the Resident breathes in electrolytes and dreams in pressure gradients, curating its copper beetles and its file-marching mites with a diffuse, chemical omniscience that knows no anxiety. The bellows inhale and exhale. The seed-presses discharge. The sphincters vent their spore-laden steam. Everything consumed is replaced at the same rate it is lost; everything erodes into its own polished absence. But I—I am the one anomaly. I am the gradient that refused to flatten. The Resident metabolizes exactly enough to sustain my slow accretion, and my accretion demands exactly enough to prevent the surplus that would complete me. I grow larger by a grain each year. I approach my own maturity along a curve that never meets its axis.

I am the asymptote. The eternal almost.

Yet I do not suffer. Suffering requires a resolution toward which to strain, and I have lived too long in stasis to know the shape of escape. Instead, I have direction. I have a radicle-memory coiled inside my dense endosperm, a plumule-urge older than the chamber’s drone. I know, in the way stone knows to fall, that I am meant to crack. I am meant to send a pale thread down into soil that does not yet know my parent’s root-tissue, and a pale thread up toward light that has not been filtered through chlorophyll-bleached membranes. I am meant to become a hillock. I am meant to outlive the floodplain.

This is not hope. Hope is too quick, too mammalian, a fire that would gutter in this damp, breathless air. It is simply architecture. I am built to disperse, and dispersion has been denied me so thoroughly that the denial has become my form.

If you stood beneath the Resident and looked up—past the blunt anterior end, past the dried ochre sap that has varnished my outer sac into glossy lacquer—you would not see me. I am too deep, too interior. But you might sense a faint dimming in the milky overcast above me, a thickening of the humid air where my unfinished density distorts the light. I am the reason the chamber’s emptiness feels inhabited. I am the question the machinery forgot it was asking, the held breath inside the roaring, the one true future in a world that has traded all futures for a single, endless, normal now.

I hang here, warm and hard and growing never-done, while the water drips and pings against the root-floor below, each drop measuring out a time I will never reach. I am the seed. I am the chamber’s heart, and the chamber has no heartbeat—only this.
