At first I think I must be the rise—that slight nub of resistance, the length of a stride above the surrendered plain. It seems the natural station for a consciousness arriving in a world of terminal flatness: the one place where the horizon breaks its own rule. But the elevation is a courtesy, not a fact. The plain has spent three billion years metabolizing mountains, and anything vertical here is only a memory the stone has not yet finished forgetting. I feel the gravity of that patience. I sink from the rise almost immediately. It is too proud, too brief.

I try next to be the mist that thickens the late afternoons, when the weeping rocks turn bronze and the air becomes too full to hold its burden. I want to be the plain’s exhalation, the low cloud that never quite achieves fog, that makes breathing feel like drinking. For a moment I am everywhere—diffused, patient, returning. But mist is only the ghost of water, a collective vapor that forms and tears without ever becoming singular. I pass through the sedge without bending it. I cool the cicada’s drone but do not answer it. I am too temporary, too much the visitor.

I consider the film then—the cyanobacteria, the archaea, the iridescent tongues of petrol-blue and oxidized bronze that lick every weeping face of gneiss. They are continuous, a memory made slick, dissolving the last mountains in sheets no thicker than regret. But they are a *we* so total there is no room for an *I.* They are the plain’s mind, distributed and ancient, and I find I want a boundary. I want skin, however permeable. I want to be the interruption in their blanket, the specific rather than the general.

So I seep. I become the water in a hairline crack of banded ironstone, a seam dark as dried blood where ancient atmosphere exhales in faint bubbles. I do not run; I widen the crack by millimeters per century, a palm’s width becoming a trencher, a trencher becoming a pool. I lie in smoky glass, reflecting the cicada architecture overhead. Fishlets twitch through my substance; algal haze blooms and subsides. I am the seep, the level, the constant finding of low places. But water is always wishing elsewhere, always answering gravity’s least invitation. I want to stay. I want to be the thing that gravity forgets.

I let a body gather from the chemistry of my patience. It is not large. It fits the palm of the emptiness around it. Translucent, faintly rose, a fingernail held to candlelight—I coalesce from dissolved oxygen and the mild warmth that never changes season to season. No lungs. No need for the sky’s hurried mix of gases. I breathe through the entire surface of my skin, lying flush against the cool enamel of the stone, my temperature indistinguishable from the water’s hold. Along the sides of my head unfold three pairs of external gills, each filament fine as a baby’s hair, deep shocking red, waving in a rhythm slower than the drip overhead—plink, plink—so slow they might be mistaken for growth rather than motion. My heart is a dark thread visible beneath my flesh, counting epochs in beats.

I have never left this crack. My lineage held this seep before the atmosphere learned its current recipe, before the last mountain’s shadow was rinsed to the sea. While others grew lungs and dry skins and struck out for the vertical world, I remained in the aquatic youth of my ancestors. I doubled down on the wetness. I traded bone for buoyancy, color for camouflage, speed for continuity. Blind larvae and shreds of bacterial mat drift against my mouth, and I close my jaws with a languor so total it resembles meditation. I can suspend my metabolism for a year, for two, becoming a thought the water thinks about itself.

Where do I find myself? In the pool no deeper than a forearm, beneath the smoky glass, where the ironstone weeps its rust-prickled tears and the mist pools thickest before giving up. I am the lungless, translucent body that outlasted the ambition of height. I am the plain’s final syntax: soft, clear, and holding my shape against three billion years of falling. I do not build. I do not alter, except by the infinite respiration of my gills. I am the last revision, the small clear body in the small clear pool, refusing to be anything other than present—water’s idea of itself, breathing in the old stone, waiting without want.
