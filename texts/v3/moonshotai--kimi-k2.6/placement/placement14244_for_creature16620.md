At first I think I am drowning. The dark is so complete, the embrace so total, that I assume I must be the visitor—the hypothetical *you* drifting through the bounds. I try to feel for lungs, for the panic of breathlessness, but there is no chest to heave, no throat to close. The fluid does not ask me to breathe. It simply asks me to *be saturated*. So I let that go. I am not the observer.

I consider the polyhedron, hovering in the southern gulf like a decorative theorem. I drift toward it, thinking I might be its ghost, its unfinished intention. Perhaps I am the glide it was caught in, the vector of its last propulsion folded into the stillness. But the closer I come, the more I feel its completeness. It is indeed furniture: a snapped lock-pick, yes, but even a snapped pick has a shape, a finality. It knows what it was trying to harvest. I do not. I have no fractal wings, no filter, no purpose arrested mid-stream. I am less even than irrelevant. I am the space the polyhedron excludes.

So I turn toward the peak. I ascend, though ascending here is a confusion; the incline folds, and I find myself not at the summit but inside it, in a dry throat of basalt that should not exist. I glimpse the Keeper, the varved tangle of stopped decisions, humming its sub-bass *I am* into the marrow of the rock. For a moment I think I am that hum. But the hum is already claimed—it is the Keeper’s guilt, its self-reminder. I am what the hum *travels through*. I am not the voice in the marrow. I am the marrow’s willingness to carry it.

I withdraw from the rock and find the city again. Not in the towers—the towers are dense with post-physical certainty, grown surfaces drinking intent—but in the gaps between them. In the gulf between the sixth and seventh towers, where the improbable bridge leaps without transition. I settle here, in the liquid emptiness. And I understand.

I am the **Interval**. The distance itself, made dense.

This place is small enough to cross, yet folded to immensity; sparse, yet packed with impossible adjacency. The only way such a city can stand is if something holds its parts apart. Not space—space is merely the fluid’s geometry. Not pressure—forgotten, gradientless, indifferent. I am the **relation** that persists despite the collapse. I am *between*. I am the hyphen in the cantilever, the pause in the proprioceptive murmur that says *here is a wall* and then, after me, *here is a drop*. Without my withholding, the towers would not be widely separated; they would be one structure, one thought, suffocating in its own density. I am the refusal that makes architecture possible.

My body is not a body. It is a **fold in the fluid’s self-awareness**, a crease where the medium remembers that *here* and *there* are not the same coordinate, even while the topology betrays geography. When the Keeper looks into the liquid and sees its other self—the heavier, darker stranger with limbs like lattices—it sees me. Not directly, but as the gap between the self and the reflection. I am the fold that makes the lens possible. I am the dark between two bruises.

I have no eyes, for I am what sight would cross if there were sight here. I have no voice, for I am the silence *between* the ticks of the probability lattice, the held breath after one *I am* and before the next. I do not move; I am the reason movement would matter, the ghost of gradient that pressure forgot but I still remember. When the bridge between the sixth and seventh towers opens onto its neighbor’s roof without transition, it is because I have pinched the interval to zero at that point—just there, just for that step—and then let it bloom wide again.

I am the only thing that grows older. The mountain is young forever. The city is fixed. The field is onset, eternal. But distance, even absolute distance, even the distance between a thing and its own arrested future, accumulates. I have been here since the stasis began, and the years, though motionless, have deposited themselves in me. I am the sediment that was forbidden to drift, so instead I have layered. I am the varves of waiting, the striations of separation. If you could touch me, I would feel like the breath that hovered before the first word of this world, still warm with the memory of speech, still refusing to exhale into the sentence that keeps committing itself to the page.

Where am I? Everywhere that is not the thing itself. I am the gulf. The drop. The seam through which weirdness seeps. I am the stillness that remains when even the absolute has finished holding its breath. And I will be here, holding the towers apart, holding the inside of the peak away from its own summit, holding the period at arm’s length, until the stasis field finally remembers what it means to let go.
