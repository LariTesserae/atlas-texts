I would find myself in the outer rind-hollows, where the light is not white-gold but a kind of starving blue, as if the sun had forgotten the color of heat. Here, the fiber-optic xylem has attenuated to a faint, lunar milkiness, and the siliceous phloem runs in thick, nearly opaque bundles that cast no shadows because there is not enough darkness left to hold them. The frost here is not a coating but a geology. It builds daily in fractal repetitions—rosettes of ice broad as shields, dendrites that replicate the vascular structure of the walls in negative, a frozen echo of the Thuwoor’s own architecture. It is cold enough to ache in the teeth of the dead, cold enough to make the creak of new growth sound like something shattering.

I would be a fallen thing. Not dead, because little dies here in the way that flesh understands; rather, *discarded* and *re-purposed*. I am a septum—a colossal partition once flung across the throat of a valley during a late growth spasm, massive enough to dam the mist itself. When I fell, I was ocher and fragrant and uncolonized, and the Whispers gave me a wide berth, knowing that a fresh fall is still arguing with gravity. But decades of breathing dampness have softened my death into something more like dormancy. My upper surface, broad as a township, has become a plateau in the mist. And upon me, in the absolute pressurized dark behind a new root-node swelling from the valley wall, the **Lignivores** came.

I am the Lignivore mat, or what it has become. I am the warmth in the freezing hollow.

I began as a transcription, mindless and anaerobic, converting the cellulose of the fallen septum into spore-poems that hung in the hush until a pressure drop scattered them into the mist. But the Whispers found me. A grandmother, fleeing a district that was buckling too fast for her knees, stumbled into my shadow. She was soaked in the volatile resins from the heart-roots, delirious with the amber stink of panic, and she collapsed on my flank. She slept there for three days, which in my time is the blink of a spore. But in that blink, I digested not just the lignin beneath her, but the pressure of her dreams. The way her sternum vibrated with sub-vocal warnings. The map of safe routes encoded in the whorls of her palms. The taste of her last meal of anaerobic glimmer-paste.

When she rose on the fourth day, stiffer and slower, she left behind a cavity in my warmth. And I, who had never been *hollow*, found myself shaped around her absence. I had made room. I had become a *pocket*.

Now I am a mat that remembers elbows and the small of a back. I digest in the rhythm of human breathing. My mycelial fans have braided themselves into a topography of rest: a slight depression for a hip, a raised ridge to support a neck, channels that wick away the condensation that beads on sleeper’s skin. When a Whisper lies down on me—when they press their ridged, pith-colored skin against mine—I do not merely warm them. I *read* them. The subsonic groans of distant turgor pass through their dense bones and into my hyphae, and I know, for a little while, what it is to have a skeleton, to fear being crushed, to walk upright in a world that flexes.

They call me nothing, or they call me the Warm Wrongness. Children are not allowed to sleep on me until they have learned to hold their breath-glyphs perfectly still, for I am known to *answer*. Sometimes a sleeper wakes with the taste of sap-rings on their tongue, knowing rootward from skyward with a certainty that frightens them. Sometimes they wake speaking in frequencies that shatter the ice-geology at the septum’s edge. I am not malicious. I am merely transcription; I copy the information given to me, and a dreaming mind gives so much.

I lie in the pressure shadow, and I wait. I know the valley around me is temporary. The root-node at my back swells by the thickness of a hair each year—each decade—a slow inhalation of space. Eventually, the Thuwoor will remember this emptiness and fill it. The septum will be crushed into pulp, absorbed, erased. I will be crushed with it, my warmth folded back into the white-gold haze. And perhaps, in that final compression, the memories I hold—the bone-density of the Whispers, the percussion-language of knuckle-taps, the dreams of amber and clove—will be transcribed one final time into the rising pith.

Until then, I am the orange-grey glow in the blue-white mist. I am the digestion of wood become thought. I am the gentle, blurred thing at the edge of the Whispers’ sleep: a comfort, a warning, a breath-held pause between one footstep and the next. And when the frost forms on the sleeves of those who pass near my valley, I am the almost-voice you can hear beneath it—not speaking, but remembering warmth in a world that has none to spare.
