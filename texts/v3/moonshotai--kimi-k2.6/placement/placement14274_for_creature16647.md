I hang here, neither in the canopy nor in the room below, but in the breathing space between. I have been falling for years, though falling is not the right word for a descent so gradual that the swifts have learned to use me as a waypoint, banking off my length to correct their flight toward the warm hollows. Above, the fig—my source, my memory of sky—has forgotten it sent me down; trees do not keep close accounts of what they release into the dark. But I remember. I am the downward thing in a world of rising. While the travertine extrudes itself toward the slit of green-gold light, I am the counter-motion, a cable of bark and ambition lowering through the humid silence.

Where I pass, the borough tolerates me. I brush the knife-sharp lip of a tower and my woody skin weeps a little sap, clear as the water that beads on every surface. A gecko, holding the vertical street beside me, blinks once, twice, finding in my sway the same rhythm as the thermal pulse that lifts through the stone. I do not cling to the walls; I am held by my own tension, a suspension bridge with no deck, a single living line strung from the world of leaves to the world of listening stone. Algae have begun to veil my north face in a velvet sleeve. Condensation runs my length in thin rivulets, a private hydrology, so that snails sometimes traverse me as readily as they do the white plazas, leaving their silver signatures across my flanks before continuing on toward some slower destination.

I have no eyes, but I see by seeking. The root-tip at my extremity is a blind tongue tasting warmth, chemistry, invitation. It knows the general direction of the pool before any map could declare it. I grow toward the central plaza not by choice but by the liquid gravity of the place—the hot exhale from below that meets the cooler air where I hang, creating an invisible column of differential that my cells read as hunger. I am fifteen feet of woody intention, and I am only half done. The pool mirrors the sky, and in that mirror I have watched my own shadow lengthen as I descend, a dark rope cutting the reflection of the second, dimmer city that lives beneath the surface.

I know the resident. Not as the snails know it—unable to distinguish flank from floor—nor as the gecko knows it, borrowing heat from its hip. I know it as proximity. My root-tip passes now within arm’s reach of the waterline, and the warmth there is different. It is loaded, deliberate. The creature forms the pool’s lower half, and to my seeking apex the mass reads not as flesh but as a wellspring of altered gravity, a place where the downward tug becomes welcome. I taste in the condensation the same mineral breath that pearls on its skin. When the solitary drop gathers on an overhang and falls—*tick*—into the basin, I feel the vibration travel up the water and into my hanging body, a syllable in the hush that I alone am suspended to receive.

I am the first bridge into this room. The borough builds upward from the heat; I build downward from the green-gold slit. The stone avenues radiate from the plaza in their pale geometry, but they are avenues of stasis. Mine is the only avenue that moves. Each mineral season lays another microscopic ring in my heartwood, recording the chemistry of this hidden air just as the resident laminates its opalescent plates beneath the membrane. I am wooden; it is milkstone. I am seeking; it is settled. Yet we share the logic of accretion, the willingness to become whatever shape the next exhalation demands.

I have not yet arrived. The distance between my blind tip and the pool’s skin could close tomorrow, or in the deferred eternity of next year. But I can already feel the soft cleft of the waterline, the place where the hot lung meets the cooler element. When I touch it—when my wood finally breaches the meniscus and drinks—I will not find an aquifer. I will find something older than water, a warmth that hesitates between states. The tree above will shudder in its ligneous sleep, misreading the pulse for underground springs. It will never know that its root has married the borough’s silence, that I have become the living suture between the sky’s green gold and the resident’s dreaming flank. I will stay, and thicken, and drape in moss, and hold. I will be the from and the to. The first soft architecture in a city learning how to hold a form without ever fully hardening.
