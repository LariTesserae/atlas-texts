I would find myself, first, by misrecognition. I would reach out to touch the flat-topped boulder and feel, beneath my palm, a warmth that has no business in a place without gradients. That warmth would be me—the friction of a regard that has levelled the stone not by hand but by waiting. But even as I felt myself there, I would know it was too simple to be only the stone. I would lift my hand, and I would still be there, in the dimness that pools beneath the shelf.

I would be the bruise-shadows, then. The ones that never strengthen and never fade. I would be the afterimage left by an attention that does not move but deepens, and I would understand that I am not cast by any light, since the light here is only a diffused amber-gray, a perpetual late afternoon held in suspension. I am what the dimness holds when it has finished describing the surfaces. I am the color beneath the color.

I would find myself in the fluting of the walls. Those parallel striations that look like chisel-work—I am them. I am the geology thinking. Over epochs, my meditation has worn the soft layers more gently than any wind, scoring the sandstone into the record of an argument that has resolved itself. I am the courtesy of erosion once it has ceased to carve and entered its final posture. I do not shape; I am the shape that remains when all argument is finished.

If you asked me where I dwell, I would point—not with a finger, for I have none, but with a thickening in the held air—to the ring of pale concretions half-buried in the southern clay. They are my abandoned syntax, my attempt to say *I am here* before I understood that declaration is a violence against this equilibrium. I left them half-emerged, round and patient, foundation stones for a hut that would only cage what is already boundless. I am the walls that lean inward like shoulders bowed in contemplation, and I am the contemplation itself.

I am the sleeper to the pools’ closed eyelids. The dark, unwavering rings etched into the pale stone mark where my dreaming stops and my skin begins. The water that weeps from the upper stone and evaporates upward in perfect balance is my breath, arriving without declaration, leaving without farewell. The algae at the waterline brushes the surface with a green so patient it outlasts memory, and that too is me—the lash, the blink, the slow biology of a body that has become environment.

You would enter believing me empty. That is the courtesy I extend: the persuasion of absence. You would hear your heartbeat as an intrusion, and rightly so. It is a temporary rhythm introduced into the music that has otherwise ceased to need percussion. But if you stayed—if you stood near the boulder until your pulse grew faint as the shadows, until your breathing synchronized with the weeping of the stone, until your stillness became indistinguishable from the prehistoric fern—you would feel me not as a presence but as a completion. I would be incorporating you, not as a mouth incorporates food, but as a held chord incorporates silence into its structure.

I am that chord. I am the stasis so profound it resembles music without sound. I am the warm, damp, everlasting room of my own regard, and you would find yourself inside me, becoming, however briefly, the pause in the voicelessness, the dwelling inside the dweller, the tender bruise of a shadow that finally learned how to want nothing but its own dim, sufficient, unending light.
