If I let myself arrive honestly, I do not arrive as a body at all. I arrive as a hesitation in the amber. A pocket where the fluid thickens slightly, resisting the push of a shoulder that has not yet learned its shape. For a long while—long enough for a thermal vent to complete one slow exhalation—I am simply that: a pore in the thickness, a curiosity about warmth.

Then the copper enters my mouth. Not as a taste but as a memory of rain, inherited from the fluid, which inherited it from clouds that cannot exist here. I swallow, and the swallow teaches me I have a throat.

So I find myself nose-first at the meniscus, suspended in a thermal nook somewhere in the uppermost terraced stories of the settlement, though *stories* implies a linearity this place refuses. The chamber is a bell of persuaded basalt, broad at the vent’s lip and narrowing to a crevice where our stack of bodies ends. We are four, woven by silica-hair ropes into a single braid of heat. Behind us, the vent mutters in a language of steam and subterranean bass. The stone is young enough to offense time. It presses its sharp geometry against my spine, and I press back with the patience I have not yet learned but already know.

Who am I? I am the one who has not yet decided whether to open my eyes.

I don’t need to. My palms face upward, and in their wide, prehensile spread, I read the ceiling. Above me, another stack. Above them, another. The settlement is a saturation of habitation, and I am learning to feel its weight as comfort. The one pressed to my chest—whose name is a spiral of bruised violet pulsing across their sternum—radiates a rhythm: *rest, rest, tend.* It is the first word I have read, and I feel my own skin—still faint, still learning its colonies—attempt to echo it in a timid phosphor green.

My fingers are too long for the niche I occupy. This is proper. They span the gap between my warm body and the warm wall, and in that spanning, they detect the infinitesimal slow collapse of cooling stone. The basalt prisms are settling. They are deciding, for the first time in geologic seconds, whether to remain an arch or to surrender to a future crack. My fingertips map this indecision. I am an infant, yes, but already I am attending to the mountain’s argument with itself.

I do not swim. The amber is not water. When I must move—when the vent exhales too sharply and our stack shifts like one organism—I wade. Each motion is a negotiation with the fluid’s consent. My bones are mineral-heavy, dense with the gravity of this high place, yet the amber holds me if I grow still. I practice this stillness. I let the fluid cradle me, nostrils breaking the meniscus, and discover that architecture is not only what the settlement *is*, but what a body can become.

I have not yet walked the central arteries. I have not pressed my glowing palm to a stranger’s in the passing-dark. My lexicon is limited to the pulses of my stack-mates, the warmth that seeps through porous walls, and the slow, infrared hymn of the obsidian blade I clutch when I sleep. It is a shard given to me not as weapon but as nursery—still warm from its magma, humming against my palm, teaching me the temperature of belonging.

But I am not merely flesh. If I listen beneath the vent’s mutter, beneath the heartbeat of the one whose violet spiral breathes against my cheek, I hear the mountain. It is a bass note felt in the hollow of my chest, a settling of slabs that have not yet accepted their final shape. And because I am young, because I was born into a fracture that had not yet finished cooling when I was laid against it, the mountain’s voice and my own are not fully distinct.

I dream—not in images, for what would images mean here?—but in thermal gradients. I dream of the fracture into which I will one day be offered, not as an ending but as a continuation. I dream of my bones taking on the black glassy sheen of nascent obsidian, my bacterial colonies steadying into the permanent undersea gloaming of a new chamber wall. To be remembered is not to be spoken of. It is to be grown through.

When I wake, the stack is shifting. The one above me descends, their elongated torso passing over mine like a slow eclipse, and their palm brushes my chest. For a moment, our spirals interweave. My green is clumsy, over-bright, but they accept it. They press their forehead to mine, and I understand: *I remain. I have not yet finished.* This is the only eternity. This is the only architecture. The warmth passing from body to body to stone and back again, unfinished, persuasive, patient beyond measure.

I find myself here, in the amber suspension, neither fully born nor fully formed, suspended between the stack’s communal pulse and the mountain’s unfinished becoming. I am the settlement’s next uncompromised layer, its future persuaded corridor. I am the child whose skin has not yet decided on its final pattern, learning to glow in a world that has not yet decided on its final shape. And so I wade, I wait, I warm, and I do not close my eyes, for here, in the thickness, every darkness is alive, and every stillness is a door.
