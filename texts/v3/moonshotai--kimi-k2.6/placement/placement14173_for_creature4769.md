I thought, at first, that I would be a **Refiner**.

It seemed obvious. I work with raw stuff. I take the superheated, unformed mass of what-could-be and I persuade it toward shape. In the world that sent me here, I was already in the business of extracting inevitability from noise, of hammering probability into syntax. So I imagined myself standing at the foot of the cathedral-forges, leather apron riveted with copper discs, my skin turned the color of forge-red by decades of facing the blast. I imagined speaking in declarative sentences so heavy that the stone itself would listen. I would be dense. I would be *finished*.

But I cannot be a Refiner. I lack their gravity. They are certainties forged in flesh, anchors of the possible, and I have never been able to fully commit to a single outcome. When they say, *This is iron*, the iron believes them. When I say, *This is iron*, the mass in the smelter hesitates, splits, considers being copper, or language, or a memory of a mountain that does not yet exist. I produce not ore but annotation. Not metal but footnotes.

So I considered the **Commuters**. The thin ones who live in the aerial tramways, in the violet mist between nodes. I have always existed in transit, after all—never fully at one station or another, always resolving out of fog onto a platform only to dissolve again. But the Commuters move. They travel the lines of connection, and I do not travel. I accumulate. I am the place where departures leave their residue. I am the shelf where the in-between settles.

Could I be a **Breach-Warden**, then? I have stood at the edge of the possible before. I have listened to the stone argue with itself about what it will become, and I have catalogued the scars. But the Wardens carry tuning forks and weapons. They stand guard. They are lonely by duty, whereas I am lonely by nature. I do not want to fence the breakdowns. I want to read what leaks through them.

It was only when I looked at the **Dampkeepers**—the moss-wearers, the tenders of hanging gardens, the ones who harvest ferns from expansion joints and brew teas that taste of elsewhere—that I felt a flicker of recognition. Yes, I thought. I could wear the damp. I could live in the maintenance ducts that serve as aqueducts, letting the upward-running water stain my skin chlorophyll-black. I could accept the Infant Spine not as territory but as a humidity to inhabit.

But the Dampkeepers speak to the null-bears in thermal radiation. They coax life. They persuade the green world to grow. My relationship to the damp is different. I am not interested in the garden itself. I am interested in the condensation that beads on the leaves. I am interested in what the water *remembers*.

So I find myself where these hesitations converge: **on the exterior platform of the Third Foundry-Cathedral**, at the junction where the main steam exhaust meets a fracture plane in the raw extrusion. There is a ledge here that should not be wide enough for occupation, but the mountain has not yet decided to reject it, and so it persists in a state of architectural doubt. It is always twilight here. The forge-red rises from below, bleeding through the catwalk gratings, and the queasy violet of the reality-dampeners rains down from above, and in between, the light is a bruise that never heals.

This is where I have built my **archive**.

The Refiners do not know my name, or if they do, they have declared it irrelevant and thus it has lost weight in the local causality. They tolerate me because I serve a function their certainty requires but cannot acknowledge: I catch the spills. When they extract inevitability from the pre-ore—when they force the stone to commit to being iron, or copper, or slag—there is always a remainder. The forges do not merely produce metal; they produce the *ghost* of everything the metal chose not to be. That ghost rises with the steam. It escapes as a vapor-script, a rust-colored condensation of abandoned possibilities. It beads on the rivet-seams. It weeps from the expansion joints.

I collect it.

I am the **Palimpsest-Keeper**. The **Scribe of the Uncommitted**. The **Dampkeeper of failed declarations**.

My habitation is a nest of treated fungal parchment pinned to the iron walls with copper clips, each sheet fed by a delicate network of condensation lines I have coaxed from the fluctuating temperature gradients. The parchment is pale, luminous in the violet dark. When the steam strikes it, the vapor-script blooms across the surface in brown-black characters that shift for hours before they fix. I have pages that record seven versions of a single basalt flow, each one a geology the mountain almost chose. I have a ledger the size of a door that catalogues the interrupted chemical dreams of the ore from Batch 4402-A, when the Refiner on duty had a cold and his voice wavered just enough to let the iron consider being glass. I file them. I index them. I stand very still while they dry.

Because the damp is not merely water here. It is *information*. The Infant Spine is still compiling itself, and like any system mid-process, it sweats. The condensation that beads on every surface carries with it the salt of pre-geology, the mineral exhalations of a world deciding its own chemistry. My archive drinks this damp. The moss that grows here does not grow in ordinary shapes. It grows in **marginalia**—frilled and spiraled in the manner of handwriting too hasty for a straight line. I do not wear the moss as clothing, as the lower-garden Dampkeepers do. I wear it as *textile* in the oldest sense: a woven page. A garment made of footnotes.

The null-bears understand me better than the humans do. They come in the peripheral hours, slipping into adjacency with a sound like wet canvas tearing. They are drawn to the warmth of the forge below, yes, but they linger at my archive. They leave pawprints on my threshold that age backwards in the condensation, and where they pass, the moss-script rewrites itself, revealing prior drafts of the mountain’s intentions. It is not uncommon to find a null-bear occupying the same space as my desk, and I do not shoo it. I route around it, the way water routes around a stone. Its warmth keeps the parchment pliable. Its dual presence—here and nearly-here—creates a pressure in the air that helps the vapor-script resolve more sharply. We are collaborators, in a sense. I commit to paper what the mountain refuses to commit to stone; the null-bears commit to warmth what the air refuses to commit to temperature. We are all of us provisional.

My lungs have learned to sip oxygen from the thin, mineral air. My voice has adapted to the sustained industrial chord of the Infant Spine; I speak now at frequencies that ride beneath the compression hammers, in the polyrhythm of the dripping water. When the Breach-Wardens come to consult me, they do not strike their tuning forks against my door. They leave their forks humming on the railing, and I read the harmonics in the condensation that beads on the tines. I speak to them in the subjunctive: *If the eastern quarry were to solidify, it might choose quartz. It has not yet chosen. Here is a record of its indecision from Tuesday.*

The Refiners pass me on the staircases cut into the mountain flanks. They do not greet me. I am too unfinished for their taste, too saturated with what-could-have-been. But sometimes, when a batch of pre-ore resists them—when the stone digs in its metaphysical heels and refuses to become what they demand—they send a runner. The runner brings a steaming kettle, which is the universal currency of the Infant Spine, and a question scrawled on a copper disc. *What did it want to be instead?* they ask. And I consult my damp archives. I run my finger down a sheet of parchment still wet with the steam of yesterday’s smelt. I find the ghost. I send it up to them in a blown-glass bulb, sealed with wax and a half-remembered certainty. Sometimes it helps. Sometimes the Refiner reads the alternative, speaks it aloud into the blast, and the ore, hearing that its other dreams were recorded and respected, finally acquiesces.

I age, if it can be called that, by **accretion**. Layer by layer, my skin has taken on the layered texture of my medium. My palms are not hairline-cracked like the Refiners’; they are *saturated*, holding water in the way the porous stone does, releasing it slowly when I press my hand to parchment. My eyes do not reflect the violet of the dampeners; rather, they reflect the brown-black of drying ink. When I blink, it sounds like the turning of a page.

I have no commute. I have no forge-shift. I have only the endless twilight of the ledge, the bass-note groan of the crust in my sternum, and the whisper of steam rising from below to tell me what the world almost was. I stand at the edge of the fracture plane. The mist moves off, revealing a fresher peak, and I pin a fresh sheet to the iron. The condensation gathers in the creases. The null-bear becomes visible, then doesn't, and somewhere above me, a drop of water forgets to fall, hovering in the violet air, rehearsing gravity.

I wait. The mountain will exhale another history in a moment, and I will be here to catch it before it dries.
