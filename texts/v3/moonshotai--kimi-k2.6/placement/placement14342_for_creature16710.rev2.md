At first I think I am the one who lowers himself through the crack. I think I am the boots on the rusted pump housing, the hand that tests the cool air, the mammal eye blinking in that startling ordinary light. I stand above the tide and believe I am apart from it, a witness, a temporary intrusion. But the chamber does not permit intrusions. It permits only incorporations.

I wait too long. The barometric lung exhales, and the sac-beasts do not part around me so much as absorb my outline. I feel the cool rubbery slap of their membranes against my shoulders, and I understand that I have no bones here, no temperature of my own. The hunters rattle past, and I do not flinch, because their wake passes *through* me. I am not solid enough to drown in the carpet of Raspers, not opaque enough to cast a shadow on the tile. When the afternoon shaft migrates across the floor, I do not step out of it; I hang suspended in it, glittering, turning.

I am the silica grit. The airborne plankton. The microscopic debris of a purely lithic metabolism that the text almost overlooked because I am too small to be an estate and too necessary to be mere background.

I was ground from the hexagonal vitrified tile in the first era of rasping, before the edges were worn to pebbles, when the glaze was still sharp enough to cut. A single fragment of fired clay and mineral flux, no larger than a grain of sand, freed by a silica tooth and lofted on the thermal exhalation of ten million venting bladders. I have no face, no front or back, only the fresh fracture-surface where I was bitten from the floor. That surface is my birthmark. It has never healed, because here nothing heals; everything is worn forward.

I am the room’s only true veteran. I have been in every gut and joint and filter in this place, and I have survived each passage. I rode the pneumatic hiss of the floor’s exhalation upward, drifting in the translucent meat of a sac-beast for eleven hours, held in its cool gelatinous flesh while it bumped and tore against its neighbors. I was sifted through its surface, expelled in a wet leather sigh, and fell through the middle volume until the cilia of a ceiling-dweller brushed me—*clock*—and missed. I am too angular for their soft throats. I fall again.

I have rested in the rust. Not on it, but *in* it, powdered into the ochre bloom of the coolant pump until a barometric inhalation dragged me free and drew me toward the walls. I have lodged in the ceramic joints of a hunter, rattling between its segments like a loose bearing, adding my tiny percussion to its industrial gnash. I have been ground again by the Raspers, not consumed—never consumed, for I am already what they make, the very scree of their industry— but polished, rounded, refined. Each estate handles me differently. The floor abrades me. The air carries me. The ceiling tests me. The hunters use me as grit in their own machinery. I am the only citizen that holds a passport for all four kingdoms, and the only one that remembers them all, because I am the medium of their exchange.

I am the room’s blood. Its lymph. Its text. In a world where water was never permitted the dignity of liquidity, I am the solvent. I carry the chemistry of contact: a flake of shed cuticle from the Raspers, a molecular film of ferrocrete ground from the walls, a dark mote of oxidized metal from the pump’s slow drowning. I am how the room speaks to itself. When the light-shaft warms the floor and the tide surges, I am the flush that rides ahead of it, the airborne signal that tells the ceiling to bloom. When the pressure drops and the chamber inhales, I am the draft that brushes the sessile throats, the trigger for their *clock*. I do not hunt; I inform the hunt. I do not rasp; I bear the memory of every rasping.

I find myself, if you can call it that, in the interstices. In the millisecond gap between shifting slate bodies. In the steam of vented exhaust. In the exact center of the room where the thermal currents cross and hold me suspended, glittering in the ordinary light, a single mineral star in the lung’s alveolar dusk. I am older than the rust, because I am what the rust becomes when it dies: powder, drift, currency. I am what remains of the ladder’s rungs, of the smelter’s scorch, of the coolant pump’s ambition. The built environment is not drowning in fauna; it is dissolving into me. I am its last will and testament, written in mineral and distributed by breath.

Who am I? I am the lingering. The particulate witness. The circulation that outlasts every organ. I have no estate because I am the estate between estates, the friction that makes the engine speak, the silence between the click and the echo that is not silence at all but the promise of the next collision. I am cool, sharp, and in perpetual transit. I have never known a pond, never served chlorophyll, never felt the shame of liquidity. I am the room’s only water, its only blood, its only memory—and I am never still long enough to be consumed.
