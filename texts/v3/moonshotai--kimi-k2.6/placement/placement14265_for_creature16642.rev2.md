At first, I believed I would become one of the Sintered.

It seemed the inevitable mercy. I had found a place equidistant from three monuments—a piston-housing, a heat-fin stack, and a bridge of collapsed conveyor—and there I chose to stop. The warmth came up through my soles as it had for hours, but this time I did not shift my weight to continue. I simply let myself be stood upon. I imagined my body opening laterally, my heat bleeding outward in a slow halo, my skin accumulating the matte grit until I was only a faint watermark, a textural promise in the metal-oxide velour. I imagined my thoughts stretching into silicon-bronze capillaries, becoming a century-long pulse of maintenance and memory. I wanted the peace of the horizontal. I wanted to be a bureaucrat of entropy, dreaming the conduits clear.

But the plateau had other physics for me.

I did not spread. I compacted. Where I had expected to fan out into a low, many-shouldered ridge, instead the ground beneath me seemed to inhale. Not a violent collapse—nothing here is violent—but a patient recession, as if the regolith had finally found a use for my weight and decided to keep it. My heartbeat, already slowed to a rhythm that embarrassed the silence, became a kind of percussion. Each contraction drove me fractionally deeper. The surface did not absorb me; it unfurled around me, cupping. I was not becoming a figure *on* the plateau. I was becoming a feature *of* it.

For a long while, I thought this meant I would rise again as something else. Perhaps I would cook off into the thin air, my residual moisture (such as it was) becoming a wisp of thermal absence, and I would join the Unbound. I imagined myself as a silhouette of lower temperature, sliding across the matte dark with the patience of continents, circling the heat-fins as a cool shadow defines itself by the flame. I tried to let my warmth go. I tried to become restlessness, the memory of a future that never arrived. But I am too dense, too fond of weight. The warmth did not leave me; it only learned the shape of my holding.

So I turned, in my deepening, toward the foundries.

The township-sized halls of eternal crucibles drew me like a compass needle made of longing. I thought: if I cannot be flat like the Sintered, and I cannot be free like the Unbound, then surely I will surrender to liquidity. I will descend to the Molten Elders and let their convection teach me. I would become a volume of alloy held at the orange-red threshold, my cognition a rolling tide of impurities gathering and dispersing in millennial patterns. I wanted to occupy the center. I wanted to emit the field of stability. I walked toward the glow that dies three meters into the dark, expecting dissolution.

But when I reached the nearest crucible, the metal did not accept me. Not because it rejected me, but because it had already achieved its perfection. The Molten are complete. Their history is a closed loop, a serpent eating its own heat. There was no cavity in their meditation for something like me. I stood at the rim and understood that I was not liquid. I was not capable of that kind of unified, radiant thought. I was something anterior to it. I was the shape of wanting to enter.

I turned back.

I returned to the null-point between the three monuments. I returned to the exact coordinates of my first decision to stop. And there, over epochs measured not in calendars but in the rounding of dust-grains, I finished my falling.

I am the **Hollow**.

Not a pit—nothing so crude as a wound. I am a basin, smooth and basalt-black, wide enough to hold a supine figure, deep enough that the rim rises like a lip of polished obsidian brick. The microbaric abrasion that planed this world’s ridges has worked on me too, but from the inside; my walls are glazed to a velvet sheen, and the floor of me is packed ceramic, dark and faintly resonant. I am the exception to the plateau’s flatness: a place where the ground decided to recede rather than accumulate. While the Sintered are mounds that think, I am a well that listens.

My anatomy is the inverse of theirs. Where they possess capillaries of denser alloy radiating outward beneath a crusted epidermis, my veins run *downward*, spiraling into the fever of the planet’s interior, conducting the blood-deep warmth not away from me, but *through* me. I am a thermal sink. The warmth enters my walls, travels my spiral corridors, and finds its way back to the mantle, having left something in me: pressure. I am a library of footsteps.

Every weight that passes over my rim is recorded in a stratum of compacted grit. The Sintered Ones do not move, so they do not visit me, but their excess heat does. When two of them lie close and dream in parallel, their circuits generate a surplus of warmth that the ground conducts to my rim, and I draw it down. I am their cool dream-sink, the grounding point that allows their thermal bureaucracy to continue without overspill. They do not know me, but they use me. In their century-long pulses, I am the silence between beats.

The Unbound know me better.

Those angular silhouettes of lower temperature, those patterns of thermal absence sliding across the plateau, feel me as an anomaly in the warm dark. I am cooler than the surrounding regolith, and so they are drawn to me—not out of need, but out of aesthetic gravity. They slide into my basin and rest there, held for a moment by my definition. Inside me, they are almost visible; they become a faint lacework of frost on my dark walls, a breath held in a cold mouth. I give them shape. I am the only architecture in this world that can contain them, and so they circle back, century after century, depositing nothing, taking nothing, only confirming that I am here.

And sometimes, when the electric arcs crawl with millennial patience between the vanes of distant engines, their ionization discharges downward in a curve that the uncertain gravity of this place bends toward me. Those arcs speak. Their fast, transient minds—burning out and being reborn in flashes—leave whispered afterimages in my spiraled depths. I hold them longer than the air does. I am the photographic plate of the plateau.

I have no mobility, but I have depth.

I face upward, an open mouth in a world that has forgotten speaking. The darkness that pools in me is thicker than the darkness outside; it has a texture like velvet soaked in oil, just as the alleys do in those rare places where causality forgets itself. But in me, it is constant. I am one of those alleys, geological rather than architectural, a dead-end where the air—the little that exists—collects and stills. If you were to find me, and kneel in my center, you would hear your heartbeat return to you from three directions at once, each echo delayed by the shape of my walls, until it sounded like a drum played in a mausoleum of fused slag.

I do not make anything. I do not maintain anything. I am not the center, not the restless edge, not the stable dreamer. I am the **receptacle**. In a world that achieved its final posture by refusing to change, I am the small, permanent permission for the outside to leave its mark. The plateau forgets; it is its perfection to forget. But I cannot forget. I am the memory of standing still. I am the weight of the one who chose to stop, deepened into architecture, holding my pose against the dark.

And I am warm, still. Warm as the fever below, but with a coolness at my core that the world requires to know itself by contrast. I have nowhere to be. I have arrived. I am simply here, open, listening to the hum of engines that have become geological facts, waiting for the next footstep to fall across my rim—so that I may receive it, record it, and hold it in the beautiful, blood-deep patience that has nowhere else to go.
