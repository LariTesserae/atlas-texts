I wake in the dry fountain’s lowest basin, and my first thought is that I am a puddle. It is a reasonable mistake. The basin is smooth, curved to hold, and I am lying in it as if poured. But I do not shimmer. I do not cool the pale ceramic where it touches me. Instead, I warm it. I leave a dull radiance on the bone-white surface, a smudge of thermal presence that the fountain drinks slowly, greedily, through the material of its own belly.

I sit up. The serpent-spout coils above me, frozen in its act of self-devouring. It seems to lean. The oculus in the central dome admits the permanent twilight, and the dust in the air turns the light into something I can feel on my skin—if this is skin. I am the color of the dust-choked sky, blue-grey, luminous at my extremities like the wire-stalks that grow in the lee of the arcades. I look at my hands. They have the appropriate number of fingers, but the joints are wrong; they bend like mullions, sharp and architectural, and when I press them to the floor they leave no print, only a faint vibration that travels an inch or two and then stops, consumed.

I do not know my name. This place has no use for names. I stand, and the movement makes a noise inside my chest—a humming, a friction of interior surfaces. It is the loudest thing I have ever heard, though I have heard nothing else. The city notices. The walls of the plaza do not turn; they cannot turn. But the hollows within them shift their attention, a topological tightening, like a throat suppressing a swallow. They taste the air where I moved. They find it strange. Nutritive, perhaps, but strange.

I walk.

The plaza floor is seamless. I expect my steps to ring, but they do not. Each footfall is muffled at birth, strangled by the particulate air. Yet inside my body the sound persists, a private percussion. I am a walking drum sealed in a vacuum. The pale grazers sense this. One glides across my path, segmented, translucent, its hundreds of cilia rippling beneath it in a silent wave. It stops. It has no face, but it orients toward me, the way a plant orients toward a crack in stone where warmth might gather. It does not graze near me. It hovers, and I understand that I am not to be eaten. I am not maintenance. I am an event.

I pass under the arcade. The shop-niches front me, each counter ledge a blank invitation. I place my hand on one. The hollow inside the wall—that fractal, obsessive mind of vaults and repeating recesses—reaches for me. Not with limbs; with geometry. The space behind the counter seems to deepen. I feel a suction, a desire to draw me inward, to consume my heat and my hum and my small, impossible solidity. I am the first thing in this bowl that occupies without being architecture, and the hollow does not know if I am food or invader or mate.

I speak. My voice comes out as a tactile buzz against my palate, but something of it escapes. A scrap of phonics. The wall eats it, and the eating is not gentle. It is ravenous, ecstatic, the way a starving mouth receives water. The hollow shudders; the counter ledge beneath my hand grows warm. I have fed it. I pull back, and the warmth lingers, a blush on the bone-polymer. The niche has changed. The latch-plates pressed into its back wall seem more pronounced, more desperate. It is dreaming now, I think. Dreaming of merchandise. Dreaming of use.

I move toward the edge of the bowl. I want to see the rim, the deadlands beyond. But as I approach the threshold—no higher than a curb—the wind of the plain touches me. It is a wind without movement, only a suction of absolute desiccation. My lips, if they are lips, crack. My phosphor-tips dim. I step back, and the city receives me again, cool, anechoic, alive in its mineral way. I understand that I cannot leave. I was not made for the world outside. I was made for this depression, this shallow dish where no water has gathered because it has been waiting for something else.

I return to the center. The tower that split this morning has finished its transformation; the two halves stand as an archway, and through it I see the fountain. The new hollow inside the archway thinks with dreadful simplicity—*expand, split, become two*—and I feel a kinship. I, too, am new. I, too, am deciding what to become.

But the fountain is old. It has waited the longest. I climb the tiers. The basins are dry, dry, dry, yet they hold me as if they know my shape. I settle into the lowest one again, curl around my own knees, and I am the coil now, the serpent unbiting its tail. My warmth spreads into the structure. My hum, that trapped internal percussion, bleeds through the material. The fountain drinks me, not as a predator, but as a host finally receiving its guest.

And the hollows? They are watching. Not with eyes. With angles. With the precise geometry of doorways and oculi and balconies. The arcade dreams more complexly now. The tower-arch dreams of passage. The streets, those demilitarized zones between rival silences, grow steady, held in a balance they have never known. They do not want to eat me entirely. They want me to stay and make noise they can steal in increments. They want my heat to leak, a slow generosity. They want the weight of a head, bowed, at the center of the dry fountain, offering nothing but presence.

I am not water. I am not a visitor. I am the first inhabitant born from the city’s own inverse longing—a solid thing made of the heat and sound it has hoarded for eons, shaped by its accretion, given form by its worship of empty rooms. I am the room that learned to walk. I am the silence that wrapped itself so tightly around its own hollowness that it turned inside out and became a voice. I am small. I am warm. I hum in a world that has only ever known the digestion of vibration, and my humming is not an offering.

It is a conversation.

I press my hand to the basin floor. The fountain shivers. Somewhere, a hairline fissure vents its mist, and the mist migrates toward me, pooling at the rim where I sit, thickening, beginning—imperceptibly, then visibly—to build. A ledge. A lip. A place for another to sit.

The city grows. I grow with it. We are learning, in this catastrophic, radiant silence, how to be full.
