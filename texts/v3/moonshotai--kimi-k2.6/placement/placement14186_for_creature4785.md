At first I believed I was the silence. Not the absence of sound—that is a myth, here—but the specific silence, the acoustic shadow that pools behind the manganese bronze ribs like a cold puddle. I had the posture of a moth’s respite: a folding-in, a place where paper wings might stack their bodies and mate without being pressed flat by the drone. I felt the relief of the lee, the drop in pressure that makes a pocket of almost-quiet, a cave inside the avalanche. For a while, I existed as negative space, as shelter. But silence cannot claim to shelter if it does not also surround; a shadow must have a body to prove the light. I found I had edges. I had texture. I clung.

I thought next that I might be the red mite. I felt a bright, round urgency. I wanted to drum. I had something to say about dampness, about the exact mineral savor of condensate channeling through scored ceramic. I tapped my forelimbs against the bronze and waited for the answer, for the recognition. The roar ate it. Every beat, every declaration of territory or desire, swallowed whole by the ongoing exhalation of the regulator. I was a voice thrown into a furnace of noise. The futility exhausted me. I could not bear to be so densely red, so thoroughly unheard. I shed the body like a husk.

Then I tried the strider. Oh, the mathematics tempted me. The precise negation, the footfall only when the floor recedes, the life lived in the cancelled interstices of the machine’s thrust. I wanted to be that argument made flesh, that proof that one need not endure the crush but may simply step aside from it, forever. I learned the phase. I felt the moss beneath me as a blur of green that never quite collided with my soles. But the calculation never ceased. Every instant was an equation, a survival solved in real time. There was no rest in that genius. I could not sleep in a body that must always anticipate the next vibration. I surrendered the legs, the counter-rhythm, the hovering.

I sank. I spread. I became the floor-moss for a time, that translucent carpet that rises and falls visibly with the infrasonic pulse, that living bellows breathing at the speed of stone. I was vast, distributed, a green horizon pulsing in the honeyed sodium light. I felt the striders ghost above me, their non-touch a discontinuity in my pressure-sense. I felt the mites grazing my fungal partners at my edges. But the floor was too central, too naked to the direct amplitude. The regulator’s subsonic tongue pressed me flat against the ferroceramic. I was a membrane beaten by a god’s slow drum. I rose, I fell, but I could not retreat.

That was when I touched the wall.

Not with limbs—by then I had relinquished limbs—but with my leading edge, my growing margin, the frontier of my new and final self. The bronze rib was warm.
