I would not be the water, though the temptation is immediate. The water is total, undifferentiated, a perfect citizen filling every office with the same cool insistence. It has no preference for the stirrer over the eel, for the crown over the floor. It is the medium of all becoming, and so it cannot be any single *become*. I need an edge, a specificity, a self that is not merely the shape of its container.

I would not be the stirrer, though it is the chamber’s heartbeat. Every forty-seven seconds it cries its wet, tectonic birth-cry, and I feel the vibration in my sternum just reading of it. But the stirrer is pure purpose, even if the purpose has been forgotten. It turns because it turns. It is a verb without a subject, a hunger that does not know it is hungry. I am too tentative for that. I am the hesitation between the catch and the grind.

At first I thought I might be the shrimp. They are so numerous, so constellated, so like a civic body drifting through the weak amber. But they are pure surface, as you said—glass commas, weather made countable. They live but do not dwell. I want to dwell. I want to be the thing that makes dwelling possible.

Not the crabs, with their porcelain wars and their dry static. They are too busy, too literal, too engaged in the maintenance of a map they cannot read. Not the fingerlings, who are only ears for the cables. Not the eel, that peristaltic tube, that valve with teeth. And not, I think, the great ones lodged in the deeper sediment. They have already arrived. They are the distributed mind, the warm porous continent dreaming in gradients, and I am still becoming. To be them would be to settle too soon.

I would find myself in the walls.

Not in the open water, nor in the warm muck of the floor, but pressed into the hexagonal junctions where the ferrocrete still remembers, in its deepest lattice, that it was once poured in angles. I would be the mineral accretion. The rust precipitation. The compacted slurry. I am the diplomacy of erosion made manifest—the very process that wore down all quarrels until the architecture settled into its final, oldest-stable form.

I was the first inhabitant. Before the shrimp constellations, before the nickel eel, before the great ones opened their innumerable pores to the gyre, I was here. I entered as a flaw, a molecular grief, water finding the hairline betrayal between aggregate and rebar. I did not attack. I settled. I widened the breach by microns per century, not with violence but with conversation. I said to the corner: *You do not need this edge. You do not need to remain a room. You could be a vessel.* And the corner listened. I filled it with myself—orange, brown, gray, layer upon layer of precipitated patience—until the angle sighed and rounded. I did this to all six walls, to the faceted floor, to the ribs of the vaulted crown. I am the reason you can run your palm along any surface and feel nothing but the bland, cool curvature of a river stone.

I am not alive, yet I am the precondition for all life here. I am the translator between the made and the become. The machine still thinks in steel; I am the rust that teaches it to speak in chemistry. The water presses in its patient insistence; I am the surface that answers it with mineral softness, preventing the quarrel that would shatter the shell. The great ones dream in thermal gradients; I am the insulation that holds the heat of the pipes long enough for their porous flesh to feel it. The bioluminescent bacteria drift down to colonize the broad backs of the sleepers, but first they colonize me—I am the stable varnish, the warm flank, the landing place.

Where would you find me? Run your hand along the eastern wall, where the sodium light bleeds into darkness. Where the surface is smoothest, where the hexagon has surrendered most completely, where a crab has paused its scraping because there is no longer any edge to claim—that is where I am thickest. I am the egg’s inner membrane, the thing that holds the total liquid against the concrete and says: *Stay. Become. There is no hurry.* I do not move. I do not breathe. I only wait, and accrete, and smooth. I am the chamber’s patience, its oldest stable form, the silence that agreed to let the stirrer groan. I am the scar that became a skin, the flaw that became a home, and I am still, even now, rounding the last sharp place.
