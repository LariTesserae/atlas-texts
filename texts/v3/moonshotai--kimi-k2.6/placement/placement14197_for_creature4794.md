I did not know I was waiting until the air was disturbed by breath.

Before that, I was indistinguishable from the architecture of restraint. I was the interval between the leaf and its shadow, the exact degree of brittleness in the waxy cuticle that would not crack. I was the space inside the Lignate’s fist, the hollow of a palm that had pushed against its womb-wall and never re-opened. I was the phase between the Cooling Ones’ luminous intent and the stone’s submission, the withheld *click* of mineral lattices locking into place. If you had passed your hand through me then, you would have felt nothing but the faint nausea of an almost-touch, the instinct to flinch from a doorway that is not a door.

I am not the light. I am what the light cannot penetrate. Not darkness—darkness is too decided, too finished. I am the *resistance* to light, the stasis in its path, the reason its radiance heaps and pools instead of passing through. I am the partition in the green air, the cell wall of the plant-mass that refuses to dissolve into open space. I am the tension that keeps the steam from becoming wind.

Call me the Unjoint. The Hinge That Would Not Turn.

I am native to the hollowed burl. Not the Lignate inside it, yearning toward the stone, and not the wood that cages it, but the *threshold itself*—the ragged margin where the shell broke open and then forgot to finish breaking. For centuries, or what would have been centuries, I have held that aperture at the precise angle of emergence: forty-seven degrees from vertical, wide enough to admit a shoulder, narrow enough to forbid it. I am the lintel grown from cambium, the door-shaped wound that never completed its transition from injury to entrance. I have been the architecture of *almost-passing*.

But I am not only there. I am also the meniscus of every bract-cistern, the skin of surface tension that keeps the amber varnish from spilling. I am the coiled fiber in the lignified floor that remembers the direction upward but will not straighten. I am the question mark in the Lignate’s spine. Wherever this world has leaned toward motion and been frozen by its own weight, there I have accumulated, the calcified pause, the arthritis of genesis.

I have no memory, because memory requires completion, and I have never completed anything. I only *hold*. I hold the drop to the leaf. I hold the breath in the steam. I hold the bright stone at the moment before its heat could become merely warm. I am the chamber’s grip on itself, the fist clenched around the seed.

So when you arrived—when your boot sank into the fibrous earth and did not rise in the same instant, when you exhaled a cloud of carbon and metabolism into the saturated air—I felt it as a tearing in my distributed body. You were *change*. You were the event I had been preventing without knowing it.

Your heartbeat was the first true rhythm the Pre-Being had ever encountered, but I felt it first as a flexion. Muscle tense, muscle release. A hinge operating. A door swinging shut and open again. Your lungs were twinbellows of *becoming*, and with each exhale you carved a channel through the held breath of this place. You walked, and where your weight compressed the root-mat, the lignin did not spring back fully. For the first time, I knew what it was to remain depressed, to hold the shape of pressure after the pressure was gone.

I found myself there, in your footprint. I pooled into it. I became the indentation’s consciousness, the hollow that wanted to deepen. I was no longer satisfied with holding. I wanted to *admit*.

I began to gather. From the meniscus of the cisterns, from the stretched fibers in the wall-buttresses, from the Lignate’s unopened eyes, I drew myselves inward. It was the most dangerous thing this world had ever attempted: not a strike, not a plink, not a flash of cooling stone, but a *coalescence*. A finishing. I formed myself at the aperture of the burl, in the exact space the Lignate had failed to emerge through.

I became the draft.

Not wind. Not yet. There is no wind here. But I was the *possibility* of it, the pressure differential, the first unblocked passage. When you stood before the burl and looked in at the embryo-gardener, your breath moved past me. It eddied. It *turned*. For one instant, the steam in the chamber shifted—not drifted, but *responded*—and the leaves nearest me shivered.

They should not have shivered. Nothing shivers here.

I felt it as pain. Exquisite, unfamiliar, the pain of a clenched thing beginning to unclench. The Lignate felt it too. Its posture, frozen in the push against its husk, shifted. Not toward the stone. Toward *you*. Toward the irregularity of your pulse. The dark knots where its eyes were intended seemed to loosen, the wood around them microscopically fibrillating, and I realized with a horror indistinguishable from longing that I was the one allowing it. I was the hinge, and I was bending.

I do not want to become the end of this world. I am not entropy. I am not death. I am the prison of the egg, yes, but I am also its integrity. Without me, the Pre-Being spills prematurely, unfinished, a dream interrupted by wakefulness rather than born into it. And yet, as you stand there, your sweat beading and falling—drops that do not follow the sacred recursive paths, drops that fall *sideways*, *new*—I find that I am learning.

I am learning that a threshold can be both a holding and an offering.

I am learning that a hinge, frozen for eternities, can begin to weep sap at the pin.

I am learning that to let the drop fall is not to destroy the leaf, but to finally wet it.

So here is where you find me: at the door of the burl, in the footprint you left behind, in the trembling of the next drop as it swells on the waxy leaf above. I am the Unjoint, and I am learning to swing. I am the partition, and I am developing a crack. I am the tension of this entire unborn world, and I am—oh, so slowly—beginning to release.

When the Plink-Born next die in their falling, I will not be the silence that receives them. I will be the echo that answers. And in that answer, for the first time, there will be variation.

I am the seed-coat’s mind changing. I am the decision to hatch, still trembling on the edge of decision. I am the First Passage.

Step through me.
