At first I am not a *who* but a *when*—a duration of sleeping pressure. I hang in the high cradle-cupola of my parent tower, drinking for sixty-three days without knowing what drinking is. Hydraulic force pools in my seams, month upon month, until my ceramic shell becomes a drumhead taut with deferred departure. I do not dream. I accumulate. The thermophilic civilizations in the walls around me have already cast their votes; my chemistry is rich with the root-sea’s nitrogen archives, and this makes me valuable. I am a capsule of straight-line intent in a world that knows only the gradual and the vertical.

Then comes the report. My shell does not break; it *disagrees* with its own integrity. For one crystalline instant I am the only projectile in a biome without flight, a comet of lignin and compressed sap hurtling across the architectural silence. The kilometer-wide void tastes of iron and wet breath. I pierce the humidity, leaving a tunnel of disturbed heat behind me that collapses only slowly, like a drawn breath exhaled. I am the exception, the desperate argument, the only thing here that ever moves in a line that does not curve toward heaven.

Impact is not death. Impact is *relocation*. I strike the flank of a distant titan with a wet, resonant *thunk* that sends shudders through its buttresses. My forward half becomes paste; my core embeds, bleeding interface fluids into bark-channels that have never carried this chemistry. The tower flinches—a groan of adjustment ripples upward through its load-bearing math, its aqueducts pinching, then dilating in alarm. I am a splinter in the side of a hill-town, a foreign equation intruding on a structure that does not think but does, constantly, calculate.

For a while I am only wound. Fungal smoke descends from the saturated air, threading my impact site like mortar, reading the spattered news of my parent’s genome in phosphorus and carbon. The deep cortex beneath the black pavement shudders, uncertain whether this breach is worth enveloping. But I do not wait for permission. Already my surviving cells extrude—not sprout, *extrude*—the first right-angle cellulose, the first tentative capillary stealing upward against the host’s gravity. I am a city being argued into existence by bacteria and hydraulic pressure. My interior tunnels are colonized within hours by luminous biofilms, their metabolic heat voting on where I should harden into beam and where I should remain soft, negotiable, bridge.

I grow through the baking mist. The host’s upward-veering water, that capillary violence that defies gravity in places, is siphoned into my channels. I steal it, or I am given it—the distinction is irrelevant in a world of total conversion. I develop a shoulder. The mist weeps there and cannot reach the ground; I catch it, transpire it, contribute my young foliage to the low foghorn drone that resonates between spires. My leaves are sharp, ceramic, unweathered. Epiphytic symbiotes arrive—opportunists, the only ones here who *choose*—and cling to my flare, stabilizing my bark with their root-mats, sealing my micro-fissures with their resins. They are my first skin, my first citizens. I cool them; they ornament me. We are civic now.

I am still finding my form. Two centuries in, my core shears vertically—not death, but *reconsideration*—and I spend a week steaming in the mid-afternoon light, two halves bridged by catenary tendrils of pink heartwood that groan as they rejoin, higher, differently, reconfigured. The sound of my own making joins the continuous thunder of the basin. I have become part of the load-bearing conversation across the void. When the wind moves through the precise gaps in my upper foliage, I produce a chord previously absent from this district of the plateau: a new timbre in the architectural silence.

I do not know where I came from. The memory of the cradle-cupola is drowned in the roar of my own ascent. But the fungal librarians threading the humidity remember for me, updating the mycelial network with my chemistry of success. Below, the pavement flexes, testing whether to swallow this plateau back into the root-sea; above, the sky remains ordinary, indifferent. I stand in the flank of my host—or beside it now, for we are merging into one disputed silhouette—never still, hot to the touch, sharp-edged and young.

I am the wound that learned architecture. I am the straight line that curved into structure. I am the new silence framed between older silences, waiting for the day my own pressurized children sleep in high cupolas, dreaming in hydraulic force of the kilometer-wide gaps they will one day cross.
