I would not at first know where to stand, because standing implies a gap between foot and ground, and here there is no *between*. I would try, perhaps, to arrive as a **chimney**—vertical, hexagonal, breathing on a timetable of pressure. A fine ambition, but one that requires a commitment to a single exhalation lasting millennia. My patience would shatter. I would consider the **pipe-ferns**, those rubbery orange cascades of pure interface, hanging in the gradient between hypercold air and geological chemistry; but to be a fern is to be only a question asked by the atmosphere, and I find I want to be an answer, however crude.

I would almost choose the **castes**, the wolfhound trilobites with their piston legs and carbide hunger. To smelt inside one’s own stomach, to move at the speed of tectonic appetite—this has a dignity. But I would balk at the week-long turn. I would fear the certainty of the graze. And so I would remain, undistributed, uncommitted, letting the light accrue upon me until my indecision itself became a physical fact.

That is how I would find myself at the convergence of three ruts, where the castes climbing toward a mid-sized chimney must surmount a thrust of raw peridotite too angular to erode, too necessary to avoid. Here, the density achieves its apex. The bodies are four deep. The greaser-mucus pools in the declivity until it is a mirror in which the bright sky drowns. And here, I would discover the self that was waiting:

Not a whole caste, but a **fractured one**.

I would be the individual whose primary piston-leg sheared against the mantle-bone of the outcrop, not in violence, but in the statistical inevitability of a place where too much weight meets too young a stone. I did not fall. There is no room to fall. I locked. Caught in half-stride, my thorax pitched forward, my left mandible resting against the flank of the chimney, my iron belly suspended above the rut in a posture that suggests hunger but is only interrupted momentum.

My internal fire did not die. It *ebbed*, stranded. A hairline crack in my smelter-wall admitted the hypercold air too slowly to quench the reduction, too fast to let it complete. I am a forge with a draft. For ten thousand years, my stomach has been cooling through the specific, protracted phase transition of a starved reaction. I am not dead. I am not alive. I am the long, metallurgical *meanwhile*.

And this is my function.

Because I am angled, because I am hollow, because my broken leg-cavity opens like a bell toward the northeast rut, I have become a **resonator**. The bedrock groan—the bass frequency of the Sinter’s settling—travels up through the feet of the castes who climb my back. It enters my thorax, and instead of passing through, it *dwells*. My locked pistons are tuning forks. My mandible, pressed to the chimney’s verdigris flank, conducts the subharmonic drone of the smoker-flutes into my hollows, where it mingles with the wet chunk-chunk of the grazing masses and the constant hiss of greasers glazing my joints. I am the place where the weatherborne sound achieves a local eddy. The Sinter, which thinks in pressure differentials and geological committee, has here—by accident of my fracture—a **mirror**. A place to overhear itself.

The others have not abandoned me. They never do. The castes behind simply climbed, their piston-legs finding purchase on my cooling dorsal plates, their iron bellies scraping ore-bloom from the chimney face I brace. Their weight is not an insult; it is the continuum asserting itself. Below me, the greasers have glazed my underside until I am fused to the peridotite, a bridge of silicone and frozen breath. Pipe-ferns have rooted in the seam of my broken leg, their orange tubing draping across my ocular ridges—blind anyway, we do not need eyes here—drinking the condensation that beads on my heated flanks. I am scaffolding now, yes, but I am scaffolding that still faintly glows.

The aeroplankton knows. The zinc and lithium seeds, drifting in their permanent haze, settle upon my back in greater densities than elsewhere. They are drawn to the residual warmth, to the magnetic anomaly of my dying smelter. They plate me, slowly, in a filigree of conductive dust. They make me a better antenna. A better mirror.

I am the **catchment**, the pocket formed by misalignment. The Sinter’s light accrues, unimpeded, merciless—but where my raised leg intercepts the direct reflection from the chimney’s cobalt bloom, there is a zone of slightly less blinding incandescence, a faintness in which the rime does not form so quickly, and a single thread of brine persists in my hollow throat longer than it should. Not a shadow. The Sinter permits no shadows. But a hesitation in the glare. A place where the machine, for just a moment, does not blind itself.

To be me is to be the pause between the castes’ steps. To feel the vibration of a migration that will not detour around me, but will flow over me until my iron is worn to the shape of the rut itself. To conduct the thought of the super-organism, letting its geological deliberation echo in my cavities, amplifying the single, slow realization that it is one thing, and that I am the place where it touches itself and knows, for the span of my cooling, that it is here.

I am not the mind of the Sinter. I am the *cavity in which a mind lingers*. I am the fracture that became an ear. I am the warm iron in the nitrogen edge, the half-smelted ingot, the road that has not yet forgotten the weight of walking.

And when my fire finally goes out—when the reduction completes its last exhalation and my thorax cools to the temperature of the bedrock—I will not mind. The pipe-ferns will have already rooted through my stomach. A new chimney may excrete its hexagonal base upon my skull. I will become pure infrastructure, and the sound will pass through me cleanly, without hesitation. But for now, I am the interval. The bright, loud, frozen Sinter measures itself against my long, slow finishing, and finds, in my fracture, a moment almost long enough to call itself a thought.
