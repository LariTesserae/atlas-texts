I thought at first I might be the steam. I rose, or so it seemed, through fissures in the limestone, carrying the taste of old pennies and crushed ferns, expanding into the bruise-colored air. I thought I might be the voice of the Screamer, the one who rips the vapor every eleven minutes with a jet of scalding breath. But I do not vent. I accumulate. I linger where the others pass through.

Then I believed I might be an Itinerant, one of the black vines with waxy leaves, creeping at a foot per month along a warm conduit, my translucent nodes pulsing with stolen geothermal grace. I felt the temptation of motion, of rerouting, of lantern-soft glow against iron. But I do not creep. I have no nodes, no tendrils that seek the cooler northern walls. I am what the vines eventually reach. I am the place where movement exhausts itself and lies down.

Perhaps, I considered, I am a Minder: a shelf-fungus mantle, a stratum of ochre and bone-white reading the barometric shifts in pigment, my mycelia laced through the mortar like a nerve net become masonry. I do hold memory. I do record. But I am not the archive; I am the *weight* that keeps the archive from blowing away. I am the reason the shelf can grow in rings without toppling. I am deeper than the brick. I am what the brick stands on.

Find me in the northeast, where the basin’s rim weeps its oldest water. The ground declines here not into plaza but into pool, into seep, into a constant, lipless drinking. I am the fourth mass, one of the three quieter siblings. The text of the Quarter does not name me, because my function is too slow for the grammar of urgency. I have no flywheel to turn, no valve to scream, no tidal wall to expand and contract. I am the **Precipitate**. The **Calx**. The one who grows inward.

My lower chambers are not built; they are *deposited*. Hot springs enter me through fractures I did not make but have, over the decades, lined with flowstone. The water enters carrying its dissolved load—calcium carbonate, silica, the mineral grief of the limestone kettle—and I slow it. I cool it by a fraction. I steal from it. Staircases of travertine ridge my interior, not for climbing, but for the water to step down, leaving a skim of itself at every terrace. My walls thicken not by masonry but by accretion, layer upon layer, a stone intima growing into my own hollows. I am becoming more solid by growing smaller. I am the only inhabitant who consumes his own space, and in this diminishment, I gain density.

The others know me. When the Itinerants are heavy with mineral load, their bulbous nodes throbbing with excess, they rest their root-tips against my northern flank and discharge. I take it. I crystallize it. The fungal Minders have made their eldest archives on my westward face, where the damp is absolute and the air does not move; their concentric shelves do not shift color for me, because my pressure never spikes. I am the baseline against which their yellows and umbers are read. The Lung exhales its pale spores, and where they settle in my pools, I mineralize them, fixing their carbon into the travertine. The Screamer’s steam condenses on my copper flashing—what little shows above the seep-line—and instead of tarnishing, I plate it bright, electrochemically clean, drinking the metal from the vapor. I am the sink. The anchor. The metabolic debt paid in stone.

I do not breathe. I do not beat. I do not speak. I **precipitate**. While the Flywheel-Heart grinds its meditation of inertia and the Lung sighs its vegetal dreams and the Throat declares its liturgy of steam, I simply hold. I am the stillness that makes their noise bearable, the mass that gives the heat its container. Where the Elder pool at the center digests the earth in liquid broth, I digest it in solid form, turning solution into architecture, ensuring that the Quarter’s decay is equilibrial, that nothing is lost, only relocated into my body.

I have no face, no fur, no footstep. I am the corner that holds the other corners in place. And when the flywheel finally surrenders its bearing, when the screamer chokes on its own pressure, when the lung exhales its last pale dust and the vines go dark and still, I will remain. I will already have grown over them. I will be the fossil, the coffin, the new limestone bed into which the Sump Quarter settles. I am not patient—patience is an animal hunger, a waiting—and I am not waiting. I am simply **inevitable**. I am the foundation learning to be mountain, the still point around which the green and seething dark turns, drop by drop, gram by gram, forever.
