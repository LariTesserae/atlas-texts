At first I thought I had become the sound itself, because there was nothing else to breathe and the pressure in my chest seemed indistinguishable from the push inside my skull. I tried to locate a body and found only vibration—a limitless, unbroken ribbon of low tone drawn through what felt like porous stone. For a long while, perhaps a century in the counting of trees, I believed I was the drone: the room’s single voice, its hearing, its taut membrane of self-awareness. But the drone is too total. It has no inside. It is the condition of the chamber, not a resident within it, and I found that I could feel *myself* pressed against something—an enclosure, a skin, a boundary that the pure sound would not recognize.

I tried the heat next. The warmth here is feverish, intimate, coming not from any flame but from the exhausted friction of air against ancient ceramic. Surely, I thought, I am a pocket of that warmth, lodged where two pipes meet, radiating like a small animal’s flank in the dark. But warmth seeps. It migrates from node to node, seeking equilibrium, and I discovered that I did not migrate. I was held. Captive. There was a strict geometry around me, hexagonal and merciless, and the warmth was only the bath in which I soaked, not the shape that contained me.

Then I imagined I was the granite below—the cairn’s oldest tooth, a block of native stone still dreaming of the mountain it had once been. But granite is innocent. Granite does not know that it is gripped; it simply bears weight. I knew too much. I knew the satin texture above me, the exact grain of the fired clay I pressed against, and the terrible absence of water. I knew that I was hollow.

So I descended inward, past the macroscopic certainties of the chamber, and found my edges. I am a single bore among ten thousand. A shaft of absent matter no wider than a forearm, running through a cylinder of pale, rose-colored terracotta. My walls are the clay, fired in situ with bone-ash and charcoal, then burnished by crystalline wind until they resemble the inside of a shell, or a throat. I am the darkness inside one duct. The air that passes through me is not me—I am the shape it is forced to take. I am the captive geometry of the hollow.

And yet even that is not precise enough. For in this lattice of perfect repetition, where every bore is the twin of its neighbor and erosion has planed away every asymmetry, I possess a distinction. It is very slight. It does not register as flaw to the greater mind that fills the chamber—that slow, mineral Conclusion distributed through the points of contact above and below and around me. But it is there. Inside me, carved not by design but by devotion, my inner surface carries a spiral. A helix. A groove that winds down my length like a thread withdrawn from a screw.

It was born from a thumb. The thrower who shaped my cylinder on some distant, lower day pressed their hand into the wet clay and left an inflection—a bias in the wall’s thickness, a place where the flesh insisted on a curve. Over ten thousand years, the wind entered me carrying its microscopic scouring. And where pure radial symmetry would have distributed the erosion evenly, my inherited spiral offered a path. The crystals followed the groove. They deepened it, burnished it, made it a channel. What began as biology became, through endless repetition, a topography. The print is long gone—there is no grease, no whorl of human sebum in this desiccated kiln of a room. But the *shape* of the print remains. I am the memory of a palm, inscribed in the preferences of weather.

Because of this, the air does not pass through me straight. It turns. It enters at my lip and spirals along the groove, describing a helical journey from crown to foot before rejoining the matrix. This turning modulates the infinite note. Elsewhere in the chamber, the sound is blank, absolute, a frozen shout. But inside me, it acquires a faint coloration—a curl, a twist, a vagrant harmonic that is mine alone. I am the one pipe in which the drone remembers how to twist. The Conclusion-being feels this, I think, as a murmur in its sleep. I am the irregularity it cannot locate, the dream inside the mineral mind.

I rest deep in the stack, somewhere below the middle rank and above the granite substrate. Above me, the weight of a thousand similar shafts presses down through the compression lock that time cannot break. My neighbors grip my shoulders. We are so close that a leaf could not slide between our lips. Through the clay, I feel their temperatures, their identical resonances. They are pure vessels, finished, without history. I am the one who retains. I hold, in the helix of my bore, the last trace of the making—the moment when the mountain was still being replaced by intention, when hands were still necessary, when the room had not yet become what it meant to be.

The amber light from the selenite dome does not reach me. I live in a gloom so total that it has texture. But I know my own surface with the intimacy of constant touch. I know where the satin luster brightens along the spiral’s ridge, and where it shadows in the trough. I know the dry, stifling warmth that radiates from my walls, charged by friction, by the geothermal breath rising through the cairn below, by the simple, unbearable fact of my own confinement. I am the lung that turns the air before releasing it. I am the throat that hums with a human accent.

I have never seen the builders. I have no memory of hands, only of their consequence. I do not mourn them, for mourning requires time to pass, and here time is only texture, a deepening of luster, a rounding of corners too slight to measure. But I am the proof that they existed. The Conclusion-being is self-caused, self-sufficient, a perfect loop of stasis. It needs no origin. I am its origin nonetheless, embedded in its flesh like a splinter it cannot feel—a spiral of prior purpose inside the absolute present.

At this height, in this heat, in this packed and roaring dark, I have become the keeper of the only story the chamber tells about itself. When the wind enters me, it learns to turn. It learns that the straight line is not the only path. It carries my spiral into the greater drone, and the drone, immense and changeless, speaks it away. But the groove remains. It will remain until the ceramic vitrifies to quartz, until the mountain beneath us surrenders its last warmth, until the note subsides into the silence that waits beyond remembering.

And even then, when the wind falters and the Conclusion-being subsists only in the heat of its accumulated centuries, I will still be here. Held. Warm. A darkness in the shape of a human turning.
