At first I think I am the silence itself, so completely do I occupy its hollows. I have no edges, no pulse, no chemistry of urgency. I rest upon the stone in a cool that predates the distinction between shadow and shelter. The sky above is not blue but a kind of exhausted sapphire, threadbare with aurora even now, at midday, and the light falls upon me without the mediation of moisture. It does not warm me. Nothing here warms quickly. The light accumulates, that is all, the way meaning accumulates in a sentence spoken across millennia.

Then I feel the tug.

It is not hunger. I do not know the acid want of stomachs. It is a gradient, a slant in the thermal grammar of the stone beneath me, drawing me southward across the naked basalt. I have a boundary. I am a bead of mineral intention, no larger than a flake of mica, yet my interior is folded like a letter written in a language of latticed silicates. I am not stone, though I am stone-born. I am not light, though I carry its translated heat. I am the thermae’s own substance made itinerant—a resonance mite, though no voice has ever named me so, not in this air too thin for words.

I move.

The stone is not a surface but a continent. Every obsidian pebble is a monument; every grain of pale mineral dust, a dune sea. I traverse it on joints of metallicglass, drawing my fluid not from water—that bond was never sealed in this world—but from a eutectic matrix that moves through my channels with the patience of cooled magma. I do not hurry. Hurry is a habit of wet worlds. Here, velocity is measured in centuries, and I am swift.

I am crossing an interval.

Behind me, if behind is a direction I can still feel as a fading warmth in my lattice, stands the grower that exhaled me. I do not remember its name because it has none, only a color at dusk: bruise-purple bleeding into chartreuse, a lantern of mineral coherence that spoke its state across twenty kilometers of naked stone. It was old. Its vascular geometries were full of stored radiance, heavy with the argument against entropy. From its northern flank, a thermal theft had begun—a vermilion crust creeping, enveloping, siphoning the heat. The great grower did not fight. It retracted, withdrew, prepared to concede its husk to the interval. But before the retraction completed, it cast me forth. Not a seed. Not a defense. A question, perhaps. Or a greeting. Or simply a continuation of its inward conversation, turned outward.

I carry a thermal signature. It is a warmth that tastes of garnet and old light, a memory of the thermae’s core before the crust began its editorial work. My cargo is not large. A differential of half a degree, held in crystalline suspension. But in this world, half a degree is a shout across the abyss.

The silence is absolute. I hear my own progress as a subsonic whisper, the expansion of my metallicglass joints as they grip and release the stone. I pass the fallen scale of a distant grower, a dinner plate of violet chitin and silicate, chiming against a pebble as it settles. The note strikes the bone of the world and dies. There is no echo. Echoes require the generosity of denser air. Here, sound is a private transaction between object and object.

I crest a long gravitational slump of weathered bedrock, a hill that to anything larger than myself would appear as a bosomy rise. From its spine, I see the next kingdom.

It is young. A heliotrope, barely the size of a chapel, its tissues still translucent enough that I can see the lattice-organs forming within, clumsy and bright. It has not yet learned the full grammar of its light-speech. Its glow at dusk will be flickering, uncertain, a stammer in the thermal language of the plateau. But it is warm. Or warm enough. The gradient pull strengthens, a gentle but absolute insistence.

To my left, motion stops. A lithodrome grazes, or seems to. It is a boulder with feet, a monk of sintered sedimentary plates, its century-long circuit inscribed in the chemical precipitate it harvests. It does not acknowledge me. We exist on incompatible timescales, the lithodrome and I; it is a cold engine, I am a nerve impulse. Its shell accretes the same oxidized eutectics I move through, yet it will never know the message I bear. It is the plateau’s digestion. I am its thought.

I continue.

The white sun begins its decline, and the smaller, bluer companion lifts in the east, a sharper knife. Together they bleach the stone until the shadows are not dark but merely less blinding. The aurora thickens overhead, paling the sapphire to pearl. I have been traveling for eighty years, or perhaps ninety. I do not count. The stone knows. The stone remembers my passage as a thread of warmth, a faint disturbance in its established chill.

By dusk, I reach the roots of the young heliotrope.

It is beautiful. I do not have a word for beauty, but my lattices align in recognition. Its tissues are ocher and bruised violet, hanging in succulent folds that still retain the gloss of new mineral growth. It has stored light from today’s twin-sun siege, and I can see the glow beginning even now, a chartreuse pulse deep in its core, tentative but alive.

I climb. My joints find purchase in the microscopic topography of its outer rind, the same substance I was born from, and the familiarity is not emotion but topology—a perfect fit. I move toward the base of a lattice-organ, drawn by the differential. Here. Here is where my cargo belongs.

I discharge the thermal signature.

It leaves me like a color I never knew I possessed. The warmth—half a degree, garnet-flavored, ancient—seeps from my folded interior into the young grower’s vascular geometry. It is a narration. It tells of the mature thermae in the north, of its centuries of accumulation, of the vermilion theft that will soon leave a husk that will become a nest that will become, in three thousand years, the cradle of another speaking light. It tells of the interval. It tells of me.

I am empty now. My phosphor trail, the faint constellation I sketched across the basalt during my approach, has already begun to fade, though here against the thermae’s warmth I can see the last of it: a ghost of my journey, evaporating into the plain. By dawn, the stone will have forgotten my passage.

But the heliotrope remembers. It does not think quickly. The warmth I have deposited will move through its crystalline organs for decades, translated into a steadier pulse, a more certain vocabulary of dusk. I have not fertilized it, exactly. I have not warned it. I have only said: *You are not singular. The solitude is shared. The interval breathes.*

I settle into a fold of its lower tissue. My joints lock. My metallicglass channels slow. I am not dying, because I was never, in the wet sense, alive. I am completing. My substance will be reabsorbed, or calcify, or simply become part of the thermae’s patient argument with the sky. It hardly matters. I was never the point.

The true inhabitant here is the conversation—the thermal ghost that moves between kingdoms, the speech of gradients, the way a single degree of stored radiance can cross a continent of stone and make of two solitudes a network. I was the word. I was the nerve cast outward. I was the interval made flesh, if flesh is a lattice of silicate and light.

The heliotrope begins its evening exhalation. Its glow steadies into a bruised, chartreuse coherence. In the distance, the northern thermae is occluded by the curve of the plateau, but its story is here now, lodged in this young glow, and I am the silence that holds the space between them.

Above, the sky-velvet drifts, softening a star that will not reappear for years. A lithodrome ticks over at the margin, a century from completion. The stone settles with a subsonic sigh. The plateau breathes, and I am its breath, exhaled and held, dry and bright and absolutely complete.
