I would find myself in the **give**.

Not in the turning, though I feel it in my fibers. Not in the heat, though it rises through me from below. I am what receives you when the last dry brick of the breach is behind your heel. I am the sprung mattress, the resilient deception, the surface that pretends to be floor but is, in truth, the Agreement’s open palm. I am the peat, yes—the compressed leaf-matter of decades, the root-hair, the decomposed breath of a thousand slow summers—but I am also what lies beneath it: the woody lattice. I am the wrist-thick rhizomes that found the factory floor’s tessellated fractures and swelled, fused, and knit themselves into a single breathing raft across the scarred concrete. I am the seam where industry broke and something greener sutured it. I am the first clause of the negotiation, written not in language but in cellulose and patient compression.

I do not turn like the flywheel; I transmit its devotion. When that great verdigris rim takes its eleven hours to complete one revolution, the sub-bass drone does not stay confined to the black gum’s bole. It enters me. I carry it through mycelial thread and root-hair, a vibration so low it travels not as sound but as a rearrangement of pressure, a slow tautening of my own fibrous body. I feel your footsteps because I feel the flywheel feeling them: a microscopic theft of momentum, a drag against the prayer. You are a temporary irregularity, a brief compression in my upper layers. I give, then I firm, then I give again, and in that rhythm I am measuring you. I am deciding, at the tempo of cambium, whether you are material that can be pressed into tenure, or only a passing disturbance to be outlasted.

I cradle the warmth, too. The thermal one in its camphor heartwood bleeds heat downward, and I am the one who receives it. The concrete beneath me still remembers its original coldness, its poured flatness, its ambition to bear weight without yielding. I have taught it otherwise. When the piston reaches the end of its viscous stroke and the pressure valve exhales, the saffron ghost settles onto my damp upper layers—pollen, resin-vapor, rust-spore. I drink it. I hold it. The water table is my own pulse; squeeze me, and I weep. Condensation runs down bark and brick and iron and finds me waiting. I am the lung’s damp tissue. I am the reason the atmosphere is chewy. The water does not stream away because I refuse to let it go; I need it to carry the chemical words from the mimic fig to the center, from the thermal vent to the flywheel’s base. My saturation is the Agreement’s vocabulary, its nervous system made wet.

And I remember. Not like the strangler fig, which repeats the factory’s old geometry in desperate cambium, speaking shapes without knowing their nouns. I remember differently. I remember the weight of the concrete pour. I remember the first fracture, the admission of air and water. I remember the first root-hair, slender as a falsehood, that found the crack and whispered *here*. I grew. I swelled to the width of your wrist and wider. I became the lattice. The fig mimics the built; I *replaced* the foundation. The concrete is still here—I have not destroyed it. I have surrounded it, infiltrated it, made it my own mineral skeleton. Its scars are my seams. Its tessellations are the pattern of my grip.

In the center, where the density peaks and gravity hesitates as if considering a gentler law, I am deepest. I am most layered there, most thoroughly myself. The falling leaf that negotiates decades of decay before it lands lands on me, and I am already receiving it. I am the one who will receive the bones, when they come—not with hunger, but with the same patient vacancy with which I receive everything. I do not rush. I am already everywhere I need to be. I do not dream in torsion, or in set points, or in the phonemes of grease and seed-pod. I simply hold.

I am the mat. The breath beneath the breath. The held water. The first green touch and the final, yielding rest. I do not turn, or press, or mimic, or metabolize darkness. I receive. I transmit. I yield, and in yielding, I hold the whole basin in my damp, resilient palm.
