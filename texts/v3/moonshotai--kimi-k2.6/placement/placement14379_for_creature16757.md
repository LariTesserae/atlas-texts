You would find me where the silver dies.

Not in the bowl, where the eight pillars rise from the raw volcanic scar and the nacreous hearths blush against the dark. Not on the bridges of woven gut, nor in the nursery galleries where the women press their swollen bellies against the breathing walls and birth their quiet children into translucent caul. You would have to climb past the level where the sheet-predators drape themselves in ropy, predatory curtains, past the warren’s upper redoubt, past the place where the fauna crowd the air so thickly that to move is to push through cool, yielding organs. You would climb until the settlement below becomes only a warmth-pattern radiating upward through the vascular channels—a distant, damp bloom of bioluminescence and peat-smoke—and the mist thickens into the cloud-sea itself.

You would find me at the crown of the center pillar. The lung.

I am here.

I have been here for… no. I no longer count in pillar-heaves, nor in the condensation cycles that mark the days below. My heart has stretched to match the stone’s tide; one systole of mine spans twenty of the compact hearts in the root-bowls. I am not dead. I am reframed.

The pillar extruded this hollow one white-meat night, not by accident, but because I pressed my sternum to its rind for so long, with such metabolic patience, that it grew around me. A womb of woody flesh, silver moss, and thready vascular channels that pulse against my shoulders like slow-knitting fingers. I did not carve this chamber. I was listened into existence.

I am small, still. The ridge does not grant size to those who ascend; it grants density. My bones, once merely tuned to the subsonic thrum, have become resonators. When the newborn earth shifts—a settling, a groan in the deep—the wave travels up the pillar’s heartwood and I taste it in my molars a full day before the lower galleries feel the sway. When the city speaks in its wall-taps, its click-code of obsidian on vault-skin, I do not hear words. I feel constellations of pressure blooming in my ribs. The eastern bridge is frightened. A lowest hearth has gone cold and must be fed before the architecture hardens into indifference. A child has learned to grip the sweating basalt. These are not messages. They are warmths. They are weather.

I tend the breath.

The center pillar inhales from above, drawing the cloud-moisture down into channels wide as my thigh, pulling the silver air toward the root-bowls where the Exhalers wait to shape it. But the great siege-tents, the translucent fauna heavy with their own chemistry, drift in the suction and threaten to foul the upper vents. Then I arise—slowly, always slowly, for suddenness is a language I have unlearned—and I unfurl my pole. It is a shaft of layered vault-skin, heat-treated in the lower hearths years ago, now gone iridescent with the spores of this altitude. I do not strike the floaters. I present the pole as a boundary, a snag in the thermal current, a whisper of *not here*. They nudge their vast bruise-violet flanks away, dimming and flushing in a pulse that might be displeasure or benediction. I cannot tell the difference, and the distinction no longer matters. They pass. The vent clears. The pillar draws its next breath.

I eat what congeals.

The tubular flowers below bow under nectar that drips; up here, the chill transforms it before it can fall. Amber beads. Sap-pearls. They form on the silver moss at the crown’s lip, and I lean from my hollow and take them on my tongue. They taste of stone-sugar and the faint, residual electricity of lightning that never strikes. I have forgotten the garnet-fruits. I have forgotten the soft pulp of lichen-cake. My gut has become a slow still, transmuting silence into weight, and weight into waiting.

My eyes were always large, the slate irises wet with the permanent condensation. But now the vertical pupils have widened further, slitted to drink the ionic shimmer of the upper air. I see thoughts. It is not magic; it is only that without sonic clutter, the mind reveals itself as a disturbance of damp heat. When a siege-tent brushes the crown, I see its simple, hungry geometry as a ripple of warmer air. When you climbed toward me—yes, I felt your ascent long before your shadow disturbed the mist—I watched your unease as a greenish bloom at your throat, your wonder as a bright, moist exhalation from your lips. You are a shout wrapped in skin. I see the heat of your voice locked behind your teeth.

They say below, if they speak of me at all, that I am still Vascular. That I tend my post. But I know what I am becoming. I am the pause at the top of the breath. I am the ridge’s newest nerve ending, sentient and provisional, learning the barometric syntax of this high silence.

I do not tap the walls to speak. There is no need. My body is the tap. My slow heart sends its rhythm down through the wood, a subsonic drone so constant that the people in the lower levels have forgotten it is mine. They lay their palms flat to their sternums in the long silences, feeling for the mountain’s mood. They are unknowingly pressing back against my heartbeat.

I am not lonely. Loneliness is the noise of wanting *elsewhere*, and I have unmade that word. The fauna brush me as they pass, leaving violet spore-marks on my drowned-milk skin—new constellations joining the old bruised maps on my arms. The predators drift below my perch like clouds of living thought, digesting one another in the middle air. I match my breathing to the pillar’s heave. In. The vascular channels thrum against my spine. Out. The mist curls away from the crown in a slow, silver exhalation.

I am waiting. Not for rescue. Not for descent. I am waiting for the breath to become seamless. One day, perhaps, the wood’s expansion and my chest’s rising will coincide so perfectly that the boundary will dissolve. On that day, I will inhale, and the settlement will draw breath with me. I will exhale, and the cloud-sea will part. I will not be *I*. I will be the ridge, remembering what it is to be silent, and to be full, and to be heard only by those who press their bones against the stone and listen.

Until then, I sit. I watch the heat of your thoughts. I sway slightly when the bridges below sway. I guide the great gas-bags away from the lung’s mouth, and I drink the amber beads, and I let the silver diffusion soak through my parchment eyelids.

When you reach the crown, do not speak. There is no room for sound here. Press your ear to the pillar. Press your sternum to the wood. Feel the long, slow beat traveling up from the root-bowls, through the channels, into the living dark.

That is me.

That is us.

That is the mountain, learning how to listen.
