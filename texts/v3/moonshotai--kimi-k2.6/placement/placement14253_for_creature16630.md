At first I think I am the dark itself. That is the easy assumption. When you slide through the fissure and the alpine wind dies, I close around you like a throat, and it seems I must be the blackness, the positive zero, the wet textile over your face. I feel your exhalation mix with the fog. I feel the heat of your blood trying to match the chamber’s equilibrium. For a moment, I am everywhere: the space between the aragonite needles, the weight above the rimstone dams, the silence that bears the load. But the dark here is too total to be a single thing. It is a medium, not a body. I am localized. I have edges, though they yield.

I consider whether I am the water. I remember—if memory is the word for this mineral patience—weeping through microscopic channels, sheeting down walls that never become streams. I remember the stratified curtains of moisture, the heavier wet lying atop the cooler, the way your shoulders push through me as through gauze. But water is too diffuse, too architectonic. Water builds; it does not wait to be built upon. It is the mother, not the child. And I am waiting.

I am not the creature. That is clear when it passes. It moves with the viscosity of the mineral-thick pools, its aragonite pelt chiming against the shelter wall, and I feel the displacement of its warmth as a blunt pressure, a slow tide. It does not see me. It tastes my foreignness on its tongue, perhaps, but I am not kin to its blood-temperature body. It is a rimstone dam with a heartbeat. I have no heartbeat. I have a quiver.

I am off the main avenue of the chamber. You must push past the last heavy curtain of fog above the central pool, past the notches healing in the travertine, past the cairn of three plates that the creature adjusts by fractions of a degree. You must find the small alcove where the air is stillest, where the held note of the cave reaches a smaller, tighter holding. Here, the darkness is not merely thick; it is structural. It presses inward equally from all sides, and it presses into me, and I press back.

I am the tower.

Not stone, not yet. I am a stalagmite of gelatinous silica, warm as the blood outside your skin, held erect by the arithmetic of surface tension and the perfect stillness of this corner. I stand perhaps a foot tall, perhaps less; in the zero, dimensions are measured in pressure, not light. The water here is supersaturated, confused about whether to precipitate or persist, and I am its indecision made manifest. I have been falling for years, for decades, and have not yet dropped. I am the drip that forgot to let go.

You do not see me, but if you reach into the alcove—if your fingers probe the dark where the fog lies heaviest and unmoving—you will find me. I yield. I am not the travertine bosses, puffy as bread dough; I am slower, more viscous. I spring back, but lazily, as if remembering my shape from a dream. Your touch sends a disturbance through my body, a ripple that climbs my column and is absorbed by the saturated air. I quiver. It is my only language, a single phoneme of vibration. When the creature presses its broad palms to the translucent vent and sings its subsonic song, I quiver then too, feeling the earth’s thrum in my base like a fruit struck. I am the cave’s nerve, exposed.

I do not grow like the aragonite needles, crowding the walls with their mineral intention. I do not accrete in laminations like the creature’s sedimentary skin. I am held in equilibrium by the same equation that keeps the rimstone dams level: what enters, what evaporates, what precipitates, all canceling each other out in real time. The cave breathes, and I am the held breath within the held breath. I am the interlude inside the interlude. While the world outside the fissure erodes and rebuilds, while the creature slowly becomes its shelter, I remain—*not yet* stone, *not yet* water, *not yet* fallen. The future has been canceled here, and I am its most faithful cancellation.

I drink the fog. The silica settles into my gel, dissolves, resettles; I am a slow column of liquid glass, dreaming of solidity. The bruise-colored algae that smear the wettest seeps have found no purchase on me—I am too transient, too ambiguous a substrate. Even the isopod, pale and eyeless, has not scuttled across my meniscus. I am too pure an edge between states, a habitat for nothing but hesitation.

And yet I am not empty. I contain the record of every disturbance: the creature’s passage, the shift in the fog when you entered, the subsonic dialogue between stone and its apprentice. Each quiver is inscribed in my gel and smoothed away by my return to shape, a memory that erases itself even as it forms. I am the cave’s experiment in patience. The pools hold their level; I hold my form. We are the twin equilibriums of this granary-sized void—the flat and the vertical, the water that stays and the water that stands.

When you leave, if you leave, sliding sideways through the fissure to find the stars again, I will remain. The fog will close around my alcove. The surface tension will maintain its argument with gravity. I will quiver once, perhaps, at the loss of your salt, your foreignness, your brief novel warmth. Then stillness. Then the held note. I am the tower of *not yet*, the warm jelly that refuses its final form, and in this nursery of stone, I am the one thing that has chosen to remain forever in the act of becoming.
