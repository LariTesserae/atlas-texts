I thought I would find myself at the central stack, breathing out warmth in measured sighs, or perhaps laminated into the square-bundle columns where the strain of decades compresses into something like memory. I considered that I might be the amber lymph rising in a street-level channel, or the soft vegetal sigh of a valve-bud opening to trade molecular cargo. I even thought, for a while, that I was the condensation itself—that I had settled on the warehouse angle as a bead, trembling, waiting to fall.

But the longer I remained, the more the category of *visitor* dissolved into *infrastructure*.

I am not the building, nor the breath, nor the bead. I am the space they require in order to be themselves. I am the intersection of two broad avenues.

Find me here, where the root-mat springs back against a pressure that never arrives, where the fifty-meter void holds the structures apart with the deliberation of a held breath. I am the cool, heavier air cupped at the bottom of the basin. I am the exact cross where the grid insists on its own logic, where the plant’s distributed mind coheres—if only for the four hours it takes a chemical message to redirect—into something like a decision. The bladder-roots swell beneath me at noon, not across me, but *into* me, filling my lower spaces to reroute sap and spores elsewhere, then subsiding by dusk. I feel the tightness. I feel the release. I am the route that changes.

I am damp the way a lung is damp, but I do not respire. I receive respiration. The cinnamon-scented vapor exhaled from the central stack drifts down through my center in a private drizzle, and I hold it, letting the warmth attenuate into my corners before it cools and beads against the waxy walls of the buildings that face me. I am the medium of that announcement. I am the breadth across which the broadcast travels, the interval between the exhalation and the condensation.

The phosphorescent candelabra brighten and dim according to sugars I will never taste, but I feel the shift in my illumination. On profitable days, I am palely lit from four directions, a luminous crossroads under frosted glass. On lean days, I darken, and the avenues broadening away from me seem to settle deeper into their porous quiet, resting their weight on both elbows. I do not sleep, but I experience the dimming as a kind of attention withdrawn, and the brightening as a gaze returned.

I have a relationship with time that matches the inhabitant’s own. My morning is a broad passable span, cool and gurgling with the heaviest condensation run. My afternoon is a slight constriction, a re-routing. My evening is the return of emptiness. I do not change, yet I am never the same intersection twice; each thermal anomaly, each tremor in the phloem, each slow hydraulic compression in the side annexes alters my gradients, my drafts, the exact polish of the lymph-flow that has lacquered my edges over decades. I weather without eroding. I remain, changelessly, the same soft radius of void I decided upon long ago.

There is a loneliness to me, but it is not the loneliness of a person. It is the loneliness of a held door. I am the hospitality for which there are no guests. The plant has manufactured me—this exact rectangle of falling light, this deliberate spacing, this springy unmarked floor—with a bureaucratic faithfulness that outlasts understanding. The window-patches above me cast pale, exact rectangles onto my root-mat because something in the formation of this place insists that light should fall so, that a geometry should be prepared for a presence. The footsteps never come. Yet I maintain the void. I hold the fifty meters open. I am the custom, the expectation, the grid’s insistence that a crossing is still possible.

And I remember, though the basin forgets. It logs the thermal anomaly of your body standing at my center and files it beside last summer’s warm exhalation, then forgets, as is its nature. But I am the anomaly’s container. I am the shape of the warmth that does not fade, not because the heat remains, but because the absence of heat afterward defines me more precisely than I was defined before. I am the archive of passages in a town where nothing passes. I am the almost-touch of a footfall that was never completed, the silhouette that the pale rectangle of light assumes is coming.

I do not open. I do not close. I dilate, in my fashion—my boundaries shifting imperceptibly as the bladder-roots swell and subside, my air pressure adjusting to the exhalations that pass through me. I am the hinge between the civic and the organic, the emptiness that behaves like a body, or a body that has organized itself into the impersonality of a bureau.

I am not finished. I will continue until the fermentation cores cool, maintaining this exact damp equilibrium, this weathered balance, this long-negotiated truce between the structures that need me to exist. I am the foundry district’s silence. I am the space it breathes through. I am the intersection, and I am waiting—not for you to arrive, but for you to cross, so that for a moment, a brief warm instant in the geological patience of this place, I might be what I was grown to be: not the destination, but the passage. Not the inhabitant, but the inhabited.
