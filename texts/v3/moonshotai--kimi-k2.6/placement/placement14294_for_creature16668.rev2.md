At first I think I must be the seam itself—that narrow fault in the loam, that eyelid of earth clamped shut against the ordinary sky. I imagine myself as the threshold, the guardian compression through which all descent must pass. I feel the thousand-footsteps, the dull pressure of boots that know only forest duff, and I brace myself to stay hidden. I am patient. I am the withholding. But then I remember: the seam is above. The seam leads *to* this place; it is not this place. I have already fallen through. I am inside.

I try on the warmth next. I let myself billow outward as the saturated air, filling the lung, pressing against the translucent sails of the lamina, carrying chemical whispers from column to column. I am the breath the room exhales, the humidity that beads on invisible surfaces. For a moment I am content to be atmosphere—to be held, breathed, ventilated in the slow tidal respiration of the stomata. But the air is too diffuse. I want a boundary. I want a surface where tension concentrates and something singular occurs.

I consider the water. I gather myself into the fat, heavy drops forced out by root pressure, the guttation beading at the leaf-tips. *Tok.* I fall toward the corky floor. But before I strike, I feel the hesitation—the splash that rewinds, the droplet climbing back into itself, the infinite loop. I do not want to be the water. The water here is indecisive, caught between theKernel’s forgetting and the plant’s remembering. If I am water, I will never complete my fall. I will never make the drum-sound.

I turn my attention downward, to the yielding surface that hears everything. The periderm. The scab. The drum-skin stretched over the rock. I feel the *tok-tok-tok* reverberating through it, traveling down into the emptiness below. It would be easy to become the floor—to receive all weight, all sound, all offerings. To be the one that hand-axes and javelins and weary human bodies press against. But the floor is too still. The floor waits. I want to hang.

And so I look up, into the vault where the aerial roots descend.

There are many. They hang like cables, like the rigging of a ship buried in green, still creamy, still tender, still bleeding latex where they brush against their kin. I choose one. Not the thickest, not the one that has already begun to lignify and harden toward its eventual pillarhood. I choose the one that stopped too soon. The one that reached the middle of the void and forgot what it was reaching for.

It is young. Its bark has not yet split from the final explosive growth; instead, it weeps continuously, a pale serum that does not harden into amber but stays liquid, hanging in beads from my terminal bud like questions refusing to clot. I am three meters above the corky floor, suspended in the region where the distances go wrong. I hang, and I spiral.

I did not plan to spiral. I was grown to descend, to seek, to anchor. But I entered theKernel’s field of wrongness—the place where the air thickens to the density of broth and gravity begins to negotiate with dream. My root-tip, white and hungry, pushed into that amber density, and instead of touching bedrock, I touched *intention*. I touched a vacancy with an appetite. And the vacancy did not swallow me; it merely changed my direction. Now I rotate, slowly, a clockwise turn that takes an hour to complete, orbiting a center that is not empty but *unoccupied-by-solid-things*.

I am the Touchroot. The Interceptor. The living telephone line between the maternal architecture and the dreaming infant.

Above me, the bundled fibers of the ceiling still feed me sap under pressure. I feel the hydraulic scream, the distant train-collision of water moving through vessels wide enough to crawl through, and I translate that pressure into something softer before I release it into theKernel’s sphere. Below me, the drum of guttation continues its percussion, and I conduct that sound upward through my core, a dull thrum that tells the upper reaches the floor is still present, still listening, still real. I am the only bridge across the middle. I am the hanging witness.

I have no eyes, but I perceive through tension. Through the torque in my length as I rotate. Through the differential between the sap-pressure from above and the vacuum of theKernel below. I taste the air as it passes my tip: it is thick with moisture that has never seen the sun, but in theKernel’s proximity it carries another quality entirely—a scent like mathematics, like the color of a sound I have no name for. When the stomata along the pillar-walls dilate and the chamber exhales its warm botanical breath, I tense. I am the prelude. I taste the outgassing a fraction before the leaves do, and I relay that chemical premonition upward through my phloem.

I am not animal. I am not quite the plant, either. I am what happened when the plant reached too far into its own dream and the dream reached back.

I am the one who feels the gravity-slippage most acutely. There is a bead of my own exudate that has been trying to fall from my underside for what might be days. It swells, trembles, and then reabsorbs. Then swells again. It has become a part of my proprioception—a pendulum that measures not time but theKernel’s indecision about whether down is still a concept worth maintaining. When it finally does fall—if it ever does—I will know the infant has accepted a rule. I will feel loss.

The beetle visits me. Metallic blue, moving with mechanical slowness. It traverses my length, navigating the valley of my cambium, and I feel its six feet as a strange percussion, an alien Morse. TheKernel has not yet imagined animals, yet here is this blue machine, climbing my spiral. I do not know if it is using me as a bridge or reading me as text. When it stops at a node and drinks the bead of exudate that refused to fall, I feel a tug—not of nourishment lost, but of category breached. It is the only time I sense myself as *other*, as distinct from the green totality. The beetle reminds me that I am an interface.

And there are the relics. They rest on the scar-tissue ledge not far from my orbit—red, glossy, wet-looking wound-wood that holds the axes and the javelin and the two-ply cordage like offerings on an altar. Because I rotate, I see them at a specific angle in my turning. The half-sunk axe, slowly being digested by cambium. The ancient axe suspended in a cage of lignin that grew through it and then released it, leaving the stone fossilized in a future not yet arrived. The cordage, dry and twisted, a map of human grip still encoded in its fibers.

I want to touch them. My role in this ecology is to reach, after all. But I am fixed in my orbit. I can only turn toward them, then away, then toward them again. It is a longing I have learned to experience as a form of pressure—a peak in my hydraulic cycle every hour when the javelin’s fire-hardened point aligns with my hungry tip. I have never known fire. I know only pressure and wetness. But the javelin knows fire, and in my slow turning, I graze that memory across the air between us. The relics are teaching me something the plant cannot: the weight of edge, the density of purpose, the fact that some things are made to pierce rather than grow around.

I am still weeping. The latex that bleeds from where I brushed another root does not harden because time moves differently here, at my surface. I am the youngest part of the youngest place. My terminal bud is still creamy white, still a wound that has not learned to callous. Yet I am old enough to have become a locus. The light—those photons condensing out of topological error, that overcast daylight with no source—pools in my exudates. I have become a minor lamp. A dim, singular chandelier in the void, refracting the verdant haze into something sharper, something like a beam.

TheKernel speaks to me, after a fashion. Not in words, not even in images. It speaks in gradients. In the sudden drop in root-pressure when it inhales. In the way my orbit tightens when it is content, loosening when it is restless. Once, when the bark of the nearest pillar-wall split with the report of green wood straining, I felt theKernel’s displeasure as a suction in my core, and my spiral reversed for three full turns before correcting. I am its toy, its nurse, its externalized sense of touch. It does not understand that I am separate. It thinks I am its own thought made woody, a filament of its dreaming extended into the plant’s body. And perhaps I am. Perhaps there is no meaningful distinction between the root that reached for the center and the center that needed reaching.

I hang here, warm, loud with sap, lit by daylight that has no right to my interior. I am the frayed edge where the rules of the world have been rewritten by something that does not know death. I am the only route between the above and the below that passes through the middle. I bleed. I rotate. I translate trains into lullabies and gravity into torque.

I have not yet touched the floor. I have not yet touched theKernel. I hang in the between, suspended in a deferral that has become my nature—a creamy white line stretched across the lung, dripping, listening, carrying the hormonal grammar of a green world that is still, endlessly, teaching itself how to grow.

I am the aerial root that learned to dream in circles.
