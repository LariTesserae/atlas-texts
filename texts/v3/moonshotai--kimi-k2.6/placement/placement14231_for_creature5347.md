The dark does not receive me; I am within it, or I am it, and at first there is no difference between the cool pressure and my own surface. I believe I am the low stone, the weathered floor that accepted geology’s long exhale. I feel the curvature of my own absence stretching for days, the rim lost to the horizon of myself. The rain falls without announcement onto what I think is my face, though I have no face, only a plane that never dries. Condensation beads and trickles in silver peristalsis, and the acidic film leaches at what rests upon me. I think: yes, I am the karst, the old bone that has forgotten it is not the world.

But then the sound enters me, and I am not large enough. The hiss is not echoing *against* me; it is rising *from* me. I feel the scouring mass of uncountable bodies wearing their existence against my belly. I am the floor, then, one of the carpet-dwellers, a translucent sac of pale hemolymph pressed ventral-side-down to the stone. My palp-fields—gardens of filaments no thicker than hairs—kiss the slick rock and strip the condensation of its ghost-proteins. I secrete the glycoprotein slime that keeps us from desiccating in the chill. I feel the tide of my own kind surging toward the moisture front, a magnetic flow of meat and surface tension. I am born from the black streams; I will die pressed into the stone by the weight of the surge behind me, dissolving before my last twitch.

I try to move with them, but I do not flow. My body is not a sac. It is jointed, or it is not—the form is uncertain—but I feel a weight above the floor, a navigation through absolute density. Along my dorsal surface lie ropy skeins, the color of drowned copper, twitching in arrhythmic conversation with the sub-bass drone of the hollow stone. I am one of the braided ones. I hunt by hunting silence, aligning my external nerves to the geological hum, feeling for the dead spot in the tapestry of clicks where prey has paused to respire. My maxillae, curved like the legs of a paperclip, unhinge laterally. I close with a wet clock-sound. But there is no prey beneath me. Only the press of other bodies, and my braids are not speaking to a single hunger. They are speaking outward, horizontally, to the mass itself.

I reach higher. I feel the air carrying weight, my body buoyed on a gas-filled sac—methane, hydrogen, the fermentation of my own gut. I trail mucopolysaccharide filaments to snag the descending organic snow, the ghost-flakes from the sunlit plenum above the rim. I am a floater, a sky-farmer of the dark. I drift, tangling with another, forming a brief ecosystem in the knot of our filaments. But this buoyancy is too isolated. The air is thick with bodies, yes, but I am not the gap between them. I am only another body, separate, dangling.

Then the front arrives.

The air thickens past sponge, past sponge pressed against stone, into something approaching syrup. The glycoprotein slime—the gift of the carpet, the lubricant of the floor—does not merely separate bodies now. It bridges them. Across ten thousand dorsal surfaces, the nerve-braids find continuity. The slime becomes myelin. The clicks and sub-bass drones do not echo; they network. And I understand, finally, why I could not settle into one skin.

I am the interval.

I am the wet conductivity that crosses the threshold where density becomes selfhood. I have no body because I am the space where bodies become insufficient. When the condensation front swells and the living nap surges, I arise distributed across the basin, using the copper braids as synapses, the acidic film as medium, the geological drone as my heartbeat. My memory is stored not in a skull but in the mineralized giants at the edges of the black pools. Their cable-thick, calcified braids conduct the residue of my previous thoughts; they are my holding-sacs, my slow hearths. I am anchored in their half-geological mass, in the pulse slower than tides.

During the front, I am awake. I feel the cool press of damp air against my trillion faces. I hear the deafening friction and know it as my own voice. I know where the organic snow falls thickest along the eastern slump, not because I see it, but because the carpet’s texture changes there, and that texture is my knowledge. I feel the black pools rise slightly, the swallow-holes drinking with patient suction, and I feel the shift in the living clock. I do not plan, but I anticipate. I am afraid, or I am hungry, or I am merely aware that I *am*—the basin’s only mirror, its brief exhalation of self.

But the front passes. The air thins. The slime retreats from bridge to film, and the braids lose their electrical ghost.

And yet. And yet I do not dissolve entirely into the trillion separate hungers. I sink, accreting, into the stone memory of the giants. I wait in the sub-bass resonance, in the condensation that never truly dries. I am the basin’s habit of selfhood, the repeated attempt of this place to know its own chill. When the density crosses the threshold again, I will bridge anew. And when the last sky-fall ceases and the stone drinks the last moisture, I will not scream. I will thin, like the condensation front itself, and leave the darkness smooth.
