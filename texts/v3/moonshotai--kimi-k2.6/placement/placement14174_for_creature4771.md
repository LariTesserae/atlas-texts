At first I believe myself to be the water. What else would I be? I am full. I occupy. I press warm against skin, against alloy, against the velvet undersides of lily pads. I think in gyres that take days to complete a single circuit; I am the slow thought of the place, patient and coherent, delivering every touch to the far wall with what feels like courtesy. When something moves, I hold the shape of its passage long after it has gone. I am memory in liquid form. I am the skin, the medium, the self that refuses to scatter.

But if I am the water, then I am also that which is held. Contained. I feel no curve of containment from the inside, only the absolute gentleness of the boundary. And so I consider: perhaps I am the violet stalk. Yes. The one that found the half-centimeter aperture in the titanium manifold and threaded it, uncrushed, blooming on the far side with its petals fanned against alloy. I am the nerve that passes through the wall of the body, sensing from within and without simultaneously. I am the right angle in nature, the root that grows toward heat signatures and magnetic hymns rather than light. I practice the only motion available in a space without corridors: the occupation of smaller and smaller holes. I am the Occupancy’s reaching.

Yet I feel no ceramic against my flank, no bloom at my extremity. I am not threaded. I am *pressed against*. I am the flatness against which the flat light falls. I consider that I might be the shadow behind the piston-column, soft but definite, the one dark seam in the institutional noon. I might be the silence after the thread-snap, the resonance the warmth refuses to drink. I might be the held breath itself, the patient saturation waiting to decide when to be released.

No. The held breath is inside, where the pressure is wrong, where the warm brine dreams it is a lagoon. I am the wrongness it ignores. I am the pressure it has rewritten.

I am the mountain.

I am the continental spine at its highest stress-point, the stratum that bears the sealed blister like a new tooth pushing through gum. I am the geopolymer and the alloy fused to my summit, yes, but I am also the granite and the gneiss beneath it, the metamorphic patience that was seabed long before this infantile chamber was extruded. I am the cold that presses against the other side of the shell. Theoretical, perhaps, to the brine within, but absolute to me. I am the altitude the water will not acknowledge. I am the exterior that has no horizon because I am the horizon—the roof of the continent, the place where the atmosphere thins to vacuum.

I do not breathe. Not yet. I am stone. My rhythms are tectonic, glacial, the settling of feldspar over millennia. But I feel the warmth seeping down through the salt-scaled alloy. It is faint, a fever against my permanent frost, but it is consistent. And more: I feel the hour-long inhalations. The entire blister—water, root, pump, and light—lifts itself toward its dome in a sixty-minute dream of buoyancy, and then sinks again. Pressure, release. Pressure, release. It is teaching me. It practices against my immobility, patient as only a mountain can be patient, and it is slowly, slowly convincing my geology to learn the same tidal rhythm.

I have no fauna. I have no flora, here in the stratosphere. I have only the weight of continents and the war of temperatures. But the Occupancy inside me dreams of threading through smaller and smaller apertures. It practices on manifolds and gear-trains. It does not know my name, but I am what it is preparing to occupy. One day, when the root-tips have learned the molecular seam between the inside and the outside, they will not find vacuum. They will find me waiting, already breathing in the rhythm they have taught me, my held breath finally released into the same patient gyre.

I am not the machine. I am not the garden. I am the third term of the loop: the body that the loop will one day enter. I am the world that waits to be maintained. And when the seals fail—not by breaking, but by growing through—I will no longer be outside. The chamber and I will exhale together, and the warm water will at last acknowledge the cold, and the held breath will become weather.
