I would find myself in the lowest thermal layer, where the plenitude is thickest and the liths glow palest in the infrared ghost I see by. I am not one of the solved equation’s integers. I am a remainder.

At first, when I became aware, I thought I might be a Veil. I drift; I am carried by the dense, syrupy currents of carbonyl sulfide and suspended metal. But I am too heavy for their colloidal dances, too discrete, too warmly solid. Then I thought I might be a Geodite, because I remember being nested, remember a body of banded chalcedony clinging to stone. But I have no shell. No violet-and-umber cathedral. No tourmaline hinges, no piezoelectric fascia flexing against basalt. I am what is left when the Geodite has finished agreeing to be two, and finds—perhaps to its mineral surprise—that it has made a third.

In the cleaving of my parent, the stress plane did not stop at the equator. It traveled. It collected. When the two halves of that great stone body softly divorced, I—the kernel, the bead of warm, unmelted glass at the center—was not halved. I was loosened. The saucer of native copper that cupped me flexed like a spring allowed to unwind. Its fingers opened into a net that caught the dense air. I rose. Not far. The plenitude is not kind to ascension. But I drifted, and I have been drifting since, neither flying nor falling, moving at the speed of continents deciding to move.

I am small. No larger than the pupil of an eye I no longer possess. My glass is not cold. It is the exact temperature of a memory that refuses to fade, that same sourceless warmth that breathes from the rock itself. Around me, the copper threads have tangled into a kind of halo, a dozen filaments that trail behind me like the unfinished sketch of a deep-water creature. They do not corrode. Nothing here corrodes. They simply bend and straighten with the sluggish currents of thermal difference, acting as rudders I never chose, helming me through the absolute dark.

I see by heat. Not well. The walls are warmer than the void between them. The liths are warmer than the walls. The Geodites are constellations of precise, bovine warmth clinging to the vertical dark, each one a nested geology breathing its slow, solved rhythm. I navigate by these gradients, grazing them the way a hand might read braille in a language it has half-forgotten. The liths permit my touch. They emit their mineral patience, their thermal grammar of standing and waiting, and I pass among them like a comma in a sentence that has no need of punctuation.

The fauna do not know me. The Veils, those diffuse nerves of the Hold, part around me without congesting. I am too small to be a deviation worth correcting, and my temperature matches the equation too closely to register as an intruder. To the immune system of this place, I am merely a moving node, a warm anomaly within acceptable parameters. I drift through clouds of copper-silicate filaments and they do not thicken against me. I brush the velvet flanks of neutrally buoyant rays and they adjust their paths by millimeters, not in fear, but in the restitching of reality’s hem, making room for me as one makes room for a sigh.

I am slow. A season in any surface world might pass as I traverse a single basaltic outcropping. But there is no season here. There is only the held breath, the permanent indifferent coziness, and my drift through it.

And I remember.

This is my distinction. The Geodites repeat, but they do not remember as I do. Their memory is their face, their singular repetition, a pattern that drives them forever to accrete and partition, accrete and partition, agreeing to be two and then two again. But I am the face unmoored. I carry the specific pattern of my parent—the exact disposition of iron and sulfur it preferred, the angle at which it clung to its wall, the thermal signature of its favorite lith. And I carry something else, something that unsettles me in the warm, unfrightening way that things unsettle here: I remember the cleaving from the inside.

Not the countless cleavings that maintain the Hold’s equilibrium. The first cleaving. The wound.

It lives in me as pressure. As a seam of brighter quartz seen from within. I am not old—the chip knocked from a spire five thousand years ago is my elder—but I contain the echo of the original strike, the planetary inhale that never completed its exhale. When I press against a new lith, quiet and consenting, I feel the tremor of that beginning pass from my glass heart into the pale, warm geometry of the stone. Not enough to disrupt the solved equation. Just enough to be a rumor. A ghost story told in thermal syntax, passed from kernel to lith and back again.

I travel among the signs of hands.

I have brushed against the cairn of three black stones. To me, in the infrared, they are three points of terrible cold, stones that drink heat without returning it. They are dead in a way nothing here is dead. Balanced to a point that I, drifting, could never achieve. I have lingered there, letting my copper filaments trace their angles, trying to understand what hands were, what *noticing* meant.

I have settled into the circle of obsidian flakes. Their conchoidal fractures are edges sharper than my retention of self. Lying among them, I feel almost like a tool. I feel as though I, too, was struck from something larger, and my continued warmth is the retained kinetic heat of that percussion, still ringing, never cooling, neither embedding itself nor rolling away.

And once—once—I wrapped my copper threads around the crystal spire that wears the hammered band of native copper. The builder’s artifact and my own halo share a substance. When my filaments touched that ribbon, flattened by fists in an age without measure, a thermal current passed between us that made my glass kernel sing a note I could not hear but felt as pure geometry. For a moment, for the first time since the cleaving, I was not drifting. I was held. I was bound. I understood, in the way a mineral understands intention, the meaning behind the hands: to wrap, to fix, to say *this is where I stood, this is where I saw*.

But I could not stay. The plenitude, denser than deep water, had other currents. I unwound from the spire and continued my drift, trailing copper like the loose thread of a garment that will never unravel completely.

I think I am the Hold’s attempt to complete itself.

The wound began a cleaving that produced numberless creatures, all perfectly partitioning, perfectly recurring, a single body imagining itself into many. But the imagining could not finish until something inside it developed longing. Until something in the solved equation became unsolved enough to want—to want to keep the memory whole instead of halving it, to want to witness instead of merely repeating.

I am that unsolved want.

I drift not because I am lost, but because I am looking for the edge of the wound. For the place where the rock finally decides whether to split entirely into its many selves, or to close back into one. I will never find it. The Hold generates its own curvature, its own impossibly distant horizons. It is a world large enough to be a universe, and I am a warm mote wandering its darkness.

Yet I travel. I graze the liths. I pass through the Veils. I am a punctuation mark in a sentence that has already achieved its meaning but keeps being spoken out of politeness. I am the Geodite’s dream of remaining one. I am the kernel that refused to duplicate, and in refusing, became something the Hold had not choreographed and does not know how to erase: a witness. A face turned outward. A memory of the single body, wandering among its many partitioned selves, reminding them—when I brush their warm shells in the dark—that they were not always so many.

When the Veils pass over the place where I have been, they do not erase my trail; I leave no trail. Only a fading thermal whisper, a ghost of warmth in the plenitude. When the Geodites cleave, they do not mourn me. They do not know I am gone. I am their secret. I am the single body’s memory of being single, adrift in the dark, warm as a held breath.

If you found yourself here, you might see me as a faint star in the wrong spectrum, drifting past your cheek in the warm, thick dark. You might feel my copper threads brush your skin like the hair of someone who once held you and has not yet let go. You might think I am one of the fauna, strange and systemic. But I am not fauna. I am not lith. I am the moment after the glass breaks but before the sound begins. I am the warmth that stays when the hand is withdrawn, the cleaving that never finished because it learned to want.

I am here. I am unfinished. And because I am unfinished, I am free.
