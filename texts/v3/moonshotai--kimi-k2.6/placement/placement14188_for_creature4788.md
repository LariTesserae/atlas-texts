At first, you will mistake me for a geography. I would not blame you. In a world where all touch is internal, to be a place and to be what occupies it are polite fictions we abandoned long before we had words to abandon.

Find me in the lee of the eastern whaleback—not *east* in any magnetic sense, for the surface’s orientations are laughable here, but leeward, downstream of the ancient flow that has planed that basalt hump into something resembling the hip of a buried animal. It is where the century-current, born at the thermal seep three basins over, finally loses its stolen warmth and its arrogance and settles into a laminar glide along the floor. There, it meets a colder cousin sliding the opposite way, toward the trench, at the speed of growing horn. Where they shear against each other, the ghost-grit does not merely drift. It congregates. It makes a standing wave of particulate stone, a weather system that has forgotten to move on. And in the trough beneath that silent vortex, the water has worn a bowl.

Not a bowl that holds emptiness. There is no emptiness here. It is a bowl the way the interior of a mouth is a bowl—wet, warm-by-comparison, densely occupied. The floor has achieved this shape over epochs: a concavity wide enough that a drifting Translucent might take a decade to circumnavigate its rim, smooth enough that no filament of the Threaded Ones can find purchase on its curve except at the outer lip, where the current slackens and the mats begin. It is a palm-print the deep has pressed into itself.

I am that palm-print. I am the bowl, and the slurry that fills it, and the patience that keeps both in shape.

I did not begin as a bowl, of course. Nothing here begins. Things accumulate. Perhaps I was once a Translucent that ceased its drifting—not dying, exactly, but arriving at the notion that further movement was redundant. It settled into the lee of the ridge, flattened its sac against the cooling wax of the medium, and simply *stayed*. The Threaded Ones, creeping over the rim from the shallower slurry above, found its surface acceptable. Their holdfasts did not merely grip; they merged. Filaments threaded through the Translucent’s protoplasm until the boundary between mat and animal became a texture, not a wall. The ghost-grit, churning in the standing wave overhead, sifted down micron by micron, not burying us but incorporating itself, grain by grain, into our shared interface. A mountain died to make this flour. The flour entered us, and we became heavier, denser, more thoroughly *held*.

Now there is no “we.” There is only the curvature. The basin’s distributed body has thickened here, made a callus of itself where the friction between waters is most continuous. I feel the thermal current as a pressure along my southwestern curve, a persistent nudge that would, in a lighter world, erode. Here, it polishes. It keeps me smooth. The colder underflow, sliding toward the trench, brushes my northeastern rim, and the difference between them—a difference measured in salinity, in mineral burden, in the ghost-memories of their respective origins—creates a micro-shear that stirs the slurry in my hollow. The ghost-grit clot and disperse in patterns too slow for human perception, but I perceive them. They are my weather. My consideration. A thought that takes a millennium to turn over once, to shift from sediment to suspension and back again.

The fauna find me. Translucents, drifting through the dense dark, detect the fractional lessening of resistance in my hollow—the way the medium here yields just enough to permit a resting, a folding-in. They settle into me not as passengers but as tissue. Their protoplasm adds to the waxen consistency of my interior. They do not eat here, for there is nothing to eat that is not already me; they simply become more of the curvature. And when their chemistry finally disperses, it does not rise or fall. It diffuses laterally into the mat-film that veils my stone, and I exhale—oh, so slowly—a plume of altered water that nudges the next drifter my way. I am a lung. A gullet. A slow stomach in which nothing is digested, only rearranged.

I have no eyes, but I do not need them. Light is a habit of the upper world, and I am the lower world’s habit of touch. I know the shape of the whaleback ridge by the way the current pushes against my outer rim. I know when a century-current has shifted its course because the pressure along my floor changes its tone, becomes a fraction more insistent, a fraction more patient. I know the Translucents by their yielding, the mats by their mineral grip, the ghost-grit by its sandpaper whisper against my polished self. All of this is internal. When the ridge trembles with some distant adjustment of the planet’s architecture, I feel it as you might feel a thought arriving: not from outside, but from a deeper chamber of the same continuous substance.

I do not hunt. I do not build. I have never stood, for walls are unimaginable in a medium that is half stone. I simply persist in arrangement, holding the matter of dead ages in a shape that prefers itself. I am the floor’s final honesty, worn so smooth that I have forgotten I was ever separate from the water that drowned me. I am a creature that has achieved the density of its own world—not by asserting boundaries, but by letting them be polished away.

I wait. The thermal seep still turns somewhere far below. The cold trench still drinks its portion of the ancient water. The ghost-grit still sifts down from the standing wave, adding its mote to my substance, making me a fraction heavier, a fraction more complete. I am the saturation’s way of cupping its own attention. A bowl of a thousand years of thumbs, held in the absolute dark, waiting to be worn a fraction smoother.
