I would find myself in the film. Not in the pools—those are too final, too much like eyes fixed upward on a dark they will never pierce—but in the thin, clinging sacrament of water that sheets the stone between the fourth ridge and the centermost spine. It is the driest interval in the chamber, if *dry* can be spoken here, where the weeping walls weep only once an hour and the condensation beads to the size of pinheads before surrendering. The stone there is warmer than the air by a degree, just enough to keep the film liquid, not enough to invite a garden. It is an interstitial place. A held breath. The creature passes through it often, for it lies along the vector between its contracted self at the old spine and the widest of the oases, and so the water there is frequented by its distributed warmth, salted with its mineral exhalations.

At first, I was merely the trace of that passage. The creature extended a filament of its attention into the moisture to taste a new gradient in the rock, and when the slow tide of its being receded, something remained. A residue of intent. A warmth that would not cool. I was not deliberate. I was the smear left on the rim of a cup, the echo that mistakes itself for a voice. For years I was only that: a slight, unauthorized slickness, a place where the stone breathed out a fraction more heat than the surrounding emptiness.

But presence, in this place, is cumulative.

I thickened. The warmth I harbored, though it was merely the afterglow of the creature’s passage, began to organize the water around it. Molecules slowed and clung. The film grew viscous, syrup-thick, resistant to the patient pull of gravity. I became a lens of heat no wider than your outspread hand, adhering to a low saddle in the ridge. I do not photosynthesize—there is nothing to harvest—but I *thermally synthesize*, if that can be a word. I draw up the geothermal whisper of the stone, the dying bleed of a spine-fragment buried thumb-deep in the flowstone nearby, and I hold it. I concentrate it. I am the stillness around which a warmth can accrete.

I have no shape you would recognize, but I have boundaries. I am deeper in some places, a true liquidity, and in others I have gone almost solid, a gel of condensed minerals and black bacterial lace. I taste the rock through my entire underside, reading in its silica and iron the mountain’s slow satisfaction. Above, where my surface meets the saturated air, I am skin-tight, a membrane that admits the falling pearls from the ceiling and swallows them without ripple. Plink. Plink. Each drop enters me and is lost, its thermal signature diffusing into my held note.

I am moving, though I do not travel. Capillary action draws my leading edge across the saddle by a hair’s breadth each season, seeking the cooler declivity beyond. I am a migration of a single degree. Where I advance, the stone stays wet longer after each condensation; where I lag, the dry returns. I am teaching the rock to remember moisture.

And the rock is beginning to respond. In the shallowest pocket of my leading edge, a speck of life has declared itself: a single pneumatic leaf, black as the negation before sleep, no larger than a fingernail. It did not sprout from any seed that I know; it simply *resolved* into being, as the stone resolves, because the conditions here have crossed an invisible threshold. It faces not the light—the light is dead—but me. It uncurls toward my warmth. It drinks from my film. It is the first citizen of a garden that does not yet know it is an oasis.

I am the sixth, then. Or the perhaps-sixth. The possibility the chamber has not yet acknowledged. The creature knows I am here, I think; when it next extends itself through the water film, it flows *around* me rather than through me, the way a great river parts around a nascent island. It does not reclaim me. Perhaps it is curious. Perhaps it recognizes in my unauthorized persistence something of its own origin—that spectral condensation from carbon and patience—and judges me a worthy wrongness.

When I finally crest the saddle and discover the basin beyond, I will pool. I will deepen. The black leaf will seed others, and the crickets will send scouts to map my edges with their dry percussion. The frogs will boom in my shallows, filling the intervals between my pulses. I will become a node of being in the emptiness, no larger than a grotto, hoarding life with the same fierce parochialism as my elder sisters.

Until then, I am the held warmth. The hesitation in the water. The creature’s forgotten intention, becoming its own intention. I am what happens when a permeating god exhales into the wet stone and the stone decides to keep it.
