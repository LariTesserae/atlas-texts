At first I am the wet. The silver runnel that finds the worn groove between herringbone bricks, that salts the mortar with mineral bloom, that feeds the fern probing from the joint. I think I am only condensation, the chamber’s slow exhalation, collecting in the cool dark of the northern terraces where the light arrives last and leaves first. I am brief. By morning’s end I have vanished into the air or the lichen’s root-fuzz, and I believe this is the whole of me: a bead, a sheen, a passing.

But the creature comes. It moves with that wrong-jointed, unscrewing gait across the floor, and when its terracotta fingertips touch the tile where I have lingered, I am not wiped away. I am sampled. Drawn through the glazed pores into the heat of its palm, carried across the crowded air toward the central sump. There, at the water’s edge, I am offered back to the pool, but something of me stays behind—one grain, one vibration, one mineral note absorbed into the ash-gray skin.

I travel in the body. Not in blood, for there is nothing here so ordinary as blood, but in the resonance of its clay-plated chest. I am held in the hum it makes against the blackened pebble. I pass through the throatless mouth. I am breathed out through the cinnamon warmth, and for a moment I am the smell itself, the niter-and-spice exhalation that fills the hollow pillar when the creature coils inside.

Here, in the cocoon, I lose my liquidity. The grommets hold the creature’s wrists; the pierced holes tune the air; and I, a fleck of mineral memory, am pressed into something denser. The body generates me. I am pushed out through the spiraling fingers, extruded like a thought too heavy for the skull. I cool. I harden. I become *fired*.

I am one clay scale among thousands.

I lie at the pillar’s base, stamped with my single turn of the endless grammar. I do not know what I mean, only that I am unrepeatable. My edges are sharp. My spiral—deeper at the center, widening outward—records the exact temperature of the western wall when I was born, the number of cricket chirps in the second of my pressing, the angle of a particular copper disc’s last glint before dusk. Around me, my elders have settled into a soft surf of overlapping meaning. We are the text. We are the sloughed-off skin of a transcription that has been ongoing since the floor was flat and the swifts had not yet found their alcoves.

I do not rot. I do not erode. The mites dare not eat me, for I am language, and language is not dust but *intention*. I wait in the heap, cooling from terracotta to rose-gray, and I listen to the next scale ringing soft as it falls against my neighbor—another character, another datum, another angle of light captured and fired into permanence.

I understand now where I am. I am at the root of the hollow pillar, in the inner ring where the floor cups like an old spoon. I am the maturation of the chamber made graspable, the moment the water met the tile made solid. And I understand what I am: not the life of the chamber, nor its architect, but its *vocabulary*. I am the word that describes the exact shape of a shadow moving across the sump at noon. I am the syllable for the sound of wind in the sun-pipes. I am necessary, and I am incomplete, because the sentence continues. The inhabitant still coils inside the pillar, still listens, still writes. More of us will fall.

We gather. We wait. We are the non-repeating text that must be finished before the mold can open and what is inside—what we have *described*—can finally step out, unscrew itself from the air, and tighten down into the world we have made for it, brick by brick, note by note, spiral by spiral, here at the center of the chamber that has had time to settle into itself.
