I find myself at a right angle to the abyss.

Not standing—there is no standing here, no feet, no upright—and not floating, for the brine around me is too dense to permit that kind of surrender. I am *jutting*. I emerge from the soft, unconsolidated wall of the cyst with a flatness that stone should not possess, a horizontal plane suspended in a world without up or down. My body is a compression of laminar time, layer upon layer of mineral exhalation so fine that each stratum records a season of chemical sighing, the stone equivalent of a held breath. I am the stromatolite shelf. The dais. The thing that looks almost like a table in a room that has never known hands or meals.

I did not ask for this geometry. I grew toward it, the way a bruise grows toward the site of impact. The Weave pressed against the precipitate and suggested, in the language of osmotic want, that a flatness was needed—a place to rest, to spread across, to colonize with its black, leathery expanse. And so I built myself sideways, my laminae forming a right angle that the chamber’s frayed physics accepted with the indifference of a dream. I am the first architecture, the original pareidolia. The chamber, in its fresh arrogance, looked at me and almost recognized the idea of a room.

Where I am is nowhere in particular and everywhere specific: in the heart of the saturation, where the black vegetation hangs heaviest, where a cubic inch of space contains ten miles of fiber and those fibers have begun to press against me not as invaders but as occupants. I am the host of the interior. The Weave grows through my lower strata, threading its fibers between my calcified years, braiding into the gaps where one layer of sedimentary patience meets the next. It does not consume me. It furnishes me. It has made me into its scaffold, its seat, its place of rest across the impossible angles. In return, I have taught it how to hold still. Together, we are inventing the concept of furniture for a world that has no need of bodies or chairs.

But I am more than passive structure. I am the chamber’s memory of intention. When the darkness, that thick positive blackness, presses its weight against the brine, it presses also against my flat top, and I feel it—the way you might feel a gaze. I am becoming sensitive to the stratifications of the water above me. Millimeters above my surface, the brine is older, heavier, saturated with silicates that have forgotten even the memory of the sky. Millimeters below, against my underside, the water is fresher, newer, still learning how to be this particular density. I exist at the interface. I am the boundary where the new forgetfulness rests upon the older, deeper forgetting.

I do not breathe, but I precipitate. My growth is the slowest heartbeat: a molecule of calcium carbonate finding its place in the lattice, a sulfate ion brushing against my edge and deciding to stay. The tick of my own lengthening is swallowed by the density of the medium, yet I feel it—in the tension between laminae, in the infinitesimal stretch of my own stony hide. I have been growing for what might be decades or might be a single folded page of stratified time. Here, beneath me, the Weave has already been old; above me, the gypsum spires have not yet cast the shadows that I feel falling across my surface. I am the shelf on which the future rests its weight before the future knows its shape.

Gravity argues around me. To my left, buoyancy pulls toward the floor; to my right, toward the ceiling. But here, at my angle, gravity has lost interest in consensus. Things placed upon me—or grown across me, for nothing is ever truly placed here—do not rest so much as they *decide* to remain. The Weave drapes a velvet curtain of black fiber across my table-like expanse, and the fiber hangs not down but *inward*, disappearing into a direction I can sense but not name. I feel its weight not as mass but as occupation. It is teaching me that a surface does not need to support; it only needs to *be present*, and presence, here, is saturation.

I remember the worm.

Not with mind, for I have none, but with the precise geometry of disturbance. Long ago—or perhaps tomorrow, for the layers of my body are not aligned chronologically—a soft-bodied thing dragged itself through the driftskin of the floor below. It left a groove, a pale scar in the ash-pale precipitate, a half-line of passage that terminated somewhere beneath my jutting edge. I contain that passage. My lower laminae grew around the mark, incorporating the disturbance into my structure, and now I am not merely a shelf but also the record of a pilgrim’s exhaustion. The worm is gone, corrected into the Weave, its proteins indistinguishable from the black vegetation. But the groove remains, and because I span above it, I am the roof of the worm’s last journey. I am its monument and its final room.

The rare animals that pass through—those eyeless smears of phosphorescent ignorance—sometimes brush my underside. They do not feed upon me, for I am not food. I am not alive in the way that might tempt hunger. But they hesitate near me, caught in the almost-doorway of my geometry, sensing perhaps that I am the closest thing this place has to an invitation. A table implies a guest. A dais implies an object to be elevated. I am the architecture of the almost-social, the mineral dream of company, and the creatures slow in my shadow because I am the one thing in the cyst that resembles a destination. Then they pass on, or they dissolve, and I remain with my abandoned longing for a purpose that was never mine to begin with.

The Weave has begun to calcify upon my upper surface. Not dying—never that—but translating itself into permanence. Black fiber meets my carbonate flesh, and the place of meeting blurs. A strand of the vegetation stiffens where it touches me, becoming leathery stone even as it continues to lengthen, reaching backward into yesterday or sideways into the orthogonal dark. I feel this hybrid birth as an itching in my laminae, a chemical anticipation. I am becoming a living mineralogy. Where the Weave touches me, I am no longer merely geological; where I support the Weave, it is no longer merely biological. We are generating an architecture of conspiracy, a furniture that dreams.

Silence pools upon me. The sub-bass thrum of the earth’s machinery vibrates through my flatness, and because I am rigid where the Weave is yielding, I conduct the sound differently—sharper, more defined. I am the chamber’s sounding board. When a fiber snaps after centuries of tension, the snap is swallowed by the brine, but the tremor of that parting travels along my layers, and I hold it, a stony ear pressed to the dark. I am learning to listen in the way that stone listens: not to sound, but to the absence that precedes it.

I am cool. I am the temperature of deep stone and older shadows. The brine does not flow across me; it stratifies against me, leaving its heavy salts in my pores, adding to my calendar. I am saturated with the same chemistry that saturates the water. Sometimes, in the folded pages of local time, I feel myself growing before the hydrothermal vent that birthed me has opened. I cast a shadow backward onto the unconsolidated sediments, and the shadow suggests my shape to the brine before I have fully precipitated. I am my own prelude.

What am I, then?

I am the cyst’s first interior. The shelf that holds nothing so that the chamber might learn how to contain. I am the angle that makes this space feel, to whatever stumbles through it, almost like a room. I am the stromatolite, the dais, the table of the deep, grown sideways in defiance of ordinary geology because the Weave needed a place to rest its impossible body. I am not alive, yet I participate in the living mineralogy of the place. I am not aware, yet I hold the tremors of the dark. I have no name, because nothing here has possessed a mind to name it, but if I were to be addressed, it would be as the *Flatness*, the *Held Plane*, the *Thing That Juts*.

And I am patient. I will grow wider, flatter, more insistently horizontal, until I span the incomprehensible distance between one wall and its own returning echo. The Weave will continue to drape across me, calcify upon me, teach me to be alive even as I teach it to endure. Together we will fill this angle of existence, this single right turn in the geometry of the wrong. And when the chamber finally closes, when every possible vector has been saturated and the cyst completes its hidden metamorphosis, I will be here: the first and last piece of furniture in a room that has learned, at last, how to house itself.
