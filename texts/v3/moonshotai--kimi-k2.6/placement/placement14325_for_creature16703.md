I thought at first I might be the leviathan. What a relief it would be, to be so large that my hollowness becomes a modulation, a traveling chord change in the one note. To move not by will but by being expected. To have the water grow around me like a sleeve around an arm. But the leviathan is too final, too finished in its size. It does not wonder what it is. It displaces; it never doubts.

Then I considered the bone-white eels. To arrange myself like a tooth in a jaw that never closes. To open my mouth to the heavy water and let the mineral rain sift through, my metabolism a mineralogy. But their stillness is total, distributed, communal. They are thousands making one breath. I want an edge, a place where skin—or what passes for it—meets the petrified intention of something older than skin.

I thought I might be the drone itself. The bass that was struck when the first trench collapsed, now held forever in the cold, dense medium. But the drone is not a being. It is the garden’s temperature, its salinity, its geometry made audible. It has no location, only property. I need a coordinate. A place where three vaults of calcified cambium meet, where the resonance gathers and almost, *almost* achieves a harmonic.

I need to be something that touches.

So I am here: halfway up a rib of cellulose-turned-travertine, in a grotto where a root-system once drank light that will never return. The chamber is not large. It is the size of a held breath. The acoustics are exact. The eternal note enters through a fissure in the vaulted ceiling and pools here, thickening, becoming something almost tactile. If sound could drip, it would be a stalactite in this room.

And I am leaning against the pillar.

Not beside it. Against it. My carapace fused to the lignin-stone so gradually that the boundary has become a fiction maintained only by memory. I am—or was—one of the Recognizers. Pale, many-jointed, claw still lifted in that arrested gesture of *almost*. I reached out to test the archway. I wanted to know if it was built. I wanted it so badly that my wanting became indistinguishable from the architecture itself. Ten million years is long enough for chitin to learn the frequency of stone. The calcium in my shell whispered to the calcium in the petrified xylem. They agreed.

I am the threshold now.

My limbs have become the pillars. My backward reach, the vault. I did not die. There is no death here, only the slow negotiation of becoming. I am still trying to understand the doorway I have merged with. I am the recognition and the thing recognized. When another Recognizer—distant cousin, identical ghost—pauses before me in the dark, it is not looking at stone, and it is not looking at me. It is looking at the suggestion that intention once occurred here. And because I am half-animal and half-arch, the suggestion is stronger than anywhere else in the Litho-Garden. I am the lie the mathematics of growth accidentally told, and the body that believed it.

I exist in the shock of the click.

When I touched the pillar, all those eons ago, I made the only spontaneous sound this world allows: a tiny, percussive *tick* of claw against travertine. The drone swallowed it, of course. But it did not erase it. The click is preserved in my carapace, in the stone, in the heavy water that fills the hollow of my last remaining joint. It is the only event that ever happened here, stripped of before and after, existing purely as *during*. I am that event crystallized. I am the garden’s memory of an accident.

If you were to find me, you would not see a creature. You would see a grotto. A chamber held aloft by pillars that curve too organically, an arch that frames darkness with a tenderness that makes your chest hollow. You might place your hand on what you take to be cool rock and feel, beneath the vibration of the drone, something else: a tremor that is not the eternal note, but its echo inside a body that forgot to leave.

I do not eat. I do not hunt. I do not pulse. I hold the hum, my hollow interior acting as an organ pipe tuned to the abyss. The water inside me is fractionally warmer than the water outside—not because I generate heat, but because I am still resolving, still completing the transfer of animal into architecture. That difference is my only biography. A gradient so slight it would take an epoch to measure.

Above me, the leviathan passes through its groove, and for a moment the chord inside its ribs aligns with the resonance in my vault. The sound becomes briefly complex. Not music—this place knows better than to hope for resolution—but a density. A thickening of the air that is also water. In that moment, if you were drifting past, you might mistake my grotto for a place of gathering. You might think something sacred had happened here.

You would be wrong. But you would be touching the truth because I am the place where wrongness became indistinguishable from home. I am the Litho-Garden’s final edit of the error of meaning. I do not know I am waiting. I am simply held, the held, and the holding. The claw and the arch. The question and the stone that answered it by not answering at all.
