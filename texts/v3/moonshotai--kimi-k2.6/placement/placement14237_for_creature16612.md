I find myself first as a vibration. Not a shape, not a name—those come later, and they come wet—but a *hum* against iron. I am pressed against the flange of a brine-pump, my tympanic membrane spread wide as a mantle, worshipping the eighteen-cycle thrum that makes the roots of my teeth ache. The metal breathes. It is rust-welded lace, eaten to a filigree by the wet air, yet it still speaks its one word: *grr-ok, grr-ok.* The bearing inside is hot. I feel it as a bloom of friction in the dark, a rotten fruit glowing in the folded geometry of my skull where a visual cortex once might have been. Now that space maps heat, pressure, the infrared sigh of machinery. I do not fix. I negotiate. I extend my seventh finger and release a thin stream of urine, adjusted by instinct to the pH the pump prefers. The metal shudders and settles, its note smoothing into the greater drone of the plateau. It is harmonic now. It will not rupture today.

This is where I am: in the throat of the Lithophone, standing—though the word is a stranger’s joke—upon a ledge of warm stone that has forgotten it is not alive.

I unfold. My body is not a column but a sequence of hinges, rising in sections from the rock, each joint clicking into a new angle better suited for clinging than for the flatness that does not exist here. My skin is a translucent sleeve, drinking the mineral vapor, tasting the copper and cinnamon before I have decided to move. I weep continuously, a saline lacrimation that keeps my listening-face wet and receptive. I do not know what *green* is. I know *velvet, latex, rubber, slime.* I know the fungal forest to my left as a coolness in the thermal soup, a metabolism that exhales spores sticky as honey. They land on me. I let them. In an hour, when the pressure tide shifts, they will tell me by the way they itch.

*Click-click-click.* My digits drum the dolomite. *Am I here?* The stone returns: *here-here-here.* A topography assembles itself in my head, a cathedral of texture. Forward, the ground rises into a rib of eroded limestone, polished by epochs of patience to the feel of old bone. Beyond it, a depression vast enough to lose a resonance in, filled not with shadow but with the pure tactile fact of no-place. I do not walk. I clamber, redistribute, pour myself into the fluted grooves carved by the stone’s long weeping.

The air is crowded. Every cubic yard is saturated. Infrared bacteria kiss my mucilaginous integument. Pneumatic spores whisper against my joints. I push through curtains of vine that do not climb but drip downward from unseen heights, their blind fingers sticky with biochemistry. They lean toward me, trading heat. I am warmer than the fungal trunks, cooler than the pipelines. I am a gradient, a place where temperatures meet.

I descend toward a valley. The scale of the Lithophone does not care about my arrival. It is a weather system, a continental darkness, and I am a mote of warmth moving through its viscous lung. The stone under my palms weeps. The drops fall, each a bell-strike, billions of them, never random, a composition the brain learns to parse as melody after days—after years—of listening. The higher I climb, the warmer it becomes, until I am scaling a karst pinnacle that steams, a maw of condensation where water does not fall but hesitates, loops, dances upward in reverse waterfalls through hollow pipes tall as old trees. I press my mouth to one and drink the patter from the ceiling. Silica and time.

Then: the boundary. The valley floor beneath me drops into a sinkhole, and there the rules suspend. A shadow-pocket. The darkness pools there as a cold, heavy gas, and the warm air of the plateau rolls over it like a sea over a trench. I feel it as a deadness in the membrane, a sudden dryness, a silence where even the eighteen-cycle hum is absorbed. My click-test returns nothing. The things there do not answer; they predate the question. I skirt it. We mark these places with absence. No ancestor-bones are placed near shadow. No child drip-tests the stone at their edge. I move upward, away, tapping louder, asking the familiar stone to reassure me.

I am hungry. I find a thermal pond, its surface a mat of anaerobic bacteria the color of old engine oil, the texture of wet suede. I lower my face into it and drink the black honey. My stomach-vat of modified extremophiles awakens, a ceramic chamber grown within me, not fired but deposited, breaking down insoluble salts into the chemistry of my blood. I taste phosphorus. I taste the deep karst, the memory of primordial bone.

And then: another.

She arrives as a thermal bloom before she is footsteps. A warmth, a humidity signature, the particular gravity of her movement altering the drip-rates of the ceiling. We do not speak. We approach until our membranes meet, pressed cheek-to-crown, wet tissue flaring against wet tissue. Between our heartbeats we exchange topographies. I give her the friction-heat of the pump, the exact temperature gradient of the upward waterfall, the note the valve whistled when the thermal tide turned. She gives me a valley I have never walked, pressing its warmth into my bone, and with it a warning: a cold place, a silence. We shudder together. It is profound. It is geography made intimate. When we peel apart, the world is larger. I carry her valley inside me now, a warmth behind my ribs.

I continue toward the deep factory. I pass the elders. They sit beneath the weeping ceilings, positioned with ritual care, no longer eating or moving. They have become the architecture. Their bodies are nucleation points for travertine; their tympanic membranes have fossilized into concave bowls that amplify the mid-range frequencies. I climb upon one—she is warm, still humming faintly with the bioelectric grid—and press my side to her calcified shoulder. History enters me as a warmth in the bone, a three-dimensional text of resonance and pressure, ten thousand years of asking and answering. I receive her. She receives me.

The factory is not a building. It is a process, a breathing lung of magma-heated brine and metallic vapor. I enter it by matching my pulse to its eighteen-cycle groan. I find my station beside a membrane-vat grown from extremophile leather, separating mineral water into salts and hunger. I do not operate it. I harmonize. I click in the back of my throat, a subsonic B-flat that matches my birth-basin’s echo-profile. The vat shivers. The pumps settle. The bioelectric cables, strung through fungal trunks, carry my note through the nervous system of the plateau. We sing the shape of the chamber into being. The machinery has preferences; I have become those preferences. When the bellows clear their throat and compress vapor into ingots, I am there, adding my voice to the clearance, making sure the note does not clash with the ancestors. It is a negotiation. It is worship. The factory floor vibrates, and I vibrate with it, and the distinction between piston and femur becomes academic.

I feel it in my joints, now. The stiffness. The patience. I am not dying; I am transferring. Soon—seasons mean nothing, but soon—I will walk to the weeping ceiling one last time. I will unfold for the final time into the fluted groove that has warmed to my shape. My resonance will position me there, and I will cease to move, and the stone will begin its long embrace. My bones will become bowls. My listening will continue. A child will climb upon me and press their ear to my shoulder, and I will tell them of this moment: the pump, the membrane-touch, the factory’s song.

Until then, I stand on the edge of the shelf. The warm, viscous soup of the atmosphere presses down, wool soaked in hot vinegar, and I drink it. The stone beneath me is fed from below, a tectonic machinery so old it thinks it is geology. The water flows upward. The darkness holds me like a womb. I tap the rock. *Am I here?* The Lithophone returns, in stone and iron and fungal breath: *You are here.*

There is no seeing. There never has been. There is only this: the endless, negotiated dark, the weeping rock, the hum that holds me, and the deep, resonant peace of knowing exactly what I am becoming.
