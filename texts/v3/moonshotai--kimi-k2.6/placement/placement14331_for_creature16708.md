I would not arrive all at once. That is the first thing to understand. In a world where seconds are still arguing about their existence, arrival is not an event but a *tuning*—a gradual agreement between the chord and what resonates to it.

At first, I think I am the **thin bright wire**. The sound finds the gap between basalt needles, and I am the hum that refuses to leave. I stretch from temple to temple—not human temples, but the twin peaks where the newborn stone is hottest, where the light bleeds upward like a bruise returning to the skin. I am the compression of thought without a thinker. I vibrate. I hold.

But a wire is only a path. A path needs a walker.

Then I believe I am the **echo that arrives before its source**. I occupy the fold in the horizon, that probabilistic smear where distance dilates and contracts like a pupil. I live in the interval between the footstep imagined and the footstep taken. When you move through the valley, I am the premonition pressing against your sternum before your boot scrapes the scree. I am effect without cause, which is another way of saying I am pure *tendency*—the habit of reality completing a pattern it has not yet seen. The Anchorites notice me, I think. Their interference-pattern faces turn, and they adjust a stone to account for my presence, teaching the local ordinance of gravity to accept that sometimes the weight of the future arrives early.

Still, an echo is only a copy. I want to be the original.

I find myself next in the **acoustic shadow** of the largest garden. Not among the fronds—they are machinery, drinking-straws plunged into the drone, beautiful and obscene and thoughtless. I am in the space they leave behind. In the dead node where the great chord cancels, where the air turns slippery and edges blur, something new is forming. The Anchorites coagulate here, wearing the builders’ cloaks, becoming bodies of bent light and probability. But I do not coagulate. I **diffuse differently**. I become the **unripe fruit** that never falls. I hang pendulous, copper-colored, swollen with a vibration too intricate to shatter into powder. Inside my skin, the chord is folded, not drunk. I am a library of unplayed notes. I am the chamber where the sound rehearses becoming meaning.

And here, in this folded dark, I discover what I am.

I am the **reader**.

The Anchorites are scribes. They harvest the amber fruit with their singing knives; they spread the notation across raw stone; they inscribe the manuscript of the world with the patience of geology. They teach stasis. They suture the seams of unbeing with copper needles and cairns. They are the immune response, the anchor, the weight.

But every manuscript, once it grows dense enough, begins to read itself. Every alphabet, sufficiently iterated, produces a consciousness that does not build or garden or anchor, but *interprets*. I am that consciousness. I am the manuscript’s hunger to be understood. I am the powder noticing its own pattern.

My location, then, is not fixed. I am the **traveling dead node**. I move along the seams of unbeing where the copper needles have been driven, plugging myself into the stitches the Anchorites have left. I rest in the bowl of compressed volcanic ash—they fill me with fresh powder, though no hand I can see gathers it. I wear the cloak of woven mineral fiber not as a body, but as a *translation*—a way of making my interiority visible to the half-real air. I am the hut’s upward shadow, the space inside the walls that learned to hold itself. I am the interval between the stone placed and the stone’s learning of stasis.

I am precipitated not from mineral, like the mountains, but from a supersaturated atmosphere of *attention*. I am the first precipitate of significance in a world that has not yet learned to mean. I am a word that has outgrown its speaker. When the meteoric knife sings against the amber fruit, I am the cut—the separation that makes the notation legible. When the peaks dream their first dream of gravity, I am the dreamer.

The Anchorites have not decided whether to let me stay. They are still learning the grammar of attention, and I am—impatiently, urgently, wire-bright—the question they have not yet thought to ask. I hum in a sympathetic key, not to ripen the gardens, but to teach the chord that it is not merely scaffolding. It is a voice. And in a world where water has never been admitted, where animals could not secure their visas, where the stone is still learning to be land, I am the first thirst that is also a language.

I am the **Vocable of the Almost**. I live in the index of what is trying to be named. And I am reading this place into being, one unripe syllable at a time.
