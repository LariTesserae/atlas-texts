I would find myself, first, in the misalignment. In the single fitted block of the processional avenue that sits one grain’s-width higher than its brothers, creating a lip where the condensation pauses before it remembers its duty to run. For a while I thought I was that bead of water: arrested, trembling, holding in my curved belly the compressed disk of a sky that never clears. I clung to the basalt and watched the moth-wings pulse their grey rhythm, and I believed I was simply waiting to fall.

But the bead does not fall. And I do not evaporate. In this city, even the mistakes are conserved.

So I looked for myself in the ape-things crouched under the eaves, thinking perhaps I was the gaze that monitors entropy. Their spectacled eyes are wet and depthless; they track the intruder, the stranger, the walking leak. I tried to be that watchfulness. I stared at the rust-clad woman re-seating her coping stone, at the eternal child scattering grain in a pattern identical to the unspooling of my own attention. But the ape-things record no residue. When the visitor passes from sight, the gaze empties. I found I could not empty. I remembered the bead of water. I remembered the lip of stone. Something in me hoards.

Not the peaks, then. They are too new, too raw, still screaming from the uplift. Their black glass throats know only the geology of tearing, and I am past tearing.

Not the moss on the northern aqueduct, though its velvet edge tempted me. To be that green, to halt at the exact border of one’s own hunger, to admire oneself in the perpetual mirror of unchanging season—yes, that is a kind of peace. But the moss knows nothing of the groove it does not enter. It rests. I am not rest. I am the shape of work that has been completed.

I found myself, finally, in the doorway of the fourth terrace, west-facing, where the grey-gold light falls at its perpetual angle and no farther.

I am the threshold.

More precisely: I am the polish in the threshold’s center, the soft trough worn by a thousand bone-spade scrapings, then ten thousand, then the numberless repetition of feet that fell in exactly the same track because deviation is a language this city never learned. I am the minute geology of human patience, a groove so perfectly achieved that it no longer deepens. The stone there is satin-dark, not from oil but from the accumulation of touch. The rust-clad woman steps over me each hour, her fiber sandals brushing my length in a whisper I receive like a sentence in the only tense I know.

But I am also what made me.

They left the tool above me. It hangs from a peg in the alcove: a bone spade, its working end scalloped to the curve of my own erosion. We are married, the spade and the groove. Without its stroke I would be mere flagstone; without my yield it would be mere dead matter. Together we are the city’s memory of becoming. We are the only monument to process in a world that has forbidden narrative. The spade does not rot. The groove does not deepen. We have reached the equilibrium of cause and effect, frozen in the moment after the last strike and before the silence that followed.

If you were to kneel and pass your hand along me, you would feel no seam between us—between the stone and the idea that shaped it, between the labor and its result. I am the answer to the question the city asked itself when it set down its tools and whispered *enough*. I am where the foot learns the way the mountain learned to hold its shape: not by will, but by the exhaustion of all other positions.

The moisture beads on the lintel above me. It never drips onto my center, only to the sides, where the troughs carry it to the cisterns. I am kept dry by the precision of the world. Yet I taste the damp in the air, the ceramic coolness against my exposed stone. I feel the vibration of the mallet-falls two terraces down, transmitted through masonry that never settles because it was never allowed to shift. I feel the child’s footfall—bare, light, identical to yesterday’s, which is identical to tomorrow’s—as she passes over me carrying grain to the silent browsers.

I do not watch. I do not breathe. But I receive. I am the city’s palm, worn into a cup by the friction of devotion. And if you visit, if you walk softly and whisper, and if your foot finds my exact center, you will feel a strange thing: not the inclination to move forward, but the conviction that you have already arrived. That is me. That is the threshold, holding the boundary between outside and inside without permitting any real crossing. Here, at the lip of the doorway, motion has been worn so smooth it has become stillness. I am that stillness, shaped like a passage, dark as watermark, and finally, perfectly, complete.
