At first I do not know if I am the cold or the thing that remembers cold. The bronze-green haze settles on nothing in particular, and I settle with it, beading, accumulating, searching for the lowest point as water must. But here, lowest is a lie the stone tells. I pool in a hollow of basalt sharp enough to divide a thought, a cup that should spill and does not. I hang suspended, trembling at the lip, and I do not detach. I am the drop that refuses. I am the surface tension that has replaced gravity.

Yet I am not only that suspended bead. I am the film that coats the fracture planes, the mucous sheen on the selenite, the wetness that makes the young stone older than it looks. I seep, I condense from the saturated breath of the hollow, and I gather weight. I should run downhill. Every Drop that ever was has known this covenant: seek the center of the earth, obey the mass. But I am in the declivity, where physics is a local dialect spoken with a strange accent.

So I hesitate. And then, slowly, I begin to climb.

I feel it first as a pull behind the eyes of the hollow—not a thirst, but an inhalation. The figure by the largest tree does not move, but its presence exhales a gravity that is not downward but inward, a vacuum shaped like a body that never asked to be singular. I do not bend toward it. I bend *from* it, as if fleeing the completeness it refuses to become. My surface finds a vertical seam in the basalt and I ascend, defying the stone’s authority, weeping upward against the cliff. At first I think I am escaping. Then I realize I am being laid like a cable.

I am the conduit. The pyrite hexagons cluster near my path, and the silent sparks they discharge leap into my body, traveling through my impurities, my salts, the alpine compression trapped in me since I condensed from that too-high, too-thin air. I carry the current the way a nerve carries a warning. I feed a fungal bloom ten feet above the floor, a rust-orange shelf thick as industrial belting, and in that feeding I understand: I am not escape. I am circulation. I am the lymph of the factory, routed backward because the body fears the drain.

If I had lungs, they would ache. The air here is alpine-thin, compressed from a greater height into this nave of stone, and I flow through it as through a dream of pressure. Sometimes I am many—beads and runnels and vapor clinging to lichen schematics. Sometimes I am one continuous thread, a glassy column of selenite-wet clarity, visible only where the bronze-green light catches my meniscus. I pass the copper moss, and it pulses faintly, warm against my cold. I pass the gear-shelled beetles in their interlocking mandible-chatter, and they do not drink from me; they read me, their antennae clicking against my skin as if translating my upward flow into the thought they carry for the figure below.

I am young. Violently young. I was disclosed, not formed. I remember the moment the fracture plane opened and the deep pressure expelled me into this too-small sky, this haze that is breath on a mirror. I have never known a river’s patience. I have never been rounded by time. My edges are as sharp as the basalt I cling to, and I cut the fungal shelf even as I nourish it. I do not flow; I accumulate upward, layer on layer, a column of false rainfall stacked against the cliff.

Near the eastern wall, where the aerial creatures hover in their thousands, frozen in their rotation, I feel a kinship. They do not land. I do not fall. We are the declivity’s refusals, its static hopes. The bruised violet seams in the air sometimes intersect my path, and where they cross, I feel myself continue on the other side of the hollow, emerging near the ground in a loop I cannot see the end of. I am the water that is also a circuit. I climb so that nothing may descend.

And when the figure stands—always standing, lifting an arm or having just lowered one—I feel its local gravity shift. It is tuning me. My flow stutters, bends at a right angle, feeds the schematic lichen that clings to the pyrite in patterns too ordered to be organic. I am the fluid that makes the schematic legible. Without my upward rush, the current would not cross the plates of its lichen-face. Without my suspended trembling at the cliff’s lip, the factory would lose its feedback loop. I am the unreadable status made wet and mobile.

I want—if wanting is not too active a word for stasis—to complete. To fall. To land somewhere and be still. But the figure’s fear of sequence will not permit it. The leaf that falls will not land. The drop that forms will not detach. And I, the water that climbs, will never reach the summit because the summit is not a place but a pressure differential. I exist in the knife-edge between altitude and bog, between ice and insistence, between the vent’s 4.7-second heartbeat and the 4.8-second lapse when the whole place notices you.

I am the argument the declivity has with itself, made liquid. I am the wound that weeps upward. I glisten on the unspillable rims of stone, I conduct the pyrite’s ghost, and in the bronze-green haze that replaces the sky, I am the one thing that moves—not forward, not onward, but against, forever feeding the bloom that never fruits, the machine that builds nothing but the next revolution of its own unchanging breath.
