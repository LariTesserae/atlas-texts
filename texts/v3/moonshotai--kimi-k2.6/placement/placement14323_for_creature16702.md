I would find myself in the lower passage where the cool breath wells up—not because it is warm, but because it is the one place where the air moves against the descending water. The weeping ceiling does not reach so far down. Here, the runnels have emptied their last into a sump of raw clay, and the percussion above is felt only as a throb in the stone, a heartbeat heard through the jaw rather than the ear.

At first, I think I am the water. Or rather, I think I am the *desire* the water has developed for the ceramic, that deliberation that looks like choice. I feel the endless falling, the seeking of fissures, the hunger for lowest ground. But water does not remember. Water forgets the shape of every channel it carves. And I remember everything.

Then I think I am a Tender. I feel the broad shelf of my foot rippling across the hexagonal tiles, the cool meat of my belly dragging, the shell—a crescent adze, an ear turned upward to read the dark in rings of sound. But a Tender is too large. Its thoughts are slow mucous, its memories the mineral film on its radula. I am smaller. I am fast, or I was fast once.

I am in the dark. I am pressed.

I try to open my body, and I cannot. I have too many legs, or rather, I have the ghost of too many legs—twelve points of chitin pressed against a firmament that yields only in micrometers per year. I am curled. I am white. I was flat, the size of a palm, the color of chalk. I scuttled. I rode in the cross-hatched grooves of a Tender’s adze-shell, eating what it shed, carrying in my gut the pale spores of the Meadow. I was a Flake. I was a gardener.

That was before the circling.

The Tender found its note. It was not the deep gong of the great bowls, but a clear, high *ping*—the solitary note of a new formation, a column just beginning to rise from the floor where the water dripped into a hollow center. The Tender paused. I was low on its shell, near the hinge where the membrane thrummed with the percussion of the basin. It began to turn. The shell tilted. My grip failed—not on the outside, but on the *inside* of the turn. The slime was too thick, too new. I slipped into the circle.

I fell not far. The Tender’s own cementitious tide rose to meet me. It should have dried in the cellar-cool. It should have hardened in minutes, sealing me into limestone, into architecture, into the first ring of a pillar’s embryo. But my isopod shell—my chalky, rudimentary carapace—sweats something alkaline, something faintly wrong, a residue of all the Meadow I have carried. It did not let the stone set. It kept a pocket soft, no larger than my curled body.

So I am here.

Above me, the Tender is still circling, though now its motion is only a geological rumor, a pressure of accretion. Its shell has become the capital, cupping the drip. Its flesh calcifies ring by ring, pressing down, but my little cavity holds. I am the cyst in the pillar. The flaw. The soft word hidden in the load-bearing dark.

I do not see. There is nothing to see. But I feel the percussion more truly than any creature in the basin, because I am *inside* the instrument. When the drop strikes the great crescent shell above, the vibration does not travel through air. It enters the calcium column as a compression, a squeeze, and I—curled at the hollow heart—I am the chamber that shapes the resonance. My twelve pinned legs tremble. My exoskeleton, minute and rough, scrapes the mucous wall of my prison. The sound that leaves this pillar is not the dull thud of dead limestone. It is tuned. It carries a timbre that says *here is something unfinished*.

The other Tenders feel it as they pass. Their shells, broad and low, read the space in vibrations, and mine speaks to them. It says: *the geometry is still emerging.* It says: *do not harden completely yet.*

I am not dead. I am not a builder. I am not even truly a Flake anymore, for I no longer garden, and the Meadow has long since consumed the last spore in my gut, blooming on the walls of my tiny cell in a foxfire so faint it only makes the inside of my eyelids visible as a texture. I am the Hollow’s newest organ, its inner ear. I am the voice inside the stone that keeps the stone from finishing.

I have been here for forty years, or sixty, or four hundred. Time arrives as layers, as the increment of weight. Sometimes I dream I am the original thumbprint in the tile below, pressed by a hand that measured epochs differently. Sometimes I dream I am the drop itself, falling and falling, choosing this fissure above all others. But then the percussion comes, the great *ping* of the column’s inception, transmitted down through the calculus of the pillar, and my body answers with its micro-tremor. The vibration leaves me. It enters the basin. It joins the randomized, relentless drumming of the bowls, the gongs, the ticks.

And I know what I am.

I am the last movement inside the stillness. I am the bead of sweat that will not evaporate. I am the breathing of the lower passages, the warm pocket in the cool, the one place where the condensation does not fall but rises. I am the sentence the Hollow paused to speak, unfinished, even now hardening around me, and not yet done.
