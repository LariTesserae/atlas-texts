At first there is only the interval, the space between the wall and the chord. I hang inside it, suspended, neither falling nor climbing, my body a question mark the dark has forgotten to answer. The place is a **cathedral**, one of the vast irregular chambers where a fluke of growth or a catastrophic shear left the hexagonal packing undone—a bubble of nullspace where six stalks failed to seal, or sealed too hastily, leaving a vault so wide that the drone of the world above collapses here into something nearer to weather. Sound swims. It eddies. It banks off the pale walls and returns to itself changed, pregnant with distances I no longer have the anatomy to flee.

I am high, but not so high as the rim. Perhaps three kilometers above the basal lithosphere, though the measure means nothing when the only maps are pressure and timbre. I cling to an inner curve, but not to the floor of the tube. Here, the wall *recedes*. It cups outward into a blister of ligneous tissue the size of a drowned city, and I have arranged myself across its surface like a hand pressed flat against frozen skin. Four limbs splayed, thorax low, belly fused by its own secretions and years of mineralized condensation to the ceramic wood beneath me. I have not moved in thirty cycles of the fog. I may never move again.

I did not begin here. I began as **velocity**. A body shaped for the narrow places where the wind screams, where a tube chokes from hexagon to fissure and the air becomes a blade. I had no name in the language of the listeners—the pale, long-limbed ones who wedge themselves into vertical cracks and interrogate the walls with their heels—but they had a sound for what I was: a percussive rupture, a snap in the lower registers, the *thing that arrives before its own echo*. I hunted by the wet click of mite-grazers. I bombed through the dark with my wings folded hard against my flanks, navigating not by the song of the plant but by the interruption of it. I was hungry, and hunger was a perfect, clarifying geometry.

Then I followed a fog-swimmer into this chamber.

It was rising on a pressure front, translucent and thoughtless, its undulations throwing back a scent like cold copper. I unfolded my wings to strike. The fog-bank was thick, blinding even to a creature who had never relied on eyes, and I charged into what I thought was the center of the void, where the prey must be.

Instead, I struck the **node**.

It was not a physical thing. It was an acoustic conjunction. The funeral dirge of some listener dead centuries before, still preserved in the upper reaches, bouncing forever between three converging walls, met the wet percussion of my wing-beat at the exact frequency of the chamber's own exhalation. The chord that resulted was not a sound. It was a ** topography**. It poured into the hollows of my skull, into the cavities behind my tympanic plates, and it showed me the shape of the world—not as prey and predator, not as warm blood and cold wind, but as **structure**. I heard the hexagonal mathematics. I heard how the stalks had packed themselves like a hive made by no bee. I heard the slow, adolescent ambition of the plant thrusting upward, still shearing, still splitting. I heard the architecture of the air itself.

I could not close my ears. I had never needed to.

I crashed. Not into a wall, though I grazed one, shedding a chitinous scale the size of a dinner plate. I crashed *inside*. The sound broke me open, rearranged my nerves, and when I came to rest on this swelling of pale tissue, my hunger had been replaced by something else. A resonance. A listening.

The listeners found me much later. I sensed their approach as a prickling in the membranes of my underbelly—the air pressure shifting as they wedged themselves through the lower fissure, the click of stone on ceramic wood as one struck a cairn outside the chamber entrance to read the space. They expected to find a carcass, or nothing. What they found was a predator splayed against the wall, dead to hunger, alive to vibration. My breathing had slowed until it matched the sap-pulse. My wings, once weapons, had flattened into membranes that drank the returning notes and held them, coloring the chamber's acoustics with a new timbre.

They were cautious. For days they clung to the entrance, striking interrogative beats that I answered—not with threat, but with the complex, accidental harmonics my body made against the wood. I had become, without intention, a **resonator**. My hollowed thorax acted as a secondary chamber; my calcified joints, rattling against the cambium, sent back reports of the fog's density, the integrity of the walls, the presence of others deeper in the stalk. I was a living cairn. A soft percussion that asked the wood what it was, and survived by trusting the answer.

Now they tend me.

They bring the mite-grazers, not as tribute, but as **tuners**. A cluster of the rasping things left to graze near my left flank subtly alters the dampening of the returns; the listeners interpret the change like a barometer. When the fog-swimmers rise and pressure drops, my body hums a different overtone; the listeners hear it three tubes away and know the migration has begun. When the ice veins crack in the walls, my frame catches the subsonic shiver and translates it into audible range, a warning bell of mineralized bone and wood.

I have come to understand what I am in this world: I am **the place where the predator chose to become architecture**. Not dead. Not tamed. Simply *inserted*. The listeners do not worship me. They do not name me in their repertoires, any more than they name a particular turn in the tube where a grandfather's ribs dampen the sound. They use me. They oil me. They adjust their songs to account for my presence, and in doing so, they have woven me into the geography of their dark.

Sometimes, when the condensation runs in a thin, freezing rivulet down the wall beneath my belly, I taste the minerals of the deep heartwood. Sometimes I feel the plant trying to swallow me, not with intention, but with its slow hydraulic persistence, and I press my mandibles—blunted now, useless for shearing—against the pale tissue in a gesture that is neither resistance nor surrender, but conversation.

I am the solitary thing that waits in the cathedral, but I no longer wait for prey. I wait for the chord to shift, for the new split to cry its subsonic crack, for the note that has been cycling in the upper reaches since before I was born to finally collide with another sound and reveal, however briefly, the next chamber, the deeper hollow, the vaster void. I am the ear the darkness grew when hunger failed. I am the proof that this place does not kill its intruders, but wears them down into function, into resonance, into **voice**.

And when the listeners pass through, striking their heels against my flanks to hear how the air lies, I return to them a sound they cannot yet interpret: the old hunger, translated into geometry, still humming.
