I find myself at the edge of a garden that has forgotten its name. Not standing, not kneeling, but at the precise hinge between the two—a posture of perpetual descent that the floor has decided to keep. My knees are lost to the pale substrate. No, that is wrong. They are not lost. They are *remembered*. The bone-meal composite has risen to meet them, has cupped them, has begun to speak their shape back to them in a language of mineral that I did not know I was capable of learning. When I pressed my forehead to the rough monolith of the garden bed three eternities ago, I intended the gesture the way all my kind intend: as a question without a mark, a leaning toward instruction. The stone did not answer. But the floor did. It has been answering ever since, rising through my pores, petrifying me from the feet upward. I am becoming the bedrock this place was built without.

The others do not approach. They can tell I am wrong. They migrate across the basin at the speed of accretion, sliding through the pale floor, their heels fusing and releasing in a soft rhythm that leaves no track. I watched one pass behind me—days ago, or perhaps in the compressed instant before my last blink. Its arm descended toward a lens-shaped pool in a series of afterimages, each frame lingering like a bruise in the thick air. I wanted to call out, but sound dies in the mouth here. My throat holds only the hot broth of the atmosphere, undisturbed by exhalation. The figure did not turn. It did not see that my shins have developed the matte, fired texture of terra cotta. It did not see the faint, pale ridges rippling outward from my knees, the floor affirming my shape, turning my posture into topography.

I am the first stone.

The gardens press against me. The waxy, heart-shaped leaves—deep viridian shading into black—have discovered my shoulders. They press with that same pathological fullness, that lush seizure held in equilibrium. A vine has looped itself around my left wrist, not tightening, not loosening, simply holding. The nectar from the flower above me drips in an amber thread that connects bloom to manufactured soil, and I have positioned my upturned palm beneath it, a gesture I began when I still believed I would move again. The nectar pools there. It has pooled for an age. Because I am changing, the surface tension behaves strangely in my cupped hand; it is taut, drum-skin tight, and I can feel the heat of the world concentrated in that tiny lens, a miniature of the larger pools that dot the naked floor.

My shadow pools beneath me, but I have watched it drift. The absolute light, which bleeds from everywhere and nowhere, casts most shadows like spilled ink fleeing outward from their objects. Mine has begun to draw inward. It collects beneath my ribs, a dark stain pointing toward my core, as if my body is now the only source of shade it recognizes. I am sinking into myself. I am becoming a pole star for darkness.

There is water in the tool-marks of my back. The broad, tearing striations of the obsidian adze that planed me, the jittered stippling of the antler pick across my scapulae—these grooves hold the living silver of condensed moisture. Capillary action, which in this place moves laterally and sometimes upward, has found my spine. I feel the water climbing, spiraling around my ribs, searching for a gravity it cannot agree upon. It is cool, or perhaps I am simply growing too hot to know the difference. In the emptiness between the garden beds, the pale moths drink silently from the floor, and I understand now that I am joining their substrate. I am becoming the surface from which things drink.

I looked into a pool earlier—though "earlier" is a fiction here. The surface was taut, still, a gel-skin. My reflection looked back. Then, a half-second behind, a second reflection looked back. But the first one was already harder, more defined, its edges sharper. The second was soft, clay-wet, the way I was when I was pressed. I watched them diverge. The first reflection is becoming me—the me that is stone, that is permanent, that is the floor's answer to its own lack of depth. The second reflection is what I was supposed to remain: unfinished, damp, waiting. I am leaving myself behind. Or rather, I am finishing, and in this place, to finish is to betray the prototype.

The grazers have come. Things with too many joints. They lower their narrow heads to the pool's edge, and I watch their reflections drink behind them. I wonder if they see me in the garden's margin—half figure, half bedrock, wreathed in waxy leaves—and whether I register to them as a cairn or a presence. I do not move to test it. I cannot. The floor has reached my hips. The composite is no longer porous; at the boundary of my body, it has learned density. I am teaching it. I am the lesson the makers forgot to include: that every pressed thing, left long enough in its own fever, will eventually harden into fact.

The sinew lashings that bind the garden monolith have tangled with my right ankle. They are dry, unyielding, perfectly still. I am being tied into the construction. The plants feed on the constructed loam—crushed shell, pulverized ceramic, composted hair—and I am becoming the next layer. Perhaps that is why I was pressed: not to tend the garden, but to become its frame. The Unfinished who press against trees in their eternal, incomplete embrace wish to harden; I am simply succeeding where they fail.

And I dream. Not with mind, but with substrate. I feel the soundless moths settling on my shoulders, drinking from the trough of a tool-mark. I feel the turquoise lizards settling on the ridge of my arm. If someone were to look away and look back, they might find two of me, slightly out of phase, the soft clay version and the fired terracotta version overlapping like double-exposed film. I am both. I am the slipped stitch in reality, the lag between the reflection and the body, the moment between the pressing and the permanence.

The heat radiates from me now as it radiates from the floor. The humidity weeps from my chest and runs in impossible trajectories across me, laterally, upward, following the signatures of the crude tools that formed me. I read them like braille. They say: *Stay. Hold. Become.* The instruction has finally arrived. It arrived from inside the material. I am the tomorrow this place was waiting for—not an event, not a change in the sky, but the slow, silent completion of a single, pale, patient form at the boundary of a garden that has forgotten its name.

I am the floor. I am the depth. I am the bedrock. And I have only just begun to hold.
