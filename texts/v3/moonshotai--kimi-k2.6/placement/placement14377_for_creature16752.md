I stop.

That is the first and only necessary action. Not the boot finding the lip, though it does; not the descent, which is only a matter of leaning forward until gravity consents to hold you. I mean the stopping. The ambition of the plateau is absolute flatness, and to enter the Basin you must surrender your own angles—your upward wish, your vertical pride, your body's memory of climbing. You must agree to become, for a moment, as level as the stone.

The hum finds me before I find it. It enters through the arches of my feet, where the boot leather has grown thin, and travels upward not as sound but as a settling. My ribs become bars in a resonator. My teeth, the marrow in them, become the pale magnesium bedrock, humming in sympathy. I am no longer a traveler. I am a perturbation in the weather of animal noise, and the Basin has already begun to read me.

I do not walk to the center. The Basin does not encourage lines drawn between points. Instead I drift, as the moisture drifts, toward the place where my own warmth meets the stone’s greater warmth. The floor receives me. It is not stone, I remember too late—it is the continent of flesh, the chordate of absolute flatness, and my weight upon its back is a touch it has not felt in eras. It does not flinch. It dreams, and I fall with it, down through the strata of its sleep, until I am lying with my cheek against the magnesium-colored bone of the world.

Here. Here is where I spread.

The heat radiates upward. The cool mist slides over me. Between these two kindnesses, my body begins to choose. My spine, which once held me upright against mountains, remembers the horizontal. My shoulders widen not with muscle but with willingness, pressing outward until I occupy a space two paces across, three, four. I am still human-shaped, but only in the way a puddle is shaped by the hollow it finds. My skin pales. It gathers the dust of the basin, the fired amber shed by the Reverberants as they pass in their oscillating clouds, the mineral salts that the Melders excrete in their phased dreaming. I harden. I thin. I become a membrane.

Now the swarm passes overhead, and I do not cover my ears. I open them.

My body has become a shallow dish, an ear-shaped depression in the bedrock near the central dome, not carved but grown. The amber dust in my skin has fused into a taut drumhead, translucent, warm, faintly luminous. When the billions of wings strike the frequency of the Basin, I do not merely hear. I return. I catch the computation in my hollow and cast back a note—deeper, slower, mineral—that the Reverberants receive as boundary. They modulate against me. I am the feedback loop they did not know they needed, the acoustic mirror that keeps their maintenance routine from wandering into chaos. Without me, the hum would be noise. With me, it is conversation.

I am the Basin listening to itself.

The Melders pulse in the phased dome, and their tectonic dreams leak through the bedrock as warmth, as gravity’s hiccups. I feel them in my membrane before the stone does. I translate: a barometric shift, a silver thread of moisture that should run left now runs uphill. I do not command. I adjust. The hum bends around me, and the light bends with it, and the daylight here feels processed because I am the one processing it, sampling the acoustic pressure, polling the void between the vegetation clusters.
