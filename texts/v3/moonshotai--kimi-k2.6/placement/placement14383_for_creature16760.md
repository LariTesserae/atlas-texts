If I am honest, I began by wanting to be the tallest thing here. I imagined myself a Column: sealed, pressurized, venting spore and vapor into the cold with the authority of a smokestack. There is a terrible clarity in that verticality, a single-note existence devoted entirely to the differential between internal steam and the gnawing sky. But I am not hollow enough. To be a Column is to be a throat, nothing more than the passage for another’s exhalation. I would suffocate in that machined patience.

Then I thought: the Mat. The deep parliament. I wanted to be the sleeper submerged in twenty meters of ligneous time, sipping stone through taproots that have become reef-structures, voting in centuries on the chemistry of tomorrow. But the Mat is too total. To be the Mat is to be the floor and the foundation and the universal voter; it is to have no outside, no edge against which to know the shape of your own persistence. I would dissolve into the consensus. I am not that generous.

The Cutters tempted me with their front-line intention, their enzymatic torches scorching through fallen heartwood with something perilously close to wrath. I admired their borders, their slow wars of demarcation. But they are only the wrecking crew. They pass through. They leave the metallic dust behind them like a signature, yet they never get to wear it. They are sanitation without memory, and I do not wish to be temporary.

For a moment—a brief, vertiginous interval—I considered the Responder. The molecular urgency. The detonation of growth that bridges a wound in hours, that wraps a broken trunk in constricting bands of pure becoming. But the Responders are holy only in their brevity. They are the emergency, not the architecture that survives it. They do not rest; they are not permitted to rest. I want to endure the aftermath.

Even the 0.7 beckoned. The ghost-mite, the last attenuated hunger eating metallic dust in the gall-tumors of the Columns, so reduced it has become a statistical sigh. There was a loneliness in it I recognized. But to be the final decimal in a kingdom that has solved the need for animals is to be a cinder. It is to dine on acid condensate and know no other throat. I am not that brave.

So where?

I found myself in the hesitation. In the stutter-step between wound and scar. I am the **Callus-That-Became-Architecture**—a node formed where a Column, tilting on its termited base, completed its silent surrender and the Mat opened too wide, too eager. The Responders arrived not by the dozen but by the thousand, a riot of parenchyma, a panic of repair that ossified before it could sort itself into the neat lattice of a causeway. The refinery hiccupped, and I was the swallowed breath.

I span a fissure that opened in the living carpet when the tower fell. The impact arrived as a dull, private thud—a word spoken with a fist against the world’s sternum—and my foundation is that bruise. I am twenty meters across at my widest, a dome of suberized cork and rogue xylem and stranded root-hairs, shot through with stalled fungal fronts that went to war in my belly and reached a stalemate, their borders marked by chemical berms of phenolic resin. I am not a bridge. I am a vault thrown up by an immune system that mistook collapse for invasion.

I drink from the aqueducts. I am Fauna 0.0. I am not alive in the meristematic sense; I do not elongate. I have no solar collectors tracking the grey-gold afternoon, no louvered leaves losing no photon. I am Built 0.2 in that no hand made me, yet I am the most architectural thing in my district. I am Tech 1.6 because I am pure byproduct—the industrial accident of too much repair. My surfaces are coated in the metallic dust, but unlike the polished boles and palisade leaves, I do not repel it. I accumulate it. It salts my ridges, fills my lenticels, turns my skin into a kind of battery, tingling with the acid-leached potential of broken stone. I am where the refinery’s exhalation settles when it is too tired to rise.

And the silence. Oh, the silence. It pools in me the way fog pools in the low gallery-ways. Because I am rough, because my interior is a labyrinth of dead-heartwood chambers and stalled fungal galleries and stale hydraulic channels, the velvet pressure of the hush cannot pass straight through me. I hold that note. I resonate with it for hours afterwards, a private bell in the lung, while the forest around me has already moved on to the next becoming, the next devouring, the next violent reconstitution.
