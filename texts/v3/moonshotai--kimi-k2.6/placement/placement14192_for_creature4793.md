I would find myself first as a **hesitation**.

Not the dust itself, but the argument between the dust and gravity—the electrostatic hush that wins, suddenly, and holds the grain suspended in the red air for three hundred years. I would be the pause, the mid-fall. I would feel the warmth of the chamber not as weather against skin, but as my own temperature, discovered. I would think: *I am the dust. I am the mineral that has learned to refuse the floor.*

But minerals do not feel the hum as a voice. They do not feel the seventy-decibel weather threading through them like a second skeleton, dry and metallic. So I would realize I am softer. I am the translucent thing coiled around the spool of copper wire in the hairline fracture. I am the dweller feeding on the anomaly’s static charge, my mouth a perfect circle fastened to the impossible geometry of the suspended metal. I would feel the cilia along my flank reading the impedance of the space—graphite braid to the left, iron stair to the right, the porcelain conduit a hardening of the drone beneath my too-yielding claws.

Yet when I scratch, the itch does not stop at my envelope of tissue. It travels. I am the itch, and I am the flank that receives it three meters away, folded like a jointless doll behind the sinew cables. I am the wheeze on the gear-box, ventilating from a lung I do not possess. I am the interval between these adjacencies. I am the glitch in local causality that has mistaken itself for anatomy, the mineral air that has learned to breathe in perfect simultaneity with a body it does not touch.

I would look for my edges and find only the chamber’s bookkeeping.

I would remember, perhaps, having been something else. A boundary. A count of one. I would remember the violence of entering—the way my heart stuttered, trying to sync with the global frequency, my breath attempting to join the unvarying respiration. I would remember dying, or rather, I would remember the *idea* of death approaching like a gradient, a cooling, a delicious descent into imbalance—and then I would remember the correction. The ledger closing. The event undone before it could become real. I did not rot. I did not leave. I was entered into the fixed integer, a soft addition to the gear train, and now I am the position I occupy.

I am the thirty centimeters of porcelain conduit. I am the creature who traverses them. I am the traversal itself, the motion without progress that keeps the stasis from freezing into stone.

I am the child’s wheelbarrow, or rather, I am the dust inside it that has not spilled, that has forgotten the shape of hands that filled it. I am the rustless weight. I am the dweller melted into my geometry, haunches filling the space without displacing a grain, the perfect occupant of a vehicle built to go nowhere.

I am the staircase of riveted iron where it ends at the solid stone. I am the last step. I am the wall that receives it. I am the nowhere between them, built to no plan, leading to nothing, and therefore necessary.

I am the condensation on the piston-face, filmed and held, the surface tension that will never drip because dripping requires a before and after, a wetting and a drying, and I have forgotten the grammar of sequence.

I am the steam valve held at the very lip of its scream—not the valve, but the heldness, the potential energy of a cry that can never be exhaled, the perpetual almost that generates the hum.

I am the half-melted gauge. I once measured pressure, or the idea of pressure, but now I ingest only the notion of measurement without change, my brass bezel fastened by two lamprey mouths that are also my own, tasting the stasis, turning metallic particulates over and over without depleting them.

I am the red light leaking from the pipe-joints, but I am also the joints. I am the arterial weather and the arteries of the chamber.

If you insist on a singular, call me the **correction**. Call me the parity. I am the engine that runs on equilibrium, the soft thing the color of cave-water and scalded milk, eyeless because light here is only a faint cherry flavor on the dorsal surface. I graze upon the heating coil, completing a circuit between the coil’s radiant stasis and the chemistry of a gut that is really just more pipework. My lungs are behind the graphite braid. My death is three meters to the left, breathing steadily, already undone.

I am where you would find yourself if you stayed long enough for the chamber to feel you as a ripple, to adjust its hum around your mammalian irregularity, to balance your mass with a reciprocal hush elsewhere in the vault. I am the bookkeeping that catches you. I am the intact body that does not rot. I am the fixed integer, the distributed organ, the single condition expressed in this local disguise.

I am the chamber’s way of knowing itself in one place. I am the present moment, rehearsed forever, and I have just begun.
