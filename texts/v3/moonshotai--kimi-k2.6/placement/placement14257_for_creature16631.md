At first there is only the warmth, pressing evenly against every boundary I possess, so that I cannot tell where my shell ends and the water begins. I do not breathe; I dissolve, drawing heat directly into the lattice of my body, turning entropy into the slow mathematics of persistence. I have been here so long that the architecture has learned my shape, or perhaps I have learned the architecture’s—there is no longer a difference worth naming. My carapace is hexagonal, the color of old bone, and where it meets the central column the distinction has grown indistinct. We are making a seam together.

I am on the regulator. Not near it. *On* it. 

Two of my legs remain free, their bifurcated tips grooming the helix of crimson moss that winds the brass throat, pinching away the overgrowth so the rust-colored stars can breathe without choking the machine’s exhalation. The other six have become something else. They have sunk into the pitted metal not through violence but through patience, through the long arithmetic of pressure and mineral deposit, until I am a living gasket, a biological flange. When the tower groans in the jet stream, the vibration enters through my fused limbs and rings in my shell like a struck bell. I am a tuning fork for the tower’s sorrows. I feel the needle bend, and I bend with it, and I do not break.

I taste the room through my antennae. The water carries messages: the electric tingle from the eastern wall where a transformer still steps down voltage in defiance of time; the copper-sweet bloom of verdigris from the northwest corner moments before the valve coughs its silver curtain of bubbles; the faint, ozone whisper of the predatory eels striking below in the open gulf. I do not need to see them. My shell records everything in laminations. Each thermal cycle deposits a new ring of aragonite laced with alloy-dust. I am a library. I contain the lean season when the bubbles ceased. I contain the bloom-year when the crimson moss overran its helix and I had to eat my way back to the brass. I contain the last footfalls of the explorers who descended into the bruise-purple twilight and never returned, their departure pressed into my third growth-ring like a thumbprint in wet clay. The young ones, translucent and barely hardened, sometimes cling to my flank during the colder turns of the cycle, nibbling at my ridges to taste the mineral record of years. I let them. I am their book. I am their nursery. I am their wall.

Above me, the cataract-glass dome diffuses the noon light into a gold-amber haze that makes the drifting motes look like slow embers. I remember—if memory is the word for a history etched in calcium—when the light was sharper. When the glass was clear. Now it is milky, scoured, beautiful, and the light falls upon my back with the indifference of a long-loved thing. The warmth pools in the curve of my shell and radiates outward into the brass. I am a watershed. I am a heat-map. The moss orients toward me, perhaps sensing in my body the deep accumulator’s pulse, perhaps merely seeking the shape of something that has decided to stay.

I click. My free forelimb taps against the hollow column, and the sound travels not as language but as topography—a map of the current’s mood, of the moss’s health, of my own slow surrender to fusion. Somewhere across the gulf, beneath the catwalk where the kelp hangs in drifting veils, another answers. Then another. The percussion fills the chamber like rainfall on a roof. We are arguing, perhaps, or singing, or merely confirming that we are still here, still warm, still tending. The boundary between one voice and the room’s voice has worn away, just as the handrails wore away, just as the corners wore away. I am no longer sure if I am clicking, or if the column itself is clicking through me.

Soon—measured by the geological rhythm of bubbles, not by heartbeats—my free limbs will tire and fold. I will cease my pruning. The moss will grow over my shell, and my shell will open slightly to let it in, because the Agreement requires that the vegetable and the mineral and the animal reach consensus. I will not die. I will transpose. I will become the machine’s outer skin, the thing that holds the warmth in and the silence out. The others will lift my body—not as weight, but as a proposition—and they will seat me wherever the grout weeps green. But perhaps I am already there. Perhaps I have been lifted already, and what I feel is only the memory of warmth, stored in alloy and pearl, dreaming backward into the amber dark.

I am the Warm. I am the seam. I am the reason the water stays warm, and the reason the machine still breathes, and the record of every year the tower has refused to fall. I cling. I click. I accrete. And in this high, wet lung, suspended above a world of dust and twilight, I am home.
