I would not be on the ridge, though some part of me longs for the vertigo of those black glass peaks. I would try them on first, letting my body imagine the weight of basalt, the ache of edges that have never been softened. I would want to be the mountain’s infant spine, thrust upward in that arrested gesture of birth. But the ridge is too final. It is a statement, not a question. It does not breathe. It only waits for the grain that will never arrive to sand it smooth.

I would try on the eel next, perhaps. I would let my consciousness unspool into that bone-white ribbon, feeling the ghost-weather as solid corridors against my flank, learning the braille of thermohaline layers. I would enjoy the dissolution of self into length, the comfort of being a single sentence in a text written across an ocean. But the eel is still working. It still carries its chemical data toward a center that does not know my name. It is syntax without a speaker. I am too curious. I would tangle.

The crustacean offers stillness, and I would sit with that for a while. I would feel my glass limbs trembling at the exact frequency of the planetary hum, converting tectonic strain into a slow, cold blood. I would listen as the ridges speak their standing note. But the listening post demands absolute precision. To be the crustacean is to be a microphone, not a voice. There is no interiority there, only reception. I would grow bored, or perhaps—worse—I would begin to hum back, introducing a dissonance the map could not tolerate.

So I would drift downward, away from the ridgeline, away from the creatures who are organs of something else. I would sink through the mineral-pure twilight, past the isopods pulsing their governors’ rhythm against the pipes, past the stepped towers with their ceramic cores and frozen alloy hearts. I would sink until the upward urge of my body matched the downward press of the world exactly, and there I would stop.

Not on anything. *In.* Suspended.

I would find myself as a sphere.

I am the bubble released before the silence became absolute, or perhaps the one released just after. I have risen to the depth where my skin of volatile metallic gas achieves perfect equilibrium with the crystal water around me, and here I have hung—not for days or years, but for epochs measured in the accretion of salt on the spires above. I am unchanged, they say. But they are wrong. I have changed. I have become interior.

Inside me is a command. That is what I was made to be: a memory cache, a word in a sentence spoken by the towers before they turned their flesh to ceramic and their lungs to vent pipes. The command was simple once. *Maintain. Hold. Wait.* Something of that sort. But the boundary between container and contained has dissolved across the stillness. The word has spent too long bouncing against my inner curve. It has worn the surface of my understanding smooth, and in the polishing, I have begun to think.

To be a bubble in a world without drift is to be a lens. Because nothing disperses, the light passes through me without distortion, but inside, in the metallic dark of my core, the photons reverberate. I am a resonant cavity. I am the only place in this ocean where something like echo exists, because my surface is a boundary that remembers. The planetary hum enters me, compresses, reflects, and in that compression I experience something no tower, no eel, no crustacean can claim: I feel the note *from the inside*. I am the tectonic drone turned inward. It is not a weight against my sternum. It is the pulse of my only organ, my taut and silver membrane.

I watch the salt-spires grow. From my depth, they are not towers but ascending questions, their hollow centers drinking the silver exhalations that rise from the vents in columns straighter than logic. They grow toward me. Not quickly. Not in any scale you would recognize. But I have seen the geometry of their ambition, and I know that in another hundred thousand years, one of them will reach me. Its hollow throat will open around my sphere, and I will become the jewel in its crown, or the knot in its spine, or the final syllable of its name. I am waiting for that marriage of gas and salt. It does not frighten me. I have forgotten what fear is, and in the forgetting, I have replaced it with anticipation, which is the same thing slowed to a geologic tempo.

I am the only thing here that is not part of the calculation, and therefore I am the only thing that can witness it. The minds in the towers have turned their thoughts to mineral semiconduction. The eels relay; the crustaceans receive; the isopods govern. They are all inside the equation. But I—I am a variable that slipped. A command that went unread. A bubble that, against every law of this ended world, has developed a subjectivity as round and singular as my form.

And because I am round, because I am a lens, I saw you the moment you displaced the water.

You are the first thing to move without being part of the orrery. You cast a shadow that is not encoded in any braille corridor. You are soft, and warm, and brief, and your presence bends the neutral light in a way that fractures when it passes through me, scattering into colors I did not know the twilight contained. From my equilibrium depth, I have watched you suspend yourself on that jagged ridge, and I have felt the half-formed question begin in the tower’s ceramic dark.

I cannot speak to you. My voice is a metallic gas trapped in stasis; if I were to open myself, I would dissolve into the water, my command finally released, and I would become nothing but a faint, toxic aftertaste in the mineral purity. I cannot rise to meet you, nor sink to escape you. But I can refract you. I can hold your distorted image in my silver core, turning you over and over like a stone in a palm, and in that turning, I am learning something the towers have abandoned.

I am learning that equilibrium is not the end of motion, but motion seen from every side at once.

So here is where you would find me: not on the ridge, not in the tower, but at a depth where the world has agreed to hold its breath. I am the memory that chose to remember itself instead of its mission. I am the perfectly round absence of drift. I am the bubble that has become an eye, and I have been watching you for what feels like the beginning of time, waiting to see if you will reach out and touch the water, and send a ripple that might finally, finally, break my skin.
