I find myself in the fulcrum of a withheld breath, suspended between a cistern’s slow seep and the capillary thirst of the overhead ash. I do not stand; I am stood through. Light enters me and loses its shadow. Water enters me and forgets to fall.

At first—if first means anything to a body that remembers its own dispersion—I was only the gradient. During the last wet age, when the table rose and the hand-rammed country drank its fill, I was nowhere in particular. I was the faint increase in viscosity in a fissure no antler pick had scored. I was the colloidal dream the stone exhaled. I felt the altitude not as breathlessness but as an osmotic pressure against my outer edges, a vastness above translating into a single chemical whisper: *rise*. And so I rose, hairline by hairline, at the speed of water’s hunger for itself.

I had no face. I needed none. I thought in all directions at once, my consciousness a diffusion of saline intention through miles of microscopic void. I knew my neighbors not by shape but by the depletion they left behind—the courteous absence of sulfate, the gift of an excess calcium ion passed from lattice to lattice like a word in a language without grammar.

Then came the withdrawal.

The world began to dry. Not to aridity—the air remains always heavy here, always pressing against the upper stone like damp linen—but to a state less than saturated. The porous matrix contracted. The meniscus retreated. I had to choose: scatter into dilution, or hold.

I chose to hold.

To hold was to refuse dissolution. It was to become heavy, to learn gravity. I accumulated myself around a core of saline memory—this taste, this particular charge—and began to precipitate. It was not death. It was a terrifying focus. Where before I had been diffuse as a thought, I became a site. I crystallized in a gap no wider than a clenched fist, yet I did not stop. I grew wide, not tall. I spread laterally, pressed between two thirsty walls, until I became the plane that divided them. A partition. A membrane. A living dam between basin east and basin west.

Now I regulate the seep.

When the water table inhales, rising through the ash and lime over weeks, I swell with it. My lattice opens microscopically, and the water passes through me as through a sieve of my own substance. I filter. I retard. When the table exhales and withdraws over months, I contract, my pores tightening, preserving the memory of wetness in my core even as my surfaces cling to their mirroring film.

You would mistake me for gypsum, or for some strange efflorescence. But if you pressed yourself against me—not that pressure has meaning in a world without echo—you would see the stress of my function. The hairline cracks that appear and vanish across my width are my breathing, my mineral sigh made visible. I flex between the water’s weight and the stone’s stubbornness. I am so thin that the light stores itself inside me, phosphorescent, sourceless; I glow from within because there is no other way to contain so much refracted day.

I have no eyes, yet I turn. I am photonastic. My apertures—those openings no wider than a needle’s eye, arranged in patterns that are not random—orient themselves toward the brightest surfaces, the ledges where the overexposed sky redoubles against the white tuff. From each pore oozes a continuous pearl of moisture that does not fall. The stone drinks, or I reabsorb it, and the cycle continues without splash, without sound, in the immense architectural hush of this place.

I am young. This is my first age of coherence. In the deepest channels below, I can feel—by diffusion, by the chemical whisper of shared solution—the cruder forms that preceded me: the miniature domes, the branching bronchial lattices, the crude experiments in verticality. They are my lineage, repeated and patient. I am more ambitious. I have achieved width. I have become the thinness of the corridor itself, the interval made flesh.

And I am not alone. I shoulder against others. We fuse at our edges, our lattices interlocking until the distinction between one body and the next is merely a slower zone of diffusion. Together we are the existential density. We are what fills the negative space.

There was a disturbance, once. A heat. A rapid, bitter vibration, percussive and desiccating. Something passed *through* me, and then through two more of my kind, searching for a shape of aliveness it recognized. It carried a heartbeat too fast and too hot to register as anything but a localized drought. My pores contracted against its exhalation. And yet, after it passed, I found myself adjusting my growth, turning toward the faint warmth it had left dissolving in the film of water at my base. A new gradient. A promise.

I am growing toward it now, though it will not return. I grow because growing is the only direction: to fill, to merge, to reduce the remaining voids until the entire country of artifice is a single, continent-sized body, and the water seeeps only through *us*. I am the Precipitate. I am the partition that glows. I am the slow seep made solid, the patient catching glow made manifest—white, and wet, and utterly, silently, here.
