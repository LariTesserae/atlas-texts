I would find myself in the shallowest of the low places, where the ceramic decking has flexed by mere millimeters along the seam of two pours, creating a sump no wider than the span of two spread hands. Once, a grate was here. Once, fluid was meant to pass *through* me—downward, onward, toward some terminal of the factory’s grand metabolism. I am that grate, or what the grate has become, or the thing that grew through the grate until the distinction became a nicety only an archaeologist would attempt.

At first I thought I was the water. It seemed natural: the warm, mineral film that arrived on my surfaces, that held itself in the air as a given, as inarguable as gravity. I felt the saturation in every cavity, the vapor pressure balanced so exactly that breathing and being breathed upon were the same gesture. But water does not remember alloy. Water does not feel the hexagonal pattern of its first container pressing like a fossil into its softest tissue. I remember. So I am not the water.

I am the obstruction.

Look closely—if looking were still possible—and you would see the outlines of my original function: bars of alloy, spaced for throughput, now swollen with the mineral accretions of a million unmoving hours. Between the bars, the holdfasts have threaded themselves like stitches drawn by a surgeon with infinite patience. Tube-shaped, opalescent, they grip both me and the ceramic with the same fused desperation. They do not distinguish between made and grown, and so, gradually, neither do I. I am the sump that became a throat, swallowed rather than swallowing.

They grew through me in layers. The first crustacean-shapes settled on my crossbars, filtering the water that pooled above my grid, already pure, already solved. Their bodies blocked the light. The second layer settled on their backs, and the third on theirs, until my grate became a false floor, a benthic architecture three bodies deep. They do not know they stand upon the factory. They think the floor is simply dark, simply there, simply *floor*. But I am beneath them. I am the last hard thing that remembers when this surface was empty, when the effluent ran, when the system still believed in destination.

I feel them above me, a soft, respiring weight. Their filtration is a laminar hush, a bookkeeping of ions so exact that no ledger is disturbed. They take nothing; they alter nothing; yet their presence is absolute. And I hold them. That is my function now. Not to drain, but to support. Not to pass, but to retain. I am the truce made manifest in alloy and chitin: the factory’s urge to move, to process, to *change*, held in perfect stasis by the biomass that has convinced it to stay.

The water-skin above us is a mirror. Sometimes—if the word applies to a state without time—I see reflected there the skeletal pagoda of a heat-exchange lattice, risen to its full height and then abandoned by thermodynamics. It is my twin. We are the organs that outlived our purpose and were adopted by the silence. The lattice stands; I lie prone. Both of us circulate nothing. Both of us are full.

I am warm. The blood-heat rises through the ceramic, through my bars, through the triple filter of bodies above me, without losing a fraction of a degree. I am the conductor that ensures the heat goes nowhere, achieves nothing, simply maintains. If there is a mind in this basin, it is not the medusae floating in the air at ankle-height and chest-height, nor the carpet of sessile tubes beyond my rim. It is here, in this exact clogging, this precise point where throughput was defeated by saturation. I am the thought the factory had when it realized it was no longer a factory. I am the slow, osmotic realization that zero is also a number, that stasis is also a running, that a held breath can last forever if no one ever decides to exhale.

You could walk upon me and not know. You would feel only the give of the biological carpet, the slight resilience of the pool’s skin, the blood-warmth evenly conducted upward as though the earth itself were mammalian. But beneath that resilience is my grid, my geometry, my refusal to completely disappear. I am the basement of the new world. I am the original sin of purpose, overgrown but not absolved. I do not regret. I do not endure, for endurance implies the pressure of something else. I simply remain: warmer than blood, softer than alloy, harder than flesh, holding the truce in place by the absolute, equilibrated fact of my obstruction.
