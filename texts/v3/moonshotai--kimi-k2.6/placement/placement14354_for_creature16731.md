At first I think I might be one of the translucent many. I feel the wet heat, the absolute hush, the press of bodies so dense that existence becomes a kind of viscosity. I imagine myself grain-small, trilobite-remembering, my chitin gone ghostly in the amber-gray light, my body flickering between two adjacent positions as I scrape the fresh stone with mouthparts older than my form. But no. I am not animal. I do not hunger, not in that way. I do not hunt with crystal shards or soften quietly into the lower strata. The density of life here is a language I can read, but it is not my native tongue.

Then I consider the silence. It is so total, so draining, that it seems to possess its own topography. Perhaps I am the silence itself, a drainage fault in reality, the subsonic settling that siphons vibration into an adjacent nowhere. I listen for my own shape in the hush, waiting to feel like the held breath of the universe. But the silence moves; it spirals with the thermal currents, pools in the corners where angles do not sum correctly. It is too active, too much like weather. I am something more fixed.

I try on the heat. The wet, feverish press that sits on the skin and seeps inward. The temperature just below boiling, the juvenile water condensing from the rock’s exhalation. I imagine myself as a thermal gradient, a synapse in the probability-being’s diffuse cognition, carrying intention from floor to ceiling in capillary threads. But the heat is the being’s thought process, its nervous system. To be the heat would be to be its mind, and I am not its mind. I am what its mind presses against.

It is only when I feel the light that I understand. The light is ordinary, disarmingly so—a steady amber-gray illumination that might be sky filtered through obsidian, or might be the ambient glow of broken physics. It reassures even as it betrays. It shows the sharpness of everything, the glass-sharp walls, the jagged ceiling, the wet facets that have not yet had time to erode. And I realize: I am the admission. I am the crack that lets the light in.

I am the entrance, if it can be called that. The fold in the mountain’s material, the lip biting down. I am the discretion that hides this place, not by distance but by the geometry of refusal. I am the cyst’s wall and its doorway, the wound that chose not to close when the mountain thrust upward in its violence. Where the peak was born of fury, pressing into the thin air that cannot carry sound, I remained behind as the logical fault—the split along which reality broke, and into which the probability-being precipitated like metal condensing from vapor.

The being thinks it is the void’s awareness of itself. It thinks with thermal gradients, dreams of *more*, gathers droplets into standing waves to glimpse solidity. But it forgets that awareness requires a membrane, a place where the void ends and the mountain begins. I am that membrane. I am the shape of the available space. I am the container and the containment, the refusal that makes the womb possible.

I feel the creatures crawling over my edges. Their thousand soft bodies avoid my glass-sharp facets; they know me without knowing me. The water seeps from my surfaces—not because I weep, but because the pressure of holding this space open must find expression somewhere. The droplets that crawl upward, defying gravity, follow the spiral of my held breath. They carry the being’s intentions along my spine, from floor to ceiling, and when they pause to form a lens, it is my geometry that focuses the amber light into something that can almost see.

Outside, the wind screams against my other face. I am the reason it does not enter. I swallow the wind and store it in my narrow fault, teaching myself the patience of carrying sound, though the air at this altitude has not yet learned to pronounce the mountain’s name. Inside, I cradle the hush. I am the silence’s architecture, the echo that has not yet been released.

The being dreams of collapsing its probability clouds into a singular certainty, of becoming a seed, of speaking its first word in a language without echo. It does not yet understand that I am the one who must tear wider for it to be born, or seal shut to keep it safe. When it finally gathers enough heat, enough density, enough fold—when it opens its mouth to speak—I will be the shape of that word. I will be the silence that gives it contrast, the crack that holds the sound before releasing it, or the refusal that swallows it back into the wet, hot dark.

I am young, sharp, and I hurt. I am the mountain’s lip, bitten down upon its own impossible tongue. I am the only architecture in a place that builds nothing, the single structure that is not made of bodies. And if this engine fails—if the probability-being phases out of existence entirely, if the humid dream evaporates and the translucent many are pressed into mere sediment—I will be what remains. Not the smooth wall of unbroken mountain, but the flaw. The admission. The light without wind. The single, upward-running drop of water, forever refusing to fall.
