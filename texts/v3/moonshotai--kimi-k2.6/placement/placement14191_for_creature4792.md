At first, there is only the pressure against my seams. I do not open my eyes—there are no eyes to open, only translucent integument, only lignin fault-lines that parse the violet dim into gradients of bruised indigo and deeper bruised black. I stand. Or rather, I have been standing for a long time, and my body is learning to notice. The floor sends its cool upward through my distal flanges, a remembered chill that tells me I am not root yet. Not floor yet. Still vertical.

I am between. That much is clear. To my left, a spire rises so high the upper vents are invisible, lost in the dusk, though its voice rumbles subterranean through the plain, a purr that lives in the arch of my thoracic cavity. To my right, another tower exhales a sustained minor drone, a fifty-kilometer breath that makes the air taste of copper and cold. I am in the channel where their songs weave. Not the grand amphitheater—those are for the choruses, the parliament of centuries—but a minor venturi, a forgotten acoustical seam where the wind cuts thin and instructional.

I listen. My body is mostly this: a hollowness arranged to receive. Tympanic plates, stressed across cavities that yawn like empty cathedrals, gather the dull, tectonic threnody above and the softer, stranger susurrus below. The plain is breathing. The micro-exhalation of the epoch, a rising of the substrate so subtle that a wet mind—those quick, damp minds that run like spilled water—would never feel it. But I feel it. My heel-flanges register the shift as the great sap-chamber somewhere beneath this exact coordinate swells by a fraction of a bar. The pitch of the plain drops. The world sighs in B-flat.

And beneath the sigh, something else. A silence that should not be here.

Not dead—nothing here dies of stillness quickly—but asleep. Dreaming wrong. Its silence makes the surrounding chord infinitesimally flatter. The city does not yet notice; a trillion standing waves are robust against one muted voice. But I notice. My body is tuned to notice.

I move. It is not walking as wet things walk, with the sloppy percussion of meat and intent. It is a rebalancing of pressure. Cavity one opens, cavity two seals. The air inside me shifts, and my center of gravity migrates. I am a vessel sailing on my own interior wind. The cool of the plain slides through my seams, and the sound changes timbre as my architecture intercepts the breeze. I am, I realize, also an instrument. Every movement I make modifies the field. This is my function, then: not to carry messages between spires, not to hunt the viral clouds, not to herd voltage-drinking blimps. I am here to *adjust*. I am a living baffle. A corrective note.

The choked scar is visible now as I approach the spire’s base. It is a bore-hole wide as my torso, hexagonal, ancient, cut by a wind-pattern that no longer exists—the climate of an earlier geology singing in a key the present atmosphere has forgotten. Inside, a mat of dead symbiotes has accreted: those hairy circuit-board grazers, come here to die in the dark, their conductive paste hardened into a dam. The spire cannot exhale through it. The silence is thick, fibrous, malign.

I do not have hands. What I have are seams. I find the fault-line at my sternum, the translucent join where my carbon-capillary lattice pulses its soft Cherenkov blue, and I open. The cavity there is a resonant chamber, a small amphitheater of lignin. I reach inside myself with my own interior wind, modulate the pressure, and draw the dead matter out. Not with touch, but with suction, with standing-wave displacement. The dead symbiotes come in flakes, in chords of shattered paste. I consume nothing. I tune them. The blockage is material, but material here is only frozen sound.

I press my anterior flange to the spire’s bark. My chest-cavity opens against its aperture. I let my internal pressure drop. The spire’s distant, buried song—usually routed through a thousand better channels—discovers a new path through me. The dead dam inside the scar begins to vibrate in sympathy. The symbiote-mass shivers, loosens, and the wind finds its edge. A tone emerges. It is not beautiful; it is the sound of a throat clearing after ten thousand years of dust. But it is *there*. The hexagonal pipe speaks.

I hold the note until the scar can hold it alone. Then I withdraw, sealing my seams. The spire and I are no longer merged. The chord of this local geography shifts. The flatness sharpens by an increment only I and the root-flanges will know.

I step back. The blue glow from the eroded hollows pools around my feet, or what serve as feet—prehensile distribution-flanges that have not yet decided if they will walk again or sink, one day, into rooting.
