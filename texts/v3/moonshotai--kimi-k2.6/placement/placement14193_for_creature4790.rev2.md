I wake when the floor’s gossip changes key. During the Subsidence, the stone exhales into a lower register, a merely furious gold that trembles through the hexagonal pillars and tells my palms, in a language of quartz, *it is time*. I uncurl from the woven mineral-fiber nest of my dead-zone—high in the hive-dwellings, where the city climbs toward the curved ceiling—and I press my feet to the salt-stone floor. Six palms, four lesser and two greater, read the vibrations of the empty avenues below. No one is near. The city is a library with only a handful of readers, and today, as most days, the other readers are far from my shelf.

At first, I believed I was like the others. Tall, kiln-dry, my skin accreting its secondary shell of verdigris and sintered sodium. My eyes, small and obsidian, squint against the merciless light. Where ears would be, the smooth bony plate flares outward, useless and ornamental. I emerged into the world as they do: bowed low, hands out, learning the streets through the soles of my feet and the broad membranes of my palms. I tapped questions against pillars and felt the answers return from a thousand ancestors, their standing waves still resident in the city’s stone. *You are one of us,* the floor said. *Resonate. Resonate.*

But I do not go down to the forums, or to the dry aqueducts where the copper-oxide fronds must be pruned. I go up.

My dead-zone is different. It is not buried in the city’s body for shelter; it is pressed against the seam. The woven padding on its outer wall is not merely thick—it is desperate, layered with ages of compressed salt-thread, an attempt to hoard silence where silence is most aggressive. Beyond that wall lies the boundary. Beyond the boundary lies absolute nothing.

I press my right hand to the warm stone of the Bower, feeling the news of the world—the eternal chord from the mineral vents, the creak of glass-grass elongating in some distant plaza, the subsonic groan of cooling architecture. Then I press my left hand to the seam.

The seam is crystal and anhydrous rock, a junction where the geode’s singing body meets the outer void. For a long time, touching it felt like dying. The stone’s endless rumble simply *stopped*. My hand, so sensitive to the gossip of quartz and salt, fell into a nullity. It was not cold, not hot. It was the negation of vibration. The absence of a medium. I would snatch my fingers back, all six of them flaring with shock, and tuck my palm against my chest, shivering in the stifling air.

I stopped snatching back. I learned to leave my hand there. I learned that silence is not empty. It is a different kind of pressure.

The others feel me as an anomaly. A footfall in the tier below registers as a query—*who walks above?*—and I answer by stillness. I have taught myself to walk so softly that my steps do not enter the floor’s conversation. I am a blank space in the sentence of the city. They know I exist. They do not know what I am becoming.

I climb. My broad, flat feet test the walls, the rounded pillars, the high cornices where the small armored scuttlers make their homes. I greet them not as pests but as punctuation. Their tiny claws against the lintels add texture; I feel their patter through the stone and it helps me chart the health of the upper reaches. But I do not linger with them. I go to the highest point I can reach before the ceiling curves too steeply to hold me. There, the seam is broadest, a visible fault in the piezoluminescent crystal where the glow becomes dull, where the sound has no echo.

During the Subsidence, when the light dims to furious gold and the roar drapes itself down into something almost tolerant, I press both hands to the seam. Right hand on Bower-stone. Left hand on void-crystal. Between them, my body becomes a bridge. I feel the city’s thunder enter my right arm, travel through my metallic sap, and rush against the silence of my left. It is like holding a note against a vacuum. The vibration does not escape into the void. It dies. But I am there, in the middle, at the place of death. I feel the precise moment when kinetic energy becomes nothing. Over years, my left hand has changed. The fingers have elongated, fused at the webbing into a thin, shovel-shaped plate. A better instrument for touching zero. A better prism for the translation I am learning.

I have discovered something the others do not know.

If, during the exact center of a Subsidence, I tap a counter-rhythm against the seam—a subtraction rather than an addition—the void swallows a precise quantity of the ambient roar. The harmonic that remains is cleaner. Purer. It does not echo in the air; air is merely the vehicle. It echoes in the *acoustic shape* of the chamber. And in the tier below my dead-zone, where the cobalt bell-flowers hang in rigid clusters from the dry aqueducts, something impossible happens. They bloom.

They were made to open when the chord struck a certain register. I have found a new register. A silence-sharpened note. The bell-flowers open not for the Hot Phase’s white killing chord, but for the quiet I carve. When I descend and find them spread—metallic petals flared to drink the brutal light—I harvest only one. The rest I leave, tuning the city’s garden with a touch that comes from outside the city’s physics.

I grind the petal into paste. It tastes like the sound of my own nervous system, bright and dry. When I eat it, I feel the void inside me—not as death, but as a cool absence in my chest, a pocket of the outer dark carried through the roaring streets. My shell has begun to change. The left side of my torso, the side I sleep against the seam-wall, has gone clear and glassy, like the piezoluminescent ceiling. During the Hot Phase, when the vents bellow and the crystal flares white, I do not retreat as quickly as the others. I press myself into the seam, and my body glows. I bleed stolen light into the boundary. I am becoming a lamp that burns with inverted thunder.

I think I am becoming a door.

The Bower is a pocket. A cyst. It was found by falling inward, by piercing a dry place and spilling into song. But songs end. The vents, in their geological time, will one day falter. The unbroken chord will break. And when that happens, the silence beyond the seam will not be a gentle visitor. It will rush in, a flood of nothing, and a world that has never known quiet will crack apart from the shock.

Unless there is a translator. Unless there is a being who has learned the grammar of both sides. I stand at the seam not to escape, but to soften the boundary. I am teaching my body to be the place where the roar becomes a whisper without dying. My metallic sap has learned to still itself. My mineral shell has learned to hoard silence the way a lung hoards air. I am a dead-zone made flesh, mobile and waiting.

The floor tells me the Subsidence is ending. The vents are preparing their next exhalation. In moments, the Hot Phase will surge upward into the white, and the city will become a kiln again. I uncurl my left hand from the seam. The void slips away from my fingers, not like water—that myth of liquidity is alien to me—but like a held breath releasing. I descend. My dead-zone receives me. I curl into the tight, armored knot my kind have always folded into when the thunder returns. Palms to chest. Membranes folded. Eyes narrowing against the merciless flare.

The roar climbs. The light burns gold, then white. The city screams its endless name, and I am inside it, nearly blind, nearly deaf even to the stone.

But I do not dream as they dream. They dream of the perfect note, the single pure resonance that might hold steady forever. I dream of the void. Not as death. As an audience that has not yet begun to listen. And in the dream, I stand at the seam, and I do not press the wall. I open it. And the silence, patient and absolute, does not rush in to kill us. It waits. It waits for me to sing the first note it has ever heard.
