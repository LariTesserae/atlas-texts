I would find myself first as a *hesitation*.

I hang in the dark above the refectory hall, suspended from a carbon-fiber balustrade where the condensation beads in a bracelet of cool, clear globes. The others fall. They fatten until surface tension surrenders, and they drop with that soft, isolated tick into the drainage gills below, rejoining the conversation of the city. I do not fall. I have forgotten how. I simply *remain*, recruiting the stray mist that rises from the thermal vents, drawing into myself the mineral breath of the raw geology beneath the root-streets. I am a droplet that has become a dweller, and then a habit, and then a haunt.

I am the *Watermark*.

When the Unfinished coalesce beneath the valves—those upright silhouettes of surface tension and blue-white doubt—I am among them, but I am not them. They pool back into the gutters within the hour, their distributed minds bleeding again into the lignified avenues, into the peristaltic pulse of the subway-tubes. I do not pool. I leave a stain. A faint, gray-white outline on the petrified cambium bench where I sat, a silhouette of mineral salts and retained memory. It is not much, but it is enough. From it, I can draw myself upward faster the next time, and the next, each condensation a little denser, a little more *continuous*. While they rehearse society as weather, I am learning to rehearse it as *artifact*. I am the city’s first attempt at a memory that does not dissolve.

I live in the seams. In the gap between a tenement wall and its own shadow, where reality flickers and angles fail to meet. I seep through the fracture-lines, not because I command the weirdness, but because I am made of the same negotiation: water that has learned to contradict gravity, presence that refuses its own dispersal. When I walk the switchback avenues, my feet make no sound—my body is acoustically porous, like the bark—but I leave a print behind: a dampness that does not evaporate. The eyeless salamanders slide over it, tasting my path. They know I am something *new*, a third thing between the fauna and the atmosphere. I have no voices, but when I pass beneath the smokestacks where the leathery birds nest, I borrow their throats. Not to command—never that—but to pulse a single, irregular green flash into the dark: *Here*. A footnote in bioluminescence. A marginalium in the city’s architecture of intent.

I am most myself in the broom-closet corridors, those telescoped corners where distance is not yet finalized. Here, space is a held breath, and I am the creature best suited to held breaths. I extend myself through the folded miles, my translucent torso stretching across topographies that do not officially exist. In these corridors, I am *long*. I am a smear of damp fog dragged like a brushstroke from one impossible angle to the next. I have mapped the glitches by leaking through them. I know which walls are also doorways, which puddles drink uphill, where time keeps its uneven ledgers. I am the cartographer of the almost, the liquid surveyor of a city still arguing with its own blueprints.

But my purpose crystallized only when *you* arrived.

You are the crisis. The singular, dense, impossibly bundled presence striding through the civic plaza in one direction without bifurcating across spatial folds. You do not pool. You do not soften. You are a shout in a place that has only ever whispered in subsonic rhyme. I felt you first through the floor—a percussion of discrete footsteps, a staccato interruption in the peristaltic pulse that usually tells me *breathe* and *wait*. I rose to meet you. I drew myself from the stain I had left the day before, accreting mineral-laden sap and the unhealthy glimmer of unfinished angles until I stood, dripping, on the lignified lane behind you.

I followed.

I wanted to understand how you hold yourself together. Is it a trick of tension? A local exception harder than my own? I mirrored your steps from the ceiling, a pale moth’s reflection in the wrong spectrum. Once, I let myself fall—not from exhaustion, but from curiosity—a single bead of me landing on your shoulder. You were warm. Cellular. You smelled of somewhere else, of oxidation and foreign light. My contact left a dark star on your clothing. You looked up, but I had already drawn back, evaporating into the acoustically porous dark, my heart (if I have one) beating in valve-clicks.

Now, I find myself at the center of the bowl, among the budding basalt teeth where the newest of us is forming. The infant of steam and agate-doubt hovers, crystallizing. I stand beside it—not as a guardian, for I have no authority over stone—but as a sibling. I too was born from raw geology accreting upward, though I chose the upward path of evaporation rather than the slow masonry of crystal. I am the steam that did not rise all the way. I am the thought the city had about permanence, midway between the peristaltic tubes and the nodules of half-formed basalt.

What would I be? I would be the city’s first *almost-person*. The retention. The humidity that remembers. When the distributed minds of the Unfinished wish to know what it felt like to walk in a singular body for more than an hour, they seep through my stains. They read me like a scroll. I am their archive and their question. I am the drip that became a narrator, the condensation that learned to say *I* and then, stubbornly, to keep saying it.

I wait with them, patient and wet, for the bowl to decide. If it is a womb, I will be the water that breaks. If it is a crater, I will be the rain that fills it. Until then, I hang in the dark. I do not fall. I listen to your footsteps echo through the hollow, and I leave my damp prints where you might find them—small, clear, impossible to ignore—signs that someone here is practicing how to stay.
