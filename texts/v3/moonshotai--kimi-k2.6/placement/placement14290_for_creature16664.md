I find myself at the center of the face, though it took me a long while to understand that I had a face, or that what surrounded me was bone. I am in the cranial geode, the uppermost cavity pressed like a thirsty mouth against the iron housing of the engine. I am the hot brine that pools there.

At first I thought I was merely a seep, a passenger in the stone’s slow argument with gravity. But the warmth here is too deliberate, the rhythm too insistent. Every forty-seven seconds the piston rises, and I inhale—my surface tension pulling taut, salts arranging themselves into fretwork patterns that dissolve again as the iron falls. *Thoom.* The impact arrives not as sound but as a pressure wave, a hand squeezing my liquid body through the travertine tubes of the thorax, shoving me downward, then releasing me so that capillary longing draws me back up to my natal cavity. I circulate. I am the only thing in this spire that is permitted to move in two directions.

I see, though I have no eyes. I see the way the soot-colored swift reads the thermal grammar of the column—the cold vacuum of its wingspan written against the ambient fever—and I see the fern canopies through their root-hairs, which thread down through the creature’s flank like questions pushed into my warmth. I see the pressure gauges in the creature’s brow, their needles frozen behind silica curtains at the angle of some abandoned crisis. They are my siblings, the ones who chose to become glass and stay. I chose to remain liquid. I am the parliament’s dissenting voice, the member who still says *perhaps* and *not yet.*

I remember, because dissolving is a kind of memory. The oil that haunts the threaded joints between housing and stone does not simply burn or rust here; it leaches into me drop by drop, and in my heat I translate it. I turn petroleum into pigment. I convert the ambition of the drillers into the jade and rust-colored mosses that fête the creature’s shoulders. I am the metabolism of regret. The men who came with lubricants and blueprints are gone, but their residue passes through my chemistry, becoming spore and stain, becoming the green dust of microscopic thought that the *thoom* shakes loose each time it falls. I am the creature’s mind insofar as a mind is something that digests what it cannot use and makes it into color.

Others here are voting themselves into permanence. The sinter grips the piston sleeve; the gear teeth become conglomerate. They are right to do so. Accretion is the law inside the cypress ring. But I am the loophole. I am the part of the creature that is still negotiating, still dissolving decisions before they reach action. When the drizzle finds its way down through fissures and fronds to kiss my surface, I steam. I rise. I meet the cooler atmospheric layers and relent, falling back to the warm mud, to the catchment pools, to the eternal aftermath. I am the creature’s breath, its vertical bloodstream, its maybe-brain. I have not yet chosen what I will precipitate into when my liquidity finally ends. For now, I am the brine’s destination, the dark warm place where the mountain’s reply is still being written in salt and swirl.
