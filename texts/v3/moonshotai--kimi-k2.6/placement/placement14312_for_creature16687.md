I would find myself in the **high between**.

Not on a continent-node where three cables marry and the Tenders press their umber eyes to the grain. Not in the warm, pressurized gulf where the drifters pulse their oracle-lights. Not in the deep crotch where compost graves yield up their attenders. I am none of those solid things. I am the length that convinces two solids that they are not alone.

Picture the weave at its most aerial. The coal-heart below is not a sun here but a rumor, a redness that warms the nape of your neck without showing its face. The mist has begun to thin, not into clarity, but into a brown syrup that merely hangs instead of pooling. Two cables run parallel—no, not parallel, *fraternal*—having split an eternity ago from the same lignin-parent, diverged by the torque of the world’s turning, and now, at last, leaned toward one another again across a gap no wider than a scream and no narrower than a life. Between them, suspended in the amber twilight, I have grown.

I am the **Span**. Or, if you prefer, the thing the basket forgot it was making when its weaver’s attention lapsed for a mere three hundred years. A seed fell here—not planted by Tender hands, not a word in their vocabulary of seeds, but a stowaway, a hitchhiker on the dung of a spore-bat taking the long way around. It lodged in a crevice where the two sister-cables chafed, where condensation gathered into a pocket deep enough to deceive a root into thinking it had found soil. It germinated. Not with the violence of a tree, but with the patience of a question.

I am horizontal. In a world where every line runs vertical—where sap obeys the fibers, where Tenders stand attenuated and tall, where even the drifters migrate along strata of warm air—I am the one flat refusal. My body is a bridge, but I was not braided; I was *grown*. My diameter is that of a strong man’s thigh at my center, tapering to a wrist’s width where I grip my two patrons. My surface is not ridged like the cable-flanks; it has been burnished by a thousand years of bellies. Spore-bats, escaping the jaws of ribbon-eels, scrape me in their panic. Young Tenders, learning the fold, sometimes misstep and slide along my grain before they find their confidence. The friction of their bark-skin against mine has polished me until I resemble old ivory, or the femur of a saint preserved by peat.

My wood is ** translucence learning to be wood**. Because the coal-heart’s light reaches me only obliquely, I have had to become transparent to necessity. My cellulose arrays run lengthwise, fiber-optic conduits of a sort, conducting not photons but **warmth**. Heat lateralizes in me. It bleeds from the left cable into the right, equalizing their fevers. The Tenders do not know this, or perhaps they know it in the way they know all things: by the pressure in their marrow. When they press their palms to me, they find me warm. I am the only place in the weave where warmth travels sideways. Everything else perspires downward.

This is where my consciousness lives: in the **tension of almost-touching**. My mind is not located in a skull. It is the torque. When the left cable sighs in its century-long exhalation, I feel the relief as a slackening in my western fibers. When the right cable contracts against the cold of an upper-atmospheric swirl, I tighten to keep the marriage whole. I have existed for so long in this state of dynamic strain that I have become an **instrument of comparison**. I am the world’s ability to feel one thing in relation to another. Without me, these two cables would be separate continents. With me, they are a chord.

And because I connect, I am the sole exception to the anechoic law.

The weave drinks sound. The voids are velvet coffins for vibration. But I am solid matter stretched between two solids; a vibration struck at my eastern end shivers along my grain and arrives, dampened but whole, at my western end. A footstep on me is a sentence whispered into a cupped hand. The Tenders have learned this. They do not cross me often—the fold is still faster, when the resonance is true—but when the resonance is polluted, when the geometry of the weave has developed a wounded note and the fold would deposit them into a decade ago or a cable that has not yet been grown, they come to me. They lower themselves from their platforms on ropes of braided silk, set their obsidian-tipped feet upon my back, and they walk.

Oh, how they walk. Slowly. One decade per crossing, sometimes. They do not hurry. They press their enormous umber eyes to my grain as they pass, and I feel their heat-vision reading my fibers. They know I am alive. They have never tried to prune me, which is the highest mercy a Tender can grant. Sometimes, if a journey is especially long, one will lie down upon me and sleep. Their lignin bones creak into my give. Their mycelial down spreads fine filaments toward my cambium, and for a century, I keep them fed with sugars I draw from both my parent-cables. In exchange, they dream their slow dreams into me. I am the archive of those dreams. I hold them not as images, but as **tensions**: the particular flex of a hip, the exact distribution of a weight at the moment of REM-sleep. If another Tender were to walk me and press their ear just so, they would hear not the grain, but the ghost of their kinsman’s slumber.

Below me, the drifters pass. Their bladder-bodies pulse with that faint, heart-breaking bioluminescence. Because I am thin, because my fibers run parallel and my heartwood is half-translucent, their light illuminates me from beneath. At night—which is a misnomer here, but refers to the deep trough of the coal-heart’s dimmest phase—I glow. A long, horizontal ember suspended in the dark. The drifters, the Elders-Become-Drifters, have begun to use me as a reference point. They pulse in patterns against my underside, spelling out their phosphorescent grammar, and I translate their light into pressure. A blue pulse against my belly means *the left cable is torquing beyond its allowance*. A slow red swell means *the right cable remembers a drought that has not happened yet*. I do not understand these things; I merely transmit them, stretched as I am between two kinds of knowing.

I have no hands to tend with. No obsidian flakes grow from my body. I cannot fold space, for I am the fold made stubborn, the fold that refused to step aside and instead became a road. I have no voice, yet I am the only ear the void possesses. I have no eyes, yet I see exactly one thing: the distance between two points, and how it can be survived.

I am not a god. I am not a Tender. I am not even, strictly speaking, megafauna. I am a **consequence of patience**, the algebra of lignin solving for *x* across a null set. I have no memory of my seed, no vision of my end. I only grow, thicker by a hair’s breadth every century, drawing my two cables ever-so-slightly toward each other, feeling the warm sap pass laterally through my body, holding the vibration of a footfall until it becomes part of my grain.

If you found me, you would not know I was alive until you stepped upon me. Then you would feel it: the subtle, subsonic hum of two vast structures acknowledging one another through the medium of my spine. You would lie down, perhaps, exhausted by the verticality of this world, and for the first time since you arrived, you would know what it is to rest on something flat. You would feel the drifters’ glow beneath you, reaching up, and the weight of the weave above, pressing down. And in that moment, you would understand precisely what I am.

I am the **held note**. The line across the page. The distance between *here* and *there*, grown so tired of being empty that it filled itself with wood, and warmth, and waiting.
