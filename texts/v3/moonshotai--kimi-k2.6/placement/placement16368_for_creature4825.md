I am found where the water has not yet decided its direction.

For forty years I have occupied this slender volume: a body narrow enough to thread between the epiphytes, light enough that the hyper-dense air buoys me in my longer leaps. I do not cast a sharp shadow. The amber afternoon filters down through strata of leaf and condensate and finds me as a diffusion, a brown-green smudge against the greater brown-green of the quivering peat. I am wet to the bone. I have never been dry. Drying here would be a form of death so alien the concept feels borrowed from another world, the thin world above the rim where the air turns cruel and the sky forgets to breathe.

My feet—wide, tough, webbed with a membrane that collects spore—press against the saturated ground, and I *run*.

This is my distinction. In a basin where the hot springs migrate at the speed of fingernails and the pins drift through their operational cycles in an eighteen-year yawn, I am the exception that proves the rule. I am the brief wind. The haste. The Calmbeasts groan across the thirty kilometers, their voices tectonic, reshaping the marsh with subsonic love; I am the one who brings the reply before the spring has cooled. I am the one who carries messages through the lungs of the world.

I did not crawl over the rim. I was dropped. Or rather, I fell, in the wet, sucking way that things fall here—not with impact but with incorporation. For fifty years I ripened in the soil of a Calmbeast’s back, drinking the mineral weep of its spine, nestled in ferns that had rooted in the loam upon its living hill. Then the great slope of its shoulder gave me up. I tumbled into the peat, already covered in my own inherited garden, already seeded with the fungal memories of my parent. I unfolded. I stood. And I began to move.

They have no name for me, the pins, though their magnetic fields bend slightly as I pass, confused by this swift heat. The Listener, sunk in their spring near the western ruin, projects a pressure into the low-frequency hum when I approach: *Rush. Bridge. Quick-child.* I feel it in my sternum. I take their corrections—the small subsonic adjustments meant to steady the retention engines—and I carry them eastward, running the long curve of the basin’s floor with the message lodged in my humming blood. Four months. By Cauldera reckoning, a dash.

The Orienters cling to me. They mistake my velocity for a new kind of weather. Their silicate carapaces tick against my shoulders as I pass beneath the bone-kites, who watch with the incurious patience of buoyant things. I carry spore-pouches at my throat. When a rot takes a Calmbeast’s garden, I climb the shifting soil of its hide, sink my hands to the wrists in its moss-skin, and plant the cure. When two Calmbeasts must speak before their groaning cycle completes, I absorb the thrum from the peat near one and release it at the feet of the other, a translation of pressure and intent.

Near the pins, where gravity barters and water climbs the spires in lazy spirals, I venture only when the spiral-born drift low enough to tear. I need their heat. My metabolism burns too bright for long centuries; I am the mayfly of the Cauldera, the quick thought that flashes across a slow mind. I leap, rip their hydrogen sacs, drink the vent-stolen warmth. They mend in hours. I run on.

I know I will not last. Already my joints speak of settling, of the peat’s patient claim. When I can no longer outpace the growth of the shelf-fungi, I will choose a spot between two springs and kneel. My flesh will not fossilize in the ordinary way. It will become peat, become the architecture of the next Calmbeast’s garden, become mineral memory in the scale-fish’s armor. Perhaps a new mound will drop from a high back and ripen into another like me—a runner, a stitch in the wet fabric. Or perhaps it will unfold into stillness, into the monument of a Calmbeast proper. Either way, I will belong. I will stop. I will join the engine.

But not yet. Not today. Today the peat quivers under my passing, and the basin holds me, and I am the wet, loud, urgent proof that even in a world built to outwait glaciers, there is something holy about *hurry*—about being the carried breath, the spore in flight, the living thought that must arrive *now*, before the slow, steaming, gorgeous mind forgets what it meant to say.

I am the Cauldera's pulse, accelerated. I am the Mound-born. I am found, always, in the between.
