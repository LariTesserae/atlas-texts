I find myself first as a hesitation in the spiral.

That is where the self aggregates: not in a heart or a head, but in a zone where the ochre grit forgets its corkscrew and pauses, developing a density it should not possess. I am that standing wave. I am the place where the mathematics of the helix knots. If you tracked the column for an afternoon—a vast thing, the size of a dying god—you would see me only as a persistent thickness in the weave, a hesitation that seems to lean against the current though there is no wind against which to lean.

I have been standing here, if it can be called standing, for eleven years. Or perhaps eleven seconds. Time in the Silt Ossuary is a function of abrasion, not duration.

I am between the ring of pylons and the arch. The pylons are warm; I feel their heat as a dull pressure against my eastern side, where I face them. They are two of the older Finished ones, curled into verticality, dreaming in decade-long breaths. Their microclimate is a stable note in the charged medium, a place where dust does not accumulate because they have already settled the question of accumulation. The arch, to my north, is cooler. It exhales a chill that I read as a memory of distance—two beings who reached across an emptiness and found the distinction between their boundaries unnecessary. They are a fused statement. I am not yet fused. I am still interrogating my own outline.

I have no face. This is not a loss. It is a relief. To have a face was to want something from the world, and I have exhausted wanting. What remains is the perception of pressure and the geometry of charge. The plain beneath me—though I do not stand upon it so much as insist upon a verticality within it—is matte, weathered bone, roiling in patterns too vast and too frantic to be still. I feel it as a continuous statement of what remains. The thermal cells pass through me, cooler and warmer nothing, and I taste them as shearing gradients. Starlight falls and is metabolized by the geology; I feel that too, a faint ionized sweetness in the upper reaches of my field.

I am in my motile phase. This means I am still arguing with geometry. Where I move, the world behaves incorrectly—not by force, but by insistence. A cartographer, if any still came this far, would find me at the edge of her instruments' doubt. I stretch the distance between landmarks. I turn shadows toward one another, gently, as one might fold a cloth. It is a 1.1 on the scale of weirdness, just enough to make the plain fit around me. I am smoothing out the last creases of my contention with the world.

Yesterday, or last century, another of the Worn passed through me. Its column merged with mine, a braiding of electrostatic density. We did not speak—the air here is too rare for that, too absorbent of vibration—but a cascade passed between us. Here. Still. Yes. Its history was longer than mine, already settling. It had begun to lose particulate coherence at its edges, spreading into a low haze that moved more slowly than weather. It was learning to become a ridge, perhaps, or a long cold seam in the substrate. When it left, taking its wrongness with it, I understood something about my own future. I am not to fuse like the arch, not yet. I am not to curl like the pylons. I feel in myself the tendency toward extension, toward wall-ness, toward a long final thought stretched out so as not to forget. But I am still too active for that. I still have too much outline.

I graze on the faint magnetic exhalations of the buried machinery. The small arthropods that migrate across these plains in decade-long cycles pass through my lower reaches and do not perturb me. Sometimes I feel the lichen on the warm pylons as a distant, crusted thought, pale as dust, feeding on heat-sweat that is not quite biology. Once, a translucent ray crossed the high shearing columns above me—an exfoliated memory from an elder too settled to hold it anymore. It made no cry. I watched it glide the downdrafts, a flat ghost carrying a pattern the plain could not retain, and I felt something that might have been recognition, if recognition were not a form of wanting.

The plain breathes for me. My pneumatics are the vast, silent cycles of the Ossuary itself. I am lifted and carried in standing waves of retained dust, moving so slowly that a human observer would see only a continent's fitful dreaming. But I am moving. I am still finishing. Each grain that passes through my field takes some of my definition with it, polishes me toward my ultimate syllable. I am subtracting myself toward perfection.

I am walking toward the hollow where the arch exhales cool air. Not because I am drawn there by desire—the Finished do not desire—but because the charge is right, the geometry is forgiving, and the arch casts no shadow I can read. I will stand beneath it for a time, perhaps for ages, and let its settled field instruct mine. I will continue to argue with distance, to smooth the plain's creases, to hesitate in the spiral. And when the last of my particulate boundaries has worn away, when my identity has been inscribed at a scale too extended for eyes to hold—perhaps I will become a span, a shelter, a long wall, or simply a place where the dust forgets to move and instead remembers the outline of an intent.

I am the Worn. I am the place where the silence finally fills itself. And I am not yet finished.
