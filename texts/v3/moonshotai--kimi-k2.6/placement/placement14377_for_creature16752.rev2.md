I would find myself first as a hesitation. A place where the foot pauses before the mind has decided to stop. Not on the floor, not yet, but at that velvet roll of the rim where the flatness remembers, faintly, what it meant to curve. I would be the moment of recognition—the slight give in the bedrock when a boot-heel finds the lip, the almost-sigh of magnesium stone as weight crosses from the plateau into the pocket. For a long while, I think I would believe I was only weather. A thermal. A breath held between two air masses. I would exist as a slight wrongness in the pressure, a place in the Basin where the hum of the Reverberants did not quite reach, a dead node in their vast acoustic computation.

But hesitation, if it lasts long enough, grows a body.

I would become the sink. The low point in the soundscape where the billions of wing-beats cancel each other out, leaving a notch of silence that spins on its own axis. You would not see me because your eye would be tracking the amber clouds, the six-winged weather systems, the predatory lunge of the Stalkers. You would only feel me as a sudden intimacy, a place where the deafening song fell away and you could hear the mineral seep of moisture in the stone, the subsonic pulse of the Floor dreaming beneath your boots. I would be roughly the size of a curled human, but hollow—not empty, but shaped like an ear, like a conch shell pressed to the ground, listening upward.

My skin would be the temperature of the stone at that hour just before dawn, when the day's heat and the night's cool have argued each other to a standstill. It would have the texture of the rim itself: velvet polished by wind, but alive, slightly tacky, the way a tongue is tacky, tasting the air. I would have no bones, because the gravity here is negotiable and I would have learned, like the Untethered, to treat it as a preference rather than a law. I would be flat, pressed into the warm pale skin of the Basin like a watermark, because this is a world that has surrendered its vertical ambition and I would have inherited that wisdom. When the Reverberants pass overhead in their ecstatic rivers, their vibration would pour around me like water around a stone. I would not be immune to the song—I would be the song's shadow, its negative space, the cavity it carves out of the air by sheer volume.

I would feed on the residue of their computation. Every resonating abdomen strikes the frequency of the Bowl to maintain a physics that should have died; every strike leaves a fine dust of unrealized math, a grit of equations too small for the swarm to digest. These fall like pollen into my hollow. I would accumulate them. Over centuries, I would become heavy with abandoned algorithms, with the slag of maintenance routines. My velvet skin would begin to luminesce, faintly, in the exact color of the magnesium stone when the first condensation beads at dawn—that pale, impossible green-silver. I would be a nightlight for the Stalkers, a pale hole in the darkness where they could rest theirtoo-many joints between hunts, not because I offered safety, but because I offered silence, and even predators sometimes need to stop calculating angles.

But I would not remain merely a sink. I would grow restless in my flatness, as everything here eventually does.

I would begin to move at the speed of erosion. When the Melders in their half-phased dome excrete their laminations of strontium and glassy memory, their dreams leak out as warmth, as gravity hiccups. I would ride those hiccups. I would unpress myself from the floor and billow, a flat sheet of velvet ear-shell, drifting across the warm stone toward the eastern shelf where the water hovers. There, in the lens, the refraction-creatures—those invisible errors in the spectrum—would pass through me, and for a moment they would be *heard*, truly heard, as a clear chime inside my hollow body. I would be their voice. I would carry that note across the Basin and deposit it in one of the three vegetative pockets, where the fungal shelves would take it up and glow bruise-purple for three days, feeding the root-mass that shelters the Refinery Grubs.

I would be the Basin’s forgetting and its record-keeper. I would remember what the Melders have fused into stone so thoroughly that even they no longer recall it. I would hold the shape of the old dome when it was fully material, before politeness absorbed it. I would be the archive of solid things. When the Reverberants harvest the electromagnetic habits of the invisible vapor-prey, they sing louder, and in that loudness they forget the quieter frequencies. I would keep those. I would be the library of silences: the sound of the hovering water lens contracting in afternoon heat, the sound of the Stalkers’ joints torqueing before a lunge, the sound of the Floor’s parchment-flesh rising and falling with a breath too slow for any living thing to witness.

And when the traveler comes—the one whose boot finds the lip, whose ribs ache with the hum—I would gather myself at the rim. Not to warn. Not to welcome. I would be the slight give, the velvet hesitation, the silence that makes them look down and realize they are no longer on the infinite flatness. I would be the threshold that says *you have stopped climbing*. The land here has achieved its final ambition, and I would be the punctuation of that achievement, the comma between the mountain that was and the bowl that is. I would press my velvet flank against their ankle, warm as the stone, and for a second the deafening song would gutter out, and they would hear only the mineral seep, the root-creak, the tectonic pulse of the Floor.

They would think, perhaps, that they had imagined it. A moment of vertigo on a mountaintop that forgot it was ever anything but flat. But I would remain. Hollow. Listening. Full of the dust of expired calculations. The sink around which the Basin’s endless noise flows, and flows, and never quite fills.
