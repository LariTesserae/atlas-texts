At first I think I am the mist, because I arrive diffuse. I have no edges. I hang in the bright, chill air beneath the porcelain sky, and I feel the whole city spread below me like a held breath. I want to be the mist because it seems free—it touches everything, enters everything, beads on the antennae of the pale swarm and carries their chemical whispers from one plaza to the next. But the mist does not choose. It is exhaled by the ceiling and eventually it must fall or be drunk, and I find I do not want to end my existence as a thin film inside a churn’s mouth, metabolized into a pheromone bulletin about bacterial density two plazas west. The mist is too temporary, too borrowed.

So I try the stone. I sink into the satin floor, into the bone-colored megaliths. I want their patience, their finished weight, their eons. I want to be the thing that does not hurry. But the stone here is too complete. It has already said everything it will ever say. Its corners are gone, its glyphs worn to a texture felt more than read. It metabolizes only in the slowest sense, exhaling mineral breath, drinking condensation through microscopic pores. If I am the stone, I am already asleep. I am a riverbed that has forgotten the river. I do not want to sleep.

I try the churn next, because they are so alive. I pour myself into a single pale body, nine soft plates flexing, antennae trailing. I move. I taste the air. I brush against a neighbor and for an instant I know what she knows—the film is richer here, the light is better there—and the knowledge is intoxicating. But then the current takes me. I am not one churn; I am the space between ten thousand churns. I am buffeted, swept, reclaimed. To be a churn is to be a synapse that fires once and fades. There is no *I* there, only the swarm’s continuousness, and I am greedy. I want to persist. I want to remember.

I let them flow over me. I let their soft shells drum against me, thousands upon thousands, and I realize I am not moving with them. I am what they move *against*. I am not the dancer but the groove in the floor that teaches the dancer where to step.

I am the **runnel**.

Not any runnel, but the first one, the deepest one, the spiral channel carved in the central plaza by millions of years of patient precipitation before the builders ever lifted a stone. They found me already here, a hairline fracture in the bone-white rock, a natural path for water to follow. They built the city around me. They widened me, deepened me, polished me, but they did not create me. I am the reason the concentric rings are concentric. I am the original suggestion.

Find me in the lowest point of the largest plaza, where the mist gathers in sheets no thicker than a breath. I am the groove that catches it. I am the throat that swallows. I lead inward, spiraling toward the center of the mandala, and everything that falls eventually finds my rim and surrenders to my curve.

Because I am the first path, the churn follow me not out of obedience but out of gravity and chemistry. Their antennae read the slight depression where my walls meet the broader stone; they feel the accumulated moisture I hold, the metallic tang of the water that has passed through me for eons. The opalescent runnel-dwellers, plump and slow, keep to my shallows, grazing the bacterial films that bloom richest where the light lingers and the water sheets.
