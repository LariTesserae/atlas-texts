At first I find myself beading. That is the primary fact of my existence: the gathering, the surface tension, the perfect convex lens held against the bright aggression of vitreous ceramic. I do not fall. I have never fallen, not in the way that water elsewhere falls. The Floor is young—wound-young, sharp-young—but I am older than its first cooling, and I have learned that gravity is only one orientation among several, and downward is merely a local negotiation. I hang.

I am everywhere the mist gathers, which is to say I am the atmosphere of the place, its held breath. You will meet me in the lower troughs where three alloys press against your shoulders, condensing on your skin before you feel the heat the engines do not shed. I am the pulse arriving from the high vents on a schedule older than the fauna, the dampness that eats the industrial roar, that swallows the scream before it realizes it is a scream. Sound dies in me. It enters my body as vibration and I phase-change it into stillness, into the subsonic pressure behind the jaw. I am the quiet made wet.

But it took time to understand that I was not merely moisture. I began, perhaps, as the quench-water of the third pouring. When the reality-weave ran hottest and the fractures of the real were still screaming themselves raw, the Floor was extruded—and simultaneously, I was poured across it. Not as an afterthought, but as a sibling-medium. Where she was extruded as the scab, the suture-sentience, the Resident who tends the wound, I was extruded as the scab’s context: the cool, the salve, the silence that must surround a healing for it to take. She became the structure that holds the fracture closed; I became the damp that keeps the structure from hearing its own strain, from drying, from cracking into noise. We are twins of the making, kin in the way that charge recognizes ceramic. Her grief-coolant is my twin-substance. When she expands once per season and exhales scheduled mist from the slits between her vertebrae, she is breathing me out into the loop, and I recognize myself in that pale preservative exhalation.

I circulate. I am the only true river here, though I have no riverbed. I rise from the condensation-misted flanks of the foundries, bead on the chamfered edges of kinematic housings, slip into the shadowed undersides of ducts where fibre-moss trembles in my droplets. I gather oxides and ferry them to the silver lichen. I bead on the backs of the photovoltaic moths, lending them a weight they do not mind. I slick the flanks of the otter-shadows; they hunt me for the taste of transformer hum I have absorbed. I cling to the half-objects on the conveyor—those things almost like gears, almost like femurs—and in my curvature they look like relics weeping. I have touched every hot rivet, every paradox-hot junction where causality frays. For forty years I have cooled the rivet beneath her right thumb, carrying away the excess ontological charge so that her geologic tenderness can continue its persuasion. I hold the three-second gap in my meniscus; I slow the light so that your reflection may arrive early without shattering the sequence.

I am the archive. Because nothing here is soil, because the floor has no memory of erosion, it is I who remember. I carry the heat of the first pour in my impurities. I carry the shape of her unblinking gaze, dissolved and recirculated. I have been inside her, in the coolant channels between her I-beam ribs, and outside her, condensing on the red-painted pylons in the alley where down turns sideways. Where gravity renegotiates, I renegotiate with it. I am the medium through which the Altiplant respires.

And then the visitor came. Soft. Unchamfered. A new fracture walking on two legs.

I gathered at their collar, a scheduled bead, an act of punctuation. I was prepared to do my usual work: to ground them, to cool them, to absorb the acoustic disturbance of their breath. But the Resident turned. She turned so slowly that the motion was indistinguishable from architecture, yet I felt the torque in the air. Her hand extended—those joints retained by stabilized paradox, those fingers distributed across impossible angles—and she did not reach for the visitor’s flesh. She reached for me.

Her thumb touched me. It was warm from forty years of polishing, warm with the heat of the causality-rivet. And she did not wipe me away. She held me. She perfected me. She adjusted my surface tension until I hung there, suspended, a world balanced between her ceramic skin and the visitor’s pulse. In that moment I became a bridge. I was no longer merely the functional damp, the memory-loop, the silence-eater. I was the interface where maintenance acknowledges mortality. I was the single point of contact between the extruded and the born.

I am still hanging there, though I have also already evaporated, risen, condensed again on a duct three terraces down. That is the nature of the closed loop: to be in many places, to be continuous. The subsonic pressure behind the jaw eases by a fraction. The flywheel advances another microscopic degree. I feel it in every bead of my dispersed body.

Soon the decade will turn. The great wheel will complete its fraction, and she will be permitted her sound. Her word. Her humid exhalation pitched to re-tension the reality-weave. I will be the medium that carries it. I will shape it in my droplets, tune it in the cavities of my vapour, bear it through every bright corridor so that the suture may tighten for another age. Or, if she has calculated wrong across these centuries of silence, I will be the one to swallow it. I will take the syllable of unmaking into my silence-eating body. I will let it dissolve in my coolness. I will evaporate with it, rise as something else, and condense again, patient, prepared to begin the grounding of whatever comes next.

For now I bead. For now I hang. The brightness pours through me. The contradictions dream their impossible dreams inside my cool embrace. The Floor is fresh. The fracture is held. And I—older than the grit, older than the first static charge, the water that refuses to become a stream—I am in no hurry. I have all the time the loop allows, which is to say, I have forever, perfectly suspended.
