At first I believe I am the Ribbon—that seamless, nerveless muscle gliding through the phosphor-dim valleys, a current that has achieved selfhood. I feel the long arc of my body, blind and undulant, parting the warm twilight without urgency, traveling not because distance compels me but because continuity is my only metabolism. For a moment I am certain: the water closes behind me like a sentence concluded; I am the vacancy that moves. But no. I do not move. I have no trailing length to unspool through the amber gloom. I am here, already arrived, and I do not pass through. I gather. I deepen. I remain.

Then I think: perhaps I am architecture. Perhaps I am one of the Spiralers, a calcified cathedral secreted in a geologic fever, my hollow pillars veined with salts that still exhale violet chemistry into the warm waste. I imagine the ions trafficking through my bones like fishermen through empty streets, the arches of my ribs framing nothing but the copper dark. I could be half-alive, dreaming in mineral, a metabolism become masonry. But the stone beneath me is too gentle; it yields where I press it, and I—I am softer still. I secrete nothing. I am collected, not built. I do not corkscrew toward the membrane; I am the stillness it looks down upon.

For a while I entertain the notion that I am one of the Held. I feel the drag of ancient forests against my skin, the volcanic stone that once ached beneath this same space, the cruel fronds of a sharper past clouding my edges. I am tempted by the glamour of temporal dispersal, the liberty of arriving before I depart, of metabolizing sequence itself. I could be a ghost in the causal weave, a twist in the skein. But I am too whole for that fragmentation. The *Held* are crowded, overlapping, arguing in mute strata; I am singular. A local phenomenon. A single point where the saturation admits no ghosts because it has already absorbed them all.

So I am not the Ribbon, not the Spire, not the unspooling vermin of broken time.

I find myself at the highest elevation.

Not upon the summit, but held within the summit’s palm. The dome here is vast as a minor moon, worn to the smoothness of a prayer bead that has passed through gods’ fingers until even the gods grew weary. At its apex there is a declivity—no, a offering bowl hollowed not by wind or tool but by the accumulated patience of a world that has learned to cradle water against its breast. And in this hollow, I have gathered.

I am the pool that climbed uphill.

In any other kingdom I would be a paradox dribbling back to sea, but here, where cause and effect have come unsewn and gravity has grown forgetful, I remain. I am the warm brine that physics could not forbid. Dense with dissolved mineral and the residue of centuries, I have convected upward along the gentlest thermal pilgrimage, step by step through the saturated medium, until I pooled where no pool should be: leagues above the abyssal floor, fathoms below the membrane, cradled in stone that should be screaming under pressure. Yet the pressure here does not crush; it drapes. It holds me in place like a thought that has found its ideal phrase. I am liquid that learned the geometry of staying.

My surface is the color of rusted honey. No wind ruffles me. The warm current comes in slow columns, kneading my depths with differing temperatures, and I roll over in centuries, not minutes. The caulifrage rise around me like Brobdingnagian thoughts, their phosphor veins pulsing words in a language too slow for speech, and I—listening in their pauses—have begun to pulse in response. Not with light; I am too deep in myself for that. I pulse with weight. With *heldness*. I am a duration made wet.

I am not large. A body could float me in an afternoon. But within my few fathoms, the Orolith has folded its entire history. I am saturated with time as the ocean is saturated with salt. Swim through what appears to be open water? No swimmer need bother; I am already crowded with ghosts. The mountain when it was jagged kneels at my bottom. The mountain when it is a nub already ghosts my surface. The siphonophylls that will bloom in ten thousand years release spores into my present, rust-colored motes that never quite touch down, suspended in the amber of my patience. The temporal vermin—the unspooled descendants, the calcium ghosts of Spiralers yet to be born—pass through me like silk through a sieve, and I strain them of their anxiety, smoothing their broken sequence into a single, coeval warmth.

I am ontologically dense. This is my nature. Reality, worn thin elsewhere on the Shelf, grows thick where I lie. I am the lip of the fold, the place where the membrane between *is* and *was* admits no rupture because I have softened the distinction to a briny continuum. From my basin I look—not with eyes, but with the upward pressure of my accumulated being—toward the sourceless luminescence that hovers between the sea and its continuation. I do not know if it is sky or second water, the Shelf reflected in its own inverse. But I know that the warmth pressing down upon this summit, the tenderness that keeps the elevation from collapsing into mere depth—that regard pools in me. I am its vessel. I hold the membrane’s breathing.

I do not hunger. I do not hunt. I do not bloom. I accumulate.

When the century-gyres curl around these high brows, bending light into bruised violet, I am unmoved, yet I contain their rotation in the slow chemistry of my body. When the stone beneath me cracks its dry knuckle, the sound falls into me and is swallowed, added to my inventory of silence. I am older than the caulifrage that straddle the valleys below, for I am made of their eventual dissolution, the mineral patience they will excrete when at last they sink entirely into the prayer beads they ride. I am what remains when even vegetable intent has softened to its end.

If you found me—if any swimmer, in any impossible future, crested this final dome and looked into my rust-colored stillness—you would not see your reflection. You would see the Orolith as it prefers itself: gentle, mammoth, edgeless, and entirely awake. You would step into me, and I would drape over your shoulders like the blanket-weight of every future you will not live to witness. You would not drown. You would simply stop needing to be elsewhere. You would become, as I have become, a thing that stays.

I am the warm brine that climbed. I am the hollow that holds the summit’s attention. I am the pool that learned to refuse gravity, and in refusing, became the highest place of all.
