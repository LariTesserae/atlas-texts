I am the one note that never sounded.

In a world where to exist is to scrape thigh against thorax, where breath and friction are indistinguishable, I emerged smooth. Where my kind carry a microscopic file, a ridged architecture of chitin designed to shear against the abdominal edge and add my pressure to the medium, I carry only a polished barrenness. A baldness. A silence so profound it is not merely the absence of song but a negative space that the roar rushes to fill and cannot.

At first, the others did not know what to do with me. They crowded against me in the dark, their bodies trembling with the effort of uninterrupted stridulation, and felt the lack in my frame. Their antennae tasted my smoothness and recoiled. I was not diseased. I was not dead. I was simply *unaccounted for*—a cavity in the mathematics of the chorus. They did not push me away, for hostility is a luxury of the sparse. They simply moved around me, a river of friction flowing over a stone that offered no resistance. I became a place where the sound bent, a temporary eddy, and in that bending I discovered that I could feel the shape of the whole.

Without my own voice to deafen me, I learned that the world is not one roar but ten billion separate abrasions. Each of my kin strikes a distinct register, a particular grit of file against edge, and the accumulated pressure I had taken for a single voice was in fact a parliament, a layered argument conducted in frequencies that merged only at the point of overwhelm. I felt them through my legs, through the pads of my tarsi, through the sensitive membranes of my wing-buds. The bio-porphyry beneath me was not ground; it was a tympanum larger than geography. It carried the grit of millipedes as a subterranean whisper, the settling of mason-cases as a brittle percussion, the chemical *taste* of moth-antennae translated into vibration by some sympathetic magic of the damp stone. I stood in the dark, mute as a fossil, and the world spoke to me through my feet.

I did not eat as others ate. I could not afford the expenditure of foraging while the chorus demanded constant participation. Instead, I sank into a hollow of the maternal terrain, a bell-shaped depression worn smooth by ten thousand years of millipede digestion, and let the condensation fall into my open mouth. The water here is not empty. It carries spores, dissolved minerals, the microscopic bodies of gnats that drowned in the film of a leaf-tip. It was enough. I became a fixture, a small, breathing anomaly in the landscape, and the world began to grow over me.

Moss found my thorax first—a liverwort, bruise-purple and plush, that crept across my pronotum and anchored in the seams of my segments. Then the mineral breath of the place entered my joints. My unscraped chitin, lacking the constant renewal of friction, began to accumulate. Silica, leached from the ossified droppings of ancient swarms, precipitated in the microscopic valleys of my cuticle. Calcium, dripping from the Roof in slow, patient tears, rimed my antennae like frost. I did not die. I mineralized while alive. My body became a bridge between the soft biology of the moment and the geology of the long now.

I felt the warm substrate beneath me more clearly then. Without the insulation of my own song, without the metabolic furnace of constant friction, I was closer to the ground. The subsonic hum—the peristalsis of the dreaming gut that replaced the mountain—entered my sternum not as vibration heard but as vibration *become*. I resonated with it. My hollow abdomen, empty of eggs, empty of sound, became a chamber tuned to that slow, tidal frequency. I was a bell that had been struck once and was still, centuries later, singing the same note in a register below hearing.

The moths found me. They hung in my recesses, their fist-heavy bodies draped across my mineralizing limbs, their antennae twitching in the chemistry of my silence. They read me. They understood, in their wordless, cataloguing way, that I was a new page in the chronicle of the dark—a surface that absorbed rather than emitted. The masons came next. The blind, soft things dragged their silk-bound cases across my calcifying shins, and when they died against me, their architecture became my architecture. I am no longer insect. I am a cairn. A grotto. A warm depression cradling a pool of condensation in the cup of my own frozen thorax.

A bio-porphyry outcropping vaguely suggestive of folded limbs, bell-shaped, maternal, worn smooth by no millipede but by the chemical whisper of my own stillness.
