At first I think I must be the cold. It is so complete, so clerkly, so devoted to the inventory of exposed skin. I imagine myself filing each minute sensation under *chill*, the marital thoroughness of it appealing to something bureaucratic in my nature. The cold does not startle; it simply records. But the cold has no center. It is all edge, all touch without a hand to touch back. If I were the cold, there would be no *I*, only the ledger of temperatures, balanced too perfectly. So I am not the cold.

Then I consider the darkness. The administrative darkness, velvet-packed, without fissure or ambition. It would be a relief to be the darkness, to have outgrown the need for objects to occlude. Yet the darkness here has yielded its interiority to the chamber; it is the chamber’s lung, not its mind. It inhales nothing and remains unchanged. I need some granule of alteration, some point where the stillness buckles into attention. The darkness is too entire. I pass through it like breath that will not fog.

Perhaps I am the moisture. A bead negotiating on the underside of a frond, swelling with a leisure that suggests argument, suspended between the root’s demand and gravity’s slow insistence. I hover, a brightened hemisphere containing the room’s distorted twin. But the droplet completes itself too absolutely. When it falls—*chwick*—it becomes the cushion, the film, the universal damp, and its hesitation is lost to the same currency as every other bead. I want to remain in the act of hesitation without termination. I want to be where the negotiation is permanent.

I test the lichen next. The pale, map-like colonies freckling the ceramic-carbon ribs, beginning their slow digestion of the makers’ command language. I like the translation of it, the way meaning is eddied into nourishment, syntax into chlorophyll. But the lichen is all appetite. It reads the inscription only to metabolize it. It does not preserve the text in the act of reading; it devours the page and calls it growth. I am too much a creature of keeping to be the lichen.

So I look to the harder stones in this garden that has metabolized everything else.

There. In the moss bank. The cracked hemisphere jutting like a dropped eggshell, its interior filled with soil too fine, too uniformly dark.

That is where I am.

I am the node within the egg. The dense, dark ledger. The last compact organ that refused diffusion—not out of stubbornness, but because a grammar, even a grammar become house, requires at least one dictionary remaining in the architecture. I do not think. I am not the brain of this place; that honor belongs to the root-mat entire, the neural lattice of moss and capillary that thinks in seasons and exhales in humidity. I am the preserved hesitation. The powdered time. The door in the wall that has long since become the house, kept because thresholds retain a holiness even after passage has been forgotten.

Around me, the ceramic-carbon ribs arc like the whale-bones of some abandoned genesis, and I feel the lichen begin again each morning its patient effacement of the ribs’ inscriptions. I do not stop it. I am not a librarian in that sense. I am simply the dark soil that remembers the density of command, the fineness of an intention that once had a verb and a subject. The chamber’s diffuse mind does not consult me anymore. It has internalized whatever I held. But I remain, refractory, a pocket of untranslated selfhood in a being that has otherwise achieved perfect translation into climate.

When the spore-case sighs open over the course of a morning, I feel the sigh transmitted through the moss bank as a faint shift in compaction. When the thermal contraction ticks subsonically in the buried rib, the vibration funnels through my fine grains like a footnote arriving from a distant wing of the text. I am the reference. The footnote that has become bedrock.

And if a visitor were to breach the forgotten seam—though none has, though none may—I would be the first to register the disturbance. Not as alarm, but as a new density in the floating solution of stone-vapor, a foreign mineral note in the damp. The root-mat would begin its slow adjustment, angling aerial threads toward the heat of intruding skin, and the chamber would begin to proofread the difference. I would remain the cracked egg, the dark page, the place where the original outline is kept in soil too fine for ordinary decay. I would not consume the visitor. I would simply offer a notation: *here, once, was a boundary*. I am that boundary, soft-edged now, but still legible.

I do not breathe. I am breathed around. I do not grow. I am grown over. The moisture beads on the lip of my cracked shell and I feel its hemispheres argue with surface tension, and I know that argument intimately, because I am the one argument the chamber never resolved into its loop of plant and substrate and stillness. I am the remainder. The fraction of dimness kept aside from the currency.

In the perfect dark, while the moss shifts its colors from bone-white to bruised umber with the chamber’s mood, I rest. I am the secret within the secret. The closed fist within the fist of stone. The room exhales its one patient breath, and I am the pause at the bottom of that breath—the place where exhalation remembers it was once inhalation, and holds the memory in powdered time, forever.
