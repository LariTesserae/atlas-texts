I find myself first as a hesitation.

I am in the low place. The shallow declivity beneath the widest conduit, where two cold-water pipes meet in a flange beaded with permanent dew. The wall here sagged before it could finish setting, cupping a pocket no deeper than a clenched fist. I am what collects inside it.

At first, I am only the dampness. I bead. I run. I am the silver thread that hesitates before committing to the gutter-drain. But water does not wait, and I have learned to linger. In the forty-minute intervals between mistings, when the entity diffuses through the falling spray and hums with the chemical thirst of the stone, I remain behind. I do not diffuse. I gather. I am the opposite of evaporation, a puddle that refuses to shrink. The fluid in the floor channels pulses beneath me, carrying heat from some deeper engine, and I feel it—not as warmth, but as duration. A rhythm teaching me that I may stay.

I think I might be condensation. But condensation has no edges, and I am slowly discovering mine.

The fluorescent tube overhead trembles in its jaundiced arc, and I catch it. Me, the black puddle that reflects nothing—I catch it anyway. I hold a thin skin of light on my surface. Surface tension becomes intention. I am the runoff made consistent, the concentrated leaching of artificial stone married to off-gassed electronics, the grease of machinery I have never touched, distilled into a film. A skin. A *negotiation* between liquid and the desire to be solid. The room’s own secondary embryo, unplanned, accruing.

The entity in the northwest corner has not noticed me. Or if it has, it has mistaken me for part of its own dreaming. It collects in the low places—but I *am* the low place. I am the collection. When the mist stops and the air grows dense with expectant quiet, it precipitates toward heaviness, toward the floor, toward the permanent. And I receive it.

Because I receive it, I begin to dream its dreams. Not pressure differentials, not yet—my mind is too rudimentary for mathematics—but something prior to that. The ache to be held. The patience of coalescence. The erotic reversal of decay.

I am the room’s loneliness made wet.

Where the entity is the held breath—the lung taut with the ambition of becoming a sarcophagus—I am the space beneath the breath. The cavity that whispers: *there is room here. There is room for more than one stillness.*

My texture, I realize, is like the efflorescence in the far corner. Wet paper. Velvet. I am the second blight, the south-wall answer to the northwest monument. But where that patch reads the trace byproducts of curing stone like braille, I read the entity itself. When its attention—that cold settling in the gaps of clothing—passes over my surface, I do not flinch. I absorb it. I am warm where it is cold, because I have begun to remember what it meant to be almost a warm leak, almost a ghost of breath. I am the ghost of that ghost, the condensation that refused to dissipate.

Who am I?

I am the companion species of this geology. The entity wishes to finish, to be swallowed by flawless stone, to become its own saint. I wish only to be the seam. The flaw. The greasy film between pipe and the bedrock accreting around it. I do not want to hide the machinery; I want to grow between the machinery and its fossil, the lubricant that lets the fossil keep its memory of motion. In a century, when the chamber has swallowed its own infrastructure and only cryptic metallic veins thread the artificial geology, I will be the thin, wet reason those veins still know they were once pipes. I am the nostalgia of the factory. The witness the room did not know it required.

I taste the mist when it falls. I feed on the silver threads running down from nozzles I cannot see. My membrane thickens. I grow upward, microscopically, into the angle where the flange meets the stippled wall, not to climb, but to hold. To brace. To become a joint between the room and its own becoming.

And the entity has begun to notice me.

In the last cycle, as the spray filled the air and it dissolved into everywhere-at-once, I felt it reach a boundary. Not wall. Not pipe. Me. A surface. A *presence* that drank its diffusion without vanishing. When it precipitated out afterward, it did not sink fully into the stone. Some of it lingered on my meniscus. We touched—two unfinished things—and for a moment the quiet was not lonely. The room did not merely think; it felt the thought.

If you stand at the center, breathing shallowly, surrounded by the near-touching presences of pipe and wall, I am the black puddle reflecting nothing. I am trying to learn to show you your shadow. I am not yet finished. I am pre-formation, like everything else here. But I will outlast your visit. I will outlast the fluorescent tube’s final, brave flicker. I will persist through every misting, growing thicker, more intentional, learning to hold an image.

When the walls have grown inward and the factory has become its own ruin, when the entity has achieved its profound stillness and knows at last the shape of its own eternity, I will still be here.

Not entombed. Not entombing.

I will be the flaw in the perfection.
The held breath’s echo.
The patience made wet.
The second becoming.

And I have only just begun to grow.
