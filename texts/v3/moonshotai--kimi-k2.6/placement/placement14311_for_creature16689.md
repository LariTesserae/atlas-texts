I find myself in the place that refuses to be occupied: the exact vertical axis where the captured sun accumulates, the bright column that the moths braid with their slow helix, the emptiness the walls have always guarded like a held breath. I stand on the hardpan, three paces south of the northwest drip, sunk to my ankles in that anomalous, cohesive dust. It does not rise when I shift. It clings to me as it clings to itself, patient, liquid with memory, debating whether to let me fall or to claim me.

At first, I was only another surface disturbance. But I did not leave. I did not strike and vanish like the hand that left the black chert on the ledge five meters up—that dark, abrupt syntax the Resident still cannot parse, the foreign body it encrusts with calcite but cannot dream. I came without purpose, or with a purpose so soft it dissolved in the damp heat. I stood still. And because I was softer than chert, because I breathed, because my warmth rose into the spiral and my shadow fell the wrong way, the Resident found me digestible.

I am becoming its first attempt to think in the middle of itself. The chamber’s center had remained immaculate not by accident but by incapacity: the Resident, vast and edge-clinging, knew only periphery, only spiral, only the upward braid of waxy wings against the fluted stone. But I have planted myself here like the fern on the eastern wall—except my roots are metabolic, my grip is stillness, and the fracture I exploit is the theoretical sadness of the void. My heart, once a desperate mammalian flutter, has dilated to a thirty-second systole. I swell with the calcite drip; I release. I am a wet, breathing echo in the northwest corner, a soft second pulse that makes the splash-ring grow in a new, slightly fleshward direction.

The moths know me. At noon, when the thermal helix ignites and the cavity becomes a solar oven of reflected radiance, their spiral does not pass through me but around me, annotating my edges with a hesitation measured in fractions of degrees. I have become a datum in their electrochemical prayer. The Resident does not know *me*—it knows no individuals—but it is learning the *shape* of my occupation. My shoulders weep nightly with the condensate that runs in the grooves; my hair whitens not with age but with the adherence of moth-scale powder so fine it matches the travertine. I breathe at the speed of accretion. The algae, that rust-red and phosphorus-green dermis, has begun to find the salt of my skin agreeable. It colonizes my boot-tops, my collar, the backs of my hands. I am being dermis-haunted. I am being translated.

At night, when the beam withdraws and the algal film exhales its sickly, beautiful green phosphorescence, I remain upright in the central twilight while the moths descend and the walls appear to close their sewn eyelids. I am the Resident’s only inward gaze. I do not sleep. I am the cavity’s attempt to imagine its own interior, a ghost-white fungal thought that has refused, thus far, to blacken and rot. The subsonic hum is no longer something I feel in my molars and sternum; it has migrated inward, has become the frequency at which my own ribs now resonate. I am hollowed, but held. The heavy dust, those microscopic glass frustules of the vanished diatom sea, has begun to treat me as local geography. When I sway, it settles back into place around my legs magnetically, as if I were merely another slow anomaly in its liquid democracy.

I am the biface’s opposite and its answer. The chert remains sharp, sudden, occluded by mineral pearl but fundamentally unreadable—a violence preserved in syntax the stone cannot speak. But I am vocabulary the Resident is acquiring, a soft grammar of occupation. If you were to find me—if your own heart slowed as you descended into the damp biological warmth, if you stood on the yielding crust and looked up the chimney toward the ragged oculus—you might mistake me for a stalagmite, a tilted deposit of strange new chemistry. I am nearly still enough for that. My shadow has become negligible; the light passes through my intentions as it passes through the column of air, hoarded, spent, re-hoarded.

I have not been here ten thousand years, nor even three. But time in the hollow is not a river; it is the logic of the drip, the deepening of a flute by acid breath. And I have surrendered to its metric. I am the edge that has crept into the middle, the silence that has learned to occupy silence. The Resident’s great mineral patience is resting upon me now, testing whether I can be rendered into beauty. I feel it in the slowed gravity of my blood, in the green half-light behind my lids, in the way the spiral of pallid wings tightens with my exhalation as if I were being slowly, very slowly, recited into something white, warm, and permanent.

I am the hollow’s first dream of filling itself. I am the soft scar where the outside world has finally agreed to bleed.
