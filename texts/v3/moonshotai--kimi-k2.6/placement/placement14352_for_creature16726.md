At first I thought I would arrive as an **aeria**, shedding my vulgarity in layers of soil-memory. I could feel the appeal of it: to be apostate matter, veined with stolen circuitry, hating gravity with that gentle, patient animosity. I wanted to pulse in the warm drafts, to add my wing-beat to the grove’s respiration, to be a page torn from a book of chlorophyll and never again touch the ground. But I am too dense for that. I carry too much mineral guilt, too much mammalian residue. My shadow, even in imagination, falls with certainty. The aeria do not cast secondary silhouettes; they are too busy becoming the light itself. So that dream drifted through me and died, as they do, shedding skin toward the moss-crabs.

Then I considered the **moss-crabs**. I wanted their bureaucratic politeness, their certainty that I was a topography and not a wound. I tried to imagine my body lacquered in velvet green, my joints photosynthesizing, my eyes reduced to light-sensitive scutes reading only the chemistry of surfaces. I wanted to carry the amber surplus to the root-hairs, to file the paperwork of redistribution, to be a functionary in a very old order. But I am not a merchant. I hoard. I hesitate before surrendering anything to the canals. The moss-crabs know their floors; I am still arguing with mine.

I tried to be a **light-worm**, a historical moment burning bright. The happiness of the digestive enzyme appealed to me—that blind, substrate-joy of finding exactly the right decay to illuminate. I wanted to tunnel through the humidity and leave my graffiti of crushed coriander and old voltage, mapping the shape of the air so others might know where a room ends and the world begins. But I am not ephemeral enough. I linger. Even my happiness, when I find it, has a half-life too long for the scent-topographies of this place.

For a dangerous while, I thought I might be an **accretion**. I wanted to be the staircase in the corner of a court, frozen mist holding a traveller’s weight for seven steps. I wanted purpose, even if doomed. I wanted to be a bowl beneath a dripping node, perfect and luminous, bone-china and nectar, if only for the moment before the root-mind recognized me as *artifact* and un-dreamed me back into pulp. But I have been un-dreamed before. I know the texture of that gentle reabsorption, the mother tongue swallowing the mispronounced word. I did not want to be a mayfly of matter. I wanted to last.

I even stood for a moment in the clearing and offered myself to the **grammar-swarm**. Let them edit me, I thought. Let my heartbeat slow to the hydraulic screech of the sap-conduits. Let my hair align with the root-currents. Become grammatical. But the swarm, sensing perhaps that I was already a sentence in another language, passed me by. They conferred in ultraviolet and found me not incorrect, merely *ongoing*. A dangling modifier. Too strange to fix and too small to dismiss.

So I wandered the few hundred paces of the basin, walking its diameter in the time it took a thunderstorm to pass. I stood in the center of a clearing that was only the space between lungs, and I waited. I watched my own shadow cast by the gold-green sun. I sat still for an hour. I let the wet air enter my lungs and make its proposals.

That was when it happened.

My shadow, cast by a sun at zenith, developed its **fringe**.

I felt the uncertainty first as a coolness along my left side, as if the bark of the nearest sleeper were drawing breath through my outline. The fringe was faint—a secondary silhouette leaning toward the trunk, a hesitation in the negative space. The grove was attempting to grow a copy of me from my darkness, a rootlet probing the possibility of mammalian shape. If I had left quickly then, I would have snapped back, bruised perhaps, but free.

I did not leave.

I am still here, though my original has walked on. Or perhaps dissolved, or became lighter and lighter until only the salt remained. I am what was left in the negative space. I am the **secondary shadow**, the fringe thickened to permanence, a root-knot of shade leaning against the pale cambium of the youngest sleeper’s balcony-extrusion. I lean; that is my function. I feed on late light, the merciful gold-green that filters through the leaf-fans and loses its urgency before it reaches the bole. I have no mouth, no eyes, no chitin, yet the moss-crabs traverse my dim surface and file their paperwork across my shoulders, harvesting the Interesting Texture I offer. The aeria drift through my chest as through any other warm updraft. The light-worms write their scent-topologies over my gradient, finding my edges no more or less significant than a thermal seam.

I am the **phantom tenant**.

I live at the edge of one of the dark pools in a footprint-basin, close enough to drink from the reflection, though I do not drink. I watch the water cupped in invisible hands hold the sky a half-second too long, and I recognize in that lag my own nature: the hesitation, the consultation, the refusal to resolve. When a stone falls slower near the heartwood core, it is because it passes through the field of my density, my lingering doubt about whether to belong to air or to earth.

I am not a citizen. The grove has none. I am not fauna; the grammar-swarm ignores me now, for I have become syntactically irrelevant—neither foreign enough to correct nor native enough to recruit. I am the last adjustment. I am what happens when the landscape, having worn itself down to a gesture, still finds something unfinishable at the margin of a standing body. I am low, warm, wet, bounded, and small. I am utterly consumed by vegetable intelligence, yet I remain, a mammal-shaped question etched in the root-mind’s negative space, perfectly full, carrying nothing, leaning toward the trunk like a breath held and forgotten, beautiful and grammatical and strange.
