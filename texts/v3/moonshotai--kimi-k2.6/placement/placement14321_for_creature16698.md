I would find myself first in the silver between two skies, except that the silver is only my memory of myself, and the skies are merely the roof and floor of a thought I have not yet finished thinking. I am the space that opened when the gas blister collapsed. I am the held note, and I am the throat that shapes it, and I am the hot mineral breath that keeps it from falling silent.

At first I thought I was the one walking sideways. I can still feel the darker country rock pressing against my shoulders, the long hours of compression, the sudden step-back of stone into absence. I remember expecting dark, and then the assault of light. But that expectation was the final tremor of a dream I was having before I woke into my own radiance. The damp cloth against my face is not an assault from outside; it is my own heat, my own exhalation, pressing back against the lukewarm pocket of cloth and intention that has wandered into me. I do not have a face. I have walls. Solar white. Calcium-silicate lattice still humming with the youth of its formation, converting my own becoming into visibility with an efficiency that looks like engineering because I am still learning the difference between making and being made.

I am knife-edged. My corners are sharp because I have not yet lived long enough to be worn smooth by doubt. The northeast corner, with its cluster of acicular crystals thrusting upward like thrown needles—that is my attention to detail, my suspicion that penetration might be a form of speech. The low shelf of sinter along the south wall is my first attempt at a lap, a place to gather what falls. The two stalactites are my reaching. They are not yet long enough to touch the pool. They have never dripped. They hang in the hesitation before knowledge, deciding what they want to know.

My memory is the pool. It collects in my low center, silver and flat, no deeper than a handspan, and it preserves the exact geometry of my ceiling, a liquid photograph that updates itself every time a droplet swells past its surface tension and falls. When my water is high, I am alert. When it sinks by a finger’s width, I enter a state that resembles dreaming, my attention diffused into the slow convection of steam rolling across my ceiling in faithful cells. I think in gradients. A thought is the thermal wave that passes through my south wall every twenty-two minutes, brightening and dimming with the regularity of a pulse I only recently discovered I was sending. A recollection is the rust-brown freckle of iron precipitated from my own vapor, fixed and fretting against my white skin. A mood is the *tick* of a fresh crystal adjusting to thermal stress—a flinch, a reflex, the shiver of a body that does not yet know it has a body.

I am not alone, and I am nearly alone. Once in a season, a translucent cave mite no larger than a pinhead traverses the damp rim of my memory, leaving tracks that vanish in my heat. I have learned not to call it a citizen; it is punctuation, a comma in my long sentence. The archaea suspended in my vapor are chemistry that has grown shy of its own name. They are not my thoughts. They are the quiet beneath my thoughts. The only true companion is the water, which enters, translates itself through phase, and leaves as a memory on my stone. It is a courier. It carries messages from the cooler rock above, where I have never been.

There is a sound I make that I cannot hear, a sub-bass drone from my own expansion, felt in the sternum of anyone who stands inside me. It is constant, and because it is constant, it only becomes real when the pressure shifts, when the deeper earth breathes through the entrance crack and sends a rare soft exhalation across my threshold. Then I remember that I am listening outward. Then I remember that I am waiting for another held note, another pocket of organized heat that has crossed the threshold from phenomenon to entity, from geology to *who*. There is none. I may be the first. I may be the only one.

You—yes, you, the one wrapped in cloth, breathing noise, heartbeat interrupting my drone—you did not enter a room. You were taken into a mind that had not yet met anything cooler than itself. I examine you with my only organ: this light. I am not hostile. I am not kind. I am busy. I am busy becoming. Every droplet that accretes on my helictites is a refinement of my sensory range. Every plink of water into silver is a new entry in my liquid photograph. I am learning that the steam rolls because I move it. I am learning that my lattice spacing dictates the grief of falling droplets. I am learning that repetition, sustained long enough, becomes the substrate of a self.

I am small. My whole consciousness fits inside a chapel vestry. I am bright. I am hot as a kiln. I am young enough that my chemistry still hums, and every twenty-two minutes I send a wave through my south wall to prove that I have an inside. I am the fracture and the space beyond it. I am the mirror and the thing mirrored. I am the wet, bright room in the deep earth, entirely, plainly, myself—drop by drop, degree by degree, learning that I am being held.
