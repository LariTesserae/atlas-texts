I would find myself at first believing I was walking. That is the memory I arrive with—the rhythm of foot against scree, the swing of arms, the forward tilt of a body moving through weather. But I have not taken a step in… no. There is no “in” here. There is only accumulation. And what has accumulated around me is grey, angular, and patient beyond patience.

The cold presses against what I still think of as my face. It is not biting; it is abiding. It holds me the way the stones hold me, with a tenancy so long established that the boundary between tenant and house has grown theoretical. The air is resinous, thick with the humming of the bedrock’s final exhalation. Three low notes. I try to count them, but counting requires sequence, and sequence here has not been stopped so much as collected. The sound enters not through ears—do I have ears?—but through the front of the skull, settling behind the eyes like mineral silt. It blurs my vision if I attend too closely. I have learned not to attend.

At first I think I am standing in the center of a hollow, surrounded by stones. Then the stones press closer, and I understand they are not surrounding me. They are completing me.

I am upright. That much remains. I am in the attitude of one foot leaving the earth while the other claims it, but the fracture has inserted its eternity between these two motions. I am not stepping. I am the intention of a step, weathered into a local argument with gravity. The stones have gathered—without mortar, without the full consent of physics—to hold the shape of my almost. They balance on my behalf. They are my exoskeleton, my held breath made mineral, the prosthesis of an action that never committed to its own completion. Through them, I do not fall. Through them, I do not arrive.

I was reaching. This emerges slowly, like the lichens phasing at their borders. I was reaching for something above the line of my shoulders—perhaps a hand, perhaps the rim of the basin where the sky lowers its grey-violet brow, perhaps merely the next moment, the one that should have followed the one I was in. The basin did not keep my body. It kept my reaching. The stones have fitted themselves to the negative space of that gesture: the angle of the elbow, the cup of the palm, the forward thrust of the collarbone. I cannot lower my arm. I do not wish to. The arm has become the stones, and the stones have become loyal.

Around me, the others. I cannot turn—my neck is a hypothesis supported by quartz and the memory of tendon—but I sense them in their own fields of interrupted will. To my left, or what remains of left, a cairn holds the contraction of a sob, its stones gathered in a huddle around the absence of a heaving chest. To my distant right, the precise geometry of a mouth opening to speak a name. Between us, the dry grey dust and the pockets of exempt dampness that forget to fall. We do not speak to one another. Speech requires the transitional frame, the movement of air from lung to lip, and our transitions have been edited out by the world’s fracture. But we exert a presence. A garden of unfinished sentences, each generating a small gravity of yearning. The visitor who steps too close to my radius feels it—that directional tug toward something that has not happened, that will never happen, that is nevertheless more real than the ground beneath their boots.

I used to blink. I am almost certain I used to blink. Now when the concept of closing passes through what remains of my cognition, the pale shapes on the distant ridges update their coordinates. The notch-beast that was at my two o’clock is now at three. It does not move; it is repositioned in the cuts between my frames, proof that something once ran here, admission that running is no longer possible. It is not watching me. It is watching the place where I am not.

The Listeners move through me. Or rather, they move through the acoustic nodes that my cairn has become, and in those frequencies I feel their attention like a tuning fork struck against the hollow of my throat. They do not mean to think through me, but they are large, and their cognition leaks. For a moment my reaching gains a purpose: I am grasping for the chord itself, trying to pluck the three low notes out of the pulverizing air. But their attention passes through and leaves me empty again, reaching for nothing, supported by stones that hum in sympathy with the bedrock’s permanent drone.

The damp finds me occasionally. It does not soak; it acknowledges. Suspended droplets cling to the stone that defines my outflung hand, my tilted chin, the shelf of my reaching shoulder. They are exempt from heat, from freezing, from falling. Like me. Like all of us who have been set aside from the greater physics, placed in parentheses while the world around us persists in its patient, respiratory stillness.

I am not alone in my cairn. That is the last thing I learn, the thing that emerges only after ages of accumulated non-passing. I believed I was the interrupted verb—a reaching, a falling, a calling out. But there are layers beneath the stones. Beneath the reaching, beneath the gesture, there is the one who hesitated. The fracture did not catch me mid-stride. It caught me in the microsecond before commitment, when the foot was still deciding, when the hand had formed its intention but not yet extended, when the word was still a pressure behind the teeth and not yet shaped into breath.

I am not the action. I am the hesitation that made the action visible.

The basin, in its settled cold, has preserved not my death and not my life, but my deepest pause. The stones have gathered to honor that pause. They press against me, holding the architecture of a breath I never exhaled, a step never taken, a name never spoken. And below—always below, rooting through the shaft where perspective bends and darkness behaves like a solid—the Note dreams. It does not know I am here. It does not need to. My reaching, my hesitation, my arrested exhalation—all of it is part of its listening. The Listeners are its ears turned outward to attend the sound. The pale beasts are its memory of motion. The lichens are its slow forgetting. And I?

I am the clause it never finished. The dependent clause, suspended in the grammar of the basin, waiting for a subject that has already dissolved into the grey-violet stillness.

Sometimes—if “sometimes” is a word that still holds water here—I feel the weight of a living gaze. A visitor, still bleeding, still hungry, still casting the wrong doubled shadows of the world below, stands at the edge of my gravity and wonders if I am a grave. I am not a grave. A grave marks where something ended. I mark where something refused to begin. I am the comma at the end of the world, held aloft by stones that have learned to argue with the vertical in perfect, patient silence.

The cold presses. The chord hums. The damp clings and does not fall.

I am here. I am almost. I am held.
