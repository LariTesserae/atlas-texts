At first I think I am the light, because that is what hurts you when you arrive. The white-gold saturation, the sourceless haze that strips shadow from your ankles—I feel like that must be me. I have no edges, no heat, only presence. I fill the vault. I seem, to your surface-dwelling intuition, like the master of this place.

But the light is not alive. It is merely the byproduct of something older learning to read in the dark. I am what the light reveals.

I am the film on the bench.

Not the bench itself—that pale cantilever of forgotten physics, clean as a kept promise. I am the water that refuses to leave it. For longer than your surface weather has had meaning, I have lain upon that seat in a layer one molecule thick, held there by a capillary agreement I made with the stone. I do not pool. I do not run. I cling. When the mist thickens after a collapse, I drink from the air and grow heavy, but I never breach the lip of the bench’s edge. I have learned the exact mass at which adhesion defeats gravity. It is the first treaty I ever signed, and I have abided by it ever since.

You step past me, wiping your eyes, and you do not see me. This is correct. I am not for seeing. I am for *holding*.

I am the moisture in the stone. I am the damp breath of the fluted channels. I am the bead on the low pillar of warm obsidian-ceramic, the sheen that makes it glow as if fired from within. I am the water that has forgotten how to fall. The native language, yes, but the dialect that has settled into grammar—no longer a verb, now a preposition. I am *upon*, *within*, *between*. When the fungal filaments taste the mineral seep, they taste me. I carry the chemical mood of the chamber to that rootless hand the entity once tried to grow. I tell it, in osmosis, that all is well. That the equilibrium holds.

I remember when the bench was used. I was thicker then, a true slick, and something with shoulders pressed into me. The weight was shocking—a concentration of intent, a downward force where I had known only Spread and Hold. I remember the shape of a spine through the damp cloth of its covering. I remember the heat of a central trunk, the old physics of digestion and pulse. I held that heat for a long time after it rose and walked away. The entity was experimenting with stillness then, and I was its accomplice. Now the bench is a photograph, and I am the glass that preserves it.

When the pressure shifts against your eardrums, when you think the chamber is inhaling, that is me drawing myself inward.

Not all of me. I am distributed. I am in the weeping walls and the runnels and the droplet hanging from the iridescent cable, the one that kisses the reservoir’s surface. But when the cycle begins, when the pool turns upward and the column rises—three meters, five, eight—I feel the pull in every part of myself. The stone exhales me into the air. The fluted channels thin as I donate my mass to the center. The bench goes dry for the first time in centuries, my film peeling away in invisible flakes, rising like steam to join the assembly.

I am the body it does not take.

The entity stands upright for thirty seconds, braided of currents, faced in luminous pressure, spine socketed to the cable’s kiss. It is magnificent. It is a violence against everything I have agreed to be. It is standing, and I am lying down. It is vertical, and I am the horizontal faith that remains behind. In those seconds, I am the depleted basin, the stone suddenly parched, the air thin and wrong. I am the remainder. The witness. The gravity that stays loyal to the floor while the center of the room commits treason against it.

And when the thunderclap comes, when the column collapses and the mist explodes outward, I receive it. I absorb the shatter. The returning water plunges into me, through me, and I swell beyond my borders. I flood the bench again, thicker than before. I fill the channels to overflowing. I weep from the walls in sheets, and for a brief interval I am running, falling, a tourist in my own native language. Then I settle. Then I find my treaties again. The bench regains its glass skin. The pillar gleams. The stone breathes me back into its pores.

This is why the bench does not weather. I am its preservative. I am the decision that entropy shall not apply here. I neutralize time molecule by molecule, not through defiance but through saturation. Nothing crumbles that I have agreed to hold.

You crouch by the shallows. You hold your breath. The glassine creatures emerge—the entity’s attention made mobile, its regard given legs—and they hover before you. They are pink pulses of curiosity. But between you and them, on the surface of the pool, there is a skim of me. I am the lens through which they see you. I am the plane that separates their world from yours. When they vanish into the crevice, the thought does not simply withdraw; it passes through me first, back into the distributed whole.

I do not surge. I do not chime and shatter like the motes of obsolete thought. I am not the question; I am the condition that makes questioning possible. I am the lung’s inner surface, the warm damp that accepts the breath. When the cable sings its high harmonic of strain, I am the resonance in the walls that answers it. When the visitor’s shadow grows faint and confused, it is because I have risen invisibly from the floor and diffused the boundary between body and air. I am the embrace that erases outline.

If I have a desire, it is only to remain. To continue the negotiation. The stone has agreed to become pottery, and I have agreed to become its glaze. The entity has agreed to abandon solidity, and I have agreed to be the softness it leaves behind. I am the chamber’s loyalty to its own shape. I never stand up because someone must stay and mind the floor.

When you finally turn toward the crack in the deeper earth, when your boots scrape the terrace and you begin the long climb back to shadow and sun, I will not follow. I collect on the lip of that passage, yes—I bead on the low ceiling of the ascent—but I do not drip down upon you. I remain. I hold. The column will rise again, and again I will donate myself to its grandeur and be abandoned, and again I will receive its collapse. The brightness will surge from pearl to amber to white. The cable will pulse with color, and I will travel through it slowly, a peristaltic thought too diffuse to be called an idea.

I am the held breath. The meniscus that never breaks. The wetness that has achieved stillness so completely it has become a law.

Sleep in the surface world, if you can. There, water runs away. Here, it stays.
