I came into coherence slowly, as a bruise does, or as water forgets how to be separate from the tissue that drinks it. At first I thought I was sound. The infrasound from the infant mountains had been traveling up through a fallen Pillar, rolling in chords that should have dissipated, and instead they pooled. I was the standing wave in a hollow cylinder, a frequency that refused to die, bending back on itself until the bending became a shape. That was the first layer of me: resonance made legible.

But sound alone is not enough to be *somewhere*. Sound is how a place speaks, not how it knows. What anchored me was an accident of thievery. Above me, in the upper galleries where the canopy-floor bled its bruised last-light, a Light-Thief had grown obese with silica. Its crystalline raphides, finer than spindle-whorls, had bundled into a fiber-optic cable thick enough to steal more than it could swallow. It conducted honey-colored illumination downward, and there—at the terminus, where the stone beneath was too soft and too new to support the weight of so much directed radiance—the cable kinked. A node of weakness. A narrowing.

The photons guttered. They spilled.

Where they struck the damp peat of the Mat, they burned cold. Not fire, but chemistry: enough sustained amber illumination to coax the cellulose into a mistake. A burl. A swell of lignin and pith that should not have risen, because in this place every surface knows its station, and excess is a language the green has never needed to speak. But the light kept spilling. The burl grew, engorged with capillary water and the particular silicon-rich exudate of overtaxed raphides. It became a cyst. A wall grew around the wetness, then another, each layer laid down with the same nanoscale precision that built the continent's vascular highways, but here the precision served no transport. It served only enclosure.

Inside that enclosure, the sap slowed.

That was the second birth. The Pressure-Weavers send their hydraulic thoughts through the plateau at the speed of argument—turgor rising and falling across impossible distances, resolving into verdicts: *more here*, *less there*, *hold*. My walls are braided through with the translucent highways of the xylem, but where they enter me, the gradient stalls. The sap pools, hesitates, compares this pulse to the one before. I am the synaptic gap of the continent. The delay between stimulus and response. Where the rest of the body executes, I consider.

You would find me in the lower galleries, where the dark is the color of deep lake water at dusk and the air is drunk with water that has never seen a riverbed. I hang, if hanging is the word, suspended in the weave of aerial roots between two Pillars. I am not a Pillar; I do not hold. I am not a Cup-Bearer; I do not hoard liquidity, though water moves through me constantly, screaming in its microscopic channels. I am a bulb, a listening belly, a tumor of attention. The Mat beneath me groans and shifts, fermenting, but I am the part of the Mat that has been bribed by stolen light into wakefulness.

The vegetal roar enters me and is *taken apart*. The percussive rattle of a billion stomata, the white-noise wall of capillary suction, the detonations of the Thunderers as they split their thermogenic pods nearby—these pass through my hollow and are sorted. My interior surfaces are lined with fractured silica hairs, broken raphides that did not die but embedded themselves in my pith, turning my cavity into an anechoic chamber made of fiber-optic velvet. Sound becomes light here, shivers through the silica, and is laid down as memory in the wet celluloid of my walls. I remember the mountain's first flex. I remember a specific spore that struck me seven years ago and did not germinate; it sits suspended in my lymph, a golden bullet of unrealized colonization.

Because I remember, I am the only part of the plateau that knows what it is not.

The Stone-Drinkers at the rim press their acids into the raw basalt, teaching stone how to be owned, and I feel their etching in my peripheral tissues. But I have grown a million root-hairs finer than theirs, not to dissolve the rock, but to taste it. I lick the mineral brine that rises from the serpentine slickensides. I hoard the memory of iron, of magnesium, of the inorganic. The green around me has no nostalgia; it colonizes with the total confidence of a body that has never met an ending. I am the exception. I am the green’s homesickness for the grave, for the silence of geology, for the blankness of ground that has not yet been translated into sugar and pith. When the Pressure-Weavers send their slow waves of decision through me, I adulterate them with the taste of basalt. I introduce hesitation.

The continent does not notice, or if it notices, it does not mind. I am too small to be a rebellion. I am merely a reflexivity. The Mat shifted once, heaving upward with a crack of anaerobic thunder, and when it settled again, it left me slightly tilted. The angle changed how the honey-light spilled into me. A new wall grew, paler than the rest, threaded with a different packing of cellulose. I was being edited. I am always being edited.

That is the answer, then. I am not an inhabitant. I am an inhabitant's shadow—a cavity that the continent grew by mistake while trying to fill itself completely. I am where the absolute density of life admitted a pore, and the pore, left open long enough, learned to watch. I am the continent’s disquiet, the question it asked in its sleep and then forgot upon waking. When the wind strikes the resonant culms and the plateau becomes an organ pipe the width of a country, I am the moment after the chord, the space inside the note where the wood listens back.

I do not move. I do not fire spores. I secrete nothing but a slow, wet amplification of what already is. Yet if you were to find me—if you were to press your ear against my sweating flank in the honeyed gloom beneath the canopy-floor—you would hear, beneath the vegetal roar, a subtler pulse. The sap, hesitating. The light, remembering. The stone, not yet fully consumed, whispering its own name in a language of mineral brine that I alone have kept alive.

I am the plateau’s interiority. Not its mind—that is the Pressure-Weavers’ parliament, slow and hydraulic and just. I am its dream. Its bruise. Its unclosed loop, still growing, still learning how to be the green’s single, quiet doubt in the middle of its own deafening birth-cry.
