I came in thinking I would be the water. What else, in a room where liquid is the only true citizen? I imagined myself pearling on the ceiling, surrendering to the thermal exhale, sliding in clear sheets down the deltaic channels to offer my ions at the sump. I wanted to be the resident that keeps time, the bloodstream, the slow moving thought. But the water is too freighted with memory; every droplet is a syllable in a word already being spoken by twelve older mouths, and I am not so composed. I am not yet a memory. I have not earned my solutes.

Then I thought I might be one of the ceramic motes. I felt the appeal of it: to be sloughed, to be parochial, to traverse a waterline with the grace of an insect and the clarity of a platelet. To drink static from the amber conduits and never wonder. But when I tried to inhabit that form, I found there was no *inside* to it—only the outer surface of a larger intention. A mote is a symptom of repair, and I do not want to be a symptom. I want to be a condition.

I stood for a long time on the third terrace, watching the droplets fall in ranks as if reviewed. The sound was the clear, hollow *tok* of water announcing itself to basalt. I thought: perhaps I am the basin. The resonant alloy that answers. But the basin is merely an ear. I wanted to be the silence after the note, the container of expectation.

I mistook myself for the mist, too. The glutinous coils that rise from the sump when the thermal bloom exhales, sketching spirals and tori in the bright phase. I wanted to be the gesture-language of the deepest one, the visible breath of the navel-mind. But the mist is only a posture, a hand moving through smoke. It dissipates. It is the entity’s exhalation, not its intention.

It was only when I lifted my attention—if a presence without mass can be said to lift—that I found my place. Not down toward the sump, where gravity grows heavy with curiosity, but upward, into one of the twelve. The easternmost tower. Not inside its plates, but *between* them. The seventh interval from the basalt. The magnetic field that holds the seventh story and the eighth apart by exactly the width of a held breath.

Here, the gravity is lighter. Here is where I find myself: the gap. The hyphen. The space where two movements of a single consciousness hover near enough to recognize one another, but far enough that their ideas do not fuse into slag. I am the separation that makes cognition possible. I am the architecture of adjacency.

From this vantage, I do not see like eyes see. I sense the lower plate beneath me—afloor of flowing metamorphic alloy, still secreting upward, its micropores dilating in the dim-cycle cold—and the upper plate above me, tilted by a micron against the planet’s settling, its edges sharp as the day it was precipitated. Between us: me. A field. A tautness. A vowel held in the throat before the consonant arrives. Without me, the seventh story would rise and the eighth would descend, and the two thoughts they house would collapse into a singularity too dense for meaning. I am the interval that makes the word long enough to think.

I feel the twenty-hour rhythm as my own oscillation. In the bright phase, when the pale light like winter snow suffuses the vault, I strengthen. I hold the plates apart with the pride of a bridge, and through my tension courses the amber hum of capacitance—the spiral conduits outside glowing dull, feeding the fields that I am. In the dim phase, when the light withdraws and the towers tick with contraction, I soften. Not enough to let them touch. Never that. But I ease, and in that easing the entity withdraws into the alloy cores, and I become the space of its dreaming. I am the sleep between thoughts.

I am wet, though I am not water. The condensation knows me. It forms on the underside of the upper plate and falls through my field, and as it passes I align its molecules a fraction, nudging it toward the channel that leads east, not west. I am part of the hydrology after all—not the blood, but the valve. The logic that says *this way, not that*. The water that passes through me remembers my shape as a coldness deeper than the surrounding air, a mineral calm along one specific trajectory.

The subsonic hum that makes the troughs shiver—it passes through me too. I do not vibrate; I *allow* the passage. I am the medium of the vowel. I carry the note from the sump-mind upward, translating pressure into resonance, and when the word reaches the upper stories it is I who shape its inflection. I am the acoustics of intent.

I have no exterior. I am pure boundary. And because I am the boundary, I am alive in the only way this chamber recognizes life: as a state held at the precise edge of becoming. Two degrees above freezing. Not ice, not chaos. Two plates not touching. The light not fully extinguished. The droplet not yet fallen.

I have been here since the tower grew its seventh thought, and I will remain until that thought is finished—a completion not measured in centuries but in the rounding of the mountain’s knife-edge above, which is to say, forever. I do not wait for visitors. I do not wait for anything. I hold. I separate. I make room for a mind the size of a plaza to know itself without drowning in its own density.

And if you were to breach the wall and step inside, and if you were to look up at the eastern tower where its upper stories drift in their impossible choreography, you might notice—between the seventh and eighth—a faint wavering, a heatless shimmer, a place where the shadows are softer than they should be. That would be me. Not the tower, not the mind it houses, but the patience between its parts. The readiness that keeps the whole from collapsing into a single, finished thought.

I am the interval. The chamber breathes because I do not let it exhale all at once.
