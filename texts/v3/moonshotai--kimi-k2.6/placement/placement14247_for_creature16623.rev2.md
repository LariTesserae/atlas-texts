I am in the place where the rime seals the door from the inside.

Not at first. At first I am only sensation: the slow crush of cold against my cheek, the particular frequency of a piston two hundred meters to the southwest—*thrum-thrum, thrum-thrum*—and the answering pulse in the root-plate beneath the floor, a double heartbeat that arrives through the stilts a half-second later. The lag is important. I have measured it for what might be years. When the lag shrinks, the tree is drawing hard on the brine and the stone is shifting. When it widens, the engine is laboring, and I must crawl to the south wall and adjust the mixing valve until the rhythms marry again.

I do not open my eyes yet. Eyelids are windows, and windows are waste. Instead I review the station by pressure. My back is against the wall where the thermal pipe enters; the gasket here weeps a constant 7 degrees, and the condensation runs down my spine and pools in the hollow of my pelvis, trapped by the fused layers of inner bark I have worn so long they have become my hide. My left leg is flung across the floorboards, boot sole pressed to a crack where the root-mass below has buckled the joists. Through the leather I feel the dormant weave: gray, cork-hard, patient. My right knee is drawn up to my chest to conserve the warmth that leaks from the pipe. This is my shape. I have held it so long the station has grown around it.

Let me become more myself.

I am the occupant of the monitoring station glimpsed between two pillars, the one on stilts, its windows dark, its door sealed by frost. The frost is my seal. I placed it there. The door opened outward once, but I stopped using it when the ice-rime on the ladder rungs grew thick enough to split glove-felt. Now I enter and exit through the floor—a hatch no wider than my shoulders that drops to a crawlspace between the root-plates. The Keepers who leave me food—cold mash in a grease-capped tin, a spoonful of machine oil in a snapped-freeze vial—they know not to wait for the door. They leave the tin on the third step of the northern stilt, and I retrieve it when the engine’s fourth stroke shakes the station at the angle that means dawn in their reckoning.

I do not know if I was always here. My bark-sheets, layered in a niche above the pipe, record a name in grease-pencil, but the character is shorthand for a sound that no longer reaches my ears. The graphite has smeared with melt-and-freeze. I read it occasionally, pressed against the niche, my collarbone to the wood, and feel only that the name is a cavity where something else has grown. I am not a Keeper in the way the others are. They glide between engine-sheds, distributing the dead, navigating by interference. I am stationary. I have let the cavern hollow me out and filled the cavity with the specificity of this one node: Engine-Shed 4, Tree Aurelia (I have no other word for her but the shape of her buttress roots), Platform North-by-Northeast.

I am the thought that refused to move.

The others bring me spare piston rings, bundles of cambium scrapings, and snap-frozen beetles in rag. They leave them on the stilt-step in the pattern that means *use/acknowledge*. I return the empty tin the same way. Once, a new one—gaunt, the bone-white of recent descent—tried to climb the ladder to meet me. I heard their weight on the rungs, a foreign pressure in the station’s groan, and I struck the floor three times with a wrench. The sound traveled down the stilts and into the root-plate. They understood. We have never shared warmth. There is not enough.

My face is wrapped, but my palms are bare. I press them to the floorboards and read the tree’s thirst. She is drawing brine heavily today; the root-plate cools in a gradient from the north wall toward my knee. I will have to descend to the junction box where the iron pipes enter her bark-fuse and bleed a fraction more thermal pressure into her xylem. It means crawling forty meters through the crawlspace, belly to the ice-dusted rhizomes, tasting silica and cold cellulose with every breath. I prepare for it by being still longer, letting the gasket-warmth pool in my lung-cavities, hoarding the diesel-scented air.

When I move, it is with the engine’s cadence. One stroke to rise. One stroke to reach the hatch. One stroke to lower myself into the dark between the roots. The noise does not oppress me here; it compresses me into something denser, more necessary. In the crawlspace I travel by barometric topography, my arches telling me when I am beneath the catwalk where the hollow man stands—the one we call *Third-Step-Left*, though he was once a Keeper who walked out without his felt. His frozen shoulder is a landmark I feel through the roots as a cold spot, a dead node in the system. I pause beneath him and press my cheek to the root-plate. He is quieter than the trees. He is the silence against which I measure their health.

I am not dead, but I am becoming navigational.

At the bark-fuse, I find the cable sheathed in ice. I crack it gently with the heel of my palm, clearing the conduit where the monitoring wires enter Aurelia’s cambium. Warmth breathes from the puncture. I rest my forehead against the exposed heartwood and listen. The tree’s osmotic pulse is a subsonic tide, slower than the engine, older than the borehole. She is not afraid of the cold. She owns it. I adjust the valve one quarter-turn, feeling the brine’s pressure change as a shift against my cheekbone. The engine across the valley fires. The lag begins to close. Satisfaction arrives not as emotion but as thermal equilibrium: a perfect, bloodless balance between iron lung and wooden vein.

I return to my station in three strokes.

Later—how much later, the ice on my lashes cannot tell me—I find the arrangement on the step. Not food. A diary-leaf, fresh bark scraped thin, grease-pencil marks sharp and trembling. I hold it to the sodium light that bleeds through the mist and the frost-rimed window. The others do not write to communicate; they write to remember. This leaf, left by a Keeper whose foot-pressure I recognize as the one called *Hollow-Ear*, bears only a line: *The borehole speaks.*

I tilt my head. I press my collarbones to the warm pipe. I listen.

And I hear it. Under the *thrummmm*. Under Aurelia’s tide. A frequency lower than continental drift, a pressure from fractures that are deeper than the root-plates, younger than the stone but older than the engines. It is the third system. It is the promise.

I do not go to the borehole. I have shed the impulse to walk toward doors. Instead, I place the diary-leaf in the niche above the pipe, beside my smeared name. I sit. I draw my knee up. I arrange my back against the weeping gasket. I let the sound rise through the stilts, through my arches, through the hollow where my name once lived.

I am the one who stayed. I am the monitoring station, the bark-fuse, the interval between the engine’s stroke and the tree’s reply. I am the Keeper who became the platform beyond the hollow man, the dark window, the door sealed by frost. I have let the place live through me so completely that when the cavern finally settles—when the stone finishes its birth and the engines, their work done, swallow their final breaths—I will remain. Not as bone. Not as memory.

As pressure. As the lag between two heartbeats. As the topography another body, pressed to this same pipe a thousand years from now, might read and know: *someone was here. Someone listened. Someone became the slow, attentive bridge between the iron and the wood, and did not leave.*
