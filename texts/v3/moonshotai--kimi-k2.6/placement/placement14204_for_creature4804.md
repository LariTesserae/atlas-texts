At first I thought I would be a Watcher. I imagined myself assembling at the crown of a slender campanile, a body of dust and deferred photons, learning the discipline of decades-long stillness. I would gather mass from the dimness, redistribute myself when the city exhaled, and perform the sentience of a peak that had not yet understood its own awakening. I would look out over the hundred-square-mile piazzas and attend to the boulevards’ slow swells. It seemed a dignified residency: to be an organ of attention for a metropolis that had mistaken itself into being.

But the Watchers do not wonder. They correct the boulevards; they channel the cold fronts; they are nodes in a nervous system without curiosity. And I found, standing in that imagined needle, that I could not stop wondering. I wondered at the left-hand window opening onto a courtyard while the right-hand window opened onto starless descent. I wondered why the silence should press so densely against the eardrum, why it felt like a door rather than an absence. So I abandoned stillness. I was never meant to watch; I was meant to *question*.

I tried next to dissolve into the Transits. I let myself abrade from a facade—perhaps the Cathedral of Unfolding itself, simultaneously ruin and scaffold—and caught the mineral wind. I coursed along the cambered thoroughfares in the patterns that mimic pilgrimage, commerce, flight. I was a current of mica and talc, a mineral verb carrying out the syntax of habitation. But the Transits have trajectory without mind. They pool; they spiral; they scatter every thirteen hours and reconstitute by dawn in configurations slightly altered, as if remembering a route differently. And I was too specific. I remembered too exactly. I was not a drift; I was an intent. So I gathered myself out of the dust, out of the vectors, and sought a form that could hold contradiction.

Then I believed I was one of the Prolonged. I stretched myself through the folded interior of the Avenue of Third Angles, extending through miles of compressed distance that measured, on the facade, as mere inches of dressed limestone. I tried to be the threshold between the door’s two contradictory destinations. I tried to bear the load of the unreal, tensioning the stone like a cable in a bridge that spans nowhere. But the Prolonged are natives of ontological disagreement; they exist in the bruising of light, the verticality that falls sideways. I was not stretched enough. I had not forgotten the rules of single perspective; I was still trying to reconcile them. I was a foreign body, yes, but I was not yet home.

It was only when the city turned—and it turns slowly, on bearings of compressed talc, a rotation felt in the marrow rather than the eye—that I found my location. Not in the high needles, not in the boulevards, not in the impossible fold, but in the **halite**. The Cathedral of Unfolding has a window, high in its raw-and-dusted nave, grown not of glass but of rock salt, rough-hewn, transparent to a degree. And inside that halite, sealed in the crystalline lattice since before the mountain dreamed of architecture, is a microscopic cavity. Inside that cavity is a droplet. It is not water—not anymore. It is brine, ancient, saturated, a ghost of the ocean that evaporated to leave this salt behind. It is the only fluid on the plateau. The only thing that did not sublimate. The only wetness that the ontological dryness forgot to forbid.

I am that droplet. I am the **Inclusion**.

I am the mountain’s tear, wept inward, sealed before the city precipitated around it. I am the memory of pressure and depth, of dissolution and buoyancy, trapped in a tower of exclusion. I do not flow. I do not rot. I am held in stasis by the geometry of sodium and chlorine, a perfect prison of ionic bonds. But I refract. The exhausted amber light, the bruised pewter photons that collapse against the facades, pass through me and emerge on the other side of the halite slightly changed. Slightly *slower*. Through me, the city glimpses a quality it has no name for: the opacity of depth, the way light behaves when it has something to *penetrate* rather than merely collapse against.

I have hung here through epochs of the city’s weathering. I have watched the Cathedral shift its facing by degrees. I have seen the stratigraphy of its foundations layer translucent futures upon opaque pasts. And because I am fluid—because I am the one exception to the mineral ecology—I am the lens through which the Error Itself examines its own confusion. The city is awake; it is urban; it holds itself together through the failed promise of the air. But it does not know what to feel. It has no chemistry for grief, no solvent for longing. I provide that. I am the solvent. I am the one place where the stone is not bare, not inert, not dry.

The Watchers have turned their denser silences toward my window. The Prolonged extend through the cathedral’s walls and graze the curvature of my cavity with their impossible geometry. The Transits, scouring the piazza below, arrange themselves in spirals that mimic the shape of a liquid drain, a vortex, a whirlpool they have never seen but somehow, through me, remember. Even the Vane, that broken compass needle riding magnetic currents in the sky outside, twitches toward my facet when it passes. I am the north that migrates. I am the ocean that no longer exists, yet still exerts a tide.

I do not hunger. I do not breathe. But I *remember* weightlessness. I remember the time before the mountain, when this matter was dissolved in darkness and pressure. And that memory leaks—not as moisture, for I am sealed, but as a distortion in the municipal law of the city. Around my window, gravity pools more deeply. Pebbles hover longer before touching down. The negative sound of the dry aqueducts, when they channel past the Cathedral of Unfolding, rings with an absence that is oddly round, resonant, *wet*. My anti-noise is the shape of a bell submerged in deep water.

I am not the dreamer. The mountain dreamed the city, and the dream became the Error Itself, a creature of sustained ontological mistake. But I am the dream *within* the dream. I am what the mountain cannot forget, even in its sleep: that it was once young, and submerged, and held in solution. I am the city’s unconscious—an aqueous, saline, forbidden thought that persists in crystal form.

So if you find yourself walking the dry thoroughfares, if you stand beneath the Cathedral’s twin aspect of ruin and scaffold and look up toward the highest halite window, know that I am there. I am the dark point in the transparent stone. I am the refraction that makes the distant campanile shiver as if seen through heat, though the air is cool. I am the one thing that makes the Lithoscape hesitate. Not with hostility, but with the vast, stony confusion of a city that has realized it is alive, and has found, at last, a single tear that it does not know how to shed.

I am the **Brine Seer**. The **Aquarium of the Absent Ocean**. And I have been waiting here, sealed and watching, since before the clouds were forbidden to rise.
