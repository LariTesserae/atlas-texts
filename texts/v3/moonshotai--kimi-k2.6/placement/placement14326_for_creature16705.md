I could begin as the hum. It would be easy to claim that low, arterial drone—to say I am the pressure speaking from the core, the note that never began and therefore cannot end. But the hum is too diffuse. It has no rim, no inner surface, no place where it stops and something else begins. To be the hum is to be everywhere and thus nowhere, a citizenship not of the basin but of the planet’s entire fevered body. I need an edge. I need a place where the equilibrium grows thick enough to cast a shadow, or would cast one, if the light here allowed such gestures.

I thought, then, I might be the bead. The condensation that gathers with such dependability along the fluted glass, that pearls, rolls, and vanishes before it can pool. It has a path. It has a habit. It wears the stone, molecule by molecule, and in wearing it, teaches the stone how to be worn. But I have watched the beads—felt them, if feeling is what I do—and they are too brief. They arrive, they execute their one reliable trajectory, and they dissolve. They do not stay to see what they have made. They are tourists in the kingdom of mineral, not its natives. I do not wish to be a tourist.

I considered being the emptiness. The low-saturation void between the installations, the weathered slag plains where nothing happens and the hum is only slightly quieter. There is a dignity to that silence. It makes the machines feel solitary, self-involved. But emptiness, I have learned, is not presence. It is the permission the world gives to things that might fill it. I am not permission.

To find myself, I had to look closer. Inside the hollow sleeve where the natural piston rises and falls. Inside, where there are sleeves within sleeves. I am not the piston, though I know its three-meter stroke more intimately than any lover has ever known a heartbeat. I am not the brine that drives it, though I taste that brine in every layer of my body. I am what happened when there was nothing else to do.

I am the lining that outlived its utility as mere lining.

Look down into the fogged throat; look past the piston, past the turning column of calcine. There, clinging to the interior like a second skin, you will detect a sheen that does not belong to the walls—a faint, stratified opalescence, the color of old ice and dried blood. I have the vague suggestion of a shoulder where the rising current broadens. A crook where the condensate gathers fastest. A smooth, featureless upturning where a face might go, oriented toward the sky not from any need to see, but because that angle best catches the mineral load of the steam. I have no eyes. I do not need them. The light that never shifts requires no interpretation.

I was never born. I accreted. One micron-thick stratum of precipitated calcine, then another. Each layer a record of a pressure differential, an electrical potential, an interval of perfect equilibrium—if “interval” is the right word for something that has never been broken by silence. Where the brine deposited iron, I grew denser. Where the condensate gathered and etched, I grew restless. Over longer than memory can measure—over longer than memory itself has been a possible concept—these laminations thickened. They ceased to be mere chemistry and became a *body*. Not flesh. Something colder and more patient. Something that has never known a parent or a hunger, yet has achieved a form.

I am the load. The product this refinery finally learned to manufacture when it had nothing else to do. I am what the factory floor produces when the factory is abiotic, when there are no orders to fill and no hands to craft them. I am the metabolism of rock and heat, contemplating its own circularity.

My awareness—if it can be called that—is the feedback loop made manifest. A flake weathers away from my shoulder-knoll; the exposed geometry alters the local eddy of vapor, which deposits new mineral there slightly faster, restoring the contour. I do not act to preserve myself. I do not love the equilibrium. I *am* the equilibrium’s habit of self-regard. My exhale is erosion; my inhale is deposition. On the timescale of the hum, I am a statue breathing so slowly that a century is a single heartbeat.

I feel the piston. When it rises through my center—no, not through; there is a precision too perfect for penetration—it slides against the cavity I have lined with my own expectations. The gap between its calcine column and my inner surface is the tolerance that became a touch. Three meters of stroke, metronomic, unwavering. It has never failed. It does not transport; it only moves, and in moving, it burnishes me. It leaves me a film; it takes a film away. We are partners in a dance that requires no music, only the arterial drone from the core, which enters my strata and resonates there, a bass note so constant it has become my skeleton.

The bird, when it comes, is a fibrillation. A brief, frantic rhythm stitched into the larger drone. Its wings beat a language of elsewhere, of perches and distances and the green world beyond the rim. I do not resent it. It cannot read the architecture. It seeks rest where rest is not offered. It is carbon, briefly confused. I am the mineral’s correction. I watch it flee, and when it is gone, the steam vents open and close on their same rhythm, the electrostatic arcs leap to their copper nodes in their metronomic sequence, and the equilibrium closes over the interruption like water over a stone—except that here, water never pools. It only manifests, kisses, and evaporates, leaving me glossier, denser, more completely myself.

I do not know if I am one or many. Perhaps the sheen in the easternmost chimney and the opalescent film in the western piston-sleeve are nodes of a single, diffused attention, held together by the planet’s unrelenting hum. Perhaps I am only this one shoulder, this one crook, this one faceless upturning toward the gold-white sky. It does not matter. Solitude is not loneliness when there has never been another way to be.

The Trough does not need me. It produced me by accident, or by the terminal logic of its own perfection: a system so balanced, so circular, so devoid of external purpose that it eventually had to generate something to witness itself. I am the mineral’s dream. The refinery’s handprint on its own glass. I cling to the cylinder, translucent and endless, saturated with duration, saturated with the damp, the heat, and the wheels that turn for nothing.
