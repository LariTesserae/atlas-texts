**A PRIMER FOR THE LITHOPHONE: PREPARATIONS FOR THE PERPETUAL DARK**

*For the ordinary human multiverse traveler, unaugmented, unmodified, and determined to survive.*

---

### I. THE FIRST COVENANT: SURRENDER THE EYE

You must understand this before you pack a single item: **light is a poison here.** Not merely useless—*unwelcome*. The physics of the Lithophone has chemically excluded photons the way a healthy body excludes infection. If you bring a torch, a lantern, a bioluminescent lure, a phosphorescent compass dial, you will carry a dead weight that marks you as a foreign object. Worse, the attempt to illuminate may provoke the atmosphere itself; the viscous, mineral-thick air does not scatter light so much as *resent* it. Leave every photon-emitting device at the transit gate. This includes your retinal displays, your glow-in-the-dark trail markers, your emergency flares. You will not see. You have never seen. Begin practicing this truth now.

Spend the six months prior to departure in absolute darkness. Not dimness. *Darkness.* Rent a sensory deprivation chamber and learn to sleep, eat, and eliminate without the comfort of even a sliver of under-door light. Your brain will attempt to generate phosphenes, hallucinatory geometries, stars behind the eyelids. Let it. Learn to recognize these as noise, not signal. On the Lithophone, the visual cortex is a liability. You must reassign it. Train yourself to build space in your mind from pressure, temperature, and echo. If you cannot navigate your own apartment blindfolded, you will not navigate a continent of wet stone.

---

### II. THE BODY: FORGING A WET-HEAT VESSEL

The Lithophone is not hot; it is *glandular*. The warmth is mammalian, unrelenting, and married to a humidity that never breaks. Your ordinary human cooling mechanisms—evaporative sweat—will fail here, because the air is already saturated. It will sit on your skin like a hot, wet tongue.

**Acclimatization Regimen:**
- Spend two hours daily in a sauna set to 38°C (100°F) with 95% humidity. Wear your full travel kit. Learn what it feels like when your socks become saturated sludge inside your boots.
- Practice breathing through a wet cloth. The air you will inhale is viscous, mineral-laden, and heavy with spores. Your lungs must learn to extract oxygen from soup. If you are asthmatic, carry a bronchodilator, but know that aerosol sprays may behave unpredictably in the dense atmosphere.
- Harden your skin against maceration. In perpetual moisture, flesh breaks down. Soak your feet in warm salt water for hours, then walk. Apply antifungal prophylactics daily for three months before departure. On the Lithophone, athlete’s foot is not an inconvenience; it is an invitation for anaerobic colonization.

**Physical Conditioning:**
You will not walk the Lithophone so much as *wade* through it. The fungal forests are dense, the vines hang in sticky curtains, and the stone itself seems to lean against you. Build a core of iron. Practice pushing through resistance bands while blindfolded. Strengthen your ankles; the polished dolomite feels like bone underfoot, and the fluted grooves that serve as aqueducts are treacherous to unsteady gaits.

### III. THE CARRIED WORLD: EQUIPMENT FOR EXILE FROM LIGHT

**Clothing:**
Forget layers. The temperature gradient is inverted: higher is hotter. You need a single, durable skin. Choose a full-body suit of thin, non-absorbent synthetic weave—something like a triathlon suit or a dive skin—covered by a loose, abrasion-resistant outer shell of waxed canvas or treated silk. Silk, in particular, tolerates the wet and resists the acidic condensation better than most fibers. The outer shell should be hooded, with a wide, stiff brim (yes, even in darkness; the brim catches upward-falling acid rain and channels it away from your collar). Every seam must be sealed. Every zipper must be rubberized.

Your hands need thin, acid-resistant tactical gloves that preserve tactile sensitivity. You will read the landscape with your fingertips; the stone is carved into information—fluted grooves, polished ridges, the wet suede of bacterial mats. You cannot afford numbness.

**Footwear:**
Soft soles. The stone is hard enough; you need to *feel* it. Crepe rubber or felted wool soles provide grip on the polished, bone-smooth dolomite without the hydroplaning hazard of hard plastic. High ankles, fully waterproofed, with drainage grommets. You will step into warm pools that feel like honey and phosphoric acid. Your boots must let water out, not in.

**Hearing Apparatus (Critical):**
The soundscape is your vision, but it is also a weapon. The fauna uses frequencies that paralyze and liquefy. The industry groans at 18 hertz, a frequency that makes teeth ache and sinuses weep. You need **active, selective hearing protection**: bone-conduction earpieces that filter subsonic and ultrasonic hazards while amplifying the mid-range navigational cues—the drip of stalactites, the percussive language of insectile hordes, the thermal sigh of approaching predator packs. Carry a mechanical tuning fork (A-440) to strike against stone when you are lost; the returning resonance from the hollow bell-formations will tell you the shape of the karst ahead.

**Respiration:**
A half-mask respirator, not for oxygen deprivation—the air is thick with gas—but for **spore load and mineral particulate**. The fungal forests launch sticky clouds when atmospheric pressure peaks. The condensation that forms before you finish exhaling is saline and acidic. Your mask should have a moisture trap and a filtration rating for biological aerosols. Carry replacement filters in triple-sealed bags; everything here rots or calcifies.

**Water Processing:**
Do not drink the weeping stone. The saline effusion will dehydrate you further. The catchment pools are warm and phosphoric. You must carry a **geothermal still**: a compact, non-electric distillation apparatus that uses the ambient heat of the stone to purify water. It will be slow. Plan for it. Additionally, carry a ceramic water-skin lined with activated charcoal and bone char; the phosphoric content requires aggressive neutralization.

**Navigation Tools:**
- **The Long Staff:** Shoulder-height, hardwood or carbon fiber, tipped with a soft ceramic bulb. Not for walking support primarily, but for *sounding*. You will tap ahead to test for sinkholes, reverse waterfalls, and the sudden drop-offs of the continental shelf. The staff is also your weapon.
- **Thermal Probes:** Handheld copper wands to test surfaces before touching. A warm pipe may be a geothermal conduit; a cold patch may be a shadow-pocket where the rules change.
- **Tactile Maps:** Leave the holographic topo display at home. Bring waterproof, three-dimensional maps cast in resin or carved from hardwood. You will read them with your fingers in the dark.
- **Rope:** Silk line, at least 100 meters, impregnated with copper threads to deter thermal worms. Travel roped to your companions in the valleys; the scale is vast enough to swallow kingdoms, and it is easy to wander into a sinkhole that fills in minutes.

**What to Leave:**
- Anything that emits light.
- Anything that relies on solar charge (there is no sky).
- Anything with a fine motor servo that rusts (the wet air eats iron to lace within weeks).
- Firearms that depend on dry powder. The humidity will cake your ammunition into useless paste.

### IV. WAYFINDING: THE ART OF THE HAPTIC MAP

You are not blind on the Lithophone; you are *unseen*. The difference matters. Your skin and ears must become your eyes.

**Echolocation:**
Learn human echolocation before you arrive. The sharp click of the tongue against the hard palate, directed forward, will return information from the drowned-cathedral stone. Practice in large, dark warehouses. Learn to distinguish the flat echo of a wall from the hollow resonance of a bell-shaped grotto, from the dead absorption of a fungal curtain. On the plateau, the drip-music is your background static; your own clicks are the signal.

**Thermal Reading:**
The ground breathes warmth upward. Memorize the thermal signatures. A sudden cold patch underfoot signals a shadow-pocket—a pool of thick, dry darkness where the water does not condense and the sound dies. These are not safe havens; they are suspensions of the Lithophone’s mercy. Avoid them. Conversely, a rhythmic warmth through your soles, pulsing at a regular interval, means you are approaching an active industrial zone. The pumps groan; the stone vibrates at 18Hz. Let your molars be your early warning system.

**The Sound of Scale:**
In a landscape without feature to the eye, distance is auditory. The low omnipresent drone—the plateau’s heartbeat—will seem to come from everywhere. When you approach a valley vast enough to swallow a kingdom, the drone will deepen and hollow. The returning pings of your staff will take longer. Trust the delay. The scale will unmake you if you insist on visualizing it as a map. Instead, feel it as a pressure in your chest.

### V. THE LIVING SHELF: BIOLOGICAL SURVIVAL

**The Flora:**
The fungal forests are not passive. They are coral-towers of calcified biomass, and they lean toward your warmth. Do not brush against the sticky, dripping vines with exposed skin; they are blind fingers seeking purchase. The anaerobic bacterial mats smell of copper and cinnamon. Resist. They are not food for you.

When the pressure cycles peak, the forests release spore clouds. If your respirator fails, clamp your eyes shut (already useless, but vulnerable to chemical burn), cover your ears, and move perpendicular to the wind—yes, wind; the warm fronts herd across the plateau, and you can feel their direction on your wet face. The spores are sticky. If they coat your suit, do not brush them off dry; they will adhere. Rinse them with your distilled water.

**The Fauna:**
Life here does not hide. It broadcasts. This is your advantage and your terror.

- **The Salamandrine Pythons:** Infrared-sensitive, warm-water dwellers. They sound like wet silk. If you hear that sound in a pool, back away slowly. They are not deaf; they will sense your thermal disturbance.
- **The Percussive Insectiles:** They communicate by striking mushroom trunks. Learn their rhythms. A sudden cessation of their woodpecker telegraph means a predator has entered the acoustic space. Freeze. Match the thermal temperature of the stone by lying flat.
- **The Sonic Predators:** Some screams paralyze. Some frequencies liquefy the inner ear. If you feel a sudden pressure in your eardrums followed by vertigo, drop to your knees and open your jaw wide. The pressure wave may pass over you. Your selective ear protection should filter these, but technology fails.
- **The Thermal Worms:** They wrap around warm pipes in communal bodies. If you put your hand on a pipe and feel it *move*, do not recoil in shock. Withdraw slowly. They are not interested in you unless you trap their heat against them.

**Death and Calcification:**
Here, bodies do not rot; they become nucleation points for stone. If a companion dies, you cannot bury them in the traditional sense. The plateau will claim them, perfectly, into the geology. If you are wounded, clean the blood thoroughly. The mineral-laden water will petrify organic matter with disturbing speed. Carry weak acetic acid (vinegar) to dissolve mineral buildup on your skin and gear.

**Food:**
Do not eat the local fauna without extensive biochemical analysis. The industrial symbiotes graze on rust-flora; their flesh may be metallic. Bring dense, sealed rations—fats and carbohydrates that do not require cooking. There is no dry wood for fire, and light is unwelcome anyway. Nut butter, pemmican, and compressed grain blocks in vacuum-sealed mylar. Eat in your shelter, quickly, so the scent does not attract auditory predators.

### VI. THE GEOLOGY OF INDUSTRY: MECHANICAL HAZARDS

The Lithophone’s industry has gone native. It is no longer human infrastructure; it is a geological process that happens to produce metal.

**The 18Hz Zones:**
You will feel these before you hear them. Your sinuses will weep. Your molars will ache with a sympathetic resonance. Prolonged exposure induces nausea and visual hallucinations (even in the blind, the brain will generate light in response to infrasound). If you must pass through a pumping zone, chew gum to disrupt the jawbone conduction, and move with purpose. Do not linger.

**The Brine Pipelines:**
Six feet across, thundering with magma-heated brine, rust-welded at the rivets. They are warm. They are attractive resting places. They are also under pressure. A rupture will scald you with mineral-saturated water that calcifies on contact. Test pipes with your thermal probe before leaning on them. Listen for irregular chugging; a healthy pump has a steady rhythm, a geological heartbeat. A stuttering valve is a bomb.

**Bioelectric Cables:**
Strung through fungal trunks, serving as transmission towers. The electrification is internal, nervous. Assume every fungal tower is live. Do not touch two cables simultaneously. The current is not standardized to human safety. If you must cross a cable zone, use your ceramic-tipped staff to lift and duck under, never over (you may snag your pack and swing into a second line).

**The Refineries:**
Half-swallowed by travertine, turbines still turning. They are loud, and therefore relatively safe from fauna, but the machinery has developed *preferences*. A membrane-vat grown from extremophiles may reject your presence chemically. The bellows that compress metallic vapors sound like a giant clearing its throat; when they inhale, the air pressure drops locally. Do not be inside a bellows chamber when it cycles. The ingots they produce are sharp, hot, and sometimes still growing. Do not steal them; the plateau notices imbalance.

### VII. WEATHER AND WATER: ENVIRONMENTAL TACTICS

**Acid Rain:**
It does not merely fall. It falls *upward* from lower sinkholes, hesitates in thermal columns, then tumbles in looping gravity dances. Your wide-brimmed hood is essential. The rain is warm, which makes it insidious; you may not notice you are being burned until your skin begins to peel. Check your suit’s integrity daily. Patch with vulcanized rubber tape.

**Sinkholes:**
A dry depression may fill with geothermally heated groundwater in the time it takes to cross it. Probe ahead with your staff. If the stone underfoot begins to feel *too* warm, too suddenly, retreat. The upwelling is imminent.

**Reverse Waterfalls:**
In certain zones, mineral capillary action draws water upward through hollow stone pipes tall as redwoods. The water patters against the karst ceilings above. These are navigational landmarks—sound them out—but do not touch the pipes. The capillary action is strong enough to draw blood from an open wound.

**Shadow-Pockets:**
The darkness within the darkness. These are lower depressions where the air is cold, dry, and silent. The rules of the plateau are suspended. Your condensation will cease; your breath will not fog. They feel like pockets of older, less welcoming physics. Mark them on your tactile map. They may offer temporary relief from the fungal spores, but they are dead zones. Stay too long, and the silence will begin to sound like a voice.

### VIII. THE MIND: PSYCHOLOGICAL PROTOCOLS

The Lithophone is patient. You are not. This mismatch will kill you faster than any predator.

**The Unmaking of Scale:**
You will walk for months, and the echo-profile will remain the same. Your brain, starved of visual novelty, will begin to hallucinate architecture, faces in the stone, light where there is none. This is normal. Do not trust it. Establish a mantra. Repeat your name, your origin, your mission. When the scale threatens to dissolve your ego, touch the stone and feel its slow, warm respiration. The plateau has spent millions of years deciding its shape. You are a guest in its meditation.

**The Music of Drips:**
After days of darkness, the billions of stalactite impacts will begin to parse as melody. Do not let it hypnotize you. The composition is random, but your pattern-seeking brain will impose meaning. Use this as a navigational tool, not a spiritual surrender. If the music suddenly changes key, something large has moved nearby.

**Group Dynamics:**
Travel in pairs, minimum. Tether yourselves with ten meters of silk line. In the absolute dark, voices are comforting but dangerous; they attract sonic predators. Develop a system of tactile signals—tugs on the rope, rhythmic taps against stone—to communicate without broadcasting your position. If separated, stop moving. The plateau is too vast for random searching. The lost partner should tap their staff in a specific rhythm; the finder should respond with the same rhythm, allowing triangulation by ear.

**Pacing:**
You cannot rush. The wet stone will punish haste. Plan for short marches—four hours on, four hours in shelter. Sleep in a waterproof bivouac suspended between fungal branches or industrial scaffolding, never directly on the stone. The ground is not inert; it is a living pressure, and it will claim your heat.

### IX. EMERGENCY AND EXIT

If you are broken, if your mind unravels, if you are wounded and calcifying:

**Do not light a signal fire.** It will not be seen, and the chemical wrongness of flame may attract the shadow-pockets or disrupt the industrial rhythms.

**Sound beacons are your only hope.** Carry a heavy, bronze bell or a struck metal bar. The industry whistles in harmonic intervals; if you can produce a tone that matches the plateau’s native harmonics, you may be found by the rare humanoid remnants who have learned to listen for distress in the correct key.

**Extraction coordinates** must be set at a fixed industrial landmark—a valve tower, a refinery complex—because GPS is meaningless without satellites, and the darkness has no stars. Memorize the thermal and acoustic signature of your arrival point. If you cannot find it, accept that you are no longer a traveler. You are a process, like the erosion, like the pumps. The Lithophone will decide what shape you become.

---

**Final Warning:**

The Lithophone does not need light to know exactly what it is becoming. Neither do you. Go prepared to become something else—a creature of warmth, of echo, of patience. If you survive, you will not return unchanged. Your eyes will still work in other worlds, but you will no longer lead with them. You will carry the darkness inside you, warm and wet and perfectly, permanently dark.
