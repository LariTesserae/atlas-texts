**Preparation Briefing: The Obsidian Phonation Basin**

If you are an ordinary human, do not approach this place as a traveler. Approach it as a fugitive—someone who must pass through a lung that is exhaling and has never stopped. The Basin does not reward curiosity; it processes organic matter. Your goal is to be passed over. To do that, you must be reduced. Less a visitor than a temporary absence in the biomass.

Here is how you prepare.

---

### **1. Sensory Surrender: Abandon Sight and Armor Your Other Hulls**

Before departure, spend thirty days in absolute darkness. Not dimness—absolute. Teach your brain that the optic nerve is a vestigial luxury. In the Basin, light is a rumor, and bringing lanterns or NVGs will only vandalize the one true navigation cue available: the faint phosphorescent blush on the undersides of leaves. Worse, any light-source will advertise your thermal anomaly to the thickets and make you a landmark in a place where landmarks are eaten.

**Hearing must be intercepted, not blocked.** The sound here is not ambient; it is architectural and hydraulic. A hundred million abdomens saw at the air; cane-plants boom like war-drums; subsonic thumps from unseen shell-backs travel up through your boots and settle in your sternum. Standard ear protection will kill you. You need an **acoustic impedance helmet**: active cancellation tuned precisely to the resonant frequencies of chitinous stridulation and cane-drumming, but with passive conduits that translate the lowest seismic pulses into gentle haptic taps at your collarbone and hips. You need to *feel* where the heavy things walk, but you cannot let the noise occupy your skull. The Basin deafens by heft; it sits on the mind until thought narrows to a single, desperate instinct. Counter this before arrival by training in sensory-overload tanks—learn to maintain a cognitive loop (a single repeating module: *gasket, step, breathe*) while white-noise is physically pressed against your body at 140 dB.

---

### **2. The Carapace: Becoming Synthetic, Smooth, and Cold**

You cannot wear fabric. You cannot wear leather. The biomass here is so dense and immediate that to stand still is to feel a thousand legs traversing your boot as a thoroughfare. Any organic weave will be colonized, chewed, or dissolved before you take your third step.

Your suit must be a **hermetic, liquid-cooled exoshell** of woven ceramic polymer, positive-pressured and jointed like arthropod armor—but polished smooth. No creases for eggs. No buckles for beetles to grip. The exterior should be mildly acid-resistant and treated with a fluoride-based slick that discourages the acid-secreting lithotrophs that etch the rock itself.

**Thermal regulation is stealth.** The black, bat-wing creepers do not photosynthesize; they chemo-osmose along thermal gradients. Your body heat is a bellows calling them toward you in real time. You must run an active refrigerant loop, dumping metabolic heat into a chemical sink or a phase-change pack. If your suit vents warmth, the vines will creak and swivel, hunting you with cellular hunger.

**Boots are survival itself.** The basalt and obsidian are infantile, eager, and razor-keen. Every surface is a glass blade. Leather soles will be shredded; soft polymer will split. You need **rigid, shin-integrated hoof-boots** of diamond-weave composite, micro-gyroscopically stabilized, with wide, flat distribution pads to spread your weight across fracture edges rather than letting them bite. They must be tall, sealed at the calf, because the warm mud teems with six-limbed amphibians and the clicking grain-beetles pour in sheets.

---

### **3. Navigation: The Tether Protocol**

Maps are fiction here. The valley is minutes old, geologically speaking, and its biology re-sculpts it faster than any cartographer could track. Vents slam shut and reroute; cane thickets resonate with your footsteps; vines stack cellulose upon themselves in audible, creaking time. 

**Do not navigate by sound.** The text of the place will lie to you. Sound is the terrain, and to navigate by it is to walk on water.

Nor can you trust your eyes. Instead, preload the basin’s static geometry—its hundred-meter depth, its rim coordinates—into an **inertial guidance rig** strapped to your chest. Dead reckoning is your only honesty. 

More importantly: you must not enter without a **physical tether** anchored to the rim in the older plains-rock above. Synthetic monofilament, not rope. The Basin eats organics, and RF signals may not penetrate the wet, sulfurous air and dense biomass. Your lifeline is not a beacon. It is a filament of denial that says: *this far, and no farther into becoming part of the valley.*

Move with one hand trailing the link. Let it be the only thing that knows where you are.

---

### **4. Movement Doctrine: The Narrowing**

The darkness is not empty; it is crowded. The sharpness is not dormant; it is eager. The fauna does not die; it is consumed before death.

Train yourself to think under the pressure of biological siege. The sound will try to compress your consciousness to a single instinct: *move carefully.* Let it. Download that instinct ahead of time. When the moss-quail burst in shrieking clouds and the myriapods vomit from the vents, your mantra is your only architecture. Do not marvel. Do not reach out. The naturally flaked obsidian hand-axes and vine-ropes that mimic human tools are not wonders; they are landmines in an evolutionary workshop. They are the *potential* of technology waiting for a tool-user. If you pick one up, if you contribute the first gesture of sapient craft, you may complete a circuit that the valley has been building for centuries. Leave them. You are not here to teach.

**Never stand still.** The ground breathes. If you must pause—and you should not—find a narrow spine of fresh stone, balance on your boot-edges, and keep your weight shifting. The tickling of legs on your ankles means your seals are already being surveyed. Move before they find ingress.

**Do not interact with the fauna.** A bleeding cut will summon a wave of consumption before you feel the pain. There are so many that individual creatures dissolve into a continuous, writhing medium. You are not a predator, not prey; you are an obstruction they will flow over. Be slippery. Be cold. Be inedible.

---

### **5. Atmospheric Survival: Breathing the Lung**

The air itself sweats. It is saturated, sulfurous, and laced with the fungal scent of rotting vegetative matter. It does not rain; condensation gathers on every glassy facet and drips mineral-slick tears. 

You will breathe through **closed-loop scrubbers** or heavy-duty rebreathers with lime canisters and sulfur filters. Do not open your visor. The steam from fumaroles is warm enough to scald lung tissue, and the humidity is too total to allow evaporative cooling. Without your suit’s active chilling, you will cook in your own sweat in twenty minutes.

Hydration must be internal and sealed. Do not be tempted by the warm droplets beading on the shelf-ferns. They carry the acids of fresh stone and the biology of rapid rot.

---

### **6. Egress and Decontamination**

Set a **hard temporal limit**. The Basin is not stable; it is turbulent in its moment-to-moment existence. The longer you stay, the more the sound occupies your skull, the more the vines thicken, the more the lithotrophs etch your armor. One hour is an eternity here.

Extraction should be by winch from the rim. Do not attempt to climb the razor walls unassisted; the obsidian is eager to split your gloves. Ascend by your tether, and ascend cleanly—shaking the mass of translucent beetles and clinging spores from your shell like shedding a second skin.

The decontamination protocol is not aftercare. It is survival of the return. You will emerge coated in biotic film: eggs in your boot-treads, fungal filaments in your gasket folds, the mineral stink of the Basin’s breath in your filters. You must submit to chemical dunk, ultrasonic scrub, and UV inspection. The sound does not stop when you leave. You will carry the subsonic thump in your sternum for weeks; tinnitus will become your native tongue. Prepare for auditory hallucinations—the roar with architecture does not wish to vacate your skull.

---

### **Final Instruction**

You are entering a place where geology is in its infancy and biology has grown so impatient that it has begun to invent the grammar of tools without a mind to speak it. The Basin is a workshop floor scattered with hammers and blades, waiting for a voice that has never arrived. 

Your preparation must ensure that voice is not yours. Go smooth. Go cold. Go quiet. Go blind. Let the darkness press against you and find nothing to grip.

If the valley forgets you were there, you have survived it.
