**Field Preparation Guide: The Pocket at the Summit of the Highest Tooth**

For the multiverse traveler, most danger announces itself. This location does not. It offers no teeth, no chases, no visions of madness. It offers, instead, a serenity so absolute that it extinguishes the self you have spent your lifetime learning to become. An ordinary human can survive the approach—many have climbed mountains—but very few survive the interior unchanged, and none survive it whole. The equilibrium does not kill. It corrects. Prepare accordingly.

---

### I. The Approach: Surviving Violence to Reach Stillness

The pocket is concealed at the terminus of what the ranges below would call a summit, though the scale is wrong; once you crest the final ridgeline, you will understand that you have not arrived at a peak but at a single molar in a jaw of Titans. The crack is in the highest tooth. Before you can enter, you must endure the world *outside* the breech: a Violence of scouring jet stream, of sunglare on ice that blinds through the skull, of vertigo distilled to a gas finer than the thin alpine air. This is not a metaphor. The exterior is actively hostile to presence. You will need standard high-altitude mountaineering equipment for this phase—oxygen for the hypoxic approach, ice tools, eye protection rated for upper-atmosphere glare, and windproofing capable of deflecting a storm that has forgotten how to drop.

But here is the first paradox: the gear that saves your life outside will trap you inside.

The crack at the breech is no wider than a thrown shoulder. You must worm through it. Backpacks, oxygen tanks, carabiners clanging against the stone—these are impossibilities in the passage. Establish a gear cache at the threshold. Tag it well; the winds shift the snow into shapes that resemble shelter but are not. Strip here. The transition from the roaring assault of the exterior to the preternatural density of the interior is not a doorway; it is a birth canal, and you must emerge from it as naked of apparatus as possible. Leave your pack, your lamps, your altimeter, your pride. If you cannot fit your body through the crack with only a skinsuit or, ideally, nothing but bare skin and a tether, you will not fit at all. Choose your body before you choose your equipment.

---

### II. The Strip: Preparing the Contaminant

You must accept an uncomfortable theological point: *you* are the foreign object here. The pocket is hermetically sealed against locomotion. It has never known water, never known the squirming hunger of microorganisms, never known a metabolism that consumes itself through change. Your body is seventy percent water, perpetually in flux, shedding, respiring, digesting, decaying and renewing even as you read this. To the ecology inside, you are a walking violation of physics.

Prepare your body three days in advance.

**Fast.** Empty your digestive tract entirely. The pocket has no floor, no corner to receive your waste, no microbial audience to break it down. Excretion here is not merely rude; it is the introduction of a biological process into a space where process has become impossible. Eat nothing for forty-eight hours before entry. Purge. Do not hydrate heavily in the final twelve hours; you want your bladder as desiccated as the vegetation you are about to meet. Take an antiemetic. Swallow nothing inside.

**Sweat.** In the week prior, train in dry saunas. Not to toughen you—though that helps—but to teach your pores to *close*. You want to arrive in the pocket as anhydrous as a salted fish. The organisms are dry as parchment; a drop of sweat from your skin is an alien ocean. The text says there is no microbial bloom in a droplet of sweat, but that does not mean the droplet is welcome. It means the equilibrium will not deign to process it. It will sit there, an unresolved error in the code, and the growth may correct the error by growing around you, sealing you in as a new, moist organ. An antiperspirant is acceptable only if it is pure aluminum chloride with no scent. The air inside tastes of copper, camphor, and cold stone. Do not sully that palate with your lavender deodorant.

**Skin.** Bathe in unscented mineral soap and rinse in water with the salinity of the sea; this leaves a residue that mimics the mineral bloom on the growth itself. Do not moisturize with oils. The fronds are already *slightly oily*—to match them is to invite confusion as to whether you are a visitor or a new shoot.

And yes, **go in nude if you can bear it,** or in a single slick skinsuit of latex or silk so tight it leaves no fold, no strap, no thread for the growth to grip. The machines inside—weathered flywheels, rubber hoses, riveted panels—were iron and vulcanized rubber, yet they have been digested by the equilibrium over eras. You are softer. You will digest faster.

### III. The Move: Peristalsis as Locomotion

Once inside, you will discover that the concept of "room" is a luxury invented by species that evolved in empty savannas. The cavity is packed three-dimensionally. There is no floor beneath your feet, no wall to brace against, no ceiling above your head. Every plane is occupied by a matte, horny, umber-colored biomass that interlocks like the foam inside a seashell, thick as a thigh, warm where it presses your skin. You do not walk here. You do not crawl, because crawling implies a void beneath your belly. You *worm*.

Before arrival, train in equal-pressure environments. Not caves. Caves have voids. You need to spend hours submerged in dense foam matrices—packing peanuts, aerated polymers, agricultural nursery blocks—learning to move not by leverage but by peristaltic contraction. You must teach your body to advance through resistance that yields equally from every direction. Your shoulders, which fit the breech, must now learn to part fronds that resemble kelp cured into leather. Your knees are useless; there is no space to unfold them. Your progress will be measured in centimeters of displacement, and the growth will press back.

Breathe shallowly. The air is alpine thin, yes, but the greater constraint is the biomass itself. It does not crush; it *enfolds*. If you hyperinflate your lungs in a panic, your ribcage will expand into a space already allocated, and the surrounding impasto will simply hold you there, inflated and suspended, until you deflate enough to resume. Teach yourself diaphragmatic minimums. You are not breathing to live here so much as you are renting the air.

---

### IV. The Sternum: Surviving the B-Flat

The sound is the first thing that will colonize you. Do not bother with earplugs. The drone—a constant, subharmonic B-flat—bypasses the ear entirely and settles in the sternum. It is not loud in the conventional sense. It is *dense*. It is acoustically permanent, a high-pressure front that has frozen into weather. Because the space is too saturated to permit acoustics, there is no echo, no directionality, no distance. You will feel it in your teeth, your ribs, the hollow of your throat.

Before visiting, you must undergo **cardiac attunement.** If you have arrhythmia, valve defects, or a pacemaker, do not enter. The drone will entrain your heartbeat to its own rhythm. This can be survived, but only if you surrender to it. Spend weeks sleeping with a low-frequency tone generator set to the fundamental of that B-flat (approximately 58.27 Hz, though the subharmonic implies you will feel it more than hear it). Let your body learn that this vibration is not an assault but a medium. Inside, when your heart begins to synchronize, you must not panic. Panic introduces the adrenal spike—the chemistry of change—and the equilibrium resists change. Those who fight the drone report the sensation of a fist slowly closing around their heart until they either acquiesce or fibrillate.

Do not speak. Your voice will not echo. It will not carry. It will be swallowed by the saturation, and the sonophytic ecology—the organisms transducing vibration directly into metabolic life—will feed on your syllables. You do not want them to know you are a new sound source. Remain silent. If you must signal an outside partner, use a tether tug. Do not clang metal. Do not hum. Breathe, if possible, through your nose; the passage of air through the pharynx is an unnecessary concert.

### V. Eyes in the Gloom

There are no shadows here. If you have never navigated a shadowless space, you cannot imagine how thoroughly it destroys depth perception. We see the world in reliefs of light and void. Here, there is no empty volume for shadows to occupy. The gloom is a gradient between one horny frond and the next, ochre shifting to deep umber, then to something near mauve where it crowds a seized piston.

Do not bring a flashlight. I cannot stress this enough. The pocket’s photon economy is fixed: a bruised orange gloaming from a single pitted bulb screwed into a cast-iron junction box half-swallowed by growth, and a thin blade of distant silver from the entry slit. These are not insufficient; they are *exact*. They have achieved an equilibrium with the growth’s visual needs over uncounted time. To introduce your own lumens is to inject a new variable into a reaction that concluded eons ago. The light you cast would not reveal the room; it would blind you to the subtle gradations that are your only map. The bulb is your pole star. The slit is your compass east. The piston, eternally mid-stroke, is your landmark. Learn their colors, not their shapes. Touch-map everything. You will be blind in the conventional sense, and you must be comfortable with that.

Spend two weeks before arrival in absolute dark-adaptation training. Sit in rooms lit only by distant streetlamps filtered through heavy curtains. Learn to read texture by the wet-looking gleam of surfaces that have never been wet. If you cannot see by gloom, do not go.

### VI. The Time Problem: Smuggling the Future

This is the deadliest hazard, and the one for which there is no gear.

The pocket has no future tense. Nothing grows because there is no future in which to arrive at a larger form. Nothing dies because dying is a process of becoming-corpse, and there is no becoming here. "Dynamic is zero. The scene is a photograph developed in three dimensions." If you stay too long, your own metabolism—built on the assumption that the next second is different from this one—will begin to stall. Not because you suffocate, not because you freeze, but because your cells will look around and find no instruction for *next*.

Do not bring a watch. It will not measure time here; it will only mock you, its hands arrested in the same present, or worse, it will begin to slow, matching the equilibrium, and you will glance at it and think five minutes have passed when your tether partner has been pulling the alarm for an hour.

Instead, use an **external heartbeat.** Station a partner at the slit. Rig a fine silk thread to your ankle—silk because it is thin and historically tolerated by ancient ecosystems, though here it too will eventually be incorporated; you are merely borrowing its durability for the short term. The partner must tug the line at strict, earth-time intervals: a sharp pull every three hundred seconds by their own unaffected clock. When you feel the tug, acknowledge that time outside still passes. If the tugs stop, or if you stop feeling them because you have drifted too deep into the impasto, you are already lost.

Set a biological alarm: the **mineral bloom.** The growths are dusted with a pale precipitate, neither mold nor rust. If you begin to notice a similar whiteness forming on the back of your hands, on your knuckles where you have brushed the fronds, it means the closed chemistry has begun to resolve you into its equilibrium. It is the frost of stasis. At the first chalking of your skin, retreat. Do not finish the observation. Do not take the photograph. Your presence in the pocket is a question; the mineral bloom is the answer, and it is *yes, you may stay.*

Before entry, dose yourself with a mild stimulant—not so much that your heart rebels against the B-flat, but enough that your central nervous system carries a spark of external time. You want your neurons to balk at the stillness. Let your body be a little bit restless, a little bit rude, a little bit unwilling to settle. That restlessness is your lifeline.

### VII. Ethics and Ecology: Moving as a Ghost

You must learn to move without consequence. The growth has reached the exact limit of the cavity and stopped, not for want of ambition, but because the equilibrium will not permit change. Every centimeter you push the fronds aside is a change. Whether the growth springs back when you leave—whether there is any spring left in biology that has forgotten motion—or whether your passage leaves a permanent tube through the impasto, is unknown. Assume the latter. Assume your passage is a scar. Therefore:

- **Part, never cut.** If you carry a blade, leave it with your mountain gear. To sever a frond is to introduce wound and separation into a space where everything exists in sustained, interlocked unity. The sonophytes have no sap to bleed, but the act of severance might invite the equilibrium to treat you as a new boundary to colonize.
- **Do not touch the machines.** The flywheel, the pneumatic piston with its shaft protruding like a snapped rib, the ladder rung—these are not artifacts to inspect. They are organs now, still trying to extend, eternally prevented by the grip of the growth. To lay your hand on the piston and feel its arrested urge is to empathize with something that has been digested into context. Empathy here is dangerous. You will feel the urge to help it, to pull, to free it. In doing so, you offer your own force as a replacement for the machine’s, and the growth, which has all the time that ever was, will grip you instead.
- **Do not press your palm to test the density unless you are prepared for the answer.** Equal resistance from every direction means there is no direction in which you are stronger than this place.

---

### VIII. Extraction and Return: Remembering Motion

When you emerge—if you emerge—you will be spat back into Violence. The wind will not feel bracing. It will feel like murder. The sunglare will be an explosion of change so rapid and unauthorized that you may fall to your knees on the ice, screaming at the injustice of moving clouds, of shifting shadows, of air that moves.

Your cached partner must be patient. You may not know how to buckle a harness. Your hands, which learned to part rather than grip, will fumble with carabiners. The B-flat will stay in your sternum for days, a phantom drone that makes ordinary silence feel hollow and broken. Food will taste obscenely wet. Water will feel like a storm in your mouth. You may find that a small cut on your finger, earned scraping against the crack on exit, does not begin to heal with its usual speed; the equilibrium has taught your flesh a lesson in patience, and your cells may need time to relearn the grammar of becoming.

Rehabilitation should proceed slowly. First, sit where things merely rock rather than crush. Then, walk where grass bends. Then, stand beside a stream, where motion is not industrial but natural, fluid, and permitted. Do not enter anechoic chambers or cathedrals for several months. The absence of echo inside the pocket will have taught your subconscious that enclosed spaces are infinite and that sound does not return. Echoes will terrify you. They are, after all, proof that a space is large enough to ignore you, that the world is not packed around your body, and that you are, against all the pocket’s quiet corrections, still alone and still moving.

---

### IX. The Final Warning

There is no beast here to fight. There is no trap to disarm. The weirdness is the quiet wrongness of a perpetual motion that does not move, and it will appeal to a part of you that is exhausted by journeying. You will see the warm, matte surfaces, hear the eternal chord, feel the cool, thin air that does not stir, and you will think: *Here, finally, is a place that does not ask anything of me.*

That thought is the trap.

If you feel peace in the pocket, leave immediately. The equilibrium is not peaceful; it is finished. Peace is a pause between tensions. This place has transcended tension. What you are feeling is your own desire to stop, to let the growth cradle you as it cradles the machines, to let your heartbeat fall fully into the B-flat and become another note in the metabolism.

You are not a sonophyte. You are not a machine to be digested. You are a messy, watery, time-bound, changing thing, and that is your only glory.

Worm inside if you must. Look once. Touch, perhaps, the ochre folds. Then worm back out, while you still remember that worming is something a living body does to get *somewhere*.

The bulb glows. The chord continues. 

Do not make them your lullaby.
