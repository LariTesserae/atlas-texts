**Orientation: This Is a Nursery, Not a Ruin**

Do not pack for a planet. Pack for an *ovology*—a world still sketching its own anatomy. The first thing you must burn into your mind is that the architecture you will see was never built, the silence was never chosen, and the darkness is not punitive; it is merely the color of a sky that has not yet earned its stars. You are not exploring. You are trespassing in the drafting room of a geology that has not decided what it wants to be. Act accordingly.

---

### I. The Body: A Rejection Suit

**Thermal regulation is survival.** The ambient darkness is a lie. You will stand on stone that radiates with the steady, murderous patience of a kiln floor. Ordinary cold-weather gear will cook you; ordinary heat gear assumes solar input that can be reflected. Here, the heat is endogenic, rising from below like a slow exhalation from the core. You need a **full-body excursion suit with active cryogenic micro-loops**—not insulation, but heat *rejection*. The garment must pump your metabolic warmth outward while dumping environmental infrared into radiator panels at your shoulders and spine. Under the suit, wear a capillary-cooled mesh. You will still feel the warmth pressed against you, but you will not die from it.

**The atmosphere is an afterthought.** It is thin, metallic, and choked with silicate dust that has never settled. A filter mask is insufficient. You need a **sealed helmet with a positive-pressure oxygen supply** and a closed-loop scrubber. The dust is not particulate in the way a desert is particulate; it is a permanent estratosphere, fine enough to infiltrate seals and abrade lenses. Gold-laminate faceplates are recommended—not against sunlight, for there is none, but to protect your eyes from thermal bloom and particulate scouring.

**Your feet are the most vulnerable point.** Standard boots will melt, slash, or transmit kill-heat into your arches. You require **segmented ceramic-titanum sabatons with replaceable carbide soles**, minimum six to eight centimeters thick. The obsidian is newborn, chemically pure, and *sharp* in a way that terrestrial erosion has not seen in four billion years; it will part synthetic weave like mist. Gaiters should be puncture-weave rated for hypersonic micro-meteorites. Treat the ground as a blade that has not yet been dulled.

**Protect your viscera from the silence.** The geological violence here is subsonic. A tower collapsing will arrive not as sound but as a pressure against your sternum, a thrum in your molars. Line your suit’s inner chassis with **electroactive gel padding or smart-foam rated for low-frequency blast dampening**. Without it, you will hemorrhage internally from tectonic events you never hear coming.

### II. Seeing Without Photons

**Do not bring lights.**

This is counterintuitive, but critical. The darkness here is *palpable* and possesses its own strange visibility. After fifteen to twenty minutes of sensory adjustment, shapes will resolve by thermal gradient alone—a grey, sourceless sight where warm stone edges print themselves against slightly cooler air. Introducing artificial photons will destroy this adaptation. Worse, they will flare off the silicate dust and create ghost halos in a world where depth is read by temperature, not shadow.

Instead, equip your helmet with a **millimeter-wave lidar overlay** or passive thermal contouring. You need to see mass and fracture at fifty meters, not color. If you must carry a personal beacon, use a single, dim amber bulb at ankle level—enough to find your own feet without bleaching the thermal map your brain is learning to read.

---

### III. Navigation in a World That Unmakes Itself

**Maps are fiction here.** A ridge you cross at noon may blister into a dome by mid-afternoon; a plain may buckle into a mountain range before you finish your rations. Erosion has not been invented, but neither has stability.

Do not plan to hike out the way you hiked in.

Use **self-anchoring radio beacons** with tungsten-carbide thermal spikes. Drop one every kilometer. They are your only landmarks in a landscape that renegotiates its own topology. Better yet, arrange for **airborne or orbital extraction**; do not rely on a terrestrial return route. If you must walk, trail a **high-tensile fiber tether** from your insertion point—physically tie yourself to the outside world, because the ground in between has no loyalty to continuity.

Navigating by the "built" structures is seductive but fatal. The hexagonal colonnades and ziggurats are geologic fruits, not waypoints; they extrude, sag, and are reclaimed by viscous magma on timescales you can witness. A tower that serves as a bearing may become a lava coil before you reach it.

### IV. Provisions: The Closed Loop

There is no water. Not a drop. Not a vapor. The dryness is absolute and will actively desiccate you.

Your suit must include a **reclamation still-function**—every exhalation and bead of sweat must be harvested. Carry triple your anticipated water ration in reserve canisters stored at your hips, where the suit’s cooling loops can keep them from flash-boiling. Food should be calorically dense and non-crumbly; you are not worried about attracting wildlife—there is none—but you must leave absolutely no biological trace. This world has not yet learned how to rot. A dropped crumb is an alien artifact.

---

### V. Protocols of Movement

**All stone is alive.** Not in a biological sense, but in a state sense: what appears solid is merely slow, and what appears still is merely between motions.

- **Do not touch the architecture.** The colonnades and arches are crystallization paths, not ruins. They are hollow, unstable, and extruding. Standing beneath a sixty-meter hexagonal pillar to gawk is akin to standing under a calving glacier.
- **Move perpendicular to seams.** When the obsidian splits with a sound like a drawn breath, the new glass rises with perceptible speed. Do not try to outrun it along the fissure; cross-step away.
- **Never sit on the ground.** The surface is a feverish membrane. Carry a **portable thermal hammock or hover-rest** that keeps you elevated. A "sheet of cooling slag" is still remembering that it was recently magma; it may decide to soften and reclaim your boots.

**Infrasound as alarm.** Learn to read the pressure in your gut and the vibration in your teeth. When you feel a subsonic thrum building, freeze and assess: something large is about to collapse, inflate, or crack. Your suit dampeners will save your organs, but your best defense is distance.

---

### VI. The Silence: Auditory Etiquette

You will carry the noisiest thing in the universe: your own biology.

Before you arrive, spend **no fewer than seventy-two hours in an anechoic chamber**. Learn the sound of your heartbeat until it becomes uninteresting. Learn the sound of your swallowing, your blink, the rustle of your own clothes. In the total, architectural silence of this place, these sounds will seem like vandalism. They must bore you, or they will unnerve you.

Strip your gear of all auditory alerts. No beeps, no chimes, no voice reminders. Convert telemetry into **haptic taps at the collarbone** or thermal warnings at the wrist. If you travel with a companion, communicate by pre-arranged hand signals or subvocal bone-conduction; spoken words, even over comms, will feel like shouting in a cathedral made of void.

---

### VII. Psychological Armor: The Danger of Irrelevance

The true hazard of this place is not the magma. It is **insignificance**.

You will stand before a ziggurat of layered pyroxene so regular it seems drafted by a god, and you will feel the chill certainty that you are walking through a temple or a tomb. This is a trick. The world is pure nature, pre-tool, pre-intent. It does not know you exist. It is too busy becoming itself to notice your intrusion. You must carry your purpose inside you like a heavy stone, because the landscape offers no reflection, no need, no cycle of nutrients to welcome you into.

Before departure, write down exactly why you are going. Fold the paper and seal it in your suit’s breast pocket. When the silence becomes absolute and the thermal dark begins to feel like a blanket tightening around your sense of self, read it. It will be the only architecture within a hundred kilometers that means anything.

You must also prepare for **temporal vertigo**. You are used to a world where mountains are older than nations. Here, a mountain range may grow in an afternoon. A tower that has stood for a decade is an infant, already sagging back into the melt. You will feel that your own lifespan is out of scale with the reality around you—not because the place is ancient, but because it is *youthful* in a way that makes your years seem petrified.

---

### VIII. Extraction and the Time Limit

You cannot camp here. You cannot wait out a storm, because there is no weather—only the solid storm of the lithosphere, which does not pass.

**Limit surface exposure to four hours.** If you must stay longer, establish a quiescent zone by surveying for zones of recent, cooled extrusion and no active inflation. Even then, understand that "quiescent" is a local, temporary fiction. Schedule extraction on a merciless clock. The planet will not hurt you out of malice; it will simply raise a new ridge across your path because it was time for the ridge to exist.

---

### IX. Final Preparation Checklist

- **Suit:** Sealed, positive-pressure, active-cooling, infrasound gel-lined, dust-proof.
- **Boots:** Thick ceramic/titanum soles, replaceable carbide tread, thermal gaiters.
- **Helmet:** Gold-laminate visor, milli-wave lidar overlay, no photonic lamps.
- **Navigation:** Tether to insertion point, self-anchoring thermal beacons, orbital extraction arranged.
- **Water:** Triple ration, suit-integrated reclamation.
- **Rest:** Portable thermal hammock/hover-pad; never contact the ground directly.
- **Sensory:** Seventy-two hours anechoic isolation training. No audible gear.
- **Mind:** A written reason, sealed in your suit. A vow to remember that the built quality is a math, not a message.

Enter with the humility of a witness. The world is sketching its own face in sharp, raw, mineral lines, and it has not yet chosen to need eyes to see itself. Stay warm, stay light, and do not stay long enough to become part of the geology.
