First, abandon every intuition you have about caves. This is not a hollow in the earth waiting to be explored; it is a cabinet, a larder left unstocked, a chapel sculpted by chemistry with the patience of a devotee. You are not entering geology. You are crawling inside an artifact that happens to be buried. If you approach it as a spelunker, you will fail before your palms touch the porcelain floor. Approach it as a thief breaking into a reliquary that does not want to be opened.

### **1. Reconcile Your Body with the Geometry of a Pocket**
The space is the length and breadth of two outstretched arms. That is all. Before you arrive in the valley, measure it: lie on the floor and trace the perimeter with your hands and feet. Imagine the ceiling low enough that standing is a physical impossibility, so close that a deep breath would scrape your shoulders against sintered crystal. If you are broad, if your shoulders do not compress easily, or if your ribcage expands with the indignation of claustrophobia, do not go. The entrance is a puckered gap, a lip of earth just wide enough to admit a body on hands and knees. You cannot carry a pack. You cannot wear a bulky harness. You should consider greasing your outer garments—something thin, slick, compressible—or wearing a compression layer that allows you to slide through the gap like a knot through a needle.

Your knees and palms are your primary vehicles here. The floor is cast mineral, pale and fine-grained as unglazed porcelain, absolutely unforgiving. There is no sediment to cushion you, no soft alluvial give. Pad your joints beforehand. Wear gel kneepads thin enough not to snag on the threshold. Your hands will bear your weight on a surface harder than kiln-fired tile; keep your nails short and your palms calloused, or they will shred.

Enter feet-first if at all possible. You will likely find this place by falling, as everyone does—the entrance is invisible until the ground betrays you—so as you tumble, tuck your chin, cradle your skull, and try to land with your legs angled toward the gap. Once inside, reversal is your only exit. There is no room to turn around.

### **2. Prepare for the Dryness Like a Desert Crossing**
The air inside is not merely arid; it is *occupied* by absence. It is old, still, and stripped of moisture to a degree that feels almost personal, as though the room is drinking from your lips. Minutes inside and your skin will tighten, your lips will chap and split. Do not bring open water in a flask—spilling moisture onto those precipitated salts and compacted selenite walls may trigger chemical micro-reactions you cannot control, and in a space that small, you cannot escape the consequences. Instead, hyper-hydrate for twelve hours before entry. Coat your skin in a heavy occlusive barrier: lanolin, petroleum jelly, something that seals. Swab the insides of your nostrils with saline gel; the dry air will otherwise crack the mucous membranes until they bleed. Bring nothing that relies on humidity: paper will curl instantly, bread will fossilize.

### **3. Dress for a Thermal Paradox**
The ambient temperature hovers ten degrees above freezing—cool enough to leach heat from your motionless body, yet the mineral surfaces you press against are warm and dry. Dress in thin, insulating layers that compress to nothing. Bulk is your enemy; cold is your second enemy. The warmth of the stone will betray you, making you think you are safe while the air steals core heat from your exposed neck and ears. A close-fitting wool skullcap that does not protrude. A compression shirt, a thin thermal layer. Nothing with dangling drawstrings or loose flaps that could catch on the accreted shelves and wrist-thick columns.

Do not expect the brightness to warm you. The light is a cold phenomenon.

### **4. Arm Yourself Against the White**
A seam in the vault above, no wider than a paper cut, admits a thread of daylight that the translucent selenite and crystalline lattice amplify into a saturated, chalk-white luminance. It is relentless. Shadows are thin and sharp as blades. The room does not flicker; it does not dim. It glows from all directions at once, leaving your eyes with no shadow to rest in.

Bring dark-lensed eye protection, but not bulky glacier goggles. Choose close-fitting sunglasses with side shields—something that will not knock against the low ceiling when you flinch. Even then, expect disorientation. The uniformity of the light scrambles depth perception; the accreted shelves projecting from the walls at irregular heights will appear to float. Your brain, starved of shadow cues, may hallucinate motion in the stillness. Close your eyes periodically, press your palms over them, and let your retinas reset.

### **5. Accept the Sound as a Second Skeleton**
You cannot block the sound. Earplugs are useless. The drone is a standing tectonic vibration transmitted not through the air primarily, but through the crystalline lattice itself, conducted into your sternum, your teeth, your marrow. It is a dense mid-range note, unchanging in pitch and amplitude, eternal as the light. It lives in your chest more than your ears.

Before you enter, spend time in vibration-heavy environments—subways, industrial plants, the engine rooms of ships—until your nervous system learns that a constant tone need not signal alarm. Practice breathing against the drone: slow nasal inhales that harmonize, somehow, with the invariant frequency. Do not try to "listen past it." That will only amplify your awareness of its permanence. Instead, try to treat it as a physical fact, like gravity. If you are prone to anxiety or cardiac arrhythmia, be warned: the sensation of your own ribcage acting as a sounding board for geology is profoundly uncanny. Some travelers report feeling their heartbeat sync to the hum and then panic when they cannot distinguish their pulse from the room’s voice.

Sleep is impossible here. Do not plan to rest. Plan to endure.

### **6. Prepare for Stasis, Not Time**
This place is locked in a frozen equilibrium. The light has not shifted in centuries. The moss—sparse, colorless, the only soft thing in the chamber—does not grow, does not brown, does not green. It is paused. So, too, will your sense of duration be paused.

Humans orient themselves through micro-changes: a shadow lengthens, a breeze shifts, a stomach growls. Here, nothing shifts. Bring a mechanical timepiece—something silent, something that does not rely on digital display, for the rasp of a ticking watch might drive you to madness against the drone—and set a hard limit. Do not exceed forty minutes inside. Without temporal markers, your mind will elongate minutes into hours or compress hours into a blink. You may emerge convinced you stayed a day, or certain you had only just crossed the threshold.

Train beforehand by sitting in perfectly still, quiet rooms with no sensory variation. Learn to catalogue your thoughts without external stimulus. Some travelers recite poetry, run multiplication tables, or trace the mineral seams with a fingertip to generate input. The room offers none.

### **7. Leave Your Organic Expectations Outside**
There is no life here. No scuttle, no drip, no whiff of guano or fungus. The silence is not empty; it is *mineral*. You will be the only biological intrusion in a space that resembles a chapel built by a non-human intelligence. The columns hold up nothing. The shelves are empty. The geometry is deliberate-looking, coursed like masonry, weathered to satin by micro-abrasion until it resembles hand-planed wood. The cognitive dissonance is severe: your ape-brain will scream *intention*, *maker*, *ritual*, even while your intellect insists it is only chemistry.

Prepare for spiritual vertigo. The room waits. It has waited longer than your culture has existed. You are not a pilgrim; you are a speck of moisture, a temporary soft thing surrounded by finished construction. Touch as little as possible. Do not chip souvenirs. Do not leave hair or skin or breath-moisture if you can help it. The chamber is already complete. It does not need your contribution.

### **8. Egress and the Return**
When it is time to leave, you must reverse out through the same puckered gap, on hands and knees, blind to what is behind you. Tie a thin cord to your ankle before you enter and anchor it at the threshold, or place a small reflective marker just outside the seam so the absence of light behind you reads as a signal. The brightness inside is so uniform that the exit may appear only as a slightly darker patch of shadow; youradjusted eyes may miss it entirely.

Back out slowly. The salts and selenite are not razor-sharp—their edges have weathered to satin—but they are unyielding. Do not rise to your knees in haste; you will strike the vaulted ceiling. Do not pause to look back. The room does not change. It will look exactly the same when you are gone.

### **Final Counsel**
This is not a destination for the ordinary tourist. It is a place to visit if you need to understand what it means for the natural world to imitate the made one, and then to abandon its creation in perfect stasis. You will not leave with memories of grandeur. You will leave with the whisper of a chalk-white hum behind your eyes, the dryness of the desert in your throat, and the unsettling knowledge that chemistry, given time and brine and vapor, can build a chapel and choose to leave it empty forever.

Go hydrated. Go small. Go with your mind already narrow as the gap you must crawl through. And do not stay past the moment when the stillness begins to feel like recognition.
