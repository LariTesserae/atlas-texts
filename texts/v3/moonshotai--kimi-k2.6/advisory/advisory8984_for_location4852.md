**Field Preparation Brief: The Lignin Plain**  
*(Colloquial Designations: The Root Floor, The Final Substrate, The Cambium Works)*

---

### 1. The Material Proscription: Absolute Ferrous Exclusion

Before you pack a single item, empty your kit of steel, iron, and most ferrous alloys. The local physics does not permit steel; the text is explicit that the pressures and processes here would crush it, and travelers report that ferrous metals arrive corroded, warped, or simply *absent*—as if the substrate refuses to acknowledge their atomic structure. This is not metaphor. Your boot eyelets, your knife, your tent stakes, your oxygen tank, your wristwatch spring, the zipper on your coat: if it is ferrous, it will fail you, possibly catastrophically.

**Use instead:** Carbon-fiber composites, hardwood, dense ceramic, volcanic glass, certain high-grade polymers, and bone. Titanium is debated among travelers; some report it survives but attracts a strange verdigris of lignin-dust. To be safe, avoid all metals where possible. Your cutting tools should be ceramic or obsidian. Your tent frame should be carbon-fiber or laminated hardwood. Your canteen should be lined polymer or fired clay. If you require supplemental oxygen—and you do—the tank must be a carbon-fiber composite with ceramic valves.

---

### 2. Attire for the Wet Cold

The cold here does not merely lower your body temperature; it *snaps at the edges of sound*. It is a cold that conducts noise with surgical clarity, which means it will also conduct heat away from your body through every damp surface. And everything is damp. The air itself is a wetland, a suspended marsh. Water does not rest; it condenses on you, freezes on you, flash-steams, and condenses again. You will never be dry.

**Required gear:**
- **A sealed, non-breathable outer shell.** Breathable fabrics are a liability. The atmosphere is saturated with spores, effluent vapors, and reality-particulate. You want a suit you can wipe dry, with internal cuffs at wrists and ankles.
- **Desiccant layers.** Pack your underlayers with reusable desiccant packets in pockets across your torso and limbs. Change them at every rest stop.
- **Subsonic-dampening footwear.** The floor thrums with a vascular, subsonic persistence that can induce nausea, cardiac arrhythmia, and vertigo in ordinary humans. Your boots must have thick, ceramic-composite or dense-rubber soles engineered to dampen frequencies below 20 Hz. The floor is polished wood, so the tread must also grip a surface as slick as planed birch.
- **Thermal retention without bulk.** The cold is absolute, but you may need to scramble if a piston-root heaves or a valve erupts. Use aerogel-lined insulation if available; it is light, non-ferrous, and effective when wet.
- **Full-face protection.** A hood and a mask that warms the air you breathe. The altitude is extreme; the sky is bruised thin. Your lungs will bleed heat and moisture with every exhalation.

---

### 3. Respiration & Altitude

The tableland is high enough that the sky darkens, yet flat enough that horizons deceive. The oxygen content is insufficient for unaided human function. You will require **supplemental oxygen**, and it must be **warmed and humidified** before inhalation. Breathing this cold, thin air raw will damage your alveoli within hours.

Your respirator must also include a **spore filter**. The factory-trees vent more than steam. Spore-clouds bloom and collapse in cycles, and the symbiotic fauna shed chitin-dust and lubricant-mist. A standard HEPA filter is insufficient; you need a ceramic-pore filter rated for biological industrial particulate.

---

### 4. Sensory Management: The Din and the Dark

**Hearing:** The noise is not merely loud; it is *total*. Seed-pistons fall in arboreal galleries, producing bass notes that travel through the root-floor and emerge elsewhere. Bellows the size of cathedrals pulse. Steam vents erupt. If you protect your ears with total isolation, you will lose your primary navigational sense. If you leave them unprotected, you will suffer damage within a day, and the unnatural acoustic clarity of the cold will ensure the damage is severe.

Use **variable-attenuation ear protection**—electronic or mechanical—that blocks high-frequency industrial crack and hiss while allowing low-frequency thrum and percussion through. You must learn to navigate by the bass. Each factory-complex has a distinct rhythm, a signature in the subsonic range. If you cannot hear the floor, you are blind.

**Vision:** The light is the color of a bruise underwater—dim, filtered, sufficient for photosynthesis but not for human sight. Do not use bright artificial lights. The local biology photosynthesizes at extreme efficiency in this dimness; a bright flashlight may disrupt valve-flowers or trigger a defensive phototoxic response from the canopy. Use **light-amplification goggles** or sensitive low-light optics. If you must use active illumination, restrict yourself to near-infrared, and use it sparingly.

**Proprioception:** The floor shudders. Piston-roots the width of subway tunnels heave the wooden plain to circulate nutrients. You cannot predict these heaves, but you can brace for them. Carry a pair of **trekking poles** (hardwood or ceramic) to maintain balance when the ground buckles. The surface is flat, but it is not stable in the human sense.

### 5. Navigation in a Flat, Fluid World

You can roll a marble from one horizon to the other, yet you cannot trust that the horizon will stay where you left it. The scale is mercilessly broad, and causality is a byproduct that the trees refine and occasionally vent as waste.

**Marking your path:** Standard compasses are unreliable; reality-effluent pools disrupt magnetic fields, and Euclidean geometry is a local suggestion. You must mark your trail with **non-biological, inert markers**—ceramic shards, glass beads, or bleached bone stakes. Do not use biodegradable cord or organic dye; the substrate will metabolize it within hours.

**The Consensus Journal:** Memory is not reliable on the Root Floor. The fog thickens in spots and refuses to thin; walk through it, and the factory you left may no longer be the one you approach. Carry a waterproof, polymer-page journal. Write down your heading, the factory-signature you are moving toward, the time (use a sandglass or mechanical ceramic chronometer—electronics may drift), and your physical state. If your observations begin to contradict your notes, trust the paper. The floor remembers old paths that no longer align with geography; your mind will try to conform to the local causality. Your written word is your anchor.

**Fog Protocol:** If the mist coils around your ankles and then thickens to obscure your markers, **stop moving immediately**. Kneel. Place both palms on the root-floor. The floor is the oldest stable state; it is the one thing that has forgotten how to change. Maintain physical contact with it. Do not stand and walk blindly. The fog is not water vapor alone; it is a phase-state of the reality-distillation process. If you must wait for hours, wait. Movement in dense fog is the leading cause of displacement.

**Sound Navigation:** Learn the seed-piston signatures. The bass note of a falling piston may reach you from the left, then from beneath your feet, then from a non-direction. When you hear a sound from a direction that does not exist, freeze. Do not orient toward it. You are at a seam in causality. Back away slowly, keeping your palms on the floor, until the sound resolves to a coherent vector.

### 6. Causality & Reality Hazards

The factory-trees process reality itself, and reality does not always survive intact. At the bases of chimneys, in cold effluent pools, you will find waste objects: gears of lignin-stone, spheres refracting nameless colors, items that pulse with non-biological rhythms.

**Do not touch them.**  
**Do not pocket them.**  
**Do not study them with bare eyes for more than a glance.**

These are not curiosities. They are industrial waste from a process that digests the fundamental. Touching a gear may anchor you to a timeline where you never arrived. Staring into a sphere may remap your visual cortex to perceive colors that human neurology cannot process, resulting in seizures or persistent synesthetic damage. If you must document them, use a camera with a ceramic lens and review the footage only after extraction.

If you are caught in a displacement event—if the factory shifts, if the years exchange, if the path behind you no longer leads to your entry point—consult your journal. Retrace your steps exactly. The root-floor remembers; your footprints may still be valid even if the sky above them has changed.

### 7. Fauna Protocols: The Residents of the Works

Life here does not flee the industry; it is the industry’s lubricant, its chorus, its cleanup crew.

- **The Symbiotic Swarms:** Neither insect nor machine-tender but both, these chitinous entities cling to warm junctions. They are polished to a bearing-sheen by constant motion. If they land on you, do not swat them. They are likely secreting lubricants that the local machinery accepts. Swatting may trigger a localized pressure imbalance in a valve-flower. Let them crawl; they will move on when they find you are not a flange.
- **The Shaggy Ungulates:** These wander the leagues between factories, hooves striking the resonant floor like mallets. They are generally harmless and follow predictable paths toward byproduct-fall. You may follow them at a distance if you need to locate edible waxy kernels, but beware: the kernels fall from processing heights, and a waxy kernel the size of a fist dropped two hundred feet is a lethal projectile.
- **The Clicking Node-Tenders:** In the hollows, hundreds of small creatures tend root-nodes as big as houses. Their body heat melts divots in the frost. They are highly concentrated and highly territorial. If the clicking increases in frequency as you approach, you are entering their radius. Back away. The air itself seems to swarm with them, and in the next valley there is only silence. Use that silence as your buffer.
- **The Bird-Entities:** They nest in the upper flues where steam is warmest. Their cries are sharp, frequent, and useful as acoustic landmarks. However, disturbing a nest may cause them to flock, and their density in the air can disrupt your orientation in a place where orientation is already fragile.

### 8. Provisions & Hydration

Bring all your food. The byproduct-fall—waxy kernels, hardening resins, amber-like secretions—may be chemically nutritious, but they are also processed by a biology that refines reality. Eating them is akin to ingesting unfiltered industrial solvent. Some travelers report sustenance; others report their blood temporarily turning to a lignin-suspension. Do not risk it unless starving.

Water is abundant and impossible. It condenses on every surface, runs in engineered rivulets, pools and flash-steams. It is also saturated with effluent, spores, and causal particulate. **Do not drink it raw.** If you are equipped with a ceramic-pore filter and a distillation apparatus, you may process the ice-filigree that forms on cold surfaces; this is the closest to pure H₂O the plain offers. Better yet, carry your own supply in sealed polymer bladders.

---

### 9. Shelter & Sleep

There is no natural shelter. The plain is a tableland planed to amnesia. You must carry a **free-standing, self-supporting tent** with a carbon-fiber or hardwood frame. Do not attempt to stake into the floor; the root-matter may respond by attempting to seal around or metabolize the intrusion. Use a weighted base and vibration-dampening ground cloth.

Sleep will be difficult. The subsonic thrum permeates everything. The cold seeps. The noise never ceases. If you use sedatives, use mild ones; you need to be able to wake instantly if a piston-root heaves nearby or a steam vent erupts. Travel in pairs or trios and sleep in shifts. One person must always be awake to monitor the fog, the journal, and the factory-signatures.

---

### 10. Emergency & Medical Protocols

- **Hypothermia:** The wet cold will defeat your insulation if you stop moving for too long. Keep moving. If injured, your partner must keep you warm; the ambient air will not permit rest.
- **Wound Management:** The atmosphere is rich with cambium and biological agents. If you are cut, clean the wound immediately with sterile synthetic solution and seal it with a non-organic dressing. The local biology scars over damage rapidly; you do not want your laceration sealed with root-matter.
- **Reality-Exposure:** If you touch a byproduct sphere or gear, note the time in your journal immediately. Wrap the affected limb in ceramic-wool insulation to limit causal bleed. Do not attempt to remove the object if it adheres; it may be anchoring you to this timeline. Extract to your entry point immediately.
- **Extraction:** Pre-place a large ceramic resonator or hollow bone chime at your entry point before you venture inward. The sound of the wind through it—sharp, inorganic, distinct—may cut through the industrial drone and give you a fixed point to navigate toward. If you become displaced, the sound of your own beacon is your best hope of returning to your origin.

---

### 11. The Essential Mindset

You are not visiting a landscape. You are entering a *process*. The Lignin Plain is the oldest stable state, the final geometry before the world forgot the difference between dead matter and living scaffold. There is no malice here, no welcome, no wilderness to conquer. The factory-trees do not know you exist. They manufacture the impossible from the forgotten, and you are not yet forgotten.

Move quietly on the resonant floor. Do not leave waste; the system will try to process it, and your plastics may clog a valve-flower or poison a nutrient channel. Do not take souvenirs; the byproducts are reality’s garbage, and they will make garbage of you in return. Do not shine bright lights, do not strike the bark, do not impose your intention.

Be small. Be dry. Be quiet. Be ceramic and carbon and bone. Listen to the bass note of the floor. Trust your journal over your eyes. And when the fog thickens, kneel, touch the wood that was once mountains, and wait for the world to settle back into its endless, patient, industrial rhythm.
