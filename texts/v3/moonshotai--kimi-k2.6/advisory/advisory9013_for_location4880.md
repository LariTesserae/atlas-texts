**Prelude: The Aqueous Heresy**

First, you must accept a fundamental indignity: you are a wet thing, a sack of aqueous chemistry, preparing to enter a world that has transcended water entirely. The Dry City does not hate you, but your moisture is an anachronism, a biological dialect it stopped speaking epochs ago. Your preparation, therefore, is an act of translation. You are not packing for a trip; you are designing a temporary exoskeleton that allows a single raindrop to stroll through a desert that has outlived the concept of thirst. Begin your preparations months in advance. Condition yourself to think of water not as a resource to be consumed, but as a secret to be kept.

---

**I. The Breath: Oxygen and Metal-Vapor**

You will suffocate here gracefully if you are not careful. The atmosphere is thin, sharp, and laden with ozone-sweet vapors and microscopic metallic particulate—the exhaled breath of circuitry that has long since gone to vegetable seed. Standard high-altitude acclimation is insufficient. Spend six to eight weeks prior to departure in a hypobaric chamber, but do not merely reduce pressure; introduce trace metal vapors and elevated ozone in controlled increments. Your alveoli must learn to extract oxygen from air that feels like it wants to etch them. 

Your breathing apparatus should not be a crude mask. A full-face seal is necessary, but the delivery system should be a low-flow nasal catheter paired with a permeable hood. You need to feel the *sharpness* on the exposed skin of your face—this is sensory information, a barometer of altitude and atmospheric mood—but you must not hemorrhage moisture through your mouth and nose. The catheter should deliver supplemental oxygen in pulses timed to your inhalation, preserving the negative pressure of your lungs without forcing you to gasp. Gasps are water-loss. Gasps are for the desperate.

Inside the mask, you need a dual-stage filter: HEPA for the crystalline spores you will inevitably encounter, and an activated-copper mesh to neutralize ozone and metallic aerosols before they can corrode your mucous membranes. Carry spare filter wicks. The air here is not merely empty; it is *active*, and it will clog your equipment faster than any terrestrial smog.

---

**II. The Skin: A Canteen with a Soul**

Your skin is the enemy. In this world, perspiration is treason, evaporation is exile. You must become a sealed system. Two months before arrival, begin coating your body in clinical occlusive wraps during sleep to train your pores into dormancy. Cease using water-based moisturizers; switch entirely to anhydrous lipid barriers—lanolin mixed with ceramide esters and a trace of silver sulfadiazine to prevent microbial bloom in the sealed environment.

Your suit should be a stillsuit in the truest sense, but inverted from the Arrakeen model: not designed to capture sweat for drinking, but to prevent sweat from ever forming. It must be a trilaminate membrane. The inner layer is a soft hydrophilic weave that traps any inevitable transepidermal water loss and wicks it via osmotic channels to a central reclamation bladder at the small of your back. The middle layer is thermal insulation—light, aerogel-lofted—to hold your body heat against the dry chill without provoking a sweat response. The outer layer must be abrasion-resistant and matte, colored in the bruised violet and dulled bark-grey of the plain, because you should not arrive as a bright shout in a world of whispered bioluminescence.

Treat your lips, nostrils, and eyelids with medical-grade silicone sealant. Not enough to glue them shut, but enough to slow the desiccating wind that wants to carve your breath into frost. You should feel, when you stand upon the fossilized velvet floor, the cool press upward through your bootsoles, but nowhere else should the world touch your chemistry directly.

---

**III. The Ears: The Aeolian Compromise**

This is the preparation that will save your life, and it is the one most travelers neglect. The Dry City is *loud*, and its noise is not incidental—it is structural, a form of maintenance. To block it out is to wear a blindfold in traffic. Do not bring noise-canceling headphones. Do not bring wax plugs. Do not retreat into silence, because a silent spire is a dead spire, and if you miss the moment when the tectonic groan of the phloem-pipes shifts from a healthy drone to a pre-collapse whine, you will die buried in cellulose.

Instead, commission a pair of custom labyrinthine ear-cuffs. These should be machined from porous titanium and filled with helical baffles tuned to specific frequency ranges. They must attenuate the piercing Aeolian whistles that cut through the hexagonal erosion-scars—the titanium-nitrogen chords that can induce vertigo and permanent tinnitus—while allowing the sub-bass frequencies of sap-pressure and root-shift to pass through, conducted via bone to your inner ear. You need to *feel* the city’s digestion in your jawbone.

For sleep, you will require a white-noise implant or a subcutaneous bone-conduction oscillator set to match the city’s baseline drone. If you try to sleep in true silence, the sudden percussive snap of a static storm will send you into cardiac shock. More importantly, you must train your autonomic nervous system to recognize the city’s acoustic signature. Spend your evenings in the weeks before departure listening to recordings of tectonic groans, aeolian flutes, and low-frequency electromagnetic pulses. Learn to sleep inside the chord. Learn to wake instantly when the chord changes key.

Carry a tactile resonance patch on your sternum. If the sound drops below a threshold—if the spire you are in begins to go silent—the patch should vibrate against your ribs. This is your evacuation alarm.

**IV. The Eyes: Navigation by Corpse-Light**

There is no sunrise. There is no noon. The senescent sun offers only a bruised opalescence, and the true illumination comes from below—from the Cherenkov blue of inner bark glowing with harvested cosmic radiation and the long half-lives of an ancient bio-metabolic economy. Your eyes must become instruments of twilight.

For three months prior, live in perpetual dimness. Remove all blue-light LEDs from your environment; they will bleach your rhodopsin and blind you to the subtle gradients of the Plant’s phosphorescence. When you arrive, bring no conventional torch. A standard flashlight is an act of vandalism here; the burst of photons will wash out the delicate quantum-capillary webs and leave you stumbling in a self-induced eclipse for twenty minutes afterward. If you must carry artificial light, use a single photon-thrower tuned to 620 nanometers—a deep amber so dim it is barely perceptible—and only for emergencies.

Instead, equip yourself with electrophoretic lenses that amplify ambient light in the 400–500 nanometer range, the precise band of the lignified Cherenkov glow. The lenses should be polarized to cut the glare of the amber electrical harvests that occasionally web the sky; looking directly at an overhead discharge without protection will leave afterimages burned into your retina for hours.

Learn to read the plain by its own dimness. The eroded hollows pool with blue radiance; the elevated ridges are violet shadows against violet sky. Your navigation is not by contour but by luminescence. The brightest paths are often the most eroded, the most ancient, the most fragile. Step carefully.

---

**V. The Feet: Walking on Forgiven Topography**

The floor is alive, or it is the memory of aliveness compressed into a final posture. It is fossilized velvet, polished periderm, cool and slightly yielding. Do not insult it with cleats or spiked crampons. You are not conquering a mountain; you are stepping across the skin of an organism that has forgotten topography.

Your boots should have thin, conductive soles—felted carbon-fiber or soft vulcanized rubber no more than four millimeters thick. You must feel the cool press upward. You must feel the micro-tremor when the plain begins its epochal exhalation, the nearly imperceptible rising of the floor as central sap-chambers expand. If you are standing on a spire’s balcony when the plain exhales, the altitude shift of mere meters will alter the resonance of the structure. You need to sense this in your ankles before your instruments detect it.

Traction is still necessary. The polished bark can be slick, especially where the silver computation-flakes have fallen like metallic snow. Your boot treads should be hexagonal, small, mimicking the erosion-scars of the spires themselves—an aesthetic and practical homage that distributes your weight like a grazer rather than concentrating it like a predator.

Walk slowly. The ground has forgiven all mountains; it has no patience for human hurry.

---

**VI. The Blood: Radiation, Electricity, and the Dry Storm**

You are standing on a high plateau under a sky that has forgotten to shield. Cosmic rays arrive here unimpeded, harvested by the Plant for its bioluminescent appetite, and the inner bark glows with the light of radioactive appetite. This is not immediately lethal, but it is chronic. Your water supply—double-sealed in flexible bladders against your torso and thighs—will serve dual duty as radiation shielding. Water is hydrogen-rich; hydrogen stops neutrons. Carry more water than you think you need, not only to drink but to live behind.

Supplement with anti-rad precursors: potassium iodide, dimethyl sulfoxide, and nucleotide precursors to assist your DNA repair mechanisms. The Plant has evolved to sing in this radiation; you have not.

Then there are the static storms. The spires discharge into one another, painting amber webs across the violet dim. During an electrical harvest, the interstitial space between towers becomes a conductor. Do not be the tallest object, but more importantly, do not be the *most aqueous* conductor. Your suit should have a Faraday weave in the outer layer, and you should carry a grounding staff—a simple carbon-fiber pole with a conductive tip to bleed off induced charge. When the webs begin to weave, seek the interior of a spire, close to the xylem walls. The Plant routes its own lightning internally; it is safer inside the circuitry than outside in the space between thoughts.

---

**VII. The Gut: Fasting in the Lignified Kingdom**

There is nothing here for you to eat. The fauna run on mineral appetites, on voltage and silica and computation-mold. Their chemistry is lignin and chitin, quantum dots and crystalline salts. To attempt to eat local matter is to invite poisoning, obstruction, or a slow dry starvation.

Enter the Dry City with your body pre-loaded. Maximize your subcutaneous and hepatic fat reserves in the weeks before arrival. Your diet should shift toward ketogenic, high-fat emulsions that produce maximal energy per gram of metabolic water consumed. Bring nutrient pastes in sealed mylar tubes—anhydrous, concentrated, requiring minimal chewing and minimal digestive water. You should defecate as rarely as possible; every gram of feces is a water-loss. Carry broad-spectrum enzymatic supplements to wring every joule from your rations.

You will fast for longer stretches than you are accustomed to. Embrace this. The cool, dry air suppresses human appetite anyway. Let your metabolism slow to match the half-sleep of the world around you.

---

**VIII. The Mind: Unlearning the Day**

This is the most difficult preparation, because it requires you to abandon the heartbeat of your home world. There is no dawn. There is no dusk. There are no seconds here; there are growth rings. A corridor that leads eastward now may seal itself in a century, but for your brief visit, the relevant fact is that the architecture *breathes*. Turgor-pressure gates open and close with the slow rhythm of sap. Balconies exhale pollen on schedules tied to electrical harvests, not clocks.

You must abandon your wristwatch. Abandon your circadian rhythm. Before you arrive, practice polyphasic sleep in undifferentiated dimness, triggered not by light but by internal cues of exhaustion. Learn to nap when the body demands, for fifteen minutes or four hours, untethered from the twenty-four-hour lie.

Do not carry a map. Maps are blasphemy here. The spires shift, not capriciously, but organically. Instead, prepare a *tendency journal*. Record not fixed locations but relational states: *When the wind whistles in C-sharp minor, the western gate is open. When the root-flanges throb at two-second intervals, the lower avenues are passable.* You are not navigating a city. You are reading the habits of a single vast organism. Be patient enough to let it reveal its currents.

Meditate on erosion. Imagine your own topography—your ambitions, your urgency—flattened into humility, into a final settled posture. If you arrive impatient, the plain will teach you patience by breaking you against its time scale.

---

**IX. The Etiquette of Symbiosis: Smelling Like Repair**

The Dry City has an immune system, and you are, by default, a potential pathogen. The predatory pollen fronts roll between spires like weather, seeking invasive viral weeds to choke. The crystalline spore clouds shriek as they hunt. The cleaner-worms and the hairy circuit-board symbiotes maintain the urban tissue, exuding trails of dry petrichor-without-water—the pheromone signature of *repair*.

You must not smell like a weed. Bathe, in the days before arrival, in anhydrous antimicrobial solvents, but do not use perfumes, alcohol-based sanitizers, or the sterile scent of human medicine. If you can acquire or synthesize the pheromone compound of the cleaner-worms—a dry, sweet, mineral smell like hot stone after rain that never fell—apply it to your suit’s outer layer. You want to register as maintenance, not invasion.

When the blimp-beasts drift overhead on their voltage-drinking migrations, freeze. Do not intersect their tentacle-trails. They are pollinators, parasites, mobile thoughts of the city. To snag one is to give the plain a migraine. If a grazer with its silica shell and clicking mandibles approaches, let it. Its rhythm is part of the soundscape. Do not disrupt the click.

If a symbiote swarm—those hairy circuit-boards—investigates your gear, permit it. If they begin to consume dead fibers from your clothing, you have been accepted. They are eating your dead skin, your shed hair, recycling you into conductive paste. This is an honor. Stand still until they pass.

**X. Emergency Canticles: When the Music Stops**

Finally, memorize these protocols as instinct, because thought will be too slow.

**If the spire goes silent:** The cellulose lattice is losing coherence. Dead wood does not sing. Do not panic-sprint—the vibrations of your running could trigger collapse—but move with deliberate haste toward the nearest singing spire. Your sternum patch should guide you. If you cannot reach another acoustic field within minutes, press yourself against an inner xylem wall and deploy your emergency inflatable Faraday/pressure cocoon. Pray the plain does not exhale.

**If a pollen front approaches:** You will hear it before you see it—a high, choking soprano on the wind, toxic and seeking. Seal your suit immediately. Retreat into the deepest xylem chamber you can find. The front hunts viral weeds; if you smell like repair and remain sealed, it will pass over you like weather.

**If the plain exhales:** The floor will rise. Drop to a crouch, widen your stance, and place your palms flat against the fossilized velvet. Feel the shift. Do not fight it. If you are inside a spire, the balcony arches may creak. This is normal. The city is adjusting its posture.

**If you become lost in the violet dim:** Stop moving. The worst thing a water-creature can do is thrash. Crouch, listen for the tectonic love-song of the phloem-pipes, and orient yourself toward the nearest subsonic thrum. The city is always singing its maintenance. Move toward the noise. Silence is death; sound is the only compass that matters.

---

**Final Counsel**

Go hydrated, sealed, and slow. Bring no fire. Bring no brightness. Bring no demand. You are visiting a world that traded water for permanence, aqueous chaos for dry, resonant order. Walk as a small, warm noise across the polished corpse of a megafloral epoch. Let the cool press upward through your boots. Let the Aeolian chords sculpt your dreams. Be patient enough to become, for a little while, a harmless auxillary—a mobile thought in the dry dream of the Plant—and it may let you pass through its architecture of emptiness, alive, singing softly back.
