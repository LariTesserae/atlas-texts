**Field Preparation Briefing: The Ovum Hollow (Bog-Seed, Unnamed Marsh)**

---

### I. First Understand the Ontology

You are not going to a *place*. You are going to an **event**—a slow, wet explosion of digestion and intention trapped inside a finite membrane. TheOutside is only a hairline crack in peat. TheInside is a cathedral built by thumb-sized intelligences to a scale that renders you titanic and absurd. You will not explore this city so much as you will haunt it, a silent giant moving through the architecture of another species’ hunger.

Before you pack a single item, accept three truths:

1. **You are a disaster.** A human foot descending on a bridge of living cambium does not travel; it obliterates. The city is three-dimensional and delicate. You are gravity incarnate.
2. **Your senses are wrong.** Eyes are useless here. Ears are nearly useless. The inhabitants do not see or speak. They taste, they tremor. You are entering a world of absolute pitch where the only regular rhythm is the percussion of dilute sap striking wet meat from a ceiling you cannot see.
3. **The host is alive and uncomprehending.** The seed does not want you. It does not know you. It is busy trying to germinate, seal wounds, and digest its own endosperm. Its immune system speaks in phenolic amber and enzymatic chill. You are a foreign body that may, at any moment, be sealed, digested, or grown around.

Prepare yourself to become small, quiet, and chemically invisible.

---

### II. Thermal Preparation: Surviving the 0.4 Chill

The cold here is not weather. It is the temperature of **eons of wet forgetting**—a damp, enzymatic cold that does not knife through you but pools in the hollows of your collarbones, thickens your synovia, and teaches your water to move sluggishly. Water here is pregnant with dissolved chemistry; it will suck heat from your core faster than any alpine wind.

Do not bring hardshell winter gear. Do not bring anything crinkling, windproof, or crisp. The air is still, and your clothes must become part of the silence.

*Recommended:*
- A **soft, hydrophobic thermal suit** of dense, lanolized wool or modern quiet-fiber fill. It must be warm when saturated, because saturation is inevitable.
- An outer membrane of **supple, non-crinkle waterproofing**: not stiff Gore-Tex, but something like waxed canvas or a treated soft-weave polymer that sheds lymph and canal water. The enzymatic activity in the walls can degrade untreated natural fibers over time; your shells should be chemically resistant.
- **Hood integration**: Your head must be covered continuously. The cold collects around the throat and clavicles. A close-fitting balaclava and a wide, soft hood that creates a still air pocket around your skull.
- **Handwear**: Thin inner gloves of silk or synthetic liner, with soft outer mitts of felted wool or latex-dipped weave. You will be reading architecture with your fingertips in absolute dark; dexterity is survival. Bare hands will go numb in minutes and absorb whatever chemistry beads on the walls.
- **Footwear (see section IV)**.

Before entry, acclimate your body to working in cold water. Train in flooded basements or cold springs until shivering is a language you can silence.

---

### III. The Discipline of Absolute Pitch

The darkness inside the seed is not the darkness of a cave. It is the darkness of **being inside something that has never known light**. Bring no visible light. A lantern here is not illumination; it is an act of violence. It will bleach the sensitive proteins of eyeless beetles, disorient nematodes that navigate by faint thermal gradient, and mark you as a screaming anomaly. More practically, your night vision will never adapt if you cling to photons, and the weirdness (1.6) will never resolve into comprehensible shape.

*Preparation:*
- Train for **forty-eight hours minimum** in absolute pitch sensory deprivation. Learn to build a cognitive map from texture, temperature gradient, and air pressure. Practice walking with your eyes closed in crowded, cluttered spaces.
- If you absolutely require technological aid, bring **tactile-translation sonar**: a device that renders distance and density into vibrations against your wrist or jawbone, not light against your retina. Ensure it is encased in felt, its battery warmed to prevent the 0.4 cold from killing it instantly.
- Polish your **echolocation**. In the drowned plaza, click your tongue against your palate. The time it takes for sound to return from the far woody rind is long; you are inside something large. Learn to hear the wetness of a wall versus the yielding open of a canal.
- Physically mark your own body with texture-tape. You must be able to identify every piece of your gear in total dark without sound.

---

### IV. Locomotion: The Tremor Doctrine

The floor is a **resilient mat of root-pith and mycorrhizal felt**. It drinks vibration. The builders—soft, many-legged, mute—communicate by tremor. A footfall here is a paragraph shouted in a library. A stumble is an earthquake.

Moreover, the infrastructure is primitive and scaled for beings the size of your thumb. Galleries, bridges, and avenues of compressed pith will not bear your weight. The Upper Rind, the Lower Vessels, the Navel—districts carved into walls in thin, precise strata—are inaccessible to human dimensions.

You must become a creature of the **void**.

*Gear and Training:*
- **Full-body climbing harness**, minimally buckled, padded at every contact point with thick felt or moss. No dangling metal. Rig yourself to traverse the central plaza by **rind-anchors**: soft, expanding friction cams that grip the woody ceiling without piercing the living tissue. You are not permitted to wound the seed; a breach will summon a phenolic sap-flush that could entomb you in amber.
- **Suspension slings**: When you must pause, hang in the void. Do not rest on the bridges. The living cambium buttresses are still receiving sap; they pulse. Feel their rhythm and synchronize your breathing to it.
- **Silent soles**: Abandon boots. Wrap your feet in layers of felted wool, soft leather, and latex—a kind of primal tabi. Practice walking on springy, uneven biologic ground so your weight disperses through your entire sole and ankle. Roll your steps. Shift your mass continuously, never dropping your heel.
- If you absolutely must descend to the drowned plaza floor, do so by rope and lower yourself onto a mat you carry—something like a thick, dampened circus pad that distributes your mass across the mycelial felt without crushing the strata beneath.
- **Vibration buffering**: Pad every item on your body. Isolate hard containers in soft fleece. Wrap ceramic canteons in fiber. The sound floor here is 1.0; even a soft clink will propagate through meat and root like a bell.

### V. Biological and Chemical Armor

You are entering a **total vegetation** inside a digestive event. There is no stone, no metal, no sterile substrate. Every surface is food, exhalation, and growth. The atmosphere tastes initially of **copper and bruised grass**, then deepens into the exhaled breath of ten thousand metabolisms. The walls weep osmotically. Dilute sap beads and drops with a slow percussion. Canals of pale, sweet lymph thread like streets.

*Threats:*
- **Phenolic sap and latex**: When the seed is wounded or stressed, it flushes amber sealant. This substance is sculpted by the inhabitants into doors and baths, but on your skin, it will polymerize into a sticky, burning cage. Full coverage is mandatory.
- **Fungal and nematode load**: The water is not merely wet; it is **thick with life**. Nematodes thread the canals in cloud densities. Predatory mites hunt them in the aqueous streets. The fungal gardens flush and fruit on cycles you cannot predict; they will release spores into every breath you take.
- **Enzymatic contact**: The cold itself is enzymatic. Endosperm walls are dense with stored life meant to be consumed. Protective creams will break down. Reapply a barrier of petroleum jelly or dimethicone onto every exposed seam of skin—especially around eyes, nostrils, and wrists.

*Gear:*
- **Soft silicone dive mask + half-mask respirator**: Your eyes must be protected from dripping condensation and chemistry; your lungs from fungal bloom and phenolic vapor. The mask should seal without hard buckles.
- **Sealed seams**: Your suit should be one piece, or double-layered with the inner layer tucked into quiet, watertight cuffs. You do not want lymph pooling in your boots or nematodes finding the warm tunnels of your sleeves.
- **Chemical neutralizer wipes**: Carry soft, biodegradable wipes soaked in a mild solvent to remove sap from gloves before it cures. Store them in a sound-dampened pouch.
- **Nutrition / Hydration**: Bring self-contained, sealed caloric paste and distilled water. Do not drink the lymph. It is alive, sluggish, and populated. Do not eat here. Your crumbs are alien nitrogen in a zero-waste system; your garbage would be pressed into walls as mortar and might accelerate a callus-growth that disrupts the city.

---

### VI. Waste Management

The civilization has no concept of garbage. The dead are pressed into walls as mortar; excrement is reprocessed instantly. You must not violate this.

- Pack out everything. Use a **self-sealing waste bladder** designed for spaceflight or deep caving; store it inside your suit or in a soft, neutral-smelling pack.
- Relieve yourself before entry. The nitrogen in your urine is concentrated and foreign; introduced to the root-pith, it could trigger a growth surge that the builders would have to prune-extension-shift to correct. Do not burden them with your biology.

---

### VII. Fauna Protocol: Becoming a Stone in the Stream

The fauna is overwhelming. Not hostile—*saturating*. On every bridge, in every channel, coating the floors in living tesserae. Eyeless beetles moving softened pulp. Mites grazing the mycelial pastures. The builders themselves, soft and many-legged, parting around obstacles like water around a stone. They are mute. They do not sing or call. The weight of their biomass is a physical pressure on your psyche.

*Preparation:*
- **Hygiene**: Shower in unscented, pH-neutral water before arrival. No deodorant, cologne, or residual detergent. The builders communicate by taste and touch; a single molecule of soap is a blasphemy.
- **Outer scent-dampening**: Some travelers coat their outerwear in a thin layer of local peat-mud before descent, so they smell of the fissure itself. This is recommended.
- **Stasis discipline**: When stationary, let them flow over you. Nematodes will thread your ankles. Mites may taste your boots. The builders may walk across your hanging sling. Do not brush them away. Do not flinch. Practice meditation while insects crawl on your skin until you can dissociate from the mammalian panic reflex.
- **Avoid the canals**: The water is filled with microscopic predators and chemistry. If your suit breaches, the enzymatic cold will enter, and so will the worms.

---

### VIII. Navigating a City of Meat and Water

There are no landmarks visible to human perception. The walls are pale endosperm; the ceiling is woody rind sweating condensation; the floor is root-pith. Everything is yielding, curved, organic. The city exists in three dimensions—avenues following cellulose grain, a second city hanging from cambium struts, a third sinking root-taps into the flooded substratum.

*Preparation:*
- **Tactile landmarking**: Learn to read the grain of cellulose with your fingertips. The Upper Rind is drier and harder; the Lower Vessels are soft, aqueous, and gurgling; the Navel is warm with fermentation. You must map by texture and temperature, not sight.
- **The exit thread**: This is non-negotiable. Pay out a **line of carbon thread or braided silk** from the moment you pass the fissure. Anchor it at the surface. The exit is a knuckle-wide seam in peat, invisible from below and easily sealed by a shift of the marsh skin. Without this line, you will never find your way back through the maze of vascular-bundle streets and the absolute dark.
- **Silent timekeeping**: There are no light cycles. Biological rhythms control the city—sap drops, fungal flushes, the slow tide of groundwater pressing through the peat. Bring a **vibration-silent wrist device** or learn to count your own heartbeat in the dark, syncing your movements to the long, abdominal gurgle of the canals.

---

### IX. Psychological Containment (Weirdness 1.6)

The weirdness here is subtle but corrosive. It is the blurring of boundary between city and organism, between tool and flesh, between waste and wall. You will stand in the drowned plaza and feel the heat of ten thousand metabolisms exhaling against your suit. You will realize the *void* is intentional—the emptiness is architectural, a planned breath between strata of habitation. You will see a shoot questing from the seed’s heart and understand that it is not an obstacle; it is a thoroughfare being negotiated. You will see the dead pressed into walls as mortar and understand that to them, this is not horror but gift.

Train for this. Spend days in absolute darkness in a damp, enclosed space. Practice surrendering your human expectation of horizon and sky. The seed is alive, growing, adjusting. The weirdness will whisper that you, too, are just temporary chemistry waiting to be used.

Do not let it.

---

### X. Emergency Doctrine

- **If entombed in sap**: Do not struggle. Reach for your solvent wipes. If the flush is large, you may need to cut yourself free—but remember, cutting the seed invites more sap. Use a **soft ceramic blade** only, and only as a last resort. Anchor yourself immediately to your suspension line so you do not drop into the drowned plaza.
- **If separated from your line**: Stop moving. The plaza is thirty strides across, but the city beyond is a fractal of galleries. Attempting to search blindly will collapse galleries and strand you deeper. Click your tongue and listen for the largest void—that is the plaza. Move toward it by texture-memory. Then ascend toward the rind, where the air is drier, and feel for the faint updraft of the fissure.
- **If the seed begins rapid germination**: Sometimes the radicle thrusts with surprising force. The city does not destroy it; they prune and train. You must move. If the shoot is becoming a thoroughfare beneath you, climb. Unhook, ascend, find a new anchor in the woody vault until the growth shift ends.

---

### XI. Departure

When you leave, tread softly up the thread. Brush the peat seam closed behind you with the sole of your quiet boot. The marsh above must look unchanged: low, wet, and empty. 

You were a ghost in a digestive miracle. Let it keep breathing its air of copper and bruised grass without you.

Pack out your waste. Wash the scent of endosperm from your suit. And when you sleep the next night in a world that knows light, you will still feel the sap-drop percussion in your sternum, and the memory of ten thousand silent things parting around you in the total dark.
