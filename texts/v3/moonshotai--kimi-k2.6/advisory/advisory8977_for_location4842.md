**Preparation Guide for the Infant Spine**  
*For the ordinary human multiverse traveler entering a 2.6-reality zone with active lithogenesis*

---

### **I. Pulmonary Baptism**

Do not arrive proud of your fitness. The air at the railhead is already an insult, and every node beyond is higher. Begin altitude conditioning three months before transit: sleep in hypoxic tents, run staircases wearing breath restriction masks, learn to measure your exertion in *sips* rather than gasps. The atmosphere here is thin enough to make your lungs work for every breath, but it is also *chemical*—laden with ozone, hot iron particulate, and mineral outgassing from stone that has not finished deciding what elements it contains. 

Carry a portable oxygen concentrator rated for at least six hours of continuous low-flow supplementation. Not because you will use it constantly, but because the distances between complexes are measured in vertical leagues, and a tramway missed by minutes can mean hours of waiting on a stabilization platform no wider than a dining table. If your blood oxygen drops below 85%, the reality gradient will notice. Dizziness in the Spine does not mean altitude sickness; it means the world is considering whether your inner ear ought to obey the same laws as your boots. A well-oxygenated brain is your first and most important anchor.

Practice breathing slowly. The local rhythm is geological. If you pant, you will hyperventilate, and hyperventilation here can ripple outward into the fog, attracting things that navigate by the disturbance of warm, frightened air.

---

### **II. The Philosophy of Footwear and Contact**

There are no streets. There are fracture planes, knife-edged talus, scree slopes that have never felt the patience of water, and staircases cut into flanks of basalt that remembers being magma yesterday. Your boots must be *armored*—full steel shank, puncture-resistant sole rated against fresh extrusions, ankle support rigid enough to prevent rolling on stone that shifts with tectonic indigestion. Gaiters are non-negotiable; the talus will seek entry at every cuff. Bring spare laces made of aramid fiber; ordinary cordage frays here as if the fibers are reconsidering their braided allegiance.

Carry a walking staff, but not the telescoping kind. Collapsible geometry is unwise in a place where platforms can be longer on the inside than the outside. Use a solid rod of dense hardwood or machined steel, something with a single, fixed length that you can trust implicitly. Test it at the railhead: mark a span with tape, measure it thrice. If the marks disagree, do not ascend until they reconcile. Your staff is not for defense; it is a one-dimensional proof against spatial uncertainty.

Gloves. The stone is damp, yes, but it is also *young*—still half-capillary, still learning to be solid. It weeps not only water but mineral solutions that have not committed to their chemistry. Skin contact with raw extrusions can leave chemical burns that mutate subtly over days, rashes that drift across the body like weather systems. Heavy leather gloves, gauntlet-length, are mandatory when gripping tramway handrails or resting palms on rock faces during climbs.

### **III. Raiment for the Permanent Almost-Rain**

The climate is damp but cool, and the moisture behaves like a confused guest. It condenses from cloudless sky. It drips upward to feed hanging gardens. It collects in pre-formation stone and seeps out against gravity. You will never be fully dry. Accept this early.

Dress in layers of non-cotton, non-down insulation: synthetics or treated wool that retain warmth when saturated. Bring three times as many socks as you think you need. Pack your sleep clothes in vacuum-sealed bags, and do not open them until you are inside a habitation block. Foot rot and trench-grade immersion injuries are the most common medical evacuations.

Your outer shell should be robust against industrial grime—soot from the foundries, slag dust, mineral smoke—but do not trust any waterproofing absolutely. The rain here moves sideways. It falls in silver ropes that stop mid-air and reconsider. Bring a wide-brimmed hat, not for sun (there is none to speak of) but to keep upward-migrating drizzle from entering your collar while you wait on tramway platforms.

A note on color: Do not wear white or reflective materials. The queasy violet aurora of the reality-dampeners will make such fabrics glow with an unsettling fluorescence, and the null-bears are drawn not to flesh but to visual anomalies in their peripheral causality. Dress in forge-red, soot-black, industrial ochre. Become part of the foundry spectrum.

### **IV. Nutrition and Hydration in a Pre-Geological Context**

Do not drink the local water without processing. It weeps from stone that is still choosing its mineral content, and springs run upward because gravity is weighing its options. The water may be chemically H₂O, or it may be *rehearsing* H₂O, carrying suspended probabilities that play havoc with metabolism. Carry a ceramic gravity-fed filter rated for heavy metals, and add stabilization tablets—ideally those synthesized in a 3.0-reality zone where covalent bonds are fully committed to their marriages.

Bring all food from outside, or purchase it from the foundry commissaries, where it has been processed in the industrial certainty extractors. Do not forage. The lichens grow in dimensions they have not agreed upon, and the ferns photosynthesize violet flicker into sugars that may be carbohydrates in superposition. The hanging gardens are lush, yes, but their fruit might be a protein today and a crystalline memory of protein tomorrow. A ration pack from a mature dimension is worth more than gold here.

Eat slowly. Chew deliberately. The local tempo is glacial, and your digestive system should emulate the stabilization pistons: patient, rhythmic, unhurried.

### **V. Navigation and Personal Anchors**

Maps are suggestions. Compasses may point toward futures. GPS is useless in a zone where signal propagation is negotiable. Learn the tramway schedule by heart; the aerial lines are the most reliable geography, because they are tensioned by human intent against the rising crust.

Carry a *geometry kit* as vital as any medicine. You need: a thirty-meter hemp line (natural fiber, consistent molecular history), a plumb bob of dense lead, a folding carpenter’s rule, and a waterproof notebook. Before entering any new platform, stairwell, or pipe-bridge, measure. If the staircase has two hundred steps from the outside and two hundred and forty from the inside, back away. Wait one hour. Measure again. The mountain is not trying to deceive you; it simply has not finished rendering.

Your most important possession is a *certainty object*: a small, perfect sphere of machined tool steel, or a cube of monocrystalline silicon, or a simple iron nail forged in a universe where metallurgy is settled law. Carry it in a pocket over your heart. Touch it when you see yourself looking up from a chasm, or when rain falls upward, or when a null-bear’s pawprints age backward in the dust. It is not magical; it is *inevitable*. In a world mining the certainty of iron, an object that already knows what it is becomes a lighthouse.

Also carry a timepiece with a visible mechanical beat—a wind-up watch, a metronome, a string of beads you count in prime numbers. The local noise is a sustained chord that lives in your sternum, and silence would feel like a wound. Create your own micro-rhythm to keep your biology from syncing too completely with the subsonic mountain growl. If your heartbeat begins to match the thud of the compression hammers, stop, cover your ears, and recite something fixed: a childhood address, a multiplication table, the precise atomic weight of stable carbon.

### **VI. Ocular and Auditory Armoring**

The light is dim—a tarnished pewter that never casts a sharp shadow—but it is also *aggressive* in spectrum. The queasy violet of the reality-dampeners will make your eyes water and, over weeks, induce migraines that feel like causality itself is pressing against your optic nerves. Bring lenses specifically rated to filter 380–450 nm violet light. Photochromic glasses are advisable; they will darken in response to the arc-welder white of the ore-crushers and the forge-red hemorrhage from smokestacks. Do not stare at the dampener arrays. The photons there have not fully committed to their frequency, and prolonged exposure can leave afterimages that persist on the retina for days, ghosting your vision with possible colors.

As for sound: earplugs are a temptation and a mistake. The subsonic growl of the mountains accreting will bypass your ears entirely, settling in your chest cavity. Standard hearing protection will only isolate you from the audible warnings of tramway cables, the steam vents, and the electric crackle of insect swarms hitting containment fences. Instead, carry filtered earplugs designed for industrial zones—those that attenuate harmful decibels while preserving directional awareness. Learn to communicate in the local dialect of gesture and shouted keywords. Conversation is already a struggle here; do not make it impossible by sealing yourself in synthetic silence.

### **VII. Fauna Protocols: Living Adjacent**

The wildlife is abundant because the carrying capacity is negotiable. This does not mean the wildlife is safe; it means the wildlife is *inconsistent*.

**The Null-Bears:** Large, many-jointed, occupying two points at once. They are not predators in the traditional sense. They are drawn to warmth and weak rules, which means foundry exhaust vents and, unfortunately, your body heat if you sleep too near a reality-breach. If one slips into your peripheral vision with the sound of wet canvas tearing, do not turn to face it directly. Look at it as you would look at a bright light—indirectly, through the corner of your awareness. They are not dangerous so much as adjacent, but adjacency to a creature that steps between shadows cast by unlit lights can leave a traveler spatially dislocated. Mark your sleeping area with a ring of iron filings or salt from a settled world; the null-bears avoid zones of high mineral certainty.

**The Insects:** They swarm in geometries—hexagonal clouds, cubic droning masses. They are drawn to the electric hum of the fences and die in crackling showers. If you encounter a swarm away from the containment grids, move through it quickly but smoothly. Swatting disrupts their geometries and can cause the swarm to collapse into a localized probability storm, leaving you with stings that exist in a superposition of pain and itch until you leave the zone.

**The Grazers:** Glass-eyed, walking up vertical cliff faces. They are generally indifferent to humans, but do not position yourself directly beneath them. Gravity is still weighing its options in the Spine, and a grazer dislodged from a vertical slope may not fall in the direction you anticipate.

### **VIII. Industrial Etiquette and the Slow Rules**

This is not a city you explore. It is a city you *submit to* at the pace of crustal formation. The complexes are separated by miles of vertical emptiness, and the tramways run on schedules measured in geological patience. Do not rush. A missed tram is not a twenty-minute wait; it may be a six-hour interlude in the damp and dark.

The foundries do not mine iron. They mine the *certainty* of iron. If you are permitted inside a stabilization plant or extraction cathedral, do not touch the raw ore. Do not touch the conveyor belts. The material is in superposition, and human contact can collapse its wavefunction prematurely, contaminating a batch worth more than a year’s labor. Follow the foundry workers’ gestures without argument. They move at the tempo of compression hammers, and their impatience, if provoked, is the only thing in the Spine that moves quickly.

The fences around exterior platforms are not for humans. They are reality containment grids. Do not climb them. Do not lean on them. The humming is a lullaby holding the world together; touching the mesh can ground you into the local probability field in ways that leave your fingerprints temporarily non-Euclidean.

If you need assistance, ask early. A repair to gear might take months, not because the smiths are slow, but because the metal must be taught what shape to hold, and metal here is an unruly student.

### **IX. Shelter and Bivouac Discipline**

Between the nodes, there is only wind, fog, and the groaning of the crust. Do not plan to camp between complexes. The distances are too vast, the terrain too raw, and the reality gradient too steep for unsupported overnighting. Coordinate your movements so you arrive at a habitation block, a foundry dormitory, or a stabilization tower by the local twilight—which is to say, by the time your watch tells you it is time to stop, since the sky is always the color of a bruise.

If you must bivouac due to tramway failure or misadventure, choose a platform adjacent to a foundry—not for the warmth, but for the *certainty*. The red glow bleeding through smokestacks stabilizes local space. Pitch your shelter with the understanding that poles and fabric may experience dimensional drift; secure guy-lines with heavy iron stakes, and check the interior volume of your tent every hour. A bivouac sack is safer than a tent here; less geometry to go wrong.

Sleep with your certainty object in your hand. Dreaming in the Spine can be porous; travelers report dreams that leak into the fog, or that continue in the minds of null-bears passing nearby. Maintain a bedtime ritual of reciting fixed facts to remind your subconscious where its loyalties lie.

### **X. Psychological Inoculation**

The true danger of the Infant Spine is not the height, the damp, or the fauna. It is the slow erosion of your conviction that reality is finished.

You will see rain fall upward. You will look down chasms and see yourself looking up. You will walk platforms that are longer inside than outside. You will hear mating calls that seem to originate inside your own skull. The light is always twilight, always the hour before a storm that never arrives, and after weeks, you may begin to feel that your own shadow is provisional.

Before arrival, memorize a long text—something dry, factual, and personally meaningless: a tax code, a technical manual, the lineage of a royal house. Recite it daily. When the world frays, speak it aloud. The words do not need to be magical; they need to be *certain*. In a place refining inevitability from raw matter, the ritual of fixed speech is a spell of identity.

Prepare for isolation. The complexes are islands. You will spend hours alone in transit, suspended in fog over leagues of empty air, surrounded by noise but utterly solitary. Bring a journal, but write in it obsessively. Document the tramway schedules, the number of steps, the color of the moss. The act of recording imposes narrative stability on a world that has not yet settled its plot.

Finally, abandon any schedule measured in days. The Spine teaches the slow rules. A new spur of mountain emerges over a generation. A tramway is extended across years. If you arrive demanding efficiency, the mountain will educate you in patience, one breathless, damp, violet-lit moment at a time.

- [ ] Memorized text of absolute, dry certainty  

Stand at the railhead. The mist will move off, eventually, revealing a peak sharper and younger than the last. Let the red glow of a distant forge exhale against your face. Feel the subsonic growl in your sternum. Adjust your pack. Check your certainty object. Step forward.

The mountain is still being born. Walk as if you are willing to be born alongside it.
