If you are going to the Attanni Ridges, you must first accept that your body is an argument the planet will win. The water is not your element; it is warm, viscous with dissolved minerals, and it presses against you not with malice but with the same patient indifference it applies to the ridges themselves. You are not diving. You are being swallowed, slowly. Prepare for that.

**1. The Suit: Becoming Compatible with Weight**
Do not bring metal. The Voort have no concept of it, and in an ocean this thick with precipitating minerals, ferrous alloys will foul, corrode, and mark you as something that does not belong. Your pressure garment must be a soft isobaric membrane—gel-layered, ceramic-weave, or living polymer—something that equalizes the crushing twilight-zone depth without rigid joints. Rigid suits will not fit through the blood-frond forests, nor will they allow you to turn sideways on the Voort’s ropeways. You need a second skin, not a vehicle.

Seal every seam twice. The warm water is not content to simply surround you; it wants to seep, to occupy. Your rebreather must be a closed-circuit unit housed in fired ceramic and basalt composite. Bubbles are not merely rude here; in an acoustic dead zone, the sudden exhalation of gas is a scream in a cathedral—an obscenity. The Voort communicate in silence. Your apparatus should exhale nothing.

Weight yourself with obsidian or dense basalt. Lead is a metal, and worse, it is inert and silent; the Voort understand stone that sings. Your ballast should be of the ridge itself, purchased or knapped before descent, so that your buoyancy is mediated by the same geology that mediates theirs.

**2. Sensory Retraining: Learning the Dark and the Quiet**
Before you depart, spend a week in an anechoic chamber. Not a few hours—a week. Sleep there. Eat there. Learn what your mind does when every echo dies. The Attanni Ridges exist inside an acoustic grave. The blood-fronds absorb vibration. The porous ceramic of the Voort domes drinks sound. Even the vents exhale in strangulated silence. Your voice will die in your throat. Your sonar, if you carry it, will be stillborn. The silence is not empty; it is full of the subsonic song of cooling stone, a grinding lullaby felt in the sternum rather than heard. It will resonate through your chest cavity for every hour of your stay. Wear a sternum damper or resonance gel-plate if you are prone to nausea, or better yet, train until you can sleep inside that grinding, patient note. It is the sound of a world deciding what shape to take.

Your eyes are next to useless without retraining. The sun is a pale blemish, and the abyss begins just below the ridge crests. Bring no white light. White light is vulgar here; it will bleach the amber bacterial gardens and mark you as unmannersome. Instead, culture bioluminescent symbionts in ceramic vials weeks before your journey—low amber, ember-red, the colors of the Voort’s own trenches. Strap them to your wrists and knees so you become a source of dim phosphorescence rather than a consumer of darkness. If you must use technological light, restrict yourself to narrow-beam amber, and only when the blood-fronds fail to outline your path. Let your eyes learn to trust the faint glow of the Voort themselves as they pass, and the vascular light of the fronds. You are visiting a landscape of embers. Do not pretend it is noon.

**3. The Body in the Forest: Moving Through Urgency**
The vegetation is not scenic. It is urgent, crowded, and slimy. The blood-fronds and chemosynthetic towers grow so densely that you will move through the outer forests sideways, pressing through rubbery stalks that exude a warm, glutinous mucus. Do not wear anything that snags. Coat your membrane suit in a hydrophobic silicone slick or a silicate-based lubricant. The mucus is harmless, but it is tenacious, and if you struggle against it, you will exhaust yourself long before you reach the city. Move like you are being digested: slow, compliant, without resistance.

Every surface that is not alive is newborn and murderously sharp. The obsidian spires have not been blunted by time; they are blades of glass thrust out by the ridge’s convulsion. Wrap your joints, your knuckles, your knees. Your gloves should be woven fiber, lined with ceramic scales. Your boots must be narrow, with felted fiber soles—heat-resistant, because the stone beneath is often still cooling through the Threshold of Plasticity. When the Voort treat a street as alive, they mean it is still hot enough to burn, still singing its subsonic song of crystallization. Test every handhold with the back of your wrist before gripping. The basalt is infantile and has not yet learned to be kind.

**4. Communication: Silence as Language**
You must practice being mute. Not quiet—mute. In the markets, barter happens in total silence. The Voort speak by conducting vibrations through the jawbone and the transparent cartilage of their faces. Speech is touch. For them, to speak to another is to press close and let the body carry the word. For you, an accidental brush in a narrow walkway is equivalent to shouting in someone’s ear. Train yourself to a sphere of absolute intentional touch. Do not flail. Do not gesture widely. If you must signal to a companion, use hand-signs close to the chest, or tether yourselves with a fiber line and tug in prearranged patterns.

To communicate with the Voort, you will need a resonance slate: a thin, fired ceramic plate that you can press gently against their cartilaginous face or translucent mantle. The slate should translate your finger-taps into low vibrational patterns. It will not convey nuance. Nuance requires time, and you do not have ten thousand years of slow thought. Keep your messages simple: gratitude, query, apology. If you bring a gift—and you must—let it be something shaped by heat and patience. A hand-knapped obsidian blade, a coil of fiber rope spun under tension, or a small ceramic vessel fired in your own kiln. They will understand the labor, if not the form.

**5. The City: Verticality and Thermal Etiquette**
The Voort city is a lattice of domes barely large enough for a family cluster, lashed across basalt chasms. There is no flat ground. You will traverse walkways of woven cable lined with porous clay tiles that drink the water’s mineral wealth. These walkways are narrow—barely a body’s width—and they sway. Train your proprioception on slacklines before you come. If you are prone to vertigo, medicate for it, because the surrounding seafloor falls away into vertiginous darkness on every side.

Do not disturb the ceramic heat-channels. The Voort have laid these to guide vent-heat, encouraging the bedrock to cool into vaults and arches. To block a channel is to interrupt a conversation between the city and the mountain. Do not stomp on streets that are still warm. The Threshold of Plasticity is a sacred boundary; above it, the stone is still in the process of becoming architecture. Treat it as you would treat a nursery.

**6. Biological Provisions: What to Eat and What to Avoid**
The Voort are farmers of heat and gradient. They do not hunt; there is nothing to hunt. Their protein comes from fungal films grown in warm, wet caves gouged into the cooling flank. Do not expect to eat their food without preparation. Chemosynthetic proteins, sulfurous metabolites, and alien fungal cell walls may pass through a Voort colony’s shared stomach with ease, but your human gut will revolt. Bring broad-spectrum enzymatic primers and a portable protein synthesizer if you wish to share meals. Better yet, carry your own rations and do not pretend to a biochemistry you do not have.

If you must sample the local ecosystem, stick to the bacterial lace. It is simple, mineral-rich, and unlikely to colonize your intestines. Avoid the blood-fronds; their mucus is defensive, and some carry anaerobic parasites tangled in the root-mats. Seal your cuffs. The thumb-sized parasites are harmless to the Voort, but they will burrow under human skin in search of warmth.

**7. Faunal Awareness: The Shapes Above and Below**
Animals are rare, but the mobile fauna that does exist is unsettling. Down here, you may encounter clusters of eyeless shrimp ranching bacterial fur on their backs. Do not disturb them. They are livestock analogues, and the Voort value their symbiotic tidiness.

Above the ridge, in the higher currents, vast shapes drift—things with too many joints and mouths like torn sails. They are filter-feeders, trailing through the vegetable snow that falls from the forests. They are not predatory, but they are indifferent, and a collision with one in the open water would be fatal. Do not swim in the open heights. Stay near the structures. Let the ridges hide you.

**8. Psychological Inoculation: The Weight of Embryonic Time**
This is perhaps the most important preparation. The Attanni Ridges should not exist yet. They are embryonic. The stone is still arguing with the mantle about whether it may stay. The Voort build with geologic patience, and they measure a district’s completion not in years but in cooling epochs. Your entire lifespan is a hiccup to them. If you arrive with hurry, with the nervous metabolism of a surface creature, you will suffer. The warm, thick water will feel like stagnation. The absolute silence will feel like isolation. The darkness will feel like burial.

You must learn to move at the speed of cooling basalt. Meditate on impermanence and permanence until they feel the same. The water presses against every surface with the indifferent weight of a planet—not to crush you, but to remind you that you are small, temporary, and soft. Let it. Surrender your urgency at the ridge crest. The Voort will not meet your gaze, because they have no eyes in the human sense, but they will feel your vibrations. Make sure those vibrations speak of patience.

If you can accept that you are a brief, warm intrusion into a place that is still being born, the Attanni Ridges will accept you in return. Turn sideways. Press your palm to the stone and feel it sing. Move in amber light and absolute silence. Be heavy with obsidian, soft with fiber, and slow, slow, slow.
