You do not prepare for this place the way you prepare for a voyage. You prepare for it the way you prepare to be digested: slowly, humbly, and with the full awareness that the host organism may not distinguish you from any other raw material.

This plateau has only recently decided to exist. It is still learning its own outline, still coagulating from possibility into basalt and frost. An ordinary human does not visit a landscape like this; an ordinary human is corrected by it. If you must go, you must first accept that everything your species has ever learned about planets, cities, and biology is a local custom that does not apply here.

**1. Abandon Light Entirely**

Do not pack torches. Do not install luminescent toggles on your suit. Do not bring photonic rangefinders, headlamps, or bioluminescent markers adapted from your home biosphere. Light is not merely useless here; it is a rude foreign mineral, and the darkness is a saturated, intentional presence that will swallow your beam before it leaves your hand. Worse, the Gastrah and the city’s embedded machinery parse the electromagnetic spectrum as damage. A flashlight is a causality-wound, a screaming intrusion into a medium that has spent three million years learning to see with sound.

You must undergo **total visual deprivation therapy** for a minimum of ninety standard days before arrival. Not a digital detox. Not a weekend in a cave. Ninety days in absolute aphotic isolation, retraining your occipital lobe to cease its hungry searching. Your visual cortex must be colonized by somatosensory and auditory processing, or it will panic within hours of arrival, hallucinating geometries that do not exist, fleeing from Gastrah shadows that your eyes invent while your ears know better. Some travelers opt for temporary surgical rerouting, patching visual pathways to subvocal resonance receptors. Others simply blindfold themselves and learn to trust the pressure of air against their face.

If you cannot live without seeing, you cannot live here. The rare cerulean sparks from the city’s machinery and the faint ghosts of drifting larvae are not illumination; they are geological accidents, brief as neurons misfiring. They will teach you nothing. You will navigate this world with your chest, your sternum, your open palms, and your teeth.

**2. Remake Yourself as an Instrument**

The atmosphere here is sound. Not *has* sound. *Is* sound. A constant deafening drone between twelve and forty hertz, seismic, viscous, and physical enough to pool in your lower lungs like a second atmosphere. Standard human hearing is a liability: it bottoms out around twenty hertz and is easily shredded by the percussive grinding of Gastrah mandibles against fresh towers. Yet you cannot deafen yourself. To block the noise is to be blinded twice over.

You need a **dual-circuit auditory apparatus**. One path: surgical implants—subcutaneous piezo-resonators anchored to your sternum, clavicles, and mastoid bones—to receive infrasonic tides as spatial pressure. You do not hear the elders approach; you wear them. A herd migration begins as a slow compression against your thoracic cavity, a sensation like a very deep hand pressing the air from your lungs over the course of hours. You must learn to read that pressure as topography, to feel open corridors as acoustic thinning and dead ends as bass-heavy pools.

The other path: active, adaptive filtration. You need cochlear dampeners that shield your inner ear from the barotrauma of standing waves while permitting the precise navigation-band frequencies to pass through. Ordinary noise-canceling technology is a death sentence here. If you silence the city, you are a man buried alive in stone who refuses to touch the walls.

Practice by submerging yourself in anechoic chambers flooded with recorded infrasonic chords. Learn to identify objects by the way they return a thirty-hertz hum. Learn to stand in a forty-hertz field until your vision blurs—not from light, but from the physical vibration of your own eyeballs. This is the price of sight.

**3. Learn the Plateau’s True Name**

There is no spoken word for this place. The locals—if the Gastrah are locals and not merely a slower expression of the plateau’s digestion—identify their home by emitting a thirty-seven-hertz harmonic. It is not a word. It is a resonance that declares: *this ground, this pressure, this endless night.*

You must learn to subvocalize it. Thirty-seven hertz sits at the border of audition and touch, more a shudder in the throat than a note. Carry a thoracic resonator as backup, but the plateau recognizes organic emission. When you arrive, you will broadcast this tone constantly into your own chest cavity, using it as a compass. It will return to you differently depending on whether you face the frozen nitrogen ocean, a dense warren of sleeping elders, or the raw glass zone where the city is still precipitating. Without this harmonic, you are not a visitor; you are an error in the acoustic architecture, and the architecture will correct you.

Do not confuse this with Gastrah communication. Their bands overlap, but their infrasonic language is communal, territorial, and digestive. You are not fluent. You never will be. At best, you are a reverberation that they do not feel compelled to scrape from their shells.

**4. Wear Something That Can Weep Back**

The plateau is incomprehensibly wet. Supercooled saline, black mercury-heavy water, liquid xenon, metallic salts, amorphous water kept fluid by impurities and the low-grade fever of the city’s growth—all of it rains upward, sideways, and through. The moisture is metabolic. It is the perspiration of a geology learning to circulate. It will find every seam, gasket, and microscopic flaw in your protection, because the city is learning to excrete, and you are novel matter.

Your environmental suit—call it a **Weeping Husk**—must satisfy paradoxical demands:

- **Cryogenic integrity**: The ambient cold is patient and near-absolute. Your suit must maintain your core in a controlled hypothermic trance, slowing metabolic demand without freezing your aqueous humor into slush. Think deep-sea isotherm, not parka. You want to move through the environment at a thermal whisper.
- **Amphiphobic exterior**: The mirror-slick condensation freezes in fractal filigree moments after touching a surface. Your suit must shed these films before they laminate you into a shell of frozen acoustics. Once encased in black frost, you are an inert obstacle, and the Gastrah do not swerve.
- **Tactile permeability**: Paradoxically, your hands and feet must remain sensitive. You will read the city’s corridors by dragging gauntleted fingers across the walls, feeling the harmonic signature of raw felsic glass versus braided tungsten-carbon. Your boots should transmit the subsonic ring of the substrate: geology rings differently than architecture, though here the distinction is nearly theological.

Install a pressure-distribution exoskeleton beneath the Husk. Not for strength—though that helps—but because in the denser districts, crowd density is tectonic. You will, at some point, need to wedge yourself between two somnolent Gastrah while a migration occupies the only horizontal passage. Their armored bodies will lean against you with the slow, indifferent weight of continental drift. Your ribcage must survive it without snapping. Your suit should convert that crushing pressure into distributed load, as if you were a temporary strut in the city’s endless scaffolding.

**5. Adjust Your Tempo to Geology**

A door dilates over a season. A tower’s collapse takes four centuries, supported by the viscosity of the metallic air. If you maintain ordinary human urgency, you will die of impatience, which is to say you will die of physics.

Before departure, enter **chronometric dilation conditioning**. Spend months in a facility where every action is stretched by an order of magnitude. Learn to observe a single object for hours without moving. Learn to understand that the three-day scraping of an elder’s shell against a tower is one continuous event, not a background annoyance to wait out. The Gastrah move at a rate measured in melted and refrozen frost strata. You cannot outrun them. You cannot even outwalk them. You must learn to occupy space at their speed, to become a feature of the landscape that the city’s assemblers do not feel compelled to grow over.

Sudden motion is read as threat or prey. A sprinting human is a shrieking anomaly in a medium that communicates in glacial tides. The Gastrah may not chase you—they are not predators in any earthly sense—but they will not stop. The young in their gelatinous swarms may simply plaster themselves against your faceplate, filter-feeding on the metallic mist you disturb, clogging your respirator with bioluminescent mucus until you suffocate in a slow, drifting blizzard.

Move as if you are liquid xenon creeping through basalt. Move as if your bones are precipitating.

**6. Do Not Eat, Drink, or Breathe the Continent**

The atmosphere is a metallic soup: off-gassed nitrogen from the frozen ocean below, xenon condensate, cryogenic vapor vented from spiracles the width of storm-drains, and the particulate shroud that has eaten every star. There is no oxygen cycle. There is no pollen, no decaying leaf matter, no microbial loft. Seal yourself in a **closed-loop rebreather**. Scrub your carbon dioxide and reconstitute your own atmosphere. The air here is not hostile; it is simply indifferent to the demands of hemoglobin.

Do not drink the black water. It is metabolic, alien, heavy with dissolved alloys and exotic phase-impurities that keep it stubbornly fluid at temperatures that should render it stone. It will pass through your kidneys like a solvent. It is the plateau’s sweat, not nourishment. Some travelers have mistaken the weeping channels for aquifers. They became precipitates, mineral stains on the lower avenues, their own water content drawn out by osmotic desperation.

As for food, there is nothing to forage. The sinter-mats—those manganese-purple and iron-rust films—draw energy from piezoelectric stress and radioactive decay. They are the only pasture, and the Gastrah graze them with silica mandibles that could lap up a human femur as incidental roughage. Bring molecularly synthesized rations or nutrient slurry. If you must consume local matter, you will need industrial-scale isotope filtration and a stomach lined with tungsten. You have neither.

**7. Navigate by Sin and Stone**

The city is not built; it precipitates. Molecular assemblers extrude hexagonal rod-warrens and filament-towers directly from the mantle. This means there are no maps, only paleontology. Engineers here read the strata of emergence as it happens. You must learn to do the same.

Bridges layer upon bridges until they become tunnels. Towers nearly touch across abysses, and the darkness between them is absolute. You will not know if you stand on geology or architecture until you strike it and listen to the ring. Carry a **tunable resonator rod**. A sharp tap against raw basaltic froth returns one harmonic; a tap against extruded tungsten-carbon returns another. Learn the difference, or you will commit the cardinal sin of trying to climb a tower that is still deciding its geometry, its balconies budding and retracting like coral in a time-lapse you cannot perceive.

Remember: **water flows upward** here. It seeks the quantum-heat of decaying computational cores in the heights. Look up for drowning. The channels are not streets; they are veins. Walk the higher scaffolds where the condensate has not yet pooled into crawling, blood-slow rivers.

**8. Anchor Yourself in Causality**

The city’s machinery ruptures local causality to mine light from nothing. Near the brighter blue sparks, time may fork, probability may soften. Carry a **chronometric anchor**—a personal stabilizer that locks your local entropic flow. Without it, you may become a bifurcation in the bedrock, a traveler who both entered and did not enter a tower, a smear of superposition slowly resolved by the plateau’s patient, absolute zero cold. The dark here is thick enough to hold paradox. Do not let it hold yours.

**9. Understand What the Density Will Do to Your Mind**

This is not a wilderness. It is an empire of presence. Every cubic meter is occupied by a sleeping elder, a swarm of fist-sized larvae, a self-extending conduit, or a reservoir of weeping brine. There are no parks, no empty lots, no windows meant for looking out. The scale is planetary, yet the suffocation is intimate. You will stand on a rim overlooking a district the size of a nation, and you will feel the crowd pressing against you as if the entire world were a rush-hour train that never breaks formation.

This causes a specific dissociation. Your primate brain evolved seeking savannas, horizons, the comfort of chlorophyll and empty sky. Here there is no green. There is no patience of photosynthesis, only the mineral appetite of silicate and slag. The color purple will become sinister. The texture of chitin will replace bark in your dreams. You must meditate for hours each day upon the image of a world without plants, without sunlight, without the mercy of an empty horizon. If you do not condition yourself for this, your mind will seek escape where there is none, and you will claw off your own suit in the absolute dark to find a sky that does not exist.

**10. Arrival: What the First Hours Will Demand of You**

When you first set foot—or anchor—on the plateau, the experience will not be visual. It will be a full-body assault of pressure and silence.

The sound will not seem loud. It will seem *deep*, a density in the air itself. Your chest cavity will resonate with the thirty-seven-hertz name of the place, and for a moment you will feel as though you are falling upward into the earth. Your inner ear, accustomed to gravity and human-scale acoustics, will ferment with vertigo. You will vomit into your rebreather. The condensate will begin to coat your suit, and you will watch—no, you will *feel*—the mirror-slick of alien water freezing into fractal filigree across your shoulders, sheathing you in the city’s own skin.

You will see, perhaps, the faint cerulean ghosts of machinery sparks in the far glass, or the drifting lanterns of Gastrah larvae passing through your arms like snow made of jellyfish. You will try to use your eyes, and your eyes will report nothing but the afterimage of your own panic. Then you will strike your resonator rod against the ground, and the returning wave will paint your first picture: a canyon of half-formed towers, a bridge draped in somnolent, pill-shaped beasts, and above it all the velvet occlusion of a sky that has never known morning.

You are not welcomed. You are not rejected. You are merely permitted, for now, to circulate through the body of something unfinished.

Move slowly. Hum the thirty-seven hertz into your own bones. Do not touch the sinter-mats. Do not stand beneath an elder’s thousand-petaled tread. And when the black frost claims your footprints moments after you make them, accept that the city is already forgetting you, even as it learns, very slowly, to make room for your weight.

You are walking inside the birth of a world. Try not to be stillborn.
