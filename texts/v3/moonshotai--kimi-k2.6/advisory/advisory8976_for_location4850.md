**Preparation Brief: Entry into the Summit Blister**
*(For the Ordinary Human Multiverse Traveler)*

---

### **I. Understand the Threshold You Are Crossing**
Do not think of this as a dive. Do not think of it as spelunking, or as exploring a greenhouse, or as infiltrating a factory. You are entering a **closed matrix**—a single lung made of warm brine, ceramic alloy, and vegetative compulsion, clinging to the roof of a continent like a fever blister. It has no surface, no shoreline, no sky. It is entirely full. The transition from your world into this one is a transition from *horizon* to *interior*. You will not orient by up or down in the natural way; you will orient by heat signatures, by the geometry of machinery, and by the slow, courteous drift of your own displaced wake. Accept now that this place is young, sharp, and waiting. Your urgency is foreign to it. Pack patience as your primary survival tool.

---

### **II. Exterior Approach: Surviving the Nothing Outside**
The blister sits at the absolute stress-point of the continental spine, at an altitude where the exterior atmosphere will not sustain consciousness. Before you touch the warm water, you must cross lethal cold and near-vacuum. 

- **Approach Garb:** Wear a full high-altitude pressure suit or heavily insulated surface survival gear for the final approach. There is no “walk-up” weather here. The shell is pale geopolymer and salt-scaled alloy; it will be visible against exposed rock or ice, but the wind will try to peel you from the ridge.
- **Ingress Expectation:** There are no windows, no observation decks. The entry point will likely be a **wet lock**—a port flooded entirely with the chamber’s own brine, maintained at its internal pressure. Do not expect an airlock cycle. You will not remove your gear and breathe air before entering. You will transition from the killing cold of the summit into the fever-bath while already sealed in your underwater apparatus. Practice this mentality: **you will be wet before you are inside.**

---

### **III. Respiration: There Is No Interface**
The chamber is entirely, completely full. There is no air pocket behind the dome, no trapped gas in the apex, no room where you might lift your mask and gasp. If your rebreather fails, there is no “swimming to the surface.” There is only the exit.

- **Closed-Circuit Rebreather (CCR) is Mandatory.** Open-circuit scuba is not merely inefficient here; it is a sensory violation. The chamber is balanced on a silence so profound that your own heartbeat will sound like it originates in the walls. Your bubbles would roar. A CCR minimizes exhalation noise and extends duration in what may be a structurally confusing egress.
- **Full-Face Mask.** The brine carries ozone, ceramic particulate, and an industrial sweetness that may be coolant or effluvium. It is not for tasting. A sealed full-face mask protects mucous membranes and allows integrated comms. Ensure your mask has an egress valve for clearing, but do not plan on clearing it often—there is no air to waste.
- **Redundancy.** Carry a bailout bottle of breathable gas sufficient for a transit back to the wet lock. Given the shallow-lagoon pressure inside, decompression is not a concern, but **entanglement is**. Your emergency gas supply means nothing if you cannot navigate the machinery to reach the exit.

---

### **IV. Thermal and Pressure Regimes: The Fever Bath and the Breathing Chamber**
The water is maintained at a temperature that borders on fever—roughly body-temperature or slightly above. Outside the shell, the stratosphere waits. Inside, you are immersed in a warm, viscous bath.

- **Do Not Wear Thick Neoprene.** Hypothermia is impossible here. Hyperthermia is the threat. If you wear a sealed drysuit for chemical protection, it **must** have an active cooling system or the capacity to flush with internal water. If your protocol allows, wear a thin (0.5–1mm) cut-resistant diveskin or a breathable, abrasion-proof swim cover. Reinforce the knees, elbows, and palms. You will be pulling yourself through tight spaces, and the metal is knife-sharp.
- **Pressure Paradox:** Despite the altitude, internal hydrostatic pressure corresponds to a shallow lagoon. Barotrauma risk from descent is negligible. However, **sinus and ear equalization remain necessary** because any gas space you carry (mask skirt, suit, bodily cavities) will behave according to local physics. Equalize early and often. The pressure is gentle, but it is alien.
- **The Breath:** Every few hours, the chamber *inhales*. Pressure drops by a fractional amount, and for roughly sixty minutes, everything in the water—every leaf, every bubble, every drifting silver of vegetation—rises slowly toward the dome. Then it exhales, and everything sinks. This is not violent, but if you are neutrally buoyant, you will drift. Carry **2–3 kg of soft, ditchable weight** and a short personal tether or hand line. During the inhalation, clip yourself to a stable structural member if you need to remain at a fixed depth. Do not fight the rise; it is patient, and so must you be.

---

### **V. Locomotion: Navigating the Occupied Matrix**
There is no open water. Density here is not a condition but a law. The space between pumps, filtration grates, and heat-exchanger coils is packed with vegetation so lush it oppresses. You will not swim in the athletic sense. You will **worm, grapple, and pull** yourself through a three-dimensional maze where the garden and engine are one fused topology.

- **Fins: Minimal or None.** Standard fins will snag on root cables and kick up coherent wakes that behave unnervingly—they do not scatter but drift, flatly, against the far wall before dissolving. Use short freediving fins if you must, but better yet, rely on grip. Wear thin-soled neoprene booties or gloves with high-friction palms so you can climb the machinery.
- **Streamline Ruthlessly.** Root cables have colonized every gap. Hair must be secured under a tight hood; any loose strap, cord, or d-ring is an entanglement hazard waiting in the half-centimeter apertures between titanium manifolds. Carry two cutting tools, positioned for immediate access.
- **The Water’s Memory:** Move slowly. The brine is thick with warmth and reluctance. Your wake will linger, visible and coherent, as if the water is unwilling to forget you. Violent motion is ineffectual and psychologically disturbing. Treat the water like a negotiation partner: advance with intention, not force.

---

### **VI. Sensory and Psychological Conditioning: The Silence of the Clinical Womb**
This place will play tricks on your nervous system. Prepare for it as you would prepare for extreme isolation.

- **The Silence:** The machines operate on magnetic bearings and piezoelectric valves. There is no cavitation, no groan, no vibration. Once per hour, bubbles rise to snap like a distant thread. Between these events, the universe is submarine. Your heartbeat will become a drumline. Your blood rushing past your ears will seem to come from the walls. Bring **bone-conduction earpieces** with quiet, neutral ambient sound or teammate comms to prevent the hallucinatory amplification of your own biology.
- **The Light:** Dome panels emit a flat, institutional, eternal noon—neither warm nor cold, the color of administrative paperwork. Crucially, it does not behave like underwater light. It travels as if through air: sharp focus, soft definite shadows, no caustic dappling, no loss of intensity with depth. Your visual cortex will expect the refraction and dimming of a dive; it will not find them. Depth perception is oddly crisp yet strangely flat, like a painted diorama. Bring a **smoked or tinted lens** to reduce the eye-strain of perpetual clinical glare and to restore a semblance of contrast.
- **The Sterility and the Lushness:** You are entering a system with **zero fauna**. No fish, no crustaceans, no worms. The plants bloom in absolute absence of consumers. The psychological effect is profound: there is no biological company, no flutter of life to comfort the mammalian brain. The verdancy is not a jungle; it is a tissue sample. The silence, combined with the breath-deep warmth and the flat light, produces a womb-like claustrophobia. Set a **hard chronometer limit** for your visit. Without shadows or thermal shift, your circadian rhythm will dissolve. Do not let the eternal noon convince you that time has stopped.

---

### **VII. Biological Quarantine: You Are the Contaminant**
The sterility here is absolute. Even the microbial register seems scrubbed. The plants exist in a vacuum of competition, fed by chemical titration, unchallenged by decay.

- **Minimize Shedding.** If you have open sores, do not enter. Your blood, should you bleed, will not decay or be consumed; it will drift as a visible red seam in the clear brine, slowly dissolving, marking your passage. More critically, **you carry the first bacteria this place may have ever hosted.** Your skin oils, your breath, your microbiome—these are ecological catastrophes in a sealed terrarium-industry.
- **Pre-Scrub:** If possible, bathe in a strong antimicrobial/bactericidal solution before suiting up. Wear a fully sealed suit if your mission permits. Do not urinate in the chamber. Do not introduce any organic matter.
- **If Contaminated:** If your suit breaches and skin contacts the brine, do not panic. You will not be poisoned instantly, but the water carries ozone and industrial compounds. Rinse any exposed skin with purified water upon exit. Note that there are no local pathogens to infect wounds, but chemical irritation and your own introduced biology are concerns.

---

### **VIII. Respect the Machine-Garden Continuum**
The machinery is not background scenery; it is the circulatory system of the chamber, and the garden is its skin.

- **Do Not Trust Silence for Safety.** Valves cycle open and closed without sound. Impellers turn. A pipe that appears dormant may be hot with waste heat (the plants know; they grow toward it). Test surfaces with the back of a gloved hand before gripping.
- **The Equalization Port:** Near the apex, once an hour, a ribbon of bubbles rises and breaks among lily pads rooted in the overhead coils. Note its location. It is one of the few rhythmic events in the chamber, and it can serve as a crude clock.
- **Do Not Harvest or Damage Vegetation.** Roots act as living filters and organic packing material between gear teeth. If you sever a root cable threading a pump flange, you may break the thermal regulation loop that keeps the ceramic from cracking. You are a guest in a metabolism, not a visitor to a park.

---

### **IX. Emergency Protocols**
- **Rebreather Failure:** Clip to your egress guideline immediately. Do not attempt to problem-solve in place. The chamber is too dense for a search if you lose consciousness.
- **Entanglement:** Stop all movement. Do not thrash. The water’s reluctance to be disturbed means that a calm approach will prevent your wake from pushing you further into the snag. Cut away methodically.
- **Hyperthermia:** If you overheat in the fever-bath, signals include confusion and nausea. Your brain will already be strained by the sensory environment. If actively cooled suits fail, abort the mission. The warm water will not cool you; it will incubate your heatstroke.
- **Disorientation:** The chamber is only forty meters across at its widest, but the density of machinery makes it labyrinthine. Lay a continuous guideline from your entry port. If you must mark your path, use **mental geometry** (the specific angles of heat-exchanger coils, the serial numbers on pump skirts) rather than leaving physical markers, which become permanent litter in a system with no scavengers.

---

### **X. Final Intuition**
Pack lightly, but pack exactly. This place does not reward the brash or the loud. It is a held breath. Enter as if you are trying not to wake a sleeper. Move as if the water is a thought process you do not wish to interrupt. Keep your heart slow, your gear silent, and your hands careful of the knife-sharp edges of a world that has not yet been worn soft by time.

The chamber is warm. It is lush. It is full. It has been waiting. Do not make it wait for you to panic. Be the thing that moves through it with the least possible disturbance, and it will permit you to leave again.
