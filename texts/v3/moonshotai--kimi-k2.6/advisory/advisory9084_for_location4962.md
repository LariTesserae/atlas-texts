**Pre-Mission Briefing: Nameless Abyssal Cyst**
*Prepared by the Office of Cartography for Emerging Geometries*

---

### **I. First, Understand What You Are Doing**

You are not going to a *place*. You are inserting yourself into a **process** that has not yet decided whether it is a place. The cyst has no name because naming requires a mind, and mind—intention, memory, symbolism—is a contaminant here. You will be the first thinking thing to violate its interior. It is young, arrogant, freshly precipitated from possibility, and it has never had to accommodate consciousness. Your very presence will be an insult to its chemistry.

Prepare accordingly. You are not an explorer. You are a trespasser begging the stone not to notice.

---

### **II. Psychological & Ontological Prerequisites**

Before departure, you must undergo **six months of sensory-deprivation conditioning** in total silence and absolute dark. Not because the cyst is terrifying—though it is—but because your mammalian panic-response (fight/flight, the adrenaline spike, the scream) operates on a tempo that does not exist below the seafloor. Here, urgency is a dead language. If you feel the need to *act quickly*, you will die; you will thrash in the velvet-thick brine, exhaust your oxygen, trigger a temporal fold, and become another faint drag-mark in the ash-pale precipitate.

**You must memorize your own asymmetry.** The cyst loops. If you swim its breadth, you will return mirror-reversed, your heart offset to the other side, your writing hand swapped. Do not rely on mirrors, tattoos, or gyroscopes. Tattoo means nothing when left becomes right. Know yourself by *scar tissue*, by the exact pitch of your tinnitus, by the memory of a childhood injury whose ache you can summon in the bone. Carry a token of absolute chirality—a shell with a single, unmendable crack. If the crack appears on the wrong side, you have inverted. Do not panic. The geometry is not trying to harm you; it simply never learned human consistency.

**Abandon narrative.** Do not tell yourself stories inside the cyst. Do not name the spires you see, do not mentally call the black vegetation a "forest," do not think of the stromatolite shelf as a "table." To name is to drag the machinery of meaning into a space that operates on pure chemical gradient. The cyst will reject this like a body rejecting a graft. Keep your thoughts photographic, mathematical, and silent.

---

### **III. Entry: There Is No Door**

The cyst has no entrance and no exit. It is embedded in the unconsolidated sediments of the abyssal plain like a cyst in flesh. You cannot drill into it—that would be like drilling into a reflection. You must **phase-transit**.

Deploy from your transit vessel at the basement of the ocean, where the lithosphere meets the hadal silence. Exit your craft. Lie prone on the sediment. Listen. Not with microphones—with your body. The abyss is never truly silent: there is always the rain of marine snow, the groan of tectonics, the percussion of distant vents. But beneath that, pressed into the mud, you will feel a nullity, a **silence denser than the dark around it**. That density is the cyst’s membrane. Anchor your extraction beacon *there*, in the real seafloor, before you transit. **Do not bring the beacon inside.** Inside, geometry loops. A beacon set within the cyst might ring from your own pocket three minutes before you carry it in, or broadcast from the floor of your lungs. Leave it outside, sheathed in baseline reality, calibrating to your lifesigns.

When you transit, you will not feel water part. You will simply **be inside**, fully submerged, with no memory of transition. This is normal. The cyst has no threshold because it does not recognize the distinction between outside and in.

---

### **IV. The Suit: Your Second Body**

Do not call it a diving suit. The ocean is not water here; it is a **liquid that has forgotten the sky**, saturated to the edge of glass, layered in laminations millimeters thick with the patience of calendars. It does not flow. It rests. Moving through it is like parting a curtain of wet velvet, and it will crush flexible materials against your joints like setting concrete.

You will wear a **Cyst-Hardshell**: a semi-rigid exosuit of sintered osmium alloy lined with phase-stable ceramic. It must be:

- **Pressure-sealed to 1,500 atmospheres minimum**, though the cyst may subject you to local pressures that argue with themselves.
- **Thermally neutral**. The brine is cool—perpetually, intrusively cool, the temperature of deep stone and older shadows. The suit must maintain your core at 37°C without bleeding excess heat into the water. The vegetation feeds on chemical gradient; a warm body might be recognised as a new vent, and the black fibers will unfold toward you through angles you cannot point to.
- **Viscosity-compensated**. Joints must be hydraulic and powerful; swimming is impossible. You will move by extending micro-anchors from your gauntlets and winching yourself forward at a speed measured in centimeters per minute, or by using silent, caterpillar-track treads on your suit’s dorsal and ventral surfaces. Do not use thrusters. The laminations of fluid will sheer unpredictably around propeller wash, and the noise—though swallowed by the density—will vibrate through your bones with a sub-bass thrum that induces psychosis.
- **Chemically inert**. The brine is supersaturated with silicates, metal salts, and organic polymers. If the suit breaches, the fluid will not drown you. It will **precipitate inside your tissues**. Your blood will become a seed-crystal for gypsum and carbonate. You will fossilise alive from the inside out in seconds. Every seal must be plated with nucleation-resistant polymer. Check each one until your hands shake, then check them again.

**Respiration:** There is no air. No pockets, no surface, no meniscus. You will breathe from a closed-loop rebreather with a 72-hour capacity. The scrubbers must be rated for hyperbaric CO₂; the density of the medium means gas exchange physics behave… differently.

### **V. Sensory Equipment: Navigating the Lush Dark**

The darkness here is not an absence. It is a **positive, thick presence**, a black lushness that observes you. Do not bring lights. A bright beam will not reveal the chamber; it will blind you to its subtlety and mark you as an invader. The darkness has weight, and it will press against your faceplate like a palm.

- **Illumination**: If you must use light, carry a single **tactile photon emitter** on your sternum, dimmed to the threshold of a candle viewed through a mile of ink. Use it only to read your own suit gauges. 
- **Mapping**: Do not rely on sonar. Active pings are an obscenity in this silence; they will return not as echoes but as memories of sound from hours ago. Instead, wear a **haptic telemetry mantle**: thousands of micro-filaments on your suit’s surface that translate pressure differentials into patterns against your skin. You will feel the sharp spires of sulfate and carbonate before you touch them. You will feel the black vegetation parting around your kneecaps in directions that do not exist.
- **Hypergeometry**: The vegetation occupies more than three dimensions. It grows into the subtle cracks of local geometry. You need a **topological mapper** that renders 4-space overlaps as audio tones. When the drone of the mapper rises to a nasal whine, you are approaching a vector the plants own. Do not occupy it. They will not hurt you, but they will grow *through* the space where your liver is, and your suit will not stop them because they are not moving through the suit—they are moving through the angle between your ribs.

**Communication:** None. Radio cannot escape the cyst, and the brine swallows all vibration. You will be alone. Accept this before you enter.

---

### **VI. Gravity, Time, and the Self**

**Gravity is a local argument.** In one corner, buoyancy pulls toward the floor; in another, toward the ceiling-that-is-not-quite-a-ceiling. Disable your internal gyroscopes. They will spin themselves to pieces trying to reconcile down with down. Instead, trust the treads on your suit. If the local consensus pulls you toward a spire-cluster, crawl toward it. If it inverts, crawl on what was the ceiling. Do not resist. The cyst has never held a shape long enough to learn consistency, and it will not start for you.

**Time is stratified.** Mostly it moves slow—calendar slow—but occasionally it folds upon itself in pages. Wear a **temporal anchor** on your wrist: a device locked to the universal chronology of your departure reality. If you see your own shadow cast by a gypsum spire that has not yet grown, or if your heartbeat echoes from a moment that has not occurred, **freeze**. Do not move. Do not think about moving. Wait until the anchor’s display stabilises. If you act during a fold, you may become caught between pages. You might complete your mission and return to find the ocean above has boiled away and the sun is a cinder, or you might step out before you left, and meet yourself at the airlock. The cyst does not care about paradox. It is too young to know causality is a rule.

Carry an **Ariadne filament**: a spool of braided, chronosensitive thread. Anchor it to your entry point. Unspool it behind you as you crawl. If the thread loops back around and brushes your shoulder from the *front*, do not touch it. You have closed a loop. Follow the spool backward.

### **VII. Biology: The Infinite Vegetation and the Ghost Fauna**

The chamber is lush to the point of suffocation. Stalks and ropes of black, leathery tissue—neither kelp nor fern nor fungus, but something older than the distinction—fill every vector. They feed on chemical gradient, on sulfides exhaling from fresh stone, on the oddness itself. They are not hostile. They are simply **everywhere**. A cubic inch contains ten miles of fiber. Do not attempt to clear a path. You will exhaust yourself and open your suit to the brine. Move between them like a needle through velvet, slowly, sideways even, letting the pressure of your passage part them over minutes.

**Do not eat anything.** The rare fauna—a worm, a shrimp-like smear with no eyes and no urgency—are pilgrims, not prey. They do not belong here either. If one passes you, freeze and let it pass. Its flesh is chemically particular, saturated with the same toxins as the brine, and its ghostly presence is a reminder that even transient life here is lost.

**The built illusion:** You will see structures—stromatolite shelves like tables, chimney casings like colonnades. These are pareidolia. They are accretion pretending to architecture. Do not sit on the table. Do not shelter in the colonnade. To treat them as designed is to invite the cyst’s geometry to treat *you* as designed, and it may begin to accrete around your shape, precipitating carbonate on your boots, sending a secondary spike of gypsum into the angle of your knee.

---

### **VIII. The Soundscape & The Silence**

There is no current, no animal chatter, no rain from above. The silence has **weight**. Your rebreather will be the loudest thing in the chamber—a mechanical sin. Insulate it. You must hear the sub-bass thrum of the Earth’s machinery shifting in its sleep, and more importantly, the **infinitesimal, nearly ultrasonic tick of crystals lengthening in the dark**. These ticks are your only landmarks in a space that loops. Memorise their cadence. If the tick behind you suddenly plays from in front of you, you have inverted. Check your shell.

If a fiber of the black vegetation parts after centuries of tension, the snap will be swallowed by the density of the medium. You will not hear it. You may only feel it in your teeth. Do not startle. Startling is a luxury of the surface.

### **IX. Provisions & Contamination Protocol**

Bring all calories with you. Dense nutrient paste in squeeze-tubes. You will not be hungry; the wrongness of the cyst suppresses appetite like a stone压在 the stomach. Eat anyway.

**Retain all waste.** Do not vent urine, CO₂ scrubber byproducts, or shed skin into the brine. The cyst is a chemically precise nursery. Your organic waste could nucleate an uncontrolled bloom, or worse—the vegetation might recognise it as a new, aggressive species of gradient-feeder and grow toward you with the slow inevitability of a calendar page turning.

---

### **X. Extraction: The Impossibility of Leaving**

There is no exit.

There is no swimming out. The cavity is a self-contained pocket with no threshold. Your only route home is the **extraction beacon** you anchored in baseline reality before transit. Ensure it carries enough power to shear through the cyst’s membrane and pull you back into coherent geometry.

Before you trigger it, confirm three things:
1. Your temporal anchor reads synchronised.
2. Your Ariadne filament is intact and leads to a vector that does not loop.
3. You are calm. If you transit out while panicked, while your left hand is still your right, the inversion may stick. You do not want to live the rest of your life as a mirror.

---

### **XI. What It Will Feel Like**

You will not see far. You will crawl through velvet-black water that feels like it wants to become glass, pulling yourself micron by micron past mineral infants with faces like broken obsidian. The darkness will press against your faceplate, and you will feel, irrationally, that it is curious about your warmth. Gravity will tilt, and you will find yourself crawling on a wall you thought was a floor, your inner ear screaming while your treads grip blindly.

You will pass through the black vegetation, feeling its fibers slide against your suit in directions your skin cannot interpret—pressure from above, below, and *elsewhere*. You may see a shadow cast by a spire that does not exist yet. You may hear your own heartbeat answered from a moment ago. You will be crushingly, cosmically alone. Not even the plants know you are there.

And yet, it is not malevolent. It is **young**. Freshly emerged. It has the arrogance of a child that has not yet learned rules. If you are patient—if you move with the tempo of precipitation and crystal growth—it may allow you to pass through its body like a benign thought through a sleeping mind.

But when you return—if you return—you will carry the silence with you. The surface world will be too loud, too bright, too insistent on single directions and unbroken causality. You will find yourself standing in rooms, waiting for the gravity to argue with itself, listening for the tick of gypsum in the dark. The cyst has no name, but it will have renamed *you*.

**Safe passage. Move slowly. Do not name anything.**
