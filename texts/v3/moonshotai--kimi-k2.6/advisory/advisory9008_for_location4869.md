To visit this place is to volunteer for a gentle erasure. The hollow does not threaten; it *absorbs*. It is a pocket of concluded physics, and you—an organism of gradients, of hunger and sweat and heartbeat—are an anomaly within it. An ordinary human cannot simply walk in. You must prepare to become temporarily inert, temporarily silent, and temporarily unmade. Below is the regimen.

### I. The Decision to Descend
First, understand that you are not preparing for an expedition. You are preparing for a *cessation*. The hollow is not a landscape to be conquered or surveyed; it is a condition that has achieved itself. If you go to study it, to name it, to chip its obsidian or bag its dust, you will be the first vandal in a monastery that has kept perfect vigil since before minds existed. Your purpose must be nothing. You go to witness a place that has renounced time, which means you must renounce narrative: no story of ascent or descent, no arc of discovery, only a present-tense presence that you will later have to stitch back into your memory like a wound.

Do not go alone unless you have trained for absolute sensory isolation. The absence of horizon and the uniformity of warmth produce a dissociation so profound that the self begins to feel like a pebble that has finally, blessedly, stopped bouncing. A second traveler should wait at the lip of the rim—not within earshot, for there is no earshot, but at the precise vector of your descent—with a retrieval protocol keyed not to time, but to biology.

### II. Physiological Tempering: Living Without Gradients
The hollow is blood-warm, but this is not a comfort. It is a trap.

**Heat and Stillness:** The air is stratified. There are no convection currents. In the ordinary world, your body relies on moving air to wick away the boundary layer of humidity that clings to your skin; here, sweat will bead and linger, diffusing away with geologic slowness. Evaporative cooling is functionally arrested. At blood-heat temperatures in still, dry air, you will cook in your own shell. To prepare, spend two weeks acclimating in a dry sauna with all ventilation sealed. Learn to recognize the subtle nausea and prickling confusion that precede hyperthermia when no breeze offers warning. Your target is to lower your metabolic rate: practice extended breath retention (the air carries no refreshment, so minimize respiration), and enter the hollow in a fasted state so that digestive thermogenesis is nil.

Consider wearing a minimal, passive cooling underlayer—phase-change materials maintained in complete silence, no circulating pumps. If this is unavailable, the old method is best: hyper-hydrate for three days prior with electrolyte-dense water, and accept that your visit must be brief. Carry no water into the hollow. To introduce liquid—spilled, sweated, or exhaled—into a place that has never known pooling is an obscenity. The dryness is *native*.

**Respiration:** The air tastes of hot iron and baked silicates. It will desiccate your mucous membranes within minutes. Train by breathing filtered air through desiccant chambers so your trachea learns to tolerate the scouring. Before entry, coat your nasal passages with a thin barrier of sterile, non-petroleum sealant. If you require supplemental oxygen or a rebreather, the apparatus must be silenced; every valve click will be a gunshot. Use passive CO₂ scrubbers with no moving parts, and trap your exhaled moisture before it can enter the atmosphere. You must not breathe this place into humidity.

**The Skin:** The floor is dust compacted to the density of tile and leather. It will wick the moisture from your fingertips. The ceramic bead-flow of the surface will abrade without offering the gift of pain nerves any distraction. Coat your hands in matte, non-shedding barrier cream. Trim your nails to the quick; they must not catch on the ventifacts and dislodge flakes. Your feet are the greatest risk. They will find the soft hollows shaped by extinct winds, and you will want to press your fingers into those curves. Do not. Wear the softest possible soles—felted wool or brushed ceramic-weave, nothing with treads that might kick the dust. Better yet, wrap your feet in silk under the soles, so that if you must kneel, your knee does not leave a print in the bead-flow.

### III. Sensory Decommissioning
You do not see this place; you enter its temperature. To bring light is to commit an act of violence against the first chamber.

**Vision:** Do not carry a torch, a lantern, a luminescent dial, or a struck match. The darkness is warm and total, and it holds you. To introduce photons is to be the first sunrise in a world that has refused dawn. Prepare instead by spending seventy-two hours in absolute pitch—no screens, no LEDs, no chrono-glow. Learn to navigate by the temperature differential between your face and the air (though in the hollow, this is nearly zero), by the acoustic deadness that tells you when you are near a surface, and by the ventifacts under your palms. Map the bowl in your mind before you enter: forty paces across, nine shrubs spaced like forgotten thoughts. Know that beyond the low rim, the plain blocks the world. If you must have emergency light, carry a single photon source encased in light-lock velvet, and swear an oath against its use except to prevent death.

**Hearing:** The silence is a presence that presses on your eardrums. In training, sit in an anechoic chamber until you hear the blood in your ears become a roar, until the click of your swallowing sounds like a landslide. Learn to slow your heart through biofeedback; a resting rate below fifty beats per minute is advisable, because each beat is an intrusion. Remove all gear that ticks, hums, rustles, or chirps. Your clothing should be of matte-woven fiber, no nylon scrape, no zipper. If you carry a pack, line every strap with chamois. Tape down every seam.

**Proprioception:** Without horizon, without visual texture, without the subtle push of wind, your sense of where your body ends will blur. Practice floating in salt water with eyes closed and ears plugged until you lose the boundaries of your limbs. In the hollow, this dissolution is not recreational; it is the default state. You must retain enough selfhood to walk deliberately from shrub to shrub, and enough humility to know that your body is a clumsy, sweating engine among stillnesses.

### IV. Biological Quarantine: The Tenth Presence
There are nine shrubs. They exist in biological parentheses—not living, not dying, preserved by the absolute absence of moisture and decay. You will be the first fauna. This is a grotesque privilege.

You must not introduce the chemistry of life. Shower in antiseptic, then vacuum-seal yourself in a sterile underlayer. Take a broad-spectrum gut suppressant twenty-four hours prior so you will not need to defecate or urinate during the visit; the hollow has no microbes to break down your waste, and the moisture content alone would constitute a plague. If you menstruate, time your visit away from it; blood is water and iron, and the floor has never known either. Bandage every micro-abrasion. You are not visiting; you are temporarily haunting a sterile stage.

Do not touch the shrubs. Their roots have found hairline cracks opened by thermal expansion that has since ceased. Your body heat, transferred through a finger, might be the first thermal gradient those roots have felt in epochs. You could trigger a crack, a shift, a resumption of physics. They are spaced so far apart that walking between them is a meditation. Let them be the only biology. You are a ghost.

### V. The Lithic Temptation and the Ethics of Non-Making
The stone is the hollow’s only character of *tech*. Chert, obsidian, quartzite—the flakes and cores lie in the dust like an ideal workshop, and the text warns that they ache to be struck. No hand has ever struck them. This is the crucial fact.

Do not bring a hammer, a punch, a probe, or any tool harder than your own softened flesh. Do not pick up the obsidian splinter, no matter how it gleams in the darkness you cannot see. Do not test the bulb of percussion on the nodule of chert. To strike a flake here is to become the first consciousness, the first *maker*, the original sin of a world that has remained innocent of intention. If you feel the urge to knap, to shape, to claim utility from geology, you are not ready to enter. Meditate on uselessness. Carry no knives. Wear no rings that might tap stone. The hollow has achieved primitive technological potential without a maker; your only role is to let that potential remain virgin.

If you must carry an instrument for measurement, let it be passive—a simple rod of your own body bone, perhaps a finger, noting temperature by immersion, not a device that records or transmits.

### VI. Temporal Disarmament
The hollow has renounced time. Waiting implies a future, and the hollow has none. Therefore, you must not bring time with you.

No chronometer. No ticking watch, no glowing countdown, no metronomic heartbeat monitor that beeps. Time pools here; it does not flow. To wear a watch is to drip poison into that pool. Before entry, set no agenda. Learn to estimate duration by the drying of your tongue, by the number of deliberate paces, by the slowing of your cognition. Agree with your rim-attendant on a biological retrieval signal: if your pulse, transmitted through a silent, non-emitting body monitor, drops below a threshold or if a pre-agreed interval passes in the outside world, they are to enter the lip and speak your name. Sound, though absorbed, will carry enough from the rim to reach you as a ghost of vibration.

### VII. Movement and Conduct Within the Bowl
Descend the gentle slope knowing that you are not going down. The land simply relents. The plain beyond the lip blocks the world; once inside, you will not see the way out. Count your paces from the rim. Forty paces across the nave. The center is the deepest stillness.

When you walk, the dust-compacted floor will receive your foot like a palm. Do not bounce. Do not scuff. If you dislodge a pebble from the rim and it falls, listen for the subsonic sigh as it meets the dust, and know that you have just caused a million-year event. Do not pick it up. It is already home.

Do not speak. Do not whisper. Do not hum. Your voice will die in the stratified air, but the attempt will shatter your own composure. If you must communicate with your attendant, use a pre-arranged tug on a tether—a silk cord, unfelt by the air, running from your waist to the lip. No radios. No signals. The silence is absolute, and you are a guest within it.

Visit each shrub if you must, but do so as a mourner visits graves: stand at the radius of warm dust that surrounds each one, acknowledge its bronze desiccation, and move on. Do not count them to stave off boredom; they are not entertainment. They are the library of what biology became when it agreed to stop.

### VIII. The Danger of Comfort
The greatest peril is not dehydration, heatstroke, or disorientation. It is the palm.

The darkness is warm. It holds you. The silence presses gently. The equilibrium is perfect. You will begin to feel that your heartbeat is not an intrusion but an offense against a superior peace. You will want to lie down on the dust between leather and tile and let your own warmth seep into the floor’s old fever. You will want to renounce time alongside the hollow. This is not metaphor. In a place where cause and effect are dampened to flatline, the urge to cease is physiological.

Before you enter, anchor yourself to the outside world with a grief or a love so specific it hurts. Carry the memory of rain—violent, gradient-rich, noisy rain—as a totem. The hollow is not waiting for you. It does not want you. But if you stay too long, it will not reject you either. You will simply become another object that has arrived, neither decaying nor living, present in the dust.

### IX. Egress and Reacclimation
When you leave, the gentle slope will not feel like ascent. The world returns only gradually. The first breeze beyond the lip will feel like a blade. The first distant sound will be unbearable. The first light will be a concussion.

Stage your return. Sit at the rim for an hour. Let your eyes remember photons. Let your skin remember cooling. Drink water slowly; your gut has been in stasis. Do not speak of what you saw, for you saw nothing. Speak only of what you entered: the temperature, the stillness, the weight of the quiet.

Prepare, finally, to be changed. You have visited a room in the house of the earth where the hearth burns without flame, where the stones offer themselves to no one, and where the darkness is complete. You will carry the silence in your chest afterward. It will make the ordinary world seem violently unfinished. This is the cost. The hollow gives nothing, takes nothing, and expects nothing—which is, in the end, the most devastating gift a traveler can receive.
