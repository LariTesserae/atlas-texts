To prepare for this place, you must first abandon the illusion that you are going *somewhere*. You are entering a *process*—a warm, wet event that happens to have geography. The basin is not a landscape; it is a wound closing, or a womb contracting, or a thought being thought by the mountain. Your presence there is tolerated only in the way a splinter is tolerated: briefly, and with the potential for painful extraction. Prepare accordingly.

**The Body: Conditioning for the Humid Womb**  
The air is not breathed here; it is swallowed. Start now—weeks before transit—by training your lungs to work against resistance. Swim in hot, chlorinated pools and practice controlled hyperventilation. Your alveoli must learn to extract oxygen from broth, because the humidity is absolute and your body will try to drown in passively inhaled condensate. More critically, acclimate your vestibular system to betrayal. Spin in place until you can walk a straight line drunk, then do it again on a tilting platform. When the spindle skips and gravity lurches four degrees east, you will have perhaps three seconds before your inner ear convinces you that the ground is a wall. Ankles snap when reality tilts; practice falling into rolls.

Thermoregulation is inverted. The altitude should be cold, yet the mountain runs a fever, and the stone beneath your feet is hot as a fresh bread crust. You will be soaked at all times—not from rain, but from the sweat of the world itself. Do not bring wicking athletic wear; in air this saturated, “wicking” merely means transporting scalding mineral water directly to your groin and armpits. Instead, wear ventilated, waxed canvas or treated silk that can be scraped clean daily. You will grow a rind of precipitated salts and accreted minerals. Accept this. The fauna do not green; they silver. You, too, will begin to mineralize if you do not abrade your skin each dawn with a pumice stone brought from home. Do not use local stone—it is too young, too soft, and will smear like wax across your pores.

**Footwear: Walking on Flesh**  
The ground is cooling, not cooled. It yields. It remembers footprints. You will sink, and if you stand too long in one place, the stone’s overnight healing will grip your boots like a scab knitting into a scab. Wear wide, snowshoe-like soles—call them basin walkers—to distribute your weight across the yielding igneous flesh. Bring three pairs; the chemical exhale of infant rock degrades rubber and synthetics faster than salt waves chew steel. Tread lightly. The ground dreams, and heavy steps leave psychic bruises.

**The Eyes: Against Sourceless Light and Dream-Residue**  
There is no sun, only a bruised, nacreous vault that filters illumination to an underwater amber. Your eyes will strain, then adapt too well, and then the phosphorescent needle-reeds will burn violet afterimages into your retinas. Bring photochromic lenses calibrated for extreme low-light, but more importantly, bring sealed goggles. The mist is not merely wet; it is saturated with the mineral sweat of geological birth, and it will crust on your lashes. 

More dreadfully, sleep here mines your unconscious. Your dreams leave a gritty sediment on your eyelids by morning—actual mineral theft from your psyche. Before you lie down, coat your eyelids and the skin around your eyes with sterile petrolatum or a heavy, neutral barrier cream. Wear rigid eye cups, not soft fabric masks, because the sediment is abrasive and will scratch your corneas while you dream. If traveling in a group, maintain a watch rotation whose sole duty is to brush the dream-dust from the sleepers’ faces every hour. Suppress REM if you can pharmacologically; the basin does not deserve your dreams, and you cannot afford to have your subconscious fractionalized like the local fauna.

**Hearing: Listening to the Spindle**  
The sound is orchestral and merciless: the gunshot cracking of cooling stone, the white-noise roar of a million weeping surfaces, the glassy chime of territorial fauna. Bring active noise-canceling ear cuffs, but do not seal yourself entirely. You must remain able to feel, in your sternum, the drone of the machinery. Learn the spindle’s rhythm. Its phase-skip presages the gravity shear. When the drone stutters—when it catches like a heart skipping—drop into a crouch and widen your base. The fauna adjust their orbits without alarm because they know the tempo. You must learn to know it too, or you will find water running uphill into your open mouth while you tumble sideways.

**Navigation: Mapping a Provisional Reality**  
Do not bring a magnetic compass; magnetism here is a suggestion at best. Do not navigate by shadows, for they fall in directions that disagree with the light, as if the creatures casting them remember a different sun. Carry a small, rugged gyroscopic inclinometer—a mechanical one, not electronic, as the probability spindle may cause quantum sensors to hallucinate. Map by thermal signature, not topography. The floor extrudes new pimples daily; the western wall bulges in slow motion. Your map should be a journal of gradients: where the stone is hottest, where the mist clings thickest, where the cloud ceiling (which rotates on an eleven-hour cycle, your only reliable clock) parts to reveal the scaffolding above. Do not look too long at that scaffolding. It induces ontological vertigo, and travelers who stare upward too often forget which way their own feet point.

**The Fauna: Do Not Induce Decoherence**  
The creatures here do not breed; they fractionalize. One moment a thing is lapping at a puddle, and the next there are two, phase-offset. Observation seems to stabilize them, or perhaps destabilizes them—no one is certain. The rule is: do not touch, do not count, do not name. The thumb-sized quartz-chitin rollers are filter feeders and harmless unless crushed beneath a boot, at which point their mineral draught spills as a chemical irritant. The translucent many-jointed baskers are territorial; give warm slopes a wide berth, or you may be struck by a ventral plate and chime like a bell. The swarms that orbit thermal vents eat heat differentials. Insulate your body’s thermal signature with a thin reflective underlayer, or they may begin to orbit you, siphoning your warmth into their endless ellipses until you are hypothermic in the feverish air.

**The Structures: Geologic Technology**  
These are not ruins. They are active valves in the wrist of something vaster. Approach none without necessity. The blue-black blister slides light around it; looking directly at it means seeing the world behind it with a nauseating lag. If you must pass, close your eyes and proceed by guide stick and partner’s voice. The ring on the ground is made of solidified instants; stepping within it may bind you into a spiral that completes before it begins. The grass—or its metallic equivalent—grows there in violation of sequence. Mark its location on your gyroscopic map, then never speak of it again. The spindle is the most dangerous and the most vital: it threads probabilities into the crust. Do not approach within ten meters; the probability shear may cause your blood to coagulate into a different chemistry, or your memories to congeal into the stone itself.

**Water, Food, and the Chemistry of Breath**  
You will not thirst. To breathe is to drink, and you will need salt—immense quantities of electrolyte tablets—to keep your cells from bursting with passively inhaled humidity. Food must be dense, dry, and sealed in vacuum pouches. There is nothing edible here; the reeds feed on thermal exhale, and the fauna eat heat and phase differentials. Do not attempt to cook. Fire is an insult to this place, and the super-saturated air would merely steam your rations into a paste anyway.

**Sleeping: Above the Dreaming Stone**  
Do not sleep directly on the ground if you can avoid it. The stone is still soft enough to dream, and your body heat may cause it to reshape around you, cradling you in a warm mineral coffin that will heal over by morning. Bring a free-standing, self-supporting cot—no stakes, as the ground will swallow them and spit them out somewhere else, sometime else. Sleep above the stone, but not so high that you miss the spindle’s drone in the floor. Before sleep, check that your eye cups are sealed and your barrier cream is thick. If you wake with grit on your lips as well as your lashes, evacuate. The basin has begun to digest your unconscious.

**The Mind: Becoming Temporary**  
Finally, and most importantly, you must prepare your sense of self. This is a frontier of formation. The rules are provisional. Water will flow uphill toward the vents. Your footprints will fill in by morning. The sky rotates every eleven hours, and the scaffolding behind it does not care for your geometry. You are not an explorer here; you are a witness to an emergence. Adopt the humility of a splinter, or a stitch, or a single red blood cell passing through a capillary. Do not try to own, claim, or fully understand. Set a strict time limit for your stay—forty-four hours, four rotations of the nacreous sky—and adhere to it with religious discipline. To remain longer is to risk becoming part of the geology, another warm presence weeping mineral water into the absolute humidity, waiting for the stone to finish its dream.
