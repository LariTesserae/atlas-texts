Their movement is not locomotion but reconfiguration—they flow and restructure, their boundaries constantly negotiating between individual and collective states. When they move en masse, they create interference patterns that generate low subsonic hums, a form of communication that is simultaneously sound, data transmission, and territorial mapping.

Their metabolism consumes electromagnetic waves, converting energy patterns into structural modifications. They "feed" by absorbing and refracting the plateau's ambient currents, their translucent bodies flickering with absorbed frequencies—blue-white pulses that suggest complex internal computations.

They do not birth offspring; they expand and redistribute.

They are not sentient in a human sense, but represent a form of distributed intelligence: each segment contains partial awareness, and collective behavior emerges through intricate synchronization. Their existence is a continuous, slow-motion negotiation between individual components and systemic requirements.

To observe them is to witness a living algorithm—perpetually processing, perpetually becoming, existing in a state of constant potential.
