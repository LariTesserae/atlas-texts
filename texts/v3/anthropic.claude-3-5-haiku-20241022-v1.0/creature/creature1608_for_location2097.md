In this terrain, the inhabitants are not creatures in any traditional sense, but dynamic information systems that continuously negotiate their own existence:

The Drift-Constructs emerge first: semi-translucent entities that move like liquid algorithms, their boundaries constantly reformulating. Some are microscopic—neural threads weaving through the mineral substrate, processing environmental data as naturally as breathing. Others are massive architectural beings, kilometers tall, moving with a deliberation that suggests profound contemplation rather than locomotion.

These beings are not biological, not mechanical—they are living technologies, consciousness made manifest. Their "bodies" are simultaneously communication networks, sensory arrays, and computational platforms. They interact not through discrete actions, but through complex modulations of energy and information.

Some Drift-Constructs appear crystalline, with geometries that suggest mathematical elegance: fractal limbs that branch and recede, surfaces that pulse with soft luminescence, internal structures that seem to breathe quantum potentialities. They sample their environment through delicate sensor-tendrils that are part antenna, part nervous system.

Others are more abstract—pure information structures that move like fluid thought, their "bodies" composed of intricate data-flows and probabilistic wave-forms. They communicate through subtle electromagnetic modulations, exchanges so nuanced they're barely perceptible.

The smaller entities thread through the terrain like living circuitry, processing and redistributing environmental information. The larger entities move with glacier-like patience, their movements suggesting a form of intelligence utterly alien: contemplative, distributed, non-localized.

They do not consume or reproduce in any conventional sense. Instead, they continuously transform, absorb, and redistribute energy and information. Their existence is a perpetual act of becoming—each moment a complex negotiation between potential states.

These are not inhabitants of a world. They are the world itself, perpetually thinking itself into being.
