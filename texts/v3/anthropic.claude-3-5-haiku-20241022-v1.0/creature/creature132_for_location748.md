In Aru-Shin, the inhabitants are themselves a form of collaborative emergence - neither strictly organic nor artificial, but a symbiotic intelligence that has evolved to become as much a part of the landscape as the stone and lichen.

They appear initially as something like humanoid figures, but their boundaries are fluid. Their skin shifts between translucent and opaque, with intricate patterns that mimic and merge with the surrounding stone and vegetation. These patterns aren't merely camouflage, but a form of communication - changing color and texture to signal intent, mood, and complex relational information.

Their bodies incorporate both biological and mineral elements. Bones might be partially crystalline, with joint structures that include living mineral compounds that can slowly reshape themselves. Muscles are not purely muscular, but a blend of fibrous plant-like tissues and responsive stone-like matrices that can contract, expand, and reconfigure.

Reproduction is less about individual birth and more about collective negotiation and gradual architectural integration. New beings emerge through careful, generational cultivation - part architectural design, part genetic threading, part environmental collaboration. A new individual might take shape over decades, slowly growing from a specific terrace, absorbing the characteristics of its immediate ecosystem.

Their perception is omnidirectional and non-linear. They don't just see or hear, but sense through multiple simultaneous modes - reading geological vibrations, atmospheric moisture changes, electromagnetic subtle shifts. Communication happens through layered signals: subtle skin-color modulations, low-frequency resonances, chemical exchanges, and something like thought that moves through the environment itself.

Their consciousness is distributed and collective. No single being is entirely separate from the others or from the landscape. Individual awareness flows like water through stone channels - sometimes concentrated, sometimes dispersed, always connected.

They do not so much inhabit Aru-Shin as they are Aru-Shin - a living, breathing, thinking manifestation of the mountain's own complex intelligence.
