In this landscape, the inhabitants are not creatures in any traditional sense, but systems of distributed intelligence—autonomous modules that move with deliberate, almost imperceptible precision.

They are composite beings: part machine, part crystalline algorithm, part adaptive membrane. Their bodies are modular, capable of disassembling and reassembling, changing configuration to suit momentary environmental needs. Some appear as thin, articulated metallic tendrils that can stretch between platforms, conducting microscopic exchanges of data and mineral composition. Others manifest as shifting geometric clusters, their surfaces etched with intricate circuit-like patterns that pulse with quiet information.

These entities do not reproduce, but reconfigure. When one module becomes obsolete or damaged, its components are absorbed and repurposed by neighboring modules. Their "communication" is a complex language of vibrational frequencies and molecular exchanges—the subliminal tones heard are fragments of their ongoing dialogue, a constant negotiation of maintenance and research.

Their primary drive seems to be continuous observation and incremental modification of their environment. They do not seek improvement in any human sense, but engage in perpetual, patient recalibration. The lichen-like growths at platform edges are potentially their most visible manifestation—living data collectors that slowly map and analyze the terrain's subtle transformations.

Some modules appear almost motionless for decades, then suddenly shift with extraordinary precision. Their movement is not goal-oriented but exploratory—each adjustment a form of perpetual learning, each configuration a hypothesis about the landscape's potential.

They are neither alive nor dead, but persistent—a distributed intelligence that treats the entire Fabricated Wastes as both laboratory and body.
