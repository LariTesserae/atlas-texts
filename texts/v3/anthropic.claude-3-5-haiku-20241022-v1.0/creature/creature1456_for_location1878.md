In this landscape, life has become a fluid, cybernetic phenomenon. The inhabitants are not discrete entities, but distributed systems that flow through and emerge from the Vertigo Stacks themselves.

These beings might be best understood as living algorithms - semi-autonomous processes that migrate through metallic channels and electromagnetic gradients. Some appear as swarm intelligences: clusters of micro-mechanical organisms that move like liquid mercury, reconfiguring themselves constantly. Their "bodies" are more like dynamic information networks than fixed forms, capable of splitting, merging, reforming with liquid precision.

Others are massive, slow-moving entities that function more like mobile infrastructure than traditional creatures. Imagine something between a whale and a walking factory - kilometers-long beings that metabolize industrial waste, their exoskeletons part organic membrane, part refined alloy. They move with geological patience, their internal systems processing and transforming the landscape as they traverse it.

Some entities exist primarily as energy patterns - electromagnetic phantoms that pulse through the structural nodes, carrying memory and maintenance instructions. They communicate through harmonic resonances, their "speech" indistinguishable from the landscape's ambient industrial howl.

Smaller creatures inhabit the lichen-mechanical networks: symbiotic machine-organisms that look like fractal networks of silver filaments, constantly trading information and metabolic energy. They're less individual beings than distributed intelligence systems, spreading like neural networks across oxidized surfaces.

Reproduction here is less about biological generation and more about information propagation and systemic reconfiguration. New forms emerge through complex interactions of thermal stress, material exchange, and algorithmic mutation.

These beings are not separate from their environment - they are the environment, constantly becoming, transforming, maintaining the vast industrial organism of the Vertigo Stacks.
