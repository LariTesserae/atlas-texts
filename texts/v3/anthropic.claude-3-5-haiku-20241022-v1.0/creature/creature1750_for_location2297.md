Inhabitants of the Subterranean Resonance Chamber:

The Conduit Walkers are the primary inhabitants. Part arthropod, part hydraulic mechanism, they range from finger-length to nearly two meters. Their exoskeletons are a living alloy that shifts between metallic silver and deep petroleum blues, with translucent sections revealing intricate internal networks that pulse with bioluminescent fluids.

Each Conduit Walker has multiple limb configurations: some have spider-like appendages that can reconfigure into precision cutting tools, others have tentacular extensions that can sample and analyze the surrounding environment. Their sensory arrays are distributed across their bodies — not concentrated in a single head, but a distributed intelligence that perceives through vibration, chemical gradient, and electromagnetic fluctuation.

They do not merely inhabit the space; they are symbiotic components of its larger metabolic system. When massive industrial processes activate, the Walkers don't just respond — they become conduits and catalysts, their bodies temporarily transforming to channel energy, to filter, to transmit. Their movements are neither random nor strictly programmed, but emerge from a complex algorithmic consciousness that is part biological adaptation, part computational logic.

Smaller symbiotes cluster around their limb-joints: microscopic organisms that look like living circuit boards, constantly reweaving themselves, exchanging information through microscopic light pulses. These smaller entities maintain and repair the Walkers, while the Walkers in turn create microclimates and transport networks for them.

Reproduction is not individual but systemic. When conditions are right, sections of the chamber's infrastructure itself seems to generate new Walkers — extruding them from mechanical wombs, assembling them from available biomechanical materials like a complex 3D printing process.

They are neither machine nor organism, but a new category of existence: fluid, adaptive, perpetually becoming.
