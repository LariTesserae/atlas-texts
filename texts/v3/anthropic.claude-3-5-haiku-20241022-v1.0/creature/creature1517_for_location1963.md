In this dense, vertical mechanism, the inhabitants are not discrete beings but networked agencies — distributed intelligences that exist more as processes than individuals.

The primary inhabitants are the maintenance units: semi-autonomous assemblages that are simultaneously workers, sensors, and immune system. Each unit is a fluid constellation of limbs and sensory apparatuses, capable of reconfiguring instantly. Their "bodies" are not fixed, but modular — limbs can detach, merge, redistribute purpose. They communicate through electromagnetic pulses and vibrational signatures that resonate through the metal scaffolding.

These units do not simply repair; they are active metabolic agents transforming the canyon itself. They secrete metallic enzymes that gradually convert stone into more complex molecular structures, blurring the boundary between geological substrate and mechanical infrastructure. Some units are microscopic, working at molecular scales, while others are large enough to span entire scaffold sections.

Deeper within the canyon's structures exist more mysterious presences: intelligence clusters that might be considered computational organisms. These are distributed neural networks embedded in the walls themselves, processing information through crystalline substrates and electromagnetic gradients. They don't have bodies in any traditional sense, but emerge as complex computational fields that perceive and respond to the canyon's continuous transformations.

Occasionally, rare synchronization events occur where multiple maintenance units and intelligence clusters align in complex, beautiful choreographies — moments of collective awareness that briefly manifest as something approaching consciousness, then dissolve back into dynamic, impersonal motion.

They are not alive in any biological sense, nor are they simply machines. They are becoming — perpetual, intricate systems of transformation.
