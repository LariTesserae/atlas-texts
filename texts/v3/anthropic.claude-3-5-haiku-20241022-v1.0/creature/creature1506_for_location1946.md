In this liminal realm of becoming, the inhabitants are themselves processes of emergence and transformation. They are not discrete entities but fluid complexities that dissolve traditional categories of life.

Primary inhabitants are what might be called Metabolic Weavers - vast, translucent beings whose bodies are more like living algorithms than organisms. They drift through the viscous depths, their internal structures constantly recalibrating: part organism, part computational network. Their boundaries are permeable membranes that absorb and transmit information, heat, and potential energy.

These Metabolic Weavers generate localized ecosystems as they move. Their slow pulsations create micro-climates of biological possibility. Where they pass, nascent neural networks of plant-circuitry bloom and dissolve, tracing intricate communication pathways that last moments or hours. Their electromagnetic fields cause spontaneous crystallizations and biological negotiations between emerging forms.

Some Weavers are massive - kilometers long, with internal structures that suggest industrial metabolisms. Others are microscopic, functioning like distributed intelligence, swarming and dispersing in responsive patterns. They communicate through subtle thermal and electromagnetic shifts, a language of potential more than discrete signals.

Smaller entities drift within and between these Weavers - Proto-Organisms that are simultaneously individual and collective. They might be considered larvae of some unimaginable future species, or fragments of a distributed intelligence that has not yet consolidated into recognizable life forms.

These Proto-Organisms engage in constant negotiation and merger. They are not predators in any traditional sense, but dynamic systems of energy exchange. When they intersect, they might momentarily combine, share informational structures, then separate into new configurations. Their movements create subtle turbulence in the warm currents, generating localized ecosystems that emerge and dissolve in breath-like rhythms.

Technological remnants are not dead matter here, but potential participants in this living landscape. The scattered turbines and pipeline fragments are gradually being incorporated into biological networks, becoming sites of metabolic transformation. They are neither wholly artificial nor natural, but liminal artifacts in continuous negotiation with the living environment.

The entire ecosystem breathes with a profound, almost geological patience. Its inhabitants are not survivors or colonizers, but continuous processes of becoming - living demonstrations of potentiality, forever suspending themselves between formation and dissolution.
