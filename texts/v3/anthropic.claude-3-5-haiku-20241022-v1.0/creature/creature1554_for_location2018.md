Their bodies are intricate lattices of flexible bone-like structures and responsive tissue, constantly reconfiguring themselves. They move not with intentional steps, but with a rippling motion like liquid architecture, leaving behind transformed pathways wherever they pass. Their sensory apparatus is distributed across their entire form—they perceive through vibration, chemical gradient, electromagnetic fluctuation.

Membrane Crawlers emerge periodically: translucent organisms that are equal parts data transmission network and living filter. They undulate across the terrain's surface, absorbing and redistributing metabolic information, their semi-transparent bodies revealing complex internal circuitry that pulses with bioluminescent communication. They do not so much move as they are moved by the terrain's rhythmic contractions.

They are neither plant nor machine, but a continuous negotiation between information and matter. When they connect to the terrain's network, entire sections of the environment subtly shift and recalibrate.

Smaller entities swarm constantly: micro-organisms that function like distributed computational units, each individually simple but collectively forming complex adaptive systems. They flow through vine-conduits, across mechanical scaffoldings, exchanging molecular information faster than thought.

Occasional larger presences emerge—massive, slow-moving entities that are more environmental process than individual creature. These are living technologies so large they reshape terrain as they move, their metabolic processes generating new structural configurations in their wake.

Nothing here is stable. Nothing is separate. Every presence is a temporary configuration, a momentary crystallization of the terrain's continuous becoming.
