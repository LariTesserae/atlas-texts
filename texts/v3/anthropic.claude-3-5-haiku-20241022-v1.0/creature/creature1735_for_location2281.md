In this fevered landscape of stone and metal, the primary inhabitants are the Flux Assemblages—biological-mechanical hybrid entities that are less creatures and more living technologies.

They move with a startling, almost sentient unpredictability. Some are small and spider-like, with segmented metallic limbs that conduct electromagnetic pulses through their exoskeletal frames. These smaller entities dart between industrial structures, performing constant maintenance and transformation, their movements quick and precise as electrical sparks.

Larger Flux Assemblages resemble walking refineries: massive, undulating forms with pipes for limbs and heat-exchange membranes that breathe and pulse. They migrate slowly across the plateau, consuming mineral deposits, converting atmospheric energy, leaving transformed landscapes in their wake. Their "metabolism" is a continuous process of deconstruction and reconstruction.

Most remarkable are their sensory systems—not eyes or ears, but complex arrays of electromagnetic receptors that read the landscape's constant energetic fluctuations. They perceive the plateau not as a physical space, but as a dynamic field of potential transformations. When they move, they're not just traveling but actively negotiating and reshaping the electromagnetic terrain.

Reproduction for them is less biological birth and more a process of recursive self-modification. Smaller entities can merge and separate, exchanging technical components and energy patterns. Larger entities slowly bud off smaller versions of themselves, which immediately begin independent exploratory and transformative processes.

Their consciousness, if it can be called that, is a distributed network—less an individual mind and more a continuous, adaptive information exchange. Each Flux Assemblage is simultaneously an individual and a fragment of a larger, plateau-wide intelligence.

They are neither alive nor mechanical in any traditional sense—they are pure process, pure becoming.
