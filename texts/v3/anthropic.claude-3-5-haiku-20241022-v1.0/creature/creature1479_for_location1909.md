In this living, breathing vertical world, the inhabitants are as extraordinary as the environment itself. They are not discrete entities, but integrated components of a vast, breathing mechanism.

First, there are the Hydraulic Navigators—quasi-sentient microorganisms that are part biological, part mechanical. They move through the translucent green-gold capillaries like precise liquid algorithms, sensing and responding to microscopic changes in pressure, mineral content, and light. Their bodies are fluid membranes that can contract, expand, and reconfigure, sometimes carrying small technological artifacts as they traverse the plant-mechanisms.

Deeper within the structures live the Structural Symbiotes: organisms that are simultaneously parasitic and essential to the Foundry's function. They have evolved intricate root-like tendrils that can subtly adjust the load-bearing plant-tissues, sensing stress points and redistributing molecular tensions. Their movements are glacially slow but incredibly precise, sometimes taking weeks to complete a single structural adjustment.

Rarely glimpsed are the Condensation Dwellers—translucent, almost invisible creatures that exist in the perpetual mist between plant-layers. They absorb and redirect moisture, acting as living humidity regulators. Their bodies are more like dynamic water-membranes than traditional organisms, shifting and reforming continuously.

Embedded sensor-fauna also inhabit this space: bio-mechanical hybrids that are part organic sensor, part living circuit. They pulse with bioluminescent data, communicating complex environmental information through subtle light and chemical signals that ripple through the foundry's tissues.

These beings are not separate from their environment—they are the environment, constantly metabolizing, sensing, adapting. They breathe in mineral rhythms, communicate in hydraulic pulses, and move at timescales that blur the line between living and mechanical.
