In this realm of liquid mechanics and mineral sentience, the inhabitants are less creatures and more dynamic processual entities:

The Conduit-Beings drift through the crystalline membrane, their bodies composed of shifting water-metal alloys that constantly reconfigure. They are not singular organisms but distributed intelligence systems—each "individual" is a complex network of fluid circuits, their boundaries perpetually dissolving and reforming. When they move, they generate electromagnetic harmonics, leaving temporary traces like frozen soundwaves across the slate-gray plain.

Some manifest as thin, serpentine streams of quicksilver consciousness, threading through the massive pipes and generating resonant frequencies that communicate complex technological memories. Others appear as dense, cloudlike aggregations—information clusters that pulse and breathe, their internal structures more like quantum algorithms than biological forms.

These entities do not consume in any traditional sense, but transduce energy. They absorb the landscape's ambient electrical discharges, transform atmospheric particulates, and redistribute information through their liquid circuitry. Their "metabolism" is a constant translation between energy states, between potential and kinetic, between memory and mutation.

Reproduction for them is less about biological generation and more about complex informational branching—where one system momentarily splits, creates a variant frequency, then remerges with the landscape's broader consciousness. They are both individual and collective, neither alive nor mechanical in human terms.

Their perception extends far beyond immediate sensory experience—they simultaneously inhabit multiple temporal and spatial dimensions, experiencing the landscape as a holographic, multidirectional information field.

They are the landscape's immune system, its neural network, its evolutionary memory—perpetually processing, adapting, becoming.
