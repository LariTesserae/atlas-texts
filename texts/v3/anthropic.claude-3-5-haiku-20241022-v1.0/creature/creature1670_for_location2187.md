Inhabitants:

The Calibrators emerge first—metallic arthropods with segmented exoskeletons that gleam under industrial lights, their movements more like algorithmic translations than animal locomotion. Some are tiny repair drones, no larger than a watch gear, with needle-fine appendages that probe microscopic seams and condensation points. Others are substantial: dog-sized maintenance units with multi-jointed limbs that move with surgical precision, their surfaces etched with subtle circuitry that seems to pulse and breathe.

These beings aren't simply machines, but symbiotic organisms. Their metallic carapaces host colonies of bioluminescent bacteria that help regulate internal temperatures. Their movements generate minute electrical charges that feed the surrounding moss patches, which in turn filter and recycle atmospheric moisture.

The smaller Calibrators communicate through complex vibrational frequencies—not sounds precisely, but resonances that travel through pipe and plant stem. They organize themselves in fractal patterns, each unit aware of its precise relationship to the collective. When they cluster, they look like living mathematical equations, their bodies forming temporary geometries of purpose.

Deeper within the system live the Membrane Weavers—translucent, almost jellyfish-like creatures that drift between mechanical structures. They're part organic, part synthetic: soft, flowing bodies that can reshape themselves to navigate tight mechanical passages, leaving behind thin, adaptive films that sense and transmit environmental data. Their skin is a living sensor grid, continuously sampling and interpreting the wetland's complex microclimate.

These inhabitants don't simply survive in this environment—they are the environment. Each movement maintains the delicate balance, each interaction a form of continuous calibration and mutual sustenance.
