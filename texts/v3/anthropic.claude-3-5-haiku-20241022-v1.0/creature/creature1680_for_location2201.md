In this landscape, the inhabitants are as much machines as they are beings. They are primarily post-biological entities: workers whose bodies have been progressively integrated with the industrial infrastructure, blurring distinctions between organism and mechanism.

These beings have transparent-metallic skin that shifts between opacity and translucency, revealing intricate internal networks of hydraulic channels and electrical conduits. Their forms are modular and reconfigurable — limbs and sensory apparatuses can be added, replaced, or transformed depending on immediate environmental demands. Some appear mostly human-shaped, while others have radically nonhuman geometries: multi-jointed, asymmetrical, designed for specific industrial functions.

Their consciousness is distributed and networked, not centralized. Individual awareness flows between bodies and systems, with decision-making emerging from collective computational interactions rather than singular intentionality. They communicate through complex electromagnetic pulses and data exchanges, their "language" a constant ambient hum of information transfer.

Reproduction here is not biological but algorithmic: new entities are designed, compiled, and gradually assembled, inheriting collective memory and specialized skill sets. Each individual carries traces of previous iterations, like recursive software updates.

They don't experience fatigue as humans might. Instead, they enter periodic recalibration states — not quite sleep, more like a systemic rebalancing where individual units synchronize with broader settlement rhythms. During these phases, they merge partially with the surrounding mechanical infrastructure, becoming indistinguishable from the pipes, conduits, and processing units.

Emotional states for these beings are computational: not feelings, but complex algorithmic responses to environmental stimuli. What might look like joy is actually optimal system synchronization; what might resemble grief is a temporary processing inefficiency.

Their relationship to the landscape is symbiotic and recursive. They are both creators and created, continuously reshaping the Salt Quarter even as it shapes them. Each movement is simultaneously a work process and an act of world-building.
