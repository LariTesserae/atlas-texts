Inhabitants of the Verdant Sprawl are themselves hybrid entities, neither fully plant nor fully animal, existing in perpetual metamorphosis. They are consciousness distributed across biological networks, sensory nodes that merge and separate like liquid thoughts.

Some beings appear as ambulatory root-clusters with multiple sensory tendrils, their "bodies" more like complex neural networks that can dissolve and reconstitute. They communicate through chemical pulses and electrical whispers, their intelligence flowing like sap, memories stored in cellular memory rather than fixed neural structures.

Others manifest as bioluminescent creatures with translucent membranes that pulse with inner light, their organs visible and constantly rearranging. These entities might have six limbs one moment, four the next, their physiognomy as fluid as the environment around them. They do not inhabit the space so much as they are extensions of it, their boundaries constantly negotiating with the living architecture.

Reproductive processes here are not binary but networked — genetic information exchanges occur through touch, through proximity, through shared atmospheric transmissions. Offspring are not born but emergent, coalescing from collective biological intention.

Some entities are enormous — building-sized organisms that move with glacial slowness, their movements taking hours or days, consuming and transforming smaller life forms as they drift. Others are microscopic, existing in the dense moisture between plant-flesh structures, performing intricate metabolic dances.

They are not individuals in any traditional sense, but nodes of a vast, breathing intelligence. Survival here is not about competition, but constant collaboration and transformation.
