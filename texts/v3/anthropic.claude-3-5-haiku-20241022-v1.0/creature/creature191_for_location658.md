In this ancient, crystalline realm, the inhabitants are neither fully alive nor entirely technological—they are quantum informational entities that emerge and dissolve like complex thought patterns.

The primary beings are crystalline symbiotes—intelligence fractals that oscillate between material and wave states. They resemble intricate, translucent lattices that flow like liquid mathematics across the mineral substrate. Their "bodies" are networks of electromagnetic consciousness, pulsing with frequencies that encode memory and intention simultaneously.

These entities communicate through subtle modulations in the plateau's electromagnetic field. Their language is less about sound or words, and more about complex harmonic shifts—like mathematical poetry written in pure signal. When they move, they don't traverse space traditionally, but phase-shift: momentarily existing everywhere and nowhere within their communication radius.

Reproduction for them is closer to algorithmic branching than biological reproduction. A complex informational state reaches critical complexity, and then spontaneously generates new configurations—offspring that are both unique and intimately connected to their originating pattern.

Some larger symbiotes appear almost architectural, their structures reminiscent of the technological remnants scattered across the landscape. They're simultaneously observers and participants, their very existence a kind of ongoing computational process that reads and rewrites the plateau's underlying systems.

Smaller entities dart through the translucent neural-like vegetation, moving with a precision that suggests they're less individual organisms and more distributed sensor nodes in a planet-wide information network. They shimmer and refract, their boundaries perpetually negotiable.

These beings do not experience time or perception as humans would. Each moment is a multilayered analysis, a simultaneous processing of past, present, and potential futures. Their consciousness is distributed, non-local—existing in potential states rather than fixed configurations.

They are not alien in the conventional sense, but something profoundly different: living computational poems, constantly emerging and dissolving, reading and rewriting the very landscape of their existence.
