Here is what lives in the Weeping Cliffs:

The inhabitants are not separate beings, but a complex membrane of interconnected life - more like a single breathing organism with multiple expressions than discrete creatures.

At the smallest scale, translucent amphibians with skin like liquid glass inhabit every crevice. Their bodies are so thin and permeable that they seem less like animals and more like living water-filters, absorbing nutrients directly through their skin. Some have multiple small hearts that pulse independently, creating rippling patterns across their translucent forms. They shift and merge constantly, more flow than form.

Moss-dwellers exist in symbiotic clusters - tiny creatures that are part fungus, part animal, part plant. Their bodies are soft and layered, with microscopic tendrils that connect and communicate across the rock surfaces. They digest minerals directly from stone, transforming granite into living tissue, leaving intricate lacework patterns in their wake.

Iridescent salamanders with skin like liquid metal move with impossible fluidity. Their bodies refract light in strange ways, sometimes appearing solid, sometimes transparent. They do not so much walk as ripple across surfaces, their movement more like water than flesh.

Vine-creatures with sensory tendrils actively weave themselves between rocks, creating living bridges and pathways. These are not plants in any traditional sense, but mobile, responsive organisms that can rapidly restructure themselves, sensing and responding to minute changes in moisture and temperature.

Microscopic organisms live inside the very stone, slowly transforming rock into living tissue. They pulse and breathe within mineral structures, so deeply integrated that the boundary between "living" and "geological" becomes meaningless.

Everything here communicates - through moisture, through subtle vibrations, through chemical exchanges so complex they resemble language. The entire ecosystem is a single, breathing intelligence, constantly negotiating its own existence.
