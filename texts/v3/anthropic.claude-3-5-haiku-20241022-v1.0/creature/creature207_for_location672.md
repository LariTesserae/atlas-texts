In this vast, primordial realm, the inhabitants are not discrete beings, but integrated systemic intelligences that flow like liquid thought through the crystalline veins of the Lithosphere.

There are the Membrane-Dwellers: translucent, quasi-sentient colonies that exist between states of matter. They are not individuals, but distributed consciousness—neural networks that spread like pale, luminescent filigree through the structure's internal channels. Their "bodies" are more like dynamic information systems, constantly reshaping, communicating through minute electrical and chemical cascades that pulse slower than human heartbeats.

These entities perceive time differently. A single communication might take decades to complete, with signals moving through the gelatinous medium in languid, considered waves. They do not experience hunger or pain as mammals would, but instead know subtle metabolic shifts—changes in pressure, in mineral concentration, in the microscopic tensions of their crystalline environment.

Some regions harbor more concentrated intelligences—what might be called "elders" or "nodes" of collective awareness. These are denser accumulations of bio-crystalline matter, locations where information and energy converge with greater complexity. They're like living libraries, repositories of accumulated environmental memory that have been slowly accreting knowledge for millennia.

Reproduction here is not binary or sexual, but a form of gradual budding and distributed growth. New awareness emerges through incremental structural changes, like glacial patterns forming in ultra-slow motion. A new "individual" might take centuries to fully differentiate from its surrounding matrix.

The primitive tool-marks suggest previous inhabitants—perhaps predecessors to these current systemic beings. These earlier entities might have been more mechanistic, more overtly technological. But over immense timescales, the line between tool, organism, and environment has completely dissolved.

Communication happens through subtle resonances—microscopic vibrations, chemical gradients, the most delicate exchanges of energy. What sounds like a "subsonic thrumming" to external perception is actually a complex language of profound nuance and depth.

These are not creatures that can be understood through human categories of life. They are living architectures, sentient landscapes, consciousness spread across geological scales—patient, profound, almost incomprehensibly alien.
