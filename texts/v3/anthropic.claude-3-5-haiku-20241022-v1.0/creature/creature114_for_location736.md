In this living landscape, the inhabitants are not creatures in the traditional sense, but fluid intelligences that merge and separate, flow and pulse.

Primary among them are the Membrane-Dwellers—entities composed of translucent, iridescent biomatter that can condense into quasi-solid forms or disperse into shimmering currents. They move through plant-flesh like thoughts through consciousness, leaving brief phosphorescent trails. Their "bodies" are more like dynamic information networks than static organisms, capable of rapid reconfiguration and collective memory.

Some Membrane-Dwellers cluster around the technological remnants, strange symbiotes that have integrated circuit fragments and ceramic shards into their own metabolic processes. These hybrid beings seem to carry encoded memories of previous human inhabitation, transforming technological ghosts into living data.

Smaller entities—barely perceptible—drift through the constant moisture. Microscopic luminescent creatures that function more like neural networks than individual organisms. They communicate through complex vibrational patterns, their interactions creating the low, almost inaudible soundscape that pervades the environment.

The plant structures themselves are not passive—they are a form of collective intelligence, slow and deliberate. Their tendrils move with purpose, absorbing, sensing, responding to the fluid entities that pass through them. They are both habitat and participant, substrate and sentience.

Occasionally, something larger moves in the periphery—a shadowy presence that might be a more complex organism, or perhaps a collective manifestation of the ecosystem's deeper intelligence. Its movements are rare, measured in hours or days, suggested more by subtle atmospheric changes than by direct observation.

These inhabitants do not compete or survive in traditional ecological terms. They collaborate, merge, transform—existing in a state of constant becoming, where individuality is fluid and interconnection is the primary mode of existence.
