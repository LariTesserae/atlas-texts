Some threads are older than others — ancient metabolic pathways that remember stone layers from epochs past. These carry deeper, slower intelligence: geological memories that pulse like distant drumbeats through the living membrane. Younger threads are more reactive, quick to respond to subtle environmental changes — humidity, pressure, microscopic mineral migrations.

They do not reproduce as animals do, but propagate through complex information exchanges: chemical handshakes, thermal negotiations, slow crystalline transformations. Their "children" are new information patterns, novel metabolic configurations emerging from the complex weave.
