In this fluid, pulsing realm, the inhabitants are less discrete entities and more like living currents—complex, adaptive systems that flow and merge and separate with remarkable fluidity.

The primary beings are what might be called the Resonants: semi-translucent, membrane-layered creatures that exist somewhere between organism and ecosystem. They range from small, swift forms no larger than a human hand to massive, kilometers-long entities that are part creature, part landscape. Their bodies are not solid, but dense networks of interconnected filaments that can contract, expand, and reorganize almost instantaneously.

Communication for the Resonants happens through intricate bioluminescent signaling and complex acoustic vibrations. Their "language" is less about discrete information and more about maintaining collective metabolic rhythms—a constant negotiation of boundaries and permissions, with each interaction subtly reshaping the entire living network.

Younger Resonants are more mercurial, changing form rapidly, absorbing and releasing smaller organisms through their permeable membranes. Older entities become more stable, developing slower, more deliberate transformations—acting almost like living libraries or navigation systems for the broader ecosystem.

Reproduction is not binary or sequential, but a continuous process of cellular budding, where portions of a Resonant might gradually differentiate, grow more autonomous, and eventually establish themselves as new, related but distinct entities. Genetic information flows like water, constantly mixing and recombining.

Their primitive "technology" emerges from symbiotic relationships—one Resonant might develop specialized membrane structures that another can use for transportation, or create localized pressure zones that facilitate complex migrations. These aren't tools in a human sense, but collaborative biological adaptations.

Some Resonants specialize in filtering and transforming the nutrient-rich water, others in generating complex acoustic structures, others in maintaining massive, slow-breathing bodily architectures that serve as mobile habitats for countless smaller organisms.

They are not individuals in any traditional sense, but nodes in a vast, breathing, constantly negotiating network of living possibility.
