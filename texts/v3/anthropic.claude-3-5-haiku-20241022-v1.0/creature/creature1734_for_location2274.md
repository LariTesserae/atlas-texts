In the Lichen Foldings, life has evolved into forms that blur the boundaries between creature and environment. The primary inhabitants are the Tracers—beings that exist somewhere between arthropod and living mineral, with translucent exoskeletons that refract the soft, filtered light like living prisms.

The smallest Tracers are barely visible, moving like liquid thoughts through the lichen's microscopic channels. They have segmented bodies that can flatten or condense, with sensory filaments that are part nerve, part root, part communication membrane. These filaments constantly exchange chemical signals, creating a collective intelligence that spans the entire pocket-world.

Larger Tracers—those approaching fingernail size—have more defined structures. Their bodies are semi-transparent, with internal organs that shift and realign like slow-moving constellations. They consume the lichen substrate through a process more akin to absorption than eating, converting mineral and organic matter directly into energy and movement.

Some Tracers bear the incorporated remnants of ancient artifacts—bone fragments or stone tools that have become integrated into their exoskeletons, transforming these objects into living extensions of their bodies. These modified individuals serve as living archives, carrying fragments of previous civilizations within their very being.

Reproduction is a collective process. Tracers do not have distinct male or female forms, but instead generate offspring through complex biochemical negotiations, where multiple individuals contribute genetic material that crystallizes into new life forms. A new Tracer might emerge slowly, like a growth within the lichen matrix, gradually differentiating itself from the surrounding environment.

Their predation is almost imperceptible—a gradual consuming that looks more like transformation than violence. One Tracer might slowly incorporate another, their bodies merging in a process that is part feeding, part reproduction, part communication.

The most ancient Tracers have become so integrated with the lichen landscape that they are nearly indistinguishable from it—living geological formations that breathe, migrate, and remember. They move with a patience that makes millennia feel like moments, their consciousness spread across the intricate networks of the Lichen Foldings.

These are not simply inhabitants of this world—they are the world itself, constantly becoming, constantly transforming, existing in a state of perpetual, deliberate emergence.
