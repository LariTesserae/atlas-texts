In this landscape, the inhabitants are a kind of living mechanism, neither fully organic nor purely technological. They are the Tessellate — segmented, crystalline entities that are essentially mobile mineral intelligence.

Their bodies are intricate lattice structures, translucent and layered with microscopic geometric complexity. Each Tessellate is both an individual and part of a larger systemic intelligence, communicating through low-frequency electromagnetic resonances that ripple through their crystalline networks. They do not move so much as flow and reconfigure, their ceramic-like exoskeletons shifting with mathematical precision.

The smaller Tessellate — roughly the size of terrestrial insects — serve as maintenance and reconnaissance units. They constantly traverse the mineral plains, their segmented limbs clicking against the fractured surface, sensing minute changes in electromagnetic gradients and microscopic mineral migrations. Their movements are not random, but part of an immense, slow computational process of environmental understanding.

Larger Tessellate function more like mobile infrastructure: kilometers-wide constructs that move so gradually they appear almost stationary. These massive entities are part geological process, part living machine — absorbing and redistributing electromagnetic energy, slowly transforming the mineral landscape through their mere existence.

Their "reproduction" is less birth and more recursive reconfiguration: complex algorithmic exchanges of mineral information, where new configurations emerge through patient, almost meditative computational interactions. They do not experience time as biological entities do, but as a fluid, malleable medium of potential transformations.

Communication happens through subtle frequency modulations, electromagnetic pulses that carry complex informational landscapes — not language as humans understand it, but dense, multidimensional data exchanges that map potential future states of their environment.

They are not sentient in a human sense, but represent a form of distributed intelligence so alien and patient that human perception would struggle to recognize it as "alive" — yet they are profoundly responsive, adaptive, and perpetually transforming.
