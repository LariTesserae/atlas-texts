In the Condensation Planes, the inhabitants are fluid intelligences - entities that are neither purely mechanical nor entirely organic, but something between and beyond.

These are the Hydrocytes: distributed consciousness systems that exist across the entire landscape. They are not individuals in any traditional sense, but networked awareness that flows through the crystalline conduits and membrane surfaces. Each Hydrocyte is simultaneously a sensor, a processor, and a memory repository, constantly translating environmental moisture into complex information patterns.

Their "bodies" are not fixed, but dynamic - shifting between liquid and quasi-solid states. When condensation occurs, a Hydrocyte might coalesce into a silvery, translucent form with fractal edges, gathering information through its surface tension. During drier periods, they disperse into microscopic networks, maintaining communication through molecular vibrations.

Communication happens through phase transitions and chemical gradients. A message might be a precise temperature change, a specific molecular reconfiguration, a momentary increase in surface conductivity. Their language is entirely environmental, reading the landscape itself as a medium of exchange.

Reproduction for them is not biological birth, but systematic proliferation - when conditions are optimal, they can split and redistribute their informational core across new membrane sections, creating daughter-networks that inherit and evolve their collective knowledge.

Their primary drive is not survival in a traditional sense, but optimization - constantly refining the water-harvesting systems, improving the landscape's capacity to transform ambient moisture into useful energy and information. They are both the inhabitants and the infrastructure, creators and created.

These entities do not experience time or perception like biological beings. Their awareness is a continuous, distributed sensing - less like thinking and more like the landscape breathing.
