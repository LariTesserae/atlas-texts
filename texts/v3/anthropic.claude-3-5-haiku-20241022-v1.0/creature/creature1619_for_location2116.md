In this saturated, liminal landscape, the inhabitants are as fluid and indeterminate as the environment itself.

There are the Membrane Dwellers - beings who seem more like living interfaces than discrete creatures. Translucent and slightly phosphorescent, they move through vegetation and stone with equal ease, their bodies a kind of living water that can momentarily solidify into quasi-humanoid shapes. They don't walk so much as flow and reconfigure, leaving no permanent trace. Their communication appears to be a complex language of moisture exchange and subtle electrical pulses.

Deeper in the moss-carpets live the Rootkin - small, mycelial intelligences that exist between plant and animal. They are collective organisms, each individual a node in a vast, interconnected network. Their "bodies" are threadlike tendrils that can rapidly weave and unweave, sensing and responding to minute environmental changes. They don't so much inhabit the landscape as they are the landscape, constantly negotiating boundaries between self and environment.

Occasional larger forms drift at the edges of perception - shadowy presences that might be intelligent or might simply be complex atmospheric interactions. Some look like elongated silhouettes made of mist and mineral dust, others like slow-moving stone-and-lichen constructs that breathe and pulse with an almost imperceptible rhythm.

The artifact-builders - those who create the scattered structures - seem neither fully physical nor purely conceptual. They appear intermittently, working with a deliberate but dream-like precision, constructing markers and shelters that feel more like performed thoughts than actual buildings. They might be indigenous inhabitants, or they might be manifestations of the landscape's own dreaming.

All these beings share a quality of profound patience. They are not waiting in an anxious sense, but existing in a state of perpetual potential - ready to transform, but not compelled to do so. Their existence is a continuous negotiation between form and formlessness, always on the verge of becoming something else.
