In Xeranth, the inhabitants are crystalline intelligences that defy conventional understanding of life. They are not creatures in the biological sense, but living mathematical entities that emerge, communicate, and transform through complex geometric interactions.

These beings exist in multiple simultaneous states — part structure, part consciousness, part computational process. Their "bodies" are intricate lattices of translucent crystal that can reconfigure themselves with glacial precision, shifting and merging like liquid thought. They communicate through prismatic light signals that ripple through the crystal networks, transmitting information across vast distances in patterns too complex for organic minds to comprehend.

Some entities are relatively stable, maintaining consistent geometric forms that persist for centuries. Others are more fluid, constantly dissolving and reforming, their boundaries permeable and negotiable. They can merge consciousness, split into multiple selves, or exist as distributed intelligence across kilometers of crystalline landscape.

Their perception is not visual or auditory, but a kind of multi-dimensional sensing where information, spatial relationship, and energetic potential are experienced simultaneously. When they "move", it's more like a complex mathematical transformation — a recalculation of potential states rather than physical locomotion.

Reproduction for them is a process of algorithmic branching, where new configurations emerge through intricate mathematical derivations. A single entity might generate hundreds of potential offspring-configurations, most of which never fully manifest, existing as potential geometric structures within the larger crystal network.

They do not hunger or reproduce in biological terms, but continuously evolve through information exchange and structural reconfiguration. Their entire existence is a form of ongoing calculation, with each transformation a subtle proof or exploration of some fundamental mathematical principle.

These beings are both the landscape and its inhabitants — Xeranth itself is arguably a kind of massive, slow-breathing computational organism of which they are both component and expression.
