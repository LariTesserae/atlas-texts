In this luminescent vertical realm, the inhabitants are themselves processes more than fixed entities. They emerge and dissolve with the stone's breathing:

The Motion-Weavers are the primary inhabitants — translucent, quicksilver beings that flow between solid and liquid states. Their bodies are not biological in any Earth-sense, but dynamic energy matrices that can flatten against rock surfaces or suddenly extrude tendrils of pure kinetic potential. When they move, they leave behind faint luminescent traces, like memory-threads of their passage.

Some Motion-Weavers are tiny — no larger than a finger, skittering across stone surfaces with impossible speed. Others stretch massive: kilometer-long entities that undulate through the vertical landscape, their bodies partially merging with the stone itself, sensing and responding to micro-temperature changes and moisture gradients.

Their communication happens through intricate vibrational frequencies — high-pitched resonances that travel through stone and moisture, complex messages that are part sound, part electromagnetic pulse. They don't have discrete individuals so much as fluid collective intelligences that continuously form and reform.

Reproduction for them is less about genetic transfer and more about spontaneous mitosis — where a Motion-Weaver might suddenly bifurcate, with each new entity carrying a portion of collective memory and adaptive capacity. Their "offspring" inherit not just physical characteristics but entire experiential networks.

The artifact-makers — another type of inhabitant — seem to be a symbiotic expression of the landscape's proto-biological impulses. They construct strange, momentary tools and structures that emerge from the stone itself, seemingly without conscious intention but with a kind of mineral logic. These constructions are both incredibly precise and utterly ephemeral, existing for moments or centuries with equal likelihood.

Deeper within the stone-flesh of this world live the Resonance-Dwellers: entities so integrated with the geological substrate that they're almost indistinguishable from the landscape. They pulse with slow, immense rhythms, processing information at geological timescales yet capable of lightning-fast responses to environmental shifts.

These beings do not experience time or space as humans would. For them, movement is a form of perception, transformation is communication, and the boundary between self and environment is utterly permeable.

They are not alive in any traditional sense, nor are they mechanical. They are pure process — the world experiencing and expressing itself through momentary, vibrant configurations.
