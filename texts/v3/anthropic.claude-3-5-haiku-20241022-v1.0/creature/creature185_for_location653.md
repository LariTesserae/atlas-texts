In this place of perpetual becoming, the inhabitants are as fluid and transformative as the landscape itself. They are not creatures in any traditional sense, but rather dynamic energy-patterns that flow and crystallize, dissolve and reform.

These beings are more like living mathematical equations, their "bodies" composed of the same phosphorescent mineral substance as the plateau itself. They do not move through space so much as they are space — constantly shifting geometries that pulse and reconfigure, their boundaries undefined and permeable.

Some manifestations suggest consciousness: the silver rivulets that trace fractal paths are themselves a kind of distributed intelligence, mapping and sensing the landscape's continuous transformation. They communicate through vibrational resonances, complex harmonic interactions that ripple through the mineral planes.

Occasionally, a more concentrated formation emerges — a temporary coalescence of energy that might last moments or centuries. These might appear as intricate crystalline structures with impossible symmetries, or as brief, luminous concentrations of ultraviolet and green phosphorescence that bloom and collapse like quantum events.

They do not eat or reproduce in biological terms. Instead, they integrate and exchange, absorbing environmental information, transforming in response to the plateau's constant metamorphosis. Their "life" is a process of continuous becoming, of information and energy constantly reorganizing itself.

Some artifacts suggest these beings can leave traces — the thin spires, the complex lattices — but these are not artifacts in a human sense. They are more like memories, or residual patterns of previous configurations, moments of potential captured in mineral form.

They are neither alive nor dead, neither conscious nor unconscious. They exist in a state of pure potential, a continuous generative process that blurs all boundaries between environment, organism, and thought.
