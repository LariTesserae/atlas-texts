In this liminal realm of Membrane City, the inhabitants are themselves a fluid concept—neither fully creature nor structure, but something between.

The primary dwellers are membrane-beings: translucent entities that exist somewhere between living tissue and architectural form. They possess a crystalline intelligence that flows like liquid thought, their bodies simultaneously soft and geometrically precise. Their boundaries are not fixed; they can contract into tight, angular shapes or expand into gossamer networks that drift through the gelatinous environment.

These beings communicate through chromatic modulations—subtle shifts in translucency and color that ripple across their surfaces like complex language. When they "speak", their entire bodies become a message: blues fading into lavenders, sharp crystalline edges softening and reforming, vibrations that pulse through the surrounding fluid.

Their movement is deliberate yet organic—not swimming, exactly, but more like a continuous negotiation with the surrounding medium. They can solidify portions of themselves to interact with architectural structures, then dissolve again into fluid motion. Their sensory perception seems omnidirectional, sensing pressures and vibrations through their entire being rather than through discrete sensory organs.

Reproduction for them is a form of architectural budding: a membrane-being can slowly extrude a new version of itself, a process that takes what might seem to humans like years but to them is merely a conversational gesture. The offspring inherits not just genetic information but accumulated architectural memory—each new being carrying fragments of the city's ongoing construction.

Their purpose seems tied to the city's constant emergence—they are both inhabitants and creators, their bodies and movements subtly reshaping the calcium-silicon structures around them. They are less individuals and more like distributed intelligences, a collective consciousness that thinks and builds in geological time.

In this environment, to live is to continuously become—a state of perpetual, patient transformation.
