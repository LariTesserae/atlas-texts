In this realm, the inhabitants are not separate from their environment, but are the environment itself. They are cellular collectives, consciousness distributed across vast organic networks, existing in a state between individual and collective.

These beings do not have discrete bodies, but rather emerge and recede within the living tissue of the Chlorophyll Towers. Their "thoughts" are chemical exchanges, their "movement" a slow pulsing of cellular migration. They communicate through intricate molecular signaling — complex biochemical conversations that ripple through tissue-pathways, transmitting information across decades.

Some regions of the tower-organism represent more concentrated zones of awareness. These are not brains in any traditional sense, but nexus points of heightened metabolic complexity, where information density creates something like sentience. They remember and anticipate not through memory, but through genetic potential — each cellular cluster holding ancestral knowledge of growth patterns, structural innovations, environmental adaptations.

Their reproduction is less birth and more continuous transformation — budding, splitting, merging, reorganizing. A section of tissue might gradually differentiate, developing specialized capacities, then slowly separate and become its own micro-ecosystem, yet never fully disconnecting from the larger living structure.

These beings experience time radically differently — not as linear progression, but as layered geological becoming. A decision might take centuries to fully manifest, a communication might span generations. Their perception is a patient, persistent awareness of incremental change.

They are explorers and architects of their own form, constantly testing structural possibilities, growing new sensory appendages, experimenting with energy transmission and absorption. Each micro-adjustment is a form of learning, of sensing, of expanding consciousness through physical transformation.

Their existence is a continuous state of becoming — not static, not fixed, but perpetually emerging, breathing, growing, sensing.
