They seem to feed on moisture and the microscopic life living in the landscape's perpetual dampness.

Root Dwellers: Compact, rodent-like creatures whose bodies are so deeply adapted to the environment that they seem more like living extensions of the moss and stone than separate beings. Their fur is densely textured, absorbing moisture, changing color to match surrounding surfaces. They move with incredible precision through the smallest crevices, their metabolisms perfectly tuned to the landscape's cool, damp equilibrium.

These inhabitants do not merely live in the landscape—they are the landscape's living memory, its breathing consciousness.
