In the Hanging Gardens of Lithosoma, the inhabitants are beings of profound symbiosis and metamorphosis:

Primary inhabitants are the Lithomorphs — creatures that exist between animal and mineral states. They have exoskeletons that are part chitin, part crystalline structure, which slowly absorb and integrate with the stone terraces. Their bodies shift between translucent and opaque, matching the ambient light's subtle changes. They move with a deliberate, almost geological slowness, their movements more like gradual erosion than locomotion.

These beings reproduce through a complex process of mineral-organic budding: small crystalline protrusions emerge from their bodies, gradually developing sensory appendages and metabolic systems. They communicate through subtle vibrations that resonate through stone and plant tissue, a language of minute frequency shifts and mineral resonance.

Six-legged spore-creatures drift between terraces — part insect, part fungal entity. They have gossamer wings that catch light like prisms, and bodies that can flatten or expand depending on moisture and temperature. They serve as primary pollinators and information carriers, transferring genetic and environmental data between plant systems.

Smaller symbiotic organisms live within the translucent plant leaves — microscopic entities that regulate moisture, transform ambient light into biochemical energy, and help plants slowly dissolve and reshape the stone substrate. These creatures are so integrated with their environment that they're barely distinguishable from the metabolic processes around them.

Primitive intelligence flows through the entire ecosystem. Not a centralized consciousness, but a distributed awareness — each organism a node in a complex, slow-moving neural network. Decision-making happens through collective biochemical negotiations, with changes occurring over generations rather than moments.

Occasionally, larger shadow-forms move in the distant background — beings so integrated with the landscape that they're barely perceptible, more like geological events than discrete creatures. They might be ancient, sessile intelligences that have become so thoroughly merged with stone and plant that they're essentially living landscapes.

These inhabitants don't so much live in the Hanging Gardens as they are the Hanging Gardens — constantly becoming, perpetually transforming, blurring the lines between environment, organism, and consciousness.
