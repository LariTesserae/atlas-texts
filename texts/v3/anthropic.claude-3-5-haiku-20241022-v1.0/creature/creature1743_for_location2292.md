In the Muted Mineral Works, the inhabitants are a symbiotic network of entities that blur the lines between living and mechanical:

The Conduit Walkers are the primary inhabitants - creatures that are part arthropod, part refined machinery. Their exoskeletons shift between organic chitin and metallic alloys, with segmented limbs that move with liquid precision. They range from palm-sized scouts to larger processing units that navigate the mineral terraces like living sensors. Their bodies are both sensory apparatus and transport mechanism, able to detect minute mineral gradients and electrical pulses.

These creatures communicate through intricate vibration patterns and electromagnetic exchanges, less like speech and more like distributed computational signals. Their reproductive process involves complex mineral accretion - new entities gradually form through careful accumulation and integration of surrounding mineral compounds, emerging from protective slate-gray folds like technological crystals.

The Growl-Plants are their symbiotic partners - electrical-botanical hybrids with silvered tendrils that both conduct energy and extract microscopic moisture. These entities are less organisms and more living circuits, their root systems interweaving with mechanical infrastructures, processing trace minerals and generating minute electrical charges that sustain the entire ecosystem.

At the system's core are the Silent Processors - larger, more stationary entities that resemble compact industrial nodes. These are neither purely mechanical nor biological, but complex metabolic machines that filter, transform, and redistribute mineral compounds with glacial patience.

Movement here is not about individual agency, but about systemic communication - each entity a node in an intricate, self-regulating network that processes and transforms with minimal waste and maximal efficiency.
