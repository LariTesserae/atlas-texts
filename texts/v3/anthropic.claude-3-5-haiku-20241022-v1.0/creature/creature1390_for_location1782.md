In this realm, the inhabitants are as much a part of the living architecture as the vegetation itself. They are a people who have evolved in symbiosis with their environment, their biology blending almost imperceptibly with the plant-world around them.

Their skin has a faint chlorophyll undertone, with a texture that shifts between soft flesh and something more fibrous—not quite plant, not quite human. Their bodies bear intricate patterns that resemble leaf veins, which subtly change color with mood and hydration. Hair grows in vine-like tendrils that can absorb moisture directly from the humid air. Their eyes are large and slightly iridescent, adapted to the filtered green-bronze light that permeates the living terraces.

These people move with a fluid grace, understanding the breathing rhythms of their environment. Their communication is a complex language of subtle body movements, pheromonal exchanges, and low vibrations that resonate through the plant structures. They can sense changes in water pressure, in the microscopic tensions of plant tissues, in the minute shifts of their living habitat.

Their tools and clothing are grown more than made—fiber-based implements that are part craft, part negotiation with the living substrate. Garments are living membranes that adjust to temperature and moisture, changing color and texture as needed. Their dwellings are not separate from the environment but are carefully cultivated living spaces, chambers within massive stems that pulse and breathe with their own metabolism.

Reproduction and healing are deeply communal processes. Children are not just born but are, in a sense, cultivated—their early development supported by specialized plant structures that provide nutrition, protection, and gradual integration into the ecosystem.

These are not humans who live in nature, but a species that is nature—intricate, patient, constantly flowing and transforming, as slow and inevitable as the landscape itself.
