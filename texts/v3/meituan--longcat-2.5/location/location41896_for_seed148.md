# The Hollowing

It began as a bubble in the clay. Not a cave — caves are carved, and this was never carved. It was *refused*. The earth here simply declined to be solid, and the resulting space has been growing, almost imperceptibly, for centuries. You can feel it in the walls: they are not yet rock, not yet anything committed. They are dough. They are the geology of maybe.

The hollow is the size of a cathedral nave but feels smaller, because what fills it is not air. What fills it is *green*.

---

The plant does not grow on the floor or the walls. It *is* the floor and the walls. Every surface is a dense, layered mat of something between moss and sinew — pale green, faintly translucent, threaded with darker vascular channels that pulse with slow peristalsis. It is warm to the touch. It breathes. In the absolute dark of the hollow, this is the only light: a dim bioluminescence that rises and falls like a sleeper's chest, casting everything in the color of deep water.

Water arrives without rain. The walls sweat — not condensation exactly, but secretion, a slow weeping from the plant-mass that beads and runs in hairline rivulets down the curved surfaces. The floor is perpetually slick. Puddles gather in the slight depressions of the living substrate, and in these puddles, things move.

---

The settlement — if it is a settlement — occupies the center of the hollow like something the plant has not yet digested.

It is industrial in the way that a mill is industrial: mechanized, purposeful, scaled. But the machines here are not steel. They are *grown*. You can see the attempt at geometry — straight lines, right angles, the ghost of a factory floor plan — but the plant has compromised every edge. Conveyor structures are ropes of braided tissue, their surfaces studded with contractile nodes that squeeze and release, squeeze and release, moving material along in a rhythm that matches the walls' pulse. Vats are hollow pods, translucent, filled with fluids that range from amber to something too dark to name. Pipes are living stems, some as thick as a torso, and they *flex* — they are not fixed in place but adjust, reach, reattach. The whole complex is a negotiation between intention and growth, and growth is winning.

There is power here. You can feel it in the air — a faint ozone sharpness, a charge that makes the hair on your arms stand. Somewhere, generators run on something the plant produces, or the plant runs on something the generators produce, and the distinction has ceased to matter. Cables — or are they roots? — run from the machinery back into the walls, and the walls' bioluminescence brightens near these junctions, as if the plant feeds on the current.

---

The fauna is everywhere and nowhere.

You do not see them at first. You hear them — or rather, you don't. The hollow is quiet in the way a held breath is quiet. But if you stand still, if you let your eyes adjust to the pulsing dark, you begin to notice movement at the edge of vision. Things that are not insects but occupy the insect niche: small, chitinous, quick, skating across the wet floor on too many legs. Things that live in the walls, visible only as slight distensions in the plant-mass, pressing outward from inside like faces under a sheet. Things that hang from the ceiling — not bats, nothing so vertebrate — translucent and many-jointed, swaying in the thermal updraft from the machinery, catching whatever drifts.

They are abundant. They are the true population of the hollow. The machines, the settlement, the plant itself — all of it may exist to support them, or they may exist to support it, and the direction of that relationship is not clear.

---

And then there is the wrongness.

It accumulates slowly, the way the water accumulates. You notice that the walls are closer than they were. Not much. A hand's width, perhaps. But the hollow is growing smaller even as the bubble in the clay grows larger — you understand this without being told, the way you understand that a dream's geography is not fixed.

You notice that the plant-mass sometimes mimics the shapes of the machines, growing duplicates of gears and pistons that serve no function, or perhaps serve a function you cannot perceive. You notice that the fauna, when you catch one in your light, has eyes that reflect not your beam but something else — a different light source, from a direction that doesn't exist.

You notice that the floor, in places, is not plant at all but something harder, something fired, something *made* — and that it is older than the settlement, older than the hollow, older than the clay that surrounds it. Tiles. Ceramic. Laid in patterns that might be decorative or might be a language, and the plant has grown over them, through them, around them, but has not been able to erase them.

---

The hollow is not a place that was found and occupied. It is a place that is *becoming occupied*, that is *becoming a place*, and the process is not complete. The erosion here is not erosion at all — it is construction. The geology is being built, not worn down. The plant is the mason. The machines are the scaffolding. The fauna are the workers, or the product, or the purpose.

And the darkness — the darkness is not absence. It is a medium. It is the amniotic fluid of something that has not yet decided what it is going to be.

The hollow breathes. The walls weep. The machines pulse. The things in the walls press outward, patient, waiting for the geometry to soften enough to let them through.

You are not the first to stand here. The tiles know that. The plant knows that. The light in your hand is not the first light this place has seen, and the others — the others are still here, somewhere, incorporated, digested, made useful.

The hollow grows. The hollow shrinks. The hollow *hollows*.

It is almost ready.
