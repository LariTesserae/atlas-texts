The sound is a hammer on metal. Three strikes, then a pause. Three strikes, then a pause. The pause is the length of a held breath. The strikes are the length of a held breath. The rhythm is the rhythm of breathing, but slower, and harder, and made of stone. Sometimes there are four strikes instead of three. Sometimes the pause is longer. Sometimes the strike is softer. The variation is slight — almost not there — but it is there. The variation is the sound's way of being alive.

The sound is not just heard — it is felt. It presses against your chest. It makes your bones vibrate. It fills your head until thought becomes impossible. You cannot hear anything else. You cannot think anything else. You can only feel the hammer in your bones. The sound is a physical presence. A weight. A pressure. A force. It is deafening.

The chamber is small — twenty paces across, perhaps twenty-five. The ceiling is low enough that a tall person must stoop. The walls are stone, but there is no natural stone here. Every surface has been worked. Chipped, scraped, drilled, polished, marked. The marks are layered — older marks worn smooth by the sound, newer marks still sharp. But there are no newest marks. The making stopped long ago. The chamber is a palimpsest of every action ever taken in it, worn down to its final form.

The floor is polished to a mirror finish. Not by feet — no feet have walked here in an age — but by vibration. The sound has been bouncing off these walls for so long that it has worn the stone smooth. The floor reflects the dim amber light faintly, like still water. But there is no water. There has never been water here. The air is dry as bone.

In the center of the chamber stands an anvil. It is a single block of dark stone, worn smooth on top, with a shallow depression where the hammer has struck it ten million times. The depression is polished to a higher sheen than the rest of the anvil — the sound has worn it deepest. Beside the anvil lies the hammer. It is stone — a rounded head, a worn handle. The handle is grooved where a hand once held it. The groove is deep, precise, the shape of a hand that is gone. The hand has been gone so long that the stone has begun to close around it. The groove is becoming a smooth surface. The hand is being forgotten.

The chamber is full. Packed. Every surface holds objects — tools, raw materials, half-finished pieces, broken pieces, pieces that were never started and never will be. A chisel, worn so smooth it has become a stone. A bowl, worn so thin it is transparent. A needle, worn so smooth it is a bead. A blade, worn so thin it is a ribbon. A mirror, worn so smooth it reflects nothing. All the objects are becoming the same thing. All the objects are becoming stones. The sound has worn away the distinctions. The sound has worn away the purposes. The sound has worn away the names.

The objects are arranged. Not piled — placed. Someone placed them. Someone who is gone. The arrangement is deliberate, but the deliberation is worn smooth. The arrangement is a message, but the message is worn smooth. The arrangement is a map, but the map is worn smooth. The arrangement is a memory, but the memory is worn smooth. The arrangement remains, but the meaning is gone.

The marks on the walls are layered. The oldest marks are worn smooth — they are part of the stone now. The middle marks are half-worn — they are becoming part of the stone. The newest marks are still sharp — they are still marks. But there are no newest marks. The making stopped long ago. And among the marks are marks that don't match any tool — marks that are too regular, too precise, too strange. These marks were made by the sound. The sound has been vibrating the walls. The sound has been shaping the stone. The sound has been making the chamber. The sound is the maker now.

The heat comes from the sound. The friction of the sound against the walls, against the air, against itself. The chamber is warm — not hot, but warm, like a room with a fire in it, or like a body. The air is warm. The stone is warm. The objects are warm. The hammer is warm. The anvil is warm. The warmth is constant. It has been constant for so long that it is the temperature of the place. The place is warm like a body that has stopped moving but has not yet cooled.

The light is dim. It comes from the walls. The walls glow faintly, a warm amber, the color of the heat that has soaked into them. The stone has been heated by the sound for so long that it has become phosphorescent — or the stone has been worn so smooth that it reflects the sound as light. The light is not bright enough to read by. It is bright enough to see by. It is bright enough to see the hammer, the anvil, the objects, the walls, the ceiling, the floor. It is bright enough to see that everything is worn smooth. The light does not flicker. The light does not change. The light is constant, like the sound, like the warmth.

There is no door. There is no entrance. The chamber was sealed. The makers sealed it. They sealed it with stone, with clay, with something that has worn smooth and become indistinguishable from the walls. The chamber is a sealed environment. The air inside is the air that was trapped when the chamber was sealed. The air is warm, thick with the sound, saturated with the vibration. The air is the sound. The air is the sound made solid.

Outside, the plateau is bare. Stone and sky. No water, no plants, no animals. The air is thin. The wind is constant. The wind has worn the plateau smooth. The plateau is at its final form. The plateau is ancient. The plateau is the end of erosion. The plateau is the end of geography. The plateau is the end of the world. And the sound escapes. The sound is audible on the plateau. The sound is audible for miles. The sound is the only sound on the plateau. The sound is the voice of the plateau.

The makers are gone. They are dust. They are forgotten. They sealed the chamber and left. They left the hammer and the anvil. They left the sound. They left the objects. They left the marks. They left everything. They left nothing. The chamber is their only trace. The chamber is their only monument. The chamber is their only memory. The chamber is their only grave.

The sound never changes. The temperature never changes. The light never changes. The place is in equilibrium. Nothing moves. Nothing decays further. The sound has worn everything to the point where it can't be worn further. The place has reached its final state. The final state is warm, dim, deafening, still.

The sound is the last thing. The sound is the only thing. The sound is the place.
