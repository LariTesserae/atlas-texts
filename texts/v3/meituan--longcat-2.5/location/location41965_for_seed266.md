# The Khar Glaze and the City of Qash

**Terrain.** The Glaze is a continent of fused mineral — a sea that died so long ago the memory of water has sunk into stone. It sits high, a plateau lifted nearly four kilometers into the thin air, and its floor is a polished plain of salt, soda, and glass: white most days, amber when the light drops, mirroring the sky so perfectly that horizons double and travelers walk between two heavens. Ancient shorelines terrace the flanks of the plateau in bathtub rings kilometers wide, each bench a former sea-level, each one carved with the patient graffiti of extinct tides. Wind has done the rest. Erosion here is not violence but biography — yardangs of layered sinter lean into the prevailing wind like old men, ventifacts are polished into fluted horns, hollows have been blown through ridgelines into windows you can frame a caravan with. The land is settled. It has been this shape long enough to be *in balance* with its own wearing-down, and it shows: nothing jagged, nothing new.

**Atmosphere and light.** The air is thin, dry as a kiln, and warm. The dark mineral drinks the sun and gives it back slowly, so the ground holds summer heat long past midnight and the city sleeps on rooftops. Days run hot and shadowed shade runs cool; frost is a rumor at the highest yards. There is no rain. There has been no rain for epochs. Weather here is made of dust and electricity: thermal columns rise off the Glaze at noon like invisible towers, dust devils walk for days, and sometimes a front of static charge will build along the horizon — a wall of dark mineral haze shot through with silent blue lightning, the *firing*, that scorches the glass clean and leaves the world afterward so sharp it hurts to look at. The sky at noon is a pale blade; at dusk it goes through amber, rose, and a deep altitude-indigo while the long blue shadows of the yardangs stretch across the white floor. The air smells of hot alkali, faint sulfur from the vents, and — during the Pale — something sweet and yeasty that has no business existing on a dead mineral world.

**Sound.** The Glaze is mostly quiet, and it is quiet in a specific way: the thin air swallows sound, so speech carries only a few paces and the city learned long ago to talk with its hands. But the ground does not swallow anything. Sound travels *through* the plateau — mine-hammers ten miles off arrive in the soles as a slow pulse; a herd of khottal crossing the flats is felt before it is heard; the molten-salt heat pipes under the streets hum a chord that never stops. This is the **Listening**, the baseline drone of the world, and everything alive here hears through its feet first and its ears second. The city's etiquette is built on it: to walk loudly is to shout into every room below you. Footwear is soft. Arguments are silent. During the Pale, the spore-drift seems to muffle even the Listening, and Qash becomes so still that residents say you can hear the dunes growing.

**Ecology.** No plant has ever grown here, and yet the Glaze is abundant. The base of everything is fungal. Black **rocktripe** crusts dissolve salt and bare sinter; **glass-stalks** — fungi that precipitate silica into pale, reed-like spires — stand in groves that fools mistook for vegetation until they crumbled like chalk; **sporecandles**, tall white columns, wait decades for the right thermal and then bloom into the Pale. On this foundation: **clickers**, silica-shelled arthropods that swarm after spore-rise and chatter like a thousand knuckles. **Dust-sharks** — flat, blind things a meter long — swim the loose sinter and surface at dusk to filter falling spores, leaving only a slow dimpling of the ground. **Khottal** are the great grazers: six-legged, patient, with silica teeth and a gizzard full of grit, herded across the Glaze in numbers that darken square kilometers of white floor; they hear the Listening through their feet and move in eerie unison. Above everything ride the **silt-wings**, six-meter soarers that live on the thermals, nest on Qash's highest stacks, and drop like folded knives onto clicker swarms. Their cries are so rare that a hearing is an omen.

**The city.** Qash spills down the head of a dry canyon in terraces — old salt-cut warrens at the bottom, sintercrete and iron above, the newest districts poured in pale khassar-concrete that cures in the sun. It is layered like a sediment: a thousand years of building, none of it ever demolished, only built over. The low district hugs the geothermal vents where the calciners, spore-vats, and dye houses drink the plateau's heat through a grid of molten-salt pipes. Above them, the industrial mid-city: **breath-engines** — great Stirling machines that run on the day–night temperature swing, exhaling white alkali vapor at dusk; solar furnaces of polished glass that can slag stone; cableways strung out to the yardang mines, their creak the loudest honest sound in Qash. Khassar — a mineral that is faintly piezoelectric — is the city's nervous system and its strangest export: it goes into engines, into compasses that are only *approximately* honest, and into the deep chamber called the **Vault of Echoes**, where the dense khassar sings, endlessly, a low chord that the oldest residents swear is the sea. The fossil sea. The one that was here before.

**Time.** Everything in Qash is slow. Contracts run a generation. Engines are built to work for a century and mostly do. The sinter dunes drift meters per year, and outlying districts are periodically buried and patiently re-excavated — architecture as an act of faith in the future's return. The city keeps its own calendar: the spore-rise, the firing season, the herdstones, the dune-years. Nothing is urgent. The Pale, when it comes, slows everything further — the city signs, waits, and makes no decisions it will have to live with, because decisions made in the Pale are binding for a generation.

**Rules of the world.**
- **Water is memory.** The only liquid water is fossil brine, pumped from deep mines and rationed by the drop. It is currency, sacrament, and law. To waste it is the gravest crime — *water-debt* — because it is spending what the dead sea left behind.
- **The ground hears.** Speak low, walk soft, sign when you can. Loud noise is violence against everyone.
- **The Pale commands.** When the sporecandles bloom, the city slows and listens. What is decided then stands.
- **Do not dig past the warm line.** The heat below is the plateau's slow heart. The mines stop where the rock starts to breathe.
- **The dead return to the Glaze.** Bodies are laid in shallow salt pans and given back. The oldest levels of the city are quarried, reverently, from ancestral deposits.
- **The khassar remembers.** Compasses drift. The Vault sings the sea. Whether the plateau is dreaming, and Qash is its slow thought, is not a question the city answers — only one it lives inside.

On a clear evening, when the breath-engines exhale and the silt-wings bank home against a sky going indigo, Qash looks less like a city on a dead world and more like something the Glaze grew on purpose — a warm, quiet, patient excrescence, listening to itself, remembering water.
