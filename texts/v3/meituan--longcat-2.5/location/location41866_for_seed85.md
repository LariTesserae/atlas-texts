**The Stillness**

The lake has no inflow and no outflow. It sits in the crater of a volcano that died before the world learned to remember — a vast, steep-walled basin high on a mountain, filled with water that has no business being still. The water is dark, clear, cool, and motionless. It has been motionless for as long as anyone can say — which is not long, because no one comes here, and the city at the bottom does not keep time.

The lakebed is a sheet of volcanic glass — obsidian, pumice, and something else, something that doesn't have a name in the languages of the surface. The glass is sharp, fresh, unworn — the crater is young, geologically, or the glass is maintained in a state of perpetual newness. It is black, dark gray, dark green, dark blue — it absorbs the faint light from above and gives nothing back. It is smooth in some places, jagged in others, fractured in patterns that suggest a single catastrophic moment of formation, or a single catastrophic moment of placement. The crater rim is jagged and steep; the walls drop sheer into the water; the floor is flat and dark and bare.

The city is built on the glass. Or the city is built of the glass. Or the city grew from the glass — the distinction doesn't matter, because the city and the glass are the same thing, or the city is a pattern in the glass, or the glass is a record of the city. The city is small — a hamlet, a village, a single district — perhaps a few dozen structures scattered across the lakebed with much empty space between them. The buildings are geometric: cubes, spheres, cylinders, pyramids, and more complex shapes that don't have names. One building is a perfect cube of black glass, ten meters on a side, with edges sharp enough to cut. Another is a sphere of dark metal, five meters across, resting on the glass like a held breath. Another is a cylinder of dark ceramic, three meters tall, with a surface so smooth it seems to absorb the water around it. The buildings are dark, smooth, seamless — no seams, no joints, no marks of construction. The edges are sharp, the surfaces are clean, the materials are unmarked. The buildings look as if they were made yesterday — or as if they were made a thousand years ago and preserved in perfect condition.

The city is arranged in a pattern — concentric rings, or a grid, or a spiral — around a central structure: a tall, slender spire that rises from the center of the city, up through the water, toward the surface. The spire is the heart of the city — the stasis engine, the memorial, the archive, the thing that keeps the city unchanged. The spire is dark, smooth, seamless, sharp, unworn. It is a needle of dark glass, a hundred meters tall, rising from the center of the city, tapering to a point that is lost in the dark water above. It rises higher than the other buildings, higher than the crater rim, higher than the surface of the lake — or it doesn't; it's hard to tell, because the water is dark and the light is dim and the spire is the same color as the water and the glass and the city.

The city has infrastructure. Roads run between the buildings — smooth, dark, unworn roads, five meters wide, with no marks of traffic. Tunnels connect the buildings — smooth, dark, unworn tunnels, with no marks of passage. Bridges span the gaps — smooth, dark, unworn bridges, with no marks of crossing. Pipes and cables run along the roads, or through the tunnels, or hang in the water — smooth, dark, unworn conduits, with no marks of use. The city is layered — some buildings are multi-story, some are built on multiple levels of the lakebed, some are partially buried in the glass. The city is organized, planned, deliberate — not haphazard, not organic, not grown. It is built.

The water is still. It does not flow, does not circulate, does not move. There are no currents, no tides, no waves. The water hangs in the crater like a held breath, like a held thought, like a held moment. Light passes through the water without shimmer — the light is static, frozen, unchanging. Particles drift in the water but do not settle — they hang, suspended, motionless, as if the water is a solid, or a gel, or a void. The water is clear but dark — you can see the city, but dimly, as if through a dark veil, or through a dark memory, or through a dark dream.

The light is dim. A faint, diffuse glow filters down from the surface — far above, so far that it is only a rumor, a dim, cold light that has traveled through the water and lost most of its strength. The light is blue-green, colorless, the color of deep water and old glass. It casts no shadows. The city's tech emits a faint glow — a subsonic hum of light, a vibration that suffuses the buildings like a held breath. The rare creatures emit a faint bioluminescence — a soft, distant glow, a memory of light, a rumor of life. The light is static — no shimmer, no flicker, no change.

The sound is quiet. The water is still — no currents, no waves, no sound of moving water. The buildings are silent — no creak, no groan, no sound of settling. The only sound is a faint, low hum from the tech — a subsonic vibration, barely audible, that suffuses the city like a held breath. And maybe, occasionally, the sound of a creature — a soft, distant click, or a low, resonant tone, or a whisper, or nothing. The sound is static — no echoes, no reverberations, no change. The silence is deep, complete, unbroken — the silence of a world that does not move.

The temperature is cool. A constant, unchanging cool — neither warm nor cold, just cool. The water is cool, the buildings are cool, the glass is cool. There is no variation, no gradient, no change. The cool is static — it does not rise, does not fall, does not shift. It is a held temperature, a held moment, a held world.

There are no plants. The water is sterile — no algae, no weeds, no moss, no lichen, no life. The glass is bare — no moss, no grass, no lichen, no life. The city is mineral — glass, ceramic, metal, stone — no life. The only life is the rare creatures — a few strange, rare entities that move through the water or the city, with no obvious purpose.

The creatures are rare. Maybe there are only a few — a handful, a dozen, a score. One is a sphere of pale light, a meter across, drifting through the water, emitting a faint hum that is almost, but not quite, inaudible. Another is a long, thin filament of dark metal, ten meters long, hanging motionless in the water, twitching occasionally, as if responding to a signal that no one else can hear. Another is a cloud of tiny particles, a meter across, drifting through the city, passing through the buildings as if they are not there, or as if it is not there. They are not quite alive, not quite machines, not quite anything that has a name. They move slowly, or quickly, or not at all. They make no sound, or a faint sound, or a strange sound. They have no obvious purpose — they just are. They are the only movement in a world that does not move, the only life in a world that does not live, the only change in a world that does not change.

The city is in stasis. A frozen equilibrium, a time-invariant state. Nothing changes. The water does not flow. The structures do not decay. The city does not grow, does not shrink, does not shift. The light does not shimmer. The sound does not echo. The temperature does not shift. The creatures do not age, do not die, do not reproduce. The city is a still point — a place where nothing changes, a place where time is suspended, a place where the world is held in frozen equilibrium.

The stasis is maintained by the tech — the beyond-modern tech that built the city, or that preserves the city, or that is the city. The tech is subtle, integrated, not obviously "high-tech" — it is in the materials, in the structures, in the field that suffuses the city, in the water itself. The buildings are not made of anything that current physics can fully describe — they are not quite glass, not quite metal, not quite ceramic, not quite stone. They are something else, something that holds its shape forever, something that does not corrode, does not erode, does not change. The field that suffuses the city is not quite a force field, not quite a time field, not quite a probability field — it is something else, something that holds the city in place, something that holds the water still, something that holds the world in frozen equilibrium. The tech is the reason the city is unchanged — it maintains the stasis, it preserves the city, it keeps the water still. The tech is the reason the city exists — it is a city built to last forever, unchanged, a monument, a memory, a seed, a tomb.

The city is strange. The stasis is strange. The stillness of the water is strange. The city is strange. The creatures are strange. The tech is strange. But it is coherent — it is a place that makes sense, just not in a normal way. It is a place that exists outside of time, outside of change, outside of the normal rules of the world. It is a place that is what it is, and will always be what it is, and has always been what it is.

The city is a still point. A place where nothing changes. A place where time is suspended. A place where the world is held in frozen equilibrium. It is a place of silence, of darkness, of coolness, of stillness. It is a place of strange creatures, of impossible tech, of fresh, sharp, unworn structures. It is a place that is what it is, and will always be what it is, and has always been what it is.
