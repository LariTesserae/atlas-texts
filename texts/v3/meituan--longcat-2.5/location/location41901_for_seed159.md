# The Kiln

*(called the Nursery by the few who travel it)*

You come in on a warm wind that smells of wet chalk and crushed shell, carrying a hum so constant you feel it in your teeth before you hear it. The passage doesn't end so much as open — the way a mouth opens. You stand at the lip of a cavern so wide that its far side is only a rumor in a bronze haze. There is no sun. The light here is animals.

Overhead, the air is a slow blizzard of **duskflits**, winged midges carrying cold fire in their bodies, drifting in columns and sheets. Beneath them the towers wear collars of **glow-grubs**, the sessile young of the same creature, strung along every high edge in ropes and curtains. The combined light is the color of late afternoon seen through dust — shadowless, sourceless, thick enough to work by. It has no direction, so it gives you nothing to steer by. The cave offers a noon with no sun in it. The brightness rises and falls over minutes, like light under high cloud, and everything below — towers, terraces, pools — swims in the slow tide of it.

The floor of the cavern, the **Floor**, is a plain of mud towers stretching to the haze. They stand hut-high and shoulder-to-shoulder in the crowded districts, near-touching, layered, a city built by no hands. They are made of grout — mud, spit, chitin meal, layer on layer, each layer a slightly different color, like growth rings. The oldest spires have drunk the mineral seeps long enough to turn to stone, and stand pale and fluted among their soft young neighbors. The walls of the cavern itself are sharp-edged, unrounded, raw. This rock is young. A stalactite here can grow a hand's length in a season, because the seeps are warm and mineral-sick, and the masons encourage it — they channel drips onto their towers' shoulders so that stone grows over mud. They are farming rock. The whole place is under construction and always has been: a building site the size of a county, worked by animals.

Water is everywhere and nowhere, on a schedule. Warm seeps braid down the east wall and gather into the **Weep**, a river that is sometimes a river. When it runs, it spreads through the **Shallows** in a maze of dam-chains and channels the masons have kept up for no one knows how long; when it fails, it vanishes into swallow-holes and leaves behind a cracked flat that shines like glaze, and the plain smells of wet dust. Everything is damp to the touch. Condensation beads on every surface, and every so often the ceiling unloads it all at once in a warm drizzle that drums for an hour and stops.

The builders are the **masons** — pale, soft-bodied things as long as a finger, moving in rivers of themselves across the Floor. No green thing has ever grown here; their gardens are cells of white loom-fungus, fed on seep-water and chitin meal, on their own shed skins and their own dead. The whole economy runs on warm water, mineral, and each other. On the tower tops they plant the glow-grubs, tend them, feed them, and the grubs give light. A bright tower is a rich tower. Cutting a grub-cluster is theft, and theft is answered with war — front lines of drummers that advance like weather.

And the drumming: masons strike their heads against the grout, and the whole city is tuned to it. It sounds like rain on a tin roof, if the rain were fists. It carries territory, alarm, warning. It is the carrier wave the whole cave hums on.

Scattered among the towers live the **kex** — pink-grey, big-eyed, cat-sized, with long clever fingers. They are the only tool-users here. They flake pebbles with hammer-stones; they stop clay cups with gum and carry stolen glow in them, little hand-lanterns bobbing between the towers at the dark hours. They stack stones in cairns that mean something — territory, jokes, prayers, no one asks. They scratch tallies into soft mud walls before the sinter hardens. They steal, they beg, they are tolerated like rats in a city, and their leavings — flaked stone, a notched bone, a cup, a cairn — are the only built things here that masons didn't make. There is no metal in the Kiln. There is no wheel. Everything is hand-made, or mouth-made.

The cave breathes. Warm air rises in slow tides, and the weather is the swarm's mood: when a hatch comes boiling up off the tower tops — a bronze cloud of billions, with a sound like a fire catching — the updrafts pull the ceiling-mist upward and the rain falls sideways, or not at all, and the light thickens until the far side of the cavern is erased entirely. Floods come on their own pulse, a brown hymn moving through the towers, and the dams hold or they don't, and a channel you walked yesterday becomes a wall, and a new pool shines where a path was. Geography here is a weather system. Maps rot.

Other life crowds every layer. **Chalk-goats** — pallid, six-legged grazers — crop the seep-films on the terraces and leap the sinter dykes like, well, goats. The pools cloud with shrimpsnow and needle-eels. Dust-mites fill everything. And there is at least one thing here big enough that the drums go quiet when it passes. The masons stop mid-stride. The kex climb high and freeze. What it eats, no one knows; what it is, no one has seen whole. The kex say it tends the deepest seep, where the heat scalds and the oldest grubs hang in curtains and the drums hold one slow constant note — the note they call the nursery-rhyme. Beneath that, they say, something is gestating. The warmth, the swarms, the endless building — all midwifery. The cave is a nursery, and the child is not yet born.

The sound is the true sense here. Sight is flat, shadowless; the cave is made of echoes. You learn to read the water's voice for where the Floor lies, the drums for colony and mood, the vent-whistles — warm air through fissures, a chord that changes with the weather — for what's coming. And silence for the tall thing.

The rules, as the kex live them:

- The light is owned. Carry your own if you must, but buy it. Don't cut it.
- The map is a lie. Follow the water. Ask the drums.
- Nothing rots here; everything gets eaten. Leave nothing sweet or soft unattended — including your dead.
- Don't go into the **Unglow**, the dark crevices where no grubs hang. The dark here is not empty.
- When the drums stop, be still.
- The warmth deepens as you go down. The lowest seeps will scald.

From the **Dry Rise**, among the crumbling old towers, you can watch a flood come — the brown hymn moving through the city of mud, the swarm rising to meet it, the light thickening until there is no far side, no ceiling, no stone, only a warm bright roaring everywhere, and the rain starting up like the cave clearing its throat.

You realize you were never in a landscape. You are inside a body. It is warm, it is loud, and it is busy being born.
