The seed is the world.

It is a seed of no known species — or of a species so old that no record of it exists, or of a species that never existed in the light. It is large. From inside, its size is impossible to determine. The shell is thick, woody, impermeable — a wall of lignin and cellulose compressed to the density of bone, of stone, of time. The shell is the boundary of existence. Beyond it, nothing. Or something that does not matter. Or something that cannot be imagined.

Inside the seed, there is a hollow.

The hollow is the chamber. It is a single vast cavity at the heart of the seed — a bubble of nothing that formed as the seed dried and its tissues contracted, or that was always there, a void at the center of the thing. The chamber is roughly ovoid, or irregular, or shaped like the inside of a body — rounded, curved, flowing. Its dimensions are impossible to judge in the absolute darkness, but it is large. Large enough to contain a city. Large enough that the far wall is lost in blackness, and the ceiling is lost, and the floor extends into darkness in all directions.

The walls of the chamber are the seed's interior tissue. Endosperm, or cotyledon, or something else — the nutritive tissue that once fed the embryo, or would have fed it, or was meant to feed it. The tissue is dense, starchy, ancient. It has dried over eons into a hard, woody material — like lignin, like amber, like bone, like the heartwood of a tree that has been dead for a thousand years. The tissue is not dead. Not entirely. It is dormant, nearly dead, but still alive in a slow, barely-there way. It breathes — impossibly slowly. It contracts and expands by fractions of a millimeter over the course of a year. It is the slowest breath in the world.

The walls are smooth. Eons of slow cellular turnover, of chemical weathering, of the seed's own subtle movements, have worn the walls to a polished, almost glassy finish. There are no sharp edges anywhere. No corners. No angles. Everything is rounded, curved, eroded. The chamber has settled into its final form. The erosion is complete. The chamber is old beyond measure — ancient, primordial, the oldest stable state. It has been this shape for longer than anything can remember. It will be this shape until the seed germinates or decays.

The chamber is dark.

The darkness is absolute. No light enters. The seed's shell is opaque, and there are no openings, no cracks, no pores through which light could pass. There is no bioluminescence. No phosphorescence. No light at all. The darkness is complete and ancient. It has never been broken. Anything that lives here has never known light. The darkness is not the absence of light — it is the presence of darkness, a substance, a medium, a condition of existence.

The temperature is cool. Stable. The seed's shell insulates the chamber from the outside world. The temperature is the temperature of dormancy — cool, not cold. Like a cellar. Like a cave. Like the inside of a body. It never changes. There are no seasons, no days, no nights. The temperature is constant, patient, ancient.

The air is dry. Extremely dry. The seed has been desiccated for eons. The moisture is gone — all of it, or almost all of it. The tissue is hard and dry. The air is thin, still, dry. No water. No humidity. No condensation. The dryness is absolute. The air tastes of dust, of resin, of old wood, of the inside of a seed. It is the driest air in the world.

And the chamber is loud.

The sound is the most striking thing about the place. In a dark, dry, empty, ancient space, there should be silence. But there is no silence. The chamber is a resonant cavity, and it resonates. The seed's slow breathing — the contraction and expansion of the tissue — creates a constant, low, deep creaking. The sound is like wood contracting. Like a ship's hull in a slow swell. Like ice settling. Like the groan of a tree in wind. It is loud — not deafening, but omnipresent, filling the space, impossible to escape. The sound is the voice of the seed. It is the sound of time passing.

There are other sounds. The city creaks in its own voice — higher, thinner, more complex. The structures settle and shift. The tissue cracks, slowly, as it contracts. The air moves through the passages, creating low, breath-like sighs. The sound is layered, textured, ancient. It is the voice of the place. Anything that lives here hears the creaking as the background of existence — the sound against which all other sounds are measured. The sound is so constant that it becomes silence. The sound is so loud that it becomes stillness.

The chamber is not empty. It contains a city.

The city is grown, not built. Or built with such patience that the distinction has dissolved. The city is made of the same tissue as the walls — the same dense, woody, ancient material. It is part of the seed, or a symbiont of the seed, or a parasite, or a separate organism that has fused with the seed over eons. The city rises from the chamber floor, clings to the lower walls, hangs from the ceiling in places. It is architecture — chambers, passages, platforms, bridges, towers, vaults. But it is also organism. It is alive, in the same slow, barely-there way as the seed. It grows. It breathes. It creaks.

The city is vast in extent but sparse in substance. The chamber is enormous, and the city occupies only a fraction of it. Structures are scattered across the floor, the walls, the ceiling, with vast empty spaces between them. The density is very low. The city is more like a handful of buildings in a cavern the size of a world. Or like a few thoughts in a vast, slow mind. The emptiness is part of the city's character. The city is mostly space. The city is mostly absence. The presences are small, isolated, patient.

The architecture is organic, grown, not constructed. Passages curve and branch like capillaries, like roots, like the chambers of a heart. Rooms are rounded, eroded, smooth-walled — no corners, no angles, everything curved. Platforms are thin, curved shells of tissue, grown out from the walls like shelves, like brackets, like fungal fruiting bodies. Bridges are narrow, arched spans, grown across gaps, their surfaces worn smooth by ages of use. Towers are hollow columns, rising from the floor or hanging from the ceiling, their walls thin and resonant — they hum and creak with the seed's breathing. The city is a body. The city is an organism. The city is a slow, patient, ancient thing.

The construction is primitive. Simple. Hand-crafted. If there are inhabitants — and it is not clear that there are — they use simple tools. Blades of stone, or hardened resin, or dense bone. They carve, they cut, they scrape, they shape. They graft, they tie, they bend. They guide the growth of the city with cuts and bindings, with pressure and tension. Over generations, the city grows into the shapes they desire. The tech is simple. The craft is manual. The city is the product of patience, not power. There is no metal. No machine. No engine. No fire — or if there is fire, it is small, precious, rare, and the smoke has nowhere to go. The city is shaped by hand, by tool, by time.

The city is old. Ancient. The erosion is complete. The structures are worn smooth, fused with the walls, settled into their final form. The city has stopped growing. Or it grows so slowly that growth is indistinguishable from stasis. The city is in its final form. The city is dying, or has already died, or is in a state of near-death dormancy like the seed itself. The city is deeply aged, near-flattened, settled into its final shape. It is the end of life, or the oldest stable state, or both.

The ecology is simple. The seed. The city. The tissue. There is nothing else. No animals. No insects. No birds. No fish. No worms. No bacteria that can be detected — or if there are, they are too slow, too sparse, too ancient to be distinguished from the tissue itself. The ecology is the seed and the city, locked in their slow, ancient symbiosis. The city feeds on the seed, or the seed feeds on the city, or they feed on each other, or they are simply two aspects of the same slow organism. The city may be a gall — a growth induced by a parasite that no longer exists, or that exists only as the city itself. The city may be a cancer — a growth that has outlived its purpose. The city may be the seed's own architecture — the structure it grew for itself, for reasons that are lost to time.

The city may be inhabited. There are signs — or there are not. Passages that seem too regular, too purposeful. Chambers that seem too shaped, too designed. Platforms at heights that suggest someone stands on them. Notches and grooves that suggest tools, hands, use. Scratches on the walls that might be writing, or decoration, or the marks of growth. But no inhabitants are seen. Or if they are seen, they are not animals. They are slow, dense, ancient things. They are part of the city, or they are the city. They are the city's awareness, or they are separate organisms that have grown into the city's tissues. They are patient beyond measure. They think slowly. They move slowly. They are the city, and the city is them. Or they are absent, and the city is empty, and the city grew itself for no purpose, and there is no one. The city is a body without a mind. The city is a mind without a body. The city is a slow, patient, ancient thing, and it is alone in the darkness.

The rules of the world are simple. Time is slow. Change is slow. The seed breathes once a year. The city grows a millimeter a century. The erosion is complete. The chamber is in its final form. The world is ending, or has ended, or is in a state of near-death dormancy that will last until the seed germinates or decays. The world is patient. The world is ancient. The world is dark, cool, dry, loud, empty, and alive.

The world is hidden. The chamber is a pocket, a bubble, an intimate room within the seed. The seed is somewhere — in a vault, in the ground, in a museum, in the void. But the chamber is the world. The chamber is the entire universe. There is nothing outside. There is no outside. The seed's shell is the boundary of existence. Beyond it is nothing, or something that cannot be imagined, or something that does not matter. The chamber is the world. The world is the chamber.

The world is strange. The world is a seed. The world is a city. The world is a body. The world is a mind. The world is a slow, patient, ancient thing, creaking in the darkness, growing in the silence, waiting for something that will never come.
