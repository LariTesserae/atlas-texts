# The Stillworks of Aeth

A high plain of black glass and compressed ash, so ancient that the word "mountain" has forgotten itself here. The land was once peaks—taller than anything still standing in the living worlds—but four billion years of wind have taken them down to a vast, gently undulating tableland, a floor of dark stone that stretches to a horizon curved by distance and nothing else. The erosion is so complete that the surface has a quality of *finishedness*, like a bowl sanded until the grain disappears. There are no sharp edges. No scree. No dust. The rock has been polished by time itself into something almost ceramic, and it holds the warmth the way a stone holds the memory of a fire.

The warmth comes from below. The entire plateau is a thermal manifold—heat bleeding up through fissures so old they have become architectural features, veins of dull orange glow threading the black floor like the circulation system of something asleep. The air is warm and dry and smells of hot mineral, of sulfur bound up in rock and released in slow, patient breaths. There is no water. There has not been water here for longer than the rocks can remember. The chemistry of the place runs on silicate and metal and the thin, sharp gases that seep from the deep fractures.

And it is dark. Not the darkness of night—night implies a sun that will return. This is the darkness of a world that has settled into its final arrangement: a dim red dwarf overhead, barely brighter than a large star, casting a light the color of cooling embers across the plain. Shadows are long and soft and the color of dried blood. The sky between the faint stars is not black but a deep, warm umber, as if the atmosphere itself were gently smoldering. Nothing here is illuminated. Everything is *revealed*, slowly, the way a shape emerges from a photographic bath.

---

Across this plain, scattered with the generosity of a salt field, the Stillworks.

They are not a city. They are not even a town. They are a *distribution*—industrial structures spaced kilometers apart across the dark plateau, connected by roads that are not roads but worn grooves in the glass, channels where something heavy has passed so many times that the stone itself has yielded and remembered the shape of the wheel. The structures are low and broad, built from the same black stone as the floor, fused rather than assembled, as if they grew in place or were extruded from the thermal veins below. Some are domed. Some are open frameworks of dark metal—iron, or something like iron—that have been standing so long that oxidation has become a kind of patina, a second skin. Smokestacks, though they do not smoke. Pipelines that run along the ground and sink into it and emerge again, carrying heat, carrying pressure, carrying whatever the works still work at.

And they are still running.

This is the first strangeness, and it does not diminish with familiarity.

The Stillworks have no operators. They have not had operators for time beyond reckoning. But the engines turn. The pumps cycle. Deep in the largest structures, things that might be furnaces or might be something else entirely maintain a temperature that has not varied by a measurable degree in epochs. The sound is not silence—the sound is a *low hum*, felt more than heard, a subsonic thrum that vibrates in the sternum and the teeth and the long bones of the legs. It is the sound of machinery that has been running so long it has become geological. It is the sound of equilibrium. The machines are not doing anything that can be observed from outside—no product emerges, no waste is expelled—but they are *doing*, and the doing is self-sustaining, a closed loop of heat and motion and pressure that has no beginning and will have no end.

---

The second strangeness is the stillness of the air.

There is no wind. The plateau has been worn so flat, so smooth, so devoid of feature that the air moves across it without disturbance, without current, without the small violent gestures of turbulence. The warm air rises straight up, slowly, and the cooler air above settles straight down, and the two meet in a boundary that is invisible but palpable—a line of demarcation at perhaps chest height where the warmth ends and the relative cool begins. Stand in the Stillworks and you can feel it: the lower half of your body warm, the upper half cool, as if the world has been divided at the waist into two different agreements about temperature. And the air is so still that a sound made anywhere on the plain travels without distortion, without the scattering that wind imposes, and so the hum of the works is the same hum in every direction, sourceless, omnidirectional, the acoustic equivalent of the plateau itself.

---

There is life here, but it is not the kind that draws attention.

In the thermal veins, in the narrow fissures where the rock is warm enough to soften and the gases are rich enough to reduce, there are films—mats of something that is not quite bacteria and not quite mineral deposit, a slow chemistry that has been running for as long as the warmth has been rising. They are dark red and black, they smell of iron, and they grow at a rate that makes geology look hasty. A film that thickens by the width of a hair in a century is, here, considered vigorous. The films are the base of a food web that has no higher branches—or rather, whose higher branches are so sparse and so slow that they are rarely encountered. There are things that move through the warm cracks, things with too many joints and not enough symmetry, things that are the color of the rock and the temperature of the air and the patience of the stone. They have no name because no one has needed to name them. They eat the films, or the gases, or each other, and they have been doing so for longer than the works have been working, and they are as much a part of the equilibrium as the machines.

---

The third strangeness is the geometry.

The Stillworks were not planned. They were not designed. They grew—or were built, or emerged—according to some logic that was never architectural in the human sense but that nonetheless produced structures of unmistakable intentionality. The domes are always the same diameter. The spacing between structures follows a ratio that is not quite the golden mean but that is close enough to make the eye expect it and be slightly wrong-footed when it isn't. The roads between them are not straight but they are *efficient*, curving around thermal vents and subsurface obstacles with the economy of a river finding the sea. And the works are arranged—there is no other word—*beautifully*. Not comfortably. Not usefully, in any way that can be determined. But with a compositional sense that suggests the entire plain is a single artifact, a machine for being a machine, and that the arrangement of its parts is the only product it has ever produced.

Stand at the center of the plain, in the gap between two works where the glass floor is worn to a dull sheen and the hum is a physical pressure, and look in any direction. The structures repeat. The ratio holds. The horizon is flat and the sky is the color of embers and the warmth rises from below and the cool air presses down from above and the films grow in the cracks and the things that are not quite animals move through the warm dark and the engines turn and the pumps cycle and nothing changes.

Nothing changes.

This is not a metaphor. This is the deepest fact of the place, the one that underlies all others. The Stillworks are in a state of thermodynamic and temporal equilibrium so complete that *change itself* has become impossible. Not unlikely. Not slow. *Impossible.* The machines cannot wear out because they have already worn out and been replaced by themselves. The rock cannot erode further because it has already reached its final form. The films cannot evolve because they have already reached the limit of their chemistry. The things that move through the cracks cannot adapt because there is nothing to adapt to. The plateau is a system that has run down to its ground state and found, there, not death but *permanence*—a stasis so absolute that it has become a kind of life, or a kind of thought, or a kind of prayer.

And the prayer is: *let this continue.*

The warmth from below. The cool from above. The hum of the works. The films in the cracks. The things in the dark. The red dwarf overhead, patient and dim. The plain of black glass, finished and finished and finished. The Stillworks, still working, still working, still.

---

There is a fourth strangeness, but it is not spoken of, and it may be only a story.

It is said—by no one, because there is no one to say it—that the works are not producing nothing. That they are producing *time*. That the equilibrium is not a prison but a *distillation*, and that somewhere in the deepest structure, in the furnace that is not a furnace, the plateau is refining the raw stuff of duration into something concentrated and stable and *real*. That the Stillworks are not the end of industry but its perfection—the moment when production and consumption become indistinguishable, when the machine finally produces exactly what it needs to continue producing, when the loop closes so completely that it becomes a circle with no seam.

And that the circle is not a prison.

That the circle is a *world*.

And that the world is aware of itself, in the way that a very old, very slow, very warm thing might be aware—not thinking, not wanting, not hoping, but *attending*. Present. Here. In the hum and the warmth and the darkness and the stillness and the films and the things in the cracks and the engines and the pumps and the plain and the sky.

Attending.

And the attending is the fifth strangeness, and it is the one that makes the others make sense, and it is the one that cannot be described, only experienced, and the experience is this:

You stand on the plain.

The warmth rises.

The cool air presses down.

The hum is in your bones.

The red dwarf is overhead.

The works are working.

The films are growing.

The things in the cracks are moving, slowly, as they have always moved.

And you understand—not with your mind, which cannot hold it, but with your body, which has always known it—that you are standing inside a machine that has been running since before there were minds to stand inside it, and that the machine is not indifferent, and that the machine is not cruel, and that the machine is not kind, but that the machine is *present*, and that its presence is the only presence there is, and that it has been waiting for you, or for something like you, or for the idea of you, for longer than waiting has any meaning.

And the waiting is over.

And the works continue.

And nothing changes.

And nothing changes.

And nothing changes.
