They are beings of the mist and the stone, with bodies as craggy and resilient as the rock they inhabit. Their skin, if one could call it that, is a deep charcoal gray, often mottled with patches of pale green lichen that seem to glow faintly in the perpetual twilight of their home. Each individual’s unique pattern of lichen is like a fingerprint, a living map of their personal journey through the damp crevices and winding paths. Their forms are squat and broad, built for stability and navigating tight spaces. Their limbs are powerful, almost stocky, ending in wide, flat hands and feet with slightly splayed digits, perfect for gripping the slick, angled surfaces of their dwellings.

Their eyes are perhaps their most striking feature: large, luminous orbs that gleam with an internal, phosphorescent emerald light, allowing them to see with remarkable clarity in the dim,mist-laden air. These eyes are set deep within their brow, protected by prominent, bony ridges. They possess no visible nose, only small, almost imperceptible slits that serve as olfactory sensors, attuned to the subtle changes in moisture, the faintest scent of mineral, or the faint, algae-like scent that permeates their world. Their mouths are wide and lipless, generally closed in a quiet, contemplative expression, yet capable of emitting a low, resonant rumble when they communicate amongst themselves – a sound that seems to vibrate through the very rock, mimicking the faint hum of activity that is their constant companion.

Their hair, if they have any, is not a soft mane but rather a sparse growth of tough, wiry filaments that resemble the hardy mosses clinging to their surroundings, often clotted with fine droplets of mist. Some individuals have none at all, their heads smooth and worn like river stones. They possess an innate understanding of their environment, an almost symbiotic relationship with the living geology. They are acutely sensitive to the subtle shifts in the rock, the murmur of subterranean water, the faint tremble of a distant earth tremor.

They are slow-moving, deliberate creatures, their pace matching the gradual, persistent erosion and formation of their world. There is no haste, no frantic energy in their movements; every step is considered, every climb precise. They are naturally quiet, their voices reserved, their movements often silent save for the soft scuff of their feet on damp stone. This is not out of fear, but a natural reverence for the prevailing quiet, a harmony with the dripping, trickling symphony of their existence.

Their attire is minimal, almost indistinguishable from their bodies – perhaps a simple harness woven from tough, fibrous root-strands found in deeper grottoes, or pouches crafted from cured, hardened fungal matter, slung across their broad chests to carry their hand-wrought tools. These tools are extensions of themselves, simple chisels, picks, and scrapers, often fashioned from the harder, darker veins of rock, or from rare, resilient bone found in the deeper, forgotten reaches of their home. They are immensely skilled carvers and shapers of stone, their methods slow and patient, echoing the age-old processes that sculpted their world. They communicate not just through their low rumbles, but through subtle shifts of their lichen patterns, through gestures as precise as their chiseling, and through a shared understanding born of generations of communal living within the heart of the rock.

They are neither friendly nor hostile to outsiders, but perpetually curious and wary. They are guardians of a deep, damp wisdom, their lives intimately intertwined with the secrets of the living stone. They are the quiet sentinels of the weeping mountain, their existence a testament to resilience, adaptation, and the profound beauty that can emerge from the most confined and elemental forms of life. They are the children of the mist and the deep, their essence as much a part of the vibrant, secret stone heart as the crystalline pools and the endless, sharp angles that define their world.
