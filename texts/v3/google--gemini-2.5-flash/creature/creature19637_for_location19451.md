They are of the quiet. Not merely quiet by disposition, but fundamentally interwoven with the silence of their living world. Their voices, when they are heard at all, are not designed to carry far. They are soft, resonant whispers, like the rustling of large, supple leaves, or the gentle hum of a deep root drawing water. Communication is often in subtle gesture, in the way a hand, long and slender, brushes against a moss-covered column, or the slight inclination of a head adorned with fibrous, almost fungal, protrusions that serve less as ornament and more as extensions of their sensory perception.

Their forms are a testament to adaptation and symbiosis. They are tall, perhaps even lanky, their limbs possessing a graceful elasticity. Their skin is not skin as we understand it, but a continuous membrane that is slightly porous, cool to the touch, and often flecked with subtle variegations of green, brown, and grey, allowing them to fade effortlessly into the organic architecture they inhabit. One could imagine their epidermis having a texture not unlike the smoothed, resin-coated pathways or the undersides of the leathery leaves that form their ceilings. There is an almost translucent quality to their extremities, hinting at the chlorophyll that courses beneath, giving their blood a verdant hue.

Their eyes are large and dark, reflecting the dappled light of their world with an ancient, knowing patience. They do not blink often, their gaze seeming to absorb rather than merely observe. These eyes might possess multiple pupils, or perhaps a nictitating membrane that provides not protection from dust, but a subtle filtering of the intense photosynthetic glow from the canopy.

Their physiology is intimately bound to the plant-city. They do not eat in a conventional sense; instead, they absorb nutrients directly from the rich, damp air and the specialized substrates embedded within their homes. Perhaps they have an internal, biotechnical process mirroring the organic pumps, drawing sustenance from the omnipresent, nutrient-rich moisture. Their breath is not a sharp intake and release, but a slow, continuous exchange, merging with the exhalations of oxygen and vapor from the bio-engines. They move with a deliberate slowness, a grace born of efficiency, their footsteps almost soundless on the polished plant-matter. Their bones, if they possess them, are not rigid calcium, but a fibrous, flexible network, allowing them to bend and twist with serpentine ease, navigating the winding corridors and ascending the woven pathways with effortless fluidity.

Their technology is an extension of themselves, and themselves an extension of their environment. The bioluminescent fibers that criss-cross their city might be partially alive within them, illuminating intricate patterns on their skin, perhaps even changing color to reflect their mood or status. They might possess an innate understanding of the plant processes, able to communicate silently with the massive, living structures, easing their growth or guiding their direction. The "bio-fabrication" is not a chore but a meditative art, a process of co-creation where their touch coaxes and nurtures the living materials into desired forms.

Their garments, if worn, are not textiles but naturally grown filaments, draped and woven to mimic the forms of the plant life around them. They are not for warmth, for the air is always temperate, but perhaps for subtle ceremonial purposes, or to define subtle social distinctions. They might wear no adornments at all, their own forms so perfectly integrated with their world that external distinction is unnecessary.

They are few in number, reflecting the sparse density of their city. Each individual is a vital node in the living network, a caretaker and a conduit. Their lives are long, measured not in years but in the slow, deliberate growth cycles of their architecture. They are born, perhaps, from specialized flowering chambers within the plant-structures, emerging fully functional and instinctively attuned to their environment. Death, when it comes, is not a dramatic event, but a gentle reabsorption, their bodies dissolving back into the rich earth, feeding the very plants that sustained them in life, a final, complete integration into the grand, continuous cycle.

They are the cultivators of equilibrium, the silent guardians of a biomechanical perfection. They do not strive for conquest or expansion, for their world provides all they need. Their purpose is simply to maintain, to nurture, and to understand the intricate dance of life and process that defines their exquisite, living cathedral. They are the soul of the damp, green hum.
