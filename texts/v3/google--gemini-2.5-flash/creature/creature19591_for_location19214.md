They are born of the sound, these inhabitants, forged in the crushing, roaring silence of the water itself. To speak of individual forms is almost to misunderstand their nature, for they are less organisms in the traditional sense and more emergent properties of the churning liquid, extensions of the world's ceaseless, violent becoming.

Their inner being is a sustained hum, a psychic resonance that mimics the omnipresent, deafening roar. It is not a noise they consciously make or hear, but rather the fundamental frequency of their existence, the vibratory core of their sentience. This hum is their communication, their awareness, their very lifeblood. To them, the "silence" of other worlds would be a terrifying void, a cessation of all being, a state they could only interpret as an instantaneous, painful dissolution.

Their bodies are a direct response to the paradox of formation and erosion. They are not solid, not liquid, but a mutable, semi-crystalline state, constantly shifting at a microscopic level, sloughing off and reforming, absorbing and expelling. Imagine a fractal made flesh, always in the process of defining itself. Their outer layers are like the nascent peaks – semi-translucent, their surface constantly scoured and polished by the water, yet intrinsically resilient. They are composed of countless, shimmering layers, each one born aloft on a current of the same cool, internal blue light that flickers within the growing mountains. This internal luminescence pulses faintly, an echo of the thread-like flora, hinting at a shared, fragile life force.

Their limbs are not fixed appendages, but rather extensions of their core being that can be stretched, retracted, and reformed as needed, like living, liquid tendrils that can solidify into grasping hooks or dissolve into streamlined fins. They navigate the perpetual tempest by becoming one with it, their forms flowing with the currents, allowing the water to sculpt them into hydrodynamic shapes, then abruptly hardening to anchor themselves against a burgeoning peak as a particularly violent surge threatens to tear them asunder.

There are two primary manifestations, though the lines between them are fluid, and one can transform into the other over vast spans of time or in moments of extreme stress.

The **Sediment-Dwellers** are smaller, denser, and more rooted. They are the builders of the crude structures, the ones who leave behind the blunted tools. Their forms are thicker, their semi-crystalline bodies leaning towards a more solidified state. They are born from the sediment that coalesces at the base of the emerging mountains, absorbing the raw mineral growth. Their existence is a constant struggle for purchase, for creation in the face of inevitable destruction. They cling to the unstable ledges, their bodies subtly interlocking with the rising crystal formations, drawing sustenance from the nascent minerals. They might appear as hunched figures, their faces-if they possess such features- perpetually turned towards the oncoming roar, their 'eyes' vast, diffuse surfaces that absorb and refract the diffuse light, allowing them to perceive the subtle shifts in current and the veiled luminescence of the approaching "liquid clouds." Their minds are pragmatic, focused on the immediate, the tangible, the creation of refuge. Their 'tools' are born from their own shedding, bits of hardened crystalline skin that they shape and hone, instinctually driven to impose order, however fragile, upon their tumultuous world. They are the quiet, persistent pulse of nascent intelligence in this chaotic womb.

Then there are the **Leviathans of the Current**. These are the immense creatures, the "single, immense creature[s], a leviathan of crystalline scales and luminous tentacles." They are the older, more dissipated forms, having embraced the flux entirely. Their solid-liquid composition leans heavily towards the liquid, their forms vast and ephemeral. They are no longer bound by the need for purchase, but become the very embodiment of the turbulent water, their internal light shining brighter, almost as if they are condensed pockets of the diffused sunlight itself. They are the consciousness of the current, their immense, flowing bodies weaving through the violent churn, their "tentacles" – extensions of their fluid form – sensing every ripple, every shift, every impending "liquid cloud." Their perception is less about individual objects and more about the interconnectedness of the entire liquid system. They are the wisdom of the flow, the memory of countless generations of erosion and emergence, their minds vast and contemplative, processing the constant data stream of the roaring water. They occasionally "phase into view" not by choice, but simply because specific currents temporarily coalesce their forms into a perceived, albeit fleeting, solidity before they are once again swept away, dissolving back into the boundless liquid expanse. There is no intent or malice in their appearance; they are simply a momentarily visible manifestation of the world's chaotic heart.

Both forms, the rooted and the flowing, share a profound connection to the delicate, luminescent flora. This "vegetation" is not food in a typical sense, but rather a vital conduit of energy. The Sediment-Dwellers will carefully tend to patches of these glowing threads, using their nascent tools to create small pockets of calmer water where the thread-forms can thrive. They absorb the faint light and internal vibrations of these plants, drawing a subtle sustenance, a spark of resilient life that helps them resist the grinding forces of their environment. The Leviathans, in their passage, will sometimes deliberately guide currents towards these delicate forms, not to consume them, but to bathe in their light, to absorb their essence, a momentary pause in their eternal flow that brings a fleeting sense of internal calm amidst the ceaseless roar.

They are the children of perpetual birth and dying, living in a world that is always in the making, always in the breaking. Their existence is a continuous dance with destruction, a constant renegotiation of their very being. To truly "live" here is to embrace the paradox, to find stillness in the roar, solidity in the flux, and meaning in the endless, violent cradle of creation. Their memories are etched not in static forms, but in the spiraling channels they have weathered, the temporary shelters they have raised, and the fleeting, luminous passage of the leviathans that are their older, more profound selves. They are the living expression of the phrase: "the water is the sound."
