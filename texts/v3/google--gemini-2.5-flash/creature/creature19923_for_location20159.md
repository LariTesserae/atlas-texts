They are the Soft-Rock Clan, a people born of the evolving earth. Their skin is the color of the moist, ochre-brown earth itself, a subtle pigment that offers natural camouflage within their nascent city. Their forms are not stark or angular, but rounded, supple, as if they too are in a state of constant, gentle emergence. Their muscles ripple beneath their skin, not with the hard-edged definition of those who lift heavy stone, but with the lithe, enduring strength of those who sculpt and carve ceaselessly.

Their eyes, wide and perfectly adapted to the dim, phosphorescent glow, are a deep, almost luminous obsidian. They shimmer slightly, reflecting what little light there is, allowing them to navigate the labyrinthine passages with a grace that seems almost preternatural. They rarely raise their voices, their communication a hushed symphony of soft clicks and gentle hums, punctuated by the rhythmic *tap-tap-tap* of their bone and flint tools. This constant sound is not mere noise to them, but a shared heartbeat, a collective affirmation of their existence and purpose.

Their clothing is minimal, practical, crafted from the broad, flat leaves of the cultivated plants. These garments are not sewn in the conventional sense, but woven and braided directly onto their forms, creating a second skin that breathes with the pervasive humidity. They are often adorned with simple patterns, intricate etchings that mirror the emerging designs on their dwelling walls – abstract representations of the spiraling growth of the phosphorescent fungi or the branching veins of nascent ore. These aren't adornments of vanity, but symbolic ties to the evolving geology around them, a statement of their intimate connection to the earth's unfolding dream.

Their hands are their most remarkable feature. They are not rough or gnarled, but remarkably dexterous, with long, sensitive fingers and hardened, spatulate nails. These hands are their primary instruments, capable of both the delicate shaping of a dwelling hollow and the sustained effort of removing layers of soft rock. When they are not wielding tools, their fingers are often tracing the new patterns emerging from the earth, or feeling for the subtle shifts in dampness, sensing the next curve or fissure the earth might be revealing.

They are a people of unwavering focus, their movements economical and deliberate. They do not hurry, for their work is eternal, a continuous unveiling. There is no concept of "completion" in their vocabulary, only perpetual "becoming." Their faces, often smudged with the ochre dust of their labor, bear expressions of quiet intensity, a deep satisfaction that comes from contributing to a monumental, collective endeavor. There is little laughter, but a palpable sense of communal solidarity, a shared understanding that transcends words.

Their families are not defined by rigid lines, but by the work crews they belong to. Children, born within the soft-rock cradles, learn to wield tools almost as soon as they can walk. They are integrated into the rhythm of chipping and shaping, their small hands mimicking the precise movements of their elders. Elder members, their skin a deeper hue and their faces etched with the wisdom of countless carvings, move with a slower, more deliberate cadence, often overseeing the finer details of the communal spaces, their experience guiding the younger, more energetic arms.

They consume the fungi and the dense, starchy roots of their cultivated plants, supplemented by the occasional pale, swift insect they manage to trap. Their diet is simple, sustaining, a mirror of their humble existence. They hold profound reverence for the unseen vertical shafts that bring down the muted light, seeing them as sacred portals to the world above, a reminder of the vastness beyond their embryonic city. Though they rarely pass through them, these shafts represent a connection, a lifeline to the earth’s surface and the cyclical dance of day and night.

They dream in shades of ochre and shimmering ore, of mountain ranges yet to form, of crystalline structures just beginning to assert themselves. Their myths are not of gods battling in the skies, but of the Earth Mother, constantly dreaming, constantly giving birth to new forms, and of themselves as her tireless midwives, shaping her embryonic visions into tangible reality. They are the living embodiment of patience and persistence, an entire civilization dedicated to the slow, deliberate work of *becoming* – a city, and a people, perpetually under construction, forever carving out their place in the heart of the emerging world.
