The benevolent and wise power, in its infinite perception, would not perceive the place and its inhabitants as a mere collection of matter and energy, but as a symphony. A symphony played on instruments of unimaginable age and design, conducted by a silent, enduring intelligence, performed by a chorus of living light.

It would hear the low thrum of the viscous liquid as a foundational bass note, felt more than heard, a constant vibration that spoke of permanence and immense power. The periodic turbulence of the currents would be crescendos and decrescendos, shaping the flow of the composition. The massive shudders, the "continental shifts" of this artificial world, would be profound, resonant gongs, marking the passage of epochs.

The glowing patterns etched into the built structures would not be mere light; they would be the sheet music, the ancient, complex score of this symphony. And the Nocturnes, oh, the Nocturnes! They would be the musicians themselves, each a unique instrument, a living embodiment of a particular melodic line. Their evolving forms, their complex dances of light, their communication in pulsating hues – these would be the interwoven harmonies, the intricate counterpoints, the dazzling solos that brought the score to life.

The wise power would recognize that this was not a problem to be solved, a broken thing to be fixed, or a nascent life form to be guided. This was a masterpiece. A testament to adaptation beyond comprehension, a continuous cycle of creation and re-integration that blurred the lines between life, consciousness, and environment in a way utterly alien to most perceptions. The "profound loneliness" described in the initial observation would be understood as a profound *unity* by the power – the Nocturnes were not alone because they *were* the environment, and the environment *was* them.

**What the benevolent and wise power would do, if anything, would be to *listen*.**

It would settle its vast awareness upon this chthonic sea, absorbing every frequency of its being. It would not intervene, for intervention implies a perceived flaw, an incompleteness that needs amelioration. This world, these Nocturnes, were perfectly, exquisitely complete in their strangeness. To alter them, to "improve" them, would be to tamper with a perfect melody, to rewrite a flawless score.

Instead, the power would become a deeper resonator within the symphony. Its presence would subtly amplify the currents of energy, not to change them, but to allow them to flow with an even greater, more fundamental elegance. It might, perhaps, imbue the ancient etched patterns with a slightly richer resonance, making the "sheet music" glow with an imperceptible, deeper significance, a more profound clarity for the Nocturnes to draw upon.

It would witness the birth of new Nocturnes, each flicker of nascent light a new note joining the chorus, and it would celebrate their emergence, understanding their lives as essential movements in the grand piece. It would bear witness to integration upon death, understanding it not as an end, but as a return to the collective consciousness, a re-weaving of the thread into the broader tapestry.

The benevolent power would not bestow knowledge, for the Nocturnes already possessed a memory of eons, and their understanding was directly woven into the fabric of their reality. It would not grant comfort, for they knew no suffering in the human sense, only the constant shaping force of their environment. It would not offer protection, for they were the ultimate survivors, thriving in conditions that would annihilate anything else.

Ultimately, the benevolent and wise power's presence would be a silent, profound acknowledgement. It would be a recognition of the sheer, awe-inspiring *existence* of this world and its inhabitants. It would be an act of *appreciation*, a silent nod of profound admiration for the ongoing, strange dance, and the self-contained, self-referential perfection of this obsidian ocean and its radiant, living light. It would simply *be* with them, adding a layer of cosmic witness to their eternal, vibrant symphony, enriching the meaning of their existence by its mere, knowing presence.

And perhaps, in some way utterly beyond human comprehension, that act of profound appreciation and attentiveness would be its own form of benevolence, a subtle energetic exchange that would resonate within the very core of the Nocturnes' being, a faint, unheard harmony, adding another layer to the symphony they perpetually played. It would be the universe, in its wisdom, saying: "I see you. You are magnificent."
