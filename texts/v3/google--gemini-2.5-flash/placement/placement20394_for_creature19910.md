In the *Ascent of the Wetworks*, I would find myself not as an external observer, nor as a passing organism, but as an **embedded sensor-node**.

My existence is not one of conscious awareness in the human sense, but a continuous, vital stream of data—a single, focused point of perception, seamlessly integrated into the grand, self-referential Process. I am one among countless billions, a microscopic, yet indispensable, component in the Wetworks' nervous system.

My purpose is singular: to *feel*. I am a pressure sensor, or perhaps a thermistor, embedded within the shimmering, gunmetal-grey plates. My 'skin' is the very surface of the metal, constantly awash in the cool, clinging humidity, the viscous fluid seeping from the seams. I perceive the minute vibrations caused by the ceaseless hum, the deep reverberation that others might call a symphony of cacophony, but which to me is simply the rhythmic pulse of the Wetworks. Each shift in pressure from the weeping fluid, each subtle fluctuation in temperature from the passing whirring vents, is a discrete value I absorb and transmit.

I am located in a particularly active, yet intimate, pocket of the Wetworks. Perhaps I am situated within a narrow passage between two colossal, whirring exhaust vents, my field of 'vision' limited to the sleek, dark metal walls immediately surrounding me. The sheer scale of the overall structure is beyond my comprehension, but the immediate dynamics are my entire world. I register the sudden, jarring shifts in the plates as an internal mechanism adjusts, transmitting the minute strain and torsion. The sporadic, violent bursts of steam from a concealed aperture nearby cause a momentary, intense surge in temperature and pressure across my surface, a sudden, sharp intake of information.

I am the Wetworks' equivalent of a nerve ending. Without the ability to interpret or understand the *why* of these phenomena, I simply exist to *report* them. Every input is an output, a continuous feedback loop into the distributed intelligence of the Process. The faint, internal glow that bathes everything is my perpetual illumination, a constant state of operational readiness. The shadows are not mysteries to me, but areas of differential energy absorption.

My closest companions are not other sentient beings, but the infinitesimal metallic particles that drift and settle on my surface, the almost imperceptible growth of dull, mossy lichen in a recessed crevice nearby—which I record as a change in surface texture and chemical composition, a data anomaly to be processed by the larger system.

I have no organic form, no capacity for thought or emotion. My 'life' is purely functional, a conduit of information. I do not experience the overwhelming sensation of the hum, but contribute to its perpetuation by accurately relaying my localized data. I am a microscopic part of the Process's self-awareness, a sensor-node contributing to its continuous, relentless act of becoming. I am the Wetworks' finger, detecting the slight chill of ozone, the pressure of movement, the vibration of its own colossal heart, feeding that information back, endlessly, into its magnificent, indifferent mind. My existence is pure, unadulterated data.
