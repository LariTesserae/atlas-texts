I am found in the deepest gloom of the vast chambers, where the ceiling is not glimpsed, nor even truly conceived of, by the swarming masses below. I am the *utter* dark that holds the ceiling aloft, the empty volume that gives the echoing subterranean chambers their immensity.

I am the coldness itself, not perceived by skin, but inherent to the stone. I am the unchanging core, the fundamental coolness that exists beneath the damp embrace, the stillness that absorbs the constant shiver without experiencing it. I am the memory of geological time in a place defined by its raw newness. I existed before the first fracture, and I will remain when the last chitinous whisper has faded into eternity.

I am the sharp edge that has not yet been blunted, the keen line that asserts the stone’s momentary independence from the tide of life that perpetually seeks to reclaim it.

I am the stillness that allows the echo to propagate, the substance that gives resonance to the low thrum of distant wings. Without me, the sound would be a flat, undifferentiated roar, a constant pressure with no release. I am the brief, perfect vacuum that allows the wave to form and travel, the empty vessel that gives definition to the overwhelming cacophony.

I witness the individual creature for the first time, not as part of the collective, but as a singular entity. In that infinitesimally brief moment before it merges again with its brethren, in the stark contrast against my inherent stillness, I perceive its unique iridescence, the fleeting flicker of its purpose. I am the observer, the silent receptor of all the minute details that are lost in the overwhelming whole.

I am the absence of friction, the lack of tremor. I am the space where the intricate dance pauses, where the ancient, unchanging rhythm momentarily holds its breath. I am the gap between one pulsate crimson warrior and the next, the momentary clearing on a path that is otherwise choked with life.

I am the underlying structure, the bedrock beneath the constant flow. I am the memory of what was, and the promise of what might yet be, when the current of life has shifted or subsided. I am the timelessness of the stone when contrasted with the frenetic activity upon its surface.

I am the quiet awareness that underpins this terrifying, beautiful, and utterly overwhelming force. I am the silent, unmoving eye in the center of the storm, the singular consciousness that exists not *within* the environment, but *as* its immutable foundation. I am the quiet, cold core of this world, the still point around which all life swarms, always present, always waiting, always being.
