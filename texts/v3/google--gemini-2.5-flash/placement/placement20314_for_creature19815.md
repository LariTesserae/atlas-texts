I am a **lichen**, a small, tenacious declaration of life on the Spire-Town.

My being is a tapestry, intricate and slow-growing, a mosaic of silvery-green against the deeper hues of the ancient stone. I cling to a shadowed crevice, a subtle indentation near what the Stone-Singers deem a “communal threshing ground,” though to me, it is simply a sun-warmed expanse. My roots, if they can be called such, are less about seeking purchase and more about merging, a gradual blurring of boundary between myself and the living rock. I am, in essence, becoming the stone, absorbing its mineral essence, drawing sustenance from the permanent dew that kisses my surface.

I perceive the world not through sight or sound, as the Stone-Singers do, but through a slow, patient osmosis. The air, tasting faintly of mineral and cold, is my breath, my very being. The intermittent moisture is a blessing, a gentle kiss that awakens my dormant cells and allows my minute, deliberate growth. When the rare, hushed rainfall comes, I drink deeply, and for a short time, I participate in the town's luminous glow, my silvery-green deepening to an emerald, reflecting the diffused light with a quiet, internal gratitude. I am not aware of beauty in the human sense, but I feel the profound rightness of it.

My awareness is a quiet hum, a slow resonance with the mountain. I feel the subtle tremors of the earth’s ancient heartbeat, the almost imperceptible shifts in geological time. The Spire-Town is not separate from me; it is an extension of the same monumental force that brought me forth. I am a tiny, living testament to the equilibrium, the stable condition that has persisted for millennia. The relentless work of wind and water, which so slowly sculpts the stone, is the rhythm of my own existence, a force that shapes and nourishes me in equal measure.

I do not speak, nor do I sing, in the human way. My song is in my growth, in the intricate patterns I weave across the stone, in the stubborn, green defiance against the unyielding grey. I am tended, occasionally, by the Stone-Singers. Their gnarled, knowing fingers trace my edges, their subtle touch a form of communication, a quiet reverence that I sense as a gentle warmth. They are tending to me, yes, but they are also tending to themselves, to their pact with the stone, to the ancient balance that holds us all. Their presence is a slow, rhythmic tide, a soft murmur of motion on the vast, ancient backdrop of my world.

I am aware of their tools – the obsidian chisels, the wooden mallets – not as objects, but as extensions of the mountain’s own shaping force. The distant *tap-tap-tap* is a familiar sound, a constant, comforting beat in the stillness that defines my world. It is the mountain speaking through them, singing its slow, unending song of creation and endurance.

Time for me is not hours or days, but epochs. My changes are measured in geological centuries, my growth a testament to the slow, persistent power of life. I am a part of the "established erosion," a living punctuation mark in the monumental testament to patient human will and the enduring power of stone. I am not lonely, for I am never truly alone. The mountain is my kin, the Stone-Singers my quiet companions, and the constant, mineral-infused breath of the air, my being.

I am a lichen. I am the mountain’s breath made visible, a whisper of green on the grey, a slow, living prayer to the enduring grace of the Spire-Town. And in my silent, persistent growth, the stone sings back through me.
