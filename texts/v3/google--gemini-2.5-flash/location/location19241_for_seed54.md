The air is a constant, biting whip, thin and sharp, carrying with it the scent of cold metal and something vaguely chemical, like ozone mixed with a whisper of electricity. This world exists in a perpetual twilight, a deep, bruised purple that absorbs rather than reflects the sparse, ambient luminescence. There's no sun, moon, or stars in the conventional sense, only an even, diffuse gloom that presses down from above like a vast, bruised ceiling.

High up, very high, this twilight gives way to sheer, dark faces of newly formed rock, embryonic mountains that haven’t yet decided their final shape. They rise in stark, craggy formations, still bearing the fresh scars of their violent genesis. They look provisional, like clay giants still being molded by unseen hands. Mist clings to these vertical surfaces, a weeping condensation that seems to ooze directly from the stone itself, tracing dark, glistening paths down into the abyssal depths below. This isn't rain; it's the very atmosphere weeping.

Despite the near-total lack of light, an almost overwhelming density of presences fills every crevice and cranny. These are the fauna: not creatures of flesh and blood, but something akin to vast, inorganic swarms. They are, for lack of a better term, "crystal-moths" – intricate, geometric entities, each no larger than a child's hand, composed of a thousand shimmering facets of an unknown, obsidian-dark material. They are everywhere, a living, breathing darkness that flutters and swirls, a constant, low thrumming that manages to be simultaneously loud and ubiquitous, like the sustained roar of a thousand distant waterfalls, or the hum of an unthinkably vast, organic engine. The sound is not just auditory; it's a pressure on the eardrums, a vibration felt deep within the chest.

These crystal-moths are drawn to the moisture, to the weeping rock faces where the air condenses. They cluster in vast, impossible formations on the embryonic mountainside, forming shifting, obsidian tapestries that catch the faint, internal light of the urban structures.

For there *are* structures here, built with a technology that feels both impossibly ancient and utterly alien. These are not buildings in the traditional sense, but vast, interlocking spires and platforms that seem to grow directly out of the nascent rock. They pulse with a faint, internal luminescence – a cold, blue-white light that struggles against the pervasive gloom, casting long, distorted shadows that dance in the air as the crystal-moths swirled. The structures are impossibly complex, like cities built within cities, composed of materials that shift and reform at a glacial pace, reflecting the slow, patient processes of this world. There are no straight lines, no obvious entrances or exits. It's an architecture designed for entities that don't perceive space or movement as we do.

This "urban" landscape is woven entirely of a dark, crystalline substance, almost indistinguishable from the mineral world around it, save for the faint glow and the geometric precision of its forms. Pathways are not paths, but rather channels of deeper darkness, where the crystal-moths gather in even greater density, creating a turbulent current of living darkness that pulls at the edges of one's perception.

The water, ever-present, does not fall as rain, but simply *is*. It saturates the very air, condenses on every surface, and flows in slow, thick rivulets of dark, icy liquid through channels carved into the dark, slick rock. These channels are often dense with the crystal-moths, which seem to drink or absorb something from the perpetually flowing moisture, their internal light flickering in response.

The atmosphere is heavy, thick with the unceasing hum and the invisible weight of the sheer number of crystal-moths. They brush against one, a sensation like cold, brittle glass. There’s no wind, only the internal, generated turbulence of their collective movement. This world is a place where every surface is wet to the touch, alive with the incessant, resonating hum of the overwhelming fauna, and where the very fabric of existence is in a constant, infinitesimal state of becoming. The structures are not built *on* the emerging terrain, but *into* it, or perhaps *are* it, a symbiotic, or parasitic, relationship between nascent geology and impossible engineering. There are echoes here, not of sound, but of something deeper, a vast, unheard resonance that permeates the obsidian city and the nascent mountains alike. It’s the sound of creation, played on an instrument beyond mortal comprehension.
