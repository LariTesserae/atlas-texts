The world is a high, cool, and uniformly submerged urban landscape, a testament to a physics beyond our understanding. Imagine not a sunken city, but one that was built to be eternally underwater, its very existence defined by the liquid embrace.

The terrain is a sprawling, almost cathedral-like metropolis, built into the upper reaches of a colossal, utterly placid ocean. Though "high" might suggest peaks, here it refers to the elevation within the water column, closer to the surface than the crushing abyssal plains, but still utterly sub-aquatic. The structures are sharp, young, and impossibly pristine, formed from materials unknown, glimmering faintly in the perpetual gloom. There’s no soft sediment or coral growth; the city itself is the geology.

These aren't buildings in the traditional sense, but vast, interlocking crystalline formations, carved and polished by processes that leave no trace of erosion. They rise like titanic, submerged spires and buttresses, interconnected by translucent, arcing bridges and pathways that seem to hum with a silent energy. The edges are razor-sharp, the facets perfectly planar, giving the impression of an unfinished sculpture caught in a moment of creation, yet utterly complete. There's a subtle, angular repetition to the architecture, a fractal complexity that speaks of intelligent design rather than organic growth.

The air, or rather, the water, is a uniform, cool medium, utterly still. It is not murky with silt or plankton, but impossibly clear, so much so that distance loses meaning until a structure looms unexpectedly into view. The visibility, however, is limited by the profound darkness. Scattered light sources, emanating from within the crystalline structures themselves, cast long, slow-moving motes of diffuse luminescence. These are not spotlights or streetlamps, but rather internal glows, like the slow pulse of a colossal organism or the quiet hum of a perfectly efficient machine. The light is a deep, bruised violet or an ethereal, cool blue, barely piercing the pervasive darkness, giving the entire setting a melancholic, almost reverent atmosphere.

There is no vegetation, not even the simplest algae. The surfaces are utterly bare, sterile. This is a mineral world, a constructed one, devoid of biological decay or growth. The materials themselves are alien: some are like polished obsidian that absorbs the scant light, others shimmer with an inner luminescence, while still others are perfectly transparent, revealing intricate internal workings that defy comprehension. Some sections of the city are entirely composed of this transparent substance, allowing glimpses into seemingly empty but clearly functional chambers, like the inner workings of an impossibly complex clock.

Sound is almost entirely absent. The water itself acts as a perfect insulator, and no natural currents or seismic activities disturb the profound stillness. Occasionally, a faint, resonant hum might thrum through the structures, a deep, harmonic pulsation that seems to originate from the very fabric of the city itself – the sound of its internal systems operating without friction or resistance. It's a sound felt more than heard, a vibration in the bones, a reminder that this world is alive in its own, non-biological way.

Fauna is exceedingly rare. Once in a long while, a creature of pure light might drift through the deeper shadows, a shimmering, ephemeral form that coalesces out of the darkness and dissipates just as quickly. These are not fish or cephalopods, but beings of pure energy, almost optical illusions, their movements slow and deliberate, as if subject to different laws of physics. They leave no trace, interact with nothing, and contribute to the profound sense of otherness. Their rarity only serves to emphasize the stark, constructed nature of the environment.

The scale is cathedral-like, not because there are individual buildings resembling cathedrals, but because the entire city feels like one immense, interconnected sacred space. One might navigate a narrow passage between two colossal, glowing blocks, then emerge into a vast, empty chamber, its ceiling lost in the darkness above, its floor stretching into the inky depths. Boundaries are clearly defined by the abrupt angles and clean lines of the architecture, but within these boundaries, the spaces can be immense and awe-inspiring.

This is a world that is "urban" in the sense of being an organized, highly structured construction, but with a density that is sparse. There are vast, empty avenues and plazas between the monumental structures, allowing for an overwhelming sense of space and distance. The inhabitants, if there are any, are not numerous, or perhaps they exist on a scale or in a dimension not readily perceptible. There's a balance of presence and absence, a deliberate emptiness that frames the colossal constructions, giving them even greater weight and significance.

"Beyond modern tech" manifests in every aspect. The materials are self-repairing, self-illuminating, potentially even dimension-shifting. The very purpose of this submerged city, its function and its origin, are entirely alien. There could be entire systems operating on principles of quantum entanglement or gravitational manipulation, powers that our current physics can only theorize. The "young" erosion parameter might indicate that this civilization has mastered the very fabric of matter, allowing for creations that never degrade or show signs of age, or perhaps it was constructed in a moment, fully formed, by an unknown power.

It is a world of suspended animation, perfectly unchanging. The currents are nonexistent, the temperature constant, the light levels immutable. There is no weather, no growth, no decay. It is a world outside of time, or at least, operating on a temporal scale so vast it appears frozen. Every facet, every shadow, every fleeting shimmer of light is locked in an eternal, quiet tableau. It’s a place of profound, almost religious stillness, a monument to an unknown civilization’s mastery over its environment, or perhaps, an abandoned testament to its ultimate disappearance.

This is a landscape of strange, cool majesty, a silent and submerged city of impossible geometry and luminous darkness, where the only movement is the slow drift of pure energy or the soft thrum of incomprehensible technology, a monument to an existence completely orthogonal to our own.
