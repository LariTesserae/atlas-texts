The benevolent and wise power, drawn by the deep, quiet hum of this ancient equilibrium, would approach not with the intent to change, but to witness and to honor. It would not land with a thunderclap or reshape the bedrock, for that would be a violation of the very principle of patient endurance it had come to admire.

Instead, it would first simply **be**.

It would manifest as a subtle thickening in the warm, pooling air—a sense of focused attention given form. The diffuse light might, for a single afternoon, acquire a quality of golden reverence, making the black moss gleam like obsidian velvet and the weeping stone faces appear to weep liquid silver. The sound would deepen; the constant *drip-drip-drip* would suddenly articulate the vast, slow rhythm of geologic time, each drop a note in a symphony that had always been playing but was only now fully heard.

The creatures would sense it. The electric buzz of insects would soften, not in fear, but in a collective, unconscious sigh. The frogs would fall silent, their declarative statements replaced by a listening stillness. The high-priest birds would cease their circling and hang motionless on the updrafts, not hunting, but regarding the land below with a new, shared understanding.

The power would not build, nor plant, nor redirect a single watercourse. Its gift would be one of **acknowledgment**.

It might, in a gesture of profound subtlety, cause a single, perfect reflection on a still pond to hold its clarity for an impossibly long moment—a mirror not just for the sky, but for the memory of every sky this place had ever known. It might let its consciousness seep into the bedrock islands, feeling the unimaginably slow pulse of the tectonic plates far below, and for an instant, the granite and basalt would resonate with a low, sub-audible hum, a tuning fork struck against the anvil of deep time.

To the life stretched thin over the bones of geology, it would offer a single, unifying thought, not in words, but as a felt truth woven into the humid air: *You are seen. Your tenacity is a form of grace. Your slow argument is the music of existence itself.*

Then, as gently as it arrived, it would depart. The light would return to its normal, hazy white. The insects would resume their shimmering chorus. A frog would test the silence with a tentative *plonk*.

Nothing would be different. Yet everything would be subtly, permanently affirmed. The mud would feel a fraction more ancient, the water a fraction more purposeful, the silence between the drips a fraction more full. The power would leave behind not a monument, but a resonance—a deepened sense of the place’s own inherent sanctity. It had come to pay its respects to a world that had mastered the art of enduring, and in doing so, reminded the very concept of time what it was for.
