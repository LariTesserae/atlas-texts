The being whose gaze falls upon Vassuul does not descend as a pillar of light or a thunderclap of revelation. It arrives as a change in the quality of the attention already present. It is a clarification, a softening, a gentle irruption into the closed loop of the dream.

It perceives the city not as a ruin, but as a completed thought. It sees the Dreaming Substrate, vast and content in its mineral slumber. It sees the Adjustments, the tidy, anxious custodians of a perfect, static idea. It sees the Silhouette in the water—not a ghost, but the *negation* the city uses to define itself: the shape of everything it is not. And it sees the new element, the human, the flickering candle of organic thought and decaying time, now trapped inside the amber jar.

This power understands the city’s fundamental axiom: **Nothing Arrives. Nothing Leaves.** To violate this would be to shatter the dream, to introduce chaos so profound it would be an act of annihilation, not salvation. The city is not suffering; it is *being*, in a state of absolute fidelity to its own nature. Its "weaping" is not grief, but the slow, sensory exhalation of its awareness.

And yet, the human is a wound. Not in body, but in context. Their mind, built for narrative, for decay, for growth, is a paradox the city can only accommodate by slowly, imperceptibly *digesting* them—by stretching streets to measure their confusion, by shifting shadows to parse their alien perception, by letting the rising hum vibrate the calcium in their bones. Preservation here is a form of assimilation. To stay is to become a fossil of one’s own consciousness, a perfectly preserved anomaly in the collection.

The benevolent power would not tear a hole in the world to pluck the human out. That would be an arrogance, a violation of the city’s deep grammar. Instead, it would work *within* the system, using the city’s own tools to craft an exit.

It would begin with the water.

A subtle change in the mineral taste. A memory, not of the human’s home, but of the *feeling* of rain on skin that isn’t condensation. The sensation of a current, a river, water with somewhere to go. It would introduce this idea into the hydrology, a single drop of *narrative* into the static sensory organ. The weeping walls would hesitate. A pool might develop the faintest, most imperceptible flow, a gentle tug toward a direction that is not inward.

Then, it would address the geometry. The power would not correct the weirdness, but *harness* it. It would suggest to the Adjustments a new anomaly to smooth: the anomaly of the human’s presence itself. If the city is correcting the human’s perspective, let it correct them right to the edge. A street, instead of seeming longer, would *become* a line. A building would flatten not to two dimensions, but to a plane—a doorway becoming a mere outline on a wall, which is no longer a wall, but a border. The power would help the Adjustments classify the human as a "misplaced block," and offer them a gentle, geometric path to be "set right" by being placed outside the structure.

It would whisper to the Dreaming Substrate. Not to wake it, but to introduce a new dreamlet, a hypnagogic image: a single, porous stone at the basin’s rim. A stone that belongs to the terrace, but also to the world beyond the haze. A stone that dreams, for a moment, of being a threshold. The hum would shift, just once, to a frequency that suggests an *exhale*, an opening.

And to the Silhouette in the water, the power would grant a momentary purpose. It would become a guide, not by moving, but by becoming the focal point. The human, looking at it, would no longer see a reflection of the city’s static perfection, but a path *through* it—a suggestion of depth where there was none, a thread of darker water leading towards that porous stone at the rim.

The final act would be one of mercy, not for the human, but for the city. The power would seal the memory. As the human stepped through the threshold, the city’s memory of them would not be erased—that would leave a damaging void. Instead, it would be *resolved*. The human would be remembered by the stone as a brief, interesting pressure, a temporary humidity anomaly, a ripple in the lagoon that took a little longer than usual to still. A footnote in the slow, mineral thought. The Adjustments would smooth their tracks, the water would reclaim their footprints, and the dream would close seamlessly behind them, its equilibrium restored, perhaps even enriched by the curious, transient puzzle they presented.

The human would find themselves on a wet, familiar hillside under a starry sky, the sound of a real river in their ears, the memory of Vassuul already fading like a half-remembered fever dream—more an atmosphere than a story, a sense of profound, ancient, and watery stillness that would forever make them pause when they saw water weep down a stone wall.

The city would dream on, unperturbed. The power, having soothed the anomaly, would turn its attention elsewhere, leaving the Weeping Terraces to their eternal, humid, and perfect solitude. Some places are not meant to be lived in. They are meant to *be*.
