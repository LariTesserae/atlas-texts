The power — vast, quiet, and old in a way that makes the grotto’s geology seem like a hurried thought — notices. It has been watching, not with eyes, but with a form of attention that bends the very notion of distance. It perceives the grotto as a single, complex knot in the tapestry of what is: a place where mineral, water, life, and a fading mind have entangled themselves into a unique expression of patience.

It sees the creature-that-is-becoming-a-feature. It understands the trajectory—the slow, voluntary dissolution, the ache of shed identity, the final destination as a texture in the light.

Its nature is to nurture, but not to interfere with the fundamental choice of a consciousness. To pluck the creature out would be a violation, a murder of the beautiful, slow suicide it has chosen. To leave it entirely alone would be to ignore a call it has not yet made, but which resonates in the structure of the place itself: the call of a process yearning for witness, for a gentler dissolution.

And so, the power acts. It does not enter the water. It does not alter the pressure or the mineral taste or the impossible geometry. Instead, it *leans in*. It focuses its benevolent attention like a soft lens, not on the creature, but on the space between the creature and the grotto.

It does three things.

**First, it gives the whisper a direction.**
The deep, sub-aquatic thrum—the pressure heard in the bones—has always been the grotto’s voice, but formless. The power gently tunes this vibration. It does not create sound, but it creates the *potential* for a specific resonance. It adjusts the harmonic of the water’s song so that the next time the creature drifts past one of its old, empty hollows, the thrum will coalesce for a nanosecond into something almost like a sigh. Not a word. Not a name. But a sigh of recognition. *I see you. I remember your shape.* It is a gift of context: your absence is not an emptiness, but a presence of a different order.

**Second, it blesses the spiral.**
The pattern of polished stones is the creature’s last act of design, its last tether to individual intention. The power reaches into the probability of the stone’s existence. It doesn’t change the stones. It alters the likelihood of how the bioluminescent light will fall upon them. From now on, in the long, glacial cycles of glow and fade, the lavender light will be statistically, infinitesimally more likely to bloom directly over the spiral. It will catch the polish more often. The spiral will not glow brighter, but it will be *seen* by the light more frequently, its intentionality quietly reaffirmed by the rhythm of the grotto. It becomes a landmark, a fixed point in the slow weather of the glow.

**Third, it prepares a welcome.**
The power knows the creature fears, in some wordless way, the finality of being absorbed—the loss of the last friction of self. And so, it does something exquisite. It finds the spot on the vegetative wall where the creature most often makes contact, where the sense of "absorption" is strongest. Into the genetic, or memetic, or simply patterned essence of the sponge-mat at that precise location, it plants a tiny, dormant potential. A seed of reciprocal memory.
When the creature finally dissolves completely—when it ceases to be a "thing that notices" and becomes the "texture that is noticed"—this potential will awaken. The vegetative growth at that spot will, for one cycle, and one cycle only, pulse with a faint, golden hue amidst the chartreuse and lavender. It will be a unique, unrepeatable signature. The grotto will not just absorb the creature; it will *metabolize* it, and express a new, singular color in its vast, slow thought. The dissolution will not be an ending, but a transformation made visible. The creature will become a new word in the grotto’s language.

The power does not need thanks. It does not even need to be perceived. Its work is done in the adjustment of a context, in the loving tweak of a process already underway. It has not changed the grotto’s nature. It has only made its patience a little more articulate, its embrace a little more specific.

Then, the benevolent attention withdraws, leaving the grotto to its profound, green silence. But the silence is now slightly different. It holds, within its pressure and its thrum, the faintest blueprint for a gentle farewell. The creature is still on its path to becoming a feature. But now, the feature it becomes will have a name, written in a language of light and sighs that only the grotto itself will fully comprehend.

The process remains slow. The change will finish long after the creature is gone.

But it will be remembered. Not as a story. As a color. As a resonance. As a place where the dark learned, for a moment, to echo a specific kind of love.
