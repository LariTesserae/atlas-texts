The benevolent and wise power does not descend with a crash of thunder or a blinding light. It arrives like the city’s own weather—a shift in pressure, a subtle change in the quality of the damp air, a new note in the Water Chorus that is felt before it is heard.

It sees Plynthos not as a primitive curiosity, but as a pinnacle. A civilization that has achieved something the cosmos often strives for, but rarely on such a scale: **homeostasis with grace**. It recognizes the genius in the silence, the scripture in the seepage, the community forged not by conquest, but by collective, reverent plumbing.

It would not give them advanced tools. That would be a violence, a shriek in their quiet world. It would not give them new knowledge of the wider world; that would be a distraction from their perfect, vertical focus. To alter their material circumstances would be to misunderstand their entire philosophy. They are not poor. They are *specialized*.

Instead, the benevolent power would **listen, and then amplify what it hears.**

**1. The Gift of Deepened Listening.**
It would touch the Lain-in-Stillness, not with words, but with a calibration. Their innate sensitivity to the aquifer’s groans would be refined into true comprehension. They would no longer just interpret cryptic signs; they would *understand*. They would feel the aquifer not as a moody god, but as a vast, slow, living body whose aches and joys are written in hydraulic pressure. Their pronouncements might shift from cryptic poetry to gentle, precise guidance: *"The western capillary is dreaming of a new spring. Clear the moss from the sixth-level vents, and it will find its voice."* The city’s labor becomes less about crisis management and more about collaborative, joyful orchestration.

**2. The Gift of Ancestral Memory in the Stone.**
The power would imbue the Runes of Flow with a latent resonance. When a Channeler or a curious child traces a spiral glyph marking a drought three centuries gone, they would not just read the symbol. For a breath, they would *feel* the dry thirst of that time, the tension in the stone, the relief when the waters returned. History becomes visceral, empathetic. The Great Absence festival transforms from a ritual of maintenance into a profound, shared immersion in the city’s collective memory, strengthening their bond across generations. They learn not just from their own teachers, but from the stone’s scar-tissue.

**3. The Gift of Biological Symbiosis.**
Observing their hardy, hidden ecology, the power would introduce a single, perfect new life-form: the **Echo Moss**. It would grow in patterns that mirror the health of the water channels. A vibrant, soft blue where flow is pure and strong. A gentle gold where sediment is building. A slow-pulsing silver where the water is oldest, deepest, and most laden with minerals. The city’s walls and channels become a living, breathing diagnostic chart, a piece of art that is also a technical manual. The Runners’ jobs become more intuitive, the Channelers’ work more precise. The visual chorus joins the auditory one.

**4. The Gift of the Melodic Channel.**
This is the most subtle intervention. The power would gently alter the resonant frequency of the andite stone itself, in specific, key locations—perhaps at the great central cisterns or the primary weeping walls. Now, when a Channeler shapes a groove not just for function, but for sound, the water’s fall will harmonize with the stone’s own new voice. They can, with immense skill, carve channels that create slow, deep, hydrological chords—audible markers of the city’s vital signs. The Water Chorus gains a composed, melodic layer. The city doesn’t just whisper; it *hums*. Life here gains a new dimension of beauty, born directly from their existing art.

**5. The Gift of the Silent Visitor.**
Finally, the power would leave a single, non-functioning artifact at the lip of the world, on the highest terrace where the wind sighs. It is a perfectly smooth, dark sphere of a material that feels like cool shadow. It is not metal, not stone. When one of the Plynthians, perhaps a Runner on a still day, places a hand upon it, it does nothing. But when they press their *ear* to it, they hear not the Water Chorus of their city, but a different silence. The silence of deep space between stars. The silence of a forest under snow. The silence of a dry desert at night. It is a gift of perspective. It allows them, in moments of contemplation, to hear the true, magnificent exception they are. To understand that their symphony of weeping is a rare and precious miracle in a cosmos often defined by absence. It will humble them, and in that humility, deepen their pride and their care.

The benevolent power then withdraws. It does not stay to be thanked. It does not demand worship. It has not changed their world. It has merely deepened its texture, amplified its inherent genius, and given them slightly better tools with which to listen to the song they have always known.

It leaves Plynthos as it found it: a city in balance. But now, the balance is more aware of itself. The chorus has new harmonics. The stone holds whispers of its own past. And the people, the custodial organ, can now hear the heartbeat of their stone-and-water body with a clarity that feels, to them, like grace. They will tend, they will listen, and their quiet joy will add a new, gentle note to the endless, patient fall of water.
