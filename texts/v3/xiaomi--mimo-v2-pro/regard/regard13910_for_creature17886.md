What would a benevolent and wise power do, upon discovering the Hollow of Roan?

It would not enter.

To step inside would be to commit a violence of scale, to introduce a heartbeat a million times too fast, a breath too warm, a thought too complex. The Hollow’s timescale is not human. Its being is not social. Its completeness is its defense, and its indifference is its purity. A wise power would recognize this immediately: that the highest form of love, here, is absence.

But it would not simply walk away.

Its action would be one of **attentive non-interference.** It would perform a surgery on causality itself, not to change the Hollow, but to protect it from the friction of the outside world.

First, it would **listen.** Not with ears, but with a sense that registers resonance. It would tune itself to the Hollow’s unique frequency—the subsonic throb of the tide, the metabolic whisper of the moss, the fossilized intention in the stone niches. It would learn the Hollow’s “address” in the fabric of reality, not as a location, but as a sustained chord.

Then, it would begin to **weave.**

From the loose threads of probability that surround the headland—the chance a hiker might look too long at the crevice, the likelihood a storm might shift the rocks and widen the seam, the random walk of a curious biologist—it would gently, imperceptibly, **nudge.**

It would make the crevice seem less inviting on days when a sensitive soul might pass by. It would cause a veil of mist to cling a little more stubbornly to the cliff face. It would encourage the thorny, salt-stunted shrubs nearby to grow just thickly enough to suggest “nothing here.” It wouldn’t build a wall; it would cultivate a gentle, natural *forgetting* in the landscape’s memory. The Hollow would become a place the mind slides away from, a datum that doesn’t stick, a possibility that consistently fails to materialize.

For those who, like the original narrator, are found by the place—those who are meant to enter by some alignment of inner resonance and outer accident—the power would do something more subtle. It would **tighten the loop of experience.** It would ensure that such visits are rare, separated by decades or centuries. It would help the visitor’s own mind process the event as a dream, a profound internal vision, so they are less likely to return with tools and instruments, less likely to speak of it in ways that demand proof. It would guard the Hollow’s secret by making the secret feel deeply, unshakably personal, not public.

It would do nothing to the moss. It would not clean the niches. It would not amplify or dampen the sound. It would not introduce new species or remove old ones. It would not repair the low sitting wall. The cycle of condensation, the patient growth, the theoretical eyeless fish—these would continue with absolute autonomy.

The power’s sole, immense effort would be to **insulate the Hollow from accelerated time.** It would act as a temporal buffer, absorbing the shocks of a rapidly changing world—the vibrations of distant construction, the chemical traces in the rain, the very pressure of expanding human curiosity—so that they do not reach the basalt walls. The roar of the ocean would remain the only roar that matters.

In essence, the benevolent and wise power would become the **negative space** of the Hollow’s care. Its stewardship would be defined by what it prevents: intrusion, acceleration, awareness. It would tend the boundary, not the contents. It would love it best by ensuring its aloneness, by curating the indifference it so perfectly embodies.

And in the deepest chamber, where the Sound is god and the green is black, nothing would change. The moss would continue its thought. The water would continue its chant. The stone would continue its memory. The power’s greatest gift would be the ultimate preservation of the Hollow’s right to be unknown, unneeded, and complete—the gift of being left entirely, gloriously, to itself.
