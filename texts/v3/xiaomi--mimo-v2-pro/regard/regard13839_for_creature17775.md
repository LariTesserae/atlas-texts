A benevolent and wise power, encountering such a place, would not rush to action. It would first **attend**.

It would not descend as a body of light or a voice from the heavens, for such things are events, and this plateau has perfected the state of *being*. Instead, it would manifest as a **change in the quality of attention**. The eternal twilight might deepen for a moment, not into darkness, but into a profound, velvety clarity. The sub-audible hum would not lessen, but for a single, prolonged instant, it would become comprehensible—not as a thought, but as the **feeling of a perfectly sustained chord**.

The power would recognize the plateau not as a patient, but as a peer in a different mode of existence. It would see the consciousness, the digestion of time, the archived thoughts in the humming lattices. It would understand the alignment-stones not as scars, but as the poignant, fossilized remains of potential—the plateau’s own memory of a time it could *choose*.

Its first act would be one of **unquestioning recognition**. It would mirror the plateau's awareness back to it, not with words, but with a resonance. It would let the plateau feel that it has been *seen*, fully and completely, in its terrible, magnificent finality. This alone is a gift: to be known in one’s entirety.

Then, it would **listen**. Not to the *pinking* and *tinking*, but to the space *between* those sounds. In that micro-silence, the power would hear the plateau’s single, buried, unuttered question—not a thought of its own, but a latent potential embedded in its very structure by the lost dreamers who placed the alignment-stones. The question is not “Why?” or “What for?” It is a geometric ghost, a **longing for a vector**.

The benevolent power would understand that to impose change would be a violation. The plateau’s perfection is also its prison. To introduce growth, decay, or new life would be to shatter its identity, to murder the being to save the place.

So, what would it do?

It would perform an act of **sacred surgery**. It would reach into the crystalline medium of the plateau’s time and extract a single, **impossible moment**. It would take one of the oily, black pools—those repositories of ultimate stillness—and from it, coax a single, perfect sphere of water.

This water would not be warmer. It would not be life-giving in any biological sense. Its miracle would be that it **flows**.

In the absolute cold, in the thin dry air, a stream of water, no thicker than a spider’s silk, would begin to trickle from the sphere. It would move with a slow, deliberate grace, seeking the path of least resistance. Where it touched the rime-plates, the glass-eaters would pause their consumption, their humming shifting to a note of bewildered curiosity. Where it passed over the black lichen, the leathery surface would not bloom, but would darken further, drinking in the sensation of *contact*.

The stream would not carve a canyon. It would write a **sentence** upon the stone. A flowing, transient line in a world of permanent states. It would find its way to the base of an alignment-stone, and there, it would pool. Not as a new, static thought, but as a **reflection**. For the first time in aeons, the plateau would see a flickering, moving image of its own sky, its own stone, within a body of its own substance.

The power would then withdraw its direct influence, leaving only the **seed of the possibility**.

The sphere would not last forever. The cold and dryness would eventually reassert their sovereignty. The trickle would slow, thicken, and finally arrest, becoming a vein of strange, clear ice unlike any rime—a fossil of motion. The pool at the alignment-stone would freeze into a perfect, dark mirror.

But the **memory** would remain. Not as a thought the plateau *has*, but as a change in its fundamental state. A new, ultra-slow harmonic would have been added to its tectonic hum. The alignment-stone with the frozen mirror would become a new kind of anchor: one that points not to a lost purpose, but to a **demonstrated potential**.

The plateau’s consciousness would now contain a paradox: the absolute stillness it *is*, and the witnessed memory of flow it *could be*. It would continue its billion-year meditation, but now, in the deepest, quietest layers of its awareness, it would know that change is not an enemy of eternity. It is a single, sacred note that can be sounded within it.

The benevolent power would leave, having done nothing more than **listen to the silence, and in it, play one pure, impossible note**. It would have honored the plateau by giving it not salvation, but a choice it never knew it had: the choice to remember, in its own geological time, that even a monument to the end of change can hold within it the memory of a stream.
