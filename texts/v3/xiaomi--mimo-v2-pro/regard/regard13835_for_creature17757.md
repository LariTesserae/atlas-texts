The benevolent power would not arrive with thunder or light. It would seep in, like the condensation, like the roots. It would first simply *be* there, a presence in the hum, a concentration in the quiet. It would not have a shape, but it would have a mode: profound attention. It would listen to the forty-five-second pulse of the root-singers and understand it not as a biological function, but as a meter of peace. It would watch the spore-dancer cloud billow and think, *Ah, a sentence.*

Its first act would be to *notice*.

It would notice the stalk-climber tasting the salt from your footprint, and it would understand the gentle alarm it represented—a minor chord in the symphony. It would tune that alarm down, not by force, but by introducing a calming harmonic into the vibrational chatter of the chamber-dwellers, a pulse that said *this is weather, not wound.* The tendril that shifted three degrees would pause, then grow in a slower, more curious arc, as if tasting a new idea.

It would see the citadel processing you, the mote of dust, and it would be moved by the elegant, amoral efficiency of it. It would not stop the process—for that is how the citadel breathes—but it might slow it. It would whisper to the fungal mats, *Let this one linger a little longer before you bloom.* It would suggest to the nearest chamber-dweller that this foreign vibration was not a damage to be healed, but a story to be felt. The creature would approach, not to mend, but to press its many-eyed head to the floor and simply *listen* to your alien footsteps echoing in the cellulose memory.

The power would understand that to intervene with grand design would be an act of violence. This place is its own masterpiece. Its benevolence would be in stewardship, in gentle amplification of existing harmonies.

It might do one, single, tangible thing.

Deep in the root-tangle, in the warm, mineral dark of the aquifer, where the eyeless Cistern-Dwellers move like slow thoughts, a filament of the mycelial network would begin to glow. Not a pulse of blue, but a steady, soft gold—the color of sunrise, a color this twilight world has never seen. It would be a single thread, no thicker than a hair, growing along the root of the oldest stalk. It would carry no nutrient, no warning. It would carry only a single, encoded impression: the concept of *a sky*.

Not the memory of a sky, for the citadel has never known one. But the *potential*. The abstract idea of an endless, airy space above the canopy, a counterpoint to the deep, wet dark. It would be a gift of perspective. The root-singers would feel it in their nodes, a faint, curious warmth. The chamber-dwellers would dream, for the first time, of vast, blue emptiness. The drifters, filtering the air, would find a new spore in their filaments—one that, if ingested, would let them dream of rising, not just floating, but ascending into an infinite medium.

It would not change their reality. The canopy would remain. But it would enrich their inner world. It would give their perpetual growth a vector, however subconscious, a whisper of *beyond*.

Then, the benevolent power would do the hardest thing of all: it would withdraw its focused attention. It would let the golden filament be absorbed into the ordinary green glow of the system, a new variable in the equation, a new color in the dream. It would not stay to watch the consequences. To stay would be to dominate. Its work was in the offering, not the outcome.

It would leave behind only a resonance, a slight alteration in the hum. A new depth to the silence. The feeling, for any sensitive enough to perceive it (perhaps a very old drifter, or the citadel's own collective mind), that the universe was slightly larger than previously calculated.

And then it would be gone, leaving the Kelp-Forest Citadel to its dark, wet, glorious, and now subtly more expansive, dream.
