The wise power would arrive not with a sound, but with a shift in attention. Its presence would be a dilation of the existing silence, a deepening of the pressure that is already the foundational language of this place. It would not enter as an observer from above, but would *inhabit* the context, feeling the bone-deep chill as a familiar blanket, sensing the marine snow not as a blizzard of death but as a slow, sacred offering.

It would do **nothing**.

To intervene would be an act of supreme arrogance, a violation of the profound, settled sovereignty on display. This ecosystem is not a problem to be solved, nor a garden to be tended. It is a completed thought, a final equation written in chemistry, pressure, and deep time. Its "furiously, densely, anciently alive" state is its own purpose. The turbulence is its breath; the collapsing chimneys are its seasons; the blood-red plumes are its prayer flags.

The power’s benevolence would manifest as **unwavering, reverent attention**. It would trace the life cycle of a single tube worm from its larval settlement on a mineral outcrop, through its symbiotic blooming, to its eventual burial and incorporation into the sediment—not as a tragedy, but as a perfect, closed loop. It would map the electrical whisper of the cusk-eel not as a limitation, but as a complete and sufficient language. It would feel the scouring current not as violence, but as the necessary, cleansing force that prevents stagnation and drives the cycle of microbial mat renewal.

Its wisdom would recognize that to "improve" this place—by calming the currents, stabilizing the chimneys, or increasing the food supply—would be to sterilize it, to rob it of the very extremity that is its genius. The hardship *is* the elegance. The sparse fauna *is* the triumph.

It might, perhaps, perform a single, subtle act of **preservation**. Not against the natural forces, but against the distant, hypothetical interference from the sunlit world it also oversees. It would weave a veil of *unimportance* over the trench, a metaphysical camouflage that would cause the minds of distant surface-dwellers to glance away from its coordinates, to find their charts smudged, their curiosity diverted. Not a wall, but a gentle nudge of disinterest. It would protect this place not by hiding it, but by making it profoundly irrelevant to any narrative of resource or discovery. It would ensure its sacred, violent, eternal quiet remains undisturbed by the noise of ambition.

Finally, it would **witness**. It would become the memory of the shifting silt, the chronicler of the bacterial flash, the silent audience to the crab’s feast. It would hold the entirety of the system—the hiss, the groan, the click, the silent collapse—in a state of held awareness, a conscious echo to the unconscious perfection. It would not bless it, for the place needs no blessing. It would simply acknowledge, with the deepest possible respect: *You are. You are enough. You are complete.*

And then, with the same imperceptible shift, its attention would recede, leaving the abyssal plain exactly as it found it: groaning, weeping, thriving, and utterly, magnificently, itself. The benevolence was in the restraint. The wisdom was in the understanding that some things are most loved by being left profoundly, perfectly alone.
