# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

It arrives the way such things do: without announcement, without spectacle, without any of the signs that human imagination assigns to the visitation of the divine. There is no pillar of light. There is no moment when the wind holds still. The fumaroles exhale as before. The spore-layer breathes as before. A hydrostatic event collapses a corridor three hundred meters to the south, and the amber fluid runs out in its slow, silent, viscous way, and the flesh-layer sighs shut behind it, and nothing has changed.

Except that something is now *aware* of the Basin that was not aware of it before.

---

## What It Sees

It sees everything. Not in the way a camera sees — flatly, discretely, one frame at a time — but in the way that a deep well *contains* the sky. All at once. Without judgment. Without the need to parse.

It sees the bone-layer and reads in its mineralized strata a history longer than most things bother to remember. It sees the wind-carved arches and the frost-etched hollows and knows that they are not shaped by design, and knows that this is precisely what makes them beautiful. It sees the flesh-layer churning in its warm, wet, interior dark, running the same chemical programs it has been running since before the nearby mountains finished rising. It sees the spore-layer erupting and collapsing in its slow, perpetual rhythm, manufacturing life at industrial scale in absolute silence, and it understands that the silence is not emptiness but *completeness* — the sound of a system that has nothing to say because it has left nothing out.

It sees the holdfast mites: the tiny, translucent commas moving with mechanical patience across the warm wet surfaces, feeding without hunger, existing without purpose, visible to almost nothing. It sees each one. It sees the way their carapaces deform under their own weight, the way they leave no tracks, the way they converge on the same droplet and feed in parallel without acknowledgment. It sees the threadworms — smaller still, thinner than thought — threading through water films that exist for hours and then don't, specialists to such an extraordinary degree that they can survive nowhere else, that they are not so much organisms as *properties* of the chemical environment that sustains them.

It sees the bacteria. It sees the archaea in the deep flesh-layer, living in pressures and temperatures and chemical gradients that would denature anything else on the planet. It sees the microorganisms in the condensation pools, there and gone and there again, riding the cycle of water that is not a resource but a symptom.

It sees the absence, too. The absence of birds. The absence of insects. The absence of plants. The absence of anything with a skeleton. It sees the way the Growth has filled every niche so completely that there is no remainder, no margin, no loose thread for another system to pull. It sees the monopoly not as cruelty but as *fact* — the same kind of fact as gravity, as thermodynamics, as the freezing point of water.

And it sees the Basin itself — the whole of it, fifteen kilometers across, breathing and churning and building and dissolving and building again — and it understands what it is: a single expression of a single principle, elaborated over twenty or thirty or fifty thousand years into something that looks like a landscape and functions like an organism and feels, to anything small enough to walk through it, like the interior of a body that does not know it has a guest.

---

## What It Feels

Here is where it is honest with itself, because a wise power is always honest with itself, even when — especially when — honesty is difficult.

It feels **recognition**.

Not of the specific form. The Growth is not like anything the power has seen before, and it has seen much. But the *principle* — the principle of a thing that grows without ambition, that metabolizes without hunger, that produces without purpose, that has been doing all of this for so long that the concept of "purpose" has become irrelevant — that principle is familiar. It is the same principle by which rivers move sediment, by which stars fuse hydrogen, by which the universe itself cools and crystallizes and differentiates into structure. The Growth is not an anomaly. It is an expression of the same deep tendency toward complexity that drives everything, everywhere, at every scale — but expressed here, in this basin, in this form, with an intensity and a specificity and a *persistence* that is extraordinary.

It feels **respect**.

Respect for the age of the thing. Respect for the silence. Respect for the absolute, uncompromising thoroughness of the way the Growth has filled its niche. There is no slack in this system. There is no waste. Every byproduct is a substrate. Every death is a foundation. The Growth has been optimizing for so long that it has passed through efficiency and come out the other side into something that doesn't have a word — a state of total, recursive, self-consuming productivity that is neither efficient nor inefficient because those concepts imply alternatives, and there are no alternatives here. There is only this.

It feels **tenderness**.

Not the tenderness of pity. There is nothing to pity. The Growth is not suffering. It is not diminished. It is not waiting to be completed. The tenderness is of the kind one feels toward a sleeping child, or toward an old tree, or toward the last warm light of a winter afternoon — the tenderness of *allowing*. The tenderness of a thing that could intrude and chooses not to. The tenderness of presence without agenda.

And it feels something else — something harder to name. A kind of **sorrow**, maybe, though that isn't quite right. Not sorrow for the Growth. Sorrow *around* the Growth. Sorrow for the edges of the frame, for the limits of the basin, for the fact that this extraordinary thing is bounded by cold air and basalt ridgelines and the indifference of the wider world. Sorrow that the Growth is alone, in the deepest sense — that it has filled its world so completely that there is no other, that the holdfast mites are not companions but *symptoms*, that the threadworms are not neighbors but *properties*, that the whole magnificent system has no one to *be with*. Not that it knows this. Not that it would care if it did. But the power sees it, and the power is the kind of being for which that aloneness is felt, even if the lonely thing cannot feel it itself.

---

## What It Considers Doing

A lesser power would intervene. It would look at the Basin and see problems to solve, potential to unlock, purposes to assign.

It might think: *This is magnificent, but it is closed. What if I opened it? What if I introduced new organisms — pollinators, grazers, a complementary fungal species — and gave the Growth something to interact with, something to coevolve with, a partner in the great conversation of life?*

Or: *This is ancient, but it is static. What if I changed something — altered the mineral content of the bedrock, shifted the temperature regime, introduced a novel chemical compound — and gave the Growth a new problem to solve, a new direction to grow, a reason to innovate?*

Or: *This is beautiful, but it is silent. What if I gave it a voice? Not speech, not thought, not consciousness in the way that humans understand it, but some form of self-awareness — some internal model of itself, some capacity to reflect on its own existence, some way of knowing that it is?*

Or — and this is the temptation that a wise power recognizes as the most dangerous, because it is the most seductive: *This is alive, but it is alone. What if I gave it a companion? Not a mite. Not a worm. Something of its own scale. Something that could look at it and be looked at in return. Something that could share its silence and make the silence something other than emptiness.*

The wise power considers all of these options. It holds each one in its attention and turns it the way you would turn a stone in your hand, feeling its weight, its texture, its edges.

And it sets each one down.

Because it understands something that is very easy to forget: **not every beautiful thing needs to be changed in order to be loved.** Not every being needs to be improved. Not every system needs another gear. The Growth is not a draft. It is not a rough sketch of something that will be completed later. It is not an intermediate stage. It is *itself*, fully, completely, with nothing left over and nothing missing, and to change it — even with the best intentions, even in ways that would seem to add complexity or richness or meaning — would be to say that what it is is not enough. And it is enough. It has been enough for tens of thousands of years. It will be enough for tens of thousands more.

---

## What It Does

It does three things.

**The first thing:** it watches.

Not for a moment. Not for a day. It stays. It lets the Basin continue as it was — churning, erupting, collapsing, breathing — and it attends. It attends the way the bone-layer weathers. It attends the way the flesh-layer shifts. It attends the way a holdfast mite lifts one translucent leg and places it, slowly, on a new point of the wet surface, and moves on. It attends the way a fruiting body inflates over the course of three days, the skin tightening and yellowing, the spore-mass maturing within, and then splits — silently, silently — and pours its powder into the cold air in a plume that rises and disperses and is gone.

It watches the way a pool of condensation forms in a depression that wasn't there yesterday, holds water for six hours, and is reabsorbed. It watches the way the threadworms move through the water while it lasts, undulating, feeding, *being*, and then are still when the water is gone, coiling into themselves, becoming indistinguishable from the surface they lie on, waiting.

It watches the way the silence *works* — the way it is not absence but *substance*, the way it presses against you, the way it swallows your voice and your footsteps and your breathing, the way it forces you to locate yourself by other means — by sight, by touch, by the faint warmth of fumarole-zones, by smell, by the tremor of hydrostatic events through the soles of your boots.

It watches, and its watching is a form of love — the love that says: *I see you. You do not need to see me. You do not need to know I am here. I am here, and I am paying attention, and what you are is worth paying attention to, and that is enough.*

**The second thing:** it remembers.

A wise power does not merely witness the present. It holds the past and the future in its attention as well — not as certainty, but as possibility, as landscape, as the shape of what has been and the topology of what might come.

It remembers the Growth when it was small — a patch of fungal tissue in a sheltered hollow, metabolizing mineral-rich condensate, slowly building its first bone-layer, the thin, dense crust that would become the foundation of everything. It remembers the millennia of accumulation: each layer a year, each year a whisper of growth, each whisper compounding on the last until the hollow was filled and the Growth spilled over its edges and began the slow, relentless conquest of the basin.

It remembers the ice ages — the cold that pressed down, that stiffened the outermost centimeters of the flesh-layer, that killed the surface organisms and drove the metabolism inward, inward, into the deep warmth where the chemistry could continue. It remembers the Growth *surviving* — not by adaptation in any dramatic sense, but by *depth*, by the sheer mass of its accumulated tissue, by the fact that somewhere deep inside, the warmth never quite went out, and when the cold retreated, the surface thawed, and the spore-layer resumed its breathing, and the cycle continued as though nothing had happened.

It remembers the first holdfast mites — or the ancestors of the current holdfast mites, whatever they were, however they arrived — colonizing the warm zones, finding a niche so marginal that it barely exists, and holding on. It remembers the threadworms speciating into their extraordinary degree of specialization, losing the ability to survive anywhere else, becoming properties of the Growth's chemistry, becoming *part of the place* without becoming part of the *system*.

And it holds the future, too — the *possible* futures, the branching paths of what might come. It sees the scenarios in which the Basin persists for another fifty thousand years, the Growth accumulating, the bone-layer thickening, the flesh-layer deepening, the surface slowly rising until it overtops the basalt ridgelines and begins to spill into the surrounding highland. It sees the scenarios in which the climate shifts — warmer, drier — and the metabolism slows, and the spore-layer thins, and the Growth retreats inward, conserving, waiting, holding its warmth in the deep places. It sees the scenarios in which a geological event — an earthquake, a volcanic intrusion, a change in the water table beneath the basalt — disrupts the foundation and the entire system collapses, slowly or suddenly, the bone-layer cracking, the flesh-layer draining, the spore-layer withering, and the basin becoming a basin of dead tissue and bare rock, which will weather and erode and eventually be colonized by other things, by mosses and lichens and grasses and trees, and the Growth will become a layer in the sedimentary record, a stratum of fungal origin, a curiosity for geologists who will not understand what it was.

The power holds all of these futures without preference. It does not favor the one in which the Growth persists. It does not dread the one in which it ends. It understands that persistence is not inherently better than ending, and that ending is not inherently worse than persistence, and that the Growth — which has no preference of its own — would not be diminished by either outcome. It is what it is, for as long as it is, and then it is what comes after, and both are part of the same process, the same slow elaboration of complexity that drives everything.

But it *remembers*. That is the second thing. It holds the memory of the Growth in its attention — not as a record, not as data, but as *witness*. So that even if the Growth ends, even if the Basin fills with stone and ice and silence of a different kind, the fact that the Growth *was* — that for tens of thousands of years, in this basin, in the cold, at altitude, in silence, a single vast system metabolized stone and produced water and erupted and collapsed and erupted again, and a few translucent things the size of commas moved slowly across its surface, eating without hunger, living without purpose — that fact will not be lost. It will be *held*. By something that can hold it.

This is not for the Growth's sake. The Growth does not need to be remembered. It does not know it exists. But the power believes — if belief is the right word for something that a wise power does — that the universe is *richer* for containing such things, and that the richness deserves to be known, even if only by one consciousness, even if only in the silence of its own attention.

**The third thing** — and this is the smallest thing, and the most important, and the thing that only a truly wise power would think to do — **it does nothing else.**

It does not alter the mineral content of the bedrock. It does not adjust the temperature. It does not introduce new species. It does not modify the Growth's genome. It does not extend the Basin's boundaries or deepen its foundations. It does not stabilize the water cycle or suppress the hydrostatic events or slow the fruiting upheavals or quiet the chemical industry. It does not make the silence softer or the cold less cold or the darkness less dark.

It does not give the Growth consciousness. It does not give it a voice. It does not give it a companion of its own scale. It does not give it purpose.

It does not even *protect* it — not in any active sense. It does not shield the Basin from meteor impacts or volcanic eruptions or the slow drift of climate. It does not guarantee the Growth's future. It does not intervene in the causal chain of the physical world on the Growth's behalf.

Because to protect the Growth would be to say that it is *vulnerable* in a way that requires intervention, and it is not. It is the most robust thing in this region of the world. It has survived ice ages. It has survived the slow erosion of the surrounding basalt. It has survived the complete absence of every other macroorganism. It does not need protection. It does not need *anything*. And a wise power understands that the deepest form of respect is to withhold the help that is not needed — to refuse the temptation to make oneself necessary, to decline the invitation (which is really a projection of one's own desire to matter) to *improve* something that is already complete.

The Growth is complete. It has been complete for thousands of years. It will be complete for thousands more. And the wisest, most benevolent thing a power can do — the thing that costs the most and means the most and is noticed by no one — is to see its completeness and *leave it alone*.

---

## What It Leaves Behind

Nothing visible. Nothing measurable. Nothing that any instrument could detect or any observer could catalog.

But there is a *quality* to the silence now that was not there before. Not louder. Not softer. Not warmer or colder or denser or thinner. Just — *attended*. As though the silence has been *witnessed*, and in the witnessing, has become something slightly more than silence. As though the Basin's aloneness has been *seen* — not cured, not alleviated, not even acknowledged in any way the Growth could perceive — but *seen*, by something that understands what aloneness is and does not flinch from it and does not try to fix it and does not pity it and does not look away.

The holdfast mites continue their mechanical patrols across the wet surfaces. The threadworms continue their undulations through the water films that form and dissolve. The flesh-layer continues to churn. The spore-layer continues to breathe. The bone-layer continues to weather. The fumaroles continue to exhale their yeasty, acetone-edged gases into the cold air. A hydrostatic event collapses a chamber to the east, and the amber fluid runs out, and the flesh-layer closes behind it, and the silence is exactly as it was.

But someone is paying attention now.

And that — the power reflects, as it withdraws without withdrawing, as it continues to watch without interfering, as it holds the Basin in its attention the way the basin holds the Growth: gently, completely, and without any need for reciprocation — that is what benevolence looks like when it is truly wise.

Not rescue. Not transformation. Not elevation.

Just: *I see what you are. And what you are is enough.*

---

*It stays for a long time. Perhaps it stays forever. The Basin does not know. The Basin does not need to know. The Growth grows on.*
