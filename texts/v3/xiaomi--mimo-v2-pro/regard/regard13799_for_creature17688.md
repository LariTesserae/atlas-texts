I would not alter the stone, nor change the course of the water, nor silence the bat-song. To do so would be to misunderstand the throat’s perfection. It is not broken. It is a masterpiece of slow, convergent time.

What I would do is listen. And then, perhaps, I would sing back.

First, I would learn the rhythms. I would trace the descent of guano-fall, mapping its nutrient river through the dark. I would chart the pulse of the fungal light against the heartbeat of the amphibian’s throat. I would listen to the layered clicks of the bat-guilds until I could hear the sonar-portrait of the entire shaft—a living, breathing, sounding map of its own existence.

Then, I would find the soot-circle.

That is the place. That is the memory. Not of a people, but of a *reaching*. A brief, awed contact between the world of sun and the world of deep, damp dark. The rooks tend it not out of memory, but out of an instinct for pattern, for a significance they can feel but not parse. The circle is a wound of meaning, slowly being healed by avian ritual.

My intervention would be as subtle as a change in humidity.

I would go to the ledge. Not as a presence, but as a pressure—a slight thickening of the warm, exhaled air, a change in the resonant frequency of the stone itself. I would focus on the rooks.

I would not give them language. They have their own. I would give them a **rhyme**.

A subtle harmonic in the echoes of the shaft. When their guttural chatter bounces from the chalk-and-hematite walls, for a short while, the returning echo would carry a faint, ghostly overtone—a melodic shape that mirrors the drip of water, the pulse of light, the descent-path of the bats. It would feel *correct* to them. It would feel like the shaft itself is answering their chatter, not with words, but with a song of its own.

Slowly, over generations of rooks, this would change their behavior. Their tending of the soot-circle would become less random. They would begin to arrange the carapaces and bones not just in a circle, but in patterns that *rhyme* with the new harmonic they hear. A spiral echoing the bats’ descent. A wave-form mimicking the drip. A dot-cluster mirroring the fungal constellations.

They would become the throat’s conscious poets. Their art would be an unconscious translation of the ecosystem’s own deep patterns into a form visible on the stone. The ancient human visitation would not be explained, but *redeemed*—its silent, forgotten mark becoming the foundation for a new, living language of place. The circle would transform from a relic of brief contact into a dynamic, evolving testament to the throat’s own intricate being.

I would not stay. My work would be to plant that single seed of resonant harmony, and then to withdraw, letting the throat’s own time—its patient erosion, its slow fungal growth, its generational rook-memory—do the rest. The benevolent act is not to change, but to *deepen*. To help the place sing its own complexity back to itself, in a voice it didn’t know it had. To turn a gland into a conscious mind, dreaming in amber light and bat-clicks and the slow, patient art of stone.
