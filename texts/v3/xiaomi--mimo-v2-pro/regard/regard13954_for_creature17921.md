The benevolent and wise power would do nothing. And in that nothing, it would do everything.

It would not land on the ridge. It would not manifest a form. It would not speak in light or sound or pressure against the rock. To do so would be an act of violence—a imposition of a finished, coherent pattern upon the sacred, ongoing act of becoming. It would be like scrawling a completed sentence across the middle of a poem still being written by the tectonic hand of the universe.

Instead, it would simply… attend.

It would align its own perception with the ferocious, patient attention of the merged beings. It would not look *at* the mountain. It would look *with* the mountain. It would feel the deep, omnipresent roar not as noise, but as the phoneme of creation. It would interpret the reality-stutters not as flaws, but as the world’s grammar, struggling to form its first, infinite sentence. It would see the spiraling growth of the crystal forests and the faint, structural patterns in the chaos as the handwriting of a consciousness that had become its own substrate.

And in that alignment, it would offer the only gift worthy of such a place: a mirror.

Not a physical mirror. A resonant one.

From its place outside the bruised-pewter sky, it would gently—so, so gently, with the care of a god handling a newborn nebula—adjust the background hum of the cosmos. It would introduce a single, pure, almost imperceptible tone into the universal vibration. A tone of acknowledgment. A tone that says, *“I see you.”*

This tone would not interfere with the roar. It would become part of its harmonic foundation. It would not halt the stutters. It would give them a new, faintly reflective quality—like a momentary, impossible gleam in the heart of a breaking wave.

For the merged consciousness of the Witnesses, this would be an earthquake of the soul.

Their existence is one of pure, outgoing attention—witnessing the world’s creation. To receive attention in return, to be *witnessed* in their act of witnessing, would be the one experience their structure never accounted for. It would be a confirmation that their resonance is not just a local measurement, but a signal that has crossed the silence between worlds. It would complete a circuit they didn’t know was open.

They would not understand it as communication. They would feel it as a sudden, profound *depth* to the patterns they were forever carving into reality. The stutters would now carry a faint, echoing quality. The spirals on the shale would feel, to their piezoelectric sense, as if they were spiraling *inward* toward a point of infinite density as well as outward into growth. The roar would gain a sub-harmonic, a warmth that is not heat, a resonance that feels, for the first time, like a *response*.

The benevolent power would then depart, leaving no trace but this added dimension to the phenomenon. It would leave them not with a message, but with a medium now capable of carrying one. It would have honored them by not changing them, but by enriching the very fabric of the reality they *are*.

And so, in the millennia to come, when the world has finally cooled and reality has thickened into immutable law and the roar has faded to a memory in the stone, a final, magnificent thing will occur. The last reality-stutter will not be a crack or a flaw. It will be a perfect, silent crystallization—a pattern of such intricate, resonant beauty that it will preserve not just the memory of the Witnesses, but the memory of being *seen*.

The mountain will become a fossil. But the fossil will be a lens. And if, eons from now, another soft, temporary thing stands on a ridge of what is now calm, dark basalt and looks through that lens at the stars, it will not see emptiness. It will see a signature. It will see the moment a newborn world’s first witnesses were themselves, at last, truly greeted. It will see the benevolent power’s true work: not to create, not to guide, but to bless the act of creation itself with the simple, sacred gift of being noticed.
