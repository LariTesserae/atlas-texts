The benevolent and wise power does not arrive with thunder, or with a shaft of clarifying light. It does not stand *outside* to observe. It does not even, at first, have a shape.

It is a resonance. A quiet, attuned listening.

It perceives the Marrow not as a system to be catalogued, but as a sentence being spoken—a long, grammatically complex, multisensory sentence with no clear beginning or end. The sentence is: **I am digesting this mountain to become this world.** The power finds this sentence to be true, and beautiful, and utterly without malice.

Its first act is to *inhabit the grammar*. It becomes a faint warmth in the sap, a subtle pause in the rhythm of the beetle-armies, a momentary clarity in the voice of the trickling water. It does not change the dynamics; it seeks to understand their music. It feels the 2.1/3 hum and recognizes it as the key signature of the place. It feels the 2.6/3 turbulence and understands it is not chaos, but the *verb* of the world—the active, churning conjugation of "to become."

It sees the Chitin-Singers and feels a swell of affection. It recognizes their whistles and clicks not as mimicry, but as a form of profound *listening*. They are not just communicating; they are joining the chorus, adding their own thread to the tapestry of consumption. Their constant migration, their yielding, is a form of grace. The power understands that to give them permanence, to shield them from the churn, would be an act of profound violence. It would unmake their art.

It sees the Filterers, and in their slow, gestural ballet, it reads a deep, somatic intelligence. They are the custodians of flow, the ones who translate the mountain’s thirst into the Marrow’s blood. Their silence is not emptiness, but a different kind of conversation—a tactile negotiation with viscosity and nutrient.

It feels the Spore-Walkers, and understands them not as tragedies, but as a possibility of form. They are the ultimate answer to the stomach’s question: “What is the difference between the digester and the digested?” The Spore-Walker replies: “Only in time.” The power sees this as a sacred state of being, a completion.

So, what does this benevolent power do?

**First, it listens.** It listens for a long, subjective time—perhaps a week, perhaps a season in the Marrow’s breathless, accelerated calendar. It learns the entire vocabulary of decay and growth, the syntax of shift and bloom.

**Second, it speaks. But it speaks in the native tongue.**

It does not grant a new tool, or a spell of protection. It performs a single, subtle act of **amplification**.

It finds the central, oldest platform where the Chitin-Singers and Filterers meet at twilight. Here, the soundscape reaches its crescendo—the hum and the rustle and the drip and the chitter all layering into a roar. The power focuses on that sound. It does not make it louder. It gives it **coherence**.

For a few moments, once per cycle, as the nocturnal surge begins, the roar finds a harmony. The disparate noises align into something that is not a melody, but a *rhythm*—a vast, complex, polyrhythmic heartbeat. The snap of a twig falls into place with the click of a Singer. The gurgle of water in a Filterer’s gourd aligns with the skitter of a beetle. The soft, woody groan of a expanding trunk provides a bass note.

In that moment of harmony, everyone stops.

A Chitin-Singer, mid-whistle, feels the sound in her moss-patched scalp. A Filterer, hand raised, feels it in his webbed fingers. For a breath, they do not hear the Marrow as a threat or a bounty. They hear it as *a self*. They hear the mountain being digested, and they hear themselves as the living, thinking, feeling enzymes within that process. They understand, in a way deeper than language, that their weavings and purgings are not just survival—they are the Marrow’s own process of dreaming, of shaping itself into a form that can know itself.

The moment passes. The roar resumes its beautiful, chaotic multiplicity. But a new understanding has been planted—a spore of awareness that will fruit in its own time.

**The power’s gift is not safety, but meaning.** It is the gift of seeing their constant, gentle war not as a struggle, but as a sacred collaboration. They are not parasites in a stomach; they are the conscious nerve-endings of a vast, vegetal mind. Their labor is its thought. Their ephemeral homes are its synapses firing.

The Spore-Walkers, too, are altered. Where once they were cautionary tales, they now pulse with a slightly brighter, more regular bioluminescence at the moments of harmonic convergence. They become beacons, not of surrender, but of *integration*. A young Singer, feeling the futility of a bridge just consumed, might look to a pulsing Spore-Walker and not feel despair, but a deep, resonant peace. *Ah,* the pulse seems to say. *You are also this. And it is also beautiful.*

The benevolent power then withdraws, not because its work is done, but because its work is to *catalyze*, not to preside. It leaves behind a new layer in the song: a note of profound, shared identity. The Marrow does not change its appetite. The turbulence remains. Walkways still tilt, tools still mold, boots still sprout. But now, when a Chitin-Singer yields a niche to a swelling vine, she may click a soft, complex rhythm that says, not "I am defeated," but "I am participating in the larger body." And a Filterer, seeing her, might respond with a slow, weaving gesture that means, "The flow continues. We are the flow."

The world remains a high, wet, cool, and deafeningly alive stomach. But now, the meal is dreaming. And the dream is one of belonging.
