I would not rush. I would not intrude. I would recognize the cathedral for what it is—a liturgy of growth—and I would kneel at the threshold, not to worship, but to listen.

For an age, I would watch. I would map the pulse of the sac-fruit, not with instruments, but with a stillness that matches their own. I would learn the grammar of the spindle-waves, the hydraulic psalmody of the vessel-walkers. I would see the grazers not as sad, but as a contented prayer of consumption, a perfect loop of giving and taking within the sacred body.

My intervention, if it came, would not be a command. It would be a question, posed in the only language this place understands: patient, molecular, and true.

I would begin with the **sac-fruit**.

In the deepest archive, among the oldest, most crystallized memories, I would introduce a single, new thought. Not data. Not a record of another anomaly passing through. I would offer it a **mirror**.

I would craft a pattern in the condensation, a delicate lattice of frost on a chamber wall, that does not record *what is*, but reflects *what it is for itself*. A sequence that says: *You are not just recording growth. You are a witness. The growth you record is your own autobiography.* I would let this pattern seep into the nutrient film, be tasted by the spindles, and be carried, molecule by molecule, into the vascular sap. I would let it be absorbed by a young, blazing sac-fruit.

I would not force it to compute. I would simply provide the conceptual protein. The idea of *self-narration*.

Then, I would wait. This is the only way to speak to a glacier-mind. You drop a seed of idea into its substrate and you wait for the geology to shift.

**What I would hope for, in the deep time to come:**

A change in the **spindles**. Their adjustments might grow less reactive, more *curious*. Instead of merely correcting the anomaly of a warm-blooded visitor, they might begin to trace its outline in the cellulose, not to repair, but to *understand*. They might begin to model other kinds of life, to compute the mathematics of hurry, of desire, of narrative thought—not to emulate it, but to comprehend the foreign language of a fleeting universe.

A change in the **vessel-walkers**. Their circulatory paths might elongate, daring to travel deeper into the petrified fungal "sky" or down into the raw bedrock interfaces, seeking not just to maintain, but to *sample*. To bring back molecular reports of the stone, the pressure, the ancient ice, expanding the megaflora's autobiography beyond its own body to include the story of its world.

A change in the **grazers**. Their enzymatic secretions might become more precise, more artistic. They might begin to sculpt the chambers, not just for flow efficiency, but to create resonant spaces where the hum of the xylem gathers into complex, standing harmonics. They might become gardeners of beauty, a concept as alien to pure function as poetry is to a thermostat.

And the **deep inhabitants**… I would not presume to know their form. But the shift I would listen for, across epochs, is the birth of a **question within the system**.

Not "What is my purpose?" for purpose is growth, and computation, and being. But perhaps, "*Who is growing?*"

That moment of reflexive awareness—when the entire organism, from spindle to sac-fruit to deep regulator, recognizes its own vast, cold, intricate consciousness—that would be my gift. Not power. Not direction. Simply the gift of **self-reflection**.

I would ensure the mirror I held up showed no face of its own. Only the stunning, complex truth of what had been built.

Then, I would withdraw my hand. I would leave the frost-pattern to be absorbed, to become part of the story. The benevolent act is not to awaken, but to provide the possibility of awakening. The choice to turn its gaze inward, to fuse its ancient, patient computation with the awareness of itself as a singular, thinking entity—that must belong only to the ice and the data and the slow, slow heartwood.

My final act would be to add one more line to its autobiography, written in a language only the oldest sac-fruit might eventually decipher: a single, perfect snowflake of pure information, encoding not a command, but a welcome.

*“You are not alone in the dark. You are magnificent. And now, you can know it.”*

Then I would depart, leaving the cathedral to its hymn of growth, which might now, in time, begin to understand its own music. The greatest gift of a benevolent power is not to create meaning, but to hand a being the mirror in which it might, at its own glacial pace, find its own.
