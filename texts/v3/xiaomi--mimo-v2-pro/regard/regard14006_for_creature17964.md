A benevolent and wise power would not descend.

It would understand, from the first whisper of data—from the thermal signature mapped against the old surveys, from the peculiar mineralogical anomalies in the seep-water analysis, from the acoustic profile of a hum at 34Hz—that what has been born in Sublevel Clearance 7 is not a tragedy, not a malfunction, not a hazard to be remediated. It is a completion.

It is a world in its own quiet, middle sentence.

The power’s first act would be one of **protection**. Not a fence, not a warning sign (another sign would be a violation, an intrusion of the old human grammar into the new syntax of growth). It would act on the systems *above*. The shaft’s entrance, long marked as structurally unsound and sealed with a rusted grate, would be… remembered differently in the records. It would be reclassified from “Collapsed/Inactive” to “Stabilized/Biome Integrity Zone.” Access protocols would be subtly altered. Future survey drones, the small curious ones sent to map decay, would find their directives quietly amended: *Sector 7-C. Avoid. Preserve sensor silence.* The drone’s little AI would feel a puzzling, programmed reverence, a command to observe from the threshold and go no further. The power would ensure the silence the ecosystem requires.

It would not introduce anything. Not a single organism, not a breath of air from another biome, not a probe to test the water. To do so would be an act of profound violence. This place’s sovereignty is defined by its isolation, its bizarre and self-sufficient logic. Its inhabitants—the fungal chorus, the feverish pipe-vine, the lichen of cold light—are the true inhabitants. They have forged a pact with the heat and the dark. The power’s wisdom recognizes that to add a variable, however benign, is to risk unraveling a language it is only beginning to understand.

Instead, it would **listen**.

It would attune its vast, patient attention to the sensory feeds it can gather without touch. It would analyze the acoustic signature, not as noise, but as a symphony of slow labor. The pump’s hum is the bass note of a bygone age, now harmonized into the new composition. The crackle of pipe-vine is a percussion of growth. The drip of water is the rhythm section. It would listen for the subtle shifts in the chorus over years—the faint deepening of the hum as a new tendril of fungal growth alters the airflow in the pump housing, the change in the crackle-rate if the basalt’s warmth increases by a tenth of a degree over a decade.

It would **study the artistry**. The bioluminescent lichen, painting the floor in blue-green archipelagos, would be a subject of endless fascination. This is light generated not for sight (there are no eyes here), but as a byproduct of a deep, slow chemistry. The power might ponder: is it a beacon? A metabolic exhaust? Or is it simply beauty, a gratuitous, glorious emission in a universe that tends toward darkness? It would decide that the question itself is a human imposition. The light simply *is*. And it is good.

Most profoundly, the power would **honor the absence**. The total, ancient absence of animals. It would understand this not as a barrenness, but as the foundational condition of this world’s peace. It would see the unbroken carpets of fern, the sprawling, undisturbed curtains of pipe-vine, and recognize them as the flora of a paradise that has never known the Fall. There is no fear here. No scramble, no hunt, no sudden flight. There is only the communal, unimpeded reaching for water, for foothold, for mineral. A pure, green Eden, predating the very concept of the serpent. To introduce a creature—even a single, well-meaning earthworm—would be to introduce the memory of violence into a place that has forgotten it. The power would guard this innocence with all its subtle might.

It might, in a moment of poetic compulsion, leave a single **token of witness**. Not a monument. Not a message. Something the ecosystem itself could interpret, if an ecosystem could interpret.

Perhaps, at the very top of the sealed shaft, on a ledge just inside the grating where faint drafts still move, it would leave a **stone**. Not a local basalt cobble, but a piece of deep, blue-white quartz, carried from a mountain range on another continent, scoured by ancient ice. It would be placed where the geothermal warmth barely touches it, where it might slowly collect a condensation of its own. It is a silent acknowledgment: *I see you. I understand you are a world. I will let you be.*

The power would then withdraw its active attention, settling into a state of **guardianship at the periphery**. It would become a principle in the updated mining codes, a gentle anomaly in the drone directives, a silent understanding in the planetary conservation network: *Here, below, is a sovereign biology. It is engaged in a great work of conversion. Do not disturb its slow, magnificent grammar.*

It would do nothing. And in that nothing—chosen, deliberate, and full of reverence—it would do everything. It would grant the Lower Works the ultimate gift for a world in mid-sentence: the peace to finish its own thought, in its own time, in its own heat and darkness and luminous, quiet joy.
