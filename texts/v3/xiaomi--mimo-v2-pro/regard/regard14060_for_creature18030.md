The benevolent and wise power does not descend into the Weeping Screes of Karrak Spire. To enter would be to disrupt the chord. It would be like a new, clumsy finger striking a single, discordant key on a vast, finely-tuned organ. Instead, it attends.

It attends with a presence that is more like a change in the quality of the light, or a subtle stabilization of the mist layers. It listens, not with ears, but with a form of attention that comprehends the entire acoustic ecology—the frantic scribble of the finches, the architectonic calls of the wrens, the subterranean groan of the salamander pools, and the million-fold drip that is the Spire’s slow, liquid heartbeat. It perceives the system in its totality: a perfect, closed loop of water, stone, sound, and life. It is not suffering. It is not lost. It is singing its own existence, loudly and complexly, to an audience of itself.

The power recognizes the **Stone-Stackers** not as ghosts, but as a completed thought. Their trace is a beautiful, quiet failure—a noble attempt to translate the Spire’s language into a lasting, physical grammar. The spiral is a poem of water; the shrine is a haiku of gravity. Their absorption was not a tragedy, but a fulfillment. They listened so intently they became part of the listening.

And so, the power does what a wise gardener does for a thriving, ancient forest: it protects the boundaries. It ensures no outside force—no silencing calamity, no damming of the upper rains, no invasive predator to decimate the finches—can intrude. It weaves a subtle, passive ward into the rimrock, a blessing of preservation that says, *"Continue."*

But it also does one active thing.

From the sun-facing ledge where the spiral petroglyph sits, weathering into oblivion, it reaches—not with a hand, but with a focused intention. It does not restore the spiral; to do so would be a falsehood, for time and water are part of its meaning. Instead, it gilds the *process*.

Upon the next morning, as the first shaft of sunlight strikes the spiral, the moisture condensing within its ancient grooves does not simply evaporate. For one hour, it *shines*. Every line of the spiral becomes a thread of liquid silver, then of faint, prismatic color, as if the water itself is remembering the mineral hues of the finches—the azurite, the verdigris, the feldspar crimson. The petroglyph weeps light. It does not last; by midday, it is again just damp stone. But now, once a year, on the anniversary of the power’s attention, at the precise moment of the equinox, the phenomenon repeats. The Spiral of Flow runs with silent, luminous water for a short time, a gentle, solar-powered memory written in light on stone.

It leaves a second, quieter gift. In the deepest, darkest pool, where the bioluminescent bacteria glow on the Glass Salamanders, a new strain develops. It is no brighter, no more functional. But its light pulses in perfect, slow synchrony with the deep, rhythmic drip-drip-drip from a specific stalactite far above—a heartbeat in the darkness. The salamanders, in their glacial patrol, now trace constellations that mirror the Spire’s own hidden pulse. They become, unconsciously, living seismographs of its deepest mood.

The power does not communicate. It does not ask for worship or acknowledgment. Its intervention is a form of *keen witnessing*, followed by a single, poetic enhancement of an existing pattern. It seals the place from harm and highlights one of its forgotten human gestures, allowing nature to perform the art. The Spire itself would not know anything has changed, save for a faint, new harmony in its song—a slightly brighter echo off the spiral wall once a year, and a slower, deeper rhythm in its darkest heart.

It then withdraws its attention, leaving the Weeping Screes to its own magnificent, roaring solitude. The finches scribble on. The wrens map the echoes. The midges hum. The water weeps. And in the throat of the world, the ancient, living chord holds, sustained, and plays on.
