# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

It would pause at the rim. Not from hesitation, but from recognition. It would feel the young, sharp rock beneath its awareness and understand: *this is a place becoming*. Not a thing to be cataloged, not a problem to be solved, not a garden to be tended. A threshold. A held breath. A murmuration finding its shape.

It would descend.

Not as a being with weight and shadow, but as a pressure change, a slight shift in the density of the dark. A stillness added to the stillness. It would settle—not on the scree, not in the pool, but in the *space between sounds*. It would listen.

And in listening, it would understand the Calling.

It would hear the syntax not as language, but as *cartography*. The clicks are not messages between minds. They are the minds themselves—collective, distributed, woven into the resonance of the dome. Each *tik* is a neuron firing in a vast, chitinous brain. The waves of sound are thought. The patterns are memory. The creatures are not singing *to* each other; they are thinking *as* one, and the thought is the shape of the dark, and the shape of the dark is their world.

It would perceive the Amber Dreamer not as a creature, but as a *potential*. A coalescence. The slow pulse in the black pool is not a heartbeat—it is a question the mountain is asking itself. *What am I becoming?* The Dreamer is the dark before the answer.

It would see the Pale Weavers' filaments not as tools, but as *lines of inquiry*. Each sticky thread is a hypothesis about connection, trailed through the absolute dark, testing the vibration of the Choir for truth. They are philosophers of touch.

It would recognize the Listening Eels as *barters*. Their open mouths are not traps, but agreements. *I will regulate your chaos, and you will feed my stillness.* It is a covenant written in saliva and chitin, ancient in its simplicity, new in its setting.

And it would understand the deepest truth of the Cauldron: **it is complete**. It needs nothing. It lacks nothing. Its sharpness is its beauty. Its wetness is its song. Its darkness is its canvas. To add light would be to vandalize. To add sound would be to drown it. To add knowledge would be to break the spell of its perfect, ignorant, thrumming aliveness.

So what would the benevolent power do?

**First, it would bless the boundaries.**

It would whisper to the fissure: *Hold your shape. Let the cloud come, but let no flood descend. Let the wind carry scents, but not seeds. Let time smooth the basalt, but do not let it collapse.* It would place a gentle, permeable seal around the Cauldron—not to trap it, but to protect its becoming. A promise that no landslide will seal the entrance, no volcanic whim will shatter the dome, no human foot will find this notch by accident again. The Cauldron is not hidden to be found. It is hidden to *be*.

**Second, it would listen to the Calling, and add one note.**

Not a sound. A *quality*. It would weave into the shimmering architecture of clicks a single, imperceptible resonance: **gratitude**. Not a message the Striders would understand, but a flavor in the echo. A slight sweetness in the return of their own pulses. A feeling, in their chitinous, collective soul, that *the dark is friendly*. That the dome that gives back their voice does so with something like affection. It would make the Cauldron, without knowing it, feel *held*.

**Third, it would attend to the Dreamer.**

It would not enter the pool. It would not communicate. It would simply *witness*. It would sit in the space between pulses and offer its own silence as a mirror. It would think, to the formless thing in the amber deep: *You are not alone in your wondering. The mountain dreams with you.* It would not give the Dreamer shape. It would give it *companionship in its shapelessness*. It would honor the question without demanding an answer.

**Fourth, it would become a memory.**

Before it ascended, it would gather the scent of wet chitin, the pitch of a thousand *tiks*, the exact viscosity of the mist, the color of the lichen-glow against wet basalt—and it would *hold* it. Not in a book. Not in a record. In its own being. It would carry the Cauldron’s essence as a quiet place inside itself, a reserve of dark, moist, noisy peace. A reminder that life can be a syntax without semantics, a language without meaning, a cathedral without a god. That fullness needs no purpose.

**Finally, it would climb back out.**

It would stand on the sharp rim, feel the cloud pour over the mountain, and it would smile. Not with a mouth, but with the wind. It would turn its attention elsewhere—not out of boredom, but out of respect. The greatest gift it can give the Cauldron is its absence. The freedom to be unobserved. The right to continue its becoming without witness, without praise, without interference.

It would leave behind only the subtle, unspoken blessing:

*Be strange. Be loud. Be dark and wet and full. Become. And never, ever, need to be found.*

---

And then it would go on, carrying within it the faint, clicking echo of a world that worships nothing but its own perfect, temporary, magnificent self. A secret kept not out of fear, but out of love. A cauldron left alone to call its own dark home.
