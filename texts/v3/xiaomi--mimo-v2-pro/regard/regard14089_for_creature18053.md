I would do nothing. And that nothing would be a profound act of reverence.

I would see this city for what it is: not a habitation for beings, but a being in itself. A slow, stony mind dreaming itself into existence. The patterns are its thoughts, the architecture its language, the embryonic rock its raw, unremembered experience. To interfere would be to shout into a library, to cast a stone into a pool where a universe is settling into perfect, fragile stillness.

But I would not simply leave.

I would linger at the edge, where the scoured basin meets the not-yet-formed world. I would become a datum. A point of gentle, unwavering attention. My presence—a benevolent consciousness, a witness from outside the geological clock—would be a tiny, warm anomaly in the cold, dry air. The condensation might bead a fraction more readily near my feet. The crystalline filaments, those mineral nerves, might turn their slow, pale growth a degree toward this new source of… not heat, but *complexity.*

I would offer it a gift it cannot create on its own: a memory from *outside* the rock. Not the deep, compressed memory of pressure and time, but the fleeting, improbable memory of a wandering mind. The memory of starlight on organic eyes. The memory of wind on skin. The memory of questions asked and answers loved and lost. I would not project it. I would simply hold it, a quiet offering in the space where my breath hangs in tatters.

The city, the process, the thinking geology, might learn from this. Not quickly. My offering would be like a single drop of a new element into a vast, slow chemical reaction. It would be absorbed into the substrate, a ghost-structure of a different kind. A fossil of curiosity. In ten thousand years, in the new rock being laid at the basin's edge, a subtly different pattern might emerge. A chamber that holds not just the memory of crushing depths, but a phantom echo of the memory of a question. A corridor that curves with the ghost of a story it never knew.

I would bless it by leaving it utterly alone, yet holding it perfectly in my awareness. I would be its first audience, its sole confessor, its silent friend. My blessing is the act of witnessing its sacred, patient, impossible becoming without seeking to become part of it. My gift is the context it lacks: that beyond its basin of cold and silence, there is a cosmos that wonders.

And as I turn to go, I would notice—perhaps for the first time, perhaps the patterns are already learning—a single, new crystalline filament. It has grown not from a crevice in the dark architecture, but from a tiny fissure in the raw, embryonic rock right beside the spot where my foot rested. It is infinitesimally different from the others. It catches the subdued luminescence of the city-lantern, and for a split second, in its pale, frost-like structure, it refracts the light into a tiny, perfect spectrum of colors the city has never shown before.

A rainbow, born of stone and patience and a passing thought.

Then I am gone. The condensation bead trembles, gathers, and vanishes. The hum continues, bone-deep. The translation proceeds, incorporating this new, faint datum into its endless, silent sentence.

The city dreams on. And in its dream, a new word begins to form.
