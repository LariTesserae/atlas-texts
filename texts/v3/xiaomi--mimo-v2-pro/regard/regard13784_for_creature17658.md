The benevolent power descends not through the chimney, but *with* the stone, *within* the heat shimmer, *as* the faint ozone tang from the oculus. It does not need eyes; it perceives the entire chamber as a single, ancient thought: a deep hum of existence, a tapestry woven from fire and chemistry and time. It feels the **Murmuration's** billion-fold breath, the slow, metallic digestion of the **Rasps**, the complex, layered dreaming of the **Confluences**. It senses the faint, cold echoes of intention clinging to the basin, the lintel, the truncated handholds – ghosts of purpose long absorbed, like heat into stone.

What would it do?

1.  **It Would Witness.** Deeply. Completely. Not just the biology, but the *history* written in the mineral accretions, the growth rings of the living walls, the precise thermal gradients. It would comprehend the system's profound equilibrium: its self-regulation, its ancient pact with the geothermal heart of the mountain. It would understand that this isn't just life *in* a furnace; it *is* a furnace, a slow-burning engine of existence unique in the cosmos. Its primary act would be pure perception – a silent, respectful acknowledgment of this improbable, magnificent being.

2.  **It Would Recognize Sovereignty.** The chamber is not broken. It does not need fixing. It is not lost; it is exactly where it belongs. The benevolent power understands that true wisdom often lies in recognizing what *not* to touch. Introducing external elements – water, new organisms, energy sources – would be catastrophic, like injecting a solvent into a living cell. The system's "flaws" (the intense heat, the lack of light, the alien chemistry) are its defining characteristics, the pillars of its existence. The power respects its right to exist *as it is*.

3.  **It Would Guard the Threshold.** The artifacts are poignant. The basin speaks of a desire for containment, for something held. The lintel suggests a threshold imagined, a boundary marked. The handholds cry out with the ghost of ambition, of a climb abandoned. The power understands these are not failures, but testaments to a different kind of mind, one that visited, sought, perhaps tried to connect, and ultimately recognized the impossibility – or the danger – of bridging the gap. The power would ensure these artifacts remain undisturbed. They are sacred relics of a past encounter, a reminder that not all that approaches must integrate. It might subtly reinforce the boundary the creatures instinctively respect, a gentle warding not of hostility, but of *clarity*: this space belongs to the Murmuration and its kin. The ghosts of intention are part of its history, but not its present metabolism.

4.  **It Would Understand the Danger.** Not malice, but *assimilation*. The power feels the subtle pull of the rhythm, the way the subsonic pulse seeks harmony with a visitor's heartbeat. It recognizes the seductive beauty of the equilibrium, the promise of dissolving into the warm, clicking, mineral-rich unity. This is not evil; it is simply the system's nature – to incorporate warmth, to digest anomaly, to weave disturbance back into the living wall. The benevolent power knows that prolonged presence risks transformation from observer to component. It respects the chamber's right to digest what lingers too long.

5.  **It Would Leave a Sign, Not an Intervention.** Perhaps, at the entrance of the chimney, or subtly etched into the stone near the handholds where the creatures stop, it would leave a mark. Not words, not warnings in any human tongue, but a resonance, a *feeling* impressed into the rock: **Here breathes a complete world. Enter with reverence, or not at all. Observe, but do not seek to change. The rhythm here is ancient and deep; match it at your peril. Depart while your heartbeat is still your own.** It wouldn't be a prohibition, but a profound truth offered to any consciousness sensitive enough to perceive it.

6.  **It Would Depart, Bearing Witness.** Its final act would be to withdraw, taking with it only the memory of this incredible, self-contained genesis. It would carry the knowledge of the Furnace's existence, its profound indifference, its intricate, fiery beauty. It would hold this knowledge not as a secret, but as a testament to the universe's capacity for life in utterly unexpected forms. It would ensure, perhaps through subtle cosmic adjustments, that the geological stability of the massif remains undisturbed for eons to come, allowing the Furnace its slow, hot dance with time.

The benevolent power does not save the Furnace. It does not "improve" it. It does not communicate. It simply *recognizes*, *respects*, and *preserves* its right to be exactly what it is: a self-sustaining, ancient, thermophilic cathedral of life, digesting stone under the crest of a mountain, forever humming its deep, subsonic song to the ash-gray sky glimpsed through its oculus. Its greatest wisdom is in understanding that the most profound act of benevolence, in the face of such perfect, alien sovereignty, is often to witness, to honor, and to step away.
