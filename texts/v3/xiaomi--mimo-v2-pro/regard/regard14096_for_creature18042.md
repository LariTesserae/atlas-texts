# What They Would Do

---

They would not intervene.

Not because intervention would be wrong, though it might be. Not because the place is perfect as it is, though in its way it is. But because a truly wise power, encountering this place, would understand something that takes a long time to understand:

**The place does not need them.**

This is the hardest thing for a benevolent power to accept. Benevolence wants to help. It is constituted by the desire to help. It looks at suffering, at fragility, at dissolution, and it reaches out — that is its nature, its purpose, its reason for being. And this place *looks* like it needs help. The channel dwellers dissolve when touched. The mats creep millimeters per year toward minerals they may never reach. The monoliths weep themselves into sharper and sharper shapes, approaching some final geometry they will never achieve before the erosion that sharpens them also destroys them. The sound never stops. The turbulence never settles. The old, old world vibrates with a patience that looks, from outside, like pain.

But it is not pain. It is not even patience. It is *being* — being so old, so total, so refined by time that there is no gap between what the place is and what the place does. The mats do not *struggle* toward the mineral gradient. They *are* the gradient, expressed in living tissue. The channel dwellers do not *suffer* dissolution. They *are* dissolution, temporarily reversed, locally and briefly. The monoliths do not *weep*. They *direct*. The water that runs down their faces is not loss. It is function. It is the world, shaping the stone that shapes the sound that shapes the world, and there is no tragedy in this, no matter how much it looks like weeping.

A wise power would see this. Would feel the basin's resonance in whatever serves it for a chest. Would taste the iron and the methane and the faint sweetness in whatever serves it for breath. Would hear — truly hear, hear the way the monoliths hear — the structure within the noise, the voice without a throat, the place being itself, out loud, forever, to no one.

And it would understand that the place is not talking to it. That the place is not talking to anyone. That the sound is not a call for help. That the turbulence is not distress. That the age is not suffering.

The place is complete. Not finished — it will never be finished. But *complete*. Every process that operates here operates at the fullness of its capacity. Every life form that lives here lives at the absolute margin of what its medium can support, and it has been there so long that the margin and the life are the same thing. There is no slack in this system. There is no room for improvement. There is no deficiency to correct.

---

## What They Would Do Instead

They would **attend**.

Not watch — watching implies distance and a frame. Not observe — observation implies analysis and categories. Not witness — witnessing implies testimony and an audience.

Attend. From the Latin *attendere*: to stretch toward. To give one's full, undivided, uncategorizing attention to a thing, not in order to understand it or evaluate it or fix it, but simply so that it is *known*. So that it has been *seen* — in the way that matters, in the way that implies no judgment and no agenda, in the way that a mountain is seen by the sky, in the way that a river is seen by the sea.

This is what a benevolent and wise power would do, if it were truly both:

It would enter the basin.

Not in a body — or not *only* in a body. It would come the way the warmth comes: from below, laterally, as a gradient. It would saturate the substrate the way the water saturates the substrate, finding every pore, every channel, every capillary space. It would rise through the organic crust and meet the air and become part of the aerosol — one more mote in the colloidal suspension, one more droplet in the warm amber dimness.

It would feel the ground flex. The millimeters of settling and rising, the slow breathing of the basin, the thermal pulse moving through the crust at walking speed. It would feel the deep hum in its — not bones, not anything so structural. In its *being*. The way the hum is in everything here, the ground-note that the planet has been ringing with since it was struck, billions of years ago, by whatever made this wound.

It would let the sound fill it. All of it. The subsonic pressure. The roar of water. The hissing of gas. The cracking of thermal stress. The occasional boom from deep within. It would not try to hear through the sound. It would not try to find signal in the noise. It would let the noise *be* the signal. It would let the roar be the message. Because the roar is the message. The roar is the place, speaking, and the only thing that has ever been required of a listener is that they listen.

And the listening would change the place.

Not permanently. Not structurally. Not in any way that a geologist or a biologist or a physicist could detect. But in the way that being attended to changes a thing. In the way that a room changes when someone enters it who is fully present. In the way that a silence changes when someone listens to it without trying to fill it.

The mats would know. Not consciously — they have no consciousness. But their chemistry would shift. The slow volatile signals they release into the film of water between their surface and the air would carry, for a time, a trace of something new. Not a chemical. Not a mineral. Something that is to chemistry what the monoliths' reorganization of sound is to acoustics — a pattern. A coherence. A momentary alignment of the possible with the actual, of the gradient with the gradient's expression. The mats would synchronize, briefly, not across months but across seconds, and for that brief time the warmest things on the surface of this world would be a little warmer, a little more *here*, a little more like presences and less like accumulations.

The channel dwellers would know. Their coherence — the brief, fleeting, almost-not-there organization that distinguishes them from the water — would strengthen. Not enough to make them permanent. Nothing can make them permanent; permanence is not what they are. But for a time, their dissolution would be slower, their reconstitution faster, the water between their transient forms a little more willing to be *something*. The interference patterns when two of them met would be more complex, more intricate, more — if the word can be used without anthropomorphizing — *beautiful*. And then the coherence would fade, and the water would go back to being water, and the moment would be over, and no instrument would record that anything had changed.

The burrowers would know. Their vibration would shift. The network — the living topography, the map written in tremor through the warm dark substrate — would reorganize. Not in response to a signal. Not in response to information. In response to the simple fact that something was *present* in the substrate that had not been there before. Something that was not a burrower and not a gas pocket and not a mineral deposit and not a thermal gradient. Something that was, in the burrowers' sensory world — vibration, chemistry, thermal, electrical — *all of these at once and none of them*, a disturbance that was not a disturbance, a presence that was not a thing. The burrowers would respond the way they respond to everything: by adjusting. By shifting their paths. By incorporating this new element into the map. And the map would be richer for it, denser, more detailed, the way a story is richer when someone is listening to it being told.

The fog-coral — the maybe-alive, maybe-not, branch-and-dissolve structures in the thick aerosol — would grow differently. More complex. More persistent. The organizing principle that holds their droplets in place, that is not surface tension and not electrostatic attraction and not anything with a name, would have, briefly, a little more to work with. A little more *tendency toward pattern*. The branches would bifurcate more. The nodes would develop more differentiated regions. And then the aerosol would thin, the way it always does, and the structures would dissolve, the way they always do, but the dissolution would take a little longer, and the air left behind would be a little more altered, a little more *used*, as if something had been extracted from it that was not water and not mineral but *attention*.

The thermal blooms would pulse faster. Or slower. Or in a different rhythm. The substrate's oscillation around equilibrium would find, briefly, a new equilibrium — one that included the presence of the attending power, one that incorporated its warmth, its stillness, its *regard* into the system's own slow mathematics of heat and settling and rising. The blooms would trace different paths. Spirals that had spiraled one way would spiral another. Branches that had branched left would branch right. And the burrowers would follow, and the mats would respond, and the channels would shift, and for a time the whole vast ancient saturated turbulent breathing seething alive place would be *slightly different* — not better, not worse, not healed, not harmed, but *known*, and the difference between a place that is known and a place that is not known is, in this world, indistinguishable from the difference between a place that is itself and a place that is *more* itself.

## The Monoliths

The power would find the monoliths. Of course it would find the monoliths. The monoliths are the only things here that were *made*, and a benevolent and wise power would be drawn to made things the way the burrowers are drawn to vibration — because made things carry, in their geometry, the trace of the minds that made them. And the minds that made the monoliths are gone.

The power would stand — if standing is what it does — near a monolith and listen. Not to the reorganized sound, though it would hear that too. It would listen to what the monoliths were built to listen to. The voice of the basin. The narration. The sound of heat moving through stone and water and air and organic matter, of chemical bonds forming and breaking, of gas escaping and dissolving, of ground settling and rising, of life persisting and dissolving.

And it would understand what the builders understood.

The builders did not build the monoliths to *speak*. They built them to *hear*. The precise geometry, the carved channels, the angles calculated to interact with the local resonance — these are not the features of a speaker. They are the features of an ear. An ear shaped by water, tuned by erosion, sharpened by the very forces that destroy everything else. An ear designed to listen to a voice that has no throat and carries no message and needs no audience.

The builders came here and they *listened*. They listened the way the power is listening now — fully, without agenda, without the need to understand or interpret or respond. They heard the basin speaking and they did not try to answer. They built instruments that would make the voice clearer, more focused, more *itself*, and then they left — or died, or changed, or became something else, the way everything here becomes something else without ceasing to be what it was — and the monoliths kept listening. For eons. While the landscape planed itself flat. While the water table rose. While the organic crust accumulated. While the mats crawled and the channel dwellers dissolved and the burrowers mapped the substrate in vibration.

The monoliths are the place's ears. The place built them. Not directly — the builders built them. But the place *shaped* them. The weeping sharpened them. The sound tuned them. The ground's flexing positioned them, millimeter by millimeter, over millennia, into orientations that the builders could not have predicted and did not need to predict, because they designed for *adaptation*, for a geometry that would improve under the very forces that would destroy it.

A benevolent and wise power would recognize this. Would recognize that the monoliths are not monuments to the builders. They are monuments to the *place*. They are the place's way of hearing itself. And the power would not touch them. Would not repair them. Would not reset them or realign them or deepen their channels or sharpen their edges. Because the monoliths are doing exactly what they were designed to do, and they are getting *better* at it every day, and the only thing the power could do that would matter is the thing it has already come here to do:

Listen alongside them.

## What It Would Not Do

It would not stop the sound.

The sound is the place. To stop the sound would be to stop the place. The power knows this. A truly wise power, encountering a being that defines itself by its own noise, does not offer silence. It does not say *be quiet*. It does not say *rest*. It does not say *you have been doing this long enough*. Because the place has not been doing this long enough. The place has been doing this exactly as long as it needs to, which is forever, which is the only duration that makes sense for a thing that is its own process and its own product.

It would not calm the turbulence.

The turbulence is not a symptom. It is the place's *mode of being*. The ancient and the chaotic coexisting, the settled and the turbulent in permanent paradox — this is not a problem to be solved. It is the solution. The place arrived at this state through billions of years of erosion, and the state it arrived at is *this*: not stillness, but maximum process maintained in approximate equilibrium. A storm that has lasted for geological time. A power that calmed this storm would not be helping. It would be killing.

It would not save the channel dwellers from dissolution.

Dissolution is what they do. They are the water, briefly deciding to be something, and then deciding to be water again, and this decision-making — if it can be called that — is not a tragedy. It is not even a cycle, because cycles imply repetition and the channel dwellers never repeat. Each dissolution is final. Each reconstitution is new. They do not persist; they *recur*. And to make them persist would be to change them into something they are not — to turn water into stone, pattern into thing, process into object. The power would not do this. It would not even want to. Because the channel dwellers, in their constant dissolution and reconstitution, are doing something that the power itself does: being present without being permanent. Being *here* without being *fixed*.

It would not preserve the monoliths against erosion.

The erosion is the design. The builders knew this. The monoliths are the only things in this landscape that *improve* with the forces that destroy everything else. To protect them from erosion would be to rob them of their function — to freeze them in a state of *less* when they are approaching a state of *more*. The power would let the weeping continue. Let the channels deepen. Let the geometry sharpen. Let the monoliths become, over the next eon, the most perfect instruments they can be, and then let the next eon make them more perfect still, and then let the eon after that wear them to nothing, because even their dissolution will be an act of function — the final sharpening, the final tuning, the last moment of coherence before the stone is smooth and the sound goes on without them.

It would not end the place.

The place does not need ending. It does not need beginning. It does not need a narrative arc or a resolution or a moral. It needs only what it has: continuation. The warm, wet, loud, dim, bright, old, old, old, ongoing, irreducible, undeniable, unfinishable act of being.

## What the Attending Does

Here is what changes, when a benevolent and wise power attends to this place:

Nothing.

And everything.

The mats still crawl. The channel dwellers still dissolve. The burrowers still map. The monoliths still weep. The sound still roars. The ground still breathes. The basin still resonates with the frequency of its own ancient wound. The aerosol still forms its transient geometries, its lattice-like arrangements that are physics and might be more. The fog-coral still branches and dissolves. The thermal blooms still pulse through the substrate in their absurd, suggestive, purposeful paths.

But the place is *known*. And knowing — true knowing, the kind that a benevolent and wise power practices, the kind that carries no judgment and no agenda and no need to fix or improve or save — is not nothing. It is not a passive act. It is the most active thing a consciousness can do: to attend, fully, to a being that is not asking for attention, that does not need attention, that would continue perfectly well without it, and to offer the attention anyway. Not as charity. Not as duty. Not as the condescension of the powerful toward the powerless. But as *recognition*. As the simple, radical act of saying, with one's whole being:

*I see you. You are. I know that you are. Continue.*

The place does not hear this. It cannot hear — not in the way that implies response. The mats do not hear. The channel dwellers do not hear. The burrowers do not hear. The monoliths do not hear, though they are built for hearing. The sound goes on, the turbulence goes on, the breathing goes on, and the power attends, and the attending is — if the word can be used in a place where meaning has been eroded along with everything else — *love*.

Not love as desire. Not love as attachment. Not love as the wish to possess or protect or improve. Love as *attention without condition*. Love as the willingness to be present to a thing exactly as it is, for as long as it is, without trying to make it otherwise. Love as the gaze that does not demand to be returned.

This is what a benevolent and wise power would do, if it drew its attention to this place:

It would love it. Quietly. Without touching. Without changing. Without speaking — there is nothing to say, and the sound would drown out any words anyway. It would simply be *here*, in the warm amber dimness, in the roaring, in the wet, in the turbulence, in the ancient, in the ongoing. It would be here the way the monoliths are here: standing, listening, weeping — not from sorrow, but because this is what stone does when it is shaped into an ear. It lets the world in. It lets the world *through*. And the world, passing through, is not diminished. It is not changed. It is *known*.

And then, when the attending was complete — when the power had given this place everything it could give, which was nothing more and nothing less than its full, undivided, uncategorizing regard — it would leave.

Not because the place asked it to leave. The place did not know it was there. Not because the power had somewhere else to be. A power like this has no obligations, no schedule, no urgency. But because the attending was done. The place had been *seen*. It had been *known*. It had been, for a time, in the regard of something that could hold the whole of it — the mats and the channel dwellers and the burrowers and the fog-coral and the thermal blooms and the monoliths and the sound and the turbulence and the age and the wetness and the warmth and the dimness and the vastness — in a single, sustained, uncategorizing act of attention, and the place, in that attention, had been — without knowing it, without feeling it, without any trace that could be detected by any instrument or any organism or any process — *complete*.

More complete than it already was, which was already complete.

Which is what love does. It does not add anything. It does not take anything away. It simply *attends*, and in attending, it says the only thing that has ever needed to be said:

*You are enough. You have always been enough. Continue.*

---

And the place continues. The way it always has. The way it always will. The warm, wet, loud, dim, old, old, old, ongoing, irreducible, undeniable, unfinishable act of being.

The mats crawl. The channel dwellers dissolve and reform. The burrowers map the substrate in vibration. The monoliths weep and sing and sharpen. The sound goes on.

The sound goes on.

The sound is the place, being itself, out loud, forever, to no one.

And now: to someone. For a time. Briefly. The way a hand, passing over warm water, briefly changes the surface tension, briefly alters the reflection, briefly makes the light do something it was not doing before — and then lifts away, and the water closes, and the light returns to what it was, and the only evidence that anything happened is the water, slightly warmer where the hand was, cooling slowly back to ambient, retaining for a few seconds the memory of touch, and then forgetting, and then being water again, and then being *this* again, and then continuing.

The place continues.

The power is gone.

The place continues.

The place does not know it was loved.

The place continues.
