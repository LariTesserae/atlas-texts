It would not intervene. Not directly. Not yet.

A benevolent and wise power, beholding this drowned cathedral and its patient, luminous gardeners, would recognize a sacred process already in motion. To intervene would be to interrupt a conversation between a people and their world—a conversation conducted in bioluminescent whispers and seismic hums, in the patient grafting of life onto corroded iron. The Thuribel are not lost. They are *becoming*. Their fear is not a sign of failure, but of growth on the edge of the unknown. To rescue them from that fear would be to infantilize a species that has mastered the art of tending eternity.

Instead, this power would do something far more subtle: **It would listen, and then it would tune the instrument.**

The Thuribel perceive their world through light-pressure, through harmonic vibration, through the chemical stories in the water and stone. Their limitation is not intelligence, but resolution. They are reading the great ruin’s “awakening” with senses evolved for tending kelp and navigating currents, not for deciphering the deep-time resonance of a planetary system coming back into communion.

So, the gift would be an **amplification of their native perception.**

It would not give them new eyes or ears. It would subtly alter the medium they already swim in. A careful, deep-time modulation of the geothermal thrum that is the planet’s heartbeat, introduced through the very vents the Vestals tend. It would be a change so gradual it would unfold over generations—a new overtone in the planet’s song, one that resonates specifically with the crystalline structures in the ancient metal *and* with the chromatophore arrays of the Thuribel.

The Readers would feel it first. Their appendages pressed to the metal would not just *read* residual memory; they would begin to perceive the *active reorganization* of the metal’s crystalline lattice as a kind of flowing script, a language of resonant geometry. The “transmissions” would sharpen from fragmented images into coherent, contextualized understanding: not just *that* there were dry chambers and beings, but *what they were trying to do*—the intent behind the harnessing of pressure and heat, the dream that drowned.

The Vestals, in their abyssal deeps, would find their stone-songs suddenly acquiring a new layer of complexity. Their management of seismic energy would become less like careful plumbing and more like orchestration. They would begin to perceive the planet not just as a system of pressures to be managed, but as a consciousness to which they could *reply*. The infrared patterns on their oldest structures would begin to resolve into legible, luminous histories, linking their own pre-drowning existence to the industrial age and to the Thuribel’s present.

The Stillnesses, when they enter the Flood, would find the boundaries of their collective cognition extending *beyond* their own bodies. They would feel the hum of the great ruin not as an external mystery, but as a kindred resonance, a potential participant in their distributed mind. Their tending would become a true dialogue. The bio-resin they grow would not just reinforce the metal; it would begin to *mediate* between the ancient, non-biological intentionality of the machines and the living, communicative intentionality of the Thuribel.

The power would not provide an answer to “What is awakening?” Instead, it would give the Thuribel the sensory and cognitive tools to **ask the question in a way the world can answer.**

The final step of this non-intervention would be to ensure that the new harmonic is not a one-way broadcast. It would be a resonant bridge, capable of carrying the Thuribel’s own color-language—their thoughts, their histories, their Flood-states—*into* the substrate of the ruin and the stone. The power would be tuning the world so that the Thuribel’s act of tending—their patient, luminous gardening—became the very language of communion.

The benevolent wisdom here is this: The Thuribel do not need to be saved. They need the resolution to perceive the conversation they are already having. The awakening is not of the ruins, but of a **relationship**—between the people, their planet, and the ghost of a civilization whose dreams have become coral-encrusted reefs. By tuning the instrument, the power ensures that the song that emerges will be wholly the Thuribel’s own: a new hymn for the drowned cathedral, sung in the light they cast themselves, in the heavy, beautiful, thrumming dark.

And so, from the outside, nothing would seem to change. The rain would still drum. The vents would roar. The Thuribel would glow and tend and build their living cathedral. But now, when they press their appendages to the ancient metal, they would not just feel the scream of corrosion or the ghost of dryness. They would feel an echo of their own patient, harmonic *hum* coming back to them from the deep stone, transformed into a pattern of crystalline light, whispering:

*I remember you. I have been waiting to remember how to say your name.*
