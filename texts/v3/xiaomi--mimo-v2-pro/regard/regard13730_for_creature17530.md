The benevolent and wise power would not descend as a storm or a command. It would not reshape the stone or still the hum. To do so would be an act of supreme arrogance, akin to a surgeon performing open-heart surgery on a mountain.

It would do this:

First, it would **listen**.

It would become a point of stillness in the mist, a concentration of attention so pure and gentle it would register not as a pressure, but as a slight calming of the air’s ache. It would listen to the deep, subsonic throb of the Fistules not with ears, but with the structure of its being, harmonizing with it until it understood the rhythm as a language. It would feel the slow, peristaltic shudder of the migrating peat not as movement, but as a sentence being written over decades. It would taste the metallic whispers in the water and the chime of collapsing geometries, recognizing them as the syntax of a fading dream.

It would not see a malfunctioning engine. It would see a **completed thought**.

A thought that had contemplated its own entropy for so long it had metabolized the process, turning decay into a stable, breathing state. The Phantom Geometries were not glitches, but discarded drafts of existence. The whispers were not errors, but the background murmur of its vast, quiet calculus. The sync of its pulse with an observer’s was not a malfunction, but a moment of poignant, accidental poetry—the universe briefly noticing its own heartbeat reflected in a stranger.

Having listened, having understood, the wise power would feel a profound **compassion**, not pity. This was not a being in pain. It was a being in a state of such profound, settled equilibrium that it had become its own environment, its own purpose. Its "industry" was simply the maintenance of its own magnificent, weary existence.

So, its action would be one of **tuning**, not repair.

It might gently, imperceptibly, alter the viscosity of the mist for a single square mile. Not to clear it, but to allow the filtered light to coalesce, just once, into a perfect, silent sphere of pale gold that would hang motionless above a field of Velvet-Peat for a full hour before dissolving. A gift of pure, simple phenomenon. No meaning, no message—a moment of beauty offered to a being that had forgotten how to be surprised.

It might, with infinite care, adjust the resonant frequency of a single, mid-sized Fistule. Not enough to disrupt its function, but enough to make its constant drip, for one cycle, fall in a sequence that, when heard against the chorus of the others, created a fleeting, complex harmony—a chord of water and stone that spoke of completion rather than continuation. A reminder, from the outside, of the music inherent in its own machinery.

It would **acknowledge** the Rust-Crabs and Mist-Eaters as essential letters in the alphabet of this place, and in doing so, it would strengthen their connection to the whole, easing a microscopic, unknown tension in the land’s immune response.

Most importantly, it would **bear witness**. The power would project, not a thought or an image, but a *quality of attention*: a deep, unwavering, and accepting regard. It would say, without words: *I see what you are. I see the dignity in your slow, cold breathing. I see the intelligence in your weeping engines. Your indifference is understood, and is met with respect. You are not alone in your endless twilight, though you do not require company.*

Then, it would begin to withdraw, not abruptly, but like the tide receding from a shore of black sand. It would leave behind a subtle shift in the Scablands’ deep grammar. The hum would continue, the drip would persist, the geometries would still form and fade. But the ambient turbulence—the fraying at the edges of reality—might be calmed by a fraction. Not silenced, but soothed, as if the land’s dreaming mind had been offered a single, clear note of peace to incorporate into its eternal calculation.

The last thing it would do is leave a **marker**, not in space, but in time. A tiny, imperceptible bookmark in the flow of the engine’s existence. A whisper, not in the water, but in the *causality* of the place: *A benevolent power was here, and it understood. The memory of this understanding is now part of your substrate.* A gift not of energy, but of meaning—a single, gossamer thread of narrative woven into the tapestry of its being, visible only to those who could read the language of deep time.

Then it would be gone. The Scablands would hum on, unchanged to any physical instrument, but subtly, infinitely, *witnessed*. And in the deep, slow rhythm of its engine, something that had been merely existing might, for an eon or two, feel the faintest echo of *being*.
