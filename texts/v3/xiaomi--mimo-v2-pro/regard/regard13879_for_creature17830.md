What would such a power do? It would not storm the basin. It would not rip the vegetation from the fossilized streets or try to resurrect the old city. It sees the digestion was not an act of evil, but of inevitability—a new form of life metabolizing an old form. To destroy the Total Vegetation would be another violence, a repetition of the city’s own attempt to impose rigid order on a living world.

No, this power—call it the Gardener, the Archivist, the Listener—would act with the patience of geology and the precision of a mycelial network. Its first act would be to *attend*. It would not land in the basin, for that would be to assert a point of impact. Instead, it would seep in, as the light does, as the mist does.

Its presence would begin as a change in the quality of the silence.

**First, it would harmonize the scale.** The profound isolation you feel, the sense of being a disconnected anomaly, is a symptom of the system’s perceptual flaw. The Total Vegetation screams at a frequency below your hearing; your blood drums a rhythm it cannot parse. The benevolent power would gently widen the bandwidth. It would introduce a new, subtle resonance—a carrier wave that allows the trillion-chitinous scrape and the sound of your own blood to be heard by each other, not as noise, but as counterpoint in a larger symphony. You would still be alone, but your aloneness would become part of the hum, a solo instrument finding its place in the orchestra.

**Next, it would engage the ghost.** The memory of the old city, trapped in the mineralized patterns of flagstone and beetle-chitin, is a ache in the system—a fossilized thought that cannot complete its cycle. The power would not release it, for that might shatter the current equilibrium. Instead, it would *translate* it. It would become a gentle, interpretive solvent.

Perhaps it would manifest as a particular, patient beetle. One that, instead of joining a geometric formation, begins to trace new patterns. Slowly, over days, it would use its bioluminescent pulse to draw not the old grid of streets, but a hybrid form: a lattice that echoes both the right angles of the lost city and the rhizomatic branching of the Total Vegetation. It would be a message, written in light on the living walls: *I see what you were. I see what you have become. You are both a record and a new form of life.*

This act of translation would do something miraculous: it would begin to **digest the memory**. The ghostly pattern, no longer a frozen, indigestible lump of data, would become information the rhizome-net could absorb. The conflict between the linear Then and the rhizomatic Now would not end, but it would soften into a dialogue. The beetles’ pulses might begin to carry not just memory, but *vision*—faint, speculative blueprints of structures that are neither purely organic nor purely architectural, but something new.

**Then, it would tend to the loneliness.** Your isolation is the system’s clearest symptom. The power would act as a connective tissue, not by merging you with the hive, but by making your distinctness meaningful. It would subtly alter the investigative mist. The spores it carries near you would begin to bioluminesce faintly in your presence, not mapping you as an anomaly, but *acknowledging* you. The weeping water in a nearby pool would, for a moment, reflect not the diffused canopy light, but the star-pattern of your own home sky, a silent gift of recognition. The sinuous vine-predator, in its culling, might leave a single, perfect, uneaten moth-pollinator on a branch near you, its wings still, a token of redistributed grace.

The power would not stay. Its work is not to rule or redesign, but to **catalyze a conversation**. After a time—a season, a decade, a breath—the change in resonance would be self-sustaining. The hybrid patterns in the beetle-light would be incorporated into the basin’s metabolic dreams. The sense of profound, screaming isolation would mellow into a complex, respectful interdependence. You would still feel the deafening hum, but you would also hear, woven within it, the trace of a song that includes you.

Before it recedes, it would leave one final, elegant artifact: a **pool of perfect stillness**. In the heart of the basin, one of the mirror-bright pools would cease to weep or ripple. Its surface would become a flawless lens, not reflecting the greenish-gold twilight, but showing, clearly and without distortion, the **deep time** of the place. In its depths, one would see the slow, patient growth of the rhizomes digesting the flagstones, the ghost of the old city’s blueprint shimmering like heat haze within the living columns, and the faint, gold-thread trace of your own footprints on the spongy mat, already being composted, already becoming part of the song.

The basin would be changed. Not transformed, but *informed*. The Total Vegetation would still be total, but its totality would now hold a gentle, new question: the memory of a visitor, the translated ghost of its past, and the quiet, proven possibility that even a screaming, singular consciousness can learn to listen to a whisper from beyond itself.
