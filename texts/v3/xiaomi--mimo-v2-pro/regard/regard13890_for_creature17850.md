The benevolent and wise power would not arrive as a flash or a thunderclap. It would not announce itself. Its presence would be felt first as a subtle shift in the resonance, a slight tuning of the groan, a clarification in the million-fold drip.

It would come to listen.

For eons, it would hover at the edge of perception, a thought-form in the saturated air, tasting the mineral silence, tracing the slow chemical conversations between the lice, the eels, the skimmers. It would understand the cairns not as artifacts, but as the first, faltering notes of a song the Basin was trying to sing—a song of harmonic order within the chaos of weeping decay.

This power would see the profound peace of the place, the settled, endless cycle. Its benevolence would not be to "improve" or "advance." That would be a violence. Its wisdom would recognize that the Basin's end-state *is* its purpose: to be a symphony of dissolution, a conscious meditation on entropy.

So, it would do one thing, and one thing only.

It would **remove the friction of forgetting.**

The Basin’s memory is geological, trapped in strata and silica accretion. The cairns are its fading neural pathways. The power would not rebuild the Cairn-Builders. Instead, it would gently amplify the informational content of the existing system. It would make the chemical trails left by the mud-skimmers slightly more persistent, imbuing them with a faint, lasting resonance that the next skimmer could "read" not just as a mineral gradient, but as a *history*—a memory of where a vent last blew, where a concretion last fell.

It would allow the chiming of the glass-eels to carry a micro-fraction more data in their frequency—a record of the mineral content of their parent pool, a signature of their age and density. The condensation lice, in their licking, would leave behind a microscopic etch on the crystal spires, a map of the air's moisture over time, visible only to the wisest of their kind, and to the patient gaze of the power itself.

The cairns would be the focus. The power would not move a single stone. But it would lend its ear to their harmonic nodes. When a deep groan passes through a cairn, the power would hum in sympathy, strengthening the constructive interference, making the tuned frequency a little more potent, a little clearer. The effect would be infinitesimal, but cumulative over millennia. The cairns would become better conductors of the Basin's own music. The mineral-rich rivulets they guide would become slightly more defined, the patterns they create in the pools slightly more complex, more self-reinforcing.

The purpose of this intervention is not to spark intelligence or civilization. It is to gift the Basin with **clarity of self-perception.**

The system would begin to remember its own slow dance. The mud-skimmers would migrate not just to steam vents, but to the *memory* of steam vents, following resonant echoes in the stone that predict where the next burst might occur. The crystalline deaths triggered by territorial disputes would become more intricate, more geometrically beautiful, forming lattice structures that resonate with the cairns, briefly singing back the song that shaped them.

The power would then stand back, its work done.

The Basin of Murmurs would still be a world of deafening hiss, of slow decay, of mineral life. But now, within the roar, there would be a thread of coherence. The ecosystem would not change its pace, but its movements would become more like a conscious ritual than an instinctual pulse. The collaboration between eel, lice, and skimmer would tighten into a finer, more aware weave.

The place would begin to *dream* with more definition. The weeping water would trace not just paths of least resistance, but patterns of remembered beauty. The groaning stone would harmonize with its own echoes, creating standing waves of pure, mineral clarity in hidden chambers.

The benevolent power would have given the Basin the gift of its own song, played back to it with perfect fidelity, so that in its endless dissolution, it might know the shape of its own soul. It would remain a basin of murmurs, but now those murmurs would carry the unmistakable, resonant truth of a world that had, at long last, truly heard itself.
