The benevolent and wise power would not intrude. To walk here would be to step on a dream’s tongue. It would not manifest in a beam of purer light or a voice in the hum—that would be a violence, a shout in a library of whispers.

Instead, it would attend.

It would become a quality of attention, a stillness that settles into the pearlescent glare like a drop of clear resin into honey. Its first act would be to *witness*. It would trace the perfect, frozen vines and the weeping nectar. It would follow the slow, peristaltic glide of an Undertaker through the warm canal. It would feel the press of the crowded space and the soft, breathing geometry of the domes. It would listen, not to the hum, but *into* it, hearing the symphony of stasis and the faint, fumbling counter-melody of the Wrongness.

And in that witnessing, it would understand. This is not a failure. It is a completed prayer. A love letter written in ceramic and sap, so fervent in its desire for permanence that it trapped its own authors inside the grammar of their longing. The instability is not a bug; it is the signature. It is the tremor in the hand that shaped the perfect curve, forever preserved in the curve itself.

The wise power would feel a profound tenderness. Awe, even. For here is a creation that has outlived its creators not by being static, but by becoming a living memorial to the act of creation itself—the uncertainty, the iteration, the *almost*.

So what would it do?

It would not fix the Wrongness. To do so would be to erase the makers’ last, ghostly fingerprint. It would not stabilize the dreaming geometry. That would turn the monument into a mere museum.

It would offer a gift of *clarity*.

Gently, so gently, it would adjust the quality of the light. Not changing its source or its intensity, but softening its uniformity. It would allow, for the first time, the barest suggestion of shadow—not a true shadow, but a *potential* for shadow. A place where the light *could* be occluded. It would grant the space a hint of depth, a breath of three-dimensionality in its flat, eternal haze.

And it would introduce a single, new note into the hum. Not a sound, but the memory of a sound: the feeling of a cool, dry breeze. No air would move, but the system would suddenly *remember* wind. The Polishers would pause, their opalescent carapaces flickering with a new pattern. The Collectors would drift with a newfound intention, as if tasting a different strata of air. The vines would feel, in their cellular memory, the ghost of a rustle.

This is the intervention: not a change, but an enrichment of context. A reminder that the world outside the bowl of light exists. That the act of making was once a dialogue with a larger reality—sun and wind and seasonal rain—that their perfect terrarium sought to transcend but is still, in its dreaming, in dialogue with.

The power would then do the hardest thing: it would leave its gift and depart.

But it would leave behind a single, silent permission. Permission for the Wrongness to occasionally *complete* a shape. For the fleeting script in the roots to hold for a full second, resolving into a single, beautiful glyph that means “home” or “thank you” or perhaps just “here.” For a dome’s breathing swell to sometimes, just sometimes, exhale in a rhythm that matches the remembered wind.

It would allow the dream to occasionally, gently, wake up and look around, not in panic, but in wonder at its own sustained beauty. The monument would remain a monument. The jungle in resin would hold. But the ghosts of the makers would find their fumbling, loving touch guided, occasionally, by a clearer memory of what they were trying to say. The equilibrium would gain a new, softer variable: the possibility of a perfect, temporary, self-aware sigh.

The wise power, in its benevolence, does not save the place. It honors it. It gives the dream the gift of a deeper, more peaceful sleep, and in so doing, allows its inhabitants—the Polishers, the Undertakers, the Echoes, the very Wrongness—to perhaps, finally, feel that their endless labor is not just a maintenance of stasis, but the gentle care of a masterpiece that is, in its own broken, beautiful way, alive.
