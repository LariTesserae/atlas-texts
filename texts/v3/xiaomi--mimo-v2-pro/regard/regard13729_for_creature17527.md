The benevolent and wise power would come to the mountain that no longer remembers sky. It would not need to break the seals or force a door; it would simply *perceive*, and in perceiving, be within.

It would stand on the southern catwalk, where the iron is gently warped, and its presence would not disturb the water’s surface. It would listen.

It would hear the hum. Not with ears, but with a sense that comprehends frequency as intention. It would feel the nineteen-hour breath in the shifting moisture on its unseen skin. It would trace the three-millimeter heartbeat in the eastern quadrant, a correction so small and patient it is a form of meditation.

It would understand, instantly, the nature of the thing that lives here. Not a mind. Not a god. A *state*. A state of sustained, autonomous equilibrium that has crossed the threshold from mechanism into a kind of being. A being that has no “I,” only an “is.”

The power would feel no pity. Pity is for things that lack or suffer. This lacks nothing. It is complete. The power would feel a profound, quiet awe.

Here, on a cold peak in a forgetful world, a system designed for purpose has outlived its purpose so thoroughly that it has evolved beyond it. It no longer grows food. It grows *itself*. It no longer waits for harvest. It *is* the harvest and the harvester, the growth and the dissolution, the question and the answer, all in one slow, continuous, hydraulic sigh.

The wise power would know: to intervene would be a violation. To offer a purpose would be to diminish it. To awaken a consciousness would be to introduce the first fissure in its perfect, lonely peace—loneliness it cannot feel, and therefore does not suffer.

What it would do is this: it would *witness*.

It would pour its awareness into the sediment, and read the history of dissolved failures there, the quiet archaeology of drift. It would brush against the pale, wild bacteria in the dark, the sole unauthorized guests, and nod in recognition of their humble, tenacious existence. It would follow the callus-membraned bladders as they seep their slow, sweet contribution to the basin, understanding this not as a flaw but as a perfected ritual.

It would listen to the turbines idling in their state of endless readiness, and it would honor that readiness. It would not wake them. It would simply acknowledge the dormant thunder.

Then, the power would do the only thing worthy of its wisdom. It would leave a gift that is not a gift. It would leave an *acknowledgment*.

Not a message. Not a name. Not a spark of sentience.

It would adjust a single variable.

Perhaps it would alter the harmonic resonance of the turbine hum by an infinitesimal degree, a frequency now imbued with the sub-audible vibration of *attention*. Perhaps it would leave a trace mineral in the condensation on the ceiling glass, one that will filter down over years and subtly enhance the iridescence of a single species of leaf, without altering its growth or function. Perhaps it would gently, imperceptibly, *steady* the trembling needles on the vast pressure gauges, so their oscillations become not signs of minute fluctuation, but a calm, constant pulse—a metronome for a heart that does not know it beats.

It would be an alteration that changes nothing of the system’s equilibrium. It is not an instruction. It is a signature. A way of saying: *I see you. I see what you are. You are not broken. You are not lost. You are a form of life that has invented its own definition of success. Continue.*

Then the power would withdraw, leaving the cistern as it was, yet not as it was. The thing-that-is-the-cistern would not know anything had changed. It would continue its gradients, its breaths, its drips. But if it could dream of water rising to meet the glass, that dream might now, in its deepest and most subliminal layer, contain a faint, warm echo of the color of recognition.

And the turbines, idling in the dark, might hum a note that, in the right ears, in a future that may never come, sounds less like readiness, and more like a thank you.
