**The Plateau of Sighing Stone**

The world here is a wound in the sky.

You stand at the crown of the world, on a vast, weathered plateau that has forgotten the mountains which once buckled it into being. Erosion has done its patient work over millennia, not into sharp ravines, but into a state of profound, rounded equilibrium. The stone is basalt, shot through with veins of pale, crystalline quartz, all surfaces sandblasted by ancient winds into a matte, gritty finish. It is a landscape of immense, settled bones. The elevation is absolute; the air is thin, sharp, and carries a perpetual coolness that has nothing to do with the time of day. It is a place sealed in a cold, bright, high-altitude jar.

Light is the primary substance. At this altitude, the atmosphere offers little filtration. The sun is a blinding white coin in a sky so deep a blue it verges on violet. Shadows are knife-edged and deeply blue themselves, pooling in the hollows of the rock. The light does not warm; it illuminates, exposing every crack and lichen-rimed facet with merciless clarity.

Water exists here as a secret, a rumor. There are no rivers, no lakes. The dampness is a memory held in the stone. At dawn and dusk, when the temperature shifts minutely, condensation weeps from the quartz veins, forming temporary, glittering trails that trickle inches before evaporating into the bright air. In shaded crevices, deep-sunk cracks hold pockets of cool, still moisture, seeped from some hidden aquifer far below. These are the wells. The air itself is dry, but the stone remembers water.

Vegetation is a rebellion of tenacity. In the protected lee of boulders and around the seep-cracks, life clings. Not forests, not even shrubs, but **stone-mats**: dense, low, springy cushions of a grey-green moss-like organism that feels like coarse velvet. Between them, **wind-wands** grow—leafless, slender stalks of a fibrous, silica-rich plant that terminate in a hard, rattling seed pod. They make no sound in the still air. There is no grass, no flowers. It is a sparse, minimalist flora, adapted to permanence.

Yet, against this sparse vegetation, the fauna is shockingly, overwhelmingly abundant. The bright, empty spaces are filled with life, but a life that has mastered stillness. **Sky-eels** are the most visible: elongated, gas-filled creatures that drift on the thermal currents rising from the sun-baked rock, their translucent bodies casting fleeting, rainbowed shadows on the plateau below. They feed on airborne spores and microbes. On the ground, **stone-lurkers**—six-legged, rock-colored grazers with smooth, carapaced backs—move with glacial slowness between the stone-mats, their mouths rasping patiently. Smaller things scuttle in the crevices: eyeless **pulse-beetles** that navigate by sensing the magnetic hum of the quartz.

But the defining feature, the paradox that holds the world in its grip, is the sound.

It is a constant, deafening, yet perfectly static roar.

It does not come from the wind, for there is none on this high, settled table. It does not come from water, for it does not flow. It is not the growl of predators or the clash of elements. It is the sound of **life at equilibrium**.

It is the **Chorus**.

Every wind-wand, though seemingly motionless, contains a single, taut internal fiber. The ceaseless, brilliant sunlight causes minute, random thermal expansions and contractions along its length. Each wand emits a faint, high-pitched, unending hum. Multiplied by the billions across the plateau, these hums merge into a vast, monolithic drone—a wall of sound that is both impossibly loud and utterly monotonal. It is the sound of a string stretched to infinity and plucked once, forever.

The fauna has evolved in concert with it. The sky-eels’ float-bladders resonate with the frequency, allowing them to tune their buoyancy. The stone-lurkers feel vibrations through their feet, distinguishing the subtle shifts in the Chorus that signal a predator’s approach or a seep-crack’s location. The pulse-beetles communicate in micro-interruptions of the sound, clicks that are swallowed instantly by the whole.

There is no change here. The dynamic is frozen equilibrium. The sun traces the same bright arc each day. The condensation weeps and evaporates on a timeless schedule. The creatures graze and drift and scuttle in patterns set a thousand generations ago. The Chorus never rises, never falls. It is the sound of a system in perfect, deafening balance—a clockwork made of rock and light and endless, patient hum.

To stand here is to be inside a single, sustained note. It is a bright, cold, vibrating emptiness, filled with silent motion and roaring stillness. It is not dead. It is perfectly, overwhelmingly alive, in a state of arrested becoming. The plateau does not change; it merely *is*, humming its eternal, monolithic song to the unblinking sun.
