The air is thick, warm, and carries the scent of wet green and old iron. You stand at the threshold of what was once a clearing, but is now a single, breathing organism.

**The Canopy and Walls.** The space is not large—a vast, ruined cathedral of a grove, perhaps, its boundaries defined by the trunks themselves. But the trunks are not separate. They are fused, woven, and layered into a continuous wall of wood and bark. Vegetation at 2.9 is not an overstatement; it is the *only* statement. Great, gnarled trees have grown together, their bases swelling into butresses that merge with the next tree’s, forming a porous, living palisade. Between them, vines as thick as your thigh have calcified into woody pillars, themselves furred with mosses and liverworts. Every surface is occupied. Ferns erupt from knotholes; shelf fungi form spiraling staircases up the bark; air plants dangle roots that never touch the earth. This is the **totality**.

**Water at 1.6.** The ground is not soil, but a dense, springy mat of roots and humus, saturated. Every step releases a soft, sighing squelch. Water doesn’t pool; it *perspires*. It beads on every leaf, runs in glittering threads down the weeping bark, and collects in the cupped hearts of giant, waxy leaves. The sound is constant—a soft, multi-layered dripping, a background rhythm to the place. Condensation forms on cooler surfaces, like the rusted flanks of the artifacts.

**The Scattered Built at 1.3.** The artifacts are here, absorbed into the growth. A iron girder, twisted and rusted a deep ocher, juts from a tree trunk, the wood having flowed around it like wax. A small, square building of brick is half-dissolved, its roof gone, its interior now a riot of pale, shade-seeking vines and fungi. The most prominent feature is a massive, horizontal cylinder—a boiler or a grain silo, perhaps—lying on its side. It is almost entirely sheathed in a thick, spongy moss. From seams and rivet holes, water weeps steadily. This is the **faint mark**, the **industrial 1.8**, now inert, its function subsumed. It is not rusting so much as digesting.

**Sound at 1.6.** The dripping is the baseline. Above it, the place hums. It is a low, organic drone, a vibration felt as much as heard. It comes from the plants themselves. Some of the broad-leafed ferns, when the humid breeze stirs them, emit a faint, papery rustle. Others, with hollow stems, create a deep, flute-like moan. The sound is **loud**, not in decibels, but in presence. It fills the packed space completely, leaving no silence.

**Fauna at 0.6.** There are no birds, no mammals. The sound is not theirs. Life here is micro-scale and larval. In the spongy mat, pale, eyeless insects move in slow, purposeful paths. On the bark, translucent slugs with faint, bioluminescent veins graze on bacterial films. Their motion is **slow**, part of the **gradual cycle**. They are the fauna, rare and strange.

**Weirdness at 2.3.** The strangeness is in the synthesis. The plants are not merely growing *on* the industrial remnants; they are interfacing. Near the great mossy boiler, a cluster of fungal stalks has grown into the shape of pressure gauges, their caps swelling and contracting minutely, as if monitoring internal steam that no longer exists. The vines that entomb a collapsed steel beam have developed a metallic sheen along their tendrils. The drip from a certain pipe falls into a pool where the water is faintly, iridescently oily, and the plants that drink it have leaves of a unsettling, chlorophyll-less white. This is a **plant-as-architecture** that has remembered and reinvented the **engine**.

**Dynamic at 1.4.** Change is imperceptible in the moment. A new shoot uncurling, a bead of water forming and falling, a root finger slowly tightening around a brick. The ecosystem is in a state of **established** equilibrium, **weathered** into its current form. The rust proceeds at the speed of the lichen. The moss advances millimeter by millimeter per year. The place feels eternal, patient, and utterly self-contained, a warm, wet, loud, and densely packed world unto itself, humming its low, green, metallic song.
