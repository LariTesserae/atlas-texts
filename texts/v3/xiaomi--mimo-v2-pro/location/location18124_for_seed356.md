# The Resin Dark

Imagine a world that has only just arrived.

Not a world shaped by time — not carved, not weathered, not ground down. A world that *assembled itself* from some catalytic event, probably recent, possibly ongoing, and then simply... stopped changing. What it became, it remains. A single gesture, frozen mid-fling.

---

You are standing — if standing is the word — on a surface that is not ground. It is a wound. Or rather, it was a wound once, and now it is a scab ofiteiteite bark-substance half a meter thick, still weeping a clear warm sap from hairline fractures that never fully closed. The sap collects in shallow depressions and runs in threads toward channels that are not streams. They are *veins*. They pulse, but only barely — a peristaltic slow-roll that takes minutes per contraction, like the world is breathing in its sleep.

Beneath the bark-floor, there is no soil. There is no rock. There is only *more plant*. Press your ear to the ground — though there is no one here to press an ear to anything — and you would hear, if you could hear at all, a silence so thick it has texture. Not the absence of sound. The presence of *listening*. Everything around you is alive and attending to itself with the patient, self-contained focus of a cathedral organ tuning a single note it will hold for a thousand years.

---

The darkness here is not the darkness of night. Night implies a day that ended. This darkness has never known a sun. It is *structural*. The canopy — if you can call the upper reaches of this world a canopy — began forming from the ground up and from the sky down simultaneously. Root-structures erupted from a shallow warm sea that has since been absorbed. Frond-vaults descended from spores that colonized a stratosphere. They met somewhere in the middle, fused, and sealed the interior from light. That was the event. That was the whole event. The world closed like a fist, and inside the fist: this.

What light exists is bioluminescent and accidental. Thin veins of pale chartreuse run along the undersides of vast overhead membranes where photosynthetic chemistry has gone *wrong* — or gone sideways — producing cold fungal glows instead of the warm chlorophyll-greens you might expect. These veins branch like river deltas and are slowly, imperceptibly growing, but they will not reach the floor in your lifetime. Maybe not in any lifetime. The distance overhead is too great. The interior of this world is *vast* — not in the way a continent is vast, with edges you could theoretically reach, but in the way a lung is vast: you are inside it, and it goes up and in and around you in every direction, and there is no horizon, only *depth*.

The light you can see: think of it as a nightlight. Think of it as the glow of a pilot flame in an empty factory. Which is closer to the truth than it should be.

---

Here is the strange thing. The industrial thing. The thing that doesn't match.

Somewhere below you — or above you, or laterally, direction having lost its meaning inside this sealed sphere-of-vegetation — there is *machinery*. Not built machinery. No one built anything here. There are no hands, no minds, no animals of any kind. The fauna parameter is zero. You are alone in the way that the interior of a seed is alone: surrounded by living matter, but no one is home except the matter itself.

The machinery is *grown*.

Deep in the central trunk-columns — and they are columns, grown as straight and vertical as anything in nature has a right to be, wider than rivers — the vascular channels thicken and branch into something that resembles a factory floor. Xylem tubes the width of highway tunnels. Phloem networks carrying sugars and complex polymers in quantities that would supply a city. And at junction points, at nodes where major channels intersect, *organs* — for lack of a better word — that perform chemical synthesis on an industrial scale.

You can feel the warmth radiating from these nodes if you are near one. Not fire-warmth. Process-warmth. The exothermic reaction of ten thousand simultaneous biochemical conversions, catalyzed by enzyme-complexes the size of buildings, converting raw sap-constituents into structural polymers, signaling hormones, and a class of molecule for which you have no name — something between a resin and a fuel, amber-dark, viscous, hot to the touch, that gets pumped outward and upward through the body of the world to reinforce and expand it.

This world is a factory that builds itself. It has no workers. It needs none. The production lines are xylem. The assembly floors are cambial membranes. The product is more world.

---

The sap that weeps through the bark-floor is warm. Not hot — warm, the way skin is warm, the way a fever patient's forehead is warm. The ambient temperature inside this sealed biosphere holds steady at something that would feel like a mild summer afternoon if you could see the sky, which you cannot. The heat comes from below, from the metabolic engines at the core, and it distributes unevenly — pooling in low areas, rising in slow convection columns that carry moisture with them. The air is humid. Not dripping, not foggy, but *saturated* — you would sweat here, if you were here, and your sweat would not evaporate because the air has already taken all the water it can hold.

Where the warm updrafts meet cooler overhead membranes — and there are cool spots, pockets where the bioluminescent veins have gone dormant and the tissue has thinned — condensation forms. Droplets gather on the undersides of membranes the size of plazas and fall in long, slow, silent arcs to the floor. Each droplet takes perhaps thirty seconds to fall. You can watch them descend like inverted stars in the faint green glow — if there were a you to watch. They hit the bark-floor without sound. The bark absorbs them. The cycle continues.

It has always continued. It will always continue. Nothing here changes. The dynamic is zero. Not because the world is dead — it is profoundly, overwhelmingly alive — but because it has *finished arriving*. It reached equilibrium at the moment of its completion and has held there since. The metabolic engines produce exactly enough resin to replace what weeps away. The condensation cycles return exactly the moisture that the updrafts carry. The bioluminescent veins expand at exactly the rate that older veins die and go dark. This is not a landscape in motion. This is a landscape in *balance* — perfect, mute, and strange.

---

If you could map it — and you cannot, because you are not here, and even if you were, there is no light, no sound, no landmark, no path — you would find that the interior of this world is *layered*. Not stratified like geology, with clean separations. Layered like a body is layered: organ over organ, tissue over tissue, each functional layer serving the next, none of them clean-edged, all of them interpenetrating.

**The outer bark.** Densely structured, resin-hardened, dark — so dark it absorbs the faint bioluminescence rather than reflecting it. This is the wall between the interior and whatever exists outside. You do not know what exists outside. You cannot reach it. The bark is kilometers thick in places. It may be the only thing holding this world together. Or it may be the only thing keeping something out.

**The canopy-void.** The upper interior space, between the overhead membranes and the bark-wall above. Relatively open. Relatively dry. The bioluminescent veins are concentrated here, and their glow gives this space the quality of an aquarium viewed from inside — greenish, diffuse, sourceless. The air here is slightly cooler. The membranes overhead flex imperceptibly, driven by the same slow peristalsis that moves the sap below.

**The mid-body.** Where you would be, if you were. The densest zone. Trunk-columns rise here like the pillars of a nave, each one warm, each one humming with the faintest subsonic vibration of internal flow. Between the columns, smaller structures branch and rebranch — fronds, shelves, fans, spirals — all of them the same organism, all of them growing, none of them *going anywhere*. The density here is high enough that movement would be difficult. You would push through tissue. You would displace moisture. You would be absorbed into the texture of the place like a word absorbed into a sentence.

**The core.** You cannot reach the core. No one can. The industrial heart of this world, where the synthesis happens, where the enzymes churn, where the resin is born. It is warm there. It is loud there, if you define loud as *full of sound below the threshold of hearing*. The core vibrates at frequencies that propagate through the plant-mass as infrasonic pulses — you would feel them in your chest, in your teeth, in the fluid of your eyes, if you were here. But you are not. And the pulses do not carry meaning. They are the sound of a process, not a message. A factory hum. A heart-beat that takes an hour to complete.

---

The strangeness — the 2.2 on the weirdness scale — is not in any single feature. It is in the *combination*. A world made entirely of a single organism, sealed against light, industrial in its metabolism, silent, warm, wet, vast beyond comprehension, and utterly, *utterly* still. Not still in the way a forest is still, where stillness is an illusion broken by birdsong and wind. Still in the way a *thought* is still. A thought that has been thought and is now simply *held*.

There is no wind here. There is no weather, despite the scale — or rather, the weather is *internal*: convection, condensation, the slow redistribution of moisture through a closed system. There is no horizon, no sky, no ground. There is only *inside*. You exist, if you exist, in the interior of a living thing that is large enough to have its own ecology but too simple, too unified, too *self-same* to support diversity. One species. One body. One process. Running forever. Changing never.

The sap runs warm. The veins glow green. The droplets fall. The resin thickens in the dark.

Nothing watches. Nothing moves. Everything breathes.

---

*You are not here. But if you were, you would feel, for the first time in your life, that you had been swallowed by something that did not notice you go down.*
