You stand in the Cathedral of Knotted Root, a place that is not built, but *woven*. It is the one thing here: a vast, single construction of living, treated, and dead material, shaped over centuries by hands that never discovered metal. There is no ground beneath it, only the first layer of the weave.

**The Architecture**
The substance is a fusion of root, stone-wood, and aite-wax, a resinous secretion from now-extinct beetle-herds. Primitive tools—flint scrapers, bone awls, obsidian blades—shaped it while it was soft. Now it is hard, and weeping. The "stone" is permeable. Cool water from a deep, captured aquifer is drawn up through capillary action, condensing in the cool, dim air. It beads on every surface, drips in a slow, percussive rain that never ceases, and runs in tiny rivulets down the curving walls into collection basins hollowed from the floor-weave. The air smells of wet stone, mildew, and the faint, sweet decay of the aite-wax.

**The Space & Scale**
You are in a single chamber the size of a hamlet, but its roof is a mere fifty feet above, a tangled canopy of hardened tendrils and fungal plates. It is not empty. The density is low, but the *presence* is high. The vast space between pillars—the size of ancient, gnarled trees—is punctuated by silence and dripping. Then, sound crashes in: the roar of a waterfall in a distant alcove, the frantic skittering of a thousand tiny feet, the low, resonant hum of a large creature breathing behind a woven wall.

**Life in the Weave**
Fauna is abundant, and it is all part of the Cathedral's ecosystem. Colonies of silver-backed moths nest in the high weave, their fluttering causing the fungal plates to glow with a faint, pulsing bioluminescence—your only light source. They are preyed upon by long, silent lizards with adhesive pads, who scale the walls. In the water basins, blind, translucent eels feast on insect larvae. The "dynamic" quality is the constant, slow-motion battle between growth and decay: fungal threads dissolving dead root-fibers, new shoots pushing through cracks, animals burrowing into softer patches. Nothing is still.

**The Rules of This World**
This is a made-environment, but the makers are long gone. Their primitive technology is embedded in the very fabric of the place. The "built" parameter is total: you walk on a woven floor, lean against a grown pillar, drink from a carved basin. There is no "nature" here, only the legacy of a culture that shaped biology itself. The "strangeness" is in the logic of it: doors that are sphincter-like valves in the weave, tools left fused to walls as the material grew around them, pools that reflect not your image, but faint, bioluminescent patterns from the fungus within the water. It is a settled, established system, weathered and in-balance, but the balance is alien. It is cool, dim, wet, and loud with the sounds of water and small, busy lives in the dark. It feels hollow, yet teeming. You are inside a living fossil, and it is still breathing.
