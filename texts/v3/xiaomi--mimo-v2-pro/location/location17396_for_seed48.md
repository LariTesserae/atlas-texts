The basin is a vast, shallow concavity of heat and steam, a place where the world has settled into a state of slow, relentless cooking. The air itself is a visible participant, thick with a perpetual haze of mineral vapor that catches the low, directionless light, diffusing it into a dim, coppery gloom. The ground underfoot is not soil but a crust of fused silicates and crystallized salts, cracked into a mosaic of hexagons that shift and whisper with a glassy chime when disturbed. This crust is the ‘established’ erosion—aeons of thermal cycling and mineral deposition have worn every sharp edge into a rounded, settled form.

There is no plant life. The 0.2 vegetation rating manifests in the occasional, brittle filaments of calcium carbonate that wick up from deep fissures, forming fragile, skeletal mats that crumble to dust at a touch. They are not life as recognized elsewhere, but a mineral mimicry of it, a chemical process wearing a biological shape.

The true inhabitants are the fauna. They are overwhelming in their quiet, relentless presence. Not animals of flesh and blood, but creatures of the heat and the constructed world: vast, slow-moving **Convection Beetles**, their carapaces of iridescent obsidian siphoning thermal energy from the ground; swarms of **Volt-Weavers**, gossamer-thin filaments of conductive metal that drift on thermal updrafts, occasionally arcing with stolen electricity; and deep within the plumbing of the city, the **Geophagic Worms**, eyeless, ceramic-skinned behemoths that chew through the mineral crust, processing rare earths and excreting refined slag. Their movements are the primary dynamic process—a constant, slow-motion ballet of consumption, energy transfer, and mineral cycling.

And the city. It is urban, but not in the way of dense streets. It is a city of **infrastructure**. Towering, slender heat-exchangers rise like black needles, their surfaces rippling with thermal distortion. They feed into a web of polished conduits and aqueducts that carry not water, but pressurized steam and superheated mineral slurry. The buildings are low, domed, and armored against the heat, their walls thick with insulating ceramic foam. Many are half-buried in the mineral crust, entered via steep, descending stairs. The density is 0.2—this means the structures, while large and numerous, are widely spaced, separated by kilometers of shimmering, empty basin floor. The scale is regional; you can see the silhouettes of other factory-complexes shimmering in the haze, linked by the dark lines of the conduit-web.

The sound is mostly quiet, but it is a profound, textured quiet. It is the hush of immense space filled with a low, omnipresent **hum**—the vibration of the great heat-exchangers conducted through the ground. You hear the distant, rhythmic *chuff* of pressure vents, the faint, glassy clatter of the shifting crust, and, if you listen closely, the dry, metallic *skritching* of the Convection Beetles against the rock. The atmosphere is weeping—condensation forms in shimmering droplets on the cooler upper sections of the conduits and the armored undersides of overhangs, dripping steadily into carved channels. This is the water: not rain, not a river, but a constant, harvested condensate, collected and redistributed through the urban veins.

Light comes dimly from the sky, filtered through the permanent haze, and from the structures themselves: the dull red glow of furnace doors, the sharp blue-white arc of welding drones, the pulsing amber of signal lights on the heat-exchangers. It is an industrial glow, painting the steam in shifting, ominous colors.

The rules of this world are thermal and economic. Energy is the prime currency, harvested from the planetary heat. The fauna are both a product of this environment and a component within the industrial ecosystem—the Volt-Weavers are "milked" for their stored energy, the Geophagic Worms are guided to new mineral veins, the Convection Beetles are sometimes corralled as living heat-sinks. It is a hot, dim, empty, and yet profoundly busy place—a settled, industrial basin operating in a state of oppressive equilibrium, where even the silence is mechanical.
