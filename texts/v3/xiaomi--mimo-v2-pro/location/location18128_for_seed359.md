You stand in the **Vitreous Grove**.

The ground underfoot is not soil, but a seamless, slightly yielding surface of polished salt—a vast, unblemished plain of crystalline halite that stretches to the soft, rounded walls of this chamber. The salt is not white, but a deep, translucent amethyst, shot through with veins of milky quartz that pulse with a faint, internal luminescence. This is the only substrate, and it was poured, grown, or sung into place. It is young; the edges where it meets the curved walls are still sharp, the facets of the salt crystals distinct and unweathered.

There is no water. Not in the air, not in the ground. The atmosphere is dry and warm, carrying the faint, clean scent of ozone and heated stone. Yet, from this anhydrous salt-field, **vegetation** erupts in impossible profusion. It is a lushness of crystal and silicate. Slender, leafless stalks of bismuth grow in tangled thickets, their surfaces iridescent with oxidation patterns of blue and gold. Great, fractal clusters of selenite rise like groves of gnarled trees, their crystalline branches catching and refracting the ambient light. Fungiform growths of pure, fibrous gypsum cling to every surface, softening angles and creating a dense, layered underbrush. This is the 1.6 of lushness: not green, but geometric, mineral, and utterly abundant.

The **fauna** are the tenders and the expressions of this place. They are quiet, moving with a slow, deliberate grace. Some are clearly **constructed**—small, multi-limbed automatons of copper and polished bone, clicking softly as they prune a bismuth stalk with a tiny, vibrating blade. Others are harder to define. A translucent, manta-ray-like form glides through the air between the selenite trees, its body a shimmering membrane of what looks like captured light. It makes no sound. From the dense thickets of gypsum, you catch glimpses of small, warm-blooded creatures with fur like spun salt and eyes of black tourmaline. They watch you, still and silent. The ecology is a web of quiet, purposeful interactions; pollination is a matter of precise vibrations, growth is guided by hand-tools and innate biological programming. The tech is simple, but the understanding behind it is profound, intuitive, and old.

The **light** is normal, but its source is obscure. It seems to emanate from the amethyst salt floor itself, a soft, violet-grey glow that illuminates everything evenly, casting no distinct shadows. It is constant and mild.

The **scale** is intimate, cathedral-like but domestic. This is a single, grand hall, perhaps two hundred meters across and fifty high, its vaulted ceiling merging seamlessly with the walls. It feels like a room, or a sacred grove contained within one. Boundaries are absolute. You are inside something.

The **density** is high. The crystalline growths are packed tightly, their forms interlocking and overlapping. The air itself feels layered, saturated with fine, glittering dust motes that hang motionless. You must often turn sideways to pass between the thickest clusters of bismuth. It is a crowded, busy stillness.

And the **weirdness** is a pressure behind the eyes. The rules here are soft. The selenite trees don’t merely grow; they *remember* growth. You see a branch, and in the corner of your vision, you perceive its ghostly, younger self superimposed upon it, a phantom of a month ago. The ground occasionally ripples, a slow wave of distortion passing through the salt, as if the entire floor were a single, dreaming muscle. The automatons sometimes pause, cock their copper heads, and listen to a frequency you cannot hear. The manta-ray of light will sometimes freeze, fold in on itself, and unfold a meter to the left, with no sense of travel. The boundary between the made and the born, the mineral and the living, the present and the remembered, is thin and permeable. This is not chaos; it is a deeper, quieter logic. The reality here is soft-focused, layered, and prone to gentle, recursive folds.

The **sound** is mostly quiet—a profound, absorbed hush. The only noises are the soft, granular *shiff* of salt grains resettling after a ripple, the occasional high, pure *ting* of a crystal spontaneously realigning, and the nearly subsonic hum of the light in the floor. It is the sound of slow, immense patience.

**Change** happens here at the pace of a growing crystal. The bismuth stalks elongate by a millimeter per year. A new, small cavern might be hollowed from the salt wall over a century by the patient, acid-tipped tongues of unseen burrowers. The patterns of luminescence in the floor shift so slowly they constitute the only true calendar. It is a world of **slow** dynamics, where the only flux is the gradual, inevitable deepening of a pattern already set.

You are not an intruder here. You are simply another temporary, conscious pattern in a landscape that understands patterns intimately. The grove watches you with its many mineral and constructed eyes, not with hostility, but with the calm, patient curiosity of a place that has all the time there ever was, and very little need for water at all.
