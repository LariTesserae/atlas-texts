The air is a soaked weight, hot and tasting of wet stone and rust. You have come to the **Bleed**.

It is not a city in any remembered sense, but an *infrastructure*. A single, sprawling mechanism of brick, riveted iron, and sweating concrete, built in and around a shallow basin where the land has given up. The scale is intimate, oppressive. You are always in a room, even when outdoors—a room defined by the flanks of colossal, silent tanks, by the close press of warehouse walls weeping dark moisture, by the low ceiling of perpetual grey cloud that seals the basin. There is no horizon, only the next pipe junction, the next crumbling brick facade.

**Water** is the medium of existence here. It does not flow; it *bleeds*. It seeps from every joint in the vast, grid-like network of pipes that web the low sky, dripping with a slow, heavy rhythm. It oozes from the cracked foundations of the dormant factories, forming stagnant, iridescent pools that reflect nothing. It condenses on every metal surface, running in crooked tears down the sides of silent generators. The ground is a mosaic of damp flagstone, mud, and rust-stained gravel. The air itself is a drinkable thickness.

**Vegetation** exists in a feverish, silent revolt. In the sparse spaces between structures, in the cracks of heaved pavement, things grow. Thick, rubbery vines with waxy, black-green leaves coil up drainpipes, their pale, fleshy flowers giving off a scent like overripe fruit and damp plaster. Clumps of tall, pale grass, bleached of color by the poor light, press against foundations. Mosses and lichens in shades of ochre and deep grey paint every shaded surface in velvety continents. They thrive on the constant moisture and heat, but they grow in utter silence, their progress a slow, vegetable scream against the metal.

There is **no animal life**. No birds. No insects. The only movements are the drip of water, the occasional sigh of venting steam from a cracked valve, and the imperceptible creep of the vegetation. The **sound** is immense and mechanical—a deep, foundational hum that you feel in your teeth, the residual vibration of a thousand tons of machinery at idle. Beneath it is the symphony of water: the patter, the gurgle, the slow *plink* into a puddle. It is a loud place, but the sound is monolithic, non-conversational. It is the sound of a process, not an event.

The **industry** is all present-tense and absent purpose. Great furnaces stand cold, their mouths choked with vine. Transmission belts hang slack between wheels. The factories are cathedrals to a forgotten production line, yet the power is on. The air shimmers with heat from pipes that still carry steam. Arc-lights, caked in grime, cast a dim, jaundiced glow in perpetual twilight, buzzing faintly. This is not a ruin; it is a *stasis*. The mechanisms are maintained by a logic of their own—self-oiling joints, pressure regulators that still hiss and click. A dynamic, automated decay.

The **weirdness** is in the purpose. What is being bled, condensed, and circulated? In the central square—a open space where four great brick conduits converge—you find it. Not a plaza, but a *sump*. A vast, slowly churning pool of warm, silty water. From its center rises a structure of riveted iron and milky, opaque glass, shaped like a skeletal tree or a cooling tower. It is humming, drawing the water in, and from vents near its crown, it exhales a steady, warm fog that adds to the blanket above. This is the heart. The Bleed is not a town. It is a cooling system, a metabolic organ for something vast and buried below, or perhaps for the structure itself. The "urban" grid is its vascular network. The "industry" is its circulatory pump. The "vegetation" is its slow, benign infection.

You are in the lung of a sleeping giant, or perhaps in the giant's dream of itself. The air is hot, wet, and loud with the sound of its own slow, perpetual breathing. Nothing dies here, because nothing is truly alive. It simply persists, in a state of established, saturated, mechanical grace.
