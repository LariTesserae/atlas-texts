The world is a throat, and it is screaming.

You are beneath a sky that is not sky—a ceiling of interlocked chitin and leaf-vein, vaulted high above, yet still pressing down with a humid, vegetative weight. There is no sun, no moon, no stars. The **light**, a sickly, photosynthetic green-gold, emanates from the tissues themselves: vast sheets of luminous fungal gills pulse in slow, rhythmic waves, and clusters of fruit-bodies glow with a bioelectric blue, like frozen lightning. This is not a forest. It is an organ. The world is a single, gargantuan, plant-derived organism, and you are inside its living body.

**Water** is a myth, a forgotten chemical. There is only sap. It weeps in viscous, amber tears from wounds in the fleshy walls, pools in sticky, shallow basins, and hangs in the air as a colloidal mist that coats your skin in a sweet, fermenting film. The **temperature** is warm, a consistent, metabolic heat radiating from every surface, a fever-dream warmth that never cools. You are in the gut of a planet-sized seed, a vault of **total vegetation** that has consumed everything, even the memory of stone.

The **elevation** is low, a sprawling network of tunnels and chambers that descend into the core. The **terrain** is not soil or rock, but layers of compressed, woody pulp and root-knot, a soft, fibrous geology still under **emerging** construction. Walls shift and groan as new chambers swell into being, sealed shut by the rapid knitting of bark-like calluses. The ground is a spongy mat of mycelia and decaying leaf-matter, constantly being broken down and remade. This is **erosion** in reverse: not wearing away, but accreting, proliferating, embryonic growth on a monstrous scale.

The **scale** is regional, yet oppressive. Corridors the size of river valleys branch into cathedral-sized fruiting chambers, then constrict into capillary tunnels. Everything is layered and **dense**. You are never alone. The air itself is thick with spores, motes of life so dense they form shimmering curtains. Surfaces are encrusted with shelf-fungi, climbing vines with sucker-mouths, and bulbous growths that pulse with their own faint, rhythmic light.

And the **fauna**. Life **overwhelming**. Not animals as you know them, but mobile organs of the greater plant. Thinkers—shimmering, wasp-like swarms with bodies of polished seed-case and wings of membrane-leaf—dance in complex, **deafening** patterns, their collective buzz a physical pressure that vibrates in your teeth. Grazers—slow, armored things like ambulatory artichokes with rasping mouthparts—scrape the walls, their digestion a wet, grinding counterpoint to the drone. Predators are silent gliders, mantis-limbs of thorn and vine, that snatch thinkers from the air with a crack of chitin. The **sound** is a symphony of decay and growth: the creak of expanding walls, the drip and hiss of sap, the constant chitinous clatter of ten thousand tiny lives, a roar of biological industry that never, ever sleeps.

**Built** remnants are here, but they are being digested. Faint, geometric angles of a harder material—aite or ceramic—pierce through the pulp in a few places, the skeleton of some prior civilization now being slowly dissolved. A single, half-consumed archway stands in a vast chamber, its surface pitted and etched by acidic nectar. A scattering of smooth, round stones, unnaturally uniform, lie half-sunk in the mycelial floor, their purpose and makers long since rendered into nutrients. They are not ruins; they are fossils in the making.

The **tech** of the current inhabitants, the thinkers, is primitive and organic. They cultivate specific fungal crops on designated walls, using sharpened thorn-tools to prune and direct growth. They weave leaf-fiber into nesting pouches. They communicate through complex pheromone plumes and vibrational taps on the resonant walls. It is all a part of the greater system.

But the system is **turbulent**. This is not a stable biome. It is a hyperactive, **chaotic** engine of creation and destruction. Entire sections of the world-body can die back in a catastrophic rot-collapse, a sudden, deafening avalanche of putrefaction that releases a tsunami of spores and scavengers. New growth erupts with violent speed, shoots thick as towers spearing through old tissue in hours. The light pulses erratically during these "storms," shifting from green to a furious, reproductive red. The thinkers swarm in frenzy, the grazers flee or are consumed. The air grows hot, thick, and explosively fecund. It is a state of constant, **strange** revolution—an anthill the size of a continent, a bazaar where the currency is biomass and the only law is relentless, weeping, screaming growth.

You stand on a lip of woody pulp, overlooking a basin where sap-light pools. The thinker-swarm parts around you, not out of fear, but indifference. You are a mote, a temporary anomaly. The world-throat hums and groans, building and unbuilding itself around you, a landscape that is also a stomach, a lung, and a mind, dreaming in chlorophyll and chitin. To stay still is to be subsumed. To move is to become part of the dream.
