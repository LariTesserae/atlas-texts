**The Shardfield of Keth’varin**

The air is thin and bites sharply at the lungs, carrying the metallic tang of ozone and wet stone. You stand at 2.7/3 elevation, atop a world that never learned the word ‘soil’. The ground is not earth but a fractured plain of quartz and feldspar, a mineral tapestry shattered by ancient forces and now settled into a state of permanent, brittle tension. This is a place of extreme height, not as a single peak, but as a raised platform of the world’s crust, tilted and exposed to the unfiltered glare of a high-altitude sun. The light is blindingly bright (2.7/3), refracting through countless angled facets of stone, creating a disorienting latticework of sharp shadows and searing glints. There is no canopy to soften it, no green to absorb it. The world is a mirror and a prism combined.

**Terrain & Materials:**
The landscape is a dense (1.7/3), small-scale (0.8/3) labyrinth of crystalline shards. These are not orderly formations, but chaotic, leaning spires and jagged plates, some as tall as cathedral naves, others no higher than a man, all packed tightly together. The dominant materials are translucent quartz in smoky greys and faint violets, alongside opaque, rust-red jasper and bands of midnight-black basalt. Everything is scored with fine, parallel lines—the record of ancient glacial grit and millennia of wind-borne dust. The erosion (1.9/3) is not the rounded softness of water, but the sharp, settled erosion of a system in equilibrium. Edges are blunted but still keen; surfaces are sandblasted to a satin sheen. The rock is *weathered*, not worn down. It has found its final, stable form.

**Moisture & Atmosphere:**
Water (1.0/3) is present only as a ghost and a thief. There is no flowing source, no river, no lake. Moisture exists as perpetual, chilling condensation that beads on the cold stone each twilight, running in brief, silvery rivulets down the scored faces of the shards before freezing again in the shadows. Occasionally, thick banks of fog roll in from lower elevations, clinging to the shard forest, depositing more rime ice that sublimates away in the next day’s fierce sun. The dampness is a transient visitor, never establishing itself, yet constantly returning. The air is cold (0.3/3)—a dry, penetrating cold that leaches warmth from bone and stone alike. The temperature hovers near freezing, ensuring any liquid water is a temporary phenomenon.

**Life & Absence:**
This is a mineral world. Vegetation is 0.3/3, and that fraction is generous, accounting only for rare, crustose lichens that are so slow-growing and mineral-like they are more akin to the stone itself than to any recognizable biology. They appear as faint, black or chartreuse stains in the most sheltered hollows. Fauna is non-existent. The silence is profound (0.7/3), broken only by the sounds of the world itself: the high, thin whistle of wind through the narrow gaps between shards; the occasional, startling *tink* or *crack* as rock contracts in the cold; the soft, gritty whisper of airborne ice crystals scouring the surfaces.

**The Built & The Old:**
Scattered among the shards are traces of a prior presence (built: 0.6/3). They are not buildings, but interventions. Here, a flat, hexagonal plate of a strange, matte-grey ceramic material is wedged into a crevice, its surface etched with spiraling grooves now filled with rime. There, a line of smaller, perfectly spherical basalt cobbles—too round to be natural—leads from one collapsed spire to another. A faint, circular depression in a sheltered amphitheater of stone holds a pile of uniformly shattered quartz, as if something was pulverized there long ago. These are the artifacts of a primitive (tech: 1.2/3) culture, one that understood stone and simple alloys, but left no grand monuments. Their tools were hand-tools, their purpose now inscrutable. They are part of the landscape’s erosion, more marks to be weathered.

**Dynamics & Rules:**
The place is surprisingly active (dynamic: 1.7/3). The action is geological and climatic. The bright sun heats the dark basalt bands differentially, causing minute expansions that, over centuries, propagate new fractures. The intermittent dampness and freeze-thaw cycles work tirelessly, prying apart grain from grain in a slow-motion explosion. The wind is a constant sculptor, carrying abrasive ice dust. The "weirdness" (0.9/3) is low, but present in the unnerving density of the shard forest, the unnatural regularity of some of the scattered artifacts, and the eerie, focused way light will suddenly lance through a perfect alignment of gaps, illuminating a single artifact for a brief, dramatic moment before the earth’s turn moves the alignment apart.

**The Experience:**
To walk here is to navigate a glittering, frozen maze of razors. Your breath plumes white, instantly caught and shredded by the wind. The light is so bright it forces a squint, turning the world into a dazzling network of cracks. Your boots crunch on a carpet of fallen mineral flakes. You are small, and the world is dense with sharp, silent, enduring presences. It feels ancient, settled, and profoundly *other*—a world that had its chance for life and chose, instead, the intricate, cold, and luminous path of the crystal. It is a library of stone, where every facet records a second of eons, and the only readers are the light and the wind.
