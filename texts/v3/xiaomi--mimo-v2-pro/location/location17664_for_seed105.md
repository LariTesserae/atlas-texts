The world is called the Drown, though no one is left to call it that. You are sinking through the last of the air, into the perpetual twilight of the flooded peaks.

**The Medium:** It is not an ocean, but a drowning. The air is thick, wet, and heavy, falling in curtains of perpetual, vertical rain so dense it ceases to be weather and becomes the very atmosphere. You breathe water-mist, each inhalation a chilling weight in your lungs. The pressure is constant, a gentle but immense hand pressing from all sides. Light, what little there is, is a diffused, blue-grey gloom that filters down from a sky you can no longer see, swallowed by its own weeping. The darkness is not total, but it is profound, a twilight that has forgotten the sun.

**The Terrain:** You are descending past the drowned shoulders of mountains. What were once alpine peaks are now islands in a limitless, liquid world. The rock is sharp, basalt and granite, but sheathed in a cold, clear ice that glints faintly in the gloom. This is a high place, laid low. Erosion has done its work over ages; the stones are not jagged but *settled*, worn into their drowned state, balanced in the new equilibrium. Valleys are now deep, silent trenches where the mist-rain pools into true, black water.

**The Life:** Where there is light, there is growth. Lush does not begin to describe it. Great, slow forests of kelp-like tendrils rise from the mountainsides, their fronds broad and translucent, glowing with a faint bioluminescence of bruised purple and cold blue. They are not green; photosynthesis is a forgotten dream here. These are chemosynthetic gardens, feeding on minerals wept from the rock. Corals, bone-white and branching, form sprawling, silent cities on the slopes, their structures intricate and ancient. Other plants resemble vast, pulsating anemones or curtains of hair-thin filaments that wave in unseen currents. There is no animal life—no fish, no crustaceans, no insects. The vegetation itself is the fauna.

**The Motion & Sound:** The world is **loud**. It is the sound of a drowned planet breathing. A deep, sub-aquatic groan resonates through the rock—the sound of tectonic plates shifting under unimaginable weight. Closer, there is the constant, hissing roar of the atmospheric rain impacting the mist-sea surface, a white noise that never ceases. The great kelp forests *rustle*, a dry, leathery sound as their broad fronds scrape against each other and the ice-sheathed stone. And there are the clicks, pops, and eerie, singing moans of the vegetation itself—biological processes manifesting as sound in the dense medium. Anemones pulse with a low thrum; the filaments sing like wet finger on glass. The entire ecosystem is in motion, a slow dance of growth, decay, and chemical exchange. A frond detaches and drifts down, a silent, slow-motion avalanche. A coral branch, overgrown, cracks and tumbles into a trench, its impact lost in the symphony of noise.

**The Marks:** They were here. Scattered along the more stable ridges and in the hollows of what must have been high caves, you find them. Not ruins, but **remnants**. Simple structures of fitted stone, their roofs long since collapsed, now mere circles of wall filled with drifting silt and cradling new, pale growths. A single, obelisk-like pillar of dark rock, perfectly smooth, stands alone on a plateau, its surface etched with spirals now filled with luminous moss. A line of smaller stones leads from a ruined doorway down into a trench, vanishing into the deep—a path to nowhere. These are the artifacts: a pile of shaped flint blades, their edges still sharp, half-buried in sediment; a hollowed stone that might have been a bowl, now holding a single, perfectly still droplet of condensed mist. The technology is primitive, elemental. The marks are not of dominion, but of brief, fleeting habitation. A whisper that said, "We were cold, and here, we tried to be."

**The Rules:** This is a world of slow, cold process. The dynamic is in the growth of the crystal-ice on the rock, in the imperceptible drift of the kelp forests seeking faint chemical plumes, in the constant, quiet competition for footing on the drowned slopes. There is no predation, but there is overgrowth, smothering, and the patient, grinding work of the water itself. To exist here is to be rooted, to feed on the weeping of the stone, to glow in the dark, and to listen to the mountain groan under the weight of the sky. It is a place of profound, lush, and eerie stillness, held in the belly of a weeping world.
