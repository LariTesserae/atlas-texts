You are in the crushing, silent dark of the abyssal plain, a trench so ancient its slopes have been worn to a vast, gentle curve over geological aeons. This is the low place, the drain of the world, and the water is everything. It is not merely deep; it is the substance of existence, a cold, eternal weight that presses on every atom of this realm. The temperature is a constant, bone-deep chill, not freezing, but a cold that has forgotten warmth, settled into the water and the silt for millennia.

The scale is regional, but a region of featureless gloom. You perceive it not by sight, but by a sense of immense, open distance in every direction—a void filled with presence. For the density is paradoxically, overwhelmingly high. The water itself is not empty. It is a saturated soup of fine, eternal silt, a ghostly blizzard of organic snow—*marine snow*—the ceaseless, slow rain of dead matter from the distant, sunlit world above. This is the vegetation, the sparse but unending harvest: a 0.7 of life, not rooted, but falling. It coats everything in a slow, grey sediment, the very material of this world’s floor.

And on that floor, the packed density becomes clear. It is not a barren mudflat. It is a city of the ancients, a metropolis built by no one. The terrain is a dense, necrotic forest of **hydrothermal vent complexes**. They are not scattered; they cluster, they crowd, their mineral chimneys rising in packed, ruined districts, some shattered, some still weeping dark, superheated fluid. They are the only topography in the flattening erosion, primordial towers in the final state of collapse. Their flanks are encrusted with life, the other fraction of the sparse vegetation: thickets of **giant tube worms**, blood-red forests in the dark, their plumes filtering chemical soup, and mats of pale, ghostly bacteria that coat every surface in a slick, living felt. This is the ecosystem—chemosynthetic, alien to the sun, yet utterly ordinary in its own deep-sea logic.

The **turbulence** is here. This is no calm depth. It is a hyperactive, silent storm. Currents, driven by geothermal heat and the planet’s own rotation, tear through the chimney forests. They whip the marine snow into vortexes, scour the bacterial mats, and cause the fragile worm-plumes to shudder and bend. Clouds of sediment billow and settle in slow-motion avalanches, re-shaping the landscape constantly. The chimneys themselves groan and crack as superheated fluid bursts through new fissures—a chaotic, creation-and-destruction cycle that plays out in slow motion over decades, yet feels frenetic in the unchanging dark.

**Sounds** are few, but they carry far in the dense water: the deep, tectonic *groan* of the shifting crust, a subsonic vibration felt in the bones more than heard. The high-pitched *click* of a rare crustacean, lost in the chimney maze. The endless, soft *hiss* of particulate rain against solid mineral.

**Fauna** is rare, but present in the crevices. Translucent, ghostly **amphipods** drift like motes in a sunbeam that doesn't exist. A solitary, blind **cusk-eel**, its body a pale ribbon, navigates by sensing the electrical fields of the bacterial mats. A cluster of **deep-sea crabs**, their exoskeletons thin as paper, scavenge a fallen tube worm. They are sparse, accidental visitors to this dense mineral city.

The **darkness** is total, save for the faint, spectral bioluminescence of disturbed bacteria—a cold blue glow that winks out as quickly as it appears, painting the ruins in fleeting, ghostly light. There is **no built structure**, no artifact. The only "tech" is the primitive, instinctual engineering of the life here: the worms' symbiotic bacteria, the crabs' simple tools of claw and antenna. The world is untouched by anything from above, a sealed, primordial basin that has reached its oldest, stable state—a state of packed, eroded, turbulent silence. It is not dead. It is furiously, densely, anciently alive, in a way that feels eternal and profoundly settled, even as the currents tear through it.
