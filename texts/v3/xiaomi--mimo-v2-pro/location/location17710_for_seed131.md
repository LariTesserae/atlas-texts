You are in the Engine Root.

It is not a place for walking. It is a place for standing still and letting your eyes adjust to the gloom. The air is a warm, wet blanket, thick with the scent of ozone, rust, and a thousand damp, leafless plants. It sweats. The walls weep. Condensation beads on every metal surface, tracing slow, black rivers down plates of riveted iron before dripping onto fleshy, emerald mosses below. There is no ground. There is a floor: a vast, pitted grid of iron mesh, now choked and invisible under a slow, patient carpet of moisture-loving life. Ferns with fronds like dark green lace erupt from the grates. Pale, fleshy fungi cluster around bolt-heads. Vines, slender and black, thread through the lattice, not reaching for a sun that does not exist here, but simply *filling*.

The light is a uniform, sourceless grey, filtered through grime and vegetation high above. It comes from everywhere and nowhere, a testament to the facility’s internal glow-panels, now dead, but their diffuse legacy persists, trapped and softened by the perpetual humidity. You are low, so low you feel the deep, tectonic hum of the place in your bones. It is the only sound. A deafening, singular, ultra-low-frequency **THRUM** that makes the iron mesh vibrate under your soles. It is the sound of the world’s engine, the eternal dynamo, settled into its final, perfect RPM. It does not speed up or slow down. It just *is*.

This is a pocket of total construction, a chamber of industry so ancient it has become a geology. The iron walls, streaked with mineral deposits like rust and verdigris, have been here so long they have developed their own sedimentary layers—the slow accumulation of dust and spores, solidified by moisture into a porous, living stone. The scale is intimate, yet immense in its emptiness. You are inside a single cog of a machine the size of a mountain, and the cog is hollow. The spaces between the weeping ducts and tangled, root-like conduit bundles are vast, silent voids. Nothing moves but the slow drip of water. Nothing lives but the parasitic, rootless flora and the occasional, ghostly pale insect that navigates the humid air on vestigial wings.

There is no soil. There is no natural substrate. The plants grow from the infrastructure itself, their roots gripping the engineered metal, deriving nutrients from the ceaseless moisture and the metallic decay. The ecosystem is a closed loop of condensation, decay, and silent, photosynthesis-less growth. It is a terrarium for a forgotten god of industry, a shrine kept alive by the very machine it was built to house. The warmth is not from a sun, but from the deep, steady heat of the ancient engines below—a heat that never varies, a gradient long since flattened into perfect, oppressive equilibrium. The world outside, if one exists, is irrelevant. Here, in the Engine Root, time has stopped. The water falls, the plants drink, the iron hums, and the great, silent, deafening process continues, as it always has and always will.
