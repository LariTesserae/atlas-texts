## The Sound

The sound is not like sound anywhere you have been. It is not the sound of wind through trees, though there is wind and there are things that function as trees. It is not the sound of a machine, though the plateau functions, in some sense, as a machine. It is the sound of a *field* — a continuous, layered, resonant drone that rises and falls in slow cycles. The pitch is low, mostly, but harmonics shift through it constantly, and occasionally — when the wind changes direction, when some subterranean process shifts the alignment of deep crystalline strata — a high, clear tone will emerge from the drone and sustain itself for minutes, hours, sometimes days, before sinking back into the general sound.

The fauna know how to use this.

## The Settlement

There are structures here that are not plants. Or rather — they were plants, once, and have been modified. The inhabitants — and I have not described the inhabitants yet, and I am not sure I can, because they are difficult to see and difficult to describe and I am not certain they are *beings* in the way you mean — the inhabitants have been here for a long time. Longer than the erosion. Perhaps *as long* as the erosion. They have shaped the plateau. Not by building on it, but by building *with* it.

## The Atmosphere and Light

The air is cool. Not cold — the elevation is high, but the thin atmosphere traps little heat and radiates little. The temperature is steady, barely varying between the dim day and the dimmer night. There is no moisture in the air. There is no moisture anywhere. The chemistry here does not include water in any meaningful quantity. The reactions that sustain the lattice, that power the piezoelectric systems, that drive the metabolism of every living thing on the plateau — they are electrochemical, driven by the interaction of crystalline structures with the constant wind, with the mineral substrate, with each other. Water would be destructive here. It would corrode the lattice. It would crack the columns. The inhabitants regard the concept of liquid water with something between horror and fascination.

The light comes from above and from within. During the day, the amber-brown sky admits a dim, diffuse illumination — enough to see by, if you are adapted, not enough to cast sharp shadows. The crystalline canopies of the columns catch this light and redistribute it, so that the deeper you move into a grove, the more the light comes from the plants themselves rather than the sky. At night, the glow from the structures is sufficient. The fauna do not need light. They navigate by resonance, by the feel of the field, by vibrations conducted through the substrate. You are the one who needs light. You are the one who is poorly adapted here.

## The Weirdness

Something is wrong, or rather, something is *different*, in a way that sits just below the threshold of conscious perception. The field does things. The resonance of the plateau, the aggregate hum of every column and microorganism and creature and structure — it has effects that are difficult to attribute to any single cause.

Time feels — not slow, not fast, but *textured*. Moments have different densities. You will be walking across bare regolith and a minute will pass that feels like ten, and then you will be threading between columns and an hour will pass that feels like a breath. This is not subjective. The inhabitants measure it. They have always measured it. The field warps — not space, not time in the relativistic sense, but the *experiential substrate*, the medium through which events propagate. Some pockets of the plateau are event-dense. Things happen there — complex, rapid, chaotic. Other pockets are event-sparse. Nothing happens there for long periods. The inhabitants prefer the sparse zones. They are patient. They have always been patient.

And sometimes, rarely, the field does something that has no name. A column will grow in a direction that contradicts its own structural logic. A creature will appear that was not there, that has no developmental history, that seems to have been *extruded* from the lattice fully formed. A sound will emerge from the drone that carries information — not communication, not language, but raw *information*, patterns of such complexity that the inhabitants cannot parse them, can only note that they occurred and store the recording in deep crystalline memory banks that have been accumulating data for longer than some stars have been burning.

The plateau is old. The plateau is alive. The plateau is loud, and dim, and dry, and vast, and strange, and it has been doing what it does for so long that the doing has become a kind of being, and the being has become a kind of place, and the place is what you are standing in now, listening to the hum, feeling the vibration in your teeth, watching the blue-white glow pulse in the distance like a heartbeat that is not yours.
