The place is called the Stillgrowth, though it has no inhabitants to name it. You arrive at its edge not by foot, but by a kind of ontological shift—one moment you are elsewhere, the next you are *here*, where the rules are different.

**Terrain & Materials:**
There is no soil. There is no stone. There is no separate ground. The entire landscape is a single, continuous, living organism—a colossal, vascular plant-architecture that has achieved total environmental dominance. What appears as a valley or a hillside is merely a fold or a bulge in its body. The "surface" is a composite material: a tough, resinous outer bark-like layer, cracked in geometric patterns that reveal a spongy, luminous green underlayer. It is warm to the touch, radiating a gentle heat from within. In the "low" areas, the outer layer is softer, almost fleshy, and sweats a clear, viscous fluid that pools in shallow, temporary basins. These are the "marshes"—not water, but the plant's own internal fluid, exposed. The weeping rocks are nodules of hardened, crystalline sap that extrude from vertical surfaces, perpetually dripping this same substance with a slow, heavy *plip... plip...*

**Atmosphere & Light:**
The air is thick, humid, and sweet, tasting of chlorophyll and heated resin. A perpetual, hazy vapor hangs in the open spaces, filtering the normal light into a diffuse, golden-green glow. Shadows are soft and indistinct. The light seems to come not from a sun, but from the bioluminescent undersides of massive, canopy-like structures far above—huge, flattened organs of the parent plant that pulse with a slow, internal luminescence. It is always mid-morning here.

**Scale & Density:**
The space is open, but it is an open like the interior of a cathedral. Enormous hollows, like atriums or amphitheaters, are carved into the mass. Between them stretch long, relatively flat corridors, empty save for occasional protrusions. The density is sparse because the organism *is* the space. Presences are not scattered objects; they are features of the same body. A towering, tapering spire is not a building; it is a hardened respiratory chimney. A cluster of bulbous sacs hanging from a ceiling is not a fruit; it is a sensory organ. You are walking through the insides of a single, world-sized being.

**Sounds:**
It is loud, but with a deep, organic, interior sound. The air throbs with a low-frequency hum, the circulatory noise of sap moving through continent-sized vessels. From deep within the structure come muffled, liquid *groans* and *sighs* as internal pressures shift. The constant *plip* of the sap-drops is a percussive counterpoint. Occasionally, a high-pitched *tinkling* sound comes from the crystal sap-nodules vibrating in resonance with the hum. There is no wind, so the sound is constant, directionless, and enveloping.

**Ecology & Fauna:**
There is no separate ecology. The plant is the substrate, the architecture, and the nutrient cycle. It recycles its own dead matter directly. Fauna is rare and utterly integrated. Small, translucent creatures—like salamanders made of glass—live in the sap-pools, filtering nutrients. Blind, pale insects with feathery antennae crawl along the resinous walls, tending to patches of symbiotic fungus that appear as geometric, glowing molds. They make no sound you can hear over the hum. They are not independent beings; they are mobile organs of the greater whole.

**Rules of the World & Weirdness:**
The strangeness lies in its total, silent, static unity. The **built** parameter is absolute: this is a made environment, but the maker and the made are one. The **tech** is none, for it is all biology; there is no tool-use, only specialized growth. The **erosion** is emerging—you can see it in the crystallizing sap, the slow hardening of new structures at the periphery of your vision. The plant is not growing outward, but *differentiating inward*, slowly crystallizing its own tissues into more permanent forms.

The **dynamic** is never changing. This is its most unsettling feature. Nothing decays in a way that creates space for new growth. The sap pools do not evaporate and return as rain; they are reabsorbed by the porous floor. The sound never changes pitch or rhythm. The light never shifts. The creatures do not reproduce in visible generations. It is a frozen equilibrium—a system so perfectly closed it has become time-invariant. It is not alive in the sense of growing and dying; it is alive in the sense of *being*, an eternal, vegetative dream. You have entered a monument to a form of life that won, and in winning, entered an eternal stasis. Your footsteps make no sound on the warm, resilient surface. You are a brief, contradictory thought in a vast, sleeping mind.
