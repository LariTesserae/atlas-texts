The world is called the Shard Plain, though “plain” is a misnomer born from a first, fleeting glance. It is a recently-uplifted continent of basalt and granite, its peaks and valleys still bearing the sharp, unweathered angles of their violent birth. The air is thin, bitterly cold at this altitude, yet it glitters. Not with ice, but with a perpetual, brilliant light that refracts through a suspended haze of crystalline spores. This is the breath of the world, a glittering fog that turns the vast sky into a blinding, luminous dome. Light is not a condition here; it is a substance, a blinding white currency that fuels everything.

Sound is the second currency, a deafening, layered cacophony. There is the deep, resonant *hum*—the planet’s own vibration felt in the bones of the land. Upon this sits the chittering, clicking, roaring wall of noise from the fauna, which is not merely present but *the* primary feature. This is an ecosystem where biology has subsumed all industry.

What appear to be towering factories are, upon closer inspection, colossal termite-like mounds of fused obsidian andite. They soar thousands of feet, pockmarked with countless glowing portals, their flanks crawling with chitinous engineers. Steam—a mix of volcanic venting and biological exhalation—billows from grated vents, contributing to the bright, refractive haze. The "machinery" is alive: vast, millipede-like creatures with crystalline carapaces grind stone into substrate; winged swarms, like metallic bees, ferry glowing nectar-sacs between fungal groves; and deep within the mound-cities, bioluminescent veins pulse with electrical currents, powering strange communal organs. The tech is industrial in scale and function, but wholly organic, grown and maintained by instinct.

The vegetation clings in defiance. Lush, dark green mats of cryo-adapted moss and lichen coat every surface, drinking the constant condensation that drips from the spore-mist. In sheltered ravines, forests of glass-needled pines rise, their sap a slow, luminous gel. The most striking flora are the “Condensation Spines”—translucent, pillar-like plants that wick moisture from the air, their interiors glowing with captured light, creating a landscape dotted with frozen fountains of pale green radiance.

Despite the industrial-biological frenzy, change is slow, patient. The geological uplift that created this realm is measured in eons. The great mound-cities grow imperceptibly. The cycles of light and sound are daily, but the evolutionary arms race between flora and fauna plays out over millennia. It is a saturated, packed world with no empty space—every niche is occupied by a creature, a root, a mycelial network, or a colony of microscopic light-harvesters. Yet the grand dynamic is one of slow, immense pressure, like a continent dreaming of becoming a mountain.

There is no "built" environment because the entire landscape *is* the construction. It is a continent-sized organism, a supercolony performing geology. The rules are those of a hive: absolute communal purpose, sensory overload as communication, and light as the primary energy and data stream. To stand here is to be inside the engine, deafened by its roar, blinded by its output, and chilled to the bone by the high, clear air of its birth.
