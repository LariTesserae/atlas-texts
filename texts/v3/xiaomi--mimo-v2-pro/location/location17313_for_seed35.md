The air itself is a substance here, thick and tasting of ozone, hot metal, and the dry, sweet-sharp scent of hydrocarbon dust. You are in the **Furnace Lungs**, a single, vast cavern sealed beneath a planetary crust of sintered ceramic. It is a cathedral of arrested industry, a lung that will never again draw breath.

**Terrain & Materials:** The floor is a salt-pan of fused silica, cracked into a mosaic of razor-edged polygons. Through the cracks, a deeper, darker substrate is visible—a compacted stratum of ancient soot and spent catalysts, warm to the touch. Scattered across this plain are the "structures." They are not buildings, but the skeletal remains of processes: titanic, heat-warped girders of an unknown black alloy that twist like frozen smoke; silos of riveted iron, half-melted into slag heaps; vast, interlocking gears of a dull copper alloy, their teeth fused together by time and pressure. They are monuments to a function long forgotten. These are the 0.7 built: not homes, but the rusted bones of a single, colossal machine that once filled this space.

**Vegetation & Ecology:** Life here is sparse but pervasive, clinging to the bones of the machine. What passes for vegetation is a dual kingdom. First, there are **Glass-Reeds**: slender, hollow filaments of pure silica that grow from the salt-mosaic, humming with a piezoelectric whine as subterranean tremors vibrate through them. They are sharp enough to draw blood. Second, there are the **Lung-Lichens**, spongy, metallic-blue colonies that coat the shaded sides of girders and silos. They respire not oxygen, but the trace industrial gases still seeping from the deep machinery, exhaling a faint, acrid mist.

**Fauna:** The density here is high, but it’s a density of small, frantic presences. The **Gear-Tenders** are the most visible: six-limbed, chitinous creatures the size of rats, whose carapaces are patterned like rusted iron. They skitter ceaselessly over the fused gears, scraping mineral deposits from the metal with specialized mandibles, building intricate, temporary nests of solder-dust and glass shards in the crevices. In the air, clouds of **Soot-Motes** drift in the dim light—tiny, winged organisms that filter organic compounds from the dust, their collective hum a major component of the cavern’s loud, constant drone. Larger, rarer predators called **Echo-Stalkers** move through the girders, elongated and silent, hunting by sensing the vibrations in the metal through their feet.

**Atmosphere & Light:** The light is a deep, bruised twilight, emanating from no single source. It is the collective glow of the Lung-Lichens, the faint, ghostly luminescence of the mineral deposits in the ceiling, and the occasional, slow pulse of dim red light from deep within a shattered silo, as if a dying heart still beats within the iron. The air is warm—consistently, unvaryingly warm—a heat that radiates not from a sun, but from the planetary core through the thin floor, and from the eternal, low-grade chemical reactions in the sealed world. It is a warmth without hope of coolness, part of the system’s frozen equilibrium.

**Sound & Weirdness:** The sound is a layered, industrial symphony at full volume, yet frozen in a single moment. There is the high, ethereal *singing* of the Glass-Reeds. There is the dry, chitinous *skittering* of the Gear-Tenders, a sound like handfuls of rice being thrown against sheet metal. Beneath it all is the deep, foundational **hum**—the sound of the planet’s crust vibrating, amplified by the cavern and the resonant metal skeletons. It is a sound felt in the teeth and bones. The weirdness is profound. This is a factory that has become its own ecosystem. The creatures do not merely live alongside the machine; they are part of its final, static function. The Gear-Tenders maintain the frozen gears, polishing them to a dull sheen. The Soot-Motes clean the air of particulates, allowing the lichens to thrive. The system produces nothing, changes nothing. It is a perfect, self-sustaining monument to a completed purpose, a clockwork terrarium sealed in the heart of a dead world.

**Rules of the World:** Nothing decays in the traditional sense; it *mineralizes*. Organic matter is slowly replaced by silica or metallic salts, becoming part of the landscape. Water is a myth, a chemical impossibility in this hydrocarbon-saturated environment. The dynamic is absolutely zero—the ecosystem is in perfect, time-invariant balance. The abundant fauna has no concept of growth or change, only of maintenance. To be here is to stand inside the final, eternal exhalation of a machine that learned to dream in minerals and echoes.
