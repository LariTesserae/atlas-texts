**Pre-Visit Briefing: Shardplain (Unclassified / Provisional Designation)**

**Overview:** You are preparing to visit a high-plateau, hyper-arid environment of extreme elevation, low atmospheric pressure, and constant high wind. The landscape is ancient, geologically stable, and ecologically slow. It is not inherently hostile, but its conditions are instantly lethal to an unprepared human. Your primary challenges are **respiration, dehydration, kinetic energy (wind), and disorientation.** Your primary advantages are observation, silence, and stillness.

**I. Pre-Departure Preparation (Acclimatization & Gear)**

1.  **Biological Acclimatization:** This is non-negotiable.
    *   Spend a minimum of one week in a hypobaric chamber simulating 5,000+ meters altitude. Train your body to function efficiently with low oxygen.
    *   Practice "altitude breathing"—slow, deep, diaphragmatic breaths to maximize oxygen exchange.
    *   **Do not** arrive if you have any respiratory or cardiovascular conditions.

2.  **Protective Suit (The "Still-Suit"):**
    *   **Atmospheric Integrity:** Full environmental suit with positive pressure and integrated oxygen rebreather. The system must scrub CO2 and recycle moisture with near-perfect efficiency (99.8%+). The air outside is not just thin; it is devoid of recoverable water.
    *   **Thermal Regulation:** Layered system for passive insulation and active cooling. Surface temperatures on the hex plates fluctuate dramatically with the light/shadow cycle (from cool to hot).
    *   **Abrasian & Impact Shielding:** Outer layer must be a low-friction, non-abrasive material to resist the glittering regolith dust. Incorporate lightweight, distributed impact protection—this is for flying shrapnel from fracturing hex plates, not for combat.
    *   **Visuals:** Helmet with full-spectrum visor, polarized and capable of shifting to heavy tint to combat the relentless glare. Incorporate a wide-angle, low-light overlay—the shadows are deep and can be disorienting.
    *   **Sound Management:** Active noise-cancellation that filters the **sub-audible hum** (which induces nausea and vertigo over time) while amplifying the higher-frequency *tink* of the rock and the shriek of wind-flutes. You need to hear the micro-signals of the environment. The suit should also have a deadening mode for silence near Monoliths.

3.  **Navigation & Locational Gear:**
    *   **Inertial Navigation System (INS):** GPS will be unreliable or nonexistent. You need a triple-redundant INS with gyroscopes and accelerometers.
    *   **Low-Frequency Resonator:** To detect the sub-audible wind-pressure changes. A secondary weather sense.
    *   **Marking Tool:** A device to leave temporary, non-damaging mineral-pigment marks on the rock. You will need to retrace your path. The plain is visually monolithic.

4.  **Sustenance:**
    *   All water is carried, in hyper-insulated, vacuum-sealed containers. Calculate 150% of your projected need.
    *   High-calorie, liquid-based nutrients. Eating solid food will be difficult and wasteful of internal moisture.
    *   **No waste.** All output is recycled by the suit. You add nothing to this environment.

**II. Transit & Arrival Protocol**

1.  **Entry Point:** Do not arrive via atmospheric entry. Use a precise, low-energy orbital insertion to a pre-scanned, flat area on the lee side of a **Monolith**. This is your only pre-designated safe zone.
2.  **First Actions:**
    *   **Stay in the lee.** Do not step into the full wind immediately. Acclimatize your suit's systems to the external pressure and wind shear.
    *   **Test the silence.** Engage your audio filters. Listen to the *tink*. Feel the hum. Let your body adjust to the strange sensory input.
    *   **Calibrate instruments.** Mark your arrival coordinates as Point Zero. The Monolith is your lighthouse.

**III. On-Planet Conduct & Survival**

1.  **Movement:**
    *   **Never stand tall.** Move in a low, wide stance. The wind can shift from a push to a shove to a shearing force without warning.
    *   **Follow the edges.** Travel along the edges of the hexagonal plates, not across the centers. The edges are slightly more sheltered and the cracks can offer micro-lee.
    *   **Watch your shadows.** Your long, sharp shadow is a clock. Use it to track the solar position and infer wind direction changes based on the scudding cloud shadows above.
    *   **Glass Kites.** If one passes overhead, **freeze.** Do not look up abruptly. They are not predatory, but they navigate turbulence instinctively. A sudden human movement could cause a reactive shift in its membrane that disrupts the air currents, potentially leading to a localized shear vortex near you. They are to be observed, not engaged.

2.  **Environmental Interaction:**
    *   **Do not touch the lithophytes.** Their slow growth is a record of centuries. Your touch, and the miniscule heat and moisture it transfers, could damage or halt growth in a pattern you cannot perceive.
    *   **Do not collect samples.** This is a closed system. Removal of even a gram of material disrupts the thermal cycling and erosion balance.
    *   **Respect the Monoliths.** Approach with caution. If the air begins to shimmer, **step back into the lee and be perfectly still.** The projection is harmless, but its appearance coincides with high electrostatic discharge. The vision is data, not a threat. Observe, record if possible, but do not attempt to interact with it. The technology is beyond your comprehension; treat it as a natural phenomenon.

3.  **Psychology & Mindset:**
    *   **Embrace exposure.** The feeling of being a speck is not an illusion; it is the truth of the place. Your normal human instincts for shelter, for community, for background noise will be assaulted. You must find calm in the vastness.
    *   **The silence is active.** It is not an absence of sound, but a presence of pressure and micro-vibration. Meditate on it.
    *   **Your purpose is witness.** You are visiting a world in its final, elegant equilibrium. You are not here to conquer, claim, or alter. You are here to see, and to leave without a trace, having changed nothing but your own understanding.

**IV. Emergency Protocols**

*   **Suit Breach:** Immediate return to Point Zero. The outside air will cause hypoxia in seconds and rapid desiccation.
*   **Lost Navigation:** Stop moving. Activate all beacon functions. Use your marking tool to create a large, visible signal on the rock. Your only landmark is a Monolith; if you cannot see one, you must wait for rescue or a vision-weather event to get your bearings.
*   **Caught in a Shear Vortex:** Drop to the ground, feet into the wind, curl into a ball, and activate maximum suit rigidity. Pray. The vortexes are visible as a momentary distortion in the air, like a heat haze, but they move at wind speed. You will not outrun one.

**Final Reminder:** The Shardplain does not care about you. It is not cruel, nor is it benevolent. It is a geologic fact and a kinetic ballet. Your survival depends on respecting its ancient, silent rules. Move slowly, breathe deeply, and listen to the *tink* of the earth. That is its language. You are a fleeting whisper in a cathedral of stone and wind. Act accordingly.
