**Preparation Dossier: The Furnace Under the Crest**

**Primary Threats:**
1.  **Extreme Heat & Radiant Energy:** Ambient temperatures exceed 200°C (392°F) at floor level. Radiant heat from stone and living colonies will rapidly cause severe burns and hyperthermia.
2.  **Atmosphere:** Oxygen-poor, laden with sulfur dioxide, hydrogen sulfide, metal vapors, and unknown metabolic exhalations. The air itself is a scalding, toxic fluid.
3.  **Ecosystem Density & Behavior:** The space is a continuous, interconnected living system. Movement risks damaging it; its reactions to intrusion are unknown but potentially defensive (shell-creature closures, sessile-organism filter-feeding).
4.  **Psychological & Neurological Influence:** The subsonic rhythmic pulsing and the oppressive, rhythmic click can entrain human brainwaves and heart rate, inducing trance-like states, altered perception, and a dangerous sense of belonging or lethargy.
5.  **Physical Terrain:** No clear floor. Must traverse a uneven landscape of fused, armored mounds, risking falls, entrapment in narrow gaps, or damage to the ecosystem's "cities."

**Required Gear & Preparation:**

**1. Environmental Suit (Non-Negotiable):**
*   **Thermal Protection:** A multi-layered, actively cooled suit is mandatory. Passively insulated suits will fail. Consider a Phase-Change Material (PCM) inner layer with a powered external cooling loop and heat-reflective outer shell. Suit integrity must be rated for continuous 250°C+ exposure.
*   **Respiratory System:** A closed-circuit rebreather with advanced chemical scrubbers (for SO₂, H₂S, and organic volatiles) and a dedicated oxygen supply. A simple filter mask will result in lung scalding and poisoning within minutes. The system must cool inhaled air to a breathable temperature.
*   **Sealed Visor & HUD:** The visor must be optically clear, anti-fog, and thermally shielded. A heads-up display is critical for navigation in low light, monitoring suit integrity, core temperature, O₂/CO₂ levels, and tracking vital signs against the subsonic pulsation. **Crucially, integrate a rhythmic counter-pulse or white-noise generator into the audio feed to disrupt the ambient frequency entrainment.**
*   **Exoskeletal Support (Recommended):** To aid movement over the treacherous, uneven terrain and reduce exertion (and thus metabolic heat) in the heavy suit.

**2. Lighting & Observation:**
*   **Light Discipline:** Use only low-intensity, red-spectrum LED lights. Bright or white light may trigger a defensive response in the photosensitive (or chemosensitive) organisms. The bioluminescent shimmer is sufficient for adapted vision; rely on it primarily.
*   **Non-Contact Tools:** Long-range thermal sensors, laser spectrometers on poles, and macro-lens cameras on articulated arms. **Do not make direct physical contact with any organism or mat.** Unknown chemical or thermal transfer could occur.

**3. Behavioral & Mission Protocols:**
*   **The Buddy System & Tethering:** Never enter alone. Maintain a physical tether to your partner and a base station at the entrance. Prepare for rapid extraction if vitals show signs of heat stroke or neurological alteration.
*   **Time Limit:** Set a hard, pre-determined time limit (suggested: 30-45 minutes maximum). The physiological and psychological toll is cumulative. Assign a non-entranced "timekeeper" outside the chamber to enforce recall via comms.
*   **Movement:** Move slowly and deliberately. Plan each step to avoid crushing the mat or mounds. Use the half-meter perimeters around the stone artifacts as your pathways—they are your only "clear" lanes.
*   **Psychological Self-Monitoring:** Constantly self-check for feelings of euphoria, deep peace, or a sense of "understanding" the rhythm. These are not signs of enlightenment; they are symptoms of neurological compromise. Recite technical data, focus on your cooling system's hum, maintain active mental tasks. If you begin to find the pulsing "not unpleasant," initiate extraction immediately.
*   **Artifact Interaction:** Do not touch the stone bench, lintel, or hand-holds. They are unknown variables. Photograph and sample from a distance. The organisms' avoidance of them suggests a chemical or historical anomaly—treat it as a potential hazard or a sacred boundary.

**4. Post-Visit Decontamination & Medical:**
*   **Full Decon:** Assume your suit's exterior is coated with chemical residues and microscopic organisms. A rigorous, multi-stage chemical and thermal decontamination shower is required before suit removal.
*   **Medical Evaluation:** Immediate check for thermal injuries (especially airway), blood toxicity (sulfur compounds), and neurological function. The auditory and subsonic exposure may require monitoring for aftereffects like tinnitus or arrhythmia.

**What to Expect (The Visitor's Experience):**
You will feel the heat as a physical blow the moment the chimney opens. Every breath inside the suit will be a conscious act, a reminder of the machinery keeping you alive. Your world will shrink to the circle of your visor light, the glow of your HUD, and the alien tapestry of rust-red life that seems to *pulse*. The sound will be inside you—a pressure in the chest, a vibration in the bones. The sheer *density* of life, not as individual creatures but as a single, wall-to-wall organism, will be psychologically overwhelming. It is beautiful in the way a star or a tectonic plate is beautiful: immense, impersonal, and utterly indifferent to you. Your primary struggle will be against your own biology, which will want to slow down, synchronize, and join the rhythm. You are a temporary glitch in a system that has been perfecting itself for geological time. Your goal is to observe, record, and leave before the system—or your own mind—decides you belong there.

**Final Warning:** The Furnace does not attack invaders. It simply is. Its defense is its existence: a reality so thermally, chemically, and rhythmically potent that unprepared human physiology and psychology will fail. Respect it as you would respect the vacuum of space or the heart of a star. Prepare accordingly.
