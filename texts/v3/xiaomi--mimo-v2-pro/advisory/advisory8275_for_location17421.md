## **Traveler's Advisory: The Glass Marshes**

**Core Principle:** You are entering a landscape that is a dormant, geologic-scale machine. It is not hostile, but it is profoundly *indifferent* and *reactive*. Your survival and sanity depend on understanding that you are a disruptive element in a system of ancient, dissipated equilibrium. Prepare not for adventure, but for immersion in a resonant archive.

### **I. Mind & Psyche Preparation**

*   **Accept Saturation:** Your primary mental adjustment is to accept that everything here is full—of water, of history, of humming energy. Do not seek dryness, sharpness, or clarity. Your thoughts will feel slower, heavier. This is not a symptom; it is the environment. Practice meditative focus beforehand to avoid mental fog.
*   **The Echo Rule:** Every action you take will have prolonged, visible consequences. A misplaced footstep creates ripples that alter Still-Zones for minutes. Shouting may cause crystalline chimes to cascade across a lagoon. Move with the deliberate, slow grace of someone in a library or a temple. This place has perfect, damp memory.
*   **Accept the Weird:** The floating droplets, the upward-flowing micro-currents, the shadows that fall wrong—these are not hallucinations. They are the "operating system" of the marsh. Do not panic. Observe them as you would weather. They are the only "weather" here.

### **II. Physical Gear & Attire**

*   **Footwear:** High, tight-gaitered boots of a **non-metallic, non-porous material**. Think vulcanized rubber or a dense, synthetic polymer. The weeping stone is slick, and the water's silty sheen may conceal sharp, glassy edges of petrified reed-stone. The gaiters are to prevent the slow, ooze-like water from creeping in.
*   **Clothing:** A full-body, layered system. A base layer of moisture-wicking wool or synthetic (avoid cotton—it will never dry). An outer layer of **oilskin or waxed canvas**. The goal is to create a barrier against the absolute humidity, not to stay dry (impossible), but to regulate the rate at which you become saturated. Seams must be sealed.
*   **Head & Breathing:** A wide-brimmed, waterproof hat. For breathing, a simple **dust mask or cloth buff** is essential. The air is not toxic, but its "plasmatic" quality and colloidal particles can coat the throat and lungs, leading to a hacking, silicate-laden cough and a deep chill. In Still-Zones, the mask also muffles the unnerving hum.
*   **Sensory Aids:**
    *   **Goggles:** Clear, anti-fog, with a good seal. Protects eyes from the constant silty mist and allows you to see the refractions and suspended droplets clearly without irritation.
    *   **Ear Protection:** **Acoustic filters or musician's earplugs** that reduce volume but preserve fidelity. The constant, layered thrum, hiss, and chime is psychically draining and can lead to disorientation, nausea, and auditory pareidolia (hearing voices in the noise).
*   **Tools & Items:**
    *   **Non-Metallic Probes:** A staff of cured wood or dense plastic. Use it to test the depth and solidity of water-covered plates and to feel for vibrations in the thrum. **Never use metal tools**; the resonant properties of the archive-land might cause them to vibrate uncomfortably, heat up, or interfere with Still-Zones.
    *   **Ceramic or Glass Containers:** For water or samples. Metal may leach or resonate.
    *   **Light Source:** A **chemical light stick (glow stick)** or a sealed, waterproof LED with a *diffuser filter*. A sharp beam of light can act like a thrown stone, agitating the Still-Zones and creating prolonged visual echoes. You want a soft, ambient glow.
    *   **Rations:** Dense, dry foods in sealed waterproof containers. Anything porous will become damp within an hour. The water here, while not immediately toxic, is saturated with colloidal silicates and residual energy—**do not drink it** unless in a dire emergency, and even then, filter it through multiple layers of cloth first.

### **III. Movement & Interaction Protocols**

*   **Travel in "Pulses":** Do not walk continuously. Move for 10-15 minutes, then stop for 5. Listen. Observe how your passage has affected the local Still-Zones and water patterns. This allows the environment to "re-settle" around you and prevents you from walking into a newly formed physics anomaly.
*   **Avoid Still-Zones:** Do not enter them intentionally. The suspended physics is unpredictable. If you must pass near one, move slowly and smoothly. Sudden movements can cause them to collapse or merge violently.
*   **Interacting with Fauna:** The Weaver-Runners are harmless if undisturbed. They are the archivists. Do not interfere with their work. They may approach you out of what seems like curiosity, likely to assess if your presence is a "fracture" needing repair. Remain still. They will lose interest.
*   **The Petrified Reed-Stone:** It is tempting to break a piece to see the circuitry. **Do so with extreme caution.** The act of breaking it creates a sharp, high-frequency vibration that will carry for miles through the water and stone, attracting the attention of every Weaver-Runner in the basin and potentially triggering a localized stabilization event as the system tries to "heal" the breach. Take only a very small chip, and do it once, at most.

### **IV. The Danger Assessment**

*   **Primary Dangers:**
    1.  **Psychic Saturation:** Losing your sense of self to the resonant, memory-heavy atmosphere. The thrum is a siren song to dissolution.
    2.  **Physical Exhaustion:** The humidity saps energy, and the careful movement is slow. Hypothermia is a real risk, as the dampness leaches heat relentlessly.
    3.  **Getting Lost:** With a hazy, unchanging sky and diffused light, traditional navigation is impossible. The landscape of ridges and lagoons is subtly repetitive. **Mark your path** with biodegradable, non-reflective indicators (e.g., twists of dried grass from the edges). Trust your probe's vibrations over your eyes.
*   **Secondary Dangers:**
    *   Falling into a deeper lagoon basin concealed by the glassy surface.
    *   A Still-Zone merge event happening near you, causing temporary disorientation and nausea.
    *   The Weaver-Runners, if they perceive you as a massive, walking "fracture," may attempt to "repair" you with their ceramic saliva, which would be painful and immobilizing.

### **V. The Final, Crucial Advice**

**You are not the protagonist here.** You are a brief ripple in a system that measures time in geological epochs and data decay. Your goal is to witness, to survive, and to leave without causing a major echo. Do not seek to understand the archive's original purpose; it has weathered beyond that. Instead, experience the profound, eerie peace of a world that has completed its work and now dreams in water and stone. Listen to its thrum. It is the sound of a lullaby sung by a dead god to itself. Respect that, and you may pass through unscathed, carrying with you the chilling, beautiful memory of a place where even the air remembers.
