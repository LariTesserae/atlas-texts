To prepare for a journey to the Sill is to prepare for a pilgrimage to the end of time itself. This is not an exploration of a new world, but an audience with a finished one. The ordinary rules of travel, survival, and even perception must be discarded and rebuilt around the core principle: **you are the only dynamic thing here.**

### **I. Psychological & Philosophical Preparation**

This is your most critical gear.

1.  **Embrace the Concept of "Finished":** Meditate on the idea of completion, of an equation finally solved, a story with its last period placed millennia ago. Your presence is a temporary annotation. Cultivate a mindset of profound humility and passive observation. You are not here to conquer, collect, or even understand, but to witness.
2.  **Pre-Perception Training:** Your brain will struggle. It will try to animate the geometries, to find patterns in the stillness, to impose narrative on the barren. Practice "soft gaze" meditation. Learn to look at things without assigning immediate purpose or motion. Train yourself to accept superimposed, non-interacting realities—a rock being *only* a rock, and simultaneously *only* an anchor for a frozen helix of light. Do not seek resolution. Let the contradiction be.
3.  **Sanity Anchors:** The hum, the static geometries, the immense pressure of stillness can induce derealization or existential dread. Establish mental anchors *before* you go: a memorized poem, a tactile object from your home universe (a smooth river stone, a worn coin), a specific, warm memory. These are your cognitive emergency kit.
4.  **Acceptance of Futility:** There is nothing to *do* on the Sill. There is only *being* and *seeing*. Abandon all goal-oriented thinking. Your purpose is simply to be present at the wake of a universe.

### **II. Physical & Logistical Preparation**

**Gear for a Heat-Death Environment:**

*   **Atmospheric Protection:** The air is thin, dry, and full of fine, abrasive mineral dust.
    *   **A sealed, positive-pressure respirator helmet** with excellent particulate filtration and internal humidity control is non-negotiable. The dryness will crack skin and lungs.
    *   **Full-body, layered thermal wear** to manage the cool, stable temperature. Outer layers should be seamless and smooth to minimize dust accumulation.
    *   **High-impact, polarized eye protection** that seals against the face. The light is clear but unchanging, and the visual strain from the geometries will be immense. Polarization may help reduce some of the harder, disorienting reflections from the spherical entities.

*   **Mobility & Shelter:**
    *   **Footwear:** Stiff-soled boots with a very low profile. The polygonal ground is a treacherous, ankle-snapping mosaic of sharp edges and hairline fractures. You need sensitivity and stability, not cushioning.
    *   **No Tents:** Nothing that relies on stakes or creates wind resistance in a windless place. A low-profile, self-supporting bivouac sack or a rigid, ground-hugging shelter is your only option. Your shelter is not for protection from weather, but from the *sight* of the geometries, to give your mind periodic respite.
    *   **No Fire, No Cooking:** Combustion is a violent, dynamic chemical change that would feel blasphemous here. All food must be pre-prepared, nutrient-dense, and require zero preparation. Liquid meals are ideal. Water is your heaviest burden—calculate and carry every milliliter you will need, in reinforced containers. There is none to be found, and condensation does not occur.

*   **Navigation & Tools:**
    *   **Forget GPS or Compass:** The magnetic field is likely ancient and erratic, and the flat, featureless expanse defies conventional mapping. Navigation is by inertial tracking and meticulous, constant logging of your relation to the fixed geometries and scattered artifacts. Your primary tool is a **high-resolution, multi-spectrum panoramic camera** with flawless data backup. Your memory will fail; the record will not.
    *   **Passive Instruments Only:** Bring tools that measure, not interact. A seismometer to listen to the deep tectonic creak. A spectrometer to analyze the mineral "lichen." A precise magnetometer. Do not bring drills, hammers, or sampling tools. To break a piece of the Sill would be to misunderstand it utterly.
    *   **Light:** Low-intensity, diffuse, warm-spectrum LED lighting only. Harsh beams will create jarring, complex shadows from the geometries and may interact unpredictably with their fields. Your light should be for reading your instruments and finding your way to your bivouac, nothing more.

### **III. Behavioral Protocols for the Sill**

1.  **The Rule of Minimal Impact:** Move slowly. Speak in whispers, or not at all. The hum is a pressure; your voice is a disruption. Step on the largest, most stable polygonal stones. Avoid the mineral lichen and the artifacts. You are a ghost in a museum of ghosts. Act accordingly.
2.  **Interaction with the Geometries: NEVER.** Do not touch them. Do not throw objects at them. Do not shine lights directly on them. They are not machines or lifeforms; they are *aspects of the local reality*. To interact is to risk becoming a fixed part of their layered, static truth—a human fossil in the final layer. Observe them only through your instruments and your sealed visor.
3.  **Study the Artifacts, Don't Interpret Them:** The circles, slabs, and walls are your only companions from the stream of time. Record them with reverence. Note their alignments. But do not project stories onto them. They are not messages *to* you; they are the marks of those who learned to listen before the silence became total. They are the ultimate lesson in humility.
4.  **Manage Your Duration:** Your psychological stamina is your only true clock. Plan for short, intensive periods of exposure to the open plateau, followed by long, dark rests in your sealed shelter, anchored by your familiar objects and memories. The Sill does not tire, but you will, at a soul-deep level.

### **IV. What You Will Experience**

Expect no climax. The revelation is cumulative and oppressive.
You will feel the crushing weight of *scale*—of time, of space, of stillness.
You will fight vertigo not from height, but from the *depth of time* you stand upon.
You will hear the hum not with your ears, but with the bones of your skull.
You will dream of polygons and silent, rotating spheres.
You will return forever changed, carrying the silence of the Sill within you. You will understand, viscerally, that the universe does not end in a bang or a whimper, but in a vast, geometrically perfect, and utterly silent sigh.

**Final Warning:** Only those who feel a calling to witness the absolute end of narrative should go. This is not a destination. It is a conclusion. Your preparation is not for adventure, but for a respectful attendance at the final, quiet closing of the book.
