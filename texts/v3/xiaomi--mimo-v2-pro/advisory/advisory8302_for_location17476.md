# Preparing for a Visit to the Cauldron Towns: A Traveler's Guide

## Mindset & Expectation
Before you pack a single item, adjust your mind. This is not a destination; it is a *condition*. You are visiting a place that exists on the edge of habitability, a human settlement clinging to geothermal fury. Expect relentless, damp heat. Expect a closed system where every resource is sweat-equity. Expect a social fabric as tense and functional as the bolt-holds in the basalt. Leave any notion of leisure, privacy, or aesthetics at the grate-stair. You are entering a body—a sweating, laboring, communal body. Your role is to be a quiet, unobtrusive cell within it.

---

## Gear & Clothing

### **Clothing: Your Second Skin**
*   **Material:** Merino wool or high-quality synthetic blends. **Cotton is your enemy.** It absorbs moisture, becomes heavy, chafes, and will not dry. You need fabrics that wick sweat away, dry quickly, and resist the constant damp.
*   **Layers:** Wear a single, lightweight layer. A long-sleeved, loose-fitting shirt and pants. The sleeves protect your arms from scraping against the ever-present sharp basalt and the hot pipes. Light colors may reflect some lamp light, but will quickly become stained.
*   **Footwear:** Sturdy, broken-in, closed-toe boots with excellent grip. The floors are wet, the channels are slick, and you will be ducking and climbing. Thick soles are essential; the rock transmits heat from below. Consider boots with a waterproof membrane—not for external water, but to keep the omnipresent condensation from soaking your socks.
*   **Head & Neck:** A wide-brimmed hat or a simple cotton scarf. The hat provides a psychological and slight thermal barrier from the low ceilings. The scarf can be soaked in cistern water (if permitted) and worn around the neck for cooling.
*   **Gloves:** A pair of thin, durable work gloves. You may be asked to help, or you may instinctively grab a hot pipe. The metal is everywhere.

### **Essential Kit**
1.  **Water & Electrolytes:** While they have water, its mineral quality is unknown. Carry a durable water bottle and a supply of electrolyte tablets or powder. Dehydration will come not from lack of water, but from profuse, non-evaporative sweating that leaches salts from your body.
2.  **Personal Light:** A headlamp with a **red-light mode** is non-negotiable. The passages are navigated by memory and residual glow. Your white light will feel like an intrusion, blinding others and destroying the carefully managed low-light environment. Use red light minimally, only to prevent a fall or read a tool.
3.  **Respiratory Protection:** A simple N95 mask or a breathable cloth mask. The workshop air will contain fine metallic and stone dust, and the general atmosphere is thick with mineral vapors. It also helps with the psychological feeling of breathing soup.
4.  **Small Toolkit:** A multi-tool, a roll of high-quality duct tape, and a few zip-ties. In a place where everything is maintained, offering a practical fix to a minor problem (a loose hook, a fraying rope) is a universal language of goodwill.
5.  **High-Calorie, Low-Residue Food:** Bring your own calories. Bars, nuts, dried fruit. Do not expect to be fed from their limited mushroom supply. Your food should produce minimal waste—wrappers must be carried out.
6.  **Ear Protection:** Simple foam earplugs. The constant, arrhythmic pump-cough and sub-audible rock groans can create a profound psychological fatigue over time. Sleep will be difficult enough.
7.  **Mental Anchor:** A small, solid object—a smooth stone, a metal token. Something cool to the touch, that you can hold in your hand when the heat feels like a physical weight. A reminder of another, cooler world.

## Health & Sustenance

*   **Heat Acclimatization:** If possible, spend time in a sauna or steam room for several days prior. Practice slow, deliberate movement. Learn to recognize the signs of heat exhaustion in yourself: dizziness, nausea, cessation of sweating, irritability.
*   **Gut Preparation:** Your diet will change abruptly to mushrooms and possibly preserved staples they may trade for. Start taking a robust probiotic now to fortify your gut biome against sudden change.
*   **Skin Care:** Pack a potent anti-chafing balm (like Body Glide) and antifungal powder. Your skin will be constantly wet. Fungal infections are a near-certainty without preventative care.
*   **First-Aid Focus:** Pack for cuts (from sharp basalt), burns (from pipes, tools, steam), and fungal issues. Include antiseptic, butterfly closures, and burn gel. Oral rehydration salts are more valuable than band-aids.

## Cultural & Behavioral Protocol

This is the most critical preparation. Survival is physical; acceptance is behavioral.

1.  **The Rule of Use:** **Touch nothing that is not offered or clearly communal.** Every pin, hook, and tool has a purpose and an owner, even if they are not present. To move something is to disrupt a carefully balanced system. Keep your hands clasped behind your back or at your sides when observing.
2.  **Labor is Currency:** Do not wait to be asked. If you see someone struggling to lift a panel, brace a ladder, or hold a light, **step in silently and help.** Mimic their pace and method. When the task is done, give a single nod and step back. Do not expect thanks. The work *is* the interaction.
3.  **The Gate:** Do not look at it for more than a passing glance. Do not approach it. Do not ask about it. Your awareness of the rule is shown by your perfect, unspoken obedience to it.
4.  **The Heat:** You may mop your brow. You may sigh. You may move slowly. You may **not** say, "It's so hot," or complain. To voice it is to force everyone else to acknowledge a truth they have integrated into their existence. It is a social violation of the first order.
5.  **The Sound of Voice:** Speak softly. The low ceilings carry and compress sound. A normal speaking voice will feel like a shout. Listen to the volume of the locals and match it, or go slightly quieter. Conversations are murmurs with edges.
6.  **The Mushrooms:** View them with reverence. They are life. Do not pick at them, taste one without explicit invitation, or comment on their paleness. If given some to eat, consume every piece.
7.  **The Condensation:** This is their water source. Do not spit, rinse your hands, or contaminate any channel or cistern. If you drip sweat into a cistern, it is a contribution. If you drop a wrapper, it is a desecration.

## Navigation & Survival Tactics

*   **Move Like Smoke:** Slow, fluid, deliberate. No sudden gestures. Avoid brushing against walls, pipes, or partitions. You are a large, damp object in a space of precise utility. Minimize your footprint.
*   **Listen to Learn:** The "texture" of sound is your map. The pump-cough means water and the active, deeper town. The clank of metal means the workshop. The silence (or deeper groan) beyond the gate means *stop*. The direction of airflow (hisssss of the compressed air) can guide you.
*   **Eyes Downcast:** Make eye contact briefly and infrequently. Observe the town, not its inhabitants. Staring at people in their close, private-not-private spaces is aggressive.
*   **Your Exit is Your Responsibility:** Remember the path back to the grate-stair. It is your only way out. The town is a labyrinth of chambers, and the sameness of the stone is disorienting. Use your red light to note a unique fracture pattern or bolt arrangement at key turns.
*   **When to Leave:** Plan a short visit. 24-48 hours is likely the maximum a newcomer can handle without succumbing to heat stress or social fatigue. When you feel your thoughts begin to simmer with irritability, or your skin begins to itch unbearably, it is time to thank no one in particular and climb back into the cool, weeping throat of the grate-stair.

---

**Final Thought:** You are not going to see a place. You are going to *inhabit a condition* alongside others who have no choice. Your preparation is about making your body and your behavior so unobtrusive, so useful, and so respectful that the Cauldron Towns, in their brutal, ongoing negotiation with the earth, might tolerate your presence for a short while. Prepare for the heat, yes, but prepare more for the silence that is not silent, the community that is a single organism, and the deep, unspoken truth that the pumps must never, ever stop.
