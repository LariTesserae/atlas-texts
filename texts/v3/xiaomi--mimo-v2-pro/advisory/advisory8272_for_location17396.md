# Traveler's Preparation Guide: The Geothermal Basin

## **Primary Hazard Assessment: HEAT**
This is not a casual destination. The basin is a living furnace, and your preparation must reflect that.

**1. Thermal Protection:**
*   **Suiting:** Your primary defense will be a multi-layered **active cooling suit**. Do *not* rely on passive insulation alone. Look for:
    *   **Outer Layer:** Reflective, ceramic-fiber weave to deflect radiant heat.
    *   **Mid-Layer:** A network of phase-change coolant tubes or a thermoelectric (Peltier) system. Power it with a high-density, long-duration battery.
    *   **Inner Layer:** Moisture-wicking to manage sweat.
*   **Helmet & Visor:** A full-coverage helmet with a **gold-leaf or multi-layer infrared-reflective visor** is mandatory. The dim, coppery light is heavy with infrared. Your visor should have an anti-fog coating and an internal heads-up display (HUD) linked to your suit's thermal and atmospheric sensors.
*   **Gloves & Boots:** Insulated, with grip soles rated for hot, smooth silicate crust. The ground can be hot enough to melt standard polymers.

**2. Hydration & Atmosphere:**
*   **Water:** The condensate harvest is your only potential water source. Bring a **high-capacity, insulated hydration pack** (at least 4L). Assume all local water requires purification; carry a compact, robust filter capable of removing mineral particulate and potential industrial contaminants.
*   **Air:** The "perpetual haze of mineral vapor" is a lung-shredder. A **powered air-purifying respirator (PAPR)** with P100 particulate filters is the *minimum*. For longer stays, a closed-circuit rebreather with a carbon dioxide scrubber is advisable to avoid drawing in the hot, thick air directly. Monitor oxygen levels—the haze can be displacement-heavy.

## **Navigation & Terrain Traversal**
**1. The Crust:**
*   The hexagonal mosaic is unstable. **Do not trust it.** Always wear your boots. Use **trekking poles with carbide tips** to test the ground ahead. A step can shatter a "tile," plunging your leg into a fissure or exposing you to scalding subsurface heat.
*   The "glassy chime" is your auditory warning system. A sudden increase in cracking sounds means you are in an unstable zone.

**2. Spatial Awareness:**
*   The 0.2 density means vast, empty stretches between structures. **Navigation is by landmark and instrument.** A GPS unit (if compatible with local physics) or a inertial navigation system is crucial. The heat haze and permanent steam will severely limit visual range. Use the towering heat-exchangers as your primary waypoints.
*   **Do not wander at night.** The industrial glow is disorienting, and the cooling crust may become more brittle.

## **Interaction with Fauna: A Strict Non-Interference Policy**
These are not animals; they are **industrial components and energy systems.** Treat them as such.

*   **Convection Beetles:** Their obsidian carapaces can focus heat like lenses. **Do not approach.** Do not block their slow, deliberate paths. They are likely semi-wild assets; interfering with them may trigger an automated or custodial response from the city's operators.
*   **Volt-Weavers:** The gossamer filaments carry lethal voltage. If you see a drifting swarm, **freeze.** Assess their drift path. If they are arcing, the air itself may become conductive. Your suit should have some dielectric properties, but do not test it. They are "milked" for a reason—touching them is theft and suicide.
*   **Geophagic Worms:** You will likely only encounter their excreted slag fields or the deep tremors of their passing. If you see one, you are too close to a major processing shaft. **Retreat immediately.** Their ceramic skin is hot, and they are the primary reason the ground is unstable.

## **Interacting with the "City" & Its Inhabitants**
*   **Assumption:** This is a **company town** or a **state-run industrial complex.** You are an anomaly. Your presence is either a curiosity, a nuisance, or a security threat.
*   **Demeanor:** Be slow, deliberate, and non-threatening. Keep your hands visible. If you use lights, keep them on a low, diffuse setting to avoid blinding or alarming operators.
*   **Communication:** They may not speak your language. Prepare icon-based translation boards or a universal translation device. Focus icons on: **Water, Rest, Trade, Direction.**
*   **Shelter:** Do not assume you can enter any building. The low, domed, armored structures are likely pressurized or climate-controlled interiors. Seek designated **"traveler's havens"** or **"process observer galleries"**—structures with external observation ports. If none exist, your best shelter is the **leeward side of a large, insulated conduit,** where you can benefit from its residual heat gradient without being in the direct flow.
*   **Economics:** Energy is currency. If you have something to trade, it should be **high-density data, unique material samples, or advanced repair components.** Do not offer physical currency from other worlds. A **portable, efficient solar charger** (if the haze doesn't block the wavelengths) might be a novelty, but a **superior thermal insulation sample** or a **data-crystal with novel engineering schematics** would be far more valuable.

## **Psychological Preparation**
*   **The Hum:** The omnipresent low-frequency vibration will permeate your body. It is fatiguing. Noise-canceling headphones that specifically target low frequencies are a worthy luxury.
*   **The Emptiness & The Scale:** The combination of vast empty space and immense industrial structures can induce profound agoraphobia and insignificance. Have a mental routine or a familiar audio file to center yourself.
*   **The Oppressive Equilibrium:** Nothing is frantic, yet everything is in motion. It is a place of immense power held in perfect, grim balance. Do not expect grand welcomes or dramatic vistas. Your reward is the sight of a **cosmic-scale industrial process,** a city that is a single, functioning machine built into a planetary wound.

## **Final Checklist: The Essentials**
1.  **Active Cooling Suit** with full HUD and environmental sensors.
2.  **PAPR or Rebreather** with ample filters/scrubbers.
3.  **4L+ Insulated Hydration Pack & Purifier.**
4.  **Sturdy, Heat-Resistant Boots & Trekking Poles.**
5.  **Navigation Suite** (GPS/INS) and **Physical Maps** of the conduit-web.
6.  **First Aid Kit** specializing in **burns, dehydration, and respiratory distress.**
7.  **Trade Goods:** High-value, low-weight tech or information.
8.  **Mental Fortitude.**

**Remember:** You are not visiting a landscape; you are visiting a **function.** You are a foreign particle in a slow, hot, and precise engine. Move with respect, observe with caution, and your passage will be recorded as a minor, tolerated fluctuation in the system's eternal hum.
