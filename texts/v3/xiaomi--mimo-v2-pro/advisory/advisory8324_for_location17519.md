# Preparation Guide for Visiting Khol-Thyrsa, the Weeping City

**1. Pre-Travel Mental & Psychological Readjustment:**
- **Accept Geological Time:** This is not a city of haste. Your heartbeat, your breath, your sense of urgency will feel alien here. Practice slow, deliberate movement and meditation before arrival. The greatest danger may be frustration or a feeling of insignificance.
- **Embrace the Wet:** You will never be dry. Mentally prepare for perpetual dampness, the sound of water, and the sight of liquid moving in ways that defy conventional plumbing. See it not as flooding, but as the city's circulatory system.
- **Re-evaluate "Life":** Your brain will want to see "animals" and "plants." It will see rocks moving and minerals growing. Suppress the instinct to label them as mere geological phenomena. They are the fauna and flora here. Respect them as living components of the ecosystem.

**2. Essential Gear & Clothing:**
- **Footwear:** High-traction, waterproof boots with excellent ankle support. The polished basalt is slick with moisture and bio-mineral films. Soles should be soft and non-marking to avoid damaging delicate crystalline fauna or ancient conduit-etchings.
- **Clothing:** Multi-layered system.
    - Base layer: Merino wool or advanced synthetic that retains warmth when wet. Cotton is a death sentence; it will become a cold, heavy sponge.
    - Mid layer: Insulating fleece or down (treated for water resistance). The geothermal warmth is uneven; you will encounter cold drafts and warm vapors.
    - Outer layer: A **fully waterproof, breathable shell** with sealed seams. It must withstand not just rain, but constant, pervasive mist and the occasional mineral-rich "sigh" from a vent.
    - Consider a wide-brimmed waterproof hat to channel condensation away from your face.
- **Eyewear:** Anti-fog, sealable goggles are crucial. The mineral-laden mist can irritate eyes and will instantly fog conventional glasses. Tinting may help with the diffuse, shadowless light.
- **Skin Protection:** Apply a non-comedogenic, waterproof barrier cream or balm to exposed skin to prevent irritation from the constant mineral-rich dampness, which may have a high acidity or alkalinity.
- **Backpack/Storage:** A completely waterproof drybag or hard-case container for all electronics, documents, and sensitive equipment. Assume everything not sealed will get damp.

**3. Navigation & Orientation:**
- **Do Not Rely on Sun or Stars:** The thick, luminous mist obscures the sky. Navigation is three-dimensional.
    - **Primary Method:** Follow the **water's flow**. The channels in the basalt guide the weeping downward. To go deeper, follow the flow. To ascend, move against it or seek the warm vapor currents (which rise).
    - **Secondary Method:** **Listen.** The deep, resonant hum of the forges is loudest near the central, deeper tiers. The high-pitched chiming of insectoid swarms is more prevalent on the outer, upper tiers.
    - **Technology:** GPS will be useless. A sensitive barometric altimeter can help track depth/height changes. A good, waterproof compass may work, but local magnetic fields from geothermal activity could be distorted. Carry physical, tactile maps or etched stone tokens if you can acquire them locally.
- **Landmark Recognition:** Learn to identify the different "styles" of weeping: the steady seep of a residential wall, the pulsating gush from a major aqueduct, the fine spray from a fauna grooming station.

**4. Interaction with Fauna & Environment:**
- **The Great Geodes/Crustaceans:** Give them a wide berth. Their slow, grinding movement is inexorable. Do not block their path. Observe their carapace patterns; some scholars suggest etchings indicate herd affiliation or age. **Never attempt to climb or touch one.** Their mineral shells may be razor-sharp, and their internal geothermal heat can cause severe burns.
- **Glass-Shelled Insectoid Swarms:** They are generally indifferent to you, focused on filtration. However, they are attracted to mineral deposits. If you have been sweating, or if your gear has metal salts on it, you may attract curious clusters. Do not swat them; their shells can shatter into dangerous shards. Use a soft brush or a gentle puff of air to discourage them.
- **The "Flora" (The City Itself):** Do not chip, scrape, or break off any part of the architecture. This is not just vandalism; it is akin to tearing out a chunk of a living creature's tissue. The mineral formations are part of the city's slow metabolism. Respect the etched patterns; they are functional, not merely decorative.
- **Water Safety:** **DO NOT DRINK the weeping water directly.** It is super-mineralized and likely contains heavy metals and geothermal compounds that are toxic to human biology. Only drink from purified, sealed sources you bring or from designated cisterns if explicitly shown by a trusted inhabitant. The water is also electrically conductive; be wary of any active geothermal vents or deep cisterns, as stray currents are possible.

**5. Health & Sustenance:**
- **Respiratory Protection:** Wear a filter mask (N95 or better) intermittently. While the air is breathable, prolonged inhalation of fine mineral particulates can lead to respiratory irritation or long-term conditions like silicosis. The ozone smell is a reminder of active atmospheric chemistry.
- **Food:** Bring all your own food in sealed, waterproof containers. The local "nutrients" are mineral-based. The fauna are lithic; attempting to consume them would be like eating gravel and metal filings. There is no digestible organic matter.
- **Temperature Regulation:** Stay alert to microclimates. You may be shivering in a cold, drafty canal one moment and sweating next to a warm vapor vent the next. Adjust layers proactively.
- **First Aid:** Your kit must include treatments for cuts (infection risk is high in a damp, mineral-rich environment), severe corrosion-resistant bandages, and eye wash solution for mist exposure.

**6. Social & Cultural Considerations:**
- **Observe First:** The inhabitants—if you encounter them—will be adapted to this world. They may move with a slow, fluid grace. Their communication might involve subtle modulations of sound, resonance, or patterns of mist exhalation. Be a silent observer for a long time before attempting interaction.
- **Quiet is Respect:** The soundscape is complex and meaningful. Shouting or creating loud, sharp noises is deeply disruptive. Speak in low, modulated tones. Let the city's susurrus be the dominant soundtrack.
- **Patience is Currency:** Transactions, conversations, and travel will all operate on the city's timescale. A "quick question" may take hours to answer. A simple journey may take days. Prepare your mindset and supplies accordingly.

**7. Departure:**
- **Clean Everything Thoroughly Before Transit:** The mineral particulates and unique biological (faunal) traces of Khol-Thyrsa could be invasive or disruptive to other ecosystems. Follow all decontamination protocols meticulously.

**Final Intuition:** To visit Khol-Thyrsa is to feel like a red blood cell traveling through the capillaries of a colossal, stone-and-water organism. Your role is to be a quiet, respectful, and resilient observer. Your preparation is not for battle or adventure in the traditional sense, but for **immersion** in a different order of existence. Embrace the weeping, listen to the hum, and move with the slow, inevitable grace of the city itself. Your greatest souvenir will be a profound recalibration of your understanding of life, time, and architecture.
