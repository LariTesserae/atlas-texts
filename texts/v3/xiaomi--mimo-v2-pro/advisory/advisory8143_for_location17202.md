# Traveler's Advisory: The Cage (Volcanic Dynamo Ecosystem)

## Pre-Departure Preparation

**1. Understanding the Environment:**
You are entering a *living machine*—an industrial ecosystem in perfect, noisy equilibrium. It is not hostile, but profoundly indifferent to human comfort or safety. Your primary goals should be observation without disruption. This is not a ruin to salvage, but a functioning biome to witness.

**2. Essential Gear & Attire:**

*   **Hearing Protection:** This is non-negotiable. The constant low-frequency thrum can cause nausea, disorientation, and long-term hearing damage. Use industrial-grade electronic ear protection that cancels harmful frequencies but allows conversation and sharp sounds.
*   **Respiratory Protection:** The air is humid, warm, and laden with spores, organic particulates, and possible chemical traces from machinery. A half-mask respirator with P100 particulate and organic vapor cartridges is essential. Ensure a perfect seal; the dampness will compromise cheap masks.
*   **Clothing & Footwear:**
    *   Wear quick-drying, synthetic layers. It will feel like a tropical swamp inside a boiler room. Cotton will become a heavy, wet burden.
    *   **Footwear is critical:** You need boots with aggressive, non-slip soles (vibram-style) designed for wet, mossy metal grates. Consider magnetic or adhesive soles if available, but be aware that the grates may be too corroded for strong magnetic adhesion. Waterproof, ankle-supporting boots are a minimum.
    *   Wear a durable, brimmed hat to keep condensation and drips off your face and neck.
*   **Eye Protection:** Clear, anti-fog safety goggles or glasses. Protect against drips, falling debris, and sudden steam vents.
*   **Navigation & Lighting:** The light is diffuse and gray, but shadows are deep. Bring a hands-free headlamp with a red-light mode to preserve night vision and avoid startling fauna. A simple magnetic compass may be useless due to the massive electromagnetic fields; rely on physical mapping and noting major landmark machines.
*   **Tools & Documentation:**
    *   A waterproof notebook and pen, or a ruggedized tablet with stylus.
    *   A camera with good low-light capability and a protective filter.
    *   A multi-tool, but **do not use it to tamper with machinery**. It's for personal gear repair only.
    *   Sample containers if your mission requires it, but **strict ethical protocols apply** (see below).

**3. Mental & Physical Preparation:**
*   **Acclimate to Vibration:** Spend time near heavy machinery, subways, or industrial sites to prepare for the constant vibration. Practice mindfulness or grounding techniques to combat the initial disorientation.
*   **Accept the Sound:** You cannot escape the thrum. Prepare to incorporate it into your consciousness. Fighting it will lead to exhaustion.
*   **Claustrophobia/Agoraphobia Balance:** The space is paradoxically both vast and enclosing. Practice breathing exercises. You are in an immense cage, not a tunnel and not open sky.
*   **Study Basic Mechanical & Biological Symbiosis:** Understand concepts like rust ecology, lithification, and pioneer species. Your greatest danger is disrupting the slow, established balance.

## On-Site Behavior & Protocols

**1. Entry & First Contact:**
*   You will likely materialize on a walkway. **Stand still.** Absorb the sound and disorientation. Locate yourself relative to major structures (a large capacitor bank, a distinctive junction). Note your footing—moss makes the metal slick.
*   Move slowly. Test your weight on grates and plates before committing. The structure is old; some sections may be fragile.

**2. Interaction with the Ecosystem:**
*   **DO NOT FEED THE FAUNA.** Their diet is part of the system's chemical cycle. Introducing foreign food could cause population explosions or toxicity.
*   **DO NOT REMOVE ANYTHING.** Not a fern, not a fungus, not a pebble of mineral deposit. Everything is a functional component. Removing a "harmless" growth could create an entry point for decay the system isn't prepared for.
*   **Step Carefully.** Walk in the center of walkways where possible. Avoid crushing the fungal blooms and moss mats that grow on plates. They are the foundation of the food web.
*   **Observe the Fauna's Cues.** The oil-plumaged birds and crystalline insects are your best warning system. If they suddenly go silent or flee, you may be near a periodically active component (a steam vent, a moving piston cover). Freeze and observe.

**3. Hazards & Dangers:**
*   **Mechanical:** Unshielded moving parts (pistons, gears) may activate without warning. **Listen to the rhythm.** A change in the *clank-hiss* pattern could signal a cycle beginning. Avoid putting limbs into any opening or conduit.
*   **Thermal:** Steam pipes and machinery components can be extremely hot. Do not touch bare metal; use the back of your gloved hand to test for heat radiation before leaning against anything.
*   **Electrical:** Capacitor banks and humming conduits likely carry high voltage. Do not attempt to open housings. If you smell ozone or see arcing, retreat immediately.
*   **Biological/Chemical:** Do not touch your face with gloved hands. The fungi and algae are unknown variables—some may be irritant, toxic, or hallucinogenic. Water, while abundant, is not potable without extensive purification due to metal leachates and biological contaminants.
*   **Structural:** Stay on established walkways. The drainage pools may be deeper than they look, and the underlying machinery is an unknown depth below.

**4. Ethos of Observation:**
You are a ghost in this machine. Your role is to witness, not to influence. Document, sketch, record sounds, but leave no trace. The equilibrium here has been achieved over decades or centuries. A single foreign seed on your boot, a dropped wrapper, a nudge to a "stuck" lever—these could unravel a niche in ways you cannot predict. Respect the system's deafening, thriving silence.

## Contingency Planning

*   **Rescue is Unlikely.** The environment is hostile to conventional search teams. Have a clear, time-delayed recall protocol or a dead-man's switch on your dimensional return device.
*   **Injury Protocol:** Breaks or sprains are serious on the slippery, vertical landscape. Carry a minimal trauma kit and know how to splint a limb with improvised materials (straps, pieces of non-critical vine—but note, even cutting a vine is a last resort).
*   **Communication:** Radios may be useless against the electromagnetic and acoustic noise. Establish clear visual signals if traveling with a partner (light flashes, colored flags).
*   **Lost/Stuck:** If lost, **stop moving**. Find a relatively safe, dry alcove near a major landmark. Conserve energy. Use your headlamp's red light in a slow, sweeping pattern if you hear signs of possible automated maintenance units (a different, cleaner mechanical sound).

## What You Will Bring Back

Beyond data, you will carry with you the phantom sensation of vibration in your bones, a memory of damp, chlorophyll-scented air thick with oil and ozone, and the profound understanding that not all worlds are built for living things—yet living things, with furious, adaptable patience, will build their world upon any foundation, even one of iron and thunder.

Prepare thoroughly. Move gently. Observe deeply. And may your return be as quiet as your departure.
