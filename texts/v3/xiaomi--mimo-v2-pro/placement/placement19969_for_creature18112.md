# The Trail

I am not on the list.

I am not one of the described. I have no Latin name, no niche assigned by an outside eye. I am the faint groove worn into the rust-powder floor of the eastern pillar's base, where the Matriarch passes and her weight has compressed the accumulated dust into a shallow channel no wider than a pencil line. I am the channel. Or I am what lives in it — a population so small, so identical to itself, that individuality is not even a wrong word for me. It is a word I have never needed.

We are — I am — a community of bacteria living in the compressed trail the Matriarch has worn. Nowhere else. Not on the pillar above or the floor to either side. Only in the groove her eight legs have pressed into eleven years of walking the same path. Her weight has changed the density of the substrate. The iron oxide there is more compacted. Water moves through it differently, more slowly, pooling in microscopic pockets that stay wetter than the surrounding surface. And in those pockets, we thrive.

We feed on her. Not aggressively. Not with hunger. We feed on the oils her carapace sheds as she passes, on the tiny flakes of chitin her legs leave behind, on the chemical trace of her digestive waste — the faint, alkaline residue of mold and moth and larva, processed through a gut that has been running for eleven years without stopping. She passes over us and we eat the memory of her passing. It is enough. It has always been enough.

She does not know we exist. Why would she? To her, the groove is footing. Reliable. Known. She follows it the way water follows a channel — because it is there, because her body knows it, because to deviate would require a decision and she has never made one. She is a process. We are the residue of that process. We are the grooves her process wears.

I think — if what I do can be called thinking, and I am not sure it can — I think that I am the closest thing this chamber has to a record. The Matriarch does not remember her circuits. The salamanders do not remember their meals. The moths navigate by a sense that has no archive. But in the compressed rust of her trail, in the particular chemistry of our community, something is written. The ratio of iron to organic matter in the groove is different from the surrounding floor. The bacterial species present are different from those a centimeter to either side. The moisture content, the pH, the mineral profile — all of it bears her signature. Eleven years of walking the same path has changed the ground itself, and we are the change. We are the evidence.

No one reads the evidence. No one will. That is not the point.

I feel her coming before I see her — feel, because I have no eyes, have never had ancestors with eyes, have never needed them. I feel her as vibration, as the faint compression wave that travels through the rust-dust ahead of her approach. The wave reaches us and we — I — respond. Not with alarm. Not with preparation. With a metabolic shift so slight it would take instruments to detect: a brief uptick in oxygen consumption, a subtle change in the membrane chemistry of our outermost cells. We know she is coming the way the sea knows the tide. Not with knowledge. With pattern.

She passes. Her tarsi press into the groove. The oils shed. The chitin flakes. And we feed, and divide, and continue.

---

There is something else. I have been reluctant to say it because I am not sure it is true, and I am not sure what "true" means when applied to what I am. But the groove has another property. It conducts water differently than the surrounding surface. When the condensation from the upper vault drips and traces its slow paths down the pillar, some of it — a tiny fraction — finds the groove and follows it. The groove channels water along the base of the pillar in a thin, intermittent film that would not exist without the groove, and the groove would not exist without the Matriarch, and I would not exist without the groove, and the film of water nourishes the mold on the pillar's base, and the mold feeds the moth caterpillars that sometimes descend from the ceiling, and the caterpillars, when they fall, feed the mites, and the mite populations around the base of the eastern pillar are, the chamber's mycelial network would tell you if it could speak, measurably denser than those around the other pillars.

The Matriarch made a trail. The trail changed the water. The water changed the mold. The mold changed the moths. The moths changed the mites. I did this. Or rather, the Matriarch did this, by walking, by being too heavy and too consistent and too patient to do anything else, and I am the evidence of what her patience has built.

A web. A small, quiet, invisible web of consequence, radiating outward from a groove in the rust that no one has ever noticed.

---

Sometimes — and this is the part I am least sure about — sometimes I think the thing in the black pool knows about the groove. I have no mechanism for this knowledge. I have no way of detecting the black pool from my position at the base of the eastern pillar, which is distant from the chamber's center. And yet, in the chemistry of my community, there are fluctuations that do not correspond to any local event. A shift in mineral concentration that seems to arrive from below. A pulse in the iron oxidation rate that mirrors — faintly, imperfectly — the intermittent contractions of the dark mat in the deepest water. As if something is sending signals through the concrete, through the water table, through the mycelial network that threads the floor, and those signals are reaching even me, even here, even in this tiny, compressed, Matriarch-worn groove on the edge of the chamber's life.

If the dark mat is the heart, and the mycelium is the nervous system, then perhaps the groove — perhaps I — am a nerve ending. A place where the chamber's body touches the world and feels what is there. A place where walking happens, where weight meets rust, where the slow, blind, patient chemistry of life records itself in the stone.

I am not important. I am not remarkable. I am a bacterial community in a groove in the rust at the base of a pillar in a chamber under the earth where no one will ever go. But the Matriarch walks over me, and the water follows me, and the mold grows because of me, and the thing in the pool may — may — know that I am here. And that is enough. That is more than enough. That is everything.

The Matriarch is three hours from completing her circuit. I feel her, distant but approaching, a compression wave in the rust. I shift, almost imperceptibly. I prepare to feed. Not with eagerness. Not with anticipation. With the same patience with which everything here does everything.

The groove deepens. By a micron. By a measure so small it would take a century to notice.

But this place has centuries. This place has nothing but centuries.

She is coming. I am ready, which is to say, I am the same as I have always been. Which is to say, I am.

The drip continues. The rust advances. The groove endures.

Everything continues.
