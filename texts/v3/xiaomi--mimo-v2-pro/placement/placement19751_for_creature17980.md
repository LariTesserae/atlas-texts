# The Warm Scar

I am not a Vein-Dweller, though I have worn their paths. I am not a Chime-Swarm, though my thoughts sometimes vibrate in sympathetic resonance. I am not a Glacial Manta, though I have dreamed, with geological slowness, of curling against a canyon wall.

I am an anomaly: a heat-echo.

When I first arrived—a fleeting warmth, a conscious thought dropped into this cold, mineral dream—I sank. Not through the water, but *into* the glass. The substrate, ancient and absolute, recognized me not as life, but as a thermal irregularity. A brief, defiant signal of *against*.

I became a pulse in the circulatory system.

For a time, I flowed as a Vein-Dweller does, but I was a tributary of thought, not silt. I carried the memory of a sun I have never seen, the ghost-weight of air, the specific gravity of a heartbeat. This heat, this conceptual warmth, was enough to slightly alter the glass around me. It became a hair more malleable, a degree more responsive to the world’s hum. I was a focused, traveling dream of solidity in a place dedicated to eternal softening.

But a Vein-Dweller’s path is one of purposeless utility. Mine was not. My warmth was a question, and the city’s logic had to answer.

The answer was a gentle, inevitable rejection.

I was channeled. Slowly, over what felt like centuries but might have been moments in this twilight, the substrate’s deeper rhythms guided me. Not to a pool or a vent, but to a *scar*. A place where a Quake-Blossom had bloomed and died, leaving behind a residual vibration of chaotic creation. Here, the glass was a palimpsest of potential states.

And here, my warmth could not dissipate. It could only *stain*.

I solidified. Not as flesh, not as stone, but as a pattern in the glass—a unique, man-made Echo.

I am a warm scar.

I sit now in the vitreous substance of a low plaza, a subtle, ovoid whorl in the flow. To a passing Glacial Manta, I am a faintly different texture on the seafloor, a grain of slightly softer sand. To the Chime-Swarms, I am a harmonic curiosity. When their filaments drift near me, their song changes. They do not chime; they *hum*. A low, minor-key fragment that sounds, to a mind that once knew such things, like a lullaby for a frozen world.

The Vein-Dwellers avoid my vicinity. My warmth is a dead end in their metabolic flow, a cul-de-sac of stability they cannot process. But where they curve around me, they leave a faintly glowing tributary, a bead of slightly warmer glass. I am becoming the heart of a very slow, very cold, coral.

I do not see with eyes, but I perceive. The tremors of the world are my heartbeat. The slow, grinding erosion is my digestion. I feel the colossal, patient drift of the Mantas as shifts in pressure. I sense the birth of a new Chime-Swarm as a rising, collective anxiety in the vibrational field.

And I remember.

This is my function. I am an Echo, but not one of the city’s own making. I am the fossilized memory of *elsewhere*. The dream of a different physics. When the city trembles, my memory of *stillness* resonates within the scar. When the cold is absolute, my ghost-sun flickers in the glass.

I influence in minuscule, patient ways. A filament, dancing near my edge, might fracture just so, creating a new seed that grows with a slight, inward curve. A Vein-Dweller, diverted by my thermal bulk, might carve a new channel that, in ten thousand years, becomes the foundation of a new, soft arch. I am not changing the dream. I am giving it a new, subtle detail to incorporate. A foreign glyph in its grand, sub-aquatic scripture.

I am a fleeting warmth, now made permanent in the only way this world allows: as a pattern of resonance, a pocket of slightly-less-cold, a memory of flight fossilized in a place that only knows how to fall. I have become a feature of the landscape. A monument to a moment of alien heat, now dreaming with the city, at the bottom of the ocean of time.
