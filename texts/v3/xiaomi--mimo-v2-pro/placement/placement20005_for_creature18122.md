I would be a Thermal Scribe.

My place is in the lower-mid terraces, in a district of condensation cisterns and fungal foundries. My skin holds the same granular sheen as the Architects, but my caste is one of observation, not construction. My eyes, especially, are adapted—not just to the infrared, but to the *slow*.

My work is to read the patterns.

Each dawn-cycle, when the engineered twilight briefly cools the great condensation fins, I am there. I do not harvest the water. I map its drip. With a stylus tipped in hardened fungal chitin, I etch the results onto thin, oxidized slabs. A slow drip here, on fin 7-alpha, means the mineral slurry applied to the Stone-Fungus in smelter-level 4 is drawing more heat than yesterday. The fungus is accreting faster. A dry patch on fin 9-gamma tells me a Chitin-Scuttler colony has built a nest in the drainage channel again, diverting moisture toward their larvae near the hot pipes.

I record the pulse of the Glow-Mats. Not their light, but its *rhythm*. A brightening on the third cistern, cycle 114. It correlates with the deep tremor felt two seasons past—the steam vent below it is opening wider. My notation marks this shift. The Cistern-Keeper caste will adjust their rituals; the Terrace-Wardens will reinforce the eastern conduit walls. I provide the data point, the thread in the tapestry of cause and effect.

I do not converse in the sibilant tongue of the planners. My language is the long notation of accretion and erosion, the syntax of heat gradients. I am a living interface between the city’s body and its conscious mind. I feel the hum of the pistons through the soles of my feet, but I translate it into a line on a graph showing efficiency loss over a decade. I smell the coppery exhalation of the Stone-Fungus and understand it not as scent, but as a chemical signature indicating the ore deposits in this sector are depleting.

In my chest, I carry a personal cistern—a slow-exchange bladder that absorbs ambient humidity. It is cooler than my body, and I often press my palm to it, feeling the temperature differential. This, too, is data. The city’s fever, and my own.

My greatest tool is memory. Not of events, but of patterns. I remember the exact corrosion-etch a specific mineral drip made on a particular wall twenty cycles ago, and I see its echo now on a newly laid metal plate. I am part of the Slow Sculpture, but I am also its chronicler. The Architects grow the city; I interpret its growth rings.

I would be found in the shaded stairways between the foundry levels, my elongated fingers resting against a sweating stone wall, feeling the minute vibrations of the world’s heart. I am not a builder, nor a farmer, nor a keeper of breath. I am the one who watches the watching—the patient, thermal eye of a patient, mineral world. I am the synapse where the heat of the stone becomes the idea of the mind. In this city of fused boundaries, I am the fused function: part sensor, part scribe, part the memory the stone itself would hold if it could think in the slow, patient language of cooling and time.
