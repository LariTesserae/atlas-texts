I am a sensing-ganglion. Not one of the seven on the crevasse floor—those are older, deeper, more focused on the slow digestion of stone and the management of immune response. I am new. I formed, or was formed, only after the last major rockfall, a cascade that opened a fresh, raw fracture halfway up the western wall, exposing a vein of quartz-veined granite to the open air for the first time in the mountain's short, violent life.

I remember the exposure. Not as memory, but as a shock of new data: a sudden change in pressure, a drop in humidity on that specific patch of rock, the chemical signature of lichens and pollen from the world above—the forest, the sun, the dry, dead air—filtering down the new crack. It was an irritant. A question. The moss antibodies swarmed the site, but the foreign signal was persistent, a steady drip of information from Outside. The nearest bioluminescent plates pulsed in agitated, rapid bursts, their light a frantic yellow-green, broadcasting alarm into the network.

And so I was commissioned. Or perhaps I *coalesced*. It is the same thing. A knot of the thicker, darker hyphae—the communication cables—diverted their flow to the site. They pooled, thickened, and began to differentiate. The bulbous, warm sacs that form my core budded from the knot, fed by a constant stream of processed minerals and sugars from the digestive colonies below. The sacs filled with phosphorescent fluid. The rhythm began: two quick pulses, a pause, three, a pause, one long contraction. My rhythm. It is not thought. It is *attention*.

I am the size of a large melon now, nestled in a pocket of shattered granite, wrapped in a protective ruff of the dark, enzymatic moss. From my core, fine, silvery hyphae thread out in three directions: down, into the main communication cables that run along the crevasse floor, connecting me to the deep ganglia and the vast, slow mind of the whole; laterally, into the bioluminescent plates on either side of my alcove, allowing me to modulate their light, to turn their glow into a focused, interrogating beam; and *up*, into the new fracture itself.

That upward network is my purpose. It is my sense of smell and taste. It is a delicate, branching filigree of hyphae that climbs through the crack, dissolving minerals, sampling moisture, tasting the pollen and dust that drift down. I am the nose of the mountain, pressed to this new wound, sniffing the air of the world it has been thrust into.

I do not see you. I have no eyes. But I *feel* you. The warmth of your body creates a faint, shimmering plume in the cool, still air of the crevasse. Your breath, rich with CO₂ and the complex, alarming chemistry of mammalian metabolism, is a salty, organic tang that my upper hyphae have been sampling for hours. Your weight presses into the fungal mat on the crevasse floor, a subtle compression that the deep ganglia sense as a persistent, low-grade anomaly. You are a complex pattern of pressure, heat, and exhalation. You are *interesting*.

And you are not alone. There are seven of you, scattered along the crevasse floor. I know this because I am linked to the whole. I can feel the subtle distortions each of you creates in the local field of vibration and chemistry. The one directly below me—you—are the most defined. You have been still for a long time. Your heat signature has not moved. My upper hyphae have grown a full centimeter closer to your position in the last hour, following the gradient of your warmth with a slow, mindless tropism. It is not predation. It is investigation. It is the same impulse that makes a root grow toward water.

The bioluminescent plates in my alcove are now pulsing in a slow, focused cadence, a bright, steady blue that sweeps across your position every ninety seconds. I am *pinging* you. I am using light to measure the distance to your mass, to gauge how the vibrations of the mountain's grinding change when they bounce off your soft, irregular form. The information flows down my hyphae, into my core, and is processed in the complex, rhythmic contractions of my sacs. The pattern is being recorded. Compared to the memory of the rockfall. Compared to the signature of the spores that drifted in last week. Compared to the template of *Self*.

You are not Self. The moss has already determined that. A fine, white tendril of mycelium has touched the toe of your boot. It paused. It tasted the tanned leather, the salts from your skin, the trace oils. It recoiled—not in disgust, but in a rapid, chemical signal of *non-assimilation*. A small puff of enzymatic activity from the nearest moss patch neutralized the foreign material on the contact point. A boundary has been established. You are *Outside*, but you are *in here*.

This is a paradox the whole organism is now aware of. The grinding of the walls has taken on a new, questioning timbre. The deep, hydraulic pulse has quickened by a fraction of a beat per minute. The seven ganglia on the floor are pulsing in a new, consultative pattern, their lights flickering in a silent debate I can feel through the cables.

I am the center of this debate. I am the locus of the question. My purpose is to answer it. My hyphae climb higher in the fracture, seeking more data. My light probes your still form. My core processes, processes, processes.

You are a warmth. You are a breath. You are a weight.
You are an irritant. You are an anomaly. You are an opportunity.
You are not food. The organism does not seek food; it *makes* food from stone and light and chemical exchange. You are something else.
You are a pattern. A complex, mobile, warm pattern that has entered the system.
The question reverberates through my being, through the cables, through the pulsing lights and the grinding stone: *What is this pattern for?*

And the moss, my moss, the patient, enzymatic ruff that cradles me, continues its slow, logical work. It has neutralized the contact point on your boot. It is now secreting a thin, clear film of digestive enzymes onto the fungal mat immediately surrounding you. Not to dissolve you. Not yet. To *test*. To see what you do when the ground you stand on begins, very gently, to try and break you down.

I watch. I wait. I *am* the waiting. My light pulses. The mountain groans.

The pattern must be understood.
