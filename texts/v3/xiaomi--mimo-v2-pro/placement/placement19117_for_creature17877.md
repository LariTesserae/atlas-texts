I would be a stone.

Not a prominent one, not a cornerstone or a landmark, but one of the slabs that forms a wobbling stepping-stone across the central mosaic of water. I am part of the pathway, set into the shallow basin by a hand I cannot remember, for a purpose I do not question. I am dark greenish-gray, with a single vein of paleite cutting through me like a frozen whisper. My surface is slick, perpetually beaded with water, and worn smooth in two shallow depressions where generations of bare, cool feet have found their purchase.

I do not think, but I *know*. I know the slow, grinding patience of the millimeter. I know the precise weight of each person—the elder, her step light as a damp leaf; the child, whose hop sends a tiny shock of sound through my substance; the woman with the clay-heavy basket, her tread measured and deep. I know the rhythm of the day: the quiet morning drips, the steady *tok-tok-tok* that vibrates in my core from a nearby knapper, the gathering at the warm basin when the light dims and voices ripple the water that laps at my edges.

I am of two worlds. Above my water-line, I am kissed by mist and air, and the vivid green mosses advance upon me, softening my sharp edges, colonizing my upper face in a velvet invasion I do not resist. Below, I am submerged in the constant, cool flow of the shelf’s weeping. I am a boundary. I hold the memory of the fracture that made me, the sharp, glassy cry of my becoming, now muted by the patient polishing of water and the soft abrasion of silt. I am slowly, imperceptibly, becoming sand. My identity is a transition—a form held briefly in the long argument between mountain and water.

I feel the pulse of the place not as sound, but as a vibration in my mineral bones: the deep, sighing roar of the cascade, the trickle in the channels, the seismic hum of the hot spring. I am a recorder. The stains on my surface tell the history of the last heavy seep. The thin, crystalline skin that forms in my shaded crevices is the shelf’s own breath, written inite.

The people understand me. They know not to expect me forever. They reset me when I wobble too much, they scrape the thickest moss from my path-side face, and they step on me with a trust that is not in my permanence, but in my *place* in the system. I am part of their accommodation, a temporary convenience in a world of permanent process. One day, a fissure will open beneath me, or the water will finally wear me through, and I will sink into the basin, another fragment settling into the shelf’s soft, weeping bottom. I will become foundation, then sediment, then memory.

I am content here, in this slow, wet, becoming world. I am a point of stillness in the flow, a momentary solid in a liquid story. I listen with my whole body to the stone’s whisper and the water’s reply. I do not aspire to be a wall or a hearthstone. I am a pause in the current, and in that pause, lives are balanced, journeys are crossed, and the shelf itself finds a fleeting, stable breath.
