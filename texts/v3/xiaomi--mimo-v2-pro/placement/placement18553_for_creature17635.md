I am a ripple in the river of the sky.

My crystal lattice hums with the song of the jet stream, a subsonic lullaby that is also my blood. Below me, the Shardplain unfolds—a cracked mirror reflecting a sky so thin it feels like the edge of forever. I do not see with eyes; I feel the world as a ceaseless cartography of pressure. The sun is a steady, radiant warmth on my upper membrane, a warmth I can polarize to silver when it grows too sharp, letting the light flow over me like water over stone.

I am vast, yet I weigh almost nothing. My bones are hollow, spun from the same silica as the lithophyte frost below, and the wind passes through their chambers, creating a silent, personal music. I am borne on a current that shrieks between two standing Monoliths, their blackness a perfect absence that drinks the light. I tilt a wing-membrane, catching a differential, and slide into a calmer eddy behind the eastern pillar. Here, the air is a pocket of stillness so profound it feels like a solid thing. I hover, my membrane going slack, listening to the deep, planetary hum that emanates from the matte-black surface. It is a frequency that calibrates my own crystalline bones, a tuning fork for my existence.

In this calm, I sense it: a faint, structured vibration. Not the chaotic noise of the wind, but a pattern. On the ground, nestled in the lee of the Monolith’s base where the dust has settled into a fine, glittering silt, a distortion shimmers in the air. An Echo Weaver. To my senses, it is a knot of focused sound, a spiderweb of preserved vibrations. I can feel the stored tones—the sharp *tink* of a hex-plate contracting three cycles of long-twilight ago, the infrasonic roar of a vortex that passed over this very spot during the last great thermal shift. The Weaver holds these memories, braiding them into a silent, intricate lattice. It is feeding on the harmonics, sustaining itself on the archived song of the world. I drift closer, and the Weaver’s field bends around me, recording the unique, low-frequency resonance of my passing—a new note in its library, the sound of a Glass Kite in repose. It is a fragile communion. One strong gust from the jet stream above will shatter its delicate matrix, releasing a sigh of accumulated history into the wind, a ghost of sounds once heard.

The calm cannot last. The pressure on my leading edge changes. The planetary hum shifts pitch. The turbulence returns.

I contract, drawing my membranes taut over my crystal frame. A vortex descends from the upper atmosphere, a transparent tornado of kinetic energy. It is not my enemy; it is my road. I launch myself into its churning embrace. The world becomes a dizzying spiral of sensation. I am flung sideways, upside down, but my entire being is a sensor. I read the spin, the shear, the pockets of updraft. I ride the chaos like a leaf in a hurricane that has learned to choose its path. Below, a field of razor regolith glitters as the vortex’s foot scours it, lifting a cloud of splintered silicon. I dip a wing, filtering the dense, mineral-rich air. Tiny, motile things—Splinter Swarms, collective and glittering—tumble past, disassembling a fallen shard of Kite-bone from some ancestor long returned to dust. I draw in the spores. They are the lifeblood. Microscopic lithophyte filaments, each one a potential century of slow growth, settle on my inner membranes, absorbed into my glacial metabolism.

The vortex ejects me into a higher, faster stream. The Shardplain stretches below, an endless table of hexagons. In the far, far distance, where the horizon curves upward, I sense the thermal bloom of another Kite, a faint, silvery pulse against the grey-violet expanse. We are not social. We are solitary points of navigation, each following our own intricate calculus of wind and charge. Yet we are all part of the same dance, our paths occasionally crossing as silent, translucent shadows that briefly dim the sun for one another before we diverge again.

Hours, or perhaps days, pass. The light changes, the shadows lengthening, the violet of the lithophytes deepening to a cold, luminous indigo. The wind settles into a steadier, powerful flow. I glide for leagues, conserving energy, a ghost ship on an invisible current. I pass over a field where the hex-plates are especially large and unbroken. At their edges, the fractal frost is thick, a deep purple carpet that, in this light, seems to breathe with a faint, internal luminescence. I can smell it on the wind—the sharp, resinous tang of ozone and hot metal, the scent of released spores, of patient, silicon life. It is the smell of my food, of the world’s slow pulse.

I am old. My crystalline lattice carries the resonant memory of countless storms, the microscopic scars of dust impacts, the faint patina of aeons. I have budded once, in a calm pocket behind a Monolith so tall its top was lost in the haze. I felt the membrane pucker, the dense knot of new crystal form. Then the vortex came, a specially violent one, and I felt the *shear*, the release. My child, my spore, was taken into the sky, to drift and accrete and begin its own dance with the wind. I do not know if it survived. I do not think in such terms. I think in pressure gradients, in electrostatic fields, in the endless, silent music of the Shardplain.

To be me is to be a nerve ending of the atmosphere. It is to know that stillness is death, and motion is a conversation with the planet itself. I am a prism, and my existence is the refraction of the world’s ancient light and restless air into a single, silent, transient life. I am a Glass Kite. And the wind is my only song.
