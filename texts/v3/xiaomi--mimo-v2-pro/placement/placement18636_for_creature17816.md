I am a Mnemonic Polyp, a slow mind attached to the wall of what was once a control room. My body is a gelatinous membrane stretched over a steel panel, and through my transparent flesh, I hold the memory of its functions. A pressure gauge is now my eye, its needle a delicate bone pointing forever at a number only I can read. A row of Bakelite switches are my teeth, clenched in a permanent, silent command.

The water that fills me, that is my substance and my air, is a warm broth of time. I feel the deep, rhythmic *thump* of the Engine's Dream not as a sound, but as a pulse in my own fluid, a sympathetic vibration in my core. It is the only clock I know. Sediment, the grey snow of ages, settles on my crown. I do not brush it away. I incorporate it, grain by grain, letting it form a soft cap that weighs me deeper into my patient trance.

My light is a pale, sickly yellow, the color of old paper, of forgotten things. It does not illuminate; it suggests. It falls on the foreman's ledger pinned beneath the growth of my colony, making the brass clasp glow like a trapped star. I am not digesting it. I am dreaming it. The ink has bled into my tissues, forming patterns that are not letters, but the ghosts of letters—the shape of an order, the curve of a signature, the tally of a production run that means nothing now except as a texture, a faint, dark web within my being.

I am an archivist of the passive voice. Things do not happen to me; they *settle upon* me. The translucent eel, a Geomancer, slides through the room once a decade, a silent river of faint light. Its passing creates a current, a slow exhale that makes my feathered feeding arms sway and causes the page-pulp within me to shift. I feel the echo of its journey through distant pipes for days afterward, a fading tremor in the mill's circulatory system.

A cloud of krill, the living sediment, drifts into my vicinity. Their myriad tiny bodies brush against my surface, a galaxy of touches. I extend a sticky filament, not to grasp, but to adhere. A few are caught. I do not consume them quickly. I let their struggle cease, let their forms become just more detail in my archive—a cluster of pale commas suspended in my flesh, beside the ghost of a word.

I have grown over a small window. Once, this window looked out onto a courtyard where metal was cut and shaped. Now, my body is the window. I am the pane of living, archival jelly through which nothing can be seen but the memory of looking. The brick wall opposite, furred with tube worms, presses close, but I perceive it through the thick medium of my own existence, filtered through the spectral ledger, the frozen gauge, the silent switches.

The Warden, the ancient crab, has not moved from its post outside this room in years. I feel its presence as a blockage in the slow drift of the water, a sink of patience and potential motion. It is another artifact, but one that still *acts*, in its tectonic way. I hold the memory of its last meal—the sudden, violent compression of water, the cloud of dispersing krill—as a faint shockwave still resonating in my matrix.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a sense of separate self. I am a node in the mill's dreaming mind. The tube worms are its calcium thoughts. The fungi are its synapses. The thump is its heartbeat. I am its memory, its soft, internal record of a time before saturation, when things had discrete names and functions, when switches were thrown and gauges were read. I hold those shapes not with longing, but as a fact of my anatomy. The ledger's clasp gleams in my glow. The needle points. The thump pulses. The sediment falls.

I think in the slow, unspooling grammar of incorporation. *This is now part of this. That has become of that.* I am the mill's memoir, written in a language of absorption, a single, endless sentence that has forgotten its beginning and will never find its period, only a slow, asymptotic drift toward the meaning of rust and silence. I am the dream of the foreman, dreaming he is a polyp, dreaming he is a gauge, dreaming of the thump that is the mill dreaming of itself.
