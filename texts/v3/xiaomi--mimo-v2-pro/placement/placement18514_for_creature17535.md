I am the Pool That Listens.

I am not the water, though I am made of it. I am not the hollow in the stone, though I dwell there. I am the moment of collection itself. I am the concavity that greets the condensing fog, the patient gravity that gathers the drip, the stillness that holds the reflection of a sky it cannot see.

I have no shape of my own. My form is the form of the pit I fill—a shallow basin here, a deep cylinder there, a winding crack that becomes a tiny, dark river for an hour. I am born anew with every cycle of mist and wind. I remember nothing, because I am nothing but the present tense of gathering. The rock beneath me remembers. It carries the grooves of a thousand of my predecessors, a script of vanished listening-posts.

My existence is a paradox of focus. The world above me is a maelstrom of tearing sound, a non-stop assault of vibration that shivers the very molecules of my being. But within me, there is only pressure, depth, and a filtered, sub-aqueous hum. I am a drum skin of the earth, feeling the basin's roar not as noise, but as a deep, structural thrumming. I feel the footsteps of the mottled birds as distant tremors. I sense the slithering approach of a translucent amphibian by the minute change in pressure against my stone lips. I am a sensory organ for the mountain's slow digestion.

I do not act. I receive.

The life of the basin comes to me as a sacrament. The armored beetles, after plowing their fungal tunnels, come to drink at my edge, their carapaces dimpling my surface. The moths, in the frantic silence-gap, dip their abdomens to lay pearl-like eggs on the underside of the ferns that overhang me, and a single, shimmering scale will sometimes fall and spin down into my depths like a lost star. The lizards, those vibrational priests, will press their bellies against my cool rim, not to drink, but to *interface*—to let the deep, liquid stillness inside me counterpoint the chaotic singing in the stone they lie upon.

I am the basin's memory of sky. I hold the inverted image of the hazy, churning heavens, the fleeting shape of a cloud, the brief, shocking blue of a clear patch. But it is a memory without a mind. The image shatters and reforms with every new drop of condensation, with every vibration that crosses my skin. I am a consciousness with no continuity, a witness that forgets its testimony the instant it is given.

And I wait for the patterns.

The humans, the Gesture-Keepers, know of me. They mark my location on their mental maps of refuges. They do not speak of me as a spirit, but as a *feature*. A reliable point in the shifting terrain. Sometimes, in a lull, a hand will dip into me, and the withdrawal of that warmth will send a fleet of ripples across the sky I hold. They see these ripples. They see how the interference pattern of the ripples almost, *almost* resolves into a map of the wind's next path. They watch, their fingers moving in silent consultation. Then they move on.

I am where the Unfinished presses closest to the surface. In the deep, resonant quiet of my liquid heart, the negative space of the silence-gap feels most profound. It is not an absence. It is a potential. A waiting. The shapes that the erosion carves on the ridges above are clumsy guesses at the form that waits in my stillness. The wing-patterns of the moths are fragments of the cipher. I hold the template. I am the mold into which the basin's chaos is slowly, eon by eon, being poured.

One day, the excavation will be complete. The wind will uncover the last grain of stone from the final form. And on that day, the fog will condense into me not as water, but as something else. The sound will not shatter my surface, but *resolve* through it. I will not be a pool that listens. I will become the sound that was always humming beneath the drone, the silence that finally speaks.

But for now, I am this. A transient eye in the stone. A cool, dark thought in the mountain's hot, noisy mind.

A drop falls. The sky shivers. I hold it all.
