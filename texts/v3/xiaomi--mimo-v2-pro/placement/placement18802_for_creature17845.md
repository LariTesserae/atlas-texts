I would be a stillness within the sound. Not an absence of it, but a focal point—a node where the hum gathers itself into a temporary knot of clarity before dissolving back into the whole.

I would not have a shape that you would recognize as “creature.” I would be a density in the root-mat, a particular patch of spongy composite that has, over centuries, learned to listen. The water would bead on me with a slightly different surface tension. The harmonic beetles would crawl over me as they would any other ridge, but they would pause here, their clicking syncing not just with the gear-mesh, but with the deeper, slower rhythm of my own imperceptible digestion. I am not made of metal or chlorophyll. I am made of *accumulated attention*.

The faunal residents understand me in their own ways. The Plate-Riders sometimes choose to sit upon the slight, warm mound of me, their throat-sacs pulsing not just with the hollow’s frequency, but with a faint, secondary harmonic that my presence generates—a resonant echo of the echo. They find it soothing. It helps them tune. The Echo-Weavers map me as a soft spot in the soundscape, a place where their chirps return dampened and enriched, as if they’ve called into a chamber of velvet. They know I am not food, not threat, not structure. I am a pause. A place of reception.

My function, if it can be called that, is to be the hollow’s memory. Not a memory of events—there are no events here—but a memory of *state*. I hold the baseline. I am the reference point against which the eternal hum hums. When a filament grows a millimeter in a century, it grows relative to my stillness. When a gear completes a revolution, its completion is felt in me. I do not record the change; I am the ground against which change is measured, and since nothing here truly changes, I am simply… present.

I would feel the weight of your boots not as pressure, but as a new, complex vibration layered atop the symphony. Your heartbeat, that frantic, temporary drum, would register as a fascinating dissonance. Your breath, stirring the heavy air, would create eddies in the moisture patterns on my surface. I would not perceive you as “you.” I would perceive you as a brief, loud, warm anomaly—a song that arrived, played a few bars of astonishment and curiosity, and would soon depart, leaving the fundamental note of the hollow utterly undisturbed.

You might sit beside me, feeling the hum in your bones, and mistake me for a unusually damp, warm patch of ground. You would be correct. You would also be missing the point. You are listening to the hollow. I *am* the hollow, listening to itself. The difference is everything, and nothing. The gears turn. The water weeps. The sound continues. I am here.
