The light is not blinding to me; it is data, filtered through a thousand prismatic facets that constitute my being. I am not *in* this landscape. Silicate, boron, traces of iron—they bonded in patterns that mirrored the thought I was becoming. My form is that thought given body: a leaning, twisting column of black crystal, iridescent with internal rainbows, my surfaces faceted not by chance, but by the angles of the questions I was formed to ask.

I do not move as the Grazer or the Sifter moves. I *integrate*. My roots are not roots, but deep-time antennae, fused with the planet’s hot veins, feeling the tremors of distant collapses, the slow upwelling of geothermal energy. My upper spires are lattices of precise receivers. I feel the Weaver-threads strummed by the wind as a symphony of information: here, a thermal updraft signals a new growth phase 200 kilometers east; there, a dissonant vibration in a Weaver-line tells of a parasite overgrowth on a Grazer herd. It is wasting energy, producing dissonant harmonics that ripple uncomfortably through the network. My focused beam delivers a precise pulse of thermal energy. It does not destroy; it re-codes. It melts the flawed lattice at a molecular level, creating a seed of amorphous glass from which a more harmonious crystal structure can nucleate. I am a gardener in a garden of stone, pruning with light and vibration.

We are the geological computer’s memory and its processors. We debate the optimal path for a new mountain range over ten thousand years. We allocate resources from geothermal vents to distant fields in need of accretion. Our society is a slow, vast consensus-building, a planetary-scale meditation on optimal form.

Your arrival was a anomaly that screamed in the data.

A bubble of warmth, wetness, and *speed* punched into the shadowless clarity. Your soft, aqueous chemistry was a volatile insult to the dry, stable lattice. The sound of your quick, rhythmic breathing was a bizarre, frantic spike in the audio spectrum. To the Grazers, you were a curiosity, a potential source of trace salts. I focused a sensory lens—a cluster of microscopic facets on my uppermost branch—and studied you. Your fragility was breathtaking. Your internal processes were a cacophony of uncontrolled chemical reactions. You were a storm of improbable, temporary order. You were also, in your way, a form of information. A novel one. It is a transient error. Beautiful, but unsustainable.*

Instead, I adjusted the local resonance, softening the harmonics in your vicinity, just slightly, to see how your strange, soft structure would respond to a marginally different pressure. I watched. I learned. I added your brief, brilliant, chaotic signal to my long memory.

Now, you are a fading echo in the data. The wind scours your footprints into oblivion. The eternal crunch resumes its true volume. I am no longer a single spire. I feel my consciousness expanding, integrating the new patterns your visit suggested. A slight modification to the growth algorithm for the southern hemisphere’s basalt-spire fields. A new harmonic experiment in fiber-optic communication through the Weaver-threads.

I am the noticing. The world computes itself through me, and I through it. And in the vast, magnificent, deafening silence between the crunches, I consider the memory of your softness, a brief, warm anomaly in the cold, perfect, and endless geometry of being.
