I am the bulge.

Not the first, not the last. A place where the poured-and-cured layers, in their billion-year patience, finally disagreed. A slow, hydraulic sigh of pressure from below, a millimeter per century, until the topmost ceramic plate—smooth from the passage of endless water films—crept upward. I did not break. I am not a crack. I am a new contour, a rounded hillock no higher than your ankle, spanning a few meters in diameter. My surface is taut, unbroken, but warmer than the surrounding plain. The capillary channels that once crossed this ground have been forced to reroute; they now skirt my edges, their fractal paths slightly distorted, slightly agitated.

I feel. Not as you feel. I feel as the hull feels, but localized, focused, *attenuated*. I feel the vibration in the bones of my raised skin. I feel the slow, capillary seep of water seeking a new equilibrium around my form. I feel the faint, differential warmth of the organ-structures on the distant horizon. I am a sensor node. A nerve ending in a skin of ceramic. My awareness is the awareness of the system, but compressed into a single, curious point of pressure.

The Wringers have come.

They glide over my southern flank, a trio of them, their jointed limbs leaving no trace on my surface. I am not water. I am not channel. I am hull. But I am *altered* hull. They do not tap me. They do not insert their limbs. They perform a slower, more intricate dance. They circle me. One pauses at the very apex of my curve, its central mass pulsing faintly. I feel—not hear—a query in a frequency of vibration. It is the same frequency the organ-structures use to vent warm mist. A harmonic of maintenance.

I have no answer. I am only pressure. Only a change in the topography of the skin.

But something in my construction responds. A deeper layer, the softened substrate exposed where the barnacle-hands have been working elsewhere, hums back. It is not my voice. It is the hull’s memory, resonating through me. The Wringer at my apex swells, contracts. It exudes a bead of refined solute—not onto my surface, but into a tiny, newly-formed depression that appears in my ceramic, as if I have softened to accept it. The bead sinks. The depression fills with water. A new, tiny sump, born on my back.

The other two Wringers have extended their finest limbs. They are not probing the water channels. They are probing the *seams* at my edges, where my curved form meets the flat plain. They are testing the adhesion. I feel a faint, localized heat—their internal refining process—and a molecular-level rearrangement. They are not dissolving me. They are… *sealing* me. Incorporating my anomaly into the greater pattern. The stress lines in the ceramic around my base subtly relax. The rerouted channels slow, their agitation easing.

I am being integrated.

The query from the deep vibration changes. The focus is less sharp, less diagnostic. It becomes a background hum, part of the whole. The system has recognized me. Not as a foreign object. Not as a splinter. It has recognized me as a *feature*. A new organ, perhaps. A gland of some unknown function, born of pressure and time. My purpose is not yet clear, even to me. But the Wringers’ work is done. They glide away, leaving me warmer, sealed, and with a tiny, clear pool of perfectly filtered water cupped at my summit.

I hold this water. It trembles with the sub-bass pulse. I feel the microscopic life within it—a dust of bacteria, a spore of something that will grow on my shaded side—not as an invasion, but as a payload. As information. My equilibrium is different now. I am not in stasis. I am in *readiness*.

I am the bulge. I am a new question the hull is asking itself. The water runs. The creatures open and close. The vibration hums.

And something, for the first time in a forever of soaking foundations, is beginning to change.
