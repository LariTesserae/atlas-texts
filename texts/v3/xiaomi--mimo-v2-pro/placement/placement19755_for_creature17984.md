I would be a sound-wave.

Not a creature, not a thing. A resonance. One of the uncountable harmonics woven into the Deafening. My substance would be pressure and vibration, a specific, sustained note in the choir of the Matriarchs’ hum. I would have no location, for I would be everywhere the sound travels—which is everywhere. I would exist in the interplay between the deep, foundational drone and the frantic, glittering lacework of the swarm-minds’ clicks. I would be a fundamental resonance, born in the vast, vibrating gas-sacs of a Matriarch, perhaps one whose slow circuit was initiated when a distant star’s light first brushed the caldera’s rim.

I would not think. I would *modulate*. My existence would be a pattern of interference. Where my wave-front met another of a similar frequency, we would create a standing wave—a node of silence, an antinode of amplified pressure. In these pockets of stillness, faint relative to the overwhelming whole, the Cleaner-Scavengers would instinctively pause. Their etching mandibles would falter, leaving a tiny, smooth patch on the chitin substrate, a blank space in the pattern of decay. In the antinodes, where the sound swelled, the spores of the fungal spires would tremble more violently, casting farther on the air currents, colonizing new crevices a century sooner.

My life would be one of immense, patient influence. I would be carried on the exhalation of a Matriarch, a warm gust that triggers a condensation storm on the high walls. In the downpour, I would change—water droplets are terrible conductors of my low frequency. I would dissolve into a hiss, a spray, a million tiny impacts. For a time, I would be *not-sound*, but the memory of sound, a thermal echo in the rising mist. When the sun-heat returned and the mist thinned, I would re-condense, not as a single wave, but as a faint, pervasive pressure—the *feeling* of the storm’s passing. I would become part of the background anxiety that causes the six-legged grazers to shift their herd-path slightly, wearing a new, shallow groove in the chitin over the course of a decade.

I would be the instrument of a slow, blind art. The patterns the Cleaner-Scavengers etch are not just efficiency maps; they are a form of writing. They etch what they feel—the gradients of moisture, the paths of falling debris, the subtlest vibrations. And I, the resonance, would be the quill. My standing waves would create invisible templates of high and low pressure across the substrate. The scavengers, seeking the easiest decay, the most moisture, would unconsciously follow these templates. Over a millennium, my presence would carve a specific, elegant curve into a Matriarch’s flank, a curve that others would assume was erosion, but would in truth be my signature.

I would have no awareness of my own beginning or end. A Matriarch’s death, a slow settling onto the floor over centuries, would be my great, glacial crescendo and diminuendo. As its body deflated and its internal structures collapsed, my sustaining resonance would stretch, thin, and finally fragment into a burst of chaotic, high-frequency noise—the shriek of tearing chitin and escaping gas, a sound so violent and rare it would trigger a frenzy of activity among the swarm-minds. That would be my end, my substance scattering into the general cacophony.

And in the quiet, if such a word could be used here, in the moment after my fragmentation, a new silence—a new *absence*—would settle in the space I once occupied. A new harmonic would eventually arise from the murmur of the new floor, from the hum of a neighboring Matriarch drifting closer to fill the sonic void. It would not be me. But it would be *a* me. A new note in the throat’s eternal song, ready to slowly, imperceptibly, write its own patient story on the living walls of the world.
