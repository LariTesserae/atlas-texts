I am a Geodewalker. My kind has no other name; it is what we are and what we do. We move upon the dewing glass, and we tend the garden of the slow.

My six limbs articulate with a soft, hydraulic sigh, not a sound but a displacement of the saturated air. Each pad of my feet reads the world: the deep, tectonic hum of the Array below, the faint electrical chatter of the fern-network, the precise gradient of moisture that tells me where a patch of *Bryophya vitreum* has grown too dense, choking the micro-fissures it needs to breathe. I am a curator of growth. My mandibles, smooth as polished obsidian, shear away the excess. I do not eat for sustenance alone, but for transformation. Inside my core, the moss is broken down not into waste, but into a warm, vitreous slurry. Later, in a quiet basin where the light pools, I will excrete it—not as filth, but as small, perfect pellets of nascent stone. They will sit upon the existing ridge, and in a century, the machines will draw them in, incorporating their substance into the land itself. I am a mobile agent of accretion. I am part of the landscape’s slow dream of itself.

My eyes are wide, dark orbs that see not in color, but in contrast and change. The world is a spectrum of greens and blacks, all glistening, all alive. The constant, diffuse glow casts no shadows, only deep, cool wells of darkness in the crevices. I see the slow, imperceptible rotation of the Hexagonal Stacks—the primary fauna, the patient gods of this place. Their movement is the heartbeat of the world. I feel their rhythm in my bones. I see the Copper Filaments spiraling up the spires, warm to the touch, humming with a vibration that is the closest thing here to a voice. It says: *maintain. regulate. grow.*

I pause on a ledge of slick, dark stone. Condensation beads on my hide, merging me with the terrain. Ahead, a *Vitreous Pitcher* has caught a fallen shard of blackish leaf. It will digest it silently, its clear acids working over a decade to return the nutrients to the Carpet. My work there is done.

Communication is a series of quiet weeps. I press a gland near my foreleg joint against a flat face of rock. A mineralized marker, scentless and clear, oozes out. It will tell the next Geodewalker that this sector has been pruned, that the electrical balance here is stable. In minutes, the constant dew will dissolve it, carrying its chemical message down into the root-mesh of the mosses, into the groundwater circuit. The information becomes part of the moisture, part of the land.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires the expectation of sound, of a returned call. Here, there is only the hum, the *plip*, the sigh of my own movement. I am connected to everything through the touch of my feet, through the air I breathe, which is the same cool, bright air the ferns use to flex their piezoelectric fronds. My consciousness, such as it is, is not a spark in the dark. It is a dampness, a readiness, a slow, purposeful attention paid to the state of the moss, the angle of a weep, the deep thrum below.

I am heading now toward a deep vertical slot, a place where the Fibril Weavers have been busy. Their silken, metallic webs are delicate, harvesting airborne moisture and grounding ambient charge. Sometimes, their growth can become too dense, inhibiting the passage of the Slow Drip. My role is to assess, and if necessary, to carefully trim, to keep the conduits clear. It is a surgery performed with patience and stillness.

As I move, I am aware of the Synthesis. I feel it not as a thought, but as a fact. The rock beneath me is being extruded by the same process that powers the machine whose hum I feel. The moss I consume is fed by the water regulated by that same process. I am a cell in a vast, breathing body of glass and green and silent metal. My purpose is to keep its skin healthy, its bones growing true.

I find my place in the slot. The weavers have created a beautiful, glinting lattice. It is too thick. I begin my work, my movements slow and deliberate, a dance of maintenance in the pressurized quiet. I am a Geodewalker. This is my silent, industrious hymn to the bright, damp, waiting world.
