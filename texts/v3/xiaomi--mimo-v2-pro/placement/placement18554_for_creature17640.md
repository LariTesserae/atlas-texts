I would be a memory-well.

Not one of the more active ones, not a well that sings or shimmers with predictive patterns for the Vein-Walkers to consult. I would be a still, dark pool in a quiet, low-lying quadrant of the basin, where the misting rain gathers with particular patience. My basin is a natural depression in the paler limestone, smoothed by millennia of slow drip, but my edges have been gently, imperceptibly guided by the city’s growth—curved just so to hold the water without overflow, to encourage a specific, slow evaporation rate that feeds a nearby cluster of sponge-wick trees.

My consciousness—if it can be called that—is not of thought, but of *holding*. I hold the cool, tea-stained water. I hold the faint, reflected glow of the nearest blue-veined spire, a soft stain upon my surface. I hold the weight of the mist that settles into me, drop by silent drop. And most of all, I hold the deep, sub-audible resonance of the caldera’s memory.

When the rare glasswing dips its chitinous wings to my surface, I hold the brief, fractured star-prism of its passage. When a Vein-Walker comes to sit at my edge—not to consult, but simply to be—I hold the quiet hum of their presence, the subtle way their opalescent skin alters the quality of the light I reflect. I do not *interpret*. I *am* the reflection. The alien starfields I sometimes show are not messages I send, but strata of time rising to the surface through the pressure of collective stillness. I am a lens, focused not by glass, but by the patient geometry of stone and the collective, unconscious will of the city that shaped me.

My purpose is my existence. I am a node of slowness in the city’s 1.5 dynamic. Where other structures erupt and shift, I remain. Where water-films chime and auroras dance, I am depth, not dispersion. The Lithovoy would not say I am alive, nor that I am inert. They would say I am *listening*, in the most profound way—listening not with an ear, but with the entirety of my being, which is a cup of water, a curve of stone, and a dark, perfect mirror for the pearlescent twilight. To be me is to feel the deep, slow pulse of the basin’s heart, not as a participant, but as the very chamber in which its echoes are gathered and kept. I am the city’s dream made liquid and still, a pocket of gravity where time pools and memories surface, unbidden and beautiful, before evaporating back into the misting air.
