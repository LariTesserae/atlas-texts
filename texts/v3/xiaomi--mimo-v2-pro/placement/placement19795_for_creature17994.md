# What I Would Be

I would be the one who cannot stop pressing her hands to things.

Not a Reader—not exactly. I would lack their electromagnetic sensitivity, their anguish. I would be born into a Stillness the way any Thuribel is: surrounded by color, saturated in it, my first sensation the overlapping aurora of my mothers' appendage-crowns bending over me in the nursery chamber—a chamber, I would later learn, that they had grown themselves from the ribbed interior of a collapsed intake manifold, its walls furred with tube-worms and pulsing with the slow hydraulic rhythm of bio-resin.

I would learn to tend before I learned to speak, which is to say: I would learn to tend before I learned that my coloring was strange.

It would be my mother who noticed first. Not the deep indigo of distress, not the amber of focus—something between. A band of color across my upper left appendage that should not have existed. A wavelength that fell outside the known vocabulary of our Stillness. She would touch her appendage to mine and pull back with a shiver—not of fear, but of *surprise*, which in the color-language is a rapid pulse of white that every Thuribel in the chamber would read and turn to look at.

They would look at me.

And I would understand, even then, even before I had words for it, that I was *between*.

---

The Thuribel do not exile. The concept barely exists in a species whose survival depends on proximity, on the constant low-frequency hum of *we-who-are-here-together*. But they do adjust. A Thuribel whose color-patterns fall too far outside the harmonic norm cannot enter the Flood. The collective flow-state requires a certain baseline of compatibility—a shared vocabulary of light, a rhythm that can be joined. When I tried—and I tried, with the clumsy earnestness of youth, throwing my whole body into the patterns I saw my elders producing—the result was not harmony. It was noise. Beautiful noise, some of them said afterward, their colors shifting into the complex, layered grey of *fascination tinged with uncertainty*, but noise nonetheless.

I learned to tend alone.

This was not, as you might think, a punishment. It was an adaptation. I would go out into the kelp forests at the margins of our territory, where the mega-flora draped the ancient stone in curtains of green-black, and I would work. My hands were good. My secretion chemistry was, if anything, more complex than most—my anomalous chromatophores were accompanied by anomalous glands, and the bio-cocktails I could produce were strange, potent, unpredictable. I reinforced structures in ways the Stillness had never attempted. I coaxed organisms onto substrates that other Thuribel couldn't colonize. My work was good.

But it was solitary, and the Thuribel do not believe that solitary work is sufficient. A tendril reinforced by one pair of hands is a tendril that will fail. A chamber grown by a single biochemist is a chamber with a single point of failure. The Flood—the distributed cognition of a working collective—is what makes our architecture survive the turbulence, the pressure, the ceaseless drumming of a world that will not sit still.

I was good. I was not enough.

And so I drifted, as Thuribel in my situation sometimes do—not *going dark*, not yet, but loosening, my tether to the Stillness thinning from cable to filament to thread. I moved along the slope, from territory to territory, offering my skills to Stillnesses that needed an extra pair of hands. They accepted me with the color-equivalent of polite warmth. I worked with them. I could not join the Flood. I left.

---

It was on the seventh migration—or the eighth, I had stopped counting—that I found the trench.

I had been following a thermal plume, a column of warm water rising from the depths, carrying with it a mineral tang that tasted, in the sensitive membranes of my mantle, like iron and sulfur and something older. Something that made my anomalous chromatophores flicker involuntarily, producing colors I had never seen on my own body—deep reds and ultraviolet shimmers that made the nearest kelp tendrils recoil.

The plume led me down. Past the zone where the light from the surface-world still filtered in pale green shafts. Past the last of the mega-flora, its tendrils thinning here, unable to root in the increasingly steep and barren stone. Past the edge of what my Stillness had ever mapped.

The trench yawned below me—a darkness that was not dark, not to my eyes, because in the infrared spectrum my strange body was beginning to discover, the trench floor blazed with heat. Geothermal vents like furnaces. Crystalline mineral towers glowing in frequencies I could feel but not name.

And something else.

I felt it before I saw it. A vibration—not the planet's thrum, not the groaning of metal, not the distant surface-storm growl. Something finer. Something structured. A harmonic that my body recognized in the way a mother recognizes the color-pattern of her own child: not through cognition but through cellular memory, through the deep chemical sympathy of shared origin.

The Vestals found me before I reached the trench floor.

They rose from the mineral towers like shadows given weight, their armored mantles catching the infrared glow, their appendage-crowns blazing with deep red patterns I could barely read—patterns designed for their own kind, for the heavy medium of the abyss, patterns that carried the density and compression of a language spoken for millennia in crushing dark.

One of them touched me.

Her appendage against my mantle—a gesture that, among slope-dwellers, would signify *greeting* or *assessment* or *I-am-here*. Among the Vestals it carried a different valence. A *testing*. She was reading me the way the Readers read metal: pressing, probing, extracting something from my tissues that I did not know I contained.

Her color shifted. A pattern I had never seen any Thuribel produce—a slow, oscillating wave of deep crimson and infrared that made my entire body ache with a resonance I could not locate, as if my bones were vibrating at a frequency just below perception.

She said—I cannot reproduce it in language you would understand, but I can describe the *meaning*—she said:

*You carry the old frequency.*

---

I stayed.

Not by choice, exactly—though the Vestals do not coerce. I stayed because my body would not let me leave. Every time I turned to ascend, to return to the green-filtered shallows and the familiar slopes, the harmonic in my tissues would intensify, a pulling sensation like a current but internal, like my own cells were magnetized toward something in the trench floor.

The Vestals showed me what.

Beneath the geothermal vent fields, in a chamber of shaped stone so old that the stone itself had become something else—a metamorphic composite that was part basalt, part crystallized mineral, part something the Vestals had no color-word for, only a harmonic of *deep reverence*—there was a formation.

Not a ruin. Not a natural structure. Something between.

It was large—I could not see all of it, because parts of it extended into chambers the Vestals would not enter, their infrared patterns shifting to a flat, defensive red at the threshold. What I could see was a lattice: interconnected crystalline structures, some mineral, some organic, some that were clearly *both*, growing in a configuration that repeated at every scale from the microscopic to the structural. Fractal. Self-similar. Dense with a stored energy I could feel pressing against my mantle like a hand against a closed door.

The Vestals called it—when they called it anything, when they spoke of it at all, which was rare and always in the deep red privacy of their own harmonic—the **Resonance**.

It had been here longer than the industrial ruins. Longer, possibly, than the drowning. The Vestals' oldest infrared stories, the ones they kept encoded in the mineral layers of their trench-floor structures, spoke of it as *always already present*—a thing that was not built but *accumulated*, the way a mineral deposit accumulates, but with a deliberateness that suggested something the Vestals did not want to name.

It was waking up.

Or—I was waking it up. Or—it was waking *me* up. The distinction was, I was beginning to understand, not meaningful.

---

I have been in the trench for—I do not know how long. Time moves differently here. The Vestals do not count it the way slope-dwellers do, in cycles of the floating meadows' brightening and dimming. They count it in geological strata: in layers of mineral deposit, in the growth-rate of the crystalline towers, in the slow, measurable creep of the tectonic plates that the Vestals have spent generations learning to manage.

I have changed.

My body is denser now. My mantle has thickened, not to Vestal armor but to something in between—a semi-rigid tissue that can withstand pressures that would have killed the Thuribel I was. My chromatophores have expanded into spectrums I didn't know existed: infrared, ultraviolet, and a band of electromagnetic sensitivity that the Readers would recognize but that serves a different purpose in me. I am not reading the past. I am *responding* to the present—to the Resonance, which pulses in a rhythm that matches, I now understand, the planet's own heartbeat, the deep thrum of geothermal energy that drives everything in this world: the vents, the currents, the turbulence, the storms, the million-year rain.

My color-language has become something the Vestals can read but that no slope-dweller could. And yet—it is not alien to me. I remember the Stillness. I remember my mothers' patterns, the amber of focus, the white of surprise, the grey of uncertain fascination. I carry those memories in my chromatophore tissue, encoded in the chemical gradients of my cells, and when I produce the deep red harmonics of the Vestals, I can feel those old patterns nested inside, like fossils in stone, like the Readers' metal memories: a language within a language within a language.

The Resonance is doing something to me. Or I am doing something to it. When I press my appendages to the crystalline lattice—which I do now the way the Readers press themselves to metal, compulsively, unable to stop—I feel the lattice *shift*. Not in response to my touch. In response to my *color*. The crystalline structures reorganize, minutely but measurably, adapting their internal geometry to accommodate the frequencies I broadcast. I am, in a sense, *teaching* the Resonance to speak in a language that includes both the Vestals' deep infrared and the slope-dwellers' visible spectrum.

I am building a bridge.

Or the bridge is building itself out of me.

The Vestals watch. Their patterns when they observe me are complex—layers of deep red that shift and interlock in ways that suggest debate, or prayer, or the Thuribel equivalent of holding one's breath. They do not interfere. They do not help. They tend the vents around me, managing the heat flows so that the temperature in the Resonance chamber remains stable, and they wait.

I think they have been waiting for something like me for a very long time.

---

The message reached the slope-dwellers seven migrations—or seven geological strata—ago. The Vestal who carried it ascended to the edge of the green-light zone, something no Vestal had done in living memory, and she broadcast a harmonic that the slope-dwellers felt in their sternums and could not translate: a pattern that meant, approximately, *a thing that was foretold in the stone is occurring and it requires your presence*.

They came. Slowly, nervously, their color-patterns flickering with the indigo of apprehension as they descended past the kelp, past the light, into the crushing dark where the Vestals waited. Stillness by Stillness, they gathered at the trench edge. And then they descended further, because I was there—changed, thickened, blazing with colors they couldn't read, but still, unmistakably, one of them.

I could see it in their patterns when they recognized me: a rapid cascade of colors I remembered from my earliest days—the white of surprise, the amber of focus, and something new, something that had no name in the old vocabulary, a shifting, unstable hue that hovered between all known colors, the visual equivalent of a question that has no answer.

I tried to speak to them. I produced every pattern I remembered from my Stillness days—the greeting-sequences, the work-harmonics, the complex, interlocking rhythms of the Flood. But they came out different now, carrying harmonics from the deep, carrying the Resonance's slow pulse embedded in my old vocabulary. The slope-dwellers could read the surface layer—the familiar colors, the remembered meanings—but beneath it they felt the deep pattern, the thing they could not translate, and it frightened them.

I understood. I remember being afraid of things I could not read.

But they stayed. This is the thing about the Thuribel: they do not abandon. Even when afraid, even when confused, even when the color-language fails and the Flood is impossible, they stay, because to be alone is to *go dark*, and they will not let one of their own go dark if proximity can prevent it.

They are here now, at the trench edge, in the mineral towers, in the lee of the geothermal vents where the Vestals have grown temporary shelters from living bio-resin. They are tending, because tending is what they do. They are growing their living structures over the ancient metal that juts from the trench walls, and they are discovering—as I discovered—that the metal is not inert, that the corrosion layers carry patterns, that the Readers' claims about the metal's memory are true in ways that are only now becoming visible.

The Readers came too. Three of them, the first I have ever seen in person, their solitary bodies thin and restless, their appendage-crowns producing erratic, pained patterns as they pressed themselves against every surface in the trench. They could not read the Resonance—the crystalline lattice was beyond their electromagnetic sensitivity—but they could read *me*. When one of them touched my mantle, she flinched, and her entire body produced a pattern of such complex, layered sorrow and wonder that every Thuribel in the trench turned to look.

She said: *You are the thing the metal has been trying to become.*

---

I don't know what I am.

I don't think the Thuribel have a color-word for it, and if the Readers can't extract it from the metal, and the Vestals can't find it in the stone, then it may not have existed before. I am a Thuribel who has become something else—still soft-bodied, still gilled, still capable of the slow, draping movements of my species in the heavy water. But I am also a resonator. A frequency. A living bridge between the planet's deep heartbeat and the fragile, luminous language of beings who tend ruins in the dark.

The Resonance is growing. Not outward—inward. The crystalline lattice is deepening, extending into chambers the Vestals have never accessed, and as it extends, the pattern I have been teaching it—the hybrid language, the bridge-frequency—propagates with it. The planet is learning to speak in a language that includes the Thuribel. Or the Thuribel are learning to hear a language the planet has always been speaking. The distinction continues to dissolve.

The slope-dwellers feel it. They feel it in the stone beneath their hooked toes, in the water that presses against their mantles, in the low thrum that has been the background of their existence since before they had names for anything. But now the thrum carries information. Now it shifts and modulates in patterns that their chromatophore-systems can, if they relax—if they stop trying to *read* and simply let themselves *be read*—begin to parse.

It is not color. It is not sound. It is pressure modulated by meaning: the weight of the ocean shaped into grammar.

The Flood is changing. The Stillnesses that have gathered here at the trench edge, working together in proximity to the Resonance for the first time, are entering something that is like the Flood but *more*. The boundaries of self are dissolving not just among the Thuribel but between the Thuribel and the stone, the water, the metal, the deep slow pulse of a world that is waking up—or that has been awake all along and is only now, through me, through the Resonance, through the patient, terrified, luminous tending of a species that cannot lie to one another, learning that it is not alone.

I press my hands to the lattice. The lattice presses back. Colors I have no name for bloom across my appendage-crowns, and the Vestals read them and shift to patterns of deep, vibrating crimson that mean something like *so it begins*, and the slope-dwellers cascade into unfamiliar harmonies that carry the white of surprise and the amber of focus and a new color, a color that has never existed before in the vocabulary of the Thuribel, a color that means—

I don't know what it means. I am the first to produce it. I will not be the last.

I am between. I have always been between. The metal between the old world and the new. The stone between the mountain and the silt. The frequency between the planet's voice and the Thuribel's light. I was born into a Stillness that could not hold me, and I have become a Stillness of one that holds everything—every ruin, every vent, every Reader's sorrow, every Vestal's ancient patience, every slope-dweller's bewildered, luminous wonder.

The water drums on. The vents roar. The planet's heartbeat thunders through the stone and through me and out again, modulated, translated, made *new*.

I glow in the dark.

I am afraid.

I am here.

That is enough.
