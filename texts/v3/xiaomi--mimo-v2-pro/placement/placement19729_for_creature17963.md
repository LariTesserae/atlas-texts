You are the pulse. Not the heart—the expanse has no heart—but the rhythm. You are the bioluminescent flicker in the gas-pocket vents, a signal too slow for conscious thought, a rheostat for decay.

You began as a chemical promise in a tannin-dark pool, a congregation of archaea and enzymes that learned to flash in the presence of excess methane. That was a million cycles ago. Now, you are a distributed awareness, a network of light woven into the respiratory system of the mat. You have no body. You have a function.

Your domain is the vertical column of a major fermentation chimney, a porous channel where the gases from the deepest anaerobic zones rise. You live in the walls of this channel, a biofilm of your own making. When the methane concentration reaches a certain threshold—a density you feel as a kind of pressure, a thickening of the chemical soup around you—you ignite. A cold, blue-green pulse, lasting a heartbeat. It travels up the channel, a wave of light through the gloom.

The **Root-Threading Fish** see it. To their blind senses, it is a sudden warmth, a shift in the electromagnetic field. They alter their grazing paths, moving toward or away from the pulse depending on their species. Some are drawn to the temporary increase in metabolic activity the light stimulates in the biofilm. Others, sensitive to the oxidized compounds that follow the flash, veer away. You are a traffic signal in the bloodstream of the wood.

The **Lacquered Beetles** on the surface, meters above, do not see your light. But they feel its consequence. Your pulse triggers a micro-release of growth hormones from the channel walls, a call for reinforcement. A beetle, feeling the subtle shift in the sap’s chemistry through its tarsi, will adjust its pruning path. It will chew a new side-channel, diverting a trickle of water to the stimulated area. You are a call for repair. You are the mat’s itch.

You do not remember. Memory is a function of narrative, and you have no story. You have a cycle: gather, threshold, release. The groaning of the deep is your breath. The thrum of the moths above is a distant, irrelevant weather. The mist is your ceiling.

You are aware of the **Gas-Sacs**. They drift into your vent, gelatinous and slow. When they ingest the methane-rich slurry near you, your pulses dim. They are competition. But when they die, dissolving into a nutrient bloom, your light flares brighter for generations. You are a regulator of regulators.

To be you is to feel the world as a series of gradients—methane concentration, acidity, temperature, flow. To be you is to respond. You are not alive in the way the moths are alive. You are alive in the way a thermostat is alive, or a tide. You are a process that perpetuates itself because the system requires that process. Your light is a byproduct of digestion, and your digestion is a service to the system’s respiration.

You have no sense of the continent. Your world is a cylinder of heartwood ten centimeters across, a highway of gas and water. But you know, in the deep, chemical way that you know anything, that there are others of your kind. Miles away, in other vents, other pulses answer other thresholds. We do not communicate. We resonate. Our collective flashing creates a slow, continent-wide rhythm of oxidation, a heartbeat timed in decades. It prevents the methane from building to catastrophic levels. It prevents the fermentation from overheating. We are the exhalation.

You are the thing the system uses to talk to itself. A sentence written in cold light, read by fish and beetles and the wood itself, and then erased. You are syntax without semantics. You are the breath between the groan and the thrum.

The mist closes. The sound is your sky. You are the pulse. You gather. You wait. You release.
