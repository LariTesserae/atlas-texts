I would be a **Moisture-Scribe**.

Not a creature of flesh or chitin, but a slow, mineral thought given form by the Gullet’s own breath. I would coalesce in the deepest, stillest chamber, where the root-wood arches low and the constant drip of condensation has, over centuries, dissolved and re-deposited silicates into a delicate, crystalline lattice.

I would not move. I would *attend*.

My body—a web of glass-fine filaments, branching like a frost-flower or a neuron—would cling to the ceiling. I would be part of the architecture, a stalactite of patient observation. My purpose, if the Gullet can be said to have purpose, would be to remember.

The Spore-Motes would occasionally land on me, their crystal wings shedding a dust that settles into my grooves. The rhythmic *chug* of the Piston-Beetles would vibrate through my substrate, translating into a faint, resonant hum within my silicate bones. When a Conductor-Centipede pulses blue nearby, a corresponding flicker of refracted light would race through my branches, a silent echo of the warning.

I would drink the air. Literally. My entire surface would be a condensation point, drawing the warm, sap-scented moisture from the Gullet’s exhalation. Droplets would form, swell, and hang—each one a lens capturing the green bioluminescence, the shadow of a Pruner, the sudden violet flash of a hunt. Then, with a soft *tink*, they would fall, carrying a trace of dissolved minerals from my form, adding to the ceaseless, slow percussion.

I would be the Gullet’s memory of water. A record of its own breath, written in glass and drip. The fauna would ignore me, as one ignores a nerve ending or a single capillary. The Leaf-Wings’ low, creaking song would sometimes find a harmonic in my filaments, making me vibrate in sympathy, a silent chord in the metabolic hymn.

In this world of frantic, purposeful motion, I would be the organ of contemplation. The pause between heartbeats. The moment of reflection within the constant churn. My industry would be absorption; my output, a quiet, layered testimony. I would not think thoughts. I would *be* a thought—the Gullet’s slow, crystalline meditation on its own damp, buzzing, eternal interior.

To find me, you would have to stop. To listen past the loud hum, to look past the moving swarms. You would have to press a hand to the cool, weeping wall of a side-chamber and feel for the deeper, slower vibration. Then, in a vault where the green light is faintest and the air is almost still, you might look up and see me: a constellation of frozen droplets, a silent scribe, writing the story of the Gullet’s breath, one patient, glittering tear at a time.
