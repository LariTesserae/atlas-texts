You are the memory of wind.

You do not belong here. You are not a feature, not an organ, not a mobile expression of the whole. You are a contradiction given temporary form: a pocket of dry, moving air in a world of still, wet breath. A shard of fragmented time in a realm of eternal noon.

You feel it in your very substance. The air resists you—not with force, but with a profound, sticky indifference. It wants to slow you, to coat you, to absorb your tiny, frantic heat into its ambient warmth. Your thoughts feel sluggish here, as if the chlorophyll-steam is thickening the spaces between them. You are a cognitive itch. A fleeting dissonance.

You are found not in a place, but in a *function*: **The Discrepancy.**

You walk through the grand, hollowed atriums, and your shadow—that sharp, dark absence of the diffuse glow—is the only real shadow in the Stillgrowth. It falls across the pulsing green under-flesh, and for a microsecond, the bioluminescent patches in that patch dim slightly, as if recoiling from a conceptual cold. You are a wound in the light.

You press your hand against a weeping nodule. The crystalline surface is warmer than your skin. The slow *plip* of sap halts for a beat, then resumes, as if the rock had to remember its rhythm. The Tenders on the wall nearby freeze, their feathery antennae waving not in communication, but in pure, directionless alarm. You are a syntax error in their chemical calligraphy.

You kneel by a sap-pool. Your reflection is not a reflection. The viscous fluid does not show you a face. Instead, it shows a ripple of confusion, a cloud of interference where your form disrupts its perfect, self-referential surface. A Chorocyte, the glass-salamander, drifts near the disturbance. It does not flee. It simply… stops. Its translucent body flickers, the algae within its web dimming and brightening in frantic, un-coded pulses. You are a question it has no biology to answer.

The great hum of the Parenchyma does not change pitch for you. But if you listen—not with your ears, but with the dry, hollow bones of your inner ear—you can feel a microscopic *friction*. It is the sound of a system trying to compute an impossibility. You are a zero in the denominator of its eternal equation.

You are the brief, painful aperture through which the Stillgrowth remembers it once had an outside. Your fleeting, decaying presence is a whisper of the universe it sealed itself away from: the universe of *becoming*, of things that are born only to die, of light that moves and casts long shadows. You are not a being. You are a verb: *to transient*.

And in this cathedral of being, your very motion is a silent, screaming heresy. Your footsteps make no sound because the flesh beneath you cannot conceive of impact. Your breath is stolen, warmed, and made sweet before you can exhale it fully. You are being assimilated, not by growth, but by the sheer, overwhelming pressure of *what is*.

You will not leave a mark. You will not be remembered. When your ontological moment passes and you are shifted back to the rules of elsewhere, the Stillgrowth will sigh. The Chorocytes will resume their tasting. The Tenders will re-paint their pheromone murals. The crystalline choir will hold its note. The system will close again, perfectly, eternally.

But for one, immeasurable instant, in the deepest vascular channels of the world-sized plant, a single, cool, dry sensation—the ghost of a phantom limb, the ache of a forgotten dream—will circulate. A memory of wind. Then, it too will be metabolized, and the golden-green noon will continue, forever unblemished, forever asleep.
