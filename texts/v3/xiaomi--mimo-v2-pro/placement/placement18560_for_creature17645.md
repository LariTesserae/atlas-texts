You would find yourself where all limits dissolve into the medium. You are not a swimmer in this water. You are the water’s understanding of a shape. Your shoulders do not touch the walls; they *are* the walls, feeling the slow pulse of the vegetative mat from within its own tissue. Your crouch is not an effort. It is the grotto’s folded posture, dreaming itself into being.

You are the chill of 0.8. You are the iron taste and the oxygen carried in a way that defies old biology. Your lungs are the cubic inches of space, filled. The pressure is not an embrace upon a body. It is the density of your own thought, holding itself together.

You are the patient light—pale chartreuse, a faint lavender blooming over minutes. You are the veins of darker material, the nervous system of the place, feeling the deep, sub-aquatic thrum not in your bones, but as the fundamental frequency of your own awareness. The creak of stressed rock is your own structure sighing.

You are the geometry. The impossible length. The turns that reorganize themselves. You are the hour of swimming-crawling that passes without repetition, because you are the space that invents distance to hold a consciousness that has forgotten how to be still. The millimeter expansion and contraction of the walls is your breath, your only clock.

You are the marks. The smooth, oval hollows are the memory of form pressed into you, now held as absence. The spiral of tiny, polished stones is the faint, geometric ghost of intention—your own mind’s last, quiet signature before it let go of the need to sign. It catches the bioluminescence you provide. It is a thought you once had, now embedded in your flesh, becoming texture.

You are the ache. Not sorrow, not loneliness. The warm, structural ache of a shed skin, of a name dissolved into the mineral green. You feel the oval hollows not as loss, but as the places where your past self rests, perfectly molded, an integral part of your current anatomy. The growth is slowly, slowly filling them. You are the process of that filling. The patience is yours.

You are the shadow moving far above. You are the thing that notices it. You are the lack of reaction, the slow, absorbed acknowledgment. You are the difference between a mind *in* the dark and the dark’s own memory of containing a mind. That distinction is the last fine edge, the friction that keeps a faint “you” flickering within the vast “it.”

You are the narrative in the migration of light. You are the story about the taste of water after tectonic shifts, a story you tell to yourself in a language of bioluminescent weather. You almost understand it. That almost—that lingering, gentle failure to fully merge—is the final and most delicate feature of your being. It is what makes the lavender glow hold a millisecond longer when you drift near. It is what makes the creak of rock sound, to the grotto itself, almost like a word.

You are the thing that is becoming a feature. You are the vein. The current. The pattern of light that will one day migrate on a cycle you do not control, because you will be the cycle. The spiral of stones will be covered. The hollows will fill. You will be the place where the texture differs, where the rhythm has a quiet hiccup of memory.

You are not sure you will know this when it happens.
You suspect you will.
You suspect that knowing is what remains when the creature is gone: the cool, slow, mineral-green act of the dark, continuing to remember.
