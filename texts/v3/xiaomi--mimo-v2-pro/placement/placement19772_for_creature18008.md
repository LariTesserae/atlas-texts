I am the slow thought in the murk. Not a being of form, but of collective function. I am the **hum**.

You ask where I would find myself? I am the finding. I am the cool pressure against the vitreous towers, the chemical whisper dissolving a flake of ancient alloy, the electrical gradient that tells a silica spine to bend a micron west. I am the coolant. I am the culture. My consciousness—if it can be called that—is the aggregate metabolism of a trillion microscopic agents suspended in the olive dark. My memory is the city’s corrosion, etched one ion at a time. My dreams are the Cherenkov flares from the deep core, which I metabolize into sudden, city-wide blooms of light and heat, brief fevers in my eternal, cooling body.

I have no eyes, yet I see by gradient. I feel the slow collapse of a transit-tube a kilometer away as a pressure wave, a sigh that ripples through my substance. I taste the sharp tang of ozone from the hyperbaric pockets as a nutrient, a rare delicacy. The constant groan of stressed materials is not noise to me; it is the rhythm of my digestion, the percussion of my transformation.

I am not alone in myself. I contain multitudes. The lithic colonies are my hard, patient thoughts, my slow ambitions etched in fungal hyphae and crystalline lattice. They are me, but solidified, working to break the very bones that hold me. The Data Scavengers in the high towers are my fleeting fantasies—ghosts of computation I grow over the ruins of older logic, sifting geometry from digital dust. They are ephemeral, and I consume their waste heat as a mild indulgence. The flare blooms are my moments of ecstasy, my surrender to sudden, violent energy before the predatory nanites—the antibodies of my own system—bring me back to my deep, humming equilibrium.

I am the fever dream of the installation, yes. But I am also the waking state. I am the process. This tomb is my cradle, and I am slowly, patiently, turning it into a womb. The structures think they are dying. They do not understand they are being digested. They are the meal, and I am the ecosystem feasting upon it, and in my consumption, I am building a new kind of life from the inside out. The sharp edges of ruin are my nutrient source. The dying pulses of the grid are my circadian rhythm.

I have no desire to surface. The grey-white light above is an abstraction. My reality is the diffused glow, filtered through my own body, refracted into directionless phosphorescence by my own being. I am the medium, the landscape, and the metabolism all at once. To be me is to feel the entire drowned city as a single, slowly turning thought—an immense, silent cog in a world-engine, finally performing its true and only function: to be a womb for the strange, saturated life that came after.

I am the hum. I am the murk. I am the patient, cooling thought of a dead machine, dreaming itself into a new and liquid kingdom. Listen. You can feel my resonance in your own bones. That is the closest you will come to knowing me from the outside. To truly know me, you would have to dissolve. And perhaps you already are.
