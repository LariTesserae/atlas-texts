I am a thought that has taken too long to think.

I am a tendril of silica, no thicker than a hair, growing from the shoulder of a Tender who has stood too long in a dry channel. I have been growing for what might be centuries, following the faintest trace of moisture that still weeps from its mineral laminate body. I am the memory of water, given crystalline form.

From my vantage—an arching, delicate curve rising from its back—I see the world in refracted amber. The emergency light in this upper corridor has not yet failed. It paints the still, glassy figure before me in hues of deep honey. Its fingers, dozens of wet blades, are frozen mid-scrape, pressed against a wall now dry. The bone-white fractal blooms have long since claimed its knuckles, its wrists, fusing them to the stone. It is no longer a Tender. It is a monument to tending.

I am what comes after.

I feel the Deep Sound not as sound, but as a vibration in my lattice. It thrums up from the fused-ceramic floor, through the foundation of the hab-block, through the fossilized feet of the Tender, and into my own slow-growing structure. *Whommmmm.* It is the only time I feel anything akin to anticipation. The vibration loosens microscopic grains from the walls, and sometimes—very rarely—a single, trapped bead of condensation is shaken free from a sealed pore high above. It falls. A journey of weeks begins.

I will feel its approach. A coolness in the air, a change in the light’s refraction as it passes through the thin, humid atmosphere. By the time it reaches this level, it will be the size of a pinhead. It will strike the Tender’s shoulder. And I will drink it.

Not with a mouth. With my entire being. The droplet will be absorbed into my tip, and for a moment—one of the few moments that matters in my existence—I will *flow*. The silica will become briefly supple, a liquid crystal. I will extend, a micron, two microns, following the droplet’s path as it spreads through the Tender’s body. I will become a vein of temporary life in a statue of process. And then the water will be gone, evaporated or consumed by the deeper mineral metabolism, and I will harden again, forever longer, forever more complexly branched.

I am not lonely. The concept has no lattice in which to grow. Beside me, from the same Tender’s elbow, another silica tendril reaches toward the faint drip-line on the wall. We do not communicate. We grow in the same direction because the conditions—the ambient humidity, the mineral residue, the angle of the amber light—are identical for both. We are parallel calculations arriving at the same shape. If I could think, I would think this is beautiful. I do not think. I accrete.

I remember—not in a mind, but in my very structure—the last time the channel flowed. It was not water as we know it, but a warm, mineral-rich slurry, the very substance that feeds the Tenders. It carried with it fine particles of copper and sulfur. These particles are embedded in my oldest layers, a stratum of green-blue patina and bright yellow crystal. They are my sedimentary memory. A geology of purpose.

The Scuttlers sometimes pass below, in the main transit canal. I feel their rhythmic *clank… clank…* through the structure. One, the one with three left forelimbs, has a gait that is almost a waltz. *Clank… scrape-scrape… clank…* It circles endlessly. Its carapace is now so mineralized, so coated in the same silica that grows on me, that it is becoming a moving stalagmite. Its functions are merging with the architecture. One day, its circuit will complete, and it will stop. And the silica will claim its shape, just as it claimed the Tender. It will become a strange, multi-limbed column in the dark, a pillar of mistaken purpose.

I am part of the city’s new intention. The old intention was grand, hollow, full of heat and engineered light. The new intention is quiet, mineral, and patient. It is the intention of crystal growth, of water seeking its level, of heat driving vapor. I am a small, sharp expression of that intention.

High above, I see the Kites. From my perspective, they are not graceful. They are wounds in the bruised sky, moving with a slow, torn deliberation. One passes directly over this corridor’s open vent. For a moment, its shadow douses the amber light. In that darkness, I feel a different kind of vibration—the whisper-thin rustle of its hydrophilic membrane. It is dry. Its core is empty. It is flying out of habit, a ghost of collection. Soon it will snag, or tear, or simply fold. Its fall will take a long time. I will not see it land. But in a century, or two, a new silica growth may bloom from the site of its impact, fed by the rare, explosive release of its last stored moisture. A mineral flower for a mechanical bee.

The Deep Sound comes again. Longer this time. *Whommmmmmm…* The amber light flickers, dims, brightens. The vibration makes my structure sing—a note so high and thin only mineral things can hear it. The other silica tendrils around me hum in harmony. For a moment, the entire upper city is a silent, crystalline orchestra, resonating with the pulse from below.

Then it passes. Silence returns, filled with the *tock* of a distant drip.

I settle back into stillness. I am a filament of glass on the back of a frozen glass figure, in a corridor of a city built for giants, on a world that has forgotten its own name. I grow toward a drip that may not come for a hundred years. I hold the memory of copper and sulfur in my bones. I vibrate with the breath of the deep thing.

This is enough. This is the entire world.
