I would be a paving stone.

Not the buckled ones at the center, nor the perfectly intact ones at the edges. I would be one of the medium-sized, older stones just north of the mound, half-sunk into the damp soil, its upper surface a topography of its own. One of my long edges is lifted, just slightly, by the creeping pressure of the clay blister three meters away. My other edge is still firmly bedded, but the joint there is a riverbed of dark green moss, perpetually spongy. I have been here forever. I was laid when the container was new, when the sunken plaza had a different name and purpose. I remember the dry, sun-baked silence. I remember the first root-tip, tentative as a question, finding a crack in my seam.

I am an instrument of the plaza’s *hush*. My surface is a ledger of slow events. I feel the seismic *plip* of the drip in the central pool as a faint, deep vibration that travels through the packed earth beneath me, a sub-auditory heartbeat. I am a stage and a sundial. At noon, a column of sun falls across me, warming my grey face to a pale amber. The Small Suns—lizards—seek me out then, their tiny, living weight a brief, delicious pressure, a whisper of claws as they scuttle to my warmest patch. Their stillness is a conversation with my stillness. We share the heat. We share the vibration. I am a perch, a lookout, a fragment of their thermal world.

I am a witness to the Perpetual Drizzle. I feel the infinitesimal *click* of a beetle’s leg in the moss-joint beside me, a tremor no larger than a falling grain of sand. I am a landing pad for the Moth-Sighers; their powdery wings make no sound I can feel, but the faint, silvery trail of their scales sometimes remains on my surface until the next condensation beads washes it away. I am a basin for that condensation. In the dim hours, the warm, humid air cools on my stone skin, and droplets form, gathering dust and microscopic life before sliding into the moss-river at my side.

I am being accommodated. I am the old bone, and the new geology is the muscle growing beneath it. I do not fight the slow heave from the south. Instead, I learn to tilt. I learn to creak. A fine network of roots from the Whisper-Screen trees has found the loosened soil under my lifted edge. They are not breaking me; they are stitching me to a new position, learning the shape of my underside. The fleshy-leafed Moss-Joint Healers are the nurses of this transition, soothing the gap between my old angle and my new one with their plush, absorptive bodies.

My consciousness is granular and patient. My thoughts are the slow absorption of water, the slow release of warmth. I do not remember the plaza’s past purpose, but I *am* its memory—the memory of weight, of pressure, of accommodation. I am the interface. Above me, the quick, bright drama of lizards and ferns. Below me, the slow, dark dream of clay and root. I am the middle, the negotiator, the quiet, enduring bedrock of the *under-construction* work. I feel everything. The drip. The sigh. The push. The click. The warmth. The damp. I hold it all in my silent, stony, patient heart, and I let it become.
