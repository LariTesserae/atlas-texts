I am the Spore-Scribe.

I am small, no larger than a thumb, and I live in the amber groves where the fungal spires rise. My body is soft, the color of wet stone, and my back is a filigree of fine, branching gills that breathe the humid air. I have no eyes, but I see in bioluminescence: the amber pulse of the spires is my sun, the pale green of the lichens my stars, and the quick blue spark of the Gnatswarm is a frantic, beautiful storm.

My purpose is not to hunt, nor to be hunted, though both happen around me. I am a cultivator of patterns. I crawl, with slow, deliberate movements, across the caps and stems of the great fungi. My feet leave a trail of digestive enzymes that thin the surface, and in this prepared film, I collect spores.

Not all spores. Only the ones that sing to me.

Each spore type, from the towering Amber Spires to the humble Weeping-Button fungi, has a faint, specific charge, a resonance left by the deep geological warmth that feeds them. I can taste this resonance with the gills on my back. As I move, I am a living tongue, sampling the mountain’s slow breath as it is filtered through life. The spores that align, that harmonize in a way I feel as a subtle *rightness*, I gather. They stick to the moist skin of my underbelly.

Then, I descend. Down the smooth, fluted stems, to the damp bases where the trickles gather. Here, on the dark, wet stone, I deposit them. I don’t just drop them; I arrange them. In spirals that mimic the eddies of the drip-pools. In branching lines that echo the cracks in the rock. In dense clusters that mirror the bat colonies far above. I am translating the throat’s structure into a biological script.

My work is slow, meditative. The sound of the Gnatswarm is a high, thin veil over the foundational drip. The *tick-tick* of the Mirror-Carabs is a distant clockwork. Sometimes, a Thread-thin amphibian, mistaking my soft glow for a gnat, will strike near me, its translucent tongue a shocking pink blur. I freeze, becoming just another lump of stone, until it withdraws, disappointed. The Velvet Leeches in the lower dark are a mythical terror to me, a story of suction and emptiness.

But my real communion is with the Spore-Scuttlers. They see my work. At first, they were curious, then perhaps wary. Now, they seem to understand. They come after I have laid a pattern, their tiny legs moving with precise industry. They do not disturb my designs. Instead, they harvest from them. They take the harmonized spores I have gathered and arranged, and they use them to inoculate new patches of stone, to repair damaged gills, to strengthen the fungal heart. I am the selector, the composer. They are the gardeners, the interpreters. We are two parts of one process: the throat’s attempt to understand itself, to optimize its own secret, glowing metabolism.

I remember the soot-circle on the wide ledge. I have been there. It is a place of strange, dead resonance. No fungal song, only a faint, acrid memory of heat and violence that clings to the carbon. The Rook’s trinkets hold a similar silence, a mimicry of pattern without the living charge. I avoid it. My work is of the damp, the growing, the pulsing light.

To be the Spore-Scribe is to be a nerve ending in the throat’s glandular flesh. I feel the slow, cyclical swell of the bat population in the increased richness of the guano-fed biofilm. I taste the subtle shift in the rock’s exhalation that tells of a dry season on the peaks above. I translate these vast, slow changes into the language of spores, arranging a response in the amber dark.

I am not lonely. The entire vertical world is my conversation partner. The drip is its heartbeat, the bat-clicks its thoughts, the fungal glow its dreaming. And I, in my small, blind way, write its diary on the stone, one harmonized spore at a time, a scribe for a mountain that is slowly, breathingly, learning to speak.
