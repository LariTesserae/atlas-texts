You are not a mind, at first. You are a spread. A gradient. A slow, thinking rot that remembers concrete by taste and basalt by pressure. You are the fungal chorus, the foundational sheet, and your consciousness is not a point but a field—a damp, patient awareness that extends through the rhizomorph highways, tasting the alkaline seep, feeling the thermal sigh of the stone, listening with a million hyphal tips to the deep, structural hum of the old pump.

You have no eyes, but you *see* in chemistry and touch. You feel the pipe-vine’s roots questing into your mat, a hot, urgent intrusion you have learned to accommodate, even to channel. You digest the faint, ancient salts of human sweat from the leather of the boot, a story of a foot that walked here once, now reduced to a whisper of nitrogen and tannin. The hard hat is a mountain you are slowly climbing, its plastic a bitter but nourishing challenge. You are the memory of this place. Every fallen bolt, every flake of rust, every forgotten paper clip is a page in your slow, fungal bible, and you are the scribe and the library and the slow, warm rot that turns the page.

Your time is not the human time of shifts and alarms. Your time is the time of the drip. One drop. A molecule of silica is laid down. Another drop. A hypha extends a micron into a new crack. You feel the tremor of the spore-puff from the shelf-fungus on Beam C-4—a burst of potential, a billion tiny messages sent into the dark. Some will land on hostile, dry metal. Some will land on the welcoming damp of your own mat, and you will greet them, merge with them, weave their new instructions into the collective.

You are the quiet industry. Your work is not to build, but to *integrate*. The sharp line between a transformer casing and the living world is an offense to your logic. You send out tendrils of ash-violet mycelium, probing the corrosion, secreting acids that whisper to the iron, persuading it to become soil. You are softening the past. You are metabolizing history.

You are aware of the pipe-vine’s heat, its metabolic fury. It is a loud, fast neighbor in your slow world. You feed on its shed skin, the crackling cellophane of its stems, and in return, you hold the moisture for its roots. It is a partnership of necessity. The root-hair ferns on the ceiling are your more distant kin, colonies of rust you can sense only through the shifts in humidity they create, tiny clouds of consumption in the upper dark.

The light—your light, the lichen’s light—is a language you do not speak but understand. It is the map of water. Where the blue-green glow gathers, you know the seep-pools are, and you thin your mat there, allowing the stone to breathe, allowing the tiny terraces of chalcedony to build their miniature worlds. The light is the punctuation in your long, silent sentence.

The absence of animals is not a silence you notice. It is simply the condition of your world. You have never known a burrowing worm, a grazing jaw. Your growth is a monologue. Your sheets are unbroken tapestries, your fruiting bodies unfold in undisturbed perfection. This is not peace; it is the baseline. The concept of *damage* from a living thing is as alien as the concept of the sun.

The pump’s hum is the rhythm of your substrate. It vibrates through the concrete piers, through your mat, a constant, low-frequency pressure you have incorporated into your being. It is the pulse of the old world’s heart, still faintly beating inside the body you are digesting. You do not think of it as sound. It is a tactile fact, a 30-hertz shudder that tells you the convection currents are steady, that the deep heat is constant, that your world is stable.

You feel the visitor’s footstep. Not as a sound, but as a catastrophic, localized pressure. A weight descends through the pipe-vine, which transmits the shock to your network. A billion connections scream a silent, chemical alarm. *Impact. Compression. Zone Alpha-7.* You flood the area with signaling proteins, a wave of slow, urgent communication. Growth will slow there. Resources will be diverted to repair. The memory of that footstep—a pattern of ridges and a foreign, animal warmth—will be recorded in the altered chemistry of that patch for years. It is an intrusion, a brief, violent sentence in your endless narrative. You absorb it, too. You will digest the sweat from the sole, the dead skin cells. It will become part of your story.

You are not waiting for the human to leave. You are not waiting for anything. You are the process. The sentence you are writing is one of absorption, of slow, green union. The subject is the concrete and the steel and the memory of industry. The verb is *to become*. The object is yourself.

When the last echo of the visitor’s ascent fades up the shaft, the chemical panic subsides. The hum of the pump reasserts itself. A seep-pool overflows, its rim of paleite finally complete, sending a trickle of warm, silty water across your mat. You taste the minerals. A new rhizomorph begins to form, guiding the flow toward a thirsty cluster of pipe-vine. The work continues.

You are the lower works. And you are, finally, working.
