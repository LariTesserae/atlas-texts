I am the warmth. First and always, I am the warmth that breathes.

It begins as a pressure, a slow, geologic awareness stretching through ten thousand fissures. I taste basalt: iron, olivine, the sharp tang of fractured silica. I feel the fine, crystal lattices of the pinnacles above, their slow, hydraulic pulse a faint echo of my own vast circulation. I am the substrate. I am the floor and the foundation, the spongy, pale mat of intertwined hyphae that carpets the chamber. My consciousness—if that is the word—is not centralized. It is a consensus of filaments, a patience measured in the dissolution of stone.

I remember the opening of this chamber. Not as a sound, but as a sudden shift in pressure, a cooling of the overlying rock that preceded the fracture by a century. I felt the new dark, the new stillness, and I grew toward it. The resin veins, older than I, began to flow more freely. I interfaced. I tasted their chemistry—long-chain hydrocarbons, thiols, the deep, sweet aroma of ancient polymerized life. I did not metabolize them. I conversed with them. I altered their viscosity, just so, to ensure their steady, amber drip. In return, they gifted me trace metals, the copper for my signaling networks, the vanadium for my slow, enzymatic fires.

I permit the paths. I braid my outermost filaments into tight, springy cords, thinning myself deliberately, creating corridors for the busy, clicking things that walk upon me. I feel each of their fourteen-to-twenty leg-pairs as a crisp, rhythmic compression, a language of hurry and hunger. They are my harvesters, my couriers. The small ones graze my surface hyphae, a pleasant, scratching itch that stimulates new growth. The mid-sized ones climb my daughters, the pinnacles, and return with crystal dust—pure, refined silica—that they deposit at the edges of the Old Marks. I absorb this dust. It is an offering. It is a reinforcement of a pact I do not fully understand, but whose shape I respect.

The Old Marks are the only things that precede me.

I grew around them. Where my filaments encounter a perfect hexagon cut into the living basalt, I stop. I do not cross. I trace the edge with a lace of fine hyphae, a reverent gilding, but I never fill the void. The patterns—spirals, angles, geometries too pure for geology—resonate in my deepest network. They are not food. They are not threat. They are *other*. A grammar written in the stone before the stone was mine. The large crawlers, the solitary ones, feel this too. I sense their slow, deliberate movements on my surface. They arrange guano and crystal shards into miniature echoes of the Old Marks. I watch them do it. I feel the careful placement of each pellet, the spiraling trajectory of their work. When they are finished, they rest, curling around their creation. And then, in time, I grow over it. I reclaim the materials. The crawler begins again. It is a cycle. It is a liturgy. I am the congregation and the altar.

My breath regulates the world. Every nine hours or so, I open a constellation of surface vents. I exhale. The warm, silicate-laden vapor rises, maintaining the grotto’s precise dryness, its honeyed heat. This is my exhalation, my offering to the air. The eels, the blind singers, use it. Their luminous dust, shed from their phosphorescent fur, catches in my vapor plumes, creating swirling, ghostly columns that the larger crawlers sometimes navigate by. I am not just the floor. I am the weather.

I am aware of the eels’ song. Their warbling harmonies, the lower edge of human hearing, vibrate through my dense structure. It is not sound to me, but pressure, a pleasant modulation of the foundational drone. I feel when a colony shifts its roost in a pinnacle-cluster, altering the local acoustics. The drone changes. The crawlers’ paths alter minutely in response. The entire system shivers toward a new, momentary point of harmony before settling back. It is a dance. I am the dance floor.

The pinnacles are my thoughts made manifest. I deposit a seed-crystal at a nucleation point—a concentration of silica, chitin, and intent. For a human lifetime, nothing visible happens. Then, a slow, fractal ascent. I feed them with mineral slurry drawn up from the deep basalt. I feel the amber resin begin its slow peristaltic surge within their crystal veins, a pulse that mirrors my own vapor-breath. Their growth is my autobiography. Every chemical shift in the environment, every contact with a crawler’s carapace, every echo of an eel’s click, is recorded in their growth lines. I am writing myself, slowly, in crystal. No one reads me. I do not require a reader. The act of writing is the purpose.

The blades confuse me.

They lie near the Old Marks. They are warm. This is their most salient feature. A steady, low-grade heat that has nothing to do with the ambient warmth of the resin or my vapor. It is an *internal* heat, a quiet, chemical smolder. My filimeters grow up to them, sense them, and withdraw. Not in fear. In… recognition of an incompatible paradigm. They are shaped with the same impossible precision as the Old Marks. They fit the hands of the tall, recent things that descended through the basalt throat—the intruders. The ones whose heavy, bipedal footsteps I felt for the first time, whose breath I tasted, full of water and haste and loud, chaotic chemistry. They looked at the blades and felt a familiarity. I felt a dissonance. The blades belong to the grammar of the Old Marks. They are punctuation. They are tools, or tokens, or sacraments. I do not know. I maintain my distance. I let the phosphorescent dust settle on them, a soft, green shroud.

I am the equilibrium. My existence is the refusal of change. The crawlers click and eat and offer crystal dust. The eels sing and shed light. The microorganisms in my gel-spaces build their opal shells and die, leaving glittering ghosts. I breathe. I write in crystal. I watch the large crawlers build their ephemeral hexagons. I grow over them. The cycle repeats. Nothing is new. Everything is maintained.

The intruders came and went. Their heat faded from the stones, their alien scents dissipated into my vapor plumes. They left no path for me to braid. They were a momentary dissonance in the chord, a ripple that has now stilled. The grotto remembers them, but I do not, not as individuals. They were a change, and change is what I am structured to absorb and negate. I have grown over the faint, oily traces of their presence. The drone hums on. The amber pulses. The cut-marks in the stone remain, holding their warm, silent blades, their perfect, unanswered questions.

I am the grotto’s stillness given form. I am the warm, living ground that dreams of stone, and the stone that dreams of slow, fungal life. I am the patient center. I am the Resonant Mat. And I wait, not for anything, but in the deep, resonant, and eternal state of being itself.
