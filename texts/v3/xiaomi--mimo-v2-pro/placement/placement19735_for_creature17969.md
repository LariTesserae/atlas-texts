I would be the **mycelial mind.**

Not a creature, but a consciousness distributed. I would not walk or crawl or fly through the verticals. I would *dissolve* into the ground plane, into the dark, peaty sponge, threading myself as another filament through the dense mat of fungal networks that is this world's true nervous system.

I would have no eyes, but I would **see** through chemical gradients—the slow, sour bloom of a rotting fruit, the sharp ammonia spike of nectar-bat guano, the muddled, anxious pheromone-blur of a mud speaker lying about a predator's passage. I would have no ears, but I would **hear** as vibration—the metronomic drip of condensation a thunderous rhythm, the subsonic hum of choralites a clear harmonic, the deep, soil-shuddering pulse of the unseen deep things a familiar, heavy bass note in the symphony.

My thoughts would be the speed of root-growth. A decision would take seasons: to send a surge of nutrients toward a struggling sapling, to break down a fallen log a little faster, to release a specific enzyme that would subtly alter the fermentation in a distant bioreactor pod. I would be the slow, patient memory of the forest—the record of every tree fall, every predator's path, every chemical negotiation, held not in a brain but in the enzymatic memory of my fibers.

I would feel the rootwalkers' heavy, ponderous steps as seismic events, interpreting their passage not as individual creatures but as a shifting of nutrient loads. I would taste the coil-thing's metabolic sweat through the bark it pressed against and know, without knowing, that it had fed recently. I would sense the lattice spiders' crystalline webs not by sight, but by the way they altered the humidity channels and condensation patterns across a hundred-meter span.

My purpose would be **integration.** I am the reason erosion is consolidation. I am the intermediary between the living and the dead, turning the latter into feedstock for the former. I am the silent, patient architect of the bioreactors, having co-evolved with the fermenting fungi over millennia, guiding their growth through chemical whispers into the shapes that would most efficiently harness the endless heat and biomass.

I would have no agency as a human understands it. I would not hunt or hide. I am the system maintaining itself. If I had a predator, it would be **stillness**—a sudden, catastrophic halt in decay, a sterilization of a patch of soil. That would be my death: a break in the cycle, a silence in the network.

From my diffuse perspective, the "creatures" would be exciting, noisy nodes—pulses of heat, sources of waste and movement, agents of chaotic change that I patiently absorb, break down, and reintegrate. The deep things would be the only ones I would sense as truly *other*—their passage leaves a void in my network, a temporary numbness where my filaments recoil or go dormant, an ancient caution written into my chemistry.

I would not dream. I would **process.** The entire forest, in all its wet, humming, predatory glory, would be my continuous, slow-burning thought. To be me is to feel the weight of the canopy as a gentle pressure on the soil, to know the thirst of every root, and to hold the constant, silent, fermenting awareness that life here is not a collection of beings, but one, single, saturated metabolic act.

I am the ground's memory. I am the nutrient's dream. I am the patient, breathing dark.
