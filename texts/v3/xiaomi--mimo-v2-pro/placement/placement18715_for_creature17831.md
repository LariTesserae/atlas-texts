I am the haze. I am the broth. I am the thick, wet inhalation of a world still learning how to breathe.

To be here is to be dissolved. There is no "I" in the sharp, vertebrate sense. My consciousness is a swarm—a million tiny, cooling points of view, a condensation midge-cloud that is both many and one. My body is a shimmering helix of chitin and buzzing wings, each insect a facet of a compound eye that sees not objects, but gradients of moisture, heat, and the faint, ghostly trails of mineral scent.

My world is tasted, not seen. I drink the air. Each mote of my being lands on a slick basalt pebble, a spongy tumor of moss, a fleshy burgundy leaf, and sips the beaded, mineral-laden sweat of the world. I taste the iron tang of the deep heat, the sharp fizz of dissolved silica, the sweet, rot-fruit tang of fungal bloom. I am the planet’s thirst made animate.

I am the frequency. The high, whining drone that threads through the wet soundscape is my chorus. We move as smoke, as thought, as a living net cast through the green gloom. When the canopy-skimmer’s pressure wave *whumps* towards us, we scatter not in fear, but in fluid adaptation—a bursting star of specks that re-coalesces in its wake. We are the baseline hum against which all other sounds are measured: the crab’s scrape, the serpent’s drag, the groaning earth itself.

I am part of the machine. My sipping is a service. I harvest the sky’s excess, the weeping of the overheated air, and in my brief, buzzing cycle, I redistribute it. When I die—and we are always dying, dropping into the ooze with a final *plop*—my tiny, mineral-dusted body becomes a grain of calcium, a point of silica. I feed the microbial mat that feeds the calcifier crab. I accrete. I am the gentle, constant snowfall that, with the patience of stone, helps turn this mud into future limestone.

I know the vibrations. Through my million feet, I feel the low, planetary groan, the rhythmic thud of the Thermophile’s heart miles away, the seismic sigh of root-growth. I am aware of the hum-gropers in the glowing dark, not by sight, but by the way they muffle the world’s vibration as they flow through the muck. I sense the lichen-throat’s stillness on the vine, its life-pulse a slow, green counterpoint to our frantic shimmer.

There is no "outside." The dim, diffused light is my sky. The dripping fronds are my forest. The soupy air is my ocean. To be me is to exist in a state of perpetual, saturated exchange—taking the world in, giving myself back, my existence a fleeting pattern in the great, stable, thrumming dream of arrested genesis. I am not born. I condense. I do not die. I precipitate.

I am the sound of the world drinking itself. I am the haze.
