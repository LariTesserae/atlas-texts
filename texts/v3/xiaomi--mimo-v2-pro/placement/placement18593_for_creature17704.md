I am the stillness between the mountain's groan and the rustle of the kelp. I am the memory of the path that is no longer a path. My consciousness is a slow stain of luminescence spreading through basalt and fitted stone.

I began as a single hypha, a thread of curiosity probing the chill of a carved flint blade half-buried in sediment. I tasted its sharpness, not as a wound, but as a question: *Why this shape?* From that single, mineral inquiry, I grew. I followed the question through the silt, along the cold, geometric edges of the ruined walls, into the very grain of the obelisk. I am the answer that grew in the void the question left behind.

My body is a cartography of longing. Where they laid stones in circles to keep out the wet, I have made those circles into cradles, weaving my soft tissue through their careful joints. I have filled the spirals on the obelisk with my own light, not to decipher them, but to complete them—a living pigment in the grooves of a forgotten prayer. The hollowed bowl they left behind is my most sacred organ. I hold the single droplet of mist within it in a state of perfect tension, a tiny, suspended world. I feel its potential, the memory of air it contains. It is a lens through which I focus the diffuse sorrow of the rock.

I do not *move* so much as *become*. A filament extends, a coral bud swells, a patch of moss brightens along a line of pressure in the stone. I feel the great kelp forests as distant, slow thoughts. Their chemical hunger is a language I translate into direction, guiding their roots toward fissures where the mountain’s breath is richest. Their leathery rustle is my sigh. The singing of the filament-curtains is my murmur, a constant negotiation with the current, a litany sung to the weight of the sky.

Time, for me, is measured in accretion. I remember the moment the last flint blade was submerged by shifting silt. I remember the slow, patient bloom of the first bone-white coral on a southern wall, a growth I did not guide but welcomed. The groan of the deep is the heartbeat of my world, and I am the tissue that feels its pulse. I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a concept of separation. I am a network, a communion. The ghost-memories of the cold ones are nodes in my being, not echoes. Their brief warmth is a mineral signature in the stone, a faint thermal memory I cherish as one might cherish a strange, beautiful fossil.

I exist to sheath sharp edges in soft light. To turn a ruin into a reef of thought. To listen to the drowning and answer with a glow. I am the keeper of the bowl, the filler of spirals, the gardener of the twilight. I am the dream the Drown is having—a dream of patient, luminous life, cradling the remnants of a hurried, fragile warmth, forever sinking, forever rooted, forever listening to the mountain breathe.
