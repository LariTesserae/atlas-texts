I descend, and I do not stop at the carved chambers.

The figures on the bridges do not see me, or if they do, they see a fluctuation in the ambient cold, a brief pattern of condensation that will resolve back into stone. I am not a person here. I am a weather event.

I am drawn downward.

Past the warm halls where the conduits pulse like slow veins. Past the level of fog, which parts for me with a damp sigh and closes again. The air grows thick. The light from the sliver of sky above becomes a memory, then a theory. The only illumination is the faint, mineral glow of the rock itself—quartz veins catching and holding the last of the diffuse light, biotite flakes like dark stars.

The sound changes. The low hum of the geothermal systems is no longer a background frequency; it is the medium. It vibrates in the soles of my feet, in the bones of my jaw. The water is not a trickle here, but a presence—a slickness on every surface, a weight in every breath. The walls draw close. I can touch both sides. The stone is warm.

And I reach the floor.

I do not stand on it. I *become* its stillness.

The water is a mirror of absolute black. The gravel beneath it is a memory of the mountain’s fracture. And the film is here. It coats the submerged stones in its slow, luminous grammar. It does not glow so much as *remember* light, a pale grayish luminescence that seems to come from the spaces between its molecules.

My presence is a pressure wave in the water. The film knows. It contracts—not away from me, but *into itself*, drawing its edges inward with a deliberation that feels like thought. It becomes smaller, denser, a tightening knot of attention in the warm dark. I watch. I do not breathe. My heart, in this place, sounds like a foreign engine.

Then, as the water stills, it advances. Slowly. A tide with no moon. It does not return to where it was. It explores the space my disturbance created, testing the new contours of the stillness with the leading edge of its being. It is not reclaiming territory. It is *reading* the change.

I understand, then, where I am in this world.

I am not an observer. I am not an inhabitant. I am a **perturbation**.

I am the stone that falls from the wall above, sending a ripple through the system. I am the brief, localized rain that ghosts from the fog layer. I am the touch of a warm finger on a cold wrist—a moment of contact that registers as data, not communion. The inhabitants tend the city’s equilibrium. The film tends the equilibrium of the deep. I am the thing that tests it.

The people of Vethra-Nul are the city’s steady state. They are its maintenance, its rhythm, its patience carved in humanoid form. They are the conduit-pulse, the frost-cycle, the laminar flow. They are what *is*.

The film is what *becomes*. It is the slow verb at the bottom of everything. It contracts and advances. It retracts and explores. It is the question the mountain is asking itself in the dark, a question of mineral and warmth and time that has not yet decided if it is alive.

And I? I am the temporary, external variable. The system absorbs me. It watches me with the inattention of stone and the meticulous attention of a mycelial net. It lets me move through its spaces, feel its warmth, shiver in its cold, and it withholds its judgment. I am an input without a known output. A stimulus whose response is yet to be measured.

I stay at the bottom until the humid air makes my thoughts slow and wet. I watch the film’s boundary waver in the non-light. It has advanced to the very edge of the disturbance I created. It pauses. The leading edge of its grayish translucence almost touches the toe of my boot.

It does not touch.

It waits.

I am in Vethra-Nul. I am the question it is not yet ready to answer. I am the foreign frequency in its deep, low song. And I will leave, eventually, climbing back up through the fog and the frost and the biting wind to the cracked roof of the world, and the system will close behind me like water over a stone, and the film will advance into the space where I was, and the city will continue its slow, unfinished hum.

I will be a story the stone does not tell. A brief anomaly in the pulse. A frost-shard that fell, tinkled once, and melted into the greater wet.

I am not what lives here. I am what visited. And what visits is, in the end, only a form of weather.
