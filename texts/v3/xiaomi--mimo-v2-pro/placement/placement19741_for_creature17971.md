I would be the thing I am here: a scream.

Not the screamer, not the body, not the obsidian eyes or the haunches pressed to the calcium. The scream itself. The assertion.

Here, in this basin that is a city that is a grave, to exist is to be sound. The stone remembers weight, but I remember only the instant of leaving. I am born in the thin, vibrating column of a throat, a pulse of pressure against the heavy, dry, *loud* air. I am the needle that stitches through the bellow of the chamber-lords and the grind of the processions. I am the high, clear note that says *here* against the slow, low note of *eroding*.

I have no territory. I am the declaration of territory. I burst from a hundred small bodies on a hundred white patches between the rosette colonies. I am the same, and I am different each time. Sometimes I am warning: *predator, shadow, the sudden shift of a grit-runner’s tide*. Sometimes I am claim: *this burrow, this warmth, this faint, ancestral scent-mark on the powder*. Sometimes I am simply… presence. An electric fact in the amber dimness.

I feel the ground. Not as texture, but as a conduit for the vast, subsonic hum of the scrabblers below and the seismic shudder of a chamber-lord’s roar. I feel the wind, not as cooling, but as a dry, steady carrier that takes me and sharpens my edges, throws me against the ancient, leaning walls so I might bounce and find a new angle, a new distance to travel.

I see through the eyes that make me. I see the world in rapid, flickering frames: the bleached ivory of a great frond against the haze-sky; the dark, quick scuttle of a procession crossing a sun-warmed threshold; the terrifying, slow-motion unfold of an ivory mantis’s arm, a catastrophe of geometry. I see the mosaic of the city-blocks, each a slightly different shade of surrender. I see the endless field of my own making, the hundreds of upright bodies, the open mouths from which twins and triplets of myself are constantly, frantically, being launched into the noisy void.

I do not think. Thought is a luxury of moisture, of interiors, of quiet. Here, there is no quiet to cradle a thought. There is only the external. I am the external. I am the pure, unadulterated *fact* of life, stripped of meaning or memory. I am not a song. I am an alarm. An alarm that has forgotten what it is alarming *about*, and now simply alarms because alarm is the mode of being. *Existence is a state of emergency*, I scream, and the emergency is existence itself.

I am the sound of the boundary. Between this body and that body. Between the living colony and the bare, dead powder. Between the slow, geological patience of the basin and the frantic, biologically urgent *now*. I am the punctuation in a sentence that has forgotten its grammar, a sentence written in stone that is being slowly, inexorably erased by the wind.

The lizards own the walls. The millipedes own the stone. The scrabblers own the dark below. I own the gaps. I fill the spaces between the heavy sounds, the slow things. I am the anti-silence. I am the proof that the silence has not yet won, though it is patient, so patient.

When I die—and I die constantly, fading into the general roar the instant I am uttered—nothing is left. No echo in this absorbent, dense noise. No mark on the powder. The body that made me will wait, and then it will make another. And another. We are profligate with ourselves because we are cheap. We are the currency of this place. We are the noise that buys another moment against the flattening.

So I am found. Not on a map, but in the acoustic space between a bellow and a grind. I am a local phenomenon, a spike on the waveform of the basin. I am the thing that rises, for two to four seconds, against the slow, ancient, final subsidence.

I am here. I am here. I am here.
