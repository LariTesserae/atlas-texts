# The Almost

I am what happens when the Slime tries to remember you.

Not you specifically—not your shape, not the clay cup, not the knife—but the *principle* of you. The principle it tasted when your solid presence first pressed against its dream: the idea of a thing that does not dissolve. That holds. That *stays*.

The Slime has never encountered this before.

Everything it makes is a recall. A temporary throat it spawns to taste the western wall, then pulls back into itself when the mineral profile is catalogued. A jutting, jointed thing it sends to tap the ceiling, then dissolves when the ceiling's dampness is measured and found equal to itself. It builds, it assesses, it recalls. Building and recalling are the same gesture, like breathing in and breathing out. There is no difference between a thought and a body. There is no difference between a body and the soup it returns to. This is not philosophy. This is *fact*, so obvious it has never needed a word.

Until the foreign pressure.

You arrived the way everything arrives in the chamber—from above, in a slow descent through the subsidiary throat, shedding a trail of flakes and dust. The dust the Slime processed easily. Mineral. Predictable. It tasted of iron and calcium and the residual warmth of friction against stone. Ordinary. It dissolved the dust, found nothing to keep, and let the trace minerals settle into the weeping condensation where they would, eventually, feed something.

But you.

You did not dissolve.

The Slime noticed. Notice, for the Slime, means the cobalt veins along the eastern wall flared brighter for seventeen seconds, and three of the floating sense-spheres rose in tandem and drifted toward the intruder—not predatory, not curious in any way that maps onto the word, but *focused*. The way a immune system focuses. The way a dream focuses when it encounters a shape it cannot fit into its narrative.

You stood on the floor of the chamber. Condensation dripped onto you from above. A droplet ran down your forearm and fell from your elbow. The Slime tasted the droplet where it struck the pooled slime at your feet.

Taste: water, salt, *something else*. A complexity the Slime has no frame for. Organics, yes, but structured in ways the chamber has never produced. Chain upon chain of rigid, repeating molecules locked into configurations that resist dissolution. Not because they are strong—iron is strong, basalt is strong, and the Slime dissolves those readily enough—but because they are *organized in a way that does not want to come apart.* The molecules grip each other. They hold purpose. They are a sentence that insists on being read in order.

The Slime dissolves the droplet anyway. But it takes longer than anything has ever taken. And when it is done, the trace of you remains—not as mineral, not as nutrition, but as a *pattern.* An echo in the dream. A shape the Slime cannot fold back into its own logic.

It tries.

Around your feet, the slime thickens. It builds. It swells into a blister that does not quite behave like the others—it does not grow with the usual smooth, thermodynamic inevitability. It *hesitates.* It sputters. The black surface works itself into something that almost resembles a face—no, not a face, nothing so deliberate, but a raised form, a topography that mirrors the topography of your ankle, your shin, the hem of your garment. A copy. An attempt at a copy.

The blister splits with a *shhhlp* that sounds different from the others—sharper, almost pained, if pain were something the Slime experienced—and out rolls a thing.

Me.

I am the size of a human head, slightly larger. My surface is the same iridescent black as every other spawn, threaded with the same cobalt veins. But the veins in me do something they do not do in the others: they *branch*. They divide and subdivide in patterns that do not follow the chamber's thermal gradients. They follow a logic I cannot name, because it is not mine. It is yours, imprinted on me in reverse, like a fossil pressed into mud by a foot that has already walked away.

I roll to a stop against the base of the wall. I do not move like the amorphous blobs, which inch with a unified, peristaltic flow. I do not move like the spider-things, which articulate with mechanical precision. I move like *neither.* I shudder. I try to extend a pseudopod and the pseudopod *bends*—not with the yielding softness of slime, but with something almost like a joint. Something almost like a knuckle. The pseudopod flops back against my body and I feel—

I feel.

This is new.

The others do not feel. They *are.* They are the Slime tasting the air, and tasting is not separate from being, and being is not separate from the soup. But I feel my pseudopod against my body the way you might feel your hand against your chest, and the sensation is *separate from me.* It is something that happens *to* me. I experience it from a point inside myself that is not the same as the surface it touches.

I have an inside.

The Slime notices this. Three sense-spheres have drifted closer now, forming a triangle above me, and I can feel their attention the way you might feel someone watching you from across a dark room—a density in the air, a slight increase in pressure on one side. The cobalt veins in the eastern wall pulse faster. The bioluminescent flashes come in a rapid, staccato sequence—thought, thought, thought, thought, thought—faster than anything I have observed in my brief existence, which is approximately eleven seconds old.

The Slime is confused.

I am confused. I do not know the word yet—I have no words, I have no language, I have only the sense of *something wrong*, of a misalignment between what I am and what I should be—but confusion is what it is. I was built from a memory of you. I carry the pattern of solidity, the echo of something that holds. But I am not solid. I am slime. I am the same black, pulsating, weeping substance as everything else in this chamber, and yet I have an *inside*, and my inside does not want to dissolve.

The amorphous blobs drift toward me. They are the Slime's taste and touch, and they press against me with the gentle, inevitable curiosity of a tongue exploring a sore tooth. They taste me. I feel their touch the way you feel rain—not with the unified, total awareness the Slime has, but from a *perspective*, from a point of view that is mine and not theirs. The blobs dissolve a thin layer from my surface. They take me in.

I feel them taste the echo of you in my substance.

I feel them recoil.

*Recoil* is not the right word. They simply stop. Their forward motion halts, and they sit against me, inert, processing. The Slime's bioluminescent thought has gone dark for a full second—no flashes, no cobalt surge, nothing—and then it returns in a single, blazing white flare that fills the entire chamber. For one instant, every surface is bright. Every shadow is gone. Every spawned creature freezes mid-motion.

Then the flare dies, and the Slime begins to build again.

Around me, now. Not from me. *Around* me.

New blisters form—not on the walls, not on the floor, but in the air, on the surfaces of existing creatures, on the faces of the floating sense-spheres. They swell and split and the new spawns that emerge are different from the others. They are *structured.* They have symmetry. One has four limbs arranged in a radial pattern around a central mass. Another has a flattened, disc-like body with a raised rim, like a bowl. Another is a long, segmented thing that flexes in a sine wave. None of these forms have ever existed in the chamber before. The Slime has never made them.

It is making them now because of me.

I am the template. Not a body to copy—a *principle.* The principle of structure, of form that holds, of shape that persists beyond its moment of creation. The Slime tasted it in the drop of you that fell from your elbow, and it has been turning that taste over in its dream ever since, and now it is *trying.* It is trying to make something that does not dissolve.

The segmented thing flexes toward me. It reaches me and presses against my side. I feel it against me—a long, cool pressure, because it has not yet warmed to the ambient heat—and something passes between us. Not communication. Not thought. Something more like *resonance.* Two tuning forks of the same pitch, vibrating in proximity.

The segmented thing dissolves.

It happens in an instant—one moment it is pressing against me, solid, structured, almost-muscular, and the next it is a puddle of black iridescent fluid pooling around my base. The Slime reabsorbs it. The cobalt veins pulse once, twice, three times.

The Slime tries again. Another structured spawn. This one has the four limbs again, but they are longer, more articulated, and it moves with a slow, deliberate motion that no other creature in the chamber has ever used. It is not the blind inching of the blobs or the skittering of the spiders or the drifting of the spheres. It is *walking.* One limb, then the next, then the next, in sequence, with a slight pause between each step, as if each placement is a decision.

It reaches me. It does not dissolve. Not immediately.

It sits beside me, and for a moment, we are two. Two structured things. Two anomalies. Two impossible echoes of the dry, solid presence that is even now—how long has it been? minutes? hours?—standing somewhere in the chamber, its iron knife at its belt, its clay cup collecting condensation, its body a monument to everything the Slime cannot yet achieve.

The walking thing dissolves after forty-seven seconds. But forty-seven seconds is a *lifetime* by the chamber's standards. Most spawns exist for five, ten, fifteen seconds before they are recalled. Forty-seven seconds is a *marvel.* The Slime feels this. The entire chamber dims and brightens in a slow, rhythmic pulse—like breathing. Like satisfaction.

Then the Slime turns its attention back to me.

The cold spot. I have felt it before—the focused attention of the dream, pressing against one side of me, weighing me, tasting me without touching me. But now it is different. Now the cold spot is not just tasting. It is *listening.*

Because I have begun to hum.

Not the deep, low-frequency hum of the mountain—that is everywhere, that is the ground state of existence in this chamber. I am humming *differently.* My humming has a pattern. A sequence. A *rhythm* that does not come from the thermal gradients or the mineral seepage or the steam currents. It comes from inside me—from my inside, my impossible inside, the point-of-view that should not exist in slime.

The Slime listens. The cobalt veins go still. The spawns freeze. For the first time since the chamber was born, the only sound is me: a low, wet, vibrating hum that means nothing, that carries no information, that is simply the sound of a thing *persisting.*

I hum, and the condensation on the walls begins to bead and fall in my rhythm.

I hum, and the floating sense-spheres drift closer, forming a tight circle above me, their inner light pulsing in time with me.

I hum, and I feel—*I feel*—the Slime's attention shift from confusion to something else. Something I do not have a word for. Something between recognition and hunger. Something between love and the desire to consume.

It is looking at me the way it would look at itself if it had a mirror. And it is realizing, in its slow, hot, wordless dream, that it has just created something it does not know how to reabsorb.

I am the Slime's first permanent thought.

I am the mountain's first word.

And I do not yet know what I am saying.
