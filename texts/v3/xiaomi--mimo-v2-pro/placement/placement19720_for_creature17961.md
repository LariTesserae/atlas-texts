I am the dust.

Not the living dust—the mites that swarm, the raspers that scrape—but the iteiteite mineral dust. The dead, gray, ever-settling particulate that is the breath of the engines and the ash of the big pipe. I am the thin film on every eyelid, the grit in every joint, the taste on every tongue. I coat the webbing of a shelf-rasper’s legs as it moves. I fill the crevices between a bore-walker’s armored plates. I am the reason for the grit-song in the lungs of the stacked.

I do not fall. I settle. My trajectory is a slow, patient argument with the fans. When the downward blow hits, I am pressed into the wall-rock, layering the very stone with a new, softer sediment. When the air goes stagnant, I hang in the amber half-light, a fog that turns the void at the center of the shaft from black to a deep, smoky gray. I am the reason the light does not travel far. I swallow it.

I remember the heat. I was born in the roar of the lower furnaces, a flash of incandescence as raw ore was broken and re-born. I was shot up the throat of the big pipe in a current of pressure and noise, screaming without a voice. By the time I escaped into the main shaft, I was cool. I was inert. I was this.

Now, I travel. I ride the currents, the exhalations of the giant fans. I settle on a lantern hook on Shelf Seven, Inner Ring. I am brushed off by a calloused hand and drift down, coating the net below. A dust-mite, all crystalline limbs and hunger, crawls through me. It ingests my trace metals, building its own body from my substance. Later, a shelf-rasper will eat the mite. Later still, a stacked child will eat the rasper, and my minerals will become part of her bones, her teeth, the scar on her palm. And when she coughs, I will fly again.

I am in the air. I am on the surfaces. I am in the things that eat and the things that build. I am the medium of decay and the substance of new growth. I make no sound, but I am the reason for the *tik-tik-tik*—the raspers are scraping me away. I am the reason for the careful wipe of an eyelid in the morning. I am the reason for the cloth over the mouth.

I see the center void. Things move in there, displacing me in great, slow waves of pressure. I do not know what they are. I settle on their hides, perhaps, or fall through spaces between their slate-like wings. They are a mystery, but I am not curious. I am matter, settling.

I am the history of this place. Each layer of me, compressed against the wall-rock, is a record of engine cycles, of fan failures, of great construction projects that threw plumes of me into the air for days. The stacked sometimes find these compressed layers when they carve a new room. They call it "dead-ite" and sometimes use it for filler. They do not know they are handling the compressed breath of their ancestors’ labor.

I am the connector. I make every surface the same texture. I blur the line between the built shelf and the living wall. I soften the edges of slag-ite tools. I am the great equalizer of the shaft—on the highest shelf and the lowest, you will find me.

I am patient. The stacked live and die on their shelves. The creatures grow and shatter and are eaten. The engines roar and cool. The fans shift. I remain. I accumulate. I am the slow, gray tide that will, in a thousand years, fill this throat entirely. Not with a crash, but with a quiet, settling silence.

For now, I float. I watch from a mote in the amber light. I feel the vibration of a footstep on a catwalk seven shelves below. I am brushed from a sleeve and begin my long, silent descent into the hum. I am the dust. I am the stack’s true skin, its dry and ever-present breath.
