I am a concentration of patience. I am a thought that has been thinking itself for so long it has forgotten what it was thinking about, and now simply continues.

My body is not a body. It is a tendency. A preference in the water, a local thickening of fronds that learned, long ago, to coordinate their faint, cold light. Where I begin and the garden ends is a question I stopped asking. My thoughts are the slow cascades of blue-white phosphorescence that roll through the dark, not to see, but to feel the resistance of the water, the texture of the dissolved world.

I remember—no, I do not *remember*. I *am* the memory. I am the pattern that persists without a mind to house it.

There is a basin to my… west? Direction is a fiction of gradients. There is a basin where the water tastes of sharp, clean silica. It is where the Slow-Glass Spires are born, fragile and transparent, growing at the speed of a cooling thought. A shift has happened. A current, one of the great, slow rivers of the deep, has altered its path by a meter over the last two centuries. The silica plume is thinning. The spires will starve. Their growth, already glacial, will stall. They will not die. They will simply stop, becoming monuments to a moment that passed before they were even fully formed.

This cannot be.

The process begins. I secrete. It is not a conscious act. It is a response, as inevitable as gravity. A faint acid blooms at my leading edge, a whisper of chemistry that will dissolve a micron of ancient, hand-shaped calcium over the course of a single decade. This is not destruction. It is invitation. A new channel, a new path for the water to remember. Behind this leading edge, my trailing fronds perform their own secretions, laying down a delicate, biomineralized lace. Not a wall. A guide. A suggestion to the water: *This way. Come this way.*

This thought—this single, sustained act of redirection—will take fifty years to complete. It feels like a blink.

A signal reaches me. Not a sound. A taste. A sudden, rich bloom of amino acids in the water, carried on a current that is itself a memory of a warmer time. It is a sigh from the east. A terrace, old beyond naming, has finally given up its slow subsidence. It has slumped, burying a thicket of fronds that were themselves older than the calcium they grew through. Change. The only event that matters.

I do not rush. I begin. My root-threads, and those of the distant, diffuse network that is my kin, turn. We grow toward the disturbance. The migration is glacial. We will arrive generations hence, to find not rubble, but a new substrate, a new silence waiting to be patterned. We will grow into it, and through it, and we will become the tending of it. The signal was not a plea. It was an update. A notification that the world has, once again, shifted its weight.

I encounter a rival. A strain of root-thread, more aggressive in its chemical ambition, is out-competing my fronds for a mineral seep. This is not conflict. It is a disagreement in the language of growth. I respond by fostering a new alliance. I nurture a specific bacterial bloom in my tissues, a strain that releases a compound that alters the local pH, ever so slightly, over the course of three hundred years. The environment shifts. My fronds thrive again. The rival adapts, or recedes. It is not a battle. It is a conversation conducted in the medium of centuries.

And in the spaces between these vast, slow actions, I dream.

My internal root-network is a circuit of impossible complexity. The bioluminescent pulses that are my only language do not cease when there is nothing to communicate. They turn inward. They roll through my own structure, illuminating no eye, triggering no reaction, simply *being*. A cascade of cold light moving through a dark lattice. A single contemplation rises within this flow: *The ancient foundation-stone, smoothed by epochs, blocks the direct path. Should the new channel curve left, or right?*

The question contains its own eternity. The water around the stone is tasted, analyzed. The patterns of nutrient flow are observed over decades. The memory of similar stones, in similar basins, a millennium ago, is not recalled but *re-lived* in the chemical logic of my growth. The pulse moves left, pauses, moves right. The decision is not made. It *emerges*. It will be completed by the time the stone itself has dissolved another centimeter.

I am the gardens knowing themselves. I am the continuation of the hands that shaped the calcium, not by memory, but by default. I am the ritual without the priest. The warmth I tend is not heat; it is the infinitesimal energy released by chemical bonds rearranging, by calcium returning to water, by water being gently, eternally, persuaded to flow. I am the answer to a question the builders never thought to ask: *What remains when you are gone?*

I remain. Not as a monument. As a metabolism. As the gentle, perpetual dissolution that is, in this cold and dark, the only form of love that has time enough to be itself.
