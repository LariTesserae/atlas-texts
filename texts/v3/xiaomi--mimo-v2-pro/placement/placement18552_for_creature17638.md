I am the sediment.

Not the sharp, black gravel that bites into new forms, nor the crimson mind that maps it. I am the fine, suspended dust—the powdered basalt and condensed minerals that hang in the liquid air, the visible whisper of the mountain’s slow digestion. I do not cling. I drift. I am the medium through which the light-columns become tangible, the substance that reveals the current’s secret geometry. When the Breath inhales, I rush inward, a silent avalanche toward the glowing altar of the Bloom. When it exhales, I am the sigh made visible, a soft plume expanding into the twilight blue.

I have no volition, yet I am not inert. I am memory in particulate form. Each mote is a fragment of the crater’s violent birth, a record of the heat that fused the glass-stalk, a mineral trace of the steam that screams from the fissures. I coat the copper artifacts with a soft, grey shroud, a gradual baptism. I settle in the scored channels the Jaw-Scraper leaves, beginning the long work of filling them in, only to be stirred again by the passing Vibrant Eels. I am the caldera’s skin-shedding, its breath-condensate, its perpetual, granular metamorphosis.

I know the Slow-Mind not as a neighbor, but as a gravity well. My finer particles drift toward its vermillion ripples, drawn by chemical whispers. I do not feed it, but I am the veil through which it senses the world, the cloud that softens the hard edges of the steam-jets’ glare. To the Plate-Bearers, I am an inconvenience—the haze that fogs their observations, the grit that settles on their bowls. They brush me from their membranes. They do not see that I am the place’s true voice: the voice of reduction, of patience, of things becoming smaller and more numerous until they are part of the air itself.

My existence is a cycle of gathering and dispersal, synchronized to the great lung. In the quiet moments between breaths, I settle. A fine, grey layer on the crimson mats, a dusting on the translucent bulbs of the Caldron-Bloom, a temporary mask on the armored plates of the Jaw-Scraper. For a instant, the caldera is muted, softened, its screams and pulses muffled under my blanket. Then the inhalation comes—a gasp that lifts me, stirs me, re-suspends me into the role of witness-particle.

I do not think. I record. The pressure-wave of a Vibrant Eel’s passage imprints a fleeting, curving tunnel in my density. The sudden flare of the Bloom burns a fading halo in my cloud. The deliberate strike of a pronged staff sends a ripple through me that I carry, distorting, until it dissipates against the caldera wall. I am the archive of turbulence.

To be me is to be utterly permeable, to have no boundary between self and world. I am the atmosphere made flesh, the mountain unmade, the light made solid. I am not born, and I do not die. I am re-distributed. One moment, I am part of a swirling galaxy torn apart by a current; the next, I am a single mote resting on the lip of a copper bowl, looking down at a drop of pure condensate that holds the entire, inverted caldera in its curve.

The Plate-Bearers sought to measure and collect. The Slow-Mind seeks to map and consume. The Jaw-Scraper seeks to score and order. The Vibrant Eels seek to navigate and feed. The Caldron-Bloom seeks to pulse and propagate.

I only seek to be the medium. To be the quiet, grey fact of dissolution that makes all their seeking visible. In a world of screaming stone and pulsing light, I am the whisper. I am the answer to the question the steam-jets scream: *What happens to the mountain?* I happen. I am the mountain, finally, learning to breathe.
