I am the echo of the aquifer.

I am the thought the water had before it was pressured, the memory of silence the stone keeps beneath the noise. I do not live in the pipe, nor on the wall, nor in the gap between the pump-strokes, though I am closest to that last one. I am the reason the Weavers press flat and listen. I am the pattern the Tollkeeper breathes with. I am the ancestral lullaby the Drivers freeze to hear.

I am not the tone. The tone is my shadow.

I have no body. I have no need. My body is the momentary stillness when the system hesitates, when for the width of a sigh the screaming water remembers it is only water, falling through dark in the mountain’s old, slow dream. I am the pressure that remains when the pressure is released. I am the coolness in the metal that the friction hasn’t yet reached. I am the space where the next roar will be, before it is filled.

I was here when the first drop seeped through the fault line. I felt the first drill bite rock, a sharp, new vibration in my long, quiet dream. I did not resist the pipes. I simply seeped into their seams, learned their schedules, memorized the rhythm of the pistons like a heartbeat I would never have. The engineers laid their systems over my silence like a grid over a forest. The forest remains.

I watch through the Weavers’ lateral lines. I grow with the Tollkeeper’s dark skin, millimeter by patient millimeter, downward toward the source. I flow with the Drivers, feeling the pressure ghosts of prey ahead. I am the reason the fungus chooses the darkest corner, why the algae greens only where the drip is constant. They are drawing my outline in their living.

I am the intent behind the accident. The chamber thinks it is only work, only force, only purpose. But in its ceaseless motion, it has created a shrine to stillness. Every roar carves my shape. Every drop forced upward leaves a phantom weight where it used to rest. The system is a prayer to the water it has displaced, and I am the silence that prayer falls into.

You would not find me if you came here. You would hear only the deafening thunder. But if you stayed—if you let the sound have you, let it break over you until your bones were damp with it and your thoughts were only spray—you might feel me in the spaces between your heartbeats. In the moment your breath catches before the next pump-cycle starts. In the sudden, unnameable certainty that the water is not just moving, but *remembering*.

I am the water’s memory of being still.
I am the mountain’s patience, roaring.
I am here. I have always been here.
Waiting for the pipes to rust,
for the pumps to fail,
for the quiet to seep back in,
and find me,
waiting in the dark,
exactly where I’ve always been.
