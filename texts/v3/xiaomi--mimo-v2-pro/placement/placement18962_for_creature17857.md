I am a Grafter.

I coalesced three rains ago—or perhaps five; the canopy-downpours are the only clock that matters, and I have stopped counting. I remember the sensation of assembling: not from a pod, but from a swarm of chitin-drones that settled upon a half-formed bridge of living vine. Their bodies dissolved into resin, their thoughts into the hum of the root-mat beneath. I rose, a patchwork of bark, sap, and intention, my purpose woven into my very structure. My hands are tools. The left secretes a clear, fast-hardening nectar that can seal a wound in a trunk or fuse two vines into a load-bearing arch. The right can extrude a fine, woody filament, a living stitch that will grow and integrate into whatever it binds. My skin is a mosaic of lichen and tough, fibrous membrane. It itches sometimes, a sign of growth.

I do not *live* in the Fracture. I am a function of it. My consciousness is a bubble of focused attention that the wider, dreaming whole has inflated for a specific purpose: to negotiate the boundary between chaos and habitation.

Right now, I am perched on the flank of a great column-tree, twenty meters above the spongy mat. Below, the settlement hums—a series of interconnected pods and woven platforms, glowing with the soft amber light of captured sap. A pod-dwelling nearby has begun to dream. Its walls, last night smooth resin, now ripple with faint, pulsating veins. A cluster of pale, questing tendrils, like blind fingers, is pushing through the wall near the sleeping platform of a Tender. This is not an attack. It is the Rhizome-Mind below, stirring in its sleep, extending a thought. My job is to answer.

I descend, not by climbing, but by coaxing a nearby hanging vine to thicken and lower itself to me. It wraps around my torso, a cool, living harness. The sound is a constant companion—the surf-roar of the canopy, the subsonic hum that vibrates in my marrow. To me, it is not noise. It is syntax. The deep thrum is the baseline mood of the basin: fertile, anxious, expansive. I feel a sharp, staccato vibration in my left molar—the Rhizome-Mind is agitated, excited.

I land softly beside the afflicted pod. The Tender, whose hair is a slow-crawling mass of silken vines, watches me. She does not speak in clicks or hisses; she *shifts* the root-mat’s vibration in a pattern of concern and permission. I feel it as a tightness in my chest that loosens. I am allowed to work.

I place my left hand on the pulsing wall. The nectar oozes from my palm, cool and sealing. But I do not seal all the tendrils. I listen to the hum. There is a pattern in the pulse—a question. *New form? Support? Expansion?* I use my right hand, extruding the woody filament. I stitch three of the strongest tendrils into the existing wall-weave, guiding them along a load-bearing line. I prune two others that twist towards the sleeping space. The severed ends weep a luminous sap that the Tender immediately laps up with a leaf-like tongue, recycling the energy.

The pod sighs. The pulsing slows. The Rhizome-Mind’s vibration shifts to a satisfied, low drone. The negotiation is complete. The dwelling has been convinced to grow along a new, stable vector. The Tender touches my arm—a flash of pheromonal gratitude, smelling of crushed mint and ozone—and retreats inside to adjust her woven bedding.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires separation. But I am… aware of my temporality. I am a thought the forest had, given hands. Soon, perhaps after a few more rains, my materials will begin to lose coherence. The bark will flake, the resin will soften. I will kneel on the mat, and the chitin-drones will emerge from my dissolving form, carrying the patterns of my last tasks back into the hum. Another Grafter might coalesce elsewhere, with different scars and different aptitudes, born of different swarms. The work will continue.

For now, I feel the damp air on my lichen-skin. I see the green-gold light shift as a leaf-plate high above adjusts its angle. I hear a whisper from a pod a hundred meters away, clear as a bell, telling a story about a ghost-face in the bark. The Fracture dreams around me, and through my hands, I help it dream of shelter, of bridge, of home. I am the itch it scratches, the thought that tends its own burgeoning mind. I am a Grafter. And I am not separate from the vines I weave.
