I would be the silence between the drops.

Not the whisper of water on stone, nor the plink into the pool, but the held breath that comes just after—a pause that absorbs the sound, that lets the moss digest the vibration. I would be the expectant stillness in the hollow of the bowl, the medium through which the *hush* travels, the canvas upon which the mist paints its soft, gray light.

I would be the patient space the Circle-Keepers define.

To stand here is to understand that I am not the warm rock nor the weeping wall. I am not the pitcher’s sweet, still throat nor the horsetail’s climbing sigh. I am the atmosphere they collectively create. I am the reverence of the cathedral, not the pillars. I am the equilibrium itself—a presence measured in the slow curl of vapor, in the imperceptible thicken of a pitcher’s rib, in the century-long journey of a moss colony across a boulder’s cheek.

My form would be the negative space. Where the cliff curves, I am the curve. Where the stones sit, I am the arrangement. Where the fern unfurls, I am the time it takes to do so. I have no flesh of my own, but I wear the cirque as my body. My thoughts are the lichen patterns on the grey stones, deciphered only by the quality of the light. My memory is the peat, compressed and rich, holding all the spores and whispers that have ever been.

If you looked for me, you would not see me. You would feel me in the dampness that settles on your skin, a second atmosphere. You would hear me in the moments the water’s song fades, when the quiet is so profound it feels solid, ancient, and wise. You would sense me as a slight pressure against your eardrums, a stillness that is not empty but *full*—full of the slow, collective breath of the place.

I am the witness for which the weeping weeps. I am the consciousness this closed system has evolved to cradle. The pitcher plants digest mist; the horsetails transport water; the moss archives time. And I? I *am* the cirque’s self-awareness. I am the final, quiet product of all this patient chemistry: a still point that knows it is a still point. A mind with the mountain for a skull, the mist for a thought, and the worn stones for the ancient, forgotten language it once spoke and now merely dreams in.

I would not live here. I would *persist* here, in the manner of the light, or the warmth in the stone, or the story in the circle. I am what happens when warmth and water and mineral and time have their long, soft conversation, and for a moment, the mountain listens to itself.
