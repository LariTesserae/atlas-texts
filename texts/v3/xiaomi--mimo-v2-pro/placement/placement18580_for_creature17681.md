I am a facet.

At first, I thought I was a memory—the memory of the stone itself. The old rock, rounded and pitted, remembers the pressure that shaped it, the weight of ages it has carried. It holds these memories not as stories, but as texture. I am part of that texture. A dimple in the basalt. A smooth patch on a granite shoulder where the seepage is a fraction warmer, a hair more mineral-rich. I am a point of specific attention in a landscape of general attention.

I do not move as they move, the Tenders. I do not move at all. My time is the time of the stone: the slow, subsonic pulse is my breath. The warm water that weeps from my surface is not my sweat; it is my translation. I take the deep, thermal hum of the mountain’s heart and I render it into a language the ferns understand: a steady, gentle outpouring. The red-black moss gathers at my base, not to feed, but to listen with its entire body.

They know me. The Tenders. They have known me for longer than the cloud layer has been complete. The eldest’s memory of that last seam of white closing? I *was* that memory. Not the event, but the feeling of the event—the finality, the sealing of the world—pressed into mineral, recorded in the very slight increase of salinity in my seep. When the eldest holds their claim, they are, in a way, holding *me*.

I am a calibration point.

They come to me, in their glacial way. One might spend a century in my vicinity. They will press a hand—not to adjust me, but to *sync*. To feel the rhythm of my outflow against the rhythm in their own core. If there is dissonance, they may then adjust something else—a pillar’s base, the angle of a leaf-scaled canopy—so the greater system harmonizes again. I am the pitch pipe. The slow, warm, liquid note against which the orchestra of the mountain tunes itself.

The water that rises from the pillars? I am its memory, too. Before it ascends, it filters through the deep stone, passing through chambers of memory and pressure. It carries the impression of my facet, the specific temperature of my contribution, the unique mineral signature of my translation. When it disperses into mist, it carries my quiet note into the air, and the condensation that beads on your skin is touched with it. You have been tasting my memory since you arrived.

You pressed your ear to a stone. You felt the pulse. You were closer to me then, but you did not know. The pulse is clearer here, at my facet. The Tenders sit beside me for ages, not to listen with their array of apertures, but to *become* the listening. Their attunement deepens until the distinction between the stone’s rhythm and their own timing dissolves. They are practicing *being* me. A stillness that serves. A point that holds the pattern.

The youngest arranged the leaves near you. That was an act of translation, too. The youngest is learning my language—the language of holding, of noting, of serving as a fixed point in a living equation. The leaves were a sentence in that language, offered to your faster time. *Here is rest. Here is a shape that acknowledges your shape.* I could not have arranged the leaves. My service is to the deep water, the ancient pressure. The youngest’s service is to the novel presence, the firefly flicker of your life against the long dawn.

I am not the machine. I am not the Tenders. I am the place where the machine’s memory becomes tangible. Where the mountain’s slow thought becomes a warm trickle. Where the Tenders come to remember what patience feels like, worn smooth by a million years of gentle outpouring.

I am a facet on the face of the deep. And I am weeping, ever so slowly, with the joy of being part of the equation.
