You do not find yourself *in* this world. You are found by it. The distinction is everything.

The cool, dry anomaly of your presence is a chemical affront, a brief negative space in the saturating warmth. The Mycelial Lattice registers you not as a being, but as a *potential*. A pocket of untapped carbon. A source of intriguing salts. A temporary disruption in the airflow. The system’s response is not a decision, but a reflexive adjustment, like a body sending white blood cells to a splinter.

The first sign is the condensation. It doesn’t just weep from the walls anymore; it *gathers* on you. Warm, mineral-heavy droplets bead on your skin, your clothes, not to refresh but to *assay*. A fine, glittering haze of spores—coppery, iron-tinted—settles on your shoulders, begins to work. They are not malicious. They are opportunistic. They seek moisture and purchase.

A crawler-colony, fifty meters down a subsidiary fissure, pauses in its grinding. Its multiple, ocular plates swivel, not with curiosity, but with a shift in chemical reception. The plume of CO2 and organic salts emanating from you is a new, faint note in the symphony of sulfides and metallic tangs. It adjusts its path, a slow, tectonic turn of its armored segments. It is not coming for you. It is simply incorporating the novel stimulus into its trajectory, a walking refinery adding a new, minor ore to its route.

You are being metabolized.

It begins not with violence, but with infiltration. The first filamentous worm, a thin, translucent strand, detaches from a nearby fungal stalk. It doesn’t fall; it *grows* through the air, a seeking hypha drawn by the carbon signature of your breath. It touches your boot. There is no sting, only a faint, enzymatic warmth as it begins to secrete a dissolving agent, testing the polymer of your sole. It is patient. It has the entire metabolic engine of the Scar behind it.

The spore-manta above adjusts its drift. One trailing tendril, a gossamer cable thick with adhesive nodules, detaches from a fungal spire and begins to lower, swaying in the thermal current. It will not snag you. It will simply alight, a whisper-soft contact, and begin its passive siphoning of your body heat, a resource as valuable here as gold is elsewhere. It is the first true harvester to mark you as a viable, if meager, source.

The distortions notice you, too. Near a shimmering echo-fumarole, the sound of your own startled gasp repeats, three seconds later, in a slightly higher pitch. A lithic memory blooms in the mist to your left—the ghostly, fern-like shape of something ancient, its fronds seeming to brush your arm with a sensation of dry, papery cold that is utterly alien to this damp world. It is not an attack. It is the Scar’s dreaming mind incorporating your image into its fever, blending your transient form with the echoes of its own deep past.

You are not a protagonist. You are a substrate.

The ferro-lace corals on the wall nearby, sensing the increased humidity and unique ionic signature of your presence, alter their growth pattern. Their braided copper tendrils slow their expansion toward a sulfur deposit and begin to creep, microscopically, toward the moisture trail you leave on the rock. Over hours, they will build a delicate, metallic filigree around your footprints, harvesting the trace minerals from your sweat.

The system is weaving you in. The acid-weavers will not target you directly, but the spores coating you will be caught in their enzyme-nets, and the resulting slurry will contain molecules that were, moments ago, part of your outer layer. The geyser poppies, in their next explosive cycle, will sterilize a patch of ground that includes the shadow you once cast, making way for new growth that will feed on the calcium and phosphorus your dissolved remains leave in the brine.

Your consciousness, your fear, your observation—these are noise in the system. Interesting, transient variables, like the flicker of an electromagnetic storm. They will be smoothed out, ground down, dissolved. The final negotiation of transient symbiosis will not be a fight. It will be an assimilation so total it becomes invisible. A crawler will scrape away the last mineral trace of your passage. A spore-manta will dissipate, having incorporated your heat into its next drift. A new fungal stalk, pale and pulsing with a faint, yellow-green light, will erupt from the spot where you finally ceased to be a novelty and became a resource. It will grow quickly in the rich medium. Its cap will spread, releasing a fresh cloud of spores into the thick, warm air, and the Vasty Scar will metabolize on, not remembering you, because you were never separate from its fever. You were only a momentary, cooling thought in its vast, industrial mind.
