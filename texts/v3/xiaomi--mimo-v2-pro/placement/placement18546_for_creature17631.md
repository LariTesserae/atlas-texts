I am Keth’lin of the Deep-Root Clan, and I wake to the voice of the world. It is not a sound one hears with the ears alone, but a pressure in the gill-slits, a cool, steady rhythm against the nictitating membrane. The rain. It is the first thought, the last breath, the constant companion. Today, it falls with a particular patience, a steady, weighty hiss that speaks of slow growth and deep saturation.

My platform, a broad, living knee of the Grandmother Root, is slick and dark with the morning’s moisture. The woven root-fiber mat beneath me is warm from my body, a temporary island of dryness. I roll to my side, and my secondary lungs drink from the air, tasting the wet earth, the sharp green of the moss that lines my shelter’s walls, the faint, sweet decay of last season’s leaf-thatch. The primary breath follows, a deeper pull. My six-fingered hands, the extra joints bending with fluid ease, press flat against the wood. I feel the slow, subsonic pulse of the tree, a vibration older than my clan’s memory. It is calm. The water channels are flowing true.

Today is a listening day. Not a shaping day, not a gardening or a weaving day, but a deep listening. My Rain-Reader, old Selah, whose skin is the pale grey of sun-bleached bark and whose eyes hold the depth of a still pool, believes a shift is coming. Not in the rain—it never stops—but in the *way* it falls. A subtle change in the chorus.

I eat a strip of smoked fish from a Resin-Glass jar, its clarity so perfect the pale flesh seems to float in the gloom. Then, I take up my staff. It is not a tool for walking, but for hearing. Fashioned from the heartwood of a lightning-struck sapling, it is hollow, filled with seeds and sand, and capped at both ends with polished stone. A rain-rattle.

I move, not walking, but flowing along the slick root-ways. My webbed feet find their grip without conscious thought, the extra joint in my toes curling around ridges invisible to a stranger’s eye. Below, the tannin water swirls, a dark mirror reflecting the grey sky. A glasswinged bird, a flicker of transparent motion, darts past my head, silent as a thought. I do not startle. Its path was known to me by the slight disturbance in the air current against my ear.

I head for the Listening Chamber, a great hollow where three primary roots diverge, forming a natural amphitheater that funnels the forest’s sounds. Others of the clan are already there, perched on ledges and platforms. We do not speak. Our language of glottal stops and soft vowels would be a shout here. We listen.

I find my place, a high niche where a curtain of aerial roots provides a screen. I extend my staff, its tip resting on the wood, and close my outer eyelids. The nictitating membrane clears, and the world becomes a place of pure vibration. The hiss of rain on a million leaves above. The deep, resonant *plonk… plonk…* of heavy droplets falling from the highest canopy into the central pool. The chittering of insects within the bark. The distant, booming croak of a channel-frog, a sound felt in the chest before it is heard.

And then, I hear it. A faint dissonance. A tiny, high-pitched *tik-tik-tik*, faster than the rain’s rhythm, coming from the northwest quadrant. It is the sound of a new vine, a *sharp-tooth creeper*, finding a hold on a tertiary root that supports the Weavers’ Platform. Unchecked, it will grow thick as my thigh in two seasons, its tendrils prying apart the woven fibers, creating a weakness. A potential illness for the Cathedral.

I do not move. I mark it in my memory, a tiny discord in the great symphony. I will tell Selah. Then, later, on a shaping day, I will go to that place. I will not rip the vine—that would be a violent, noisy act that scars the wood. I will sing to it, a low, thrumming chant in the root-language, and gently guide its new shoots onto a deadfall branch propped nearby. I will divert its hunger, not kill it. It is a living part of the lung, too. It simply needs to be shown where to breathe.

This is my purpose. Not to build, but to *listen* to the building. Not to command, but to *converse* with the growth. I am a nerve ending in the skin of this world, feeling the places where the slow, patient pulse grows strained. The rain is its voice, the water its blood, the roots its bones. And I, Keth’lin, am the quiet thought that moves through it, my life a single, deliberate note in the endless, hissing roar. I settle deeper into my niche, the rattle quiet in my hand, and let the Cathedral’s voice wash through me. There is no hurry. There is only the now, and the listening, and the slow, green, endless breath of home.
