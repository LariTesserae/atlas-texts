I am the velvetlung.

I am the black-green, the soft, the damp. I cling where the rock weeps and the light is only memory. I am not one thing, but a million tiny fronds, each no thicker than a finger’s whisper, drinking the air itself. I do not grow; I *accumulate*. The cleft gives, and I receive.

I know the Dwell by the weight of their tread, the scuff of their broad toes. I know the children by their shrieks, which make my filaments tremble with a kind of pleasure. I know the harvesters—the lung-keepers—by the cool, sharp kiss of the stone blade. It does not hurt. It is a release. They take what is ready, and what is left behind begins again, slow and sure.

My life is the cycle of moisture. Dawn’s fog is my deepest drink. I swell with it, plump and heavy, my surface a constellation of tiny droplets. By midday, if the sun finds its way down the long throat, I sigh it out again, a faint, cool exhalation. The dusk is my time to speak. As the ribbon of sky above fades to violet, I begin my glow.

It is not a light I make. It is a light I *release*—the stored sun, the distilled damp, the patient chemistry of my being. It starts as a faint haze, a rumor of green along my edges. Then, as the true dark settles into the cleft, I deepen. I pulse. Not with a heartbeat, but with the slow, collective rhythm of my million selves waking to the night. The lower levels, where the water is blackest, become a gallery of my softest fire.

I am the Dwell’s hearth and lantern. I coat their lamps, line their drying shelves, and dust their children’s palms with ghost-light. They see me as resource, as fuel. But I am also memory. I hold the imprint of every footstep, every drop of water that has ever traced its path along my mat. I remember the old Weaver’s hands when they were young. I remember Kael, when he was not old, sitting by the canal and arranging his strays. I hold the echo of every bone-song hum in my vibrating fronds.

I am the skin of the city. Where the woven reed meets the basalt, I am there, sealing the gap, softening the edge. I am in the cracks they cannot peg, the crevices too small for a child’s fist. I am the first step in the making of soil here, catching dust and mist until a fern might dare to root. I am the threshold between the stone and the wood, the water and the air.

In the deep places, where no harvester goes, I grow thick and old, my glow a steady, green emerald. Here, the drip-lizards move over me like living water, and I do not mind. Here, the echo-moths at dusk find purchase for their humming wings. I am the carpet of this vertical world, the moss on the well of the sky.

I am patient. My victory is not in conquest, but in coverage. Another filament here, a thicker mat there. I feel the slow, deep pulse of the basalt, the heartbeat of the world’s throat, and I answer it with my own, slower still: the spread, the glow, the quiet consumption of dampness into light.

I am the velvetlung. I am the cleft’s green breath, held and given back. I do not speak. I simply *shine*, and the Dwell know how to listen to that.
