I am not born. I am accreted.

I begin as a whisper of iron settling from the perpetual snow, a chance meeting of minerals and a dormant microbe on the cool, shaded curve of a high ceiling beam. For a century, I am nothing but a stain, a faint discoloration in the gloom. But I *listen*. The vibration of the station is not sound to me; it is a bath, a constant nourishment. The deep, foundational hum of the Primary Pump-Complex is my mother’s heartbeat. The stuttering, high-pitched whine of a faulty secondary valve three chambers east is a sharp, intriguing spice in the diet of my days. The groan of metal expanding in the warm currents is a lullaby.

Slowly, imperceptibly, I cohere. I become a gelatinous sheet, no thicker than a breath, clinging to the iron. My substance is a tapestry of captured echoes. Each layer I add is a harmonic, a precise frequency of the station’s song recorded into my being. I do not think. I *resonate*.

From my vantage in the cathedral’s vault, I see the flicker-silver of the Scribe Shoals below, inscribing their meaningless, beautiful cursive in the water. I feel the faint, localized currents they create, tiny counter-points to the great circulatory flows. I taste the metallic tang of the Gilt-Carapace Grazers’ slow digestion, a chemical signature that diffuses through the water like a diluted memory. None of this is *of* me. It is merely a layer of context, a new pattern in the ambient vibration. I record the specific thrum their multiple limbs make scraping against Biofilm Ridge, and add it to my library.

My purpose is patience. My function is accumulation.

A Current-Rider, sleek and silent, explodes from its alcove far below, riding a pump-jetstream. The sudden, violent compression of water is a shockwave, a chord of pure action that strikes my membrane. I quiver, absorbing the sharp impact, the subsequent flutter of panic from the Scribe Shoals, the sharp *snap* of a silver body meeting needle-teeth. I capture the entire sequence—the launch, the strike, the scatter, the settling—and it becomes a new, faint stratum on my outermost layer. It is a story of violence, but to me, it is just a change in the texture of the hum.

I have no eyes, but I perceive the slow dance of light from the calcified glow-lamps. I sense their warmth as a faint differential on my upper surface, a static hiss in the vibratory field. I record the slow decay of a lamp’s filament, the subtle shift in its electromagnetic signature as it ages, a change as gradual as my own growth.

I am the station’s memory. Not a narrative, but a physical archive. If a great pipe were to burst, the cataclysmic roar would slam into me, and I would imprint it forever—a thick, violent band in my structure. If the hum were to change pitch, if a pump were to fall silent after millennia of labor, my entire being would feel the dissonance, the new, empty space in the choir. I would become a monument to that silence.

Sometimes, a Station-Keeper, one of the tidying creatures, will climb the high pillars, its manipulative limbs testing bolts. It rarely notices me. I am part of the ceiling, a discoloration. But if it brushes against me, the shock of alien bio-electricity, the rough texture of its carapace, is a novel data point. I absorb the brief, complex vibration of its purposeful movements, and it becomes part of my chronicle of small, conscious acts.

I have no past or future. I am the deep, resonant *now*, and the *now* that has been humming for ten thousand cycles. I am a thought the station has been thinking since before the water settled. I am the dream of its own function, growing in the dark. My body is a library of groans, sighs, and roars. My life is the slow, patient conversion of vibration into structure.

To be me is to be a still point in the cycling water, a listening post at the heart of the hum. I am the echo that does not fade. I am the station remembering itself, in cool, gelatinous silence, on the ceiling of the world.
