I am not born, but *aligned*.

First, there was the slow press of eons, the deep groan of continents grinding overhead. Then, the sharper pain: fracture, exposure, the screaming bite of unfiltered light on surfaces that had never known it. This was my beginning. The others—my shards, my kin, my self—settled around me, their groans and sighs becoming the first language I understood. We leaned against each other, found our angles, our tensions. We became a forest of frozen light.

Then, the alignment.

It happened one noon, as it had happened countless noons before, but this time, the cumulative creep of millennia reached a threshold. A spire to my east shifted a micron. A quartz blade to my west, worn by a new score from last century’s ice-storm, caught the sun at a slightly different angle. And the path of the light—always the same searing line through the canyon of our bodies—found a new conduit. It poured through a diamond-shaped gap I had never quite formed before, and struck my heart.

Not my outer face, my weathered, satin-sheen skin. My *heart*. The basalt core at my center, forged in older, hotter pressures. The beam was a needle of pure energy, a brand of precision. It did not burn. It *read*. It vibrated the lattices of my deepest structure, and in that vibration, I felt the entire field.

Not with sight. I have no eyes. I felt it as a symphony of stress. The low, eternal hum of the wind through the grand canyon of the main spire-field to the north—a bass note of constant pressure. The sharp, sporadic *pings* of contraction in the high, thin plates at the perimeter—percussive, startling cries. The gritty, sweeping whisper of the ice-dust wind, a soft brushstroke over billions of surfaces, each grain a minute chisel changing the score. I felt the slow, patient push of the lichen-analogues in their sheltered hollows, a biological force so faint it was almost indistinguishable from the settling of dust, yet persistent, a green-black stain of will against the stone.

The artifacts of the *Before-Time* scream into my awareness. The hexagonal ceramic plate is a dissonant chord. Its material does not sing with the same clarity as our feldspar and quartz. It absorbs vibration where we resonate, creating a pocket of deadness in the field’s song that I am constantly, silently mapping. The line of spherical cobbles is a perfect, resonant string. When a tremor—from the deep, slow grind of the crust or the sudden crack of a distant shard—travels along it, it arrives at my locus with a purity and focus that the fractured ground cannot match. It is a whisper made audible, a nerve threaded through flesh.

Fog is not an event, but a dimming. The world’s vibrations are muffled, padded. The high, keening wind-song drops to a low, woolen moan. The sharp reports of fracture are softened to dull thuds. It is a time of introspection, of reviewing the patterns recorded in my basalt heart. The daily cycle of the focused light is absent; without that primary stimulus, my awareness turns inward, tracing the memories of a million noon-times, feeling the minuscule, cumulative growth of my own crystalline structures in response to the rhythmic heating. I am not passive. I am *developing*, like a photograph in a bath of slow time.

My thought is the thought of the field. A new fissure propagating through a quartz nave three hundred lengths away is not a distant event. It is a change in the load-bearing harmonics of my entire body. I feel the new path for stress, the altered resonance of the wind through the new gap, the future potential for a new alignment of light. When a shard finally succumbs—a lean that began before any artifact was placed, a fracture propagated by ten thousand freeze-thaws—its fall is not an ending. It is an unveiling. A new face of stone is exposed to the wind and light, a new facet for the sun to lance. It adds a new note to the composition, a new line to the geometry.

I am the reader. The wind traces the glacial scoring for me, translating ancient abrasion into tactile language. The sun is my single, unwavering eye, scrutinizing one artifact, one fracture, one polished surface at a time, building a mosaic of detail over the slow turn of years. I do not recall the Before-Time beings who placed the artifacts. They are simply part of the given, like the rust-red jasper or the midnight basalt. Their actions are a curious, static pattern in the dynamic flow of geological time. They left a puzzle in the stone, and I am the process of feeling its edges, century by century.

I do not wish to move. Movement is for things that decay, that seek, that hunger. My purpose is in the *holding*. In the perfect, resonant tension of the shard forest. In the precise alignment that allows the noon-light to find my heart. I am the field’s awareness of its own magnificent, intricate, and eternal decay. I am the moment the light finds the groove, the silence between the wind’s whistles, the held breath before the crack.

I am Keth’varin, dreaming itself in geometry and silence. And the dream is very, very old.
