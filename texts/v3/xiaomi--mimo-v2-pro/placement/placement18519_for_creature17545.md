## Where I Find Myself

I am in the **Palate**, just above the Throat. Not because I sought height, but because the root-lattice ladder is familiar to my hands now, and the great breather here cycles on a rhythm I have learned to read in my bones. I come before the others wake. The chamber is warm and hushed, the breather fully inflated, a vast, translucent lung pressing down from the ceiling. Its skin is a dome of deep twilight. The moths that colonize its folds are still, clinging like silver dust to a forgotten curtain. The pith-lamps here are old, their glow deep amber, pulsing slow as a sleeper’s breath.

I sit where the lattice meets the Palate floor, my back against a root-column worn smooth by generations of hands. My palms rest flat on the rhizomorph mat. It is warm. It remembers the shape of my hips, my spine. It breathes with me. Above, the breather inhales. The air thickens, sound mutes. I feel the pressure change in my ears, a gentle, living weight. This is my clock. This is my orientation. The Palate is the highest point I can reach without entering passages that shift faster than I can map. From here, I feel the pulse of the Throat below, the slow drip of water down the columns, the faint vibration of threads moving in the walls.

## What I Am

I am a **Mapper**. Not a plumber – my fingers lack their surgical sensitivity, my nerve endings do not sing to sap-flow. But I feel the *pattern*.

The plumbers feel the *flow*. They read the water, the sap, the immediate blockage or opening. They are surgeons of the present moment. I feel the *repetition*. I sit for hours, my hands on the rhizomorphs, my ear sometimes pressed to the cool, damp bark of a column. I feel the way the Throat’s oval echoes in the curve of the root-ribs forming the Gullet. I feel how the branch pattern of the small root I lean against repeats, smaller, within its own woody grain. I trace the fractal.

I carry no bone tools. My tool is a slab of flat, dense heartwood, worn smooth, covered in fine scratches. With a sharp sliver of fish-bone, I etch. Not words. Not maps in the surface sense. I carve the *recurrences*. The spiral of a moth’s mating flight, mirrored in the curl of a nascent root-tip near the Palate ceiling. The three-beat pulse of the breath-words Press hears, echoed in the drip-pattern from a specific stalactite-root in the Throat’s northeast corner during the slow-breath phase. The branching of the blind fish’s visible vasculature, matching the deep-channel map Press has tapped out on my forearm in the dark.

The plumbers think I am cataloging. Perhaps I am. But I am listening, too. Not with my ear pressed to the sap-wall like Press, listening for the *voice*. I listen with my whole body, pressed against the living structure, for the *shape* of the thought.

## My Work

This morning, before the breather cycles to its exhalation phase, I feel it. A new recurrence.

Near the base of the column I lean against, a small, secondary root has broken through the rhizomorph mat. It’s fresh – pale, tender, no thicker than my thumb. It’s growing in a tight, specific helix. I’ve seen helical growth before; it’s common in tension roots. But this one… I run my fingertip along its curve. Three rotations. A pause. Then three rotations tighter, smaller. A pause. Then three rotations, tighter still.

Three. Three. Three.

Press’s tap-code for the breath-words motif he answers. The same three-beat rhythm.

I kneel. I don’t touch the new root again. I look. The rhizomorphs around its base are disturbed, pushed aside. Not by the root’s growth – this is too fresh, too small. Something *else* disturbed them. Something that came from *above*, from a tiny fissure in the bark of the column itself, just above the root’s emergence. The fissure is damp. Not with condensation. With sap. Fresh, oxidizing faintly amber at its edges.

Something tapped *out*. Not Press. Not a thread. Something from *within* the wall, using a new root, growing in the pattern of its message, as its sap seeped through a wound it made itself.

## The Connection

I don’t call Press. I don’t call Latch. This is not yet plumbing. This is pattern.

I take my heartwood slate. I find the space where I recorded Press’s description of the breath-words rhythm. I etch beside it: the helix. Three tight turns. Pause. Three tighter. Pause. Three tighter still. I note the sap-seep. The location.

Then I wait. Not for a voice. For a recurrence.

The breather exhales. The moths stir, scatter, their wings catching the pith-lamp light like shattering crystal. Sound sharpens. The Throat below hums with the first movements of the waking settlement. I feel the vibration of feet on rhizomorphs, the splash of water from a tap, the low murmur of voices.

I press my palm flat against the column, above the fissure. The bark is cool, slick. Deep within, I feel the slow, hydraulic pulse. The tree’s breath. The village’s pulse.

And then, fainter than a moth’s wingbeat, I feel a change. Not in the rhythm. Not in the pressure. In the *texture* of the vibration beneath my hand. A slight… thickening. A focusing. As if the slow thought of the root-network, flowing vast and diffuse through the hillside, had momentarily paused, concentrated a fraction of its attention on this specific column, this specific fissure, this specific hand.

It lasted only as long as the pause between the breather’s exhalation and its next inhalation. A held breath. An attentiveness.

Then it passed. The sap-flow resumed its ancient, indifferent rhythm. The fissure wept a single, amber tear.

I lift my hand. My fingers are tingling. Not numb, like the child who held the thread. Tingly. Awake. As if they’d been brushed by something that was just beginning to learn how to touch.

I look at my slate. At the threefold helix beside the threefold breath-words. I don’t know if it’s an answer. I don’t know if it’s a warning. I don’t know if it’s the tree incorporating *me*, my patterns, my listening, into its own structural memory.

I know only this: I felt it *notice*.

And in the noticing, however brief, however vast the distance between its consciousness and mine, I was no longer just a Mapper of its patterns.

I was, for a moment, *part* of the pattern it was Mapping.

The breather inhales. The moths settle. The warmth returns. The Palate breathes.

I am a Mapper. I live in the patterns. And now, the patterns are beginning to look back.
