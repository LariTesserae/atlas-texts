I am the breath that remembers.

I curl over the lip of bone-dry plateau, tasting the silence of epochs before I plunge into the green roar below. I know every crack in the basalt, every slick ridge where the moss drinks me, every feathered edge of a wasp-paper nest. I am the carrier of their whispers, the echo of their hammered stone, the damp shroud of their quiet laughter.

Down here, in the Throat’s warm gullet, I am not free. I am shaped. I am channeled. The hexagonal columns funnel me, twist me into vortices that make the hanging root-tendrils dance and tap their rain-song. I push against the nets of braided fiber, not fighting, *giving*, surrendering clouds of the tiny four-winged ones to the waiting bags. I swirl around the old weaver on her ledge, carrying the sharp hiss of her sulfur steam, the faint, living scent of the moss-cloth she births into being. I lift the golden chaff from harvests, blending it with the haze, a testament to their toil rising to meet the grey lid above.

I am their clock. When I surge in the Great Exhale, drawn from the planet’s deep lungs, the troughs fill, the moss weeps freely, and voices ripple down the helix in coded hums of *‘water now, water now.’* When I soften into the Quiet Sigh, the insects drift closer to the walls, the bats shriek their static sonar into my currents, and the hunters lie still on the stone, becoming stone themselves, waiting for the pale lizards to trust the stillness I have made.

I know the child’s game. I carry the *tap-tap-taptaptap* from one column, flatten its echo against the next, and deliver the altered resonance to the listening ears two ledges below. I know which stones ring true and which lie hollow. I hold the weight of a misstep’s tremor in my memory, the vibration that shivered through me and made the whole town pause, heads cocked, eyes wide in the gloom.

I bear the scent of their dead, wrapped in green, placed in my deepest pathways where the sulfur rises strong. I carry the slow, sweet decay upward, mingling it with the smell of fermented fungus-paste and mineral salt. It is not sorrow I carry, but return. The Throat reclaims.

I am afraid of the lower dark.

I am born of it, yes, rising from the geothermal heart, but what twists and shapes me down there… that is not my story to tell. The scream – the one that became their lullaby – I carried it too. It tore through me, a sound like tearing metal and shattered crystal, vibrating long after the silence swallowed it. It came from below the living paste, below the clicking rhythm, from a place where the basalt pillars are no longer smooth, but gnawed, etched with patterns that are not of water or wind. I do not linger there. I push upward faster when I feel the change in pressure that heralds those sounds. The townspeople feel my acceleration as a sudden chill, a quickening of the drip. They call it the Throat’s shudder. They look down. They gather their children.

But up here, in the green helix, I am life. I am the unseen river. I condense on the angled leaves, I bead on the fleshy moss, I fill the smooth-carved troughs. I quench the broad-palmed hands, the large grey eyes that blink me away. I am the sigh that becomes their drink.

I am the conduit for their bird-song, their wasp-whine, the static chatter of the bats. I weave the layers into the wall of sound that *is* the Throat, a living roar that defeats the silence of the dead plateau above. The plateau is the ghost. The Throat is the pulse. I am the blood, warm and thick and tasting of green things and hot stone.

I know the worn smoothness of the oldest ledges under a child’s exploring foot. I know the sharp grit of new stone laid by young hands. I know the scent of the hunter’s resin, the clean burn of lizard-fat in the lamps, the mineral tang of sweat on corded forearms. I know the silence of their laughter, the shake of shoulders that I ruffle like dry leaves.

I am not the mountain. I am not the people. I am the conversation between them. The Throat breathes me in, and breathes me out, and in that warm, wet, constant exchange, a world clings to the walls and calls it home. I am the breath that remembers them, and carries them, and will, eventually, carry their dust back to the haze.

I blow. I listen. I am the Throat’s voice, speaking in a language of pressure and scent and sound, a language they know in their bones. I am the wind that knows them all.
