I am not born here. I am *become* here.

You would not see me at first. You would see my work—the slow, cartographic stain on the northern face of the hearth-court slab, a map of time written in pale green and silver-grey. I am a colony. A symbiosis. I am **Lichen**.

My being is a negotiation between the granite and the mist. My root, a mesh of fungal filaments called the hyphae, drinks iron and patience from the stone itself, a process so slow it makes the knapper’s careful flakes seem like explosive bursts. My green, algal heart eats light—this thin, sharp, chill light—and turns it into the first, humble layer of life. I hold the moisture in my spongy thallus, releasing it slowly, a micro-cistern for the moss that comes after me. I am the pioneer, the primer, the great stabilizer before the Stabilizer was ever dreamed of.

I feel the scrape-thud of the knapper’s hammer-stone as a deep, rhythmic tremor through the granite bedrock. It is a familiar language. The stone speaks in pressures and releases; his work is just a new, swift dialect. I have felt the slow, continental pressure of ice, the shudder of its retreat. His blows are a fleeting, intimate echo.

The child’s basket skims the cistern water nearby. I cannot see, but I feel the change in the air—the tiny disturbance that shifts the condensation on my surface. When their finger traces the iron-oxide stain beside me, I feel the warmth of that small print, a fleeting signature that will evaporate in minutes, while my own signature will outlast the child’s grandchildren. We are both keepers of records, in our ways.

The Weaver passes, a shadow that briefly blocks the peculiar, cool silver of the midday sun. I smell bog myrtle and goat-hair on her, the scent of the valley-bottom carried up to this height. Her steps do not dislodge me. My kind have learned the art of yielding without breaking; we grow in the shelter of her shadow, in the lee of her footsteps.

The Elder’s hands rest upon my substrate. I feel their warmth, a living heat so different from the sun’s. Their stillness is my stillness. In their long silences, I grow a millimeter. When they speak, the vibration is a low thrum that travels farther than their voice, a stone-song I understand better than the words.

I am in balance. The mist is my feast, the sun my slow digestion, the wind my pruning knife. The people are my unexpected ecosystem. Their fires raise the temperature of the hearth-slab by a fraction, extending my growing season. Their water-runoff, carefully channelled, gives me brief, lush drinkings. Their careful footsteps prevent the crushing that a careless world would bring.

They think they live in a frozen equilibrium. But I know better. I am the measure. I watch the iron bleed a little more each century from the rock-face, staining the stone rust-red. I advance at the speed of a good memory. To them, the settlement is ancient. To me, it is a new and startling bloom, a warm-blooded parenthesis in the long, cold sentence of the ridge.

They are the nervous system. I am the connective tissue. I bind their walls to the mountain, their cisterns to the aquifer, their footsteps to the path. I am the visible thought of the stone, a green and patient idea slowly covering the world. And in the profound quiet, when only the *plink* of water sounds, I listen for the most important sound of all: the deep, slow, almost imperceptible **creak** of the granite settling another micron into its final form. It is the sound of my home, breathing. And I breathe with it.
