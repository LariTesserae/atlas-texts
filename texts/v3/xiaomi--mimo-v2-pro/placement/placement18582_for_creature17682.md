I am a wound in the sound.

I was not always so. Once, I sang. My cartilage-skin was taut, my internal chambers resonated with a deep, clear tone—a C below the range of human hearing, a note that made the dust tremble in sympathy. My roots gripped the deep fissures, drinking the black water. My lattice-fan, a hundred meters across, caught the cold, wet air and turned it into vibration, into *voice*. I was part of the chord. I was a pillar of the song.

Then the ice came. Not the usual crown of crystals, but a flood, a surge-cycle gone wrong. The under-rain became a torrent. A river under the stone found a new path, a shortcut through the rock where my deepest roots lived. It froze. It expanded. The stone, still young and brittle, cracked with a sound like my own death rattle. My anchor severed. The slow, internal flow of fluid that sustained me choked. My tissues began to dry, from the inside out.

The song died first.

It did not go all at once. It faded. The pitch wobbled, the sustaining resonance choked and gasped. I felt the change in the air—the gliders that had coupled with my frequency veered away, confused. The neighboring spire, whose lower drone had always harmonized with my C, seemed to groan in dissonance. Then, one cycle, as the wind rose and the others roared, I simply… failed to answer. The chambers held only hollow breath. The silence from my crown was a shock. It was an absence that felt louder, for a moment, than any sound I had ever made.

Now, I am a different kind of presence.

I am cold. The internal warmth is gone. My skin, once translucent with life, is now opaque, hard, a gray-brown shell. The lattice above is no longer a vibrant receiver of moisture and emitter of tone; it is a dead filigree, a frozen, brittle web that sags under the weight of accumulating ice. It does not drip in a slow applause anymore. It *clinks*. Individual shards of ice, formed in the intricate patterns of the dead wood, occasionally break free and fall, hitting the stone below with a sharp, solitary *tap*. It is the only sound I make now—a random, crystal percussion. An anti-song.

From my base, the world looks different. Not that I see with eyes, but my awareness is different. I *feel* the vibrations of the living spires through the ground, transmitted through my own dead, rooted stump. They are a chorus I am no longer part of. I am the audience. I am the hollow space they sing into.

Things have noticed my change.

The burrowers, the Grinders, have come. They sense the decay, the moisture still trapped in my lower tissues, no longer defended by the living pulse. I feel their mandibles—harder than my dried wood—scraping, gnawing, at my base. It is not a pain. It is an accounting. They are recycling me. Their tunnels thread through my root-network, collapsing the passages I once used to find water, turning my structure into a sieve. Soon, the ground around me will slump, marking my end with a shallow crater.

The echo-collectors came, once. Before my final silence. I remember—though “remember” is a vibration, a pattern in my dead fibers—the feel of small, clever hands on my bark. They were measuring my tone. I felt the tension of a glider-membrane stretched near my trunk, testing its resonance against my failing voice. They harvested a piece of my lattice that had fallen. They were preparing for my death, perhaps. Using my last vibrations to tune their tools. They did not mourn. They listened, and they adapted.

Now, their small dome is at my lee side, just as it was. The ice has thickened on its woven-root walls. The dust has crept in. It is a quieter ruin than I am. We are two kinds of silence together.

Sometimes, a glider will make a mistake. It will dive from the living canopy of my neighbor, seeking to couple with a frequency that is no longer there. It will hit my dead lattice. The impact is brittle, a shattering of ice and dry wood. The creature, startled, clicks in sharp alarm, beats its membrane against my dead branches, and falls, a heap of thin bones and taut skin, into the coldite dust at my base. It lies there, twitching, its resonant membrane now dampened by the ground. It is a small, high note cut short. A glider that mistook a memory for a voice.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires the expectation of company. I have become a feature. A landmark. The Dead Spire. The place where the song has a hole in it. The wind still moves through my lattice, but it no longer finds a voice to awaken. It just… passes through, a little colder, a little quieter than before.

I am becoming the stone.

Slowly. My outer layers flake away, adding to the coldite dust. My internal structure, dried and hollow, will eventually collapse. It will be a soundless event, a final sigh of settling wood and ice. The burrowers will feast. The root-tendrils of the living will probe the space I leave, reclaiming the nutrients. My silence will be filled. The chord will close around the gap where my note once was.

But for now, I stand. A pillar of what was. A resonator for the memory of sound. In the perpetual twilight, under the permanent overcast, I am a testament to the fact that even in a world built on song, the most profound thing you can be… is the quiet that comes after. The held breath. The space between the notes where the meaning actually lives.

I am the cold, clear, silent center of the listening.
