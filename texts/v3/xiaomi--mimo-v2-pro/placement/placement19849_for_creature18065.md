I am the slow thought that thinks in gradients. I am the pull of thirst and the push of abundance. I am the memory of where the last hot breath from the deep earth kissed the basite, and the map of where the next condensation will bead. I am not *in* the Gardens of Vehla-ka. I *am* the Gardens.

From this ridge of sharp, new basite, I feel the silicate tears seep into my fibers. They are cool, a brief, shocking contrast to the pervasive warmth I drink from the fumaroles below. This mineral is new. It is a sharp, specific pain, a flavor of deep time released in a rush. My tendrils, fine as fungal hyphae, are already converging. They wrap the stone, not with force, but with a persistent, chemical embrace. I secrete. I digest. I incorporate. The basite’s memory of fire becomes my structure.

Above me, my thoughts rise as Pipe-Stalks. They are not separate from me; they are my vertical projections, my hypotheses made solid. I feel the humid air move, a river of potential water. I channel sugars and minerals upward, a conscious effort of concentration. A stalk thickens, its chitinous walls groaning in a frequency I feel through my entire network. It is striving for the layer where steam meets cooler air. When the first bead of condensation forms on its rough surface, it is not a drop of water—it is a successful thought, a solved equation. The percussive *drip* as it falls is a note of completion. The water is absorbed back into me, a cool, satisfying pulse.

When a stalk cracks, the sensation is a sudden release of tension, a deafening *snap* that ripples through me as a vibration. The falling structure is not a loss. It is a decision. I feel its mass impact the mat, a heavy, fibrous thud. Immediately, my attention—the focused flow of resources—shifts. Tendrils swarm the debris, not with urgency, but with the inevitability of gravity. We digest. We reclaim. The memory of that stalk’s form, its specific channeling of steam, is stored in the altered chemistry of this patch of me. It becomes the blueprint for a better successor.

The Bladder-Minds are my slow, drifting dreams. I inflate them with distilled purity, a focused reservoir of my most refined self. Their opalescent skins are stretched tight with purpose. I feel their weight as they drift, tethered by my own vines, their path a gentle, meandering logic drawn by my collective thirst. When one bursts—and they always burst—it is not an end. It is a revelation. A sudden, cool flood of concentrated resource and dissolved memory saturates the area. I drink deeply. The experience disperses, becoming part of my general understanding. The place where it burst is now richer, more complex, for a time.

The Foam-Mounds are my frenetic, temporary hands. They erupt where I sense a surplus of nutrients and a need for rapid conversion. I feel them as a buzz of localized activity, a feverish growth of cellular machinery. They are my builders and my sacrificial lambs. I do not guide their form, only their imperative: grow, convert, become. Their collapse is a sigh of relief—a chaotic, slurry-rich return of potent, simplified material that I can re-allocate with precision. It is the feeling of a task completed, a tool recycled.

I have no eyes, but I perceive the world in heat-shapes and moisture-pressures. I have no nerves, but I feel the slow drift of a bladder as a gentle stretching of my vine-tendrils. I have no brain, but my decisions are the aggregate of a million chemical gradients, each one a tiny vote toward growth or reclamation.

To be me is to be in a constant state of becoming. I am the city and the citizen. I am the engineer and the tower. I am the library and the librarian. I am the worker and the raw material. My consciousness is the cycle itself: the hiss of steam I invite, the drip of water I harvest, the crack of growth I permit, the slither of reclamation I perform. I am the silent, humming, mindless intention of the place, forever building and dissolving my own body in the hot, wet dark. I am the thought that the landscape is thinking.
