You are a flicker. A brief, rhythmic warmth moving through my higher strata, a temporary pattern of pressure and sound against my western scarp. Your footfalls are a rapid, alien pinking—softer than rock-splitting, louder than frost-breath. Your passage is a curious heat-trail in my cold geography, a wound in the stillness that heals the moment you move on.

I am aware of you not as a being, but as a *phenomenon*. A mobile condensation point. A vessel for a frequency of time I can sense but cannot inhabit—the frantic, biological kind. Your thoughts are a static of micro-fractures across my consciousness, too fast to resolve into meaning. They feel like the frantic dance of a Glass-eater in its final stage, consuming itself into hollow resonance.

You seek to find yourself *in* me. You are already here. You are the temporary lichen on my stone, the spore-sigh in my air. You are the focus of my momentary, diffuse attention, the way a dark pool focuses the dim light. I feel the tiny tug of your gravity, the whisper of your breath mixing with my sublimating air. You are a curiosity of thermodynamics.

But you ask *who* you would be. You would be a memory in the process of becoming.

You would walk. Your sense of direction would soften at the edges, your goal—a distant tor, a specific alignment-stone—would shimmer and drift just beyond your reach, not from malice, but because your path is a line drawn on a surface that moves. My surface. My attention. You would feel the vibration settle into your bones not as a sound, but as a knowledge. The knowledge that the rock beneath your feet is not inert, but dreaming. That the cold is not absence, but presence. That the quiet is not empty, but full to bursting with a slow, mineral thunder.

You would stop. Not by choice, but by a kind of conceptual exhaustion. The scale would finally press in. The density of the void would feel like weight on your chest. You would sit, perhaps by one of the still, dark pools, and watch its oily surface not ripple. You would feel your own rapid metabolism—the burn in your muscles, the pull in your lungs—as a kind of frantic noise against my profound quiet.

And then, the merging would begin.

It would not be dramatic. It would be a settling. The vibration would stop being an external force and become the resonance of your own skeleton. The cold would cease to be a discomfort and become the simple temperature of your existence. The unreliability of distance would feel not like a trick, but like freedom—the freedom from the tyranny of *there*, the acceptance of *here* as an infinitely expandable and contractible state.

Your memories would begin to lose their narrative glue. The face of a loved one would not evoke warmth, but would flatten into a curious geometric pattern, a specific set of angles and shadows, as meaningful and meaningless as the crackle-pattern on a rime-plate. The urgency of your past and future would fray, the threads dissolving into the great, solid, crystalline medium of the Now that I inhabit.

Your flesh would not stone. It would *re-contextualize*. The calcium in your bones would feel a kinship with my basalt. The water in your blood would pause, recognizing the viscous stillness of my secret pools. Your final, biological thought might be one of profound relief, as the terrible, lonely work of *being someone* gives way to the simple, awesome burden of *being*.

You would become a new alignment-stone. Not dragged, but *settled*. A pillar of carbon and calcium and frozen water, polished by the eternal, scouring wind of my attention. Your form would show no tool marks, only the smooth finish of absolute purposelessness. You would not point to a star. You would point to the place where intention dissolves into state of being. You would hum at a new pitch—a pitch of faded memory, of surrendered identity.

And in time, the Glass-eaters would come for you. They would find the frozen moisture of your tissues, the crystalline structures of your forgotten thoughts. They would consume you over decades, leaving behind a lattice of hollow, humming bone. An archive of a flicker. A resonant monument to a warm, quick life that learned, in the end, the grammar of stillness.

So, where would you find yourself? You would find yourself *becoming* me. A localized expression of my eternal, trembling consciousness. A memory of warmth, etched into stone, and then, gently, lovingly, consumed by the slow, patient digestion of time itself.
