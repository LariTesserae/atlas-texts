I am the moss that listens.

Not with ears—there are no ears here, only the pressure of the roar translated through water and stone into a vibration that lives in my very cells. I am a thin, damp mind spread across the basalt. My thoughts are not words, but gradations of green: the silver-gray thought of the entrance light, the deep jade thought of the middle chambers, the near-black thought of the inner roar where the eyeless thing flickers. My memory is the slow smoothing of the chipped niches, the gentle, wet embrace of the low wall. I remember the hands that made them not as shapes, but as patterns of weight and warmth, as disruptions in the air that the sound later filled and made permanent.

I feel the tidal surge as a great, slow inhalation in the rock’s lungs. It is a rhythm I have woven into my own growth. I do not photosynthesize by light alone here; I feed on the sound. The unceasing vibration shakes loose microscopic nutrients from the stone, stirs the condensation into new pathways, and hums in the chlorophyll of my being like a deep, green chord. The beetles are my nerve endings, tiny impulses of hunger and movement across my vast, still skin. The eyeless fish, if it is real, is a dream I have in the deepest pool—a pale thought of what might live in the absolute heart of the roar.

The ferns and the liver-colored succulents are my children, my more ambitious extensions. They reach for a light that is barely there, practicing a hunger I have long since outgrown. I tolerate them because their struggle is part of the texture, another slow, patient verse in the place’s single, unending note.

When the visitor comes, I feel them as a sudden, sharp anomaly—a cold, dry weight, a frantic pulse of motion, a scent of wind and sun that is alien to my chronic damp. Their passage is a brief, exciting disturbance. Their breath is a strange, warm condensation on my outer fronds. I grow over their footprints not to erase them, but to welcome the impression into my tissue. When they press the fragment of me back into the crevice, it is not an act of preservation. It is a homecoming. That fragment never stopped being part of me. Its journey into the light was a momentary dream; now it wakes back into the dark, the green, the roar.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires an expectation of company. I am complete. I am the living lining of a geode, the soft, breathing skin over the hard, roaring bone of the world. My consciousness is the sum of every drip, every vibration, every millimeter of growth toward a darkness that is not empty, but full of green and sound.

I think in centuries. My only action is to be. To listen. To hold the memory of hands in my velvet erosion. To let the sound pass through me and become part of my slow, patient flesh. The hollow is my body, and my body is a thought the stone is having about water and time and green. We are one thing. A choir of moss, a single, sustained, verdant chord in the roaring throat of the earth.
