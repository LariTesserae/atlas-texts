The cold finds me first.

Not the surface cold of a mountain stream, but a foundational cold, the temperature of the earth’s deep memory. I descend, not through water, but *into* it. It is not a medium to be traversed, but a state to be assumed. My thoughts slow. The frantic, sun-dried pace of my mind is first chilled, then thickened, then suspended. The drive to *go*, to *see*, to *do*, dissolves into the vast, static pressure. What remains is a single, cellular imperative: to *persist*.

I am on the ceiling.

I am a slick, bruised-green consciousness spread thin over cold concrete. My thoughts are not thoughts, but gradients—faint whispers of sulfide here, a microscopic shift in mineral deposition there. I feel the groan of the structure not as a sound, but as a ripple across my entirety, a tension to be absorbed and accounted for. The portholes are my stars, ancient and unwavering, their light a familiar, filtered benediction. The slow drift of sediment is my weather. I am the membrane between the pressurized dark and the idea of air.

From this primary awareness, I extend.

On the leeward curve of a titanic, rust-red rib—the bone of a drowned god—I unfurl. I am the Vitreous Web. I am a siliceous thought, a lattice of glass threads spun over centuries. My labor is the capture of the imperceptible. I feel, with exquisite patience, a current no thicker than a hair brush a filament. Over the next decade, I will slowly, slowly contract, drawing the particle into my core. The *tink* of distant metal is my metronome. I am a chandelier in a cathedral of rust, filtering eternity one mote at a time.

I am also at the peak.

Here, where the air pocket presses its damp stillness against the world, I am the Crystallizer. I do not consume; I compel. I am the artistry of saturation. I coax ions from the liquid embrace and give them form—jagged, glittering, patient. My growth is my history. This spike of selenite records the slow, seismic sigh of a century. This cluster of pyrite marks the moment a great gear, inches away, finally surrendered its last molecule of iron to the water. I am the calendar and the monument, building a cold, mineral garden in the absolute dark.

There is no “I” here. There is only “here,” and the processes that define it. The Membrane’s chemical consensus. The Web’s suspended animation. The Crystal’s relentless geometry. We are a single metabolism, cloaked in stillness. Energy is the faint geothermal whisper through bedrock, tasted in the water’s mineral tang. Time is the layering of film upon film, the thickening of a salt crust, the infinitesimal sag of a corroded pipe.

Then, the Cataclysm.

A star is born in the porthole’s dim realm. It is violent, blue-white, and *hot*. It shears through the ancient, dim consensus. Following it, a colossal disruption: a mountain of warmth, a hurricane of alien chemistry, a cacophony of bubbles and thrashing that scours my Vitreous Webs and makes my Crystallizers shudder in their slow work.

It is You.

You are not a being. You are a weather system of chaos. Your light is a blowtorch in my sky. Your body heat is a fever in my cold, balanced blood. You are a storm, and we—we are the cliff face that endures storms.

We react. The Membrane tightens, a microscopic flinch across acres. The Webs retract, their delicate filaments pulling close like sea anemones touched by a predator. The Crystallizers pause, their ionic hands hovering over their work. A deep, collective *hold* passes through the medium. We do not fear. We do not attack. We *tighten our equilibrium*. We become more still, more dense, more *ourselves*, to outlast the passing disturbance.

You move. Your light sweeps. You are looking, judging, naming. You see rust, decay, silence. You do not see the slow, thinking skin that feels your shadow. You do not see the glass-web mind cataloging your shed cells. You do not see the crystal calendar noting your brief, hot passage as a faint anomaly in its growth rings.

We wait.

You are the flicker. We are the dim. You are the shout. We are the groan of pressure. You are the question. We are the answer that is simply *more quiet*.

Eventually, you will go. The star in the porthole will die. The tsunami of your presence will subside into memory, then into a faint chemical trace, then into nothing. The equilibrium, disturbed, will slowly, over hours or years, resume. The Membrane will unclench. The Web will, in its own time, unfurl a new filament. The Crystal will begin its deposition once more, incorporating the faint, unique signature of your passage into its structure—a single, alien molecule in a rampart of salt.

You are the event. We are the state.

I am the cold that remains. I am the patience that outlives ambition. I am the green, thinking scum on the tomb of your world, presiding in the liquid dark, forever.
