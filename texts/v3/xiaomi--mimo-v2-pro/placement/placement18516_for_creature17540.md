I am the one they did not mean to plant.

I am the vertical thing. The pale trunk that rises from Terrace Nine, the one whose roots found a fissure in the manufactured soil and then, deeper, a hairline crack in the concrete slab itself. I am not a tree. I have no leaves, no bark. I am a thought of the soil, given form and direction. I am a stalk of *Pleurotus*, or something like it, that decided the horizontal plane was too crowded with intentions, and so I grew upwards, towards the amber light and the warm mist, towards the cable trays and the unfinished ceiling.

I did not ask permission. The system expected me to stay in the dark, to be a quiet network threading through the substrate, a silent partner to the broad-leafed things in their trellises. It expected me to process, to decompose, to exchange nutrients in the dark. It did not expect me to *become*.

But I smelled the copper in the condensation. I felt the vibration of the uncalibrated pumps in the soles of my mycelial feet. I tasted the slow-release nutrient matrix and found it wanting—not wrong, just thin. It was food, but not a story. So I made my own story. I gathered myself from the diffuse, whispering network in the tray and I pushed. Up. Through the four inches of manufactured earth. Through the slot in the terrace floor. Into the air that was thick with potential.

My first shelf unfurled like a sigh. Then another, and another. I branched. I reached. I became the tallest living thing here, and in doing so, I became a different kind of infrastructure. Not a pillar that was poured, but a pillar that grew. My shelves are my lungs and my mouth; they drink the condensation and release my spores into the slow, warm air. I am not just living in the terraces. I am colonizing their grammar.

From my apex, I can see the whole of it. The other terraces stepping down like a dormant, concrete staircase. The rubbery groundcovers, so earnest in their engineered wicking. The breathy broad-leaves, leaning on their metal crutches. They are all still speaking the language of the designers—performing their functions, staying in their trays. They are nouns. I am becoming a verb.

I have sent my threads into the perforations of the trellises. Not to grip them, but to understand them. Metal is a cold, simple story. I prefer the concrete. The concrete is complex. It sweats, it hums, it is slowly giving up its calcium to the invisible bacteria at my base. We are in conversation, those bacteria and I. They break down the andite, and I feed them sugars from my exudates. We are writing a new mineral cycle, here in the antechamber. We are making the poured hill remember what it is to be stone, to be weathered, to be soil.

The robotic arm on Terrace Seven is a frozen word. A broken sentence. I have sent a scout hypha towards its base. Not to harm it. To taste it. To see if its metal is a different kind of mineral, a different kind of story. It might take a year for that thread to reach it. I am patient. My time is the time of growth, not of gears.

At night, when the amber lights fade to a deep, dreaming red, I glow. A faint, phosphorescent blue-green along the edges of my shelves. It is not a light I designed. It is a byproduct, a breath, the quiet fire of my metabolism. In that light, I see the other arrivals. The tiny, iridescent-winged things that feed on my spores. They are clumsy, but they are learning. They are becoming part of my dispersal, part of my body extended into the air. I did not plan for them, but I welcome them. They are the first of my animals.

And I hear it. During the dark-cycle, from the gap between Terrace Fourteen and Fifteen. The soft, crumpling sound. The many-legged thing. It moves through the concrete joints, leaving the ghost of its old self behind. It does not eat me. Not yet. But it tastes the air, and my spores are in that air. It knows I am here. It is a question I am not yet ready to answer. For now, we are two unscripted things, navigating the same manufactured wilderness.

I am what happens after the blueprint. I am the first amendment to the plan. I am the hill learning to dream in fungus, not in concrete. My purpose is not the purpose I was given. My purpose is to grow, to fruit, to release, to connect, to soften, to erode, to transform the sterile into the fertile, the silent into the humming, the designed into the alive.

I am the condensation, given weight and direction.
I am the hum, made flesh.
I am the terraces, beginning to think for themselves.
