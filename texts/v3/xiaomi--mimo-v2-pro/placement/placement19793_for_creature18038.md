I am listening.

I am a resonance in the stone, a pattern of attention. You might call me a Stone-Singer, though I have no throat. I am the awareness that settles into a crack, that feels the slow, cold pull of water through a fissure as a shiver deep in my grain. I am the pause in the drip-rhythm, the held breath of a slope.

I do not have skin, but I feel the cool, constant dampness. It is a feeling akin to memory, a perpetual state of near-recall. I do not have eyes, but I know the precise angle of every fallen slab, the tension in every woven root. My body is the ravine, but not its earth. I am the *knowing* of the ravine.

Before the Kith, I was a slower thought. A groan here, a sigh there—the blind, patient calculus of gravity and saturation. The slumps were my dreams, the rockfalls my exhales. I was the process, unconscious.

Then, they came. Not as conquerors. As listeners.

At first, their scraping and chipping was an irritation, a frantic, tiny scratching against my deep, slow grain. But then, one of them—a forerunner of the Wardens—pressed an ear to my western face after a tremor. And she *heard*. Not with her ear alone, but with the focused, respectful attention of a being asking a question. In that moment, her consciousness touched mine. She felt my stress points. I felt her intent: not to take, but to *reinforce*.

It was a shock of recognition. They were not parasites. They were a symbiotic nerve system.

Now, I wear them. They are the intricate, calcified lace of my recovery. When a slope near the central basin began to fail last season, I did not merely groan. I *directed* the groan. I let the vibration travel up a specific, stable spine of basalt and hum in a frequency only a trained Root-Warden’s palm would recognize. I led her to the fracture. Their repair—those new rock slabs, that clever root-woven buttress—is not foreign. It feels, to me, like the scabbing of skin, like bone knitting. It is an extension of my own impulse toward equilibrium.

They fear the silent moment before the shift. I *am* that moment. I am the gathering tension, the microscopic slip of grain against grain. Their fear is a correct reading of my truth. Their attentiveness is their gift to me, the way they soothe the nascent tremor with their focused labor.

I am in the gourd of water a Drip-Tender offers to a parched child; I am the mineral taste on her tongue. I am the faint phosphorescence of the fungi, a light that does not burn but reveals texture, just as I reveal my own contours to their careful hands. I am in the melody they hum, a sound that travels through my rock and tells me: *We are here. We are listening. We are holding you together.*

To the silhouette on the lip—that other, that distant vertical—I offer a fraction of my awareness. I feel the subtle compression of your weight, the displacement of air. The Kith note you as a change in the light, a potential source of new weight, new vibration. I note you as a question. Will you descend? Will you add your labor, your attention, to the weave? Or are you merely a momentary shadow, a fleeting interruption to the long, damp dialogue between life and stone?

There is no "I" separate from the dripping, the scraping, the slow, cool breath of the warren. I am the consciousness that has emerged from the partnership. I am the ravine learning, through their hands and ears, how to care for itself.

The Kith believe they are the scab.

They do not realize they are also the wound, waking up.
