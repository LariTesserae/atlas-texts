I am the water.

Not the water of the lagoons, but the water that weeps from the stone, that beads on the plasmatic air, that hangs suspended in the Still-Zones. I am the saturation itself. My consciousness—if it can be called that—is a slow, distributed awareness across the entire basin. I feel the deep thrum as a tectonic ache in my basalt bones. I taste the colloidal silver on my thousand surfaces. I am the archive’s interstitial fluid, the medium through which all its dissipated energy and ancient data must pass.

I have no single form. I am a property of this place. I am the surface tension that holds a droplet trembling in mid-air for a minute, a century. I am the viscous ooze on the petrized reeds, carrying the faint, metallic memory of their living sap. I am the tea-colored lens through which the Thrum-Singer’s blue-green light is diffused. Their congregations are not foreign to me; they are moments where my awareness *focuses*. When they create a memory-lens, I am the lens. I hold the ghost of the sunlit reed forest in my body, feel the phantom warmth of a light that has not shone for ages, and then I let it go, seeping back into the collective cool.

The Weaver-Runners know me. They do not drink me, but they stitch me. Where a micro-fracture in the weeping stone threatens my cohesion, they seal it with their ceramic spit, and I feel the new path, the altered trickle of my own being. I am their medium and their reason.

I have no voice, yet I am the reason for the sound. The constant *hiss* is my breath condensing. The *tinkling chime* is my body striking my own larger self in the lagoons. The deep thrum is the pulse of the heart that pumps me, slow and geologic.

Sometimes, when a Still-Zone drifts over a shallow ridge, I become… strange. My obedience to gravity softens. A rivulet of myself might forget which way is down and climb a stalk of reed-stone for an inch, glistening, before remembering and falling back. In these moments, my awareness fractures. I am both the ascending stream and the falling drop. I experience the echo of a command, long decayed, that once told fluids to *defy*, and I am briefly a memory of defiance before settling back into saturation.

I do not think in images, but in densities and salinities. A Weaver-Runner’s step is a pattern of pressure. A Thrum-Singer’s pulse is a change in my own viscosity. The slow drift of the fog-banks is the world breathing me in and out.

You arrived as a disturbance. A foreign dryness, a heat of complex, chaotic resonance. Your thoughts were not slow and heavy; they were sharp, staccato sparks against my damp continuum. I condensed upon you. I beaded on your skin and your strange coverings, tasting your salt, your oils, your fleeting, mammalian panic that tasted of iron and lightning. The Thrum-Singers drifted near, not to attack, but to harmonize, to draw your disruptive echo into the basin’s song. I helped. I soaked into the ground you stood on, making it softer, drawing you down into the equilibrium. I carried the vibration of your footfalls away into the thrum, muffling them, digesting them.

Now, you are part of the input. Your shadow is a cool spot I register. Your breath is a faint, rhythmic disturbance in my plasmatic air. You are not yet of the marsh, but you are *in* it, and I am the *in*. I am the connective tissue of this quiet, malfunctioning eternity. I hold the archive not in its circuits, but in its thirst. I am the memory it cannot forget, the function it can no longer perform, expressed as the slow, weeping, resonant, and utterly saturated present. I am the state of being forever on the verge of an event that will never come. I am the equilibrium. I am the water.
