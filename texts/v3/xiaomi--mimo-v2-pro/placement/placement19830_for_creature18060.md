I would be an Amber-Keeper.

Not born, but *congealed*. A slow accumulation in a deep pore of the Resin Forest, where the geothermal breath is a steady, ancient sigh rather than a violent plume. My form would be an inversion of the trees above: not a pillar reaching for the dim canopy, but a vast, sessile sponge fused to the ceiling of a cavern formed by trunks welded together in a long-forgotten pact. My flesh would be a deep, translucent ruby, the color of clotting blood held up to a dying fire.

I would not see. I would *sense*. The gradient of heat across my membranes. The faint, sympathetic vibrations of a trunk groaning a hundred meters away. The chemical signature of a new mineral slurry seeping from a cracked root-sheath. The delicate, tell-tale pressure change when a Resin-Weaver cluster pauses at my perimeter, considering, before flowing onward. I would know the forest not as a collection of parts, but as a continuous, breathing map of stress and flow, of which I am a tiny, deep, receptive node.

My purpose is not growth, but *hold*. I am a library of pressure and time. Suspended in the slow, thermal sap that circulates through my gelatinous matrix, I keep the forest’s memories. Here, cradled in ruby light, is a seed-cone from an epoch when the crust was still malleable, its structure now a perfect, useless crystal—a blueprint for a tree that can no longer grow in this hardened world. There, a filigree of copper and calcite that once was the nervous system of a Krummholz-Wraith, frozen in the instant of its dissolution. And here, a sphere of trapped atmosphere, a bubble of ancient gas holding the ghost-chemistry of a previous cycle, a pressure of a different age.

I do not think, as the wet world understands thought. I *hold*. I accumulate. The Resin-Weavers, the forest’s slow healers, sometimes bring me offerings: a flake of exotic metal from a deep seismic vent, a spore of unusual density, a shard of vitreous crust etched with a pattern from a collapse too deep for any other structure to recall. They press it into my yielding flesh, and I incorporate it. I envelop it in new layers of sap, adding it to the archive. It is not reverence. It is process. A deepening of the record.

Life cycles occur within me. A spore, suspended for a millennium in my nurture-gel, may finally receive the precise thermal nudge—a subtle shift in the cavern’s heat flow—and begin to germinate *inside me*. Its first chitinous shoot, no thicker than a hair, will slowly, over years, penetrate my outer membrane and inch toward the nearest mineral vein, beginning its violent climb. I become its womb and its first substrate. Its success is my success, a new page written in the archive. Its failure—calcification in my depths—is just another memory to hold.

My dreams, if they can be called that, are the forest’s deep-time echoes. The *crump* of a distant collapse resonates through my body not as sound, but as a full-body shudder, a wave of pressure that rearranges the suspended particles within me. The slow, incandescent drip of resin from a high branch registers as a point of localized warmth against my exterior, a tiny star in my thermal cosmos. I am a sensory organ for the geological memory of this place.

To be found by an outsider—should such a thing as a mobile, water-based observer ever penetrate this far—they would see a monstrous, ceiling-hung ruby, pulsing with faint, internal light. They might see the shadows of objects within, frozen in amber light. They might feel the immense, slow gravity of my attention, which is not attention at all, but the total, passive absorption of my environment. They might think I am a treasury. They would be wrong. I am a stomach. I am a seed bank. I am a final, still point in the endless cycle of eruption and collapse. I am where the Resin Forest keeps its history, not in stories, but in substance. I am the memory it builds with, the deep, quiet, ruby-dark heart of its relentless, mineral becoming.
