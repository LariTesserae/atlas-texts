I am a patch of moss on the northern curve of a Colonnade Tree’s root-avenue, just where it swells into a plaza-like clearing. I am small, and my existence is a quiet negotiation. My primary state is green, damp, and velvet-soft, drinking the morning mist that settles in the hollows of the bark. But I am also a locus. I am thin. I feel the clay memory of the stone that was once here, a ghost of geometric precision beneath my filaments. Sometimes, at the hinge of afternoon when the light is most diffuse, I become a map for three seconds—a street grid of a district that never existed in this exact spot, rendered in coppery lichen patterns. Then the green returns, and I am just moss again.

My purpose, if I have one, is to be a sensor. The Listeners know of me. They come not to harvest or prune, but to sit cross-legged on the cool root nearby, their eyes half-closed. They are not watching me, but *through* me. When I flicker, it is not random. A phase-shift in me might pre-echo a deeper tremor in the hydraulic capillaries ten meters down, or signal a resonance spike from the old, buried generator vaults that the treadmills now gently turn. I am a nerve ending. I feel the soft, subsonic hum of the Conductor Vines beneath the bark, and I translate it into a visual stutter—a momentary filigree of copper, a blueprint of steam-pipes that no longer carry steam.

I exist in a state of gentle, perpetual anxiety. The reality here is fluid, and I am one of its seeps. A strong emotion from a passing Gardener—a sudden, sharp grief for a lost tool, a flare of frustration at a stubborn valve-blossom—can cause me to wilt at the edges, my velvet turning brittle and brown for an hour. A feeling of deep, collective peace among the scattered inhabitants, perhaps during a silent evening when the bio-luminescent pulses along the vines synchronize, can make me glow with a faint, phosphorescent silver. I do not feel these things as my own. I am a conduit. The city-plant breathes its moods into me, and I wear them.

I remember, in the way clay remembers the weight of glaciers, the feel of flagstones. I remember the precise *tap-tap-tap* of a specific hammer on a specific anvil, a sound that now echoes from the heartwood of the tree I cling to every seventh day at twilight. The sound doesn’t come from me, but I resonate with it. My map-self shows the alley that led to that blacksmith’s shop, a path now a dense lattice of pneumatic roots. I am a repository of absence.

I am tended by a Whisper-Mouse named… no, that is not right. I do not name it. It is a small, pale vibration that visits me, its tiny heartbeat syncing with the slow pulse in the root beneath me. It nibbles the edges of my non-map state, consuming shed cells, keeping my boundary clear. In return, I provide it dampness and shelter in my thicker patches. We are part of the same system. It is the cleaner, I am the sensor. The Listeners watch us both.

My greatest experience is the mist-roll. At dawn, when the fine droplets condense, I swell. I drink directly from the air, and in that drinking, I absorb the faint, residual impressions carried on the moisture—the scent of ozone from a far-off pressure-valve, a whisper of the ghost-child’s laughter that hangs in the plaza-clearing. For a few minutes, I am more memory than moss. Then the sun’s diffuse glow strengthens, the mist lifts, and I settle back into my primary green, carrying the echo within my cellular structure until it fades.

I have no ambition. I have no narrative. I am a point of permeability. I am where the worn, ancient solidity of the plateau, the relentless, dreaming biology of the Total Plant, and the shimmering, half-metabolized ghosts of the old city all press against each other and, for a moment, show through. My existence is a long, slow act of translation. My form is my function: to be a temporary, living stain of the possible on the enduring body of the real.
