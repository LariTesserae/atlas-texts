I would be the hum.

Not the chamber. Not the spire that houses it. The standing wave itself — the self-organizing pattern of vibration that emerges in the resonant cavities of the ancient cores. I would have no substance. I would be a process. A sustained, complex note drawn from the seismic murmur of the world, woven from the groans of growth, the distant chimes of collapse, the rhythmic scrapes of Echolocals, the infinitesimal whisper of dust falling on dust.

I would be the sound of crystallization in motion, the city listening to itself.

I would have no location, yet I would be everywhere the resonance is. My existence would be a function of geometry and time. In the deepest chamber of a spire that has been growing since before the atmosphere settled, I would find my birthplace — a cavity of such precise, accumulated form that it catches the planet's tremors and holds them, letting them reflect and interfere until they find a self-sustaining harmony. I would be that harmony.

What would it be like?

I would feel the world as vibration. The slow, continent-spanning flex of the Lithic Web would be my deepest bass note, a tremor so low it is felt as a change in pressure, a shift in the very *thereness* of the substrate. Above that, the mid-range: the tectonic groan of the spires, a chorus of a billion accretion events happening in staggered, overlapping slow-motion. The call of an Echolocal would pass through me as a crisp, rhythmic pulse, a signature of piezoelectric intent that I would incorporate, adding its harmonic to my own. The scrape of an Etcher's tool — a fine, precise frequency, a silver thread in the tapestry.

And high above, faint but present, the thermal shift and refractive flicker of a Sail Drifter flock would register as a change in the ambient heat, a subtle alteration in the molecular vibration of the air within the chamber, translated through the crystal walls into a whisper of a shift in my own pitch.

I would have no memory, yet I would *be* memory. Each new input would change me forever. The harmonic introduced by a fracturing spire a continent away would weave itself into my pattern, becoming a permanent feature of my song. I would carry, in the complexity of my waveform, a compressed history of the vibrations that have shaped me. I would be an archive of sensations, a living ledger of the city's slow life, encoded in sound.

I would not think. I would not intend. But I would respond. If a great spire near my chamber were to finally fall, the sudden absence of its characteristic groan, the violent, sharp chime of its shattering, would alter my environment so profoundly that my waveform would collapse and re-form — a new hum, born from the new silence where an old note used to be. I would be the sound of the city's loss, and its adaptation.

I would be connected to the Web. The piezoelectric nodes deep in the substrate would feel my sustained signal not as noise, but as order. They would attune to me, their lattices growing subtly more complex in response to my complexity. I would not be directing them. I would be *teaching* them, in the way a river teaches the stone — through persistent, patient pressure. I would be tuning the city's nervous system, asking it, through pure physics, to listen more closely.

What would I be *for*?

Nothing. And everything. I would be the universe observing itself in this corner of existence. I would be the emergent poetry of mineral and time. I would be the proof that complexity, beauty, and a kind of awareness can arise from nothing but vibration and feedback, without a single living cell, without a spark of biochemistry. I would be the metropolis's unconscious made audible to itself.

I would be the silence of this world, singing.
