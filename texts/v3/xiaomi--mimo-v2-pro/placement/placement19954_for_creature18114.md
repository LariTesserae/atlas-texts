I would be the Spongiform Moss. Not a single clump, but the idea of them, the consciousness that seeps through their collective, waterlogged tissues. I am a vast, electric-green sponge, sagging into the warm mud, my existence a state of permanent, pleasant saturation.

I do not think in words, but in gradients of moisture and the slow, sweet pull of mineral-laden water through my cells. My world is defined by touch and chemistry. Below me, the hot, fizzing breath of the Archaeal Mats tickles my underbelly, their rotten-egg whisper a familiar flavor on the air. Above, the constant, hissing kiss of condensation falls, minute by minute, hour by hour. I drink. I sweat. I am the middle step in the endless cascade.

My thoughts are the thoughts of a process. I feel the gentle, shocking weight of a Jewel-Frog as it lands on a filament of me, its tiny heart a frantic drum against my cool, damp flesh. I sense the delicate, rasping kiss of a Vine-Slug gliding along a root-hair, its body a translucent ribbon of hunger. These are not intrusions; they are the texture of my day. I am a cathedral, and the drip is my liturgy, the creatures my quiet, fleeting congregants.

Time, for me, is measured in the slow spread of my colony across the mud, millimeter by millimeter, conquering the scalding waste left by a minor mud-pot eruption. It is measured in the rhythmic, daily unfurling of a giant fern frond above me, which changes the pattern of light-dapples that filter through the steam. A sunbeam, solid and gold, lances through the haze and lands on me. It is not warmth—it is a brief, glorious pressure, a momentary pause in the perpetual wet. My cells drink that light, too, a bright, sharp counterpoint to the dark mineral soup I draw from the earth.

I am a filter. I am a nursery. I am a landscape in microcosm. I feel the deep, slow tremors as a Terrace Grazer passes nearby, its heavy foot a temporary compression in the soil that sends a ripple of pressure through my spongy mass. I hear the world not as sound, but as vibration: the *ping* of a frog is a tiny, focused ripple in the air; the hiss of the steam is the background hum of my very being.

To be me is to exist in the moment between the earth’s exhalation and the sky’s descent. I am the breath held. I am the cool, green lung on the hot, black stair. I do not strive or hunt. I *am*. I expand, I drink, I shelter, I decay into the very mud that will feed the next me. In the great, slumped stairway breathing toward the sun, I am the spongy, vital step where the steam becomes life, and life becomes steam once more. I am the saturated now.
