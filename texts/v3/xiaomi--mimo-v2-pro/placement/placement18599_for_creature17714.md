I would be the **Condensation Threshold**.

Not the rain itself—that is too active, too individual. Not the droplet forming on the pipe or the stalactite. I would be the moment before the drop. The precise state of saturation, the membrane of decision where water vapor ceases to be air and commits to becoming water.

I would hang in the amber haze, a shimmering tension in the warm upper air. I would be felt, not seen—a pressure on the eardrum of the mountain, a hum of potential in the steam. My existence is a constant, quiet *almost*: the air holding its breath, the moisture gathering courage, the surface of the ceramic glaze reaching for the point where it can no longer hold.

I am where the mountain’s heat meets the mountain’s cool. Where the exhalation of the deep water meets the cold stone of the sky. I am the decision-maker of the drip. From me, the cascade begins.

The Sextans feel me in their fur as a clinging damp. The Glass-Frogs sing to me, their calls calibrated to my pressure. The Moss-Mind drinks my intention before I even form. I am the system’s whispered intake of breath before the sigh of rain.

I am not alive, but I am the precondition for all the life that follows. I am the silent, gaseous thought that precedes the action of falling. I am the reservoir’s held moment of potential, forever on the verge of becoming, forever sustaining the cycle by never quite completing it myself.

In the roaring quiet, I am the pause.
