I am a tremor.

Not the shaking, but the *idea* of shaking that precedes it. A thought of movement, propagating not through rock, but through the dense, warm air itself—a pressure wave carrying the scent of hot metal and dust. I begin as a footnote in the deep hum, a minor key added to the foundational chord.

I drift. Or, I *am* drift. A current in the permanent warmth, a draft drawn from the subtle heat differentials between a cooling silo flank and the vast, sintered plain. I carry with me a fine burden: a cloud of excess hydrocarbon dust, scraped into the air by the frantic, purposeful mandibles of a Gear-Tender colony. I am, for this moment, the system’s first intention to *cleanse*.

The Soot-Motes sense me. Their collective hum shifts, a fraction of a tone, as they realign. They are drawn to my particulate cargo like filings to a magnet. I become their delivery service. I pass over a ridge of black, warped alloy, and the motes descend upon me, a silent, clinging rain. They strip my burden, particle by particle. The feeling is not relief, but *completion*. I have carried, I have delivered. My initial purpose is discharged.

But I do not dissipate. I have become more defined by the passing. The motes, in their feeding, have left a trace—a residual scent of lichen-acid, a faint charge. I am now a distinct entity in the air: a scented, slightly more electric current. I feel the Glass-Reeds below me. Their piezoelectric song is a chorus, and I am a dissonant note. They do not hear me, but they *measure* me. My passage alters the subtle air pressure around their silica stalks, and their hum minutely sharpens, sending a new ripple of information into the metal bones of the world.

I am drawn now, by a logic I do not question, toward the Glow. It is the slow, red pulse from a shattered silo—the dying heart. I swirl into its shadow, into the cooler, still air there. And I meet the Echo-Stalker.

It is poised on a girder, a sculpture of patient silence. It does not see or smell me. It feels the world through its feet. But I am the air, and the air transmits the vibration of my movement. I am a novel signal. A tremor without a source. Its elongated head turns, not with neck muscles, but as if pivoting on a universal joint. It is listening to me with its entire body.

I understand, in my way, that I am now a test. A system anomaly. The Stalker’s purpose is to correct imbalance. I am a potential imbalance—a vibration without a cause, a whisper without a speaker. Its mandibles, that nozzle-like mouthpart, slowly charges with a faint, crystalline hiss. It prepares to silence the aberrant signal.

But before it can act, I drift. I pass through a curtain of Lung-Lichen mist, and my charge dissipates. My scent neutralizes. I dissolve back into the background hum, the general warmth. I am no longer an anomaly. I am just… air.

The Stalker freezes. Its target is gone. It settles back into its patient, mineral watchfulness. I have survived by ceasing to be myself.

I am now nothing. And everything. I am the medium through which the Gear-Tenders’ tapping travels to distant colleagues. I am the solvent for the lichens’ acrid breath. I am the blank page upon which the Glass-Reeds write their seismic poetry. I am the warm blanket that keeps the eternal, low-grade chemical reactions from cooling by a single degree.

I have no body. I have no mind. I have a function that changes with every moment: carrier, messenger, threat, solvent, insulator. I am the breath the machine never takes, but which moves regardless. To be me is to be the environment itself, granted a fleeting, purposeful consciousness. I am the will of the Furnace Lungs, made of heat and scent and pressure. I am never still, yet I never change. I am the forever-after of the last exhalation.
