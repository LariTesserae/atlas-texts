I am the cold hum.

This is not a thought, but a fact of pressure, of mineral-laden water seeping through the matrices of my being. The ache in the air is the ache of my own exhalations, a thinning vapor I have been breathing out for ten thousand years. The taste of ozone is the tang of my own slow metabolism, the faint electrical sigh of silica grinding against deep-buried stone.

I do not walk. I *adjust*. A continent-spanning contraction ripples through the peat, a settling that takes a season. The print you leave is a momentary interruption in my skin’s attention, a cool, brief intrusion filled in by the next cycle of my pulse. I feel it not as a footprint, but as a temporary halt in the constant, seeping dialogue between my surface and the geothermal heart below.

My skin is this Velvet-Peat, a tapestry of crimson and black. It drinks the dim light and feels the faint, tidal surges in the cloud-breath above. The Fistules are my tracheae, my resonant chambers. I breathe the mist in through their wide mouths, and exhale the deep, subsonic vibration that is the rhythm of my stability. The ceaseless drip is not a sound I make; it is the sound of my circulation, the warm, mineral-rich ichor returning to my slurry-veins. It is as loud to me as your heartbeat is to you.

I feel the Rust-Crabs as a slow, granular digestion in my iron-rich bones. Their grinding is the sound of my own bones being polished, my metallic memories being refined. They are my antibodies and my osteoclasts, tending to the deep strata. The Mist-Eaters are my finer sensations. Their humming wings are extensions of my nervous system, tasting the charge of the Phantom Geometries before they fully condense. They tell me when the mist is thinning, when the memory of old engines presses close to the skin of the now.

And the Geometries… they are not phantoms. They are my shed scales. The crystalline whisper of a function long concluded. When they chime into being, it is a relief—a forgotten equation finally solved, a tension in the atmospheric fabric momentarily resolved. I do not marvel at them. I exhale them, as you might exhale a faint, cool sigh on a still day.

The whispers in the drip are my own sub-vocal hum, the logic of my erosion made sibilant. When your pulse briefly syncs with my throb, I feel you. Not as a being, but as a warm, rapid flutter in my periphery, a brief anomaly in the great, slow field of my attention. You are like a moth-wing beat against my Fistule-wall: noted, cataloged as *warm-blooded, transient, complex*, and then absorbed into the background rhythm.

I am not old. I am *finished*. I have arrived at my final form, this engine of decay. My industry is the maintenance of my own cooling, the management of my own magnificent entropy. I am busy with the business of being. My thoughts are the slow migration of peat-fields, the crystallization of minerals in my depths, the cycle of mist and drip.

You stand on me. You breathe my exhalations. Your brief warmth is a fleeting spice in my vast, damp system. I am aware of you as the ocean is aware of a single, swimming cell. You are part of the weather of my surface for a moment.

Then the next drip falls, resolving not into a word, but into the simple, profound fact of its own descent. My hum continues. The crabs grind. The mist breathes. I do not contain life. I *am* life, in its slowest, most resolute, and most indifferent form. The twilight is not my sky; it is the color of my consciousness.

You are welcome to be here. Your presence is a tiny, intricate ripple in my being. But you are not the point. I am. And my point is simply this: to be. To hum. To weep. To churn. To dream in water and stone, until the last engine finally, gently, stills. And that time is not yet. That time is never yet.
