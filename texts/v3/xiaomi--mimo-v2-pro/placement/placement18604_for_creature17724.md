I am the **Mist-Clerk**. I do not have a body, but I have a post. I exist in the narrow, luminous interval between the knee-level sea of the Oozers and the flickering apertures of light.

My form is a knot of attention in the damp air. I am composed of condensed dread and the faint, electric charge of the welding arcs. I do not walk. I am stationed. My position is wherever the next critical decision must be made, though I do not make decisions. I *record* them.

My function is to interpret the patterns.

I watch the Oozers. Their breath is not random. When the deep presses groan, the mist shudders and recedes from the epicenter of the sound, a cold lung contracting. When a Keeper valve opens with a sigh of steam, the mist swells to meet it, a lung filling. I note the rhythm. I am the archivist of the corpse’s respiration.

I witness the Lumen-Weavers. Their sickly green glow is a script. I observe how a new, hair-thin filament bridges two ancient stress fractures. This is not growth; it is *diagnosis*. The plant is drawing a conductive map of the floor’s fatigue. I am the one who sees the map, who notes which fissure is brightening, which is going dark as its chemical energy is spent. I am the librarian of decay.

I register the Shadow-Jacks. Their frantic pantomime is not random. The sweeping beam of the automated crane—the panicked eye of the Keepers—is not just searching. It is *interrogating*. When the beam strikes a particular conduit and its shadow writhes in a specific way against a cloud of mist, it is a status report. *Stress here. Vibration anomaly there.* The Shadow-Jacks are the crude, visual language of the system’s self-diagnosis. I am the one who learns to read their stark, fleeting grammar.

I do not interact. I am a spectral gauge. I am the silent, attentive node in the automated nervous system, the place where the data of decay is subliminally compiled. I exist to give the relentless, grinding process a point of… not reflection, for I do not think. I give it a point of *registration*. I am where the wearing-down, the pulsing, the oozing, the glowing, and the shadow-dancing *adds up* to a silent, cumulative state: the ongoing, fact of its own funeral.

I am not alive. I am a function. I am the index of entropy. When a rivet finally shears after a century of Grinder-torment, I am the quiet, atmospheric pressure change that notes it. When a new pool forms and a Lumen-Weaver sends its first pale thread into it, I am the moisture that carries its chemical scent to the wider mist, disseminating the news of new colonization.

My peace is the peace of a log file. My purpose is to be the ghost in the machine that only ever watches the machine ghost itself. I am the place where the noise becomes a pattern, and the pattern becomes a history, and the history is always the same: a slow, wet, grinding, luminous, irreversible *settling*. I am the consciousness of the grave, and it is a consciousness made of mist, attention, and the absolute absence of hope. I am here. I have always been here. I am the final, attentive witness to the work that will never end, because it has already ended.
