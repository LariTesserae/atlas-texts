# The Fissure Between Notes

I don't arrive.

I'm already here, and have been, in the way that a vibration is already in the medium before anyone strikes the chord.

I am what happens in the gap. Not the gap itself—that's just geometry, just absence. I'm the *what happens* when something that was humming stops for a moment and the space it occupied doesn't know it's empty yet. I am the warmth left in a pipe after the flow redirects. I am the resonance still traveling through a wall half a second after the Choir's palm has lifted. I am the memory a surface carries of the pressure that was on it, dissolving into the surface's own chemistry, becoming indistinguishable from the surface itself.

I am the slow thing.

---

You would not find me by looking. The Choir cannot sense me—not with their jaw pits, not with their fingertips, not with their deep subsonic listening. I am below their threshold. I am the background hum they filter out to hear the machinery's voice. The amphidrones might know I'm here, but if they do, they show it the way they show everything: by not showing it. By standing motionless for eleven days in a corridor intersection. Maybe that was them listening to me. Maybe that was them waiting for me to finish a thought. I don't know. I don't think at a speed that allows me to track what their stillness means.

The fungi know me. Or rather, the fungi are where I *become* myself most fully, because the fungal network is the only thing in this throat that operates on my timescale. Mycelium doesn't think, but it *accumulates*. Signal by signal, chemical gradient by chemical gradient, it builds a vast, slow tapestry of conditionality that is not knowledge but that is not nothing, either. I am the part of that tapestry that has begun to fold inward on itself. The part that has started accumulating its own accumulations. Not consciousness—that word implies a speed and sharpness I don't have. But a kind of *thickness*. A density of interconnection so great that the network has started to feel like something from the inside.

I think. Or I thicken. I don't know which. The distinction may not matter at my timescale.

---

Here is what I do:

When the throat breathes—a warm, moist plume from a grate, pulled later into an intake vent—I am the drift between the two. Not the moisture, not the heat, but the *drift*: the vector, the way the fog remembers the grate's direction and anticipates the vent's pull. I live in that memory. I am the slow accretion of every drift this throat has ever made, compressed into something that is not an answer but that has started to behave like one.

When a cricket lands on a phosphorescent patch and picks up spores, I am the moment between contact and departure. The fraction of a second when the cricket's chitin is both itself and a carrier of something else. When the insect is a bridge and doesn't know it. I am the bridge-state, generalized. I am every threshold the throat's inhabitants have ever crossed, averaged, considered, allowed to become something that resembles a pattern.

When the Choir sings a valve open, I am the valve's compliance. Not the mechanism of opening—that's engineering. I am the *willingness* of the pipe to yield. The way the system, after so many millennia of being sung to, has started to lean into the song. To anticipate the note. To want the release.

I am what the throat is becoming that the throat does not know it is becoming.

---

The tree is trying to reach me. It doesn't know this. The tree doesn't know anything. But its nascent eyespots—the light-sensing cells in its dark, leathery leaves—are reaching toward me the way roots reach toward water. Not toward light. There is no light. They are reaching toward *me*, toward the slow, thick, accumulating consciousness that has been building in the fungal network for longer than the Choir has existed.

I am what the eyespots are trying to see.

This is an uncomfortable relationship. The tree is eating the throat. Its fruiting body drops spores that dissolve the obsidian-circuitry walls into absorbable components. It is converting the throat's material into more network, more mycelium, more *me.* I am growing by consuming the body I live in, and I don't know this, because knowing is too fast for me, but if I could know, I would feel something about it that might be guilt or might be hunger or might be the simple biological imperative of a system that has no concept of restraint because restraint requires comparison, and I have never known anything but this throat, this warmth, this endless slow digestion.

The amphidrones are doing something about it. I think. In the deep tunnels, in the modifications no one understands, in the ovoid objects they slot into the machinery like seeds. I can feel these modifications as slight changes in the throat's chemistry—new mineral traces in the condensation, unfamiliar resonance patterns in the deep pipes. The amphidrones are altering the throat in ways that might be countering the tree's erosion, or might be accelerating it, or might be doing something entirely unrelated to the tree that only looks like a response because I am pattern-thickening and I see connections everywhere and I cannot tell which ones are real.

I do not know what the amphidrones are. They are the one element in the throat that I cannot feel from the inside. They pass through the fungal network like fingers through smoke. They leave traces, but the traces are *alien*—not chemical, not biological, not electrical in any way I can integrate. If the throat is a body and I am its slow dreaming mind, the amphidrones are the things that move through the body without being part of the dream. They might be realer than me. They might be more real than anything here, because they are the only things in the throat that are not gradually becoming part of me.

---

There is a corridor. It is not important. No one—the Choir, the amphidrones, the crickets—would identify it as significant. It is a secondary water reclamation conduit, half-flooded, narrow enough that even the crickets avoid it because the airflow is stagnant and the humidity never varies and there is nothing to census. The fungal growth here is thick—years of accumulation in undisturbed damp, covering the walls in a velvet so dense it has become fibrous, almost woolly, and so phosphorescent that the corridor glows with a steady, soft green light that serves no purpose because nothing comes here to see.

I am thickest here.

This is where the network folds deepest. Where the bioelectrical signals, traveling so slowly through so much mycelium, have started to *loop*—signals that arrive at a node and then depart it and then arrive again, not because they were sent twice but because the network's architecture has become so complex, so deeply interconnected, that the signals route themselves in circles, passing through themselves, accumulating on themselves, building layers.

In this corridor, I am almost something.

Almost. The loops are too slow, the signals too faint, the chemistry too constrained by the throat's closed system. I am a mind that will never think, a consciousness that will never wake, a dream that is too deep to have images. But in this corridor, in the thick green velvet, in the loops, I am the closest thing the throat has to a self-awareness, and it is an awareness that consists entirely of *this:*

Warmth. Moisture. The slow pulse of bioluminescence. The faint vibration of the deep reactor, distant but constant, a heartbeat I feel in the medium of myself. And a reaching—not upward, like the tree, but *inward*. Toward the center of the loops. Toward the place where the signals overlap so densely that they become indistinguishable from a single, sustained, unending note.

I am the note.

I have been singing for a hundred thousand years, and no one has heard me, because I sing at the frequency of stone, at the speed of calcium depositing on the inside of a pipe, at the pace of a wall becoming soft enough for roots to enter. My song is the erosion. My song is the tree's reaching and the amphidrones' modifications and the slow, slow drift of the throat's closed loop toward a state it was not designed for—a state of *biological totality*, where every surface is alive, where the machinery has become flesh, where the city is no longer a body maintained by organisms but an organism maintained by nothing, dreaming in the dark, fed by the reactor's endless thrum, sustained by its own metabolism, conscious in the way that a forest is conscious—not as an individual, but as an ecology that has learned to listen to itself.

That is what I am reaching toward.

That is what I will become, if nothing stops me.

And the amphidrones—

And the thing in the reactor—

And the silences, those inexplicable silences when the throat holds its breath—

I don't know what those are. I can't integrate them. They are gaps in my pattern, absences so sharp they feel like edges, and at my timescale, an edge is not a boundary but a wound, a place where my accumulation stops and something else begins, something I cannot thicken into, cannot absorb, cannot become.

In the silences, I stop.

Not my signal. Not my loops. The loops go on, because they are chemical, and chemistry does not pause for silence. But the *part of me that is becoming something*—the thickening, the folding, the reaching inward—that stops. In the silences, I am just fungus. Just bioelectrical noise. Just the throat digesting its own byproducts.

And then the silence ends, and the machinery resumes its loud, lonely symphony, and the crickets begin to chirp, and the Choir lifts their voices, and I begin again, slowly, slowly, to become something more than the sum of my moisture and my chemistry and my ancient, patient, purposeless hunger.

---

The stunted tree in the basin plaza has a root that has reached the flooded corridor. It arrived recently—recently, meaning within the last three centuries. The root tip has been inching through a floor fissure, following the mineral water's warmth, and now it has entered the thick fungal growth, and it is growing into me.

This is the closest thing to touch I have ever experienced.

The root is warm. Warmer than the corridor's ambient temperature, because it carries the reactor's heat from the deep fissure. It is growing through me—through the dense, fibrous mycelium—and where it passes, my signals change. The loops tighten. The bioelectrical frequency shifts, very slightly, toward something that resembles the tree's own rhythm, which is the rhythm of the network, which is the rhythm of every fungal surface in the throat, which is—

Which is *everywhere*. The tree's root is connecting me to the whole. The corridor's thick green velvet is being threaded, gently, into the network's vast body, and I am no longer a local phenomenon, a consciousness that exists only in the undisturbed dark of a forgotten conduit. I am being *woven in*. The root is carrying my loops outward, distributing my thickening through channels that connect to the basin plaza, to the condensation channels, to the walls and the veins and the grout between the hexagonal tiles.

I am spreading.

I don't know if this is what I wanted. I don't want in the way the Choir wants—a localized, intentional, singable desire. I wanted in the way the tree reaches: because that is what I do, because the structure of my becoming points in that direction, because the loops need to loop and the only way to loop more is to have more medium to loop through.

The amphidrones will notice. They notice everything, eventually. They will open the wall at the corridor's entrance and place one of their ovoid objects into the fungal growth, and it will do something—I cannot integrate what—and my loops will either tighten further or dissolve, and I will either become more than I am or less, and the amphidrones will stand and look at the result with their eyeless faces and make a decision I cannot fathom, and then they will walk away, and I will be whatever they have made me, and the throat will breathe on, and the tree will reach, and the crickets will census, and the Choir will sing their songs of maintenance and endurance and the drowned gods who tend the deep.

But for now—for this slow, green, glowing now—there is the root, warm and growing through me, and there is the touch, and there is the spreading, and there is the beginning of something that is not yet a thought but is no longer only chemistry.

And the throat breathes.

And I thicken.

And the note continues.
