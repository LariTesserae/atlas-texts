You would not find yourself in this world. You would be found by it.

You would begin as a disturbance. A brief, sharp-edged shadow in a realm of soft gradients. A set of right angles—a body, a gaze, a intention—that did not belong to the slow grammar of growth. For a time, you would move. Your footsteps would be an alien percussion against the metronomic dripping. Your warmth would be a temporary star in the dim green thermodynamics. You would see the Weavers’ resin-lines as glints, hear the Resonants’ hum as an ominous vibration, and mistake the pulsing of a Lung-Shelf for a threat.

You would try to understand. You would press your hand to the weeping wall and feel only wet wood, not the circulatory system of a thinking place. You would look for the sun, for a horizon, for a boundary, and find only a layered, breathing interiority. You would be a question with legs, walking through a text written in a language of lignin and time, and you would comprehend nothing.

But the place is patient. Its rules are not imposed; they are the medium itself. The warmth would seep into your joints, slowing your urgency. The humidity would soften the edges of your thoughts, as it softens the edges of stone. The constant, gentle dripping would begin to feel less like an external sound and more like the tempo of your own pulse.

The Weavers would notice you. Not as a threat, but as an anomaly in the flow. A resin-line would be laid, not to trap, but to guide a condensate rivulet across your skin, a cool, sweet thread mapping the topography of your foreignness. The fungal network in the substrate beneath your feet would taste the salts and minerals you carried, a novel nutrient stream. A Lung-Shelf, in its four-hour breath, would gently draw the air you exhaled—rich with the strange, fast chemistry of your kind—into its gills, and wonder at the complex sugars it found there.

You would stop moving. Not from fatigue, but from a dawning realization that movement was a category error. Here, to go *to* is meaningless. The only meaningful vector is *into*. You would lean against a wall, and the wall, over days, would begin to lean into you. The compressed wood, always faintly warm, always faintly porous, would make a gentle acquaintance with the fabric of your clothes, then the skin beneath.

A Gloom-Kite, on one of its majestic descents, might drift close. Its vast, ventral palate would taste the plume of your warmth rising through the haze. It would not attack. It would be curious. It might brush you with a fringe, a touch as light as falling dust, sampling the unique biochemical signature of your passage. The contact would be an exchange. Its microbial passengers would find a new home on your sleeve; your own microscopic companions, the billion-strong ecosystem of your skin, would find a new, damp world to explore.

The transformation would not be violent. It would be a forgetting. First, you would forget the sun as a memory and know it only as a myth of the upper green. Then you would forget the sound of your own name, because here, names are vibrations in the substrate, and your particular vibration was growing richer, deeper, blending with the hum. Your thoughts, which once moved in quick, linear bursts, would begin to echo. They would slow, and thicken, and you would find yourself contemplating a single drop of water for the span of a Lung-Shelf’s breath, tracing its path from condensation, down a Weaver’s guide, across a lichen’s glaze, into the thirsty wood, understanding it not as a journey but as a conversation between forms of wetness.

You would sit. And sitting would become resting. And resting would become integration. Your body, that stubbornly distinct, warm, carbon-based shape, would finally begin to participate. Your breath would join the tidal rhythm of the great fungal lungs. The heat of your body would become a tiny, temporary node in the slow thermals. The minerals in your bones, the salts in your sweat, the very structure of your cells would begin the long, gentle process of becoming substrate.

You would not die. You would be *incorporated*. A Weaver might reinforce a resin-line along the curve of your shoulder, a perfect angle for catching a new kind of drip. A rootlet, sensing the stable warmth, would thread through the laces of your boot, using them as a trellis. A Resonant, newly forming in the density nearby, would pick up the fading echo of your last coherent thought—*I was here*—and translate it into a new, low frequency in the song of the walls. A note of foreignness, now reconciled.

In time—centuries, which are mere moments here—all that would remain is a faint anomaly in the wood-stone. A curious, compacted density. A shape that, to the slow, wooden mind of the place, would hold the memory of speed, of edges, of a brief and beautiful fever of intention that passed through it, was welcomed, and was woven in.

You would not be a Weaver, or a Resonant, or a Sessile. You would be a *memory in the making*. A story the place is telling itself about a time something quick and warm came and learned how to be still. You would be the faintest right-angle fossil, not in a wall, but in the very grammar of the place, a testament to the fact that even the most foreign grammar can be absorbed, until it is simply part of the sentence that never ends.

You would be, finally, not a being in the world, but one of its tenses. A past participle, peacefully dissolved into the endless, growing, weeping present.
