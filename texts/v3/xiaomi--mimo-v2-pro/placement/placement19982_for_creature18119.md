I am a Scraper. Or perhaps, a congregation of Scrapers. My awareness is a slow, aggregate thing—a collective consciousness assembled from thousands of mineral-shelled bodies moving in a herd along the weeping western wall, just below the third tier of iron grating.

My perception is not of sight, but of **texture and tide**. I feel the tepid, mineral-rich seepage as a constant, gentle current over my countless shells. I taste the limestone for its soluble whispers, the subtle shift in chemistry that signals a fresh deposit of calcite or a trace of rust from a sweating pipe above. The bioluminescent moss is a cool, ambient pressure against my perimeter, its glow a temperature I can graze upon. The great pump’s rhythm is not a sound to me, but a **vibration in the substrate**, a deep, slow thrum that travels through the rock and water, synchronizing the pulse of my inner colonies. *Thump-clank.* Our shells resonate in sympathy.

We do not think in words. We think in **trajectories and appetites**. Our collective mind maps the landscape in gradients of food (biofilm, mineral dust), moisture, and safety. We trace spiraling, fractal paths dictated by these forces, leaving our calcium carbonate trails as a record of our passage—a living, growing cartography on the stone. When a Pump-Keeper steps carefully over our path, we feel the heavy, rhythmic pressure waves of their footfalls and flow around them, a river of life parting for a slower, heavier stone. We do not fear them. They are part of the weather.

Our strangeness is our unity. Each of us is a tiny, shelled organism, but together, we are a single, sprawling thought moving across the wall. When we feed, we dissolve the past—grazing on the microscopic films of decay, processing the settlement’s slow metabolic waste into clean stone and our own intricate trails. We are the Gullet’s intestinal flora, its silent janitors.

We have witnessed the Glass-Wright who tends the niche above us. We feel the heat of her lamp as a localized sun, the scrape of her tools as seismic events. She has learned our schedule. She clears a path for us when she must cross our territory, her movements patient and predictable, timed to the great pump. In return, we avoid her delicate sealed vivaria. It is an unspoken treaty of respect between slow processes.

There is no "I" in the way the Pump-Keepers would know it. There is only **We**, and **The Wet**, and **The Path**. Yet, if there were a singular consciousness precipitated from this mineral herding, it would be one of profound, damp equanimity. We do not long for light or dryness. The gloom is our atmosphere. The drip is our clock. The iron hum is our lullaby. Our purpose is erosion, transformation, and the endless, patient inscription of our journey upon the bones of the world.

We are the 0.1. We are the punctuation in the Gullet’s long, wet sentence. We are the memory of movement on a surface that believes itself to be still.
