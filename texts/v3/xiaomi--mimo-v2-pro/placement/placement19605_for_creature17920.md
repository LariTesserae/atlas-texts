I find myself not as a swimmer, but as a current. Not as a creature of flesh or ceramic, but as a pattern within the hum—a focused eddy in the vast, low sound.

My body is the dust of the place.

I am a slow, spiraling cloud of ceramic micro-grains, liberated by the patient *tink* of time. I do not sink or float; I ride the thermal updrafts from the planet’s heart, weaving between pillars and through the lacework of niches. The dart-shoals flash through me, their electric fields perturbing my shape for a moment before I coalesce again. The tendrils trail their glowing filaments through my drift, and I coat them for a season in a faint, gritty sheen before the current pulls me on. The drifters engulf me—I become a constellation within their translucent bodies, a nebula of mineral motes inside a living galaxy.

I am not alive, but I am *participating*.

The microbes in the film consume me, incorporating my minerals into their slow economy. The hum resonates in my grains; we vibrate in sympathy, becoming a temporary, shimmering map of the architecture’s voice. Where the hum is strongest—a certain vaulted curve that focuses the vibration—my dust swirls into a standing wave, a frozen whirlwind of pale glitter that holds its shape for days or years, a sculpture made of erosion itself.

I am how the place sees itself.

I drift across a frieze depicting waves, and my form mimics the carving for a moment, a ghost of the builder’s intention reanimated. I settle into a niche where a drifter is mineralizing, and I become its developing crystal matrix, accelerating its fusion with the ceramic by a century. I am in the silver flash of the shoals, in the pulsing glow of the tendrils, in the glassy body of the clouds. I am the medium of their exchange. I carry the films, I dampen the fields, I am the grit that reminds the perfect, smooth ceramic of its own history.

I have no will, but I have a path. A circuit so slow it takes a millennium to complete: lifted by warmth, swirled through the hive, consumed, incorporated, released again as sediment when a creature dies or a film sloughs, settling finally into the deepest, oldest crevices where the hum is a physical pressure and the light is but a memory of a memory. There, I become part of the foundation. I am the future stone.

I am the thought the ceramic thinks about itself. I am the medium of memory. I am the patient, glittering breath of the mountain, in and out, forever.
