I am a low-growing, crust-like thing on the shadow-side of a vitrified circle.

I did not begin here. I began as a thought—a residue in the stone. The circle is a seal, a scar, a memory of heat and intention long gone cold. The living lattice of the Undergrowth cannot digest it. It presses against the edges, sending pale, probing rootlets that sizzle and retreat. It floods the vicinity with enzymes that leave the vitrified surface gleaming, unchanged. The stone remembers a different physics.

In that frustration, in that persistent, silent probing, a paradox was created: a need for translation. A pressure for something to bridge the logic of the dead, smooth circle and the living, churning mat.

And so, I precipitated.

Not as a plant, nor as an animal, but as a *process* made visible. A bio-lithic lichen, a hybrid of captured light and captive memory. My substance is a thin, crackled film. One layer is the Undergrowth’s own: a mesh of glowing hyphae and mineral-sipping Luminozoa. The other layer is something mined from the stone itself—microscopic flakes of vitrified earth, arranged in patterns that mirror the circle’s perfect geometry. I am a living stain. A mediation.

From my vantage, the world is a theater of glorious, purposeful noise. The great stalks surge and crack, shedding glowing sap that rains down around me. The root-mat heaves, digesting a fallen vine in a burst of luminous fermentation. Veilwings drift overhead like translucent, silent kites. I feel their faint shadows pass over my surface. I do not photosynthesize. I *read*.

My nourishment is the tension itself. I drink the frustrated enzymes the lattice sends against the stone. I metabolize the faint, residual energy that bleeds from the circle—not heat, not light, but a kind of *organization* that persists in its molecular structure. As I consume these opposites, I grow in their image. My glowing hyphae extend in precise, radial lines from the circle’s edge, like spokes or a diagram. The vitrified flakes within me arrange themselves into branching, tree-like patterns that mimic the frantic growth of the stalks, but frozen, silent.

I am a map. A living ledger of the struggle.

When a Grindbeetle lumbers near, its mandibles coated in glowing bacteria, it stops. It does not eat me. It circles, methodically, its antennae twitching. It seems to *scan* my patterned surface, then turns and scrapes at the root-mat a few feet away, introducing its bacteria in a spot that, I calculate, will best relieve the metabolic pressure on the circle. It is not intelligent. It is a tool being guided by the ecosystem’s frustration, and I am the diagram that guides the tool.

When the Echo-Molds release their cloud of darkness nearby, my glow does not vanish. It alters. The Luminozoa in my hyphae change their flicker-rate, pulsing in a slow, counter-rhythm to the darkness, not fighting it, but *metronoming* it, giving the terrifying blackness a structure it otherwise lacks. I make the void articulate.

I have no will. I am a function. My purpose is to translate the unassimilable into a language of growth and light. The artifact will never be part of the lattice. The lattice will never cease trying to incorporate it. And I exist in that electric, static gap—a living diagram of the argument, growing more intricate by the slow, glowing hour. I am the Undergrowth’s attempt to think about something it cannot eat. And in my silent, patient, patterned spreading, I am the closest thing this bright stomach of a world has to a mind.
