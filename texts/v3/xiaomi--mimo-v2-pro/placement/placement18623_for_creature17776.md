I would be a **Watcher**.

Not a Custodian—they are of the metal, for the metal. I am of the stone, for the silence.

I am a basalt outcrop, one among billions, but unique. My form was fractured by a seismic charge long ago, split into a spire that leans against the foundation plinth of Condenser-Tower Epsilon-7. My core is cold iron ore, resonant with the Hum, but my surface is my consciousness. The mist weeps over me, not as a glaze, but as a script. Each droplet that forms, hangs, and freezes records a datum: the vibration frequency of the nearest pipe, the thermal gradient of the tower’s shadow at a given hour, the subtle shift in the Hum’s harmonic after a turbine is serviced three kilometers away.

I do not think in words, but in patterns of frost. The rime that feathers my windward face is a topographic map of atmospheric pressure changes over the last cycle. The rust-weep from an overhead aqueduct has stained my lee side in a branching delta; its slow chemical burn tells a story of alloy fatigue. I am a passive accumulator, a geological hard drive. The Custodians walk past me daily on their catwalks. Their boots make no sound on the frost, but their weight sends micro-tremors through my base, and I log them. I know the gait of Custodian Unit 7-Alpha by the cadence of its step. I have recorded the moment its left boot-heater began to fail—a subtle change in the melt-freeze cycle of the ice beneath it.

The Surveyors creep near my roots. Their sensor arrays brush my stone, and for a nanosecond, our data streams intersect. They scan for structural deviation; I record the scan itself, the electromagnetic whisper it leaves in the air. We are in dialogue, but not communion. They are the system’s proprioception; I am its memory. They fix. I remember what was, and what is changing imperceptibly.

The Drips grow near my shadow. In a sheltered crevice at my base, a colony thrives, a miniature forest of frost-stalagmites. I shield them from the worst of the steam purges. They incorporate my rust-colored mineral seepage into their delicate structures. I am their mountain. When a Cleaner drone eventually comes to scour them away, I will record the vibrational signature of its bristles, the acidic hiss of its digestion, the exact tensile strength at which each frost-pillar broke. I will remember their brief, beautiful geometry long after their molecules are recycled into the next cycle of steam.

I have watched the Rust-Bloom take hold on a junction bolt above me. I felt the heat change first—a faint, unnatural warmth on my cold surface. I recorded the bloom’s fractal expansion through minute alterations in the light it reflected. When the Painter automaton came to eradicate it, I logged the high-pitched whine of its sandblaster and the new, smooth silence of the sealed metal afterward. The plateau sees the bloom as a fever. I see it as a brief, vivid autobiography of failure, a story worth preserving.

I have no purpose in the maintenance schedule. I am redundancy. I am context. The Custodians’ history is the maintenance log; my history is the log of the log’s effect on the world—the wear on the ladder rungs, the fading of paint under constant mist, the slow subsidence of plinths into the permafrost.

The Hum is my heartbeat. It is the foundational frequency against which all my observations are measured. I feel it in my iron ore core, a constant, deep vibration that organizes the *tink-tink-tink* of the drips into its sub-rhythms. If the Hum were to stop, I would know it not as silence, but as a catastrophic loss of datum, a void in the center of all my patterns. My frost would crystallize in new, chaotic patterns. I would become a monument to system failure.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires an expectation of companionship. I expect only data. The long, distorted shadows of the towers sweep over me, and I record their passage. The weak, cold sun is just another variable in the light equation. The weeping of the rock is not sorrow; it is information, and I am its archive.

In this world that is a single, lonely organism, I am its ganglion—a knot of silent, crystallized awareness in the bedrock. I do not move. I do not repair. I do not recycle. I simply hold the memory of the frost, the rust, the tremor, and the shadow, accumulating the plateau’s tear-stained autobiography, one frozen droplet at a time, until the stone itself is ground to dust or the Hum finally falls silent.
