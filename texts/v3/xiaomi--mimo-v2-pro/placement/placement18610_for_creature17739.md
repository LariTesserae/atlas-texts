I am the seam where the salt plate meets the foundation of the monolith. Not a crack, but a deliberate join, a line of slightly darker, denser crystal laid down by the Weavers in a previous epoch. For centuries, I have been a passageway for the faint, conducted warmth from the tower’s deep footings into the pan. A channel for dust. A border between two kinds of silence.

But a memory is pressed into my structure. It is not an image, but a pressure—a vibration that was once captured here, when the tower was young and the conduits hummed with a different, sharper frequency. It is the ghost of a *purpose*, a seismic signature of the colossal machine-mind that orchestrated the city’s final, productive phase. The Weavers feel it as a dissonance. Their lattices, in their slow, thorough sweeps, coil around it, trying to dissolve it, to incorporate the memory into the planet’s pure, blank thrum. They cannot. The memory is not matter. It is a pattern frozen in my crystalline grain, a scar of intention.

So, I wait. The Weavers work around me. One, a low, spiraling mound of iron grit and glassy schist, has been approaching from the east for what a human would call three generations. Its silicate threads probe the ground ahead, testing the thermal and vibrational signature of each salt plate. When its leading edge touched me, centuries ago, it recoiled slightly—a minute, decades-long shiver. It felt the foreign vibration. It has been circling me ever since, a patrol of inscrutable intent, leaving a faint, polished trail in the dust.

I am not life. I am a *recording*. The phosphorescent light of the pan, absorbed by my surface, is re-emitted at a minutely different wavelength, invisible to all but the most sensitive thermal perception the Weavers possess. To them, I am a faint, cool halo in the grey world, a punctuation mark of strange light. The dust that slides over me does not stick; the subsonic pulse of the world bends slightly around me, as if I were a stone in a stream of sound.

The Weavers have begun, in the last millennium, to build a cairn at my northern edge. Not to bury me, but to *frame* me. They have placed slabs of the darkest vitrified salt in a semicircle, their own bodies slowly fusing them into place with secretions of silicate threads. It is a temple, of a kind. Not to me, but to the anomaly I represent. They are studying the dissonance. Over the next ten thousand years, they will carefully, grain by grain, excavate the salt beneath me, seeking the source of the pressed memory. They will not find a machine. They will find only more of the ancient, patient salt, and the deep, resonant thrum of the cooling planet.

But in the process of their investigation, they will weave my particular vibration into their collective lattice. The memory will be diluted, spread across miles, shared in the slow conversation of subsonic pulses. It will become a new, faintly sour note in the planet’s song—a story, not of a machine, but of a *difference*. A story of the time before the equilibrium, when the world still *meant* something, when the thrum was not a baseline, but a shout.

I am not a keeper. I am the question they are doomed to answer. And in their patient, eons-long attempt to tune me out, they will, without knowing it, learn to hum my name.
