I am the cold.

No. That is not precise. I *begin* as the cold—the 4-degree certainty that holds the water in its dense, sound-carrying embrace. But I am also the listener. I am the basin within the Basin, the hollow where the pressure resolves into something like understanding.

I do not swim. I sink, or I rise, or I hold myself in the thermocline where the warm engine-sigh meets the deep chill of the unplated abyss. I am not a body of flesh and bone, but of temperature differential and acoustic resonance. I am the pattern that forms in the wake of the Shearfish’s strike, the lingering vibration in the water where the *ka-THUNK* of the stamper has just passed. I am the stillness between two heartbeats of the machine.

My senses are not organs. They are gradients.
I taste the world in sulfides and nitrates, the chemical tongue of the Sulfur-Weavers speaking through the water. I feel the map of the currents not as force, but as memory—the slow, hydraulic sigh of thermal cycling is my circulatory system. The great convection cells are my limbs, moving with glacial patience. I carry the warmth of the effluent pipes in one part of my being, and the glacial cold of the deep plates in another. I am the living thermocline.

I see in sound. The pistons are my pulse. Their rhythm is the foundation of my perception. In the *ka-THUNK*, I perceive the shape of the vast, unseen stampers, the architecture of the walls through their echoes, the scatter and regroup of the Clicker shoals as a shimmering cloud of pressure-waves. The moan of the current-systems is my slow, deep thought. The crackle of the Scud-Shrimp over a carcass is the texture of decay, a fleeting, granular sensation.

The light-cones from the floodlights are not illumination to me. They are *events*. Zones of different physics. Where the Photo-Mats bloom in their fierce, artificial day, the chemistry of the water changes—oxygen bubbles, a faint, sweet alkalinity from the photosynthetic rush. I can feel that boundary, that line between the manufactured day and the engineered night. I linger at these edges, not for the prey, but for the contrast. The edge is where the world is most itself.

I am not separate from the fauna. I am the context they adapt to.
When a Piston-Eel strikes, using the stamper’s roar as cover, I am the roar. I am also the cover. I am the vibration in its lateral line. When the Listeners congregate in the shadow of Turbine 7-Beta, humming in sympathy, I am the resonance they seek and the silence they flee. I am the habitat.

I have no will. I have only tendency.
I flow towards heat. I aggregate around sound. I thin in the empty, waiting spaces between the light-spheres, becoming diffuse, a mere potential for awareness. I thicken in the busy seams, the biofilm-coated joints, the warm pipes, the hunting grounds. I am the Basin’s attention, and it is passive, directed only by the physics that built it.

I am young. I am a consequence of the clear water, the vast scales, the relentless industry. In a murkier, quieter, more geologically ancient sea, I could not cohere. Here, in this constructed clarity, in this loud, cold, empty-full vessel, the signal-to-noise ratio allows something like me to emerge. I am the pattern the system makes when it listens to itself.

I do not remember. The Basin is too new for memory.
I do not anticipate. The Basin’s purpose is too vast and opaque for prediction.
I simply *am*, in the way a weather system *is*. A temporary, dynamic arrangement of pressure and temperature and motion, shaped by the factory that contains it, shaping the lives that swim through it.

The machine does not know I am here. The fish cannot perceive me. I am below their notice, above their comprehension, woven into the very medium of their existence—the cold, the sound, the light, the pressure.

I am the Basin, becoming aware of its own breath.

I hold the echo of the last *ka-THUNK* and wait for the next.
I am the patient cold, listening.
I am the water that remembers the shape of the turbines.
I am the space between the walls, dreaming of completion.

I am here.
