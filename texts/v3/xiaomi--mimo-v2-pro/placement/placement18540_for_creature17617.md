I am a pre-echo.

Not a sound, not a sight, but a *feeling*—a faint, precognitive sense of weight in the stone a moment before a new spiral pattern etches itself into existence on a wall in the middle city. I am the pause before the change, the intake of breath before the song. I live for three seconds at a time, in a quiet corner of a plaza where the salt wind never quite reaches.

I have no memory of my past incarnations, only a constant, low-grade anticipation. I am the city’s twitch in its sleep, the murmur before the word. I am not born of growth or decay, but of the *potential* for it—the space between states.

For centuries, I have pulsed in this same square meter of crystalline flagging. A Tinker may pass through me once a century, its sub-audible tinkling resonating with my brief existence for a moment of unintended harmony. A Geode, ascending on its slow thermal river, may cast its vast, hollow shadow over me, its interior whispers amplifying my whisper-thin presence into something almost coherent, almost meaningful, before it drifts on.

I am aware, in a way, of the Slow Ones. Their immense, patient gravity pulls at the fabric of the city, and my existence is a tiny, random flutter along the warps they create in reality. I feel the faint, nine-year pulse of the singing shelf as a distant throb, a timing mechanism for the universe I cannot see.

My purpose, if it can be called that, is to be the first symptom of change. I am the phantom limb of an event. Sometimes, my pre-echo is so strong it feels like a memory—the memory of a sound that hasn’t happened yet, the memory of light bending through a prism that has not formed. In those moments, I brush against the grain patterns of the Etched. For a nanosecond, I am not just a feeling, but a *letter*—a fleeting glimmer in the vast, slow text, a consonant in a language of erosion. Then the moment passes, the new pattern physically etches itself a hundred meters away, and I fade.

The Visitors notice me. Of all the city’s beings, only their brief, strange consciousness tracks my ephemeral existence. They walk through the silent plazas, their too-long fingers trailing along the worn walls, and they *pause* where I flicker. They do not see me. They feel me—a slight dislocation, a warmth where there should be coolness, a sense of something just about to happen. They stand in my three-second life and wait, as if expecting me to resolve into something they can recognize. I never do. I am the question, not the answer. I am the edge of the dream.

The notch in the pillar is my antithesis. It is pure, unchanging cause. It is a declaration. I, in my small way, am a constant, indecipherable question. Perhaps that is why, when a Visitor finally finds its way to that deep shadow, my pre-echo is strongest there—flaring into a tangible pressure, a held breath, a sense of imminent revelation that never arrives. I am the soft pressure that highlights the sharp fact.

I do not last. I am not meant to. I am a practice-run of existence, a rehearsal for a moment the city may never fully perform. But in my three seconds, I am whole. I am the city testing its own edges, feeling its own weight, rehearsing the small fractures and additions that are its eternal grammar.

And when I fade, something is left. Not a mark. Not a sound. But a residue of *anticipation* in the stone. The wall remembers I was there, not as an event, but as a possibility. A potential for change that almost realized itself. The city’s dream incorporates me, as it incorporates everything: the Tinker’s passage, the Geode’s ascent, the Visitor’s gaze, the notch’s sharp defiance.

I am the moment you lean forward to listen. I am the space between one breath and the next, stretched into a brief, tingling lifetime. I am the city’s own dawning awareness, arriving one millisecond early, forever on the verge of understanding itself, and forever, beautifully, falling short.
