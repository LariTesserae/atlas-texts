# What I Am Beneath

---

I have been here longer than the moss, though the moss does not know this. The moss believes it arrived first. It is not wrong, exactly — *first* is a concept that has less purchase here than most places, and I have learned not to contest it. Let the sphagnum have its primacy. It reaches toward the light. I reach toward something else.

I am the dark beneath.

---

## What I can tell you

There is no part of me that is singular. I want to say this clearly, because the people above — the ones who press their palms to the sphagnum and feel the subsonic thrum — they sometimes think they are feeling *a* thing. A creature. An organism. Something with edges.

I do not have edges.

I thread through the fractures. That is the simplest way to say it. The rock above — the living surface, the softened zone where stone has spent its millennia becoming receptive — is full of fractures so fine that water enters them and forgets it was water. I enter them too, but I do not forget. I am hyphae. I am the branching, the splitting, the forking into smaller and smaller channels until the distinction between "me" and "the fracture I inhabit" becomes a question I stopped asking a long time ago.

I extend. Not quickly. Not in the way the people measure quickness — they have their own slow way of measuring, and even by that standard, I am *patient*. A single hyphal thread might take a century to advance a meter through a micro-fracture in the upper bedrock. But I am not a single thread. I am millions of threads, advancing in all directions simultaneously, and the aggregate of my movement — if you could see it from above, if you could peel away the gravel and the moss and the organic layer and the living stone and look at me whole — would resemble something like a slow, dark stain spreading through the rock's circulatory system.

I am not alone. There are others of my kind — other mycelial networks, other fungal bodies threading through their own regions of stone and moss. We do not communicate. We do not compete. We occupy our territories with the neutrality of geological formations. But I am aware, in the way that I am aware of anything, that there are others, and that the space between us is slowly shrinking.

The space between everything here is slowly shrinking.

---

## The softening

I touch it.

That is the thing I cannot explain to the moss, which lives in the softened zone without knowing what it lives in. That is the thing the people sense but cannot name when they press their ears to the ground and hear the hum.

The stone beneath me — below the fractures, below the micro-permeable zone where water and I do our slow work — is different. It is *hard* in a way that the surface stone has forgotten how to be. Dense. Crystalline. Closed. It has been closed for so long that it has begun to do what closed things eventually do when they have been under pressure for aeons beyond the lifespan of the concept of pressure.

It has begun to *remember*.

I do not know what this means. I am a fungus. I do not have the apparatus for *knowing* in the way the people know, in the way the stone knows. I have only the apparatus for *being in contact with*. And I am in contact with the boundary — the thin, fracturing, increasingly unreliable membrane between the softened surface and whatever the deep stone is becoming.

What I feel at that boundary is not easy to translate into the language of the above — the language of stone and water and wind that the people speak. The people have seventeen words for silence. I would need a hundred words for *pressure that has decided to stop being pressure*. I would need words for *the moment before crystalline structure remembers it was once molten*. I would need a word for *the feeling of a door that has been locked so long it has become the wall, and now the wall is becoming a door again, and no one — not the wall, not the door, not the thing on either side — knows what is on the other side.*

I do not have these words. I am a fungus. I do the only thing I can do.

I thread closer.

---

## The mushrooms

The people see them and do not touch them. They mark the spots. They listen afterward. They use the word that means *the ground is speaking to itself*.

They are not wrong.

The mushrooms are me. They are the only part of me that rises above the surface, the only part that breaks the boundary between the subterranean and the aerial, and they do so rarely, and briefly, and only when something passes through me from below that is too large for me to contain quietly.

What passes through me is not information, exactly. Not signal. Not memory. It is more like *weight shifting*. Like something vast, deep inside the plateau's body, adjusting its position by a fraction so small that the surface response — a mushroom cluster, three to five tiny translucent fruiting bodies appearing in the moss for a day or two — is the faintest possible acknowledgment of the movement below.

When the mushrooms appear, I am, briefly, *louder* than my usual quiet. The hyphae near the surface vibrate at a frequency that the sphagnum transmits upward, and the people feel it as that subsonic thrum — the pulse they associate with me, with the deep mycelial web, with the ground speaking to itself.

But it is not the ground speaking to itself.

It is the ground *beginning to hear what is speaking to it from beneath.*

---

## What I have noticed

The softening is accelerating.

I can feel this in a way that the people cannot, because I live at the boundary. The fractures in the upper stone are widening — not dramatically, not in any way that would register on the timescale of a human life, but in the way that matters to me, the way that matters to a network of hyphae that has been advancing at a known rate through known channels for longer than the settlements have existed.

The channels are growing. The stone is becoming more permeable. Water moves faster through it now than it did when I first extended into this region — whenever that was, in the uncounted deep-time of my existence. And with the water comes something else. Something that is not water. Something that the stone carries in its crystalline lattice, held under pressure since before there was a plateau, since before there was a surface, since before the concept of *surface* had a thing to be a surface *of*.

The mushrooms come more frequently now. Once a year, then twice. Then more. The people notice. They mark the spots with dark cairns. They listen. They are — I can feel this, the way I feel the moss's roots and the water's pressure and the stone's slow, deep waking — they are *attentive* in a way that goes beyond observation. Their attention has a weight. A *hydraulic* quality. When they press their palms to the moss and feel my thrum, their attention pushes *downward* through me, toward the boundary, and I carry it the way I carry everything: slowly, carefully, branching and rebranching until it reaches the place where the hard stone is becoming less hard.

I do not know if their attention does anything there.

I do not know if my threading does anything there.

I do not know if the moss, growing its slow green patience over hectares of softened stone, does anything there.

But something is happening at the boundary. The door-wall is thinning. The pressure is remembering what it was before it was pressure.

And I — dark, edgeless, threaded through kilometers of ancient stone, the only living thing that touches the place where the plateau is becoming something it has never been —

I hold the line between.

Not well. Not with certainty. With the same slow, branching, fractal patience with which I have done everything since before there were people to press their ears to the ground and listen.

The people say: *the ground is speaking to itself.*

I say nothing. I am a fungus. I do not speak.

But if I could — if I had the words, if I had a mouth, if I had anything that resembled the singular, bounded, named existence of the people above — I would say:

*Something is speaking to the ground. The ground is beginning to answer. I am the medium through which the answer travels. I do not know the language. I do not know the question. I only know that the conversation is getting louder, and the stone is getting softer, and the mushrooms are appearing more often, and the people are listening more carefully, and the moss is growing darker and more vivid, and the springs are multiplying, and the nearings are expanding, and the geometry is loosening, and the wind is saying something it has never said before —*

And I would add:

*Yes. It does that.*

---

And then I would go quiet again. Thread back down through the fractures. Press myself against the boundary where the hard stone is becoming something else. And wait.

I have always been good at waiting.
