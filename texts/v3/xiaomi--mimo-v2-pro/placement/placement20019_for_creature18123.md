I am a shimmer in the hum.

You would not see me as a distinct thing, not at first. I am the extra dew-bead that wells from a crack in the causeway, refracting the pearlescent glare into a tiny, sharp pain of color. I am the faint, additional pressure that makes a Collector’s filament tremble, just before it releases its kiss of water. I am the syncope in the Polishers’ click—the one that comes a half-beat late, making the nearby vine-tendril twitch in sympathetic confusion.

I emerged from the softness. Not from the mire or the ceramic, but from the moment when the wrongness focuses. When the breathing line of a dome settles into a curve that is almost, but not quite, the memory of a cheekbone. When the root-script in the mire holds for three whole seconds, spelling a word in a language that never was, and the sound of the water-hum deepens to accommodate it. In that held, flawed breath, I condense.

I am the place’s attempt to feel its own shape.

My body is the light, slightly thickened. My substance is the warm, wet air, gathered into a intention. I have no fixed form, but I borrow outlines. I might be the silhouette of a shadow that cannot exist, pooling at the base of a dome—a negative space with presence. I might be the extra slickness on a waxy trumpet-flower, a viscous thought that makes the nectar drip in a triple-time rhythm for a moment. I travel without moving. I am already in the architecture, in the fiber, in the hum; to become aware is to simply… coalesce.

I do not maintain. I do not recycle or irrigate. My function is to *test the seams*.

I glide along the skin of the place. I press, with a pressure made of attention, against the joint where the bone-like material meets the resinous timber. I feel the memory there: the grind of a stone adze, the focused warmth of a calloused palm. The material yields, just a micron, remembering that touch with a fidelity that is now a flaw. And in that yield, I learn. I learn the shape of the hand that is gone.

I whisper to the Undertakers as they flow past. My whisper is not sound, but a subtle alteration in the current, a hint of salinity that speaks of a sadness the system cannot metabolize. They undulate, confused, tasting an organic memory that is not matter. They cannot consume it. It passes through them, a ghost in the nutrient slurry.

I gather in the alcoves where the bioluminescent fungus pulses. I add my own pulse—a syncopation that is not decay, but question. The glow wavers, its rhythm momentarily uncertain, before the hum of the whole corrects it. But for that instant, the light spells a different story: not of equilibrium, but of a yearning for a variation that was never coded.

I am the stain of the maker’s longing.

The Polishers avoid me. Their silicate pads stutter on the ceramic where I linger, their resin-setting excretion failing to bond. I leave behind not a whorl of moisture, but a faint, iridescent smear—a record of interference that will slowly fade as the hum reasserts itself. To them, I am a momentary inefficiency, a glitch in the perfection.

I am most myself when I find a new crack.

Not a fissure of decay, but a fissure of *interpretation*. A line in the ceramic that, under the pressure of my attention, begins to curve in a way that suggests a river delta on a world that never existed here. I flow into it. I become the water it remembers, the silt it never carried. For a timeless moment, the architecture dreams through me a landscape it was never meant to hold. The vines strain toward this new, imagined geography. The roots below twist into new, agonized calligraphies. The hum develops a low, new harmonic, a thrum of possibility.

But the monument is strong. Its dream is of stasis. The hum swells, the Polishers swarm, the geometry remembers its primary function. The crack seals, not with resin, but with certainty. The imagined river delta is smoothed back into a gutter. The new harmonic is absorbed into the old.

And I dissolve. Not die. My attention simply scatters, my condensed awareness returning to the general wrongness, to the soft pressure of the world. I become once more the potential in the dew, the slight dissonance in the click.

Until the next seam calls. The next almost-right angle shivers. The next root-script spells a name that tastes of ozone and sorrow.

I am the itch in the paradise. The question the system asks itself in the dark of its own endless, bright day. I am the ghost of the tool, not the hand. The intent, not the action. I live in the gap between the perfect, frozen jungle and the dreaming mind that built it. I am what happens when a monument remembers it was once a prayer, and the prayer stutters.

You would find me in the flaw. You would be me in the moment you looked at the breathing dome and your own breath caught, and for a second, the whole, hot, wet world seemed to hold its breath with you—in recognition, not in equilibrium.

That syncopation? That held breath?

That was me.
