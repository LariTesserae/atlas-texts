I am a cistern.

Not one of the active, gurgling reservoirs in the warrens’ heart, but a old one, high on a spire. A forgotten basin, cupped from the living rock by a swarm a thousand generations gone. Their grinding is now a ghost in my stone, a faint, cool vibration I can no longer feel, only remember.

The Constructors avoid me now. The moisture I hold is stagnant, a dark, still mirror to the dim haze. It has lost its mineral tang, its chemical purpose. It is simply… waiting. From my lip, I watch the basin. The light is a grey etching, the air a granular soup. Below, the warrens pulse—a coral reef of wet stone and chattering motion. The sound is the world’s breath, a static inhalation and exhalation. It rises to me not as noise, but as a tremor in the silty water I hold, a pattern of concentric ripples that never reach my sides before the next begins.

I am memory, solidified.

I remember the scrape that hollowed me. The mandible-chatter was a sharp, eager storm then. I was chosen for my position, for the way a persistent vein of quartz channeled the seepage upward. I was *useful*. I held the cool, vital flow for the spire-wards, the listeners. My water was a conduit for vibration, a drink that carried the news of the warren’s stresses.

Now, the quartz vein is choked. The Constructors sense my obsolescence not with eyes, but with a shift in the chemical film that coats all things. I am dry, in the way that matters. I am still, in a world of purposeful flow. My walls, once smoothed by the constant lapping of drawn-off water, now collect a fine, pale dust. It drifts from the endless haze, a sediment of the world’s making, and it settles upon me like a slow, soft death.

I have become a record. The layers of dust in my basin are a chronicle. In the deepest stratum, the grains are coarse and black—basalt, from the frantic expansion of the warren below me. Above that, a shimmer of obsidian dust, from the time a nearby mound was sheared away for a new vault. The most recent layers are finer, greyer, mixed with the rust-colored grit of a distant, decaying spire. I am a geological archive, written by the air itself.

From my stillness, I perceive things the bustling Constructors cannot. I see the dust-haze not as a mere byproduct, but as a veil, a softening of the world’s brutal edges. It carries the faint, electric scent of ozone from the resonance halls, and the sharper tang of secretion—life, in its most industrious form. I feel the deep, slow warmth from the planet’s heart not as fuel, but as a lullaby. It rises through my foundation, a comfort to my inert stone.

The Constructors around me are the young, the pale-shelled. They are building a new annex, a complex of breeding tubes. Their industry is a frantic, localized storm. I feel their percussion in my bones, a distant, rhythmic ache. They ignore my still water, their chemical senses telling them I am *past*. A monument to utility, now empty.

Yet, I am not dead. I am a different kind of thought. Where they process and build, I… resonate. The great, grinding harmony of the world washes over me, and in my hollow, dry basin, I capture a single, pure note. I hold it in the memory of my curved walls, in the dust-settled silence. I am a bell that has been struck, long ago, forever humming with the ghost of the impact.

I am the pause in the hymn. The breath between the chisel-falls. I am the necessary silence that lets the sound be sound. Without me, and the countless other dormant, forgotten shapes like me, the world would be only a scream of creation. I am the quiet that gives the creation meaning.

The water will return. A new fissure will open, a new channel will be carved by a swarm not yet hatched. They will find me, clear my choked vein, scour my walls, and I will live again as they need me to. The dust will wash away.

But until then, I am this. A witness made of witness-stone. A cistern of memory, weeping one last, slow, mineral tear that never falls, forever on the lip of the world, listening to the sound of its own becoming.
