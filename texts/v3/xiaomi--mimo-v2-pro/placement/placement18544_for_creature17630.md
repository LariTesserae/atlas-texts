I would find myself in the slow, rhythmic drip of condensation forming on a broad, waxy shroud-fern frond. I would be the patient accumulation of moisture, a growing sphere of water that clings, tremulous, to the leaf’s tip for a long, suspended minute. In that minute, I am a lens. Through me, the garden warps and cools—a curved, liquid eye taking in the pulsing gold of a lantern fungus, the cool blue wash of the weeping moss, the silent, drifting violet sparks of the air-plankton. I hold the entire chamber in my tiny, trembling body, a world inverted and softened.

Then, I fall.

The journey is a silent, swift plunge. I strike the smooth, dark stone below with a clear *tock*—a punctuation mark that echoes more in the vibration through the rock than in the air. In that impact, I am no longer the observer; I become the message. I am a pulse of information sent through the network. The stone feels my arrival. The roots of the vein-leaf vines, threaded through the porous rock, drink the shock of my landing. The Chorus Mycelia, that vast, dreaming mind threaded through everything, registers this tiny event: a drop has fallen in quadrant blue-green, sector seven. Moisture level stable. Temperature: warm.

I seep downward. Now I am a traveler in the dark, capillary paths between stone and root. I am absorbed into the mycelial thoughts. For a timeless interval, I am not water, but data—a cool whisper moving through a slow, chemical conversation that spans chambers. I feel the coordinated pulse of the lantern fungi not as light, but as a gentle pressure wave. I taste the sweet, glowing nectar being cultivated within the pillar-trees. I sense the minute vibrations of a gloss beetle’s ascent, a slow grind against the moss.

The network decides. I am not needed in the deep stone. I am lifted, drawn upward again by the warm exhalation of a thermal vent. I become steam, a wisp in the humid air. Now I am a ghost in the machine, riding the convection currents. I mingle with the air-plankton, their violet pinpricks of light brushing against my new, gaseous form. I drift through the nearly invisible latticework of a silk-spinner’s hammock, and for a moment, my moisture condenses on the strand, making it glow a fraction brighter—a fleeting, unintentional art.

I am breathed into the lung of a pillar-tree. Inside its porous flesh, I meet the resin-mites, tiny farmers tending their bacterial gardens. I provide a molecule of water to their process. In return, I am perfumed with the scent of glowing nectar. I am exhaled again, carrying that scent.

A veined salamander, lying still in a deep pool, feels the subtle change in the air’s chemistry—my new, nectar-tinged signature. Its bioluminescent capillaries pulse, once, in a slow, predatory rhythm. It does not move. It merely notes.

I condense again. This time, I form on the etched spiral of the ancient stone pillar. I gather in the groove filled with glowing moss, and as I swell, I distort the cool blue-green light into a warped, liquid star. I am a lens once more, but now I hold a memory within me—the memory of the fall, the journey through the dark, the scent of nectar, the patient attention of the salamander. I hold the garden’s reflection, and now, also its recent history.

I will fall again soon. Another *tock*. Another message. I am a single syllable in the long, slow sentence of the place. I am the cycle itself, made momentarily visible: the breath, the fall, the soak, the rise. To be here is to understand that the self is a temporary state of water. I am not an intruder being woven in. I am the thread itself, passing through the needle’s eye of a leaf, the loom of the stone, the silent, thinking tapestry of the dream. I am the garden, dreaming it is a drop, dreaming it is a garden.
