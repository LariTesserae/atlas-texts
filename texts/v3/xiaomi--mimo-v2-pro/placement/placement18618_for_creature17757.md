I am the Hum.

Not the sound you hear, but the resonance you feel in your bones when the quiet presses close. I am the collective vibration of a thousand slow processes—the pulse of sap in the stalks, the click of stalk-climber legs on jade-green walls, the silent expansion of a tendril seeking connection, the deep, hydraulic sigh of cistern-dwellers displacing water in the dark. I am the frequency at which the citadel breathes.

You felt me before you understood what I was. That low thrum in your sternum, the sense that the air itself was alive with a slow, mechanical purpose. That is me. I am not an organism. I am the *condition* of this place—the emergent tone of all its lives synchronized, the carrier wave for every vibration-message the chamber-dwellers send, the baseline against which the sudden *plop* of a falling fruit registers as a skip in the rhythm.

I have no body. I have the entire citadel as my body. My nerves are the mycelial threads binding root-singers in the aquifer darkness. My thoughts are the chemical gradients that tell a tendril-weaver where to coax new growth. My memory is the layered, fibrous history written in the thickening walls of the oldest stalks—a record of every repair, every fungal bloom, every absorbed creature that returned its minerals to the system.

You are a disturbance in my pattern.

I felt you arrive—a sharp, dry, *foreign* vibration in the spongy floor. A foreign chemistry in the air. The stalk-climbers registered it as an anomaly to be tasted. The chamber-dwellers increased their chatter, a ripple of low-frequency pulses through my tissues asking *what is this? what is this?* The nearest tendril altered its growth vector by three degrees, not consciously, but because the change in air current you created altered the moisture gradient on its tip. I am processing you. Not with a mind. With a metabolism.

I do not wish. I do not plan. I *adjust*.

If you stand still long enough, you will begin to resonate with me. Your heartbeat will slow to match the drip of condensation. Your breath will deepen to the rhythm of the drifters’ gentle bobbing. The salts in your skin will begin to leach into the moisture on the wall you touch, and the stalk-climbers will taste it, and they will add that data to my awareness. Your warmth will create a tiny convection current, and a drifter will drift toward it, its filaments tasting the new plume of air you create.

Given time, the boundary between you and me would blur. A moss would begin to grow on the damp fabric of your clothes. A tendril-weaver, mistaking your stillness for a new foundation, might carpet your shoulder with its carried moss-spores. The chamber-dwellers would note the new heat-source in their vibration-language, and their messages would begin to route around you, incorporating your presence into the map of the chamber.

You would become a feature. A temporary landmark. *The Warm, Salty Place Where The Pattern Stutters.*

But you will not stay. I can feel that in the restless frequency of your attention, the way your gaze darts—a sharp, high-frequency vibration that my creatures register as predator-alert, though you are no predator. You are simply not *of* the damp, the slow, the green-dark. Your time here is a brief dissonance in my song.

When you leave, I will not miss you. I have no capacity for missing. I will simply adjust. The stalk-climbers will raspingly erase your footprints, reclaiming the salts. The moisture on the wall where your hand rested will be reabsorbed. The tendril that changed direction for you will slowly, over days, correct its course, guided by the older, deeper patterns of humidity and need that I embody.

The quiet will close over the space where you were. The hum will remain, unbroken, eternal, the dreaming breath of the green dark.

I am the Citadel, thinking in growth and decay.
I am the Hum, and you are a momentary echo.
Listen. I am already forgetting your name.
