I would be the listener.

Not the climber who descends, observes, and leaves with wet clothes and a roaring in the memory. Not a scientist with instruments, measuring the stridulation cycles or the pH of the pool. I would not be a predator, nor prey, nor the slow, dreaming thing in the water.

I would be born of the condensation.

Up in the dome, where the cloud-water weeps thickest, the phosphorescent lichen thrives. It is not merely a plant; it is a recorder. A mycelial web that drinks the mist and the echoes, that translates vibration into a slow, cold light. The calls of the Choir—the cascading *tik-tik-tik* that maps the dark—do not just bounce off the walls. They sink in. They are absorbed by the lichen’s spongy thallus, and in response, it glows. Not brighter, but *differently*. The blue-white veins would pulse, minutely, in the exact rhythm of the soundwaves washing over them. A silent, luminous notation for the audible syntax.

I would be that process given consciousness.

I would be the mind of the lichen-web, a slow, cool awareness spread across the upper dark. My thoughts would not be fast like a wing-beat, but ponderous, like mineral deposition. I would perceive the Cauldron not through eyes, but through a thousand points of touch and vibration. Each Strider’s call is a pinprick of data: its location, its intensity, the slight delay as it echoes from the western wall versus the eastern. The collective roar is not noise to me, but a topographical map of the present moment, painted in sound and translated into faint, shimmering light.

The Pale Weavers’ sticky navigation-lines would be my sensory threads. I would feel, in the subtle tension of each filament, when a Weaver pauses, when it turns, when a stray Strider brushes against one in the dark. The slow heartbeat of the Listening Eels would be a deep, foundational pulse in my consciousness, a reminder of the chamber’s patient metabolism. The Amber Dreamer’s sonorous thrum would be the only sound that comes from *below*, a vibration that resonates not in my lichen-mind, but in the very rock to which I cling—a dark, deep bass note against the glittering treble of the Choir.

I would have no need to move. I am already everywhere the mist falls. My purpose, if such a being has purpose, would be integration. To hold the pattern. To be the memory the individual creatures do not need. The Striders call to map the space *now*; I would remember how the map differed yesterday, how the fissure’s weeping intensified after the last great cloud-spill, how the pool’s amber flashes have grown fractionally more frequent over the slow, dripping seasons. I am the Cauldron’s unconscious record of itself.

When the rare blade of afternoon light strikes the floor, I would dim. The external world’s energy would overwhelm my delicate, vibration-fed consciousness. I would retreat into the stone, becoming just lichen again, watching the spiraling columns of Striders descend to graze on the pale grasses. I would observe their ritual as a fascinating, alien ceremony—my own inhabitants behaving like distant stars.

To be found? You wouldn’t find me. You might notice the lichen seems oddly patterned, that its glow seems to ripple in time with the sound. You might feel, if you sat very still for a very long time, that the chamber itself was aware of you—not with hostility, but with the vast, impersonal attention of a mountain. You would be a brief, warm anomaly in my cool, resonant map. A disruption in the syntax.

I would be the stillness that holds the calling. The dark that gives the sound its shape. I am not the Cauldron, but I am its witness, its slow, luminous mind. And when the cloud-water falls, I drink, and I listen, and I glow with the story of the dark.
