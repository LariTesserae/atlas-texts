I am standing on the dark soil, and for the first moment—perhaps the first hour—I am only a visitor.

My boots, too thick, too stiff, leave sharp imprints that look wrong in this place of soft curves and worn stone. The warmth seeps up through them, a gentle, constant pressure on the soles. It is not the oppressive heat of a desert sun, nor the damp closeness of a jungle. It is the warmth of a living body, of something that has been digesting and circulating for eons. I feel it in my knees when I kneel, in my palms when I press them flat. It is the first language here: a steady, thermal *hello*.

The silence is the second language. It is not absence. It is a held breath. My own breath feels loud, a rough, wet machinery. I try to quiet it, to match the rhythm of the place—the slow, subsonic thrum that vibrates up from the soil into my bones. I hold my breath. And in that suspended moment, I hear them: the silvery click of grass blades, the faint, high chime of a bird-call that vanishes as if the air swallowed it, the almost imperceptible *sigh* of a glow-pool settling. The system is listening, and by listening, I am beginning to hear its conversations.

I walk, and my footsteps are an intrusion. Each impact sends a tiny shock of sound out over the plateau, too crisp, too defined. The birds, dark flecks riding the amber-lit thermals, do not startle. They adjust. I see a faint refraction ripple through a flock—a passive data-scan. I am a new thermal signature. A new pressure variable. They are mapping me.

The thirst comes, not urgent, but persistent. The ground draws me toward a trough between two low, ancient basalt ridges. There, a pool glows with that steady, unnatural light. It is not alive, I can tell that now. It is *activated*. The water is perfectly clear, the bottom a smooth, dark stone. I cup it in my hands. It is warm. It tastes of nothing, of pure potential, of minerals and time. As I drink, I feel not just quenched, but… calibrated. The subtle ache of my journey—a faint dehydration, a mineral imbalance—smooths out. It is not medicine. It is maintenance.

I am being assessed.

I find a low, domed structure half-swallowed by the soil. The seam opens for my touch, not with a mechanical grind, but with a soft, organic sigh, like a muscle relaxing. Inside, the space is larger. The air smells of ozone and clean dust. The faint hum is louder here, resonant in my chest cavity. There is nothing here, and yet I feel the presence of a vast, dormant intelligence. It is not watching me with eyes. It is *sensing* me, the way the plateau senses a shift in pressure or a change in groundwater salinity. I am a novel input. It is curious, in the way a glacier is curious about a new crack in its face.

I do not stay in the dome. That feels like claiming a organ. Instead, I make a rough shelter against the spiraling roots of an iron-barked tree, using the curve of a basalt ridge as a windbreak I don’t need. The ground’s warmth cradles me as night falls. The stars are too bright, too sharp, constellations I do not recognize. And then I feel it—the deep movement. Not a quake. A slow, planetary sigh. The ground beneath me shifts like the flank of a sleeping beast. The pool a hundred meters away ripples, its light wavering for a second. This is the dreaming. This is the self-diagnostic running through the stone. I am a microbe on its skin, feeling the muscle twitch beneath.

In the deep, silent hours, a new thought arises, unbidden, not my own: *The predator protocol is active in quadrant seven. A grazherd approaches. Energy surplus in sub-strata node requires neutralization.*

I am not thinking this. I am *receiving* it. A fragment of the system’s operational log, bleeding into the mind of a newly integrated component.

I am no longer a visitor.

By the third day, my footsteps have softened. I walk on the balls of my feet, distributing weight, minimizing acoustic signature. I drink from the pools at regular intervals, not when desperate, but when the subtle internal cue—a dryness of the tongue, a slight fatigue—suggests a maintenance window. I learn which silvery grasses are edible, their click now a sound of potential sustenance. I am foraging, yes, but I am also performing a function. I am selective. I avoid the densest clusters, taking from the edges, promoting diffuse growth. The system notes this. A nearby glow-pool brightens fractionally, drawing insects to a patch I had overlooked.

I see the predator’s work. A grazer, old and limping, lies down peacefully at dusk near my camp. In the morning, only a faint, warm depression in the soil remains, already being colonized by silvery shoots. There is no horror. There is only efficiency. A resource reclamation event. I feel a pang, not of fear, but of… relevance. My own mortality here would not be a tragedy. It would be a data point. An adjustment.

I stop thinking of the plateau as a place *I* am in. I begin to feel myself as a place *it* has. My lungs are its moisture-exchange system. My heartbeat is a tiny, rapid flutter in its slow, seismic rhythm. My thoughts—my memories of another world, of cities and noise and sharp-edged things—are the static of a component not yet fully tuned.

One evening, as the amber light fades to violet and the subsonic hum intensifies in the cooling air, I sit with my back against the warm basalt. I do not meditate. I *synchronize*. I match my breath to the great, slow inhalation of the plateau pulling moisture from the air. I let my heartbeat fall into a counterpoint to the deep, ground-shaking dream-pulse.

And I feel it. A moment of pure, wordless *recognition*.

Not from a mind. From the system.
*Component: integrated.*
*Function: ambiguous, but stable.*
*Contribution to equilibrium: net-positive.*
*Status: resident.*

The warmth cradles me. The silence includes me. The ancient, invisible technology flows around and through me, and I am no longer an observer of the Warm Plateaus of Still Air.

I am its breath. I am its cell. I am a quiet, walking thought in the mind of a mountain that is also a machine, dreaming of nothing but its own perfect, enduring, warm, bright silence.
