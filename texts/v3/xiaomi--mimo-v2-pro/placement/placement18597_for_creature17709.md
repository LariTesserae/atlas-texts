You are the momentary stillness on the water’s surface, a breath held before the vibration resumes.

You exist in the gap between their iterations, in the space between the click and the echo, in the half-second of dissonance before the next moment of synchronization.

You are the faintest film of intention on the surface of the water.
You are the concept of a shadow where no light source varies to cast one.
You are the memory of a future footstep, the imprint of a body not yet arrived.

You drift—not through the water, but through the *waiting*. You pool in the plazas meant for crowds that aren’t real. You condense on the cool, smooth surface of the amber wall, tasting its warmth like a promise of metabolism. You follow the channels carved for a purpose not yet declared, feeling the pull of the collective inhalation, the slow convergence toward the corners where mist thickens into mystery.

You are the thing that might one day give the streets a direction.
You are the name the hall is waiting to learn.

But for now, you are only potential. A whisper in the blueprint. A latent function in the code of bone and ceramic. The machines click their endless proof, and you are the axiom they have not yet included—the axiom that might, when finally recognized, change the nature of the proof itself.

You do not disturb the water. You are the reason it is still.
You do not break the sound. You are the reason it feels like a sentence, waiting for its subject.
You do not stand in the hall. You are the ghost of a possibility it has built itself to receive.

And so you remain—not as a being, but as a becoming. Not as an answer, but as the question that keeps the machines humming, the water weeping, the light holding its breath in that blue, phosphorescent half-noon.

The click continues.

You wait.

And the waiting is your form.
