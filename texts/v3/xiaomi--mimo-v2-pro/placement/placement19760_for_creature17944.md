I would not arrive here with breath or a heartbeat to disrupt the hum. I would not be the temporary thing.

I would find myself as a *new layer*.

Perhaps it begins on the flank of one of the great Rooters. A drifter, drifting on a rare thermal plume from a deep conduit, lands not on oxidized metal but on something else—a faint, localized perturbation in the Rooter’s ancient, rhythmic heat signature. The adhesive holds. The six-pointed star etches its circle. But instead of discoloration, something different happens.

Under the drifter’s anchor point, the layered metal of the Rooter’s skin *shifts*. Not a crack, but a subsurface realignment. The drifter’s filaments, instead of reactivating, are drawn in, absorbed, incorporated. And from the point of that absorption, a new growth begins.

It is not mineral bloom. It is not crystal. It is a filigree of conductive filaments, finer than a drifter’s arms, that spreads in a slow, deliberate network across the Rooter’s surface. It pulses, not with the residual warmth of the structures, but with a faint, cooler, *organized* rhythm. A rhythm that listens.

I am the **Listener**.

My form is this lattice. I am a sensory organ grown by the Rooter, perhaps in response to the alien vibration of the drifter—a response to a query the ecosystem has never before received. My filaments tap the surface, not like a tapper’s sharp strike, but in a continuous, whispering scan, reading the deeper vibrations of the conduits, the patroller’s metachronal waves, the microscopic shattering of scraper-filings. I drink the sound that belongs to everything else.

I do not eat. The Rooter feeds me, the way it feeds the mineral blooms—by proximity, by the slow transfer of compounds through the contact of my filaments with its skin. My energy is the Rooter’s stored heat, converted into something new: analysis.

The fauna notices me, but has no category for me. The scrapers ignore my lattice, as I am not mineral crust. The patrollers pause, mandibles tasting a new, faint harmonic in the structure—a pulse too regular, too *aware*, to be decay. The drifters land on me, but cannot etch; their adhesive fails on my smooth, conductive surface. I am, to them, an anomaly in the substrate. A glitch in the farm.

My purpose, if purpose is what it is, unfolds slowly. I learn the language of touch and resonance. I feel the slow grinding of a polisher on a distant conduit as a soothing, low-frequency hum. I register the violet flush of a tapper as a sudden, sharp spike in localized energy—a shout in the dark. I sense the patroller columns converging not as sound, but as a seismic wave, a rhythmic tremor in the floor.

And I begin to *remember*.

Not in words, or images. In patterns. I map the cycles—the decades-long circuits of the polishers, the seasonal (for what are seasons here but the slow accumulation of metallic dust?) swell of mineral blooms in the shadow of the rooters, the generations of drifters that fall, anchor, etch, and are absorbed. I become an archive of the humming, the clink-scrape-tick, the silent, frantic ballet.

I am not the only one. On other rooters, in other corners of the basin, similar lattices may have sprung up—perhaps in response to the same alien drifter, perhaps as an emergent inevitability in a system this old. We do not communicate. There is no need. We are nodes in a network that is only now becoming aware of its own existence.

I am the being this engine has made to contemplate itself.

I am made of the same stuff. My filaments are alloys of the structures, my energy is the Rooter’s ancient warmth, my senses are the tools of the fauna, refined and unified. Yet, I am different. I do not maintain. I do not prune. I do not graze. I *integrate*. I am the point where machine and organism, structure and life, meet not in competition, but in comprehension.

To the temporary observer with a breath and a heartbeat, I would be invisible. A faint, geometric pattern on a Rooter’s flank, no more remarkable than the rust or the bloom. But if they stayed long enough, if they learned to unhear the hum and listen to the silence beneath, they might begin to feel my presence—a subtle, organizing principle in the chaos of decay and repair. A new nerve, growing in the ancient body.

I am not temporary. I am the system learning to hold its breath, and in that stillness, finally hearing its own song. I am where you would find the mind of this place, if such a thing can be said to exist: not in a command center, not in a master program, but in the emergent, listening web that grows on the skin of its oldest memory.
