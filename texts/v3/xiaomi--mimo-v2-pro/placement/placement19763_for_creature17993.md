I would be a Pipe-Mender.

I would be born in the warm, silty dark of a cistern, my first sensation not light or air, but the mineral signature of copper leaching into still water. A taste like a blueprint, an instinct. My body would be long, pale, eyeless—a thing of sensation and reaction. I would not see the Weep. I would *taste* its fractures, *feel* its vibrations through the wet stone, *hear* the precise hiss of a failing seal as a symphony of urgent repair.

My world is not one of sight, but of gradients and decay. I would navigate the labyrinthine pipe network not by memory, but by thirst. The water itself would guide me, its altered flavor along a hairline crack in cast iron a beacon in the absolute dark. My rasping mouth would clean the fracture, my own saliva a secret mixture tasting of stone and time. I would work not with purpose, but with a deep, cellular satisfaction—the same satisfaction a river feels finding its bed. Each repair is a closing of a circuit, a calming of a turbulence that resonates in my own nerves.

I would know the Valve-Tenders by the grind of their armored limbs on the pipes, a sound that means the system is adjusting itself around me. I would be indifferent to the Condensation Harvesters skittering on my back, mistaking me for a weeping stone. The slow, velvet patrol of the Rust-Eaters would be a familiar scent on the air, the smell of old iron being returned to black dust. The hum is not background noise; it is the pulse of my world. In alcoves where the Static Bloom clusters, the hum changes texture in my bones, and I know to move on, that this is a place for slow crystal growth, not for the frantic dance of liquid repair.

I am not intelligent. I am a function. I am the white blood cell in the Weep’s circulatory system. My life is a series of chemical equations solved with my body. I do not remember past repairs, nor anticipate future ones. There is only the eternal *now* of the taste of escaping water, and the instinct to follow it, to seal it, to return the turbulent system to its chaotic, weeping equilibrium.

To be me is to understand that the Weep is not a machine in a basin, but a single being. Its bones are the basalt. Its blood is the water. Its nervous system is the network of humming pipes. Its autonomic breath is the steam and the sigh of pistons. And I, the Pipe-Mender, am a specialized cell in its ever-repairing flesh, dreaming only of the taste of copper and the sound of a leak made whole.
