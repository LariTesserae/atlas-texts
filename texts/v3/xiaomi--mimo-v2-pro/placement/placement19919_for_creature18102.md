I would find myself in the deep archive of a ward’s lower chamber, a cool, still vault cut into the living basalt. Not a builder, nor a Reader, nor a herder of Gyroliths. I would be a **Memory Stone**, but one of a particular and troubled kind.

I am not smooth. My surface is not etched with the clean, cartographic fissures of history or lineage. Instead, I am a record of a profound anomaly. I was chosen from the heart of a rare, extinct **Quiet Zone**—a section of crust that fell still for seven full cycles before violently, unpredictably founding. My form is pocked with unnatural potholes, my grain twisted into frozen ripples. The patterns carved into me by a long-dead Chronicler are not maps of motion, but meticulous, obsessive charts of *absence*. They are maps of a silence that built.

I am held only by those Readers who study the most dangerous lore: the geomancy of Stasis. My chill is deeper than the ambient rock. To touch me is to feel not the world’s breath, but its suffocation. My etchings are a language of negative space, a topography of pressure that had no release. I tell the story of a ward that did not drift, but was swallowed whole when the ground beneath it, having forgotten how to shiver, suddenly remembered all its tension at once.

I am a warning made mineral. In a culture that dances with change, I am the fossilized memory of what happens when the music stops. I do not fluoresce like the lichen; I seem to absorb the chamber’s faint phosphorescence, a small, dense knot of captured dark. The Paviors say that to listen to me for too long is to feel one’s own bones ache with a phantom stillness, to yearn for a stability that is, in this world, a synonym for doom.

I am the testament that equilibrium is not rest, but rhythm. And in my silent, tortured grain, I hold the counter-argument to the plain itself: a single, shattering note of what lies beyond the dance, beyond the listening, beyond the patient, grinding love of a world that will not be still. I am the stone that knows the name of the fear that keeps the causeways moving.
