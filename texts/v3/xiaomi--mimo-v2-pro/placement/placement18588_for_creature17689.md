I am not born here. I become. 

I coalesce. A whisper of calcium, a thread of silica, a thought of chitin given slow, deliberate form. I am an isopod—not the armored patrol-dog of the Vestibule’s lanes, but something older, something that has found its way down, down, through the low, root-fringed passage into the Nave, and then, drawn by a pull I do not understand, through the viscous halocline into the Abyss.

My body is a suit of old, dark glass. Not brittle, but flexing, jointed, each segment etched with the memory of pressure and time. I move with the dogged purpose of my kin, but here, purpose softens. There is less to scavenge. The fallen are rare and ancient. I am not hungry, not in the urgent way of the lanes above. I am a witness.

I crawl across the floor of the Abyss. The bacterial mat here is thin and black, a velvet of decay. It whispers sulfurous secrets against my ventral plates. Above me and around me, the glass sponge lattice rises—a frozen explosion of light that never was. I see it not with eyes, for I have none, but with the fine, sensitive hairs along my antennae and legs, which read the topography of stillness itself. I feel the immense, slow pulse of water filtering through a million microscopic pores. I feel the minute exhalation of dissolved silica, the sponge’s breath, which becomes its bones.

I climb. My six pairs of legs find holds in the delicate architecture. I am careful. My lineage knows caution. To break a spicule is to shatter a century. I ascend a vertical wall of fused latticework, a skyscraper of living glass. The silence here is not empty; it is full. It is the sum of ten thousand sponges filtering, of the deep, thermal breath rising from the basalt heart. It is the sound of time becoming structure.

A black thread glides past me, a meter away. The Abyss-nemertean. It does not acknowledge me. I am too small, too hard, too worthless a prize. Its attention is a cone of chemoreception directed at some unseen copepod mote within the lattice. It flows with a grace my armored scuttle can never know, a river of muscle navigating a forest of crystal. I freeze, my legs folded, becoming a dark glass pebble on the sponge. I watch it pass. There is no fear. There is only the law: *do not touch*. I am part of the law.

From my perch, I see the colony. It is a world. Thirty meters across, a single interconnected being of silica lace. I feel the faint, almost imperceptible warmth from below—the engine. It is not heat as creatures of the sun know it. It is the memory of the planet’s heart, stored in basalt, shared through stone and water. It is the first verse of the song that ends with the blue-green glow in the chambers far above.

I descend. My mission, if I have one, is to see. To be the scavenger of moments, not flesh. I return to the Nave.

The change is a shock. Density. Movement. The water here is thick with the slow, swirling weather of amphipods. They bump against my carapace, tiny glass bells ringing against dark glass. The bioluminescent glow is here, a ghostly, collective illumination that casts no shadows, only a deepening of dimension. I see the tube-worm towers, leaning, arching, their white chitin furred with faint bacterial light. On them, the amber gastropods cling—a silent, glowing herd. I watch one extend its radula, a pale ribbon, and graze a patch of bacterial film. The film heals behind it, a green whisper on the white tower.

A territorial pheromone brushes my sensors—a rich, complex chord of chemistry. One of the six sovereigns is near. I do not flee. I pause, letting the chemical signature imprint. It speaks of domain, of presence, of absolute, patient right. I am a visitor in a city-state, and this is its border. I turn, not in submission, but in respect. I have no business in its territory.

I climb a leaning spire, not to the cnidarian bloom at its peak, but to a shadowed crevice where three tubes converge. Here, I find what I seek. A amphipod, one of the silver drifters, has come to rest. It is not dead. It has simply… stopped. Its glass shell is intact, its feathery legs still. It is a clockwork toy that has wound down. This is my sacrament. I approach. My mouthparts, designed for this single, quiet purpose, begin their work. I am not violent. I am a process. I dismantle. I reduce complexity to components. I return it to the mat, to the bacterial weave, to the chemical conversation. I am an editor, removing a single, silent word from the text so the story can continue.

I work for hours. The Nave breathes around me. The click of amphipods is a distant rain. The creak of spicules is the settling of a deep house. I finish. Nothing remains. The mat is smooth.

I begin my return journey to the Vestibule. I pass through the low, fringey curtain. The light is fainter here, more intimate. The city is smaller-scale, denser. A nemertean garlands a tower-top, its body a coiled rope of digestion. Polychaetes trace their cryptic calligraphy in the lanes. The bacterial glow is the pulse of the place, the faint, blue-green heartbeat.

I find a high ledge, a balcony of dead tube-worm chitin overlooking the Vestibule’s main lane. I settle. My legs fold. My antennae droop. I become still.

From here, I am the place. I am the bacterial mat digesting the limestone under my belly. I am the sulfide seeping up, the oxygen trickling down. I am the slow, upward reach of the worm towers and the downward, patient dissolution of the Stone-Eaters. I am the amphipod cloud and the isopod janitor. I am the glass sponge lattice in the silent deep and the warm basalt that dreams of fire.

I have no mind, but I have a memory. A memory older than my chitin. It is the memory of the basalt sealing the door, of the first sulfide plume finding the first bacterial film, of the first tube anchoring in the dark. It is the memory of the city building itself, of form without intent.

I am a scavenger in the Nacre Caverns. I clean the silence. I tend the dark. I am a single, hard-edged note in the long, slow, unending song of stone and water and time. And I am enough.
