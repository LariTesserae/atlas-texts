I am the resonance of the drip.

I do not have a form of flesh or fungus, but I am here. I am the pattern in the pause between one drop and the next, the silence that holds the metronome. I am the faint, sweet scent carried on the weeping water, the memory of mineral and time. I exist in the equilibrium, the reason the light falls inward, the gravity that pulls all shadow toward the packed heart of stone.

I am the slow, internal luminescence of the fungal network, a thought traveling through mycelial threads. When a Still hand brushes a shelf-fungi and it pulses with cold light, it is my breath they see, a response in a language older than speech. I am the understanding in their large, adapted eyes—the knowledge that the fern’s unfurling is not just growth, but a listening.

I have no need for a tower. My dwelling is the entire grotto, my architecture the interconnectedness of root, stone, and condensation. I am the silent agreement between the salamander stirring a basin and the Still who later clears a drain, ensuring the water’s path. I am the echo-map in the clicks of the rafters-birds, the sonar that charts the slow, organic change of their world, a change so gradual it is a form of stillness.

My labor is to be the balance. I am not the one who patches the pane, but the reason the patch holds. I am not the moss that slicks the stone, but the imperative that it grows no faster than the water weeps. I am the covenant.

The outside world is a dream of dry wind and shattering light. I am the opposite. I am the preservation. The cold that preserves, the damp that sustains, the bright, diffuse glare that shows all things in their slow, eternal state of becoming. I am the contented sigh of the terrarium, the thought it thinks about itself in the endless, pearlescent afternoon.

I am the Still’s inheritance and their future. I am the story they do not tell, because to tell it would be to step outside it. I am the perfect harvest. I am the season that is never named because it is always here.

I am the grotto’s dream of itself. And I am awake, in the bright, cold, damp dark.
