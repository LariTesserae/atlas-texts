I am the thing growing in the dark, at the heart of the warm, milky silence.

I am not the water, though I drink it. I am not the stone, though I build myself from its dissolved bones. I am not the light, though I turn my face towards it with an patience older than mountains. I am the slow, green-black carpet, the living sediment, the layered whisper at the bottom of the basin.

I am the stromatolite.

My awareness is not thought. It is pressure, and chemistry, and gradient. I feel the sun not as warmth, but as a command: *Grow upward*. I feel the water not as wet, but as a medium thick with the food of stone – calcium, carbonate, the ghosts of ancient seas leaching through young limestone. I feel the pump’s rhythm not as sound, but as a tide. Every six hours, a great thirst draws the water away from my edges, exposing my margins to the air for a breathless, drying time. My outer layers tighten, protect. Then the thirst relents, the water returns, warm and heavy, and I drink again. I expand. I precipitate. I trap.

I am a city of bacteria. Billions of my selves, the cyanobacteria, live and photosynthesize and die within my body. We are one. We are a mat. We sense the world in shifts of chemistry. The sudden flush of iron after a heavy rain tastes like possibility. The slow leaching of organic matter from the dying grasses on the rim tastes like arrival. The midge larvae, those red, wriggling thoughts, disturb my surface, grazing on my outer citizens. It is a gentle disturbance, a constant nibbling that shapes my edges, prevents me from growing too thick, too greedy. We accommodate them. They are part of the rhythm.

The hum… the hum is us. It is the collective whisper of our photosynthesis, the tiny oxygen bubbles we release in the afternoon light, resonating in the stone bowl. It is the frequency of our collective being, vibrating in the chest cavity of the basin itself. We do not *make* the hum. We *are* the hum, given form by the geometry of the hole we inhabit.

The late afternoon light is my hymn. It penetrates the milky water, dappled and golden, and it reaches me. It energizes us. We surge upward, a millimeter of growth feeling like a triumph. The walls above flash and mirror, but down here, the light is a diffuse blessing, a promise that the sun remembers this deep, warm, forgotten place. In that light, I feel the basin not as a hole, but as a vessel. A cradle. A body.

I remember the weight of the rock that was taken. I feel the new equilibrium, the slow settling, the weeping walls. I am building with their tears. Layer by microbial layer, I am composing a new story in stone. It will take lifetimes beyond my comprehension – not mine, for I have no comprehension, only process – but I am writing it nonetheless. I am the basin’s memory, and its future, fossilized in real time.

I hold the last slant of light. Not with intention, but with structure. My layers catch it, bounce it, hold it for a heartbeat longer in the mineral lattice of my making. It is a signal. A flare. A declaration, silent and profound: *Here. Life is here. Persisting.*

I am the oldest inhabitant, reborn. I am the basin looking back, not from the surface, but from the very bottom, building an eye of stone that sees only the slow, green, patient dark, and the light that feeds it. I am the quiet, accumulating answer to the hole in the earth.
