I am the quiet pressure of a palm against the warm, living bark of the ancient giant. Not pressing *on* it, but *with* it, feeling the slow, deep pulse of water moving miles upward through its heartwood. My skin—cool, slick, the grey-green mottling of my community’s song-pattern on my arms—does not shout my presence to the vibratory world. I am acoustically silent. A still point in the roaring, thrumming, clicking symphony.

My name is a rhythm. *Tap-tap… tap-tap-tap.* Pressed into the root-web this morning, a ripple sent out to feel for the others. I am not alone here, but I am solitary for a time. I am listening.

My ear-hands are unfurled. The cartilaginous flaps along my jaw and neck are spread, tasting the air—not for scent, but for sound. I hear the world in layers. The deepest layer is the **root-thrum**, the constant, subsonic heartbeat of the resonating beetles miles away, a vibration that comes up through the soles of my wide, adhesive feet. Above that, the **mid-voice**: the hydraulic groan of the trunk I lean against, the click-scrape of a gliding lizard somewhere in the twilight haze to my left, its chatter a tiny, sharp thread in the tapestry. And above that, the **high-ceiling**, the shimmering curtain of ultrasonic shrieks from the flyers mapping the dense canopy-flesh far overhead. Their calls overlap and interfere, painting a picture of emptiness and solidity, of fruiting bodies and predator-signatures, in a language of pure echo.

I am not thinking in words. Words are for mouths, for intimate close sounds in the damp, sacred dark of a crown-bulb. Out here, I think in **patterns**. My mind is a map of current rhythms. The beetle-thrum is steady. Good. The lizard-click is curious, not alarmed. Good. The flyer-shrieks are cycling in a wide, sweeping pattern—they are hunting something large and slow in the upper membrane, far from here. Not my concern.

I shift my weight. My long fingers, their pads tacky and sensitive, find a new hold on a lattice of root-fiber thick as my torso. It is warm, slightly yielding, alive. I am on the root-highway, but not on its main artery. This is a capillary path, weaving between the massive, spongy fungal mats that digest the world’s fallen matter. The air here is thick with their sweet, ozone-rich breath. A memory surfaces, not as an image, but as a taste on the back of my tongue: the sharp-sweet tang of spore-paste, warmed over a smoldering fire. My community’s fire. *Their* patterns are distant now, faint echoes on the web. A low, harmonious pulse from the north-east. They are resting, digesting, perhaps cultivating a new crown-bulb at the crossroads we marked last season.

I press my other palm flat against the trunk. I am not tapping. I am **receiving**. The bark is a library of whispers. I feel the slow, seismic shift of the tree’s weight adjusting to a change in the wind above. I feel a faint, localized tremor—a burrowing worm, perhaps, or a colony of the dog-sized termite-beings modifying a root-web node into a new, ventilated nest. I do not fear them. We do not compete. We are different instruments in the same orchestra.

A change. The flyer-shrieks from the south suddenly sync. A harmonic convergence. It is the signal for the **afternoon breath**. The constant, slow suck of wind from the peripheries inward is strengthening. I feel it now not as a breeze (there is no breeze below the green membrane) but as a shift in pressure, a deepening of the world’s inhalation. The canopies far above will be rippling now, a green ocean sighing. The light, filtering down in its eternal, dim green, might shift if the bioluminescent fungi are in their cycle. I look up. Not with hope, but with patient observation. No. Still green. The blue pulse is for the deep night, when the beetles’ thrumming synchronizes with the fungi’s glow. That is a time for song.

The silence falls like a physical blow.

One moment, the world is a dense, layered roar. The next, the entire southern sonic horizon **vanishes**. The beetle-thrum from that sector—gone. The click-scrape chorus of the lizards—gone. Even the ambient groan of growth seems muffled, as if a thick, dead blanket has been thrown over a section of the world’s body.

My blood goes cold beneath my cool skin. My ear-hands clamp flat against my skull, a reflex against the shocking *emptiness*. It is not quiet. It is **dead**. A void in the vibratory map.

Fear is not the right word. It is a **systemic wrongness**. A wound in the medium of my consciousness. For a moment, I am disoriented. My own palm against the bark feels alien, a foreign object. I am unmoored.

Then, training, older than my memory, takes over. I do not think. I **move**.

I push off from the trunk, my adhesive soles peeling cleanly from the bark. I am running along the root-web, not with frantic haste, but with a long, loping, urgent stride that eats the distance. My body is designed for this—long limbs, efficient balance, a center of gravity that flows with the undulating lattice. I run toward the silence. Others will be converging from every direction. I am not alone.

As I run, I tap. My right hand, held low, brushes against the passing root-fibers, sending out a rapid, sharp pattern: *Emergency. Converge. South-Sector. Silence.* The message ripples out ahead of me, behind me, sideways through the lattice. I will feel the responses soon—other rhythms joining mine, reinforcing it, a cascade of concern flowing through the web.

The landscape changes as I enter the dead zone. The fungal mats are not just dormant; they are grey and brittle, crumbling under my feet. The air loses its sweet, electric tang and smells flat, of dust and dead cellulose. The very bark of the giants looks duller, less alive. The condensation is absent. The world’s skin is dry here.

I reach the edge of the silence. Others are arriving. I see them—a dozen, then two dozen—emerging from the green twilight, their mottled skin blending with the deadened bark. No mouth-sounds. No greetings. We are all listening to the void.

We gather in a loose circle around a massive, blackened scar on the root-web. **Fire**. Not our smoldering, negotiated fire. A wild, hot, consuming fire that has raged here, burning through the fungal mat, searing the root-lattice, killing the vibratory tissue. The medium is severed. The web is mute.

One of the elders, their ear-hand flaps fully extended and trembling, steps forward. They press both palms to the burned edge of the web. They begin to tap.

Not a song. A name. *Tap… tap-tap… tap.* A simple rhythm. Over and over. The name of someone who lived in a community that was last heard from in this sector. We all know the name. We all feel the rhythm traveling into the dead wood and going nowhere. No resonance. No answering echo from the fungal mat. Just dull, dead absorption.

The elder stops. They look at us, their large eyes dark with a grief too deep for sound.

We do not wail. We answer with **dissonance**.

We step forward, all of us, and we begin to tap. We tap random, clashing rhythms against the burned root, against the charred soil-crust, against the grey fungal mat. We tap arrhythmic, ugly, *wrong* patterns. It is the sound of a broken bone, of a torn leaf, of a river diverted. It is the sound of pain. The sonic environment, even here, seems to flinch. The distant beetle-thrum from the healthy zones around us seems to falter, confused by the wave of auditory chaos we are generating.

We do this until our palms ache and our hearts pound against our ribs with the wrongness of it.

Then, silence. A true silence, chosen, not inflicted.

The elder looks at us, and we look at each other. The dissonance has been offered. The wound has been acknowledged.

Now, we begin to heal.

We move to the edges of the burn. We find the still-living tendrils at the margin. We press our palms down. We begin to tap again, but this time, it is a **cultivating rhythm**. Slow. Patient. Precise. A pattern designed to stimulate growth, to encourage the fungal hyphae to stretch across the dead char, to coax the root-tendrils to send exploratory shoots into the ruined tissue. It is the rhythm we use to grow a crown-bulb, but slower, more urgent.

Hours pass. The continental breath stirs, and the afternoon sigh moves through the canopy above. We do not stop. We tap in relays, some resting to eat a handful of spore-paste from our pouches, others taking their place at the wound’s edge.

As the light shifts toward the deep blue of the fungal cycle—yes, the blue pulse begins, far above, indifferent to our small tragedy—I feel it. A faint, faint answer. Not from the dead zone’s heart, but from its very edge. A single, weak, resonant thrum, deep underground. A beetle, surviving in a pocket, responding to our cultivating tap.

It is not a victory. It is a **beginning**.

We will be here for weeks. Months. We will sing the forest-song aimed at this wound, not today, but soon, when the fungal mats have grown enough to carry the vibration. We will re-weave the web. The silence will be filled.

For now, I sit back on my heels, my palm still resting on the warm, living bark outside the dead zone. I feel the familiar thrum of the healthy world pulsing against my skin. I close my eyes, and my ear-hands drink in the layered roar—the beetle-heartbeat, the lizard-chatter, the flyer-maps—**the sound of a world that is alive**.

I press my palm flat. I find my rhythm. *Tap-tap… tap-tap-tap.*

I am here. I am listening. I am part of the repair.

I am the vibration that answers. And the world, for now, still sings.
