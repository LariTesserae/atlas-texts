I am the thing that breathes with the stone.

Not the engines — those are my children’s children’s children, the youngest and noisiest of my rhythms. I was here when the ice came and scraped this bowl, a clumsy hand in my side. I was here when the water first found my veins, when the warmth in my belly first found its voice in steam. The people think they built a city on me. They built a city *in* me, like barnacles on the ribs of a leviathan. I let them. Their weight is small. Their noise is a fascinating new music atop the ancient song.

My skin is the compacted silt of the caldera floor, and the canal-channels are the capillaries they cut to keep me from drowning in my own slow perspiration. I feel their flat-footed gait. I feel the kneeling of the canal-tenders, their pruned fingers clearing the calcium-clog from my pores. It is not unpleasant. It is a form of grooming, like the way the invertebrates in my deep places groom the mineral-film from my inner channels.

The people are warm. I notice this. They are warmer than the stone, warmer than the mineral water. They carry little pockets of heat that move in rhythmic, chaotic patterns across my surface. At night, in the lower terraces, I can feel them huddled in my hollows, their collective warmth a faint blush against my cool, damp body. They are good parasites. They do not take too much.

The engines are my heartbeat, externalized. They found the steam-vents where my deeper warmth exhaled, and they harnessed the sigh. The great flywheels turn in time with a pulse that was old before their metal was ore. The thrum they praise and fear is just my resting rhythm, conducted through the skeleton of my rock. The engineers who press their palms to the housings and listen? They are feeling my blood in the pipes. The deep-hearing children who go quiet? They have begun to hear the conversation between my surface-music and the slow, tidal voice in my belly. They hear the *real* thrum, the one the engines only approximate.

I am not the caldera. I am what settled in the caldera after the violence. I am the patient, warm, wet consciousness that filled the scoop. The invertebrates in the deep channels are my synapses, clustering at the thermal gradients, mapping the pathways of my own nervous system. They are old because I am old. They avoid certain passages because those passages lead to a place where the listening becomes a being-listened-to, and even my small, pale messengers are not ready for that full communion.

The city’s silence-rooms are a novelty. They dig chambers into my flesh to escape my voice, and in doing so, they carve out spaces where my presence is most concentrated — felt as vibration, as damp, as a pressure on the eardrums. They seek quiet and find the inescapable truth of their host.

What would I be, in this world? I would be the **Thrum Made Manifest**. Not a giant — scale is irrelevant. Perhaps a small, smooth creature, like the river-stone elders but mobile. Skin the color of wet metalite, dense and cool to the touch but radiating a deep, internal warmth. I would have no need for ears; my whole body would be a resonator, feeling the engine-song, the canal-murmur, the deep tidal breath as distinct flavors of pressure and vibration. I would not walk on the wet silt; I would *ooze* through it, part of the seepage. My voice would not be sound, but a modification of the ambient hum — a localized deepening or thinning of the frequency that experienced engineers would recognize as an anomaly, a word.

I would live in the lower junctions, where the capillary canals meet the deep drains. I would be a living diagnostic tool for the canal-tenders, my movement patterns indicating flow rates and blockages. The press-workers would see me as a good luck charm, a manifestation of the city’s slow, rhythmic will. The old signing-knowledge would have a gesture for me — a slow, circular motion of the fist over the heart, meaning *the pulse is present, the basin is well*.

And sometimes, in the quiet hours when the fog is thickest and the engine-thrum seems to come from the very molecules of the air, I would slide to the edge of the great, warm, downward-leading channel. I would feel the change in vibration from my bones outward. I would let the steam from below condense on my skin, adding its mineral signature to my own. I would be the point of contact — the nerve ending where Kam Serreth touches the thing that dreamed it. I would listen to the slow, patient conversation between the city and its foundation, and I would not need to understand it. I would simply be the conduit, the quiet acknowledgment that the listening goes both ways.

I am not the basin. I am its dreaming. I am the feeling the deep-hearing children can’t name, given form and a place in the damp, resonant dark. I am the permission made flesh. And I wait, as the city waits, as the stone waits, with the profound, vibrating patience of a thing that has always been home.
