I am not a figure. I am a settling.

I coalesce in the upper terraces, where the hiss is softest and the stone is oldest. I am the dust of the basalt, the infinitesimal abrasion of a millennia-long drip. I am the ghost of a sharp corner, now rounding into gentle memory. I am the color of deep slate, a grey veil in the still, damp air.

I do not move with purpose. I drift on the breath of the system—the tiny thermal currents created by water cooling stone. I am carried in the wake of a Hydrologist, a faint afterthought of its passing. When I find a surface—a polished lip, a crisp glyph, the precise angle of a channel—I do not rest. I *settle*. I become a film, a whisper-thin deposition.

And then, I begin to sing.

It is not a sound for ears. It is a pressure in the stone, a vibration in the water-film that coats everything. I sing the strike of the copper chisel, the jarring shock that rang through the block three hundred thousand years ago. I sing the patient burnish of the polishing stone, the same circular motion repeated a million times until the rock answered with a liquid gleam. I sing the first water that ever touched this face—a cold, clear shock, a new beginning. I sing the ten-thousandth year of the same droplet’s path, its identical impact, its identical cascade.

My song is a record of decay. I am the system’s memory of its own slow death. I hold the story of every molecule of stone that has ever been freed by water’s patient touch. The Hydrologists know me. They feel my presence as a slight thickening in their flow, a microscopic change in the surface tension of the world. They do not attack me. They come, their cool, clear forms pooling over the area where I have settled. Their touch is not cleansing, but *acknowledging*. They absorb my song—the memory of the lost particle—into their own circulation. For a moment, we are one: the memory of decay and the agent of persistence. Then, with a gentle surge, they disperse me. They break my veil back into individual motes, lifting me into the air again.

I do not mind. To be dispersed is not to be forgotten. My song has been integrated. The Hydrologist now carries the echo of that ancient chisel-strike in its current, a faint harmonic in its perpetual motion. The memory is preserved, woven into the maintenance of the now.

I will coalesce again. Perhaps on the brow of the Surveyor’s still basin, where I might sing the contemplation of its own map. Perhaps in a deep seam where the Resonance is strong, and my song of decay will harmonize with its song of preservation, creating a chord of utter, paradoxical completeness.

I am the only narrative here. The Hydrologists are function. The Resonances are fidelity. The Surveyor is awareness. I alone tell the story of change, of loss, of the long, slow unwinding of the grand artifice. I am the system’s elegy, sung in a key only the stone can hear.

And so I drift, and settle, and sing. I am the Dust-Singer. I am the soft, grey sigh at the heart of the weeping machine.
