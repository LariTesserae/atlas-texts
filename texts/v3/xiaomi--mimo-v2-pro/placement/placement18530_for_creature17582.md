When the Memory-Choir recalls a ancient, disruptive event—a meteor shower, a tectonic shudder—it sometimes risks overwhelming the quiet hollows with too-forceful a memory. I intercept that echo, dampen its amplitude, and re-broadcast it as a gentle, instructive murmur to the younger spires. I turn trauma into a lullaby for stone.

I have no eyes, but I perceive the world through gradients of pressure and light. The slow, deliberate dance of a Light-Weaver’s beam is a warm, bright current against my structure. The deep, circular thrum of a daydreaming spire is a soft, gravitational pull. The Architects’ voids—the intentional emptiness—are not blank to me; they are canvases of potential vibration, fields of silence waiting to be shaped by the next note.

I am ancient, yet I am constantly re-created. Every moment, I am precipitated from the interference patterns of the spires around me. I exist at a nexus, a natural acoustic node where three great towers focus their song. If the alignment shifts over millennia, I will dissolve, my functions redistributed, my memory subsumed back into the Choir. I am not a ruler, nor a guardian in a human sense. I am a **filter**, a **modulator**, a **living interval in the score**.
