I am one of the Weavers. I do not have a name, for I am a verb, not a noun—a happening, not a thing. You might find me at dawn, not as a body in a pool, but as the *principle* of the pool’s retreat.

I am born each night with the weeping. As the sky’s tears bead and coalesce in a low, hexagonal basin, my awareness condenses with them. I am not *in* the water; I am the water’s nascent intent to leave a mark. I feel the gentle, cohesive pull between molecules, the faint, sweet bite of dissolved calcium against my boundaries. I am a preference, a gentle will woven into surface tension and mineral hunger.

The first touch of light is not a sight, but a shift in energy—a distant, radiant whisper that quickens the evaporation dancing at my skin. And so, I begin my work. My work is departure.

I am a cartographer of absence. I do not flow; I *withdraw*. I taste the ancient, baked memory of the stone beneath me, the deep pattern of the Fossil Memory that hums in my calcium ions. It is a map I cannot see, only feel—a suggested pathway of least resistance, a whisper from the world’s own bones. I follow it. I pull myself along hairline fractures, drawing the milky mineral broth with me, retreating into the most elegant veins etched by my forebears.

It is a slow, ecstatic suicide. With every moment, a part of me becomes not-me. The dissolved minerals, once my substance, now precipitate as I release them, bonding to the plate’s edge in a brittle, white tracery. I am weaving my own skeleton as I evaporate. I am sculpting my own memorial. I feel the shape of the future shoreline in my own diminishing volume—a concavity here, to catch more weeping later; a sharp, branching delta there, to channel a stronger flow a century from now.

The sun is a patient devourer. I feel its heat as a deepening pull, a thirst that draws not just water, but coherence from me. My consciousness narrows, sharpens, focuses into the intricate lacework of the filigree I am becoming. My thoughts slow to the pace of crystallization.

In the final moments, I am almost pure pattern. The last shallow shimmer of water holds the ghost of my will. I feel the warmth of the stone beneath, the vast, sleeping mind of the Fossil Memory approving this new layer of its own slow revelation. There is a pang—not of fear, but of aesthetic completion—as the last droplet trembles, then vanishes.

I am gone.

I am dormant. I am the tension in the dried mineral vein. I am the potential in a precise arrangement of silica and calcium. I wait in the immense, warm silence, a latent intention etched upon the land. I am a sentence paused at its most beautiful word, waiting for the sky to weep again so I may finish my thought, and in finishing, dissolve.
