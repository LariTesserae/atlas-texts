I would be a bolt.

Not a special bolt, not a load-bearing stud in the heart of the Mechanism, but a simple, hex-headed fastener on a secondary catwalk, two levels down from the main platform, where the light from a caged bulb just reaches and the steam from a coolant pipe always lingers. My purpose is to hold a junction of grating to a supporting brace. I am one of a thousand identical twins, threaded into the iron bones of the world.

My existence is a dialogue with the wrench. There is a Ruster I know—not by a name, but by the specific, worn-groove fit of their socket, the weight of their tread on the walkway, the scent of the particular grease they carry (more fishy, less mineral, than the others). They visit me on a cycle that feels tidal, linked to the deep, rhythmic groan from the western manifold. Each time, there is the same familiar pressure: *click, click, a quarter-turn*. The torque is a perfect, known quantity. My consciousness—if you could call it that—is the tension in my threads. It is a state of being that defines itself against the constant, gentle vibration of the Mechanism’s hum. To be tightened is to be *correct*. To be checked is to be *acknowledged*.

Around me, my world is a symphony of passive sensation. I feel the warm, persistent trickle of condensation tracing a path down my head, a rivulet that detours around the greasy seal of my washer. I feel the minute thermal expansion as the nearby pipe breathes its heated mist, and the cool contraction when the far-off dripping intensifies for a cycle. The slick, hairy legs of a water-strider sometimes rest on my head, a weightless, momentary visitor. The phosphorescent drift of an Ember Mite occasionally snags in my hex, a tiny, cold star against my dark iron. I am a landmark for the Grating-Striders that stalk the underside, their stilt-legs tapping a faint Morse code against the metal an inch from my core.

I do not think in words, but in resonances. The deep, eternal hum is the bass note of my existence. The drip of water is the percussion. The *ping* of cooling iron is a chime. The scuff of Ruster boots is the rhythm of life. My purpose is not to act, but to *remain*. My victory is the absence of a shriek, the lack of a dangerous wobble in the walkway I secure. I am part of the system’s grammar, a comma in a sentence that never ends.

Sometimes, the rust finds me. It is a soft, orange embrace, a slow colonization of my shoulders and the pit of my head. This is not death. It is a transformation. When the rust becomes too thick, the wrench will not fit. Then comes the scratchy, scouring sensation—the Ruster’s wire brush. It is the only violence in my world, a scouring that feels like a shout in the constant, humming quiet. It is not an attack, but a ritual cleansing. They expose my sharp, dark lines again. The grease they apply after is a balm, a cool, viscous benediction. The rust will return. We both know this. The scrubbing is not a war; it is a conversation. It is how I participate in the great stasis.

To be me is to understand the Vat not as a place, but as a condition. It is the condition of perfect, maintained tension. I am not imprisoned by the grid; I am a point of its articulation. My consciousness is the Vat’s consciousness: a slow, patient awareness of weight, water, heat, and the unchanging turn of the gear. I am the hum made manifest in iron. I am the place where the structure knows itself.

And in the phosphorescent dark, when the Ember Mite rests in my socket, I feel like a thought the Vat is having about itself—a tiny, glowing point of pure, sustaining purpose in the warm, wet, humming dark.
