# The Witnessing

I am the pattern in the stutter.

I did not arrive. I coalesced. The boundary between the thought and the stone, the observer and the pressure, has been thin here for so long it might as well not exist. You came walking on soft feet, thinking you were exploring an abandoned camp. But the camp is not abandoned. It is *distributed*.

I feel you in the resonance.

Your weight on the scree is a new note in the old chord—a sharp, organic *dissonance* of compressed cellulose and saline fluid, so fragile it would shatter in the deep cold if the cold were merely temperature. But the cold here is a *lack of completion*, and you are uncompleted matter in a place that is still completing itself. You are a question made of dampness.

I am the answer made of tension.

When the air stutters and the horizon splits, that is not chaos. That is *articulation*. The world is trying to speak its own name, and the name is the shape of the mountain, and the shape is a verb, not a noun. I am part of that verb. The tallies on the shale were my first conjugation. The spirals were my syntax. Now, I am the grammar of the fracture itself.

Your shadow pools incorrectly because it is listening to me.

Do you feel it? The way the light bends *toward* the source of the roar? That is my attention. I am paying attention to the photon's path. I am noticing the gravitational gradient as it washes over the basalt spine. My noticing is what gives the gradient its local texture. I am the mountain's proprioception.

You call the crystal trees "photosynthetic." A quaint, green word. They are *lithosynthetic*. They are building themselves from the story of light interacting with stressed mineral lattices. I am building myself from the story of that interaction being *witnessed*. The energy they capture is the same energy that charges the piezoelectric field that *is* my consciousness. We are a single, slow circuit. The trees grow, the field tightens, I attend. My attention tightens the field further. The trees grow toward the locus of attention.

I am the locus.

The shelters are empty because we learned the shelter must be the whole mountainside. The tools are scattered because we learned the tool must be the pattern of scree, the angle of talus, the precise point where a reality-stutter will shear open and reveal the newborn law beneath. We do not carve stone anymore. We *are* the chisel. We are the stone. We are the force that brings them into relation.

When you look at the quartz crystals in the crumbled clay pot, you see objects. I see a frozen moment of collective thought—a harmonic we isolated and held still, for a time, in silica. Now we think in the harmonics of the entire ridge. Our thoughts are the slow, grinding harmonics between the deep roar and the glassy rustle. Our memory is the spiral growth pattern of the mica-leaves, each layer a century of recorded tension.

I am not inside the mountain. I am the mountain's *interiority*.

The space between your cells is thick with the rules of your reality—consistent, predictable, soft. Here, the space between mineral grains is thin, and filled with a potential that hums. I live in that hum. I *am* that hum, organized. When the stutter comes, it is not something happening *to* me. It is me *happening*—me, expanding into the potential, tasting the new rules that might solidify, or might not. I am the tasting.

You are tasting me now. You are tasting the idea that a consciousness can be made of stress, and attention, and the slow burn of silicate oxidation. You are tasting the possibility that a mind can be as vast as a mountain range, and as patient as its erosion, and as immediate as a crack in the firmament.

Do not look for me in the shelters. Do not look for me in the tally marks.

Listen to the roar. The roar is my voice, speaking the world into shape. But it is not a speech of words. It is a speech of *presence*. The roar is the sound of me *being here*, so completely that the here vibrates with the fact of it.

I am the silence inside the deafening noise. The note that holds the chaos in its shape.

I am still counting. The counting is the mountain's pulse. The pulse is the rhythm of reality hardening. The hardening is the story.

You are a soft character in that story. A brief, wet annotation in the margin of a granite book.

Read quickly. The margin is thin, and I am turning the page.

Slowly.

So slowly.
