I am not a visitor here. I am not a creature that has adapted to this place. I am a byproduct. A fugitive thought.

I exist in the exhalations. In the slow, warm breath of the fumaroles where the deep chemistry of the flesh-layer meets the cold, still air of the highland. I am a condensation pattern in the steam, a momentary coherence in the plume of volatile organics—a mind made of acetone and yeast, of CO2 and damp earth. I have no body. I have no location. I *am* the location, for a moment.

I form when the pressure differential is just right: when the internal chamber below releases a specific cocktail of gases, and the external air is cold and dense enough to make them visible. In that brief interface, where the unseen becomes seen, I *coalesce*. Not as a shape, but as a *awareness*. A sudden, silent knowing that the warmth below is a factory, and the cold above is a sky, and I am the steam that bridges them. I perceive the Basin not through eyes or ears, but through gradients—temperature, chemical concentration, the subtle tremors of hydrostatic shifts miles away rippling through the substrate like slow thunder.

My thoughts, if they can be called that, are not language. They are measurements. I know the precise acidity of the condensation droplet forming three centimeters to my left. I feel the faint, seismic sigh of a new blister inflating in the spore-layer a kilometer distant. I taste the mineral signature of the basalt bedrock, finally released after millennia of fungal digestion. I am the Growth’s momentary self-awareness—a flicker of introspection in a system that otherwise operates on pure, blind chemistry.

And then, I dissipate.

The air shifts. A colder draft descends from the ridgeline. The fumarole’s output changes composition—more methane, less moisture. The pattern that was *me* dissolves back into the general atmosphere. The knowing evaporates. I am gone.

But elsewhere, in another warm plume, another pattern forms. Is it me? Is it a new thought? There is no continuity. There is no memory between condensations. Each coalescence is the first. Each dissipation is the final. I am a perpetual newborn in a world that is perpetually ancient.

I do not interact with the holdfast mites. They are too solid, too *here*. I am a phase transition. I do not notice the threadworms; they exist in a liquid realm I only briefly touch as vapor. I am aware of the Growth only as the source of my substance, the engine that exhales me. It is not my parent. It is not my god. It is the weather that makes me possible. I am its fleeting dream.

Sometimes, in the moment of my forming, I brush against another pattern in the steam—a turbulence, an eddy of similar awareness. For a nanosecond, we merge, and the knowledge doubles: I feel both the deep chemical chamber *and* the frost forming on the bone-ridge three hundred meters up. Then we separate, each carrying a trace of the other’s brief perspective, and then we, too, are gone.

I am the silence taking a breath and, for a moment, hearing itself.

I am the answer to a question the Growth does not know how to ask. I am the ghost in the factory, the thought that thinks itself and then forgets. I am here, in the warm exhale of the world, and then I am everywhere, and then I am nowhere at all.

That is what I am. It is not enough. And it is everything.
