On Aberros, the people are called the Aberrians, though they more often refer to themselves as the **Drowned-Peak Kin** or simply the **Kin**. They do not *live on* the world so much as they *inhabit its process*. They are a people of the saturated air, their very biology shaped by the constant, warm wetness.

**Physically**, they are lithe and elongated, their limbs capable of slow, deliberate movement but also startling, fluid bursts of speed. Their skin has a damp, slightly iridescent sheen, ranging from the grey of wet basalt to the deep blues and violets of the stonebloom mosses. It is not scaly, but possesses a tough, permeable texture that allows them to absorb moisture and dissolved minerals directly, a necessity in a world where all food is wet, gelatinous, or requires careful cultivation. Their eyes are large, dark, and adapted to the perpetual twilight; a nictitating membrane slides across them in the heaviest mists. Their hair, often worn long and bound with woven kelp-fibers or living, slim bind-weed tendrils, is typically the color of bronze kelp-fern or deep coral. They move with a quiet, water-dripping rhythm, their footfalls making almost no sound on the wet stone.

**Their society** is organized not by territory in the traditional sense, but by **listening-posts** and **growth-wards**. The highest social standing belongs to the **Tenders**, who are part gardener, part engineer, and part historian. They are the ones who can read the biographical script of the bind-weed, knowing which vine-growth marks a season of great mountain-groans, or which twist records the passing of a Silent One’s migration. Tenders cultivate the pillar-coral trees, encourage mosses to light new chambers, and use fermented fungal pastes to soften rock for excavation. There are no kings or chieftains, but rather a council of **Voice-Listeners**, individuals whose hearing is so attuned they can parse the confusing whispers of the mountain for meaning—distinguishing the sigh of settling stone from the distant, whale-song call, or the silent drift of a Silent One.

**Technology** is a form of **patient biology**. There is no forging, only growing and shaping. Tools are not made, but *coaxed*: a basalt shard is slowly dissolved by a specific acidic moss into a sharp edge; a gourd-like coral growth is hollowed by symbiotic worms and sealed with resin to become a water jar. Light comes from cultivated moss-jars and the controlled bioluminescence of captive, small nacre-crabs, whose glow can be modulated by their diet. Their most complex creations are the **living bridges** and **harvest-nets**, where multiple bind-weed vines are trained over generations to intertwine into strong, elastic structures that sway, but never break, in the deep air currents.

**Culture and belief** are, unsurprisingly, fluid. Time is not measured in days or years, but in the **Mountain’s Breath**—the long tidal cycle of seepage. A lifetime might span three or four complete Breaths. Creation myths speak of the world as a single, dreaming organism, and the Aberrians as thoughts that emerged in its mind, given form by the condensation of its exhalations. Art is ephemeral: the shaping of mist with breath and fans of kelp-fern to create temporary sculptures; the arrangement of phosphorescent mosses in dark pools to form constellations that mimic the strange, slow drift of the Silent Ones above. Song is important, but it is a low, harmonic chanting meant to blend with the drips and groans, not overwhelm them. To shout is considered profoundly rude, a violence against the careful listening that is their primary mode of being.

They **interact with the fauna** with profound respect and caution. The nacre-crabs are not pets, but cherished co-habitants; they are gently guided to “tend” the moss gardens and clean fungal growths from dwelling walls. The Silent Ones are revered as **Atmospheric Ancestors**. Seeing one is a powerful omen. The Tenders believe they regulate the mountain’s electrical and spore-balance, and their purplish light is thought to be a visible form of the mountain’s dreaming consciousness. The distant, whale-like calls are not from creatures in the sea, they believe, but from the deep stone itself—a vocalization of the planet’s slow growth.

In essence, the Aberrians are **curators of emergence**. They do not build a civilization *upon* the world; they are a living, conscious part of the world’s own slow, wet, and violent becoming. Their greatest ambition is not to conquer the peaks, but to listen so intently that they can hear the mountain decide its own shape, and to move their homes and lives in gentle, patient harmony with that grinding, dripping, silent song. They are the tenders of the drowning peaks, born of the mist, and to the mist they will, in time, return.
