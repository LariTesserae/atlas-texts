The inhabitants of this world are not separate from its sound, its light, or its grinding, constant activity. They are phenomena of its geology, born of its particular physics.

At the foundational level, the **Lattice-Weavers** are the true architects. They are not animals, but perhaps the closest analogue to fungi or colonial polyps. They appear as slow-moving, viscous flows of silicate gel, often pooling in the shadowless depressions between spires. Their bodies are semi-translucent, threaded with faintly luminous capillaries of trace metals. They consume grit, but their purpose is to spin. From specialized orifices, they extrude impossibly fine threads of pure, flexible silicon crystal—gossamer strands that catch the diffuse light like frozen spider silk. These threads are not webs for catching prey, but conduits. Weavers connect distant spires, creating shimmering, miles-long networks that hum with piezoelectric resonance. They are the living wiring of the planetary computer, translating seismic vibration and thermal gradients into the slow, structural logic of growth.

Their work dictates the life of the **Facet-Grazers**. These creatures are low-slung, multi-legged tanks, their bodies encased in layered, overlapping plates of smoky quartz and chalcedony. They move with a low, scraping grind, their dozen or so limbs ending not in feet, but in diamond-hard, self-sharpening blades that find purchase in the compacted mineral ground. Their heads are broad, blunt scoops lined with rotating, interlocking rasps of corundum. They do not eat organic matter; they "tune" the crystal. They scrape away diseased, dissonant, or overgrown crystalline formations, their digestive systems dissolving the material to extract trace energies and isotopes, while excreting a fine, perfectly uniform sand—a raw material for the Weavers and new growth. Their presence is announced by a rhythmic *skreee-skreee-skreee* that harmonizes, or clashes, with the greater crunch.

High above, the **Thermal Kites** ride the sharp thermals. They are true flyers, but their form is stark efficiency. Their wings are vast, rigid, triangular planes of membrane stretched over a lattice of hollow, light-weight crystal bones. They have no flapping motion; instead, they adjust the camber and angle of their wings via taut, living cables of silicate muscle. They steer by flexing their long, cruciform tails, which trail filament-thin sensory threads that taste the wind shear. They are predators of the air itself, filtering vast quantities of atmosphere through internal chambers to fix nitrogen and other gases, which they later regurgitate as nutrient pellets for their nestlings—small, chirping clusters of crystal housed in high, inaccessible cavities in the spires. Their cries are a series of high, sharp *pings*, like struck glass, used for echolocation in the blinding glare.

But the most unsettling inhabitants are the **Resonants**. They are rare, and to see one is to witness the landscape itself moving with intent. A Resonant appears, at first, as a particularly complex spire—perhaps a twisted column of bismuth-like iridescence, or a branching tree of black tourmaline. It is only when it *shifts* that its nature is revealed. Slowly, over the course of minutes, a segment of the spire will pivot, a crystalline limb unfolding with a sound like a groaning glacier. A facet that seemed decorative becomes a lens, focusing the ever-present glare into a searing point of light on a distant patch of ground, perhaps to stimulate a specific growth pattern or eliminate a dissonant crystal cluster. They do not locomote; they *integrate*. They are believed to be the primary sensory organs and actuators of the geological computer—nodes where the network's calculations manifest as physical action. They communicate not with sound, but with precisely modulated vibrations through the ground and the Weaver-threads, and with focused light. To stand near one is to feel your teeth ache and your bones hum with a deep, complex chord that speaks of pressure, time, and crystalline logic.

There are also **Debris-Sifters**, small, scuttling things that erupt from the avalanche fields after a spire's collapse. They are like armored, many-legged oysters, prying open pockets in the shattered crystal to consume the freshly exposed, energy-rich fracture planes. And there are the **Silicifiers**, parasites that attach to the larger grazer-beasts, slowly encasing a patch of their host's armor in a thick, beautiful, and ultimately immobilizing crust of parasitic crystal.

These beings do not live *on* the world; they are *processes* of the world. Their life cycles are measured in the grinding of stone. Their conflicts are a matter of frequency and resonance. Their society, if it can be called that, is the flawless, brutal, and deafeningly loud economy of a planetary-scale crystal, growing, computing, and endlessly recrystallizing itself. To be among them is to be an anomaly of softness and speed, a temporary bubble of silence in the eternal, magnificent crunch.
