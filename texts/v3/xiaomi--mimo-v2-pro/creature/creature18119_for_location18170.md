The inhabitants of The Gullet are not born; they are *precipitated*. They are the slow, calcified response of humanity to this warm, wet, and mechanical womb.

First, there are the **True Denizens**, the rust-hued mosses and fungal shelves. They are the primary citizens. They coat every surface in a soft, pulsing carpet of dim light, metabolizing minerals and decay. They are the settlement’s lungs and its lanterns. Their growth *is* the calendar. A patch spreading an inch across a doorframe is a year marked. They require no governance, only the steady flow of water and seepage, and in return, they grant the gloom a living, breathing texture.

Then, there are the **Pump-Keepers**. They are human in origin, but shaped by generations of duty. They are the priests and the maintenance crew of the great, central pump. Their skin is perpetually damp and pale, etched with fine, mineral lines like living limestone. Their eyes are large, accustomed to gloom, and reflect the faint bioluminescence. They move with a slow, deliberate grace, their footsteps timed to the *thump-clank* of their god-machine. Their language is a low murmur, often lost in the sound of water and pistons, supplemented by a complex system of hand-signals concerning pressure, flow, and valve alignment. They do not own the spaces they inhabit; they *tend* them. An alcove is not a home, but a *station*—a point where a particular pipe must be monitored, a particular channel cleared of mineral scale. Their art is the polishing of a brass gauge until it gleams in the gloom, or the careful training of a luminous moss to trace a symbolic pattern on a chamber wall.

There are the **Channel-Wrights**, who walk the grating labyrinths and iron aqueducts. They are leaner, more agile, their hands and feet calloused from gripping iron rungs and wet stone. They are the couriers and the engineers, understanding the complex, ceaseless ballet of sluice gates and diverted torrents. A Wright can read the sound of water rushing through a pipe two levels above and know if a filter is clogged in a feeder channel to the east. Their most valued tools are long, hooked poles and listening-staves, hollow rods pressed to pipes to diagnose the flow within.

And there are the **Glass-Wrights**, a more solitary order. They work with the thick, greenish glass and the condensation. They maintain the gas lamps, their faces often smudged with soot and moisture. But their deeper craft is in the *Vivaria*—sealed chambers and wall-niches where they cultivate more delicate fungal strains, breeding new hues of bioluminescence or forms that eat specific mineral wastes. They are the closest thing The Gullet has to artists or scientists, though they would call themselves simply "tenders of the sealed water."

There are no children in the usual sense. New members of the community are not born, but *arrive*, or are *made*. Sometimes, a half-drowned stranger is pulled from the upper intake channels, their memories washed away by trauma and mineral water. They are dried, fed fungal broths, and slowly, through observation and mimicry, they *calcify* into the rhythm of The Gullet. Other times, a Pump-Keeper will declare an apprentice from among the fittest of the young Channel-Wrights, and a new cycle of devoted service begins.

The fauna rating of 0.1 is accounted for by the **Scrapers**. These are not animals, but colonial organisms—slow, inch-wide aggregations of mineral-shelled micro-creatures that move in herds along the wettest walls, grazing on biofilm and algae. They leave faint, spiraling trails of calcium carbonate. They are less inhabitants and more a feature of the landscape, like walking lichen. The inhabitants ignore them, stepping around their paths.

Their society has no rulers, only resonances. Decisions are made by consensus at the **Heart-Stone**, a natural amphitheater where the sound of the central pump is most profound. Here, voices are raised not in argument, but in offering descriptions of pressure differentials and moss-growth rates. The "right" decision is the one that causes the least dissonance in the pump's rhythm, that maintains the equilibrium of flow. Conflict is a blocked pipe. Resolution is a clearing.

They measure wealth in *silence* and *steady light*. A chamber where the drip of water is perfectly regular, and the moss glows with unwavering intensity, is a place of great status. Their greatest fear is not death, but *desiccation*—a failure of the system, a drying-up, a silence where the heartbeat should be.

They are melancholic, but not unhappy. Their sorrow is the deep, mineral sorrow of stone itself—the sorrow of slow, inevitable change. They love The Gullet not as a home, but as a body. They are its conscious cells, tending its weeping walls, listening to its iron veins, and glowing with its patient, fungal light. They have become part of the process, and in that, they have found a strange, damp, and enduring peace.
