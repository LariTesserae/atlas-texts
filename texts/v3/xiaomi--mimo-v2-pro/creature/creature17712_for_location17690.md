The Cathedral breathes, and its breath is a community.

What lives here is not *in* the Cathedral so much as it is *of* it—a slow, wet, perpetual congress of organism and architecture. The inhabitants are the functions, the processes, the living clockwork of a place that refuses to be mere ruin.

**The Groundswell and the Canopy Fret**
First, there is the **Fungal Weft**. Not a resident, but the foundational populace. It is a network of pale, thread-like mycelia that consumes what is dead and binds what is living. It digests the fallen root-fibers, turns aite-wax to sweet powder, and in its fruiting bodies—those fungal plates on the ceiling—it houses the **Lumina-Moths**. The moths are its symbiotic agents, their life cycle pollinating the fungal blooms, their collective wing-beats generating the ghostly, pulsing light that is the Cathedral’s starfield. They are a thousand points of silver dust, stirring the air into a whisper.

**The Clingers and the Creepers**
Upon this lit backdrop move the **Silent Weavers**, the lizards. They are the patient aristocracy of the walls. Their skin is the exact mottled grey of the woven stone; they vanish against the surface. They move with a liquid patience, their adhesive pads making no sound, only a faint, sticky release. They exist in a state of suspended motion until a moth drifts too close—then, a flicker, a gulp, and the stillness returns. They are the shadows that prune the light.

**The Dwellers in the Veins**
In the water, the true ancient blood of the place, live the **Glass Eels**. Blind, made of something less substantial than flesh, they are the cleansers. They sift the perpetually dripping basins for larvae, for fallen spores, for the occasional drowned moth. Their bodies are so translucent you can see the slow pulse of their simple hearts and the dark line of their last meal within. They are the memory of water given form.

**The Echo-Listeners**
Behind the woven walls, in chambers that are pockets of deeper dark, breathe the **Resonants**. You do not see them; you feel their breath in the hollow of your chest, a subsonic hum that syncopates with the drip-drip-drip. They are the source of the low, resonant hum, the large creatures whose skittering is not frantic but a slow, heavy shifting. What are they? Perhaps the guardians of deeper aquifer-wells. Perhaps something else—mammals grown vast and pale in the eternal cool, their ears huge and blind, listening to the vibrations of the weave itself, communicating in frequencies that feel like stone dreaming.

**The Unseen Custodians**
And then there are the **Thread-Rats**. The frantic skittering is theirs. Small, many-limbed, with long, sensitive whiskers that taste the air for moisture and decay. They are the engineers of entropy. They burrow into softening wax, gnaw at weakening root-strands, and in doing so, they aerate, they create new pathways for capillary flow, they prevent stagnation. Their work is a constant, destructive maintenance that paradoxically keeps the Cathedral’s systems alive. They are the ones who ensure nothing truly calcifies.

**The Absent Architects**
But the most profound presence is one of absence. You feel it in the tools fused to walls—a flint scraper half-swallowed by growing wood, a bone awl protruding like a fossilized nerve. The **Makers** are gone, but their intent is everywhere. The sphincter-doors sigh with a memory of hands that once turned them. The basins are shaped to hold not just water, but offerings. The very flow of air and life seems choreographed by a vanished will. The true inhabitant is the **Idea of the Makers**, encoded into the biological clockwork. The moths, lizards, eels, and rats are not wild things; they are the still-functioning organs of a body whose mind has departed. They live not in a forest, but in a thought—a thought that is slowly, beautifully, and inexorably returning to the damp, knotted earth from which it was woven.
