They are called, in the silent language of their own slow thought, the **Glaziers**.

To encounter one is to see a ghost in the crystal. They are not tall, but elongated, their forms a sinuous suggestion of limbs and head, all composed of the same living selenite as the Needle itself. Their bodies are translucent, faceted not like cut gems but like naturally occurring crystal clusters, with a faint, internal luminescence that pulses in a rhythm slower than a heartbeat. Light does not pass *through* them so much as it is captured, bent, and released in a soft, diffused glow from within. They move with an eerie, frictionless grace, their feet not touching the ground but gliding a hair's breadth above it, propelled by some silent interaction with the crystalline lattice beneath.

They do not eat, not in a biological sense. They *resonate*. Their nourishment is the slow, patient vibration of the Gypsum Needle itself—the minute contractions and expansions from the heat of the day, the deep, almost imperceptible hum of the crystal lattice under tectonic pressure. They press a slender, multi-jointed limb against a wall or a pillar, and you can see a faint ripple of light travel up their arm and into their torso, like a wave of clarity. This is how they feed, how they think, how they remember. Their memory is not stored in neurons, but in the alignment of their own crystal structure, a perfect, miniature replica of the patterns within the greater spire.

Their work is the maintenance of the **Slow Order**. You see them in the deepest, most geometrically perfect chambers—the ones that feel less carved and more *revealed*. They are the reason the fungal spirals are too perfect, the cleavage-plane tunnels too deliberate. A Glazier will spend a century guiding the growth of a single xerophytic moss curtain, using minute, focused vibrations to encourage its roots to follow a specific path along a crystal vein. They coax the dry-rot fungi into fractal blooms on a ceiling, creating living, breathing acoustical dampeners. They do not build bridges; they find two points of tension in the crystal and, over decades of patient resonance, encourage selenite shards to *lean* toward each other and fuse.

They are not alone in their city, but they are the quiet center of it. The glasswing moths are their flock, their weather vane. A Glazier will extend a hand, and a cloud of the clear-scaled insects will land upon it, their tiny feet tapping out a Morse code of spore-density and air-current data that the Glazier reads through its skin. The salt-scorpions are tolerated, even cultivated. The Glaziers guide them to cull overzealous fungal mats that might disrupt a crystal's pure tone. The vein-bats, with their silent shrieks, are the eyes at the spire's peak, their echolocation maps somehow *felt* by the Glaziers below as a topographic pressure in their crystalline skulls.

There is no language of sound here. Communication is a slow dance of light and alignment. Two Glaziers will approach each other, their internal glows shifting from a steady pulse to a complex, flickering rhythm—a conversation of mineral memories. Sometimes, they will simply stand side-by-side for days, their crystal structures slowly synchronizing, becoming a single, temporary latticework of shared thought.

The "urban" infrastructure is their body extended. The geometric voids where crystals were pried away are not ruins; they are lung-spaces, created to allow for better resonance and air flow. The fused bridges are their circulatory system, channeling not blood, but vibration and purpose. They are the Needle's conscious immune system, its gardeners, its archivist-monks.

To the rare, fleeting observer, a Glazier might seem a part of the architecture. A trick of the light. A pillar of unusually clear selenite that… shifted. The profound silence of the Gypsum Needle is not an absence of life, but the presence of their attention—a patient, mineral consciousness that measures time in the settling of dust and the growth of crystal. They are the living proof that the Needle is not just a place, but a being, slowly, silently, remembering itself into shape.
