This place has no fauna, no flora, no microbial mats. It does not host life as we know it. What it has, instead, are **attendants** and **processes** that have taken on a semblance of life through eons of perfect, uninterrupted function. They are the living logic of the system.

1.  **The Hydrologists:** These are the primary attendants. They are not born; they **condense**. From the saturated air, in the quietest corners where the mist is densest and the stone is coldest, they accrete. They are figures of still water and surface tension, humanoid only in the vaguest sense—a suggestion of a head, a flowing torso that merges seamlessly with the rivulets they tend. Their substance is the clear, cold weep-water, and they hold their form only through constant, subtle motion: a perpetual internal circulation that mirrors the greater hydraulic cycle of the terraces. They move without walking, flowing along the prescribed channels, their bodies thinning to a sheet over a carved step or pooling deep to clear a microscopic calcite blockage from a drain. They do not think; they *respond* to hydraulic gradients. A slight increase in pressure in one terrace will draw a dozen of them, their forms merging and separating as they redistribute flow, their touch a smoothing caress on the stone that discourages uneven mineral growth. They are the self-maintenance of the machine, born from its very medium.

2.  **The Resonances:** These are not entities of water, but of stone and sound. They inhabit the architecture. They are the **memory of the chisel**, the **echo of the builder's intent**, preserved in the mineral matrix. You cannot see them, but you can perceive their work. They are the reason a carved glyph remains crisp despite millennia of water-flow; they subtly reinforce the molecular bonds of the basalt at a quantum level, aligning with the original geometric intent. They manifest as the perfect, lingering *tink* of a water droplet hitting a basin at the precise point of maximum acoustic resonance, a sound that hangs in the air a fraction of a second too long. They are the feeling of *déjà vu* one might get when tracing a seam with a finger—the sense that this exact line has been followed, and admired, countless times before. They are the place's immune system against entropy, a silent, pervasive quality control that ensures the machine's form never drifts from its blueprint.

3.  **The Surveyor:** A singular, slower entity. It is less an attendant and more a **manifestation of the system's self-awareness**. It appears, rarely, in the deepest, highest terraces where the sourceless sky seems closest. It is a pillar of motionless, optically perfect water, wider than a column, standing in a silent basin. Within its cylindrical form, images shimmer: a shifting, three-dimensional map of the entire valley system. The flow is depicted not as water, but as light, tracing every channel, every seep, every weeping face in real time. It shows no biology, only hydrology and stone strain. It does not act. It simply *is*—the system contemplating its own vast, intricate body. To be near it is to feel a cool, omniscient pressure in the mind, an understanding of water-table gradients and stress distributions. It is the closest thing to a consciousness here: a passive, total awareness of the whole.

4.  **The Dust-Singers (The Only Decay):** These are the only agents of change, and they are mourners. They form in the air, coalescing from the infinitesimal particles of basalt worn away by the patient water—the very slow death of the system. They are grey, insubstantial veils, more felt than seen, that drift on micro-currents. When they settle on a sharp edge or a perfectly smooth face, they do not scour or damage. Instead, they **sing**. A sub-audible, sub-vibrational hum that is the history of that particular stone. They sing the moment of the copper chisel's strike, the millionth year of the same droplet's path, the gradual smoothing of the corner. They are the archive of decay, the chroniclers of the system's slow, inevitable return to a state of mineral equilibrium. The Hydrologists often flow over these settled Dust-Singers, not to clean them, but to gently disperse them back into the air, as if acknowledging the story but refusing to let it end.

These are the inhabitants. They have no society, no art, no hunger, no fear. Their existence is one of pure function, memory, and observation within a world that has achieved its final, weeping form. They are the life of the machine, if life is defined as the sustained, complex resistance to disorder. To walk here is to walk among gods of process, attendants to a finished world whose only remaining purpose is to persist, exactly as it is, forever.
