The inhabitants of the Lung are not visitors, not residents in the way one occupies a house. They are the **Caretakers**, and they are as much a part of the city’s engineered biology as the pulsating pump-sacs or the grafted load-bearing trees.

You see them first as movement. Subtle shifts in the jade light, where a silhouette detaches itself from a burl-dwelling’s dark hollow. They are tall, elongated, and move with a ponderous, fluid grace that mirrors the drip of water. Their forms are vaguely humanoid, but the analogy quickly breaks down. Their skin has the waxy, slightly translucent sheen of a philodendron leaf, mottled with patterns of moss-green and deep, woody brown. It is not clothing they wear, but *growth*—lichen-like patches that seem cultivated directly onto their limbs, and belts or harnesses of living, miniature vine that sprout tiny, air-filtering leaves.

Their faces are serene, placid planes. Features are suggested rather than defined: a slight ridge where brows might be, a gentle indentation for a mouth that rarely opens. Their eyes are the most arresting feature—large, dark, and liquid, like pools of still water under a forest canopy. They reflect the bioluminescent glow, not with a spark, but with a deep, absorbed luminosity, as if the light is being processed, understood.

They are **rooted**. Literally. Their feet, when still, slowly extend fine, pale rootlets into the rhizome mat, drawing nutrients and information directly from the city’s substrate. To move, they must consciously retract these roots with a soft, sighing sound, leaving behind tiny, quickly-sealed wounds in the woven floor.

**Their tools are their bodies.** Their fingers are long, dexterous, and end in hardened, woody points perfect for pruning, grafting, or etching pheromone-signals into bark. A Caretaker can press its palm to a wall and, through a symbiotic fungal network in its skin, sense the flow of water in a distant trunk, the health of a bioluminescent colony, or the slow creep of decay in a foundational rhizome. They don’t speak with sound. Their language is a blend of slow, deliberate gesture—a lowering of a branch-like arm to indicate *attention here*—and the release of complex, volatile organic compounds from pores on their neck and wrists. A smell of crushed mint might mean *growth is good*. A scent like damp stone, *needs reinforcement*. The air around them is a constant, subtle symphony of botanic signals.

**Their work is their existence.** You watch one tend a pulsating heart-sac. It does not repair it with tools, but places its hands upon the veined surface, and its own skin flushes with a deeper green. It is sharing nutrients, perhaps, or adjusting a hormonal balance through touch. Another Caretaker is coaxing a new vine-bridge to span a gap. It does not pull or tie; it sings to it—or rather, emits a low-frequency vibration from its chest, a subsonic hum that matches the thrum of the water-pumps, encouraging the vine’s tendrils to grow in a specific direction over the course of the day.

**They are patient beyond measure.** A child—seen clinging to the back of an elder, its form smaller, its movements jerky and less fluid—is being taught to listen to the sound of a single water droplet’s journey from ceiling to root-mat. This lesson will take a week. Their projects are multi-generational: the careful breeding of a new fungal light for a dark corner might be a century-long endeavor, a legacy passed from parent to child through shared chemical memories.

They have no need for agriculture, for shelter-building, for defense. The city provides all. Their sole, sacred function is **maintenance and dialogue.** They are the consciousness of the organism, the slow, thinking mind that guides the growth, prunes the excess, and calibrates the perfect, humming balance. They are not masters of the Lung; they are its self-aware lymphatic system, its cultivated nervous system.

To see a Caretaker at rest is to see it merge almost completely. It will stand, feet fully rooted, eyes closed, skin gradually taking on the exact hue and texture of the wall it leans against. In this state, it is listening to the deep, slow thoughts of the wood, the thirst of the leaves, the patient grind of the rhizomes. It is dreaming the city’s green, damp, thrumming dream.

They are the **Lung’s Keepers.** And in their quiet, rooted, fragrant way, they are breathing with it, thinking with it, growing with it—eternal gardeners in a garden that is also their body, their home, and their god.
