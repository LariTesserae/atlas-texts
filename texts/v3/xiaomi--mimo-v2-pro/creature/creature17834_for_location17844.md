Based on the environment you’ve described, here is what lives in the Undergrowth:

**The Flora-as-Fauna:**
The primary inhabitants are the plants themselves, which behave in ways that blur the line between organism and ecosystem. The great pale stalks are not merely structural; they are slow, geotropic beasts. Their growth is a conscious, if alien, process—surging upward in response to internal pressures, their tips splitting not just to release spores, but to exude a sticky, luminous sap that traps airborne moisture and microorganisms. This sap is their first digestive stage. The root-mat is their second stomach, a distributed neural net of chemical signals. A section liquefying into enzymes is not random decay; it is a deliberate, communal digestion, breaking down fallen matter (or failed competitors) to feed the whole lattice. The glowing vines are their circulatory and nervous systems, pulsing with nutrients and information, and their sudden snaps are not merely structural failure, but often a defensive severance—a sacrificial limb dropped to isolate a parasite or a foreign object, like one of the vitrified stone circles.

**The Fauna of Flux:**
Animal life here is rare because it must exist *between* the intentions of the flora. The gliding creatures—the **Veilwings**—are not true animals in a terrestrial sense. They are more akin to detached, mobile seed-cases or complex spore-structures. Their translucent membranes are photosynthetic, but they also absorb ambient moisture and dissolved organics from the air. They ride the faint thermal plumes generated by the rapid growth of the stalks below, and when they land (a slow, crumpling collapse), they are often reabsorbed by the root-mat, their collected nutrients exchanged for the energy they need to eventually inflate and glide again. They are the pollen and the scavenger in one.

The armored, isopod-like creatures (**Grindbeetles**) are the ecosystem’s grinders. They are not chewing for sustenance alone; their mandibles are coated in symbiotic bioluminescent bacteria. As they methodically puncture and scrape root-nodes, they introduce these bacteria, which help break down the toughest cellulose analogs. In return, they harvest the released sugars and the glowing bacterial blooms that sprout from the wounds they create. Their hard, segmented shells are shed periodically, and these husks are promptly colonized by vine-like growths that dissolve them, returning minerals to the mat.

**The Turbulent Microbiome:**
In the condensation beads, in the sap-oozes, and within the glowing fruit, thrives a hyperactive world of microbes, fungal analogs, and **Luminozoa**—tiny, mobile creatures that are part bioluminescent algae, part protozoan. They are the true pulse of the ecosystem, responsible for the uniform light, the rapid chemical breakdown, and the constant damp metabolism. They swarm to wounds on stalks, glowing intensely as they work, and they are the primary food source for any creature that drinks the condensation or the sap.

**The Remnant-Keepers:**
Not all that lives here is native. The artifacts are tended, but not by their makers. Around the vitrified stone circles, the vegetation grows in constrained, perfect geometries—a meter of bare, glassy stone, then a ring of exceptionally bright, stunted mosses, then a sudden explosive growth of vines. Something in the stone’s residual energy or composition edits the growth patterns, and the ecosystem seems to be slowly, patiently trying to digest these indegestible scars, resulting in these strange, ritualistic gardens.

The non-luminous pillars repel life, creating dead zones of silence and shadow. But in these quiet pockets, a different kind of life exists: **Echo-Molds**. These are dark, velvety fungal mats that consume the faint organic dust that settles in the stillness. They grow in the exact negative space of the pillars, and if disturbed, they release a cloud of dark spores that, for a brief moment, cancel out the ambient glow, creating a pocket of terrifying, absolute black before being reabsorbed by the light.

The shallow ceramic depressions collect water. In these pools, a unique symbiotic loop occurs: **Glassfish**—tiny, skeletal creatures that look like animated fish bones made of silicate—skitter through the water. They are not born, but *precipitated* from the dissolved minerals in the ceramic, feeding on Luminozoa that wash in. When they die, they sink and re-dissolve, their silica returning to the basin, perhaps to form again.

**The Fallen:**
Those who came before—the explorers, the fallen—are now part of the lattice. A hastily sharpened stone blade is found encased in a rapidly formed bulb of amber-like resin, its edge being slowly dissolved. A woven vine cord is being threaded through by a new, brighter vine, using it as a scaffold. Humanoid remains are never seen, but their chemical signature is: localized blooms of phosphorescent fungi in vaguely skeletal arrangements, and root-mats that grow thicker, richer in those spots. The Undergrowth does not bury its dead; it composts them with efficient, luminous curiosity.

In summary, what lives here is a single, sprawling, metabolizing entity with specialized organs: the stalks are lungs and reach; the root-mat is stomach and mind; the fauna are symbiotic enzymes, cleaners, and mobile scouts. The light is its exhalation. The artifacts are scar tissue. And everything that enters—from beetle to human to stone—is slowly, brightly, and irrevocably incorporated.
