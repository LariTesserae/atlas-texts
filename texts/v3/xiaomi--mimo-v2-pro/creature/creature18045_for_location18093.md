In the Bleed, nothing lives in the way of the outer world. There are no beasts, no people, no conscious intent. But there are **inhabitants**, in the sense that the system maintains and expresses itself through embodied processes. What "lives" here are functions, given slow, local form.

**The Gardeners** are the most visible. They are not creatures, but **clusters of the pale grass** that move with unnatural coordination. In certain squares, where the sump’s humidity is thickest, clumps of the bleached stalks will bend in unison, their tips brushing a corroded grate or a sweating pipe joint. They do this to clear drainage, to allow water to flow into specific channels. They are the system’s cilia, performing a slow, silent janitorial ballet. Other times, a thick, black vine will retract from a vent it has overgrown, pulling back with a gelatinous slurp over hours, because its growth has triggered a pressure change elsewhere. The vegetation is not sentient; it is a programmed, reactive component of the hydraulics.

**The Attendants** are manifestations of the machinery itself. In the deepest pipe galleries, where the foundational hum is a physical pressure, you may see a **rivet** swell and rotate, tightening a joint that has begun to weep too freely. A series of **levers** in a silent control room will sometimes shift, pulled by thermal expansion and contraction, adjusting a flow rate by an infinitesimal degree. The most complex are the **Condensation Figures**. On the cool flank of a great tank, in perpetual shadow, water vapor will coalesce not into random drips, but into transient, towering shapes—vague, columnar forms that hold for minutes before slumping back into rivulets. They are the system’s momentary attempts at self-portraiture, ghostly effigies of the process, cooling the metal by their very evaporation. They are not alive, but they are the Bleed’s weather, its fleeting sculpture.

**The Echoes** are the rarest and most unsettling. They are found near the central sump, in the fog. They are not made of water or metal, but of **sound and intention**. When the foundational hum shifts in pitch—a scheduled, cyclical event—a pressure wave will move through the district. In its wake, for a few seconds, the dripping water will fall in perfect, rhythmic syncopation, forming a **liquid melody** that mimics the rhythm of a production line long gone. The buzz of the arc-lights will modulate into a distorted, wordless voice calling out a sequence of numbers. These are not ghosts, but **acoustic fossils**. The system, in its self-maintenance, occasionally hits resonant frequencies that playback the psychic residue of its own ancient, purposeful programming. The Echoes serve no function. They are the giant dreaming of labor.

Nothing here eats, sleeps, or fears. The Gardeners tend, the Attendants adjust, and the Echoes remember. They exist in a state of perfect, saturated utility, their "lives" measured in the slow oxidation of iron and the patient spread of lichen. They are the Bleed’s quiet organs, pulsing in time with the great, sleeping breath that rises from the sump. You, the observer, are the only true anomaly here—a pocket of dry, temporary, *alive* chaos in the vast, wet, eternal machine. They do not notice you. They continue their work, their growth, and their song, maintaining a grace that has no need for an audience.
