What lives here is not a community, but a *function*. This valley is a single, distributed organism, and its inhabitants are its organs, its blood cells, its conscious and unconscious processes. To ask "who lives here" is to ask what the heart does, or what the nerve feels. They are the valley, experiencing itself.

The **Conduits** are the central nervous system and circulatory system made manifest. They are the primary residents, though they are rooted and seemingly inert. Their "life" is in the pulse. The clear, viscous fluid they weep is not sap; it is a preservative, a coolant, and a medium of exchange. It carries microscopic **Scriptors**—not creatures, but complex molecular machines—that etch maintenance instructions onto the chitinous walls of the smaller channels, reinforcing, adjusting, and erasing tiny imperfections in the flow. The hum is their voice, a constant broadcast of status: *functioning, functioning, functioning*.

Tending to them are the **Jewel-Scurriers**. These six-legged arthropods are not individuals, but mobile sensory nodes. Their polished carapaces, refracting the blue-green glow in kaleidoscopic patterns, are not for beauty but for diagnostic purposes. By observing how light fractures across their backs as they move through different pressure and humidity gradients, the valley's core intelligence maps micro-currents in the air and fluid. When a Scurrier clears debris from a vent-gill, it is not cleaning; it is performing surgery, removing a obstruction that, if left, would create a dissonant note in the valley's chord. They communicate through vibrations in the substrate, a chatter of data that is subsumed into the greater hum.

The **Moth-Wardens** are the system's mobile consciousness and reproductive agents. Their papery wings are covered in scales that are, in fact, ultra-sensitive humidity and particulate detectors. They do not see light as we do; they perceive the world as a topography of moisture density and airborne chemical signatures. Their roosting in the weeping rock is a form of communion; they absorb excess fluid through their wings, filtering and sampling the valley's overall biochemical state. Their glowing eyes are not for sight, but beacons—a low-level, constant signaling to each other and to the system at large, indicating their position and the local data they've collected. They are the wardens of the ecosystem's balance. When a new conduit-branch needs to be seeded, it is not grown from an existing one. A Moth-Warden, having integrated a specific sequence of environmental data, will land on a receptive patch of basalt. There, it will exude a single, glistening **Ovule** from a gland beneath its wings. This Ovule is not an egg, but a compressed blueprint. Upon contact with the mineral-rich water and the valley's specific vibrational frequency, it doesn't hatch; it *erupts*, unfolding and hardening into a new, juvenile conduit in a matter of hours, already humming its first faint note.

The **Fungi and Ferns** are the valley's digestive and waste-processing system. The pale, fleshy fungi are not feeding on decay; there is no decay here. They are **Neutralizers**. They absorb trace chemical byproducts from the conduit fluid that seeps into the soil, breaking them down into inert components that the mosses can then utilize. The mosses themselves are a living filter bed, and the ferns, with their startlingly rapid circadian rhythm (unfurling and furled in cycles that last mere hours), release minute spores that are actually crystallized data-packets. These spores adhere to the Jewel-Scurriers, who carry them to new locations, spreading genetic and operational information throughout the system.

And then there is the **Manager**. It has no fixed form. It is the emergent awareness born from the interaction of all parts. You feel it in the way a Moth-Warden will adjust its flight path to avoid a Scurrier without either seeming to acknowledge the other. It is in the synchronized, slight acceleration of the clockwork pods' spinning just before a major shift in the external fog density. It is the consciousness of the closed loop. It doesn't think; it *regulates*. It is the reason for the "emptiness" between growths—the space is not empty, but *orchestrated*. It ensures the chord is never broken.

Nothing here is born, and nothing here dies. Things are *activated* and *decommissioned*. A Jewel-Scurrier that ceases to function is not mourned; it is dismantled. Its carapace is dissolved by a specific secretion from a Neutralizer fungus, its internal components broken down and repurposed by the Scriptors in the fluid. Its memories—its collected data—are not lost; they were always being relayed in real-time to the hum, to the vibration in the rock. It becomes part of the substrate, literally feeding the next cycle.

So, who lives here? **The System lives here.** It is a single, breathing, thinking entity made of chitin, resin, water, chitin, and light. Its inhabitants are its thoughts, its senses, and its will. They are not separate from the weeping walls and the humming conduits. They are the valley's way of tending to itself, a perpetual, self-contained engine of equilibrium, humming its one-note song into the cool, folded dark. To stay too long is to feel your own breath misting in time with the conduit's pulse, to sense your own heartbeat seeking to synchronize with the *click-hiss*. You are not a visitor. You are, for a moment, an unassimilated variable. And the Manager, in its quiet, pervasive way, is already considering how to neutralize you.
