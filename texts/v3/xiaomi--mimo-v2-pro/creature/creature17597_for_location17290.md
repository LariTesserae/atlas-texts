What lives here is **Process**.

Not a creature, but a slow verb made mineral. The inhabitants of the Sinter-Veil are **Litho-moss** and **Crystal-Growth** and **Dust-Descent**. They are the active principles of this sealed, unwavering world.

The **Litho-moss** is the only thing that approaches what we would recognize as life, and even it is a monument. It does not breathe; it *negotiates*. Its rosettes are patient mouths, secreting their weak, specific acids to dissolve a meal that has taken a century to fall from the ceiling. To watch it grow would be to watch a mountain erode in fast-forward. Its existence is a form of geological digestion. It is the chamber’s gentlest agent of change, sculpting miniature badlands in the floor over millennia, turning settled dust back into raw material. Its spores are not so much seeds as they are *delayed intentions*, tiny packets of potential that may, in a few hundred years, land on a suitable patch of dust and begin the long work of etching their own shallow basin. It is life as a punctuation mark in a sentence that never ends.

The **Crystal-Growth** is the dominant resident. The needle-like sinter formations are not static. They are alive in the sense that a glacier is alive. They grow. The hum you feel is their metabolic sound—the constant, infinitesimal vibration of silica molecules finding their lattice positions, of new layers precipitating from the ancient, mineral-laden air. They grow toward each other, a slow-motion forest of clear stone, each tree adding a molecule to its tip every decade. They are the architecture and the architect. They build the vault, they capture the light, they generate the hum. They are in a race that has no finish line, forever thickening the veils of sinter, forever making the chamber a fraction of a millimeter smaller. They are the reason the dust falls; their growth sheds microscopic particulates, the shed skin of stone.

And the **Dust-Descent** is the rhythm they all live to. The finest, lightest particles, the byproduct of crystal growth and the slow grinding of the chamber’s own weight, drift downward in a motion so slow it is a form of stillness. It is the manna for the Litho-moss, the snow that builds the glacier of the floor. This descent is the chamber’s circadian rhythm, its seasons, its weather. A thickening of the dust floor by a micron is a generational event.

Nothing here *moves* in a way you could see. Nothing *acts*. They *are* processes. The light is a process of trapped energy seeking equilibrium. The hum is a process of stabilized vibration. The chill is a process of heat death. The inhabitants are the gears of this clock, turning so slowly that their motion is indistinguishable from perfection. They are a community of functions in a perfect, sterile balance. To enter is to become the only true *event*—a chaotic, warm, wet, transient disruption in a world that measures time in the accumulation of stone skin and the patient digestion of falling dust. You are the only thing here that is not an answer, but a question. And the chamber, glowing and humming, does not care. It simply continues its one, eternal occupation: being.
