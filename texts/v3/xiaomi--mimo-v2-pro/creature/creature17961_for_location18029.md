The ones who live in the Kiln-Stack, the ones who built the shelves and bolt the pipes and wipe theite dust from their eyelids, are simply called *the stacked*. They are human, but shaped by the shaft.

*   **Physically:** They are short, compact, and dense-boned – built low to duck under pipes and conserve space. Their skin is often pale or grayish, tinged by the pervasiveite dust that settles in every pore. Hair is sparse, fine, and constantly matted with grit; many shave it entirely. Eyes are large, adapted to the dim amber light, often protected by crude goggles or hoods when the dust blows thick. Their hands are calloused, scarred, and incredibly strong, able to grip pipes and climb ladders with ease. Their lungs are the weakest point; a persistent, dry cough is common, a rattling sound sometimes called "the grit-song." They move with a careful, deliberate economy – sudden movements risk falling or dislodging precarious loads.

*   **Socially:** They are organized vertically. "Upper," "Middle," and "Lower" aren't just locations; they are castes defined by proximity to the cleaner(ish) air above or the engine heat below. Families, guilds, and clans claim specific sections of shelves, often generations deep. Trade is vertical: raw material from the lower mines moves up, refined slag-ite tools and salvaged goods move down. Status is measured in depth control – who controls the bucket-elevator hubs, who owns space near the big pipe for heat, who has a chamber carved deep enough into the wall-rock to be called "solid." They speak in a clipped, practical jargon heavy with directional terms ("four in, two down," "pipe-ward," "wall-side"). Disputes are often settled by shoving; the loser falls. It’s brutal, but efficient. They call outsiders "floaters" or "drifters," seen as fundamentally unstable for lacking their rigid vertical context.

*   **Tools & Technology:** Everything is simple, robust, and multi-purpose. Hooks, ropes, and harnesses are as vital as food. Tools are heavy slag-ite pry-bars, wrenches, and hammers. Lanterns fueled by rendered bore-walker fat orite dust combustion are prized possessions. Communication is visual (mirror flashes across the void, colored lantern signals) or tactile (coded knocks through pipes and wall-rock). They scavenge and repurpose endlessly; a cracked shelf becomes a wall panel, discarded pipe becomes a chimney flue. Their ingenuity lies in adaptation, not invention.

*   **Culture & Beliefs:** Ancestor spirits are believed to dwell in the wall-rock, whispering in the groans of stressed metal and the scrape of shelless creatures. The shaft itself is a living entity, a god perhaps, whose breathing (the fans) must be appeased with maintenance rituals. They have a deep, superstitious reverence for the engines ("The Heart") and the big pipe ("The Vein"). Stories are not written, but scored onto slag-ite tiles or passed down in rhythmic chants timed to the piston-thumps from below. Taboos are strong: never shine a light into the center void, never wasteite dust (it's both pollutant and resource), never turn your back on a falling object. Music is percussion – the *tik-tik* of raspers is often mimicked, and complex rhythms are beaten on pipes and walls. Funerals involve dropping the wrapped body into the center void; a silent acceptance that the shaft ultimately claims all.

*   **Relationship with Fauna:** The shelless creatures are a constant, low-level nuisance and occasional food source. Dust-mites are brushed away like dirt. Shelf-rappers are sometimes eaten raw for their gritty mineral flesh. Bore-walkers are left strictly alone, seen as omens or minor spirits of the walls; finding one dead is considered extremely unlucky. The things in the center void are sources of terrifying stories and whispered warnings. The stacked live *alongside* the fauna, not dominating it, sharing the precarious vertical world. They are another layer of life clinging to the throat of the great chimney, shaped by its pressure, defined by their depth, and breathing its gritty, heated breath. They are the true children of the Kiln-Stack.
