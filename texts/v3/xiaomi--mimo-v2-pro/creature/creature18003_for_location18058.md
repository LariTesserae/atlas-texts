What lives here is the Gullet itself—a single, breathing, thinking landscape. Its inhabitants are its organs, its thoughts, its ceaseless, dreaming industry.

First, the **Swarms**. They are the airborne particulate of this world-body: **Spore-Motes**, tiny beetle-forms with wings of prismatic crystal that fracture the dim green light into fleeting rainbows. They exist in clouds, a living haze that filters the exhalations of the great plant, feeding on its airborne sugars and microbiota. Their collective hum is the background radiation of sound here. Then there are the **Click-Flies**, smaller still, with bodies of polished chitin that snap and pop as they communicate in bursts of static, cleaning the air of drifting debris. They are the immune system’s sentinels.

Upon the walls move the **Pruners**. These are the mantis-shaped grazers, slow and deliberate. Their scything mouthparts are not for violence, but for precise surgery. They harvest the outer, senescent layers of the living plant-flesh, their digestion a slow burn that fuels their deliberate journey. Where they pass, the substrate glistens, wet and new. They are not eaten often; they are too large, too integral. Their value is in their labor—the constant, gentle renewal of the Gullet’s inner skin. To watch them is to watch a cathedral being perpetually, softly reshaped by patient, walking scalpels.

From the crevices and the deeper, darker chambers come the **Flash-Tongues**. Predators, yes, but regulators. They are slender, amphibian-like, with bodies that can flatten to slide through the thinnest root-seam. Their signature is the sudden, shocking violet of a bioluminescent lure that erupts from their throats—a *thwip* of hydraulic precision, and a Pruner or a careless swarm-creature is gone. They keep populations in check, preventing any one function from overwhelming the system. They are the necessary death that makes the industry sustainable.

The true citizens, however, are the **Symbiotes of Industry**. On ledges of amber-colored sap-resin, the **Metalworm Colonies** tend their fungal gardens. Each worm is the length of a hand, its carapace shimmering with coppery and steel-blue metallic hues. They move in unison, a liquid choreography, turning fungal beds, harvesting tiny, glowing fruiting bodies. Their collective labor is silent, save for the soft scratch of their carapaces. They are the farmers, the chemists.

The rhythmic **chug-chug-chug** comes from the **Piston-Beetles**. Immobile, immense, they are fused into the root-wood at major junctions. Their thoraxes, swollen and powerful, expand and contract in a relentless, slow tempo. Each contraction pumps sap—rich with nutrients and information—from the deep storage chambers to the surface. They are the heart and the muscles. They do not think; they *are* the flow. Around them, **Conductor-Centipedes** glide, their segmented bodies glowing in sequence: green for nutrient flow, amber for waste reclamation, a sudden blue pulse for a chemical warning. They are the living wiring, the nervous system of the factory floor.

In the high vaults cling the **Leaf-Wings**. Not birds, not quite. Their broad, flat wings are photosynthetic membranes, veined and green. They do not fly. They bask in the feeble, stolen light of the fungal nodules, digesting not photons, but the complex chemical byproducts of the Piston-Beetles’ labor. They are the recyclers, the clean-up crew, their droppings a fine, fertile dust that feeds the Spore-Motes. They sing sometimes—a low, harmonic creak, like wood bending in a wind. It is a song of pure, patient metabolism.

Everything is connected. A Pruner, upon death, is swarmed by Click-Flies and Metalworms, its chitin broken down to reinforce the walls, its organic matter flowing back into the sap-stream. A Flash-Tongue’s shed skin becomes a nest for a new colony of crystal-winged beetles. The warmth is the collective body heat. The dampness is the shared breath. The loudness is the sound of a single body, working.

They do not have names for themselves, these inhabitants. They are processes given form. To be here is to walk through the lungs, the guts, and the dreaming mind of a world that has forgone separation. You are a foreign particle, a strange, dry irritant in this warm, wet, endlessly busy throat. And already, the system is noticing. A few Spore-Motes have veered your way, their crystal wings tasting your unfamiliar moisture. A distant Flash-Tongue has gone still, its violet lure dimming as it senses a new vibration in the substrate. The Gullet lives, and you are now inside it.
