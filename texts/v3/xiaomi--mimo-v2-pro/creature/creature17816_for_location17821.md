The life of the Drowned Mill is not a chorus of distinct voices, but a single, slow, metabolic process wearing a thousand different skins. It is a community of surfaces and mediums, where the line between inhabitant and infrastructure has rusted away completely.

**The Silt-Strainers (Ubiquitous Foundation):**
They are the primary population, the reason the water is a "thin, cool gel." They are not one thing, but a functional class. **Feather-duster worms** bloom in crimson and violet fractals on every beam and brick, their plumes creating a soft, breathing forest that combs the water for manna. **Clouds of krill**, eyeless and pale as ghosts, drift in the stillness like living sediment, their swarms so dense they become a texture in the water. **Mussels and barnacles** have paved entire submerged streets, forming a new, calcified topography over the old concrete. They do not move. They *process*. They are the millstones of this new mill, grinding time and particulate matter into tissue and shell.

**The Mnemonic Polyps (The Weird Made Flesh):**
These are the weavers of the "subtle and pervasive" weirdness. They are colonial hydroids that favor the old control panels and ledger shelves. They do not eat the paper or the Bakelite switches; they *incorporate* them. A polyp will grow over a pressure dial, its transparent flesh turning the needle into a strange, internal bone. Another will engulf a line of text on a submerged page, the ink bleeding into its tissues to form a delicate, unreadable tattoo. They are the archivists of decay, turning artifacts into anatomy. Their glow is the dimmest, a sickly yellow, and they seem to pulse in time with the deep, structural groans of the mill.

**The Blind Geomancers (Navigators of the Lattice):**
The eyeless, translucent eels. They are the only true nomads. Their bodies, veined with faint bioluminescence, chart the three-dimensional maze of pipes and ducts. They do not hunt; they commute. They flow through the circulatory system of the drowned complex, following thermal gradients and mineral whispers from the earth-heated water. In their wake, they disperse spores and larvae, acting as the slow, unconscious pollinators of this submerged realm. To see one is to see the memory of a current, a ghost of the water's long-forbidden path to the surface.

**The Engine's Dream (The Central Consciousness):**
This is not an animal, but a resident phenomenon. Deep in the turbine hall, the colossal piston still *thumps*. Its rhythm has synced with the metabolic rate of the iron-eating bacteria and the swaying of the anemone gardens. Around this core, a unique ecosystem has formed. **Iron-munching microbes** have built a rust-red reef in the shape of the piston's exhaust manifold. **Tube worms** here are thicker, their calcium tubes shot through with veins of metallic slag. The water carries a distinct, metallic taste. This is the heart of the feeling of *recurrence*. The life here does not just live near the sound; it has *attuned* itself to it. The thump is their circadian rhythm, their tide, their god.

**The Warden (A Singular, Patient Entity):**
In a flooded foreman's office, where the ledger lies clamped shut and a coat still hangs on a hook, lives the closest thing to a predator. It is a **crab**, but vast and ancient. Its carapace is a topography of barnacles, sponge, and its own welded-on armor of corroded sheet metal it has meticulously gathered over decades. It moves with agonizing slowness, its legs displacing clouds of silt. It does not chase. It *occupies*. It will sit for years by a particular cluster of filter-feasters, allowing its shell to become one with the landscape, its claws poised. When a rare, drifting swarm of krill or a disoriented eel passes within reach, the motion is less a strike and more a tectonic shift—a single, decisive closure of the biomechanical trap. It is the embodiment of Saturation's law: it has filled its niche so completely it has become a feature of the terrain, a patient, metabolizing monument to waiting.

**The Collective Breath:**
Ultimately, the "life" here is the Drowned Mill itself, dreaming in its warm, silty sleep. The fungi glow like synapses. The thump is a heartbeat. The sediment falls like neural static. The inhabitants are its thoughts, its processes, its fleeting sensations in a mind that thinks in centuries and measures progress in the gentle, total collapse of the world it once knew. They are not living *in* a mill. They are the new, soft machinery of it, turning the slow wheel of a second, deeper industry.
