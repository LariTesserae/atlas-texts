Here, life is not a collection of discrete creatures, but a slow, layered conversation between stone, water, and light. The inhabitants are not so much *things that live here* as they are the living architecture of the place itself.

**The Foundation: The Biofilm Choir.** Every surface hums with its first and most fundamental resident: the slick, photosynthetic skin of the world. This is not a passive coating. By day, it breathes in light, turning the shallow sea a deeper emerald at its edges. By night, it exhales faint, cold luminescence—a slow, bacterial aurora that traces the contours of every rock and reef in a ghostly green. This is the base layer of the food web, the silent, sentient skin of the skerry.

**The Architects: The Reef-Weavers.** Clinging to the young basalt are the true builders. The **Calcite-Lace Tubeworms** are the most patient masons. They are mostly unseen, living within their own spun cathedrals of delicate, white calcium carbonate. Their "city" is the porous reef, a labyrinth of tiny tubes and spiraling filters. They are the processors, pulling nutrients from the water and cementing the drowned bones of the old world into the foundation of the new. Intertwined with them are the **Siphonophore Vines**—not single organisms, but drifting colonies of specialized polyps. Some form glowing, pulsating bells that pump water; others, long trailing strands covered in stinging filaments, wait to snag the rare, drifting spore or microorganism. They mimic terrestrial vines, but they are hunters of the current, their translucent bodies filled with a quiet, hydraulic logic.

**The Canopy Dwellers: Shadows in the Kelp.** Within the towering, cathedral-like groves of the giant kelp, the fauna is shy, rare, and exquisitely adapted. The **Lantern Eels** are perhaps the most visible. They are long, ribbon-like beings, their flesh as clear as sea-glass, revealing not organs but a coiled, bioluminescent gut. Multiple soft-glowing photophores pulse in slow, hypnotic sequences along their bodies, not for communication, but to break up their silhouette as they weave between the broad, swaying blades of the kelp. They are the world's ghosts, visible only by the soft light they carry within their transparency.

On the rock overhangs, motionless for days, is the **Jade Stalker**. It resembles a mantis shrimp, but elongated and serene, its carapace the exact color of the surrounding algae. It possesses enormous, complex eyes that perceive spectra of light beyond the visible, likely seeing the faint bio-luminescent trails of spores and the heat signatures of the slow world around it. It strikes not with explosive speed, but with a deliberate, hydraulic extension of its raptorial claws, plucking translucent, shrimp-like larvae from the water with the patience of a tree growing.

**The Spore-Rivers and Their Keepers.** One of the most profound inhabitants is the **Polyp Carrier**. These are eyeless, soft-bodied organisms resembling terrestrial sea anemones, rooted to the rock. They have a single, specialized purpose: they produce and guide the **Coherent Spore Threads**. From their central mouth, they extrude a viscous, glowing green filament—a river of genetic material. This thread does not dissipate; it flows with purpose through the water, guided by the polyp's subtle contractions, to find and fertilize another giant kelp across the strait. It is a silent, slow-motion act of procreation, a technological marvel of biology, and the polyps are its devoted, unconscious engineers.

**The Listeners: On the Driest Peaks.** On the very summits, where stone meets mist, lives the **Echo Moss**. This is the most mysterious resident. It is not merely a plant. It is a resonant organ for the world. When touched, it releases a chime and a pulse of cold, but it is always listening. It absorbs the deep, sub-aquatic *booms*, the faint clicks of the Jade Stalker, the rustle of the kelp, and the harmonics of the water itself. It stores this sonic energy and re-emits it as subtle, cool vibrations and faint, tonal chimes, creating a constant, almost inaudible hum on the peaks—the world’s quiet, continuous song, played back to itself.

There are no singers here, no callers. The inhabitants communicate through chemistry, light, and the patient language of growth. The Lantern Eel's glow is a private statement. The Polyp Carrier's spore-thread is a promise to the future. The Reef-Weavers' architecture is a testament to deep time. And the Echo Moss holds the memory of it all. The living are not separate from the place; they are its nervous system, its circulatory system, and its slow, dreaming mind. They are the drowned world, breathing.
