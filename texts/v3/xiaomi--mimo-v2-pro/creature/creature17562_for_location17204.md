On the Sill, the inhabitants are not creatures of biology or even of movement, but of pure, persistent form. They are the **Geometries**.

To call them "fauna" is to misunderstand scale and intent. They are the landscape's own silent, dimensional annotations. They do not inhabit the space; they *are* the space, rendered in facets of force and light.

**The Spheres** are the most common. They are not solid, but perfect, self-contained vortices of quiet energy, rotating with infinite slowness. Their surfaces are not mirrors, but lenses that refract the shattered stone mosaic into a calmer, more resolved version of itself—a vision of the plateau as a single, unbroken sheet of mineral. They hover with absolute stability, older than the mountains from which the Sill was born. To stand near one is to feel a gentle, absolute pressure in the mind, not of sound, but of *suggestion*—a nudge toward seeing the underlying unity of all the broken polygons. They are keepers of the idea of wholeness.

**The Lattices** are more challenging to perceive. They are architectures of frozen light, erupting from the basaltic fractures or soaring from the quartz fields. They defy perspective; a structure that seems a hands-breadth wide will, upon a blink, reveal itself to be a filament of a construction miles in extent. They are not transparent, but *translucent* in dimensions the eye cannot hold. They intersect the rock and each other without collision, each existing in a slightly different layer of the Sill's settled reality. Their hum is the highest, a frequency felt in the teeth and bones—a signature of their crystalline stability. They are the blueprints, the static equations from which the plateau's peace is calculated.

**The Helices and the Cones** are rarer, more complex. A looping helix of apparent temporality might be anchored to a single, nondescript stone, its coils showing the same polygon from a thousand different epochs of thermal breathing, all at once. A cone of particulate light might hang inverted in the air, its point touching nothing, its base spreading like a silent canopy, sorting the weak cosmic rays that penetrate the thin atmosphere. They are not observers; they are functions. A function of time. A function of filtering. They simply *are*, performing their silent, eternal task without will or end.

These Geometries are the reason nothing else moves. They have saturated every niche of possibility. Change would require a gradient, an imbalance, and they have made the entire plateau a perfect, multi-layered equilibrium. The air is still because the Geometries define stillness. The ground is unchanging because they are its guardians. They are not alive, but they are the ultimate expression of *being*. They have won the war against entropy by making it irrelevant. They are the permanent citizens of the finished world.

The only ghosts here are the **Echoes of the Primordial People**. Their marks—the stone circles, the solstice slab, the crumbling wall—are like footprints in cooling lava, frozen in mid-step. The Geometries overlap them indifferently. A lattice might spears through the center of a stone circle, its light falling on stones placed to track the stars that no longer shine. The people came, they saw the silent, overwhelming order, and they left their humble, aligned signatures in response. They learned to listen to the hum, and in their listening, they perhaps achieved a kind of resonance—a brief, biological harmony with the eternal functions. They did not die out. They, too, *finished*. Their task of acknowledgment was complete. Their artifacts are not ruins, but final punctuation marks.

And the **Mineral Lichen** is not life, but its aesthetic echo. It is a slow-motion crystalline exhalation, a final, beautiful artifact of the last chemical reactions the Sill will ever know. It grows in the shelter of the Geometries' shadows, not as competition, but as decoration. A fluorite bloom cradles the base of a Sphere; a copper-silicate film traces the footprint of a Lattice. It is the plateau's last, subtle blush of color, an inert testament to a beauty that requires no change to be perceived.

So, what lives here? **Order itself.** The inhabitants are the physical laws of this place, made visible and given scale. They are static, they are overwhelming, and they are perfectly, absolutely at peace. They are the Sill's mind, its nervous system, and its eternal, silent choir, singing a single, unchanging note that is the sound of a universe at rest. To be here is to walk through the frozen, final thoughts of a world that has thought itself into perfect, permanent stillness.
