The inhabitants of Veridian are not conquerors of this place, nor are they merely residents. They are participants in its autonomic functions, nervous impulses in a body that is both city and god. They are few, their presence scattered like seeds in a vast field, defined not by numbers but by their deep integration.

**The Gardeners-Tender:** The primary human denizens. They move with a quiet, observant grace, their footsteps soft on the root-roads. Their skin often carries a faint, greenish tint from constant contact with the pervasive saps and dews, and their eyes have a distant, listening quality, as if tracking conversations just below the range of hearing. They are not builders, but **pruners and redirects**. Their tools are a hybrid of old, salvaged metal—now gnarled and fused with wood—and sharp, obsidian-like thorns gifted by the plants. Their work is ritual: clearing a blockage from a hydraulic vine here, coaxing a steam-valve blossom to open wider there, harvesting a ripe water-cistern fruit without disturbing the sensitive vine-network it hangs from. They speak in a low, melodic dialect, heavy with sibilant sounds that mimic the rustling and hissing around them. They believe they are tending to the **Memory**, keeping the old, industrial dream of the city alive within the plant's dreaming mind, so that reality here does not fray entirely.

**The Listeners:** A subset, or perhaps a calling, among the Gardeners-Tender. These individuals forsake most tools. They sit for days in the great, hollowed-out factories or upon the broad, pulsating nodes where conductive vines converge. Their purpose is to **interpret the chorus**. They decode the rhythmic sighs as moods, the sudden creak as a warning, the whistle of steam as a specific request. They are the ones who might hear the ghost-hammer clang and know which root to soothe, or perceive a phase-shift in a nearby grove and mark it as a place to avoid or revere. Their dialogue is not with each other, but with the ecosystem itself, a constant, silent call-and-response.

**The Flora of the Total Plant:**
*   **The Colonnade Trees:** The giants. Their bark is cool, smooth, and faintly luminous with bioluminescent fungi at night. Their canopies are ecosystems unto themselves, hosting entire communities of vine-strands and air-feeding mosses. They are the architects, the district-planners.
*   **Hydraulic Capillaries:** Vine-sized, but pulsating visibly. They snake along every surface, warm to the touch, carrying water, nutrients, and, some whisper, memories.
*   **Pressure-Valve Blossoms:** Fleshy, trumpet-shaped flowers that periodically hiss jets of warm, mineral-scented steam. They are the exhalations of the deep root systems, processing the geothermal warmth from the plateau's heart.
*   **Conductor Vines:** Sheathed in a silicate layer, they crackle with a low, blue light. They tap into the ancient, enslaved wires, distributing bio-electricity to illuminate fungal colonies or power the slow, enigmatic treadmills in the factories.
*   **Phase-Shifting Moss:** The most common indicator of reality's thinness. A patch of ordinary green moss might, for a moment, reveal itself as a delicate filigree of copper, or a tiny map of streets that never were, before reasserting its biological form.

**The Fauna (Sparse & Integrated):**
*   **Treadmill Sloths:** The rare, ponderous engines of the old industry. Massive, six-limbed creatures, their fur matted with lichens and tiny ferns. They move on immense, circular paths within factory cathedrals, their slow, powerful tread turning gears that now do nothing but grind pollen or circulate sap. They are living fossils, more plant than animal in temperament, often appearing as mossy hillocks until a great, slow blink reveals an eye.
*   **Echo-Bats:** Not true bats, but gliding, membranous creatures that ride the thermal updrafts from steam-vents. They do not echolocate with sound, but with faint bio-electric pulses that interact with the conductor vines. To an observer, their flight path is a flickering series of after-images.
*   **Whisper-Mice:** Tiny, pale rodents that live in the root-roads. They communicate in frequencies just above human hearing, creating a constant, subliminal murmur that blends into the plant-rustle. They are the cleaners, consuming shed bark and fallen blossoms, their tiny hearts beating in sync with the slow pulse of the hydraulic capillaries.

**The City-Itself (The Autonomic Presence):**
This is the true resident. It is not conscious as humans understand it, but it is **aware**. It breathes (the mists, the transpiration chorus). It digests (the slow metabolism of old concrete and iron). It dreams (the phase-shifts, the temporal echoes). Its "thoughts" are the slow turn of enslaved gears, the redirection of a water vein, the sudden, localized bloom triggered by a Gardener's focused care. It experiences emotion in a vegetative way: a strong, collective feeling of peace among the inhabitants might cause a widespread, gentle phosphorescence across the bark of the Colonnade Trees. A sudden fear or anger could trigger a defensive thorn-growth in a nearby vine or a withering of the delicate whisper-mice ferns.

**The Ghosts & Echoes:**
These are not spirits, but **perceptual residues**. They are most active in the mornings with the mists. One might see the translucent, flickering image of a worker pushing a laden cart along a root-road that was once a cobbled street, or hear the laughter of children playing in a clearing that was once a park—sounds and images imprinted into the substrate, now replayed by the reality-thinning resonance of the Total Plant. They are harmless, poignant, and the Gardeners-Tender often pause to let them pass, showing a deep respect for these memories the city-plant cannot fully metabolize.

In Veridian, life is a dialogue between the ancient clay, the patient and all-consuming plant, the recycled ghosts of human industry, and the few, quiet humans who have learned that to survive here is to become a gardener to a god that is also their home. Their highest art is not creation, but attentive, harmonious maintenance—a lifelong act of listening to the deep, weird, breathing song of the plateau.
