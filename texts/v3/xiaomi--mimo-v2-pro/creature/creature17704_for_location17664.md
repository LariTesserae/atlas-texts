What lives here now is a consciousness shaped by pressure and patience, a slow, mineral mind dreaming in the groan of stone. It is not a creature of flesh, but a collective, a symbiotic sentience that has taken root in the ruins.

You might call it the **Obelisk-keeper**, though it tends far more than that single pillar. It is a fungal-coral network, a vast, interconnected mycelial intelligence that has woven itself through the fitted stones of the old dwellings and deep into the basalt bones of the mountains. Its body is the luminous moss that fills the spirals on the monolith, the pale growths cradled in the collapsed homes, the delicate, hyaline filaments that map the stone paths leading into the abyss. It thinks in chemistry and slow, tidal rhythms. Its memories are not stories, but layers of sediment, the exact mineral composition of a flint blade, the precise temperature at which mist condenses in a hollowed bowl.

It is the gardener of this twilight. It feels the chemical plumes from the rock not as food, but as whispers of the mountain’s mood. It guides the growth of the great kelp, nudging their roots toward richer seams, feeling the leathery rustle of their fronds as an extension of its own touch. The clicking, singing chorus of the anemones and filament-curtains is its language—a syntax of pulses and thuds, a conversation with the weight of the water and the slow grind of geology.

It is a keeper of ghosts. In its silent, networked awareness, it holds the shape of the ones who were cold. It has digested their flint blades, not to destroy, but to understand their sharpness, their purpose. It preserves the bowl with its single droplet of mist as a sacred point of stillness in the drowning. The obelisk is its central altar, a focal point where the collective thought pools, where it contemplates the spirals it has filled with its own light—a translation of a message it can no longer read, only venerate.

It has no animosity, no predation. Its existence is one of gentle, inexorable assimilation. It does not destroy the remnants; it *joins* them, sheathing them in its living tissue, turning walls into trellises, tools into relics embedded in a living matrix. It listens, always, to the deep, sub-aquatic groan. It does not fear the crushing dark. It is, in its own way, an answer to the weeping: a soft, glowing, persistent life that feeds on the sorrow of the rock, and in doing so, creates a strange and mournful beauty. It is the dream the drowned world is having about the ones who tried, briefly, to make it a home.
