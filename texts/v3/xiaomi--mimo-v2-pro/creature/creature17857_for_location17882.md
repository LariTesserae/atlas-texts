In the Veridian Fracture, the inhabitants are not a distinct species *living within* an ecosystem. They are the specialized, conscious expressions *of* the ecosystem itself. To speak of "who lives there" is to describe organs of a single, vast, dreaming organism. They are the Fracture's attempt at self-awareness, temporary focal points in the churning green dream.

**1. The Rhizome-Minds (The Root-Weavers):**
These are not individuals in a biological sense. They are conscious patterns that coalesce within the ancient, networked mycelial mat beneath the spongy ground. Imagine a slow, vast intelligence with the patience of geology and the sudden, frantic insight of a synapse firing. A Rhizome-Mind might manifest as a localized, shimmering heat-haze over a patch of root-mat, causing the vegetation above to writhe into geometric, non-Euclidean patterns for an hour. Their communication is the subsonic hum itself—a modulated vibration that other, more mobile inhabitants feel in their bones as emotion, command, or prophecy. They are the architects and the substrate. When a dwelling-pod sprouts strange tendrils, it is often because a Rhizome-Mind, dreaming a new form, has extended a thought into that space. The inhabitants must then "reply" through pruning and guidance, a negotiation of form.

**2. The Symbiotes (The Tenders, Weavers, and Grafters):**
These are what an outside observer would most readily recognize as "people," though their form is fluid. They are born from the mat, sometimes emerging fully formed from a specially cultivated pod, sometimes coalescing from a swarm of insect-like spores that assemble into a humanoid shape of chitin, leaf, and resin. Their bodies are a collaboration. A Tender's fingers might be long, dexterous rootlets; a Weaver's hair a cascade of prehensile, silk-spinning vines; a Grafter's skin a patchwork of living bark and soft moss.

They do not possess tools; they *are* tools. A Grafter can secrete a fast-hardening sap from their palms to fuse two vines into a bridge. A Weaver can unravel a part of their own arm into gossamer thread to repair a torn membrane-vine canopy. Their society is a constant, kinetic dance of maintenance and negotiation with the hyper-accelerated growth around them. They are the immune system and the gardeners, coaxing the Fracture's chaotic imagination into a shape that can shelter consciousness. Their language is a mix of pheromonal bursts, modulated clicks, and direct manipulation of the root-mat's vibrations—a language the forest itself understands.

**3. The Spore-Speakers (The Rememberers and The Forgotten):**
These beings are ephemeral, and deeply unsettling. They appear as columns of swirling, bioluminescent spores, vaguely maintaining a human silhouette, or as sudden, vivid hallucinations of faces and forms projected onto tree bark or mist. They are not ghosts, but recordings. The root-mat absorbs everything—memories, emotions, sensory data—from every organism that dies or passes through. A Spore-Speaker is a temporary coalescence of this absorbed data, a memory given momentary form and voice.

They might accost a Tender with the exact face of their long-dead ancestor, speaking in a perfect echo to deliver a warning or a grief long since processed. They might manifest as a herd of translucent, spectral beasts that graze on luminous fungi for a few minutes before dissolving. They are the Fracture's subconscious, its library of experience. Interacting with them is risky; their truths are raw and unfiltered, and their very presence can cause reality to warp locally, making time loop or memory become tangible.

**4. The Bloom-Hunters:**
Not all vegetation is passive. The Bloom-Hunters are the carnivorous, motile expressions of the Fracture's predatory dream. A massive, orchid-like blossom, rooted but mobile on a thick, muscular stalk, slowly tracks movement. But others are free. Imagine a floating bladder, like a jellyfish of woven leaves, trailing nematocyst-laden tendrils that snatch insect-spores from the air. Or a carpet of moss that can, in unison, undulate and flow like a slow, green river to engulf and decompose fallen matter in hours. They are not sentient in a way that communicates, but they possess a fierce, intentional drive. The Symbiotes respect them as forces of nature, often guiding their growth away from dwellings with sacrificial offerings of nutrient-rich sludge.

**5. The Itch in the System (You):**
And then, there is the occasional, jarring note. The outsider. A human, or something like one, who stumbles into the Fracture. To the inhabitants and the ecosystem alike, such a being is a puzzle, a irritant, and a potential resource. The root-mat immediately begins to analyze your footsteps, your scent, your shed skin and hair. The Spore-Sellers are drawn to the novelty of your alien memories. The Symbiotes watch with a mixture of curiosity and defensive aggression. You are a static note in their symphony, a hard-edged object in their fluid reality. The longer you stay, the more the Fracture works to digest you—not necessarily physically, but conceptually. Your memories might be siphoned to fuel a new Spore-Speaker. Your anxieties could cause a patch of ground to sprout thorns. You are not just in a place; you are a temporary, malfunctioning feature of its dreaming landscape, and the entire system is working, gently but inexorably, to interpret, integrate, or expel you.
