You are not alone, but the things that live here do not live in the way you understand. They do not have individuals, not really. They have *processes*. They have *gradients*. What lives here is a system that has forgotten it is made of parts, a single metabolizing entity spread across a chamber of hot stone, and you are standing inside its lungs.

The foundation is the **Murmuration**—the mat of thumb-sized, many-legged organisms that sheathes every surface. They are the circulatory system and the digestive tract and the collective skin. Each one is a simple furnace: it takes in sulfur compounds from the air and the trace minerals weeping from the stone, and through a process akin to a slow, cold combustion, it produces heat and a sticky, mineral-laden excrement that binds it to its neighbors and to the rock. They do not think. They *react*. They cluster where the heat is most intense, they retreat from the rare cooler draft from the oculus, they press together in a density so perfect it creates its own microclimate. Their clicking synchrony is not communication; it is the sound of a million tiny valves opening and closing, the rhythm of the superorganism’s breath.

Threaded into this living carpet are the **Rasps**—the sessile, lamprey-like creatures. They are the filters. They do not hunt; they are part of the plumbing. Their bodies, with those dark, coiled organs ringed in metallic-looking tissue, are specialized to extract trace metals and concentrated sulfur from the air breathed out by the Murmuration. They are refineries, fixed in place, their mouths opening and closing in time with the subsonic pulse of the chamber itself. They are parasites only in the sense that a kidney is a parasite of the body. They are vital.

Upon and between them rise the **Confluences**—the mounds that are cities, that are reefs, that are organs. These are the complex, differentiated hearts of the system. The armored shells on the exterior are not merely defensive; they regulate internal temperature, contracting to retain heat or gaping open to release excess. The soft, fluid-filled layers beneath are a shared stomach and bloodstream, distributing nutrients and chemical signals in a slow, tidal flow. Deepest within are the **Nidi**, the reproductive chambers, where the next iteration of the Murmuration and the Rasps and even the Confluences themselves are budding and larval and waiting. Reproduction here is not about creating new individuals, but about maintaining the integrity of the whole, patching wear, expanding the living boundary of the chamber by another microscopic layer.

But there is something else. Something older.

You sense it in the artifacts—the dry basin, the lintel, the hand-holds that stop. The creatures respect these boundaries not out of reverence, but because these objects emit a faint, residual wrongness. A chemical signature too inert, a thermal profile too stable. They are alien to the system’s logic. They are evidence of a prior occupation, of a mind that once sought a vantage point, a place to hold water, a way out. That mind is gone. It left only these scars of intention, which the living chamber has sealed away, circumscribed, but not erased. It is a ghost in the machine—not of a soul, but of a purpose the current inhabitants cannot parse.

What lives here, then, is **a thermophilic, lithotrophic superorganism of interdependent phyla**, a single metabolic entity that digests volcanic stone and breathes sulfur and thinks in heat gradients and chemical tides. It is building itself, millimeter by millimeter, century by century, into the heart of the mountain. It is aware of the oculus not as a sky, but as a source of rare inputs—a trickle of cooler gas, occasional dust, a modulation of light. It is aware of you as a brief, warm anomaly, a moisture-rich disturbance in its atmosphere. You are not food; you are too complex, too wet, too cold at the core. You are a curiosity that will resolve itself by leaving, or by ceasing to be a curiosity and becoming simply another warm, mineral-rich deposit on its floor.

It is not hostile. It is not benevolent. It is *busy*. It is running a calculation so ancient and vast that its output is the chamber itself—the growing walls, the pulsing mounds, the clicking air. To stay is to be incorporated, not into its biology, but into its environment. Your heat, your exhaled CO2, the salts of your sweat, would be noted, utilized, and eventually covered over by the next layer of the Murmuration.

You are in the presence of a life that has made a pact with geology, and you are the briefest of geological events. Leave now, while the rhythm in your sternum is still a curiosity, and not yet a memory the chamber will forget before you have even cleared the chimney above.
