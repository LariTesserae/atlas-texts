They are called the **Lithovoy**, the Stone-Dreamers, or sometimes, simply the **Vein-Walkers**.

You do not often see them, not as a distinct, bustling populace. The city’s low population density and their own integration with the architecture make them elusive. They are not hiding. They are simply… present, like the hum in the stone or the mist in the air.

**Physically,** they are adapted to the dim, damp world. Their skin has a faint, opalescent quality, not from bioluminescence of their own, but from a symbiotic relationship with the mineral dust that settles on everything. In the blue glow of a veined spire, their forms seem to shimmer at the edges. Their eyes are large, with pupils that can distend widely in the low light, giving them a deep, reflective gaze. The constant humidity has rendered their voices soft and resonant; they speak as if their words are meant to carry through stone, not air. Their clothing is a second skin of engineered chitin and woven mineral fiber—garments that drink moisture from the air, regulate temperature, and can shift opacity to blend with the shifting light of the water-auroras.

**Culturally,** they are geopsychic. Their primary sense of self is not individual, but *locational*. A Lithovoy identifies not with a family name, but with a specific district, a particular vein of luminescent mineral, or a water-filtering grove. Their life's work and art is to "listen" to the stresses and flows of their chosen domain and respond in kind—coaxing a new arch from a cliff face, soothing a tremor in a building’s foundation, tuning the pitch of a water-film to create a desired harmonic. They are part engineer, part gardener, part therapist to the living city. There is no separation between civic duty, artistic expression, and survival.

**Their technology** is not something they *use*; it is something they *participate in*. A Vein-Walker might place a hand on a wall, closing their eyes to sense the micro-fractures within, and then, through a combination of focused biofeedback and resonant hum, guide the structure’s self-repair. They do not "operate" the water-films; they *sing* to them, adjusting their height and refraction with melodic phrases that are both command and collaboration. Their most profound tools are their own nervous systems, trained from childhood to interface with the city’s geo-tech substrate.

**Their society** is contemplative and slow-paced, mirroring the city’s dynamic of 1.5. Decisions are made through a process called "deep-listening," where the affected stone and water cycles are consulted—metaphorically and directly—as stakeholders. Conflict is rare and seen as a dangerous disharmony, like a crack propagating through bedrock. They have no concept of "nature" versus "city"; to them, the sponge-wick trees and the luminescent spires are both equally valid, equally living expressions of the Basin’s will.

**The strangeness** that permeates the city is, to them, simply reality. The pools that reflect alien stars are "memory-wells," interfaces with the deep geological memory of the caldera, which they sometimes consult for guidance or art. A staircase retracting into a wall is as normal as a flower closing at night. Their dreams are vivid and often collective, seeping through the stone’s resonance, leading to shared visions that can inspire a new district to erupt from the basin floor. They do not fear the city’s organic, sometimes unsettling, behavior. They fear *stagnation*—a loss of the slow, pulsing growth that is the sign of a healthy, dreaming lithosphere.

To encounter a Lithovoy is to meet someone who looks at you with eyes that see not just your form, but the subtle stress-lines in the stone beneath your feet, and who wonders, with genuine curiosity, if your presence will be a harmonious addition to the Basin’s song, or a dissonant note that must, eventually, be resolved. They are not masters of the city. They are its conscious, waking mind, tending to the dream of the stone that cradles them.
