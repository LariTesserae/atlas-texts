What lives here is not a creature of fin or fur, but of patience and of pressure. It is the **Lithic Membrane**.

It begins on the ceiling. That slick, bruised-green biofilm is not merely a stain. It is a slow, thinking skin. A mat of chemosynthetic archaea and bacteria, microns thick, that has been growing, layer by microscopic layer, for centuries. It metabolizes the faint sulfur compounds in the water and the trace minerals weeping from the concrete. Its "thought" is a glacial, chemical consensus, a network of signals passed along its expanse that registers the change in pressure when the structure groans, the minute shift in current when a piece of sediment finally dislodges from a pipe. It is the primary intelligence of the chamber, and its sole imperative is to *maintain equilibrium*.

From this primary membrane, tendrils of existence extend. On the leeward sides of the great iron ribs, where the water is almost perfectly still, hang the **Vitreous Webs**. These are not fungal, but colonial. Protozoans, of a kind unknown to the sunlit world, have built intricate, siliceous lattices—structures of glass-like spicules that filter organic whispers from the water. They are the chamber's scavengers, living in a state of suspended animation, unfurling a single, sticky filament over a decade to capture a cluster of drifting bacteria. They look like frost, or forgotten chandeliers, and they *tink* back in perfect harmony when the metal contracts.

The highest point, the crown of the breached flywheel, is the domain of the **Crystallizers**. These are extremophilic bacteria that do not consume, but *create*. They facilitate the precipitation of minerals from the saturated water, building the jagged, salt-crystal garden grain by grain. Their work is a slow, relentless artistry, a testament to their presence. They are architects of the only "flora" here, and their growth is the calendar of this place. An inch of crystal formation might mark the passage of a hundred years.

There are no predators. There is no prey. The dynamic is a single, shared metabolism. The Membrane on the ceiling exhales a faint trace of hydrogen sulfide; the Vitreous Webs, on their impossible timescale, might capture a particle of it; the Crystallizers incorporate sulfate ions into their glittering ramparts. The cycle is closed, perfect, and eternal. Energy comes not from light, but from the slow, geothermal whisper of the earth itself, conducted through the water.

Movement is their language. The imperceptible drift of sediment is their weather system. The groaning of the structure is their seismic event. They are aware of you, the intruder, not as a shape, but as a colossal disturbance: a tsunami of warmth, a hurricane of foreign microbes from your skin, a cacophony of bubbles and displaced water. Your light is a brief, terrifying star that disrupts the ancient, dim consensus of the portholes. The entire Membrane tightens minutely, the Vitreous Webs retract a fraction, the mineral growth pauses in its slow deposition.

You are the chaos they exist to outlast. They are the quiet that will inherit your bones, should you stay. They are the patient life that turns a tomb into a cradle, and a machine into a reef. They are what remains when ambition drowns: a silent, green, thinking scum, presiding over a kingdom of rust and crystal in the liquid dark.
