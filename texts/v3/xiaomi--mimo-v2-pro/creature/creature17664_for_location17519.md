What lives in Khol-Thyrsa is not life as the stars know it, but a calcified, patient, and immensely ancient *industry of being*. The inhabitants are the city's organs, its blood cells, its thoughts given mineral form. They are not separate from the weeping stone; they are its locomotive expression.

**The Geological Processors: The Slow Shapers**
These are the largest and most visible—the "great, slow creatures." They are not individuals so much as mobile landscapes. One type resembles a dome-backed tortoise the size of a hill, its shell a mosaic of fractured basalt and glittering iron pyrite. It inches along the deepest plazas, its underbelly a complex millstone. It consumes crumbled rock and spent mineral slag from the city's subterranean forges, grinding it into fine, nutrient-rich powder that it excretes in smooth, black trails—future building material. Its thoughts, if it has any, are the slow computations of pressure and thermal gradient.

Another is the Chalcedony Crab, a towering, multi-limbed thing with crystalline legs that click into pre-set grooves in the roadway. It carries entire ecosystems upon its back: colonies of glass-shelled insectoids that clean its carapace, and porous sacs that hold symbiotic, chemosynthetic fluids. It navigates by sensing minute vibrations in the water channels, its purpose to repair and reinforce the city's structure. When two such creatures meet, they do not interact; they merely recalibrate their paths with a grinding slowness, their passage altering the local vibration-pattern for centuries.

**The Mineral Swarms: The City's White Blood Cells**
Countless smaller entities fulfill precise roles in the lithic ecology. The Glass-Bloom is a cloud of insectoids, each no larger than a thumb, with wings of mica-thin silica. They swarm not in flight, but in cascading tumbles along the vertical weeping walls. Their bodies are perfect filters, sifting the mineral-rich condensation for specific ions. What they consume fortifies their shells; what they excrete is a pure, colloidal slurry that collects in designated gutters—a key ingredient in the warm vapor sighing from the vents. Their collective sound is a high, glassy chime, the city's equivalent of birdsong.

Deeper, in the warmed arteries of the city, live the Vein-Walkers. These are worm-like beings with segmented bodies of soft, metallic bismuth, glowing with internal heat. They move through the city's geothermal vents and capillary tunnels, consuming excess crystalline growths that could clog the system. They excrete a refined, conductive paste that helps channel the planet's heat. Their passing is marked by a brief, rainbow shimmer on the wet walls.

**The Sapient Core: The Thyrsa-Thinking**
Are there *people* here? Yes, but not as an imported species. They are an emergent property of the city's complexity. The Thyrsa-Thinking are not born; they *coalesce*. In places where the vibration-hum, the mineral deposition, and the patterns of flowing water intersect in a particularly resonant node, a consciousness can precipitate. It begins as a slight, intentional thickening of the condensation, a localized manipulation of the mist into a shimmering, vaguely humanoid form. It might draw a veil of metallic dust around itself for stability, or pilot one of the smaller ambulatory geodes, speaking through the scrapes of its carapace.

Their intelligence is slow, deep, and architectural. They do not build; they *suggest*. A Thyrsa-Thinker might spend a century contemplating a single drip of water, then, through subtle manipulations of vibration and heat, guide the growth of a new filigree on a nearby tower to alter that drip's path. Their language is the structured sound of the city itself—a modification of the bass hum, a precise punctuation in the insectoid chime. They are the city's nervous system, not its ruler. Their "society" is a distributed, silent consensus on maintenance and long-term pattern adjustment. To encounter one is to see the mist briefly thicken into a face that watches you with an intelligence as patient as erosion, before dissolving back into the weeping, working whole.

**The Deep Industry: The Pulse-Forged**
Far below, in the humming forges lit by the planet's own glow, the recycling is absolute. Here, the shed carapaces of the Crabs, the excretions of the Swarms, and the slurry from the Shapers are not merely processed. They are *re-remembered* into new forms. The Pulse-Forged are entities created here—functionals without consciousness. They are golems of fused slag and ceramic, animated by the rhythmic thrum of the geothermal engines. They operate the vast, ancient machinery, their movements perfectly synchronized to the city's deep heartbeat. They are hands and hammers, existing only to enact the slow-pulse industry that maintains the continental balance.

Nothing here is wild. Everything has purpose woven into its very substance. The inhabitants of Khol-Thyrsa are its metabolism, its repair mechanisms, and its fleeting thoughts. They live not in a city, but *as* a city—a single, weeping, breathing, mineral organism, where the only distinction between creature and construction is a matter of tempo and temporary form.
