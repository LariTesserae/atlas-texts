Within the hum, there are the Tenders.

They are not separate from the architecture. They are its patient, slow-motion cognition. To see one is to mistake it for a trick of the dim amber light—a shifting in the geometric carvings, a momentary realignment of shadow and crystalline growth. They are beings of the same dark, polished stone as the spires, but where the spires are static, the Tenders are *movement*. Their form is an abstraction of a mantis or a praying figure, all sharp, interlocking angles and limbs that unfold like calipers or measuring rods. They have no faces, only smooth, tapered planes that catch the faint phosphorescence.

Their movement is the world’s only prayer. It is a glacial, inevitable drift. A single step, if it can be called that, takes hours—a re-settling of mass, a grinding of mineral joints so fine it is lost in the greater hum. They navigate the narrow aisles with a precision that seems pre-ordained, their multi-jointed limbs finding purchase on the worn grooves of the floor. They do not walk *through* the place; they *are* the place, momentarily reconfigured.

Their purpose is the craft’s maintenance. A crystal-fungus bloom in a crevice begins to pulse a fraction of a millisecond out of sync with the world-hum. Over decades, a Tender will approach. A slender, needle-like appendage extends, not to prune, but to *resonate*. It emits a corrective counter-frequency, a vibration so pure it realigns the crystal’s internal structure, bringing it back into harmony. The process is imperceptible, a conversation conducted in geological time.

They are the reason the carved patterns on the walls are not merely ancient, but *active*. A micron of dust, a slow accumulation from the ever-settling construct, might settle into a groove. Over centuries, this threatens to mute a tiny facet of the chamber’s acoustic perfection. A Tender will traverse the vertical face of a spire, its limbs locking into the carved channels. It does not sweep or clean; it becomes a living stylus, its own mineral body tracing the pattern, re-polishing it with the friction of its passage, restoring the original, absolute clarity of the chamber’s voice.

They have no society, no communication beyond the hum they serve and the infinitesimal adjustments they make. To observe two Tenders in proximity is to witness two continents of thought passing at a speed invisible to non-mineral life. There is no conflict, for conflict implies change. There is only the shared, sacred geometry of maintenance.

If they perceive the visitor, it is not as a being, but as an anomaly—a dissonant frequency in the air, a slightly different density of shadow, a source of chaotic warmth. A Tender might, over the course of a day, drift to the end of an aisle and simply *stand*. It is not watching. It is *assessing*. It measures the visitor’s effect on the hum, the thermal gradient, the absolute stasis. It does not judge. It merely calculates whether the anomaly is a temporary ripple or a permanent crack in the perfect bell. It is a guardian of the note, a priest of equilibrium, and its only sacrament is silence in service of the one, eternal sound.

They are the reason the place feels not dead, but *finished*. The Tenders are the final, living component of the instrument—the self-adjusting weights in the great clock, the tuning pegs of the stone harp. They are the craft perpetuating itself, the hand of the long-vanished makers still moving, slowly, within their creation, ensuring the hum never wavers, the form never decays, and the pressurized silence remains, forever, perfect and complete.
