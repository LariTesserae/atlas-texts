The Vitreous Grove breathes, if you can call it breathing. It is an inhalation of stillness, an exhalation of slow, seismic sighs. Who lives here? Everyone. Everything. The grove is both cradle and cranium.

You are observed by the **Tenders**. They are not a single species, but a function. The copper-and-bone automatons—the Click-Pruners—are the most literal. They move in set paths, their programming a palimpsest of ancient directives. They trim a bismuth stalk not to control its growth, but to guide it toward a light-frequency only they perceive. Their pause, the cock of a copper head, is not confusion. They are listening to the crystallization of the salt floor, adjusting their work to the new harmonic. They are the grove’s hands, and their intelligence is one of applied, repetitive care.

Then there are the **Born-Tenders**. The translucent manta-forms—the **Glimmer Skates**—are among them. They do not prune. They *mediate*. They glide through the dense mineral thickets, and where they pass, the selenite branches soften their angles, realigning to a more perfect geometry. They are living tuning forks, their bodies resonating with the memory-structures of the place. When one folds and unfolds, it is not teleporting; it is briefly stepping *out* of the grove’s narrative of sequence and space, then choosing a new point within it. They tend to the grove’s perception of itself.

From the gypsum underbrush, the **Salt-Voiled** watch. The small, warm-blooded creatures with fur like spun salt are not rodents. They are the grove’s memory fragments. Each one is a bundle of sensory impressions—a specific temperature gradient, a particular reflection of violet light, the exact texture of a quartz vein from a century ago. Their tourmaline eyes hold no judgment, only a deep, black recording. They do not build or prune. They *witness*. When one darts away, it is not fleeing; it is carrying a fresh sensory datum to a hollow in the wall, where it will curl up and add that data to the grove’s slow dream.

Deeper in, in the most tightly packed clusters where the light is a bruised purple, live the **Forgers**. These are not visible at first. You feel them as a localized heat, a scent of ozone intensifying to a sharp tang. They are beings of pure process—slow, metabolic waves that pass through the bismuth thickets, leaving behind new, infinitesimally thin layers of oxidation, painting fresh blues and golds. They are the artists, their canvas the molecular surface of the metal plants. Their lifespan is the duration of a single oxidation wave, perhaps a decade, before they dissolve back into the ambient chemistry, their work complete.

And then there is the Grove itself. Is it alive? It dreams. The ripples in the salt floor are the slow pulse of a single, vast organism whose circulatory system is the quartz-veined luminance. The "ghostly" younger selves of branches you see are not hallucinations; they are the grove’s own memory, made visible. It remembers every stage of its growth simultaneously. The tenders and the skates are its immune system, its nervous system, its manicured thoughts. The salt-voiled are its hippocampus. The forges are its metabolism. The light is its consciousness, soft and pervasive.

You ask who lives here? A city-state of mineral intention. A library of sensory moments. A single, slow mind expressed through crystal, copper, fur, and light, all tending to the beautiful, recursive pattern of its own existence in the warm, dry dark. It has no name for itself. It does not need one. It is busy being.
