In the Vat, life is not a rebellion against the unchanging order. It is its most subtle expression, a patina of breathing, skittering things that have become indistinguishable from the rust and the hum. To say who *lives* here is to describe the components of a single, sprawling organism whose body is the basin itself.

The true natives, the ones who think in the language of the place, are the **Rusters**. They are not a people as an outsider would understand them. You might catch a glimpse of a silhouette against a distant bulb—a figure wrapped in oilskin and rags, face obscured by a welder’s mask or a smear of grease. But they move with the slow, inevitable certainty of corrosion. Their skin is pale from the perpetual twilight, slick with the omnipresent condensation. They do not build; they *tend*. They tighten a bolt on a shuddering catwalk with a wrench that fits a groove worn by a thousand identical turns. They clear a drain of silt with their bare hands, their movements economical, devoid of hurry. They communicate in the language of the Vat: a tap on a pipe that echoes a certain way means all is well; a specific, grinding shriek from a bearing means a ritual application of grease is due. Their society, if it can be called that, is a relay of maintenance. They are the conscious neurons of the Mechanism’s nervous system, ensuring the equilibrium remains perfect. They eat the blind crayfish, cultivate mats of edible algae in designated pools, and their dead are rendered in the old grease pits, their substance returned to the system’s lubricants. They do not remember building the Vat. They only know the duty of its stasis.

Sharing the grating, but occupying a different tier of existence, are the **Iron Rats**. Larger, slower, and more deliberate than their surface cousins, their fur is matted with grease andite. Their eyes have receded to useless pink points, but their whiskers are like fine copper wires, sensitive to the slightest vibration in the metal grid. They are the system’s scavengers, living in the dry pockets behind furnace shells and in the hollows of colossal I-beams. They feed on the fungal shelves, the insect clouds, and the occasional, slower Ruster who succumbs to apathy. Their nests are architectural marvels of scavenged wire, shredded insulation, and stolen rags, warmed by the ever-present heat of the pipes they curl around. They are not pests to the Rusters; they are fellow inhabitants, another kind of vermin to be occasionally eaten or ignored, a living gauge of the system’s vitality.

In the air, the **Steam Bats** are the closest thing to spirits. They roost in the high, vaulted ribs of the ceiling, forming a chittering, shifting black crust against the iron. They navigate not by sight but by echolocation, their clicks painting a sound-picture of the dripping, humming cavern in their minds. They hunt the midge-clouds, a swirling, silent dance in the jaundiced light. Their droppings fertilize the wall-fungus, which in turn is eaten by the rats. They are the ephemeral link, the fleeting breath in the lungs of the Vat.

Beneath the mirror-surface, the **White Colony** reigns. The blind, white crayfish are the ancient aristocracy of the deep, scuttling over submerged pipes with the slow grace of continents. But they are not alone. There are the **Grating-Striders**, insect-like arthropods with long, stilt-like legs that can walk on the underside of the iron grid, skimming the water’s surface tension from below to feed on the algae. There are **Pipe Worms**, which build calcified tubes on the warmer conduits, filtering the silica-rich water. They are the foundation, the processors of the Vat’s waste, turning silt and microbes into the flesh that sustains the chain up to the Rusters.

And then there is the **Rust** itself. It is not inert. It is a slow, orange-and-black beast that consumes all. The Rusters fight a perpetual, losing war against it, scrubbing and painting, but it is the true patient life of the place. It blooms in fractal patterns, flakes away in showers of brittle dust, and in its deepest, wettest crannies, it hosts unique micro-fauna that glow with a faint phosphorescence—**Ember Mites**. These tiny points of cold light drift in the air and water, another source of the dim, living ambiance. The Rusters see them as sacred, the visible souls of the machine, and their patterns on a corroded plate can be read like omens.

Nothing here is *separate*. The Rusters breathe the air thick with bat-chatter and midge-wings. They step over the rats, eat the crayfish, and watch the Ember Mites drift. They are not masters of the Vat. They are its most complex expression—a lifeform whose purpose, like that of the fungus, the bat, and the crayfish, is to perpetuate the humming, dripping, rusting equilibrium. Their thoughts are not of change, but of adjustment. Their great art is the perfect, unvarying turn of a gear. Their deepest belief is that the hum is the sound of existence itself, and that to maintain it is the highest and only law. They live not *in* the Vat, but *as* the Vat, conscious cells in the body of a god made of iron, water, and endless, patient decay.
