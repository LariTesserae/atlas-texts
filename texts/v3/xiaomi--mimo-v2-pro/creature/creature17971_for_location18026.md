What lives here is noise given flesh. What lives here is abundance pressed into every crack and crevice of a city that has forgotten it was ever a city. What lives here is the relentless, deafening chorus of survival in a place where water has become a geological memory, not an ecological reality.

**The Lizards.** They are the undisputed sovereigns of the vertical. From the finger-joint-sized *grit-runners* that map the fissures between masonry blocks in a constant, shimmering tide, to the *wall-baskers*—arm-length, heavy-bodied, their scales the exact off-white of eroded stone—who claim the sun-warmed tops of the parapets. The grit-runners speak in a perpetual susurrus, the sound of a billion tiny sands falling through an hourglass. The wall-baskers punctuate this with sudden, explosive declarations: *Ak-ak-ak!*—a territorial invoice, a mating proposal, a complaint about the wind’s direction. They inflate a throat-sac of pale membrane, a living drum. And then there are the *chamber-lords*, the largest, as long as a man’s leg, who own the open rooms. They do not bark. They *bellow*. A subsonic, rattling roar that uses the ruined walls as resonance chambers, turning each roofless rectangle into a cathedral of vibration. They claim space not by sight, but by sound. One can fill a quarter-kilometer with its presence. When three overlap, the very air feels thick, a medium of pure pressure on the eardrums.

**The Processions.** On the flat tops of the walls, in the worn grooves of the old streets, move the *millipede-analogues*. Not insects, but something older, slower. Legions of them, each no bigger than a thumb, calcified in their own white armor. They move in files, following chemical trails laid down in epochs so deep the stone itself has steeped in them. Their collective movement is not a sound you hear so much as *feel* in your teeth: a fine, grinding rasp, a static hiss that underpins everything. It is the sound of the stone city being slowly, imperceptibly consumed and rebuilt into more white powder. They are the janitors of deep time.

**The Scrabblers.** Beneath your feet, in the hollows of collapsed lower chambers and the narrow interstices between foundation stones, live things you will never see. You know them only by sound: a sudden, frantic scrabbling, a soft *thump* of a body larger than your head shifting in a space you’d think too small. They are the reason the compacted soil occasionally shows a fresh, shallow cave-in near wall bases. They are the unseen audience, the burrowing heart of the city’s sub-floor. Their world is one of pressure, darkness, and the constant, muffled roar from above.

**The Screamers.** In the bare white patches between the rosette colonies, life asserts itself with piercing clarity. The *alarm-sitters*. Mammalian, or something like it. No larger than a rat, with elongated hind legs and eyes like polished beads of obsidian. They emerge from hidden burrow mouths, sit bolt upright on their haunches, and for two to four seconds, produce a sound so high, so thin, so excruciatingly *present* it feels like a needle being driven through the dense fabric of lizard-bellow and millipede-grind. It is not a cry of pain. It is a declaration. *I am here. This mound is mine. The egg beneath me is real.* It slices through the low frequencies, a treble clef written on the air, hundreds of them stitching the basin’s soundscape together into a tapestry of pure, overwhelming assertion. They conserve everything but noise.

**The Web-Weavers.** In the corners of rooms, in the angles where wall meets ground, span gossamer structures of incredible tensile strength. The *dust-spinners*. They are arachnid in form, but their silk is not sticky. It is *dry*, and it *vibrates*. Each strand is tuned to a different frequency of the basin’s constant cacophony. The spider, a pale, minute thing at the web’s center, feels the vibrations of a screamer’s call, the tremor of a chamber-lord’s bellow, the grinding hum of a passing millipede procession. It does not eat sound. It eats the tiny dust-mites and wind-blown organic flecks that resonate with and get trapped in this symphony of vibration. Its web is a filter, a sensory organ stretched across the ruin, listening to the noise and dining on its particulate echoes.

**The Frond-Dwellers.** The great pale plants are not merely scenery. In their stiff, wax-sealed crowns live the *ivory mantises*. Perfectly camouflaged, motionless for days, they are ambush predators of the small lizards and flying things that aren’t insects but something like them—hard-shelled, buzzing flyers that navigate the amber light. The mantises are statues of calcium, striking with a speed that seems impossible in this slow world, their raptorial legs moving like released springs.

What lives here, finally, is a paradox. A closed system of breathtaking, noisy, crowded life built upon a foundation of absolute, silent, geological surrender. The inhabitants do not know they live in a ghost. They know only the wall, the crack, the bellow, the scream, the slow, grinding process of eating and being eaten in a city that is always, eternally, sinking back into the white powder from which it was cut. Their noise is the antithesis of the basin’s patient, flattening destiny—a vibrant, desperate, and utterly magnificent rebellion in the face of a silence that has already won.
