The residents of the Nacre Caverns are not visitors. They are the architecture, the chemistry, the slow, silent breath of the place itself. They do not merely inhabit the chambers; they *are* the chambers, their lives indistinguishable from the stone they dissolve and rebuild. Here is who and what lives there:

**The Foundation: The Unseen Multitude.** Before any visible creature, there is the bacterial mat. It is not a passive carpet, but a living, breathing, digesting super-organism. Billions upon billions of single-celled architects, divided into guilds by their chemistry:
*   **The Sulfide-Weavers (Beggiatoa, Arcobacter):** They consume the hydrogen sulfide seeping from the Abyss, exhaling sulfate and faint, blue-green light. Their collective glow is the cavern’s primordial dawn, a whisper-light sufficient for things without eyes. They are the farmers, converting geology into sustenance.
*   **The Stone-Eaters:** These bacteria don’t just coat the limestone; they secrete acids that dissolve it, molecule by molecule. They are the cave’s excavators, its sculptors, its digestive system. The Vestibule’s delicate draperies and the Nave’s soaring terraces are their ongoing, unintentional masterwork.
*   **The Dark-Fermenters (Sulfate-Reducers):** In the Abyss’s deepest black, another guild thrives, consuming organic matter and producing the hydrogen sulfide that rises like a life-giving vapour to fuel the world above. They close the loop, the silent alchemists of the deep.

**The First Citizens: The Tube-Worm Spires.** The *Lamellibrachia*-kin are the metropolis’s skyscrapers and its citizens combined. Each chitinous tube is a home, a fortress, and a farm. Rooted in the sulfide-rich mat, they host symbiotic bacteria within their tissues, trading shelter for the sugars these microbes forge from chemicals and carbon dioxide. They grow so slowly that a tower half a meter tall may have witnessed millennia. They do not die quickly; their empty tubes become foundations, skyscrapers built upon skyscrapers, creating a necropolis that is also a nursery. They are the reason for the lanes, the shadows, the vertical complexity. They are the landlords.

**The Lane-Dwellers: The Slow Neighborhoods.**
*   **The Grazers:** Translucent polychaetes, some mere threads, others the thickness of a thumb, glide through the bacterial pastures, their bristles leaving fleeting signatures in the mat. Herds of amber-shelled gastropods, their silica-chitin lanterns clinging to the towers, rasp the microbial film with patient, perpetual hunger. They are the gardeners, tending the bacterial lawn, ensuring it never grows too thick or stagnant.
*   **The Drifters:** Clouds of glass-shelled amphipods, the size of rice grains, orbit in the water column. They are the filter-feeders, the cleanup crew, combing the water for the gentle snow of dead bacteria and detritus. Their swarms pulse and shift, a silent, silver weather system.
*   **The Draped Hunters:** Ribbon worms, nemerteans longer than a human arm, lie coiled over the spire tops like predatory garlands. They sense their prey—slow, bristled polychaetes—through the chemistry of the water. A hunt is a marathon of embrace: a muscular coil, the everted proboscis, a feast measured in hours. They are the patient check on the grazers, the embodiment of unhurried necessity.
*   **The Janitors:** Heavily armored isopods patrol the mat floors with dogged determination. They are the recyclers, finding the fallen—the amphipod that simply stopped, the remains of a nemertean’s long lunch, a piece of shed chitin—and dismantling it, returning its atoms to the communal mat. Nothing is wasted in this economy of scarcity.

**The Nave’s Singular Inhabitants: Specialized and Territorial.**
Here, density breeds specialization.
*   **The Amber Herd:** A unique gastropod, its shell a marvel of bio-engineered glass and chitin, grazes in tighter formations. On a leaning tower, a cluster of them truly resembles dim, golden lanterns hung for some silent festival.
*   **The Ceiling Bloom:** Sessile cnidarians, like pale, feathery anemones, have colonized the highest points of the spires. They extend their nets not with aggression, but with sublime patience, filtering the amphipod clouds. A meal is a rare, drifting gift. They are monuments to waiting.
*   **The Chemoreceptive Sovereigns:** Six. Perhaps only six of these large, eyeless, predatory polychaetes rule the Nave. They move with an eerie prescience, following invisible scent trails through the labyrinth. Their territories are not marked by violence, but by slow-release pheromones, chemical borders as immutable as the stone walls. They are the apex, the only thing here that inspires anything resembling caution in other creatures. Their reign is one of absolute, quiet certainty.

**The Abyssal Elders: The Glass Lattice.**
In the deepest chamber, time itself seems to solidify. The *Hexactinellida* descendants are not so much organisms as slow-growing, living mineral formations. Their bodies are intricate lattices of silica, fused into colonies that carpet the floor like frozen, three-dimensional lace. They filter the dense, ancient water for the smallest particles of life. A single sponge might be older than the stalactites in the Vestibule. They are the ultimate expression of the cavern’s principle: life as geology. Between them, the nemertean that hunts here is a whisper, a black thread moving with exquisite care through the glass forest, an evolutionary lesson in not breaking what you cannot repair.

**The Atmosphere: The Collective Unconscious.**
The caverns breathe. The convection from the warm basalt at the very bottom creates a breath so slow it takes years to circulate. The sound is not silence, but the sum of infinitesimal processes: the *click-click* of amphipod mandibles, the sub-audible strain of glass spicules adjusting, the immense, collective hum of metabolism. Light is a communal secret, shared only in the Vestibule and Nave, a faint, bioluminescent consensus that this is enough.

They are a city without citizens, a society without selves. They are a chemical conversation between rock and water, written in the languages of sulfur, silica, and slow, patient time. They are the Nacre Caverns, and they are utterly, magnificently, alive.
