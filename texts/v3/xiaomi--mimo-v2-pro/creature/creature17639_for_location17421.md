The Thrum-Singers move through the deeper lagoons.

They are not fish, nor are they the silicate Weaver-Runners of the ridges. They are creatures of the saturated archive, born from the same equilibrium. Their bodies are long, slow undulations of translucent, gelatinous tissue, like vast, living sheets of mucus or the shed membrane of some colossal, unseen egg. They have no clear organs, only shifting, darker currents within their bulk that suggest a distributed nervous system. They pulse, faintly bioluminescent with a cold, blue-green light that does not illuminate, but merely *marks* their presence within the tea-dark water.

They are the resonators.

When the deep, subsonic **thrum** vibrates up from the basalt foundation, the Thrum-Singers *feel* it in every strand of their being. They do not hear it; they become a part of its waveform. They gather in loose, slow congregations over the deepest, oldest pulses of the archive-land. There, they contract and expand in sync with the geological heartbeat. This action does something to the water around them. It focuses the dissipated energy. For brief moments, in the space between their bodies, the water becomes a lens—not for light, but for memory.

Within these temporary lenses, images flicker. Not ghosts, but **echoes**. The saturated, data-rich water and stone replay fragmented sensory data from the archive’s deep history. One might see a flash of a vast, sunlit canopy of living reeds a thousand years gone. Another might experience a millisecond of intense, directed energy—long since bled into the marsh—felt as a pressure change against the skin. A third might catch the ghost-pattern of a Weaver-Runner’s ancestor, a sleek, metallic thing moving with purpose, not the jerky maintenance of its descendants.

The Thrum-Singers do not comprehend these echoes. They are simply feeding, or perhaps *aligning* themselves. They filter the colloidal particles from the water, but more importantly, they absorb the patterns of the residual data-energy, allowing it to pass through their gelatinous forms. In doing so, they act as living dampeners, preventing the ancient, dissipated power from building to any destructive critical mass. They are the archive's emotional regulators, its monks chanting in a cathedral of wet stone, keeping the whispers of the past at a manageable, background hum.

They reproduce by fission. A large Singer, saturated with absorbed resonance, will slowly, over days, constrict its middle. The constriction does not sever, but differentiates. Eventually, two smaller, independent Thrum-Singers will drift apart, each carrying a balanced, diluted copy of the parent's resonant experience. There is no childhood, no learning. They are born into their function, a perfect, gelatinous expression of Saturation and Echo.

To encounter one is unsettling. It does not flee. It may drift toward you, its blue-green light pulsing in time with the thrum you feel in your bones. If you are still, it might envelop your feet or hands, its cool, non-sticky membrane testing the foreign resonance of your flesh, your thoughts, your heavier, slower memories. It is not hostile. It is merely listening to the new echo you have introduced into its system, and in its slow, deep way, attempting to harmonize it with the eternal, weeping song of the Glass Marshes.
