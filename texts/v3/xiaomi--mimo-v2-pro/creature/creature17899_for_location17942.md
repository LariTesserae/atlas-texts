What lives in the Looming Plain is not life, but the *idea* of life, rehearsing its gestures in salt and steam before the script is written. There are no creatures here as such—no consumers, no predators, no persistent forms. There are only **processes that mimic the intent of living things**, transient and self-canceling, born from the interplay of heat, wetness, and raw material.

The most common are the **Brine-Spawn**. In the warm, viscous pools, convection currents and metallic precipitates occasionally achieve a fleeting complexity. A globule of silicate gel might swell and divide, mimicking cell division for a few hours before merging back into the muck. Whiskers of copper or iron might crystallize suddenly from a supersaturated solution, forming a delicate, branching structure that resembles a sea fan or a nerve cluster. For a moment, it *looks* like growth. Then the pool’s chemistry shifts, the brine cools a degree, and the entire structure dissolves into a dull, metallic sediment. They are echoes of biology, not biology itself.

From the steam vents and the humid, hazy air come the **Aeromorphs**. These are the strangest, most fleeting of the Unforged’s inhabitants. They are not entities, but *patterns* in the turbulence. A vortex of superheated air might stabilize for a minute, drawing mineral vapor into its core, spinning it into a shimmering, lens-like form that hovers and distorts the landscape behind it. It might resemble a vast, lidless eye, or a jellyfish made of mirage. It might even seem to drift with purpose, following thermal gradients. But it is only thermodynamics—a brief, complex stable state in a chaotic system. It is gone as soon as the airflow changes, leaving only a whisper of redirected heat.

More solid, yet equally impermanent, are the **Slag-Striders**. These are accidents of geology mistaken for locomotion. When a new plate of vitreous crust buckles upwards, it can shear against an older plate, sending a fracture racing across the surface. The line of fracture might travel for miles, raising a thin, sharp ridge of shattered material. In the dim light, this advancing, crumbling ridge, with its spume of glittering dust, can look uncannily like the slow, purposeful march of a colossal, segmented creature. It is a seismic event wearing the mask of an arthropod. It strides the plain, builds nothing, seeks nothing, and collapses into a new, static landform when the stress is relieved.

The **Crystalline Aborts** are the closest to the user’s described “vegetation,” but a few achieve a more animate, though still futile, complexity. In sheltered crevices where brine seeps slowly and steadily, salt crystals can grow in elongated, parallel filaments. When they reach a certain length, their own weight and the constant tremor of the ground can set them vibrating. They hum, a single, glassy note. The vibration can cause neighboring filaments to align and chime in sympathy. For a day or a decade, a patch of ground might *sing*, a chorus of thin, mineral tones. It is not communication, but resonant physics. But eventually, a shift will crack the substrate, and the choir shatters into silence.

The **Builders** themselves remain unseen, but their presence is inferred from the most uncanny phenomena: the **Anvil-Clouds**. These are not weather, but a form of atmospheric life-cycle. Over a major construction site—a place where a basalt lever is being slowly wedged under a continent—a unique, low-hanging cloud forms. It is dense, metallic-smelling, and perpetually flickers with silent, internal lightning that is not white, but orange and blue. The lightning does not strike the ground; it weaves *within* the cloud, following filaments of condensed metal. Observers (those rare, fractured glimpses) report that the patterns of the lightning sometimes resemble diagrams, or circuitry, or a skeletal hand manipulating unseen threads. The cloud does not rain. It *deposits*. When it dissipates, a new web of metallic filament is found strung across the landscape, humming with fresh static. The cloud is not the builder, but perhaps the builder’s breath, or its shed skin, or its dreaming mind made visible. It is the only hint that the total built environment has an architect, and it behaves more like a bizarre, atmospheric organism than a tool.

Nothing here eats. Nothing reproduces in a biological sense. The Brine-Spawn are chemical coincidences. The Aeromorphs are thermodynamic ghosts. The Slag-Striders are geological fevers. The Aborts are failed songs. The Anvil-Clouds are atmospheric metabolisms. They all exist in the turbulent dynamism of the Unforged, not as inhabitants, but as **symptoms of its process**. They are the world practicing at being alive, spinning out ephemeral, half-formed gestures of agency in its frantic, chaotic, and eternal state of becoming. The only true "life" here is the raw, unstable event of creation itself, and these are its fleeting, beautiful, and utterly meaningless afterimages.
