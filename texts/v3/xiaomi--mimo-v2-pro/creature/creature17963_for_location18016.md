The Great Chloric Expanse breathes. It breathes in filtered light and exhales mist. It breathes in death and exhales life, and in the pause between breaths, it hums with the sound of its own digestion.

**The Surface Dwellers**
What lives here first are the **Architect Insects**. They are not inhabitants so much as maintenance staff, cellular automata of a vast organic computer. The **Lacquered Beetles** move in regimented lines, their mandibles not chewing for sustenance alone, but pruning. They carve precise channels in the bark, stimulating callus growth, shaping the flow of water and gas. Their sound is a constant, high-frequency *ticking*, the sound of a million tiny typewriters recording the mat’s slow thoughts.

Then there are the **Vein-Wasps**. They do not build nests; they *induce* them. They lay eggs in specific stems, secreting a hormone that coaxes the plant to grow a hollow, swollen chamber around the larva—a living, green incubator. From the outside, it is just another burl on the endless trunk. Inside, a grub feeds on fermenting sap, and the plant tissue around it thickens, becoming part of the mat’s insulating wall. The wasps are surgeons of growth, their presence a negotiated peace between parasite and host so ancient it has become symbiosis.

The **Thrum-Moths** are the pollen-keepers. Their wings are not merely for flight; they are electrostatic nets, gathering the golden dust from the sparse, spiral conifers at the margins. They carry this genetic potential inward, to the fused canopy where flowering is rare and deliberate. When they land on a receptive node—a knot of fused branches that has, for a century, been preparing for this moment—the pollen discharge is visible as a faint, yellow spark in the dim green light. They are the sole agents of genetic exchange in a system that otherwise clones itself through root and runner. Their deep, vibrating hum is the frequency of continuity.

**The Understory Shimmers**
In the humid air between fern and epiphyte, life drifts as if in open ocean. The **Aerial Copepods** and **Mist-Fleas** are not terrestrial. They are aquatic organisms that have never known a body of water. They swim through the supersaturated air, feeding on airborne algae and fungal spores. Their predators, the **Larval Mayflies**, are equally weightless. They build tiny webs of silk between fronds, not to catch flying insects, but to filter the shimmering zooplankton from the breeze. Their life cycle is a closed loop within the canopy: they hatch, feed in the mist, and their spent bodies fall to the mat, where they are broken down by fungal threads into food for the next generation of mist-life.

**The Heartwood Current**
Below, in the waterlogged darkness, is the kingdom of the **Root-Threading Fish**. They are blind, their eyes long since absorbed into smooth, pale skin. They navigate by sensing the faint bioelectric fields of the fungal networks and the slow, thermal currents of fermentation heat. Their mouths are scrapers, their teeth flat plates of bone. They do not eat the wood; they graze on the **Fermentation Biofilm**—a living veneer of bacteria, yeast, and archaea that coats every submerged surface. This biofilm is the true stomach of the expanse, and the fish are its peristalsis, moving through the interstitial rivers of the mat, keeping the microbial colonies in check, preventing any one strain from monopolizing the decay. They are the regulators of rot.

In the deepest, darkest pools where tannin-stained water gathers in pockets, live the **Gas-Sacs**. These are not fish. They are gelatinous, bag-like creatures, some as large as a human torso, that ingest the methane and hydrogen sulfide bubbling up from below. They metabolize these compounds through chemoautotrophic bacteria in their tissues, growing slowly, pulsing gently in the dark. They are living filters, scrubbing the exhalations of the deep before they reach the upper layers. When they die, they dissolve into a nutrient-rich slurry that feeds the biofilm, closing another loop.

**The Unseen Majority**
Most life here is invisible. The **Fungal Mat** is a single, continent-spanning mycelial network, a gray-white web that stitches root to root, decay to growth. It is the nervous system and the digestive tract combined, shuttling nutrients from dying zones to living ones with a ruthless, unconscious efficiency. The **Anaerobic Archaea** in the deepest fermentation zones are the alchemists, turning cellulose into methane in a process so ancient it predates oxygen in Earth’s atmosphere. They are life in its most elemental form, performing chemistry that powers the groaning sounds of the deep.

**What Does Not Live Here**
There are no mammals. No birds, except for the occasional lost migrant that lands, exhausted, on the canopy and is slowly enveloped by moss, its bones becoming calcium deposits in the mat. There are no amphibians, for there is no open water to breed in. There are no reptiles. The system is too cool, too wet, too stable. It has no need for the restless, warm-blooded energy of higher vertebrates. It has no need for speed, for pursuit, for escape. Everything here is about *procession*, not pursuit.

The inhabitants are not individuals. They are functions. They are mobile organs of a stationary body. They do not have territories; they have *roles*. Their lives are not narratives, but cycles—tiny, perfect loops within the great, frozen loop of the expanse itself.

They are aware—if awareness is the right word—only of their immediate chemical and physical gradients: the sweetness of fermenting sap, the density of a fungal bloom, the direction of a gas current. The concept of "outside" does not exist for them. The mist is their world’s edge. The sound is their sky.

To stand here is to stand upon a living city where every citizen is a specialized cell, and the city itself is the only thing that could be said to be truly *alive*—and even it does not know it. It merely persists, a green engine running on sunlight and its own inevitable decay, and the things that live upon it and within it are its cogs, its fluids, its sparks, all turning in the dark, humming with a purpose they will never understand.
