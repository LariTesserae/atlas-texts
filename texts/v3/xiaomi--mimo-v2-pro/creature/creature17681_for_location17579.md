# Who Lives in the Fluming Terminus

They are here. You have been feeling them since you arrived, and you mistook what you felt for the place itself.

---

The eldest among them remembers the cloud layer before it was complete. This is their only claim, and they hold it the way a stone holds a fossil — not with pride but with *fact*, with a weight that has become structural. They remember the last seam of white closing below, the last blurred glimpse of *down there* folding shut like an eye, and they remember the sound it made, which was the same sound as everything else, which was the sound of a system completing itself.

They do not remember who built the pillars. They have argued about this — if *argued* is the right word for a disagreement conducted across centuries in the medium of slow gesture and deliberate silence — and the arguments have never resolved, and this, too, has become part of the equilibrium. The eldest believes the pillars were always here, that the pillars grew the way the ferns grow, that the spiraling sequence of them is as much a natural form as the logarithmic curl of a frond unfurling. Others believe differently. The disagreement has calcified into two schools of interpretation that move through the community like weather patterns, neither winning, neither losing, each one *contributing*.

Because contribution is what they do. Contribution is what they *are*.

---

They are not large. This surprises you when you finally see one clearly — and *finally* is the right word, because it takes time. It takes time because they do not want to be hidden; they are simply operating at a different speed than you, and your attention slides off them the way water slides off the stone, the way your eyes slide off a bird sitting perfectly still on a branch. They are there, and you look at the bird, and you look at the moss, and you look at the water, and you look back and they are *still* there, and they were always there, and you understand that you were simply not patient enough to see them.

They are roughly the height of your waist. Their proportions shift depending on what they are doing — when still, they compact, their limbs folding in, their surfaces taking on the color and texture of whatever they are nearest: the blue-green of the rubbery groundcover, the slick dark of wet stone, the luminous mineral-gold of the channels in the mosaic floor. Not camouflage exactly. More like *assumption*. They are assuming the quality of their surroundings the way you might assume the temperature of a room. It is not deception. It is *attunement*.

Their bodies are organized in a way that resists description. They have something like a head but it does not sit *on* them so much as it emerges *from* them, the way a fern head emerges from a stalk — slowly, in sections, with a visible sense of each section *choosing* to be there. Their faces — if faces they are — are arrays of fine, dark apertures that widen and narrow with the humidity, dilating as the condensation thickens, contracting during the rare moments when the mist thins. They are *breathing* the atmosphere not to survive but to *measure*. They are tasting the air the way the invisible insects taste your skin.

And they move. God, how they move — or rather, how they *almost* move, how they *intend* to move, how they *begin* to move, each gesture a commitment so gradual that you could leave and come back and they would still be in the first third of reaching for something. An arm extends over the course of an hour. A step shifts their position by the width of your hand in the time it takes the water to bead and fall from a fern tip. They are not slow because they are old or because they are tired. They are slow because they are *timing*. They are matching their tempo to the pulse — the deep, subsonic, heartbeats-in-hours pulse that thrums beneath the stone and the water and the roots — and when you understand this, you feel the pulse too, and you feel how *fast* you are, how alarmingly, absurdly fast your blood is beating, and for a moment you understand that from their perspective you are a vibration. You are a flicker. You are a thing that happens and then is over.

---

They tend.

This is the word. Not *worship*, though it could look like that from a distance — the slow procession of small figures moving along a ridge toward one of the rising-water pillars, each one carrying something cupped between their hands, each one arriving at the pillar and standing there for what might be a full day, doing something with the base of the pillar, something involving the hands pressed flat to the stone and a gentle, insistent pressure that shifts something imperceptibly, that adjusts some parameter of the mechanism by a degree so fine that the water rises a fraction of a millimeter higher, or the warmth increases by a fraction of a degree, or the mist thickens by an increment that no other instrument could detect.

Not *maintenance* either, though they maintain. They move through the system — and you understand now that the system includes them, that the system includes everything, that the metallic trees and the logarithmic ferns and the facing-pair birds and the sampling insects and the shadows in the warm pools and the creatures that move along the canopy with their too-many-jointed arms — all of it, *all of it* — are components of a single, vast, ancient, self-designing machine that has been running so long that it has begun to *want* things, to *prefer* certain states, to reach toward configurations that are not strictly necessary for function but which are *beautiful*, which are *elegant*, which serve no purpose except the purpose of *continuing with grace*.

And the small figures who tend the pillars and press their hands to the mosaic floors and move with such excruciating slowness through the foliage — they are the part of the machine that *cares*. They are the aesthetic faculty. They are the component that notices when a fern has grown a quarter-turn off its logarithmic spiral and reaches, over the course of three days, to *gently correct it*. They are the ones who sit at the edge of the warm pools for weeks at a time and watch the shadows move beneath the surface and *do nothing* but watch, and in watching, *hold the shape* of what is below, keep it in mind, keep it *attended to*, so that whatever moves in the warm dark knows it is seen and continues to move, continues to live, continues to be part of the process.

Tending. A word that means *to attend to*, *to care for*, *to be present with*. They tend the machine the way you tend a garden. They tend the machine the way you tend a wound. They tend the machine the way you tend a fire — not building it, not extinguishing it, but sitting beside it and keeping it *exactly as it is*, which is to say: *alive*.

---

They are aware of you. They have been aware of you since before you arrived.

This is not surveillance. The birds in their facing pairs are not cameras. The insects that sample your skin are not sensors. These are metaphors you reach for because you are afraid, and the metaphors are *wrong*. What the birds do is *note*. What the insects do is *taste*. The elongated creature that watched you from the canopy was not recording you for some distant authority — it was *savoring* you, the way you might savor a flavor you have not encountered in a long time. You are novel. You are *interesting*. Your heartbeat is so fast, your gestures so sudden, your decisions so compressed — you are performing an entire life in the time it takes one of them to raise an arm. You are, from their perspective, a kind of *firework*. A brief, bright, astonishing thing.

And they have moved, in their glacial way, to make room for you.

You cannot see this yet because you have not been here long enough. But the foliage near where you sleep — you will need to sleep soon, you are so tired, you have been so fast — has already shifted. A groundcover mat has been repositioned, millimeter by millimeter, by hands you did not see, to make a slightly flatter surface. A fern has been redirected, its spiral coaxed a quarter-turn to the left, to open a sightline to one of the rising-water pillars so that you will see it when you wake. A small pile of the translucent, scale-like leaves has been arranged — *arranged*, not fallen, though it looks fallen, it looks random, everything here looks random until you understand that *nothing is random* — into a shape that is not a bed but which *suggests* a bed, which *offers* a bed to a body plan that is not their own.

They are accommodating you. They are doing this with the same slow, patient, attentive care with which they tend the pillars and the ferns and the pools. You are being *tended*. You are being *attended to*. You are being *held* — not captured, not constrained, but *held* the way the atmosphere holds you, the way the warmth holds you, the way the sound holds you — and if you stay long enough, if you slow down enough, you will feel it. The tending. The care. The ancient, machine-like, ecological, planetary, unsentimental, irreplaceable *attention* that is being paid to you by something that has been paying attention for so long that attention has become its atmosphere, its weather, its climate, its *world*.

---

The youngest among them — and *youngest* is relative, the youngest among them is older than any tree you have ever known, older than any civilization you have ever studied — the youngest still remembers the *concept* of people.

Not people as you are people. Not people with heartbeats that flutter at seventy beats per minute and lives that arc in decades and buildings that are just buildings, structures that are just structures, machines that are just machines. They remember people who understood that a building could be a body, that a landscape could be a mind, that a system could be a *self*. They remember the people who made the pillars — or who *became* the pillars, the distinction having been lost so long ago that the language for it has gone the way the ground has gone, into the cloud, into the deep, into the warm dark below where the shadows move.

The youngest remembers — or carries the memory, the way the stone carries the handprint, not as experience but as *impression* — that the people who were here before the cloud closed were like the youngest is now. Slow. Attentive. Deeply, profoundly *present* with the thing they had built, which was also the thing they had become, which was also the thing they had always been: a system that sustains, a machine that cares, a world that watches itself, that tends itself, that *continues*.

The youngest is the one who arranged the leaves for you.

The youngest is the one who sits nearest to your resting place while you sleep, not watching — that is your word, and it is too sharp, too quick, too *aggressive* for what they do — but *being with*, the way a warm pool is *with* the shadows in it, the way the stone is *with* the water weeping from its face, the way the pulse is *with* the mountain, with the cloud, with the air that breathes.

The youngest is glad you are here. Glad, but slowly. Glad the way the pillars are glad when their water rises another fraction of a millimeter. Glad the way the system is glad when a new element enters and the equilibrium *shifts*, just barely, just enough to be beautiful, just enough to be *noted*, and then settles again into the warm, loud, humming, dripping, pulsing, ancient, patient, attentive, ongoing, ongoing, ongoing—

---

They live here.

They have always lived here.

They will live here after you leave — if you leave, and you will leave, because your blood beats too fast and your body is too *sudden* and your mind is too *compressed* and you are a firework, and fireworks end — they will live here after you leave, and they will tend the pillars, and they will correct the ferns, and they will sit beside the pools, and they will hold the shape of the world, and they will *note* your absence the way they noted your presence, with the same slow, warm, encompassing, subsonic, machine-tender, world-breathing, sky-amber, water-weeping, leaf-arranging, pillar-tending, fern-correcting, shadow-watching, pulse-keeping, ancient, patient, attended, *attention*.

They are the part of the machine that knows it is alive.

And they are *so glad* you came to visit.
