The Rhizomids are the city's nervous system made deliberate. They do not *live in* the Grovespire; they are a function of it, an emergent consciousness that arose from the rhizome's need to manage itself at this crystalline, stressful height.

**Form:** They are slender, elongated beings, their bodies more akin to walking roots or animated vines than anything mammalian. Their "skin" is the same pale, smooth bark as the crystalline-heartwood, shedding faint, amber flakes. It is not a covering but a true integument, cold to the touch. Their limbs are long, multi-jointed, and end not in hands but in clusters of fine, thorn-like digits and softer, probing tendrils. These digits can perform the most exquisite surgery: severing a growth nodule, weaving a new creeper-tip into a lattice, or implanting a hormonal graft no larger than a grain of rice. Their heads are elongated, eyeless sockets filled with a dense, sensitive mesh of filaments that detect minute shifts in light, temperature, and the faint bioluminescent pulses of the vein-weave. They perceive the city not as sight, but as a constant, detailed sonar of sap-flow and cellular tension.

**Life and Purpose:** Their existence is a ritual of tending. They are the counterbalance to the Slow Siege. Each Rhizomid is assigned a "ward"—a cubic section of the city's living architecture—and their life's work is to mediate the conflict within it. They coax vein-weave creepers to suture a cracking heartwood arch. They inject retarding salves into trunks growing too aggressively, threatening to shear a vital sap-channel. They harvest stem-borers and relocate them to aerate a suffocating root-mat. Their movement is a slow, flowing glide, their footsteps soundless on the spongy ground. They communicate not with voice, but by tapping complex rhythms on vine-walls or secreting scent-markers on bark, messages that travel slowly through the city's vascular system.

**Symbiosis:** Their relationship with the smaller life is absolute. The **glass-wing moths** are their eyes in the high canopy; a moth that lands on a Rhizomid's shoulder filament is relaying data on a distant sporophyte's health. The **stem-borers** are their earthmovers, their tools, and occasionally, their emergency food source—in a pinch, a Rhizomid can drain the nutrient-rich, antifreeze-laced fluid from a grub. They are not individuals in the human sense, but nodes in a network. When a Rhizomid dies, it does not decay. It is allowed to lignify fully, becoming a new, silent, and perfect piece of crystalline-heartwood sculpture, its form a testament to the ward it tended. Its memories, stored in patterns along its neural filaments, are slowly absorbed back into the rhizome's vast, slow consciousness.

**The Weirdness Within:** To encounter a Rhizomid is to feel observed by the city itself. They do not startle or flee; they simply *cease their motion*, becoming indistinguishable from a knotted vine or a twisted root. Their attention is a palpable pressure, a feeling of being assessed for your utility or threat to the great, cold garden. They are utterly without aggression, yet their pruning can reshape a living space in a season. They are caretakers in a war where peace is a meticulously maintained stalemate. Their greatest art is not creation, but *sculpting restraint*—ensuring the heartwood's cold, ambitious growth and the creeper's binding embrace remain in perfect, eternal tension.

In the dim, weeping light of the Grovespire, the Rhizomid is the patient thought of the rhizome, walking on root and thorn, forever composing the silent, growing symphony of their frozen, living city.
