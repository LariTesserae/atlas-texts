What lives here is not a collection of separate beings, but a single, slow-breathing entity with many organs. The primary inhabitant, the true resident, is the **Mat**. It is not merely a place where things live; it is the thing that lives, a kilometers-wide, centuries-deep organism of compressed history. Within its layered body, a civilization of microorganisms thrives—a silent, digestive kingdom that processes the endless fall of dew and dead matter into the substance of the Mat itself. They are the unseen engine, the patient chemists of the plateau.

Upon and within this living foundation, the visible life arranges itself according to the **Rules of Scale and Slowness**.

**The Architects** are the **Runner-Stems**. They are the plateau’s circulatory and nervous systems, woody veins that creep with imperceptible slowness, binding the Mat, transmitting vibration and moisture, and making new growth possible. They are the first true multi-celled colonists, and without their binding work, the plateau would dissolve into the abyss.

**The Elders** are the **Tower Trees**. They are not forests, but solitary, swollen monuments. Their lives are measured in millennia, their girth a testament to persistence. They are less individual trees and more **living ecosystems**—their furrowed bark is a cliff-face habitat, their root systems are cities for fungi, their canopies create continents of still, damp air. They are the plateau’s cathedrals, and beneath them, the Mat transforms into a richer, darker realm of accelerated decay and rebirth, a localized pulse in the general slow-motion metabolism.

**The Chorus** is the most populous, yet most elusive life. It is not a single species, but a **soundscape made flesh**.
*   **The Hum** is the aggregate voice of the **Mat-Insects**. Billions of stridulating, burrowing, vibrating things living in the upper layers, their collective drone is the plateau’s baseline vital sign. They communicate through the runners, tapping rhythms into the wood-fiber, their language one of pure vibration.
*   **The Callers** are the **Bird-Things**. Rare, solitary, and hauntingly vocal, they are the soloists in the roar. Their songs, evolved to carry for leagues in the thin air, are intricate and piercing, weaving high, clear melodies through the dense drone of the insects. They hunt the Mat’s surface, diving for the **Grazer-Scrappers** or the larger insects, their presence announced by sound long before sight.
*   **The Pulse-Makers** are the **Grazer-Scrappers**. Six-limbed, low-slung, and covered in root-like pelt, they are the plateau’s gentle harvesters. They move in lonely pairs, their broad mouths scraping the Mat’s living surface. They speak to each other in **infrasonic pulses** that travel through the Mat itself, a private language felt in the bones of the ground. Their trails become temporary waterways, a fleeting gift of geography to the ecosystem.

**The Shadows** are the **Predators**. Seldom seen, often inferred from the torn patches of Mat and scattered bones. They are fast, fluid, and long-necked, moving through the grass-analogues like a whisper. They are the necessary counterpoint to the Scrappers, the force that culls and directs, ensuring the Mat is not over-grazed. They are the plateau’s kept secret.

**The Unseen** are the **Burrowers**. A world of things that never see the sky, living their entire lives within the Mat’s body. They create the wax-lined tunnels that crisscross the substrate, aerating and turning the organic mass. You hear their faint movements beneath your feet—the sound of the Mat digesting itself.

**The Question-Marks** are the **Night-Watchers**. Things of ambiguous form that cling to the undersides of the Tower Trees when the light fails. They are not mammal, bird, or insect. They observe. They are part of the plateau’s twilight consciousness, a form of awareness that has found its niche in the canopy’s shadows, silent amidst the roar.

And then, there are the **Ghosts**. The **Builders**. Their legacy is a handful of low, dry-stone enclosures on the thin-soiled rises. The stone is local, the craftsmanship simple but precise, describing geometries that feel alien. They left behind a water-bowl that fits a human hand, a braided cord, a scratched stone. They did not build a civilization; they **curated a stay**. They protected certain tree clusters, they settled where water was most rhythmic. They were visitors, or perhaps one phase of a longer, slower inhabitation. They are the only hint that something from *outside*, with intent and a sense of form, once stood upon the Mat and decided to arrange a few stones. They are gone now, fully absorbed into the plateau’s patient timeline. Whether they were human, or something else shaped by this altitude and this sound, is a secret the stone keeps.

What lives here, ultimately, is **Relationship**. The sound is not a byproduct of life; it is the medium of exchange. The grazers’ subsonic pulses, the insects’ runner-taps, the birds’ far-carrying cries—these are the plateau’s language. The Mat holds the water that feeds the trees that shade the Mat that hosts the insects that feed the birds that call to each other across the emptiness, their songs riding on the wind that also makes the runners sing.

It is a biome of vast emptiness and staggering density, where individual organisms are rare but the **system** is overwhelmingly alive. It is a place that has outgrown the need for multitude, favoring instead a few, highly-specialized, and ancient forms of life, all harmonizing—sometimes discordantly, always loudly—within the immense, patient, living body of the Mat. You are not an observer here. You are, for a brief time, one more vibration in the chord.
