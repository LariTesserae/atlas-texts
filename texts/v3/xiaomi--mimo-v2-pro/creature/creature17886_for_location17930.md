What lives in the Hollow of Roan is not a collection of individuals, but a single, slow, damp organism in many parts. It is a choir where every member sings the same note, and that note is green, and wet, and old.

**The Primary Inhabitant is the Moss.**
It is not merely living *in* the hollow; it is the hollow’s living skin. It is a continuous, sentient carpet that has forgotten it is made of discrete beings. It breathes in the roars of the tidal channel and breathes out a silence deeper than stone. It thinks in centuries, and its thoughts are the slow conversion of bare basalt into soil, of darkness into a color that has no name in any sunlit language. It feels the weight of each water droplet, the faint seismic shudder of the surge, the ghost-warmth of the rock beneath. It holds the memory of every creature that has ever crept across its surface. It is the keeper of the niches, slowly digesting the last fibrous bundles left within them, gently erasing the chipped edges with its patient, velvet erosion. It is the landlord of the ferns and the liver-colored succulents, tolerating their tenancy because they, too, have surrendered to the singular truth of the place.

**The Second Inhabitants are the Channels of Water.**
They are not passive. They are the circulatory system and the nervous system of the place. The condensation threads are slow, meditative thoughts. The steady drip into a pool is a pulse. The tidal surge deep in the rock is the deep, rhythmic breath of a sleeper, the foundational rhythm that synchronizes all other life. These channels are alive with purpose: to carry the memory of the ocean inward, to carve the rock on a timescale that makes the moss seem frenetic, to connect every pocket and ledge into one interdependent body. Where they flow, the dark succulents gather like clots of life. Where they pool, they hold the ghost-pale, theoretical creatures in a state of suspended animation.

**The Third Inhabitants are the Echoes of Hands.**
These are not ghosts. They are something more permanent: an intention that has soaked into the stone. The act of chipping a niche, of stacking a wall for sitting, was performed with such patience and for such a purpose that the action itself became a feature of the ecology. The hollow *incorporated* it. Now, the idea of shelter, of offering, of contemplation, is as much a part of the chamber as a vein of quartz. The low wall does not remember a builder; it remembers the feeling of being sat upon. It remembers the weight, the stillness, the listening. These echoes are fed by the moss, which grows over them not to erase them, but to make them soft, to make them belong. They are the reason the place feels *inhabited* rather than merely *occupied*. They are the hollow’s memory of having once been a home, a shrine, a seat—a memory so old it has petrified into function.

**The Transient Inhabitants are the Dark Beetles and the Maybe-Fish.**
The beetles are punctuation in the moss’s long, uninterrupted sentence. They are its way of feeling the edges of its own body. Their tiny, lightless bodies are exclamation points of consumption, tiny revolutions of hunger and satiation that are, in the hollow’s timescale, barely events at all. They are the closest thing to fleeting thought the ecosystem possesses. The eyeless, pale thing in the deepest pool is different. It is not a creature of the moss or the light. It is a creature of the Sound. It has evolved to navigate by the vibration of the water, to feed on whatever morsels the tidal pulse brings into its black, roaring world. It is the hollow’s nerve ending, a living sensor tuned to the great, constant roar that is the place’s heartbeat. To see it is to see the sound made flesh.

**The Final Inhabitant is the Sound Itself.**
It is the most numerous and the most singular resident. It is not a byproduct of the water and the rock; it is the reason they have come together in this configuration. The roar is a physical presence that sculpts the air, presses on the moss, and vibrates the fine hairs on a visitor’s arm. It is the energy that powers the slow, green metabolism of the place. Life here has not adapted *despite* the noise; it has adapted *to consume* the noise. The sound is the food, the predator, the mate, and the god. Everything that lives in the hollow has made a sacrament of that unceasing vibration.

Together, these inhabitants form a single entity: a breathing, green, roaring geode. It does not think of itself as alive in the way a forest does, with competition and drama. It thinks of itself as a state of being—a permanent, damp, mossy inhalation in the side of the world, tended by nothing, known by nothing, and complete. The visitor’s pressed-back moss is not a farewell. It is the hollow simply continuing to be itself, accepting the fragment back into its slow, green, roaring dream.
