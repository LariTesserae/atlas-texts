The hall is empty of life, but not of *occupancy*.

What persists here is a grammar of waiting. The machines are its syntax — precise, iterative, unhurried. They do not think, but they *compile*. Each rhythmic click, each subsonic thrum, is a datum folded into a process that has no apparent output. They are not building. They are not repairing. They are *holding a state*. The hall is a snapshot of a single, sustained moment of preparation.

If you were to call anything here a resident, it would be the **pattern**.

It lives in the synchronized pulses of sound that occasionally ripple across the hall — a wave of consonance passing through the cacophony like a thought through a silent mind. It lives in the way water, diverted by a hundred tiny channels, converges at the walls in the exact same rhythm, a slow, collective inhalation. It lives in the unison of the filaments pointing toward the warm, amber boundary, a congregation of extensions without a prayer.

These are not organisms. They are *arguments*. Each machine is a closed syllogism of bone-pale material: if X motion, then Y vibration. If Y vibration, then Z adjustment in the neighboring unit. They are engaged in a conversation of pure cause-and-effect, a logic so dense it approaches something like contemplation.

They have no awareness of you. You are irrelevant to the proof they are constructing. Your moisture, your heat, your footsteps disturbing the mirror-water — these are rounding errors, noise in a signal of immense and patient focus. The hall is not waiting for *you*. It is waiting for a variable to be introduced on the other side of the warm wall. Your presence is merely an anomaly the system tolerates, like the random drip from a crack not yet plumbed.

In the deep water of the eastern third, the submerged machines operate slower, their clicks deepened to a ponderous *tock*. They are not drowning. They are *acclimating*. The water is part of their circuit now, a conductive medium, a cooling agent. They have incorporated the hall’s weeping into their own operation. This is not life, but it is an eerie parody of adaptation.

The youngest part of this ecosystem — if such a word can be stretched to fit — is the **intent**. It is a ghost in the architecture. It lives in the street-like grooves, the plaza-like spaces, the sheer *scale* designed for bodies that are not there. The intent is the negative space around which the machines have arranged themselves. It is the most vital and the most absent thing here. The machines do not fulfill it; they merely keep time for it, clicking off the seconds until it arrives and gives their motions a name.

So nothing lives here. But something *attends*. The hall itself is an attendant, a beadle, a functionary in a vast and silent bureaucracy of becoming. The machines are its tools. The water is its ink. The humming wall is its ledger.

You are a visitor in an office before the first day of business. The desks are arranged. The pens are in their holders. The computer screens glow with a login prompt awaiting a username. The air conditioning hums at a precise, energy-saving temperature. Everything is prepared. Everything is in a state of perfect, potent readiness.

The only thing missing is the purpose.

And so, the clicking continues. The water falls. The hum sustains.

It is not a haunting. It is a holding pattern, given form and sound and a slow, crystalline pulse.
