In the Resin Forest, life does not scurry or fly. It accrues. It echoes. The inhabitants are not separate from the architecture; they are its occasional, fleeting expressions.

**The Calcified Echoes**
Most common are the **Calcified Echoes**. They are not creatures that move through the forest, but phenomena that *occur* within it. When a major trunk collapses and its internal pressure is released, the sudden change in thermal dynamics can cause the mineral-rich vapors within the hollow to crystallize rapidly. For a few hours, or perhaps a day, a form will stand in the ruin—a ghostly, intricate sculpture of lattice-work crystal, vaguely mimicking the shape of the tree that housed it, or sometimes taking on a more spindly, almost skeletal aspect. They are silent, still, and cold relative to their surroundings. They do not eat. They merely exist as a temporary balance-point, a mineral afterimage. Then, as the ambient heat and vibrations of the living forest reach them, they destabilize. Their collapse is a soft, musical sigh of tinkling shards, their substance reclaimed as substrate. They are not born, and they do not die. They *occur* and then *un-occur.*

**The Resin-Weavers**
More active, in their glacially slow way, are the **Resin-Weavers**. These are not animals, but a symbiotic intelligence—a mobile, organizing aspect of the forest itself. They appear as clusters of thick, amber droplets, connected by strands of hardened resin as fine as glass filament. They move, if it can be called that, by a process of sequential hardening and liquefaction. The leading droplet receives a thermal signal—perhaps from a freshly broken spore-casing or a minor heat plume—and softens, flowing a fraction of an inch forward before cooling and hardening anew, pulling the rest of the chain behind it. Their purpose is repair and integration. They seal fractures in the ceramic bark with new resin. They envelop fallen spore-casings, sheathing them in a protective layer that ensures they germinate in a viable thermal niche. They are the forest's slow, viscous immune system and its agricultural tenders. Their "senses" are purely thermochemical; they are drawn to disruption and opportunity.

**The Krummholz-Wraiths**
Where growth is most contested, where a thermal vein is weakening and multiple trunks fight for its final dregs, a stranger entity manifests: the **Krummholz-Wraith**. This is a distortion in the light and heat-shimmer, a localized miasma of ionized gas and micro-spores that clings to the groaning, stressed wood. It appears as a flickering, man-high haze, sometimes vaguely columnar, sometimes spreading like a crown. It is not a being but a *process made visible*: the forest's violent struggle for dominance rendered as a phantom. It emits a faint, crackling whisper (the sound of cellular rupture on a microscopic scale) and a smell like hot metal and burnt sugar. To encounter one is to witness the forest's metabolism in its most raw, cannibalistic state. It brings no harm to a human observer directly, for it does not perceive organic life; it only knows the language of mineral stress and heat flow. It is a ghost of the conflict, lingering where the old are being forcibly recycled into the new.

**The Amber-Keepers**
Rare, and only found in the deepest, most stable pores where geothermal flow has been constant for millennia, are the **Amber-Keepers**. These are vast, sessile structures—organs of the world-organism. They resemble enormous, inverted mushrooms or sponges, fused to the ceiling of a cavern formed by welded trunks. Their flesh is a deep, translucent ruby, and within them, suspended in slowly circulating thermal sap, are preserved objects of mystery: a perfectly crystallized seed-cone from a bygone epoch, the fused skeletal outline of a long-calcified tree, or a bubble of atmosphere from a different age, captured and held in a resin sphere. They do not think. They *hold*. They are the forest's geological memory, its library of form and pressure. Life cycles occur within their gelatinous bodies—spores that may never germinate, mineral formations that grow with impossible slowness over ten thousand years. They are revered (in the way a forest reveres a mountain) by the flow of Resin-Weavers, who occasionally deposit rare minerals or unusual spores into their keeping, adding to the archive.

In the Resin Forest, there is no fauna. There are only processes, memories, and conflicts of the living rock and resin, momentarily coalescing into shapes that a mind from a wet, green world might, with profound inaccuracy, call "life."
