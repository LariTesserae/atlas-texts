The Constructors are not merely the fauna of this place; they *are* the place, in its most animated state. They are the living, scuttling manifestation of the world’s relentless geology.

Physically, they are a marvel of specialized erosion. Their carapace is not smooth chitin but a mosaic of micro-abrasive plates, dark as wet basalt and etched with the fine scratches of their own labor. Their multi-jointed limbs operate on a principle of incremental percussion; the “rasp” is not a continuous file, but a rapid, high-frequency chattering of a thousand tiny teeth, each impact adding to the world’s grinding symphony. The mandibles, more complex, can grind, sieve, or fuse with startling precision. They do not see with eyes, but sense the world through vibrations in the stone and minute chemical gradients in the perpetual seepage. Their entire existence is a dialogue with vibration and mineral.

Their society—or rather, their collective process—is a hierarchy of function, dictated by age and glandular development. The youngest, pale and soft-shelled, work deep within the warrens, tending the egg chambers and refining the binding secretion into a finer paste. The vast middle generations are the builders and harvesters, forming the living, grinding crust of the structures. The eldest, their carapaces thick and cratered, develop specialized roles. Some become “listeners,” stationing themselves at vibration-sensitive nodes in the warrens, their bodies a-tremor, guiding swarm-work to seismic stresses. Others become “seeders,” their thoracic glands bloated, crawling to new, untouched mounds to lay the chemical foundation—the first sticky, mineral-rich film—that will attract a new swarm and begin a new warren.

The constructs themselves are their nervous system made manifest. The thin, porous walls are not passive; they are a capillary network. Moisture, drawn from the weeping stone, is wicked through the structure via surface tension and the Constructors’ own secretions, which alter the porosity. The vast, amplified halls are not just meeting spaces; they are resonance chambers. Here, the grinding of thousands of individuals is harmonized into a focused, standing wave—a tool used to vibrate loose stubborn mineral veins or to fracture a harvested mound at its weakest point. The spires are sensory organs, collecting data on the dust haze, the droplet density, and the vibrations from neighboring warrens.

They do not think, as we understand it. They *process*. A problem—a collapsed corridor, a depleted mineral vein, an invasive fissure—is met with a swarm response. Chemical signals, carried on the damp air, mobilize legions. The solution is enacted through a million simultaneous micro-actions, a parallel computation in stone and secretion. There is no leader, only a flow of information as direct as the seepage of water.

To witness their “urban” life is to see a reef in fast-forward. A damaged dome is not repaired; it is *overgrown*, a new layer of structure built upon the broken one, incorporating the fracture into a new, stronger geometry. Expansion is a slow, inexorable tide. A new warren begins as a simple, damp cleft where the eldest seeder has left her chemical signature. Soon, a few scouts arrive, then dozens, then hundreds. Within a cycle of the dim light, a new cluster of tubes and vaults has taken root, its grinding joining the chorus.

They are not intelligent. They are *inevitable*. The planet sweats, and the Constructors are the fever. They are the mechanism by which this wet, warm, nascent world metabolizes its own crust. Their life is the sound. Their home is the product of their bodies. To ask what lives here is to ask what lives in a lung or a liver. The answer is the process itself: a billion abrasions, a million chisels, forever building the body they inhabit, in the warm, wet, grinding dark.
