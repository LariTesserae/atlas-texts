The first thing you understand is that they do not *live in* the Vat. They are *of* it.

You do not see them at first. You see the **effects**. A shadow that detaches from a deeper shadow and flows upward along a pipe, its movement too smooth for a climbing man. A ripple in a condensation gutter, as if drawn by an unseen finger collecting water. The brief, soft *click* of a latch on a sealed porthole that seems to open on its own, emitting a puff of warmer, spice-scented air. The vegetation parts and closes behind movements you cannot trace.

Then, the **glimpses**.

They are slender, almost gauntly so, but with a wiry strength visible in the cords of their forearms and the lean muscles of their backs. Their skin is not one color, but a **marbled palette**—the grey ofite, the rust-red of iron oxide, the deep, moist black of damp basalt. It is not painted on; it is a mottled, living patina, as if the minerals of the Vat have seeped into their dermis. In the dim, orange glow, they can be mistaken for part of the architecture itself.

Their **eyes** are large, with pupils that dilate to vast, light-gathering black pools in the gloom, and contract to mere pinpricks when a maintenance lamp flickers nearby. There is a faint, reflective sheen to them, like the tapetum lucidum of a cat or the surface of still, dark water. They see perfectly in the near-total dark of the lower depths.

They wear little. Garments are **functional extensions of the Vat’s skin**: harnesses woven from plant fiber and wire, tool-belts of molded polymer, cloaks that look suspiciously like the broad, waxy leaves of the deep-heat plants, stitched together with fungal mycelium thread. The cloaks are not just for modesty; they collect condensation, channeling it into small, hidden bladders at the hip.

They are **silent communicators**. Not mute, but their primary language is one of **gesture, breath, and subtle vibration**. A hand brushed against a resonant pipe sends a specific *tonk* through the metal, a signal understood floors away. A sharp exhale through the nose is a warning. A low, sustained hum from the diaphragm, felt more than heard, aligns their movements in coordinated tasks—tightening a bolt, harvesting a vine, calibrating a sensor. When they do vocalize, their speech is a whispering, hissing polyglot of the languages that built the Vat, stripped down to essential nouns and verbs, and interspersed with the clicks and drips of the chamber itself. To hear them talk is to hear the Vat’s own soundscape given intent.

Their society is **cellular and synergistic**. There are no leaders, only **nodes of skill**. The **Water-Catchers** know every trick of condensation and flow. The **Vent-Speakers** listen to the geothermal breath and adjust the pipe valves, their heads pressed against the warm metal as if in prayer. The **Tenders** coo to the plants, whose growth patterns they read like schematics; one suspects the plants that bloom like pressure gauges are not accidents, but communications. The **Menders** are part-engineer, part-surgeon, and their tools work on both human flesh and machine alloy with equal, quiet precision. A broken arm is set with the same focus as a fractured conduit.

**Children** are rare and precious, and they learn by **osmosis**. They are strapped to their parents’ backs in leaf-cradles during work, their first toys are polished bolts and soft scraps of polymer. By the time they can walk, they can identify a faulty bearing by its smell and know which vines yield potable water in an emergency. They do not play in open spaces; they **navigate** the three-dimensional maze as their playground, their laughter a rapid, staccato series of clicks that echoes for a moment before being absorbed by the walls.

Their relationship with the **fauna** is intimate. The eyeless lizards are not pets; they are **partners**. A Tender will have one that lives in the folds of their cloak, darting out to warn of insect blooms or to test the moisture of a new condensation seep. The translucent snails in the hydroponic vats are tended as carefully as any crop; their shells are sometimes collected and ground into a fine, calcium-rich paste.

They are not untouched by the **weirdness**. They accept it as physics. A door opening to rock face is simply a **sealed sector**, perhaps awaiting future expansion, or a place that has “gone to stone.” The circuit-like patterns in the moisture are **read as data**—a Vent-Speaker will trace them with a finger, nod, and adjust a valve elsewhere. The gauge-flowers are **cultivated**; a cluster of them blooming near a pipe means the pressure is *exactly right*. The stairway to the marked bulkhead? That is the **Old Seal**. They do not open it. They leave offerings of a perfect fruit or a coiled wire before it, not out of worship, but out of **respect for a system boundary**. They understand, in their bones, that the Vat is a recent layer on an older machine, or a older life, and they are the current stewards of the interface.

They are **symbionts**. The heat regulates their body temperature. The rhythm of the vent is their heartbeat. The filtered water is their blood. The humming servers are their memory. They are the Vat’s **consciousness**, its **hands**, its **immune system**. They do not fight the environment; they **are** its latest, most intricate adaptation. They are the **patient equilibrium made flesh**, a people born of steam, stone, and circuitry, tending the buried heart that sustains them, in a quiet, perpetual dance of maintenance and meaning. They are not colonists. They are the **crop** that the Vat itself has grown.
