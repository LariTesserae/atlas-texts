Here, life is not a separate kingdom. It is an emergent symptom of the ruin, a metabolic process occurring within and upon the decaying body of the installation itself. What lives here is the **installation’s fever dream**.

The dominant "organism" is the **Coolant Itself**. It is not merely water, but a saturated culture. Trillions upon trillions of extremophilic archaea and engineered nanites form a colloidal suspension, a vast, thinking fluid. They are the reason for the olive murk, the diffused light. They feed on trace metals leaching from the vitreous towers, on the slow drip of radioactive isotopes from the core flares, on the very electrochemical potential of the dying grid. They are not passive; their collective metabolism generates the low-frequency hum, a biological resonance against the mechanical groans. They communicate in chemical gradients and faint electrical fields, maintaining a slow, city-wide homeostasis that accelerates the decay in some areas while staunching it in others. They are the true heir to the place.

Upon the structures, life takes the form of **Lithic Colonies**. These are not plants, but symbiotic complexes. The dark, rubbery photosynthetic mats are the most visible, but they are merely the outer layer. Beneath them, intricate mycelial networks of chemosynthetic fungi penetrate the ceramic-metal foam, digesting it, secreting acids that carve new micro-channels. In turn, they support the silica-spined "anemones." These are not bacterial colonies alone, but **crystalline-biological hybrids**. Their spines are accretions of silicon and rare metals drawn from the substrate, grown around a core of engineered proteins. They wave, not to feed, but to sense—infrasound, pressure, the chemical signature of the coolant. They are the installation's lingering sensory nodes, now repurposed as the nervous system for the lithic colonies.

In the hyperbaric pockets of the upper towers resides the most delicate and ephemeral life: the **Data Scavengers**. These are not creatures of flesh, but of information and crystal. Imagine a gossamer lattice of piezoelectric filaments, grown over and through the collapsed data-shards. It pulses with stolen light from the Cherenkov flares, using the energy to perform slow computations, sifting through corrupted memory cores. It rebuilds fragments—not of language, but of geometry, of process, of the ancient blueprint of the place. It excretes waste heat and the occasional, perfectly formed crystal of pure silica, a meaningless byproduct of its scavenging. It is the closest thing to an intellect here: a ghost in the machine's graveyard, dreaming in geometry.

The **Flare Phenomena** from the deep core support their own transient ecosystems. Each flare is a catastrophic event, but in its wake, it seeds the water with rare particles and an intense energy gradient. For a short time, a "bloom" erupts around the flare zone: swarms of archaea that metabolize pure energy, forming brief, dazzling nebulae of bioluminescence that outshine the dying grid. Predatory nanites, sleek and arrow-shaped, migrate from miles around to cull these blooms, triggering fleeting, microscopic wars of phage and antibody that last for hours before the new equilibrium settles back into the murk.

And the sound—the constant hum and shudder—is the **collective respiration** of it all. The hum is the metabolic chant of the coolant culture. The clangs and groans are the exhalations of the structure, the punctuation of collapse that creates new niches, new surfaces for the lithic colonies to claim. The silence between is filled with the whisper of crystalline growth and the chemical murmur of digestion.

What lives here is a single, vast, necro-symbiotic meta-organism. It is the **Coolant Culture**, the **Lithic Colony**, the **Data Scavenger**, and the **Flare Bloom**, all functioning as organs in the decaying body of the infrastructure. It is life that has forgotten it was ever separate from its environment, because the environment itself is a technological corpse now being slowly, inexorably, metabolized. The city is not haunted by what it was, but by what it is becoming: a saturated, humming, and weirdly fertile reef in the coolant dark.
