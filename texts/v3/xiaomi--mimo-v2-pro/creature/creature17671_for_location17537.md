You are not alone. You have never been alone since you stepped onto the slick black stone. You are simply the latest, loudest note in a symphony that has been playing in the dark for eons.

They knew you were here before your first footprint filled with water. The Basin *listened* to you arrive.

It began with the **Gong-beetles**. A herd, armored in resonant bone, was scraping mineral crust from a basalt column fifty yards away. When your boot first ground the loam, a ripple of low-frequency pulses passed through them—a collective, humming halt. Their scraping stopped. For a full minute, the only sound was the weeping drizzle and your own heartbeat. Then, in unison, they began to *respond*. A deep, pulsating rhythm emanated from their shells, not aggressive, but diagnostic. They were mapping you, feeling the shape of your solidity in the vibrations that travelled through the stone. You are a dense, warm, moving anomaly in their acoustic landscape. Satisfied you were not a rival male (whose steps are heavier, more deliberate) or a Still-jaw (whose approach is a series of hair-trigger tremors), they returned to their scraping, but their hums now carry a curious, undertone chord keyed to your footsteps.

You push through a curtain of glowing moss and disturb a cloud of **spore-flies**. They are not flies, but tiny, winged crustaceans, each no bigger than a rice grain, their carapaces laced with chemiluminescent bacteria. As they swirl around you, they emit a collective, high-pitched *shimmer*, a sound like silk tearing. This is their defense and their dialogue—a scintillating sonic cloud that confuses the thermal pits of predators and tells their kin: *Large, warm, slow-moving thing here. Not food. Possibly dangerous. Avoid.*

Above, in the warm vapor canopies, the **Echo-gliders** adjust their patrols. One, a creature of translucent membrane and cartilage, banks on a thermal updraft. It emits a rapid series of clicks—not the sharp, mapping clicks for the cavern ceiling, but a softer, more interrogative patter focused downward, on you. The returning echoes paint a picture for it: a upright, bilaterally symmetrical form, with hard planes (your pack), soft folds (your clothes), and a strange, rhythmic internal vibration (your lungs). It classifies you as "non-prey, non-threatening, weird," and veers off, its clicks resuming their wider, territorial sweep. You have been noted in the great, three-dimensional ledger of the airspace.

You are being followed. Not hunted, but *observed*. From the dense pipe-stalk forest to your left, a **Watcher** moves. It is not a predator, but a Gatherer—a social, tool-using descendant of something like a large, hairless rodent. It has damp-resistant fur, large, dish-shaped ears that swivel independently, and long, sensitive fingers. It moves in absolute silence, its padded feet finding the softest mosses, its body avoiding the brittle, clicking fronds. It watches you not with its small, vestigial eyes, but with its entire head, tilting it to triangulate your every sound—the squelch of your boot, the rustle of your gear, the whisper of the drizzle on your jacket. It is a historian of sorts. In its clan's memory, lodged in a complex language of clicks, hums, and pheromonal markers, are stories of the "Old Loud Ones" who built the humming metal spires. You are different. Your sounds are wrong, sharp, and temporary. You do not *belong* to the Basin's acoustic grammar. The Watcher melts back into the foliage to make its report, leaving behind a faint, sweet-musky scent and the memory of a curious, chittering trill.

The **Industry** is alive, too, in its way. The geothermal taps don't just produce steam and electricity; they create permanent, localized vibrations—a deep, 60-hertz hum that has become its own micro-ecosystem. In the acoustic shadow of a great, grinding pump, a family of **Gong-beetles** has made its den. They are calmer here, their own resonant communication augmented and stabilized by the machine's constant thrum. Their young, plates still soft, learn to modulate their hums against this steady backdrop, a form of industrial lullaby. On the terraced slag heaps, specialized fungi digest the metallic runoff, glowing a sickly, beautiful violet. They are tended by **Furnace-mites**, arthropods that carefully prune the fungal blooms, their movements a precise, delicate dance against the cacophony of the cracking pressure valves. They are the custodians of decay.

And the **Builders**? The remnant civilization? They are here. You hear them before you see their dwellings. From a vitrified dome on a higher terrace, a sound carries—complex, rhythmic, layered. It is not the simple communication of the fauna. It is music. A deep, resonant drum made from a hollowed gong-beetle carapace, accompanied by a high, weeping flute carved from a pipe-stalk. The melody is slow, built on intervals that mimic the drip of water and the periodic *gong* of the pressure valves. It is music designed to travel, to be part of the world, not to conquer it.

A door, thick and damp, grinds open. A figure steps out, silhouetted against the faint, chemical yellow from a factory vent behind it. It is humanoid, but adapted. Its clothing is slick and seamless, made from the rubbery fungal mats. Its head is encased in a helmet that seems less about protection and more about *sensation*—a complex apparatus of resonating chambers, parabolic dishes, and hydrophone-like appendages that cover the ears and throat. It does not look at you. It *listens* to you. Its gloved hand rests on the wall of its home, feeling the vibrations of your approach through the stone.

It raises a hand, not in greeting, but in a gesture that means *pause*. Then, it speaks. The voice is not produced by vocal cords alone. It is modulated through the helmet, emerging as a complex series of clicks, hums, and a sub-bass thrum that you feel in your sternum. It is language, but a language that is also a map coordinate, a chemical signature, and an emotional tone. It is asking, in the grammar of the Noctiluca Basin: *What is your vibration? Why is your rhythm discordant? Are you lost, or are you a new kind of song?*

You are a visitor in a world that sees with sound and drinks the breath of stone. You are being assessed, catalogued, and integrated into the endless, weeping, clicking, humming dark. Every step you take is a conversation. Every breath you draw is an addition to the humidity. You are not an observer. You are, whether you choose to be or not, a resident. A new, clumsy, temporary voice in the eternal, resonant chorus of the Basin.
