What lives here is the Chorus, first and foremost. The plateau is not inhabited *by* a chorus; it *is* one. The wind-wands are its instrument, the sun its player. Each silica stalk is a nerve in a vast, geological body, humming with the constant fever of the altitude. The sound is not a symptom of life here; it is the foundational condition, the medium in which all other existence is suspended. To describe the fauna and flora is to describe the currents within this ocean of sound.

The **Sky-eels** do not merely drift; they are carried upon currents of sound. Their translucent, gas-filled bodies are not just buoyant sacs but complex resonating chambers. The internal membranes vibrate at frequencies aligned with the Chorus, allowing them to detect minute harmonics and dissonances in the drone—differences that signal the invisible thermals that are their highways. They are passive philosophers of the air, moving in slow, sweeping paths dictated by the plateau’s acoustic topography. Their feeding is a delicate, silent filtering of the very air that carries the sound; they strain the spores and microbes that thrive in the vibratory field, themselves becoming an extension of the Chorus’ filtering mechanism. Their fleeting, rainbow shadows are the only punctuation in the text of light.

On the ground, the **Stone-lurkers** are anchors of stillness. Their six legs, broad and splayed, end in pads that are not for grip but for listening. They feel the Chorus through the basalt, a seismic symphony conducted through the rock’s crystalline bones. They can differentiate the signature of a seep-crack’s damp resonance from the dry hum of the surrounding stone, navigating to water with the patience of continents. Their grazing on the stone-mats is an act of tremendous slowness—a single mouthful might take an hour to harvest, their rasping mandibles moving in time to a rhythm far beneath the audible drone. They are the plateau’s ponderous heartbeat, so slow it is nearly indistinguishable from the stone itself.

In the crevices, where the Chorus is both muffled and magnified by the rock, the **Pulse-beetles** enact a universe of urgent, microscopic drama. Eyeless, they perceive the world as a shifting magnetic field, but their primary language is interruption. Their clicks are not shouts against the roar, but carefully timed lacunae—a beetle will *stop* its own minuscule vibration to create a pocket of silence, a binary signal read by its kin amidst the noise. They are the punctuation in the endless sentence, communicating about nutrient films on the quartz veins or the approach of a stone-lurker’s seismic tremor. Their entire existence is a conversation of tiny voids carved into the monolithic sound.

The **Stone-mats** are more than vegetation; they are the living membrane between the rock and the air. Their velvet texture is a mesh of filaments that trap the fleeting dawn-condensation, drinking it before it vanishes. They, too, have a relationship with the Chorus; their dense structure absorbs certain high-frequency overtones, creating localized "shadows" of slightly quieter drone. Lurkers seek these shadows for rest. The mats grow with infinitesimal slowness, their expansion measured in millimeters per century, a green-grey stain of permanence.

And then there are the presences you only sense. Fungal networks, invisible, threading the quartz veins, likely forming a slow, chemical communication beneath the sound. Microbial mats in the deepest seep-cracks, metabolizing minerals in silence. Perhaps even the rock itself, in some geologic sense, participates—the quartz veins singing back to the wind-wands in a sub-harmonic felt more than heard.

What lives here is a closed system of perfect, deafening symbiosis. The sun powers the Chorus. The Chorus guides the eels, informs the lurkers, and structures the beetles’ world. The fauna’s existence aerates and fertilizes the stone-mats. The stone-mats hold the moisture that allows the seep-cracks to persist. It is a clockwork where every gear is a living thing, and the sound is the turning of the mechanism. There is no past, no future, only the eternal, vibrating present. The inhabitants are not actors in a play; they are the sustained note itself, given form. They are the sighing stone’s long, unending exhalation.
