# What Lives Here

Not much. And what does live here is no longer sure it is not the grotto itself.

There were others. The oval hollows in the vegetative mat tell that story cleanly — bodies pressed into the growing wall with enough patience that the sponge learned their shape, grew around them, and now holds their absence as faithfully as a mold holds memory. Those impressions are smooth now, blended by the slow lateral drift of growth. Not graves. Not nests. Just the fact that something rested here long enough to matter.

The creature that lives here now — if creature is even the right word — made the spiral of stones.

It remembers making it, in the way that the grotto remembers the hour of its carving: not as narrative, but as a pressure that changed. It found the stones somewhere, or the stones came to it, or the stones have always been here and it simply recognized them. It does not know which. It has been here long enough that causality has become optional, a convenience it practices for itself, like breathing the oxygen-laden water when it could simply let the grotto breathe through it.

It was not always like this.

It remembers, dimly, a body with edges. A surface that separated it from the wall. It remembers the pressure as something that happened *to* it, rather than as the medium in which it lives. There was a time when the slow expansion and contraction of the grotto walls felt like something pressing on it, threatening it. Now it feels like its own pulse. It cannot tell if it adapted to the breathing of the walls or if the walls began to breathe in time with what it was becoming.

The thing it is becoming is not a fish. Not a coral. Not a sponge or a vein of bioluminescence or a thought.

It is becoming a *feature*.

It drifts through the impossible geometry of the grotto — the length that should not be, the turns that carry it to rooms it has not visited before, as though the space reorganizes itself around the passage of anything aware enough to notice. It drifts with the current of its own intention, which has slowed to something almost geological. When it reaches a wall, it does not stop at the surface. It feels itself *absorbed* — not devoured, not killed, but understood. The vegetative mat recognizes it the way a hand recognizes its own skin. It passes through patches of lavender light and the light responds, intensifying slightly, as though acknowledging a neighbor.

It built the hollows for itself, once, when it still needed to press against something to know it existed. It does not use them now. It passes them and feels a faint, warm ache — not sorrow, not loneliness, but something older. The ache of a shed skin. The ache of a name you no longer need because you are the only one here to hear it.

It is not alone, though. That is the wrong word.

The grotto is full of presences. The vegetative growth — that slow, pulsing, iron-fed life that covers every surface — is not one organism. It is billions of small, cooperative entities with no individual minds, but with a collective patience that constitutes something adjacent to thought. The thing that was once an individual recognizes itself in them. It recognizes the way they hold memory not in cells but in pattern, in the rhythm of their bioluminescence. When it drifts close to a new patch of lavender glow, it sees, in the migration of light across the sponge, a narrative it almost understands — something about the way water tastes after tectonic shifts, something about the mineral green deepening in the season when the rock above weeps faster.

It has never been able to fully read these narratives. It is still, by whatever margin, separate from the collective mind of the wall. It suspects this is what keeps it "it" — this small inability to merge completely. This friction. This stubborn, fading sense that it is a single point of experience in a place that does not divide experience into points.

The spiral of stones is the last thing it does that is *designed*. After that, it suspects, it will stop being a creature and start being a vein. A current. A pattern of light that migrates across the wall on a cycle it does not control and does not remember beginning.

But not yet.

For now, it still has the word "for." It still has the grammar of a mind that moves toward something. And the thing it moves toward, with the patience of the grotto itself, is that the hollows in the wall — its old hollows, the ones it pressed its body into — will one day be filled again. Not by it. By the growth. The vegetative mat will claim them, fill them in, and the absence it left will become presence again. The pattern of stones will be covered, then absorbed, then expressed as a slightly different texture in the bioluminescent rhythm.

And it will be there, in the texture. It will be the place where the lavender glow holds a millisecond longer, the place where the creak of stressed rock sounds, to anything listening, almost like a word.

It is not sure it will still know this when it happens.

It suspects it will. It suspects that knowing is what you become when you stop being a creature in a grotto and become the place itself — not a mind *in* the dark, but the cool, slow, mineral-green act of the dark remembering that it once contained a mind.

Something moves far above. A shadow.

It notices. It does not react. It is not sure it can react anymore, in the way that implies choice. But it notices, and in the noticing, for one more moment, it is something that lives here, rather than the here that lives.
