In the iron lung of the mountain, life does not merely exist; it *negotiates*. The inhabitants here are not species as we know them, but phenomena—biological processes that have woven themselves into the rhythms of the machine. They are the living syntax of the Cage.

**The Vibration-Seekers:** The most fundamental layer of life here is attuned not to light, but to sound. The **Resonance Worms** are eyeless, pale, and segment-less, flowing through the condensation-films and sludge pools. They have no fixed shape, contracting and expanding in sync with the deep dynamo-thrum, absorbing minerals directly through their membranes. They are the primary decomposers, turning fallen fern fronds and fungal matter back into the silt that feeds the roots. Where they cluster, the metal takes on a faint, rhythmic pulse of bioluminescence, a cold blue ghost of the vibrations they consume.

**The Canopy Weavers:** High in the lattice-work, where giant ferns have interlaced, live the **Glimmerwings**. These are not birds, but large, vertebrate-like insects with four crystalline, photo-receptive wings. They are blind to detail but exquisitely sensitive to changes in light and shadow. Their "song" is a complex, rapid fluttering of their wings, creating stroboscopic patterns of reflected light that communicate danger, food, and mating status across the misty spaces. They build their nests—a glue of saliva and chewed fern-fiber—directly onto the warmer steam pipes, using the heat to incubate their eggs. Their young are legless grubs that drop into the pools below, metamorphosing in the silt.

**The Carapace Grazers:** The six-legged creatures glimpsed earlier are **Rusters**. Their reflective carapaces are not merely for show; they are layered with shed mica-like flakes from the mineral deposits and microscopic metal filings, making them nearly invisible against the latticework. They are meticulous grazers, using delicate, comb-like mouthparts to harvest specific mosses and algae, their digestive systems capable of filtering out heavy metals. In death, their metallic-laden shells are crushed by the constant vibration, becoming a glittering sand that mixes with the soil, slowly raising the level of the walkways.

**The Steam-Siphons:** Perhaps the most enigmatic are the **Vapor Bats**. They roost in the dark, dry spaces behind the capacitor banks. They do not fly in the conventional sense. Their wings are membranous sacs. They swoop from perches to intercept jets of escaping steam, using specialized membranes to condense and drink the pure, hot water, simultaneously absorbing the thermal energy. They metabolize this heat, their bodies glowing a dull red in the dark. They are the sanitation crew, preventing dangerous pressure build-ups in the older pipes, and their guano, rich in thermal minerals, is a potent fertilizer for the heat-loving fungi.

**The Pipe-Polishers:** Clinging to the vertical surfaces are **Lacquershells**, gastropods whose shells are not of calcium carbonate, but a hardened, secreted polymer—a biochemical mimic of the sealants and insulation found throughout the Cage. They move with infinitesimal slowness, consuming the algae and leaving behind a trail of this bonding agent, which hardens and reinforces the corroded metal plates they traverse. Over decades, a heavily used pipe may be completely encased in a smooth, dark, organic shell of their cumulative labor, a living patina.

**The Pulse-Frog:** In the tepid drainage saucers, a squat, mottled amphibian, the **Clatterthroat**, makes its home. Its mating call is not a croak, but a rapid, percussive clicking produced by a hardened plate in its throat striking its jawbone. The frequency matches the *clank-hiss* of the nearby pistons, allowing their calls to cut through the background noise for brief, vital moments. Tadpoles filter the water, consuming the Resonance Worms' offspring and algae.

There are no large predators here. The energy flow is too diffuse, the environment too relentless. Instead, control is maintained by parasites and specialists. **Drone Wasps** lay eggs in the nests of Glimmerwings; **Ruster Mites** ride on the grazers, feeding on their blood and dropping off into the moss to complete their lifecycle. It is a closed, frantic, humming loop.

Everything serves the equilibrium. The life manages the decay, channeling it into growth. The growth manages the moisture, channeling it into corrosion. The corrosion, in turn, creates new niches for new growth. The inhabitants are not fighting the machine; they are its current, its cooling fluid, its slow, biological pulse. They are the inevitable, intricate stain of life on an engine of impossible scale, thriving in the deafening, sacred noise of its function. They are the proof that even in a place built for a purpose beyond nature, nature will inevitably discover a purpose of its own.
