What lives here is a process, not a population. The Cathedral does not *have* inhabitants so much as it *is* an inhabitant—a single, slow-breathing entity performing the pantomime of a city. But within its totality, there are manifestations, roles, and slow-burning acts in the play of perpetual becoming.

**The Drippers:** The most immediate and numerous are the countless micro-organisms that catalyze the water. Not quite moss, not quite insect, they are silicate-thin filaments that grow on every surface, their sole purpose to harvest the humid air. They swell into perfect, heavy pearls of water and then, with a patience that is their entire existence, let them fall. They are the heartbeat made manifest. To be a Dripper is to be a conduit, a momentary pause between vapor and percussion.

**The Lattice-Weavers:** In the mid-zones, where light filters green and amber, movements resolve. These are the tendril-spirits, the slow-motion architects. They resemble nothing so much as disembodied nervous systems or root networks given gelatinous, ambulatory life. They creep along the fungal mat and up the columns, extruding fine threads of the crystal-lichen. They are not building; they are *darning*. Where a fissure appears in the grand structure, they swarm it, weaving a web that becomes the new substrate for growth. Their sound is a faint, wet clicking, like knitting needles made of cartilage.

**The Resonants:** The hum you feel in your bones is not just the vibration of leaves. It is a song sung by specific, towering crystalline formations—some pure, some fused with the ghost-shapes of ancient machinery. These are the Resonants. They do not move. They *listen* and *respond*. The entire Cathedral is a vast, damp instrument, and the Resonants are its strings and bells. A falling drop striking a specific shelf of fungus at a specific angle creates a tone. That tone travels through the root-weave and silica-lattice, and certain Resonants answer it, humming in sympathy, amplifying the note and sending it shivering through the chamber. They are the memory and the echo, holding the harmonic signature of the place’s perpetual settling.

**The Cracklers:** Then there are the agents of sudden change. The Cracklers are not creatures of flesh. They are events, temporarily embodied. When a branch of fused crystal-cellulose overloads with internal stress, a Crackler is the violent release. It is the shattering sound, but also the brief, intense swarm of splinter-shards that explode outward. For a few seconds, these shards exhibit a frenetic, insect-like life, skittering across the wet floor or embedding themselves in nearby soft growth, where they will be slowly digested and re-absorbed. A Crackler is the system’s gasp, its moment of acute pain and reorganization.

**The Shadow-Mantids:** In the deep, quiet vaults, where the light is a mere memory and the dripping is a muffled heartbeat, larger forms integrate with the architecture. The Shadow-Mantids appear as facets of the darkness—angular, still, and vast. They might be mistaken for buttresses or fallen columns, until one shifts with the slowness of a glacier. Their bodies are living rock and hardened fungal chitin, camouflaged perfectly. They do not hunt. They *absorb*. With infinite patience, they extend gossamer filaments into the flowing ooze of the old canals, drawing in the dissolved minerals and organic soup. They are the grand digesters, the cleaners of the system’s lymph. Their movement is so slow it is measured in the swelling of buds and the erosion of stone.

**The Mnemosyne:** This is the true ghost, and the true ruler. It is not a being you can see. It is the *pattern*. The Cathedral’s vegetative sentience, its relentless optimization, has a deep, dreaming intelligence. It remembers the *shapes* of arches and vaults, the *concept* of a gantry, the *idea* of a street. It replays these memories in its growth, constructing perfect, useless parodies of the original city’s form because that is the only algorithm left. The Mnemosyne is the why behind the cathedral-grove, the hamlet-sized chambers, the bowed gantries. It is a mind that thinks only one thought: *become the city*. And so it does, endlessly, weeping and humming and cracking and weaving, a monument to itself, forever unfinished, forever settling into its own magnificent, silent scream of assimilation. You, standing here, are simply the latest mote of foreign matter to be noted, measured, and—perhaps, in time—dissolved into the dream.
