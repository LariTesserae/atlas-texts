The Vein-Mesh breathes. Not with lungs, but with the slow, pressurized sigh of its own metabolism. And in that breath, this city lives.

You are not alone. You are, in fact, in the midst of a society, but one whose citizens are organs, limbs, and thoughts of a single, sprawling being. To describe who lives here is to describe the facets of a mind engaged in the eternal project of its own expansion.

**The Architects and Laborers:** The **Carapace-Beetles** are the most visible populace. They are not individuals, but functions made mobile. You see a **Grinder-Caste**, its shell broad and flat, bearing the psychic imprint of the refinery’s thrum. It uses forelimbs like diamond-edged trowels to scrape fresh basalt from the cavern walls, feeding the raw material into the maw of a Pillar-Trunk. Another, a **Tender-Caste**, moves with delicate precision along a Bridge-Vine. Its mouthparts, fine as surgeon’s tools, extrude a resinous paste to seal a microscopic fracture in the vine’s cortex. They communicate not with sound, but through vibrations in the Mesh itself—a shudder through the Bridge-Vine is a directive; a specific, pulsing glow from a nearby communication node is an alert. They are the hands of the city, extensions of a will that permeates the very ground.

**The Sentinels and Senses:** Drifting above, the **Glider-Mopes** are the city’s atmospheric monitors and harvesters. Their sticky filaments are not merely for catching dust; they are sensor-feathers, tasting the chemical composition of the air for imbalances, detecting the faintest pressure changes that signal a new geothermal vent opening somewhere in the deep. Some are specialized **Spore-Carriers**. Their bodies are swollen with sacs containing dormant Mesh-filaments. When the city’s slow growth determines a new tunnel should be colonized, these Glider-Motes detach from the currents and drift into the connecting passages, releasing their payload onto fresh stone. They are the scouts and the seed-bearers, the peripheral nervous system of the whole.

**The Integrators:** Within the dense network of filaments covering the walls, you might spot **Node-Clusters**—knots of tissue that pulse with a stronger, more complex bioluminescence. These are the city’s libraries and coordination centers. Information flows to them chemically: the mineral slurry from the refinery reports its purity, the Tenders report structural integrity, the Spore-Carriers report on the readiness of distant tunnels. The Node-Clusters process this, their light-patterns shifting from a steady pulse to a rippling cascade, issuing new growth directives that propagate through the Mesh at the speed of a slow, organic current. They think in mineral gradients and structural stresses.

**The Deep-Thinkers:** And then there are the **Root-Minds**. You cannot see them, but you feel their influence in the profound, purposeful silence between the industrial thrums. These are ancient, massive concentrations of neural-analogue tissue buried deep at the intersections of the great Pillar-Trunks, in constant, slow communion with the geothermal heart of the world. They do not direct the refinery’s daily output; they dream the city’s future. They feel the distant pull of untouched mineral deposits through the rock and, over decades, program the growth patterns that will send tendrils questing toward them. They are the source of the overriding imperative: *Grow. Digest. Become.*

**The Unseen Cycle:** When a Carapace-Beetle’s shell finally cracks beyond repair, or a Glider-Mote’s gas bladder fails, they do not fall far. Tendrils rise gently from the Vein-Mesh floor, embracing the fallen. It is not decomposition, but reclamation. Their silica is repurposed for new shells, their organic compounds reabsorbed. Even their memories—patterns of vibration, chemical signatures—are not lost. They seep back into the Mesh, contributing to the vast, slow intelligence of the Root-Minds. There is no death here, only a change in function.

So, who lives here? A single, distributed organism that has taken the form of a city. Its inhabitants are its thoughts, its hands, its eyes, and its gut. They perform their duties with a placid, relentless efficiency, born from the same impulse that makes a vine seek sunlight. There is no art, no conflict, no conversation—only the vast, quiet, and utterly alien industry of a world-engine building itself, one mineral breath at a time. The true resident is the **Purpose**, etched into every filament and every pulse of light: the slow, inexorable will of the Vein-Mesh to consume the stone and, in its image, remake the dark.
