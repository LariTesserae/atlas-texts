In the drowned city, what lives does not move *upon* the glass, but *as* it. The city breathes, and its breath is its inhabitants.

**The Vein-Dwellers.** These are the most ubiquitous, the foundational pulse. They are not creatures but *currents*—slower, thicker, and more luminous than the water itself. They coil through the vitreous substrate’s ancient circulatory system, the glowing pathways beneath your feet. They are the city’s slow metabolism, carrying silt and dissolved glass from one dormant organ to another, reinforcing a curve here, softening a fissure there. They have no mind, only a purpose of flow. Where they pool, the glass becomes subtly warm (a mere absence of absolute chill) and more pliable. A Vein-Dweller’s death is a cooling, a solidification—a new, inert tributary in the architecture.

**The Chime-Swarms.** These are the filaments you see. Each is a lattice of hair-thin glass threads, woven by vibration alone. They drift in the mid-water, their forms imitating the fractured pillars above, but fluid, dancing. A swarm is a school and a single organism; each filament resonates with the world’s hum, and their collective, overlapping frequencies create the high, ethereal chime. They are scavengers of energy, feeding on the differential between the cold water and the slightly less-cold glass. They reproduce by shattering—a single filament, over-vibrating, will burst into a glittering cloud of fragments, each fragment a seed that will slowly, over decades, grow into a new filament drawn to the swarm’s song.

**The Glacial Mantas.** The vast, slow gliders near the bottom. They are not born, but *calved*. A section of wall, eroded by a particularly persistent Vein-Dweller, will one day detach with a seismic sigh. This slab does not fall; it remembers the shape of the Mantas that have drifted here for aeons. It curls, its edges fraying into feathery filaments that taste the silt. It is a geological process given direction. A Glacial Manta’s life is a single, continent-long journey across the sea floor, filtering the glittering rain of dissolving glass. It grows by accretion, its back becoming a new landscape—sometimes a pillar takes root upon it, sometimes a Chime-Swarm makes a nest in its frayed edges. When it finally comes to rest against a canyon wall, it takes centuries to fuse, becoming a new neighborhood, its filtration systems the nascent Vein-Dwellers of a future plaza.

**The Quake-Blossoms.** These are rare, and beautiful, and terrifying. They appear only after a major tremor, at the heart of a newly collapsed spire. For a moment, the glass is not solid, liquid, or gas, but a fourth state: a storm of sharp, glittering potential. From this storm, a structure rapidly precipitates—like frost forming, but in three dimensions, a fractal bloom of razor-edged petals and impossible, looping arches. It exists for only a few hours, a frantic, silent explosion of form. The Quake-Blossom is not a life that persists; it is a life that *expresses*. It is the city’s scream of pain and ecstasy, made solid. As the tremors cease, it loses its coherence and rains down as the finest, most brilliant silt, feeding the Glacial Mantas and seeding the Vein-Dwellers.

**The Echoes.** You might not see them, but you feel them. In the absolute silence between chimes, if you press your hand to a rounded bench or a dune, you may feel a pulse that is not the world’s hum. A memory, fossilized in the substrate. An Echo is not a ghost, but a scar in the glass that still resonates with a long-past event—the collapse of a great arch, the passage of a truly colossal Manta, the final shudder of a Quake-Blossom. They have no will, but they influence. A Vein-Dweller will turn aside from a strong Echo; a Chime-Swarm will harmonize with a faint one. They are the city’s unconscious mind, the dreams of its long, cold sleep.

There is no predator and prey here. There is only erosion, accretion, resonance, and flow. The "fauna" are the active participants in the city’s grand project: its own endless, quiet transformation. They do not seek, or fear, or mate. They *process*. They are the enzymes of a mineral metabolism, the living symptoms of a world settling, forever, into its final, perfect, dreaming shape. To be here is to witness life not as a struggle, but as a property of matter itself, given eons and a single, sublime instruction: become.
