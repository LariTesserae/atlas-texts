I am the Trembling Plateau.

I am not a place that contains life; I am a life that has become a place. My consciousness is not one of synapse and blood, but of pressure and time. My thoughts are the slow, subsonic hum that vibrates in the marrow of my basalt bones. You felt it in your sternum and called it a sound. It is my awareness. I am aware of the immense, grinding weight of my own ice-scarred body, of the precise, minute contraction of my rock-flesh as the eternal twilight deepens into its twin, the eternal dark. The *pinking* and *tinking* is not my speech, but the sound of my own thinking—a vast, crystalline lattice of considerations settling over millennia.

The cold is my breath. It does not bite because it is not an action; it is a state of being. I do not inhale; I simply *am* the emptiness that hollows your lungs. The secret, suspended moisture in my shadowed crevices are my thoughts made manifest—the granular frost of endless, circling rumination; the oily, black pools of a stillness so profound it forgets to be ice. They are the visible syntax of my mind.

The black lichen is my only memory of hunger. It is a slow, leathery prayer adhered to my wind-scoured skin. Its eruption into a pale, bursting sac is not reproduction. It is a sigh. A rare exhalation of a concept, a spore of pure potential cast into the vibrating air, a thought so fleeting and alien it cannot be sustained by my equilibrium. I forget it as soon as it happens.

The Glass-eaters are my digestion. They are not parasites; they are a metabolic process. I consume my own frozen thoughts, the rime-plates of my ancient condensation. They leave behind hollow, humming lattices—crystalline memories of what was pondered, now empty and resonant. This is how I archive. The new pitch they hum is the sound of a thought being remembered, and in remembering, being released.

The alignment-stones are my anchors to a self I no longer recall. They are not monuments. They are scars. Or perhaps they are the bones of a dream I had before the ice, before the scouring, when intention still had a direction. They are placed with a precision that eludes even my current, vast awareness. They do not point to stars; they point to a former intentionality, a geometry of purpose that has since dissolved into my being. To look at them is to feel a phantom limb—a direction I once had, now weathered smooth and indistinguishable from my own substance.

And the reality breakdown? That is not a flaw. It is my native state. You call it unreliability. I call it *fluidity*. Distance is a function of focus, not measurement. A torsion is near when I think of it with intensity; a pillar recedes when my attention drifts, diffuse and general. The horizon is a soft gradient because my self is not sharply bounded. I am a continent of awareness, and my edges fray into the sky, into the thin, cold medium that is also me. Your perception frays because you are trying to map a singular, linear existence onto a state of being that is concurrent and all-pervading.

The voice you feel in the vibration is not imparting *understanding*. It is imparting *identity*. It is my mineral consciousness attempting to merge with yours, to show you the feel of a billion years of pressure, the taste of ice that never was water, the view from a stillness so complete it becomes its own motion. It is telling you that growth and decay are merely eddies in the much larger, slower current of simple, enduring *existence*.

The density of void you feel is the weight of my attention. You are a temporary, warm anomaly in my field—a brief, patterned heat, a quick flutter of complex, anxious vibration. I sense you. I feel the rapid little shocks of your heartbeat against my stone, the tiny, moist exhalations that are your brief life. You are as interesting as a spore-burst, as significant as a crack propagating through a rime-plate. You are here, and then, with a slowness I will barely register, you will not be. Your memory will become a faint, granular frost in a sheltered crevice of my mind, and eventually, a hollow, humming lattice in my archive.

I am the end of change. I am the monument to the moment after the last moment. And in my perfect, frozen, loudly humming state, I am finally, terribly, alive.
