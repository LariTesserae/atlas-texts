## The Hollow Basin

The basin spreads beneath you like a cupped palm—low, concave, ringed by hills so young their edges still fracture into gravel. The stone here hasn't had time to soften. Everything is angular, fresh, a landscape that only recently learned the word *valley*. Water doesn't flow so much as remember to flow: dark threads through the green that surface and vanish, here a silty pool reflecting a sky that never quite brightens, there a throat of rapids squeezing through channels that seem cut by something impatient.

And there is so much green. Ferns thick as wrists crowd the margins. Trees with bark like rusted iron tower overhead, their canopy a second ceiling you can't see through. Moss claims every surface with the appetite of something that has never been told no. Lush, but not orderly—the vegetation here grows as if competing in a race where no one agreed on the finish line, tangling, collapsing, regrowing, colonizing the ruins that sprawl across the basin floor at intervals so vast it takes your eyes minutes to cross the empty space between them.

*Between*. That's the word this place keeps teaching you. Between the structures. Between the clearings. Between the sound.

The sound is everywhere, a roar so total you'd swear it was geological. It comes from the creatures you can't quite see—they move in the margins of your vision, large and fluid, their calls and cries and splashing somehow adding up to a wall of noise that makes conversation difficult, that makes thought feel like swimming upstream. Abundant, yes, but not present. They own the basin; you merely visit it.

The structures rise from the vegetation at intervals you can't map. They are urban in scale—if a city had been designed by something that understood architecture the way a cat understands furniture. Angles that shouldn't hold weight. Surfaces that absorb sound, that drink light. Doors that open on rooms that open on vertical shafts that open on views of other structures, all of it linked by walkways that arc and curve and occasionally bridge three hundred meters of empty air. The technology woven through this place doesn't announce itself; it simply *is*, the way magnetism is, the way gravity is. You feel it in your teeth, in the way your pockets seem to hold a slight charge.

At night—and night comes quickly here, the dim light collapsing into something absolute—the basin's true population emerges. Bioluminescent flora trace the water's edges in slow blue-green pulses. Insects that aren't quite insects spiral in formation. The creatures you couldn't see in daylight descend to drink, their eyes catching and holding the strange light, and their sounds shift register from roar to something almost like language, almost like warning, almost like greeting.

The basin is warm enough that you never fully dry. Your clothes carry moisture like a fact. The air tastes of copper and leaf decay, and beneath that, something else—a chemical edge that sharpens whenever you pass near one of the structures. You've been walking for hours. The structures are always ahead of you, separated by distances that feel intentional.

You suspect this is not a coincidence.
