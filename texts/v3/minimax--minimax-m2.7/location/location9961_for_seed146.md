# The Bone Vaults

The tunnels do not echo. This is the first thing you learn: sound here is swallowed by walls that rise too far to see, by ceilings lost somewhere in the upper dark. The air is warm and still, the warmth rising from deep stone, and it tastes of nothing at all—not dust, not metal, not the mineral tang of water that isn't there. There is no water. There has never been water. The tunnels simply *are*, carved into a geology so young that the walls still hold their sharpness, their angles still unyielding to time.

Fauna exists here, but it moves wrong.

They call them **skitters**, the scholars who still make the descent, though no scholar has returned in forty years. Skitters are the size of your hand, pale and jointless, their bodies a kind of softite that grows rather than forms—mineral deposits that thickened and shaped themselves into something that *moves*. They cluster in the alcoves where the faintest light pools, bioluminescent fungi growing in filament-whorls that the skitters graze upon without mouths, absorbing light-eccrines through membrane-skin. The fungi don't photosynthesize. Nothing here does. They metabolize heat-gradient differentials, slowly, over centuries, the way a crystal grows. The skitters consume them anyway, and grow, and are numerous.

You walk for three days through the Bone Vaults before you stop counting alcoves. The scale defeats you—tunnel-tributaries branching and rejoining, galleries that open into vaulted spaces larger than cities, all of it unlit except for the slow, pale glow of the graze-clusters. Your lamp eats the dark at the edges, but the dark eats your lamp at the center. Somewhere ahead, or behind, or beside—the tunnels fold in directions you cannot map—the skitters are moving in currents, the way iron filings align to a field. You cannot see them, but you hear them. Not sound. Pressure. The faintest increment of weight against the air, like a held breath.

The builders left markers.

Not builders, exactly. The structures are not buildings—they are *shapes*, carved into the living stone with a tool-sense that predates your ability to read it. Walls that serve no wall-function. Arches that lead nowhere. Steles arranged in patterns that might be language or might be ornament or might be nothing at all, because the beings who made them were not using your kind of thought. Primitive technology, the surveys said, and that is technically true: these shapes were made by hands, by simple tools, by force applied over time. But the shapes *mean* something, and what they mean has nothing to do with shelter or storage or any purpose you can parse. You walk past them, and the skitters are denser here, and the graze-fungi grow taller, and the silence is absolute.

The warmth does not change. The dark does not thin. The skitters do not look at you because they have no eyes, and if they sense you, they give no sign, and if they fear you, they have no way to show it. You are the most dynamic thing in the Bone Vaults, and you are passing through like a dream.

At the edge of a gallery larger than memory, you find a pool.

It is not water. It is amber-sap, or it is pitch, or it is something your language has no word for—slow, dense, faintly luminescent at its center where something *is*, something that has been still for so long that stillness has become its only state. The skitters do not approach it. The fungi do not grow near it. Around it, the carved shapes are older here, worn smooth, and you understand finally that the builders came *before* the tunnels, that they shaped this place as a frame for whatever is pooling in its heart.

You do not touch it.

You walk on. The tunnels go deeper. The warmth rises from below, patient and absolute. Far away, or nowhere, or inside your own skull, something that is not a voice says: *nothing here has ever changed*.

And it is right.
