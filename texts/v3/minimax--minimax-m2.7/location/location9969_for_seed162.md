High above the clouds, a plateau tilts slowly toward a horizon that never quite stays where you left it.

The air is warm despite the altitude — warm and close, weighted with moisture that comes and goes without warning. Rain falls somewhere over the eastern ridge. Here, a stone trough holds a dark mirror of water that smells of copper and machine oil; three hundred meters east, the rock is dry as ash. The dampness moves through this place like something breathing, pooling in the creases of young terrain: gullies so fresh their walls still hold their fracture lines, their angles sharp enough to cut shadow.

Vegetation grows where the water gathers, a lushness that seems borrowed from somewhere more forgiving. Broad-leaved things with dark veins, trees whose roots grip the stone like fingers, ferns that uncurl in the perpetual dim. They cluster along the watercourses and thin toward the ridges, a green that deepens to black at the canopy. Where they mass, the air smells of resin and something else — something faintly acrid, the ghost of industrial atmospheres, though no factory stands here, no chimney pours smoke into this vast. The tech is not built; it permeates. The plateau's own systems run on mechanisms invisible, humming at frequencies the ear only catches in the silences between rain.

Fauna moves through this green in shifting abundance. Birds with iridescent muzzles and too-long legs. Things in the water that are not fish — that are not anything, that rearrange as you look at them. Insects that click in clusters, their sound a faint machinery of wings and jaws. The animals do not flee; they watch. They are numerous enough that you feel watched from all directions, the density of presence near-touching even in the dim.

For this is vast beyond the body. A continent of stone and verdure, its edges lost in haze or distance or the warping of the eye. Weather forms here, builds and releases, its own weather, indifferent to anything beyond the plateau's edges. The scale is its own physics. You could walk for days and find the geology unchanged: the same young formations, the same sharp gullies, the same dim light pressing down through whatever medium — dust, vapor, some industrial suspension — makes the air glow amber at evening without quite illuminating it.

And the weirdness. The way the ridge to the north seems to fold. The water in the stone trough, still when you approach, begins to move when you look away. Shadows fall at angles that do not match the light source. The vegetation grows not upward but toward something; the trees lean, and not from wind. There are moments when the geometry of the place reasserts itself, and you feel the shock of a world obeying its own rules, and then something shifts — a crack of light that is not lightning, a sound that is not thunder — and the rules become suggestions, then memories.

The plateau is mostly quiet. The rain on the stone makes small sounds, the insects make small sounds, and beneath it all a low vibration that might be the earth's own pulse or the hum of the invisible tech that saturates this place like groundwater. But the quiet is unstable. Turbulent. It can break open at any moment into that other state — the bazaar, the anthill, the storm that does not pass but builds and builds toward something you do not want to be present for when it arrives.

You are not supposed to be here. Nothing was built for you. But you are here, and the plateau is vast, and it is changing, and the horizon has already moved.
