# The Drowned Pocket

The water here has no patience for edges. It seeps, it pools, it fills every hollow with a black that seems to breathe. Not ocean—ocean has weight, has pressure, has the memory of tides. This is something else: a saturation, a wetness that has forgotten how to be anything else. Everything is wet. Everything is held in suspension, in the slow suspension of something that stopped pretending to be solid long ago.

The chamber—if it is a chamber—is barely larger than a clenched fist. A pocket in the stone, or a crack that widened into something, and then flooded, and then became this. The scale is wrong. It should feel small, claustrophobic, but instead it feels *full*—so packed with presence, with water, with the dark shapes of root and stone and something you cannot quite name, that size ceases to mean anything. You are inside a closed fist. You are inside a word. You are inside something that does not know it contains you.

The stone is young. That is the strangest thing, the thing that shouldn't matter but does: the walls are sharp. Unweathered. The angles where they meet the water's edge are crisp and sudden, unmollified by centuries of patient erosion. This space was carved recently—minutes ago, or a thousand years, or outside of time altogether—and no one has come to smooth its edges. The built shapes that rise from the water (three, four, five of them, scattered) are older than the stone around them, or perhaps they are not shapes at all, merely impressions. Marks. The suggestion of structure: a low wall, a partial arch, a flat surface that might have been a threshold. They protrude only enough to break the surface tension, tiny islands where the dark water dimples and goes still.

Above—though above is a direction you're not certain applies here—roots descend. They come from somewhere that is not visible, threading through the darkness, thick as veins, pale as bone. They do not float; they *hang*, impossibly, in the thick wet air. The vegetation is lush in the way of things that have never seen light: dense, pale, seeking. Tendrils curl along the stone, rootlets press into cracks, and the whole space feels colonized, consumed, made into a garden by something that gardens without knowing it. The plants do not move. Nothing moves. The water does not flow; it simply *is*, held in the particular stillness of deep places, of places where time has become discouraged.

Fauna is rare, which is a relief. You do not want to think of what lives here. Once, perhaps, you saw something—a flash, a tightening, a motion that was faster than the water should allow—but it is gone now, and you are grateful. Better the quiet. Better the slow, patient darkness, the weight of water that feels less like pressure and more like attention.

The weirdness is not dramatic. That is what makes it unbearable. The water should cloud with your breath, but it does not. The roots should cast shadows, but the shadows fall in directions the roots do not occupy. When you look directly at the scattered structures, they are shapes; when you look away, they are something else, something that fits more naturally in your peripheral vision. You are inside a space that does not fully commit to existing. It is not hidden from you—you can see it clearly, see every detail—but it is hidden from itself, a pocket of the world that has forgotten the rules and is slowly, slowly trying to remember them.

The cool settles into you. Not cold—cold would be simple—but cool, the temperature of deep water, of cellars, of places where breath is audible and heartbeats are too loud. The submersion means that everything you see is wet, everything you touch is wet, every surface is membrane-thin, permeable. You could drown here. You have already drowned here, perhaps. This is the after: the settled water, the patient darkness, the sharp young stone cradling the drowned pocket of a world that does not know you're there. It does not know anything. It simply persists, slow and full, waiting for erosion to catch up, waiting for light to find it, waiting for you to leave or stay or dissolve.

The water does not ripple when you move. It *accepts*.
