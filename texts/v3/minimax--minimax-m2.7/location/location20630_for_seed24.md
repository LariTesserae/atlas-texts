A dry riverbed runs through the space like a scar, its stones bleached pale by ages without rain. No water has moved through here in so long that what remain are not stones but shapes — the memory of stones, smoothed and rounded by water that left before anything else arrived to remember it. The sediment here is fine, rust-colored, the color of oxidized iron. Walking across it would leave prints that might persist for a very long time.

The terrain is low and cupped, as though the whole space sits in a shallow bowl that collects nothing, holds nothing, only exposes everything to the warm and still air. Low ridges of dark rock break the surface at wide intervals — volcanic or metasedimentary, impossible to say at a glance, their surfaces dark and dense and deeply scored by time. The grooves in them are not fresh. They have been cut and recut by windblown particles over so many cycles that the marks have become natural, part of the stone's structure rather than damage to it. The rock has accepted this. It is in balance with what has happened to it.

Sparse vegetation clings to the crevices where the rock breaks. Small, hard-leaved plants — not quite succulent but close, tough and narrow-leaved, the kind that hoard moisture in cells and wait. They are spaced far apart. You could walk a hundred meters and find only one. They are green-brown, nearly dormant in the warmth, their roots reaching deep into joints where the stone meets darker stone below. They do not bloom. They do not move.

The light is dim — the space seems to be in a low basin surrounded by higher ground that blocks the sky, or perhaps it is simply always this way, a permanent dusk or filtered atmosphere. The darkness is not complete but it is deep. Shapes at the edges of the space are uncertain. The warm air holds a weight to it, still and heavy, and there is no sound beyond what you make yourself. No insects. No wind. The silence is not empty but full — a presence of its own, the sense of a space that has settled so completely into itself that it has forgotten any other state.

A few stone tools lie half-buried in the sediment. A hand-axe, a scraper, their edges worn but their forms still readable. Early work. Someone stood here once, held one of these, and left. There is no structure, no arrangement, no sign that any of it meant anything beyond use. The tools are simply part of the sediment now, slowly joining the stones that have been here since before anything else.

This is a place that has arrived at itself. Everything that could erode has eroded. Everything that could move has moved. The basin holds its shape, the rocks hold their grooves, and the warm dark silence holds its weight. Nothing changes here because there is nothing left to change.
