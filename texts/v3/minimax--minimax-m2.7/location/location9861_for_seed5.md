The light arrives wrong — too evenly, too constantly, with no visible source. It fills the hidden courtyard and falls without angle, illuminating every surface with the same unwavering clarity, pooling in the shallow puddles that never quite evaporate, running in faint threads along the grooves worn into the cobblestones by ten thousand years of feet. The walls that contain this space are ancient, their stone darkened by centuries of damp, covered in thick moss that clings to every crack and crevice. Ferns press from between the paving stones. Flowering plants — small, low, with blooms I don't have names for — cascade from the tops of walls in thick curtains of blue and purple. Root systems lace beneath the cobblestones, holding everything in a grip that has calcified into something like permanence.

This is a courtyard — a pocket, intimate and contained, the size perhaps of a large room in some other kind of building. The settlement it belongs to is old and built-up, layers of construction from epochs that blur together, stone and timber and woven reed, primitive tools evident in every joint and seam. The architecture is dense, packed with structures: low platforms, a fountain long dry but still holding water in its deepest basin, storage jars, tables, low walls, roofed alcoves supported by wooden posts that have somehow not rotted in the cold and damp. The wood is pale and hard, the grain still sharp. No one has touched it since the last person passed through, but the absence is not decay — the structures are *inhabited*, reclaimed by other presences without being consumed.

The silence is what strikes first, given the abundance. There should be sound: insects, small fauna, the drip of water, the creak of old wood. But the silence holds. The cold holds. Everything holds. The air is damp and still, the light flat and even, the life dense and motionless, an ecology that has reached an equilibrium so complete it no longer needs to change. This is not a ruin. It is not a garden. It is a place that has finished becoming itself and now simply *is*, held in a stillness so perfect it feels like the held breath of something that has forgotten how to exhale.
