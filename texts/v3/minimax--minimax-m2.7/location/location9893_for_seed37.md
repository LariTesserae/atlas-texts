# The Damp Below

A hollow carved from wet sandstone, still unfinished — the walls here are raw, grainy, soft in ways stone should not be soft. The erosion has barely begun its work. You could scrape this rock with a fingernail. It will hold its shape for now, but the edges where moisture meets mineral are already beginning their slow collapse into something else.

Water moves through it in three ways: as condensation beaded on the low ceiling, weeping down in single drops that catch the light; as a narrow channel threading the floor, shallow enough to step across but perennial, never drying; and as damp itself, saturating the lower courses of stone until they darken like wet cardboard. The hollow sits below the surrounding ground — a pocket, an accidental room, open at one side to the sky's light which enters bright and wrong-angle, as if the sun here falls at an improper slant.

The light is the first wrong thing. It should be dark here, underground, but the geometry refuses. Light spills in from somewhere above and to the side — a mouth, perhaps, or a collapsed section revealing a wedge of pale sky. It illuminates the space in a way that feels like looking at something through the wrong end of a glass. The shadows fall backward. The bright patches glow without warmth.

Sparse vegetation: tufts of something pale and rootless clinging to the damper walls, fruiting bodies that emerge overnight and vanish before noon, and one stunted thing with leaves like wet paper that grows from a crack and will not die.

Abundant fauna. This is the second wrong thing. The hollow should be empty — it's too small, too new, too raw — yet it moves. Creatures of uncertain taxonomy: jointed things with too many legs, pale and eyeless; soft-bodied masses that cling to the wet stone and pulse slowly; something that might be insect or might be seed-pod, filling the air with its drift. They do not flee. They have always lived here. The hollow was always here. These are the facts, and the facts are not negotiable, and yet.

The small structures: half-buried fragments of something — not stone, not bone, something between — half-visible in the walls like teeth in a jaw. They do not look carved. They look grown. They might be natural formations. The geology here is too young to tell. Everything here is too young to tell.

Silence in the way of held breath. The water does not babble. The creatures do not chirp or click or sing. They move and they exist and they are quiet, as though this space exists in a moment before sound learned how to happen, or after it forgot.

And the dampness. The dampness that is not quite water. The mineral taste of it. The way it gathers in the corners of this pocket-cavern and does not evaporate despite the light. The way it feels, when you touch the walls, like something is weeping through the rock.

A threshold place. A soft spot in the world. You are inside something that is still deciding what it wants to be. The creatures watch you from their damp corners, patient, knowing that you are the temporary thing here, that you will leave, that they will remain to witness whatever this place becomes when it finishes becoming.
