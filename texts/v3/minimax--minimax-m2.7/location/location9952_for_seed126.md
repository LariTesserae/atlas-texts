# The Made Plain

The ground goes on forever, and it is not ground. It is floor.

You understand this only gradually — the way understanding comes in places where there is nothing to compare. The surface is mineral but not stone. It is substance shaped by intention rather than accident, a vast sheet of built material that has been settling into itself since before the present age of the world. Under your feet it is neither warm nor cold. It is *temperatured* — held at a precise warmth that neither rises nor falls, though the sun's light falls bright and slanted across the entire length of the visible world.

The terrain is low, nearly flat, but not quite. There are rises and shallow basins so gradual they only declare themselves at the horizon, where the eye discovers it has been descending without knowing, or climbing by increments so small the body never registers the effort. These are not dunes, not the natural contours of eroded stone. They are the traces of foundations. Here, once, something enormous rested. Here, once, a weight bore down so long and so evenly that the floor itself took the impression. Now the impressions are all that remain — the last memory of structures that were raised and lowered and raised again across spans of time that would make the word *geological* feel hasty.

In the distance, geometry. The faintest edges, barely visible against the flatness, where one built plane meets another at an angle too subtle for the eye to catch from close up but suddenly clear from across a great basin. Everything was made. Every contour, every shallow rise, every faint line of mineral meeting mineral. The substrate is artificial through and through — but it has been artificial so long that erosion has done its patient work. What was sharp is now softened. What was exact is now approximate. The builders' precision has been overtaken by time, and the world has settled, as all things settle, into its final and most stable form.

There is no water. The environment is sealed, or the water has been drawn away, or it was never here at all but in some other state — bound, perhaps, in the lattice of the built material itself. The surface is dry and slightly textured, not smooth, carrying a fine pattern like the skin of something crystallized or grown. The warmth is constant and even, a warmth that does not admit night, that does not permit seasonal shift. It is warm the way a body is warm: maintained. Kept. This is a climate that was set once and has been running on its own ever since.

Sparse life — but life. Small things. Things you might mistake for mineral formations until you see them move: slow, chemical pulses across a crystal surface; a faint luminescence where something eats the built stone and digests it into light. The creatures are rare and isolated, clinging to the few seams where the floor still holds some trace of what it once held. They move in slow motion, their metabolisms stretched thin by the thinness of everything here. A predator and its prey, separated by a basin, each tracking the other's chemical signature across days.

The built density is not visible from any single vantage, but you feel it — the sense of many presences close at hand, just beyond perception. Layers. Near-touching. The material under you is not a single surface but an architecture, a network of passages and chambers and conduits so vast and so old that they have become indistinguishable from geology. Within the lattice, things still move. A faint vibration underfoot, slower than a heartbeat. A thermal pulse, spreading through the built material in directions you cannot map. The whole plain is threaded with slow process, with mechanisms continuing their ancient functions at the pace of centuries.

There is sound, but barely. The world is a whisper. A low tone, almost below hearing, that seems to come from everywhere at once. The settling of vast material. The hum of processes that have been running since before your kind existed. When you speak, your voice goes flat and dies quickly, absorbed by the mineral surface rather than reflected. The air itself is still, unstirred by any wind, but not empty. It carries that faint tone, and something else — a chemical warmth, a residue of whatever the builders used to shape this place into existence. The atmosphere is not thick, but it is present, a skin of held gases that does the work of an atmosphere without moving.

Far out across the made plain, something catches the light differently. A different texture, a different angle. Built structures that have not yet fully eroded into flatness, rising like the bones of something that was very large and has given itself back to the floor. Around them, the faintest shimmer: air heated by processes still occurring within. The structures are not hollow now — they are filled with the slow work of continuing function, and the function makes heat, and the heat makes the air tremble, and the tremble is the strangest thing in a landscape that has settled so deeply into stillness.

You stand on it and it holds you. It was made to be held.
