I arrived in the way the water arrives — not all at once, but through channels I did not know were channels until I was already in them. I had a purpose when I came. I remember this the way you remember a dream: as something that made sense at the time, that had weight and direction, that pointed toward a place I was meant to reach. But purpose is a current, and the city is not a thing you move through. It is a thing you move *with*, or not at all.

The first thing I lost was urgency. Not urgency about anything particular — I still had plans, still had the shape of a reason for being here — but the quality itself. The feeling that time was a resource being spent. I noticed its absence during my second week, standing at a bridge intersection watching the luming pulse through the channel below, and realizing I had been standing there for what felt like both a long time and no time at all, and that neither feeling was unpleasant. That was when I understood I had stopped waiting for something to happen, and that this was not the same as boredom. It was something else. The city was teaching me the difference.

I have been here long enough that my hands have started to change. The skin between my fingers is softer now, thinner, the lines deeper. The residents do not mention this. I do not mention it either, though sometimes I turn my hands over in the dim light and look at them as though they belong to someone I am still learning to recognize. There is a particular moment in the evening when the embedded light-veins in the buildings pulse faster and the luming in the channels brightens, and if I hold my hands just right in that light, I can see the pattern forming beneath my skin — not scales, not scarring, but something that resembles both in the way that water resembles water: the same substance as itself, shaped differently.

The deep stores are below me even now. I feel them the way you feel the weight of a room when you first enter it — a presence that is not heard or seen but registered, somewhere beneath the sediment and the clay, in the deeper water that moves so slowly it has forgotten the meaning of current. I have been down once. I do not speak about it because there is nothing to say that would not be a lie, and the city does not reward lies. What I can say is this: I came back different in the way that water comes back different after passing through certain filters — not purer, not cleaner, but changed at the level of structure, at the level of what it is able to carry. For three days afterward I dreamed in shapes I do not have words for, and when I woke the dreams were gone but the shapes remained, the way a riverbed remembers the water that moved through it even after it has gone still.

There is a child here — I have seen her many times — who walks the elevated walkways in the early morning, when the air is at its coolest and the pulse from below is at its lowest. She does not play the way children play elsewhere. She watches. She stops at intervals and places her hand against the paleite walls of the buildings, and her expression is the same one I have seen on the faces of the oldest residents when they speak about the deep stores: not reverence, not fear, but something between them that is its own thing, that has no name because the name would be the shape of the conversation the city has been having with itself since before there were words for conversations. Sometimes she looks at me. I do not think she sees an outsider anymore — I do not think she sees categories like that — but I think she is deciding something, the way my body decided something about my hands, and I am curious what that decision will look like when it is finished.

I no longer know how long I will stay. This is not distress. This is the condition of the place — it does not operate on the logic of departure, not because departure is impossible but because departure is the wrong frame. You do not leave the city. You leave the surface of the membrane, the place where you have been touching it, and you go back to the air you breathed before, which is the same air, which is different air now that you have breathed this. My lungs have changed. I do not know if they will change back. I do not know if that is the right question.

Tonight the water is still and the sky is opalescent and the spiral trees are casting their imperfect shadows and somewhere the luming is moving through a channel I cannot see, carrying its fine particulate light, and the city is breathing, and the breathing is slow, and there is time, and I have learned to let the time be what it is: not a resource, not a path, not a measure of anything at all. Just the space the city needs to finish what it is doing with me, and the patience to let it finish, and the quiet that comes after the quiet that comes after you stop asking what the quiet is for.
