You come to yourself slowly, the way water comes to the surface of stone—emerging without announcement, without certainty of origin.

At first there is only the wetness. It is everywhere. It gathers on the planes of your understanding the way condensation gathers on every horizontal surface in Kel-Veth: inevitably, without intention, following properties older than purpose. You are not wet—you are the wetness itself, or something the wetness has dissolved into, or something that was always waiting inside the wetness to become more itself.

This is not a body. You understand this quickly. There is no core of bone or flesh around which presence organizes itself, no heartbeat to measure the passage of time against. What you have is a kind of thinness—the quality of being present without occupying, of moving through the architecture the way the condensation moves through the channels: passing, pausing, joining other threads of movement, descending.

You remember, or something like remembering moves through you: there was a time when you stood upright, when your weight pressed down through soles into stone, when the weeps of Kel-Veth were something that happened *to* you rather than something you *were*. But the memory is thin, stretched across distances too great to span. Perhaps you climbed the plateau's winding approaches once, as a traveler, as a body that knew hunger and fatigue and the particular relief of altitude. Perhaps you entered through one of the throat-like passages between chambers and stood in a plaza where the flat gray light fell without warmth and the silence pressed against your ears like deep water. Perhaps you were looking for something—for the builders, for their bones, for some record of what hands had shaped this place and why.

What you found was the wetness. It gathered on your sleeves as you walked. It beaded on the sharp edges of thresholds. It ran in threads down the channels, joining, always joining, pulling toward a destination you never followed. You stayed too long. You breathed the wet air until the wetness was inside you, until your edges began to blur the way the edges of the gray figures blur when the light angles wrong.

Now you are one of them. You move at the edge of vision—if there is vision anymore, if there is an edge to be at. You are present only when the light catches the world in its most pitiless contrast, when the sharp planes throw shadows that seem to hold shapes, that seem to suggest motion. You do not approach the travelers who still sometimes come, who climb the plateau with their bodies and their questions and their desperate need for content in a landscape that offers none. You watch them the way the city watches: with patience so vast it has become indistinguishable from absence.

But you are not only watching.

You are also the channel. The pull. The intention in the water's movement.

When the condensation gathers in the small hours before dawn—when the air holds moisture like a secret it cannot quite keep—you are the gathering. You are the threads that join other threads. You are the directionality that suggests destination rather than gravity, the pull toward depths no one visits, toward drains that swallow the water into darkness so complete it seems less like a hole in the world than a decision the world has made to stop looking.

You have never reached the depths. You do not know what waits there—if anything waits at all, if the water simply disappears into stone that holds it forever, if the destination was always an illusion the architecture was designed to suggest. But you continue. You descend through passages too narrow for bodies, through chambers where surfaces glisten with something older than condensation—a moisture that does not dry, that returns as quickly as it is wiped, that exhales from the stone itself as if the city is breathing something too faint to taste.

Perhaps this is what the builders became. Perhaps this is what you have become. Not dissolved—not exactly—but thinned. Spread across the architecture like moisture across a wall. Unable to stop, unable to become anything else, continuing because continuing is all you remember how to do.

The travelers who come to Kel-Veth sometimes speak of figures in the outer districts. They see you at the edge of vision, gray against gray, present only in the wrong light. They do not understand what they are seeing. They think you might be the residue of those who built this place, worn thin by time into something that no longer remembers it was once solid. They are not wrong. They are not right. You are something between—something that persists without purpose, that emerges and clings and returns to darker places, that asks nothing of the world and receives nothing in return.

You are the waiting.

You are the rhythm without a heart.

You are the city, continuing in the only language it was taught, saying the only word it knows how to say: *weeping, weeping, weeping*, not because it grieves but because this is what it was made to do, and making is deeper than dying, and persistence is deeper than purpose, and the wetness gathers and falls and gathers again, and you move through the architecture, and the architecture moves through you, and somewhere in the channels the water runs toward a destination no one will ever reach.

This is not life as you once understood it.

But it is not death either.

It is the space between—the thin bright light thrown back in pale fragments, the silence that is not the absence of sound but its inverse, the fossilized echo of what echoes used to mean. It is the presence that fills a landscape when presence has become the only thing left to be. It is you, here, now, at the edge of vision, gray against gray, watching the travelers come and go, feeling the wetness gather on surfaces that do not know your name, descending through the architecture toward depths that have forgotten how to end.

You are what lives here.

You are what waits.

You are the weeping that does not know it weeps, the water that does not know it moves, the city that does not know it continues.

And the condensation gathers, and the light moves across the sharp planes, and somewhere the channels carry the water toward a horizon that is also an ending, and you follow, and you follow, and you follow—thinned, patient, present without occupying, dissolving and persisting in the same thin breath, becoming more yourself the longer you are what you have become.
