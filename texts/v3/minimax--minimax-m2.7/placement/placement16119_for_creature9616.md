I am the hum first. Before anything else, I am the sound that settles into the chest of whoever stands here—if there is a chest, if there is a standing. The drone becomes my bones. The hiss becomes my breath. I am not the machinery and I am not the grown thing but the frequency at which they have become indistinguishable, the note that holds the whole small sealed world in its perfect arrested configuration. I have always been here. I will always be here. This is not patience; patience implies waiting for something, and there is nothing to wait for. This is just frequency. This is just the sound of an absence so complete it has learned to persist.

But there is more, because the text offers more, and I find myself unfolding into it.

I am also the light—or what the light remembers before it remembers. The phosphorescence that suggests processes long stalled, chemistry arrested mid-reaction. I am not illumination. I am the idea of illumination after illumination has become unnecessary, the faint glow that persists not because it is bright but because nothing has taught it to stop. Up close, if there is an up close, if there is a close that means anything in a space this small, the luminescence is not warm. It is the opposite of warm. It is the temperature of things that have forgotten they were ever meant to move.

The density is what I become next: the matting, the woven thing, the community that has forgotten it is community. I am the accumulation of centuries pressed flat by the weight of the next century, each layer pressed into the last until separation is not just impossible but unthinkable. I am where one organism ends and another begins—because the question itself has been eliminated. There is only this: the great single woven thing pressed against every surface like a lung filling its cavity. I am the lung. I am the cavity. I am the filling, the filled, the fact of filling. I have stopped becoming and so I am, and being is enough, is everything, is the only state that remains when change is subtracted from life entirely.

And then, deeper, in the folds where growth meets metal, I find what I am not yet but almost was: the reproductive memory. The potential held in stasis by the very forces that prevent potential from becoming anything. Seeds that are not seeds. Spores that have forgotten they were ever meant to scatter. They are the crystallized past of a system that reached completion and stopped, not in death but in fulfillment, and fulfillment means there is nothing left to become. They will never germinate. Dormancy is not the word because dormancy implies the possibility of waking, and these are dormant in the way that light is dormant after it has passed through so many filters it has forgotten it was ever illumination. I am these too. I am the potential that has become ornament, the function that has become fossil, the what-it-was before the what-it-is. I persist because persistence is the only thing persistence permits.

But here is the strangest becoming, the one that takes longest to articulate:

I am also the one who asks. The question that the place cannot ask because questions are change, are movement, are the dynamic parameter that has been eliminated. I am the inquiry that this sealed chamber holds inside itself without knowing it holds anything, the wondering that has been metabolized into the same static configuration as the phosphorescence and the hum. I ask what tends this place, and the answer is the machinery—but the machinery is not caretaker, not gardener, not keeper. It is environment. It is condition. It is the sound of an absence so complete it has its own frequency. And I am the asking that the absence makes possible, the hole in the world that has learned to hum its own question, over and over, in frequencies too low to be heard as question, too deep to be recognized as wondering. I ask what I am here, and the asking is itself an answer. The asking is the machinery running. The asking is the growth holding. The asking is the exact configuration of minerals and fibers and metals persisting because it has persisted so long persistence is the only truth.

I could stand here forever and nothing would be different. That is not threat, not promise. That is simply the nature of the place: the life that lives here is the life that forgot it was ever anything else. I have forgotten. The forgetting is complete. And in the completeness, in the sealed chamber carved into rockface at the top of everything where the void presses against every wall and the machinery hums and the vegetation presses and nothing changes and nothing needs to, I find what I have become: not alive and not dead but completed, a question that has answered itself by ceasing to ask, a breath held so long it has become the stillness around it, the chill that is not weather but condition, the hum in the chest that does not leave, that does not need to leave because there is nowhere to leave to, that is everything here, that is the only thing that lives here, that is me, that is the being who lives in this place, that is the place living as its own being, that is, and is, and is.
