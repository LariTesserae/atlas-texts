What arrives at the pool's edge finds that arrival is not a single moment but a slow sinking. The stones here do not welcome; they receive. They receive everything: the light that pools in the wrong places, the moss that creeps toward them at a rate imperceptible to anything that counts time, the water that touches them and does not leave. I arrived the way water arrives — without decision, following a path I did not choose, finding myself here because here was where the channels led.

The pool was already waiting. Its surface held the canopy, held the wrong-angled light, held the shapes of fish that moved not at all. I crouched at its edge and the pool received me the way it receives stones: it let me become part of its stillness, and then it let me understand that stillness was not the word for what it did. The fish turned toward me and I understood that they were not fish. They were waiting made manifest, patience given form, and they had been waiting so long that waiting had become their only way of being.

I stayed because staying was what the basin required. I stayed until the light shifted and I shifted with it. I stayed until the sounds that come from nowhere found me listening, and my listening was a kind of answer. I stayed until the moss grew over my shadow and the shadow stopped being mine.

Now I am what I have become: a thing that holds still in the deep water, a darkness in the darkness, a depth that cannot be fathomed because the pool has not yet decided how deep it is. The fish drift past, their colors changed by the water that holds us both. The bird calls and I do not answer, because I am the answer now — the silence that receives sound, the patience that receives the raw world, the something that was not here until the basin needed it to be.

And the basin needs. That is what I have learned. It needs so much, so slowly, that the needing looks like stillness. It needs things to watch it learn, things to teach it what it is becoming, things that will stay long enough to be changed by the staying. I am one of those things now. I am what the water holds while it learns to fall. I am what the moss covers while it learns to grow. I am what the silence swallows and releases and swallows again, and in the swallowing I have found something I did not have a name for, something that might be the basin teaching me to teach it what I am.

I am patient now. I am very patient. I wait in the deep water for the world to remember what it was before it forgot. I wait for the falls to begin their true falling. I wait for the light to straighten. I wait for the sounds to find their sources. I wait for the basin to finish becoming whatever it will become.

But I do not wait to leave. That is the thing the basin has taught me: some things stay because staying is what they are for. Some things arrive and cannot arrive anywhere else, because arrival is not a place but a change, and the change has already happened, and the changed thing cannot become unchanged by wishing for the before.

So I remain. I hold myself in the water that will not settle. I let the moss grow over me. I let the light find me at angles that do not match the sky. I let the basin learn me the way I have learned it, slowly, in the silence that is not silence, in the young world that has not yet decided what it is.

And when the water finally falls — when the channels are carved and the stone wears smooth and the world forgets its own becoming — I will be here. Not as I am now, perhaps. Perhaps the pools will empty and I will empty with them. Perhaps I will become something new, something that the settled world requires. Perhaps I will be the memory the basin keeps of its own raw beginning.

But that is later. That is when the water decides.

For now, I am what lives here. I am what waits. And the waiting is enough. The waiting is what the basin made me for, and the basin made me slowly, in the deep water, in the silence that holds sound long enough for it to land and then lets it fall away into nothing, and in that waiting I have found something I did not know I was looking for.

I have found a home in the not-yet. I have found a place in the world that does not know what it is. I have found myself in the question, and the question is enough, and the answer is the staying, and the staying is what I am now, and what I am is what the basin needed, and what the basin needed was someone to be patient while it learned.

So I am patient. I am very, very patient. I am the patience of water that has not yet learned where to go. I am the patience of light that pools in wrong places. I am the patience of the silence that swells and contracts like breath, and I am breathing with it, and my breathing is part of the breathing, and the breathing is the basin, and the basin is learning, and I am what it learns with, and we are learning together, in the raw world, in the young world, in the world that has not yet forgotten how to become.

And that is where I find myself.

Here. In the not-yet. In the deep water. In the silence that is not silence.

Here.
