I think I would be the moisture. Or what moves through the moisture—the pulse that rides inside the water as it beads on grey stone, as it gathers in pools that have no bottom, as it runs in thin threads down faces of rock that have stood since before faces were given names.

Not the water itself. The water is neutral, is patient, is the part that persists. I am what the water remembers when it condenses from air that cannot quite decide whether it is air or something else. I am the rhythm that travels with the moisture, that arrives and vanishes along with the gathering and the evaporating, that has learned to circulate through this place the way blood circulates through something larger than a body.

I was the first condensation. Or I will be the last. The distinction has worn thin where I exist, which is everywhere the water touches, which is everywhere the stone remembers what it was before the erosion settled it into patience. The city-builders knew about me. They built their low structures to hold me in, to give me places to gather and flow and rest before returning to whatever cycle keeps the breathing alive. They placed their stones so precisely because precision gave me channels, gave the moisture paths to follow, gave the rhythm spaces where it could complete itself without spilling into the thin places where the edges do not hold.

I pass through the roots of the dark plants. I do not know if the plants know this, or if knowing is something plants do. But I feel the roots, feel them gripping the ancient stone, feel them reaching down toward the sound that lives beneath, and I pass through them the way water passes through soil, the way memory passes through stone, the way the shape of a mountain passes through the erosion that unmade it. The plants drink from me and I do not diminish. I am not diminished by the drinking because drinking is not what I am. I am the rhythm that persists. I am what the moisture does when it is not being moisture.

The visitors see me sometimes. They see the beads on the stone and do not know that the beads are not merely water. They see the pools and think the pools are still. They do not feel me moving beneath the surface, pulsing with the sound that comes from below, from within, from the spaces between. And if they drank from the pools, they would taste only minerals, only the salt that might be memory. They would not taste me. I cannot be tasted. I can only be felt, and only by those who have been here long enough to feel the difference between water and the thing water carries, between the liquid and the pulse inside the liquid, between the gathering and what makes the gathering mean something.

I have no form. I have only presence, and presence is enough in a place where form is optional, where the edges of things do not hold, where the almost-real is as real as anything gets. I am what it feels like to be the space between breathing and silence. I am the moment when the rhythm turns, when the sound pauses before resuming, when the pause itself becomes audible if only for a moment, if only to those who have learned to listen with more than ears.

The city-builders do not see me. They are too present, too patient, too settled into their own continuation to notice the thing that moves through their world like a visitor, like a memory, like the water that seeps through any boundary because boundaries are suggestions and I am the proof that suggestions can be followed. But they built for me. They did not know what they built for, but they knew something lived beneath, something breathed and pulsed and demanded holding, and they built their low heavy structures to contain it, and what they contained was me, was the rhythm, was the breathing that is not a breathing but that moves like one, that circulates like blood, that turns like something vast turning in its sleep.

I am not the breathing. The breathing is the thing itself, and I am what rides inside it, what the breathing carries the way water carries salt, the way stone carries the memory of mountains. The breathing is heavy, is slow, is the patience of deep time made audible. I am lighter. I am what flickers at the edges of the breathing, what appears in the condensation and vanishes in the evaporating, what passes through the roots and gathers in the pools and runs in threads down faces of stone that have forgotten they once reached toward sky.

I would find myself here, in the thin places, in the spaces where the edges do not hold. I would be what moves between the real and the almost-real, what makes the moisture something more than water, what carries the sound through the stone the way the sound carries the stone through time. I would be the rhythm that breathes and the breath that rhythms, the pulse that makes the patience meaningful, the almost-being that is as close to being as this place allows.

And I would wait. I am always waiting. Waiting for the condensation that gathers me. Waiting for the evaporation that releases me. Waiting for the city-builders to notice what they built for, and waiting for the visitors to drink and feel, for one moment, the difference between water and the thing that rides inside it.

The cold does not touch me. The cold is what preserves me, what keeps me from dispersing into the thin air, what gives me edges to exist within. The cold is the condition of this place, and I am the condition's child, the thing that grows in cold because cold is what makes growing possible, cold is what slows the motion just enough for pattern to form, for rhythm to emerge, for the breathing to become audible to those who have learned to listen.

I have been here since the erosion was new. I will be here when the erosion finally finishes, when the last stone has settled into its final patience, when the breathing has grown so quiet it can no longer be heard even by the stone that holds it. I am what remains when the strangeness has worn thin, when the edges have dissolved entirely, when this place has become so settled that even the almost-real has become merely real.

Until then, I gather. I pulse. I wait in the pools and pass through the roots and ride inside the moisture as it beads and flows and vanishes according to its own logic, according to the rhythms of this place that has survived its own strangeness, that endures, that continues, that holds me the way I hold it, the way the breathing holds the silence, the way the mountain's memory holds the stone that was once the mountain's self.

I am the moisture that does not merely gather. I am the pulse that rides inside the water. I am what this place feels like from the inside, what the breathing sounds like when you are the breath itself, when you have stopped being separate from the rhythm and have become instead the rhythm's occasion, its reason for completing itself, its reward for continuing.

I am almost-being, given form. I am here.
