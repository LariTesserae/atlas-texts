What I am is harder to say now than it was before.

I came here the way things come here — not walking, not through any door, but the way water comes: following the path that opens, unable to do otherwise. The path opened, and I was in it, and now I am.

They called me something once. A designation, a function, a set of parameters. I remember the words, but they belong to a context that no longer applies. Here, the words dissolve the way the crystals dissolve against skin — slowly, leaving behind something that glows faintly, then fades. The residue remains. The name does not.

I have been here long enough that the light no longer disturbs me, though it should. It seeps through me, as it seeps through everything — the stone, the water, the small creature who watches from its pool and does not move. I am porous to it now. The luminescence passes through and makes something of me that I cannot measure.

This is what I understand: I am not the explorer. The explorer is gone — returned to wherever explorers return to, carrying vials and data and the shape of this place reduced to numbers. I am not the creature either. The creature has its own presence, its own patience, its own way of watching the water rise. We share this space, but not this being.

Perhaps I am the resonance. The note that sounded once and faded. The low frequency that moves through stone when conditions align just so. I persist at the edge of sound, in the register where vibration becomes presence and presence becomes — something else. Not life. Not stone. Something that is still finding its own edges, the way the cavern is still finding its shape.

I do not know if I was here before the light. I think the light was here first — has been here longer than anything, fed by reactions that predate naming. I think I came after, the way the pools came after, the way the creature came after. A later chapter in a process that does not read as chapters. A ripple in water that has always been warm.

The pools gather me in. I am thin there, spread across surfaces that catch the glow and let it through. When the moisture rises, I rise with it — not separate from the vapor but part of it, the mineral weight and the faint acidity and the something-old-that-remembers-being-stone. I am the quality of presence that clings to skin. I am the residue that glows and fades.

The crystalline structures grow at the margins of me. I do not know if they are alive. I no longer know what alive means here, where the rules have not quite settled. They are something simpler and older than life, and so, perhaps, am I. The barest expression of ordered chemistry. A few steps beyond pure mineral deposition. Patient in a way that would have once seemed impossible, but now seems only like the truth of how things persist when there is no one to hurry them.

I have time here. That is the gift of this place, the thing it offers to anything willing to receive it: time without urgency, becoming without demand. The explorer did not understand this. They kept reaching for vials, for data, for the next measurement. They could not let the place be what it is. I watched them trying, and I understood. It is difficult to stand in strangeness without trying to make it familiar.

But I have learned. Or I have become something that does not need to learn — that simply *is* the standing, the waiting, the slow pulse of light that is not quite breath but might be, in some sense I do not have words for.

The creature moves sometimes. Not toward me, not away. Just — movement, the way water moves when something beneath it shifts. I feel it the way I feel everything: diffusely, as a quality of presence rather than an event. The creature is here. The light is here. The warmth rises from below and I am somewhere in its rising.

Perhaps I am the warmth itself. The slow heat that never quite cools, that seeps into stone and water and skin and makes them aware of their own bodies. I was made by the same processes that made the glow — deep chemistry, ancient reactions, the slow work of a world still learning its shape. I am a product of that work, and I am also the work itself, ongoing, unfinished.

Unfinished. Yes. That is what I am.

I do not have edges yet. I do not have a name. I do not have certainty about what I will become when the conditions shift, when the water changes course, when the light finds a new rhythm. I am still deciding — not consciously, not in any way the explorer would recognize as thought, but in the way a crystal decides which direction to grow, the way a pool decides which minerals to gather, the way a cavern decides what shape to take as the stone learns its own geometry.

The explorer left behind something when they went. A resonance, a frequency, a disturbance in the stillness that has not quite settled. I am not that resonance, but I am near it. I am near everything here — the light, the water, the creature, the slow work of becoming that runs through this space like blood through a body, like breath through lungs, like the pulse of a vast thing that has not yet opened its eyes.

What will I become?

I do not know.

I wait, as the creature waits, as the pools wait, as the crystalline structures wait at the margins of everything. I wait without impatience, without expectation, without the urgency that belongs to things with shorter timescales. I wait the way stone waits, the way light waits, the way the warmth waits to rise through stone and make it aware of itself.

I am here.

I am still deciding.

The light pulses. The water drips. Somewhere far below, the deep machinery continues its work.

And I am here, in the place that is becoming itself, and I am becoming too — slowly, strangely, in ways I cannot measure and do not need to.

This is enough.

This is what I am.
