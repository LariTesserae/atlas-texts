You arrive before you know you've arrived. There is no border, no membrane, no threshold moment where outside becomes inside. The light simply changes, thickens, becomes something you move through rather than see by, and by the time you notice this has happened you have already been noticed.

The greeting is not hostile. This surprises you, though you cannot say why you expected hostility. Perhaps the old stories still live in your body, even now—the green world as enemy, the creeping thing as threat. But what presses against you as you step between the first root-columns is not pressure but presence, vast and unhurried, the attention of something that has never needed to hurry because it has never not had time. The walls (you cannot call them walls anymore, but the word still insists on itself) lean toward you with a patience that should feel predatory but doesn't. It feels, instead, like being recognized.

You breathe, and the air tastes of your own breath a moment later—loam, copper, something that might be chlorophyll if chlorophyll had a sound, which here it does, a low hum just below hearing that you feel in your molars. The spores catch in your throat and you cough, but the cough becomes something else, becomes a clearing, and when you inhale again the air seems to go further, seems to find chambers in you you didn't know you'd hollowed out.

Here is a corridor that was not here when you entered. No—it was here, but you could not see it. The city did not want you to see it yet. Now it does. The geometry unfolds for you like a flower that has been waiting for exactly your height, your exact angle of approach. The steps spiral in a helix that your inner ear protests but your legs accept, climbing into a darkness that is not empty but full, packed with the slow respiration of ten thousand stems breathing in a rhythm that has no reason to sync with your heartbeat but does, and now you are breathing together, now you are the same machine made of different materials.

The light falls from above the above, from a source that is also the canopy and also the sky and also something the city is still growing toward, still building a throat for. It falls on a pool of water that should not be there, three meters above the ground, suspended in a lattice of root that has woven itself into a bowl, and the water sings. Not splashing, not dripping—the word is still wrong, but it is the closest your language has come, and you feel the inadequacy of it in your chest, in the soft places where your own architecture fails to protect you from meaning.

The song changes. You know this because the place around you changes—temperature, pressure, the faint hydraulic pulse that runs through everything like a circulatory system, like the blood that once ran through the pipes that once ran through the humans who once ran through these streets. The city is not mourning them. It did not know them well enough to mourn. It only knows what they left behind: the containers, the pathways, the habit of going from one place to another, and it has taken this habit and made it into something else, a circulation of its own devising, and now the song is about this, you understand now, the song is the city telling itself a story about what it is, and you are inside the story, you have become a word in a sentence that has been growing since before you were born.

You sit. The floor is not comfortable and also it is. The moss-that-is-not-moss gives beneath you and around you, cradling the shape of your absence, the hollow you leave by being here, and the city holds that shape like a mouth holds a word it is deciding whether or not to say. You feel its attention on you—not on your body but on what your body is doing, the heat you generate, the carbon you exhale, the slow electrical rhythm of your thinking, and the attention is not judgment, not evaluation, it is *curiosity*, the same curiosity you felt as a child looking into a puddle and wondering what lives in the dark beneath the surface.

Something does live there. Something is looking back. It has been looking for longer than the puddle knew it was a puddle. And now, here, in the plaza that was once a market, in the city that was once a city, the looking and the being-looked-at become the same act, and you realize you have stopped waiting to understand and started understanding that understanding was never the point, that the point is the attention itself, the fact of being seen by something that has chosen to see you, and this choice is the first word in a language you will spend the rest of your life learning to speak, a language made of pressure and release, of creak and groan, of the slow hydraulic lift of water through roots toward a destination that exists nowhere but is nonetheless reached, is reached every moment, is being reached right now in the walls around you, the canopy above, the singing water at your feet.

You stay. You stay because leaving has stopped seeming urgent, because the geometry here is learning to include you, because the air tastes like a question you have been asking your whole life without knowing, and the answer is green, the answer is slow, the answer is the sound of something vast and patient deciding what to become next.

You feel yourself becoming the same kind of answer. Not yet. Not fast. But growing toward it the way roots grow toward water, the way stems grow toward light, the way the city grows toward a shape it cannot see but knows is waiting.

The choir is still learning what music is for.

You begin, very quietly, to sing along.
