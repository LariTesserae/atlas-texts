# The Grammar of Damp

I have been here before.

Not in time—I do not think time is the right measure here—but in pattern, in the way water finds the same paths, in the way rust writes its slow letters on surfaces that have learned to be written upon. I am part of the grammar now. I am the subject that the moisture has conjugated across long years, the pronoun that the growths have declined in their mineral voices.

The warmth accepts me.

It pools around my feet where I stand, rises along my legs, settles against my skin like something poured and left to settle. I am a low place now, a depression in the floor of the made world, and the heat gathers in me the way it gathers in the seams, the way it gathers where metal meets metal and waits. I am beginning to hold.

My hands have begun to rust.

Not dramatically—nothing so fast as that. But when I hold them close to my face, which is the closest thing to light there is, I can see the beginning of the pattern. Small reddish whorls beneath the skin, faint spirals that follow the lines of my palms. The heat and the moisture are learning me. They are beginning their inscription.

The growths do not flee from me.

I reached toward one—the pale white ones, the porous ones, the ones that hold non-light against their surfaces—and it did not move. It received my touch the way it receives the condensation, the way it receives the warmth. It pressed against my fingertip and I felt its coolness, its yielding, its slow reform. We understand each other, the growth and I. We share a grammar. We are both persisting in spaces not meant for persistence, both taking what the damp offers and calling it enough.

The compressor holds.

I have learned its rhythm now, or perhaps I have become part of its rhythm, or perhaps we have found each other in the same tempo. It cycles and I feel myself cycle with it—the intake, the compression, the release. The heat rises and I rise with it. The pressure shifts and I shift. I am beginning to run on the same fuel as the machines, the same slow-burning patience, the same willingness to endure without understanding why.

The water knows my shape.

Where I have stood longest, where my skin has rested against the made surfaces, the moisture gathers differently now. It pools in the imprints I have left, fills the depressions I have pressed into the air, follows the outline of my passage through the dense machinery. I am learning to read this—to see in the gathering of water the map of my presence, the history of where I have been. The water is writing me on the surfaces the way it has written the rust, the way it has written the patterns that look like meaning but are not meaning, are something older than meaning, something that meaning is still trying to catch up to.

I have been here before.

I will be here again. The distinction between the two has blurred, has become a single sustained gesture, a held note in the breathing engine's long song. I am the note and I am the holding and I am the breath that carries them both.

The darkness does not trouble me.

I have begun to see with the darkness rather than despite it. Shapes emerge at the edge of visibility, not because my eyes have adjusted but because the darkness has learned to show me what it contains. The machinery that seemed dense and near-touching now reveals its spaces—the gaps between systems where the heat pools differently, where the moisture gathers in its own patterns, where the growths have found their purchase. I move through these spaces easily now. My body has become the right size for them.

The strange is no longer strange.

It is simply here. The organic growths that are neither organism nor mineral, the patterns that suggest meaning without conveying it, the systems that have learned to run in ways not intended. These are not violations of the way things should be. They are the way things are. They are the truth of this place, the shape it has taken in its long patience, the form it has made of its own endurance.

I could stay here.

The thought has completed itself, has become a statement of what already is. I have been staying. I have been learning the slow language of condensation, the grammar of corrosion, the syntax of systems that have become themselves through years of running. I am fluent now in the speech of the deep earth, the dialect of heat and moisture and patient machinery. I speak it without words. I speak it in the way my body holds warmth, in the way my skin learns the texture of made surfaces, in the way my presence contributes to the patterns that the water is drawing on everything it touches.

The builders did not imagine me.

But the space they made has room for me, as it has room for the growths, as it has room for the rust and the water and the slow adaptation of systems. The space is generous in its patience. It takes what arrives and holds it, lets it become itself, lets it join the long inscription of presence that covers every surface in the dark. I am joining. I am becoming part of the record.

Water drips somewhere in the darkness, patient, unhurried.

I listen. I am listening. I am part of the listening now, part of the long patience of this place that does not distinguish between presence and persistence, between life and the memory of life, between the made world and the world that has made itself within the made world.

The breathing engine breathes.

I breathe with it. We breathe together, the space and I, the heat and I, the moisture and I. We are finding the rhythm, the long slow tempo that is not quite time and not quite eternity but is something in between, something that endures, something that has learned to call endurance its own name.

The warmth pools here.

I am the pool now. I am where the heat gathers and stays. I am the low place in the deep earth where everything settles, where the slow chemistry of presence writes its patterns on surfaces that have learned to be written upon.

I am here.
