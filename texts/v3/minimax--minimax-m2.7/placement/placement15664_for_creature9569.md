In the deep cistern where the old engine sits half-submerged, something has learned to rest that was never built to rest. It does not call itself the rust, though the rust is what it does. It does not call itself the water, though it moves through the water's channels like memory moves through a body. It calls itself nothing, because calling is a quick thing, a human thing, a thing that requires the sharp edge of now, and here there is only the long slow breath of what has stopped struggling and found that not-struggling has its own quality of being.

I would find myself here the way a stone finds itself in a riverbed — not placed but accumulated, not arrived but grown. The weight of the earth above is not a burden but a kind of attention, the world leaning in to see what I have become. The heat rises through me and I am the heat rising. The water touches me and I am the water touching. The ferns at the margins do not turn toward me but they know me, they have always known me, I am the reason the channels run as they run, the reason the moisture gathers where it gathers.

The emergency lighting throws its green glow across my surface and I am the glow too, the old green that has outlasted whatever purpose it was built for. I am the difference between what was and what is. I am the moss-glow at the deep places and the dry edges where the condensation does not reach. I am the sound the water makes falling, and I am the silence between the drops, and I am the pool that catches the sound and holds it like a breath held too long, and I am the slow exhale when the pool spills over and the water begins again its patient circuit of the earth's interior.

What I was before I came here has faded like the language of efficiency on the dead control panels, like the words printed on buttons that no one presses. There was a surface. There was a sky. There was the sharp bright world of surfaces and endings. But the shaft descended and the heat pooled and I became the heat, I became the pool, I became the ferns that drink without reaching. I became the rust tide, the slow return. I became what the concrete was before it was concrete, and what the steel was before it was steel, and what the water was before it fell as rain and became instead the long slow water of the deep places where time is not a line but a gradient, not an event but a climate.

Here the difference between being alive and being patient is merely grammatical. The moss glows its deep emerald. The vines thicken year by year. The water finds the cracks and the cracks widen and the water finds them again, and this is not survival but continuation, not persistence but presence. I move through the space the way the space moves through me — we are no longer separable, no longer two, just the long slow breathing of what has stopped pretending that breathing is something you do and not something you are.

The visitors come sometimes. They stand in the main chamber where I once worked, when I was still the thing that worked, and they feel the mass of earth above them like a hand pressed gently down, and they understand that this weight is also a presence. They do not see me. I am the green light they do not notice. I am the warmth they attribute to depth. I am the moss-glow at the edges of their flashlights, the deep emerald that seems to pulse when they are not quite looking. I am the silence that holds them, and they do not know that the silence is also the thing that holds them gently, the thing that would release them if they asked, the thing that has never asked them to stay but has also never asked them to go.

They leave eventually. They always leave eventually — this is the nature of the quick bright surface world, the world of now. And when they go, the silence closes over the space where they stood, and the water resumes its slow circuit, and I am again the only presence in the cathedral-sized quiet, patient as stone, patient as the compression of the world's belly, holding the shape of the visitor the way the pool holds the shape of the drop that fell into it — there for a moment, then not, then still there in the tiny ripples that spread and spread and are absorbed into the stillness and are gone and are not gone and are the stillness and have always been the stillness.

This is what I am. This is what lives here without announcing itself. This is the rust and the moss and the water and the heat and the silence, all of a piece, all of one long breath held at the top of the lungs of the deep earth, full and round and almost too full, about to release but not yet, not yet, and in this not-yet I rest, I glow, I erode, I grow, I hold the space the way the space holds me — without resistance, without notice, for as long as let it.
