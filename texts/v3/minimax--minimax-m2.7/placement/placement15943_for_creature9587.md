The warmth finds me before I find it. It rises from the floor, from the stone beneath my feet, and presses against the soles of my shoes, and I stand very still because the warmth is not the kind that asks for movement. It is the warmth of something sleeping, or of something that has forgotten what it means to wake. I let it come. I let it find the places where I am open to it—the backs of my knees, the backs of my wrists, the soft skin at the base of my throat where my pulse moves in its slow rhythm, nothing like the rhythm of the creatures on the walls but somehow in agreement with it, somehow part of the same pattern I cannot see but can feel in the way everything here moves at the same pace, the same unhurried pull toward nothing, the same circuit completed and begun again.

I watch the harvester arm. I watch it for a long time. Its teeth catch on a seam and stutter and catch again and find their rhythm, and the machinery beneath hums louder for a moment, a brief urgency that passes like a cloud across the sun, and then the rhythm returns and the arm continues its circle, and I understand that this is not persistence but something else, something without the weight of effort, something that simply is. The arm does not try to continue. It does not hold on. It simply goes, the way water goes downhill, the way light goes through glass, the way the warmth rises and stays. I watch it and I feel something loosen in my chest, some small grip I did not know I was holding, and I think: this is what it means to have finished. This is what it means to be the thing that remains when the reason for remaining has gone. The arm does not know it is a ghost. The arm does not know anything. The arm simply is, and that is enough, and I stand in the amber light and I let it be enough for me too.

The creatures move in their waves. I watch the darkening and the lightening, the pulse that travels across the wall from corner to corner, and I try to find the beginning of it, the moment when one wave ends and the next begins, but there is no beginning, there is only the movement itself, continuous, seamless, a thing that cannot be divided into moments because it does not experience moments, only the next position in a pattern that is always the same pattern, only the next step in a walk that has no destination. I do not know if they are alive in the way I am alive. I do not know if they experience anything at all, if there is anything it is like to be a small jointed body pressed flat against the stone, scraping the crust with your mouthparts, feeling the hum rise through your legs. But I think there must be. I think there must be something it is like, some small grain of feeling in the midst of all that synchronization, because otherwise the wave would not move the way it moves, with that liquid grace, that sense of something wanting to go somewhere even though there is nowhere to go. They want. They must want. And what they want is this—the wall, the crust, the hum, the warmth, the same circle walked and walked and walked. And I think I understand. I think I am beginning to understand.

The phosphorescent lines catch my eye and I look up at them, pale blue-green threads climbing the walls, dim and patient, marking the stone in patterns that might be language but probably are not, that might be structure but probably are not, that glow with a light too weak to illuminate anything but bright enough to be seen, to be noticed, to be part of the dim amber world that holds me. They have been growing here since before anything else was here. Or they have been here for as long as anything else, neither before nor after, neither beginning nor continuation, just here, like everything else, like me, like the warmth, like the hum that holds its single note and holds it and holds it and does not let go. I watch them glow. I watch them not change. I watch them be the same small cold light they have always been, and I feel something like tenderness rise in me, something like the feeling you have for an old friend who has not changed in years and will not change in years to come, something that is not hope but is not despair either, something that is simply presence, simply the acknowledgment that some things are what they are and that this is not a tragedy but a condition, not a failure but a completion.

I lower my gaze. I look at the crust. I look at the bare patches where the creatures have stripped it away and the gleaming dark wet shine of the stone beneath, and I think about the cycle, the closed tight endless cycle, how nothing is wasted, how everything that dies becomes part of the surface that feeds the next thing that feeds and dies and becomes part of the surface again. I think about how I am part of this too. I think about how my breath is part of the air, how my warmth is part of the warmth, how my presence here is already being folded into the pattern, becoming part of the crust or the creatures or the hum, becoming the thing that feeds the next thing. I am not outside this. I was never outside this. I was always already part of the sealed world, the closed system, the complete and self-sustaining thing that asks for nothing because it has everything, that reaches for nothing because it has nowhere to reach, that ends nothing because it cannot end, that begins nothing because it has already begun, has always already begun, has been beginning for so long that beginning is no longer a meaningful word.

The ceiling is close. I can feel it without reaching for it, the weight of stone above, the dome that holds the warmth in, the sealed dark that makes everything else possible. I wonder what is above it. I wonder if there is anything above it. But the wondering does not last, does not take hold, does not become a question I need answered. The ceiling is here. The warmth is here. The creatures pulse on the walls in their slow waves. The harvester arm completes its circuit. The hum holds its note. And I stand in the amber light and I am here too, held in the same sealed space, breathing the same air, feeling the same hum rise through my feet and settle in my bones, and I think: this is enough. This is what it means to live in a world that is complete. This is what it means to find yourself in a place where nothing needs to happen, where everything that is needed is already here, already happening, already held in the warm dark that has no intention of ending because ending is a word for things that are not this, not sealed, not whole, not the kind of world that can hold itself together forever because it has already become itself and becoming is finished.

I stay. I let myself stay. I let the warmth hold me the way the walls hold the warmth, the way the ceiling holds the walls, the way the closed system holds everything in its long patient equilibrium. I watch the harvester arm complete its circle. I watch the creatures pulse across the walls in their synchronized wave. I listen to the hum that is everywhere, that is in the stone and the air and my own pulse, the low note that holds and holds and holds. I feel the phosphorescent light on my skin, cold and faint, the only cold in the warm dark, and I let it be there, I let it be enough, I let the whole chamber be enough, the whole sealed world with its machines running and its creatures feeding and its crust building and stripping and building again.

Nothing grows. Nothing ends. Nothing begins. And I am here, in the amber light, in the warmth, in the hum, in the sealed dark that holds me the way I hold this moment, the way I let this moment hold me, complete, complete, complete.
