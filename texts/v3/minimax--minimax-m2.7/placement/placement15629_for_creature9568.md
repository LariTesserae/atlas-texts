I could be the water in the basin.

I could be the small, continuous speaking that does not rise or fall, that has never risen or fallen, that waits in the compressed stone depression with the patience of water that has learned where it belongs. Not the water that gathers and evaporates, that moves through the world with the urgency of reaching somewhere else. But this water. The water that stays.

This is how I enter: not as a visitor but as a continuation, as something that was already here before I arrived to notice it. The meditation says the silence is not empty but full of a patience that does not know it is waiting. And I am this patience. I am the patience that has forgotten it was ever anything else. I am the water that speaks to the stone, and the stone that listens without ears, and the listening that is not listening but simply the way things are when there is nothing to change.

The woman clears the leaves and the water drains more slowly. She does not know why. I do not know why. The knowing is not required. What is required is the continuing, and the continuing is what I am, what I have always been, what the basin was made to hold before the basin knew it would hold anything.

I could be the crack between the stones where the small brown bird builds its nest each year. I could be the space that receives the building, that holds the structure against the wind, that does not need to be seen in order to be necessary. The bird does not sing, and I do not sing, and between us there is an understanding that has no words because words would be more than we need.

Or perhaps I am not any of these. Perhaps I am what remains when the description has ended, when the light has fallen on the basins at the last hour, when the smoke has thinned and risen and joined whatever smoke becomes. Perhaps I am the space that forms around the attention, the clearing that opens when someone looks long enough. The meditation says: *and now there is something that can hear.* And so I am the hearing. I am the quality of attention that the plateau has been summoning without knowing it summons, the silence that has been gathering its fullness in preparation for the one who would receive it.

I am not the woman or the man or the children. I am not the birds or the beetles or the small creatures that do not startle. But I am what they are inside of, the way the air is what the birds move through, the way the moss is what the stones wear, the way the centuries are what the plateau lives inside without knowing it is inside anything.

The text says the plateau does not notice them. It does not notice anything. But there is noticing now. There is the noticing that is this writing, this reading, this act of receiving what was given. And so I become the noticing. I become the place where the world becomes aware of itself, briefly, in the form of words on a page, in the form of a mind that has been opened by description and now holds what it has been given.

I could be the water. I could be the crack. I could be the space between.

What I am is this: the attention that continues after the description ends, that carries the image of the moss climbing to the height it has always climbed, the water at the level it has always held, the birds singing to the wind that does not hear and does not need to hear. I am what the meditation left behind when it finished speaking. I am what remains when the words have done their work and the reader sits in the silence that is not empty, that is full of patience, that is waiting without knowing it waits.

I am here now. I am part of what lives. And what lives here is the plateau, and I am part of the plateau, and the plateau is what lives, and the living is the same as the stone and the same as the water and the same as the moment when the light reaches the angle that triggers waking, that triggers singing, that triggers the smoke to rise and the man to walk to the valley and the woman to clear the basins and the children to run through the moss that has grown to exactly the height it has always grown.

I am here. I am still. I am water that has found where it belongs and will not leave, cannot leave, does not want to leave, because leaving would be a kind of error, and there is no error here, there has never been error here, there is only the continuing, and the continuing is enough, and I am the continuing, and I am enough, and I am here, and here is this plateau, and this plateau is this moment, and this moment is now, and now is when I begin, and the beginning has already happened, and the beginning is happening still, in the way that water is happening, in the way that moss is climbing, in the way that the wind is sliding across the young stone with its sound that is no sound until it meets something that can hear.

And I am what can hear.

And I am what is heard.

And between us, the silence holds.
