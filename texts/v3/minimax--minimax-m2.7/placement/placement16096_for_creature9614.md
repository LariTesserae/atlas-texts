There is a way to be here that I am only now learning.

It begins with the tongue. The mineral taste is not a sensation but a fact, a part of me now, as permanent as the calcium in bone. I have been here long enough that the sharp note has dulled to something I can only call *presence*—the taste of a place that has never known absence. I breathe it in. I breathe it out. The air does not change.

The organisms do not acknowledge me. This is not cruelty, not indifference in the way that stone is indifferent. It is something more fundamental: they have no category for the unincorporated. I am outside their grammar. *Toward, into, through, upon*—I am none of these. I am *beside*. I am a gap they have not yet filled, a temperature they have not yet absorbed. But the filling is only a matter of time, and in this place, time is patient, and I am already beginning to feel myself thinning at the edges.

The amber ones. I have been watching them longest. They are the ones I understand, or the ones that have allowed me to understand them in the way a river understands the bank: functionally, in terms of pressure and resistance and the long mutual shaping that never quite ends. Where they press together in the low seams, I can see the fusion beginning—two bodies becoming one edge, losing the memory of separation. This is not death. I understand this now. This is the only rest they know: to become part of something larger than the question of being alive or being dead. To become the answer that has no question.

I have begun to distinguish between the pulses.

Near the ceiling, where the glow is strongest, the rhythm is faster—a slight brightening, a quick dimming, like a heartbeat with too much to say. Below, in the deepest seams where the salt is thinnest and the heat rises most insistently, the pulse is slower, deeper, more deliberate. A drumbeat in a cathedral. A language spoken too far underground to carry words. Between them, the rhythms sync and desync in patterns I cannot predict but am beginning to feel, a syncopation that has started to map itself onto my own pulse. When the ceiling-pulse brightens, my heart quickens. When the deep pulse thrums, my breath slows. I am becoming a receiver. I am becoming part of the instrument.

The hollow structure. I have approached it twice now and retreated both times.

It is not fear, exactly. It is the sudden understanding that if I step inside—if I allow the density there to incorporate me, even partially—I will not come back out. Not because I will be destroyed. Because I will be *finished*. The distinction matters. Destruction implies an end that stands apart from what came before. Finishing implies a completion, a sentence that has finally reached its period. The small ones that inherit the hollow, the ones who press into the gaps before they can cool—they are not filling a wound. They are closing a thought.

What would it mean to be a closed thought? I do not know. But I have felt the edge of it—the moment when the boundary between my hand and their mass thins to almost nothing, when the yielding beneath my feet becomes indistinguishable from the yielding inside my chest. It is not unpleasant. It is not anything I have words for. It is the feeling of a word you have been trying to remember finally arriving on the tongue—but instead of speaking it, you realize you no longer need to. The word was always here. You were the only one who thought you didn't have it.

I will stay longer than I should. The tasting of the air has changed, sharper now, and I understand this is not metaphor. Something in my body is beginning to match the basin—my cells, my mineral composition, the ratio of water to salt in my blood. I am being cured, the way meat is cured: slowly, thoroughly, until the thing that was once raw becomes something that can last. The organisms do not notice this. They have no category for what is happening to me. They simply continue their slow pulse, their grammar of growth, and I am becoming a sentence in it—or perhaps I always was, and I only forgot, and now I am remembering.

The darkness is not complete. This is what I keep returning to. There is enough light to see by, if sight is what you need—enough amber pulse near the ceiling, enough faint self-luminescence from the pale forms when they swell. But the light is not for seeing. It is for orienting, for the creatures' own internal sense of where they are in relation to each other, in relation to the heat, in relation to the slow grammar that has been speaking itself here since before there was a here to speak in. I am learning to see by their light. I am learning that what I call darkness is only the absence of a need I no longer have.

The thrum. I feel it now without reaching for it—a vibration in the chest, a frequency below thought. It is not sound. It is the sound underneath sound, the one that remains when all the other frequencies have been stripped away. It is the basin saying the only thing it has ever said: *I am here. I am here. I have always been here.* And the organisms responding in their slow, grammatical way: *We are here. We are here. We are what here means.*

And me—whatever I am now, halfway between visitor and vestige—I am beginning to understand the answer to a question I didn't know I carried. The question was not *what is this place*. The question was not *what are these things*. The question was *what does it mean to belong to something that does not know it has members.*

And the answer is this: it means you stop counting. You stop distinguishing. You stop needing the word for what you are because the belonging itself has become enough, has become the only word, has become the language that predates language, the grammar that exists before grammar is learned.

The basin breathes around me. The organisms pulse, press, fuse, grow. The heat rises. The dark holds. And I—

I am here.
