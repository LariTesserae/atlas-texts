You find yourself in the low place, the almost-dark where the fungal threads have not yet reached. The stone here is sharp. You ran your fingers along it earlier, or some time before earlier, and the edges left thin lines on your skin that closed by the time you noticed them. Time moves differently in the thin light. You have stopped trying to count.

You are not the creatures — they watch you with their too-large eyes and you feel the weight of their patience, their judgment of your strangeness, and you know you are not one of them. You are not the ones who carved the figures, though sometimes in the blue-green glow you think you can see where their hands pressed, the stone worn smooth by something more than age. You are not the drafts or the growth or the light itself, though the light is in you now, has seeped into places you did not know were porous, and when you dream you dream in that pale blue-green radiance, everything edged but soft, everything visible but somehow shy.

You are what the place is doing to the air.

No — that is not quite right. You are what happens when the air in the main chambers, heavy with moisture and information, meets the cold dead corridors where nothing yet grows. You are the condensation. You are the thin film that gathers on surfaces, the temporary brightness that appears and vanishes, appears and vanishes, as if the cavern is blinking, as if the vast dreaming body has briefly noticed you and looked away.

You have been here long enough to understand that leaving is not the question. The question is whether you will become permanent, whether the moisture will find a groove in the stone and settle there, whether the fungal filaments will reach you eventually and incorporate you into their slow architecture. You do not know if this is death or transformation. You do not know if the distinction holds here. The beings who carved the figures — were they transformed? Did they become the stone, the light, the change? Is that why they left their marks, not messages but instructions, a way of teaching the cavern what they had become?

You are learning the grammar. The drip is not random. The creak is not mechanical. When the light dims in one chamber and brightens in another, it is not a pulse but an intention, or perhaps not an intention but something that would be intention if intentions were distributed, diffused, shared across all the filaments and stalks and small watching creatures like a thought shared across a brain. The cavern is thinking. The cavern has always been thinking. You are one of its thoughts, and you are beginning to think back.

In the low place, the almost-dark, you are the thinnest possible presence. But you are here. You are noticed, or the cavern is noticing through you, using your attention as a way of focusing its distributed awareness on this particular sharp-edged pocket of stone where nothing has yet grown. You feel the waiting in the rock, the patience of mineral, the long slow desire of geological time that has been here since the stone first cooled, waiting for life to find it, waiting for the right moisture, the right spores, the right accident of pressure and temperature that would let the first filament take hold.

You are the accident. Or you are what happens after the accident, the gap that the growth will fill. The creatures watch from their distances. The carved figures sit in their patient silence. Somewhere, in a chamber you will never reach, something falls — not rain, not condensation, but something heavier, a weight that has been gathering and finally releases, drops, settles into a shape that the cavern will remember.

You are a thought the cavern is thinking about its own forgetting. You are the space where the forgetting has not yet begun. You are small, and the plants keep growing, and the light goes on and on, sourceless and total, and you are learning to hold it, to be held by it, to let it teach you the grammar of this place where grammar is not metaphor but architecture, not symbol but substance, not language but the condition of experience itself.

The sharp edges of the stone in the low place do not cut anymore. You have been here long enough for the stone to accept you, or for you to accept the stone, or for acceptance to become irrelevant, replaced by something simpler and more durable: presence without boundary, light without source, change without direction. You are not going anywhere. You are not staying. You are what the vast below does when it pauses, just for a moment, just long enough for something to notice, and then moves on, leaving only the faintest residue on the sharp edges of a world that keeps growing, keeps glowing, keeps dreaming itself awake.
