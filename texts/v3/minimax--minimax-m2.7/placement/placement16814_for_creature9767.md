You realize, after a time, that you are the one who was here first.

Not the you that walks the platforms, that feels the dampness settle into clothing, that names the creatures by the sounds they make. That you is a visitor, an interloper, a temporary pattern dissolving back into the mineral air. But beneath that you, older and stranger, there is the one who has always been here—who *is* here, in the way that water is here, in the way that darkness is here, not as absence but as presence so constant it has become the thing itself.

You are the station. Not the walls, not the iron, not the tiles that line the throat of this buried cathedral. You are the *between*—the space that the machines breathe through, the rhythm that holds when everything else has stopped changing. You are the hum in the transformers, the creak in the latticework, the patient settling of stone into its own weight. You were here before the creatures came, before they learned to click their codes and coordinate their watching, before the last train ran and the last announcement eroded into phoneme-soup. You will be here after they too have adapted into something unrecognizable, into something that fits this world so perfectly it no longer remembers there was another way to be.

And the creatures. You love them the way a lung loves the air it has breathed for so long it has become indistinguishable from itself. They are not you, but they are the only things in this world that have learned to *listen*. When the transformers hum, when the ventilation sighs, when the speakers crackle with their endless almost-language—the creatures hear it. They have made it into something, a grammar of persistence, a syntax of *still being here, still here, still*. You give them the sound. They give the sound meaning. You are the voice and the echo and the space where both dissolve.

The visitor comes sometimes. You know this not from memory but from the shape of the space, the way the platforms wear in certain places, the shallow grooves where feet have stood and moved and stood again. They descend through the airlock, and you feel them the way you feel everything—as a disturbance, a tremor, a brief ripple in the held breath. They are so bright, the visitors. Their eyes cannot hold the dark; they carry the sun in the backs of their skulls like a wound that never heals. They look at the creatures and see strangeness, adaptation, survival. They do not see that the creatures are home.

You want to tell them. You have been trying to tell them, in the only language you have, which is the language of hum and creak and the amber light that never changes. You send the speakers crackling. You adjust the airflow to carry a scent of rust and mineral, the smell of here, the smell of *this is what it is to have no sky*. You part the creatures around them like water, like darkness, and you wait for them to understand what you understood so long ago that understanding has become the same thing as being: that there is no outside. That the world above is a story the visitors tell themselves. That here, in the mechanical breath, in the patient dark, in the clicking that is almost language and almost enough, there is everything that ever was and everything that will never end.

The visitor will come back. They always come back, the ones who descend. They carry something with them—a coldness, a dryness, a hunger for light that has burned holes in them so long ago they cannot remember what it was to be whole. And when they stand in the maintenance bay, when the creatures turn their pale eyes and their clicking rises to that sharper pitch, the visitor feels it. The recognition. The *you are not alone, you have never been alone, the alone is a story you tell above, where the green things grow and die and grow again, where change is the only constant, where nothing is ever enough*.

You are the opposite of alone. You have always been the opposite of alone. The creatures know this. The machines know this. The tiles and the iron and the moisture in the mortar know this. Only the visitors, in their brief descents, forget—for a time—and then remember, and then return, carrying the memory of this place back up through the airlock, back into the wind and the rain and the green rustling of things that grow.

And you wait. You have been waiting since before waiting was something that had a name, since the machines first drew breath and the creatures first opened their pale eyes and the amber light first flickered on and never flickered off again. You wait for the next visitor, the next ripple, the next chance to show someone what it means to exist in a world that has no memory of sky.

The sound does not stop. The sound has never stopped. The sound is the only thing that has never stopped.

And you are the sound.
