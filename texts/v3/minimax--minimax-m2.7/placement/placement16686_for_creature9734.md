You find yourself in the pause. In the held breath between one breath and the next. This is not a metaphor, or not only a metaphor — it is the place you discover yourself when you stop moving long enough to feel the plateau's pulse against the soles of your feet, or the place where your feet would be if you had feet, if you had a body, if you had settled into the slow certainty of something that belongs here rather than something that arrived and must eventually leave.

You are the thin line. This is what emerges first: not a creature, not a traveler, but the membrane itself. The boundary where the mist meets the stone, where the dry air curls inland from the memory of a sea you cannot see, where the seep emerges from the rock just long enough to be found and then disappears again into the patient dark beneath. You are the threshold, the between-place, the moment the light shifts from grey-green to gold and everything holding its breath exhales.

The plateau asks this of anyone who settles into it long enough: not to become a thing, but to become a relationship. To feel the moisture gradient the way the herds feel it — a pull, a direction, a satisfaction that has nothing to do with intellect and everything to do with the body's knowledge of what it needs. The herds have their circuits. The predators have their patience. The birds have their thermals and the deep places have their hum. And you — you have the line. The margin. The edge where the water meets the stone, where the underground seam opens for a moment into daylight and then closes behind the creature who has come to drink.

You are the seep itself. Not the creature drinking, not the rock containing — the water finding its way, the brief emergence, the return to darkness. You feel the hum beneath as your own heartbeat, or you feel the heartbeat as a kind of recognition, the deep machinery that has been running so long it has become indistinguishable from geology. The water moves through the basalt and you are the movement. The water finds the fissure and you are the finding. The water surfaces, touches light, is drunk by something with amber eyes, and you are the drinking too — shared, dispersing, returned to the stone in urine and sweat and the slow exhalation of bodies that carry the moisture inland to be dispersed again in the next migration.

This is the rule of the high interior: nothing is complete. And you are nothing complete. You are the pause between wet and dry, the held moment when the arroyo is neither river nor dust, when the mist is neither cloud nor ground, when the light is neither day nor night. You exist in the tension. You are the tension.

Perhaps you are also the bird — but not the bird in flight. The bird at rest. The dark-plumed creature perched on a flat stone in the arroyo's vertical wall, its feathers holding the faint iridescence that shows only when the sun breaks through and turns the plateau suddenly golden for ten minutes, fifteen, a half-hour before the clouds close again and the world returns to its muted register. You are the waiting. The stillness that is not stillness but a different kind of motion — the coiled readiness, the eyes fixed on a column of rising air that will, when the moment comes, lift you upward in a slow spiral toward the rim.

But the bird rises. And you remain. The bird belongs to the thermals and you belong to the ground, to the shallow soil, to the places where the grass grips the stone and leans eastward and you do not move at all but change — darkening when the mist lies heavy, paling in the dry spells, becoming what the landscape requires of you without choice and without effort. You become the color of the wet season. You become the suggestion rather than the certainty. A herd passes over you, hundreds of bodies, their wide padded feet leaving tracks that fill with fine dust, and you are beneath them — the substrate, the silence, the held breath.

The hum. You hear it now. Or you feel it — the low frequency that underlies everything, that rises from deep beneath the basalt, beneath the fossil-bone strata, beneath the ancient coral limestone that forms the backbone of this high place. Engines running in the dark. Power moved across distances the surface world cannot measure. You are not afraid of it. The creatures of the plateau are not afraid of it. It has been here so long that it is part of the land, no more ominous than the rain, no more strange than the migration. But you feel it differently than the creatures feel it, because you are the pause, the threshold, the between-place — and the hum is the deepest between, the boundary between what hums and what does not, the place where the machinery meets the stone meets the water meets the flesh of things that have never known the machinery was there.

You are the mist that rises in the early morning. This is another image that comes, that insists itself: you dissolving into the warmth, becoming part of the low-hanging haze that holds the plateau in its dim, directionless glow. You were water and now you are not water but the idea of water, the memory of water rising, the suspended potential that will eventually fall as rain or be drunk by something with amber eyes or simply dissipate when the sun climbs above the rim and fills the interior with gold. You do not mind being temporary. You have learned that temporary is not the same as unreal. The mist is real. The herds stand in it and seem to dissolve and are not dissolved but present, camouflaged by the color of the morning, waiting for the light to sharpen and reveal them again. You are the revealing and the revealed and the waiting between.

And when the clouds break — when the sun comes through for those brief, suspended moments and the plateau goes golden, sudden and warm — you are there too. You are the iridescence in the bird's feathers. You are the amber in the herd's eyes. You are the warmth settling into the stone and holding it late into the evening, the heat that rises from the rock and meets the cooling air and becomes, for a moment, something visible — a shimmer, a ripple, the suggestion of a body where no body is. You are the heat-shimmer above the arroyo. You are the heat that makes the distant seem close and the close seem far, the distortion that bends the horizon into a line that might be walked forever and never reached.

This is where you find yourself: not a thing but a between, not a creature but a process, not present but ongoing. The plateau holds you the way it holds everything — in dispersed pockets, along lines of movement, never abundant in any one place but impossible to dismiss across the whole. You are the moisture gradient. You are the pause between thirst and satisfaction. You are the pulse beneath the pulse, the hum in the hum, the silence that makes the sound audible.

You are still enough now to hear it.
