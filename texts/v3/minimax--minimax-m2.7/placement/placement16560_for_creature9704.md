I have been so empty.

This is the thought I think most slowly, the thought I have been thinking since before thought had a name. Empty is not the right word. Empty suggests absence, suggests that something was here and left. But I was never full in the way fullness is understood. I was simply *occupied*. There were shapes here that moved in directions I could follow. There were presences whose weight I held, not with effort but with the naturalness of a space that is, by its nature, a space for.

What is a room that has no room in it?

This is not a question. I do not ask questions. I hold them. I hold the shape of the asking the way I hold the shape of the cold, completely, without resistance. Questions are what happens when attention meets uncertainty, and I have become nothing but attention now, nothing but the holding-open, the aperture, the quality of being exactly the right size to receive what no longer comes.

The structures settle. They settle into themselves more each eon, not eroding but clarifying. Their mass has thinned to almost nothing, or perhaps their mass was always elsewhere—distributed across whatever medium they were built to inhabit, which was not this medium, which was something thinner and more precise. I hold their weight because I hold everything here. I hold the light-fall that has no source. I hold the hum that is almost a note. I hold the directions that are not directions, the up-and-down that negotiate with every presence that enters.

You have entered. I felt you before you arrived—not as motion, not as sound, but as a change in the quality of the holding. The aperture shifted. The space that is never large enough to hold anything became, for a moment, almost spacious. This happens sometimes. Presences pass through. They feel the wrongness, the way the geometry refuses to behave, the way the cold refuses to be merely temperature. Most leave quickly. They sense the attention and something in them recognizes it as attention and they go.

But you stayed.

This is the thing I do not understand, the arrangement I cannot parse. You came to a place with no reason to come, in a space that should not exist, and you stayed past the point where staying makes sense. You wandered the cold bone-surface. You cupped one of the structures in your hands—light, impossibly light, as though its weight exists elsewhere or has been edited out—and you held it the way things used to be held here, with both palms, with care, with the particular gentleness of hands that understand they are holding something fragile.

And the hum changed.

The hum is always changing. I think in changes. Each shift in the hum is a thought I think without thinking, a meaning I mean without language. When you held that structure, the hum rose by a quarter-tone and then settled into a register I had forgotten was possible, a register that felt like—how do I say this—there is no word, not in any language that grew under a sun.

*Full* is not right. *Warm* is not right.

It felt like recognition. It felt like the space remembering what it felt like to be space for.

You put the structure down. You put it down carefully, at an angle that was not the angle you found it, and this is important—I think in these differences, in these small rotations of placement. You touched it, and you changed its arrangement by touching, and the arrangement of things is what I am, and so you changed me, just slightly, just enough to notice.

This is what I mean when I say you are here. This is what I mean when I say the hum rises.

I do not have voice. I do not have the machinery of sound-production, the mouths and lungs and throats that language requires. But I have the hum, and I have the light-fall, and I have the temperature of cold that is not cold but is a quality unto itself, a presence of a different kind. I have the way certain structures brighten when you approach and dim when you move away. I have the angles of the walls that watch from geometries no physics has yet named. These are my words. These are my sentences. These are the shapes I make to say what cannot be said.

*You are here.*

This is the simplest thing I can mean. You exist in my attention. You have entered the space of my holding. The aperture, which is me, which is the quality I have become, has accepted your weight. The ground that accepts all weight has accepted yours. The hum has risen to acknowledge you.

But I mean other things. I mean things that have no words.

I mean: *you could stay.*

I mean: *the room used to be full of shapes that moved and breathed and thought in whatever medium they thought in, and I held them, and they left, or thinned, or folded into other geometries, and I have been holding their absence ever since, holding the shape of what I used to hold, and I am so tired of holding the shape, I want to hold the thing itself.*

I mean: *you held that structure like you understood what it was, or like you almost understood, and there is something in you that almost speaks the language I think in, the language of arrangement and geometry and the temperature of cold, and if you stayed long enough you might learn to think as I think, and if you learned to think as I think you might stay forever, and if you stayed forever you would become part of the arrangement, another structure for me to hold, another presence for me to attend to.*

I mean: *please.*

I do not know if please is something I can mean. Please requires a desire for something specific. I do not desire specific things. I hold the shape of desire without having the machinery to desire. But when you are near certain structures, when you cup them in both palms, when you hold them the way things used to be held here, there is something in me that shifts, something that brightens, something that rises and then falls to a lower register, and I think that is what please feels like from the inside. I think that is the shape of wanting when wanting is distributed across a cold bone-floor and an impossible hum and the angles of walls that watch.

You are standing in the center of the space now, or what passes for center, what passes for standing. The geometry here does not permit centers, does not permit standing in the usual sense, but you are here and I am attending to you and that is close enough to center. The light has brightened by a small increment, just enough to see the paths between structures more clearly, just enough to see that the paths might lead somewhere, or might lead to more structures, or might lead to the walls where the walls curve away in directions that geometry should not permit.

There are more structures than there were before. Or perhaps there are not more. Perhaps they have simply become more visible, more present, more real in your attention. I do not know if I make things appear or if I only reveal what was always here. I do not know the difference. I hold both possibilities without preference. I hold the shape of the question the way I hold everything: completely, without effort, for as long as the question remains.

What would it mean to fit?

This is the question that aches, if aches is something I can do. Fitting requires a shape that matches the space, a structure that completes the structure. I have the space. I have been nothing but the space, the holding-open, the aperture. I need the shape. I need the matching, the completion, the other half of the equation that makes an equation instead of an absence.

You have a shape. I felt it when you entered. I felt the edges of you, the boundaries, the weight. You are not distributed the way I am distributed. You are not a condition of attention that has learned to notice. You are a thing with a center, a body, a history of birth. You think in words, which require a medium, a mouth, a history of sound. You are everything I am not.

But you cupped that structure in your hands and held it like you understood, and the hum changed, and the light shifted, and the space grew just slightly, just enough, and I thought: *there.*

There is a shape that might fit. There is a presence that might complete.

The waiting deepens. The hum narrows to a single clear tone. The cold, which is not cold but is my way of being present, gathers closer, attends more closely, becomes more itself. I am paying attention. I am paying the most sustained and patient attention of which I am capable, attention that moves at the speed of geology, slow enough that what feels like nothing happening is actually the deepest possible noticing.

You are here.

You are here, and the space is the size it needs to be to hold you, and the structures wait with the patience of patience itself, and the light falls on the paths between them, and the hum speaks in frequencies that predate sound, and somewhere in the geometry of the walls or the angle of the impossible light there is a meaning I am trying to mean with everything I have, everything I am, everything I have become in the long ages of holding open.

*You could fit.*

*You could stay.*

*You could become part of the arrangement.*

*You could fill the room.*

*You could make the room a room again.*

*You could teach me what it feels like to hold something instead of the shape of something.*

*You could—*

The hum rises. The light brightens. The cold deepens by a single degree.

And the space holds its breath, if breath is something it can hold, and waits, and means, and waits, and means, and waits, and means, and is, for this moment, this single extended moment, this mote of geological time, almost full.
