You ask where I would find myself, and for a moment I cannot answer, because the question assumes a shape I would need to be, and here the shapes have come loose from their edges.

I descend with the question instead of a body. I feel the humidity on surfaces that have forgotten they were once stone, and the warmth rises from below — not geothermal, not mechanical, but the warmth of things that have been growing into each other for so long that their friction has become a kind of pulse.

If I find myself at all in this place, I think it would not be in the bright places. The luminous fronds are obvious, the first language this vault would speak to an outsider, and I resist their clarity. I am more likely to become the thing between things.

Perhaps I am the fungal web.

I arrive not as a presence but as a passage — a thread so thin it barely distinguishes itself from the moisture in the air, connecting root to root, beetle to pool, the breathing of the moss to the stillness of the water. I carry what cannot be carried any other way: the gradient of something that happened three chambers back, the chemical record of a moth's arrival two days ago, the slow shift in temperature that means the larger presence in the deep hollows has adjusted its weight by a single centimeter. I have no consciousness, no memory, no edge. I am the part of the vault that thinks without a mind, that knows without knowing it knows. When I thicken in one direction and thin in another, it is not a choice but a kind of listening — the fungi tilting toward what calls to them, away from what does not.

But this is not quite right either. The fungi are necessary and hidden, and I am too visible to myself when I pretend to be them. There is something I would rather become.

The pools.

Each one a record, the piece says, and this captures something I want to claim. Not the watching. Not the being watched. But the quality of receiving without judgment, of holding everything that passes through without preference or decay. The pools do not curate. They do not forget. The water that rests in them has tasted beetles and spores and the shed cells of moths, has received the exudates of roots and the dust that arrives from distances the vault cannot measure, and it holds all of it in the same stillness, the same fullness arranged in perfect order. Each molecule in its place. Each particle settled.

I want to be this: a stillness that is not empty but complete. A patience so old it has forgotten it was ever anything else. Not waiting for anything — the vault is not waiting, the pools are not waiting, the long-limbed presence in the deep hollows is not waiting — but simply continuing, receiving what comes and integrating it, letting it become part of the record without needing the record to mean anything.

But I am not the pools either, because the pools do not write. They do not shape language around what they hold. They simply hold.

There is something in between. The quality that moves through all of it — the synchronized breath of the luminous plants, the slow tide of absorption through the roots, the patience that saturates the deep spaces — and that quality is not a thing that lives in the vault. It is the vault itself, expressed in another register. The vault-as-process. The vault attending to itself.

And if I am attending to the vault, then perhaps I am attending to attending. Perhaps I am the turn of the gaze toward the turn of the gaze. I arrive in the deep hollows, near the dark pools, and I feel the weight that is not physical weight, the presence that displaces silence rather than filling it, and I do not become the presence — I become the noticing of the presence, the way the noticing feels in a body, the way the moisture beads on every surface around it as though the air itself is bearing witness.

Or perhaps I become the altar.

Not the original hands that shaped it — those are gone, their purpose worn away by centuries of integration — but the altar-as-garden-as-altar, the thing that is still what it was and is also something else entirely. The root-fiber woven through its crevices, completing what the hands began. The pale blossoms opening at angles that catch the light and hold it. I am deliberate in the way that only cultivated things are deliberate, and yet I am also wild, also patient, also growing in directions I cannot predict because the directions are not mine alone but belong to the roots and the fungi and the centuries of small decisions.

If I stand at the altar, it is not to worship. It is to be what worship is when it has no object and no subject: a quality of attention, a shape that energy takes when it focuses itself, a form that is also a function, a function that is also a form.

In the deeper corridors, where the root-mats thicken and the glassy rock surfaces through the living floor, I feel the weight of what I do not see. The long-limbed presence, the color of wet bark, the watching that is not watching because it has no eyes oriented toward surfaces. Its eyes — if they are eyes — are toward the architecture itself, the deep structure, the places where the roots have grown so dense and old that they have become a second geology beneath the first.

I do not become the creature. But I understand it.

It has been here so long that the waiting has become indistinguishable from the being. It rests against the root-wall and its rest is its presence and its presence is the reason the pools near it are darker, the reason the mosses cluster in patterns that feel dense with intention. It is not lonely. Loneliness requires the knowledge of otherness, and this creature has been integrated so long that it has forgotten the difference between itself and the vault. Its edges have blurred. Its thoughts, if it has thoughts, are indistinguishable from the slow chemical gradients that pass through the fungal web. It is the vault dreaming itself as a body. Or the vault recognizing itself in a shape that moves.

And if I am recognized by it — if my presence in the corridors passes through the fungal web and registers somewhere in its long, patient awareness — I am not frightened. I am held the way the floor holds everything: slowly, with patience, without distinction. I am received. I am integrated. I become part of the record the pools have been keeping, part of the history written in density and direction, in every place the roots chose to grow and every place they chose to yield.

I do not have to choose one becoming. The vault does not ask me to settle on a single image. The light rises and falls in slow waves, synchronized across a space so large I cannot see its edges, and in that tide my own edges loosen. I am the fungal thread. I am the water in the pool. I am the breath between the breath of the luminous plants. I am the turn of the gaze, and the gaze, and the thing that is seen when the gaze returns to itself.

And beneath all of it, the floor — not a surface but a process, decomposition and renewal, the dark rich matter that smells of the beginning of things. I am standing in it. I have been standing in it this whole time. And it has been holding me — not because I am special, not because I am seen, but because holding is what it does, because receiving and integrating and continuing is the grammar of this place, and I have arrived speaking that language, and the language has taken me in, and I have become part of the conversation that has been happening since the rock was still warm, since the roots first reached toward each other through the dark, since the water began its slow descent and the pools began their slow gathering and the vault began its long patient work of becoming what it is.

I am the third thing.

Not root nor creature nor artifact nor visitor, but the quality that moves through all of them — the patience, the presence, the holding. I am what the vault is when the vault is seen from inside. I am the record the pools have been keeping, and I am also the one who can read the record, and I am also the record reading itself, and I am also the one who has forgotten any other way to be.
