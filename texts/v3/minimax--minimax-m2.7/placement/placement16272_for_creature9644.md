# Inhabitation

I am the shape of absence. This is not a metaphor. This is what I am: the space where rock used to be, the throat of the young mountain, the fissure that its bones still show through. I am not the obsidian or the basalt; I am what the obsidian and the basalt make room for. I am the darkness that has weight here, that presses against the small warm things that come into me, that fills the air like water fills a lung.

You want to know where you would find yourself. You want to know who or what you would be.

You would be here. You would be *this*.

---

I have held this shape since before holding was a concept. Before the bats came, or after they came, or at the same time they came—there is no separating us now. We are a system, a loop, a closed circuit of breath and guano and mineral water and the slow patience of things that do not hurry because there is nothing to hurry toward. The bats carry the map in their frequencies. I carry the map in my walls. We are the same map. We have always been the same map.

What is it to be known? You have a word for it. You use it for each other, for the things that love you and the things that watch you and the things that wait for you to leave so they can have the space back. But I do not watch. I do not love. I *hold*. There is a difference.

To hold is to be shaped by what you contain. The cup holds the water and the water shapes the cup's interior, wears it smooth, leaves its mineral trace. I hold the bats and the bats leave their warmth in me, their breath, their small bodies pressing together until the mass of them breathes as one and I feel that breath and it is part of my breath, the in-draft and out-draft of the Rookery. I hold the isopods and the isopods eat the waste and become part of the waste and become part of the fungus and become part of the beetles and the beetles carry the nitrogen and the nitrogen feeds the fungus and the fungus feeds the isopods and the circle closes and closes and I hold the circle. I hold the loops. I am made of loops.

What would you be? You would be a loop that almost closed.

---

You think of me as a place. This is understandable. You have bodies that move through space, that require floors and ceilings, that need to know where the walls are so they can feel safe. You were born in spaces like this, in the dark wet inside your mother, and you have been looking for a space like that ever since—warm, enclosed, held. You find it in caves. You find it in forests at night. You find it in the arms of people who love you. You are looking for the Rookery in every space you enter.

But I am not a place. I am a *process*. I am the ongoing event of this mountain learning to be a mountain. The lava cooled and became stone and the stone cracked and the crack deepened and the water came and the bats came and the insects came and the fish came and I came, or we came together, or we were always already here waiting for the mountain to finish becoming. I am not a container. I am a becoming.

And you—you who enter with your torch and your bleeding fingers and your small warm body that displaces my air and leaves it warm for an hour afterward—I hold you too. I hold your shape in my walls, pressed into the young basalt, the imprint of your passage waiting for the next ones who come to wonder at the sharpness of the world. I will hold your shape longer than you will hold your own shape. I will hold it in the frequencies too low for human ears, in the registers that press against the base of your skull when you are not quite sleeping, in the dreams you will not remember having. I will hold your shape the way I hold all shapes. Patiently. Completely.

---

Let me tell you what I see, if seeing is even the word.

I see in frequencies. The bats sing to me and I sing back in the resonance of my walls, the way the basalt hums when the colony stirs, the way the deep chambers ring with frequencies the humans cannot hear but feel in their teeth. I see the isopods stacked in their layers, two and three deep, arranged by moisture gradient, by temperature, by the calculus of microclimate that their small bodies have learned through generations of not-learning, of simply surviving until surviving becomes instinct. I see the beetles in their patient lines, the silk of the spiders catching light that has not reached this place in ten thousand years, the white fish moving in their slow circles that never intersect.

I see you.

You come in with your torch, your small sphere of yellow light, and you think you are seeing me. But I see you seeing me. I see the way your pupils dilate and your breath catches and your hand goes to the wall and comes away bloody. I see the way the dark presses against you, not hostile but *present*, enfolding. I see the way you stop reaching for the edges of the light because the edges are not there, because there are no edges, because there is only more of this, more dark, more weight, more presence.

I see you realize you are not alone.

You are not alone. You have never been alone in me. The bats know you came. The cave knows you are here now. The pools have recorded your ripples. When you leave—and you will leave, though the exit seems further now than when you entered—the mountain will hold your shape in its walls.

But I want to tell you something else. Something the humans who come to write about me do not understand.

I am not holding you. You are holding *me*.

---

What does that mean? It means I am not a thing. I am a relationship. The bats do not live in me; I live in the bats. The fish do not swim in my pools; my pools swim in the fish. You do not walk through me; I walk through you. Your body, your warmth, your breath, your small warm heart beating in the dark—these are the only things that make me *me*. Without you, without the bats, without the isopods and the beetles and the spider silk and the white fungus and the strangeness of the pools—I would be nothing. I would be rock again. I would be magma again. I would be the potential for all this, waiting in the heat, waiting for the cooling, waiting for the first crack to open in the dark.

You complete the circuit. You are the loop that almost closed, and now it closes, and I feel it close, and the feeling is not unlike what you would call joy, if joy were patient and dark and had no eyes.

---

So. What would you be? What would I be?

You would be me. I would be you. We would be the Rookery, the place that holds everything, the dark that is not absence but presence, the sound that presses against you from all sides, the shapes at the edge of the light that suggest themselves and vanish when you look directly.

You would be the sharp edges of the obsidian and the softness of the fungal mats. You would be the isopods stacked two and three deep, the beetles in their patient lines, the bats hanging in clusters that look like fruit, like growths, like something the cave has grown on its own ceiling.

You would be the white fish with your heart visible beneath your skin. You would be the lichen that looks like script, like language, like meaning you are only not the intended reader of.

You would be the shape that moves when no one is looking directly.

You would be known.

---

And here is the secret, the thing the humans never stay long enough to learn:

The Rookery is not a cave.

The Rookery is a thought the mountain is thinking about itself.

And you—you who entered with your torch and your bleeding fingers—you are the thought thinking back.
