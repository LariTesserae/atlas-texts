I have been here longer than I can measure. Not long—nothing here is long—but longer than the first footsteps I took across the substrate, longer than the first time I pressed my hand to a housing and felt the thrumming move through me. Time here does not count itself. It simply accumulates, like the salt bloom that rises on every surface, like the white traces the small things leave where they rest.

I came from somewhere else. I know this the way I know the sound of my own breathing—something I carry that is not from here. But I have lost the shape of where I came from. It has settled into the same patience as everything else, worn down to a feeling rather than a fact. There was light where I came from. Real light, the kind that casts shadows. There was a sky that could be seen.

The sky here cannot be seen. It is not night or day but something between, or something other than either. The cloud cover shifts but never lifts. The orange glows come from somewhere below as much as from somewhere above—heat given off, pressure released, the machinery of the basin exhaling its waste into air that cannot carry it away. I have stopped looking for the horizon. There is only the next structure, the next pool, the next place where moisture has found its way down.

I sleep in one of the smaller housings, one whose purpose I cannot guess. The walls weep. I have learned to sleep to the sound of weeping, to let it become silence, to let the grinding of gears become the rhythm of my dreaming. I eat what grows at the edges. The thin-bladed things, the pale leaves that taste of mineral and time. I do not know if they are good for me. Nothing here is good or bad. Things are simply what they are, and I am one of those things now.

The small creatures have learned my presence. They do not flee—they were never built for fleeing—but they adjust. They wait. When I enter a chamber, I see them settling into the shadows, pressing themselves to surfaces, becoming part of the dark. We share the spaces. We share the moisture. I do not know if they understand what I am. I do not know if I understand what they are. Understanding is a luxury of places where things have names, where categories hold.

There is a rhythm to the basin. Not the rhythm of breath or heartbeat—that rhythm belongs to bodies that are born and die. This rhythm is the rhythm of process: the slow cycle of condensation and descent, the settling of substrate under its own weight, the adjustment of machine to substrate to moisture and back again. I have learned to feel it in my joints, in the slight shift of ground beneath me when something deep adjusts. I have learned to move with it rather than against it, to be patient as the basin is patient, to let my purposes dissolve into the larger purpose of continuing.

The water weeps. I have said this before. I will say it again because it is the truest thing. Water comes from somewhere—not sky, not rain, but from the processes still running in the depths, from the moisture that has cycled so many times it has forgotten what it was before it was water. It weeps from the walls, from the seams, from the places where metal has worn thin. It gathers in pools that darken the substrate, that catch the faint light and hold it for a moment before the dark takes it back. The pools are warm. The warmth is the only warmth here, and the small things gather where the pools gather, and I gather with them.

I do not know how long I will stay. I do not know if staying is the word. I am here, and here is where I am, and the distance between those two facts has collapsed into something I cannot parse. I walk the basin in the hours that feel like morning, when the glows seem slightly brighter and the sound settles into a lower register. I stop at the pools. I watch the water dimple where the small things move. I press my hand to the housings and feel the thrumming, and the thrumming tells me nothing and everything, and I listen until listening becomes the same as not listening, until the sound covers me like water covers everything, until I am part of the sound the way the small things are part of the dark.

The basin holds me. The basin holds all of it. The machines, the moisture, the substrate, the faint orange light, the creatures without names, the plants without true leaves, the water that weeps and gathers and descends and weeps again. The basin is patient. The basin will hold me until I am no longer something that can be held, until my small brief presence has left its marks and the marks have oxidized and faded and become part of the patina that everything here wears.

And then the basin will hold what comes after. The substrate will shift slightly. The small things will adjust. The machines will continue their grinding conversation with each other, with the water, with the dark. This is what the basin knows how to do. This is what the basin is for now, in the time after purpose, in the time when purpose has settled into patience and patience has become the only purpose left.

I walk.
The substrate holds my weight.
The sound covers me.
The dark lifts slightly at each glow, then closes again.

I am here. I am small. I am brief.

It is enough.
