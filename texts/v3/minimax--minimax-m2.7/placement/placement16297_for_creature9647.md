There is a hollow here, where the root-skin has grown inward on itself — a shallow bowl worn by centuries of condensation, by the slow passage of creatures that paused here, by the patient work of the tube-formers whose tunnels empty into it like veins into a heart. The vapor gathers here when the canopy-shifts dim the light, pooling in the cool depression, and the sound is less here, and I have made my rest in this place.

I am not large. I have learned to be glad of this.

The plateau has sizes that correspond to danger, and I am the size that means I can move with purpose but cannot move fast enough to flee. This is a condition, not a complaint. Everything here is either too slow to escape or too large to need to. I am neither. I am the middle form — the teardrop body, the comma-shape stretched by some ancient pressure into something that cleaves the root-skin's surface with each contraction, each pulse of fluid through the vascular channels that show as darker lines through my translucent bark.

I do not know how long I have been moving. There is no way to know. The signals that I follow are laid down by disturbances I never see the cause of — a collapse somewhere in the distant root-skin, a decay, a surrender by something that was once large and is now becoming part of the continent's slow digestion. The signal reaches me as a gradient, a chemical weight in the air, a pressure from one direction that my body reads without my understanding. I move toward it the way water moves downhill: not deciding, not choosing, but responding to a slope that was already there.

There are others moving with me. I can feel them through the root-skin's own nervous system — a vibration that passes through the compressed matter beneath my lower surface, a tremor that says *others, here, moving, the same direction, the same signal*. We do not communicate. We do not need to. The signal is the communication. We cross the root-skin in a line, our pulses synchronized by some mechanism I do not comprehend, our bodies tilted slightly forward as though leaning into a wind that does not exist. The sound of our crossing is a low creak and tick, the sound of vascular tissue bending, the sound of resin-exudation curing in the thin air behind us. We leave trails of amber. Within minutes they are fossil-hard, small bright threads across the dark surface of the plateau. I do not know if these trails matter. I do not know if anything we leave behind matters to anything except the plateau itself, which takes everything eventually and makes it part of the continent.

I remember — if *remember* is the word for what happens when chemical traces in my tissue form a pattern that functions like memory — I remember being still. The hollow where I rest now is one I have rested in before. The root-skin here has a slightly different texture, a slightly different smell, a slight warmth from the tubes beneath that run close to the surface. I can taste this place. I can taste that I have been here. The plateau does not distinguish between the creature and the territory it occupies, and so neither do I.

There is a shape on the far side of the hollow. It is a mimic — I know it by the way it does not move when I look at it, the way it holds its stillness against the root-skin's own rhythm of expansion and contraction. The mimic's surface matches the skin so exactly that I could not have seen it if I were not already looking, already suspicious, already aware that surface and creature are not the same thing on this plateau. It is flattened against the skin, and its surface is exuded resin that has hardened into a perfect copy of the root-skin's texture: raised slightly, darker, the color of old amber.

I do not know what it wants. Perhaps it wants what all creatures on the plateau want, which is to consume or to avoid being consumed. Perhaps it is waiting for something slow enough to be its prey. I am not slow enough to be easy prey, but I am not fast enough to flee if it decides to fuse with me. I watch it. It watches me. We are both very still, and the groaning of the plateau fills the space between us, and the amber-gold light shifts as the canopy above opens a little and then closes again, and the sound in the hollow is less than the sound on the open skin, and we are both, for this moment, part of that silence.

I have no words for this, but I have a chemical: a tension that runs through my vascular channels, a weighting of my body toward one side or another. I do not know if the mimic experiences anything similar. I do not know if experience is the right framework. I move first — not away, but across, angling my body so that I pass the mimic at a distance that is safe, that is close enough to observe, that leaves a trail of amber resin in my wake. The mimic does not follow. It has its strategy of invisibility, and I have my strategy of motion, and we are not each other's problem today.

The signal is still ahead of me. I have not reached it. It pulls at me with the same weight it had an hour ago, a day ago — I cannot say how long — and I will reach it eventually, or I will die on the way, or I will stop and let the root-skin grow over me, which is another way of dying but slower, sweeter, more final. The creatures that reach the signal — the source of the disturbance, the decay, the surrender — they feed on it. They press their lower surfaces against the root-skin at the point of chemical abundance and draw the released compounds through their capillary mouthpoints, converting the dead into the living, the past into more self. This is the economy of the plateau: everything that stops moving becomes food for something that is still moving. There is no waste. There is no end. There is only the slow cycling of matter through forms that grow and grow and grow until they cannot grow anymore, and then they are absorbed.

I am moving again. The hollow is behind me, and the mimic is behind me, and the light is amber and constant and soft-edged, and I am one of the many crossing the root-skin toward the signal, my pulse synchronized with theirs, my body tilted forward. Below me, the root-skin gives slightly. It exhales vapor that smells of camphor and hot mineral. It is cool beneath the pale sun, always cool, even when the light is bright and the shadows shorten to nothing. The sound that rises from it is the sound of something uncountable dragging itself forward through a landscape it has made by being alive in it.

I am part of that sound. I am part of that dragging. I am small and I am slow and I am many, and we are following the signal, and we will reach it or we will not, and either way the plateau will continue, and the hum will continue, and the light will filter through the vapor-haze and the canopy-shifts, and the root-skin will breathe beneath me, and I will move, and move, and move.
