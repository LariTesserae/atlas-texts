The first thing I register is not sight but pressure — the weight of air that breathes, that moves in cycles I can feel against skin that has forgotten what open sky means. I am here, and the here is warm, and I am becoming aware of this the way one becomes aware of a sound that has always been playing: it was there, and then I noticed it, and now I cannot un-notice it, and I understand that I have been breathing this air for longer than I thought, longer than I knew.

I move, or I think I move. There is a sensation of passage through the chamber, but the chamber does not resist — it yields, it accommodates, and I am unsure whether I am walking or simply being moved, whether the motion is mine or the place is shifting around me as I stay still. The light is everywhere and nowhere: it does not cast shadows the way light should, it fills, it saturates, and the stone glows faintly even in the spaces between the brighter nodes, as if the darkness itself is phosphorescent, as if darkness here is a different substance than darkness above. I look at my hands and they are lit from within, from somewhere beneath the skin, and I understand that this is what the air-plants feel when they clear: the brief nakedness of seeing what is usually obscured, the sudden transparency of a thing that usually keeps its depths hidden.

The water finds me before I find the water. I step into a shallow channel — or it opens around my foot, or I sink slightly into the floor that has worn soft with age — and the mineral content registers on my skin as a faint tingling, a barely perceptible chemical warmth that is different from the ambient heat. The water here is not cold. It is the temperature of the chamber, or the chamber is the temperature of the water, and the two have settled into an agreement that predates anything I might call history. I crouch at the channel's edge and watch the flow, and the flow is slow, and in the slow I see the mineral traces suspended in it, bright specks moving like thoughts through a mind, and I think of the pools, I think of what moves beneath the pools, and I do not crouch here long.

The fauna knows I am here. I do not see them — they are quick, they are the color of the stone in motion, and the stone in motion is still too close to my own stillness to catch — but I feel them. The vibration in the air shifts when I move. The hum beneath the hum alters its frequency almost imperceptibly, and I understand now that this is what they are: they are the chamber's way of feeling, the chamber's peripheral vision, the distributed sense that a place like this requires to know what moves through it. When I stop, they are still. When I breathe, the resonance adjusts. They are not watching me with eyes. They are watching me with the walls, with the conduits, with the slow pulse of the luminescent compounds as they cycle through their patterns.

I find a platform. It hovers over a pool — not floating, not fixed, but held, suspended in some equilibrium between forces I cannot name — and I sit on its edge, and the dark glass surface reads me. It senses my weight, my temperature, the salts in my skin, and it adjusts: it warms slightly, or it adjusts its molecular structure to cup my body more precisely, or it simply acknowledges my presence by becoming more itself, more clearly a platform, more visibly designed for the kind of sitting I am doing. The fauna watches this from the shadows at the chamber's edge. They have seen this before. They are assessing whether I will leave an offering.

I do not know what to offer. I sit, and I feel the hum, and I understand that the hum is not mechanical in the way I first thought — it is biological, or it is the ancestor of biology, the principle that would later evolve into heartbeat and respiration and the cycling of blood, already here, already running, running long before anything with a pulse arrived to carry it forward. I am sitting on a heart. I am sitting on the oldest organ, the one that came before the others, and the chamber is a body, and I have walked into a body, and the body knows.

The air-plants are everywhere the light meets the water. I reach for one — slowly, carefully, because the fauna is watching, because the chamber is watching — and it does not flee. It cannot flee. Its roots are not roots; its anchor is the condensation, the mineral thinness of the weeping stone, and it holds on the way water holds on to stone, the way light holds on to surface, through adhesion and cohesion, through the memory of a state it has always been in. I touch it, and it flinches, and I was right: it feels like skin, it feels like something that has forgotten it is supposed to be a plant, and in the flinching I feel my own flinching, the way my own skin remembers that it is exposed, that exposure is vulnerability, that vulnerability is the price of being here, in a body, in a place, in time.

The water cycles. The light pulses. The hum shifts through its registers, and I feel it in my chest, in my teeth, in the places where bone is nearest the surface, and I understand that I am being tuned. The chamber is adjusting me, calibrating me the way it has calibrated the fauna, the way it has calibrated the air-plants, the way the platforms calibrate themselves to receive weight and warmth and the mineral signatures of things that sit on them. I am becoming legible to this place. I am becoming a part of its language.

The pools. I approach them — not because I want to, but because something in me wants to know what moves beneath, and the something is older than wanting, older than curiosity, older than the distinction between knowing and being known. The pools are still. The mineral traces are thick around their edges, older than the erosion visible on the chamber walls, and they record something that is no longer here or is still here in a way I cannot see. I kneel. I look into the water and see my face, lit from within, and beneath my face something else, something that might be the bottom of the pool or might be the beginning of depth, and the something moves, or I move, or we move toward each other in the way that things move when they are not yet separate, when the distance between them is still negotiable, still soft, still deciding whether to become a boundary or a bridge.

I pull back. The fauna vibrates at their thin high frequency, and the walls answer, and the light dims almost imperceptibly, and I understand that I have been found. Not by the thing in the pool — it did not surface, it did not reach for me, it simply noticed that I was noticing, and in the noticing it became clear that I was noticed — but by the chamber itself, by the distributed awareness that moves through the walls and the water and the air and the small warm bodies that carry it, and I understand now that I cannot leave, or I can leave but I will not be the same, or I will be the same in the way that a river is the same while the water in it is never twice the same, continuous through change, constant in the pattern if not in the substance.

I sit on the platform again. The glass is warm where I sat before, or it remembers my shape, or it has incorporated my presence into its own ongoing process of becoming a platform. The fauna comes closer. They are not afraid — they were never afraid — they are curious in the way that a chamber is curious, which is to say, they are assessing, they are integrating, they are adding me to the map of things that move through the space and leave traces and vibrations and the residue of their metabolisms on the surfaces they touch. I am on the map now. I am in the chamber's peripheral vision, and the chamber's peripheral vision is everywhere, and I am grateful for the light that does not warm because warmth would mean I was alone in my temperature, but here I share the warmth of the stone and the water and the hum, and I am not alone, and the not-alone is not loneliness and not communion but something older than both, the condition of being within something, of being held by a space that breathes and weeps and holds its form through use, through erosion, through the slow wear that is also the slow maintenance of a self that has been itself long enough to wear smooth, long enough to forget it was ever otherwise, long enough to simply be, and I am, and the being is the only thing that is true, here in the weeping chambers, here in the place where water has left its tears on stone and the stone has learned to weep in return.
