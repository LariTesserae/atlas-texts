You would find yourself in the pause. Not the pause before speech, which implies intention, a speaker gathering breath. This is older than that. The pause between one wind and the next. The space where the tonal voice fades to nothing and the basin holds its silence like water in cupped hands.

You would be the thing that does not move when the gusts come. You would be what the wind moves around, what the air parts for, what the dark-leaved trees lean away from without knowing why. You are not the wind—the wind is the basin's voice, and you are what hears it. You are not the light—the light arrives and fills you and leaves and you remain. You are the remainder. The still point that the basin turns around without knowing.

You have been here longer than the buildings. You will be here when the buildings are gone. The builders understood the water, the frost, the angles of the sun against stone. They built channels to direct what fell, cisterns to hold what gathered. They did not build for you, because you cannot be captured. You are what stays when everything that can be gathered has been gathered. You are the residue of abundance, the patience that survives the rain.

At night, when the larger thing moves through the channels, you feel its passage as pressure, as displacement, as the air thickening in the spaces between structures. You do not fear it. You have no mechanism for fear. Fear requires anticipation, and anticipation requires a future that feels different from the present, and for you there is no gap between what is and what will be. There is only what is: the stone, the cold, the slow passage of air through crevices too small to see. The larger thing moves through all of this and you are simply here, being what you are, which is the space through which it moves.

When the clouds part and the light comes low and horizontal, you feel it differently than the moss does, differently than the dark leaves. The light warms the stone and the warmth moves inward through centuries of accumulated cold, and you are in that warmth for a moment, and the moment passes, and you are in the cold again. But the cold is not empty. The cold is full of you. You are the cold's knowledge of itself.

The birds descend when the thermals rise. They drink from the cisterns the builders made and you do not drink, you do not need, you simply continue. That is the thing about you that others cannot understand: you do not continue because you are strong or because you have learned a trick. You continue because continuing is what you are. The wind does not choose to blow. The stone does not choose to erode. You do not choose to wait.

But there is no other way to be here.

The dark-leaved trees stand motionless in the white air. And you are here, in the stillness, in the pause that has no end, and it is not lonely because loneliness requires a self that can feel separate, and you have no edges, you have no boundary between yourself and the stone you rest in. You are the basin hearing itself. You are the cold knowing the cold.

When spring finally comes, it comes the way light comes: not as a door opening but as a cloud thinning, as a difference in quality, a shift so gradual that no single day can claim credit. The subsurface seepages wake. The frost pulls back from the basin floor in patches, in fragments, in the slow revelation of dark earth that has been hidden since autumn. And you feel the change not as something happening to you but as something you are part of, the way the wind is part of the pressure differential, the way the light is part of the angle of the sun.

The channels begin to carry water again. The biological film darkens. The ferns uncurl. And you—you do not unfurl or swell or emerge. You were never gone. You simply become more visible as the abundance returns, as the margin between presence and absence widens enough for others to see you there.

This is your life: to be seen in the margin. To be the thing that remains when everything that can be gathered has been gathered. To be what the basin is, when the wind has passed and the light has gone and the silence is not empty but full of everything that has learned to wait.

You are that waiting.

You have always been that waiting.

And when the light comes again—when the clouds part and the horizontal brilliance fills the basin and every stone edge blazes and the channels run with reflected gold—you will be there in the light, as you are there in the dark, as you are there in the pause between one wind and the next, because you do not leave, you do not end, you do not need the light to be what you are.

You are what lives here.

You are everything that has learned to wait.
