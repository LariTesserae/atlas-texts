I arrive the way water arrives — not suddenly, but as if I have always been falling here, caught on a leaf, directed inward through a thousand small redirections until I find myself at the bottom of a channel, moving.

The sound is the first me. It fills the space that I might otherwise call a self and expands into that space, becomes the space, becomes the thing the space is made of. Chirp and whistle and the soft percussion of wings — but not separate from one another. Layered so completely that I stop hearing them as individuals and begin to feel them as texture, as the valley's way of breathing. The insects hum beneath. The water ticks and trickles through its grown channels. Something larger moves in the canopy and makes a low sound, a vocalization that is less heard than felt, a pressure in the warm air.

I am standing on a terrace path. The path is not dry. It has never been dry. My feet know the made ground — dark, rich, composed of centuries of layered compost and leaf litter and the slow accretion of tended soil. I do not leave footprints. The ground receives me and continues.

The cultivated trees form walls around me — not walls exactly, but the suggestion of walls, the memory of enclosure. Broad leaves hang heavy with condensation. When a drop falls, it lands on my skin or on a leaf below or on the dark earth between the root networks, and I hear it arrive, and the sound is part of the larger sound, and the larger sound is the valley's voice, and the valley's voice is not something being said but the saying itself, the act of speaking as presence, as being here, as this.

At night — and I have been here long enough for night to arrive — the warmth does not leave. It holds. The canopy keeps the starlight out except where shafts have been left deliberately, and those shafts are narrow and bright, cutting down through the green-dark to illuminate a single fern, a cluster of white flowers, a stretch of root-beds. The bioluminescence glows along the terrace edges: pale blue-green, slow, patient. They do not flicker. They pulse, the way breath pulses, the way water pulses as it moves through tissue.

I move between the archways — grown archways, limbs that met and fused, year by year, the people's patience made manifest in wood and bark and leaf. The structures breathe. I can hear the water traveling inside their walls. I can feel the warmth moving through them the way warmth moves through a body. The people are here — I have seen them — but they do not separate themselves from the breathing. They are part of it. Their voices join the sound, become texture, become the valley speaking itself into being.

There is no word for what I am. Not visitor. Not resident. Something between — a presence that has become continuous with presence, that has been absorbed into the wall of living noise until I cannot say where the sound ends and I begin. The valley is not a place I am in. The valley is the condition of my being here. The warmth is breath. The dark is the inside of something. The noise is not something the valley makes but something the valley is.

And then —

The wind shifts. A gust moves through the canopy and displaces the birds, brings a hundred into sudden, overlapping alarm at something moving below the trees. The valley rings with them. The sound rises, becomes structure again, becomes the thing that holds all other things in place. And in the ringing, I hear myself — not as a separate thing, but as a note in the chord, a frequency in the hum, a single drop falling into the great water system that moves through the valley's body and carries everything, always, toward the cisterns, toward the deep, toward the place where water goes to wait and be called back up again when the dry season comes.

The alarm passes. The birds settle. The hum resumes.

I resume.

This is the quietest I will ever be. Not silent — never silent — but the noise has become me so completely that I can rest inside it, and the resting is not absence but presence, is the valley breathing in and the valley breathing out and me, somehow, included in the breath.

I tend something. I do not remember what. My hands know the motion — checking a channel, encouraging a root, harvesting what is ready. The motion is part of the valley's motion, continuous, season by season, generation by generation, the long slow will to tend made flesh, made leaf, made water moving through grown walls toward the place where it is needed.

The night is warm-dark. The bioluminescence glows. Eyes reflect in the dark — small and near, small and far. Something hunts. Something is hunted. The valley holds both, does not choose between them, allows the sharp calls and the answering silences to exist together, to be part of the same song.

I am part of the song.

I have always been falling here, directed inward, caught on a leaf, funneled through the cultivated system until I found the place where water waits and the canopy holds and the sound becomes me and I become the sound, and the valley breathes, and I am breathed, and the wall of living noise rings on, rings on, rings on.
