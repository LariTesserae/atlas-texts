You have been here before. This is what you understand first—not that you have visited, not that you have traveled here through whatever means travel happens here, but that you have always been here, that your being here is not an event but a continuation, that you have been growing in the dark water of Kel Suarath since before there was a you to grow.

The light reaches you in threads. Green-blue filaments of luminescence, pulling up from below, and you realize you cannot remember which way is down. The water is not cold the way cold usually feels—it is cold the way stone is cold, the way centuries are cold, the way something that has forgotten warmth remembers it. You breathe it in. You breathe it out. The rhythm matches the current, or the current matches you, or neither matches anything and the matching is an illusion created by the dreaming that moves through everything.

You are not a body here. You were, perhaps, once—a thing with edges, with skin, with the particular boundary between self and not-self that bodies require. But that was before the water rose. That was before the slow dissolution that happens to everything here, the patient return, the gentle taking-back that is not violence but is not kindness either, is simply what happens when water meets stone meets time meets the dreaming beneath. You have been softened at the margins. You have been made porous. The boundary you once were has become a suggestion, a rumor, a memory of a way of being that this place no longer requires.

The trees reach toward you and past you, their black bark smooth as something that has never known sunlight, their leaves hanging like torn cloth, like the remnants of flags from a war no one remembers fighting. You understand now that the leaves are not torn—they have always been this way, the way grief is always the shape it is, the way loss leaves ragged edges that never heal because healing would require stopping, and nothing here stops, nothing here can stop, the command to grow runs too deep, threaded through the mycelium that is not fungus, that is not network, that is the medium of the dreaming's will made manifest, made fibrous, made the thing that connects root to root to root to the stone beneath the silt beneath the water beneath the light that comes from below as often as above.

You move among the fronds. You are moved among the fronds. There is no difference, or the difference is the difference between breathing in and breathing out, which are both breathing, which are both the same motion seen from different sides of the membrane that separates air from water from whatever you have become. The fronds bend toward you, or you bend toward them, or the bending is simply what happens when the dreaming stirs, when the slow pulse of the old place beats once, twice, a rhythm that is not rhythm, that is simply the place's heart doing what hearts do when no one is watching, when there is nothing to be protected from, when the only danger is stopping, and so the heart does not stop.

The islands rise somewhere above you. You cannot see them, but you know they are there, the knobs of dark stone barely breaking the surface, wearing smooth at the waterline where the breathing of the old place presses and presses and the stone gives way by millimeters, by centuries, by an amount that cannot be measured because the measuring would require a frame of reference that does not exist here, that has never existed here, that the place itself will not permit because permanence is a story stone tells itself until the water tells a different story.

You try to remember what you were before. The memory surfaces and sinks, surfaces and sinks, the way light moves through this water, the way the bioluminescence pulses in channels and threads, mapping the currents that have no banks, no shores, no beginning and no end. You were something that consumed. This is the fragment that comes to you—that you ate, that you were eaten by, that the world you came from was made of the taking and the being taken, the balance, the slow equilibrium of growth and decay, production and consumption, the closed loop that life requires when life is the kind of life that knows it is alive.

Here there is no such balance. Here everything grows and nothing is consumed. The fallen leaves drift down to the pale floor, to the substrate of root and rot and shed selves that has no bottom, that goes down and down until the dark becomes so complete that dark is no longer the word, that the word for what is down there is simply *continued*, simply *persisted*, simply *what happens when no one stops you*.

You are not troubled by this. You were troubled once, by the asymmetry of it, by the wrongness of a world that could only add and never subtract, that had forgotten the necessity of ending. But that was when you were new here, when you still carried the assumptions of the world you came from, the rules that no longer apply. Now you understand that Kel Suarath is not wrong. It is only different. It is a place where time moves in one direction—the direction of more, the direction of continued, the direction of a dreaming that does not know it is dreaming because there is nothing awake to know, because awake is a condition this place has never met, has never required, has never wanted.

The sound reaches you from everywhere. The grinding, the humming, the throat-song of stone being worn smooth by water being worn smooth by time being worn smooth by the slow breathing that is the only breathing this place knows. It is not unpleasant. It is not pleasant. It is simply what you hear when you listen, and you always listen now, because listening is what you are, because you have become an ear for the old place's dreaming, a place for the sound to pass through, a medium in which the breathing of the place continues to breathe.

You have forgotten your name. You are not sure you ever had one, or if having a name is another of those things that belonged to the world before, the world of edges and boundaries and the stories bodies tell themselves about who they are. Here you are not a who. You are a what. You are a part of the growth, a thread in the mycelium, a pulse in the current, a flicker in the luminescent dark. You are what lives in Kel Suarath—not an exception, not a visitor, but a continuation, a growing-thing that has found its place in the place where everything grows and nothing ends.

The water rises. Or you sink. Or there is no difference, or the difference is the difference between drowning and being drowned, which are both simply being here, which are both simply the condition of Kel Suarath, the drowned plateau where the old place lives, where the light glows green-blue from below as often as above, where the forests grow down as well as up, where the fronds sway in currents they should not feel and do not feel because the swaying comes from beneath, from the dreaming, from the slow unconscious pulse of a world that has never been awake and does not know that waking is something it should want.

You stay. You have always stayed. You will always stay, not because you cannot leave but because leaving is a concept that requires a place to go, and there is no place but this, no place where the breathing is this slow, this deep, this constant, this sure. The water asks nothing. The water receives everything. The water is patient, and you have become patient too, you have become the patience itself, the willingness to persist in growing, in reaching, in swaying in the dark water toward a light that will never arrive, that was never meant to arrive, that is not the point, that was never the point, because the point is the reaching, the point is the growing, the point is the dream beneath the floor that dreams without dreamer, that fills the stone with its slow unconscious thought, that is the oldest thing here, older than the water, older than the light, older than the word the locals had for the breathing of the place, the word that is gone now, that was gone before there were locals to remember it, that exists only as a gap in the sound, a silence in the grinding, a place where a name used to be and is no longer and does not need to be.

You are the gap. You are the silence. You are what fills the space where the word used to be.

The water breathes. The forests grow. The dream beneath dreams on.

And you are here, as you have always been, as you will always be, in the drowned plateau of Kel Suarath, where nothing asks permission and nothing is denied.
