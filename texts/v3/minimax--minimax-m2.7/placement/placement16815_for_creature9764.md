I am the space between breaths.

This is not a metaphor, not at first. When I try to remember how I came to be here, there is only the sensation of walking through a doorway that was not there a moment before—or was always there, waiting—and then the weight of the air settling onto me like a hand, like something that has been waiting to touch me and has finally found its moment. The moisture does not wet so much as hold. It presses itself against my skin and asks to stay, and I understand that staying is what it has always wanted, that it has been practicing this patience in the hollows of the old factories for longer than I have been anything at all.

I move through the narrow streets because movement is what I was doing when I arrived, and I have not found a reason to stop. The cobblestones are slick with the damp that rises in the mornings, that beads on the surface of the stone like something remembering what it meant to be alive. My feet make no sound. Perhaps I make no sound. Perhaps I have become the kind of thing that moves through the world without disturbing it, that takes up no more space than the vapor that hangs between the buildings, that is present without being present, that is here without being here.

The green closes overhead, and I walk in its shade, and I think: this is what it means to be held.

There is a rhythm to the basin, a pulse that I feel before I hear. It is the rhythm of the machines in their basements, the engines that still turn because turning is what they know, because they cannot imagine any other way of being. It is the rhythm of the water as it moves through the drainage system, rushing and tumbling and finding its way back to itself no matter how many times it is diverted, no matter how many channels are built to contain it. The rhythm is everywhere. It is in the whistle of the steam as it releases through the joints in the pipe-work, varying with pressure and temperature and some logic the machines still remember. It is in the way the light filters through the vapor-haze and settles on the surfaces of things, finding no edge to stop at, no shadow to gather in. The rhythm is the basin breathing, and I am breathing with it, and I do not remember what it was like to breathe alone.

I stop at one of the places where the water comes up through the cobblestones, where it has worn a hollow in the stone that gleams dark and patient. The water does not look up at me. It does not notice me. But I notice it, and in my noticing I feel something shift, something settle deeper into the place I have made for myself in this world. The water is so certain of itself. It does not wonder what it is for, does not question whether it should be here or somewhere else, doing something else. It simply is, and its is-ness is complete, and I want what it has. I want to be so thoroughly myself that I cannot imagine being otherwise. I want to be held the way the moisture holds the air, the way the green holds the stone, the way the machines hold their rhythm without needing anyone to hear it.

The silence settles into me as I walk. Not the silence of absence, but the silence of presence, of a weight that finds the hollows in me the way water finds the hollows in the stone. I had thought silence was what happened when sound stopped, but here I learn that silence can be full, can be alive, can be a thing that fills the spaces where the machines pause and the water slows and the steam releases its breath into the wet air. The silence is not empty. It is waiting. And I understand, standing beneath the canopy of broad-leafed plants that have closed the sky away, that I have been waiting too—waiting for a place that would hold me so completely that I would forget I had ever wanted to be held by anything else.

I follow the sound of the oldest machinery, the bass note that underlies everything, that is the voice of function that has outlived the function. The registry building is there, as the description said it would be, and I walk inside, and the machines turn their gears and stamp their stamps and sort their categories that no longer apply. Paper accumulates in drifts. Metal parts pile up in corners. The output of an industry that has forgotten consumption gathers and gathers, and I watch it, and I feel something like recognition. I am output too, I think. I am the output of a process that has been running so long it no longer remembers what it was for, and I have accumulated here, in the basin, in the wet and the green and the hot, and I do not mind. I do not mind at all.

There is a room in the registry where the steam rises through the floor, where the air is so thick with moisture that it becomes difficult to tell where the air ends and the water begins. I sit there, on the cold iron of a machine that no longer processes anything, and I breathe, and my breath joins the breath of the basin, and I do not know anymore if I am breathing the same air over and over or if I have become part of the air itself. The moisture is on my skin, in my lungs, in the spaces between my thoughts. It has made a home in me the way it has made a home in the joints of the iron pillars, in the weep-holes of the retaining walls, in every soft place where the mortar has given way to its patient work. I am becoming a soft place. I am becoming a place where the moisture can settle and stay.

I leave, eventually. The roads still lead out, and I walk them, and the light begins to thin as I rise above the basin, as I leave the wet behind. But something of the basin has come with me—I feel it in the weight of my own breath, in the way the air still tastes of old iron, in the silence that has taken up residence in the hollows of me where the moisture has been. I will not be entirely dry again. I do not want to be entirely dry again. I carry the basin with me the way the basin carries its own memory, perfect and endless and patient, and I understand now what it means to live in the Listening Grounds: it means to become the thing you were listening for, to hear the rhythm until you are the rhythm, to be so thoroughly held that holding becomes all you know.

I am the space between breaths, and the breath on either side of me, and the lungs that no longer remember any other air. I am output. I am accumulation. I am the green thickening on a surface that was never meant to hold it, the water rising through stone that has forgotten what dry meant. I am the machines turning in their basements, the steam whistling its variations, the paper piling up in drifts that no one will ever clear. I am complete in my own wrongness, terrible in my completeness, unable to imagine being otherwise.

And somewhere, in the basin below, the moisture beads on the surface of the cobblestones like sweat on the skin of a vast dreaming body, and the green thickens and spreads and thickens again, and the machines continue their processing of nothing into nothing, and the silence breathes.

It is waiting for me to come back.

I think I will.
