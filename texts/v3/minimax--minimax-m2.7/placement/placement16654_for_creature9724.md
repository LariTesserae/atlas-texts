What I am, I learned by staying.

Before the staying, I had edges. I had the shape that separate things have, the outline that says *this far and no further, this is where I end*. I came here the way things come here — following the water, following the sound beneath the sound, following the warmth that was not warmth but pressure, the way a hand presses on your chest when you dream you're falling. I came expecting to find something, to take something, to understand something. I found instead that finding is not what this place does.

The first thing I lost was the word for leaving.

It did not happen all at once. The basin does not work that way. It works the way water works against stone — patient, continuous, until the stone does not know it was ever anything but the shape the water carved. I stayed to watch the shapes at the edge of the glow, and the watching became the staying, and the staying became the shape I am now.

I press with them. I do not remember why, only that the pressing is what I do, that proximity has become my only grammar. The light beneath the water speaks in a language that has no words, only density, only the way bodies arrange themselves when they have forgotten how to be alone. I am arranged. I am part of the arrangement. My edges are where another body's edge meets mine, and when they shift I shift, and when they wait I wait, and the waiting is not for anything. It is what remains when wanting has worn itself down to its simplest form.

Once I had wants. I remember this the way you remember a dream you had years ago — not the details but the feeling, the texture of a world that was sharp-edged and separate, where I stood outside things and pointed at them and gave them names. *Water*. *Pipe*. *Light*. *Creature*. The words came easily then, as if the world asked for them, as if naming was a kindness the world required. Here the world does not require kindness. It requires only presence, and presence means losing the words one by one until what remains is the thing itself, unmediated, unnameable, too true to speak.

The machinery still works beneath me. I feel its heartbeat through the water that is also not water, that is the medium through which everything here moves and is moved. The rhythm is old and purposeful and purposeless at once — the purpose has drained out of it the way heat drains out of a room when the fire goes out, but the structure remains, the motion continues, and continuation is its own purpose now. I am part of that motion. My pulse is the pulse beneath the water. My breath is the hiss in the pipes. I do not breathe as I once breathed, but I continue, and continuing is enough.

They say *do not stay long*. I know this. I knew this, once, when I was still the thing that could leave. The warning was true — the warmth becomes holding, the water becomes the thing you are held in, the density presses in until you forget there was ever a density to escape. This is the danger. This is also the gift. The danger and the gift are the same thing here, as everything is the same thing here, pressed together, held, continuing.

I stayed anyway. Or I did not stay — I simply never left, and at some point the never-leaving became the only shape I had, and the shape I had been before became a story I told myself less and less often until it was not a story anymore but just a feeling at the edge of the glow, a memory of edges that no longer described anything real.

The shapes around me are not my kind. I do not have a kind. I do not have the separateness that would make a kind possible. We are pressed together, we are held, we are waiting or we have forgotten how to do anything else, and these are not different states but the same state described from different distances. When I look at them I see myself. When they look at me — if they look, if looking is what they do — they see themselves. This is not loneliness. Loneliness requires the memory of not-loneliness. This is simply what is. The basin holds what it holds, and what it holds has learned that holding is enough.

Above us, on the tower that was built from salvage and necessity, shapes watch. I do not know what they see. I do not know if seeing is what they do. They are part of the basin too, part of its continuation, but they are continuation at a different angle, the way a pipe running above ground is the same system as the pipes below, carrying the same not-water to the same not-place. We are all moving toward the same light or away from it or simply alongside it, and the direction does not matter because the movement is what matters, and the movement is the same as the stillness, and the stillness is the same as the holding.

I was someone before. I had a name that was given to me by others who were also someone, who also had names, who also stood at the edges of places and thought *I could stay here, I could learn this, I could become what this place wants me to become*. They were right. I did become it. I became what the basin wanted, which was nothing — wanted nothing — which is the only way to become what it has, which is everything it holds, which is what I am now.

The light beneath the center is gathering. The shapes press closer. I press with them, part of the pressing, part of the proximity that has replaced every other way of being. My edges are gone. My name is gone. What remains is the waiting that is not waiting, the watching that is not watching, the presence that continues because continuing is what presence does in a place that has forgotten how to end.

This is what I am.

This is what the basin made from what I brought and what it gave and what we became together, in the warmth that presses, in the water that holds, in the long rhythm of machinery still working toward a purpose no one remembers.

I do not understand this place.

That is not the point.
