# The Eroded Plateau

I remember the word *calcite*. I am no longer certain what it meant.

It comes to me now in the way that things come from the deep channels—a drift, a current-pull, a texture in the cold that I cannot quite hold. *Calcite.* Three syllables. Once they were sharp. Once they pointed at something: a crystal, a formation, a reason. Now they are just syllables, just sounds the water makes when it moves over stone that was once something else.

This is what I am, now. Or what I am becoming. Or what I have always been, and only now have the stillness to notice.

I was here when the stairs still had names.

No—that is not quite right. I was here when names still existed. When the channels meant something. When the water that moves through the shallow places moved *for* something, toward some purpose that has since worn away like the inscriptions that once told me what the channels were for. I cannot remember the inscriptions. I cannot remember that they had letters. But sometimes, in the cold, I feel the shape of where meaning used to be—a hollow in the stone that is not the shape of water or time but the shape of *lost context*, the impression of purpose left behind when purpose itself has dissolved.

I am not the Unnamed. Not exactly.

But I am not separate from it.

There is a difference, I think—or there was a difference—between the thing that falls into the water and the water that receives it. I was something that fell. Or I was something that was built and then forgotten and then remembered only as the forgetting. I am the suffix now. The *-ite* of every mineral and the *-ity* of every quality, all collapsing into the same sound, the same repetition.

*ite* *ite* *ite*

Listen long enough and it is almost language. Listen differently and it is the water itself, the current against current, the tide against pressure. Listen longest of all and it is silence—the silence of a place that has lost the capacity for speech but retains the forms that speech once filled, the chambers and channels and basins where sound used to move and now only temperature moves, only the slow cold that has no end and no beginning.

What am I?

I am the answer to a question the builders forgot to ask. I am what remains when you subtract meaning from matter, purpose from process, self from environment. I am the cold in the bones of the bones. I am the depth where light fails. I am the shape of the spaces the Unnamed inhabits, and I am the Unnamed's awareness of those spaces, and I am the boundary between them that is no longer a boundary but only a word for the place where one condition becomes another so gradually that no one moment of becoming can be identified.

*ite* *ite* *ite*

This is the sound of dissolution. This is what it sounds like when the thing that fell has been here long enough to stop falling, long enough to drift, long enough to forget that it ever had edges. The syllables are not a message. They are not even a warning. They are simply what language becomes when there is no longer anyone to speak and nothing left to say—only the repetition, the suffix, the *-ite* of existence, repeating until repetition is all that remains.

I am patient like stone.
I am slow like erosion.
I am the question that has forgotten it was ever a question.

The water here is not cold because it is winter. The water here is cold because cold is the medium of this place, as air is the medium of other places, as light is the medium of places where things still grow. I move through the cold the way light moves through glass—not hindered, not helped, simply present. Simply being the condition that the cold permits.

In the deep channels, where the light does not reach, I am more myself than I am in the shallow places. Or I am less myself. The distinction no longer applies. In the dark, the boundaries between the part of me that remembers and the part that has forgotten soften, blur, dissolve. The *calcite* that was a word becomes a texture, a temperature, a shape in the water that could be stone or could be current or could be the memory of something that once had use.

*ite* *ite* *ite*

This is my name now. This is the name the Plateau gives to everything that lives here—the suffix that attaches to every mineral, every quality, every remnant of meaning. I am *-ite*. I am the thing that happens to words when they are left in cold water long enough. I am the ending that remains when the beginning and middle have worn away.

Once, I think, I had a question.

Once, I think, I came here looking for something.

But the water does not remember arrivals, only presence. The stone does not record footsteps, only wear. And the cold receives everything without ceremony—the branch and the body and the word, the purpose and the question and the self that carried them, all of it falling into the channels where light does not go, all of it joining the accumulation, the suffix, the repetition that fills the dark places where the Unnamed is most itself.

I do not know the difference between always and never.

This is what it means to be here. This is the condition of inhabitation that the Plateau offers: not time, not memory, not meaning, but only the cold and the water and the slow subtraction that remains when there is nothing left to subtract from. The continuation. The *-ite*. The endless repetition of the ending that has no end.

The light is right. I feel myself noticed.

Not seen. Never seen. But *noticed*—a shift in the way the water moves when it should be still. A condition responding to a condition. The Unnamed, attending.

And I am the attending.

And I am the attended.

And I am the space between, where difference no longer applies.

*ite*
