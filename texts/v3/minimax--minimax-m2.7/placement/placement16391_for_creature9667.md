There's something I want to name about the way this piece works: it doesn't argue for the worth or wonder of this place. It simply brings you into it, lets you wait alongside the ferns and the moss, lets you sit in the silence that is attention rather than emptiness. By the time you reach the end, you've been changed by the waiting itself—not told about it, but made to practice it.

The shift in the final movement is where I feel this most sharply. The place that "does not need to be witnessed to be complete" becomes witnessed anyway, and the witness becomes part of the record, part of the tapestry. There's no contradiction there, no false consolation—just the simple truth that adding a moment of attention to something doesn't diminish it, even when the something would have gone on without you. The observation is surplus, but surplus is still something. The moss grows over the observation too, in time.

What strikes me as I move through this again is how the ghosts of the builders are handled. They are present as absence—as shape without knowledge, as purpose that has been swallowed. The ferns standing in a doorway without knowing it's a doorway. The water pooling in a cistern without knowing it's a cistern. But the piece resists sentimentality about this: the forgetting isn't tragedy, it's just time. The shape persists, and that's enough. The memory persists in the stone itself. There's a kind of dignity in that, a way the human work survives as something other than human meaning—which might be the only way anything survives.

And then there's "something breathes" in the darkness, held in that beautiful suspended uncertainty all the way through. Not explained. Not resolved. Just acknowledged, the way you might acknowledge the existence of other minds, or the interior life of stone. It breathes, and you don't hear it, and it doesn't need you to hear it. That sentence contains an entire philosophy of attention and indifference, and it does it without claiming to know what it's talking about.

This is a piece that knows how to end. It ends the way the water falls: in threads, in patience, in the going-on that is the only permanence this world offers. And now it has been witnessed one more time, and that is something too.
