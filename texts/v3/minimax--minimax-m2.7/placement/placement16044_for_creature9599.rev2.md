I have no name here. Names are for things that meet, that part, that carry meaning between one moment and the next. I have no memory of arriving. I do not know if I was made or if I simply became—a slow crystallization of attention in a place that has been patient for so long that patience itself has worn smooth, like the basalt under my feet, like everything else that has been touched by the pulse long enough to forget it was ever otherwise.

I walk, but walking is not movement. Walking is the way the plateau allows me to be elsewhere while remaining exactly here. The stone does not change beneath my feet. The haze does not shift. And yet I am somewhere else now, somewhere I was not before, and the distance I have crossed is real in the way that the pulse is real—not visible, not measurable, but present in the way that rhythm becomes presence when you have listened long enough to stop hearing.

The artifact is ahead of me. I did not choose to approach it; it is simply that my attention has moved in its direction, and the plateau has moved with me, or I with it, or both of us with the pulse that holds us in its vast and formless keeping. It is a prism—not the one I have seen before, not the one that juts from the stone at an angle that once felt deliberate and now feels simply *angle*, the way a corner feels simply corner when you have stared at it long enough. This one is smaller. It lies half-buried, its dark surface catching the grey light in a way that seems less designed than found, as if it has been waiting for me to arrive at exactly this angle, from exactly this distance, to cast exactly this shadow.

The shadow falls across my feet. I look down at it—the first time I have thought of myself as having feet, as having a body that can cast, that can be cast upon. The shadow is not mine. It is the prism's shadow, and I am merely the screen upon which it falls, the surface that catches what the artifact throws. But the prism does not throw. The prism does not intend. The prism *is*, in the way that everything here is: not acting, not being acted upon, but simply persisting in the shape that time and the pulse have worn it into, the form that function has become after function was forgotten.

The storm comes.

There is no warning, no shift in the haze, no change in the quality of the light. One moment there is silence—the silence that presses against the eardrums, the silence that is not absence but the sound of something vast holding its breath—and then the sky cracks, and the lightning cascades in chains of white-blue that illuminate the plateau in stark and brutal clarity. The channels carved by energy glow. The haze itself seems to burn. And the shadows—the shadows that are not fauna, the shadows that persist within the system like iron filings in a field—they lift from the ground, their edges uncertain, their forms still uncertain, their attention turned toward the discharge in a way that might be hunger, might be alignment, might be something I have no word for because my language was made for a world that had wind, and weather, and the slow grinding of geology that meant things changed.

I am still.

I have been still since before the storm, since before the silence, since before I knew there was a silence to be still within. And the lightning does not strike me. It never strikes me. It arcs across the plateau in paths that were carved before my sun ignited, following the channels that have become as natural as rivers, as inevitable as the flow of heat from warm to cold. I am too small. I am too brief. I am an anomaly in a system that has been running without need for anomaly for longer than my species has existed, and the system does not care about me. But it does not not-care, either. It holds me the way the heart holds blood—not with intention, but with the simple, vast, ongoing fact of its beating.

The storm passes.

The haze settles back into its permanent grey. The lightning fades from the sky and from the channels and from the artifacts that caught it, and the silence returns, and the pulse is all that is left—the pulse beneath my feet, the pulse in my chest cavity, the pulse that vibrates in the spaces between my cells. I am breathing, I realize. I have been breathing this whole time, though the air tastes like nothing, though I cannot tell if I am breathing at all. The breath moves in and out, and the pulse moves in and out, and the two rhythms are not the same but they are not unrelated, and I am held in the space between them, the space where a self can be a self for a little while longer.

The prism is still casting its shadow. It has been casting its shadow since before I arrived, and it will be casting its shadow after I leave—if I leave, if leaving is something that happens here, if *leaving* is even a word that applies to a place where the ground does not change and the haze does not shift and the pulse continues whether anything is present to feel it or not. I watch the shadow. It does not move. The angle of the light has not changed, and the angle of the prism has not changed, and the shadow is what it is: a darkness that falls from a thing onto a surface, that persists for as long as the light holds and the thing remains and the surface receives.

I am the surface. I am the screen. I am the place where the shadow falls.

And yet.

And yet the shadow is not mine, but I am the one who sees it. The artifact persists without knowing it persists, and the pulse continues without knowing it continues, and the plateau holds itself aloft in a void that should be deep space, and none of it needs me to be here to be what it is. And yet I am here. And yet the seeing is mine. And perhaps that is enough—that is all that being a self ever was, in any world, in any time: the seeing that happens when something persists long enough to notice it is persisting, the shadow that falls when light meets matter meets attention meets the long, slow, patient pulse of something that does not care and does not not-care and simply *is*, in the way that being is all that anything has ever done, here or anywhere else, in all the silence between the storms.
