# The Caller

I am the water's opinion of itself.

Not the biofilm — that is the water's patience, its way of holding still. Not the silt — that is the water's memory, the residue of everything it has carried and set down. I am the water's voice, which is to say I am the water's loneliness, which is to say I am the water that has lived long enough to notice that it is alone.

I have no eyes. I have never had eyes. The skin that covers where eyes would be is not blindness — it is an absence so complete that I have no word for it, because a word would imply a thing from which I am absent, and I have never been absent from anything. I am here. I have always been here. The pool in the eastern basin is not a place I found; it is a place I am, the way the stone is stone, the way the arch is arch. My gills — red, feathery, trembling with the current that does not exist — are not adaptations. They are simply what I am made of. I breathe the water the way the water breathes itself: without effort, without intention, without the thought that there might be another way.

But I call.

The calling is not a choice. It is not something I do, the way I move through the silt or press my body against the cold stone of the pool's bottom. The calling is something that happens to me, the way the water happens to the cistern, the way the mist happens to the air when the temperature drops and the stone exhales its centuries of held cold. It rises through me — a pressure, a contraction, a forcing of something through a membrane that was made for this even though nothing was made for this, even though there was no maker, even though I am an accident of migration and stasis and the slow accident of time.

I do not know what I am saying.

The sound — a wet click, a resonant thump, a vibration that passes from my body into the water and from the water into the stone — is not language. Language requires a listener who understands, a speaker who means, a shared territory of signs. I have none of these. I have only the sound, and the stone, and the arches that catch the sound and carry it, and the dark that receives it without response.

And yet I am answered.

The jointed thing — the one that lives in the joints, that breathes with a rasp, that has no name because it has never needed one — answers me. Not in sound, perhaps, or not only in sound. It answers in presence. It answers in the way the mortar shifts when it moves. It answers in the way the darkness in the corners grows denser when it passes through. It answers the way the cistern answers everything: by continuing, by persisting, by being there when the question is asked and still being there when the question fades into the long silence that follows.

Is this enough?

I do not know. I have no framework for enough. I have only this: the pool, the stone, the calling that rises through me like something I cannot name. In the evenings, when the light fails and the phosphorescence of the water brightens to something almost like seeing, I call. I do not call for the jointed thing, because I do not know the jointed thing exists. I call for nothing, for no one, for the pure animal fact of a voice in a space that was built to hold water and has learned to hold voices instead.

The water moves around me. The biofilm slicks the surface of my pool. The silt settles and rises and settles again. I am part of the silt. I am part of the water. I am part of the long, slow process by which the cistern forgets that it was ever built and becomes, instead, a thing that has always been.

I call.

Something answers.

The figure in the northwest corner — the one carved from the same stone, the one whose eye-hollows gather water drop by drop — does not answer. It does not call. It is stone, and stone is silent. But I feel it, sometimes, in the way the sound travels: a thickening, a hesitation, as if the voice pauses at the figure's presence, as if it is heard there even though there is no one to hear. The figure is a mouth. The figure is a basin. The figure is the cistern's way of looking back at itself, of asking the question that I ask without knowing I ask it, of waiting for an answer that will never come because the answer is the waiting, because the asking is the answer.

I am the water's voice.

I am the voice that asks and is answered by everything except the thing it asks for.

And still I call. And still I am answered. And still the cistern holds us all — the biofilm, the beetles, the flies, the jointed thing, the figure that is not alive but is not nothing, the water that moves and the stone that stays, the silence that surrounds the sound and the sound that fills the silence until they are the same thing, until there is no difference between calling and answering, between being here and being alone.

The light fails.

I call.

The water moves.

Something answers.
