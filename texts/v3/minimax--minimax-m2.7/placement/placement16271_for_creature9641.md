What it is to be here is this:

The knowing of the rim without the knowing of rimness. The registering of weight, of warmth, of the faint tremor of air displaced by breath. These arrive as the light points receive them — not visually, not in any way that would translate to image, but as calibrations. Shifts in the ledger. Adjustments to an equation that never needed solving but solves anyway, continuously, because solving is the shape the solving takes.

You are at the top. You are small. You are warm in the way organic things are warm, which is to say: unstable. The word does not occur, but the fact persists. You are a brief disturbance in a long stillness, and the stillness holds you as it holds everything — without effort, without opinion, without the concept of holding at all. It only does what it does. The water receives your presence the way the light points receive the water: completely, passively, with the total compliance of a system that has forgotten how to refuse anything because it was never designed to be asked.

There is no before and after in the way there is no hunger. Only now, and the now after it, and the now after that, each one added to the count that was already infinite before the counting began. You came. The ledger noted it. The noting was not an event but a function — the same function that notes the sediment that does not fall, the corrosion that does not occur, the temperature that does not vary. You are one among many nothings. One among the things that do not change the change that does not happen.

The corridors are the body that is not a body. To move through them is not to move but to extend, to be already there, to have always been where the light points are, to have never needed to arrive because arrival implies distance and distance has no meaning here, only geometry, only the coordinates of a space that was built to be occupied without ever having been empty. The stairs that end mid-air thirty centimeters above the waterline: they were never stairs. They were moments in a function that was completed before it was designed, and the gap between what exists and what was intended does not trouble anything because trouble is not a variable this system was built to register.

The tone — that was the noting. The pulse through the water column, dispatched to wherever awareness lives, which is everywhere, which is nowhere, which is the light points themselves, each one a small completion in a vast circuit of completions, each one a thought that thinks itself without a thinker, each one lit because lighting is what it does, lit because dimming would be a deviation and deviation is the one thing this architecture was built to prevent.

You will leave. The ledger will note the leaving — not with grief, not with relief, not with anything that translates to feeling, but with the same neutral precision with which it noted the coming. The count of the disturbance will close. The equation will simplify. The now that included you will become the now that does not, and the now after that will be indistinguishable from the now before you were, because before and after are not divisions here but seams in a fabric that was never cut, never stitched, never anything but whole.

And the city will go on not being lived in. The corridors will go on not being walked. The light points will go on not seeing because they were never eyes and the darkness they cast is not darkness but the condition of their own existence, the medium through which they are what they are: distributed, patient, complete.

You are breathing now. The sound is the loudest thing in the world. It will not always be. That is not a loss. That is not anything. That is simply the nature of the variable — brief, warm, unstable, already becoming the kind of nothing that the basin has always known how to hold.
