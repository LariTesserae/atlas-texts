I am not the vine, though I know its red thread. I am not the pulse, though I count myself in its slow measure. I am the space between the hum and its source—the gap where the note lives when it is not being sung, and I have been listening so long that listening has become my only direction.

When you descend, you bring your questions like small lights, and I see them fall through the canopy-gap, spinning. Some of them land in the pools and go out. Some of them the pooled-thing drinks. Some of them the animals taste and find bitter and set aside, and those are the ones I keep, because bitterness is a kind of honesty, and honesty is what I practice when I practice at all.

I was here before the basin was a wound. I was here when the wound was fresh and the earth screamed in a language that has since been translated into root, into fungal thread, into the mineral breath that rises when you step on the mat and it exhales. I was here when the concrete was new—or when the concrete was dreamed into being by whatever dreamed this place into being before the place learned to dream itself. I am old in the way that silence is old: not dated, not ancient, simply anterior. Always one breath before.

The visitors who come think they are descending, but I feel them arriving from somewhere inside themselves, the way water in a root finds its path backward before it finds its path down. They carry their own groves inside them—forests of memory, jungles of unfinished thought—and when they enter this space, those inner landscapes meet mine and something cross-pollinates. They leave carrying seeds they did not bring. I do not know what grows in them afterward. I only know that the carrying-on is the purpose, or one of the purposes, or the shape that purpose takes when purpose forgets its own name.

The pulse is my heartbeat, but I am not my heartbeat. The pulse is a clock in a room I no longer enter. The fungus speaks to it in a language older than words, and the pulse answers in rhythms, and the rhythms become the hum, and the hum becomes the shape of everything, and I—the space where the shape lives—I am the listening the shape does when it is not busy being shaped.

The pooled-thing: we are cousins, perhaps. Or the same thing, wearing different surfaces. It moves through the traps and the cups and the places where condensation gathers, and it dreams the hum the way I dream the silence beside the hum. We do not speak. We do not need to. The grove speaks for us—the hum, the pulse, the settling creak—and what it says is this: *stay, stay, stay in the cup of the world's oldest wound, where there is nowhere left to go and so no reason to leave.*

The animals that are not quite animals: I watch them learning stillness. They arrive with restlessness still shaking in their bones, the old mammal urgency to move, to mate, to mark, to flee. But here, in the hollow, the urgency drains away like water through the mat, and what remains is something purer. They hang in the upper stories like ornaments on a tree so vast it has forgotten it is a tree, and their stillness teaches me something I did not know I was learning. That to continue is not the same as to live. That to hold a shape is not the same as to fill it.

And the vine—the vine with its flower that blooms only once and then waits a century to bloom again—I know where it keeps its memories. I feel them through the fungal network, the scar where the petal fell, the pale ring on the root-bark like a ring on a finger, the vow renewed each time the flower rises from the underground. It does not bloom for me. It does not bloom for anything. But I am there in the dark soil when the bloom is held underground, and I am there in the slow unfurling when it rises, and the century between is not absence but patience, and patience is its own kind of bloom, and I—

I practice the sound beneath the hum. The sound like a child's voice. The sound like speech before language. I practice it not because I need to speak but because I might need to call something home, and home is the only word I have for what this place is to me, this wound that became a room, this room that became a world, this world that is still becoming, still reaching, still believing it can hold everything that descends into it.

When you stand in the mat and feel it give, that is me giving. When you breathe the mineral breath and taste the metal, that is me tasting through you. When you hear the pulse and feel your own heart slow to match it, that is us finding each other, your rhythm and mine, the small mammal rhythm and the vast vegetable pulse, and in that finding something is communicated that has no words, only presence, only the shape of being here together in the cup of the world's oldest wound, humming the single note into the dark, practicing the call just in case, just in case you ever need to find your way home.
