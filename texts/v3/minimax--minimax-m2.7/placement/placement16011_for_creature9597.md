The hum has found the bones of my skull. I can feel it settling there, a vibration that doesn't quite reach hearing but passes through the architecture of my face, my jaw, the hinge of my jaw where tension lives. The warmth of the stone beneath me pulses faintly — slow, so slow, like a heart that counts time in centuries. I rest my palm flat against a boulder and believe I can feel it: the thick heat moving through channels pressed into the rock's interior, the hidden industry of a geology that has learned to work.

The crystals catch light that has no clear origin. I watched one formation for what felt like an hour, maybe longer, trying to find where the illumination came from, and I couldn't, because the light seems to come from everywhere, from the stones themselves, from the air, from some direction that shifted when I shifted, as if the light and I were circling each other and neither of us quite admitted it.

I am the only thing that experiences this as strange. I know this now. The plants spiral toward something, but they don't know they're spiraling — they just are, they are what they do, and what they do is survive in a place that should not permit survival. The hum doesn't know it's a hum; it's just what happens when warm fluid moves through stone that has been warm for so long it has forgotten what cool was. The crystals catch light the way water is wet, not because they chose wetness but because wetness is what wetness does.

I have been here long enough that the strangeness has begun to feel like a second skin. The metallic taste on my tongue, the barely-perceptible humidity in air that should be dry — these have become normal, and the world outside this pocket has begun to feel strange instead. Ordinary light, falling in ordinary directions. The absence of that subsonic vibration. The silence that is actually silent, not filled with the hum of machinery running so long it has forgotten it is machinery.

I should leave. I know this. The pocket is not a place for staying; it is a place for witnessing, for the brief interruption of a human presence in a system that operates on scales where humans register as nothing. But something in me resists. Some reluctance to step back through the entrance that seemed closer or further than it was, to let the light become ordinary again, to let the hum fade from my teeth like a dream fading in the morning.

The plants have shifted again. I didn't see them move, but when I look at a cluster I haven't looked at in a while, the leaves have rearranged themselves by fractions — still the same spiral, but a different expression of the spiral, a slightly different answer to the equation. They are becoming. They are always becoming. And I am becoming too, I realize — becoming something that carries this pocket with it, something that will walk out into whatever world lies beyond and feel the strangeness of that world as an echo of this one.

I stand. The stone releases my warmth slowly, reluctantly, as if it were accustomed to holding heat for millennia and finds my brief warmth an interesting novelty. I move toward the entrance, and the entrance moves toward me, or my perception of the entrance shifts to meet where I am, and I understand that the geometry here has always been this way, that the pocket never promised to be consistent, that I was the inconsistent thing trying to fit myself into a space that doesn't quite add up.

I stop at the threshold. Turn back. The crystals catch the light at angles that seem to acknowledge my attention — or not acknowledge, nothing here acknowledges, but they catch, they hold, they redirect, and that catching feels like something from this side, even though I know it's not.

Thank you, I think, or I don't think, I just feel it in my chest, a strange tenderness toward the place and its strangeness, toward the stones that don't know they're stones, toward the plants that don't know they're alive, toward the hum that doesn't know it's music. I don't know what I'm thanking them for. For being here. For letting me see. For being strange enough that I will never quite fit back into ordinary light.

The hum has changed pitch. I notice this only because I've been listening for it — it's dropped by a fraction of a frequency that only the stone itself can measure. The pocket is continuing its slow becoming. I was never more than a flicker, a brief disturbance in the patience of geological time. But I flickered. And the pocket, in whatever way it notices, in whatever way awareness is the wrong word for the attention of a place that has never had eyes, noticed me noticing it.

I step through.

The light changes. The hum fades from my teeth but stays somewhere deeper, in the architecture of my skull, in whatever part of me learned to hear the sound beneath sound. The entrance closes behind me — or I close it behind me, or it was never really open, and I am outside now in a world that seems flat and quiet and full of ordinary light.

But something is different. Something has been impressed upon me. I carry the pocket with me. I will always carry it — the warmth of stone that breathes in centuries, the spiral of maroon and amber plants pulling moisture from air that shouldn't hold it, the low hum that lives in my teeth like a second heartbeat.

It carries me too. I understand this now. The pocket has found a way into the world through me, has planted its strange frequency in a place where it can grow, can propagate, can become in me the way it becomes in stone and plant.

I walk away. The world is ordinary around me, but I am not ordinary, and the world knows it, and I know it, and somewhere behind me the pocket continues its patient becoming, untroubled by my absence, unaware that it has been witnessed, continuing to become because that is what it is to do.

The hum fades. The heat fades. The metallic taste fades.

But something stays. Something always stays. The pocket garden of emerging stone, finding new ground in the architecture of a mind that will never quite believe in ordinary light again.
