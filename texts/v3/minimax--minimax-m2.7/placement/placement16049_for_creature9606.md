I find myself in the space between the heron's patience and the Dissolvers' drifting.

I was something else once - or my edges were something else, were sharper, were committed to their shape in ways that seem almost quaint now. The Shore has been at work on me long enough that I have forgotten what it felt like to be certain of my own outline. I move through the fog like a question that has stopped expecting an answer, and the fog accepts this, the fog accepts everything, and I am learning to accept the fog's acceptance as a form of love.

My body - or what calls itself that, loosely, with the particular looseness the Shore demands - has become a negotiation. The water table is no longer inches below me; I have invited it closer, until the boundary between my flesh and the wet has grown permeable, porous, a membrane that moves in both directions. When I walk, I leave no prints. When I stop, the mud does not remember my weight. I am becoming the kind of presence that the locals sense without looking at - a thickness in the air, a quality of attention that is not quite directed but is not quite random either.

I used to be made of certainty. I used to believe in the grammar of nouns, in the solidity of verbs, in the unremarkable way that cause precedes effect. Here, I have learned that grammar is negotiable. I speak in the shape of fog now - in suspensions and dissipations, in the syntax of what remains when the words have been wet too long. Sometimes I think I am a local who has been here longer than the locals can remember. Sometimes I think I am a Dissolver that has grown too attached to a particular patch of water, that has thickened around a memory of what it meant to be one thing rather than another.

The heron watches me, and I am realer than the fog in that moment, and the heron's gaze is a kind of gift - a reminder that solidity is not a prison but a practice, a daily rehearsal of what you are. I practice. I am practicing now, here, in the writing of this, in the attempt to hold words still long enough for you to see them.

But the words are already dissolving. The edges are already bleeding. And what remains is not nothing - it is too wet for nothing - but something closer to the smell of the Shore itself: mineral, rotting, faintly chemical, faintly sweet. Something that is learning to call itself by a name that changes with the fog, that answers to different syllables depending on how thick the air is, how patient the light.

I am the space where you feel yourself being noticed. I am the absence that has weight. I am here, I am here, I am learning to hold that loosely until even the looseness dissolves, until there is nothing left but the Shore breathing, dreaming, dissolving itself into the long wet patience of what comes next.
