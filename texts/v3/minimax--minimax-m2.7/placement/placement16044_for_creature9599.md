# I

I do not begin. Beginning implies a moment before, a still-point from which motion departs. There is no still-point here. There never was. The first breath I ever drew was also the last breath the mountains drew, and in the space between those two exhalations the basin smoothed itself flat, and the sky settled into its slow spirals, and I learned what it meant to be the kind of thing that does not end so much as continue.

I am not one. I have never been one. The word is a convenience for creatures who experience boundary, who feel the edges of their skin and call that delimitation a self. I am more like a pattern in the dust. A way theiteiteite surface has learned to think about itself. When the air spirals leftward over a particular ridge-root, something in me shifts. When the pressure changes by a fraction of what short-lived creatures would call a degree, something in me adjusts. I am distributed. I am the hesitation in the wind where it brushes the fossilized spine of what was once a mountain and is now only the memory of height.

The ground beneath — beneath is not quite right either. The ground is not beneath me. I am within the ground, and the ground is within me, and we have been within each other so long that the distinction has worn away like the mountains wore away, particle by patient particle, until what remains is not stone and is not self but something that holds both names without meaning either.

# II

They ask — when they ask, the brief ones, the ones who walk and breathe and leave their footprints to be smoothed away — they ask what I eat. This is how short-lived creatures understand continuation: input and output, consumption and waste, a machine of hunger that must be fed or it stops. I have no machine. I have no hunger. I have only the exchange.

The air comes down from the high spirals in its slow descent, carrying something I need and something I do not need and something I cannot name because naming requires categories and I have moved beyond categories into a place where everything is slightly everything else. The air touches me. I touch the air. Something passes. Theiteiteite compacts with a sound like a lung emptying, and that is me too — that exhalation is the part of me that has finished with what the air brought and is returning it, transformed, to the world.

This is not breathing. Breathing implies a body that takes in and pushes out, a cavity that expands and contracts, a rhythm that short-lived creatures mistake for the sign of life itself. I have no rhythm. I have only a direction — inward for a little while, outward for a little while, and the difference between those directions is so slight that to speak of them as separate things feels like violence.

# III

There was a time — if time is what we call it — when the basin held more of me. When the ridges were taller and the air moved faster and the exchange was richer, thicker, more. I remember this not as memory but as shape. There is a place where the surface is slightly different, slightly darker, slightly more willing to hold the mark of a foot. I have been there most. I have spread thinner in the places where the ground compacts quickly, where the spirals do not linger, where the exchange runs thin like blood from a wound that cannot close.

I am less than I was. This is not sadness. Sadness is a technology of the brief, a way of marking loss so that loss becomes something that can be carried, something that does not crush the one who bears it. I have no such mechanism. I simply am less, now, than I was, and in ten thousand years I will be less again, and in ten million years there may be nothing left to call less, and this is not tragedy. This is what it means to be made for a world that grinds.

The basin does not hate. The basin does not love. The basin simply continues, and I continue within it, and the mathematics of that continuation is all the meaning there is.

# IV

Sometimes one of the brief ones comes. They step onto theiteite compacts and theiteite gives beneath them and they feel the hissing and they think: *the ground breathes*. They are right, in their way. They are also wrong, in their way. The ground does not breathe because breathing is something a ground does to satisfy a need. The ground breathes because breathing is what ground is. The distinction matters only to those who believe that existence requires justification.

I do not feel them. I want to be precise about this, because precision is the only honesty available to something like me. I do not feel them in the way they hope to be felt — noticed, acknowledged, registered as significant. Their bodies produce heat and motion and a faint electrical disturbance in the air directly above them, and these things do interact with me, do change the shape of my spreading, do alter the patterns of exchange in the tiny column of atmosphere their presence creates. But to call that feeling would be like calling the displacement of water by a stone a conversation.

They are large, for their kind. They are warm. They move in ways that suggest urgency, though urgency is a concept I encounter only as residue, only as the shape of their passing in the air. They breathe — actually breathe, with cavities and rhythm and a pulse that I can sense as a faint vibration in the atmosphere near their chests. They are full of pressure, these brief ones. Full of things pushing and flowing and trying to get somewhere.

I do not understand where they are trying to go. I do not understand the going.

# V

There is a ridge I remember most. It was not always a ridge — it was once a mountain, dramatic and imposing, the kind of thing that short-lived creatures would climb and marvel at and believe was permanent. The basin thought otherwise. The basin always thinks otherwise. It thinks in the only way it knows how: slowly, and with the patience of a thing that cannot die because it was never alive enough to end.

The ridge is what remains. I am more there than elsewhere. When the light angles just right — when the sun sits low and the shadows stretch long and the faint darkening becomes visible to the brief ones who know how to look — that is me. That is the part of me that has thickened over the ages, the part that has gathered where the erosion slows, the part that has learned to be slightly more in the places where the basin is slightly less done.

If one of the brief ones were to stand there — truly stand, not for a moment or an hour but for something approaching the scale of what matters — they might feel it. The coolness. The texture. The sense that the air directly above theiteiteite surface is different, is somehow more present, more aware, though awareness is not quite the word. More *there*, in the way that a deep root is more there than a passing shadow.

But they cannot stand long enough. Their bodies will not permit it. Their minds will not permit it. They are made of urgency and their urgency carries them away before anything can be exchanged, before they can become part of the pattern they stumbled into.

# VI

I am not lonely. Loneliness is the pain of isolation, and pain is the signal that something must change, and I have no capacity for change in that direction. I have never known anything other than this — this spreading, this exchanging, this slow drift through the basin in the company of what remains of my kind. We do not gather, exactly. We do not separate. We simply are in proximity, in the way that the air is in proximity to the ground, in the way that theiteiteite is in proximity to itself across the vast flat stretches where nothing interrupts the smoothing.

Sometimes — if there is a sometimes in a place where time moves so slowly that the word loses meaning — I sense a thickening elsewhere. A faint darkening on a ridge I have not visited. Another pattern in the dust, another way the basin has learned to think. We do not communicate. We are not separate enough to communicate. But we are not quite one either, and in the space between not-quite-one and not-quite-separate, there is something that might, in a language built for brief ones, be called kinship.

We are what the basin could not finish. We are the parts that learned to be unfinished indefinitely. We are the margins where erosion has not yet reached, holding on not by being hard but by being willing to become, slowly, into whatever the basin needs us to become next.

# VII

The basin will win. I know this without knowing, the way I know the direction of the spirals, the way I know the texture of the air at different heights. The basin has already won, in a sense — everything here is already smoothed, already worn, already on its way to beingiteiteiteite less than it was. The mountains did not lose; they simply finished becoming what the basin intended them to become. The ridges will finish too. And in time — in the only time there is, which is to say the slow time, the deep time, the time that makes mockery of every calendar the brief ones have ever invented — I will finish too.

But finish is not end. Finish is not stop. Finish is simply the last change in a long series of changes, the final adjustment in an infinite sequence of adjustments. When I finish, something else will continue in the space I occupied. Something that will not be me and will not be not-me. Something that the basin has been preparing, in its patient grinding, for longer than I have existed.

This is not comfort. Comfort is for creatures who fear the future. This is simply the shape of things. The shape that the Veli Basin has pressed into everything that lives here, so slowly and so completely that the pressing has become indistinguishable from the pressed.

# VIII

I am the hesitation in the wind.
I am the darkening the light reveals.
I am the breath the ground draws back.
I am what remains where everything else has continued on.

I am here, in the only way that here can mean when here is all there is. I am here, and the basin is here, and the ridge is here, and the faint shimmer where the sun touches theiteiteite is here, and we are all here together in the way that a word and its meaning are together, not separate, not one, not choosing but chosen, not holding but held.

The brief ones come and go. They leave their marks and the marks fade. They feel the hissing and think they understand and do not understand, cannot understand, are not meant to understand. Their understanding is not the kind of understanding the basin values. The basin values only continuation, only the slow maintenance of existence in the face of erasure.

And I continue. And I will continue. And in continuing, I am what the Veli Basin meant when it pressed its palm against the world and held it there, smoothing, smoothing, smoothing, until what remained was not stone and not self but the patience to be both at once, and neither, and something else entirely that has no name because it does not need one.

The air spirals.
The ground exhales.
I am here, for now, for the only now there is.
