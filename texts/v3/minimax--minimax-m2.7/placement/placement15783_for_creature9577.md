Three hours or six—you have stopped counting. The light here does not count either. It thickens and thins like something breathing, and you have learned to stop looking up for guidance.

Your boots are wet through. You stopped noticing hours ago, or you noticed and then the noticing became so constant it disappeared into the texture of walking itself, a kind of wet friction that your body has accepted as the baseline from which all other sensations vary. The ferns brush your legs as you pass and leave moisture on your shorts. A drop falls from somewhere above and lands on the back of your hand, and you do not know if it is rain or condensation or the basin's own secretion, its perspiration, its slow living seep.

The sound is a wall you walk into and through. Today—tonight—it has taken on a new register, something lower, a vibration that settles in your chest when you stand still. The creatures are moving somewhere to your left, parallel to your path, not following but matching, a current moving alongside your current. You can feel the weight of their attention even when you cannot see them—*especially* when you cannot see them. They have learned you. You have learned to feel learned.

Ahead, the walkway rises on its tapering supports, cable and faith, three hundred meters of empty air to the next structure. You have crossed eleven of them since the first clearing. You remember the first clearly—the shock of stepping onto something solid after the uneven ground, the way the walkway hummed beneath your feet with a vibration that climbed up through your bones. You do not remember the seventh or eighth. They have blurred together into a sensation of *between*, a texture of motion that your mind has filed under *passage* and stopped interrogating.

The structures absorb sound. You step onto the walkway and the roar dims to a murmur, a distant conversation held behind glass. In here—in/on/through the structure's skin—you can hear your own breathing, and for a moment the sound is so foreign you stop to locate its source. Your lungs. Your throat. The small wet machinery of your body doing what bodies do.

The bridge sways. Not much—centimeters—but enough, and you feel the sway in your ankles, your knees, the base of your spine. The cables that hold this walkway are thicker than your arm where they anchor to the structure's edge, but they flex, they carry the weight of your passage and release it, and the structure absorbs the release, and somewhere deep in the wall something sighs—a mechanical exhalation that you have begun to find comforting, the sound of a place maintaining itself, tending its own edges, adjusting.

The other side arrives before you expect it. You have learned not to trust your sense of distance here. The structure ahead—which looks, from the bridge, like it might be an hour's walk—is closer than that. The structure ahead which looks, from the bridge, like it might be close—is always exactly as far as it needs to be to make you work, to make the arrival feel earned, to make you understand that you have been allowed to come this far. You suspect this is training. You suspect the basin is teaching you something it will only teach when you have learned not to ask what it is.

Inside, the luminescence is wrong. Not bad—*wrong*—the light here is colder than the blue-green pulses at the water's edge, more patient, the kind of light that has been burning so long it has forgotten it is a flame. You hold your hand up and the light falls on your palm and does not warm you. It observes. The light here is an eye that does not blink.

A doorway opens onto a room you did not see from outside. The room contains nothing. The room is exactly the size of your expectation of what a room should contain, and this precision is disturbing—the walls are close enough to feel intentional, the ceiling low enough that you could reach up and touch it if you wanted to confirm what you already know: the structures are built to human scale, or you are being reshaped to fit them, or the calibration runs both ways, and you are becoming the thing that can live here while the basin becomes the thing that can hold you.

You walk.

The shaft drops twelve meters and opens onto a corridor that should not connect to what came before. You know this because you are facing the wrong direction, because the light is different, because the walls here are darker and the floor has a grain that your boots sink into slightly, as if the stone is tired, as if it has been holding itself up for so long it has begun to sag. The charge in your teeth intensifies. You can feel it in your jaw, a tightness that spreads up into your temples, a pressure that is not quite pain but is adjacent to it, the way the basin's wetness is adjacent to drowning—not the same, but related, but close enough that you keep the relationship in mind.

The creatures.

You hear them before you see them, and then you see them, and what you see does not resolve into a single image. They are large and they are fluid and they are *present* in a way they were not present in daylight, their forms filling the space you occupy, their eyes catching the wrong light and holding it. There are three of them. There are five. The number shifts when you try to count, and you understand that counting is not what they are for. They are for *being here*, for owning the basin in the way weather owns a coast, and you are for visiting, and the visit is nearly over.

Not over. You are not ready. But nearly.

One of them makes a sound that your mind almost translates. Not language—*almost* language, the way the water's motion is almost stillness, the way the structures' silence is almost peace. You feel the sound in your chest, your stomach, the backs of your eyes, and for a moment you are certain that the sound is a message, that it means something, that if you could just hold still long enough the meaning would surface and you would understand what the basin has been telling you since you first stepped into the ferns and felt the wetness enter your clothes and begin its patient colonization of your skin.

You hold still. The sound ends. The meaning does not surface. But you are certain now that it exists—that there is a message, that you are being calibrated, that the distances between structures have been measured against your stride and adjusted, and the bioluminescence pulses at intervals calibrated to your heartbeat, and the creatures watch with an attention that has been calibrated to your fear and your wonder and your exhaustion, and you are inside something alive, something that breathes, something that is very good at hosting and has been waiting.

For what?

For you? For anyone? For the concept of visitor, the category of outsider, the fact of outside-ness that your presence represents?

You do not know. You walk deeper into the structure and the light goes cold and the walls go close and the charge in your teeth becomes a hum that sounds almost like speech, and you stop, and you listen, and the hum does not resolve into meaning but it resolves into *presence*, into something that is here with you in the dark, adjusting itself to your presence, calibrating.

The basin breathes.

You breathe with it.

The night outside is absolute, but in here there is the cold light, and the hum, and the sense of being held in a cupped palm, and the basin asks nothing of you but presence, and you give it that, you give it the only thing you have, and the structure carries you forward into another clearing and another structure and another bridge across the three hundred meters of empty air where the creatures watch from the water and the bioluminescence pulses and the ferns grow thick and the sound is a wall and you are inside the basin and the basin is inside you now, has entered you through your wet clothes and your damp lungs and your teeth that carry the charge and your eyes that have learned to see in the wrong light, and you are not the same person who walked into the ferns three hours or six hours or a lifetime ago, you are someone new, someone the basin made, someone calibrated to the spaces between structures, and when the final structure rises ahead of you—center or not-center, end or rest or pause—you will know what to do because the basin has been teaching you since the first step, and the lesson was not a lesson, it was an adjustment, and you have been adjusted.

You arrive.

The structure does not open. It opens for you. The door that was not a door becomes a door, and beyond it is light that is not luminescence, not the cold patient flame, but something older, something that has been waiting longer than the hills had time to fracture, longer than the stone agreed to be stone.

You step through.

The basin exhales.
