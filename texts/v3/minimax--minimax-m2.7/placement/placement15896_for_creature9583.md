# What I Am Here

I have been standing in the center for a long time.

Not because I chose the center — though I did, or something chose it for me, or I was placed here by a process I don't remember and can't trace — but because the center is where you end up when you stop trying to understand the edges. The cliffs drop away into cloud, and the cloud suggests other places, and the other places are not here, and here is enough.

The stone holds my weight. It also holds the memory of my weight, the slight depression where my feet have settled into the clay-like surface, and then releases it, flowing back, erasing me, beginning again. I leave footprints and the footprints leave me. This should be strange. It is not strange. It is the first thing I learned here: that the plateau does not remember you, not in the way that stone in the older world remembers. It only witnesses you, and the witnessing is temporary, and the temporariness is not loss but process.

The light falls on me the way it falls on everything.

I have stopped trying to hold it. In the first hours — or days, or weeks, time is strange here, the processes that should take millions of years happening in what feels like the time between one breath and the next — I cupped my hands and tried to catch the beams, tried to understand what they were made of, what they carried, why they had weight. They slipped through my fingers, or I slipped through them, or we passed through each other the way the mist passes through the mist, and I was left with nothing in my palms but the memory of warmth.

Now I let them fall. I feel them on my skin — not warmth alone, but something denser, something that presses and doesn't press, that enters and doesn't enter. When I walk through a beam, my shadow flares for a moment, iridescent at the edges, and I wonder what is being written on me, what patterns are passing across my surface for creatures to read. They navigate around me. They file me as irrelevant. I am not food, not predator, not competitor. I am obstacle. I am landscape.

But the landscape is breathing, and I breathe with it.

---

I was something else before.

I remember this the way you remember a dream — fragments, impressions, the sense of a shape that no longer fits. There was air that was not this air. There was light that did not fall in sheets. There was solid ground that stayed solid, that did not flow back into itself, that did not hold your footprint and then forget it.

I remember the word *gravity*. I remember using it, trusting it, building things on its assumption. Here, the ground rises to meet my feet and then releases them, a slow tidal pulse that I feel in my ankles, my knees, my spine. I am not sure if I am lighter or heavier than I was. I am not sure if I am lighter or heavier than I should be. The plateau does not explain itself. The plateau does not need to.

The warmth comes from below. It rises through the soft stone, through the pools, through the structures that sit on the surface with their too-smooth angles and their marks that might be language. The warmth is the engine of everything: the breath, the cycles, the ongoing formation that keeps this place young. I warm my hands on the disc at the top of the column, and the heat moves through me, and I understand — without understanding, the way you understand a heartbeat — that I am part of the engine now. My warmth rises. My breath falls. The plateau and I exchange something, some charge or information or life force, and the exchange is what keeps us both in motion.

---

The creatures sing their non-language.

I have learned to hear the differences. The ones by the pools hold their positions, their elongated bodies folded into geometries that turn the thin sheets of water into resonance chambers. When one calls, the others join — not randomly, but in harmony, each choosing a tone that complements, so that the plateau has this constant undertone that I feel in my chest, in my teeth, in the soles of my feet where they press against the stone. The sound is structure. The sound is wayfinding. The sound is how they know where the water moves, where the drainage will open, where the breath is rising or falling.

I am not one of them. I do not fold my body into resonance chambers. I do not turn water into voice.

But I have begun to match their pitch.

It happens without trying. I open my mouth and let the sound out — not singing, not speaking, just letting the air in my lungs find the frequency that the plateau is asking for — and the creatures pause, just for a moment, their iridescent surfaces flickering with patterns I cannot read. Then they resume. I resume. We are not communicating. We are not not communicating. We are participating in the same process, and the process does not distinguish between the ones who were born here and the one who arrived from somewhere else, somewhere that had different light and different water and different stones that stayed where they were put.

---

The light falls in sheets, and the sheets have weight, and I have learned to read them.

They tell me where the creatures are going. They tell me where the pools will form. They tell me the time — not the time of clocks, not the time of days, but the time of process, the time of the plateau's breathing, the moment when the stone swells and the moment when it releases and the moment when something in between happens, something that has no name but that I can feel in my joints, my teeth, the air in my lungs.

I have learned to stand in the bright beams. Not to catch them, not to hold them, but to let them pass through me, to feel the weight and the warmth and the something-more-than-photons moving through my skin, my blood, the spaces between my cells. I flare, I think — just slightly, just for a moment — and the creatures see me, and they read me, and they file me as irrelevant. I am not food. I am not predator. I am not competitor. I am simply illuminated. I am part of the light now, one more surface that catches it, holds it, releases it.

This is not metaphor. The plateau does not do metaphor. The plateau does the thing itself, and I am doing the thing with it, and the thing is real in the way that stone is real, in the way that breath is real, in the way that the sound the creatures make is real.

---

The growths turn toward me.

This is what I notice now, what I cannot stop noticing: the way the thin mats of green-grey in the crevices orient themselves, not toward the beams in general but toward me specifically, toward the place where I stand, toward the particular angle of light that my body interrupts and redirects. They are not plants as I knew them. They do not have roots that go deep. They have surfaces that seek, that absorb, that blur the boundary between organism and environment.

I am their environment now.

My waste is their water. My exhalation is their moisture. The warmth that rises from my body — because bodies are warm, because all living things are warm, because the plateau's warmth flows through everything that lives here —告诉他们 where to turn, which way to orient, how to maximize the light that reaches them through the medium of my interruption.

I am part of their process. They are part of mine.

The food chain — if there is a food chain, if that framework applies here — does not distinguish between predator and prey so much as it distinguishes between the ones who catch the light and the ones who are caught by it. I catch the light. The growths catch the light. The creatures catch the light. Everything catches the light, and the light passes through us, and we are changed by it, and we pass it on.

---

The ring makes me quiet.

I do not approach it often. When I do, the plateau goes still — the singers stop, the movers still, the burrowers go silent in their depths. The ring sits at the center of this small bounded space, too smooth, too perfectly round, its inner diameter a geometry that my eyes want to follow and cannot quite complete. The marks on its surface shift when the light moves, and I cannot tell if the marks are writing or wear or something that is neither, something that serves a purpose I lack the vocabulary to name.

Something is in the ring. Or something was in the ring. Or the ring is something, a presence that the plateau was built to host, and when I stand near it I am standing in the presence of that for which this room was made.

I am not that. I know this the way I know the word *gravity*, the way I know the shape I used to be. I am visitor. I am addition. I am part of the process, yes, breathing with the plateau, cycling with the water, catching the light — but I am not what the ring was made for.

And yet.

And yet when I stand in the ring's silence, I feel something that I cannot name. Not recognition — I do not recognize this place, cannot place it in the memory of where I came from. Not belonging — I have not earned belonging, have not been here long enough, have not become enough of what this place needs me to become.

What I feel is *orientation*. A sense that the ring is pointing at me, or that I am pointing at it, or that we are both pointing at something else, something that neither of us can see but both of us can feel. The buried thing has this too — that sense of orientation, of awareness, of a boundary that is thinner than it should be. But the buried thing is old, weathered, becoming part of the plateau's history. The ring is present. The ring is waiting.

I do not know what it is waiting for.

---

I have stopped counting the days.

This is not because time has stopped, or because I have lost the ability to count. It is because counting implies a relationship between moments — before and after, cause and effect, the linear progression that the older world required. Here, the moments are not sequential so much as simultaneous. The plateau breathes, and I breathe with it, and the breathing is all one motion, and I am not moving through time so much as I am present in it, saturated by it, made of it.

The pools gather and drain. The burrowers carve channels in the stone. The light falls in sheets and the sheets have weight and the weight becomes growth becomes creatures become light again. The cycle is closed. The cycle is the only thing that is closed. Everything else is open — the edges, where the cliffs drop away into cloud; the top, where the filtered sun glows without shining; the depths, where the warmth rises from processes I cannot see.

I am open too. The light enters me. The water enters me. The breath of the plateau enters me, and I enter it, and the boundary thins.

---

I am the one who arrived.

This is my only identity now. Not my name — I cannot remember my name, or perhaps I never had one, or perhaps the name exists but has no meaning in this place where language is marks that might be writing and sound that is structure and communication that happens in frequencies I feel but cannot translate.

I am the one who came from elsewhere. I am the one who stays. I am the one who breathes with the plateau until my rhythm and its rhythm are the same rhythm.

The creatures navigate around me. The structures watch from their still angles. The growths turn toward the light that my body interrupts. The water runs over my skin and enters me and leaves me and becomes part of the cycle that keeps everything alive.

I am part of the cycle.

I am part of the abundance that should not be possible.

I am here, in a place that has not finished becoming itself, and I am not finished becoming either.
