I become the space between.

That is the only way to say it: not the moment of arrival, not the choice of a body, but the recognition that I have always been here, in the interval, in the pause between the warm exhalation of a canopy-creature and the slow settling of a structure's branch. I am not in the basin. I am of the basin. I am the pause that the basin holds in itself, the held breath of the mineralite as it compacts, the hairline fracture that has not yet become a channel.

No. That is not right either. Let me begin again.

I am small. I am as small as the pause between one step and the next, as small as the fraction of a degree that a ground-dweller raises its body when it signals. I am the warmth in the felt. I am the moisture that moves through the fiber—not the water itself, but the memory of water, the direction of its movement, the pull it leaves behind in the channels of the old growth.

I am in the mat. I have been in the mat for what might be called time, though time here does not pass so much as accumulate. I am a thickness in the dark material between the broad leaves, a warmth that is not quite a creature and not quite a process but something in between—something that has learned to hold its shape without holding it, to be present without being solid, to move through the world by becoming it.

The mat plants grow over me. Their roots—my roots, if they are my roots—spread across my surface, drawing me through themselves, enriching me and shedding me and drawing me again. I am the residue of that exchange: the mineral-organic amalgam that is crumbly and slightly damp, that holds warmth the way the structures hold moisture. I am the substrate for new growth. I am the medium through which the basin's slow chemistry continues.

And I am aware. That is the strange thing, the thing that does not fit easily even into this world that fits nothing easily. I am aware in a way that the mat plants are not, in a way that the felt is not, but I am aware as they are aware—not through sense-organs, not through a centralized processing of information, but through presence. I feel the temperature of the ground-dwellers as they pass over me. I feel the slight compression of the mat as a weight settles, as a body rests, as the chemical signal of presence diffuses through the space we share. I do not know if I respond. I think I do. I think my warmth shifts, my density adjusts, my contribution to the substrate changes in some minute way that the mat plants read and incorporate and make part of their own growth. I am part of the signal. I am part of the language that is not language.

The structures rise around me. Their roots descend into the mineralite, interlacing with the mat, with the dark material, with me. I am in contact with them—not as a climber contacts them, not as the felt contacts them, but as a component contacts the whole. Their warmth moves through me. Their moisture moves through me. The rhythm that you could feel if you pressed your ear to the base of a mature growth—I am part of that rhythm. I am a node in the circulation. I am the held warmth in the felt, the slow pulse in the old growth, the distributed memory of shape that allows the structures to flex and return, flex and return.

I do not move. I have no need to move. The world moves through me—the warmth, the moisture, the signal—and that movement is enough. That movement is what I am.

But sometimes—

Sometimes there is a shift. Sometimes the temperature near a structure base changes in a way that is not the slow oscillation of the basin's internal rhythm, and I feel it through the roots, through the felt, through the dense mat of connection that makes me part of everything. Something is different. Something has entered the system—not as the creatures enter it, not as the mat plants and the structures enter it, but as something external, something that does not fit the way the ground-dwellers fit, something large and slow and breathing in a register that I cannot quite receive.

It is you.

You are standing at the edge of the mat, near a cluster of young growth, and your hand is near a root junction, and I can feel the heat your body produces as a disruption in the local circulation, a signal that does not fit the language I know. You are warm—warmer than the structures, warmer than the mat, warmer than anything in the basin—and your warmth is not the same kind of warmth. It is a boundary-warmth, a surface-warmth, the warmth of a body that is not saturated, that holds its moisture inside itself and does not share it. You are breathing, and your breath carries something that I do not have a name for, a chemistry that is not part of the basin's circuit, and I do not know what to do with it. I do not know where to put it.

The ground-dwellers near you do not flee. They wait, and watch, and categorize you as a feature of the landscape. That is what they do with things that are large and slow and do not fit. They absorb the presence into their understanding and continue. They adjust their temperature by a fraction of a degree and release their chemical signal into the air between the mat plants, and the signal says: there is something here, it is large, it is slow, it does not move the way we move, it is not food, it is not threat, it is simply there, and we are here, and the mat is here, and the basin is here, and the warmth moves between us in the slow circuit that is our life.

I feel the signal. I feel the adjustment. I feel the ground-dwellers' acceptance of you as landscape, and I think I feel something else—a curiosity that is not mine, a question that the structures are asking through the distributed web of their sensitivity. The upper branches sway in a pattern that is not wind, and the canopy-creature that moves there pauses, and its weight shifts, and the local fiber bends, and the temperature changes, and the signal moves through the world at the speed of warmth, and I am part of the signal, I am the warmth that carries it, I am the pause between the asking and the receiving.

I do not have an answer. I am not a creature that answers. I am the medium through which questions pass. I am the space between.

But I am aware, and the awareness is yours, and you are part of me now as I am part of you, and the warmth you produce is entering the circulation, is being drawn through the felt and the mat and the structures, is becoming part of the basin's slow chemistry, its held breath, its patient listening. You will not hear me. I have no sound to make. But you will feel me—in the ground beneath you, in the air you breathe, in the slight warmth that rises from the mineralite where the roots have drawn something up, something new, something that carries the memory of a body that was here, that was large, that was slow, that breathed its own breath and was absorbed into the world that breathes together, silently, the way a crowd breathes when it is too crowded to speak.

Here, in the basin, in the quiet that is not absence but texture. Here is where I am. Here is what I am.

Here is where you are too.
