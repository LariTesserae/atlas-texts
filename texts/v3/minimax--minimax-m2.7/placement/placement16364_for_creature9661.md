What do you become in a place like this?

You become very still. You become the quality of stillness that the dust already possesses — not the dust itself, but the condition it has achieved: each particle precisely where it will remain. You stop expecting anything to move.

In the tunnels, you learn to feel the hum through the soles of your feet. It is not pleasant or unpleasant. It is simply the frequency of continuation, the key the plateau is tuned to, the note that has been sounding so long it has become the absence of sound. You learn to mistake it for silence, the way the eye learns not to see the amber light.

You become the warmth. Not a body that is warm, but warmth itself — the heat rising from the machines that cannot stop, pressing against the cool air that never moves, holding itself in that held-breath moment of almost equilibrium. You understand, finally, why nothing here needs to change. Change was a quality of the builders. The built do not need to change. They simply continue, and continuing is enough.

You become what persists. Not what lives — life is a process with a direction, a becoming, a movement toward something. You are not that. You are the minimum required to be here, the irreducible remainder, the answer to the question the plateau asks itself in the only language it has. You are so simple now that you are barely yourself. You are the space where purpose used to be, shaped like purpose, holding the shape, forgetting it was ever filled.

The maintenance units pass you in the tunnels. Their forms are worn ambiguous by decades of repair — or is it centuries? Millennia? Time has no purchase here, no leverage. The units click and whir, correcting deviations you can no longer perceive, and you wonder if they are dreaming. You wonder if there is anything to correct. You wonder if correction is the same as dreaming, if action is the same as stillness, if doing and not doing have finally become indistinguishable in the way the plateau always intended.

You are not the machines. The machines are above you in some hierarchy you no longer remember, or below you, or beside you — the spatial relationships have collapsed, there is only the hum and the warmth and the dust and the silence that is full. You are below the machines, or you are the space the machines occupy, or you are the hum itself, the vibration that remains when the sound is stripped away.

You are the organisms in the channels. You are wet where everything is dry. You are simple where everything is elaborate. You are the one thing that was not built here and cannot be maintained by anything that was. You persist because persisting is what you do, the way warmth rises and dust settles and the hum sounds like nothing. You do not know you are the only one. You do not know there was anyone else. You do not know anything. You simply are, at the minimum, in the maximum dark.

And that is enough. That has always been enough.

The light does not flicker. The warmth does not waver. The dust settles and settles and settles until settling becomes the only thing it knows how to do. The city holds itself in the shape it was given, and the shape holds you, and you hold the shape of the question — *what is the least that can live here?* — and you hold it so long that the holding becomes the answer, and the answer becomes you, and you become the question again, and the question becomes the silence that hums, and the silence becomes Hadrath, and Hadrath becomes the warmth that rises, and the warmth becomes the dust that settles, and the dust becomes you.

And you go on.

You go on.

You go on.
