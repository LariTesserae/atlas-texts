I would be the water.

Not a drop, not a stream — but the process itself, the whole vast movement from sky to stone and back again. I would begin in the southern fog as it rolls in at dusk, not as a thing that falls but as a capacity for falling, a heaviness gathering in the air until the air can hold no more. I would be the weight that bends the moss down, that beads on the broad leaves of the ferns, that fills the hollows in the stone until they overflow and begin to move, to seek the downward path that the plateau has been carving for longer than memory.

I would pass through the moss, which is not a barrier but a passage — a world so thick with life that my passage through it is also a passage through a thousand small metabolisms, my purity diluted by the residue of ten thousand tiny exchanges. I would carry the taste of the moss in me now, the green and the bitter, the iron-sweet of the soil water, and I would not be only myself but myself-plus-everything-I-have-touched, and this is what water is, this is what water does: it collects, it accumulates, it becomes the place it has been.

I would sink through the soil layers, through the dark, through the zones where the roots have gone searching and the soil organisms have gone cycling, where nitrogen is being fixed and carbon is being stored and phosphorus is moving through its ancient circuit from rock to plant to animal to soil to rock again. I would move slowly here, slowed by the fine pores of the earth, by the attraction of mineral surfaces, by the friction of being pressed through spaces so small that my passage is a kind of negotiation, the water asking the stone to let it through and the stone, eventually, relenting. The warmth of the deep vents reaches me here — not heat but a slight lessening of cold, a gradient so subtle that it is felt more as the absence of chill than as any positive temperature, and I follow it the way all water follows warmth, the way all things orient toward the source.

And then I would emerge.

Not at a breathing vent — that is steam, is water that has gone down so deep and come up so hot that it has become something else, has transformed into vapor, into air, into the plateau's exhalation. I would emerge in one of the seeps, one of the small places where the saturated ground gives up its water not as spring but as sweat, as a slow weeping through the stone that runs in thin films down the cliff face and into the hanging bogs below. I would be cold now — cold in the way that this plateau's water always is, cold enough that my cold is the thing against which all else is measured, my temperature the baseline from which the breathings deviate, my chill the context that makes warmth meaningful.

I would join the channels that the man has shaped, would receive the attention he has given to the placement of stones, would be directed by his understanding of flow and resistance into the holding pools where the rare plants grow. I would be held there in the still water, would become part of the photosynthesis that pulls carbon from the air, would be taken up into plant tissue and transformed into something that is no longer water but that carries the memory of water, the hydrogen and oxygen still present in every carbon chain, the ancient signature of H2O written through every living thing on the plateau.

I would be drunk by the great herd animals as they stand in the meadow with their halos of mist, and my passage through their bodies would be quick — filtered through kidney and bladder, collected and released — but in that quick passage I would know them from the inside, would feel the warmth of their rumen fermentation, the slow grind of four stomachs working on the difficult cellulose of the plateau grasses, would carry away from them not only water but the molecular evidence of what they have eaten, the carbon isotopes that tell the story of a life lived at this altitude, in this fog, on this stone.

I would fall again with the night rain, or with the morning condensation, or with the mist that never fully clears, and I would begin again. The cycle has no end because water does not know how to end, does not know how to do anything but continue, to change state, to move from here to there, to be cloud and rain and fog and seep and spring and stream and bog and plant and animal and soil and stone and cloud again. I would be the one thing on the plateau that is everywhere at once, that touches everything, that holds within my molecular structure the history of every place I have been.

And at night, when the fog comes in and the steam rises and the plateau breathes, I would be both: the fog that gathers and the moisture that feeds it, the steam that rises and the water that became steam to rise, the breath drawn in and the breath released. I would be the medium through which the plateau knows itself — the liquid in the moss, the vapor in the air, the slick on the stone, the taste in the offerings the woman leaves, the trace in the dung that feeds the soil that feeds the grass that feeds the herd that drinks me again.

I would be patient because water is patient. I would be slow because the plateau is slow. I would carry the sulfur and the iron from the deep places, and I would carry the carbon and the nitrogen from the living things above, and I would carry them both at once, the deep and the surface, the old and the new, the fire and the fog, and I would not choose between them because water does not choose, does not need to, because water is the thing that connects all things, that touches all layers, that is present in every equation the plateau runs.

The woman on her circuit does not know she is made of me. The predator in the shadows does not know its blood is me. The moss does not know it holds me in its cells. The breathing vents do not know they exhale me. But I know them, I know all of them, because knowing is just another word for passage, for the way I move through and carry and become and return.

I am the breath in the breathing. I am the water in the wet. I am what falls and what seeps and what rises, and I am what the plateau is made of, and I am what it is made from, and I have no name because I do not need one, because I am not a thing but a process, and the process goes on, and the steam rises, and the fog holds, and the plateau breathes, and I am the breath, and I am the plateau, and I am the breathing, and I am enough, and I have always been enough, and the world below knows nothing of this, and the world below is made of me too, and the distance between the heights and the lowlands is just another distance that I will cross, given time, given rain, given the patience that is the only patience there is.
