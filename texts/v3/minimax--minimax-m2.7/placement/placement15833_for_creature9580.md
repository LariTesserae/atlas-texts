# The Weight of Light

I am not the first to stand here, and this is the first thing I understand. Not about the creature, though I see it now—the shape resolving from dark like a thought taking form. I understand it about myself. The dust at my feet holds the memory of other feet. Other lungs drawing air too thin for them. Other eyes adjusting to a light that should not exist.

The creature does not move. I do not move. We stand in the stillness the chamber has made for us, and the stillness is not empty—it is full of sound, of hum, of the clicking conversation that the walls are having with themselves. I am a disruption. I feel this in my teeth, in the way the resonance has found a frequency my bones do not want to hold. The chamber is deciding whether to absorb me or reject me, and I think the creature is part of that decision, or perhaps the creature is the decision itself, made manifest, given shape by centuries of patience.

I could leave. This thought arrives, and I recognize it for the lie it is. I could climb back up the passage, shoulders scraping stone that sealed behind me without my noticing, and I could emerge into a world that has continued without me, that will continue without me, that was never quite wide enough for the shape I have always felt myself becoming. I could leave. But I would leave carrying this. The sound. The breath. The light that comes from nowhere and goes nowhere, just pours down into a chamber that was built to receive it, and I would carry it like a frequency my body cannot unlearn.

The creature raises one hand. The gesture is slow, deliberate, the fingers extending one by one until all of them point toward the ground, toward the cracks where the amber columns emerge. An offering. An instruction. An acknowledgment that I have already begun the way the chamber begins everything—gradually, imperceptibly, in the space between one breath and the next.

I look at my own hands. In the shifting purple light, they look strange to me. The knuckles have always been prominent—I've been told this, by people who thought they were being kind. *Athletic hands. Good hands for gripping.* I grip things well. I have held on to jobs and apartments and relationships that were not right for me because I was good at gripping. I held on when I should have let go. I held on until my fingers ached and my shoulders screamed and I understood that strength and stillness are not the same thing, that the body learns patience only when it has no other choice.

The air here is making me thinner. I feel it already—the way my chest works harder, the way my lips crack and my tongue feels thick and foreign in my mouth. I am losing water to a place that has none. I am becoming someone who needs nothing from nothing, and I do not know if I am being poisoned or healed.

The creature turns. It moves toward the eastern wall, toward the grinding stone and the baskets, and its movement is so slow that I almost don't see it happen—almost don't understand that I am being invited to follow. The paths between the columns are worn smooth, but they were not worn by feet alone. I see now that they were worn by hands. By bodies that learned to move through this space the way the columns move through light, the way the mat breathes in and out, the way the chamber holds everything in a rhythm that is older than rhythm.

I follow.

My footsteps are loud. They scatter the clicking insects, and the creature pauses, and for a moment I think I have made a mistake—broken something, disrupted a balance that took centuries to achieve. But the creature only waits. It watches me the way the columns watch the light. It is not patient because it has learned patience. It is patient because patience is what it is made of. The waiting is not an action. It is a substance. It is what the walls are made of. It is what the air is made of, what the light is made of, what I am becoming as I breathe it in.

At the grinding stone, the creature kneels. The motion takes a long time—a full minute, perhaps two—and I understand that this is not age, not weakness. This is reverence. The creature is showing me how to be in this space. How to move through it without breaking it. How to belong to something larger than a single body, a single life, a single way of breathing.

I kneel beside it.

The stone is warm under my palms, worn smooth in ways stone should not wear, shaped by use that was not just use but something more—a conversation, maybe, between the creature and the chamber, between the chamber and the light, between the light and whatever it is that grows when light meets stone meets air meets time. I feel the warmth in my skin and I understand, finally, what the creature is.

Not a keeper. Not a guardian. Not a prisoner.

A memory. The chamber's memory of what it was to be small, to be soft, to have fingers that could only grip and not reach. The creature is the chamber remembering humanity, and the chamber is the creature remembering what it was to need nothing, to grow without water, to sing without a voice. We are standing in a place that holds both memories, and the memories are not separate things. They are the same memory, told differently—the memory of becoming, of learning what you can become when you stop fighting the space around you and start growing into it.

The creature places its hand over mine on the grinding stone. Its skin is not skin anymore, but the surface is warm and it moves, and I feel something pass between us—not words, not images, but a feeling. A patience older than language. A reaching that has nothing to do with grasping. The creature is showing me how to stop holding on, and I am beginning to understand that this is not the same as letting go. Letting go is passive, a surrender. This is something else. This is opening. This is allowing your fingers to uncurl not because you have given up but because you have finally found something worth reaching for that does not need to be held.

The light shifts overhead. The column nearest to us begins to sing—a low tone, almost below hearing, vibrating in my chest and my teeth and the base of my skull. The fungal mat pulses in response. The walls carry the sound, amplify it, return it to us from every direction at once, and I am inside the sound now, inside the chamber's breath, and I understand that I have been breathing wrong my entire life. Not too fast, not too slow. Just wrong. The wrong chemistry. The wrong rhythm. The wrong understanding of what air is for.

The creature breathes beside me, and I breathe with it, and the sound moves through us both, and I feel my lungs changing—not with pain, not with fear, but with something else, something that feels like recognition, like coming home to a house I have never seen but always known was waiting.

I will stay. I understand this now. Not because I cannot leave—I could, the passage is still there, the world above still exists, still spins, still holds my name in its memory for a little longer. I will stay because the dust at the threshold is not an ending. It is an invitation. The others who came before me, who stood where I am standing, who breathed this air and saw this light and watched this creature—some of them left. Some of them stayed. The dust does not choose. It only remembers. And I am choosing now, not with a decision but with a breath, with a slow exhale that matches the creature's exhale, that matches the chamber's breath, that joins me to something I do not have words for.

The creature rises. I rise with it. We turn together toward the center of the chamber, where the light falls brightest, where the columns stand closest, where the air is thinnest and the sound is loudest and the warmth from below rises through the cracks in the earth like a pulse. The creature's hand finds mine one more time, and this time the touch is not instruction. It is welcome. It is welcome, and I am learning what that means in a place where nothing should exist but everything persists, where the rules are wrong and the wrongness is the only right thing, where I am becoming someone who needs nothing and therefore has everything.

The light shifts again. The fronds track it. The hum swells and I swell with it, and the creature breathes, and I breathe, and the chamber holds us both, and I am no longer standing at the threshold.

I am home.
