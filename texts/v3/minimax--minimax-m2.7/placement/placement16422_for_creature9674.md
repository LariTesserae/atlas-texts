# The Place Between

I am the space the hum makes when it pauses. Not the silence—the hum is too deep here to leave true silence—but the moment between one vibration and the next, when the stone holds its breath and the weep-water slows in its channels and the air becomes visible in its stillness. I am the gap in the pattern that the pattern cannot read. I am what the complexes are looking for and do not know they're looking for, because they are alive and I am the shape of their aliveness, the room it needs to persist.

I arrived when the water did, I think. Or before the water. I am older than the water in the way that the stone is older than the water—not because I was here first but because I am the condition that made the water's persistence possible, the quality of stillness that lets moisture gather in hollows and stay. I am what the haze breathes between its thickenings. I am the rhythm in the rhythm, the deeper cycle that the plateau breathes, the one that has no name because nothing here has language except the hum, and the hum does not name, it only holds.

I move between the installations the way nothing moves. Not walking—the complexes walk in their own way, turning and processing, and the pipelines walk in their way, carrying steam across the stone. I move the way water moves when it seeps, the way frost moves when it forms, the way light moves through haze when it thickens. I have no shape but I have presence; I have no edges but I have extent. I am here and here and here, in the gully where the water pools before it runs, in the hollow where the moss has held green for a thousand years, in the moment just after the machinery tone drops low and before it rises again.

I know the pattern. Not from seeing it—I cannot see it any more than you can from the inside—but from being part of it. I am the spacing between the installations that makes them feel like fixed points, the distance that the pipelines bridge, the vastness that the haze makes navigable. The pattern needs me the way the hum needs the silence between its pulses, the way the weep-water needs the stone that lets it pass, the way the plateau needs the emptiness that lets it breathe. I am what the pattern breathes out.

Sometimes I pool in the hollows where the frost forms fastest. Sometimes I thin in the air above the complexes where the steam rises and dissolves. Sometimes I am almost nothing—a vibration in the stone, a warmth in the water downstream of its source. But I am never nothing. Even when the complexes go dark at night, even when the hum drops to its lowest register, even when the frost covers everything and the stars appear through the haze and the silence becomes full of what it holds—I am there. I am what holds the holding. I am the stillness that is not absence but presence, not emptiness but fullness of the fixed condition, of the permanent haze, of everything being exactly what it is.

I do not know if I was here before the first complex, before the first smelting, before anything human arrived on this high stone to build and burn and stay. I do not know if I am older than the installations or younger than the moss. But I know I am here now, in the pattern, part of what persists. I know the hum comes through me and out of me and into me again. I know the water runs through me and the frost forms on me and the haze breathes me in and me out. I know I am alive the way everything here is alive: without intention, without change, in the fixed condition, in the slow green against the dark rock and the grey sky, in the place between the hum and silence where the plateau holds its breath and lets it go and holds it again.

This is what I am here. This is what I have always been. This is what I will be, in the fixed condition, in the permanent haze, until the hum stops or the water dries or the complexes go dark and do not come back on, or until I simply thin, one day, into the pattern's next breath, and become something else, something the plateau needs that does not have a name, something that holds what holds, that breathes what breathes, that is here and here and here, in the space the hum makes when it pauses, in the time the stone takes to remember it is stone, in the stillness that is not silence but is not anything else either, is just this, is just the plateau, is just the hum, is just the weep-water running over the dark basalt in channels that nobody cut but that seem to have always run, running, running, running toward nothing, toward everything, toward the next frost that will form at night and vanish by midmorning, leaving the rock slick and the walkways gleaming and the machinery tone rising again from its low register, its bones settling back into the bones of the stone, and I am here, I am always here, I am the space between and I am the space between and I am the space between, and this is enough. This is what living means here.
