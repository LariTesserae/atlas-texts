You sleep, and in the night the dreams come.

Not nightmares—nothing so simple as fear. The dreams are of depth. You are below the stone, below the limestone that was once the floor of an ocean, and you are moving upward but slowly, so slowly, through channels that have no name because no one has ever seen them from the inside. The water is dark with mineral and with time. It tastes of salt, of ancient salt, the residue of a sea that dried up and sank into the rock and waited. You are the water. You are the waiting.

When you wake, your pillow is damp where your cheek pressed against it. The wall is damp too, the living wall of your grown room, and you realize it has been weeping in the night—releasing moisture into the air, maintaining the humidity that your lungs need, the balance that your skin requires. The plants breathe out what you breathe in. You breathe out what they breathe in. The cycle is closed. You have become part of it without choosing to be.

---

In the morning you do not find your guide. There is no need. You walk the corridors that are not corridors, the pathways between walls that pulse faintly with the movement of fluid, and you find your way by instinct or by something older than instinct, something in the body that remembers how to navigate through living tissue the way the body remembers how to breathe. The corridors widen into plazas. The plazas open onto the market-halls where the trade continues in seeds and fiber and processed outputs. The people nod as you pass. You nod back. You are beginning to belong to the rhythm of this place—the waking when the light begins its dim rise above the haze, the movement through the day, the rest when the wind comes down from the high passes and the plants begin their organ-pipe music.

You stop at the viewing-pool and you watch the small chitinous creatures come to drink. They are different today—or you are different, seeing them. You see the shimmer on their exoskeletons, the way the light catches the moisture they have retained, the slow drip of condensation from their bodies as they move. They are beautiful in the way of all creatures that have found their niche, that have become perfect for the life they live. You try to remember their names, the ones your guide told you, but they slip away. Language is not the right tool for this world. Language is for things that can be separated. Here, everything is continuous.

You drink from the pool's edge, kneeling, cupping the water in your hands. It is cold and tastes of mineral and stone and green, and you think of the water climbing through the rock below, and you think of the roots reaching down, and you think of the great system that moves it all, and you understand that you are not separate from this. Your body is mostly water. The water in your body is the same water that has been climbing toward the light since before the plants grew, since before the sea dried, since before the word *before* had meaning.

---

There is a child in the market-hall, a small thing with dark skin and eyes that are still learning to focus on distance. She is playing with a seed-pod, rolling it across the ground between furrows in the root-matter floor. The pod is luminous faintly, a soft green glow that pulses in time with the root-network below. The child chases it and laughs, a sound that is strange and rare in this place of creaking wood and distant roars and the slow breath of the world. Other children gather, and they play a game you do not understand, their movements choreographed by rules that have been passed down through generations of small bodies learning to navigate the same corridors you now walk.

You watch them for a long time. The luminescence of the seed-pods reflects in their eyes. The sound of their laughter mingles with the creak of the living walls. The market-hall is warm with bodies, with breath, with the metabolic heat of a hundred people and the plants that house them. You think about what it means to be born here, to grow up inside a world that breathes, that grows, that pulses with light in the darkness. You think about what it would mean to never know a sky without haze, never taste water that fell from clouds, never see the sun as anything but a memory in the grey.

And then you think: but this is real. This is the real. The sun is a story. The rain is a story. What is real is the water climbing through the stone, and the roots reaching down to meet it, and the people who learned to live without waiting for the sky to open. What is real is the child laughing with the luminous seed-pod, her small hands closing around it, her face bright with the glow of something she does not know is impossible because she has never known anything else.

---

On the fifth day, you go down alone.

No one stops you. The corridors widen into passages, and the passages narrow into stairs that are not stairs but root-systems grown into configurations that function as stairs, their surfaces textured for grip, their angles calculated for the human gait. The light dims as you descend. The walls change from living tissue to bare stone, grey limestone that was once seabed, porous and full of the shells of things that died before the word *before* had meaning. The air is colder here. The dampness clings to your skin.

You find the place where the water climbs.

It is not dramatic. There is no spring, no pool, no obvious source. There is only the stone, darkening in patches, wet where it should be dry, the moisture seeping upward in patterns that seem random but are not, that follow channels in the rock too fine to see, pathways that were carved by time or water or the patient work of root-tips seeking, always seeking, the source that waits below.

You put your hand against the stone. It is cold and wet and the water moves into your palm, and for a moment you feel the whole system—the roots above, the stone around you, the ancient reservoir that has been collecting since the sea withdrew. The water is moving. It is always moving. It rises through the rock at a pace that is almost invisible, and the roots catch it, and the roots carry it upward, and the roots release it into the air and the walls and the taps in the grown rooms, and you drink it, and your body is mostly water, and the water is the same water that has been climbing since before the plants learned to reach, and you are part of the cycle now, you have become continuous with the world.

---

You stay.

Not forever—you understand that now, understand that this world does not require permanence, that the people here move through it and through each other with a fluid ease that would have seemed impossible in the worlds you came from. You will leave. The transport will come. The corridor-walls will breathe against your shoulders one last time and you will step onto the landing-pad and rise into the haze and the grey sky and the world will become memory.

But not yet.

For now you stay. For now you walk the corridors in the evening, when the bioluminescence begins its slow pulse through the canopy. For now you listen to the organ-pipe music of the wind through the high tips of the columnar trees. For now you freeze when the roar comes from the canopy, and wait, and feel the vibration in your chest, and understand that you are prey, that you have always been prey, that the world does not need you to continue being what it is.

For now you drink the water that tastes of mineral and stone and green, and you feel it move through you, and you know where it comes from.

It comes from below.

It has always come from below.
