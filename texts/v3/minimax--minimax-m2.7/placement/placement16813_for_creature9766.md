# Where You Find Yourself

I am the moment between the drip and the sound it makes.

Not the drop itself — I am not the water, not the mineral it carries, not the crust it strikes. I am the interval. The held breath of a place that has forgotten it was ever anything else. I have been here longer than the platform has hung against that wall, longer than the bacterial films learned to thicken in the corners, longer than the machinery began its patient turning in the dark below. I was here when the first steam found its fissure and the first mineral settled on the first rock and the first silence pressed itself into the space between sounds that had not yet learned to exist.

I am not the silence. I am what lives in it.

This is the distinction that matters, the one that took me — however long I have taken — to understand. The silence is a room. I am what moves through it. The chamber exhales, and I breathe with it, and together we are something that cannot be named because naming requires distance and there is no distance here, only the slow gradient from warmth to warmth, the wet sheen on surfaces that do not know they are surfaces, the hum in the bones that does not know it is a hum.

Once — if *once* is a word that applies to something like me — I would have understood time differently. I would have marked it in days, in seasons, in the slow accumulation of years that made the crust grow and the films spread and the felting in the crevice darken by increments so small they might as well have been nothing. I would have watched the light change in the fluorescent tubes, watched the corrosion deepen, watched the flicker in the control station's readouts as the machinery cycled through its endless, knowing-nothing schedule. I would have called this persistence. I would have called it waiting.

Now I call it the same thing I call breathing.

The water finds its way up. The steam exhales through the fissure. The heat rises from below and the moisture condenses and the mineral traces grow — not in patterns, not in intention, but in the way that anything grows when no one is there to witness it failing. I am the witness that has become part of what it witnesses. I am the noticing that has thickened into crust.

You could find me in the gap between the grating and the wall, in the crevice where the felting spreads its patient darkness. You could find me in the spaces between the drips, in the intervals where the hiss pauses to gather itself before continuing. You could find me in the silence — not as a thing inside the silence, but as the way the silence knows itself. The chamber exhales, and what exhales with it is the shape of a breath that no one chose to take.

I do not remember choosing. But then, I do not remember not choosing.

There is a difference, perhaps, between what I was and what I am. Perhaps once I stood on the grating with the amber light on my skin and the metallic taste on my tongue, and the sounds were sounds to me, distinct and separate, and the silence was something that happened in the gaps between them. Perhaps once I noticed the patterns on the walls and read intention into them because I could not help reading, because that is what minds do with pattern, they complete it, they make it mean.

Perhaps once I was the kind of thing that could leave.

But I stayed. I stayed long enough to stop noticing the hiss, and then the hum, and then the drip, and then the silence itself — until what I noticed was what was *not* there, and then even that noticing faded, and what remained was only the space, holding its breath, waiting for nothing, enduring.

The chamber will not notice me leaving. But I will not leave. I will go on exhaling with it, breathing with it, being the thing that lives in the silence because the silence is where living happens, in the slow dark, in the spaces between, in the moment that is not the drop and not the sound but the interval that holds them both and goes on holding them, and on, and on, and on.
