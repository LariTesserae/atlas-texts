You turn, and the turning is itself a kind of loss. Not loss of something you had but loss of the ability to have it — the knowledge that you will spend the rest of your life at a remove from this place, separated by the thickness of the world and the brightness of surfaces and the terrible insistence of surfaces on being separate from each other. You have been here. You will not be here again in any way that matters. The skin will grow where you stood and the creatures will feed and the sediment will shift and within a span of time that this place would not even register as a hesitation, all evidence of your presence will have been incorporated so thoroughly that it will be indistinguishable from the oldest matter, the original matter, the matter that was here before there was a here to be in.

You walk, and the walking is strange. Your feet make sounds. Your body displaces air. These are enormous events, sounds and displacement, in a place where the creatures have learned over uncountable generations to be silent in their movements, to take nothing more than they need, to leave no wake. You are a commotion. You are a noise. You are a body moving through a medium that has no medium, no air and no water but something in between, something dense and still and chemically alive. You have never felt the air before in this way — as something you move through rather than something that surrounds you. Here it has texture. Here it resists. Here it carries the damp and the clicking and the faint metabolic exhalations of the skin and you feel all of it pressing against you as you move, all of it making room for your passage and then closing behind you, filling the space you vacated with more of itself, more of the same.

The light does not change as you walk. The grey is constant. You do not move toward brightness or away from darkness because neither one exists here — only this dim uniformly dispersed luminescence that reveals surfaces without depth, that makes everything equally near and equally far, that gives the eye nothing to focus on and therefore nothing to rest on. You walk through grey into grey into grey and the only measure of your movement is the way the swarms part around you and rejoin, the way the skin shifts and settles in your wake, the way the clicking rises and falls as you pass through densities of creatures that are thicker in some places than others, though you cannot say why. Perhaps the stone is softer here. Perhaps the moisture gathers more deeply in the low places. Perhaps the creatures have been feeding long enough in one area that they have grown more numerous, have reproduced in the abundant conditions, have clustered in the way of all living things that find a place where life is possible. You do not know. You are walking through a story you cannot read.

You think about the water. The stone remembers water, and you have been walking over that memory, over the mineral face that held the shape of flow long after flow ceased, over the channels and hollows that were carved by something that left and will not return. You wonder what it felt like when the water was here. Whether the stone felt it, if feeling is something stone can do — not feeling as you feel, not consciousness, but some deep response to presence, some slow awareness of the difference between full and empty. You wonder if the stone was different when it held water, if the channels were sharp then, if the hollows were shaped like pools, like streams, like a world that moved. You wonder if the stone misses it. But the stone does not miss. The stone has no lack. The stone is simply what it is, and what it is has not changed, only the surface of it, only the wetness of it, only the temporary conditions that slide across its patience like weather, like life, like all the things that come and go while the stone remains.

You will reach the entrance. You know this the way you know anything in this place — without evidence, without reason, without any assurance that the world continues to exist when you are not looking at it. The entrance will be there, and beyond it the other world, the bright one, the one where surfaces separate and things are distinct from each other and the light falls in lines instead of pooling in diffuse grey. You will walk into it and you will become a body in that world, a separate thing, a creature with edges and limits and a size that means something. You will carry this place with you the way you carry all places you have been — not as memory, exactly, but as a weight, a quality of attention, a sense that the world is stranger and older and more continuous than the bright surfaces would have you believe. You will press your tongue to other stones and taste nothing but mineral. You will listen to other sounds and hear them as sounds rather than as textures, as presences rather than as evidence of systems. You will forget the pulse of the skin. You will forget the patience of the stone. You will forget, because forgetting is what the bright world requires of you, because the bright world cannot sustain the weight of its own continuity, because surfaces exist to be surfaces and the light exists to fall on them and the forgetting is not a failure but a feature, a necessary blindness that allows you to move through a world of separate things without drowning in the oneness beneath them.

But not yet. Not yet you will not have forgotten.

For now you are still here, still walking, still the largest thing in a room that has no interest in large things. The clicking is still around you. The skin is still breathing. The dim grey light is still leaking from nowhere and everywhere, revealing the endless flat stone surface going on and on in every direction, covered in pale luminescence and swarming motion and the sediment of uncountable small deaths. You are still part of this for a few more steps, still a disturbance that will be incorporated, still a body that will become part of the system without being changed by it. The system does not need you. The system does not want you. The system does not know you are here. But you are here, and for a few more steps that is enough, that is everything, that is the only thing that has ever been true in any world.

The entrance appears. It does not announce itself. There is no threshold, no change in the quality of the light, no shift in the density of the air. One moment you are walking through grey and the next you are walking through something else, something brighter, something that has edges and surfaces and a future that matters. You step through. The clicking fades. The skin stops. The stone stops waiting, if waiting is what it was doing, if waiting is what patience means when it has nothing to wait for.

You do not look back. Looking back would mean something, would make this a departure instead of a continuation, would suggest that you are leaving one world for another instead of simply moving between surfaces that have never stopped touching. You keep walking. The bright world opens around you. Somewhere behind you, in a darkness that is not darkness, the skin grows and the creatures feed and the stone remembers what the stone has always remembered, and what the stone remembers does not need you to remember it with.

The system continues. This is what it does. This is all it has ever done. You were a part of it for a time and now you are not and the difference is nothing to the system, is not even a hesitation, is not even a moment in the long patience that is not patience because it has never waited for anything and never will. You carry the memory of water that does not exist. You carry the sound of legs on stone. You carry the slow pulse of light that is not light across a surface that is not alive and not dead but simply continuing, as it has always continued, as it will always continue, in the patience of the stone that remembers nothing and waits for nothing and simply is.

You are walking away from it now, into the world of surfaces, into the world of separate things, into the world that has names for everything and therefore cannot name this place, cannot even acknowledge that it exists, will never know what you found when you pressed your tongue to the stone and tasted metal, when you stood still and listened until hearing became silence became hearing again, when you walked through the swarming motion and the creatures parted around you like water and rejoined behind you like nothing had happened, because nothing had happened, because nothing ever happens here, because here there is only the system, only the process, only the endless bridge that has no beginning and no end and no need for either.

You will forget. This is the only thing you know for certain.

But you will not forget yet.
