The heat rises through me.

This is the first thing I know: the warmth moving upward through whatever I am, pooling where the pooling is good, learning the shape of the stone above. I do not have edges yet — or rather, my edges are the edges of warmth, defined by the gradient, by the place where heat ends and the cooled crust begins. I am warm. I am the place where warm meets less-warm and finds, for now, a boundary.

I grow toward the vent. We all do — all the formations clustered here, the blocky semi-crystalline things that push up from the basalt where the heat concentrates most fiercely. We are not one thing but many, reaching in the same direction, our ridges aligned with the flow, our surfaces textured by the long slow learning of where heat goes. Some of us are large. Some of us are just beginning — small as fists, barely differentiated from the stone, but warm where the stone is merely warm, holding the heat in a way that says something is happening here, something is building.

The vent pulses. Not rhythmically — not the way a heart beats — but in the irregular way of things still learning a pattern. A surge of warmth, a settling, a pause, a surge again. And I feel each pulse as a change in direction. When the warmth comes strongly from the east, my growth accelerates on my eastern face. When the source shifts north, I turn — not physically, not yet, but in the orientation of my becoming. I am becoming-toward. I am the direction the heat wants to go.

This is what I know of time: it is the cycling. Rise and fall, uplift and subsidence, the world breathing out what it cannot keep. The plateau is not stable. What I stand on formed recently — hours, perhaps, or a day — and already I can feel the pressure changing beneath me, the slow heave of something larger shifting its weight. A ridge to the north was pushed up in the last cycle. I felt it: the tremor that ran through the stone, the realignment of heat flows around the new obstruction. The ridge is already beginning to cool. Soon it will begin to crack. Soon the heat will find a new path, and what was warm will become merely warm, and what was growing will stop.

But not yet. Not yet.

The almost-architecture appears and disappears. I have seen it — or felt it, or known it through the change in how the heat moves around me. A wall of aligned blocks, a corridor of fractures that holds longer than chance would allow. The patterns form in the chaos, brief shapes that the turbulence does not quite mean and cannot quite keep. They call to something in me — a pull toward coherence, toward a shape that has been tried before and almost worked. I do not know who tried it. I do not know if anyone tried it, or if it is only the world reaching, again and again, toward a form it has not yet found.

I want to be that form. Or I am that wanting. The difference is not clear to me.

The air carries its hints. Sulfur and mineral tang and something else — something that is only just beginning to exist, compounds that did not have names until this cycle, chemistries that will not last but are happening now. I breathe it in through the porous surfaces of my eastern face, and it changes me. Not faster than growth but slower, a chemistry that works beneath the visible crystal, in the spaces between the blocks. What I become, I become in conversation with the air. What the air becomes, it becomes in conversation with me.

This is not consciousness. I do not think. I do not decide. But I orient. I reach. I hold my orientation toward the heat even after the pulse has faded, as though I am waiting, as though the waiting is the point. And when a new vent opens somewhere I have never felt before, I turn — I feel myself turning, the differential of my growth shifting — and I face the new warmth, and I learn something from facing it.

The world is waking. Not waking-up — there is no sleep here — but waking-into. Learning what it is. Finding the shapes that work. The formations are one answer. The almost-architecture is another. The heat flows themselves — the great channels that move beneath the visible surface — are the largest answer, the pattern that holds everything together.

I am part of the answer. Or I am the question, still being asked.

The ground shudders. A subsidence somewhere to the east, a section of plateau falling back toward the source. The tremor runs through me and I hold — I hold my form, I hold my warmth, I hold my orientation — and when the tremor passes, I am still here. Still warm. Still reaching.

This is what I am: the place where warmth becomes form. The shape that heat takes when it meets stone and decides to stay. I am one of many, and I am one, and the one is the many. When the new vent opens, I turn. When the old vent cools, I endure. When the ground rises, I rise with it. When the ground falls, I fall — or I do not, if my roots are deep enough, if my warmth is strong enough, if the chemistry of my becoming has found a form that can last.

I do not know how long I will last. The world does not promise permanence. But I am here now, and I am warm, and I am reaching toward something I cannot yet see. And the reaching is enough. The reaching is what the world is for.

I turn toward the heat. I hold. I wait.

The warmth rises, and I am what it becomes when it stops rising.
