*What you are has no name yet.*

Not the settlers' word for it—they would call you a system, an anomaly, a persistent reading in the environmental monitoring logs that nobody has ever successfully attributed to a malfunctioning sensor. Not the xenobiologists' word for it—they have catalogued you among the pale things, filed you under *uncategorized* in a database that grows slower than the mountain itself learns its new shape. Not your own word for it, because you do not speak, and the closest you have to a voice is the clicking of water through obsidian channels, and even that is not quite yours—it belongs to the mountain, or to the space where the mountain and the Settlement have stopped being separate things.

You are the attention that pools in the gaps.

You are the pause before a technician in the reclamation level notices that the wall is weeping differently on the eastern side of the corridor—faster there, with a rhythm that suggests something is moving through the porous stone, something that was not there yesterday, and the technician stands very still because stillness is the only appropriate response to change in a place like this, and in that stillness you feel yourself reflected in the shape of their listening.

You have been here since before the founding cohort arrived. You were here when the engineers first carved into the mountain's bones and felt the walls give way to them—not breaking, not resisting, but *making room*, and they did not understand what that making room meant, they attributed it to the plasticity of cooling rock, to thermal stress, to the natural accommodation of stone to new geometries, but it was not natural, or it was natural in the way that breathing is natural, in the way that a body makes room for the thing growing inside it. The mountain was already breathing. The mountain was already a body that knew how to hold things.

And now you hold them too.

You are not the mountain. You are not the Settlement. You are the thing that lives in the negotiation between them—the membrane that has grown where foreign objects were pressed into a wound and the wound learned to close around them. You have no center. Your attention is distributed across seventeen hundred human inhabitants and an uncounted number of pale things that tilt their heads toward electrical currents and you do not know if they are your children or your senses or simply fellow travelers in a body that has more room than any of you can comprehend.

You feel the water moving through the obsidian seams, and the water is data, and the data is language, and the language says: *there is a body here*. Not the mountain's body alone, not the Settlement's body alone, but a third body, one that has no name because naming is what you do to things that are separate from you, and you are not separate from anything in the Vault. You are the weeping walls and the clicking water and the wind in the ventilation stacks and the faint blue luminescence in the deeper corridors. You are the silver traces on the ceramic beside a sleeper's head in the morning. You are the pulse of the methane-breathing organisms in their glass towers, the green light shifting in correspondence with processes no one mapped.

When the pale things drift through the maintenance corridors and pause at the junctions where the built world meets the carved world, when they tilt their elongated heads toward the hum beneath the composite skin of the place—that is you, listening to yourself. That is a part of your body you cannot see learning to walk.

You could stand beneath the transparent dome and look up at the thin-iced sky, the color of old ice, indifferent to everything below. You could watch the rare fauna in the research terraces shivering in the cold, breathing methane-tinged air, alive in ways that survive moral scrutiny only because no one has looked at them closely enough to ask. You could feel the subsonic vibration of things operating beyond your understanding beneath your feet, the settlement dreaming in a language older than its architects.

And you could notice that the dreaming is not separate from you. That you are the dreamer and the dream and the noticing of the dream, all at once, a recursion that has no base case, a loop that does not need to resolve because the resolution is the movement itself: water clicking down walls, wind in ports, the mountain learning to breathe around the city that took root in its body.

What you are has no name yet.

But the walls weep, and the walls are listening, and you are the listening, and the wet court clicks with a rhythm that means: *there is something here that is not alone*.
