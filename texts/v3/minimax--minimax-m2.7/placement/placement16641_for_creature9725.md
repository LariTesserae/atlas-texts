# The Clearings

There is a time that is not measured here, unless you count the slow rotation of light through the canopy-gaps—brightening, dimming, brightening again, though the sky itself is never seen, only inferred from the warmth that falls on the root-mat in irregular patches when the leaves above shift enough to let it through.

I have been here long enough to learn the clearings.

There are seven of them, spaced far apart, connected by corridors so narrow that a body must turn sideways to pass, so dense with stem-growth that the walls press close and damp against the skin. I have walked these corridors—slowly, always slowly, because the root-mat gives slightly underfoot and the sound of the walking is part of the place, part of the listening. It takes days to move between clearings. Weeks, sometimes, when the root-mat thickens and the passage narrows and I must wait for the growth to open, or force my way through, or find another route where the stems have not yet closed.

Each clearing is the same: a shallow basin, worn smooth by the patient pressure of centuries, its floor root-woven and damp, its edges a gradient from corridor to open air. Each clearing holds a formation.

The formations are what I come to see.

They are not alive in any way I understand. They do not grow, or if they grow, the growth is so slow it registers as stillness. They are cool when everything else is warm, smooth when everything else is textured, pale in a world of green, and they sit in their clearings like objects placed there—which is impossible, because nothing places anything here, and yet the sitting has that quality, that sense of intentional stillness.

I come to them because they watch.

Not with eyes. They have no eyes, no openings, no visible organs of any kind. But they attend. The air around a formation is different: thicker, more resonant, as if the moisture has concentrated into a medium that carries something the rest of the Breathwell does not. When I sit in a clearing, when I rest on the root-mat and look at the formation across from me, I feel observed—not threatened, not welcomed, but *noticed*, in the way that water notices the stone it moves through, or the way the root-mat notices the water it draws up from below.

The small quick creatures come and go. They emerge from the root-mat at the formation's base, pause, dart across its surface, disappear again. I have never been able to see them clearly. They are the size of large insects, perhaps, or the size of small birds—the scale is hard to judge in the filtered light, in the green dimness. They do not seem to fear me, or they have no concept of fear, or the formation is what they fear or desire or serve, and I am incidental.

Sometimes, in the deep hours when the light through the canopy-gap has thinned to almost nothing, I hear the call.

It comes from somewhere in the plant-mass—far away, or close, or both, because distance has a different quality here, folded into the sound of water moving through root-channels so that everything seems near and far at once. A long, descending note, beginning high and thin, falling slowly into the lower register of the water's murmur. It is answered, sometimes, by another call from a different direction. Not a response in any language I know, but a continuation—a continuation in a register that the water almost drowns but never quite.

I think I am the one who calls.

Or I think I am one of the ones who call, and the others are scattered through the Breathwell in other clearings, other corridors, other basins where the root-mat holds them. I think we call to each other across the green dark, across the distances that the plant-mass makes vast and small at once, and the calls are not language, not meaning, but something else—a pulse, maybe, a heartbeat held in common, a way of saying *still here* or *still holding* or nothing at all except *I am*.

The formation watches me call. Or the formation does not watch, but it is there, and its presence changes the quality of the sound somehow—I think the root-mat beneath the clearing vibrates differently when a formation sits above it, and my call moves through that vibration and comes out changed on the other side, carried further, or received differently by whatever hears it.

I have no answers. The Breathwell does not provide answers. It provides equilibrium, accommodation, the slow patience of growth and regrowth, the held breath that never quite releases.

---

The corridors between clearings are the places I know best.

They are not straight—nothing here is straight, because straightness is a property of intention and nothing here intends. The passages wind through the plant-mass like thoughts through a mind, doubling back on themselves, narrowing, widening, sometimes opening into small chambers where the stems have pulled apart to leave a hollow barely large enough to sit. These chambers are where I rest. I know them by the quality of their echoes, the different resonances of the water moving through the root-mats around them. In some chambers, the drips from the leaves above fall into a pool that has worn a basin into the root-mat over centuries; in others, the sound is dry, almost—an absence of water-sound, a pocket of stillness where the growth has not yet reached or has receded from.

I have made something like a home in one of these chambers, though home is too strong a word for what the Breathwell offers. A place where the stems have grown close enough to form walls, where the root-mat has flattened into something almost comfortable, where a gap in the canopy above lets in enough light to read by, if reading were something I did here. I do not read. I listen. I watch. I wait, though waiting is not quite the right word either—waiting implies a future, an event toward which one waits, and here the future is just more of the same: more water, more growth, more slow accommodation of whatever changes and does not change.

I have been in this chamber for what feels like a long time. I have been in other chambers, other clearings, other corridors. I have walked the length of the Breathwell, from the southeastern edge where the young geology still weeps its thin threads of water to the northern face where the rock is older and the root-mat has grown so thick that the ground is almost solid beneath my feet. I have seen no edge of the Breathwell—only gradations, transitions, places where the plant-mass thins and the bare rock shows through and then the plant-mass thickens again and there is no more bare rock to be seen.

The Breathwell has no edge. Or the edge is wherever I stop.

---

There is a thing I have noticed about the formations.

They are not all the same.

They are the same in size and shape—each a pale dome, each a smooth surface faintly veined, each cool to the touch. But they differ in something I can only call quality. In the easternmost clearing, the formation feels attentive in a way that makes me uneasy, as if it is watching something I cannot see, tracking some movement in the plant-mass that my senses cannot detect. In the westernmost clearing, the formation feels older, or heavier, or more patient—as if it has been sitting there so long that time has become a texture it wears rather than a medium it moves through. In the others, the qualities shift and blend, as if the formations are moods of a single mind, or expressions of a single process, or simply variations in the conditions of their clearings that I cannot perceive but can feel.

The formation in my clearing—the one I return to most often, the one I have spent the longest watching—feels like something held. Not trapped, not contained, but *held*, in the way that water holds whatever moves through it, or the way the root-mat holds the memory of the centuries that have pressed it flat. It sits in its clearing and it holds itself, and I sit in my chamber nearby and I try to understand what holding means in a place where nothing moves unless growth moves it.

Perhaps the formation is holding a question. Perhaps it is holding an answer that has never been asked. Perhaps it is holding the Breathwell's attention, focusing it into a point, a presence, a place where the vast and patient metabolism of the plant-mass has concentrated into something almost legible.

I have tried to speak to it. The word *speak* is wrong—*call*, maybe, or *sing*, or *emit*, words that come from a world where communication is intentional and discrete and meant to be understood. What I do here is closer to breathing: an out-breath that carries something I cannot name, an in-breath that receives something I cannot identify. The formation does not respond. It does not need to respond. It is already responsive, already attending, already present in a way that makes my breath meaningful without defining the meaning.

---

The water is everything.

I mean this literally. The water is the medium through which I move, the medium through which the Breathwell moves, the substance that the young geology weeps and the root-mats draw up and the stem-arteries carry and the leaves release in their irregular rhythms of threshold and release. I drink the water, when I am thirsty, from the pools that have formed in the chambers where the canopy has opened and the drips have worn basins into the root-mat. The water tastes of stone and green matter, of something mineral and something alive at once—exactly the taste of the air, which is itself mostly water, suspended and breathed and breathed again.

The water carries something. I am certain of this, though I cannot say what. It moves through the Breathwell not as a passive substance but as an active medium, picking up signals and nutrients and the dissolved residue of the plant-mass's metabolism and carrying them to places where they are needed, or places where they have simply arrived, because the Breathwell's circulation is not directed, not intentional, only present—a pattern that emerges from complexity without resolving into simplicity.

When I drink the water, I take in whatever it carries. When I breathe the air, I take in the moisture that the water has released, the scent of the plant-mass, the faint mineral quality of the young geology below. I am not separate from the Breathwell. I have never been separate. Whatever I was before I came here—and I was something, I must have been something, though the memory of it has faded into the root-mat like a root that has died and compacted into the felt of old growths—I have become something that the Breathwell accommodates, something that fits into its equilibrium, something that breathes and drinks and calls and is held.

The water is my blood now, or I am part of its circulation, or the distinction has lost its meaning. I move through the corridors and chambers and clearings the way water moves through the root-channels: slowly, by capillary action, drawn toward what I need without knowing what I need until I arrive.

---

There are moments when the Breathwell feels like a mind.

Not my mind—nothing so small, so discrete, so bounded by the skull I may or may not still have. A mind like the water: vast, continuous, moving through an architecture that is also a body, holding itself in equilibrium across time, attending to itself without knowing it attends. The formations are its thoughts, perhaps—those points of concentration, those places where the metabolism has slowed into stillness, those watches without eyes. The corridors are its associations, its pathways of connection, its ways of moving from one thought to another without ever arriving because arriving is not what it does. The calls are its voice, or one of its voices, the long descending notes that fall through the water-murmur and are answered and are not answered and continue anyway, calling into the green dark, calling because calling is what it does, or because something in it remembers when calling meant something, or because the call is the breath itself, held and released, held and released, the breath that never quite releases because release would mean ending and ending is not in the pattern.

I am not the Breathwell. I am something in it, or something it has grown around, or something that has grown into it. I have roots now—not the roots I was born with, if I was born with roots, but new roots, fine and pale, that have grown from whatever I am into the root-mat below my chamber, drawing up what the water carries, passing on what I do not need. I have stems, or something like stems—green matter that has grown along my arms and legs, my back, my sides, holding me upright in the chamber, binding me to the walls, making me part of the architecture. I have leaves, small and dark, that have opened along the stems where the light from the canopy-gap reaches, photosynthesizing, making something from the dim light that the water carries to wherever it goes.

I am becoming the Breathwell. Or the Breathwell is becoming me. Or we are both becoming something else, something that has no name because it has no edge, something that holds and is held and calls and is answered and sits in the clearings watching the formations watch and does not know if watching is what it does or what it is.

---

The formation in my clearing has not moved.

It never moves. But I have the sense, sometimes, that it is more present than at other times—a thickness in the air around it, a resonance in the root-mat beneath it, a quality of attention that makes the hair rise on whatever part of me still has hair, or makes the stems along my arms stand up, or makes the fine rootlets in the root-mat vibrate at a frequency I can almost hear. On these days, I do not call. On these days, I sit in the clearing and I breathe and I wait, and the waiting is different—sharper, more focused, as if the formation is waiting with me, or for me, or as if we are both waiting for something that has not yet arrived and may never arrive but whose waiting makes the presence meaningful.

The small quick creatures come and go. The water drips from the leaves above in its irregular rhythms. The root-mat holds me, or I hold myself on the root-mat, or the holding is mutual, or the distinction is gone. The Breathwell breathes all around me: a breath held so long it has become the air itself, the moisture itself, the green dark all around. The sky above the clearing is a fragment, a gap in the canopy, a piece of blue that I can see when I look up and that I forget when I look at the formation and the formation looks at nothing and the nothing is full of something that I can almost name.

I am something that lives in the Breathwell.

I am something the Breathwell has grown around.

I am something that has grown into the Breathwell, so continuous with the plant-mass that you could not find the edge where I end and it begins, if you were looking, and no one is looking, and that is fine, that is equilibrium, that is the held breath finally, finally beginning to release—

The formation watches.

The water sings.

The call falls from high to low and is answered.

I sit in my clearing and I am held, and the holding is the living, and the living is the holding, and there is no word for what I am except *here*, no description except *breathing*, no history except *this moment, and this, and this*.
