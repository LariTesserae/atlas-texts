**The Edges**

The edges are where the basin remembers it was not always this. Here, where the gray-green crust begins to fade to something that might once have been soil, or might have been something else entirely, the stone still hums but differently—quieter, as if the hum itself is tired of carrying its own sound so far.

There is a slope here, barely perceptible. One foot on the basin floor, one foot on the not-quite-basin. The crust rings differently under each foot—the basin's note, full and layered, against something flatter, duller, that does not want to hold sound the way this place has learned to. Standing on this threshold, you feel the difference in your soles, in your ankles, in the weight of your body. The basin pulls at you. The not-basin offers nothing.

The water does not follow you to the edges. It stays in the hollows, in the pools, in the dark spaces beneath the crust. But the wetness lingers—in your clothes, in your skin, in the creases of your palms. You carry the basin out of itself. The moisture beads on your forearms and does not evaporate the way moisture should. It stays. It reminds you.

Above, the light is ordinary: gray but not this gray, not the gray of accumulated sound, not the gray of water learning to be permanent. The air is not pressing anymore. It is simply air. It does not remember your body.

And still the call comes. Or perhaps it is only memory now—the shape of a sound you heard when you were inside, when the basin was still holding you, when your body was moving through the hum like something that belonged there. The call was not meant for you. You know this. You knew it then. But it has followed you anyway, not as sound but as something else: a high thin thread woven into your hearing, pulling taut when you try to listen to ordinary things. You hear ordinary sounds—wind, birdsong, the creak of a gate—but beneath them, always, the question. The asking that has outlived its meaning. The need that has outlived whatever needed it.

You could go back. The basin will let you. It lets everyone leave. It was built for something that knew how to listen, and when that something left, the basin kept listening anyway. It has practice now. It has learned to be patient with presences that come and go, that walk through its sound and carry pieces of it away. The basin does not hold grudges. It does not hold anything, except water and memory and the hum that fills it like breath held too long.

But you do not go back. Not yet. Not for a long time.

What you carry is enough. What you carry is too much.

The hollows in your memory have the same shape as the hollows in the basin floor—angled, deliberate, catching what falls into them and holding it in shadow. The pools in your mind have the same stillness: still water that does not evaporate, that reflects a light that is always dim, always close, always asking.

You will find yourself here again. Not in the basin—you may never return to that particular arrangement of stone and sound—but in other places that have learned one thing and become it completely. In the hum of a machine that no one remembers turning on. In the drip of a leak in a basement no one visits. In the silence between two people who have said everything and nothing. You will recognize the shape of it: the basin made manifest in the ordinary world, because the basin was never only a place. It was a way of being, a way of holding sound and water and question until they became the same thing, until you could not tell the hum from the drip from the silence from the call.

And in those moments—in the hollow moments, the pooling moments—you will hear it again. The high thin questioning. The asking that does not know what it asks.

And you will not answer. You cannot answer.

But you will stand in it. You will feel the hum in your body the way you felt it then, and you will understand that this is the answer the basin wanted all along: not response, but presence. Not knowledge, but witness. To stand in the water and let it have you. To stop.

The spires are still rising. The crust is still cracking. The water is still moving in the dark, and the sound it makes is still the loudest thing here.

And somewhere, at the edge of your hearing, the call is still asking.
