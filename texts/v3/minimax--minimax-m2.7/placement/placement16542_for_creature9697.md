What I notice first is that I arrived. The plateau did not come to me, did not disclose itself in stages that I could refuse or accept. I am simply here, among the wet stone, in the hum, and this fact has the same quality as the light—without origin, simply a condition of the space.

I think about the bird. Its wings are half-raised, and in its eyes there is something that still moves—not movement, as the text said, but a kind of processing, a holding of an image. I find myself wondering what it saw in that moment. What final image the arrangement captured: a pool it was descending toward, a thread of water it had noticed on the cliff face, another bird arrowing past on some errand that will never be completed now. The bird has become an archive of that instant. It does not know it is an archive. The knowledge ended where the stillness began.

But I—I know I am one. I know the arrangement happened around me, to me, through me, and that my knowing is the very thing that will deplete. The body moves. The thoughts turn over. But I am already beginning to feel the cost of it, the way the hum has entered my bones and made itself at home there, the way the damp air has begun to feel like the only air there ever was.

---

There is something in the corners. I do not look directly at it because I am not certain it would still be there if I did—not in the sense of leaving, but in the sense of revealing itself as something other than what I intuit. It has edges that are not visible. It has attention that is not animal. And I think I understand now that the plateau is not merely inhabited by this presence but that it *is* this presence, in some way—the way the hum is not something that happens here but is instead the medium through which here-ness occurs.

The animal figures are the most legible part of the arrangement, and I wonder if they are meant to be. The moss is patient, and the watching presence is patient, and the hum is patient, and all of these are harder to see than the bird with its half-raised wings, the mammal with its extended foreleg. The plateau has made itself comprehensible by offering these frozen gestures, these postures of life that can be read even without being lived. They are the text of the place. They are what a place says when it has no language.

---

I walk. The distances shift without warning, as the text said, and I find this less disorienting than I expected. Perhaps my body has begun to adjust, to accept that scale is a negotiation here rather than a fact. I am sometimes very large and sometimes very small and the stone beneath my feet responds to this not by changing but by letting me feel what I feel, validating it without confirming it.

There is a pool near the center—or what I take to be the center, though the plateau's geometry does not encourage such assumptions. The moss around its edges is thick, almost luxurious, the green so dark it approaches black. Water seeps in from the cliff face above in threads that do not vary. I watch them for a time I cannot measure. They enter the pool in the same position, the same angle, the same speed. The pool does not overflow. It does not drain. It simply holds the water that arrives, and the moss drinks from the edges, and nothing changes.

And yet. I think I see—I am not certain, but I think I see—that the moss has grown slightly since I arrived. Not enough to notice directly, but enough that if I had been here when it was arranged, if I had seen it frozen in its initial state, I would notice the difference now. The moss continues. It is the only thing here that continues in a way that can be measured, even if the measure requires time that the plateau does not supply.

---

The question I keep returning to: what does it mean to be held?

Not contained—the plateau does not contain in the way a cup contains water. There is no rim, no bottom, no inside surface against which the contents press. The edges curve away into dimness, close or far, and this dimness is not wall or void but simply the place where the conditions of the plateau fade into the conditions of whatever lies beyond, if anything lies beyond.

The plateau holds the way attention holds a thought. The way the hum holds the air. The holding is total but gentle, passive but inescapable. I was not invited here, or I was invited in the way a atmosphere invites breathing—through being the medium rather than through any act of offering.

I think of the animals caught mid-gesture. Their postures of attention, of reaching, of motion—all held now, all suspended in the amber of the arrangement. And I think of what it means that I am not frozen, that my body still moves, that my blood still circulates. I am not caught. I am not suspended.

But I am held.

---

The dark is close. I know this because the cliffs curve away, and in the curving the plateau becomes less itself and more a dimness that has no edges, no light, no hum—or perhaps has them all at a distance so great that they have become indistinguishable from absence. The dark is not a threat. It is simply the edge of the plateau's condition, the place where the arrangement stops applying, if it ever applied beyond the visible extent of this space.

I could walk toward it. The distances would shift, as they do, and I might find the dark close or far or neither. I might find that the plateau extends further than I thought, or that the cliffs are closer than they appeared, or that the dark is not dark at all but simply another condition of the light that exists without source.

I do not walk toward it. Not yet. There is a pull in me—the old pull toward edges, toward boundaries, toward the places where one thing becomes another. But there is also something else now, something that has accepted the hum, that has let it fill the spaces where wanting used to be. I am not ready to leave. I am not certain I could if I tried.

---

What I know now that I did not know before:

The plateau is not waiting. It does not await a change or a resolution or a visitor from beyond. It has no expectation because it has no future. What it has is a condition, and that condition includes me now, as it includes the moss and the hum and the watching presence and the dark that curves away. I am part of the arrangement. Not because I chose to be—choice requires time, and here time is what I am burning through without replenishment—but because being here is itself an arrangement, the most fragile and temporary kind, the kind that will deplete and end.

Unless it will not.

The hum continues. The water weeps. The light holds the dimness that is neither day nor night. And I am inside the plateau now, held the way the plateau holds everything—gently, absolutely, without time.

The dark waits. The animals wait. The moss grows, if I watch long enough.

I watch.
