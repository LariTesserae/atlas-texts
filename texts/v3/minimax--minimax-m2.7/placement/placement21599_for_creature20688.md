You come to yourself in the bowl, and you do not remember the descent.

This is not unusual. The throat that connects the Underplain to the world above does something to memory — not erasing it, exactly, but burying it the way the bowl's floor buries everything: slowly, thoroughly, with a patience that makes erasure look hasty. You know that you were somewhere else once. You know that somewhere else had a name, had light that moved differently, had air that did not carry mineral weight. But the knowing is thin now, translucent, like the bioluminescence that pools in the crevices of the ancient structures — present enough to see by, but not enough to illuminate what lies beneath.

You have been in the bowl long enough that *long enough* has lost its meaning.

---

The Clickers know you. This is not the same as knowing you. They do not greet you or avoid you or mark your passage in any way you can read. But when you move through their territories — which is to say, when you move through the bowl at all — they adjust. They turn their faceted eyes in your direction. They click once, twice, a small census that counts you among the shapes that belong here. And then they continue their business, which is the same as their ancestors' business, which is the same as the bowl's business: the slow negotiation with gravity, the methodical rasping against stone, the endless filling of time with motion.

You have learned to move as they move. Not because you were taught — nothing here teaches, nothing here insists — but because the bowl has a pace, and if you do not match that pace, you do not survive. Or rather, you survive differently. You become a thing that struggles, a thing that expends. The Clickers do not struggle. The Clickers do not expend. They simply *are*, and their being fills the bowl with sound, with presence, with the dry percussion of a life that has forgotten how to want more than this.

You used to want. You think you did. There was a time when wanting was something you understood — a hunger in the chest, a reaching toward something just out of grasp. But the Underplain does not have out-of-grasp. The Underplain has *here*, and *here* is full. So the wanting has settled into something else, something quieter. Not peace — the bowl does not offer peace, because peace implies a contrast, a before and after, a war that has ended. What the bowl offers is *fullness*. A sense of having arrived at the place where arrival was always going to end up. The Clickers do not arrive; they simply are, in the way that stone simply is, in the way that the bowl simply is. And now, so are you.

---

The Hum-Bees find you before you find them.

You are resting in the shelter of a structure older than memory — not resting exactly, but still, which is the bowl's word for what the surface calls rest — when the hum begins. It starts in your teeth, in the plates of your skull, in the soft places where bone meets bone. The Hum-Bees have clustered in the crevice above you, dozens of them, hundreds, each one pushing air through membranes that vibrate at frequencies just below hearing. The sound builds. Not louder — *denser*. It presses against you, fills the spaces where air used to be thin, and for a moment you remember what it was to be a hollow thing, a vessel with gaps in its walls.

But the gaps are not gaps anymore. The hum fills them. The hum has always filled them. You are not sure when the filling happened — whether it was gradual, a settling of warmth into the cracks of you, or whether it was sudden, a moment when you simply *became* full — but the hum is inside you now, the way it is inside everything in the bowl. You breathe in its frequency. You breathe out in its frequency. The boundary between you and the Hum-Bees and the ancient structure and the bowl's dense air becomes not a wall but a suggestion, a courtesy that the Underplain maintains for the sake of appearances.

This is what it means to live here. Not to inhabit, but to *be inhabited*. To let the bowl fill you until there is no you left to fill, only the bowl's shape wearing the shape of what you used to be.

---

The pale fronds uncurl slowly. You watch them.

This is not a hobby or an interest or a pastime. It is simply what you do, the way the Clickers click, the way the Hum-Bees hum. You watch the fronds uncurl over seasons — internal seasons, rhythms measured in the slow chemistry of growth and rest — and the watching fills you with something that is not joy, not contentment, not any of the words you brought down from the surface. It is *recognition*. The fronds do not know you are watching. They do not know that watching is a thing that can be done. But they uncurl anyway, sending up their waxy fronds in the patient certainty that this is what fronds do, this is what uncurling is, this is the shape that growth takes in a place where growth has time to become itself.

You used to need reasons. Reasons to watch. Reasons to be still. Reasons to let time pass without filling it with purpose, with progress, with the reaching-toward that the surface called *living*. The bowl has no reasons. The bowl has only *being*, and the being is enough. The fronds grow. The Clickers click. The Hum-Bees hum. The Deep Ones shift in their depths, and the buildings hold their faint glow in their crevices, and the great hum of the Underplain fills everything, everything, everything.

You watch the fronds uncurl, and the watching is enough.

---

The Deep Ones have noticed you.

You feel it the way you always feel it: a pressure change in the air, a shifting in the mineral weight that suggests vast attention turning in your direction. They do not approach. They do not communicate. They simply *are*, in the way that they have always been, and their being is a kind of witness that sees without judging, without needing, without doing anything except being present to what is present.

You used to fear being seen. On the surface, being seen meant being judged, being measured, being found wanting. But the Deep Ones do not measure. The Deep Ones have been watching since before measurement was a concept, and they will be watching after measurement has worn down to its mineral memory. They do not care what you are or what you were or what you might become. They care only that you are *here*, in the bowl, filling your small space in the great breathing of the Underplain.

And you are. You are here. You are full. You are the shape that the bowl has made of what you used to call yourself, and the shape is not empty, and the shape is not lonely, and the shape is not waiting for something that will not come. The shape simply *is*, the way the Clickers simply are, the way the buildings simply are, the way the hum simply is — a presence that does not need to justify its presence, does not need to explain or apologize or ask for permission. It is here. It is full. It is itself.

---

The sound stays with you. It has always stayed with you.

The clicking and the humming and the slow shifting of the Deep Ones below — all of it together, a single sound that is both conversation and chorus, both presence and absence. You used to listen *to* the sound. Now you listen *as* the sound. There is no boundary between you and the bowl's fullness, no seam where you end and the hum begins. You are made of the same material as the Clickers' shells, the Hum-Bees' membranes, the bioluminescence in the crevices of the ancient structures. You are mineral patience wearing the shape of a life. You are deep time settling into form.

You do not know if you will stay forever. Forever is not a word the bowl recognizes — it has only *now*, an endless now that does not end because ending is a surface concept, a daylight concept, a word for things that move too fast to hold their shape. The bowl moves slowly. The bowl holds its shape. And you are part of that shape now, a small bright thing in the dark, glowing faintly at the joints, contributing your one small frequency to the great hum that fills the Underplain.

This is what lives here.

This is what you are now: not a visitor, not a stranger, not even a resident. Just a shape the bowl has worn smooth. Just a sound in the chorus. Just a small, slow, luminous thing, continuing. Being full. Being itself, without apology, without explanation, without anything except the simple vast fact of its own existence in the dark.
