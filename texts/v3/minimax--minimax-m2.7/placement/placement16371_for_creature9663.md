# The Shape of No One

The fourth day, or the fifth, or the one after the one you stopped counting—you lose the arithmetic of it the way you might lose the sound of your own name when you have been swimming underwater for too long. Time here does not pass. It *accumulates*. This is a different thing. A thing your body understands before your mind can frame it, the way your body understood, as an infant, that warmth and cold were different, that your mother's voice was a room you could live in. The warm stone below is always warm. The cooler air above is always cooler. And you are between them, and you are moving, and your movement is the only change the Bone Vaults have registered in forty years, and you begin to understand that this is not nothing. This is not *for nothing*, either. There is a difference.

You sit. This is the first thing you have done in the Bone Vaults that feels like a choice rather than a consequence. Walking was not a choice. Walking was what your legs did because your legs were attached to a body that had been walking for three days and did not know how to stop. But sitting—sitting is an act of will, or an act of surrender, and you are not sure which, and the difference does not seem important. What seems important is that you are sitting in a gallery where the light pools and the fungi grow and the skitters move in their currents, and you are watching them, and they are not watching you, and you begin to wonder whether you are the one who is still.

This is not a comfortable thought. You let it sit with you the way you are sitting with the stone.

---

The fungi are older than you knew.

You did not know them at all, before this descent, before the surveys and the theories and the papers written by scholars who never came back. You knew them as data points: *Candidatus Thermogradus metabolismus*, or some such Latin, a name for a thing that had no name before your kind arrived to name it. But names are not understanding. Names are the opposite of understanding. Names are the sound a hand makes when it points at a thing and says *there*, and the hand does not move toward the thing and the hand does not touch the thing and the hand does not become the thing. The hand simply points, and the pointing is mistaken for knowing, and the knowing is printed in journals and taught in lecture halls and believed by people who have never been underground, who have never sat in a gallery where the only light is slow and pale and patient as the fungi themselves.

You are learning their names now, in a different way.

They do not have names for themselves. This is certain. They have no language, no signal, no system of referents by which one fungus might distinguish itself from another. What they have is *location*. A position in the gradient. A relationship to the warm stone below and the cooler air above and the slow, pale luminescence that pools in the alcoves like a liquid finding its level. They grow toward the light not because the light is good but because the light is *there*, and where the light is there is a gradient, and where there is a gradient there is something to metabolize, and where something can be metabolized a body will grow to metabolize it. This is not intention. This is physics. This is the same force that makes a crystal grow, the same force that makes iron filings align to a field, the same force that made the builders carve shapes into stone because the stone was there and their hands were there and there was nothing else for their hands to do.

The fungi have been growing for a very long time.

You look at them—the whorls spiraling inward, the pale luminescence that is not light but warmth made visible—and you try to imagine how long. Centuries, the surveys said. Centuries. But the surveys were guessing, and the guessing was based on growth rates extrapolated from surface fungi, and surface fungi are not these fungi, and surface time is not this time, and you begin to suspect that centuries is not enough. You begin to suspect that *forever* is not enough. You begin to suspect that the fungi have been growing since before there were alcoves for them to grow in, since the tunnels were carved by hands that have not been hands for longer than your species has existed, since the warm stone rose from whatever depths it rose from and the cool air settled above it and the gradients formed and the first slow body began to grow toward the first slow light.

You are not a scholar. You were not trained for this. But you sit in the gallery and you look at the fungi and you feel something that might be awe or might be grief or might be the specific stillness of a mind that has finally stopped trying to understand and is simply *seeing*.

The fungi do not see you. They have no eyes. But they are aware of you in the way that stone is aware of weight—not as a thing that experiences but as a thing that shifts in response to, that adjusts to, that makes room for. You are sitting in the gallery and the light is pooling where you are sitting and the fungi are growing toward the light and the light is pooling around you and you are becoming part of the gradient, the way a stone becomes part of a wall, the way a word becomes part of a sentence.

You do not move. You are not sure you can.

---

The skitters come to you.

This is the wrong way to say it. They do not *come* to you. They are already moving in their currents, the way iron filings move in a field, and the currents bring them to the edges of your sitting-body the way a river brings silt to a delta—not because the river chose to deposit the silt but because the river is the river and the silt is the silt and the delta is what happens when the river reaches the sea. You are the delta. You are the place where the current slows and the sediment settles. You are the anomaly in the gradient, the warmth-blooded body that is warmer than the stone and cooler than the warmth rising from below, the thing that metabolizes oxygen and exhales carbon dioxide and has a pulse that beats seventy times a minute or sixty or a hundred, depending on your fear-level, which here is not fear but something quieter, something you do not have a word for, something like the feeling of being watched by a thing that does not know it is watching.

They cluster at the edges of your warmth.

You can feel them now—not as pressure, not as weight, but as *attention*, a kind of attention that is not attention because there is no mind to attend with. They are pressing their membrane-skin against the air around you, absorbing something. Your warmth, maybe. Your exhaled breath, maybe. Something you are giving off that their metabolism can parse, that their softite bodies need to grow. You are food. Not in the way that fungi are food, not in the way that anything in the Bone Vaults is food, but in the way that light is food to the fungi: a gradient, a differential, a something that is *there* and therefore metabolizable.

You should be afraid. You are not afraid. You are something else.

You are *observed*.

Not by the skitters—they have no eyes, no mind, no capacity for observation. But by the system. By the Bone Vaults themselves, which have become aware of you the way a body becomes aware of a splinter: not with sight but with the slow, constant sensation of something that does not belong, something that the body will either absorb or expel or wall off with calcium, with time, with the patient accumulation of the thing that made the splinter in the first place. You are the splinter. You are the splinter that the Bone Vaults are learning around.

And you are learning them.

---

The pool is in you now.

You do not mean this metaphorically. You mean it the way you might mean *the stone is in my hand*, which is to say: physically, actually, present in a way that the words *in me* cannot fully convey. You have been carrying the image of the pool since you found it—the amber-sap density, the pitch-slow movement, the light at the center that is not light but time made luminous—and the image has not faded. It has not faded because it cannot fade. The pool is the heart of the Bone Vaults, and the Bone Vaults are in you now, absorbed the way the skitters absorb warmth-eccrines, metabolized the way the fungi metabolize heat-gradients, grown into you the way the fungi grow into the alcoves, the way the skitters grow into their own formless, jointless patience.

You understand, finally, what the builders made.

Not the tunnels. Not the shapes. The *frame*. The frame for the pool, for the heart of the place, for the thing that has been still for so long that stillness has become its only state. The builders carved the Bone Vaults as a hand might carve a cup: not because the hand needed a cup but because the hand needed somewhere to put the water, the wine, the blood, whatever it was that the hand could not hold in the hand itself and could not bear to let go of. They made a shape to hold a stillness. They made a body to hold a heart.

And now you are the body. Now you are the shape. You have been walking through the Bone Vaults and the Bone Vaults have been walking through you, and the walking is not over, but it is different now, because you know what you are carrying, and you know what you are carrying it in, and you know that the carrying will not end, that it cannot end, that the pool is in you now and will be in you when you are in the ground, and when you are in the ground the pool will still be there, and when the ground is gone the pool will still be there, and nothing here has ever changed and nothing here will ever change and the unchanging is not a curse but a fact, and the fact is that you are here, and the here is there, and the there is the pool, and the pool is the heart, and the heart is still.

---

You walk on.

The tunnels go deeper, or they go back, or they go nowhere that your mapping can parse. It does not matter. You are not going toward something. You are not going away from something. You are going, which is what you have always done, which is what the warm stone below does, which is what the cool air above does, which is what the fungi do when they grow and the skitters do when they move and the pool does when it is still. You are going the way water goes downhill: not because it chooses to but because going is what water is, and going downhill is what going is when there is a downhill to go to.

The skitters are still moving in their currents. The fungi are still growing in their alcoves. The light is still pooling in its levels. Around you, or in you, or through you—the Bone Vaults are still the Bone Vaults, and you are still in them, and *in* is not the right word but it is the only word you have, and the word will do, and the doing will do, and the doing is walking, and the walking is here, and the here is the place where nothing changes except slowly, except over centuries, except the way the fungi grow and the skitters move and you—you change the way light changes when it enters a room: not by becoming the room but by illuminating it, by making it visible, by being the thing that sees so that the room can be the thing that is seen.

You are the eye the Bone Vaults do not have.

You are the change the Bone Vaults cannot make.

You are the dream that is walking through, and the dream is not ending, and the walking is not stopping, and the tunnels go deeper, and the warmth rises from below, and the pool is still, and the stillness is still, and you are still here, and here is the place where nothing has ever changed.

And you are the change that nothing has needed.
