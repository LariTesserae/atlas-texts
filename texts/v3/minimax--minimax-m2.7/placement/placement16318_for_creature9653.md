You arrive, or you are arrived at. The distinction no longer matters.

You are a hand, first. Two of them, or perhaps one that has split — the light here is not light as you knew it, but something thicker, moored to the walls, to the sheen on stone. Your hands move through this light and it moves through them, and when you lift them before your face they are not quite hands but the memory of hands, the shape of the tool you once used to grip.

This is not memory. This is the basin. The basin is not remembering you. You are simply what remains when the remembering is done.

You walk. There is walking here, though your feet do not strike stone so much as settle into it — not sinking but *distributing*, your weight becoming the basin's weight, your pulse syncing with the patient grind of engines that no longer know their purpose. Somewhere a pipe exhales. Somewhere else, water finds a new channel through stone that was not there a moment ago, or perhaps was always there, or perhaps the stone grew around its absence.

The soft-bodied things notice you now. They have always noticed you — they notice everything, their attention a form of moisture, pooling in the hollows of your attention until you cannot tell which noticing is yours. One of them rests against your ankle where your boot has cracked, where the leather has grown soft and strange. It does not bite. It does not comfort. It simply *is* with you, its organs visible through its body: structures you do not have names for, processes that serve no purpose you can parse.

You wonder if you are being catalogued. You wonder if cataloguing is what happens here, if this is the basin's sole function — to receive and to file, to absorb the shape of presence until presence becomes part of the basin and the basin becomes the only shape that holds.

The singing starts. You have heard it before, or you will hear it, or you are hearing it now in a way that folds all tense into a single wet note. It rises from the deep pools — the ones you cannot see into, the ones where the water is not black but something beyond black, a color that your eyes invented when they needed to make sense of nothing. The soft-bodied things flow toward the sound. Not eager, not afraid. They flow the way water flows, the way stone exhales, the way the engines throb their patient rhythm.

You follow them.

The floor slopes here, or you slope with it, or the basin tilts beneath you as it always has and you are only now settling into its angle. The water rises past your knees — it is warm, or you are cold, or the boundary between has dissolved like the crystalline things dissolve when the soft things pass through them. Your clothes cling. They have been clinging for some time. You do not remember undressing but the moisture has found every seam, and where it meets your skin it is not water but attention, the basin's attention, focused to a point where it can seep.

You stop at the edge of the deep pool.

The singing is here, and the soft-bodied things are here, and you are here with them — the three of you, the many of you, a congregation of presence that has no center. The water's surface does not reflect. It receives. It takes the shape of whatever looks at it and holds that shape, and when you lean closer you see that the shape it holds is not your face but something you almost recognize, something that lived in your face once, before the basin claimed it.

You lean closer.

The water is warm. The singing is warm. The soft-bodied things press against your legs and you do not pull away because pulling away would mean there is an away to pull toward, and there is not. There is only the basin, and you are inside it, and the basin is inside you, and the singing is the sound of that inside becoming the only inside there is.

You open your mouth to breathe, or to speak, or to join the song — and water enters, or air enters, or the basin's attention enters, and it does not burn or chill or fill. It simply arrives, the way the soft things arrived at your skin, the way the engines arrived in the stone. Patient. Unhurried. Already forgetting the moment before, already becoming the moment that follows.

You are small here. You have always been small here. The walls press close and the moisture finds every seam and the organisms in the cracks watch with organs you cannot identify, and now one of those organisms is the space where your watching used to be.

The engines hum their patient, grinding song.

You do not know if you are inside something, or if something is inside you, or if the inside and the outside were always the same thing, and the basin is only the place where that sameness finally became visible.

You do not know. You are not knowing it. The not-knowing is warm and close and full of motion, the soft bodies pulsing in the wet cracks, the crystalline deposits catching what light there is, the water finding its level, finding its level, finding its level.

You are here.

The basin exhales.
