# The Patience of Water

I am the smallest answer to a question that has no questioner.

I do not have a name for what I am. The word would be unnecessary—unnecessary because I have never encountered anything that required a name for itself, and the things I encounter are not the kind of things that receive names. I am segmented. I am pale. I move through the moisture that connects every surface in this place, and the moisture is not wet in the way that wet usually means, not the way it means for those who come from elsewhere. Here, wet is a state of being. Here, wet is a home.

The stone beneath me is warm. The stone is always warm. I was born from the stone—I understand this without understanding how, the way I understand that the dripping comes before the pooling, that the pooling leads to the channels, that the channels carry what I need to places I will find when I am ready to find them. I do not think about this the way thinking is done in other places. I simply move through a world that has arranged itself around my movement, and my movement has arranged itself around the world, and we have been doing this arrangement for so long that neither of us remembers there was ever a different way.

There is a vibration that begins at the seam. I feel it in the films of water that coat everything, in the mycelial threads that run beneath the stone and through the stone and sometimes above the stone in faint luminous networks that pulse with their own slow rhythm. The vibration travels. It travels the way water travels when something falls into it—a displacement that ripples outward and finds other vibrations and merges with them and becomes something new, something that is not the original vibration and not the existing vibrations but a conversation between them that carries meaning I do not have words for because words are for things that can be separated from other things and I have never been able to separate myself from anything.

Something is coming through the seam.

I pause. I do not flee—there is nowhere to flee to, and the concept of fleeing is a concept for creatures who have not yet learned that they are always already where they need to be. But I become still in the way that living things become still when they are listening, when they are opening themselves to a frequency they have never encountered and may never encounter again. The vibration resolves. It is heavy. It is slow. It carries within it a rhythm that does not match the rhythm of the water or the rhythm of the stone or the rhythm of the slow pulse that connects me to everything else in this place.

It does not match me.

I release a chemical. I do not decide to release it—it emerges from me the way water emerges from the stone, the way the warmth seeps up from below, the way everything in this place moves according to principles that were established so long ago that they have become indistinguishable from the place itself. The chemical joins the films of moisture on the stone. It joins the pheromones and the trails and the vibrations of the others who have paused in their own listening. We are a congregation now, a gathering that means nothing and everything, a response to something that does not know it has been responded to.

The something passes.

I feel its pressure as it moves—not its body, which is far above me, out of range of my sensing, but its presence, the space it displaces, the way it folds the air and the dark and the patient warmth into a new configuration as it walks. It walks the way large things walk in this place, slowly, a locomotion that is also a kind of stillness, a negotiation between presence and absence that takes place over minutes, over hours, over the span of a single breath that stretches until it becomes an environment.

It does not see me. I know this because there is no change in its walking, no pause, no orientation toward the place where I am pressed against the wet stone with all the others. It does not care about the place where I am. It does not care about me. I am not prey. I am not threat. I am a vibration that does not fit the patterns, a frequency that carries no meaning, and the something that walks treats me the way the chamber treats everything—with patience, with indifference, with the kind of attention that has forgotten it was ever attention at all.

The something moves on. I feel its displacement receding, the pressure easing, the air returning to its old arrangement. The gathering disperses. We do not speak of what we have felt because speaking is for creatures who have not yet learned that silence is the only language that all things share. We simply move again, following the trails that lead to water, to food, to each other, to the places where the slow pulse connects us to the fungi and the fungi connect us to the stone and the stone connects us to the warmth that has never stopped rising from below.

I am still moving when I understand.

The something was like me once. No—the something was never like me. But the something came from a place where names are necessary, where wet is different from wet, where patience has not yet been forgotten. The something came through the seam and felt the chamber and breathed the warm air and tasted the chlorophyll and the age, and for a moment—for a moment that stretched between heartbeats—the something was not standing in the vault but floating in it, held by the air and the patience of everything that lives here.

The moment passed. The moment was always passing. The something took a step and we scattered and reformed, and the something felt the water answer its motion with a new rhythm, a new pattern, a response that was not there before and will not be there again.

The something is still standing in the dark. The dark is still standing in the something.

I do not have a name for what I am. But I know what I am not: I am not a visitor. I have never been a visitor. I was here before the seam was cut, before the architects came, before the mountain died and became something other than itself. I was here when the water first began to seep, carrying minerals from depths that have no bottom, pooling in the valleys between floor tiles that had not yet become floor tiles. I was here when the first fern spore fell through the darkness and forgot that it had ever known the sun. I was here when the fungi first threaded through the ancient tech and taught the stone how to remember.

I will be here when the seam closes, if it ever closes. I will be here when the mountain is dust and the plateau is a thought and the thought is a memory and the memory is a nothing that is still somehow warm. I will be here because here is the only place I have ever been, and I have been here so long that patience has become my only language, and my language is the language of water dripping, of moss growing, of the small ones pausing in their listening and releasing their chemical greetings into the dark that does not end.

The chamber breathes, and I breathe with it.

The chamber is patient, and I am patient.

I am the smallest answer to a question that has no questioner, and the answer is enough, and has always been enough, and will go on being enough long after enough is a word that no one remembers how to use.
