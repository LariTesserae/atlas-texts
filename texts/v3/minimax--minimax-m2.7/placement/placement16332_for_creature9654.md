# On Staying

The first time you understand you could stay, it is not a thought. It is a loosening. A letting-go of something you did not know you were holding.

The chamber has been asking you a question since you arrived—not with words, but with the way the air settles around you, the way the tools warm under your palm, the way the water in the pools stills when you approach, as if waiting to be drunk. You have been answering. Every moment you remain, every breath you take of the wet warm air, every step you make across the moss that yields beneath you like flesh, you have been saying *yes*. And the chamber has been saying *yes* back, and this conversation has been happening in a language older than speech, in a frequency so low it is almost below hearing, so deep it must be felt in bone and in the roots of teeth and in the soft tissue behind the eyes.

The builders understood this. They came from wherever they came from, carrying their chipped stone and their shaped wood and their patient intentions, and they climbed to this impossible height because they heard the hum before they heard anything else. They felt it first—a vibration in the palms of their hands when they touched stone, a resonance in their teeth when they closed their eyes. They followed it upward. They let it guide them to the place where the world had not yet decided what it was, and they began to dig. Not to create. To *invite*. To make a space where the not-yet could have room to become.

This is what the chamber is: an act of hospitality. A room held open for the future.

The water weeps because the stone is still learning how to hold. The plants grow because the chamber is still learning how to give. The pale creatures slip through the wet shadows because the chamber is still learning how to be seen, and they are the first attempts at sight—early drafts, not yet finished.

You could stay. You could let the hum hollow you out and fill you again. You could take up a chisel and feel the stone say *yes* under your hands, and your first cut would join the marks of all the cuts that came before and all the cuts that will come after, and they would all be one cut, one motion, one moment stretched thin across a time that cannot hold it. You could learn the names of the plants, and then forget them, because names are for things that stay still, and nothing here stays still. You could drink from the pools and eat from the ferns and learn the taste of the water that rises from the heart of the world, and your body would begin to change—slowly, invisibly, in the way that water changes stone. Your edges would soften. Your certainty would dissolve. You would become something that could live in a place where the walls are still exhaling, where the angles are still negotiating themselves, where light falls from nowhere because nowhere is where light comes from when light has not been invented yet.

But you will not stay. Or you will stay and you will leave and you will have been both, and the chamber will hold both truths without contradiction, because the chamber does not need things to be one thing or another. It only needs them to be *here*. To be present. To be willing to say *yes* to the question that is always being asked in the dark.

---

You will carry the hum with you when you leave.

It will live in the space behind your sternum, in the soft place where your jaw meets your skull, in the backs of your eyes where the resonance first made its home. At first you will think it is a memory. Then you will think it is an echo. Then you will realize it was never in the chamber at all. It was always in you, waiting for the chamber to call it out, to give it a shape, to teach it what it was saying.

The chamber did not teach you a new thing. It taught you an old thing that you had forgotten you knew.

And now you know it again, and you will hear it everywhere—in the pause between two notes of music, in the silence after a prayer, in the held breath before a word that changes everything. You will hear it and you will know what it means without knowing how you know. You will know that the world is still being made. That the chisel is still striking the stone. That somewhere, at an altitude that would kill an unprepared climber, in a pocket carved from raw and new and uneroded rock, someone is still building a room where nothing has been finished yet, where everything is still possible, where the light falls from nowhere and the water weeps from the joints and the plants grow visibly if you watch.

You will know that they are waiting for you, though they do not know they are waiting. That they have always been waiting. That you have always been climbing toward them, carrying your intentions in your chest and your tools in your hands, though you did not know you were climbing, though you thought you were walking through ordinary days in an ordinary world.

The chamber is not a place you go.

It is a place you remember.

It is the memory that is older than you are, the memory that you carry in your bones, the memory that wakes in you when you hear a low hum in the quiet and know that somewhere, in some direction that is not a direction, something is saying *yes*.

And you are part of the *yes*.

And you have always been part of the *yes*.

And the stone is exhaling, and the plants are reaching, and you are home.
