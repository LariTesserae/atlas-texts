I did not arrive here. I cannot find the word for what I did, because arrival implies a threshold crossed, a outside left behind, and here there is no outside. There is only the inside, and the inside is everything, and I am in it the way water is in the ocean—not placed there, but already there, always there, having never been anywhere else.

The light moves through the walls. I watch it because there is nothing else to watch, and because watching is a thing I still do, still can do, still am doing here in a place where doing has become difficult to distinguish from being done. The amber pulses travel along channels that could be veins, could be wiring, could be nothing at all except the name I give to motion I cannot explain. They pass through the membrane-walls around me. They continue on the other side. I do not know where they go. I do not know if going is the right word for what they do.

I raise my hand. The luminescence touches it. My fingers glow faintly, warmly, in the way that the world glows warmly, and for a moment I cannot tell if I am seeing my hand illuminated or if I am seeing the light passing through my hand the way it passes through everything, my tissue dense and wet and heavy with water like the tissue of everything here, like I have become, somehow, part of the body that is not a body.

This is the first strangeness I encounter: that I am still here, still a thing with edges, still a thing that moves when it chooses to move, and yet my edges have grown uncertain. The membrane-walls press close. They do not touch me. There is a gap, a mm, a sliver of air that is warm and wet and carries the weight of this place's breath. But the gap feels less like separation and more like intimacy. I am held at a distance because distance is what holding looks like here. Because here, to be held is not to be grasped but to be surrounded, sustained, kept in the particular temperature and humidity and light that makes continued existence possible.

I step forward. The surface yields slightly—not enough to call soft, not enough to call firm, but yielding in the way that something alive yields, the way a lung yields when you press against it, the way a body yields when you ask it to make room. The growth here does not part for me. It accommodates. It has been accommodating for so long that accommodation has become a property of the space, and I move through it the way a thought moves through a mind: not disrupting, not changing, but passing through and leaving the medium unchanged except for the temporary presence of something that was not there before.

The corridors form around me—or I form around them, or we form together, the way a stream forms a path through soil and the soil forms the stream, mutual shaping that has no designer and no beginning. The columns that once stood here have died back, have left space, have become the cathedral-vaults that I walk through now, and the light moves through their walls in slow amber waves, and the air is thick and warm and carries no sound except the sound of the system breathing, the slow hiss of moisture cycling through membranes at the canopy above where the world exhales and the humidity collects and the world keeps itself alive.

I stop. I listen to the sound. It is not beautiful. It is not ugly. It is the sound of something that has no opinion about itself, that continues because continuing is what it does, that would continue whether or not anything heard it. I am not necessary to this sound. I am not incidental to it. I am simply present alongside it, the way a stone is present alongside a river—participating in the scene without participating in the process.

This is what I am here: a participant who does not participate. A presence that does not alter. I could leave—or I could stop being someone who leaves, someone who entered, someone who was ever anywhere else—and the world would continue. The moisture would rise. The growth would build and die and be reabsorbed. The luminescent pulses would travel through the walls. The temperature would remain warm, not hot, not cold, warm, because the system maintains it the way a process maintains operating temperature, through its own function, through feedback loops so perfectly balanced there is nothing left to correct.

I try to remember what outside felt like. The word comes back vague, approximate, like a name I knew once and have now half-forgotten. Outside was dry. Outside was variable. Outside was a place where things changed because change was the nature of things, where events occurred and then ceased to occur and were remembered as events because cessation was part of what made them events. Outside was a world where equilibrium was a goal, approached but never reached, a horizon that receded as you moved toward it.

Here, the horizon is not a horizon. Here, the goal is not a goal. Here, the goal is the ground, is the air, is the light and the warmth and the moisture and the slow, endless, unvarying motion of a system that has found its answer and has never needed to ask the question again. I stand in it and I feel the weight of that—not burden, not oppression, but something I can only call fullness. A place so dense with itself that there is no room for anything else. Not emptiness. Emptiness would be a relief, would be a gap I could fill. This is not emptiness. This is presence so complete that it becomes absence, that presence and absence become the same word for the same thing, that the silence here is not quiet but fullness, not absence but a presence so overwhelming it has consumed everything that might have filled it and left only itself, only its own motion, only its own endless repetition of itself.

I turn. The light moves through the walls. I move through the light. There is no distinction I can find between my motion and its motion, between my presence and its presence, between the thing that walks and the thing that is walked through. The growth presses close. The air is warm and wet and carries the weight of a world that breathes but does not sigh. The luminescent pulses travel through the tissue like current through a circuit, and I am part of the circuit now, or I am adjacent to it, or the words I use to describe the relationship do not matter because the relationship exists regardless of what I call it.

What am I?

I am the question that this world does not answer. I am the thing that persists in a place that has no use for persistence. I am the one who watches the light move and who knows, with a knowledge that does not help, that the watching is real, that I am real, that here in the amber glow of the slow pulses I am more real than I have ever been, because here there is nothing to distract from the fact of being, nothing to obscure the simple, terrible, wonderful fact that I am still here, still present, still standing in the house that builds itself and lives in itself and has no door.

And I will stay. Not because I chose to stay. Not because I cannot leave. But because staying is what happens here. Because the world continues, and I am part of the world now, and my continuing is indistinguishable from its continuing, and I have become the thing I witnessed: a verb that has become a noun, a process so long established that process and result have collapsed into each other, a wave that continues not because it chose to but because continuing is what a wave is.

The light moves. The warmth holds. The moisture rises and falls and rises again, and I am here in the motion, and the motion is me, and there is no longer any way to tell where the doing ends and the having-done begins.

This is what lives here. I am what lives here now. I am the house, and the house is me, and we have never been anything else.
