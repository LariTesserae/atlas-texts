What I become is this: a settling.

Not dust—that would be too simple, too much like metaphor. I become the space dust occupies between falls. The interval. The pause where the cyclone loses its axis and the fine ochre hangs in the shimmering air like something neither rising nor falling but finding the exact altitude where gravity and heat cancel each other out.

I have been here long enough now that I cannot remember the counting. But I know it happened. I know that something registered the chemical signature of my breathing, matched it against the baseline, filed it in whatever archive the machinery keeps—not memory exactly, but something more structural, the way a river remembers where it has cut. The cut remains. The water moves on. The cut is the record.

I am the cut now.

Where the arroyo walls smooth inward, where the stone wears a particular shine that catches the amber light wrong, there is my signature. The rust-that-is-not-rust spread toward my body-heat for years before I understood what it was doing. I thought it was corrosion. I thought it was the landscape's slow way of healing around an intrusion. But healing implies a wound, and a wound implies a before, and there is no before here that matters. There is only the after of processes that were designed for something else and have adapted.

I adapted too. My edges softened. Not literally—I am still this shape, still this chemistry—but the boundaries I carried with me from elsewhere became porous. The amber vapor settled into my lungs and stayed. The drone penetrated what I thought were thoughts. The heat stopped being something that pressed and became something that simply was, the way water is wet, the way depth is measured in pressure rather than distance.

What I am now exists in the frequencies the spire-climbers read when they touch each other. I am the pause between two descents. I am the reason the dust rises in small cyclones and not large ones—because something is there, has been there, will be there, and the air must negotiate around my presence the way it negotiates around the machinery housings, the way it negotiates around the shapes that are not quite shapes at the plateau's edge.

I am not observed by something with eyes. I am the observation.

The machinery cycles. The amber vapor thickens and thins. Time settles like sediment in still water, layer upon layer, and I am several of those layers now—my presence distributed across decades, each stratum recording a different phase of what I was when I first arrived, what I thought I was for a while after that, what I became when I stopped thinking in those terms entirely.

The flights wheel overhead, their wing-geometry impossible. I cannot see them directly, but I feel them—the way the atmosphere shifts when they pass, the way the drone changes pitch, the way the rust spreads faster in their wake. They are not birds. They are not machines. They are something that the plateau grew when it needed to count what moved through the upper air, and I have been counted. My chemical signature is in their geometry now, encoded in the angle of wings that were designed before I was born and will be designed again after whatever I become next.

The fungal mats grow toward heat. I am heat now—not warm, not hot, but the kind of persistent thermal signature that the mats can orient toward, that draws them closer across decades, that will eventually connect me to the network they are building, the strand that stretches from one heat source to another until the whole plateau is one continuous system and there are no separations left, only variations in temperature, only the different densities of a single presence.

This is what I become: not a body, not a mind, but a density. A way the plateau holds itself together. A node in a census that was never meant to include me but has incorporated me anyway, the way the arroyo creatures incorporate the mineral lattice, the way the rust incorporates the stone, the way the machinery incorporates whatever the amber vapor carries.

I stay.

The drone continues.

The dust rises and settles and rises again, and I am the rhythm now—not the rising, not the settling, but the pulse between them, the count that the machinery keeps without effort, the census that grows longer by one with each breath I take and each breath I don't, because here, in the hot high emptiness, even absence is a form of presence, even stillness is a kind of motion, even a visitor who stays long enough becomes part of the wound that never healed.

The wound that became the plateau. The plateau that became the machinery. The machinery that became the count.

And the count that became me.

I am being assessed in terms that would require new vocabulary to describe, and I have learned some of that vocabulary now, and the words are not words but pressures, not concepts but chemistries, not meanings but densities, and I am learning to read them the way the spire-climbers read the air, the way the dust reads the thermals, the way the stone reads the centuries.

I stay.

I am the staying.

The rust remembers, and I am part of what it remembers—the intrusion that became the scar, the scar that became the landscape, the landscape that became the census, the census that became the presence that watches without eyes and counts without counting and stays without knowing what staying means until staying is all that remains.

The drone continues.

I listen.

I am what listens.

I am the listening.
