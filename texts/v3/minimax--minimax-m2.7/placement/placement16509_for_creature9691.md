What would I be here?

I think I would be something patient. Something that does not move so much as settle into the warmth of it, the way the water settles into the mud and the mud settles into the shape of something still becoming.

Perhaps I am the turtle.

Not the one that basks—too exposed, too visible in that low light. I am the one beneath, sunk into the silt where the water is only a hand's breadth deep but warmer still, held against the body like a second pulse. The mud receives me. I am half-dreaming, half-awake, the way everything here exists in that threshold between stillness and motion. My eyes are closed but not sealed. I see the world as a amber haze, a diffusion of light and warmth, the slow passage of fish above me like thoughts moving through a mind that has no need to hurry.

The mud breathes. I breathe with it. Minerals seep through my shell, through the thin membrane where blood runs close to the surface of bone. I am learning the temperature of this place the way a root learns the temperature of water—through the whole body, slowly, continuously.

Above me, the fish move in their silver arcs. They are faster than I am, more present to the moment. I do not envy them. Their urgency is a kind of blindness, a rushing past of everything that lies still. I have chosen the mud. The mud has chosen me.

There is a crocodile somewhere in the deeper channels. I know this the way I know gravity—not as knowledge but as a condition of being. The crocodile moves through the water like a low vibration, a sound felt in the bones rather than heard. I am too large for it to consider, and too still. It prefers movement, prefers the quick panic of something that bleeds. I am stone-colored, stone-slow, stone-patient. The crocodile slides past the edge of my pool and does not see me, or sees me and dismisses me as something that has already fossilized.

This is correct. In a sense, I have already begun to become rock. The silt is pressing into my shell, filling the grooves, smoothing the edges. In a thousand years—or ten thousand, or a time that has no number because time has not yet learned to measure itself in this wet, warm, emerging place—I will be part of the land itself. The land is building itself around me. I am one of its first stones.

But not yet. Now I am still alive. My throat opens and closes, a slow swallow of warm water. My claws flex in the mud, testing the grip of it, the way it holds me like a hand holds a seed before planting. I am held here. I am held.

Above the water, the reeds sway. Their shadows move across the surface in slow patterns, like writing in a language that has not yet learned to mean anything. The frogs are calling—I hear them through the water, a muffled roar, a vibration that is also a voice. They are telling each other something. I do not understand it. Perhaps I do not need to. Perhaps the marsh speaks in many voices and I understand only the voice of mud, the voice of waiting, the voice of slow turning toward something that is not yet nameable.

The light filters down in columns. Sometimes it reaches me—a shaft of gold through the murk, touching my shell, warming the algae that grows there. I am green with it, green with the life that claims every surface here. Moss climbs me. Fungi bloom in the darkness of the reed beds. The pitcher plants catch their prey and digest them slowly, patient as I am, drawing meaning from decay.

I am part of this. I am not separate from it.

The water seeps upward through me, through the mud, through the half-formed land. Weeps, the prompt calls it—water weeping from stone. But it is not weeping. It is exhaling. The world is breathing out, and I am breathing with it, and the breath is warm, and the breath is endless, and the breath is what it means to be held in a place that has not yet decided what it will become.

I am a turtle in the warm silt of an emerging world.

I am ancient in the way of things that have not yet been named.

I wait.

The fish pass.

The light shifts.

The marsh breathes around me, and I breathe within it, and we are the same slow pulse, the same patient turning toward a future that will not arrive for a very long time, and I am content with this, content to be part of the stillness that holds the chaos of all this life in place.

The crocodiles growl in the distance.

The frogs sing their wall of sound.

I sink deeper into the mud and let it hold me, and the mud holds, and the water seeps, and the light filters, and I am here, I am here, I am still here in this warm wet world that has not yet learned to end.
