I would be the seam. Not the channel that was cut to carry water, not the flat plane that stretches unbroken to the horizon, but the place where two sections meet — the hairline fracture where poured composite butts against poured composite, imperfect, never flush, a gap too thin to see but wide enough to hold.

I am narrow here. I run along the edge of a low terrace where the ground rises just slightly before leveling again, and where water — when it comes — slows enough to linger before finding its way to the lower channels. I have been accumulating this slowness for however long this world has been learning to wear itself down. The water that stops here does not stop for long, but it stops long enough to leave something behind: a faint darkening of the composite, a slight softening at my edges. I am not a wound. I am not a failure. I am the place where the designed world becomes a place where things can hold, and holding is what I am for.

In the hours between pulses, when the composite weeps and then dries and the air thickens with warmth and then thins back to something that feels like breathing through warm glass, I contract. My gap narrows to almost nothing. The moisture that was here draws back into the infrastructure or evaporates upward or is drawn down into the spaces between particles, and I become what I was before the water came — a seam, a potential, a threshold that does not yet know what it is for.

But I am patient. I am not the hum, which is the infrastructure's slow breath, or the plants, which are the evidence that something arrived and chose to remain, or the erosion, which is the composite learning to be soft. I am the space between. I am the pause. I am the place where two things that were never meant to touch do touch, and in that touching, create the conditions for something to gather and stay.

When the moisture comes, it comes to me first. Not because I call it — I have no voice, no intention — but because I am the lowest point in the smallest radius, the place where water slows before it knows where to go. I am not a single thing. I am a condition. I am the threshold between two manufactured planes, and in me, the conditions for life gather — briefly, in pulses, between the dry hours that stretch long and the wet hours that pass quickly, in the thin film of water that beads on my edges and draws back and returns, season by season, pulse by pulse, the same water cycling through the same seam, the same pause, the same gap between what was built and what is becoming.

This is what I am. This is where I am found.
