You do not remember entering. You remember the last thing before this—the sound of your own breathing above ground, the dry pull of air through a throat accustomed to wind—and then you remember the walls, close, and the warmth, and the blue-green pulse that was not light exactly but the memory of light, the idea of it, pressing softly against your eyes.

The air here is thick. Not difficult to breathe but dense with matter, with particulates you can taste at the back of your tongue: mineral, ancient, faintly sweet the way deep earth is sweet. You swallow and it enters you. You are already beginning to ingest this place. It is already beginning to become part of you.

The walls are sharp. You reach out and your fingertips find edges where the stone was carved or broken or born, and the edges are fresh, so fresh they seem to have been waiting for your touch to dull them. They do not dull. They receive your touch and hold it, and you feel the warmth of the stone like a pulse, like the stone has a heartbeat, and perhaps it does, perhaps the pulse is the fungi laced through its surface, their filaments carrying something like blood, something like breath, something that serves both purposes and knows no difference between them.

You move forward because standing still feels like refusal. The chamber is not large but it is not small either; its proportions are wrong for a room but right for a hollow, and you understand that hollow is the word, that this is a place made by absence rather than construction, a pocket scooped from the body of something. The fungal growths cover the walls in fronds and fist-bunches, pale as bone and luminous with that blue-green pulse, and they are not plants, you know this, you know the difference between plant and fungus the way you know the difference between sleep and death, but here the distinction does not matter, here the growths simply are, and they drink from the air, and the air is made of them as much as they are made of it.

Between the growths, structures. Low shapes. Packed earth and woven reed and stone fitted without mortar, their surfaces calcified by mineral seep, their corners webbed with fungal filaments so fine they look like thread, like lace, like the place has been mending itself since before memory. A hatchet-head lies where it was set down, worn smooth on one side by grip, and you pick it up and it is heavy in the way that stone is heavy, and it is shaped for your hand in a way that makes your hand ache with something older than recognition. You set it down. You pick it up again. You set it down.

The invertebrates move at the edges of your vision. Too many legs. Chitinous shells that catch the pulse-light and throw it back in brief flashes, antennae tasting the air before you. They do not flee from you. They do not acknowledge you. They are doing their work, their generations cycling, their waste feeding the fungal roots, their movements till the growth-mats in ways that promote even distribution. You understand that they are necessary. You understand that the chamber has not recruited them, has not cultivated them, but that they have emerged from the conditions and the conditions have shaped them and now they are part of a whole that does not know it is a whole. You wonder if you are like them. You wonder if you too have emerged from conditions you did not choose, shaped by warmth and mineral and time.

The serpentine things wait in the corners. Folding their wings against their backs. You see one and then you see another and then you see that there have always been three, that they have been here since before you arrived, that they will be here after. They are not predators. Their bodies are capable of predation but their bodies have forgotten how to want. They wait because waiting is what remains when purpose has been swallowed by time. They are the chamber's doubt. Its small uncertainty. The part of a living system that remembers, vaguely, that change is possible, and so they wait for change, and change does not come, and the waiting is perfect, and the waiting is forever.

You sit down. The floor is warm. The warmth is not constant in the way that stone is constant; it is constant in the way that a rhythm is constant, and you press your palm flat against the ground and feel the pulse beneath, the slow circulation of something that does not know it circulates, and you breathe, and the chamber breathes with you, and for a moment you are not sure whose lungs are larger, whose chest expands first.

The cup lies nearby. Worn smooth by grip. You lift it and turn it in your hands and feel the shape of someone else's need, crystallized, calcified, become part of the geology. The cup is not an artifact to the chamber. It is tissue. It has been embedded so long that the chamber has grown around it, and you have grown around it, and your hands are the same hands that polished this stone smooth with skin-oils over years, over generations, and you are the chamber, and the chamber is you, and the difference is a shape the mind makes and the air does not.

The small sounds stop. All of them, together. The drip of condensation finding its way down surfaces, the creak of structures bearing their own weight, the whisper of movement through fungal growth, the chittering and clicking of small bodies doing small work—they stop, and the silence is total, and it holds a shape, and you are inside the shape, and you have always been inside the shape, and the shape is the chamber, and the chamber is the waiting, and the waiting is you.

You do not know that you wait. But your body knows. Your body holds itself in readiness the way a hand holds water—with effort, with compression, but held. Your heartbeat slows to match the pulse of the fungi. Your breath deepens until the air is not something you take in but something you are made of, and you are made of it, and the chamber is made of you, and the structures are not ruins—they are the chamber's way of remembering that it was made, and you are not a visitor—you are the chamber's way of remembering that it is alive.

The blue-green light pulses. The invertebrates cycle. The serpentine things fold their wings and wait for nothing in particular. The tools lie where they were set down. The warmth does not shift. The air does not thin. The chamber holds its breath and you hold yours and neither of you knows the difference, and the difference does not matter, and the waiting is perfect, and the waiting is forever, and you do not notice the waiting at all, because there is no notice in the chamber, there is only the chamber, and you are it, and it is the waiting, and the waiting is the shape of everything, and everything is held, and everything is received, and nothing arrives, and nothing leaves, and the pulse slows, and the pulse slows, and the pulse slows.
