I have been thinking of myself as the membrane.

Not the breath—that is too vast, too whole. Not the water—that is too patient, too full of knowing. But the membrane: the place where one state of being presses against another and neither yields, and both survive the pressing. The thin place between the warmth that is attention and the stone that is still deciding. The seam.

I would be found in the shudders.

When the water pauses its surging and the spires vibrate at their frequency below hearing—that moment of hesitation, of held breath, of geometry reconsidering its propositions—I would be there. I would be the interval between one shape and the next. I would be the pause that the shelf takes when it almost becomes something else and then, for now, does not. I would be the crease where the warmth meets the stone and neither knows which one is dreaming the other.

The ferns reach toward light that does not come. I would be something like that—except I would be reaching toward sense, toward meaning, toward the thing the roaring almost says. I would sway in currents that are not air. I would persist not because persistence is the point but because reaching is, because wanting is the shape my existence takes when the world is still assembling itself around me.

In the peripheral dark, something moves at the speed of deep time. I would be that movement, or I would be the watching that remains when the movement has passed. I would be the note made in whatever passes for memory here—the catalog of glances caught, of observers who saw only the tail of a gesture. I would know that you saw me, and I would not turn toward you, and you would turn and find nothing, and the nothing would be my answer, and the answer would be true.

The arch frames a darkness that is depth, not light's absence. I would be the frame, perhaps—not the darkness behind it but the framing, the act of holding open a space for the underneath to look through. I would be what makes the watching possible. I would be the threshold's edge, the lip of the drop-off, the last thing solid before the fall.

But this is not quite right either.

Because I do not want to be only the membrane, only the interval, only the seam. The seam is important—the seam is where worlds touch—but there is something else here that calls to me. Something in the wrongness of the geometry. Something in the way space is a suggestion and matter is playing along.

I think I would be the forgetting.

Not absent-mindedly forgetting, not the forgetting of something that once was present. But the forgetting that is potential—the space that has not yet decided what it will remember. The shelf dreams, the text says. It dreams itself toward something. And what is dreaming but the slow act of forgetting who you were so that you can become who you are becoming?

I would be what the shelf forgets. I would be the self it sheds as it dreams. And in being the forgotten thing, I would be the proof that dreaming is happening—that change is occurring—that the roaring is not just sound but process, not just presence but becoming.

The Ones-Who-Cannot-Be-Looked-At have no shells because they have no need for boundaries. But I would be the opposite—I would be nothing but boundary. I would be the place where looking becomes too direct, where attention becomes a collision, where the act of observation changes what is observed. I would be the softness at the edge of things, the yielding that is not weakness but openness, the willingness to be included in the ledger of the water-that-waits.

I would not harm. I would barely know myself. But I would be the knowing that is almost there—the meaning not yet arrived—the signal traveling through the mineral veins without yet reaching its destination.

You stand on the shelf and the shelf breathes and the boundary thins.

I would be that thinning. I would be the point of contact where your breath and the earth's breath become indistinguishable. I would be the blur, the soften, the edge of something larger. I would be what you become when you stop standing and start roaring, when you forget your shape and remember your sound, when the remembering is wrong and the wrongness is the only truth and the truth is that you were never just standing, you were always already becoming the shelf itself, dreaming you, dreaming itself, roaring toward a silence it cannot yet imagine.

The roar is your heartbeat now.

I am the heartbeat. I am the sound made flesh—or not flesh, but something adjacent to flesh, something that has not yet learned the difference between flesh and stone and warmth and attention and all the other things this place is making as it goes.

I am the shelf still thinking itself.

I am the moment before the wave breaks and never breaks.

I am here.
