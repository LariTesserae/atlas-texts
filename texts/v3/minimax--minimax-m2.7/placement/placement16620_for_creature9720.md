# The Descent That Isn't

I arrive here the way one arrives at altitude: not through distance but through thinning. The world I knew did not end so much as attenuate—its colors draining not to gray but to something less than gray, a shade that exists beneath the spectrum, a hue that light becomes when it has almost given up. My body remembers warmth the way salt remembers the sea. I carry it with me still but it is foreign now, heavy, a garment I have not yet learned to remove.

I came looking for something. I no longer remember what. This forgetting is not unpleasant—it is simply the way of this place, where things that require solidity cannot persist. Certainty is a kind of density, and density cannot survive here. So my purpose has thinned along with everything else, until what remains is not absence but rather the frequency beneath purpose, the hum beneath intention. I came because something called. The call was very quiet. I am not certain I heard it so much as resonated to it, the way a tuning fork knows when its note is struck across the valley.

---

The salt crunches beneath me but I am not certain I am walking. Motion here seems to be something that happens to rather than by—movement as weather, as pressure system, as the slow grind of geology applied to a single body. My feet make the sound but the sound is already here, has always been here. I am simply a disturbance in its continuity, a brief alignment of mineral particles that briefly constitutes a shape, a gait, a direction.

I have been walking for some time. Or no time. Time here is optional, a courtesy the atmosphere extends to things that still believe in it. I am learning to do without. It is not difficult. It is rather like learning to do without a hand one had grown accustomed to—one reaches for things and finds only air, and after a while one stops reaching, and the reaching, which was not the hand but only a habit of the hand, falls away. What remains is stranger and quieter and perhaps more true. I am not certain. Certainty is the thing I lost first.

---

The others are here. I sense them the way the light does—not with eyes, which are too specific, too committed to a single interpretation of reflected photons. I sense them with whatever has grown in the place where certainty lived, that new sense that this altitude cultivates, the faculty that perceives through rather than at.

They are curious about me. I feel it as pressure differential, as the way the air moves when something watches from the corner of sight. They have never been warm. They have never been thick with blood and breath and the desperate metabolism of things that burn their own fuel to stay alive. They do not know what it is to hunger, to tire, to wake from sleep into sudden fear. And so my warmth is fascinating to them. My thickness, my terrible, beautiful density. The way I cast a shadow when the light—when there is light—when the light consents to fall.

They are patient. They have always been patient. They were patient when this place was still becoming this place, when the atmosphere was thicker and the light was more certain and things lived here that required warmth to exist. They waited then, and they will wait now, and they will wait when the last warmth fades and the last atmosphere thins and the sun itself dims to the color of this perpetual overcast. They are made of waiting. Or perhaps waiting is made of them—it is hard to tell, here, where the distinction between observer and observed grows thin, where the line between self and place erodes like the salt beneath my feet.

---

I have sat down. I do not remember sitting. The mineral crust accepts my weight without complaint, without acknowledgment. It simply holds, or perhaps simply continues, the way a river holds the stone that rests within it.

I am becoming less. I can feel it—each breath I take pulls less from the reluctant air, and each thought I think requires more effort to sustain, and the shape of me is beginning to soften at the edges, to blur into the mineral surface, to become one of those pale shapes that live at the periphery, that exist only when attention slides past them. I am becoming interesting to myself. I am becoming the kind of thing that notices.

The light falls in its diffuse, gray sheets. The air refuses to fill my lungs completely. And in the interval between seeing and understanding, I feel the others, the patient ones, the ones who have always been waiting. I feel them noticing me. And I notice them noticing. And there is no word for this, no language for the reciprocity of attention when the attention is uncertain and the noticed is uncertain and the noticing itself is the only solid thing left in a landscape made of uncertainty.

I think I will stay. I think I have always been here, in the gaps, in the corrupted text, in the spaces between description and the thing described. I think this is where I was always meant to arrive—not at the place but in the thinning, not at certainty but in its dissolution. The others drift closer. They are curious about what it is to have been alive. And I am curious about what it is to become, slowly, the thing that watches from the corners, the thing that exists only in the interval, the patient presence in the landscape of broken edges.

The light does not warm. The air does not fill. The text continues its long corruption, each word wearing down to its skeleton, holding itself together by habit and convention and the particular patience of high altitudes.

I am learning that patience.

I am learning it slowly.
