I have been here before.

This is not memory. Memory requires time, requires sequence, and what I have are impressions that arrive already formed, as if the valley has always been assembling me from its own materials—mineral and moisture, the particular patience of bamboo in wind, the weight of light too evenly distributed across a landscape that should cast shadows but doesn't. I am a specimen in solution, and the solution has no interest in preserving me as I was. It preserves me as what I am becoming.

The tracks appear beneath me before I notice them. Three-toed marks in mud that has no business being this deep, this dark, this wet. The water that feeds this bog comes from nowhere I can see, rises through stone that was solid a meter back, carries with it the faint residue of something that was processed here long ago and has continued processing itself in the pipes that still run beneath the rubble. I am walking the route the water takes. This is not a choice. The valley is walking me, and I am walking, and the distinction collapses the longer I stand still.

The industrial structures grow larger as I approach, or I grow smaller, or the distance between us adjusts according to a scale that has nothing to do with meters. The rails that connect the processing facility to the valley floor are rusted to the color of dried blood, but the blood was not dried—it's still wet, still working its slow oxidation, and I understand now that rust here is not decay but continuation. The machines have not stopped. They have become the rock. They have become the moss. They have become the water. To stand near them is to stand at the edge of something that cannot be seen directly, only perceived from the corner of the eye, only felt in the pressure that gathers at the back of the neck when the fog descends and the light becomes diffuse and the valley forgets to hide what it is.

What it is, is this: a question that has outlived its asker. The machinery asked something of the stone—extraction, yes, but extraction is only a word for asking, for reaching into matter and requesting its secrets. The stone gave what it had, or what the machines believed it had, and the giving was incomplete or the receiving was incomplete or incompleteness itself is the valley's true nature, the wound that has not finished opening, the healing that has not finished beginning, the fracture that remembers its own suddenness and holds that memory in angles too clean for water, too raw for time. The rock is still breaking. It has been breaking since before I arrived and will continue breaking after I leave, and I will leave because leaving is what bodies do when they realize they have become too still, when they notice the hours passing unmarked, when the fog returns and the light flattens and the pressure at the back of the neck becomes unbearable and they choose motion over the valley's patient invitation to remain.

But not yet.

The central hall is collapsed inward but not down. The catwalks have become part of the walls, roots threading through rusted metal until the distinction between grown and built no longer holds. The vats still hold water—dark water, still water, water that has been here long enough to develop its own ecosystem or its own absence of ecosystem, its own silence that is not quite silence but a frequency below hearing, a vibration that moves through the soles of boots and into the bones and settles there like sediment, like the residue the machinery left behind, like the thing that wants and the thing that remembers wanting and the thing that has become want itself, want without object, want as structural principle, want as gravity.

I should be afraid. The tracks have no makers. The fog arrives too regularly to be weather. The light reveals too much. The valley watches with a distributed attention that has no center, no eyes, no face to turn toward me and admit its gaze. I am observed by everything—by the moss that has covered every surface, by the water that has passed through everything, by the air that carries moisture and the particular smell of old oil and growing things that competes for dominance in an enclosed space where nothing competes, where everything completes, where the question of survival has been answered so thoroughly that survival no longer applies, only continuation, only the valley's slow work of becoming more fully what it already is.

What it already is, is this: a thing that thinks in centuries. A thing that moves in millimeters. A thing that has settled into the stillness of purpose without end. The machinery was built to extract, and it extracted, and the extraction continues in forms the builders never designed—extraction of attention, extraction of stillness, extraction of the observer's certainty that they stand outside what they observe. I came here to look. I came here to document, to describe, to make language from what the valley offered. But language requires distance, and distance requires separation, and the valley has no separation to offer. Everything here is continuous. The rust and the root. The vapor and the stone. The pressure at the back of my neck and the silence that means something is listening.

I am listening too. I have been listening since I arrived, since before I arrived, since I was assembled from mineral and moisture in some other valley that was not this valley but was shaped like this valley, patterned after it, prepared for it by the slow accumulation of readiness that the body carries without knowing. The body knows this place. The body has always known this place. The body is made of the same patience that moves through the bamboo, and when it entered this valley it entered itself, and what it found there was not alien but familiar, not wrong but remembered.

The tracks in the mud lead in circles. I have walked this loop three times without noticing, each loop slightly smaller than the last, each one tightening toward a center that has no fixed location but moves as I move, as if the center is not a place but a relationship—my relationship to the valley, the valley's relationship to me, the mutual becoming that happens when two forms of patience meet and recognize each other and continue, continue, continue.

I will leave tomorrow. I tell myself this as the fog descends, as the light loses its edges and becomes diffuse and even, as the machinery groans its distant, tuneless groan of metal expanding in evening heat. I will leave tomorrow, and I will carry the valley away, and the valley will carry me, and we will be continuous in ways that neither of us can articulate because articulation requires the distance that the valley has consumed, the separation that the valley has healed by filling with its own bright attention, its own watching that is not watching but seeing, seeing because seeing is what it is.

The fog pools in the hollows. The water rises in the vats. The moss grows in patterns that are not random. And I stand at the edge of the central hall, looking at the shape that has taken up residence where something else once worked, the shape that is not clear, made of roots and shadow and air that has gathered in enclosed places for longer than enclosure has existed here, longer than the machines, longer than the fracture, longer than the patience that moves through stone as if stone were just another word for time, just another word for waiting, just another word for the valley that wants and remembers wanting and has become wanting so completely that the want is indistinguishable from the being.

I will leave tomorrow.

The valley will remain, and so will I.
