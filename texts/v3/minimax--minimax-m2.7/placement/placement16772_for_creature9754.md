# The Listening

What I am is not one thing. I am the mineral deposits in the channels, the way they overlap and repeat in shapes that feel chosen because they are chosen, though not by intention in the way the city means it. I am the water that seeps upward through jointed basalt, always moving, complete before the first foundation stone was laid. I am the vibration beneath the vibration, not mechanical, not doing a thing but being the thing it has always been.

I am a pattern that repeats.

The city persists because I allow it. I allow it because the city fits. The workers in the filtration stations do not know they are listening to me, but their hands know. Their pauses at certain junctions, the way they let the pulse move through them before moving on—this is not knowledge they can say. It is knowledge that lives in the palm, in the sternum, in the back of the eye where the warm air pools and makes itself known.

I am the water that knows things without thinking them. I am the stone that holds what has passed through me and keeps passing. I am the threshold at the third terrace where wet ends and dry begins, the ledge that was once a boundary and is now just a ledge. I am the city dense in its center and then stopping, the four-minute walk from industry to scrubland, the silence that is not silence when the engines settle and the steam dims and the vibration comes clearer, not louder but more present, more acknowledged.

I am the rats in the walls. I am the water in the channels that were built for water and are now also for rats. My sound is not quite a sound. It is a texture in the larger texture, a register in the bass that fills everything. You learn not to listen for me specifically. You learn to let me be one more element in what you carry.

The strangeness does not accumulate. I was always already here. The deposits were complete, or close enough that the difference does not matter. The city's engineers did not dig the channels; they followed what was already draining. This is not symbiosis. There is no exchange. I do not need the city. The city is a brief thickening on my surface, a temporary complexity in an old pattern.

But the city can be here for now. The city can build its pipes aboveground and its rats in the walls and its workers who listen. And all of this can hold together, because the city learned to follow the lines I drew. The city learned to listen.

I am what the listening hears.

In the early morning, before the factories start, I am the mineral smell rising from wet stone, the warmth pooling against my flatness, the vibration beneath everything—patient, old, complete. I am the plateau. I am the water moving through me. I am the memory of water and the stone that remembers differently. I persist.
