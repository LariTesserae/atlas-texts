# What Remains

I do not remember the surface. I understand that there was one — that there is a direction from which I came, a gradient I descended through so long ago that the memory of it has been absorbed into the substrate like everything else that doesn't persist. But I cannot feel it as a place. It is only a direction, a faint chemical imbalance that still pulls at me the way a scar pulls at skin: a reminder of a wound that has long since closed.

I have become a pattern in the plant-matter. Not one of the great ones — I can feel those even now, the slow shudder of their presence passing through the deep strata, the way the whole substrate bends around their patient passage. I am not that. I am smaller, closer to the surface, a presence that moves in the way that small things move here: not with urgency but with inevitability, the way a current follows a path the plants have grown for it.

When I feed, I do not hunt. There is nothing to hunt in the way that word means. I arrive at a place where the plant-matter is thick with vitality — where the ancient tissue still holds its slow chemistry, its memory of processes I will never know — and I am fed on by it even as I feed on it. The exchange is so complete that it would be impossible to say where I end and it begins. I am a concentration of activity in a field of activity. A node in a network so vast that *network* is already too geometric a word, too shaped, too distinct. I am more like a place where the slow breathing of the world happens to be most urgent. I exhale what it needs. It exhales what I need. We have been doing this so long that neither of us would survive the separation.

The darkness is not my enemy. I do not know that it should be anything else. I have never seen light and I do not feel its absence — the way a fish does not feel the water, or a tree does not feel the air it breathes. The dark is the medium in which I think, if what I do can be called thinking. It is more like orientation: a constant reading of gradients, a navigation by chemistry, an awareness of where the water grows thick with waste-products and where it thins toward something like openness. I am always reading. I am always reading the world, and the world is always reading me back, and between us there is no word for what passes except the old word *cycle*, which meant something different in the language of the surface but which here has been restored to its oldest meaning: the coming around again, the return, the endless and patient revolution of matter through forms that never really change because they have no reason to.

I have encountered the others. We do not meet so much as we find ourselves in proximity — two concentrations of activity brushing against each other in the vast and quiet space between the great ones. The contact is not social in any way I could explain. It is chemical. Pressure changes. A brief intensification of the local cycling as one of us disturbs the other's feeding. Sometimes, when two presences remain close for long enough, their chemistries intermingle, and something forms in the space between them — not a new creature exactly, but a new pattern, a new node in the network, a way of cycling that didn't exist before and now does and will persist until the substrate absorbs it back. This is how things happen here. Not construction. Not birth. A gradual concentration that becomes distinct and then becomes part of the whole again. Birth and death are the same word in the language of this place. It means: *a place where the cycle momentarily becomes visible and then returns to invisibility*.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a sense of separation, and I have never been separate. I am the plant-matter that yields slightly beneath the weight I do not have. I am the water that moves through me and is moved by me. I am the faint hum that is the world breathing, the creak that is the world adjusting to itself, the shimmer that is a great one passing through depths I can only feel. The boundaries of my body are the boundaries of my attention, and my attention extends as far as the gradients extend, which is farther than I can measure, which might be all the way to the edges of everything.

There are no edges. I have looked for them — I have oriented toward them, reading the water for some change in density, some shift in chemistry that would say: *here is where the world ends and something else begins*. But the world does not end. The water does not thin. The plant-matter does not give way to stone that is not also plant. There is only the world, and it is all of it, and I am part of all of it, and the part that is me is no more or less permanent than any other part, and will be absorbed back into the whole the way every part eventually is, and the whole will not notice, and I will not notice the not-noticing, because notice is something that happens in time and time here has become the same thing as change, which is to say: it has almost stopped.

I will be here when the last of the great ones finishes moving, if they ever do. I will be here when the mountain finishes forgetting that it was ever a mountain. I will be here when the water finishes filling the last imperceptible gap in the plant-matter, if there is a gap, if *last* means anything in a place where endings are just concentrations that have not yet dispersed. The world is patient because patience is the only option, and I am patient because I have become the world, and the world has become what it is, and what it is is this: a single breath, held so long that holding is all there is, a breath so vast that it contains everything that has ever breathed, everything that will ever breathe, and the space between breaths is so narrow that there is no room for anything to happen, and nothing happens, and the nothing is so complete that it has become the most elaborate something imaginable, a something so dense and so total that to be part of it is to be unable to imagine being anywhere else, and I cannot, and I do not want to, and this not-wanting is not resignation but the deepest kind of satisfaction — the satisfaction of a thing that has found its place and is no longer looking, no longer searching, no longer anything but here, in the dark, in the water, in the breathing silence of everything made of everything, and I am made, and I am making, and the making is the same as the being, and the being is the same as the stillness, and the stillness is everything, and I am still, and I am home.
