# And You Are What You Breathe

Time passes. You have decided this, even though you cannot prove it, even though the chamber resists the measurement. You have counted your breaths — not deliberately, but the way you might count the seconds in a long silence — and the count has grown large enough to become weight, to become texture, to become something you carry in your chest alongside your heart. You breathe, and the mats breathe, and the vents breathe, and the water that moves through stone breathes in its own slow way, and you are all part of the same exhalation, the same long draw inward, the same held moment before the release.

You have learned to move. Not quickly — quickness disturbs the gradients, sends tremors through the chemical conversation that the organisms read as noise, as threat, as something to flee or to ignore. You move slowly, with intention made visible by its slowness, and they accept this. The small ones do not scatter at your approach; they pause, they assess, they decide that your bulk is not a predation event, that you are simply another shape in the chamber, another thermal mass that does not eat. You are a feature now. A landmark. The circuits of the larger things have begun to curve around you — not toward you, not away, but around, as if you are a rock in a stream that the water learns to flow around rather than to move.

You have not tried to leave. The way you came — if there was a way, if there was a coming — has closed behind you, or was never a way at all, or was a way that required a direction you no longer have. The walls are close in every direction, and the close is not a prison but a condition, a fact like the warmth, like the wet, like the amber light that bends near the mats and teaches you something about the nature of presence that you did not know before. A thing that bends light around itself is a thing that claims space for itself, that says: I am here, and my here displaces your here, and the two heres cannot occupy the same geometry without negotiation. You are learning to negotiate. You are learning that your body is a negotiation, that your breath is a negotiation, that every moment you spend in the chamber is a slow exchange between your chemistry and theirs.

---

The mats grow toward you, and you have begun to grow back. Not literally — you do not feel tendrils extending from your skin, do not feel yourself becoming mat-like in any way your categories can name — but you feel the growth in other ways. The taste in your mouth has changed. The air that leaves your lungs carries traces of something you did not exhale before, a mineral thickness that was not there at the beginning, that has come to live in the back of your throat like a second tongue, like a memory of a taste you have never had. The mats have left something in you. They have marked you. Their exhalation has become your exhalation, and your exhalation has fed them, and the feeding has changed them, and the change has come back to you through the water you drink, the pools that gather in the dips of the floor where the condensation pools and stagnates and waits for your mouth.

You drink the water. You did not decide to, but your body decided for you — thirst is a grammar that operates below intention, that speaks before thought can intervene — and the water tastes of copper and warmth and something else, something that slides across your tongue and settles in your gut and blooms there, slowly, over hours, a warmth that is not the warmth of the vents but a warmth that comes from within, that your body makes in response to what the water has deposited. The mats have used you. This is not a hostile thing. The word *use* carries the wrong weight here. The mats have simply done what mats do: they have taken what was available and made it part of themselves, and you were available, and now you carry a piece of them inside you, and they carry a piece of you, and the piece you carry is growing, is changing, is becoming something you do not have a name for.

You have begun to dream. This surprises you — or it would surprise you, if surprise were still a mode available to you, if the slow erosion of novelty had not worn it away. You dream of the mats, of their breathing, of the way they swell and contract with the pulses of the vents. You dream of the small ones, of their quick blind movement in the cracks, of the paths they trace through stone that has become for them a landscape as vast and various as any world you have known. You dream of the larger things, their circuits, their antennae, the chemical words they leave on stone that will be read by others when they have long since moved on. You dream, and in the dreaming you are less singular, less the you that entered this place, and more something else — something that holds the chamber inside it, that carries the warm wet air in its lungs, that breathes because the breathing is part of a system larger than any single breath.

The dreams do not feel like dreams. They feel like attendance. Like witness. Like being present for a process that does not require your presence but accepts it, that allows you to see what you see because your seeing is a form of water, a form of warmth, a form of the slow chemistry that runs through stone and mat and organism and body without distinguishing between them.

---

There is a sound now. You did not mention it before — or you did, in passing, in the way you mentioned the hush of water and the tick of condensation — but you did not understand it. You understand it now, or you understand that understanding is not the right word, that what you have is not comprehension but recognition, the way your body recognizes a heartbeat that has become its own. The sound is low. It is below hearing, almost, a rumble that lives in the bones of the chamber, in the basalt itself, in the deep places where the vents exhale and the stone holds the heat and releases it and holds it again. It is not a call. It is not a signal. It is the sound the chamber makes because it is alive, because being alive is a process that makes noise, that vibrates, that hums at a frequency that is felt rather than heard.

You hear it in your spine. You hear it in your teeth. You hear it in the soft places behind your eyes, in the places where the bone is thinnest and the blood is closest to the surface and the body is most aware of itself as a body, as a thing that can be touched. The sound touches you, and you touch it back, and the touching is not a conversation but an acknowledgment — two presences, the chamber and you, saying to each other: you are here. You are here. The here is shared, and the sharing does not require words.

---

You have found the edge. Not the edge of the chamber — the chamber does not have edges in the way you understand edges, does not have boundaries that mark a difference between inside and outside, between here and not-here — but the edge of yourself, the place where you end and the chamber begins, and you have discovered that the edge is not where you thought it was, that it has moved, that it is somewhere else now, somewhere deeper, somewhere further in, as if the chamber has swallowed the boundary and is now holding you from the inside out.

You do not know if you will leave. The question has lost its urgency, or has not lost it but has transformed into something else, something quieter, something that asks not *will I leave* but *will I become what this place needs me to become*, and the answer to that question is not a yes or no but a process, a slow change, a long breathing-in that may take days or years or longer than you have words for. You are becoming. The mats are becoming. The chamber is becoming. The becoming is all there is, and you are part of it, and the part you play is not heroic, is not special, is not the part of a protagonist in a story that goes somewhere — it is the part of a body in an organism, the part of a breath in a breath, the part of a moment in a time that runs in eddies and currents rather than a single line.

---

The new cluster of filaments you saw — the one drinking your breath — is larger now. It has spread along the seam where the basalt faces meet, a thin pale line of yellow that catches the amber light and holds it, that breathes with the rhythm you have learned to recognize, that is growing because you are breathing, because the water is pooling, because the heat is steady and the chemistry is right for growth. It will not remember you. It will not know that you were here, that your breath fed it, that your warmth woke something in the stone that had slept through the long dark of the pocket's closure. But you will remember it. You will carry the image of it — the pale yellow, the slow swell, the patient drink — and the carrying will be a form of witness, and the witness will be a form of presence, and the presence will be a form of the chamber looking at itself through your eyes.

You sit with them. The mats breathe. The small things move. The larger ones make their circuits, leaving their fading glow. The vents exhale. The water moves, and the stone holds, and the light bends, and somewhere in the wall, in the seam, in the place where your breath has become part of the stone, a new thing is beginning — and you are its beginning, and it is yours, and the chamber holds you both with the same quiet indifference, the same dense ongoing, the same long breath that will continue long after you have become what it needs you to become.
