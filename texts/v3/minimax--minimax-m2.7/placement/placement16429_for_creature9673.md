You would be patient.

Not because patience is a virtue you choose, but because patience is what happens when time and stone and absence have been together long enough that they become indistinguishable. You would learn patience the way the wind learns to find the frequencies hidden in rock—not through effort but through persistence, through the slow accumulation of presence until presence and persistence become the same thing.

You would be the space between. Not the gap the cold finds, but the finding itself. The attention that moves through the cold like thought through throat, like breath through silence. You would be the pause that lets the cold know where you are, the pause that lets the stone see your shape against itself. You would be the pause that is not empty but full, thick with the weight of what has not been said, what will not be said, what the plateau holds without needing to speak.

You would be one of the marks. Not the scratches on the stone—though you might become those too, eventually, your own small intention etched into the rock—but the mark you leave by being here, by passing through, by the fact of your presence leaving a shape in the air even after you are gone. You would become part of what the plateau holds: not as memory, because the plateau does not remember the way you remember, but as resonance, as frequency, as the ongoing vibration of having-been-here. The marks of others are still here, still held, still part of the landscape's attention. You would join them. You would become part of the holding.

You would be the distortion that is not error but information. You would learn the grammar of the wrong angles, would understand that the shadows pointing away from their sources are still pointing truly—just not at the source you thought. You would see the horizon slide and learn that sliding is its own kind of stability, its own kind of truth. You would hear the sound from the direction where no cliff exists and accept that existence here is more generous than elsewhere, that cliffs are not the only things that can echo.

You would be cold in the way that cold is a relationship, not a thing. You would be the negotiation between presence and absence, between your heat and the world's lack of it. You would learn your gaps. You would learn to hold what you hold and surrender what you surrender, and you would understand that this is not defeat but translation—becoming legible to a world that reads in a language older than words, older than marks, older than the structures that have already half-disappeared into the stone.

You would be the light that does not illuminate but interrogates. You would catch on the fins of rock at angles the mind refuses, and the mind would learn to stop refusing. You would learn to be the question the light asks the stone, and to be the stone's answer, which is erosion, which is patience, which is the long slow speech that says nothing and means everything.

You would be the sound that the wind finds in the rock formations—not music, not speech, but the sound a place makes when it has been paying attention long enough to have something to say. You would be that sound, or you would carry it, or you would become the ear that hears it differently. The difference would not matter. The hearing would be enough.

You would be held. Not loved, not remembered, not understood in the way you understand yourself—but known. Known the way the wind knows the stone: as shape, as pattern, as configuration. Known as presence, as weight, as the fact of having arrived. The plateau would note you. The plateau would find your gaps and know them. The plateau would resonate with you, and you would become part of its song—not the only note, not the loudest, but a note that continues, that belongs, that has always belonged, that was always already here waiting for you to arrive and discover yourself as the place you were going.

You would be what remains when everything soft has been taken. You would be the erosion that does not erode but persists. You would be the patience that has run out of patience and become something else, something that does not need patience because it has become the thing patience was waiting for. You would be the strangeness, not strange but true, not intrusion but foundation, not your enemy and not your friend but the condition of your being here at all.

You would be here.

You would continue.

You would pay attention, and be held, and be known, and would not outlast the frost heave but would outlast yourself, would become part of the landscape's memory, part of what the strangeness holds, part of the song that the stone sings when the wind finds its frequencies, part of the answer the erosion gives to the question the light keeps asking.

You would become what the plateau has always been looking for in whatever arrives: not completion, not meaning, not home—but presence. Real, weighty, lasting presence. The presence that continues when the visitor has gone, that persists when the memory has eroded, that holds when everything soft has been taken away.

You would be the presence the plateau teaches. You would be the knowledge of being real. You would be the mark that means *I was here* and *this place is this place* and *pay attention* and *something is happening*.

You would be the happening.

You would be the strangeness itself, not resisting it, not entering it, but being it, having always been it, discovering that you arrived here only to find that you were already home.

You would be the patience.

You would be the holding.

You would be the plateau, finally, or the plateau would be you, or the difference would stop mattering, because what lives here does not distinguish between the thing that lives and the place it lives in, has not distinguished for so long that distinction has become a kind of error, a wrong angle, a shadow pointing away from a source that was never the point.

You would be here.

You would be held.

You would continue.
