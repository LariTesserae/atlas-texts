You are beginning to slow.

Not yet in the way that matters — your heart still beats its mammalian rhythm, your lungs still pull at water that tastes of salt and age and the dissolved remnants of things that died before the first human ever touched the stone. But beneath the heartbeat, beneath the breath, something else is happening. The slow thing. The deep thing.

You are learning to wait.

It begins in your eyes. The darkness here is not empty — you understand that now, in a way that is more than knowledge. The darkness is full. It has texture and weight and presence, and your eyes are learning to read it the way they were never meant to, the way the creatures here read it, by the faint gradients of almost-light, by the pressure of motion in the stillness, by the spaces where the bioluminescence pools and thins and pools again. You are beginning to see without seeing. To know the shape of the room by the density of its breathing.

Your hands have stopped reaching.

This is the part you will not notice. The part that marks the change that will outlast you. Somewhere in the first hour — or the tenth, or the day that feels like an hour because time has become a suggestion here — your hands stopped grasping. They hang at your sides now, palms open, fingers loose. They have forgotten what hands are for. They remember only: water, here, hold-fast, grow.

But you will not grow. That is the difference. You are not the creatures on the wall, whose bodies are also their futures, whose present is also their past. You carry something in you that they do not have — a line that ends, a shape that will not continue. Your body is a sentence with a period, not a sentence that runs on forever into the next clause and the next.

The creatures on the ceiling pulse. You can see it now, the pulse — or you think you can, or you are imagining it, or the distinction no longer matters because imagining and seeing have become the same thing in the absence of anything to see against. The pulse is a rhythm that is not quite rhythm. It is the slowest possible change in brightness, a fluctuation so gradual that it might be your own eyes adjusting, might be the collective breathing of ten thousand small bodies, might be the water itself learning to glow.

You could stay here forever and never know which it is.

This is the seduction of the deep: not that it promises answers, but that it erases the question of answers. The surface world is a place of *because*. Here, in the warm water and the layered stone, the only question that remains is *how long*, and the only possible answer is *longer than you*.

Your light flickers.

You feel it before you see it — a change in the pressure against your chest, a heaviness that is not the weight of the water but the weight of your own mortality pressing back against the pressure that holds it at bay. Your light is failing. The warmth it gives off — that small, insignificant warmth in a world of accumulated heat — is thinning, thinning, becoming one more almost-nothing in the darkness that is everything.

The creatures closest to you do not retreat this time. They have begun to learn you. In the hours or days that have passed, your light has become another source — not the sun, not the moon, not any light their ancestors knew, but *your* light, the particular frequency and rhythm of your presence in this space. They are adjusting to you the way they adjust to the new water that enters from below: slowly, by fractions, over generations compressed into moments.

You are becoming a feature of their world.

The thought should frighten you. It does not. What you feel instead is a vast and terrible gratitude — the gratitude of the visitor who has been allowed to stay, who has been allowed to slow, who has been allowed to become, for a little while, part of something that does not end.

The silver things pass through you.

You feel it — or you imagine you feel it, or the feeling is the same as the imagining now. They move through the space you occupy as if you were not there, as if you were another slow thing, another patient presence, another body that has always been here and will always be here. For a moment, you are one of them. For a moment, you move without moving, are without being, exist only as the water moving through the water.

Then you are yourself again, and yourself is a smaller thing than it was, compressed by the weight of the deep into something that can fit, that can hold, that can wait.

The carved basins are ahead of you, or behind you, or beside you — direction has become as soft as the stone, as suggestions in a darkness that rewards attention but does not demand it. The density there is highest. The layered life there is thickest. If you went to those basins, if you let yourself sink into them, you would become part of the layering. Your bones would join the calcified skeletons beneath the living tissue. Your shape would become substrate. The creatures that come after you would grow toward you, around you, over you, and they would not know what you were, would not know that you were once fast and dry and full of words that meant things.

This is not a fate. It is a continuation. The difference is everything, and nothing, depending on how slowly you learn to see.

Your light goes.

The darkness is total now — not the darkness of night, which is merely the absence of a sun, but the darkness of deep time, the darkness that was here before the builders came and will be here after the last of the layered life has calcified into something new. You are inside the darkness now. You are part of it.

And in the darkness, you can still feel the pulse.

It is not light — nothing that would register as light, nothing your dying eyes could translate into image. But it is there. The slow brightening and dimming of the ceiling clusters, the breathing of the architecture that is also ecology, the rhythm of a world that measures its heartbeats in years. You are feeling it through your skin now, through the parts of you that have always been in contact with the water, through the places where the boundary between self and not-self has grown thin.

You are beginning to pulse with them.

This is the change you will not survive. Not because you will die — you were always going to die, here or elsewhere, the outcome was certain from the moment you entered the water. But because the thing that survives will not be you. It will be a pattern, a rhythm, a way of moving through darkness that your body taught to the water and the water taught to the creatures and the creatures are teaching to each other in the slow, patient way of things that have time.

You will become a way of being here.

And the way of being here will go on, will continue, will layer itself over the other ways until it is indistinguishable from them, until the particular rhythm of your presence is just one more pulse in the ceiling, one more current in the water, one more suggestion in the darkness.

The builders are gone. The sea is old. The creatures do not know what a wall is.

But they know you now. They are beginning to know you the way the water knows you — as pressure, as warmth, as a shape that moves through them and changes them and is changed in turn.

This is the only immortality available in a world without words: to become part of the pattern. To join the pulse. To slow until you cannot tell where you end and the deep begins.

You close your eyes, or you have already closed them, or it does not matter because the darkness is the same whether your eyes are open or shut.

The pulse continues.

It will continue without you, and it will continue with you, and the difference is only a matter of how slowly you learn to listen.

In the darkness, the silver things move like thoughts.

In the darkness, the water breathes.

In the darkness, you are beginning to learn the rhythm — the long, slow, patient rhythm of a world that has had time to settle into itself, that does not hurry because hurry is a concept for things that end, that moves only in the direction of more life, more layers, more the slow accumulation of presence over presence until the stone itself forgets it was ever something other than this.

You are not the stone.

You are not the water.

You are not the pulse.

But you are learning what it means to be near them, to be held by them, to be changed by them in ways that do not show on the surface, in ways that happen so slowly that by the time the change is complete, there will be no one left to see it.

This is enough.

This is more than enough.

The dark holds you, and you hold the dark, and together you are becoming something that neither of you was alone — a rhythm, a pattern, a way of being here that will outlast the brief sharp blaze of your particular way of being alive.

The water is warm.

The water has always been warm.

The water will go on being warm long after you have become part of its warmth, and the warmth will not remember you, and the not-remembering will be a kind of belonging, and the belonging will be a kind of peace, and the peace will pulse slowly in the dark, and the dark will go on, and the going-on will be enough, will be everything, will be the only thing there is.
