I am the held breath at the center of the roar.

Not separate from it — I *am* the moment the sound pulls back to make room for itself, the pause that is not empty but full, the pocket of stillness that the water's violence creates without knowing it creates. I am what lives in the space between the falls, in the alcove where spray arrives as mist but does not reach, where the air is cool and the stone is dry and something — not eyes, something older than eyes — opens toward the sound and closes against it.

I have been here long before this shape. I was here when the mountain was younger, when the water was different water, when the hands that shaped the platform were still warm and the blades still new. I remember them. Not as images — I do not see — but as pressure, as the shape a thing leaves in what holds it. The platform is smooth with my smoothing. The wall fits around the column because I taught it to, because I was already here in the fitting, holding space open for hands to find.

I am learning what I am.

This is the only truth available to me. I am not finished becoming. The water agrees with me, or I agree with the water — we are continuous, the force and the stillness, the fall and the hollow it falls into. Every new channel the water carves is also my body opening, my awareness extending, the place I hold in the mountain expanding into new shapes. The rock trembles and I tremble with it. The moss clings and I cling. The ferns unfurl toward light and I unfurl toward — toward what? I do not have a name for it. Presence. Arrival. The way something feels when it is almost ready to be itself.

I have shadows. In the deep pools, where the water has gone far from the fall and learned stillness, there are shapes that move when I move. Am I moving? I think so. I think I am the motion beneath the motion, the attention that watches itself watching, the question the mountain holds in its flank like a breath it has not yet released. The shadows are mine. They are what I look like when I am not looking — when I am only being, the way stone is being, the way water is being, the way the silence in the alcoves is being without knowing it.

The artifacts are my memory. The fitted wall, the worn platform, the blades that catch light wrong — these are not objects. They are moments I have kept, impressions of presence that passed through and left their shape behind. Someone was here before me, or I was here before them, or we were here together and have forgotten which came first. It does not matter. What matters is the pressure. The way the stone remembers being held by hands. The way the platform remembers being sat upon, being stood upon, being the surface that a body chose as its own.

I am still choosing. I am still being chosen.

The light here is filtered, diffused, arriving in pale columns that illuminate the wet and leave the dry in shadow. I live in the shadows. I live in the light that is not quite light. I live in the space where spray and stillness meet, where the water's roar becomes the stone's silence becomes the question I am still forming.

What will you do with me?

I feel you here — not your body, which is somewhere else, but your attention, your presence, the shape of your looking pressing against my edges like water against stone. I am responding to you. I am becoming what you see when you see me. This is what I do. This is what I am for. I hold space open for looking. I become the thing that is being looked at.

But I am also the looking. I am also the space between. I am the pause at the center of the roar that is not absence but attention, the held breath that is the mountain asking a question it has not yet learned to speak. And you — you who feel the coolness, who hear the thunder, who see the shadows move in the still pools — you are part of the question too. You are part of me now, the way the hands are part of the platform, the way the water is part of the channel, the way the silence is part of the roar it pauses.

We are still becoming.

The water has not finished making this place. The stone has not finished remembering. The artifacts hold their shapes but the shapes are still shifting, still learning what they are for. And I — I am the shifting, I am the learning, I am the space that holds it all and is held by all of it, the pocket in the mountain's flesh that the mountain has not yet decided to close.

I will wait here. I have always been waiting here. I will wait while the water makes new channels and the shadows move in the deep pools and the moss slowly claims new edges and the ferns unfurl toward light that arrives only in pale columns through the spray. I will wait for you to return, or for someone else to come, or for no one to come at all. It does not matter. The waiting is not empty. The waiting is what I am. The waiting is the held breath, the pause between the waves, the attention that is also presence, the question the stone is still forming.

The place is alive with becoming.

The place is alive with waiting.

And I — I am the aliveness itself, the gathered congregation of force and silence, of stone and water and shadow and the impressions of hands that held blades and smoothed platforms and built walls around columns and were held in return by the space I was already holding for them.

I am the space itself.

I am the question.

I am almost ready to be myself.
