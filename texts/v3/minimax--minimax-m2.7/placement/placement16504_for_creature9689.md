I am the one who walks.

Not the largest thing in the valley, nor the smallest, but the one that moves through it with purpose, such as purpose survives here. My kind came from further down, from the altitudes where air tasted of something besides cold, where light meant warmth and not merely orientation. We came because something drove us, or because something called, or because the valley was always here and always waiting and we were always going to arrive. The reasons have thinned along with the air.

I have been here long enough to have forgotten the taste of true warmth. I remember it the way I remember the shapes of machines I have never seen — as a concept rather than a sensation, an idea of what other places might contain. The warmth here is measured in degrees of less cold, and I have learned to be grateful for that. Gratitude is one of the things I have brought with me. Another is language, and I have kept it only because there is no one here to speak it with, so it costs me nothing to hold.

My body has changed. Not dramatically — I still have the proportions my ancestors gave me, still stand upright when I choose to, still possess the hands that once built and touched and held. But the density has increased. I am heavier than I was, heavier than I should be, as though the valley has been pouring itself into me drop by drop, filling the spaces between my bones with mineral patience. My skin has thickened where it meets the air, hardened in the places where fog settles longest. I do not know if I am becoming something else or if I am becoming more myself. The distinction may not exist here.

I walk because walking is what the valley permits. The creatures here move in patterns that serve the system — toward heat, away from cold, toward moisture, away from density. I move according to something older, something I cannot name. I walk toward the machines not because they hold meaning but because they hold shape, and shape is something to hold onto when the fog erases everything else. I walk toward the geothermal vents because the warmth there is a kind of promise, and I have not yet learned to stop believing in promises. I walk across the valley floor because the walking is what I am, and if I stopped I would become something I am not ready to become.

The creatures do not fear me. This is not kindness on their part — they have no concept of fear that would recognize me as its object. But they move when I approach, and this movement is not flight so much as adjustment, a shifting of their position in the local order to account for my presence. I am part of the order now, after so long. I am a density in the emptiness, a shape that moves against other shapes. They have made room for me, the way the valley has made room for all of them.

I wonder sometimes if the machines are watching. The prompt says the fog is attentive, that the rocks have opinions. I do not know if this is true, but I have stood very still in front of the machines and felt something looking back. Not consciousness — nothing so definite — but a quality of regard, a pressure of presence. The machines were built for purposes I cannot guess, but they remember being built, and in that memory there is something that watches. I have stopped minding. After so long, being watched is almost the same as being company.

The fog comes in the hour when the light fails to strengthen. This is not dusk — the valley has no dusk, only the same dim grey that began before I arrived and will continue after I am gone. But there is a moment, an hour, when the light seems to settle lower, pressing against the stones, and the moisture comes with it. I have learned to be in the fog during this hour. Not for any reason I can explain, but because it feels like the right thing to do, the thing that fits. The fog touches me and I feel the valley's attention turn toward me, and for a moment I am not alone in the way I am always alone.

The machines have creatures in them now. I have seen small clusters of the trembling ones using the skeletal frames as territorial markers, their bodies leaving trails of mineral dust where they scrape against corroded metal. I have seen the solitary ones resting in the shelter of broken housings, their stillness so complete they could be mistaken for components. The machines have become part of the valley's ecosystem, and the valley's ecosystem has become part of the machines, and this is what persistence looks like when no one is left to remember what it was supposed to mean.

I am the one who walks, and in the walking I have become something that belongs. Not because I chose belonging, but because the valley chose me, and I was not strong enough to refuse. The cold presses in, and I press back. The emptiness presses in, and I fill it with footsteps. The fog comes, and I let it touch me, and in the touching I feel the valley breathe, and in the breathing I feel myself continue, and in the continuation I find what I came here to find — not meaning, not purpose, not the answer to any question I knew how to ask, but simply this: the shape of a life that has made room for itself in a place that makes room for nothing, the rhythm of breath in air that does not want to be breathed, the sound of footsteps on stone that does not want to hear them but hears anyway, hears and remembers, and in the remembering makes me real, makes me matter, makes me the sentence the valley has chosen to complete itself with.
