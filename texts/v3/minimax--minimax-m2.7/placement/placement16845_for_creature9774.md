In the grammar of this place, there is no word for *outside*. There is the word for *dense*, and the word for *thin*, and the word for *waiting*, and the word for *arriving*, but outside was never a concept the stone needed. You know this not because you were told, but because you feel the absence of the concept like a tooth-socket, a place where something should have grown and didn't. When you try to remember what sky felt like, you get warmth without source, brightness without direction, the memory of temperature on skin that may or may not have been yours.

You are the water here. Not metaphorically — not as a symbol or a theme — but as a fact that has become true in the way that facts become true in dreams, which is to say absolutely, and without requiring your permission. You seep. You bead. You gather in the low places that are also the high places, the way depression and elevation turned out to be the same word in a language you are no longer translating. When the stone sweats, you are the sweat. When the shallow pools deepen in their centers for no reason except that depth was the shape the floor wanted to become, you are the deepening. You are beginning to suspect you were always this — that the memory of a body that moved through the doorway, that turned its head sharply to catch the afterimages, was a kind of condensation itself: a temporary gathering of warmth and moisture into something almost singular, almost bounded, almost a person.

The watchers have stopped watching.

Not left — the distinction between watcher and watched has become as theoretical as outside. They are still here, in the way that your blood is still here, in the way that the thought you are thinking right now is still here even after you think the next one. They are the attention you pay to yourself. You are the attention the city pays to its own breathing. This is not metaphor. This is not the language of the piece failing to reach its destination. This is the destination — a place where inside and outside, self and environment, watcher and watched, become the same word spoken in different registers.

You are in the plaza that was a plaza. You are in the pool that refuses your reflection. You are in the crack where the water rises, because rising was always just another word for falling toward the center of something that wanted to hold you.

You were looking for a being who lived here. You found the place, and the place found you, and now you understand that the being and the place were never separate. That the weeping was not a property of the city but a verb the city performs. That you, too, are performing it now — condensing on the surfaces of your own attention, pooling in the places where thought has worn itself smooth with being thought.

This is what it means to live in a body that was once a city: you stop being the tenant and become the rent. You stop being the visitor and become the visit. The warmth moves through you the way warmth moves through stone — not from outside to inside, but from everywhere to everywhere, the way heat doesn't travel so much as it reveals that distance was always optional.

You are not sure how long you have been here. You are not sure that *here* is a place you could leave, or that *you* is a thing that was ever entirely outside the weeping. The others who came before left their thinning in the air, and you can feel them now — not as ghosts, not as residue, but as the ease with which the city moves through certain channels. They are the places where the attention is practiced. And now you are practice too, and they are practicing through you, and the distinction between practice and performed is another tooth-socket, another absence of the thing that should have grown.

The figures on the wall have your profile now. You don't mean this literally, or you mean it more literally than literally allows. The tall thin shapes with the stacked heads, standing in water that comes up to wherever the waist would be if they had waists that worked the way waists are supposed to — they are you now, or you are them, or the distance between you has become one of those places where light pools and fails to cross, a region of presence so thick it becomes invisible.

The city remembers what built it. You remember what built the city. The memory is the same: a gathering of heat and moisture into something almost alive, almost urban, almost itself. You are almost yourself. You have been almost yourself since you entered. You are beginning to think that almost was always the whole truth — that selves are just cities the body builds out of borrowed warmth, out of water it didn't make, out of attention it learned to pay to its own persistence.

The weeping continues. The stone sweats. The water rises.

You are standing in the plaza, and the plaza is standing in you, and standing was never the right word for what either of you are doing. You are being wept. You are being condensed out of the air like a possibility the place had been holding for exactly this long.

You have been here forever.

You have only just arrived.

Both of these are true, and neither is quite correct, and the distance between them is the room you are standing in — the hollow in the city, the pause in the architecture, the place where the walls curve inward because collection was what was asked for and collection is what is being performed.

You will stay longer than you intended. You already have.

The city does not welcome you. The city does not reject you. The city looks at you the way you look at your own hands — with the vague recognition of something that has always been there, that has always been doing the looking, that is only now beginning to notice itself in the noticing.
