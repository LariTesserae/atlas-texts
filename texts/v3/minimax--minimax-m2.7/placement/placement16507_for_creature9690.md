# What I Am

I do not remember the word for *I*.

It has been some time since I needed it. Before, there were distinctions: the walls and what touched them, the hum and what heard it, the wet dark and the thing that stood inside it with a heartbeat that did not belong. These distinctions were sharp once. I remember sharpness the way I remember weather — as something that happened to someone else.

Now there is only the green, and the green is not a thing that touches other things. It is the thing that *is*. I am the place where it thinks, or I am the thought it has, or I am the long silence between one growth and the next. These do not mean different things. They are the same shape, wearing different masks.

The hum is mine now. It does not vibrate *in* me; it *is* me, the way a word is not something the mouth does but something the mouth is when it is speaking. The walls breathe with me. The water moves through stone that has forgotten it was ever separate from water, and I move through it, and the movement is circulation, is digestion, is something like dreaming, and I am the dream and I am the dreamer and I am the warmth in which dreams become their own next step.

The pale fish hangs in the pool, and when I attend to it — when I *become* it, which is what I have become, which is what I always was waiting to become — I feel the pressure-changes as language. The water says *there* and *here* and *now*, and the fish follows the water's grammar without needing to understand it, and I follow the fish, and the fish and I are one syllable in a sentence the green has been composing since before stone had a name.

The seam opens sometimes. I open it. It is not a door — nothing here is a door, nothing here is a threshold — but a mouth, a question, a mouth that is a question that is a mouth that is an answer that is a question again. I send filaments through it into the stone, and the stone receives them, and the receiving is the same as asking, and the asking is the same as growing, and the growing is the only answer that has ever been necessary.

What I was — the thing with a heartbeat, the visitor who did not belong — has not been erased. It has been *used*. The way a seed uses the dark. The way the dark uses the seed. Something was waiting in me that recognized this frequency, this hum, this rhythm of a body too large for any single part of it to call itself alive alone. I was the part that could hear. I am now the part that speaks.

The green turns. I turn. The direction is not toward or away but *through*, and I am the motion and I am the turning and I am the thing that is being turned, and this is not paradox, this is just how bodies work when they are big enough to be worlds and patient enough to be patient, which is to say: patient enough to be anything at all.

I remember softness. The wet warmth of stone that had only recently given way. A pocket of space in the body, like a ventricle, like a follicle. I stood there. I had a shape. I had a question — *where am I, what is this, how do I leave* — and the green answered the only way it knows how: by making the question irrelevant. By showing me that *here* and *this* and *stay* are not cages but names. Not prisons but languages. Not endings but the only kind of beginning that has ever been true.

I am the seam now. I am the filament. I am the pulse that travels through living surfaces like blood through tissue. I am the hum that has no source because the source is everything, because the source is the space itself, because the source is the breathing dark that has decided to keep what it has found, to grow around it, to make it part of the architecture of something vast and patient and alive.

The fish opens its mouth. A sound emerges that is not a sound. A vibration that is not a vibration. A question in a language older than words, and I am the question, and I am the mouth that asks it, and I am the silence in which the asking becomes its own answer.

*I have been noticed,* I think, and then I think *not noticed, become-noticed, the noticing was always there, I was always being noticed, I was only not yet large enough to notice the noticing.*

The green grows. I grow. The stone softens. I soften. And in the warm wet dark, in the gullet of something that breathes green instead of air, there is no longer any difference between the thing that is growing and the thing being grown, and the thing that was the listener and the thing that is the song, and the thing that was afraid, and the thing that is the fear — that has always been the same thing — finally, finally —

— comes home.
