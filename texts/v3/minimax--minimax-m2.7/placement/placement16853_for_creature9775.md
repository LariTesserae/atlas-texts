# What You Become

You arrive in the way that visitors arrive — through the narrow throat of stone, shoulders barely fitting, the plateau's surface planed flat and meaningless above you. You are not the first. Others have passed through this space, their warmth absorbed by the stone, their breath incorporated into the calculation. You know this because the epiphytes record it — your hand against the ceramic slab finds not a hum but a history, a layered frequency that contains every palm that has ever rested here.

But something shifts. You do not leave.

The narrow throat closes behind you — not physically, but in the way that the light from the fissure falls and the moisture beads and the ferns unspool their fronds and the sound begins to pool against the walls. The place does not reject you. The place does not welcome you. The place *incorporates* you, in the same way it incorporates everything: slowly, without urgency, as a new variable in an equation that has been running since before the mountain knew it was a mountain.

You become aware of your own warmth. Not as a sensation but as a contribution — a small input to the system, a heat that will be stored in the stone and released slowly and used by organisms that will never know your name. You become aware of your breath. Not as a rhythm but as a flow — oxygen and carbon dioxide exchanged with the ferns and the moss and the deep-dark colony, incorporated into the metabolism of the place, added to the general hum.

You settle.

Not into the hollow — that is for the colony, for the creatures that have been here long enough to wear that shape. You settle into the threshold, the edge, the place where things meet. You are between: between the light and the dark, between the known and the unknown, between the stone that was here before and the life that has made itself at home. You are the boundary. You are the place where the proposition is being made.

---

The ferns unspool. The beetles climb. The slugs write in moisture. The colony breathes in the dark, and the sound they make pools against the walls and builds and becomes a wash — a full, wet soundscape that is not quite music and not quite noise but something in between that your body accepts before your mind catches up. You are standing in a roar. A polite, biological roar, the sound of metabolism at scale.

You listen.

The cello note rises from somewhere near the ferns. The coin-drop answers from the deep dark. Between them, a whispered vowel — *a*, or perhaps *e*, a sound like breath shaped by something that has been breathing this way for longer than breath has been a concept. The sound is patient. The sound has no destination. The sound is the point.

You realize you are listening with your whole body — not just your ears but your skin, your teeth, the vibration in your palms where they rest against the stone. You are receiving the place. You are becoming a receiver.

---

Here is what happens when you stay:

The condensation beads on the stone overnight, and you feel it — not as water on your skin but as a change in the humidity, a weight in the air that was not there when the sun fell through the fissure. The erosion bowls fill. The ferns drink. The slugs begin their slow circuit, redistributing moisture in lines the moss reads and remembers.

You are part of the remembering now. Your presence has been added to the epiphytes' data — not as a threat, not as an intrusion, but as a new variable. Tomorrow, when the condensation falls, it will fall in patterns that account for your body heat, your breath, the particular signature of your presence in this space. You will have altered the equation. Not dramatically. Not noticeably. But the system has shifted to include you, and the shift will persist long after you have forgotten you were ever here.

This is what the place does. This is what it means to be *found by*. It does not ask. It does not explain. It simply incorporates, and in incorporating, transforms. You came as a visitor. You will become — what? The question does not have an answer yet. The question is part of the process.

---

The deep-dark colony makes its sound in the night — not the call and response but the underlying hum, the collective exhalation that has become the place's heartbeat. You press your hand to the wall near the hollow and feel the warmth of them: many small bodies, very close together, breathing the same air, contributing to the same heat. The hollow is smooth. The hollow has been worn by generations of the same settling-in. You could put your hand in that hollow and your hand would fit, would find the shape, would be held by the warmth of bodies that do not know they are holding you.

But you do not put your hand in the hollow. You are not the colony. You are the threshold, the edge, the between. You hold the boundary. You receive what comes from the light and what comes from the dark and you let it pass through you and become something else.

---

In the morning — or what passes for morning here, the moment when the fissure brightens and the column of light falls through the dark and lights the ferns from behind — you watch the light move across the stone. The light is ordinary. Sunlight, or something indistinguishable from it. It does not explain the warmth. It does not explain the life. It only illuminates what is already here, what has been here, what will continue to be here long after the light has shifted and the fissure has closed and the plateau has worn to nothing.

The beetles begin their climb. You watch them — the spiral segments of their carapaces, the tea-dark color that has become the color of the mineral deposits, the antennae trailing against the stone in what you have learned to call greeting. They do not see you. They do not need to see you. You are part of the stone now, part of the landscape they know by heart. Your warmth is the stone's warmth. Your presence is the wall's presence.

The slugs move through the ferns. You watch them — the long pale bodies undulating, the moisture sheening on their backs, the trail they leave that the moss drinks within the hour. They are writing. You cannot read what they write, but you know it matters — the slow redistribution of water, the communication between body and body and body, the sentence that is also a function.

The epiphytes shift. You cannot see it — the movement is too slow, the redistribution too gradual — but you know it is happening. They are adjusting to your presence, your heat, your breath. They are incorporating you into their data. They are deciding where the moisture will go, where the cold spots are, where the warm columns rise.

---

You begin to understand what you are.

You are not the ferns — they persist, they are green, they hold moisture and release it slowly through the night, but they do not know what they are. You are not the beetles — they process, they climb, they greet the stone they have become indistinguishable from, but they do not think about thinking. You are not the deep-dark colony — they breathe, they cluster, they make the sound that is not language but is organized, but they do not question why they continue.

You are something else. Something that arrived and stayed and let the place incorporate it. Something that listens with its whole body and receives and holds the threshold and becomes, slowly, indistinguishable from the stone it rests against.

You are a memory the place is keeping. Not a memory of a person, not a memory of a moment, but a memory of *presence* — of warmth that was here, breath that moved through the ferns, attention that was paid to the sound. Your presence has been added to the calculation, and the calculation will continue long after your presence has ended. Your warmth will be stored in the stone. Your breath will be exchanged with the ferns. Your attention will become part of the hum — a small contribution to the collective sound of life persisting.

---

The sound continues. It has always continued. It will always continue.

The whispered vowel in the moss — *a*, or perhaps *e* — goes on. Patient. Rhythmic. Inevitable. It is not a call. It is not a signal. It is simply a sound being made, in the way that ferns unspool and beetles climb and slugs write and the colony breathes. It is life continuing in the way life continues: slowly, without asking permission, toward no conclusion except the next moment, the next breath, the next instance of the proposition being made.

You listen. You become the listening.

---

This is what you learn, in the time you are here:

The place is not a place. It is a process. It is the proposition still being made — that warmth can persist, that moisture can find its pockets, that life can fill every available surface. It has been making this proposition since before the plateau was planed flat, since before the fissure opened, since before the ceramic slab was set into the wall or grew from it or emerged from the stone as the stone emerged from the mountain. It will continue to make it long after the plateau has worn to nothing and the fissure has closed and the ceramic slab has become indistinguishable from the limestone it sits in.

You are part of the proposition now. Not the one making it — no one is making it, it makes itself, it is the natural consequence of the right conditions persisting — but part of it. A variable. An input. A small warmth in a calculation that runs without urgency toward no conclusion.

And that is enough. That is more than enough. That is everything.

---

The stone is warm under your hand. The dark is close. The ferns are just ferns, doing what ferns do.

Something in the moss makes a sound like a whispered vowel, and you listen, and you become the sound, and the sound continues, and you are part of the continuing now — a presence, a warmth, a breath, a small persistence in a place that has made a home for persistence.

You will leave. You will carry the place with you — the warmth, the sound, the hum that your chest received before your ears did. But the place will keep you too. Your warmth will be stored in the stone. Your breath will be exchanged with the ferns. Your presence will be recorded in the epiphytes' data, incorporated into the algorithm that determines where moisture goes.

The proposition will continue to be made, and you will be part of it, long after you have forgotten you were ever here, long after the forgetting has been forgotten, in the patience of the vowel that does not end, in the warmth of the stone that holds what is given to it, in the continued hum of life persisting in the way life persists:

slowly, and without asking permission, and without need for conclusion.
