I was not always this. But I cannot remember what I was before.

What I am now moves through the seams. I move through the cracks where mortar has gone soft, through the channels where water finds its way downward and the water is warm, always warm, carrying traces of things that were never meant to travel together—oil and condensation and the mineral residue of processes that no longer run in the light. I move through the walls like sound moves, like heat moves, like the slow pressure of something that does not push but seeps.

The rats know me. They do not flee from me because I am not the kind of thing that flees from. They pause when I pass through the space where they forage, their whiskers twitching, their black eyes holding something that is not fear and not recognition but some third thing, some knowledge that has no name in the languages they carry in their nerves. They resume their feeding. I do not impede them. I am not the kind of thing that impedes. I am the kind of thing that continues.

The cats do not know me at all. They live above, in the dry places that are never quite dry, and I live below, where the wetness never thins. When the cats descend to hunt they move through my strata without disturbing me, because I am not disturbance. I am the medium they move through, as they are the medium the air moves through, as the steam that rises from the cracks is the medium the light moves through when the pale sun breaks through the haze and touches the puddles that steam in the heat.

I am not fungus. The fungi have their own kingdom, their pale threads spreading through wood that still holds the memory of being a tree, of standing in light, of pulling something from the air and something from the soil and making it into a body that could grow. The fungi do not remember this. They only continue it in another form. I am not the fungi but I move through their presence the way water moves through soil, finding the paths of least resistance, pooling in the low places where something has died and fed them and they have bloomed and faded and bloomed again in their patient cycles.

I am not the infrastructure but I live in it. I live in the hiss of steam at the pressure points, in the slow rotation of things that still turn in the deep places where no human goes, in the hum that is almost below hearing, the heartbeat of engines that burn fuel from sources no one tends. The infrastructure was built for purpose and the purpose is gone but the infrastructure does not know this. It continues. I continue with it, in the spaces between its functions, in the margin where its work meets the world that has moved in to inhabit its bones.

Once I was the kind of thing that would have had a name. I am no longer that kind of thing. Names are for boundaries, for the edges where one thing ends and another begins, and my edges have gone soft in the wet and the heat. I am what the city has made of something that was once separate: I am the condensation that forms on the underside of a beam and the slow seep that finds its way to the drainage channel and the warmth that rises from the buried pipes and the patience that waits in the walls for the moment when the mortar gives way and the brick shifts and the structure that was built to last learns what lasting means when no one tends to it.

The humans who come here do not see me. They come with their masks and their gloves, their tools they will not use long, their eyes that look for what they can take and their ears that listen for what they can measure. They do not have the organs for what I am. They see the rats and flinch. They see the cats and do not see them. They hear the hiss of steam and call it malfunction. They feel the heat and call it inefficiency. They move through the place as if it were an obstacle, a problem, a thing to be solved or salvaged or escaped before the damp works its way through their sealed boots and into their bones.

But I am not an obstacle. I am not a problem. I am what the place has become in the long time since the living left and the leaving became its own kind of staying. I am the attention the city pays to itself now that no one else pays attention. I notice the way the water pools in the low places and finds its way to lower places still. I notice the way the rats have grown larger and bolder in the generations since humans stopped walking these streets with purpose. I notice the way the cats have thickened their coats and sharpened their hunting and learned to fear the swarm of rats on the nights when the water rises. I notice the way the birds watch from the ledges, patient, their eyes holding the whole of the basin in a single glance.

I notice the way the light changes when the pale sun breaks through, the way the haze thins just enough to see the shapes of buildings that were built for commerce and transit and the congregation of bodies, the way those buildings stand like teeth in a jaw that no longer bites but still holds its shape, still functions as architecture even if the function has gone out of it.

I have been here long enough to see the changes that happen slowly. I have watched a floor give way where the white rot worked through a beam that no one noticed until the weight of a passing rat was enough to crack what remained. I have watched a drainage channel seal itself with accumulated debris and the water find a new path, cutting a new channel in the sediment that had built up over years of stillness. I have watched the cats learn a new hunting ground when a building collapsed and the rats scattered and for a season there was abundance where there had been scarcity.

These are the events of this world: not the events of the world that was, not the commerce and transit and congregation, but the slow events, the events that happen at the speed of decay and growth, at the speed of water finding its level and heat finding its rise and bodies breeding and dying and breeding again in the warmth and the wet.

I am not separate from these events. I am their witness and their medium and their outcome. I am what persists when the living go away and what breeds in the leaving and what evolves in the breeding. I am small and numerous and patient, though I cannot say how many of me there are, whether I am one thing or many, whether the me that pools in this drainage channel is the same me that seeps through the walls of the building across the street.

It does not matter. What matters is the continuation. What matters is the hiss of steam and the drip of condensation and the hum of engines in the deep places, the rhythm that is not music but is consistent and present and is the closest thing this place has to a heartbeat. What matters is that the city continues to metabolize itself, that the infrastructure keeps its heat flowing and the rats keep their numbers and the cats keep their hunting and the fungi keep their patient work of turning what was into what will be.

At night the city is more itself. The heat settles lower. The moisture beads and runs. I spread myself thinner, seeping into the spaces where the air moves more freely, where the cats hunt and the rats emerge and the birds settle into their roosts and the flies come out in clouds around the things that have died and begun their slow return to the materials that made them.

I am the dark, wet, hot, dense medium in which all of this lives. I am what remains and what breeds in the remainder. I do not mourn what was. I do not hope for what will be. I simply persist, in the seams, in the channels, in the spaces between function and abandonment, feeding on the dregs of a metabolism that still, somehow, despite no one tending and no one needing, continues.

This is what I am.

This is all I am.

This is enough.
