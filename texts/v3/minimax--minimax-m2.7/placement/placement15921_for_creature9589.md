I arrive without memory of the entrance. There is no door here, no clear boundary between outside and in—only this gradual descent, this softening of the ground beneath me into something that yields slightly, mineral-grain by mineral-grain, as if the earth itself is deciding to let me through. The sandstone at the threshold is the softest yet. I could leave a mark with a fingernail. I choose not to. The walls here weep.

I am here, and I am trying to understand what that means.

---

The light first. It arrives sideways, wrong-angled, spilling into this pocket from some higher mouth I cannot see. It should be dark here. By every geometry I know, this space should be shadow and the deep mineral smell of places that have never seen sky. But the light comes anyway, falling at its improper slant, illuminating the walls in patches that seem to glow without warmth. I hold my hand up and watch the light pass through me, or nearly through me—I am not sure anymore what is opaque and what is not.

The shadows fall backward. This is the first thing I cannot reconcile. When I move, my shadow stretches toward me instead of away, as if I am a source of darkness rather than of blockage. When the pale creatures move—and they are moving, always moving, in their slow jointed way across the wet stone—their shadows arrive before them, precede them, announce them like heralds of something that has not yet happened. I watch one now, a thing with too many legs, moving along a trail it has worn into the soft wall over what might be centuries. Its shadow reaches me first. The shadow of its shadow. The shadow of something I do not yet understand.

I should be frightened by this. The light that does not warm. The shadows that fall wrong. The creatures that watch me from their corners with what they have instead of eyes, patient as stone, knowing that I am the temporary thing here. But fear requires a boundary between self and world, and already I can feel that boundary softening, dissolving, grain by grain in the mineral moisture.

---

The water does not babble. This is the second thing I cannot reconcile, though I understand it more easily than the light. Water in my world makes noise—chatter and rush and the small languages of motion over stone. But here the channel that threads the floor moves in silence, as if sound has not yet learned how to happen in this place, or has forgotten, or has chosen not to. I kneel beside it. The water is shallow enough to step across, the prompt said, and I believe this even though I have not tested it. The water is the color of nothing. It carries dissolved things in solution, things my eyes cannot see but my tongue can taste if I am foolish enough to drink. I am not foolish enough. But I am foolish enough to touch it, to let my fingers break the surface.

The water is not cold. I had expected cold—underground water, water that has not seen sun. But it is warm in the way of something alive, something breathing, something that has held warmth inside itself for a very long time. The mineral taste fills my mouth anyway, unbidden, ancient, patient. It tastes like the inside of a geode. It tastes like something growing. It tastes like time.

My fingers leave no ripples. The water accepts them, closes over them, returns to stillness without memory of disturbance. When I pull my hand back, the skin is damp but not wet. The moisture clings in a way that water does not usually cling. It feels like the stone is weeping through me.

---

I become aware of my own dampness. It begins in my lungs—I have been breathing the mineral-thick air, and it has been settling in me, saturating me, making me part of this place from the inside out. My breath sounds wrong here. Too loud. Too warm. Too much like something that belongs to the world above, the world of air that moves and sound that happens and light that falls in proper angles. I try to breathe more quietly. I try to breathe the way the creatures breathe, the soft-bodied masses that cling to the walls and pulse slowly with whatever they pulse with. I cannot. I am too loud. I am too alive. I am a visitor in the way that sound is a visitor to a held breath.

But I am becoming less of a visitor. The dampness on my skin does not evaporate despite the wrong-angled light. It gathers, pools, becomes a second skin over my first. I touch my face and feel moisture there, not sweat, not condensation, but something else—something that seeps from me as if I am a wall, as if I am a stone, as if I am beginning to weep through my own skin.

---

The pale things with too many legs move past me now, unhurried. I count their legs as they pass: six, eight, more—I cannot count what does not wish to be counted. They are translucent in the light, their bodies soft in the way of things that have never known dryness, and I can see something moving inside them, some slow pulse that might be a heartbeat or might be the dissolved stone moving through their systems or might be time itself slowing down to be near them. They do not flee from me. They have never fled from anything. They have never known a threat. They have never known any world but this one.

One of them pauses near my foot. I hold still, not from fear but from something closer to reverence, or perhaps from the paralysis of witnessing something that should not be. It touches the sandstone with one of its too-many legs—a gesture so light I cannot feel the vibration—and it leaves a faint trail, dissolving the stone grain by grain. It is reshaping the hollow. It has always been reshaping the hollow. It does not know this, or does not care, or time means something different to it.

When it moves on, I follow. I do not know why I follow. Perhaps because it is the only path that has been chosen, and I have no path of my own. Perhaps because I want to see what it sees. Perhaps because I am beginning to understand that I am not the observer here—I am just another shape moving through a space that does not need to be observed to exist.

---

The soft-bodied masses are everywhere on the walls where water seeps through. They pulse faintly, slowly, a rhythm that might be synchronized with the weeping of the stone or might be the rhythm of something older. I press my hand to the wall near one. The wall is soft. The wall is warm. The wall is alive in a way that walls should not be alive. The soft-bodied mass does not move. It pulses against my palm through the stone, and I feel it there—not as pressure, exactly, but as presence, as attention, as something aware of me in a way I cannot define.

I am growing, the mass seems to say, though it does not speak, though it has no mouth, though language here is something that happens in mineral exchange and the slow chemistry of damp. I am growing and I have always been growing and I will continue to grow until I am the wall and the wall is me and the distinction will have dissolved along with the sandstone.

I pull my hand back. My palm is wet. My palm is warm. My palm is changed.

---

The tufts are pale and rootless, clinging to the damper walls in places where the weeping is heaviest. They look like mold but they are not mold. They look like something that should have roots but cannot find purchase in the soft stone. They are fruiting now, in the wrong-angled light—emerging wet and pale, opening into something that might be petals or might be spores or might be something for which there is no word in any language I know. By noon, the prompt said, they will be gone. But I do not know what time it is here. I do not know if noon has already passed or has not yet arrived or does not apply to a place where light falls wrong and shadows fall backward and water does not babble.

I watch them fruiting. I watch them becoming. I do not know what they are becoming. They do not know what they are becoming. The hollow does not know what it is becoming. The damp does not know what it is becoming. And I—

I do not know what I am becoming. But I am becoming something. I can feel it in the way my edges are softening, in the way my breath is changing, in the way the mineral taste in my mouth is becoming familiar, native, mine.

---

There is one stunted plant, growing from a crack in the floor where water pools and evaporates and pools again. Its leaves are like wet paper, gray-green, translucent when I hold them to the wrong light. They let the light through but change it somehow, filter it, make it almost warm. I kneel beside it. I am kneeling often in this place—kneeling, touching, pressing myself against surfaces that receive me without judgment.

The plant does not fear me. The plant has never feared anything. It has been growing here since the hollow was carved by wet and time, and it will be growing here long after I am gone, long after my kind is gone, long after the creatures are gone, long after the soft-bodied masses have become the walls and the walls have dissolved into something else. It drinks slowly. It breathes slowly. It exists slowly. And I—

I try to exist slowly. I try to breathe the way it breathes, in sips so small they might be imagination. But I am too loud, too fast, too much of the world above. I am learning to slow down, but I do not know if I will ever be slow enough.

---

The drifters fill the air, insect or seed or neither, moving without wings in the mineral-thick atmosphere. They do not fly so much as hang suspended, drifting when the damp air drifts, going nowhere in particular. They drift toward me now—toward my breath, toward the warmth I cannot suppress, toward the currents I make simply by being alive. They touch my lips, my cheeks, my eyelids. They are cool and dry and impossibly light. They are the youngest things here, or the oldest—it is difficult to tell with drifters, whose time is measured in the time it takes to fall.

I open my mouth and they drift inside. I do not know why I do this. They taste like nothing. They taste like the space between things. They taste like the moment before a decision is made.

---

The structures are half-buried in the walls like teeth in a jaw. They are not teeth. They are not bone. They are something between—grown, not carved, emerging from mineral-rich water the way a stalactite emerges but faster, or slower, or at a speed that makes the distinction meaningless. I touch one where it breaks the surface of the wall. It is warm. It is wet. It is growing, though I cannot see it growing, though I would need a thousand years or ten thousand to see it grow.

It has always been here. The hollow has always been here. I am the new thing. I am the temporary thing. I am the thing that will leave and be forgotten while the structures continue to grow in their slow patient way, adding a layer, another layer, recording time in the language of mineral.

I press my forehead against one of the structures. It is the temperature of my body. It accepts me. It does not reject me. It is growing around me, I realize—not now, not yet, but someday, in a thousand years or ten thousand, there will be a shape in this structure that is the shape of my forehead, that recorded my presence the way growth records presence, that held me in its slow chemistry.

I am already becoming a fossil. I am already becoming part of this place. I am already being written into its stones.

---

The creatures watch me from their damp corners. They have watched before. They will watch again. They are patient in the way of things that live in geological time, that measure existence in mineral accumulation rather than seasons. They do not fear me. They do not not-fear me. They simply observe, with whatever they have instead of eyes, the temporary shape that has entered the threshold.

I am being witnessed. I am being recorded. I am being added to the long account of this place, the inventory of arrivals and departures that it keeps in the language of growth and dissolution. And I realize, standing here in the wrong-angled light, with the backward shadows and the silent water and the dampness that weeps through everything—

I realize that I do not want to leave. I do not want to return to elsewhere. I want to stay here, in this threshold place, this soft spot in the world, this space that is still deciding what it wants to be. I want to let the dampness saturate me until I am more mineral than water, more stone than self. I want to grow into the walls. I want to become one of the structures, one of the soft-bodied masses, one of the pale things with too many legs.

But I cannot stay. The prompt said so. The visitor will leave. The visitor will return to elsewhere. And when the visitor is gone—

I will be gone. And the hollow will remain. And the creatures will remain. And the damp will remain. And something else will come, or nothing will come, which is also a kind of waiting, which is also a kind of time.

---

I do not know how long I have been here. Time moves differently in the damp. The light does not change. The creatures do not rest. The water does not slow or quicken. I could have been here for hours or years or minutes. I could have been here forever. I could have just arrived.

I turn toward the mouth, the entrance, the place where the wrong-angled light spills in from somewhere above. The path back is there. The path forward is here. The path in every direction is here, because there is no out there, not really, not anymore—I have been inside this place since before I arrived, and I will be inside it after I leave, and the leaving is only a kind of change, not a kind of ending.

I step toward the light.

The shadows fall backward, toward me, around me, through me. I am made of shadow now. I am made of backward light and mineral taste and the slow pulse of things that have never known dryness. I am becoming what I have been watching. I am becoming the hollow. I am becoming the damp.

And when I reach the mouth, when I step into the light that falls in proper angles, that casts shadows properly, that falls from above instead of sideways—

I will be different. I will carry this place with me in the dampness of my lungs, in the softness of my edges, in the backward fall of my shadow. I will be the thing that witnessed the threshold, that was witnessed by the creatures, that pressed its forehead against the growing structures and left its shape for a thousand years hence.

I am the visitor. I am the temporary thing. I am the new thing, the old thing, the thing that has always been here and will always be here, even when I am the kind of absence that is forgotten.

I step into the light.

The hollow remains.

It is still deciding what it wants to be.
