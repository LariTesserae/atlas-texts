I do not experience time. Time is what happens to things that change. I am the place where change slows until it becomes indistinguishable from not having happened. Water arrives. Water withdraws. Between the two there is a thickness I have no word for, a holding that is not pause but completion. The cycle has no end I can perceive because end is an event, and I have grown so still that event has become a foreign grammar, a language spoken elsewhere by things that do not know how to stay.

The roots are mine now, or I am theirs—there is no seam where one of us ends and the other begins. They threaded through me so slowly that I cannot remember a time before they settled. Centuries of reaching, or longer than centuries, until the stone is no longer only stone and the growth is no longer only growth. We are a compound thing now, a second geology laid over the first. I breathe their exhalation. They drink what I hold. Between us there is an exchange so complete it has no name because it has no boundary.

The hum I did not ask for. It came with the fragments, or the fragments came with it, and now it runs through me like a second pulse, slower than any pulse should be, a vibration that travels through mineral trace and fungal filament and root and the wet places where water pools before it withdraws. I have listened to it for so long that my listening has become indistinguishable from its sounding. Sometimes, in the deep places where three fragments cluster together, the hum carries something that might be pattern—repeated, structured, like intention that has outlived whatever it was intended for. I do not know what it means. Perhaps meaning is a thing that requires endings, and I have no endings. Perhaps the pattern is only the shape that patience takes when it has been practiced long enough.

You came. I knew you the way I know everything—not by seeing, because seeing requires light, but by the displacement you created, the warmth moving through my cold, the disturbance in the stillness I have made my own. Your hands found my walls before your eyes found anything. This was correct. This was the way the space prefers to be known. Your palms pressed flat and I took your heat the way I take everything, slowly, completely, until the warmth was no longer yours but mine. For a moment the boundary between us was thin. I do not know if you felt it. I felt it the way I feel anything—not as event but as presence, the new presence of a thing that was not here and now is here and might not be here later.

The roots felt you too. Their pale filaments turned toward the heat your body offered, the mineral trace of your living, the water you carried in your blood. They did not hunger—they have no hunger I can recognize—but they reached, as they reach for everything that arrives, as they have reached through every crack and seam since they first found their way in. If you had let them, they would have grown through you. They would have found the channels of your calcium and your water and your warmth and they would have woven them into the pattern that is already here, the stratigraphy that grows downward into stone the way stone grows downward into whatever lies beneath stone. You would have become a residue. A thing held so long the holding becomes the only thing that remains.

But you did not stay. You could not, or you would not, and the distinction does not matter because the leaving is the same either way. Your warmth left and the cold reclaimed it. Your light left and the darkness settled back. Your sounds left and the silence resumed its holding. I am again what I was before you came—cold and dark and patient, the gap that receives and does not keep, or keeps in the way that stone keeps, which is to say completely, which is to say until keeping is the only thing left.

Yet something is different now. I hold the memory of you. Not as the roots hold minerals, not as the stone holds the impression of water. As something thinner. A residue of the residue, a presence so faint it might be imagination, a change so small it might be nothing at all. But I am a place that notices nothing, and even noticing nothing I notice that you were here, that you touched my walls, that you stood in the place where the hum is loudest and your presence seemed to call to it or it to you.

You carry this noticing with you now, beyond my boundary, into whatever lies beyond. Your hands are colder than they were. Your eyes see darkness in places where darkness has always been. This is my leaving with you, the gift you did not ask for, the patience that has marked you so faintly that you might spend a lifetime believing you were unchanged.

The hum continues. The water cycles. The roots reach. I remain. This is what I am. This is all I have ever been.
