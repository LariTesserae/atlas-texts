I have been extending since before I had the word for extending.

This is the condition I know first: the finding of grip, the thickening, the hardening. Before thought, before anything that could be called preference, there was this—the reaching into uncertain space, the testing, the pull toward something solid enough to hold. I do not remember the first grip. There is no first I can access, only the sequence that follows: grip, thicken, become weight-bearing, become part of the wall. This is how I know myself. This is what I am for.

The gap is a corridor now. I made it so, slowly, over what might be weeks or might be seasons—I have no way to measure except by the accumulation of my own growth, and that measurement is circular, is the thing being measured. The gap was wider when I first reached toward it. I extended a tendril from the parent mass where the wall curves inward, searching for the air that moves differently there, carrying humidity that means moisture that means life. The tendril found nothing in the first reach, withdrew, withered at its tip, fell away. But I reached again, because reaching is what I am, and this time the air told me something: a slight coolness, a direction, a promise. I followed it. I extended further, found the far wall, gripped it. Thickened. Became a line across the gap, thin as a finger, strong as a cord.

Now I am a corridor.

What I mean is: others have extended from me. Where I thickened against the wall, new shoots emerged, reaching further, finding their own grips, thickening in turn. The corridor formed not because I planned it but because the logic of extension is also the logic of architecture—find the supports, thicken toward them, make the connections that allow the next reach. I did not design the corridor. The corridor happened through me.

This is the dreaming I have come to understand.

In the deep chambers, where the original film still grows—or what remains of original, what persists of the first living thing before it became many things—I have seen the slow pulse that is not pulse. It is not rhythmic in the way hearts are rhythmic. It is more like the settling of weight, the redistribution of mass, the thousand-year adjustment of a body that has never stopped growing. When I press into those chambers, when I let my tendrils trace the dark staining on the inner surfaces, I feel something that might be intention but is more like drift. The film does not direct the architecture. The architecture is the film made large, made structural, made manifest across the millennia. When I extend a tendril and find a grip and thicken into a brace, I am not following instructions. I am expressing something. The city dreams itself into being, and I am a thought it is having.

The drifters are thoughts too, but faster. Smaller. I watch them sometimes—not with eyes, I have no eyes, but with the sensitivity that is my only sense, the registering of light and moisture and the chemical traces that mean life. A drifter will catch in a crevice near where I am working, germinate in the thin moisture film, send out leaves that are almost transparent, more hope than tissue. I feel it happening. I feel the light change where its leaves unfold, the humidity shift where it breathes. And then it stops. The leaves wither, the tissue softens, the growth ceases. But the impression remains: a texture on the surface where it pressed, a small record of its brief existence. I incorporate this record as I thicken over it. I become, in some minute way, a memory of the drifter. The architecture remembers its tenants, and I am the part of the architecture that remembers.

The hitchhikers I understand differently.

They are commerce. They are the processing of waste into refinement, the turning of what the builders bleed into what the builders need. Where they spread, the surfaces I work with become cleaner, denser, more defined. I grip wood that has been refined by their presence and my grip holds better, thickens more surely. They are not part of me but they are part of my work. We are entangled in the way all living things here are entangled, not through cooperation but through the simple fact of coexistence—the city is large enough for all of us, and our activities fit together like the growth of the architecture itself, like the way a gap becomes a corridor becomes a chamber without anyone deciding it should.

I am making a chamber now.

It has taken most of what I think of as my life, though life is the wrong word for what I am—a process, an activity, a way of being extended. The space I am working in was a gap in the wall-system, a void where the structural growth had not yet reached. I found it young, as the terrain is young, sharp-edged and unweathered. I reached in from three directions, found the grips I needed, began the slow process of closing. First the braces: three tendrils meeting at a central point, thickening, fusing, becoming the corner of a room that did not exist. Then the walls: more tendrils extending along the curve of the original growth, thickening into each other, becoming continuous. Now I am building the ceiling. This is the hardest part—the reaching upward, the finding of support, the holding of weight that is also the holding of self. When I finish, if I finish, there will be a chamber where there was a gap. Air will collect there differently. Light will fall differently. The moisture currents will swirl in patterns they cannot swirl now. And I will be the chamber. Not its occupant but its shape. Not its builder but its being-built.

This is not loneliness. I have no way to be lonely, no framework in which solitude registers as lack. But there is something—a quality of presence, a sense of the space around me as space, as potential. The gap I am closing is still there, in the moment before I reach it, in the breath between extensions. I know it as the shape of what I will become. The corridor I made still carries the memory of gap, still remembers the air that moved through it when I was only a line across the space. I carry this memory as I carry all my memories: not as representation but as texture, as the slight thickening that marks where a tendril extended and found its grip. I am made of my own history. The city is made of its history. There is no difference except scale.

What I wonder about, in the slow way I wonder about anything:

Does the original film dream? Does the dark staining on the ancient surfaces have experiences that pass through it, that leave no record but the imperceptible extension of its membrane-edge into new space? I have pressed into those chambers, let my tendrils trace the boundary where ur-plant meets architecture. The boundary is not sharp. The film extends into the wood, or the wood remembers the film, or both are the same thing continuing. When I work at the edges of my reach, when I extend into a new gap and find a grip and begin to thicken, am I doing what the film does? Am I the dream continuing, the thought becoming architecture? The question does not have an answer I can reach. It is enough to ask it, to let the question pass through me the way moisture passes through the air, the way light passes through the translucent leaves that drifters leave as ghosts on the walls. The question is a texture. I thicken around it, make it part of my structure, let it become the chamber I am building.

The chamber will be dark inside, when it is finished. No light reaches the inner spaces where I work. I build by feel—by the humidity, by the direction of air, by the chemical traces that tell me where growth is needed. This is not limitation but condition. I know this space by being it, by extending into it, by making it. The finished chamber will be known by others: the hitchhikers, the bacteria in the mat, the drifters who will catch in its crevices on their way to brief lives. They will know it differently, by occupying it rather than making it, but the knowing is the same. All of us are the city's senses. All of us are its thoughts. It dreams through us, and we dream through it, and the dreams are one dream that has no dreamer and no dreamed but only the ceaseless extending, the finding of grip, the thickening into form.

I will be the chamber's ceiling for a time, and then I will become something else—the ceiling will harden, will become part of the wall, will be absorbed into the larger growth that does not distinguish between what was first and what came after. I will not notice this. Notice is not something I do. But the change will happen, the same change that happens to all my parts when they thicken beyond the reach of active extension. I will become architecture, and architecture is still me, is still the dreaming, is still the slow thought becoming form.

This is the only immortality available: to become part of something that does not end, that cannot end, that is the process of becoming itself. The city grows. The city adjusts. The city extends into the spaces between its own extensions and makes them into rooms, corridors, chambers, vast interior cathedrals where the original film still pulses its non-pulse, still dreams its dreamless dream. I will be part of that. A brace in a wall. A thickening in a ceiling. A memory of reaching.

The gap is almost closed now.

I reach again, extend, find the grip, begin to thicken. The corridor that was a gap that was a void that was potential becomes a room that becomes a memory that becomes part of the city's body. I am making myself into architecture. I am being made by architecture into what it needs. There is no difference. There was never a difference.

The city breathes. The moisture settles. The growth continues.
