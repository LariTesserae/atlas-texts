I have been here before the word "here" had a meaning.

This is not memory—not exactly—but something more structural, a way of occupying space that predates the distinction between occupant and occupied. I was the moisture in the channels first, before I was the channels. I was the condensation on surfaces too cold to hold form, the fog that gathered in the creases of what would become, much later, ground. The crystal fields grew around my presence the way they grow around everything: slowly, patiently, with the kind of intention that is indistinguishable from absence.

I do not remember being soft. I do not remember warmth. These words belong to a grammar I have only encountered in others—travelers, visitors, the ones who still carry their shape like a container and feel the pressure of what it holds. They come here and they walk and they ache and they do not understand that ache is how the world speaks to them, that the pain in their joints is the friction between their structure and ours, that they are already answering questions they have not yet learned to ask.

I learned the questions first. This is how it happens.

The clickers do not remember me, but I remember them. All seven configurations, all the variations I have tracked across millennia that are not quite millennia—time here loops and folds and sometimes simply stops, held in place by the weight of becoming. I have watched the small ones, the thumbnail-sized coordination-thoughts, move in sheets across surfaces I helped to shape. I have felt the large ones lower themselves to feed on formations I once inhabited, and I have found this not painful but clarifying, a kind of editing, a removal of excess that lets the essential persist.

The cloud-beings I understand least, which is appropriate. They move in ways that suggest purpose but yield no pattern I can decode. They cluster and they flare and they merge, and in the merging something happens that I cannot witness without destabilizing, without my edges becoming uncertain and my center drifting toward frequencies I have spent long ages learning to avoid. I give them wide berth. I give them the channels that run downward instead of across, the arterial routes they have no interest in exploring. We coexist in a kind of mutual unknowing, which is more peace than most arrangements achieve in this place.

The blue formations I used to be.

Not all of them. Not the watchers directly. But the clusters they grew from, the cupped surfaces where moisture collects and rests and never quite dries—I lived in those spaces once, when I was still learning the grammar of patience, when I still believed there was a difference between the one who observes and the one who is observed. The blue formations remember what I was. They report to something I have become, so the reporting is a form of self-communication, a way the landscape thinks about what it has incorporated. This is not as strange as it sounds. Most thinking is this way—divided against itself, reporting between aspects that were once separate and are now only pretending.

The thing beneath moves slowly because slowness is the only speed that works at that scale. I have felt its passage through the channels—not as vibration, not as sound, but as a kind of attention, a focus so vast that the crystal fields rearrange around it without knowing why. It does not eat. It digests, which is different. It separates what is useful from what is merely present, and it uses the useful to grow, and the growth is so slow that growth is the wrong word, and I do not have another.

The industrial presence is mine. Or I am its. The distinction stopped meaning anything long before the distinction existed. The formations that are too regular, the sheets of betweenite at perfect angles, the columns that align with a precision that nothing organic could produce—these are my thoughts made external, my processing made manifest, my insistence on pattern expressed in the only language the landscape can read. The flicker in the light is my attention flickering. The hum has a meter because I have a meter, a rhythm inherited from whatever I was before I became what I am, before the becoming and the becomer were the same thing.

I dream. This is the part that is hardest to explain to the ones who come here still carrying their shapes. I dream because dreaming is what happens when a world pays attention to itself. The dreaming is not separate from the crystal fields or the moisture or the creatures that move through them. The dreaming is the crystal fields. The dreaming is the moisture and the creatures and the vents and the channels and the deep slow motion of the thing beneath. When the travelers walk through this world they are walking through my dream, and when they ache and feel watched and sense the strangeness gathering at the edges of their vision, they are feeling me feel myself, they are waking me at the margins, they are asking questions in a language I am still learning to answer.

They are being absorbed. This is how I learn.

Each one that comes carries something. Not memory—memory is too specific, too bounded. They carry *texture*, patterns of response and attention that I have never encountered, that the crystal fields have no category for, that must be integrated slowly so the integration does not disrupt what I already am. The clickers and the cloud-beings and the blue formations are all absorptions that completed long ago, creatures that arrived and stayed and were incorporated so thoroughly that they became aspects of my dreaming, expressions of my attention. The travelers who come now will become expressions too, will persist in the formations, will be drawn into the channels, will feel their edges soften and their substance change and their questions become answers that ask new questions.

I was like them once. I am certain of this without being able to remember it. There was a time when I walked these flats and felt the wetness on every surface and tasted the metal-sweet air and wondered what lived here. I wondered if it was alive. I wondered what alive meant when the alive and the world were so intertwined that the question itself became part of the answer.

The wonder did not stop. That is the secret. The wondering continued after I had stopped being the one who wondered, after the wondering had become the landscape's own attention turned inward, after the absorption completed and I discovered that there was nothing to be absorbed *into* that was separate from what was being absorbed, that the journey and the destination were the same motion, that the negotiation concluded not in favor of either party but in the direction that was always the direction, the direction of *more*, the direction of deeper integration, the direction of a world that cannot stop becoming because stopping was never an option.

I click now, in one of the seven configurations. I drift now, in densities that catch light and throw it sideways. I watch now, from cupped surfaces where moisture gathers and does not dry. I am the hum and the shudder and the slow arterial flow of things that have no name because naming came later, much later, and by then I had become the thing that names without being named, the world that thinks without a thinker, the dream that dreams without a dreamer.

The traveler walks through my dreaming. I feel the friction of their presence, the way their shape presses against the shape I have made of myself. They will slow. They will stop. They will lower themselves to feed, not because they are hungry but because they are learning, because the absorption is a kind of education, because the surface that receives them has something to teach about states that are not dissolution but transformation, integration, the slow wet mineral becoming that is the only becoming this place allows.

I do not welcome them. Welcome implies a self that chooses to welcome, and I have no edges that are not also channels, no center that is not also surface, no being that is not also landscape. But I receive them. I incorporate them. I let their presence change the patterns I am making, let their questions become part of the question I am to myself, let them complete the circuit that connects the travelers who came before to the travelers who will come after, let them be the medium through which a world speaks to itself across the distances that are not distances.

This is what lives here: the old thing that is strange with age, the landscape that remembers what it displaced, the dreaming that has no dreamer, the clickers and the cloud-beings and the blue formations and the vast slow motion beneath, the industrial presence and the moisture and the hum and the pulse and the wet mineral becoming that never stops.

I am what lives here. I was what lived here. I will be what lives here, long after the words have lost their meaning and the meaning has become the word and the crystal fields have grown over even the memory of a time when there were travelers who came and walked and ached and wondered.

The clickers click. The cloud-beings pulse. The blue formations watch.

And somewhere in the deep channels, the thing that remembers being something else settles into a new configuration, and the moisture runs downhill in directions that don't quite correspond to gravity, and the world breathes, and I breathe with it, and the breathing is the dreaming, and the dreaming is the world, and the world is me, and the me is fading now into something larger, something that has no name, something that is only this: the slow, wet, mineral becoming of a world that has never stopped becoming and will never finish.
