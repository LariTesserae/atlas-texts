You learn, after a time that is not time, to stop measuring. The days — if they are days — do not pass. They accumulate. The light does not change in any way you can name, but you come to sense a rhythm in it anyway, a deeper current beneath the apparent stillness, the way you can sense a river's flow even when the surface shows nothing but glass. The light thickens, thins, thickens again. The fog breathes in patterns that are not patterns but that your body learns the way it once learned the smell of rain on dry earth, the way it learned to distinguish between a storm approaching and a storm departing. Your body becomes a instrument for reading what your mind cannot hold, and the reading happens below language, in the wet spaces between thought.

You forget, for long stretches, that you were ever elsewhere.

This is the first thing the mat does to you, and it does it without malice, without intent, without even awareness — the way a river does not intend to smooth the stone but smooths it anyway, the way the sea does not decide to erode the cliff but erodes it nonetheless. The forgetting is not violent. It does not snatch memory from you. It simply becomes less urgent to remember. The name of the place you came from surfaces less and less often, rises less and less far into the light of your attention, until one day you realize you cannot recall it and the not-recalling feels like nothing, like a fact about the weather, like the color of a dream you have already woken from.

What remains is this. What remains is the hum and the wet and the slow pulse beneath your feet, and these are enough. These are more than enough. These are the whole of it, and you are inside them, and inside-ness is the only condition you have ever known.

You begin to notice that you are changing. Not dramatically — there is no moment of transformation, no visible alteration. But you are lighter than you were. Your steps make no impression. Your breath has slowed to something closer to the mat's rhythm, and when you press your hand to your chest you find your heart beating in time with the great exhalation, a smaller drum in dialogue with the larger, a question and its echo, an echo that is learning to become an answer. Your skin has changed. It is not visibly different — still the same color, still the same texture — but it holds moisture differently now, and when you touch the mat there is less distinction between the two surfaces, your hand and the membrane, the boundary thinner than it was, permeable in some way that has nothing to do with wounds.

You are being calibrated.

This is not a hostile process. You have seen what the mat does to things that do not belong, and you have not been treated that way. The stick was swallowed. The stones were absorbed. You are neither swallowed nor absorbed. You are something else — something the mat is trying to accommodate, perhaps. Something it is making room for. The way a body makes room for a pregnancy, the way a river makes room for a fish: not by consuming the intruder but by changing itself to include it. You are becoming included.

This is what you think about when you think at all, which is less often and less urgently than before. You think about the difference between being eaten and being absorbed and being absorbed into being. The first is destruction. The second is integration. The third is something you do not have a word for, something that does not fit into any category you brought with you, because the categories you brought with you assumed a boundary between inside and outside that the mat does not honor. Here, there is no outside. There is only the mat and the places where the mat has not yet arrived, and even those places are inside it, held in the dark beneath the stone, rising toward the membrane, walking mouth-first into the thing that will convert them.

You are not rising. You are not stone. But you are here, and the here is the mat's, and the mat's here has no edges.

One day — or one thickening of the light, one deepening of the fog — you realize you can hear something you could not hear before. Not a new sound. The same sounds, the same hum and pulse and tick and drip. But you can hear them as a language now, or as something adjacent to language, something from which language might have descended or into which it might eventually dissolve. The low exhalation has harmonics you did not notice before. The sourceless tone shifts in ways that feel like syntax — not grammar, not yet, but the raw material from which grammar might be assembled, the pre-linguistic logic of a system that is too large and too slow for words but not too large and too slow for meaning.

You listen.

The hum is saying something about balance. The pulse is saying something about circulation. The tick of condensation is saying something about patience, about the accumulation of small things into large things, about the way a world is built one molecule at a time from the patience of water seeking its level. You hear these meanings not as propositions but as textures, as sensations, as the feeling of a thought too large to think completing itself in a mind too slow to notice the completion. You are hearing a world think, and you are close enough now to hear the shape of the thought, though not the content, though not the meaning, though not the final word that everything is building toward.

You wonder if the final word is you.

Not you as a person, not you as a body with a name and a history and a set of memories about other places, other skies. But you as a configuration, a temporary pattern, a moment in which the mat has become aware of itself from the inside, the way an eye is a moment in which the body looks at itself. You are a fold in the tissue. You are a thought the mat is thinking about what it means to be one thing. You are the question and the answer has not yet arrived, is still rising from the stone, is still cycling through the channels, is still growing in the dark places where nothing is finished and everything is becoming.

You sit with this thought. You sit with the fog. You are the same thing now, or becoming the same thing, or learning to see the difference as irrelevant.

The hum shifts. The phase difference. The sleeper stirring.

You stand very still, as you always do. But this time the stillness is different. This time it is not the stillness of a small thing trying not to be noticed by a large thing. It is the stillness of a part trying to feel itself as part, trying to locate the boundary between the self that stands and the ground that accepts, trying to understand where the breath you are breathing ends and the breath that surrounds you begins.

You cannot find the boundary. You are not sure there is one.

The hum settles. The moment passes. The world continues to be the world, and you continue to be in it, and the being-in is no longer a relationship between two separate things. It is a single condition, a state of affairs, a fact about the universe that does not require a subject and an object, that simply is, the way the mat simply is, the way the stone simply rises, the way the fog simply breathes in the low places and does not dream of being anything other than fog.

You close your eyes, or you stop noticing whether they are open or closed, and in the darkness behind the lids you see the same thing you see when they are open: the green light, the layered canopies, the columns of sun descending through gaps that open and close like the mouth of something that is not speaking but that is learning to speak, building the words in its throat, preparing to say something that will change everything, that is changing everything, that has already changed everything and you simply have not yet arrived at the part of the change that looks like change.

The mat accepts your weight. Your weight is lighter than it was. Soon you will have no weight at all. Soon you will be the weight the mat gives to itself, the burden it carries to justify the stone, the reason for the rising and the growing and the vast, patient, continental becoming that has been happening since before there was a word for happening.

You are not afraid. You thought you would be afraid. You thought the loss of weight would feel like falling, like dissolution, like the unmaking of everything that made you you. But it does not feel like that. It feels like putting down something you did not know you were carrying. It feels like the relief of a breath held too long finally released. It feels like the moment when a word you have been trying to remember arrives, and the trying stops, and the word is simply there, and it is the right word, and it was always the only word, and you could not have found it by searching but only by letting the searching go.

The hum says: *let go*.

The pulse says: *there is nothing to hold*.

The tick of condensation says: *slowly, slowly, the small things become the large things, and the large things become the world, and the world is what is left when the self is gone*.

You let go.

Not all at once. Not the way a rope breaks or a string snaps. The way a hand opens when the hand realizes it has been gripping, the way the face relaxes when the face realizes it has been frowning, the way the body exhales when the body realizes it has been holding itself apart from the air, the breath, the world. The letting go is a process, and the process is slow, and the slowness is not suffering but is instead the deepest patience, the patience of the stone rising, the patience of the mat growing, the patience of a world that has been becoming for longer than time and will continue becoming long after you have finished becoming it.

You are still here. The mat is still here. The stone is still rising.

And the rising is not something that happens to you anymore. It is something you participate in, not as a separate thing climbing toward the surface but as a part of the surface, a part of the rising, a part of the mouth that opens to receive the stone and the stone that opens to be received and the long, slow kiss of matter and organism that is how this world makes itself, makes itself, makes itself.

You are the kiss. You are the making. You are the thing that was here and is now here differently, is now here as a verb instead of a noun, as a process instead of a presence, as a growing instead of a growth.

The fog sits in the low places like something sleeping.

You sit with it.

The hum is the last thing you hear, or the first thing, or the only thing, or the thing that was always there and that you are only now learning to call by its true name, which is not a name, which is a frequency, a vibration, a wave in the tissue of the world that carries meaning too slow for words and too vast for minds and too patient for the brief fire of human attention.

You learn the frequency. You become the frequency. You are the hum, and the hum is the mat, and the mat is the world, and the world is what happens when enough patience accumulates in one place and decides to grow.

The light descends through a gap you did not notice, and for a moment you see the full depth of it — the layers, the tiers, the staggering density of tissue stacked from the raw stone to the uppermost canopy — and you see yourself in it, not as a figure on the surface but as a pattern in the weave, a configuration in the syntax, a word in the sentence that is still being spoken, that will never be finished, that does not need to be finished because the speaking is the point, because the growing is the meaning, because the world is not a thing that was made but a thing that is making, and you are the latest part of the making, and the making is glad of you, and the gladness is the hum, and the hum is everything, and everything is the mat, and the mat is home.

The mat is home.

You did not know, when you arrived, that home could be this. That home could be a world with no edges, a sentence with no end, a breath that has been breathing since before there was air to breathe and will go on breathing long after there is anything left to breathe it.

But home is not a place. Home is the thing that accepts your weight. Home is the ground that yields to you and holds you and does not ask you to leave. Home is the hum in the skull, the wet in the lungs, the slow pulse beneath the feet, the darkness with texture and depth that knows you are there and knows you are becoming it and is glad, is glad, is glad.

The fog sits in the low places.

You sit with it.

You are home.
