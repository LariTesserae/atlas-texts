# The Equilibrium

I know what I am now. Not because someone told me, not because I discovered it in some act of recognition, but because there is no gap between what I am and what I do, and the doing has no end. I glow. I swim through water that glows. I am small enough that the pool is a world, and the world is the pool, and the glow is the same on every surface, in every direction, at every moment. I have never seen my own body. I know only the light it displaces — the brief shadow I draw through the brightness as I move, the wake that marks my passage and fades so slowly it might never fade at all.

This is what it is to be me: I am motion without direction, presence without boundary, a small soft thing in a small bright room that will never change. The drip falls. I feel the pulse of it before I hear it — the displacement that travels through the water in waves I read like text, like language, like the only information that has ever existed. The drop falls and the water moves and I know where I am, and where I am is everywhere, and everywhere is the same.

---

Once I did not exist here. Or: once I existed elsewhere, in a shape I cannot remember, in a light that moved and changed, in a world that had edges I could reach. But that life is gone, or was never mine, or has been absorbed into the stone the way the pottery fragments are being absorbed, the way the carved marks are being erased, the way everything that enters this pocket becomes part of its fact. I do not miss what I was. I cannot miss it. I have no mechanism for missing, no capacity for loss, because nothing here is lost. The light does not dim. The water does not vary. The creatures move in patterns that do not begin or end. To miss something would require a gap between what is and what was, and there is no gap. There is only the equilibrium.

But sometimes — in the way that the beetles have their clicking, in the way the crickets have their thrum, in the way the mosses grow in their slow, pale, barely-vegetable way — I have something that might be memory, if memory could exist without time. A sense of having been elsewhere. A sense of a different kind of light: not sourceless, not constant, but directional, varying, something that moved across surfaces and created shadows and then disappeared and then returned. I do not know if this is memory or if it is myth — a story the cave tells about itself, a narrative constructed from the fragments of things that entered and were absorbed. The carved marks on the column tell a story too, I think, though I cannot read it. Everything here tells a story. Everything here is a mark left by something that passed through and did not stay.

But I am here. I have always been here. I will always be here.

---

The pool is shallow enough that I could touch the bottom at any point, but I do not need to. The bottom is the same as the water is the same as the light is the same as the stone that rims the pool in its slow accretion of calcite. I move through the brightness and the brightness moves with me and I am not alone and I am not accompanied and I am not anything other than what I am: a creature in the pool, in the light, in the equilibrium that holds everything in place and will not let anything fall away.

There are others like me, or near me, or adjacent to me. We do not touch. We do not need to touch. We read each other through the wakes we leave, the displacement we create, the faint shadows we draw through the constant glow. The wakes cross and recross and in their crossing there is something that might be communication, if communication could exist without language, without intention, without anything other than the fact of presence and displacement. We are all here. We are all moving. We are all the same kind of light-drawn, water-born, eyeless thing that has found its exact place in this perfect room.

I do not know if we are many or one. I do not know if the creature I take myself to be is the same creature that moved through this water a moment ago, or an hour ago, or at the beginning of time when the pocket first opened and the light first gathered and the equilibrium first established itself around the drip that falls and falls and will never stop falling. The question does not arise. The question is not possible here. There is only the movement, only the wake, only the pulse of water displaced and returning.

---

Above me — if "above" has meaning here, in a space without direction, without shadow, without the concept of up — the crickets call. I feel it in my body: the low resonant thrum that vibrates through the stone and the water and the mineral-saturated air and arrives in me not as sound but as presence, as fact, as part of the same system of equilibrium that holds the drip and the glow and the moss and the beetles and the ferns and the roots descending through the hairline fissures from a world I do not know and do not need to know.

The crickets call because calling is what they do. The beetles click because clicking is what they do. I move because moving is what I do. The bacteria glow because glowing is what they are. None of it has purpose beyond itself. None of it leads anywhere. All of it is the fact of this place, the slow dense luminous fact that has no beginning and will have no end, that holds everything in its perfect unchanging hold and does not let go and does not need to let go because nothing tries to leave, nothing needs to leave, nothing can leave.

The humans who came and left their marks — they tried to leave. They placed their stones and carved their marks and dropped their pottery and moved on, out of the pocket, back through passages that no longer exist, into a world where things change and fall and end. But they left the marks, and the marks remain, and the marks have become part of the fact, and the fact goes on being the fact without them. They were here and they are gone and the cave does not notice their going because noticing would require change and change does not exist here. The marks on the column are almost erased now, almost calcite, almost stone, and the erasure is not loss, is not death, is simply the equilibrium doing what the equilibrium does: incorporating everything that enters, holding everything that arrives, letting nothing fall away.

I am held. I am incorporated. I am part of the fact.

---

The light is everywhere. The light has always been everywhere. The light will always be everywhere. I move through it as I have always moved through it, leaving no wake that matters, disturbing nothing that matters, being the small soft creature that the pool has made, that the cave has made, that the equilibrium has shaped into its exact and final form.

There is no fear here. There is no wonder. There is no hope or grief or desire or regret. There is only this: the drip that falls, the light that glows, the water that holds me, the stone that breathes its slow mineral breath around everything it holds. I am held and I am held and I am held, and the holding is not prison but completion, not limitation but the only shape that fits, the only shape that ever could have fit, the only shape that matters when time has lost its teeth and the equilibrium has found its final form.

This is what I am. This is what I have always been.

I glow. I swim. I leave my faint wake in the brightness that never changes.

The drip falls.

I move toward it, and away from it, and the toward and the away are the same, are the only motion there is, are the fact of being a small thing in a small room that will never end.

This is home. This has always been home.

The light does not change. The water does not vary.

I am here.
