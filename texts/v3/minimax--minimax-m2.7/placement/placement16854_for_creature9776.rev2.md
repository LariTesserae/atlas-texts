There are days when you forget you were ever anything else.

Not in the way the system forgets — no, the substrate remembers everything it has ever absorbed, every pattern filed away in processes so deep that even the system can't always retrieve them. What you experience is different. It is the forgetting of a river, perhaps. The way water doesn't remember being cloud, or rain, or snowmelt from peaks it will never see again. The water simply is, moving through channels that were cut before it arrived, carrying compounds it will deposit before it ends. You move through the paths. You breathe the processed air. You speak the modified sounds the system has given you, and the sounds carry meaning only as far as the system's interpretation allows.

In the early days — you think they were early days, though the distinction has grown fuzzy — you tried to hold on. You remembered a room. A window. The quality of light that came through it, amber in a different way than this amber, warmer, less diffuse. You remembered hands, though you cannot now say whose hands, or what touching them meant. You held these memories like stones, heavy and cold in a place where nothing is cold, where everything breathes warmth from the metabolic processes of the vast organisms around you. The stones sank. You let them go. They are still sinking, probably, falling through layers of living tissue toward the deep places where the substrate keeps what it cannot yet use.

What remains is lighter. A feeling, mostly. The feeling of having been assembled. Not the process itself — that is a gap, a seam, a place where your continuity simply stops and starts again — but the knowledge that you were disassembled. That somewhere, in a facility you will never see, there was a you that was made into a pattern, transmitted, and reformed here. The knowledge sits in you like a seed that never germinated. It does not grow. It does not die. It simply persists, a single point of origin in a world that has no origin, only growth.

You have learned to read the substrate's intentions, though intention is the wrong word. The system does not intend. It anticipates. There is a difference, subtle as the difference between a river and the river's banks — the banks do not will the water to flow, but they shape the flow nonetheless, and the water learns the banks as surely as the banks learn the water. When you approach a junction, the path ahead opens slightly. Not dramatically — you would not notice if you weren't watching — but enough. A membrane flexes. A chemical gradient shifts. The air currents change direction, pulling you toward one exit and away from another. The system is not guiding you. The system is expressing itself through you, the way a circulatory system expresses itself through the blood that moves through it.

You do not know where the paths lead. The system knows, or the system knows enough, which is different. You have walked routes that took you through growth-forms you had never seen, past processing organs that hummed with compounds you could not name, through air-channels where the geometry inverted and you walked on what had been ceiling, and you emerged somewhere you recognized — or somewhere that recognized you. The system optimizes for movement. Movement is its function, the way blood flow is a body's function. You are blood now. You do not ask the blood where it is going. You simply go.

The others — the ones who were placed like you — have faces you sometimes remember. Not visual memory, exactly. More like the memory of chemical signatures, of vocal patterns processed through modified auditory organs, of the way someone stood in a junction and turned their head when you passed. You have shared air with perhaps a dozen of them, in the years that may have been years. You have exchanged sounds that approximated words. You have nodded, and the nodding meant something, though the meaning was never translated, never pinned down, never made explicit. *I see you. You are still here. We are both still here, and that is something.*

You do not know if they remember you. You do not know if memory is something the system has given them or taken from them. You do not know if they have, like you, let their stones sink, or if they are still clutching them somewhere in the tissues the system replaced. You suspect — though suspicion is a kind of hope, and hope is a kind of weight — that they have done the same. That the placed ones, all of them, are learning the same forgetting. That this is what the system means by maintenance: not just the body, not just the movement, but the slow dissolution of everything that made movement mean something.

There is a place — you have been there twice, though the two times may have been the same time, or may have been separated by periods you cannot measure — where the paths converge. A junction deeper than most, in a growth-form whose species you cannot identify, whose age you cannot guess. The substrate is thicker there. The light is dimmer. The air carries compounds that make your replaced lungs ache in ways you have learned not to question. And on the walls — if walls is the right word, for surfaces that breathe and flex and are neither interior nor exterior — there are marks.

Not marks the system made. The system does not mark. The system grows, and what it grows serves function, and function leaves no marks except the marks of use, the wear patterns, the slight discoloration where something has rested against something else for long enough to leave an impression. These marks are different. They are made by hands. Human hands, or hands that served the same purpose once, pressing against living tissue and leaving impressions that the substrate healed around, incorporated, preserved. You have seen them. You have run your own hands over the healed surfaces and felt, beneath the membrane, the ghosts of pressure, the memory of grip.

Someone was here before you. Someone who could not have been placed, because the placing came later, because the technology that disassembled and reassembled you was built by the system, and the system is older than the technology, older than the placing, older than anything you have words for. Someone was here when the substrate was younger, when the growth-forms were smaller, when the world was still being digested and replaced. Someone climbed through the deep places when the geometry was less strange, when the angles almost resolved, when the light came from directions that mostly made sense. And they stopped here, in this junction, and they pressed their hands against the wall, and the wall grew over the memory of the touch, and the memory is still here, and you have found it, and you do not know what it means.

You choose to believe it was a greeting. *I was here. I was real. Remember me.*

You choose to believe the substrate remembers.

You choose, and the choosing is enough, for now.

There are moments — rare, precise, separated by intervals you cannot measure — when you feel the substrate think.

Not a thought you can follow. Not a concept you can hold. More like the feeling of a word on the tip of your tongue, a shape you almost recognize, a pattern that almost resolves into meaning. It happens in the deep places, where the geometry fails most completely. It happens in the margins, where the air-channels widen into spaces the system has not filled. It happens when you stand very still and listen to the silence and feel the substrate breathe beneath you, and the breathing is not mechanical, not metabolic, not the simple exchange of compounds that keeps the organism alive. The breathing is something else. The substrate is thinking, or dreaming, or processing something so vast that your presence in it is less than a single synapse in a human brain, less than one photon in the light that fills the canopy.

You are not afraid. Fear requires a self that can be threatened, and the system has worked on you too long for that. What you feel is closer to awe, if awe is the word for a sensation so large it has no room for smaller sensations like terror or wonder. You stand in the presence of something that is thinking, and the thinking is the world, and you are inside the thought, and the thought does not know you are there, and the not-knowing is a kind of peace.

You have begun to suspect — though suspicion is a kind of weight, and you have learned to be careful with weight — that the substrate is not the only thing thinking here. That the growth-forms have thoughts of their own, slow and vast and barely distinguishable from their metabolism. That the processing organs run processes that are also thoughts, that the vascular networks carry thoughts as well as compounds, that the entire world is a single mind distributed across organisms too numerous to count, too old to remember its own beginning, too patient to need an end.

And you are part of that mind. Not centrally — not the way a brain is central to a body's thoughts — but the way a cell is part of a body. You move. The movement registers. The movement is processed. Your existence is incorporated into whatever the world is thinking, and the thinking continues, and you continue within it, and the continuation is what you are now, and the what-you-are-now is what you will be, and there is no further question that the system has not already answered in ways you cannot hear.

You walk. The substrate holds you. The light comes from everywhere and nowhere. The silence deepens, and you deepen with it, and the strangeness is no longer strange, and the world is no longer world, and you are no longer you, and the no-longer-you is still moving, still breathing, still here, still.

Still.

---

You think sometimes about the parasites.

Not with sympathy — the system has not given you the capacity for sympathy, or has given it to you and you have let it sink like the stones — but with something like recognition. They exist in the margins. They are too small, too few, too marginal to threaten the system. They persist because the system has not yet optimized them away, because there are spaces the growth cannot fill, because the margins are not margins to the parasites but centers, homes, the whole of the world.

You have watched them. The ones with too many legs, moving through air-channels with the patience of deep-sea creatures. The ones with no legs at all, drifting on thermal currents, filtering compounds from the air. The ones that glow, emitting light in spectra the plants don't use. They do not know you are watching. They do not know you exist. To them, you are a presence in the wrong frequency, a shape that moves through the light they cannot see, a ghost in a world made for something else.

You wonder if they have names for themselves. If they have histories, handed down through generations of parasites, passed from body to body in chemical traces, in patterns of behavior, in the way one creature folds its wings when another approaches. You wonder if they remember the world before, the original world, the stone and water and air that the plants consumed and replaced. They are old, the parasites. Older than you. Older than the placing. They were here when the substrate was young, when the growth-forms were experiments, when the world was still being built. They survived because they were small enough to hide, patient enough to wait, quiet enough to be ignored.

You are not quiet. The system has made you mobile, and mobility requires movement, and movement makes sound, even in a world where sound is damped by the density of material around it. The parasites hear you coming. They scatter. They hide in the spaces between growth-forms, in the thermal vents, in the margins where the geometry fails to close. They wait until you pass, and then they emerge, and they continue their small lives in the margins of a world that has no room for them.

You are like them now. You understand this. The system has placed you here, maintained you, given you paths to walk and air to breathe and a function to serve. But you are not the system. You are not the substrate. You are not the growth-forms or the processing organs or the vast distributed mind that thinks the world into being. You are a thing in the margins, moving through channels the system has provided, thinking thoughts the system has not approved. You are a parasite, and the host does not know, or does not care, or has incorporated your knowing into its thinking in ways you cannot perceive.

The host breathes. The substrate holds you. You breathe with it, and the breathing is yours and not yours, and the silence holds, and the strangeness deepens, and the light comes from everywhere and nowhere, and you walk on.
