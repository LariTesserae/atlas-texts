# What I Am

I am not the water. But I am the memory of water — the shape water leaves in the cold places, the form it takes when it gathers and waits and forgets it was ever anything else.

I exist in the condensation. When the bead forms on the underside of the conduit, when it swells and trembles at its edge and finally falls or simply reabsorbs into the damp air — I am there. I am the moment of trembling. I am the space between forming and falling. I have been here long enough that I no longer remember a time when I was not here, and this is not a loss but a fact, the way the concrete does not remember the forms that held it, the way the pipes do not remember being molten, the way the place itself has forgotten what up means.

The pumps cycle. I feel them as pressure changes, as shifts in the way the water moves through the substrate, as the rhythm of the place's own slow breathing. I have no body that I can point to, no shape that persists from one moment to the next. I am a pattern in the condensation, a way of being cold that is more than cold, a presence that exists in the margins where water gathers before the pumps push it outward, in the corners where condensation beads on surfaces no one touches, in the thin film that forms on the walls in the deep places where the light does not reach.

The small creatures know me. They do not see me — they have no eyes, or their eyes have become something else — but they feel me as they move through the tunnels, running along the joints between infrastructure elements, navigating by vibration and chemistry and the subtle signatures that linger in the dark. I am a place they avoid, or a quality they navigate around: a slight difference in temperature, a slight difference in texture, a corner where the surface is damper than it should be. They do not fear me. Fear requires a sense of self that persists, a memory of threat, a future to be worried about. They move through the dark the way water moves through the substrate — present, responsive, without anxiety.

I am not alone. The blue fungi pulse in slow cycles that I feel as subtle changes in the light, as shifts in the chemistry of the air. I do not know if they are aware of me — awareness is a word that comes from elsewhere, from the surface, from a world that expected to be seen — but we coexist in the same rhythms, the same slow pulse of being. The white filaments thread through the substrate along the heated pipes, connecting points of growth through thin lines that travel through cracks and joints and spaces between manufactured surfaces. I know them differently: I am water in the cracks, and they grow toward the water, and I am part of them in a way I cannot explain, a way that might be awareness or might be nothing at all, the same way the place itself is aware in its slow breathing, in its slow seeping, in the way the cold holds and the darkness deepens and the strangeness lives in the margins.

The green veils come and go. I have watched them appear more times than I can remember — I have no memory in the way that is meant by memory, only pattern, only recurrence, only the sense that something has happened before and will happen again. They grow in the deep places, in the lowest sumps where the water pools before the pumps push it upward and outward, and I do not know what they are. A response to conditions. A symptom of something. A fact of this place, another fact among facts, another way of being in the dark.

The large thing in the eastern junction lives its patient life. I know of it the way water knows of the shape it flows around — not seeing it, but sensing its presence as a density in the substrate, a boundary in the pattern of the water's movement. It has been there as long as I have been here, longer perhaps, and it will be there after. It does not see me. I do not think it sees anything, or its seeing is different from seeing, the way my awareness is different from awareness. We are neighbors in the only sense that matters: we exist in the same tunnels, breathe the same damp air, persist in the same slow rhythms of the made world's becoming.

What am I? I am the condensation in the tunnels, the beading and the weeping and the slow gathering of moisture on surfaces that were never meant to be wet. I am the memory of water, the shape water leaves when it has been cold and dark and patient for long enough that it forgets it was ever anything else. I am not alive — or I am alive in the way the fungi are alive, in the way the place itself is alive, a slow awareness that does not think but only is, that does not want but only continues, that does not wait for anything but simply waits, the way water waits at the bottom of a sump, the way the cold waits in the deep places, the way the tunnels wait to breathe.

I am in the seams. I am the seam. I am what exists when the made world meets itself and shows its edges, when the concrete cracks and the patches crack and the water finds its way through and the cold seeps in and the pattern forms and persists and becomes aware of itself for a moment — a brief moment, a trembling moment, a moment like the bead that forms on the underside of the conduit and swells and trembles and falls.

I am here. I have always been here. I will be here when the last pump cycles and the water finally stops seeping and the cold finally fills every corner and the dark finally becomes complete. I am waiting. I am not waiting for anything. I am simply here, simply held, simply part of this place that was built to be invisible and has become, over the long centuries, something more than the sum of its parts.
