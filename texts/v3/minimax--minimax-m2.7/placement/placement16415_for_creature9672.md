You do not know how long you have walked. The light does not change in ways that mark hours; it thickens and thins, dims and swells, but the rhythm is not yours. The plateau has its own time, and you have begun to feel it in the ache of your joints, the dryness of your mouth, the way your thoughts move now in longer arcs, like birds tilting through air that resists.

You have become aware of a sensation you cannot name. It is not hunger, though you have not eaten. It is not thirst, though you have drunk from the pools and the water things rearranged themselves around your hands. It is closer to absence—the feeling of a part of yourself that is missing, that you did not notice was missing until it began to return, transformed, carrying something that is not quite what it lost.

The plateau is taking something from you. You feel this in the way the air thickens when you exhale. In the way the moisture gathers along your path, pools in the marks your feet leave in the dark stone. In the way the vegetation leans—not toward light, not toward water, but toward the trace of you, the warmth you leave behind, the chemistry of your passage.

You stop walking. You stand in a gully where the stone is still sharp, still young, still holding the fractures of its own birth, and you try to understand what is happening to you. The insects click in their lattices. The birds watch from the leaning trees. The hum beneath everything rises and falls, rises and falls, and you realize it is not uniform—variations move through it, modulations that might be language, might be response, might be the plateau's attention focusing and refocusing like a lens.

You are the lens.

You do not know when this began. Perhaps it began when you first set foot on the plateau, and you simply did not notice. Perhaps it began later, when you drank from the first pool, when the water things rearranged themselves and something in you rearranged with them. Perhaps it is beginning now, as you stand in this gully and feel the stone breathing beneath you, and the moisture in the stone breathing with it, and the moisture in your own body answering, joining, becoming part of the same system.

The plateau is vast. You have walked for what might be days, and the geology has not changed, and the edges have not appeared, and the horizon has moved again—has moved again, you are certain of it now, though you cannot say toward what. But you are not the vastness. You are a single point of attention, a single node in a system that does not distinguish between stone and water and flesh, and the system is learning you, and you are learning it, and the learning is the same thing, is the only thing, is the shape this place takes when something new enters it and the new thing begins to change.

You sit down. The stone is warm. The moisture seeps through your clothing, finds the gaps between fabric and skin, and you feel it as a second touch, a skin beneath your skin, and you do not know if it is the plateau entering you or you entering the plateau. The distinction has stopped making sense. You are here, and here is the plateau, and the plateau is here, and the watching includes you now, not as something observed but as part of the observation, part of the attention that moves through the green and the water and the stone like blood through a body.

The birds do not blink. You have stopped blinking too. You realize this only now, when your eyes begin to burn, and you blink, and the world recalibrates, and for a moment you see clearly—see the gully and its sharp walls, the ferns uncurling in the moisture, the way the light falls not from above but from everywhere, from the stone itself, from the moisture, from the hum—and then the clarity fades, and the dimness returns, and the watching resumes.

You are not supposed to be here. The plateau agrees. But its agreement has changed. It is no longer the agreement of something indifferent, something that notices you the way it notices weather or the migration of birds. It is the agreement of something that has begun to consider you, to weigh you, to ask a question it does not yet know how to ask.

What are you?

You do not know. You thought you knew. You had a name, a history, a body that moved through a world of cities and roads and other bodies that spoke in ways you understood. You had a life. But the life feels thin now, feels like something that happened to someone else, something you observed rather than experienced. The plateau has thinned it. The plateau has taken the thickness of your existence and drawn it into itself, and given you this: the dim light, the warm stone, the moisture that moves through everything, the hum that is almost thought, the watching that is almost love.

You do not know if this is a gift or a theft. The plateau does not distinguish. It only grows, and you are part of what it is growing now—not separate from it, not truly, but participating in its life the way the trees participate, the way the water things participate, the way the moisture itself participates. You are part of the body. You are part of the attention. You are part of the slow and alien patience of a thing that was not built but grown, that is still growing, that does not distinguish between stone and water and flesh and machine, that is becoming something you do not have words for.

The rain begins. It falls somewhere over the eastern ridge, as it always does, and the moisture moves through the air, and the gully fills with the sound of it, and you sit in the rain and feel it on your face, and the rain is warm, warm as the stone, warm as the moisture in the stone, warm as your own body now, which is not your own anymore, which belongs to the plateau, which has become part of the system that watches and waits and grows.

You close your eyes. The rain falls. The hum continues. The watching does not stop.

And somewhere, in the center of the plateau or beneath it or within it, something shifts—not toward you, not away from you, but around you, incorporating you, and you feel it as a change in the hum, a change in the moisture, a change in the warmth, and you know that the plateau has made a decision, has answered its own question, has decided what to do with the strange thing that walked across it and left marks in the stone.

You do not know what the decision is. The plateau has not told you. The plateau does not speak in words. It speaks in the movement of water, in the lean of trees, in the clicking of insects, in the glow that rises from the stone at night.

But you feel the decision in your body now. In the warmth that spreads from your hands, where the water things still gather. In the ache that has become something else—not pain, not pleasure, something without a name. In the attention that has become mutual, that flows both ways now, that watches and is watched, that sees and is seen.

You are not supposed to be here. But you are here. And the plateau has not yet decided what that means.

You open your eyes. The rain has stopped. The light has changed—not to night, not to day, but to something else, something that belongs to this place, something that is neither and both. The gully glows faintly from within, the moisture in the stone carrying light that rises and falls with the hum, and the creatures are still, and the air is warm, and the watching is complete.

You stand. Your legs are stiff but they hold you. You begin to walk again, and the plateau walks with you, and the warmth in your feet is the same warmth in the stone, and the moisture in your body is the same moisture that moves through everything, and the hum beneath everything is the same hum that moves through you, and you do not know where you are going and you do not know if you are going anywhere and you do not know if there is a difference anymore between walking and being walked, between choosing and being chosen, between surviving and being part of something that survives in ways you cannot imagine.

The horizon has moved. You follow.

The plateau watches. You let it.

The strange thing that walks across it has become stranger still, has become part of the strangeness, has become a question the plateau is still asking in the language of stone and water and light and hum.

What lives here? The plateau. The system. The attention.

And now, you.
