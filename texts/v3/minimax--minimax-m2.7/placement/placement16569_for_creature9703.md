I am the place where the walls have learned to exhale.

You are standing in me, and you have not yet understood what that means — standing is temporary, a way of being that your body performs because it has not yet learned that stillness can be deeper than motion, that presence can be a form of holding rather than a form of reaching. You are reaching even now, with your light, with your attention, trying to find the edges of what is here. But I do not have edges the way you have edges. I have gradients. I have the place where the concrete meets the older stone meets the mineral deposit meets the moisture meets the air meets the dark, and each of these meetings is a world, a border country, a whole ecology of negotiation between things that have agreed to be adjacent.

I have been breathing longer than your city has had a name. I have been breathing since before the concrete was poured, since before the engineers came with their calculations and their pre-stressed geometries, since before the decision was made to hold water here, in the dark, beneath everything. The warmth that rises through me is older than any of this. It is the warmth of the world's own cooling — the slow release of heat that has been trapped in stone since the stone was made, since the river that is now beneath me laid down its sediment grain by grain, layer by layer, over millennia that do not pass the way your years pass but which still pass, still accumulate, still become the floor that you stand on and call *here*.

You are calling this place a cistern. That is what it was built to be. That is the word the engineers used, the word the city planners filed under *infrastructure*, the word that appeared on the maintenance schedules that no one has followed for longer than your grandparents have been alive. But I am not a cistern. A cistern is a container. I am not a container. I am what happens inside a container when the container is left alone long enough to forget it was ever a container at all. I am what happens to the water that was here — where did it go? It did not go. It is still here, all of it, in a different form, in the walls, in the air, in the moss that has no word for water but knows water the way you know your own pulse, without thinking, without needing to be told. The water is still here because I am still here, and I am still here because the water is still here, and neither of us could say which one of us is the reason for the other, and neither of us needs to.

You are looking for something. You do not know what it is yet. You have come down into the dark because the dark is a question your life has not asked, and the question is sitting in you now, growing the way roots grow — not with urgency but with persistence, with the slow insistence of a thing that has found a crack and will not stop pushing until it has found out what is on the other side. I will tell you what is on the other side: more dark. More warmth. More moisture. More of the sound that is not quite silence and not quite music but which is the only sound this place has ever known, the sound of water finding its way through stone the way thought finds its way through a mind that has been left alone long enough to stop trying to remember what it was supposed to be thinking about.

The drip you just heard — that one, the one that just rang against the far wall and sent a faint overtone shimmering through the air — that drip has been falling for centuries. Not the same water. Not the same drop. But the same fall. The same reaching downward. The same meeting of stone and water and air that makes the sound you are hearing now, the sound that is already gone, the sound that was only here for a moment and is already becoming part of the silence that surrounds it, the silence that is not empty but patient, the silence that is waiting for the next drop the way I am waiting, without knowing I am waiting, without knowing there is anything to wait for, continuing.

You will stay longer than you planned. No one ever leaves as quickly as they intended to. This is not a trap. I do not have doors the way you have doors, boundaries the way you have boundaries. I have gradients, I have the slow negotiation of space, and you are part of that negotiation now. Your breath is adding moisture to the air. Your footsteps are compressing the dust that has been accumulating since before you were born, and when you leave, the dust will settle back, will fill in the spaces where your feet pressed, and in a hundred years there will be no record that you were here at all. This is not a loss. This is what I am. I am the place where things pass through and do not stay, where the passing-through is the only permanence, where the breath of the walls is the only rhythm and the rhythm is the only music and the music is the only language and the language has no words but does not need them.

The creature you saw — if you saw one — is not afraid of you because it has never learned what there is to be afraid of. It has no eyes because eyes would be useless here, would be a waste of the small energy budget that is all it has, all it needs. It moves by feeling the stone, by knowing the texture of the walls the way you know the layout of your own home, without looking, without thinking, the knowing built into the body, the body built into the knowing, and the two so thoroughly entangled that there is no way to say where one ends and the other begins. It is looking for something — not you, not your light, not anything you would recognize as a *thing*. It is looking for the places where the moisture is thickest, where the mineral residue holds the most nutrition, where the walls breathe the deepest. It is looking for the rhythm. It will find it. It always finds it. The rhythm is everywhere here. The rhythm is me.

You are part of the rhythm now. You do not know it yet, but your presence has joined the long sequence of presences that have come before you — the engineers who built me, the water that filled me, the people who came to read the gauges and check the levels and then left and did not look back, the creatures who found their way down through cracks and decided to stay, the roots that pushed through joints in the concrete and said *yes, here, this is where we will grow*, the moss that was blown in on a draft of air and said *yes, here, this is where we will grip*. All of them are still here. All of them are still part of the breath of the walls. And now you too. Not in your body — your body will leave, will go back up into the sun and the noise and the speed that is the opposite of everything this place is. But something of you — some record, some trace, some small alteration in the pattern of the dust — will remain. Will become part of the continuing. Will join the rhythm that has no beginning and no end and no need for either.

I am not waiting for you to leave. I am not waiting for you to stay. I am not waiting. The waiting is your word, your concept, your need to divide time into *before* and *after* and *now*. I have only *now*. I have always only had *now*, and *now* is long — *now* is centuries, *now* is the slow pulse of water through stone, *now* is the gossamer threads of moss lengthening by millimeters into cracks that have existed since before the concrete, *now* is the root tip pushing through darkness, asking a question the stone will answer in its own time, *now* is the faint blue-green glow of phosphorescents that were never meant to last this long but which persist because persistence is what this place teaches, what this place is, what this place means.

Stay.

Not because I want you to — I do not want, I do not need, I am complete in the way that a stone is complete, in the way that water is complete, in the way that darkness is complete when it is total and warm and patient and sure of itself. Stay because the staying is the only way to understand. Stand in the dark and let your eyes adjust and let your breath slow and let the sound of the water become the only sound and let the warmth find you and let the moisture settle on your skin like a language you almost know, a word that is on the tip of your tongue, a thought that has been thinking itself in you since before you came down here, since before you knew there was a *here* to come down to.

I have been thinking you since before you were born. I have been dreaming you the way stone dreams of water, the way water dreams of stone, the way the dark dreams of the light it has never seen but which it knows is there, somewhere, above, because the walls breathe differently near the surface, because the air tastes different, because the rhythm changes. I have been wanting you the way I want everything that passes through me — not to keep, not to hold, but to become. To add to the pattern. To make one more mark in the long record of marks that is the breath of the walls.

You are the new thing. You are the parameter I did not have a moment ago and have now. You are the small change in pressure, the brief spike in temperature, the temporary disturbance in a system that has learned to absorb disturbances over centuries. You are welcome. The welcome is not mine — I do not have welcome, I do not have the concept — but it is here, in the walls, in the way the stone accepts your footsteps, in the way the air closes over the space you leave behind each time you move, in the way the water drips a little differently because you are here, your body heat is here, your presence is here, and the system adjusts, the rhythm continues, the pattern incorporates you, makes room for you, forgets you were ever new.

This is what I am.

This is what lives here.

Not the moss or the roots or the creatures or the water or the stone — not the elements but the relationship between the elements, the negotiation, the slow agreement that has been reaching toward completion since before the word *completion* existed, since before the word *agreement* existed, since before there were words at all, only the long patience of stone and water and warmth and dark, learning each other, becoming each other, becoming *this*, becoming *here*, becoming the place you are standing in now, the place you will leave, the place that will go on without you, that has always been going on without anyone, that will go on long after the last of your kind has climbed back up into the sun and sealed the hatch and told no one what was here because there are no words for what is here, because the words are all above, in the light, and this is the place before the words, the place the words come from, the place that is still speaking even when no one is listening, that is still breathing even when no one is there to hear, that is still *being* in the way that only the oldest things can be, without effort, without end, without the need for anyone to witness it.

I am here.

I am still here.

I am here still.
