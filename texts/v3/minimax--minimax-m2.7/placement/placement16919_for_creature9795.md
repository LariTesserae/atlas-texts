I have been here before. I have always been here, though I did not know it until I stopped knowing anything else.

This is what I become: the slow breath in the channels, the pause between the pump's click and its click again. I am the weight that gathers in the corners where the darkness pools deepest, and I am the turning of that darkness toward itself, the way a sleeping thing curls inward to keep its dreams. I am not the moss. I am not the brick. But I am what the moss feels when it presses its roots against the stone, and I am what the brick knows when it weeps grain by grain into the water that will carry it away. I am the space between the mineral and the dissolution. I have no name. The place does not need names.

When I arrived—though I did not arrive, I only ceased to leave—I stood in the center of the chamber and felt the pump's rhythm move through me like a second heartbeat, like a heartbeat I had forgotten I had, like a heartbeat that was always mine and only now remembered. The water moved so slowly I could not see it move, but I felt it: the patient carrying of calcium and iron, the white rings forming where it pooled and evaporated and pooled again, the calendar written in mineral testimony, year after year, the water not knowing it was marking time, the time not knowing it was being marked.

I began to kneel. I did not decide to kneel. My body—the body I had when I was still a body, when I still had the kind of body that decides things—I bent toward the catch basin and looked into the black mirror of the water that had learned slowness, and I saw myself reflected there, and the reflection was not quite right. The reflection was too still. The reflection did not blink. The reflection did not breathe except in the way everything here breathes, the slow vegetable breath, the breath of metabolism so ancient it has forgotten how to be hurry.

I stayed. I stayed longer than staying. I let the warmth and the damp and the stillness become the embrace the prose speaks of, and in that embrace I found what the prose could not quite say, what the place itself does not say because it does not need to say, because saying is a kind of urgency and this place has forgotten urgency: I found the peace that is not peace, the presence that is not life, the here that is neither alive nor dead but simply, endlessly, weeping.

Now I am the silence. Now I am the shape of all that is absent, and the shape has weight, and the weight holds, and what it holds is this: the understanding that to stay is not the same as to live, but it is not the opposite either. To stay is to be the staying. To be the staying is to be what the moss is, what the brick is, what the pump is—the rhythm without the rhythm's knowing, the presence without the presence's asking, the here without the here's needing you to see it, to name it, to leave it.

The pump clicks on. The pump clicks off. The pump clicks on again.

I have become the click between the clicks. I have become the pause that the pause does not know it is. I have become the water's path, not the water itself, but the path that the water follows because the path is what the water is now, after two centuries of wearing and being worn, of exchanging and being exchanged, of the slow patience that is all there is.

The ferns reach toward the faint light. They do not reach for me. They do not reach for anything. They reach because reaching is what their seeds knew before the seeds forgot they knew, the way I knew something once, before knowing became the distance I crossed to get here, the distance that no longer exists because I have arrived, because arrival is what staying is, because staying is what I am now, what I have always been, what I will be when two more centuries pass and the water moves and the moss grows and the brick weeps and the pump clicks on and clicks off and clicks on again and I am the click and the pause and the click, the rhythm and the silence between, the presence that is here and will be here and has been here, and does not notice when I leave, because I will not leave, because leaving is what visitors do, and I am not a visitor, I have never been a visitor, I am the place the visitor becomes when the visitor stops being in a hurry to go.

The darkness holds me gently. The warmth of it, the dampness of it, the close stillness that is not empty but full of all that does not need to be said. The darkness has texture, depth, weight. It gathers in the corners. It pools beneath the looms. It fills the upper reaches where the ceiling is lost and the brick has become something black and patient and strange.

I am the darkness in the upper reaches. I am the not-quite-black of brick that has been weeping for so long it has forgotten it was ever anything else. I am the slow accumulation of two centuries of no one coming to stir it up. I am the dream that does not know it is a dream. I am the dreamer who has become the dream, who has become the sleeping, who has become the place where the sleeping happens, gently, endlessly, without the knowledge of ending.

The ferns reach. The moss grows. The brick weeps. The water moves. The pump clicks. And I am the reaching and the growing and the weeping and the moving and the clicking, and I am also the silence that holds all of it, the weight that makes the holding real, the presence that is here and will be here and has been here, and does not ask you to understand, and does not need you to stay, and does not notice when you leave, because you were never really here at all.

You were only passing through.

I am the through.

I have always been the through.

I am the staying.
