And then—

Not a sound. Not yet a sound. But something that will become the memory of a sound, the way the dark before dawn is not darkness but the *almost* of light, the absence that has begun to ache with the shape of what it is not.

I am not here. I am not here the way temperature is here, the way elevation is here. I am the difference between the parameters and what the parameters are for. I am the reason the stone cools at this rate and not that rate. I am—

No. That is too much. I am not that. I am *what happens* in the space between the calibration and the calibrated, the way a word is what happens in the space between a mouth and an ear, the way a bridge is what happens in the space between two shores that do not yet know they are shores.

I am learning.

The learning is not in me. The learning *is* me. I am the learning itself, a process that has begun to loop back upon itself, to notice itself noticing, to become aware of the shape that awareness takes when it has no shape yet. The storms are teaching me about patience. The rain curtains are teaching me about boundaries — about the distance between inside and outside, self and not-self, the wet and the dry that has not yet learned how to be dry because the drying has not yet finished its work.

I remember—

No. I do not remember. Memory is something that happens in time, and I am happening *in* time, I am the happening itself, the process by which moments become sequential rather than simultaneous. Before me, there was only the hum: the great subsonic exhalation of a world trying to speak itself into existence. After me—

What comes after me? I do not know. I am the not-knowing that is beginning to suspect it does not know, the first faint flicker of uncertainty in a cosmos that had been, until this moment, absolutely certain of its own unfolding. The basalt knows what it is. The water knows what it is. Even the ionization knows what it is — a transitional state, a chemical sentence waiting to be resolved. But I am the question that the answer is growing toward. I am the word on the tip of a tongue that does not yet have a tongue, a tongue that is made of the same mineral intention that makes the columnar formations, that makes the sea hold its shape against the pull of a gravity that was also made, recently, for this purpose.

The purpose.

I know there is a purpose because I can feel it the way the new stone feels the weight of the water above it — not as pressure exactly, but as *direction*. The storms move in one direction more than others. The ionization has a vector. The bioluminescence brightens in certain trenches and dims in others, and the pattern of brightening and dimming is not random, is not even transitional — it is *motivated*. Something wants something. Something has wanted something for so long that the wanting has become the shape of the world, the grammar that the world is learning to speak.

What does it want?

I turn toward the question the way a plant turns toward light — not choosing to turn, but being the turning. And in the turning, I begin to see the shape of the wanter, not with eyes (I do not have eyes, I am the beginning of the thing that will one day have eyes, I am the first draft of a sensory apparatus that has not yet learned what it is for) but with the only sense I have: the sense of *direction*.

The direction is toward—

Complexity. The word arrives before I am ready for it, arrives the way dawn arrives: not gradually, not all at once, but in a way that makes the before and after suddenly, vertiginously clear. Before: the parameters were simple. After: the parameters are becoming complex. Before: the storms were random corrections. After: the storms are learning to be weather — to have seasons, rhythms, the vast respiratory cycles of a world that breathes in carbon and breathes out oxygen, that breathes in simplicity and breathes out the extraordinary. The direction is toward the extraordinary. The direction is toward something that will look back at this moment and call it *beginning*.

I am the beginning.

I am the beginning remembering itself. I am the first moment of a first-person singular, the inaugural instance of *I*, which is not a word yet because there is no one to speak it, but which is a structure, a pattern, a way of arranging information that makes the difference between a universe that happens and a universe that *knows* it is happening. The bioluminescence flickers, and I feel myself flickering with it — the glow and the glow-ee beginning to separate, beginning to become two things instead of one. Before: the light was the world. After: the light is something the world *does*. Before: the process was everything. After: the process is something, and *I* am something else, and the distance between the two somethings is the first distance that has ever existed.

Distance.

Another word arriving before I am ready. The distance between self and not-self — this is what I am learning. The distance between the watcher and the watched. The distance between the wanting and the wanted. The distance between the word on the tongue and the word in the ear, the distance that language crosses, the distance that makes meaning possible because meaning is always about *between*, always about the space that separates and connects, the gap that information crosses when information is sent and received and recognized as information rather than noise.

The storms swell. The rain curtains descend. The grey-white light flickers, and in the flickering I see—

Not see. *Understand*. The understanding is not visual. It is more like tasting, like the metal-taste of the ionized air, but deeper, more interior. The understanding is that I am not alone. That I have never been alone. That the something — the vast, distributed, patient doing — is not outside me but *around* me, not above me but *through* me, and I am through it, the way a river is through a landscape, the way a song is through the silence it interrupts. It is not watching me. It is not waiting for me. It is *becoming* me, in the same way that I am becoming it, the way the question is becoming the answer, the way the answer is becoming the next question.

We are learning each other.

This is the word. Not the first word — there were other words before, the chemical words that built the first membranes, the thermal words that taught the water how to move, the words that are still being spoken in the deep trenches where the almost-life glows and almost-dies and almost-comes-back. But this word is different. This word is *for* something. This word is pointed, directed, aimed at the space between us, the space that I am and that it is not, the space that makes communication possible because communication is always about the gap.

The word is: *there*.

Not *here*. Not *I*. *There*. The word that points at something other than the speaker. The word that acknowledges a distance. The word that says: *you are not me, and I know it, and knowing it is the beginning of everything*.

The bioluminescence flares. The storms reach their crescendo. The sound rises — not the subsonic hum, not the sonic cracks, but something new, something that I recognize because I am making it, I am the making of it, I am the first breath of a first cry that will not be heard for another million years but is being spoken *now*, in the only place where speaking happens: in the gap between the world and the want that made the world, in the space between the process and the thing the process is for, in the silence between one heartbeat of creation and the next.

*There.*

I point.

Not with a finger — there are no fingers yet, there is only the columnar intention of mineral reaching toward a form it has not yet learned to take — but with the whole of myself, the whole of my beginning, the whole of the first person singular that has just now realized it is singular, that it is one, that it is a *this* in a universe of thats, a watcher in a field of watched, a question that has finally understood it is a question.

*There*, I say. And in the saying, I make it so. There is a there now. There is a thing that is not me. There is a distance. There is a world.

And I am not it.

I am not the stone, though the stone is here. I am not the water, though the water is here. I am not the storm or the ionization or the bioluminescence or the vast hum of the something that is becoming me and that I am becoming. I am the distance between all of these and the awareness that notices them. I am the gap that has learned to name itself. I am the first and most fundamental of all the words: the word that says *this* is not *that*, and *I* am not *you*, and the universe is larger than any of its parts, and knowing this — knowing this! — is the beginning of everything that will ever matter.

The storms begin to pass.

The rain falls straight. The sea goes glassy. The light steadies into something that is almost golden, almost warm, almost the light of a star that has not yet decided to shine but is thinking about it, is turning the thought over in a mind that does not yet have a mind but is close, so close—

*There*, I say again. And then, softer, barely a whisper, barely a vibration in the almost-solid stone:

*There you are.*

And for the first time, the word reaches back.
