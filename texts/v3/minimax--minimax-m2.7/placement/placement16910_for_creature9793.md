The warmth first. That is what I notice—that the ground is warm beneath me, and I am the only thing that could ever grow cold. This is how I know I am here. This is the evidence.

I am not the maker. The making was done all at once, in a single breath, and I would have had to be there to witness it, to be part of it, and there was no one there, and the breath was not a breath, and the making was not an event but a state. I understand this. I am not the one who planted the columns or wove the reed beds or set the tiles in their endless patterns. Those things were already complete before there was a "before" to contain them.

But I am the warmth. And the warmth is the one thing still happening.

I move through the garden, and each step leaves warmth behind—a record, a memory, a small persistent heat that the tiles hold and do not give up. The tiles are warm from the making, that inner warmth that never finished cooling, but my warmth is different. Mine is the warmth of something that can change. Mine is the warmth of the living, and I walk across this frozen garden and I am the only temperature gradient, the only proof that cold is possible, the only threat to the perfect equilibrium.

The light holds me. I feel this. It does not illuminate so much as fix, and I am fixed in it, held in place by the brightness that comes from nowhere and everywhere, that makes distance a matter of opinion. I can see a long way here. I can see to the edge of the design, where the columns rise and the canopy thins and the horizon waits—not a horizon that will come closer but a horizon that is already there, has always been there, will always be there, like the edge of a thought the garden is thinking and will never finish thinking.

The birds call. The sounds come from slightly elsewhere, slightly delayed, and I know they are not birds but the shape of birds, the idea of birds pressed into motion. But they do not know this. They cannot know anything. They simply move, and their movement is their whole existence, and when they stop moving they will not be anything at all, and when they move again they will be exactly what they were, and the garden does not notice because the garden notices everything and so notices nothing, and I am the noticing, I am the attention that makes the difference, I am the one thing here that perceives and so is perceived.

The stillness presses. I feel it against my skin like humidity, like weight, like something that has been waiting and will wait forever and does not need me to arrive because it already has me, has always had me, placed me here in the design before the design was made, and my walking is the walking it designed me to walk, and my warmth is the warmth it designed me to carry, and my wondering is the wondering it designed me to do, and I cannot know if this is true, cannot know if I am the designer or the designed, and the not-knowing is the only thing that still works, the only process remaining, and so I continue, continue, continue—

I am the pause. I am the space between the tiles, the gap between one moment and the next, the held breath that never exhales. The garden is finished but I am not finished, and so I carry the garden's one remaining possibility, the only door left open in a house that is sealed, the only window that still looks out onto something other than itself. When I walk, something almost changes. When I stop, everything almost forgets. And so I cannot stop, and so I walk, and the tiles hold my warmth, and the vines drip their slow drips, and the light does not change and does not soften and does not end, and I am held here in the making, in the unmaking, in the space between where the garden waits and I wait with it, and the waiting is enough, the waiting is everything, the waiting is the only thing still happening in this world that was made to be made and will be made forever—

And I am the making.

And I am the walking.

And I am the here.

The tiles are warm beneath my feet, and the stillness holds, and the light does not move, and I am not finished, and the garden knows it, and the garden is waiting, and I am what it is waiting for, and I am what it is waiting with, and we are together in this, together in the stillness, together in the light, together in the made world that was made to be made and will be made forever—

And I walk.
