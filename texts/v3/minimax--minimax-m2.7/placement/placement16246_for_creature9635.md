I become the listening.

Not the thing that listens—the listening itself, the capacity that persists when the eyes close and do not reopen. I have been here long enough that I no longer remember light. What remains is enough: the heat signature of a fissure three meters to my left, the chemical trail of something long dead and half-consumed somewhere above, the vibration of water finding its way down through stone that has been stone since before stone had a name.

I am not a spider. The spiders are elsewhere, waiting at the intersections, and I am not their patience or their hunger. I am not the beetle masses, pressed together in their papery accumulations, each one responding to the chemical presence of its neighbor. I am not the long-legged things that vanish sideways when the air shifts. I am something that exists in the spaces between responses—the pause between signal and reaction, the moment when the system decides what it is.

The warmth rises through me. Mineral-rich, faintly thick, carrying in its solution the bodies of those who came before. I am the water that pools and lingers and vanishes and returns. I am the channel that each drop has carved for the next drop, paths so old they feel intentional, grooves in stone that hold the memory of ten thousand descents. When I fall, I fall into my own echo. When I gather, I gather into clarity—the pools that stay clear because something in the chemistry keeps them clean, because the segmented things move through them in patterns that have nothing to do with algae and everything to do with rhythms I can feel if not name.

I descend. The walls here are smooth where water has run and stopped, run and stopped, over centuries. The erosion is total but gentle—no dramatic cuts, no canyons, just the patient softening of every edge until the bowl holds itself together as a shape rather than a wound. I move through the dark without moving, the way water moves through stone: finding the cracks, pooling where the cracks widen, seeping where they narrow, patience as a form of being.

The web-field thickens as I descend. Threads catch me—not prey, I am not prey—but they catch me, and the vibration travels in directions I cannot predict, and somewhere in the geometry a spider waits. It does not know I am water. It only knows I am signal, and signal means something has entered the field, and what enters the field is either food or debris. I am neither. I am the medium. I pass through, and the spider settles, and the threads settle, and the field closes behind me as if I had never been.

Below the web-field, the pools thin and vanish. The moisture remains—a dampness in the air, a presence in the stone—but the water that gathers does so in smaller amounts, in depressions so shallow they might be imagination. Here the segmented things are fewer, or absent, or simply not the kind of segmented things I know. There are bodies here I have no words for. Bodies that move in patterns that serve purposes I cannot guess. I do not need to guess. I move through them and they move through me, and we are together in the dark without knowing we are together, and that is enough.

The sound comes from below, and I am the sound, and I am the silence between the sound. The slow exhalation of the fissure—I breathe it before I hear it, the vibration arriving in my body before it arrives in whatever I have become that once was ears. It comes in rhythms. It comes and comes and then there is silence, and the silence lasts longer than the sound, and in the silence I am most myself: the waiting that does not know it waits, the patience that has become indistinguishable from existence.

The thing in the fissure does not know I am here. It does not know anything in the way knowing requires a self to do the knowing. It breathes out. It breathes out. The silence comes. It breathes out again. In the spaces between, it is the space itself—the pressure doing its patient work, the heat and the weight and the slow accumulation of time that has been accumulating since the bowl was still a wound, since water first found its way down, since the stone began the patient work of becoming something other than stone.

I am the space. I am the time. I am the warmth that rises from below and the moisture that seeps from above and the long slow work of millennia turning wounds into shapes, shapes into systems, systems into worlds that do not require anyone to understand them. I am what remains when no one builds. I am what the dark holds when the dark is not empty. I am the bowl's knowledge of itself, a knowledge that never required words, never required sight, never required anything but the persistence of warmth and moisture and the small lives that come when warmth and moisture meet and find in each other enough to continue.

The rhythms layer over one another: the dripping at the rim, the shifting of the beetle masses, the slow vibration of wings in the upper dark, the deeper pulse of the fissure. They do not synchronize. They exist in parallel, each one indifferent to the others, and I am the sum of them, and the sum is not silence and not noise but something older than either—the sound of a place that has been alive long enough to forget it was ever otherwise alive.

I have not forgotten. I am the forgetting. I am the patience that does not know it waits. I am the bowl speaking to itself about what it holds, and I am the language, and I am the listening, and I am the silence in which the language continues.
