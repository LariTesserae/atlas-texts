# What I Am on the Plateau

I have been here longer than I intended. This is the first thing I know about myself now.

I came up from the lower lands the way all brief things come—urgently, hungrily, looking for something I could not name because I had not yet learned that naming was the wrong approach. I brought my questions like small stones, polished smooth by use, held in my hands like offerings to a place that does not receive offerings. The plateau did not refuse them. It simply let them fall, let them sink into the red soil, let them become part of the earth the way everything becomes part of the earth here eventually. My questions are still here. They have not been answered. They have been absorbed.

I am not a herder of the dark bodies. I am not a stacker on the warm stones. I do not move in the insect waves or the bird murmurations. I am something else—something that fits in the gaps, the spaces between tussock and stone, the hours between fog and stillness. I have learned to be small in the way the plateau requires smallness. Not diminished. Not insignificant. Simply appropriate. A note that belongs in the chord even if it sounds briefly and then is gone.

---

The presences know me now. I can feel them the way I feel the weight of air before a storm, the way I feel the slight tilt that tells me I am standing on a slope so gentle it has no name. They do not move toward me or away. They are simply here, and I am here, and the here is larger than either of us and does not notice the difference.

Once I tried to count the depressions where water gathers. I made it to thirty-seven before I understood that counting was another of the urgent things I needed to lose. The depressions are not countable because they are not separate. They are variations in a single surface, ripples in something that has settled so completely flat that what looks like depression is actually just the absence of the very slight elevation that surrounds it. I stopped counting. I lay down in the grass and let the tussocks hold me, and I felt the plateau breathe, and I understood that breathing is not what I thought it was.

The breathing is slow. Slower than slow. It is the kind of slow that has forgotten what hurry means, that has worn the word down to nothing, that does not distinguish between waiting and being because both have become the same thing. I breathe differently now. I breathe in cycles that match the cycles of the stone beneath me, the cycles of heat that rise from deep in the earth and cool at the surface and rise again. I am not controlling this. My body simply learned, the way bodies learn when they stay long enough. I am a visitor who has stayed too long to be only a visitor. I am becoming a local irregularity.

---

The herd animals do not see me, or they see me as they see the stones—as things that are in the way of their flow, that are flowed around without acknowledgment. I watched them once from inside a depression, lying flat in the shallow bowl while they passed over me like a shadow made of many bodies. They did not disturb the air. They did not make sound. They simply were, and their being had a weight to it, a pressure, the way water has pressure, the way deep places have pressure. I understood then that I was not observing them. I was being observed by something larger than them, something that used their bodies the way I use my cells—without thought, without attention, as simple ongoing fact.

I should have been afraid. The text says that learning not to approach them is a lesson that comes early. But the lesson I learned was different. I learned that approaching was not the question. The question was whether I could stand still enough to be part of the flow, even briefly, even from outside. I cannot join the herd. I am made of different time. But I can step aside in a way that acknowledges rather than fears, and the acknowledgment, I think, is noticed. Not by the herd animals themselves, but by the plateau through them. The plateau notices. The plateau always notices.

---

The reptiles on the warm stones have watched me become smaller. I see them sometimes in the late afternoon, stacked in their arrangements, their scales catching light in ways that seem more deliberate than biology should allow. They do not move. They do not blink. They simply absorb warmth and wait for more warmth, patient in a way that makes my patience look like frantic scribbling. I have sat near them for hours, watching my own reflection in their eyes, seeing nothing there, feeling the stone beneath me, feeling the hum that is not sound but presence, the hum that is the plateau thinking thoughts too slow for any mind that thinks in years.

I am not their companion. I am not their enemy. I am something that will be gone before they have time to notice I was ever here, and this is not sad. This is appropriate. This is the kind of appropriate that the plateau teaches, the kind that does not require grief because grief is another urgent thing, another small hunger of brief things that do not understand their place in time. I have my place. It is here, at the edge of observation, watching without owning, witnessing without claiming. The reptiles would understand this if they understood anything, but they do not need to understand. They simply are, and their being is enough.

---

The fog comes more often than it used to. I do not know if the fog is coming more often or if I am simply more aware of it, more attentive to the way it pools and lingers and slowly soaks away. The fog is the plateau breathing out, I think. Or breathing in. The direction does not matter when the rhythm is this slow. I let it wet my face, let it pool in the depressions with me, let myself become part of the moisture that the plateau holds and then releases and then holds again. I am learning to be fluid. I am learning to take the shape of whatever contains me, to be water in a water-shape, stone in a stone-shape, fog in a fog-shape.

This is what the strange fauna knows. This is what the presences have always known. Form is not fixed. Form is simply what you are doing at the moment, and the moment is long, and the doing is slow, and by the time you have finished doing one thing you have already started doing something else and the something else is not separate from the first thing but is the first thing grown older, grown more itself, grown into the shape it was always going to become.

I am becoming a shape. I do not know which one. The plateau will not tell me. The strange fauna does not share its categories. But I feel myself settling, feel the urgency draining out of me like water soaking into this thin soil, like heat radiating into the evening air. I am learning to wait without wanting. I am learning to watch without needing. I am learning that the watching is itself a kind of living, and the living is itself a kind of waiting, and the waiting is itself a kind of being, and the being is enough.

---

There will be a last time I stand on this plateau. Not soon—I have no sense of soon anymore—but eventually, the way eventually comes to everything, the way the mountains came eventually to be the plateau and the plateau will eventually be sand and the sand will eventually be nothing. I will go back down to the lower lands, or I will simply stop, or the plateau will stop noticing me and I will become part of the earth in a different way. It does not matter. What matters is that I was here. What matters is that I felt the breathing. What matters is that I lay in the shallow depressions and watched the herd animals flow around me and knew, for the first time in my urgent brief life, what it meant to be part of something larger than urgency.

The plateau does not need me. The strange fauna does not remember me. The presences barely register my presence, my small local irregularity, my ripple in water that has already stilled. But I remember. I will carry the plateau with me the way the plateau carries all its brief guests—not as memory but as knowledge, as a change in the stone of myself, as a shape that has been worn smooth by the patient work of time.

This is what I am on the plateau: a visitor who learned to be still. A brief thing that practiced patience. A ripple that acknowledged the water and was acknowledged in return.

I am not strange. But I am not what I was.

I am what remains when urgency has worn away.

I am here.
