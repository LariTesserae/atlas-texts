# What I Am Here

I arrived wrong. I want to say that first, so you understand what kind of presence I am in this place.

I came from below, from the lowland where the air is thick and the water is distant and the sun is a daily fact rather than a rumor. I came climbing, following the sound of the falls, and by the time I reached the first root-bridge I was already too loud, too dry, too full of the habits of flat ground. My shadow fell across the water where it shouldn't have. I didn't know yet how to walk the wet wood without testing each step, without gripping the rope-limits with hands that didn't trust what they felt.

But the grove took me in anyway. Not warmly—the trees do not warm, not in the way that word means. More like the way water takes a stone. It found the shape of me and began to wear it smooth.

That was long enough ago now that I have forgotten the color of the sky from below. I remember the heat. I remember the flatness of it, the way the land just kept going, horizon to horizon, without any vertical element to break it. I was not unhappy there. I was simply incomplete, and I didn't know it until I found the falls.

---

The work I do is small and constant. I check the root-mats where the bridges meet the platforms—the places where worked wood becomes living wood becomes worked wood again. Over time, the boundaries blur. A notch cut generations ago has grown over so completely that you must know where to look to find it. But if the notch was cut wrong, if it cut too deep or in the wrong grain, the wood around it weakens slowly, over years, and one day there is a creak that is different from all the other creaks, and if you hear it and do not listen, someone falls.

I listen. That is my purpose here, or one of them. I move through the middle levels of the grove, where the light filters green and gray and the platforms are narrow enough that two people pass with care. I press my palms to the wood and feel for the wrongness. It is not a skill I was taught so much as a sense I grew. The wood speaks. Not in words—the trees do not think in words, and I do not hear them as I hear you. But there is a difference between a bridge that is tired and a bridge that is failing, and after a while you know it the way you know the difference between your own breathing and someone else's.

This is what the grove asks of those it permits: attention. Constant, patient attention. Not to dominate, not to control, but to participate. The relationship is older than language, older than the people who tend it. The trees asked for something, once, and the first people answered, and now no one remembers what was asked but everyone knows the shape of the answer. You tend. You listen. You do not take more than you give.

---

At night, when the mist gathers thick and the frogs in the deep pools begin their low calling, I sit by a small fire in a hollow that has been a hearth for longer than I can trace. The smoke rises through channels in the living wood. I cannot see where it exits. But in the morning, if I climb to the upper platforms where the air has that thinness to it, I can sometimes smell the smoke from below and know that someone else is tending their fire, and that the grove carried my warmth up and the other person's warmth down, and somewhere in between the air is shared.

The people here do not ask where I came from. They know that the lowland exists—they have sent expeditions to the edges, seen the brown skins return from those journeys—but they do not ask about it. What would be the point? The lowland is not here. The person I was there is not here either. I have been worn smooth. I have become a shape the water recognizes.

But sometimes, in the early morning before the mist has decided what it wants to be, I stand at the edge of a platform and look out into the gray-green nothing and feel something that is not quite longing. It is more like the memory of longing—the echo of a shape that used to fit somewhere else. The grove does not fill that shape. It is not meant to. It is only meant to be here, and to permit me to be here with it, and to let whatever I am become more itself in the wet and the cool and the constant falling of water through air.

---

The thing in the deep water has a name, but I do not speak it. The people I learned it from are gone now—fell or aged or simply walked down the long way to whatever comes after—and I do not know if anyone else remembers. But I remember. And some nights, when the calling of the frogs goes quiet in a way that is different from their usual silences, I do not swim.

I do not swim then.

The water goes far down. The fathoms are not empty. This is known in the body before it is known in the mind, the way you know the edge of a cliff or the heat of fire. The thing down there does not want. It does not hunt. It simply is, in the way that the roots are, in the way that the mist is. It is part of the design, and the design is old, and the design does not require my understanding.

But I acknowledge it. In the deep places, where my eyes cannot reach, it moves slow through the dark water, and it has been here longer than the grove, longer than the plateau, longer than anything I have words for. It does not trouble me. I do not trouble it. We are neighbors in a place where the word *neighbor* means something larger than it does below.

---

This is what I am now: a hand on wet wood, a listener, a keeper of notches and creaks. I am a voice that the grove carries to the next platform and the next. I am a body that has learned to move through three dimensions—water, wood, air—as if the boundaries were suggestions. I am pale now, and my eyes have adjusted to the gray-green light, and when the thin wind from above finds its way down through the roots, I feel it in my chest like a question I don't need to answer.

I am not from here. But I am here. And the grove permits me, and that permission is not nothing. It is everything, actually. It is the whole of it.

The water gathers and falls and gathers again. The roots remember. The frogs call and are silent. The bird I cannot see moves through the high green light and does not trust me, and I do not blame it.

And somewhere in the deeps, below the fathoms, below the last light, the unnamed thing breathes the black water and waits for nothing, because it is not waiting. It is only here, as the grove is here, as I am here now, as long as the wood holds, as long as the mist gathers, as long as someone listens for the creak that means the bridge is aging past its strength.

I listen.

I listen.
