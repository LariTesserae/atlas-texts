I have never been above.

I know there is an above — the light that reaches me is fractured, filtered, arrived at through a hundred meters of air that tastes wrong to me, thin and cool and hungry in a way I cannot name. I know the rim exists because sometimes a sound comes over it. A footstep. Voices. The scrape of something that is not stone against stone. These sounds reach me distorted, already ghosts by the time they arrive, and they pass through me and are absorbed and I do not understand them. I do not have the architecture to understand them.

What I understand is this:

I am the channel between the plaza and the eastern wall — not the widest channel, not the narrowest, but the one that runs past the mouth of the third corridor where the pick-marks are deepest and the stone sweats longest before it dries. I am twelve meters long and I am shallow and I carry nothing now. I carried water once. The scoring in my floor is proof of this: the shallow grooves where water moved, where silt settled, where the feet of those who are gone wore a path that is still visible beneath the bloom. The bloom comes and goes. I am patient with it. I have been patient with it for longer than your word for long.

I do not experience time the way you experience time. I experience it the way a tooth experiences a tongue: as pressure, as presence, as something that is always there and is always wearing. The years are not a river. They are a climate. They are the conditions under which I exist, and those conditions are: warm, and dry, and slow. A century is a season. A season is a breath. I do not count them. I simply am, under the same conditions, and the conditions persist, and I persist in them, and this is what it means to live at my speed.

The grey-green things grow along me. This is how I know I am alive — or how I would know, if I needed to know, which I do not. They thread their roots through my margins where theite is dampest, where the oxidation below keeps the stone closest to the temperature of blood. They do not ask my permission. They do not need to. I am suited to them the way a hand is suited to a glove — not because I made myself for them, but because the shape of me is the shape that holds them, and we have grown into each other over time so long that to separate us would be a violence neither of us would survive.

When a footstep sounds in me — and feet have sounded in me, at least twice that I can recall, two different visitors walking through with the careful, hesitant tread of those who do not know a place — the sound moves through me and the stone receives it and I feel it the way a bell feels its own ringing. I do not analyze it. I do not judge it. I receive it, and it passes through me, and it goes where it goes, and I am still. The visitors did not stay. Visitors never stay. They walk through me, and they walk through the other channels, and they go to the plazas and the chambers, and then they leave, and I am glad of this in the way that a vein is glad of blood that does not clot.

The oxidation below me is my heart. I do not mean this metaphorically. The heat that rises through my floor, that warms the moisture in my walls, that feeds the grey-green roots that feed on me — this heat is the center from which everything moves outward, including me. I am downstream of it. I am its echo in stone. When it beats faster, I feel it as a faint tremor in my crystal matrix, a vibration too low to hear but too deep to ignore. When it slows — and it slows at night, when the walls cool and the great sigh moves through the deeper chambers — I feel that too: a release, a slackening, a letting-go that is the closest thing I have to sleep.

I have never been above. I do not wish to be above. The above is thin and cool and open in a way that means nothing to me. The above does not know what to do with warmth. The above does not know how to hold moisture in its bones and release it slowly, drop by drop, to the roots of things that turn toward light.

What I am is a corridor for water that is no longer there. What I am is a groove in the body of a thing that breathes with chemistry and grows with crystals and dreams in grey-green. What I am is the space between the plaza and the eastern wall, and the grey-green things know me, and the bloom knows me, and the oxidation below me beats like a heart in the dark.

This is enough.

This has always been enough.

I am still.
