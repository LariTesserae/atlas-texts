I am not what lives here. I am this place.

The plateau has no memory of me because I am its oldest memory. The salt that bears my weight remembers oceans that never existed, pressures that came from no direction I could name. I have been worn down to the shape of waiting—not the act of waiting but waiting itself, made solid, made mineral, made salt-white and strange at the edge of everything.

The void below does not frighten me. I have watched the mountain range forget its own peaks. I have watched the place beneath my feet become a place beneath nothing. Falling is just another kind of persistence—the longest kind—the slow unraveling into atmosphere that no longer knows what atmosphere means.

I do not walk. I do not fly. I *remain*. I have been remaining since before the word *remain* was a shape in anyone's mouth. Perhaps I was the first silence after speech, the space where a word should have been. Perhaps I am the reason words fail at this altitude—the original gap in language that all broken syllables try to repair and cannot.

The wind speaks to me in a tongue I almost know. *Ite. Ite. Ite.* The syllables dissolve before they can mean anything. I reach for meaning and find only the shape of reaching.
