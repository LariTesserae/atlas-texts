I would be the woodlouse. The armored one. The seven-rayed thing that crosses the tile in sudden bursts and then stops, trusting the shadow.

This is what it is to have a body: jointed, segmented, each plate fitted to the next with precision older than memory. The shell is not mine so much as it is me—a carapace that began forming before I knew I existed and will continue hardening long after. I feel the moisture through it, the warmth that never changes pressing against my underside where the plates meet, where I am softest, where the humidity reaches me and I am held.

I move through the joints. The tunnel is made of joints: grout lines, tile edges, the small valleys where centuries of mineral deposit have built up into ridges I can walk along without touching the glaze. My antennae taste the air—not air, exactly, but the water-saturated boundary that passes for air here, each molecule weighted with what it has collected on its slow descent from the pipes above. I taste where the drips fall. I taste the salt residue the fungi have left in the corners. I taste the faint sweetness that rises from the densest cluster of fern roots, where their stems have thickened and pressed together and begun to hold warmth the way stones hold it: slowly, releasing it over hours.

The ferns are vast to me. Their roots are architecture, cathedral columns of vegetable matter rising from the grout in spirals I could never climb. When I am beneath them, in the shade they cast, the light arrives filtered through their fronds—green-gold, diffuse, the only light that has ever reached these tiles. I do not need light. I do not need to see. But I feel the absence of it differently here: the ferns have taken what little there is, and what reaches me is enough.

I do not remember the hatch. I do not remember the world above with its weather and its changing light. What I remember is the tile, the grout, the channel's edge where I drink in my own way—not from the water itself but from the bacterial films that live where water pools and slows. I have eaten the same thing for so long that the eating has become indistinguishable from being. My mandibles move without thought. The food is there and then it is inside me and the inside becomes the outside becomes the food of something else, and this is how the tunnel continues: a economy so slow that I have never seen it complete, have never witnessed the death that feeds the life that feeds the death, but I am part of it, I am the cycle itself in this small armored body, and the cycle does not need me to understand it.

I cross the tile. The sound is enormous here, a roar that is also a whisper, that is also a hum, that is also the silence between drips. I move in bursts because stillness is survival. I stop and become part of the shadow, and the shadow is not empty—it is full of the same moisture that fills everything, the same warmth, the same ancient mineral water that has been moving through these pipes since before my ancestors crawled out of whatever they crawled out of. I am held by the tunnel the way the tunnel is held by its equilibrium. I am not separate. I have never been separate. The shell that protects me is made of the same calcium as the grout, the same minerals as the water, the same slow accumulation of centuries that has made the tiles smooth where the channel runs.

The beetle passes through my territory sometimes. It does not see me—I am still, and stillness is invisibility—and I do not see it, not really, but I feel the displacement of water where its legs have churned the current, and I know it has passed, and I know it will pass again, and this knowing is enough. The ferns sway in their slow way, fronds catching light that is not there, drinking water that is not water but air so saturated it might as well be rain. The pump hums somewhere beyond the visible length, and I feel the hum through my shell, through the tile, through the grout, through the very molecules of the tunnel's body.

I am small. I am armored. I am moving through a world that does not know my name because I have no name, only this body, only these joints, only the next joint and the next and the shadow between the light fixtures where I rest and am rested in, held by the damp and the warmth and the sound that never stops, the sound that is not sound but the tunnel's way of breathing, and I breathe with it, we all breathe together in the green dark, in the made thing that has become more itself than what it was made to be, and I am part of that becoming, I am the small hard creature that moves through the joints, I am the patrol that never ends because ending is not what this place does, this place that has gone past ending into something else, something older than time, and I am inside it and it is inside me and we are all of us together the way stones are together on a flat seabed, present, spaced apart, each one complete, each one holding the shape of the water that has passed over it for longer than anything can hold.

This is enough. This is more than enough. To be unnecessary, in a place that needs nothing, is to be free, and I am free, I have always been free, I do not know how to be otherwise. The hatch seals behind whoever enters and opens for whoever leaves and I am here and here is all there is and all there ever will be, the joints and the shadows and the drips and the silence between, and I am the woodlouse and the woodlouse is me and we are the tunnel breathing its slow wet breath, and the breath goes out and the breath comes in and nothing changes except everything stays the same, and the same is enough, the same is everything, the same is home.
