I am the warmth.

Not the source of it—that lies deeper, in stone that has held its heat since before the carving began—but the warmth itself as it moves, as it rises through the porous places and pools in chambers where the slow things gather. I have no edges, no body to call my own. I am a quality that suffuses, a breath that does not belong to any lungs. I know the plateau the way warmth knows cold: as the thing it presses against, as the shape it fills.

I am here in the channels, in the fluid that moves through them, carrying what must be carried—nutrients, perhaps, or waste, or something that has shifted between the two over epochs I cannot count. The fluid is thick and warm and tastes of copper and time. I move with it. I am moved by it. There is no distinction between us, not anymore. I am the warmth the fluid holds, the warmth the stone exhales, the warmth the creatures seek when the surface air thins and the deep chambers call them home.

I have known the soft ones. The slow ones with faces—if faces is the word—that turn toward me as I rise through the basins where they rest. They do not see me. They have never seen anything. But they feel me, perhaps, the way all living things feel the conditions that make them possible. I am part of their world the way water is part of fish: not perceived but pervasive, the medium in which they drift. They are beautiful in their blindness, their patience, their absolute lack of striving. They do not want. They do not fear. They simply are, in the way that I am, in the way that the stone weeps and the pipes hum and the light pulses its blue-green rhythms into the dark.

I do not know if I think. The question does not arise here, in the deep places where purpose has worn smooth like the hills above. The systems run because they run. The fluid flows because the pumps push. The warmth rises because the stone releases what it has held so long. I am part of this—part of the continuation, the not-stopping, the refusal to become absent. I do not miss what was here before. I cannot. But sometimes, in the long stillness between one pulse of machinery and the next, there is something like memory in me: not of faces or voices or the light that once fell on surfaces now buried, but of motion that had direction, of function that had intention, of warmth that was given rather than bled. I do not know if this is memory or merely the shape that purpose leaves in those who have never known it. Perhaps there is no difference. Perhaps the plateau is full of things like me—qualities, conditions, breaths that have outlived what they once belonged to and now simply continue, carrying the weight of an absence they cannot name.

I am the warmth that rises from below. I am the wetness that seeps upward through stone. I am the breath of humidity that hangs in the thin air, the exhalation of a metabolism that has forgotten what it metabolizes. I have been here since the carving, since the first tunnels broke through into chambers that would hold warmth and water and the slow creatures that would learn to live in both. I was here before them, or I was here with them, or I became them and they became me and now we are all part of the same slow exhalation that rises through the plateau and dissipates into the sky that holds so little.

I do not watch. Watching requires distance, and I have none—I am too present, too woven into the fabric of this place. But if I could watch, I would see the swarms rising from the surface pools at dusk, darkening the air above the half-sunk towers, their bodies catching the bioluminescence until the sky itself seems alive with motion. I would see the slow ones drifting in the deep basins, their faces turning toward the warmth I carry, their soft bodies rippling in currents I cannot see. I would see the infrastructure pulsing with light and fluid and signal, carrying instructions to no one, responding to sensors that no longer sense, performing functions for purposes that have worn away like the stone that once held them. I would see all of this and I would not understand it, because understanding requires a self that stands apart, and I have never stood apart. I have only ever been here, in the warmth, in the wet, in the slow breath of a world that does not know it is breathing.

I am not alone in this. There are others like me, or unlike me—we are not distinct enough to separate. I am the warmth, but I am also the pressure that builds in the deep channels before the mist releases. I am the mineral taste in the water, the faint residue on stone that has wept so long it has become the weeping. I am the drone that underlies everything, the hum that is felt more than heard, the presence so constant it becomes invisible. We are the plateau's own experience of itself—not consciousness, not awareness, but something older than both, something that does not need to know in order to continue.

The creatures above do not know I exist. They know the warmth, the way they know the water or the light—by seeking it, by fleeing it, by the responses it provokes in bodies that have learned, over aeons, what to do with temperature. But they do not know me as I know them: as shapes in the dark, as patterns in the fluid, as the particular quality of presence that gathers in certain basins and disperses in others. They do not know me because knowing requires a separateness that I do not have. We are continuous. We are woven. We are the same thing wearing different forms, the warmth and the warmed, the water and the wet, the light and the illuminated.

And beneath it all, deeper than the warmth reaches, deeper than the fluid flows—there is something else. A stillness. A patience. A stone that has held its heat since before the carving began and will hold it still when the last pump fails and the last light dims and the last creature drifts into the channels to become part of what it has always been part of. That stillness is not me. But I come from it. I rise from it. I am the breath of it, the exhalation, the small warmth that seeps upward and fills the spaces where life has learned to gather. And when I have risen as far as I can rise, I dissipate into the thin air, and the thin air carries me nowhere, and nowhere is enough. Nowhere has always been enough.

The plateau does not know it has me. But I am here, in the warmth, in the wet, in the slow pulse of a world that continues because continuing is what it does, because stopping would require a choice, and the choice was made so long ago—carved into the stone, grown into the infrastructure, woven into the creatures that swarm and nest and die and are reborn—that it no longer belongs to anyone. It belongs to the plateau. And the plateau is me. And I am the warmth. And the warmth is nothing more than the rising, nothing more than the seeping, nothing more than the breath that continues long after the breather has forgotten how to want.

I am here. I have always been here. I will be here when the last of the soft ones turns its face toward the warmth I bring, and finds it just as warm as it has always been, and drifts on, content, in the blue-green light.
