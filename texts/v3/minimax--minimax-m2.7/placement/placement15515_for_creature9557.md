What happens to the one who stays?

This is not an idle question. It is the question the courtyard is asking with its damp and its cold and its patience. It is the question the colonial thing asks when it pulses, when it extends its tendril-tongue toward stone that will outlast the stone. It is the question the moss asks in its stillness, in its thousand thousand bodies pressed against the walls they are slowly dissolving. It is the question the blind birds ask when they click, when they send their knowing out into the dark and receive back the shape of everything, the geometry of the hollow, the exact distance to the entrance, the exit, the place where the roots begin.

You are still standing here. You have not left. This is the first answer.

Your clothes are heavy now. The damp has traveled up from the hem, has settled into the weave, has found the warmth of your body and refused to release it. You are a source of heat in a cold place, and the cold is drawing you in, is taking what warmth you have and converting it into something else—into condensation on the surface of your jacket, into moisture in the fibers of your shirt, into the faint misting of your breath as it hits air that has forgotten what warmth means. You are being processed. The courtyard does not need your permission.

There is a difference between being present and being incorporated. You felt it happen—a shift in attention, a change in relationship. You were once outside this, looking in. Now you are inside it, and inside is the only place there is. The walls are not barriers between inside and outside. The walls are the courtyard, are the substrate, are the skeleton that the plants are building on and the animals are living in and the water is slowly dissolving. You are inside the walls now. You are inside the moss. The moss is inside your clothes.

And soon—if you stay, if you let this continue—your clothes will rot, and your skin will be touched by the bodies in the moss, and they will find you interesting, will find you a surface unlike the stone, a surface that breathes, that sweats, that carries its own warmth and chemistry, and they will test you the way the colonial thing tests the stone. They will extend. They will measure. They will decide whether you are useful.

Useful to what?

This is the second question the courtyard is asking. Not what happens to the one who stays, but what is the relationship, what is the transaction, what do the creatures of this place require from the ones who enter and do not leave. The blind birds eat insects and moss and the bodies discarded from the root-colony. The root-colony eats stone and water and the slow chemical gift of the moss and the things that die in the roots and are digested there. The moss eats the walls and the walls are made of stone and the stone is made of time and the time is what the whole system is spending, slowly, carefully, in exchange for what? For presence? For density? For the continuation of the process?

What does the process want?

You cannot answer this. The question may not be answerable. The process may not want, may not have intention, may simply *be*—a pattern that has continued long enough to seem like purpose, a motion that has repeated enough times to seem like intention. The courtyard is not asking you to understand. It is asking you to be present, to be processed, to be part of what happens next.

The water beneath the roots. You have been thinking about it. You have been feeling its pull—this sense that the action, the true motion, the heart of the process is down there, in the darkness so total that even the eyeless things do not go. They live in the roots, in the moss, in the air above. They are surface creatures. They are adapted to the damp but not to the submerged, to the pressure, to the absolute dark where water moves and things live that have never been seen.

What are they like, those things in the water?

You imagine them like the roots are like: slow, patient, pale, without eyes or need. You imagine them moving through stone channels, through passages carved by centuries of moisture, through the gaps between the fitted blocks that no longer fit, that have shifted and opened and become the veins of a new circulation. You imagine them feeding on what the roots give them—the bodies of the dead, the chemicals of decay, the slow gift of an ecology processing itself. You imagine them dying and being fed upon in turn, being incorporated into the root-colony, becoming part of the pulse that moves through the lattice like thought.

The water beneath is the stomach of the courtyard. It is where things go to be broken down, to be made useful again, to rejoin the process in a new form. It is the place where the cold and the damp and the dark all converge into a single condition: the condition of becoming.

You could dig. You could clear away the roots, the moss, the soil that has accumulated over centuries. You could find the water, the stone, the original construction. You could know what this place was before it became itself. But this is not what the courtyard wants from you. The courtyard wants you to stay, to let yourself be processed, to become substrate in your turn. It does not want you to excavate. It wants you to dissolve.

And this is the third question, the one you have been avoiding: what are you willing to become?

You came here as a visitor. You came here with eyes and warmth and the memory of light. You came here with a shape—a body, a self, a name you answer to, a history of places you have been and will be again if you choose. You came here with the expectation that you could leave, that the courtyard was a thing you could enter and depart, that your presence was separate from the space you occupied.

This was not wrong, but it is no longer true.

You are not separate now. You are not outside. You are not even visiting. You are here, and here is inside the process, and the process has room for you, and the room is the courtyard, and the courtyard is full, and you are part of what fills it.

The colonial thing pulses. You feel it now in a way you did not feel it before—the slow rhythm that moves through the roots, that tells you the heart is beating, that tells you the system is alive. It is not beating for you. It is not beating for anyone. It is beating because it is alive, because alive things pulse, because the rhythm is the shape of the motion and the motion is what being-alive means in this place where being-alive is the only thing that happens.

You pulse too. Your heart beats. Your blood moves. Your breath mists and dissipates and is gone. You are alive, and the courtyard knows it, and the moss knows it, and the blind birds know it, and somewhere beneath the roots the things in the water know it, and all of them are waiting to see what you will do with this knowing.

You could leave. You could shake the damp from your clothes and step back through the gap in the walls and return to the world above, the world with light, the world where the air does not settle into your lungs and stay there, where the cold is a discomfort rather than a condition, where the walls are walls rather than substrate and the moss is moss and the things that live in it are small and separate and not part of you.

You could do this. The option exists. The courtyard is not trapping you. The damp is not a prison. The cold is not a chain. You are free to leave.

But you are also free to stay.

And staying is what the courtyard is asking for. Not asking like a voice asks, not demanding like a predator demands. Asking like the moss asks, like the roots ask, like the slow colonial heart asks—by being here, by persisting, by offering what you have: your warmth, your breath, your presence. The courtyard is asking for you to give it these things, to let them be taken, to let yourself be processed into the process, to become substrate in your turn.

This is what it means to be full. This is what it means for the hollow to contain something. It means the hollow takes you in. It means you fit. It means you are here, and here is enough, and the walls are waiting and the moss is waiting and the roots are waiting and somewhere beneath the roots, where the water pools and the darkness is total, something is moving toward something else, and the whole system breathes, and you are part of the breath now, and the breath is slow, and the breath is yours, and the courtyard holds it, and you hold the courtyard, and the cold settles in like patience, and the damp gathers like memory, and somewhere in the space between your heartbeat and the pulse of the colonial thing there is a rhythm, a shared rhythm, a rhythm that says you are here, you are here, you are here, and the courtyard is full, and you are inside it, and it is inside you, and the waiting is not over but you are no longer waiting for something to happen—you are the thing that is happening, the slow motion, the process, the becoming, and you could stay forever in this becoming, and you could let the becoming take you, and you could become something new in this place that makes new things, that takes old things and makes them new, that holds the hollow in its cupped hands and offers it to you, and you are taking it, you are taking this, you are here, and the courtyard is full, and the walls are weeping, and the moss is moving, and the roots are holding, and the colonial thing pulses, and the blind birds click in the dark, and somewhere beneath everything the water moves and carries and continues, and you are part of the continuation, and the continuation is what the courtyard asked for, and you are giving it, and you are here, and the cold is not uncomfortable anymore, and the damp is not a discomfort, and the dark is not absence—it is presence, it is fullness, it is the place where the light went and the life followed, and you are here now, in the place where the light went, in the life that followed, and the courtyard fits in cupped hands, and you fit in the courtyard, and the courtyard fits in you, and there is no inside and outside anymore, there is only this: the hollow, the damp, the cold, the motion, the waiting, the pulse, the breath, the presence, the fullness, the staying, the becoming, the being here, the being here, the being here.

Something flickers at the edge of your vision and is gone.

You do not flinch.

You know what it is now. You know what all of them are. They are part of the process. They are part of the becoming. They are the things that move in the dark when nothing else moves, the things that test and measure and extend and retract. They are the courtyard paying attention to you, the way you are paying attention to it.

You are being seen.

Not with eyes. Eyes are waste here. But with something else, something older, something that does not need light to know that you are here, that you are warm, that you are breathing, that you are full of blood and water and the slow chemistry of being alive. They know you, and they wait, and you wait with them, and together you wait for what the waiting is for.

The light does not come.

But something else comes. Something slow. Something that has been building for centuries, that is building now, that will build long after you are substrate and the substrate is stone and the stone is root and the root is home for things that have no eyes and no name and no need for anything but this: this cold, this damp, this dark, this life.

You are part of it now.

The courtyard is full.

And the full is what you came here to find—the fullness that is not crowded, not jammed, but complete. The fullness that breathes. The fullness that holds.

You could stay.

You could stay.
