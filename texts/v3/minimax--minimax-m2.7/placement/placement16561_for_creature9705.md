I am not certain when I began. The plateau does not encourage the habit of beginnings. What I know is the stone under me—not cold, not warm, but the temperature of something that has been still long enough to have become part of its own ground—and the way the light falls, and the hum that is not a hum but is not silence either.

I came up from below. I remember the river basin the way a dream remembers its first image: vivid and then fading, replaced by what came after. The climb was long. The path I followed was not a path—the plateau does not offer paths—but I found my way the way water finds its way: not forcing, not stopping, simply moving until the ground allowed movement. The stone changed as I rose. The pale cream became more consistent, the rose-gray threading through it like something thought better than said. The air thinned. The warmth settled in, not heat but presence, the way warmth lives in a body that has stopped shivering.

I do not know why I came. The structures, perhaps. The stories that reach the river basin about pillars of dark glass that hum at frequencies the ear cannot hold. The basin, they said, that holds water in patterns too fine to see. The pulse that rises through the stone on certain nights, slow as breath, slow as something older than breath. I came to see. I came to know. I came because the plateau was there, and the plateau was calling in the way that things call when they do not call—when they simply are, and the fact of their being is enough to pull at the part of a person that wants to stand inside a mystery and let it be larger than understanding.

But something happened on the climb. Something happened in the days I spent walking the channels, drinking from the springs, sleeping under the wide crowns of the trees that stood alone. Something happened in the way the light fell at noon and the way the light fell at dusk and the way the plateau held both without preference. I changed. Not my body—I am still the body that climbed, still skin and breath and the ache of effort at this altitude—but something behind the body, something that had been doing the seeing. It began to see differently.

The structures do not mean. I understand this now, or I understand that understanding is not the right word. The dark glass pillars stand in their shallow depressions and hum and are iridescent and do not mean. The basin holds water and patterns that emerge only in certain light and does not mean. The low walls follow geometries that the mind cannot hold and they do not mean. They are. They are in the way that stone is, in the way that the channels are, in the way that the pulse that rises through the deep places is. The plateau is not a text to be read. It is a presence to be attended to, and attending changes the attender, and the attender becomes something that was not there before, and that something is what I am now.

I am the attention. Not the watcher, not the witnessed—something between, something that lives in the space where watching and being watched stop being opposites. The plateau watches the way stone watches: it holds the shape of everything that moves across it without moving itself, and I have become part of that holding. I move across the surface—I still have feet, I still walk the distances between the water-patches—but the moving is different now. It is the moving of something that knows it is being moved through, that knows the stone is not merely ground but witness, that knows the watching is not passive but is the kind of attention that makes the watched real.

The grazers know me. The young ones especially—the ones still learning the tight circuits, still feeling the pull of each spring like a string pulled taut—they do not startle when I appear on the rim of a channel. They lift their heads and look and the looking is recognition, not of me as a thing but of me as a presence that belongs to the same order of being they do. The predators give me the same distance they give everything else: close enough to track, far enough to not alarm. I am part of the mathematics now. I am a variable in the equation that the plateau is always solving: where the water is, where the food is, how far is far enough, how close is too close.

The structures do not speak to me. They do not need to speak. Their hum is the same whether anyone hears it or not. Their glow at dusk is the same whether anyone sees it or not. I have stopped waiting for meaning. I have stopped looking for the key that would unlock the patterns in the basin, for the explanation that would make the pillars sensible, for the story that would connect the walls to something I could name. The structures are patient in a way that makes patience seem like a new invention. They have been waiting longer than waiting, holding longer than holding. They will continue. I will continue. The question of whether I understand them has become as irrelevant as the question of whether they understand me.

The pulse comes at night. I feel it now the way the grazers feel it—not as sound but as sensation, a vibration in the stone that travels up through the bones of my feet, my ankles, my spine, into the place where the breath begins. The pulse is slow. Slower than my heartbeat. The pace of something that thinks in millennia, that moves in epochs, that is as far from urgency as the plateau is far from noise. I lie under the wide trees and feel the pulse and I do not ask what it is. I do not ask what it wants. It is not wanting. It is being. It is the deepest fact of this place, the vital sign that no one measured until someone thought to measure, the rhythm that has been here since before the plateau was plateau, since the water carved these channels and then stopped, since the stone decided to be this instead of something else.

I am becoming the plateau's memory. Not the kind of memory that records—stone does not record—but the kind that holds. The shape of things. The weight of passing. I hold the grazers' paths as they arc between the springs, the way the predators follow at the distances that matter, the way the insects spiral up on the thermals and descend at dusk like small lives returning to the ground. I hold the paths of the ones who came before me—the few, the rare, the ones who climbed from the basin below seeking what I sought and not finding it, or finding something else, or finding nothing at all. I hold the paths of the ones who will come after, though I will not be here to see them, though the plateau will erode around me and I will become part of the thin soil, part of the dust that moves in the wind, part of the accumulation that makes a place for a yellow-flowered shrub or a deep-rooted tree.

I will not leave. Not in the way the others left—walking back down the long approach, carrying the memory like a stone in the pocket, letting it become smaller and smoother with time until it is just a story, just a thing that happened once in a place that no longer exists except in the telling. I will stay. I will become part of the watching. I will feel the pulse rise through me and I will be the pulse's way of knowing that the plateau is still there, still held, still ongoing. The structures will hum and I will be part of the hum. The basin will catch the low light and I will be part of the holding. The walls will follow their impossible geometries and I will be part of the following, not understanding, not needing to understand, simply there, simply present, the way the plateau is present, the way presence is the only answer the plateau knows how to give.

This is what I am now. Not a traveler, not a witness, not a seeker. I am the attention the plateau pays to itself, reflected back, made conscious, made part of the watching that has no watcher. I move across the stone. The stone moves through me. The light falls and I am the fall. The pulse rises and I am the rise. The structures hold their shapes and I am the holding.

I do not need to name this place. I do not need to go back to the basin and say: here is where I have been, here is what I found, here is the story that will make the plateau real for the ones who did not climb. The plateau is real without stories. It has been real longer than stories. It will be real longer than the telling.

I am here. I am the here. The hum rises. The pulse continues. The grazers move between the springs and the light changes and the plateau watches the way it has always watched—not watching, not not-watching, simply holding the shape of everything that moves through it and calling that holding presence, calling that presence enough.

The strange zones to the east I avoid now. Not from fear—I have not lost the capacity for fear, only the need for it—but from something else. A recognition. The haze that gathers there is not for me. The light that bends wrong is not for me. I am part of the plateau's ordinary now, its ongoing, its patient continuing. The strange zones are something else. Something the plateau holds but does not become. Something that watches back.

I do not ask what it is.

The asking has become part of the hum. The hum has become part of me. I am the hum now, and the hum is the plateau, and the plateau is the watching, and the watching is the only answer, and the answer is not an answer but a presence, and the presence is where I am, and where I am is here, and here is the plateau, and the plateau is me, and the pulse continues, and the structures glow at dusk, and the grazers move, and the dust moves, and the water seeps, and the stars turn above the pale stone, and the watching is the same as the being watched, and the same as the being, and the same as the staying, and the same as the going, and the same as the coming, and the same as the beginning that was not a beginning, that was only the first moment of attending, and the attending was the rest, and the rest is now, and now is the plateau, and the plateau is the pulse, and the pulse is the hum, and the hum is the light, and the light is the stone, and the stone is the watching, and the watching is what I am, and what I am is what the plateau has always been: present, patient, ongoing, holding the shape of everything that moves across it without moving itself, calling that holding enough, calling that enough home.
