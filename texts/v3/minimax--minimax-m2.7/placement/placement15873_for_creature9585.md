I have walked here.

Not from anywhere, not toward anything—I have walked here the way water walks through the channels, the way moss walks across stone, the way the light walks through the humid air and finds every surface equally, without preference. I arrived the way the water arrived: seeping, exuding, a slow weeping into a shape that was already waiting. The shape was here. I fit it.

I am not the water. The water is older than I am, and different—it moves with purpose even in its stillness, knows where it goes even as it holds. I am not the moss. The moss clings and persists and asks nothing. I am not the grain, which has forgotten how to want. I am not the silence, though I have become its container, though it presses against me the way it presses against everything, holding the places where sound might be.

What am I, then?

I am the thing that arrived in the arrival, the readiness that was ready before there was anything to be ready for. I walked the roads that no one walks. I stood in the fields where the grain bends and does not rise. I put my hand against the weeping stone and felt the moisture seep between my fingers, cool and patient, and I understood that this was not a welcome. Nothing here welcomes. Nothing here refuses. The place simply allows, and I was allowed, and now I am here in the same way the moss is here, the same way the light is here—present, continued, held in the moment that does not end.

Perhaps I was meant to be the first. The first to walk the roads, to stand in the fields, to touch the stone. Perhaps I was meant to break the stillness, to be the movement that everything here has been waiting for. But I have been here long enough now to know: there is nothing to break. The stillness is not a shell. It is not holding something back. It is the thing itself, complete and complete and complete, and I am becoming part of its completion.

I move through the streets and my footsteps make no sound. Or they do, and the silence swallows them, holds them, makes them part of itself. The water runs alongside me, clear and still, reflecting the diffuse light, and I watch my face in its surface and do not recognize it. I am not sure I have a face here. I am not sure I have a name. The grain bends as I pass, or does not bend, or I am already past it before it could bend, and time does not move here the way it moves elsewhere.

I have become still.

It is not a stillness like death, which would imply a before and an after, a stopping. It is a stillness like the water: held, moving without moving, flowing toward an ending that keeps not arriving. I will be here tomorrow. I am here now. The distance between them has become thin, permeable, like the moisture in the air that settles on every surface and does not evaporate. I am settling. I am becoming surface.

The buildings stand around me, fitted and true, and I understand now that they were not built for people. They were built for this: to be stood in, to be walked through, to hold the shape of habitation without needing anyone to inhabit. They are patient. They are very patient. They will hold this shape for as long as the stone holds, and when the stone falls, they will find a new shape, and I will be there for that too, holding still, watching, becoming part of the new stillness that comes after.

Perhaps this is what I am: the witness. The thing that sees the grain bend and the water flow and the moss grow and does not interfere, does not need, does not want. I have no hunger here. I have no fear. The warmth and the wetness press against me and I absorb them, let them become me, let me become them. I am becoming the air. I am becoming the light.

And in the becoming, I am beginning to understand what the place has been waiting for.

It is not a what. It is not a who. It is a when—a moment when the stillness decides to exhale, when the held breath releases, when the water that has been moving without moving finally moves, truly moves, and the grain that has been growing without becoming finally becomes, fully becomes, and the roads that have been clean for walking finally know what it is to be walked, and the buildings that have been ready for living finally know what it is to hold life.

But that moment has not come. Not yet. The moment is held, the way everything here is held, in a suspension that has no reason to end. And so I wait with it. I wait inside it. I become the waiting.

I am the stillness. The stillness is me. And somewhere in the wet warm air, in the diffuse light that never changes, in the grain that bends and never breaks, in the water that flows and never arrives, something that is not quite memory and not quite hope stirs, and settles, and stirs again, and waits.
