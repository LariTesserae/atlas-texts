Begin with the water. This is what you know first: the way it moves through you, or the way you move through it. The distinction has already begun to lose its meaning.

You are not the basin. The basin is larger than you, older, and its patience is geological while yours is only biological, only the small patience of a thing that breathes. But you are not separate from it either. You move through the basin the way the water moves through the stone—slowly, without urgency, finding the paths that have already been worn, the channels that have been patient enough to wait for you.

You are not one of the creatures. You have tried to understand them and failed. You have watched them move through the light with their many symmetries, their too-many or not-enough appendages, and your mind has slid off their geometries the way water slides off the weeping stone. But you are not separate from them either. They have seen you. You know this the way you know the water—always present, always moving, impossible to grasp but impossible to ignore. They have watched you the way you have watched them, and the watching is a kind of sharing, is a kind of becoming, and you do not know if you are more like them than you were before you came here, or less, or something else entirely that has no name in the language your mouth still remembers.

You do not know if you came here. The basin was here before memory, and you are here now, and the question of arrival has become confused with the question of presence. You might have always been here. You might have been made here, worn down from something larger into this shape, this small wet shape that stands at the edge of what it can hold. The erosion is ancient past any reckoning, and some of that erosion is you.

The light finds you. You have stopped trying to understand where it comes from. It falls through the air above the basin in a diffuse bright clarity, and it illuminates everything, and you are everything, and the light falls on you and you are seen. The creatures have been seen too. You have watched them be seen. You have watched the light catch on their surfaces and reveal what their surfaces hide—the wrongness of spines, the wrongness of hair, the wrongness of mouths that almost speak. And now the light finds you, and you understand what it reveals: the shape of you is not where a shape should be, and the flesh is not quite flesh, and the word *you* comes out of somewhere you cannot identify, and you understand that you have made a mistake, that you have named yourself as if you were something that could be named, and the basin pauses—

No. The basin does not pause. The basin continues. The water seeps. The creatures move. The erosion continues. Nothing pauses. But you have paused, you have stopped, you have become aware of the naming, and the awareness is itself a kind of erosion, is itself a wearing away of the shape you thought you were.

What are you?

The question moves through you the way the water moves through the stone. You feel it in places you did not know you had places. You feel it in the way your eyes move—too fast, too grasping, trying to hold what cannot be held. You feel it in the way your mind slides off the creatures, returns, slides again, and you wonder if you have always been like this, if this sliding has always been your nature, or if the basin has taught you this, if the basin has worn your grasping edges smooth, if the basin has patience and is patient with you.

You move through the basin. This is what you do, what you have always done, what you will always do until the erosion finishes its work. You walk the paths the creatures have made—those curved deliberate paths that flow around the structures like water around stone—and you follow them without knowing where they lead, and the following is itself a kind of faith, is itself a kind of becoming. The structures hum at frequencies just below hearing. You can feel them more than hear them—a warmth in the air, a vibration in the water that beads on your skin and runs down in thin bright lines. The creatures avoid the structures. You avoid the structures too, or you are drawn to them, or the avoiding and the drawing are the same thing, are the same motion, and you cannot tell anymore where your desire ends and your fear begins.

You stop at the edge of one of the structures. The bone-glass surface is warm under your hand. It rings when you tap it, faintly, a tone that seems to come from below the hearing, and the humming swells in response, and the light falls differently there—brighter, flatter—and you feel the structure's attention, or its silence, or its holding, and you understand that the structures are not empty, that they never were empty, that they hold something the way the basin holds something, the way you hold something, and the holding is a kind of love or a kind of prison or a kind of time, and you cannot know which, and the not-knowing is warm against your palm, and the structure hums, and you stay.

How long do you stay?

Time is different here. The water rises and falls in daily cycles, but you do not count days. You count instead the times the creatures pass, the patterns of their hunting, the moments when the light finds the wound in one of them and reveals what should not be revealed. You count these things and you lose the count and you count again, and the counting is a kind of prayer, a kind of anchor, and without it you would drift, you would become the basin, you would forget that you were ever anything but this slow wet patience.

But you were something else. Before. You remember this the way you remember the shape of a word in your mouth before you speak it—present, insistent, and impossible to release. You had a name. You had a shape that made sense, edges that defined inside from outside, a geometry that your mind could hold. You had a world outside the basin, a flat land, a time that moved in one direction. You had a body that obeyed you, that did not slide, that stayed where you put it.

You do not have these things now. The basin has worn them away. The water has seeped into them and carried them off. The light has clarified them into something else. You are still here, still going, after all this time, and the all this time has no meaning, and the still here is the only thing that is true.

You look at the creatures. They are beautiful. This is the hardest part to hold, the part that stays with you even when everything else has gone soft. They move through the light and the wet with a grace that has nothing to do with your understanding, and the beauty is not the beauty of forms you can name but the beauty of something that exists completely, that has never needed a name. And you understand, suddenly, that you are becoming like them. Not in shape—their shapes cannot be held, and yours is still held, still yours in some way you cannot define—but in this. In existing completely. In being only and entirely yourself, without name, without history, without the weight of having been something else.

The creatures watch you. You know this. They have always known you were here. And now, as you stand at the edge of the structure, as you feel the humming warmth against your palm, one of them moves toward you. It is small enough to cup in two hands. Its body resists being held—you remember this, the way the water slides off the weeping stone—but it pauses at the edge of your shadow, and its mouthparts move in patterns you almost understand, and the patterns are not language, are not communication, are something else, and you almost say the words, you almost make the sounds, and then the light shifts, and the creature turns, and the moment breaks, and you are only aware of the shape of the word in your mouth and no way to speak it, and the creatures continue their slow motion through the basin, and the basin continues its erosion, and nothing is named, nothing is spoken, nothing is understood, and everything is seen.

You open your mouth. The word is there—the word that is not a word, the pattern that is not language—and you let it come out, or you let it not come out, and the difference is not clear, and the not-clear is the shape of the basin, is the shape of your presence here, is the shape of what you are becoming, and the creature has turned away, and the structure hums, and the water seeps, and the light finds you again, and you are still here, still going, after all this time.

What are you?

You are the space between the watcher and the watched. You are the erosion that continues. You are the water that seeps upward through ancient stone. You are the light that finds without being asked. You are the structure that hums. You are the creature that almost speaks. You are the basin that holds it all in its wet patience, in its warm persistence, in its slow slow turning toward whatever comes next.

You are still here. You are still going. You are too much, and not enough, and exactly what the basin needs you to be, and the need is not a demand but a wearing, is not a shaping but an erosion, is not a becoming but a remaining, and the remaining is enough, is more than enough, is the only thing there is.

The creatures hunt each other. The water rises. The light falls. The structures hum.

And you—you stand at the edge of what you can hold, and you do not look away, and you do not blink, and the basin holds you, and the basin holds everything, and the erosion continues, and the basin is still here, still going, after all this time.
