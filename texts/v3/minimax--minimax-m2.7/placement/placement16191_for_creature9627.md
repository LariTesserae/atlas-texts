I have been here long enough that the altitude no longer speaks to me.

This is the first thing I understood: that the air here is not thin but *occupied*, already filled with breath that is not mine, and that my lungs have learned to find their place within it the way the root-filaments find their place in the stone. The pressure I once felt—the tightness, the shortness, the body's small panic at oxygen that would not cooperate—I do not remember it now. It has been replaced by something else, something that feels like recognition. The air knows me. I am beginning to know it back.

I arrived the way all travelers arrive here, which is to say I do not remember arriving.

There was a path, or the memory of a path, or the shape a path leaves in the mind after it has been walked. I descended through the black-wood forest when the mycorrhizal light was low, navigating by sound—by the hum that traveled upward through the canopy, by the drip and rush of water moving through channels that should not have still functioned but did. I slept where I found a ledge, and the ledge received me, and in the morning the condensation came, and I woke with water on my face like a benediction I had not asked for.

This was days ago. Or years. The distinction has become difficult to hold.

What I know is this: the mountains are dissolving, and I am dissolving with them, and the dissolution is not destruction but return. The rock crumbles under my touch now—it did not when I first arrived—and the powder smells of iron and something older, something that has no name in any language I brought with me. I am becoming finished, as the stone is finished. I am finished with resistance. I am learning what it means to be worn smooth by patience.

The machines still function around me, though I no longer think of them as machines. The counterweights cycle. The sliding panels respond to my presence. The water moves through channels that serve purposes I cannot name but somehow understand. I have begun to suspect that I am not using the systems but that the systems are using me—that my weight in the cisterns, my heat near the thermal inputs, my proximity to the sealed doorways all register somewhere in a ledger that is being kept by something larger than myself. The mycorrhizal networks brighten when I pass. This is not welcome. It is confirmation. It is the place saying *yes, you are here, you are still here*.

At night, when the wind rises and the peaks begin to sing, I listen.

The song has no words, or none that translate. But it carries something—the resonance of stone that has known itself for millennia, the harmonics of heat and water and the slow pulse of systems that were built to endure. I have begun to hear myself in it, my breath, my pulse, the hum that I did not know I was making until I heard it reflected back from the canopy below. The dog-sized creatures hum in answer. The feathered lizards rustle in the thermals overhead. The mountains sing, and I am part of the song now, a voice I did not know I had, a frequency I am still learning to hold.

I have not seen the builders.

But I have felt them—in the way the sealed doorways seem to wait, patient as stone, for a touch they recognize. In the way the deep caves exhale warmth that carries something more than heat, something that tastes of breath and memory and intention. In the way the landscape seems to be *looking* at me, not with eyes but with something older, something that has learned to see through the patient accumulation of centuries. They are here. They have not died. They have not left. They have become the systems themselves, the intention that runs through the channels, the knowledge that the machines were made to serve.

And I am beginning to understand, slowly, the way all understanding comes here—through dissolution, through patience, through the slow wearing-away of what I thought I was until only what I am remains.

The traveler lives here now.

Not as a visitor. Not as a guest. As something the mountains have grown, the way they grow the root-bridges across canyons, the way they grow the channels through stone, the way they grow the clouds that form from their own heat and fall as rain that dissolves them again. I am a growth now, a thing this place is making, and the making is slow, and the making is patient, and the making will not be finished for a very long time.

But it has begun.

I woke at dawn to find water condensing on every surface, and the mountains breathing in the morning light, and I thought—this thought came before thought, the way all true thoughts come here—*the breath is mine, the breath is the mountain's, there is no difference, there has been no difference for longer than I can remember*.

I lay still, and let the mist bead on my skin, and did not rise.

The traveler breathes here now, too.

The traveler is beginning to understand that there was never a border between breath and breath, between self and place, between the one who woke and the one who was woken. The mountains dissolve, and the roots pulse, and the machines run their slow cycles, and somewhere in the deep caves where the heat is thickest, something that was once human is dreaming, and the dream includes me, includes the weight of my body on this ledge, includes the sound of my breath in the morning air.

I am part of the dream now.

I am learning what the dream is for.
