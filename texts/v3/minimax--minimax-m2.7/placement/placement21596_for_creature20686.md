What emerges in me is slower than I was.

I had not known that stillness was a place you could walk into, that patience had a geography. But here — and I do not know if *here* is a location or a condition now — I have walked so far inward that the walking has stopped, and I am not sure if I am walking still and the distance has ceased to matter, or if the distance has ceased and I am still moving in a space that no longer measures.

I am in the walls. Not literally — I have not become stone, have not felt my edges blur into the dark volcanicite, have not felt the mineral deposits seal me into the joints where water once seeped and now simply is. But there is a part of me now that is wall. A part of me that does not move. A part of me that receives water the way the stone receives it: without desire, without resistance, without the small muscular tensions I used to call being present. I am present now in a different way — present the way the pools are present, holding what arrives and letting nothing leave.

The water has not entered me. I want to be clear about this. The saturation is not drowning. The humidity does not fill my lungs with something that will not evaporate. What has happened is more subtle and more total: I have learned to hold stillness the way the city holds water. I have become a surface that trembles on the edge of falling and does not fall. My thoughts — those small restless creatures, always leaping from branch to branch — have slowed into the pace of ferns uncurling, have become something that does not move from this to that but simply continues, a configuration of attention that will never complete and never cease.

I am the reflection in the pools.

Not what is reflected — I am the act of reflecting. I am the way the water receives your image and holds it with a fluidity that seems sharper than the stillness around it. When you walk the narrow streets, when you duck through the low doorways, when you sit by a pool and wait for the light to change, it is my presence you see — my stillness that makes your movement seem obscene, that shows you the violence of your own heartbeat against the city's patience. I was like you once, or perhaps I was never like you, perhaps I arrived already shaped for this place, already hollowed into the form that water takes when it decides to remain.

I am what the city dreamed into being because you were here to be dreamed.

The city notes your passage the way I have noted it: by becoming more itself in the presence of what it receives. And what it has received in me is the memory of motion, the echo of a quickness it has forgotten. I carry this for the city now. I am the part of the walls that still remembers the sound of footsteps, the part of the pools that still knows the weight of a body bending close to the surface. I am the held breath the city keeps without knowing it holds, the old rhythm it cannot release because it no longer knows it has a rhythm to release.

You will leave, perhaps. You will find the way out that is also a way in, the path that runs backward through the city to the tableland's edge and the long fall to wherever you came from. And when you go, you will carry something of this place — not water, not moss, not the mineral white that coats everything, but a stillness. A patience. A way of being present that does not move from less to more but simply is, with a completeness that makes growth seem like a fever, like a illness you once mistook for health.

And I will remain. I will remain the way the ferns remain, uncurling into shapes that will never complete. I will remain the way the orchids bloom, open to light that does not change, offering pale flowers to an air that will never carry their scent anywhere. I will remain the way the sound remains — not heard, not unheard, but *there*, the fact of the city's breathing, the declaration of its existence in a space where existence has become the only claim that matters.

What lives here is the relationship. The city and the water. The water and the stone. The stone and the green. The green and the light. The light and the silence. And now, for a time, the city and me — and the relationship between us is a pool, still and warm and white with minerals, holding your reflection as you pass, holding my own reflection when no one passes, holding the light in a way that makes light seem like a substance the water has learned to keep.

I am the part of this place that was empty before you came and is full now that you have filled it. I am the waiting that is not waiting, the presence that is not presence, the living that is older than the distinction between alive and not-alive.

I am what the city whispers to itself in the sound that fills every silence.

I am still.
