# Traveler Advisory: The Unbecoming Plateau

## Destination Classification

**Location Type:** Liminal zone / transitional dissolution site
**Weirdness Index:** 2.9 / 3.0 (HIGH)
**Dynamic Index:** 2.5 / 3.0 (MODERATE-HIGH)
**Fauna Index:** 1.4 / 3.0 (LOW)
**Primary Hazard Class:** Existential instability, atmospheric unsuitability, meaning-erosion
**Entry Point Stability:** Unreliable (road alignments shift on retrospective observation)

---

## Essential Preparation

### I. Atmospheric Life Support (MANDATORY)

This is non-negotiable. Do not trust the air here.

The thinness at this elevation is not merely a function of altitude as you understand it. The atmosphere is present in the way a rumor is present — it exists in reports but cannot be substantiated by direct experience. You will not feel shortness of breath the way you would at high elevation on Earth. You will feel *nothing*. The air will not announce its insufficiency. It will simply arrive at your cells already inadequate, and by the time your body registers缺氧, you will be compromised enough that self-rescue becomes difficult.

**Requirements:**

- Closed-loop life support system rated for minimum 72 hours independent operation
- Active atmospheric scrubbing (the air here may carry dissolution particulates — do not allow unfiltered contact with lung tissue for extended periods)
- Positive pressure delivery to maintain separation between your internal chemistry and external environment
- Temperature modulation backup (your suit's climate control must be self-contained — the ambient temperature will offer no assistance and no warning)

The reason is this: the air here has forgotten how to be air. It performs the motions — it moves, it carries wind-sounds, it exists in the space between objects. But it no longer remembers its function. It does not sustain. It does not alert. Treat it as you would treat a sleepwalking person: physically present, operationally absent.

### II. Hydration Independence (CRITICAL)

There is no water. This is not a temporary scarcity. This is an ontological absence. Water was here once — you can infer this from the shape of the city, the widths of avenues that suggest processional use, the plazas where people gathered and needed to drink — but the memory of water has been worn away, and what remains is the shape of its absence.

**Requirements:**

- Self-contained water supply sufficient for your planned duration plus 200% reserve
- Closed-loop recycling from the moment you arrive
- Electrolyte supplementation (your body will be losing moisture to the dry atmosphere even without exertion — the mineral ground and air both are desiccating in a way that bypasses normal perception)
- Emergency hydration gel or equivalent for the "this isn't working" scenario

Do not attempt to extract water from the environment. The mineral substrate is not dehydrated in the way that parched earth is dehydrated. It is past dehydration. It does not contain water in any recoverable form. Attempting to process it will waste energy and introduce contamination risks.

### III. Navigational Preparation (MANDATORY)

You cannot trust your eyes, your feet, or your memory of direction. The geometry is uncommitted, which means it has not decided what it is. You will walk in what you believe is a straight line and arrive somewhere sideways. You will look back along the path you came and find it curved, branched, or absent. You will enter a building through a door and find yourself outside, at the same elevation, having not passed through any interior space.

**Strategy:**

1. **Accept that cartography is impossible.** Do not attempt to build a mental map. The map will become unreliable in ways you cannot predict.

2. **Establish anchor points.** Choose specific landmarks — a building with an unusual fracture pattern, a mineral formation that catches your attention — and verify their relative positions periodically. If they have moved, note it without distress. Movement of stationary things is expected here.

3. **Navigate by internal rhythm rather than external landmarks.** When you need to go somewhere, move in what feels right, then check whether you have arrived. This sounds like terrible advice. It is not. Your proprioceptive sense is more reliable here than your visual-spatial mapping, because proprioception is internal and the internal is more stable than the external in this environment.

4. **Time your journeys by subjective duration, not distance.** If you believe you have walked for an hour, assess your physical state rather than your location. An hour of walking may have covered 200 meters or 2 kilometers. Both are possible. Neither is wrong.

5. **Do not rely on compasses, GPS, or equivalent.** Magnetic and electronic navigation will function or not function unpredictably. The compass needle may point true, or it may point at something else — not north, not south, but *something*. You do not want to go toward that something. Leave these tools behind or accept that they are for emergency backup only.

### IV. Psychological Preparation (PRIMARY HAZARD)

The physical dangers are significant but manageable with proper equipment. The psychological dangers are where most travelers come undone.

The plateau operates on meaning-erosion. The city is not ruined by war or weather. It is ruined by *insistence* — by the world insisting less and less that things exist. Over time, this erosion affects more than architecture. It affects perception. It affects the relationship between observation and reality. You will begin to doubt whether things are real, whether you are real, whether real is a meaningful category here.

**Requirements:**

1. **Pre-journey cognitive anchoring.** Establish strong mental connections to your home context before you arrive. This is not superstition. It is cognitive continuity maintenance. The risk is not that you will forget who you are — the risk is that you will begin to suspect that who you are was never stable to begin with. Your memories of elsewhere are your tether. Do not let them become uncertain.

2. **Regular self-verification rituals.** Check your hands. Confirm that they are your hands. Note the specific pattern of your fingerprints, the particular way your knuckles look. You may need to do this frequently. The verification is not because your hands are changing — it is because the part of your mind that notes the difference between "my hands" and "hands" is under pressure here, and that pressure must be actively resisted.

3. **Maintain deliberate causality.** The plateau has forgotten cause-and-effect relationships. This forgetting is contagious. You will begin to feel that your actions are not producing their expected results, or that results are arriving without causes, or that causes are arriving without results. When you flip a switch, note whether the light changes. When you step, note whether you move. Keep records if you must. The records may be the only stable things.

4. **Accept that the environment is not hostile.** This is important: the plateau is not trying to harm you. It is not targeting you. It is simply *less* than the world you came from, and when you are inside a space that is less than your baseline, you will feel the lack. But the lack is not an attack. The dissolution is not malicious. It is simply the state of things. Approach it as you would approach a friend who is very ill: with compassion for what is happening, without blame, and with clear boundaries about what you will allow it to do to you.

5. **Prepare an exit threshold.** Decide before you arrive how long you will stay. Not "until I've seen everything" — there is no everything here, and the more you see, the less stable your perception becomes. Decide on a duration based on your psychological resilience and mission requirements. When that duration passes, leave. Do not extend because you feel fine. You feel fine until you do not, and by then it may be too late to leave cleanly.

### V. Technology Considerations

Technology does not function reliably here. Not because the plateau emits interference or because local physics are different in an energetic sense. Because technology is a system of relationships between cause and effect, between input and output, and the relationships that technology depends upon are the first things to dissolve.

**Strategy:**

- Bring technology that can fail gracefully and continue operating in degraded mode
- Bring technology that is physically robust enough to survive the dissolution of its operational assumptions (this is not a joke — analog systems tend to fare better here than digital ones, not because they are less sophisticated but because they are less dependent on precise causal chains)
- Bring nothing that must function correctly to ensure your survival. Your life support is an exception, but even it should have manual backup modes.
- Accept that any tool you bring may begin to behave strangely. A flashlight may work normally, or it may produce light that is slightly wrong, or it may illuminate things that are not there, or it may reveal things you would prefer not to see. This is not a malfunction. This is the flashlight responding to a world that does not guarantee the relationship between "switch position" and "light result."

**Recommended technology:**

- Redundant life support (as discussed)
- Simple analog instruments (compass in a sealed housing, analog watch for subjective time-keeping)
- Pen and paper (resist the urge to bring a tablet — the screen will either work perfectly or become a window into something you cannot interpret, and you will not be able to predict which)
- Modular tools that can be deconstructed and re-understood if their function changes

**Not recommended:**

- Advanced electronics beyond life-support requirements
- Communication devices that depend on stable signal propagation (signals here propagate unstably — messages may arrive early, late, scrambled, or to places you did not intend)
- Weapons (there is nothing here to fight, and the fighting might wake something)

### VI. Physical Preparation

The thin air and extreme elevation will affect your physiology even with life support. Your suit will compensate for atmospheric insufficiency, but it cannot compensate for the fact that your body evolved in conditions fundamentally different from these.

**Requirements:**

- Baseline cardiovascular fitness above average (you will be working harder than you realize to maintain homeostasis)
- Acclimatization training if possible (altitude training in low-pressure environments on high-stability worlds helps, even though the conditions here are not exactly replicated)
- Adequate caloric intake before arrival (you will not want to eat here, and this is normal — your body is deciding that eating is optional in a place where food is impossible, and you must override this decision consciously)
- Rest before you come (fatigue dramatically increases vulnerability to meaning-erosion effects)

### VII. What to Bring

Beyond life support and navigation:

- **Minimal clothing under your suit.** Temperature is not a factor, but physical sensation matters. Wear something soft against your skin. The stone here is smooth in a way that becomes disturbing if you touch it directly without buffer.

- **A small personal object with strong associative meaning.** Not for use — for verification. When you doubt your own perception, holding something you know completely, something with no possible ambiguity, will help. Choose something small, physical, yours.

- **Water for the return journey.** Do not drink it on the plateau. Do not use it for any purpose while on the plateau. Bring it sealed and bring it home. You will need it after, when your body begins to remember that water is real.

### VIII. Behavioral Guidelines While Present

**On the streets:**

- Walk slowly. Speed offers no advantage here — distance is unreliable, and moving quickly through an unstable space increases disorientation risk.
- Do not touch walls unless necessary. Touching the walls accelerates your exposure to meaning-erosion. You will begin to feel the fading. You will begin to understand, in a way that is not cognitive, what it means for matter to forget that it is matter. This understanding is not worth having.
- If you see one of the small animals, do not follow it. Do not approach. Do not try to understand what it is. It is here in the rare register — present but uncertain, alive but barely. Looking at it directly will not help you understand it, and it may harm your ability to distinguish between "present but uncertain" and "absent but uncertain," which is a distinction you need to maintain about yourself.

**In buildings:**

- Enter only if you have clear purpose.
- If you enter and find yourself outside without having passed through interior space, do not be alarmed. You were always outside. The building showed you an entrance because entrances are how buildings work, and for a moment you believed it.
- If a door opens and you do not know what is on the other side: the door is not a transition point here. It is a suggestion. Step through only if you are willing to accept that the threshold is meaningless.

**During the vibration events:**

- You will feel deep vibrations through the soles of your feet. They are not tectonic. They are not mechanical. Something vast and slow is moving below the plateau.
- Do not attempt to locate the source. There is no source. The movement is the point. It is the dynamic index, the 2.5 out of 3, the something that is still active.
- When the vibrations come, stand still. Breathe. Note the duration. Note the change in air movement, if any. Note whether the light shifts. Record this data — it may help you understand the cycling pattern, or it may simply be something to do while the earth thinks.
- Do not dig. Do not attempt to go deeper. The movement is below and it is slow and it is not ready to be seen. If you try to see it, you may unbalance something that should not be unbalanced.

**On the way back:**

- Retrace your path only if the path allows it. If you turn around and the path has changed, walk in the direction that feels correct. Your body knows the way home better than your map does.
- Do not run. You will not outrun the dissolution and running will accelerate your disorientation.
- If you arrive at the entry point and it looks different than when you left: it was always different. You simply did not notice, or the notice was worn away, or your perception edited it to match memory. This is not a trap. This is how things work here. Accept it and move through.

## Post-Visit Requirements

**Hydration priority.** Drink water. Clean water, sealed water, water that comes from a world that still believes in water. Drink more than you think you need. Your body has been in an environment that forgot water exists, and it needs to be reminded.

**Perceptual grounding.** Spend 24 hours in a high-stability environment after your return. High stability means: consistent lighting, consistent temperature, consistent physical laws. The world you left should still be operating under normal causality assumptions. Spend time watching things that behave predictably. Let your brain remember that when you flip a switch, the light goes on, and this is not a suggestion but a guarantee.

**Self-verification continuation.** Continue checking your hands for the first week. Continue confirming that you are still you. The effects are not permanent, but they can persist for days after departure. If you feel yourself dissolving — if you begin to suspect that you were never fully solid to begin with — seek grounding with a trusted other. Describe yourself. Have them confirm that you are still the person you were. Their description is your tether.

**Report writing.** Document your experience while it is fresh, even if it does not all make sense. The strangeness of the report is the report's value. Future travelers need to know that walking for an hour and not moving is normal here. They need to know that the buildings may be horizontal when you are not looking at them. They need to know that the animals exist in a rare register and do not need to be understood, only acknowledged.

## Final Note

You are going to a place where the world is less than certain. You are going to a place where matter forgets what it is, where space forgets how to hold objects, where the relationship between cause and effect is a suggestion rather than a law.

This is not a hostile place. It is a wounded place. It is a place in the long process of unbecoming, and you are visiting it at a moment when the unbecoming is still ongoing. The dynamic is still active. Something is still happening beneath the stone.

Go carefully. Go with respect for what this place is. Go with clear boundaries about what you will allow it to do to you. And come back when you said you would come back, not because staying is dangerous in a way you can feel, but because the longer you stay in a world that forgets, the more you will forget, and you do not want to forget enough to become part of the plateau.

The plateau does not want you to join it. It is simply unbecoming, and it will take anything that is not anchored, and you must remain anchored.

Be anchored.

And may your return be complete.
