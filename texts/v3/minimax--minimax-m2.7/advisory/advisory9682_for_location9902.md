# A Traveler's Preparation Guide to the Dissolved Shore

You've read the reports. You've heard the accounts from those who returned, and from those who came back changed, and from those who left pieces of themselves behind in that gray place. Now you're actually going. Let me tell you what I wish someone had told me.

This isn't a warning you can heed from a distance. The Dissolved Shore is not dangerous the way a volcano is dangerous, or a war zone, or a city with a pickpocket problem. It's dangerous the way a long illness is dangerous—slow, cumulative, and disarming in its gentleness. By the time you realize something has gone wrong, it has already gone wrong for some time.

So prepare accordingly.

---

## On the Nature of the Place

Before we talk about equipment, you need to internalize something about The Dissolved Shore that the reports never quite convey: **the water is not the environment. The water is the default state.** You are the anomaly. You are the temporary interruption in an equilibrium that has been settling for longer than your species has had words for "land."

Everything there is dissolving. Not dramatically—nothing is collapsing or exploding—but dissolving the way sugar dissolves in tea, the way salt disappears into soup. The solid world is being slowly sugared away, and the place has grown so comfortable with this state that it no longer remembers what solidity was for. Land is a concept in retreat. Stone is a rumor. The ground pretends at solidity while testing, always, whether it might become something else.

This is why preparation matters not just for survival but for sanity. You need to understand, down in your body, that you are visiting a place that has made peace with its own dissolution—and that this peace is patient. It will wait for you to leave. It will wait for you to forget you were ever solid.

## Physical Preparation

### Clothing

**Do not** bring anything you are attached to. Everything you wear will become damp, then wet, then sodden, then something beyond wet—a condition that fabric reaches when it has stopped being fabric and started being something that merely resembles fabric while the water works through it. Cotton will rot. Synthetics will delaminate. Wool will felt into something unrecognizable.

Bring oilskin if you can find it in a multiverse that still makes such things. Waxed canvas if oilskin isn't available. The point isn't to stay dry—you won't stay dry, you cannot stay dry, the very concept of "dry" is an insult to the Dissolved Shore's identity—but to slow the saturation long enough to function. You want damp rather than soaked. Wet rather than saturated. The difference matters when you're trying to move.

**Footwear** is critical. This is where most travelers come undone. The ground lies. It looks solid. It feels, briefly, solid under your boots. And then your foot is past the surface crust, sinking into mud that grips rather than yields, that holds you the way wet clay holds a thumb, that doesn't let go. Getting free requires time and strength and a certain kind of calm that panic destroys.

Your boots should be:

- **Tall enough to protect against unexpected depth.** Knee-height is not excessive.
- **Loose enough to kick free.** If the mud grabs your boot, you need to be able to leave it behind. Laced too tight, laced wrong, and you'll lose more than footwear.
- **Dumb.** No electronics. No complicated soles. Nothing that relies on mechanisms that can clog or corrode or fail.
- **Marked.** Put something bright and visible on them, because you may need to find them later, in fog, in the dark, and they're just boots and the Dissolved Shore has a way of making everything equivalent.

Consider bringing a second pair of something lighter, something you can move faster in once you've mapped the local ground. But accept that any footwear is temporary. The Dissolved Shore eats boots.

**Layering** is almost useless here. The temperature doesn't vary enough to make layering practical, and the moisture makes it counterproductive—layers trap water against you, and wet layers against skin are worse than wet skin alone. Bring one good warm layer for sleeping, and let the rest of your clothing manage the damp as best it can.

### Shelter

Standard tents are a waste of space. The Dissolved Shore doesn't have rain in any meaningful sense; it has constant ambient moisture that settles on everything equally. A tent will become wet inside and wet outside and will neither protect you nor make you comfortable.

What you want is something that raises you off the ground and provides a barrier between you and the constant weeping of condensation. A hammock, if you can hang it. A cot, if you can set it up without sinking. Anything that keeps your sleeping surface separated from the world that is always trying to become something else.

Bring a waterproof groundsheet—not for rain, but to kneel on, to sit on, to place between yourself and a world that will be damp no matter what. You will become wet anyway. The groundsheet buys you a few minutes of dryness, and those minutes matter when you're trying to eat, or sleep, or think.

### What Not to Bring

- Electronics. Not because of water—though water is a problem—but because **the Dissolved Shore has a complicated relationship with cause and effect**, and electronics require a very strict relationship with cause and effect. Devices may work, or may appear to work, or may work differently than intended, or may work in ways that seem correct but are actually wrong. Save yourself the uncertainty.
- Paper. It will become pulp. Waterproof your documents or lose them.
- Complicated books, sentimental objects, anything that matters. The Dissolved Shore has a way of making things equivalent. If you drop something in the mud, it may still be there, or it may have become mud, and you won't be able to tell the difference.
- Anything that floats. Floating is a category error here. The water doesn't flow in the directions water should flow, and floating things sometimes find themselves in places they shouldn't be.

## Navigation

### Finding Your Way

The Dissolved Shore offers no landmarks that function as landmarks should. The fog erases distance and sameness erases distinction. Turn around, and the fog has reshuffled. Look again, and the channel you just passed has become the channel you're looking for. The place is not disorienting in the way a maze is disorienting; it's disorienting in the way a dream is disorienting, where everything makes sense while you're in it and nothing makes sense when you try to explain it.

**Do not rely on visual memory.** The Dissolved Shore will fool you. What you remember as a distinctive bend in the reeds will turn out to be a bend much like it but not the same. What you thought was a dead tree is still a dead tree but not the one you marked.

**Do not rely on GPS.** The physics here are suggestions at best. Global Positioning System signals bend in ways that GPS cannot compensate for. You may find yourself walking in a straight line while the device thinks you're going in circles, or vice versa, or you may find yourself standing still while the device insists you're moving, or you may find that the device has become an expensive rock.

**Do rely on:**

- **Physical marks.** Stake things. Carve things. Leave things that are obviously yours, in obviously visible places, with obviously deliberate arrangements. The Dissolved Shore is good at erasing, but it hasn't learned to erase deliberately-placed human objects with the same efficiency it erases natural features. Take this with a grain of salt—the Dissolved Shore eats salt too—but carry something you can stake and check and restake.
- **The sedge tufts.** Where the ground rises slightly above the waterline, the sedge grasses form tufts that look almost organic in their arrangement. They *are* organic, but they're also your friends. The sedge grows where there's slightly more solid ground, and the slightly more solid ground is where you can walk without sinking. Learn to read the sedge. Follow the sedge. The sedge is the Dissolved Shore's attempt to be something, and it has settled on sedge.
- **The channels.** The channels between the sedge are where the water moves—or seems to move, or moves in ways the water doesn't understand. They offer consistent paths, even if they're not direct. Water has carved these channels over time, and water is at home here in a way nothing else is. The channels won't deceive you the way the open ground does.

### Knowing If You've Been Walking in Circles

This is harder. The Dissolved Shore's geometry is not adversarial—it doesn't *want* to confuse you, it's simply confused itself over geological timescales and expects you to be confused too.

One technique that sometimes works: **place objects in straight lines, and walk past them.** If you walk west for a hundred paces, stake something. Walk east for a hundred paces. If you don't find your stake, you haven't gone in a straight line. If you find your stake but from an unexpected angle, you've curved. This is crude, but crude works here.

Another technique: **listen for the hum.** The subsonic pulse that you feel more than hear is not constant, but it's consistent—where it sounds loudest, the ground is more stable, and when it goes quiet, you've entered an area where the dissolution is more active. This is not precise, but it is something.

### Getting Lost

You will get lost. This is not defeatism; this is preparation. The Dissolved Shore is a place where getting lost is the default state, and if you go in expecting to maintain your bearings, you are setting yourself up for panic, and panic here is fatal.

Accept being lost. Accept that you might not know where you are, where you've been, or where you're going. Work with it. Make decisions based on what you can observe, not on where you think you should be. If you need to go south and you don't know which way is south, pick a direction based on something observable—the way the water moves, the way the sedge grows, the way the fog is densest—and commit to it. The Dissolved Shore will respect commitment more than it respects orientation.

## The Dissolvers

They are the luminous drifting things. They are phenomena, organisms, or something stranger, and this question has not been answered and may not be answerable. They drift through the fog in loose schools, glowing with cold light that casts no warmth, and where they pass the air tastes different—cleaner, emptier, like the space left behind when something is subtracted.

**Do not touch them.** This is the only rule that seems to be universal among those who have encountered the Dissolvers and retained the capacity to discuss the encounter. Do not touch them. Do not let them touch you. Do not get close enough that they could.

The effects of contact vary in the telling—descriptions range from "numbness" to "peace" to "nothing at all, and then I was somewhere else"—but no one who has described contact has described it as something you would want to repeat.

**Do not approach them.** They are beautiful. They are the only light in a dim world, and they move with a grace that feels intentional, that feels like communication. Resist this. The Dissolved Shore has a way of making things that feel good seem safe, and the Dissolved Shore is wrong about this more often than it is right.

**Do not watch them for too long.** They move in patterns that are too regular for wind, and if you watch, you will start to see the pattern, and if you see the pattern, you will start to predict it, and if you predict it, you will start to feel like you understand it, and if you feel like you understand it, you will step closer to understand it better, and this is how people disappear.

If you see them, note their direction. If you hear them—which you may not, they are silent—note their sound. Use them as landmarks the way you would use anything else in the Dissolved Shore: provisionally, with suspicion, and with an exit plan.

### When They Approach You

They usually don't. They drift in their own time, indifferent to human-sized concerns, and the only times they come close are when someone has been watching them too long, or thinking about them too much, or doing something in the Dissolved Shore that makes them curious.

If you notice a Dissolver drifting toward you, **change direction.** Don't run. Running draws attention. Just walk, firmly, in a different direction. If there's sedge, walk toward the sedge. If there's a channel, walk along the channel. Do something that looks purposeful.

If one comes close anyway—close enough that you can see it isn't a light but a shape, something that occupies space, something that has mass—**do not breathe in.** Hold your breath. Cover your mouth and nose with your hand, or a cloth, or anything. The subtraction happens through the air. Whatever they leave behind, you do not want to breathe it in.

This is advice that may be useless. If a Dissolver has decided to approach you closely, you are already in a situation that most preparation cannot address. But it is the only specific advice I have, and I give it with the understanding that it may be insufficient.

## Sensory Concerns

### The Sound

The Dissolved Shore is not silent, but it is quiet in a way that feels like silence. Voices carry strangely, folding back toward their sources rather than outward, as if the air is too thick to properly transmit. You will call out and hear your voice coming back to you from an unexpected direction. You will listen for something and hear it behind you. You will think you heard something and not be able to tell if you did.

**Speak softly.** There's no point in shouting—the sound won't go where you want it to go, and shouting will only make you feel foolish when the shout comes back to you from the direction you were calling toward. Whisper if you must speak. Whisper and move on.

**Listen for the hum.** The subsonic pulse that you feel more than hear is the only consistent sound. It is not loud, but it is always there, and it is the closest thing to a baseline that the Dissolved Shore offers. Learn its rhythms. Learn where it sounds loudest and where it quiets. It will not tell you where you are, but it will tell you where the dissolution is stronger, and that is useful.

**Do not trust your ears for direction.** The Dissolved Shore lies to sound the way it lies to sight. If you think you hear something specific, mark it visually before acting on it.

### The Smell

The smell is wet mineral, rotting plant matter, and something else—a faint chemical sharpness that might be iron or might be something the chemistry can't name. You will adapt to it within hours. You will stop noticing it, and then you will only notice it when it changes.

**When it changes, notice.**

The Dissolved Shore's smell is consistent enough to be background noise, and any sudden shift means something has entered the area or become more active. The chemical sharpness might intensify, suggesting something geological or chemical is being disturbed. It might sweeten, suggesting decay is accelerating. It might thin, suggesting the Dissolvers have passed near, and their "subtraction" has removed something from the air.

If the smell changes dramatically, stop. Assess. Don't assume it's nothing.

### The Light

The light is dim and sourceless, filtering through the overcast or rising from the fog itself, and in it the world looks bleached, washed-out, like a photograph left in developing fluid too long. Colors are suggestions. Shadows are unreliable. Faces are harder to read, and you are reading faces more than you think.

**Bring light.** Not bright light—that draws the Dissolvers—but consistent light. A headlamp on low, a small handheld, anything that provides a stable point of reference. The Dissolved Shore will make you doubt your own shadow; having a light that is yours, that behaves predictably, will help you hold onto something real.

**Bring something that glows.** Not the Dissolvers—never the Dissolvers—but something that glows faintly, that you can see in your hand or pocket, that tells you where your things are in the dim. The Dissolved Shore has a way of making everything equivalent, but it hasn't learned to erase human things with bioluminescent patches. Not yet.

## Health and the Body

### Drowning

Not in water. In mud. In the thin crust that looks solid and isn't, in the places where the ground has forgotten how to hold its shape. You will not drown the way you would drown in a lake. You will sink, and the mud will fill your mouth and nose before you understand what is happening, and you will be held, and the only way out is the way you came.

**Move slowly.** This is the primary defense. If you step lightly, if you test your weight before committing to a step, you will feel the ground give—or you will feel the ground hold, and that is useful too—and you will have time to adjust.

**Spread your weight.** The difference between sinking and not-sinking is often the difference between standing on two feet and standing on one. If the ground is uncertain, kneel before you walk. If you must walk, keep your center of mass low.

**Know how to get out.** If you sink, do not thrash. Thrashing spreads your weight unevenly and makes the mud grip tighter. Move slowly, spread your weight, pull your legs free one at a time, and roll to solid ground. It will take time. It will take breath. Do it anyway.

### Temperature

The Dissolved Shore is neither warm nor cold; it is damp. This is more dangerous than it sounds. Damp saps heat from the body without triggering the responses that cold triggers. You will not shiver until it's too late. You will not feel cold until you're already hypothermic.

**Eat more than you think you need.** Caloric burn in the Dissolved Shore is high—not from exertion, but from the body's constant struggle to maintain temperature against ambient moisture. Bring high-calorie foods. Bring more than you think you'll need. Assume you will eat twice as much as you expect.

**Stay moving.** Not frantically—frantic movement draws attention and exhausts you—but consistently. A body at rest in the Dissolved Shore cools faster than a body in motion. If you must stop, stop for a reason (eating, sleeping, thinking) and have a plan for starting again.

### The Air

The air is wet and thin and full of things that should not be breathed. The Dissolved Shore has its own chemistry, and that chemistry is not calibrated for human lungs.

**Breathe through cloth.** Not for protection against particles—the Dissolved Shore is too wet for particles—but for protection against the air itself. Wet cloth over mouth and nose tempers the air, traps some of the chemical sharpness, gives you a barrier between your lungs and the place that has made peace with dissolution.

**Notice your breathing.** If it becomes easier, you may have entered an area where the Dissolvers have passed. The "emptiness" they leave behind is not harmful, as far as anyone can tell, but it is different, and different in the Dissolved Shore is worth noticing.

**Notice if your lungs feel strange.** Some travelers report a lingering sensation afterward—a sense of their lungs being too large, or too empty, or too present. This may be nothing. It may be something. Monitor yourself.

### Wounds

Everything is wet, and wet wounds become infected wounds become something worse. The Dissolved Shore's infection is not bacterial in any way you can treat with antibiotics. It is something slower and stranger—a refusal to heal, a spreading dampness, a sense that the wound is becoming landscape.

**Do not get wounded.** This is the most important medical advice.

**If you are wounded, keep it covered.** A waterproof dressing is not enough—the Dissolved Shore will find its way around waterproof dressings—but covered is better than uncovered. Change dressings frequently. Do not let the wound dry out, but do not let it stay wet. The balance is impossible. Do your best.

**Know that wounds here heal slowly.** Budget extra time. Assume that anything that breaks skin will take twice as long to heal as it would anywhere else.

## The Psychology

This is the hardest part to prepare for, and the most important.

The Dissolved Shore does not attack you. It does not threaten you. It does not even ignore you, exactly—it simply continues being what it is, and what it is is slowly, gently, permanently dissolving anything that thought it was solid. Including you.

### What It Does to You

Travelers report:

- **A gradual indifference to solidity.** The ground stops feeling unreliable. This sounds like adaptation, but it isn't; it's a softening of the instinct that tells you to avoid sinking. You stop testing the ground. You walk where you want to walk. This seems fine until you sink in too deep, too far, and find you cannot get out because you stopped listening to the part of you that knew how.

- **A loss of urgency.** The Dissolved Shore operates on its own time, and if you stay long enough, its time becomes your time. Hours stop meaning what they meant. Days stop mattering. You find yourself doing nothing and not minding it, and then you find yourself doing nothing for longer than you intended, and then you find that "intended" has become a word that doesn't work the way it used to.

- **A fondness for the place.** This is the most dangerous effect, and the least discussed. The Dissolved Shore is beautiful in its way—a bleached, soft, dreaming kind of beauty—and if you stay long enough, you will find it beautiful, and if you find it beautiful, you will not want to leave, and if you do not want to leave, you will not leave, and if you do not leave, you will dissolve.

- **An awareness of the Dissolvers.** Not fear, exactly, but a hyperawareness—a constant sense of where they are, where they're going, whether they're coming closer. Some travelers report dreaming of them. Some report feeling watched by them. Some report that the watching feels friendly, and this friendliness is the most dangerous thing of all.

### How to Protect Yourself

**Set departure rules before you arrive.** Tell yourself: I will leave after X days. I will not stay past Y. I will turn back when Z happens. Write these rules down. Give a copy to someone you trust, somewhere you are not. The Dissolved Shore will make you forget your rules; the written record will not.

**Do not stay past your deadline.** This sounds simple and is not. The Dissolved Shore will give you reasons to stay. The fog is thick; you should wait. You have not found what you came for; you should look longer. The Dissolvers are beautiful; you should watch them a little more. Every one of these reasons is a trap. Ignore them. Leave on schedule.

**Maintain the distinction between you and the place.** This is harder than it sounds. The Dissolved Shore has a way of making things equivalent—the water and the mud, the sedge and the stone, you and the landscape. Fight this. Keep your things separate in your mind. Keep your thoughts separate. Keep a sense of where you end and the place begins, even if you cannot keep a sense of where you end and the place begins.

**Leave something behind on purpose.** The Dissolved Shore takes. Let it take something you chose to give. This is not a sacrifice; it is a recognition. The place will take something anyway. Choosing what it takes takes the taking's power away.

## Emergency Protocols

### If You Are Alone and Lost

1. Stop moving.
2. Find the sedge. Follow it until it offers a path.
3. If you see the Dissolvers, note their direction but do not follow.
4. Wait. The fog will shift. The place will reveal something.
5. When it reveals something, move toward it.
6. Repeat until you find your marks, or until you find something else.

### If You Have Fallen Into Mud and Cannot Get Out

1. Stop panicking. Panic kills.
2. Spread your weight. Roll onto your back. Float if you can.
3. Move slowly. One leg at a time.
4. Do not rush. The mud will hold you if you let it.
5. When you are free, move to solid ground and do not move until you have stopped shaking.

### If a Dissolver Comes Too Close

1. Stop breathing if you can.
2. Cover your mouth and nose.
3. Do not run. Running draws attention.
4. Walk firmly in a different direction.
5. If one touches you: let it. Fighting will not help. Wait. Report what you feel afterward, if you are able.

### If You Realize You Have Been There Too Long

1. You will not realize this until you have been there too long.
2. If you realize it: leave. Leave immediately. Leave without packing, without preparing, without anything except yourself and your directions.
3. The Dissolved Shore will try to make you stay. It will find reasons. It will feel like the right thing to do.
4. It is not the right thing to do.
5. Leave.

## What to Bring

A summary of essential items:

- **Clothing:** Oilskin or waxed canvas. Layered for warmth, not for weather. One set you do not mind losing.
- **Footwear:** Tall, loose, marked, replaceable. Two pairs if you can carry them.
- **Shelter:** Something that raises you off the ground. A groundsheet. Nothing that expects to stay dry.
- **Navigation:** Physical markers, something to stake, something to carve with, something that glows.
- **Light:** Headlamp, small handheld, glow-in-the-dark anything.
- **Cloth:** For breathing through. For wrapping wounds. For marking.
- **Food:** More than you think you need. High-calorie. Waterproof.
- **Water:** The Dissolved Shore is wet, but the water there is not clean water. Bring your own. Treat what you find.
- **Time:** Less than you want. Less than you think you need.
- **Will:** More than you think you have. The Dissolved Shore will test it.

## A Final Note

You are going to a place that has been dissolving for longer than most things have been solid. You are a temporary interruption in an equilibrium that does not know your name and does not care. The Dissolved Shore will not try to kill you. It will not try to save you. It will simply continue being what it is, and you will either leave while you still can or you will become part of what it is, and you will not notice the difference until it is too late.

This is not meant to frighten you. It is meant to prepare you. Fear is a response to danger you do not understand. Preparation is a response to danger you have made yourself understand, and having understood, have decided to face anyway.

Go. See the bleached world. Walk the dissolving land. Find what you need to find. And when you have found it—leave.

The Dissolved Shore will miss you. It misses everyone. It is patient, and it is lonely, and it will hold you gently until you are gone.

Let it hold someone else.
