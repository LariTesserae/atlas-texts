# Provisions for the Damp Below

You won't find this place on any map you recognize. It will find you, or you will stumble into it sideways — through a mouth in the wrong hillside, through a gap in a collapsed section of ground that looks, from above, like nothing at all. Keep your eyes on the angles. The light there falls wrong, and your eyes will need practice following it.

Bring no plan you expect to follow. Plans are brittle things, and this place eats brittleness for breakfast. What you bring instead is a disposition — call it patience, call it stillness, call it the willingness to be watched without being threatened. The creatures here do not flee, and they do not attack, and if you cannot parse that third option then you are the one who needs adjusting, not them.

I will tell you what I know. It is not enough, and it will not be enough, and that is fine.

---

## I. On Clothing and Contact

The dampness of this place is not water, though it behaves like water in most of the ways that matter and behaves like nothing you know in the ways that matter most. It will get on you. It will get *in* you, eventually — in the seams of your clothes, in the creases of your skin, in the places where fabric meets sweat. You will not shake it off when you leave. It will stay a while, a mineral trace, a taste at the back of your throat like licking a stone that has been crying for a very long time.

**Clothing choice:** Wear nothing you cannot bear to alter permanently. The damp will change your things. Seams will soften. Dyes may run or deepen or do something you do not have a word for. Bring old clothes. Bring clothes you are already tired of. Better yet, bring raw fiber — undyed cotton, untreated wool — something that will enter into honest conversation with the mineral taste of this place rather than resisting it and losing.

**Footwear:** The narrow channel threading the floor is shallow enough to step across, but *stepping across* requires that you lift your foot, and lifting your foot requires that you trust the ground to remain where it is for the half-second of your step. It may not. The sandstone here is soft. Your boot may sink. Your boot may *catch* and hold and want to stay. Wear laced boots with good ankle support, but leave them untied loosely enough that you can abandon them if necessary. Some things in that hollow have already made their peace with staying. Do not let your footwear share their philosophy.

**Gloves:** Bring two pairs. Liner gloves beneath, something water-resistant over them. You will want to touch the walls. I know this already — I can feel you wanting to, even from here. The walls are soft in ways stone should not be soft, and something in you will need to know that softness the way your tongue needs to know a loose tooth. Touch the walls. But not with bare hands, and not for long, and not without the gloves between you and whatever is weeping through the rock.

**Head covering:** A hood or a hat, something to shield you from the condensation dripping from the low ceiling. The drops catch the light — they are beautiful and they are wrong and they will find the back of your neck if you let them. Moisture there, right at the spine, right where the chill can settle and decide to stay. Your body will remember this place in that spot for weeks afterward, a cold that flares when the weather changes, a dampness you cannot explain to anyone who has not also knelt in a hollow that weeps.

---

## II. On What You Carry In

Keep your pack small. A small pack has a small shadow, and you want that, here — you want less to casting your shadow backward, less to catching the light in a way this space might interpret as invitation. The hollow was unfinished when you found it. It is still unfinished. Do not become a thing it finishes *with*.

**Water.** More than you think you need. The channel on the floor will look drinkable — it is clear, it moves, it has a mineral sweetness that will tempt you. It is not safe. It is not unsafe in any way you have language for. It is *other* in the way that everything here is other, and you should not put it in your body unless you are prepared for your body to become, briefly, something other too. Bring your own water. Seal it well. Seal it twice.

**Light.** A flashlight, obviously — but note that the hollow already has light, wrong-angle light, bright patches that glow without warmth. Your flashlight will not improve this. It will only compete with it, and you will lose that competition in ways that make your vision swim. Use the flashlight only if you must. Otherwise, navigate by the existing light. Let your eyes adjust. Let the geometry teach you where the shadows fall when shadows fall backward. This is not darkness you are fighting. This is *wrong* light, and wrong light requires different handling.

**Something seed-shaped.** You will understand when you are there. The air holds things that drift — insect or seed-pod, the description fails, it is something that fills the air with its drift. You will want to contribute. You will want to be part of whatever is seeding this place, however briefly. Bring something small and seed-shaped and leave it in a crack or a corner. Do not explain this to yourself. Explanations will only get in the way.

**A sound of your own.** The silence here is not absence. It is presence — the presence of a moment before sound learned to happen, or after it forgot. If you bring sound into this place, bring something small and yours: a hum, a word, a rhythm you carry in your body. Use it sparingly. The silence is not hostile, but it is attentive, and it will notice anything you introduce and decide what to do with it. Better you choose what to introduce than have the silence choose for you.

---

## III. On How to Enter

Do not rush.

The mouth will be obvious, or it will not, and in either case it will be the right entrance because there is only the one. Step in sideways if you can — sideways and slow, letting your body register the shift in air pressure, in light angle, in the way the dampness finds the edge of your clothing and begins its slow work of getting *in*.

The creatures will see you. They have always seen you, because they have always been here and you are only now arriving, and they are patient in the way of things that know they will outlast you. Do not interpret their stillness as acceptance or rejection. It is neither. It is taxonomy. You are a thing that moves through their space and will stop moving through their space, and both of these facts are equally unremarkable to them.

Do not try to be still to match them. They are still because stillness is what they are. You would only be still the way a held breath is still, and the held breath is the loudest thing in that room.

Move. Move gently, slowly, with the care of someone who knows they are a guest, but move. Let your presence be a fact they can file alongside all their other facts. You are temporary. They know this. The hollow knows this. Let it be boring to them — let you be the thing that moves through and passes through and does not complicate their slow pulse and their quiet drift and their patient waiting.

---

## IV. On the Walls

You will touch them. I said this already and I will say it again because you are already reaching, your hand already anticipating the grain, the softness, the wrongness of stone that gives under a fingernail.

Here is how to touch them:

Press your gloved hand flat against the surface and hold it there. Do not scrape. Do not scratch. Do not pry anything loose. The walls are already losing themselves slowly, drop by drop, grain by grain, at the edges where moisture meets mineral. Your touch is not helping and it is not hurting. It is *witnessing*. Let it be that. Press your hand to the stone, feel the cold damp seep through your glove's liner, and let yourself feel what the rock feels — the slow weeping, the mineral grief, the patient dissolution of something that has not yet decided what it wants to be when it grows up.

You will want to stay there. You will want to stay a long time. The stone will feel like something that understands you, and this will be true, and it will be true in the way that a mirror understands your face — completely, and without sympathy, and with the kind of patience that only the unfinished can afford.

Set a limit before you begin. Three breaths. Five. Whatever you can hold without feeling like you are holding something. Then pull your hand away. The wall will not chase you. It will not miss you. It will simply continue being what it is, which is soft, and damp, and not yet done.

---

## V. On the Plant

There is one stunted thing with leaves like wet paper that grows from a crack and will not die. Find it.

Sit with it. It is the only thing in that hollow that has decided something — it has decided to live, in defiance of every condition that should have killed it, and it is living with the stubbornness of a fact. Its leaves are pale and sodden and they do not tear. Something about its unwillingness to die is instructive. You came from a world that expected you to leave. This plant came from a world that expected nothing at all, and it is still here.

You do not need to do anything with this knowledge. You do not need to take a cutting or gather a seed or prove you were here. Just sit with the plant. Let it be the thing that is most itself in that room. Let it teach you what decidedness looks like in a place that has not decided anything else.

---

## VI. On the Structures

The half-buried fragments in the walls — not stone, not bone, something between — like teeth in a jaw. Do not disturb them.

They are not carved. They are grown. This means they belong to a logic you do not have, a formation process that does not require hands, a patience that makes the creatures look frantic by comparison. They have been here since the hollow was nothing, since the sandstone was still choosing its shape, and they will be here long after the damp finishes its work and the hollow either collapses or becomes something else entirely.

They are not waiting for you to understand them. They are not waiting at all. If you find one near the surface, if one is close enough to see clearly, study it. Learn its shape. Memorize the place where it meets the stone — the seam, the transition, the way the grown thing emerges from the rock like a tooth in a jaw, like a thought in the mind of the world.

Then leave it. Some things exist to be witnessed, not touched. This place is full of those things.

---

## VII. On Time

You will lose track of it, and you will gain track of a different kind.

The hollow exists in a moment before sound learned how to happen, or after it forgot — and time moves strangely in moments like that. You may feel hours pass in minutes. You may feel nothing pass and then realize you have been sitting in the same spot since before you entered. Bring no clock. A clock will only tell you how little its time has to do with the time that lives here.

When you are ready to leave — and you will know, because the dampness in your clothes will start to feel like a question instead of an answer — leave the same way you entered. Sideways and slow. Let the light shift back to normal angles as you move toward the mouth. Let your eyes readjust. Let the creatures file away the fact of you, the temporary thing that came and went, and let them return to their slow pulse and their patient drift and their quiet, patient witnessing.

You will carry the damp with you. It will taste of mineral and it will gather in the corners of your body — behind your knees, at the base of your skull, in the soft places where the light does not reach. It will stay for weeks, and then it will fade, and when it fades you will forget the exact texture of it, and you will only know that something was different about you once, something damp, something soft, something that had not yet decided what it wanted to be.

---

## VIII. The Last Thing

Do not go looking for this place.

No — that is the last thing and I have already told you too much. Let me say it properly:

Do not go looking for this place. If it wants you, it will come for you. It will open a mouth in the wrong hillside, it will tilt the light at an angle that draws your eye, it will put a gap in the collapsed ground that looks like nothing and beckons like nothing and will not let you pass without ducking your head and bending your spine and entering into the dampness, the weeping, the soft unfinished silence of the hollow below.

And if it wants you, go.

Go sideways and slow. Go patient. Go with two pairs of gloves and sealed water and something seed-shaped in your pocket. Go with the knowledge that you are the temporary thing and that the creatures know it and the walls know it and the dampness knows it, and that all of this knowing is not cruelty — it is simply the patience of the unfinished, the slow regard of a space that has not yet learned to be anything at all and is in no hurry to start.

The hollow will be there when you arrive. It has always been there. It was always there.

And you will be the fact it files alongside the others.
