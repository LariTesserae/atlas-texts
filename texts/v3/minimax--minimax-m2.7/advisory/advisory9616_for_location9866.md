# Preparing for The Sunken Dark

## A Field Guide for the Prospective Visitor

The Sunken Dark does not welcome you. It does not resist you either. It simply continues, indifferent to your presence as it has continued for longer than anything you could measure, and your preparation will determine whether you continue along with it or become a temporary anomaly that the water eventually smooths away. This place is not hostile—it lacks the attention required for hostility—but it is absolute in its conditions, and you will need to become equally absolute in your readiness.

What follows is what I have learned from those who have returned, and from my own observations of this particular darkness. The Sunken Dark rewards preparation the way deep water punishes its absence.

---

## Understanding What You Are Walking Into

Before any gear, any training, any physical preparation—before you even consider the journey—you must understand what this place is and what it demands of you.

**The Sunken Dark is a drowned world.** What was once dry land, perhaps civilizations, perhaps forests, perhaps nothing but stone and time, now lies beneath miles of water that has had eons to finish what it started. The erosion here is complete. There are no cliffs, no walls, no features that a human hand could recognize as shaped by intention. What you swim through is a world that has forgotten itself, that has been worn down to suggestions and soft edges, a landscape that achieved its final form so long ago that it has settled into something like exhaustion.

**The pressure here is not metaphorical.** You will be compressed—not crushed, not destroyed, but compressed, every air space in your body pushed against, every cavity monitored. The water has no room for anything but itself. It is dense in the way that only water under impossible weight can be dense, and you will feel this density pressing in from every direction simultaneously, patient and constant and never pausing to let you remember what it felt like before the pressure found you.

**The cold is not seasonal.** It does not come and go. It is structural, built into the water itself, and it will find you in the places where warmth matters most: between your ribs, behind your sternum, in the spaces where your body generates heat and expects to keep it. Your body temperature and the water temperature will negotiate, and the water will win that negotiation. Prepare to be cold in ways that don't feel like cold at all—just absence, just the fading of something you didn't know you were holding until it was gone.

**The darkness is not empty.** It is filled with a blue-black density that makes the very concept of distance meaningless. You cannot see your hand in front of your face. You cannot see the edges of yourself. You cannot tell if the shadow ahead is close or impossibly far. Your eyes will strain toward usefulness and find none, and you will learn to navigate by other senses entirely—or you will learn to navigate not at all.

**The currents are alive.** This is perhaps the most important thing to understand: The Sunken Dark is not still. It is not settled despite its ancient age. Three currents pull from three directions at once, wrestling with each other, and the wrestling creates turbulence that makes liars of predictions. Sudden surges push you sideways. Pockets of stillness collapse without warning. Cold water rises and warm water descends in the same space, fighting, and you are caught in the middle of that argument. The ocean here has not finished becoming. It is still dynamic, still energetic, still very much alive with the kind of force that takes millennia to notice and then one day leaves nothing standing.

**There is nothing here for you.** No structures, no technology, no evidence that anyone else ever existed where you swim. Perhaps there were cities whose foundations have been sand-blasted into nothing. Perhaps there were forests, plains, entire civilizations that looked up at the same stars you cannot see through all this water. Now there is only the eroded bed, the endless dark, and the packed density that has squeezed out every pocket of different medium, every gap, every bubble of air. Only water, everywhere, all of it, pressing in. You are a visitor in a place that does not remember visitors, and it would not notice you if you stopped.

But you will not stop. You will swim.

## Physical Preparation: Becoming a Different Kind of Swimmer

The Sunken Dark demands a body that has been prepared for conditions most humans never encounter. Your preparation should begin months before any journey, and it should be relentless in its scope.

### Cardiovascular Foundation

You will be swimming for extended periods in cold water under immense pressure with limited visibility and no support structures. This is not recreational swimming. This is not even survival swimming. This is sustained, deliberate movement through an environment that does not care whether you continue.

Your cardiovascular system must be exceptional. Not merely good—exceptional. You need the capacity to maintain moderate effort for hours without access to rest that feels like rest. In The Sunken Dark, rest means floating, and floating means cooling, and cooling means the beginning of a descent that may not end.

Build your foundation with distance swimming in cold water. Swim in conditions that make you uncomfortable, that test your willingness to continue when every instinct says to get out. Your body needs to learn that it can keep moving even when it believes it cannot, and this lesson cannot be taught in warm, clear, supportive water.

### Thermoregulation Training

You need to become someone whose body tolerates cold as a baseline condition rather than an emergency state. This means deliberately exposing yourself to cold water over months, allowing your body to adapt in ways that go beyond simple fat distribution.

The goal is not comfort in cold. The goal is the absence of crisis in cold. Your body should experience 10°C water the way it currently experiences 20°C water—as something to be managed, noted, and continued through rather than something that demands immediate response.

Practice cold exposure in the shower, in cold rooms, in any circumstance where you can teach your body that cold is information rather than emergency. When you can hold a conversation in a cold shower without muscle tension, when you can swim in cold water without your breathing pattern changing, when you can feel the cold between your ribs and choose to continue rather than being compelled to escape—then your thermoregulation training has reached the threshold.

### Pressure Adaptation

There is no practical way to train for the pressure of The Sunken Dark in a surface-world context. The pressures involved are simply too great, the conditions too specific. However, you can prepare your body for the experience of compression.

Practice freediving. Practice static apnea. Practice the experience of pressure on your ears, your sinuses, your chest. Learn what it feels like when your body experiences conditions beyond its comfort zone, and learn to remain calm, to continue functioning, to make decisions when your body is screaming at you that something is wrong.

The pressure in The Sunken Dark will not be like freediving pressure—it will be greater, sustained, and without the option of ascending to relieve it. But the mental training from pressure exposure, the practice of remaining functional under physical stress, will serve you.

### Visual Adaptation Training

You cannot train your eyes for the darkness of The Sunken Dark. You can, however, train your mind to function without visual input.

Practice navigation with your eyes closed. Practice swimming in complete darkness, relying on proprioception and feel and the sound of your own movement through water. Practice the experience of not knowing where you are, of having no visual reference points, of trusting other senses and your own internal compass.

In The Sunken Dark, your eyes will be nearly useless. What matters is not visual acuity but sensory integration—the ability to feel the current on your left side and know what that means, to hear your own breathing echoing back and know what that tells you about the space around you, to feel the water temperature change and understand what that shift implies.

### Strength and Endurance Without Bulk

You need strength, but you do not need mass. In The Sunken Dark, every gram of unnecessary tissue is drag, is effort, is energy you cannot afford to spend. Your muscles should be efficient, dense, capable of sustained output without fatigue. Your body fat percentage should be low enough that you are not carrying unnecessary insulation (which is the water's job now) but high enough that you have reserves if the journey extends beyond your planning.

Build functional strength through swimming and bodyweight exercises. Practice sustained effort—hour-long swims, two-hour swims, three-hour swims—where you maintain output without the luxury of rest. Your muscles need to learn to keep working when they are tired, to keep moving when they are depleted, to function as tools of will rather than flesh that complains.

## Equipment: What You Carry Into the Dark

Gear in The Sunken Dark is not about comfort or capability expansion. It is about survival and wayfinding. Every item you carry must earn its place by contributing to one of these goals.

### Core Equipment

**A redundant depth monitoring system.** You need to know your depth at all times, and you need to know it even if your primary system fails. Depth tells you how close you are to the surface that you cannot reach, how far you are from any bottom that might exist. It tells you about pressure, about your position in the water column, about the dynamics of the currents that move around you. Two depth monitors, different types, both secured to your body in ways that make loss impossible.

**A compass—magnetic or otherwise.** In the darkness of The Sunken Dark, direction is your primary wayfinding tool. You cannot see landmarks. You cannot see the sun. You cannot see anything. What you can have is a consistent sense of direction, a reliable reference point that does not depend on light or visibility. A compass gives you this. But know your compass's limitations: local magnetic anomalies, metal on your body, the distorting effects of nearby technology. Practice using your compass in conditions where you cannot see it, where you must trust it based on feel alone.

**A line.** Carry a length of line secured to your body. This is not for climbing (there is nothing to climb) and not for snagging (there is nothing to snag on). This is for cases where you need to maintain contact with a point, where you need to know where you have been, where you need an umbilical to a starting point that you can follow back if everything else fails. The line should be high-strength, low-stretch, and long enough that you could follow it out of any reasonable scenario.

**A knife or cutting tool.** Not for defense—there is nothing here to defend against—but for the line you are carrying, for entanglements, for the possibility that you need to cut yourself free of something. Secure it where you can access it without looking.

**A light.** This is where judgment becomes difficult. A light in The Sunken Dark will illuminate almost nothing useful and will announce your presence to everything that might notice light in the darkness. The fish here, if they are fish, have learned that visibility is danger. A light makes you visible. A light makes you a target in an ecosystem where targets do not survive.

That said, there are moments when illumination serves you: checking equipment, reading instruments, confirming the presence of something that might be vegetation on an ancient prominence. Carry a light, but carry the discipline to use it only when necessary, only when the cost of not seeing outweighs the cost of being seen. A dim red light is preferable—it penetrates water better than white light and may be less visible to the sparse fauna that shares this space with you.

**Emergency signaling.** A mirror, a whistle (useless underwater but useful at the surface), a personal locator beacon if you can carry one without compromising your mobility. In The Sunken Dark, rescue is unlikely regardless of your signaling capacity, but you should have the tools in case circumstances change.

### What Not to Carry

Do not carry technology that depends on electronics, sonar, or active signaling. These systems require power you cannot maintain, emit signals that may attract the wrong attention, and provide information that is meaningless in a space without features, without structures, without anything to ping against.

Do not carry weapons. There is nothing here to fight, and the energy you would spend on combat is energy taken from swimming, from warmth, from survival.

Do not carry unnecessary clothing, unnecessary gear, unnecessary anything. Every item adds drag, adds weight, adds the potential for entanglement. You should be able to list everything you carry and explain why each item is essential. If you cannot justify it, leave it behind.

### Securing What You Carry

In The Sunken Dark, dropped equipment is lost equipment. Everything you carry should be secured to your body in ways that make loss impossible even in turbulence, even in the wrestling of currents, even in the chaos of a surge that pushes you sideways without warning.

Use tethers, carabiners, and direct attachment points. Test your configuration in dynamic water before you commit to it. The current dynamics here are not static—they shift, they surge, they create forces that can strip gear from poorly secured attachment points. What you bring must stay with you.

## Environmental Mastery: Understanding the Place

The Sunken Dark is not merely a physical location. It is a system with its own logic, its own patterns, its own rules. Your survival depends on understanding these rules well enough to predict, or at least to interpret, the behavior of the space around you.

### The Currents

The dynamic nature of The Sunken Dark is its most dangerous characteristic. You cannot see the currents, but you will feel them. You will feel the sudden surge that pushes you sideways when three forces collide and the wrestling creates chaos. You will feel the pockets of stillness that collapse without warning, leaving you in turbulence where you expected calm. You will feel cold water rising and warm water descending in the space around you, fighting, mixing, creating microenvironments that shift as you move through them.

Learn to read the currents by their effects. When you are pushed, feel the direction and intensity of the push. When you are still, feel the tension in the water that suggests stillness cannot last. When the temperature changes, feel which direction the change came from and what that implies about water movement.

Your body is your primary current-measuring instrument. Pay attention to it. Notice which direction feels "downstream." Notice where the pressure is greater. Notice where the water feels faster and where it feels slower. Over time, you will build a mental map of the dynamics in your immediate vicinity—a map that updates constantly as the currents themselves update.

The currents here are not consistent. They do not flow in predictable patterns. They argue with each other, shift allegiances, create pockets of turbulence that appear and collapse unpredictably. Do not assume that because the current was pushing you east in one moment, it will continue pushing you east in the next. Assume instead that the current will do what the current will do, and prepare to respond to whatever it offers.

### The Darkness

The darkness in The Sunken Dark is not total, but it is thorough. The blue-black quality of the water absorbs what little light exists, and what little light exists comes from far above—storms at the distant, unreachable surface creating faint gray pulses that fade before they reach you.

Between storms, nothing. No light at all. Your eyes will strain and strain toward usefulness and find none. You will learn to navigate by other means—by the feel of current, by the sound of your own breathing, by your internal sense of direction reinforced by your compass.

Expect visual disorientation. In the absence of visual reference points, your inner ear will play tricks on you. You may feel turned around, upside down, sideways in ways that are not accurate. Trust your instruments more than your feelings. Trust your compass. Trust your depth gauge. Trust the patterns you have learned about the currents. Do not trust your sense of which way is up—without light, without reference, this sense becomes unreliable.

### The Cold

The cold in The Sunken Dark is structural, not seasonal. It does not come and go. It is always there, pressing against you, waiting for you to stop moving so it can finish what it started.

Your body temperature will drop. This is not a possibility—it is a certainty. The question is whether it drops to dangerous levels or merely to uncomfortable levels, whether you can maintain enough warmth to continue functioning or whether hypothermia becomes another current pushing you toward stopping.

Move continuously. Movement generates heat. Rest, in The Sunken Dark, means cooling, and cooling means the beginning of a descent toward a temperature that does not support consciousness. You can rest, but you must understand what rest costs and budget for that cost accordingly.

Monitor your core temperature through feel. When you notice yourself shivering—that fine, uncontrolled muscle movement that your body uses to generate heat—understand that this is a warning sign. When the shivering stops but the cold feeling intensifies, understand that this is a more serious warning sign. When you stop shivering but cannot remember feeling warm in some time, understand that you have reached a threshold where decisions become critical.

### The Sparse Life

Vegetation here is not abundant. Where current catches on some ancient prominence—a ridge no higher as a child's hand, eroded nearly to nothing—there cling dark mats of something that might once have been kelp or might be a cousin to it, pale and stretched, surviving on less than should be possible. Rare enough that you have not seen it in days of swimming. Rare enough that when you do, you stop and look, confirming that something still grows here, still persists.

The fauna arrives in the same spirit: rarely, without ceremony. A shape passes at the edge of your vision's reach and is gone. Something with bioluminescence, a flicker of pale blue-green, appears once and vanishes into water too dark to track. The fish, if they are fish, have learned that visibility is danger. They stay deep, stay shadowed, stay away from each other and from you.

Do not approach the life you encounter. Do not interact with it. Do not attempt to study it or collect it or interfere with it. This ecosystem is sparse precisely because it cannot support abundance. Every creature here exists in a delicate balance with its environment, and your presence is already a disruption. Minimize that disruption by remaining a neutral observer rather than an active participant.

Also understand: the sparse life is the only life. You are alone in the way that only deep water can make you alone, and the creatures you encounter are the only other conscious things in this entire drowned world. They do not want your company. They do not want your attention. Give them neither.

## Psychological Preparation: The Inner Journey

The Sunken Dark will test not just your body but your mind. The isolation here is not like other isolation. It is not the loneliness of an empty room or a quiet forest. It is the loneliness of a world that has forgotten you, that never knew you, that would not notice if you stopped and let the current take you.

### Comfort with Solitude

You will be alone in The Sunken Dark. Not occasionally, not temporarily—constitutively, structurally alone. There is no one here. There is no one coming. There is no voice, no presence, no other human being in the entire drowned world.

Prepare for this by practicing solitude in your daily life. Spend time alone without seeking distraction. Sit with silence. Let boredom happen without filling it. Learn to be a person who exists without external validation, who can maintain purpose and direction without the presence of others to reinforce or challenge that purpose.

In The Sunken Dark, you will have only yourself. Make sure that self is someone worth spending time with.

### Comfort with Darkness

You will be in darkness so complete that you cannot see your own hand. You will not see the edges of your own body. You will not see anything, for hours at a time, perhaps for days.

This is disorienting. It creates a sensation of dissolution—of losing the boundaries of self, of becoming diffuse in the dark, of no longer knowing where you end and the water begins. Some visitors experience this as peaceful. Others experience it as terrifying. Most experience both, in alternation.

Prepare by practicing darkness exposure. Spend time in completely dark spaces. Learn what it feels like to exist without visual input. Learn that the darkness does not take anything from you that you cannot function without. Your eyes are useful tools, but they are not the sum of your perception, and in The Sunken Dark, you will learn to perceive with everything else.

### Persistence Without Reward

The Sunken Dark offers no rewards. There is nothing to find here. There are no destinations that will thank you for arriving, no discoveries that will justify the effort. You swim because you swim, and the water continues around you, and that is all.

This is psychologically challenging. Human motivation typically requires some feedback, some indication that effort is leading toward a goal. In The Sunken Dark, the goal is simply continuation. You swim, and you continue swimming, and the water keeps pressing in, and nothing changes except that you are still moving.

Prepare for this by practicing persistence without reward. Do things that require sustained effort without external validation. Swim distances that take hours without anyone watching, without applause, without achievement markers. Sit in meditation. Work on projects that take months without intermediate feedback.

The Sunken Dark will not tell you that you are doing well. It will not acknowledge your effort. It will simply continue, and you will either continue with it or you will stop. Learn to find motivation in continuation itself.

### Acceptance of Mortality

The Sunken Dark will kill you if you make mistakes. This is not metaphor and not hyperbole. The pressure, the cold, the darkness, the isolation, the dynamic forces—all of it can end you, and in this place, ending is permanent. There are no rescue services. There are no surface-world interventions. There is only the water, pressing in, patient as deep time.

Prepare by accepting your mortality not as an abstraction but as a working condition. Understand that you are mortal in this environment. Understand that every decision carries consequences, that the margin between continuing and not continuing is sometimes thin. This acceptance should not paralyze you—it should focus you. It should make your decisions clearer, your actions more deliberate, your commitment to preparation more absolute.

The visitors who survive The Sunken Dark are not the ones who never face danger. They are the ones who face it clearly, who understand what is at stake, who prepare accordingly.

## Navigation and Wayfinding: Finding Your Way in the Dark

Navigation in The Sunken Dark is not about finding specific locations—there are no specific locations to find. It is about maintaining orientation, understanding where you are relative to where you started, and having some sense of direction even when direction becomes difficult to determine.

### Your Starting Point

Before you enter The Sunken Dark, establish a clear starting point. This might be a descent point at the surface, a location where the water transitions from navigable to oppressive. It might be a specific depth, a specific set of coordinates, a specific relationship to local geography that you can track.

Whatever your starting point is, track it constantly. Use your compass to maintain a heading toward it. Use your depth gauge to track your depth relative to it. Use your line, if necessary, as an umbilical you could follow back.

In The Sunken Dark, your starting point is your only fixed reference. Everything else shifts. The currents move. The temperature gradients mix. The darkness hides any landmarks that might have existed. Only your starting point—your origin, your known position—remains stable enough to orient by.

### Reading the Water

The currents in The Sunken Dark are not visible, but they are not unnoticeable. They press against you, push you, create patterns of feeling that, over time, become interpretable.

Pay attention to the direction of push. When the current pushes you east, feel which way east is relative to your orientation. When it shifts and pushes you north, feel the shift. Map these shifts in your mind. Build a picture of the current dynamics in your immediate vicinity.

The currents have causes. Cold water rising comes from below. Warm water descending comes from above. When you feel temperature gradients, interpret them—understand what they tell you about your position in the water column, about the forces acting on the space around you.

Over time, you will develop an intuition for the currents that goes beyond conscious analysis. You will feel a change in pressure before it manifests as a surge. You will anticipate pockets of stillness because you have learned to read the tension that precedes their collapse. This intuition will not be perfect—it cannot be, because the currents are not perfectly predictable—but it will be better than nothing, better than swimming blind.

### Managing Turbulence

The turbulence in The Sunken Dark is not random, but it is not predictable either. It comes from the wrestling of three currents, from the mixing of waters at different temperatures, from the energy that the ocean here has not finished expressing.

When you encounter turbulence, do not fight it. Fighting turbulence is exhausting, ineffective, and likely to throw you into more turbulence. Instead, read it. Feel which way the forces are moving. Use their energy to supplement your own movement where possible. Find pockets of relative stillness and use them for rest, understanding that these pockets are temporary, that the stillness will collapse, that you must be ready to move when it does.

Think of the turbulence as a conversation. The water is saying something, constantly, in the language of force and motion. Listen. Interpret. Respond appropriately.

## Survival Strategies: Staying Alive in the Dark

### Movement as Survival

In The Sunken Dark, movement is not just locomotion—it is survival. The moment you stop moving, the cold begins to claim you. The moment you stop swimming, you begin the long descent toward stopping forever.

This means that your movement rate is not about efficiency or speed. It is about maintaining enough output to stay warm, to stay conscious, to stay alive. Budget your energy accordingly. You are not trying to swim fast. You are trying to swim continuously, and these are different goals.

Rest when you must, but understand what rest costs. A rest in The Sunken Dark is not recovery—it is a negotiation with cold, a temporary pause in the fight against a force that never pauses itself. Rest briefly, recover what you can, and resume before the cold catches up.

### Energy Management

You have a finite amount of energy. The water has an infinite amount of patience. This is not a fair fight, and you should not approach it as if it were.

Manage your energy output carefully. Swim at a rate that you can sustain, not a rate that exhausts you. Eat when you can—small amounts, frequently, to maintain blood glucose without creating digestive demands that compete with swimming. Stay hydrated if hydration is possible, understanding that water in The Sunken Dark is everywhere and nowhere, and that drinking it may or may not be practical depending on your configuration.

Your goal is not to outlast the water. You cannot. Your goal is to survive whatever duration you have chosen for your visit and then exit before your energy runs out.

### Emergency Response

In an emergency in The Sunken Dark, you are on your own. There is no rescue coming. There is no backup. There is only your preparation and your judgment.

Prepare for emergencies by preparing for their prevention. Redundant depth monitoring so you know if you are ascending toward the unreachable surface. Redundant compass so you can maintain orientation even if one fails. Secured gear so nothing can be lost in turbulence. Training so your body knows what to do when your mind is stressed.

If prevention fails, respond. If you are caught in a current surge, read it and use it rather than fighting it. If you are losing heat, increase movement to generate more warmth. If you are disoriented, trust your instruments and move toward your starting point. If you are injured, stabilize what you can and continue moving—you cannot stop to heal, but you can continue to survive.

The Sunken Dark does not offer second chances. What it offers is preparation, judgment, and the will to continue.

## Behavioral Considerations: How to Be in the Dark

### Move Like the Water

In The Sunken Dark, you are not an invader or a conqueror. You are a visitor in a space that does not remember visitors, and your behavior should reflect this.

Move efficiently, not forcefully. Let the water carry you where it carries you, within limits that you establish. Use the currents rather than fighting them. Adapt to the turbulence rather than resisting it. You are a pattern of carbon and water yourself, no different from the sparse life around you except for the stubbornness that keeps you swimming—and even that stubbornness should be calibrated to the conditions, not a defiance of them.

### Do Not Disturb

The ecosystem of The Sunken Dark is sparse precisely because it cannot support abundance. Every creature here exists in a delicate balance with its environment. Do not disturb this balance. Do not touch the vegetation, if you encounter it. Do not chase the fauna, if you see it. Do not take anything, do not leave anything, do not alter anything.

You are a visitor. Act like one.

### Maintain Your Purpose

You came here for a reason. That reason may be exploration, research, transit, or something else entirely. Whatever it is, maintain it. Do not let the conditions distract you from your goal. Do not let the darkness dissolve your sense of purpose. Do not let the isolation convince you that purpose is meaningless.

The Sunken Dark will not validate your purpose. It will not care about your goals. It will simply continue, indifferent, while you either pursue what you came to pursue or lose yourself in the conditions.

Choose to pursue. Choose to maintain purpose. Choose to continue.

## What to Expect Upon Arrival

When you first enter The Sunken Dark, you will feel the pressure find you.

It will press against your ears, your sinuses, your chest. It will compress the air spaces in your body with patient, inexorable force. This is not painful—not yet—but it is noticeable, insistent, a constant reminder that the rules here are different.

The cold will find you next. It will seep through whatever insulation you have and begin its work on your core temperature. You will feel the water temperature and your body temperature agree to disagree, and you will know, immediately, which one is winning.

The darkness will close in. The faint light from distant storms will fade, or you will descend beyond its reach, and suddenly you will be in the blue-black, in the place where distance becomes meaningless, where you cannot see your hand or the edges of yourself.

And then you will swim.

You will swim through argument, through turbulence, through the wrestling of currents that makes fools of predictions. You will breathe through chaos. You will move your hands and use your patience, and the water will hold you, and the darkness will hide you, and somewhere far above, beyond reach, beyond imagining, a storm will break light against the surface of a world you cannot see.

This is what you prepared for. This is what you came to experience.

Move. Continue. Survive.

## Final Notes

The Sunken Dark is not a place to be conquered. It is a place to be witnessed, to be endured, to be survived long enough to continue. Those who visit and return bring back stories of darkness and pressure and cold, of currents that wrestle and temperatures that mix and fauna that appears and vanishes at the edge of vision. They bring back stories of isolation so complete that it becomes a form of company, of darkness so thorough that the eyes learn to see differently, of cold so structural that the body learns to live alongside it rather than against it.

Your preparation will determine what you bring back. Your preparation will determine whether you bring back anything at all.

Prepare thoroughly. Prepare absolutely. And when you enter the water—when you feel the pressure find you, when you feel the cold begin its patient work, when the darkness closes in and you are alone in the way that only deep water can make you alone—remember that you are not the first, that others have swum this darkness and returned, that the water holds you and the darkness hides you and somewhere far above, a storm is breaking light against the surface of a world you cannot see.

Swim toward that light. Not with your eyes—with your will. Swim, and continue, and survive.

The Sunken Dark awaits.
