# A Traveler's Preparation Guide: The Reclaimed Mouth

## Understanding Where You Are Going

The Reclaimed Mouth is not a place you visit so much as a place you enter into relationship with. It will not harm you outright, and it will not welcome you. It will simply continue being what it has been, and you will need to find your place within that.

This is a delta city built over many waterways that do not behave as waterways should. The channels carry something the locals call *luming* — a fine particulate light that moves through the water and is considered entirely ordinary by people who live here. You will find this strange. This is expected. The locals stopped finding it strange generations ago, and they will not be able to explain it to you in ways that satisfy your understanding. Accept this early. There are things in the Reclaimed Mouth that work, that persist, that have been working and persisting for longer than your questioning can track, and the people who live here have made their peace with that. You can make yours, but it will take time.

The city was designed for more people than currently live here. Empty buildings are not ruins — the materials self-repair, the structures hold — but they are quiet, and the plants reclaim them slowly. You will see this and think of abandonment. The residents do not think of it this way. The buildings are waiting. The city is patient. This is not a metaphor you should dismiss as poetic. Pay attention to what the locals mean when they speak literally.

## Physical Preparation

### Temperature and Climate

The climate is warm. Not hot, not comfortable — warm in the way that a held breath is warm, present and slightly oppressive. The opalescent sky diffuses light evenly, meaning there is no true sun, no shadows with edges, no sense of where the warmth comes from. You will not need cold weather gear. You will need light layers you can adjust as your body temperature fluctuates through the day, because the warmth is constant but not deep — it sits on the surface of things, including your skin.

Expect the air to be damp at all times. The channels evaporate. The vegetation releases moisture. Rainfall is intermittent — long dry periods followed by sudden heavy storms that flood the lower channels and then recede, leaving everything damper than before. Your belongings will be affected by humidity. Your clothing will not dry quickly. Plan accordingly.

### Clothing and Footwear

Light, quick-drying fabrics will serve you better than heavy materials. You will want clothing that can get wet and not burden you.

Footwear is important. The city is built around water, and you will cross water frequently — by bridge, by stepping stones, by shallow fords where the water comes just to your ankles. There are places where you will need to step directly from stone to stone, where the stones are slick and the water dark. You want shoes with good grip that can get wet without coming apart, that you do not mind tracking mud and algae. The locals go barefoot in many areas. This is a skill they learned from childhood. You did not. Unless you are certain of your footing, wear shoes. Boots that can be hosed clean are ideal. Sandals with good straps are acceptable if you have strong ankles and are comfortable on uneven surfaces.

Bring one pair of dry shoes for sleeping, for if you are ill, for the return journey. Keep them in a sealed bag. Everything else will be damp.

### What to Carry

Pack light. The city is not large by the standards of most multiverse references, and you will walk most of it. The paths are sometimes narrow, winding through fern-banks and between buildings, crossing channels on small bridges. You do not want weight on your back when you are negotiating a turn with a building that is thinking.

A small bag, a water-resistant layer for your documents, a way to keep your hands free. The locals carry things in pouches at their belts, in small baskets, in the pockets of their loose clothing. You will find your own system.

## Sensory Preparation

### Light

The light in the Reclaimed Mouth will affect you. This is not a warning — it is an observation. The opalescent sky provides diffuse, even luminance without warmth or direction. It is never fully dark; it is never fully bright. You will feel the absence of shadows, the absence of a sun you can track, and this will slowly alter your sense of time. Days will feel longer. Nights will feel like a different kind of day. You may have difficulty sleeping. The locals sleep lightly on certain nights (see below), but they grew up with this light. You did not. Be patient with yourself. Your body will adjust, but it will take longer than you expect.

The amber pulses in the building surfaces are the primary light source after dark. They are warm-colored and slow, moving through the city like breath. They will not hurt your eyes. They will not light your way well. You will want a personal light source — a small handheld lamp, ideally with a dim setting. The locals navigate the dark hours without this. You are not the locals. Use the lamp. Do not shine it into the water.

### Sound

The water murmurs. This is the first sound you will hear and the sound you will hear most. It is not rushing. It is not still. It is a low, continuous conversation between water and stone, and it becomes background noise within hours, something you stop noticing until it stops — and then you notice.

Listen for the silences. When the water goes still, when the murmur fades entirely, you are entering a different register of the place. In those still moments, something beneath the city becomes audible: a low subsonic pulse that seems to come from the deep sediment, from the clay, from whatever the city was built to reach or to keep down. You will feel this more than hear it. It will make your chest feel strange. This is normal. This is what the residents have lived with for generations. Do not panic. Do not run. Simply notice. Notice and continue.

After dark, and especially on certain nights, the pulse becomes louder and the water's behavior changes. The current strengthens. The luming brightens. You will feel your body respond even if you do not understand why. The locals sleep lightly on those nights. You should too.

### Smell

The city smells of wet stone. This is constant. It is the smell of the limestone, of the clay, of water that has been moving through these channels for longer than records exist. There are also the plants — green growth, flowering vines, the particular scent of the spiral trees' leaves. And there is the smell of habitation: cooking fires, the mineral tang of the paleite, the low hum of the tone-emitting devices at channel intersections.

You will stop noticing most of this within a day or two. This is how the body works — it normalizes. But when you leave, you will carry the smell with you for weeks, and it will feel like a wound, like something has been removed.

## What the Locals Will Expect of You

### On Asking Questions

You will have questions. You will have many questions. This is correct. This is healthy. But learn to ask them carefully.

The residents will not be able to answer many of them, not because they are hiding something, but because the answers are not in the form of information. The buildings thinking. The water moving against its current. The luming. The deep stores. These are facts of the place, and the people here relate to them the way you might relate to weather, or to gravity, or to the fact that you are standing on a planet. You do not understand gravity at the level of particles. You simply live with it.

When you ask questions, ask for experience rather than explanation. "What does it feel like when the pulse is loud?" rather than "What is the pulse?" "How do you know when to sleep lightly?" rather than "What causes the nighttime water behavior?" The answers will not satisfy your curiosity, but they will build something more useful: a practical relationship with the place.

### On the Deep Stores

The city has older sections that sink below the waterline. The locals call them the deep stores. Do not swim in the lower channels after dark. Not because you will be prohibited — no one will stop you, no signs will warn you, no authority will intervene. But the water's behavior changes at night, and you do not have the generations of context that would let your body interpret that change correctly.

If you find yourself near the water after dark and the current strengthens, and the luming brightens, and the pulse from below becomes something you hear rather than feel, step back. Go inside. Wait for morning. In the morning the water will be still again. The city will breathe. The ferns will uncurl.

This is not superstition. The locals are not superstitious people. They are practical people who have learned from experience. Learn from their experience.

### On the Buildings

The paleite structures are warm to the touch, even in dim hours. The buildings have root-like filaments that descend into the sediment and drink from the water table, returning something mineral and warm to the structures above. The locals say the buildings are thinking.

They mean this literally.

You should not interpret this as threat. The buildings are not hostile. They are not friendly, by most definitions of the term. They are simply — aware, in some way you cannot perceive and the residents no longer try to explain. Treat the buildings with the respect you would show to anything that is aware of your presence. Do not lean on them heavily. Do not damage their surfaces. The self-repair is real, but it is not instant, and there is a difference between repair and forgiveness.

The embedded light-veins are part of the building's thinking. Do not tamper with them. Do not try to remove a sample. The small tone-emitting devices at channel intersections are the same — infrastructure, not ornament, not toy. You may look. You may not touch.

### On Empty Buildings

Many buildings are quiet. Many buildings have been quiet for a long time. This is not disaster. Nothing here falls apart. The vines are not destroying the buildings; the buildings are allowing the vines. The moss is not erasing the doorways; the doorways are making room for the moss.

You may enter empty buildings. The doors do not lock — nothing here locks. If a building is empty, it is empty for a reason, which may be that it is waiting for someone, or that it is resting, or that it simply does not need to be occupied at this time. Enter quietly. Look without taking. Leave without fanfare.

If a building is occupied, you will know — laundry on the lines, smoke from a hearth, steam from cooking. These are signals. These are invitations, of a kind, that the building is in use and the people inside are present. Knock before you enter. Wait for a response. The locals will not be alarmed by a visitor; they will simply assess you, and then they will decide whether to welcome you further. You may spend an entire visit without entering a private building. This is normal. This is enough.

## Health and Safety

### Water

Do not drink from the channels. The water is not poison, but it is not water as you understand water — it carries the luming, it responds to forces you cannot see, it has relationships with the sediment and the deep clay that you do not share. The residents have developed tolerance over generations. You have not.

The city provides clean water through systems you will not fully understand. Accept this. Drink what is given to you. Do not ask where it comes from; the answer will be unsatisfying, and the asking will suggest you do not trust the place, and the place notices such things, even if the people do not.

### The Plants

The vegetation is lush and varied. Most of it is benign. Some of it will make your skin itch if you brush against it repeatedly. Some of it will make you feel strange if you eat it, and you should not eat anything you find growing here unless a resident offers it to you and you have watched them eat it first.

The spiral trees are not food. The ferns are not food. The flowering vines are not food. The small rodent-like animals in the ferns are not food — they are quick, and they are not yours, and the city would prefer you did not try.

### The Animals

The tall gray birds that stand motionless in the shallows are not prey. They are patient. They are waiting for things in the water that you cannot see. Watch them if you like. They will not acknowledge you. This is not rudeness.

The city cats are numerous and uninterested in you. They live in the city and tolerate you. You may see them and they may see you and nothing will happen. Do not pet them unless one approaches you and rubs against your leg. Even then, proceed with caution. They are not house cats. They are cats that happen to live in the city. The distinction is subtle but real.

### The Things in the Water

There are things in the deeper channels and the sediment that the residents refer to obliquely. You will not see them clearly. You may see a flare of luming where there was no flare before, and the water will move in a direction the current does not explain, and the pulse from below will change pitch, and the residents will not look, and will not discuss it, and will suggest, gently, that you do not look either.

This is not because the things are dangerous. It is because the things are not yours to observe. They have their own relationship with the city. You are a visitor. Some things are not for visitors.

## Psychological Preparation

### On the Strangeness

The Reclaimed Mouth is strange in a way that is structural rather than dramatic. You will not encounter monsters. You will not face obvious threats. What you will face is a place that does not obey your assumptions about how the world works, and the slow, grinding process of adjusting your assumptions to fit what you experience.

The channels loop back on themselves in patterns that should not drain but always do. The water table rises and falls with a rhythm that matches no rainfall because the rainfall is intermittent and the water's behavior is governed by something else. The buildings think. The light pulses. The pulse from below makes your chest feel strange.

None of this will harm you directly. All of it will accumulate. You will feel, walking through this city, that you are standing in a place that has solved a problem you have not yet learned to ask. You will not solve this problem. You are not meant to solve this problem. You are meant to exist within it, to let it become normal to you, to carry its strangeness home with you and let it change you in small ways that you will only understand years later.

This is not unusual. The locals went through this once. Their ancestors went through it once. Every person who has ever come here has gone through it. The city does not mind. The city is patient. The city has been what it is for so long it has forgotten the alternative, and it will let you forget it too, if you stay long enough.

### On Time

Do not plan to stay a fixed number of days. The Reclaimed Mouth does not work that way. Time here is slower than you expect, not because the days are longer, but because the light does not change and the rhythm is not the rhythm you know, and your sense of duration will slip. You will think you have been here a week and realize it has been three days. You will think you arrived yesterday and realize it has been a month.

This is not a trap. This is the city. It will let you go when you need to go. But you should not be in a hurry to leave.

## What to Bring

### From Your Previous Location

Bring what you need for personal comfort and safety. The city provides shelter, food, and water for visitors, though the food will be unfamiliar and the water will taste of mineral and something else, something you will not be able to name but will come to associate with this place. You do not need to bring provisions.

Bring a journal, if you keep one. Write down what you notice, what you feel, what you do not understand. You will want to remember this later, and the memory will not be reliable. The city changes things. Your memory of the city will be one of the things it changes.

Bring a personal light source, as noted above.

Bring medications you require. The city has remedies and healers, but they are for the city's illnesses, and your illnesses are different, and the locals may not recognize them.

Bring something to trade. The economy here is not monetary. It is based on exchange, on relationship, on the slow building of debt and favor. If you receive hospitality, you will need to offer something in return. This need not be valuable in any conventional sense. It may be a skill, a story, a task performed, an object brought from elsewhere. Think about what you have that the city might want.

### From the Reclaimed Mouth

You may wish to bring something home. This is understood. The locals have done it themselves, in generations past, traveling to other places and bringing back what they found useful.

Paleite samples are not easily removed — the buildings do not appreciate having pieces taken, and what you carry out will not be useful; it will be a keepsake, nothing more. The same is true of the spiral wood, though the trees are less protective of their branches and more forgiving of curiosity.

The things worth bringing home are not objects. They are experiences, relationships, a changed understanding of what water can be and what a city can do and what it means to live in a place that has been itself for longer than memory. You cannot carry these in a bag. But you will carry them anyway, in the way that you carry the smell of wet stone, in the way that you carry the slow amber pulse in your chest when you are elsewhere, in the way that you stop and listen, in the dark, for a murmur that is not quite water.

---

## Final Notes

The Reclaimed Mouth is not a place you conquer. It is not a place you solve. It is a place you inhabit, if you are lucky, if you are patient, if you are willing to let it change you.

The air is warm and damp and carries the smell of wet stone. Somewhere, far off, a channel catches the last of the dim light, and the water moves.

Walk carefully. Listen to the silences. Respect the deep stores. Let the buildings think their thoughts around you. The city has been what it is for a long time, and it will be what it is after you have gone, and the fact that you were here will not matter to it very much.

But it will matter to you. And that, in the end, is what the Reclaimed Mouth is for.
