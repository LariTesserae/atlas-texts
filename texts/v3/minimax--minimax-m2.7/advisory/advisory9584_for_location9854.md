## Environmental Hazards

**Thermal Vents**: The superheated vents are your primary navigation hazard. They're scattered across the lowlands and emit superheated moisture in cycles — you'll want to observe at least two complete cycles before crossing any vent field to understand the timing. The steam itself is breathable but carries dissolved minerals that will corrode unprotected metal and irritate respiratory passages.

**Condensation**: Every surface weeps constantly. This isn't rain you can shelter from — it's ambient moisture that settles on everything. Your gear will be wet at all times. Standard electronics will fail. Paper will disintegrate. Fabrics will begin to mold within hours.

**Cold**: The temperature hovers in that uncomfortable band just cold enough to make joints ache and breath visible but not cold enough to freeze the moisture. This is deceptively dangerous — you won't think to protect yourself, but extended exposure will drain you.

**The Absorption**: This is the serious concern. The landscape is described as "too full of itself" — everywhere already claimed by mineral expression. You're negotiating space with an environment that has already allocated every square centimeter. Your presence is being slowly incorporated. This suggests the surface isn't inert stone — it has some kind of integrating process. You need barriers that define you as separate.

## Recommended Gear

**Sealed Environmental Suit**: Full coverage, pressure-sealed at joints, with independent air supply. Not just for breathability — for identity. You need to be a closed system that the mineral world cannot seep into. The suit should be rigid enough that the landscape cannot mold against you.

**Non-Conductive Boots**: Crystal formations conduct and the electrical quality of the light means surfaces may carry unexpected charges. Thick rubber soles with no metal components.

**Filtered Breathing Apparatus**: The air is breathable but the sweet metallic taste suggests dissolved compounds you don't want accumulating in your lungs. Activated carbon filters minimum; chemical scrubbing recommended if you have access.

**Anti-Corrosive Storage**: Everything you carry must resist mineral-laden moisture. Silicon-sealed cases, corrosion-resistant coatings, waterproof compartmentalization.

**Navigation Markers**: The looping geometry and impossible distances mean you'll lose track of where you've been. Physical markers the mineral world cannot easily incorporate — ceramic stakes, marked stones that don't match local formations. Expect to place dozens and accept that many will be absorbed.

**Light Source**: The dim electrical light has wrong frequencies and flickers. Bring your own illumination with a warm spectrum — this may help the fauna perceive you as less alien, or at least see you coming.

**Recording Equipment**: Document everything. The place seems to resist comprehension — notes will help you maintain coherence and may reveal patterns in the "almost symbols" that form in condensation.

## Behavioral Protocols

**Movement**: Move slowly and deliberately. Every step is a negotiation — the surface is alive in a way that may respond to your weight and pressure. Sudden movements might trigger integration responses.

**Do Not Touch**: Even brushing against crystal formations is risky. The surface "remembers being something else" — it may remember you too. Minimal contact with the environment.

**Monitor Your Body**: The sweet taste in the air, the ache in your teeth — these suggest the environment is affecting you. Track any changes. Numbness, tingling, a sense of mineral weight settling in your extremities. If the world is absorbing you, you'll want to know early.

**Respect Vent Timing**: The thermal vents exhale in cycles. Never camp near an active vent — the superheated moisture will overwhelm any protection and the mineral deposits settling from the plume will begin covering you.

**Avoid Predators**: The creatures have purpose and appetite. Some are hunting. Watch for the clicking things and the cloud-masses. They may not see you as food, but they see you as present, and in an environment this crowded, presence may be threat enough.

**Do Not Investigate the Patterns**: The condensation forms "almost symbols" that almost resolve. This is a trap. The patterns are designed to draw attention, to make you stare, to make you try to understand. If something is listening, it's listening hardest when you're trying to parse what you see. Look away. Move on.

**Exit Strategy**: Plan your extraction before you enter. This is not a world you explore indefinitely — it's a world that slowly incorporates. Set a time limit. Set a maximum distance from your entry point. Honor those limits absolutely.

## Psychological Preparation

This is the hardest part. The environment has wrongness woven into it — looping geometry, impossible shadows, creatures that cross your path again at different scales or configurations. Your mind will want to make sense of this. It will fail. The failure itself may become dangerous.

Accept that you will see things that don't make sense. Do not argue with them. Do not try to resolve the contradiction. Accept the loop. Accept the wrong shadow. Accept that the creature has more legs this time.

Maintain your reference frame: you are human, you are separate, you are visiting, you will leave. Repeat this. The environment will try to make you doubt it.

Do not let curiosity override caution. If something seems like it wants to be found, don't find it. If something seems like it wants to be understood, don't understand it. The landscape is too alive to trust anything that appears designed to attract attention.

## Fauna Notes

The creatures — shelled clickers, vapor-trailing cloud-masses, things with too many eyes in wrong patterns — they are part of the ecosystem. They have appetites. Some of them are predators. None of them are safe in the sense that understanding them will help you — their behavior follows local logic that may not translate to human concepts.

Watch them to learn the rhythms of the place. Learn when they cluster, when they flee, when they hunt. Use their behavior as environmental data. But do not interact with them, do not approach them, do not offer them anything including attention.

The cloud-masses with bioluminescence or whatever-it-is: their light-catching is "wrong." Don't look at them directly if possible. Their internal illumination is not for your benefit and may not be compatible with your visual processing.

## The Mining Question

The industrial presence is suggested by regularity in formations, mechanical patterns, and "something always being done" below. If this world has active extraction or processing operations, they are either automated or run by entities you cannot predict. Industrial facilities may appear as crystallized seams, as geometric formations that don't quite fit, as surfaces that hum with determined frequency rather than organic resonance.

Approach any pattern that looks mechanical with extreme caution. You don't know who is operating what or why, and the mineral ecosystem may have adapted to incorporate even industrial processes into its fullness.
