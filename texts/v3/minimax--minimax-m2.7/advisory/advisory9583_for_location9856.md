# Preparation Guide: Kaldenvast

## On the Nature of This Place

Kaldenvast will lie to you. Not intentionally—it's simply that the concept of truth operates differently in locations where the ground is still negotiating its own existence. When you arrive, you will want to understand what you're seeing. You will look for patterns, for logic, for cause and effect. This instinct will serve you poorly.

The city's residents have abandoned the expectation of permanence. They have learned to live in a state of gentle accommodation, trusting their feet on catwalks that may or may not connect to tomorrow's version of themselves. You will need to learn this too, but faster than they did. The preparation below is not about surviving Kaldenvast—it is about becoming someone who can exist there without falling apart.

## Physical Preparation

### The Body

Your physical preparation begins months before departure, and it is primarily psychological at its core. Kaldenvast will test your relationship with uncertainty at a visceral level. The swaying, the shifting, the feeling of ground that isn't quite ground—these will trigger deep primate fears that no rational understanding can fully suppress. What you can do is build tolerance.

**Spend time in unstable environments.** Practice standing on boats, on platforms balanced on springs, on anything that teaches your inner ear to find equilibrium when equilibrium is a moving target. Learn to trust your feet and your core simultaneously. When Kaldenvast sways—and it will—you need to be able to adjust without panic.

**Acclimatize to dim, wet environments.** The moisture in Kaldenvast is not like rain. It comes from below, through rock, through timber, through everything. It is mineral and metallic and carries something older than sulfur that your body will initially find abrasive. Spend extended time in caves, in greenhouses, in places where dampness is constant and light is fragmentary. Train your skin to accept wetness without breakdown. Train your lungs to work efficiently in air that tastes of metal and age.

**Build tolerance for strange sensation.** The climbing-scorpions are not dangerous, exactly, but they will taste you. Their contact leaves a tingling numbness that fades, but until it does, it is deeply unsettling. Expose yourself to unfamiliar tactile experiences. Let strange things touch you. Learn to remain calm when your skin tells you something wrong is happening.

### What to Bring

**Footwear** is critical. You need boots with aggressive traction that will grip wet metal, slick timber, and surfaces that may be wet moss one moment and polished stone the next. But you also need flexibility—stiff-soled boots will fight you on the catwalks and nets. Find the balance. Consider footwear designed for industrial rope work or arborist climbing.

**Layering for constant damp.** The cold in Kaldenvast is not the honest cold of altitude or latitude. Focus on materials that manage moisture against your skin: wool, certain synthetics. Avoid cotton absolutely. Bring more layers than you think you need, but expect them to be perpetually damp themselves.

**A good flashlight**, or several. The light in Kaldenvast does not behave correctly—it arrives from the past, pools in strange corners, fails to illuminate what you expect it to. Your flashlight will be your primary tool for managing this. Choose something bright, reliable, and waterproof. Bring backup batteries. Accept that even your light may not behave entirely as it should.

**A way to mark your path.** In a city where streets double back and bridges appear where gaps weren't yesterday, you will need anchors. Carry chalk, or marking tape, or something you can use to leave traces that you will recognize. The locals have generations of mental maps you cannot possess. You need to build yours from scratch, and you need to be able to find your way back.

**Ear protection.** The foundries produce a subsonic thrum that penetrates bone. Exposure without tolerance will leave you disoriented, nauseated, with blurred vision. Bring quality earplugs for situations where you need to think clearly. But also practice working through uncomfortable sound—because silence is dangerous in Kaldenvast, and earplugs cannot be your default.

**Sturdy, flexible gloves.** You will need to grab cables, railings, surfaces that are wet or strange or both. Your hands are your primary tools for navigation. Protect them, but keep them nimble.

### What to Leave Behind

Do not bring anything that requires firm ground to function. Sensitive instruments, delicate machinery, anything that depends on consistent gravity or stable surfaces. The physics in Kaldenvast are not unreliable—they are *still being decided*. Your phone may work; it may not. Your tablet may be fine; it may experience time differently than you do. Accept that you are entering a place where your technology may become unreliable and prepare emotionally for that.

Do not bring sentimental items that cannot get wet. Everything will get wet.

## Psychological Preparation

This is where most travelers fail. Kaldenvast does not demand physical excellence—it demands psychological flexibility of a rare and difficult kind.

### On Uncertainty as Default State

You have lived your life assuming that the world is fixed. That tables are tables, that ground is ground, that what you see is what is there. Kaldenvast will teach you that this assumption is a cultural artifact, not a universal truth. The ground beneath the city is still deciding what it wants to be. The light you see may have happened years ago. The building you touch may not be entirely present.

You need to internalize this before you arrive. Practice doubt. When you look at a wall, practice not being certain it's solid. When you step onto a floor, practice not assuming it will hold. This is not about paranoia—it is about releasing your grip on certainty so that you can grip something else instead: adaptability.

**Meditation on impermanence** will help. Spend time sitting with the understanding that everything you see is temporary, that stability is an illusion, that you have been wrong about the fundamental nature of reality your entire life and Kaldenvast is simply making this visible. This sounds distressing, and for a while it will be. But the locals have moved through this. They hang their lives from cables threaded through living rock. They trust the engineering not because it is perfect but because it has proven *adequate*. You can reach this place too.

### On Memory and Time

The deep-dwellers take things. They take time, memory, patches of skin that change texture and won't heal. You may not be able to prevent this, but you can mitigate it.

**Establish memory anchors.** Before you go, create a practice of strong, repeated mental anchoring: your name, your origin, the faces of people you love, the facts you cannot afford to lose. When you arrive in Kaldenvast, repeat these to yourself. When something feels wrong—missing time, a gap in your recollection—return to your anchors immediately. The locals who survive longest have developed this reflex.

**Write things down.** Not because your notes will necessarily be accurate—ink on paper may behave strangely in a place where causality pools—but because the act of writing reinforces memory. Your journal may be unreliable; your memory of having written in it will be stronger.

**Accept partial loss.** Some things will not survive the visit. This is not failure. Kaldenvast takes what it takes. Your goal is to lose less than you keep, and to lose the things that matter less than the things that don't.

### On the Sound

The foundries thrum constantly. Workers describe it as a voice trying to remember what it used to be. You will hear it in your sleep. You will feel it in your teeth. It will become the background of your existence, and then one day it will stop for maintenance and you will understand that silence is not empty—it is the sound of geology negotiating with itself.

Do not try to understand it. Do not try to communicate with it. Treat the thrum as weather: inevitable, impersonal, something to be endured and eventually ignored. If you find yourself becoming dependent on it, if you feel that you cannot function when it stops, begin planning your departure. The sound is not wholesome. It takes things too.

## Practical Knowledge

### Navigation

Trust the cables. The engineering of Kaldenvast is four generations old, threaded through living rock that was miles underground two centuries ago. The cables have held. They will continue to hold. When the city sways, the cables flex. This is not failure—it is adaptation.

When you encounter a bridge or a path that was not there yesterday, do not assume it is safe, but do not assume it is dangerous either. Test it with your weight gradually. Observe how others use it. The city's infrastructure changes, but it changes within parameters that the locals understand.

Avoid the lower districts—the Old Suspension, the Sump Quarters—until you have developed a sense for the city's rhythms. These areas are where the moisture is strongest, where the vegetation has infiltrated everything, where the deep-dwellers most often emerge. They are not forbidden, but they are not hospitable to newcomers.

### Interaction

The locals are patient. They have learned to be patient because the geology is patient, because the city is patient, because nothing in Kaldenvast happens quickly or in ways that can be rushed. When you speak with them, match their pace. Do not demand clarity they cannot provide. Do not insist on answers that do not exist.

The workers in the foundries are a different matter. They have become something else through their exposure to the thrum. They are not hostile, but they are no longer entirely like you. Treat them with respect and distance. Do not try to understand what they are becoming; simply accept that they are becoming it, and that it is not your business unless you choose to make it so.

### When Something Is Wrong

If you feel a patch of skin that has changed texture, do not touch it. Do not scratch it. Do not draw attention to it unless you are seeking medical help—and in Kaldenvast, medical help may not be what you expect. Accept that this is the cost of visiting, and focus on keeping the rest of yourself intact.

If you find yourself in silence—the profound, geological silence of a maintenance shutdown—do not panic, but do move toward light and sound. The foundries are not comfortable, but they are populated, and the presence of others helps. The deep-dwellers emerge in quiet. If you are alone in silence, you are vulnerable.

If you look at something and it is not quite where it was, if a face has moved twenty meters east, if the bridge you crossed this morning now connects to a different building—this is normal. This is Kaldenvast. You do not need to understand it. You only need to accept it.

## The Final Preparation

Before you go, stand somewhere high. A rooftop, a cliff, a bridge over something deep. Look down. Feel the fear if it comes. Then let it pass through you. Let it leave.

In Kaldenvast, you will be suspended. You will hang from cables threaded through living rock. You will trust engineering that was always meant to be temporary. You will exist in a city that should not be here, on geology that has not decided what it is, sustained by industry that harvests the future.

The locals have made peace with this. They are patient in the way the geology is patient. They do not look down.

You will learn to do the same. Not through understanding—you will never understand Kaldenvast, not fully, not the way the locals do—but through practice. Through days and weeks of existing in suspension, of trusting cables that flex, of walking streets that may or may not connect to tomorrow.

It is not comfortable. It is not safe. But it is *possible*, and the people who live there are proof.

Go, if you must. Hang your life from cables threaded through living rock. Trust the engineering. Do not look down.

And when you find your peace, when you feel the gentle sway of the city and it no longer frightens you—know that you have become someone who can live in places that should not exist.

Welcome to Kaldenvast.
