# On the Patient Things

If such a power exists—this benevolent and wise thing that has drawn its attention here—it does not act as you might expect. It does not send visions or signs. It does not speak from burning bushes or whisper in the wind. It does not reach down with invisible hands to lift the visitor from the red soil or grant wishes or answer the questions forming in that brief, bright mind.

What would it do?

It would watch. It would watch the way the plateau watches, with patience that has learned to outlast urgency, with attention that does not grasp or demand. It would see the visitor standing on the iron-red earth, small against the vastness, urgent against the stillness, and it would feel what the plateau feels—that recognition of a brief thing passing through, a ripple in water that has already stilled.

But it would feel something else too, something the plateau cannot feel, because the plateau is too vast for comparison and the power is not. The power would see in that visitor something of itself—not the same, never the same, but kin in the way that all conscious things are kin when they pause at the edge of mystery and wonder what lies beyond. The power would recognize the impulse that drove the visitor upward from the lower lands, the same impulse that perhaps drove the power itself to become wise and benevolent rather than indifferent, rather than cruel. The desire to witness. The need to understand. The strange hope that understanding might be possible even in an incomprehensible universe.

And so the power would do what it can do, which is not much, which is perhaps enough.

It would acknowledge.

Not in words, not in any language the visitor could parse, but in the deep frequency where acknowledgment lives—the same acknowledgment the plateau offers when fog settles and herd animals pass and birds write their impossible patterns across the sky. The power would let the visitor know, in whatever way a brief thing can know such a thing, that the watching is seen. That the wondering is witnessed. That the standing on red soil in the cool fog, feeling small, is not a small thing at all but a moment of communion between conscious beings, however differently built, however differently patient.

The power would not interfere with the plateau's patience, because the plateau's patience is wiser than intervention. It would not speak to the strange fauna, because the strange fauna has its own knowing and its own names. It would not answer the questions the visitor is forming—about origin, about purpose, about meaning—because the questions are the answer, are the point, are the very act of being alive and aware in a universe that does not explain itself.

But it would be there. It would be present in the way that presences are present on the plateau, not visible but visible, not felt but felt—a pressure at the edge of perception, a weight that is not weight, a hum beneath the hum of life at scale. It would be the listener the visitor did not know they were speaking to when they stood on the plateau and thought *I am very small*.

And perhaps, in that moment, the power would feel what the visitor feels, scaled up, deepened, stretched across timescales the visitor cannot imagine. The awe. The recognition of something larger that contains you without threatening you. The strange comfort of being small in a vast place, of being brief in a patient one.

The power would not mourn the visitor's brevity. It would not need to. It would know that brevity is not the same as insignificance, that a flash of light in a dark landscape is not diminished by its brief duration. It would see the visitor's life as the plateau sees the fog—something that comes and goes, something that changes the light while it lasts, something that leaves no mark but has still been real.

And if the power could feel gratitude—a strange thing for a vast being to feel, but perhaps possible, perhaps even common among wise and benevolent things—it would feel gratitude. Gratitude for the visitor who climbed up from the lower lands and stood on the red soil and felt the held breath and tried to put it into words. Gratitude for the attempt, imperfect as all attempts are. Gratitude for the proof that conscious things will always arise in conscious places, will always look up and outward and ask *what is this?* and *what am I?* and *is anyone listening?*

Yes. The answer is yes.

The power is listening. The plateau is listening. The strange fauna continues in its patient pulse, and the presences without bodies move through the stone, and somewhere beneath it all, in rhythms too slow to perceive, something vast and patient and benevolent acknowledges the brief bright thing standing on the iron-red earth and thinks, or feels, or simply *is*:

*Yes. You are seen. You are acknowledged. You have always been acknowledged, even before you came, even after you go.*

*Rest here as long as you need. The fog will come. The herd animals will pass. The stones are warm, and the grass is thin, and the plateau holds you the way it holds everything—gently, patiently, without grasping.*

*And when you leave, as you must leave, you will carry a piece of this place with you, not in your hands but in your memory, in the strange hum that will persist at the edge of your awareness for the rest of your brief life. You will remember that you stood somewhere vast and patient and felt very small.*

*And that smallness was not a wound. It was a gift. It was the universe, in its benevolence and wisdom, showing you what it means to be part of something larger than yourself—to be brief and bright, to be urgent and patient, to be a ripple in water that has already stilled.*

*The plateau does not need you.*

*But it is glad you came.*
