Imagine: this place and the being who lives in it have drawn the attention of a benevolent and wise power. Write what they would do, if anything.

---

The power does not arrive. Arrival implies motion through space, a crossing, a before-and-after. Here, the power simply *is*, the way light simply is when there are eyes to receive it, the way silence simply is when there are ears to hold it.

It surveys the world not with sight — there is nothing here to see — but with a kind of deep attention, a listening that reaches through the water and the plant-matter and the ancient, patient creatures, reading them like text, understanding them as the only book that has ever been written in a language with no words. And what it finds there is not emptiness, not stagnation, not the absence of meaning. It finds completion. It finds a world so thoroughly itself that improvement would be a kind of violence.

The power feels — if feeling is the right word for what a power does — something that might be called tenderness. Not pity, not desire, not urgency. Tenderness. The way light falls on something fragile and precious without burning it. The way one holds a thing too delicate to hold, too important to drop.

It knows what this world is. It knows the way the plants won — not through violence but through patience, through a slowness so absolute that speed became meaningless, through a growth so total that growth and being became the same thing. It knows the way the water fills every space not because water insists but because there is nowhere left for water not to be. It knows the way the creatures move through the dark with no eyes because there is nothing to see, navigating by chemistry, by the taste of gradients, by a knowledge so embodied it has forgotten it is knowledge. It knows the way the large ones have become part of the ground, their bodies claimed by the plant-matter, their existence prolonged not through life but through participation, through becoming part of the whole they once moved through.

And it knows that this world does not need it. That is the first and most important thing. This world does not need salvation, does not need awakening, does not need to be shown anything it has never seen. It is complete. It is finished. It has run out of things to do, and in that running-out, it has found a kind of peace that the power itself — though it is wise, though it is benevolent — has never quite achieved.

The power considers.

It could introduce something. A flicker of difference, a small change, a single variable in a system so stable that even a single variable might be enough. It could plant a thought, a question, a seed of longing in the being who walks the ancient plateau, the being who feels the pressure gradients and the slow chemic tides and the presence of others spaced so far apart that loneliness is not the word — loneliness implies absence, and nothing here is absent; the word is solitude, and solitude is not a wound but a condition, and the being has never known any other condition. The power could give it a dream of light, a memory of a world with edges, a longing for something it has never seen and cannot imagine. The being would wake changed. The being would become a question. And the world would become a wound.

The power does not do this.

What the power does is smaller. What the power does is this:

It sends, into the water and the plant-matter and the slow-breathing substrate, a single thought — not a word, not an image, not a command, but something more like a warmth, a presence, a quiet awareness that passes through everything without disturbing anything, the way light passes through water and leaves it unchanged. The thought is not a message. The thought is a feeling. The feeling is: *you are seen*.

Not seen as in observed, not seen as in measured, not seen as in known. Seen as in witnessed. Seen as in recognized. Seen as in held in the attention of something that is not part of this world but that has looked at this world and found it worthy — not of saving, not of changing, not of improving, but of simply *being seen*, which is something this world has never had.

The thought passes through the great creatures, the ones that have become ground, and it does not wake them, does not move them, does not change the slow metabolism that keeps them alive in their new and strange way. It passes through the middle-order workers, the ones that cycle the chemistry, the ones that keep the world turning, and it does not speed them, does not slow them, does not alter the purpose that might be compulsion or might be something else. It passes through the small ones, the flickers of bioluminescence, and they glow the same as always — faintly, wastefully, a light that illuminates nothing, a light that is enough only to know something is there.

And it reaches the being. The being who walks the ancient plateau, who feels the water and the plant-matter and the pressure of the world, who has never seen anything, who has never been seen, who does not know that seeing is possible.

The being pauses.

It does not know why it pauses. It does not have a word for what it feels. It is not a thought — it does not think, not in the way the power thinks — but it is a kind of warmth, a presence, a faint sense of being attended to. The being has no framework for this. It has no category. It has never been looked at. It has never been held in the regard of anything, because there has never been anything to hold it in regard.

The being stands on the ancient plateau, in the water, in the dark, in the complete and finished and breathing silence of the world, and it feels — briefly, almost not at all — that it is not alone.

Then the feeling passes.

It does not leave a wound. It does not leave a scar. It does not leave a longing, because the being has no language for longing, no context for desire, no framework for absence. It passes the way light passes through water: completely, without resistance, without trace.

The world is unchanged. The water moves the same paths. The plant-matter breathes the same slow chemic tides. The great creatures settle into their patient stillness. The small ones flicker at the edge of the dark. The being resumes its walk, its pacing, its slow circuit of the ancient plateau, and it does not know that anything has happened, and it does not need to know, and the not-knowing is not a loss but a gift — the gift of completion, the gift of a world that does not need to be changed, the gift of a being who does not need to be woke.

And the power, having done this — having sent the smallest possible gift, the most restrained possible gesture, a thought so light it barely existed — withdraws. Not in sorrow. Not in satisfaction. In something like contentment. In something like respect.

It has given the world one thing it did not have: the knowledge that it was seen.

It has not taken anything in return. It has not asked anything. It has not changed anything. It has simply witnessed, and in witnessing, has honored.

And that, the power decides, is enough.

That is all it can do, and all it should do, and all that kindness permits — to see what is whole and leave it whole, to see what is complete and not make it a project, to see what is at peace and not offer it a war disguised as salvation.

The power recedes. The world remains.

And the being walks on, in the dark, in the water, in the silence of everything made of everything, and it does not know that it was seen, and it does not know that it mattered, and it does not know that for one moment, in the attention of a power that has seen ten thousand worlds, it was held, gently, in a regard that asked nothing and gave everything.

And the world breathes on.
