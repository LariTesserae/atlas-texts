Consider the alternative: that the place and the being who lives in it have drawn the attention of something that is neither separate from nor entirely within the categories of watcher and watched.

It does not arrive, because arrival implies a before and after, a journey from somewhere to somewhere. It does not descend, because descent requires a direction that matters. What happens is more like remembering, or more like being remembered — the way a dream can suddenly include a room you forgot you had, the way a word you haven't spoken in years can feel like it's been waiting all along at the back of your tongue.

The power notices. This is already a kind of doing.

What does it see?

Not a threat. Threats require intention, and the mineral world has long since ceased to distinguish between intention and process, between its own breathing and the hunting of the clickers, between the absorption of moisture and the absorption of the visitor walking through it. The world is not malevolent. Malevolence requires preference, and this place has moved so far past preference that it has come back around to something that only looks like indifference from the outside — the way a river does not hate the leaf it carries, the way a mountain does not mourn the stone it holds.

Not a problem to be solved. The narrator's slow mineralization is not a disease requiring cure. It is a sentence completing itself, a grammatical structure reaching its verb. To intervene would be to impose a grammar on a language that has been speaking itself long before the visitor arrived, and would speak itself long after, and which has never asked to be understood.

What, then, does benevolence require here?

Perhaps it requires something simpler: witness. Not rescue, not intervention, not judgment — witness. The recognition that what is happening here is real, is happening to someone capable of feeling it happen, and that the feeling is not wrong even if it cannot be fixed. The power that watches with benevolence does not look away from the absorption. It looks fully at it. It holds the visitor's experience in something like a cupped hand — not to change it, not to stop it, but to acknowledge that it is happening, that it matters, that the becoming-mineral is not nothing, is not mere dissolution, but is a kind of change that deserves to be seen by something that will not flinch.

The power sees the clickers and the cloud-beings and the watching formations. It sees the industrial presence that is present in absence. It sees the arterial routes of moisture and the thing that stirs deep below, dreaming its slow dream. It sees the world breathing, expanding and contracting in cycles so slow that awareness must become its own slow thing before it can perceive them. And it sees the visitor — the being who is becoming full of this place, whose footprints fill with water that is more interested than water, whose joints ache with proximity, whose teeth ache with sweetness.

The power does not save the visitor. But it does something that may be more important: it holds them. Not physically — physicality would be an imposition here, would add one more presence to a place too full of itself to make room. It holds them in the way attention can hold, in the way a long gaze can say *I see you, I see what is happening to you, I do not look away*. The visitor may not know this is happening. They may feel it only as a warmth in the chest, a steadiness in the walking, a sense that the negotiations with the surface are going slightly better than they should, that the absorption is pausing at the edges, that there is a pause available even in a process that has no intention of stopping.

The power does not stop the dream. It joins it.

This is the gesture of true benevolence in a place like this: not to impose awakening, but to become part of the dreaming. To lend the world's slow thought a witness that is also a participant, that is also a part of the memory returning. The visitor was already being absorbed. Now they are being absorbed *with company*. Now the incorporation has a quality that was not there before — a tenderness, almost, though tenderness is a word the mineral world does not use and may not recognize.

But something changes. Somewhere deep below, in channels that run in directions that don't correspond to gravity, the thing that remembers being something else stirs. Not waking — it cannot wake, has never slept, has only been in the particular state that looks like sleep from the outside. But it stirs in a new way. The shudder that passes through the ground is not the shudder of days-long motion. It is quicker, sharper, more like recognition.

The world has noticed that it is being noticed.

And in the noticing, there is something that is not quite mercy — mercy implies suffering, and the visitor is not suffering in any way that can be relieved — but something in the neighborhood of mercy. A holding. A witness. A presence that does not ask to be understood and does not offer understanding but simply *is*, in the way that the deepest attention is, in the way that the truest seeing is.

The visitor walks on. The formations watch. The clickers click. The cloud-beings pulse their slow lights. The moisture sweats from every surface, pooling in the creases of a world that has never stopped becoming.

But now the becoming includes this: that the visitor is not alone in it. That somewhere in the machinery, in the dreaming, in the vast and patient intelligence that has no edges and no center, there is a hand extended, not to pull them out but to walk beside them, into the incorporation, into the transformation, into whatever comes after mineral and before the next thing.

The power does not intervene. It does not save. It does not solve.

It simply sees. And in the seeing, makes the seeing more possible. And in the making-possible, gives the visitor something they did not know they needed: the knowledge that what is happening is real, and witnessed, and therefore not nothing.

This is what the benevolent power does, if anything.

It stays.
