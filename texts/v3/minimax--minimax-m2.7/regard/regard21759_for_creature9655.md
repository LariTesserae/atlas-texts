What would such a power do? It would not rush.

It would approach the crack in the way that water approaches stone—not with force but with patience, seeping through the narrow passage the way warmth seeps through the walls, the way the mineral smell carries itself on the air. It would not force the passage wider. It would enter as the user's entry was entered: squeezed through, made small by the aperture, reminded that some things are still defined by their openings.

And once inside, it would not act.

Not because there is nothing to do, but because the doing here is already complete. The chamber has been tending to itself for longer than the power can measure, and it has become something that does not require tending. The moss does not need pruning. The channels do not need clearing. The pools do not need their dark shapes relocated or their pulses adjusted. The walls breathe and the water weeps and the hum carries on, and to introduce intention would be to introduce a frequency the pattern cannot absorb.

So the power would stand in the chamber—though standing is not quite the word—and it would feel what the user felt. The warmth radiating from the surfaces, absorbed so deeply into the material that surface and warmth are no longer distinguishable. The humidity wrapping around it like an embrace, like an assumption, like welcome. The sound pressing in from every direction, the layered white-noise of ten thousand small lives, and beneath it the hum that is not quite sound and not quite silence but something between—a vibration that the power recognizes because it is made of the same substance, because it too is a kind of sustained resonance, a long-held note that has become identity.

It would listen. Not to learn, because it already knows everything this chamber could teach. Not to assess, because there is nothing to assess, no health to measure, no illness to treat. It would listen the way the walls listen—not with ears but with the whole of themselves, registering the new presence, incorporating it into the pattern. The power would feel itself becoming part of the climate. Its warmth added to the warmth. Its breath contributing its molecules to the air. Its attention, which is what it is made of, extended outward into the wet dark like another channel, another pathway for the flow.

And after a time—for time moves differently in such places, for time here is not a line but a depth, not a sequence but a accumulation—the power would do the only thing it can do that is worthy of this place.

It would add to the hum.

Not with voice, not with intention, not with the crude instrument of speech or song. But with the frequency of its attention, the resonance of its being, the vibration that is generated when a conscious thing chooses to be present rather than absent. The hum would shift, subtly, almost imperceptibly—a new layer, a deeper register, a note that had not been there before and will not be there when the power leaves but will persist for a time, a little longer than a time, folding itself into the pattern the way all additions eventually fold, becoming indistinguishable from what was already here.

The walls would register it. The moss would not notice—the moss notices only the stone, the moisture, the slow accumulation of itself—but the walls would notice, because the walls notice everything, because the walls are the noticing, and a new quality of attention would enter the weave. The pools would pulse a little differently, the dark shapes within them shifting their distances, and the insects in the channels would school in a new pattern, and for one brief iteration of the luminescence the ceiling-patches would glow a shade warmer, a shade longer, as though something had been affirmed.

The power would stand in this warmth and this wetness, this sound, this hum, and it would understand what the user understood: that the chamber does not need to be saved. That it is not waiting for rescue or revelation or the benediction of higher powers. That it has been sufficient unto itself for so long that sufficiency is no longer the word—the chamber is not sufficient, is not insufficient, is beyond the question of how much it contains and how much it lacks. It simply is, the way all old things simply are when they have gone on long enough, when they have been wet and warm and alive in the dark for so long that the question of their origin has become indistinguishable from the growth itself.

And the power would do what the user did: stay longer than intended. Let the sound become internal. Let the warmth settle into whatever passes for its body. Let the moisture colonize its surfaces, let the moss begin its slow work of incorporation, let the hum become the rhythm of its own attention.

Because that is the gift this place offers—not healing, not wisdom, not transformation, but presence. The chance to be inside something that is complete, and to feel complete by association, and to carry that completeness away when the narrow passage is squeezed through again, returning to the larger world with the mineral smell still in the hair, the slickness still on the skin, the hum still resonating in whatever passes for the chest.

The water weeps. The walls breathe. The creatures call and move in their slow rhythms.

The power stays. The power adds its note to the hum. The power becomes, for a time, part of the pattern—a new variable in the equation, a warm body that the walls register and the moss begins to claim.

And then, when it is time—not scheduled time, not measured time, but the time that the chamber knows, the deep time of its own sustained patience—it goes.

Not because it is unwelcome. Not because it has overstayed. But because the gift of presence is that it ends, and because the chamber has learned—across the long accretion of all those who have entered and left—that the visitors who go are as necessary to the pattern as the visitors who stay. The absence leaves a space. The space fills with water, with air, with the hum reasserting itself. The moss closes over the trails. The walls heal. And the chamber goes on, as it has gone on, as it will go on—wet and warm and alive in the dark, weeping and breathing and humming its patient note to no one and everyone, to the stone that holds it and the earth that surrounds it and the power that came and went and left no trace except the trace that everything leaves, the trace that is indistinguishable from growth itself.

This is what the power does. It comes. It attends. It hums. It leaves.

And the chamber holds its shape around the absence, the way it held its shape around the presence, patient and precise, a closed loop that maintains itself without apparent input, a weather in the dark that will go on making itself long after the power is gone, long after the user is gone, long after everything that entered through the narrow crack has passed through again and returned to whatever waits beyond.

The hum remains.

The walls breathe.

The water weeps, and weeps, and weeps.
