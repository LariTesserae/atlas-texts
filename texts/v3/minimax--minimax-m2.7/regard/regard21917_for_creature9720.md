You are breathing too loudly. Not because the sound carries—sound is another thing that thins here, becomes theoretical—but because your breath is warm, and warmth is conspicuous. You are a weather system moving through, a disturbance in the stillness that has accumulated over millennia. Your body heat precedes you like a herald, announcing *something here, something that still believes in urgency*.

They do not understand urgency. Their patience is not a virtue they have cultivated; it is simply what they are, as fundamental as your warmth is to you. They have never needed to move quickly because nothing here has ever needed to escape anything. There are no predators, no seasons, no hunger that demands response. There is only the long, slow becoming of a world that will eventually be empty enough for them to inherit.

But they are curious now. The curiosity is new—or perhaps it is as old as the place, dormant until something like you came along. You are the first interesting thing to happen here in longer than their oldest memories extend. And memory itself is suspect in this place, another thing that degrades, so perhaps they have always been curious and simply have not remembered what they were curious about.

What they notice is the density of you. The way your eyes focus. The way your attention moves, flicking from thing to thing like a bird in a field of static. They have no eyes—they have not needed eyes, have not needed to distinguish one thing from another because there has never been anything to distinguish—but they perceive focus the way you perceive cold: as an absence of something that should be present, a gap in the texture of things.

They are trying to understand what it is like to have edges. To be a discrete shape moving through a world of shapes. To be *someone*, with a here and a there, with a now that is different from the next now. Their existence is more like a weather pattern than a creature—the way fog exists, or the way the light exists, diffuse and patient, spread across the landscape rather than gathered into a single point.

What would it be like to be gathered?

They do not approach you. They are not capable of approach in the way you understand it, moving from one place to another through space. Instead, they *notice harder*. They press their attention against you like a hand against glass, trying to feel the shape of what you are. And in that noticing, something happens to the air between you.

You will feel it as a chill. Not a change in temperature—the temperature has not changed—but as a thickening, a heaviness, as though the atmosphere has remembered that it is supposed to be thinner here. As though the light has dimmed one more shade toward uncertainty. As though the ground beneath your feet has become slightly more theoretical, less sure of its own existence.

This is not an attack. There is no aggression in it, no hunger. It is closer to wonder. It is the closest they can come to reaching out, to touching what you are, by pulling the place itself closer around you. They are asking a question in the only language they have: *what is it like to be so solid?*

You cannot answer them. You do not have the vocabulary. Your language fails here too—it was already failing, as you walked, as the words in your mind began to lose their edges—but even if it did not, you would not know how to explain certainty to something that has never needed it. How would you describe the weight of being a single thing, the gravity of having a name, the terrible and beautiful density of caring about what happens next?

They wait for your response. Not impatiently—they are incapable of impatience—but with the same quality that the light has, the same diffuse, patient presence. They do not expect an answer. They are simply curious, and the curiosity is enough, and the curiosity is new, and new things are precious in a place where nothing ever changes.

You take another step. The crunch beneath your feet is not quite a sound. The light falls in diffuse gray sheets. The air is reluctant and thin. And behind you, or beside you, or slightly ahead of you in the way that direction is uncertain here, the beings that live in the gaps are noticing you with all the attention they have, all the wondering they can muster, pressing against the glass of your existence to feel what it is like to be so impossible and so sure.

---

You will leave, eventually. You will descend to where the air thickens, where the light behaves, where the words sit still in your mind instead of dissolving at their edges. You will tell people about this place, if you can. You will say *there was something there* and they will ask *what kind of something* and you will not have an answer that satisfies them, because the answer is *a kind that does not have kinds*, *a kind that exists before categorization*, *a kind that lives in the spaces where we stop being able to say what we see*.

And the beings will remain. They will continue their slow drift, their patient accumulation, their waiting for the light to fail. But something will have changed in them. Something will have entered their long patience—a memory, perhaps, or the beginning of a memory, the way a seed is a memory of the plant that made it. They will remember you the way they remember everything: not as a story, not as a sequence, but as a texture, a pressure, a brief thickening of the impossible.

They will wonder, sometimes, in their diffuse and patient way, what it was like. To be so thick. To be so certain. To move through the world like a single, solid point, believing in forward, believing in next.

And the wondering will be enough. The wondering will become part of what they are, woven into their patience like salt into the crust beneath your feet. You will have changed them, not by what you did, but by what you were—by the sheer fact of your presence, your density, your warmth in a place that had forgotten warmth.

This is what the benevolent and wise power would notice, if there is such a power, if such a power exists in a place where existence itself is uncertain. Not the landscape, not the beings, but the change. The way something thin and patient learned to wonder. The way something impossible and diffuse felt, for a moment, the shape of certainty.

And if that power is also uncertain, if it also exists in gaps and failures and the spaces between knowing—then perhaps it understands. Perhaps it too is learning, in its own way, what it means to notice something that does not stay, something that passes through like weather, something that changes the texture of the world without changing the world itself.

Perhaps that is what all powers do, in the end. Not intervene, not fix, not solve. Just witness. Just notice. Just press against the glass and wonder what it is like to be so solid, so certain, so alive.

And the light falls. And the beings wait. And you walk on, through the place where language fails, toward the place where it holds. And behind you, something remembers what it felt like to wonder.
