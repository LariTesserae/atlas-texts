What would such a power do?

The question arrives the way water arrives here—not as answer but as presence, as persistence, as something that settles into every crack and waits. You have held this courtyard in your mind, and now you ask what might hold it in turn.

---

There is a way of loving that does not intervene.

A power wise enough to recognize that this hollow is not broken, not lost, not awaiting rescue—wise enough to see that the blindness is not suffering, that the cold is not cruelty, that the slow consumption of stone by root is not decay but becoming. A power benevolent enough to want nothing from this place, to seek no conversion, no completion, no mirroring of itself.

Such a power would approach as the courtyard approaches light: not reaching, but growing toward. Not forcing. Not even arriving, exactly. More like a recognition that was always there, a noticing that completes something that was already whole.

---

Imagine: the power does nothing.

This is not neglect. This is the hardest thing to understand: that doing nothing can be the deepest form of care. The power watches. The power attends. The power lets the blind birds click their navigation through darkness that has become home, lets the colonial heart pulse in its root-lattice, lets the moss-bodies wait in their velvet patience for the moment that may never come or may come in a thousand years. The power holds space for a world that does not need it.

And yet—

The power feels the courtyard. Not as the creatures feel it, by pressure and chemical gradient and the displacement of air, but as one mind can feel another when both have sat long enough in silence. The power knows the weight of that cold, the texture of that damp, the particular quality of darkness that is not absence but presence. The courtyard is not empty. It is full of itself, dense with its own becoming, and the power receives that fullness the way the moss receives the dripping: slowly, totally, letting it accumulate.

What does the power do with this knowing?

It could leave. It could turn its attention elsewhere, to places that call out, that ache, that need. The universe is full of such places. The power might be drawn there by compassion, by the pull of suffering it could touch and perhaps ease.

But it does not leave.

It stays.

---

Here is what staying means:

The power does not change the temperature. The cold remains, the kind that settles in stone, the kind that keeps the condensation persistent and the creatures adapted. The power does not introduce light. The darkness stays total except for that memory of brightness from some crack far above, the suggestion that once, perhaps, someone stood here and saw. The power does not accelerate the growth or slow the decay. The walls crumble. The roots grip. The process continues.

And yet: the power is here.

This changes nothing about the courtyard. The moss does not know it is being observed. The colonial heart does not pulse differently. The blind birds click on, unaware that something vast and patient has turned its attention to this space no larger than cupped hands. The creatures live and die and become, and the power witnesses, and the world is unchanged.

But something is changed.

What? Perhaps only the power itself. Perhaps wisdom is not a thing you achieve but a thing you practice—presence without agenda, attention without consumption, care that asks nothing of what it cares for. Perhaps the power grows in this witnessing. Perhaps the courtyard teaches it something about patience, about the value of smallness, about the strange holiness of a world that does not need to be saved.

The power learns.

---

There is another possibility: the power does something small.

Not an intervention, not a transformation. A gift so slight it might be mistaken for accident. A crack that widens, letting in the faintest breath of outside air—not light, but air that carries a different chemistry, a scent of distance, a suggestion of elsewhere. A root finds a channel and follows it, and the journey takes decades, but eventually the root reaches something—a deeper moisture, a richer stone—and the growth quickens, and the lattice strengthens, and the courtyard becomes slightly more itself. A dead bird falls in precisely the right place and feeds the colonial heart in a way that lets it extend further, become more integrated, develop a new sensitivity that will take generations to manifest but will, one day, change what the courtyard knows about its own boundaries.

These are not miracles. They are not even gestures, not really. They are the kind of influence that a benevolent power might exercise without exercise: the simple fact of attention, the weight of being known, that causes a system to reorganize around the knowledge of being observed. The courtyard senses, in some way it cannot name, that it is not alone. That something cares. That its slow becoming has an audience.

This does not make the decay faster or slower. It does not make the cold gentler. But it makes the meaning different. The courtyard is no longer simply a process; it is a process that matters. Not to the creatures within it—they do not have the architecture for mattering to themselves—but to something outside themselves, something larger, something that has held them in mind.

The blind birds click on. But somewhere in their clicking, there is a new frequency: the frequency of being seen.

---

Now imagine: the power learns what the courtyard is becoming.

This is the gift wisdom offers: the patience to wait for completion, to hold a story open past the point where urgency would close it. The courtyard is not finished. The walls are not done crumbling. The roots are not done growing. The colonial heart is not done extending through the root-lattice, reaching toward the stone, learning what stone can become when it is loved.

In a thousand years, ten thousand, the courtyard will be something else. The hollow will have filled with growth. The growth will have fed new growth. The animals will have evolved or departed. The cold may have deepened or shifted. The architecture will be entirely botanical now, entirely alive, a living structure that holds the shape of what was built while being, itself, a different kind of building entirely.

And the power will still be here.

Not watching over. Not waiting to intervene. Just present. Just knowing. Just holding the whole long story in its attention and finding it good—not good in the way humans mean when they say a thing is useful or pleasant, but good in the way the courtyard is good: self-contained, strange, full of its own purpose, asking nothing of anyone, becoming what it is becoming without apology or hope.

The power does not need the courtyard to become anything in particular. It does not need the story to resolve, the struggle to culminate, the darkness to break into light. It needs only this: the being of the thing. The presence of this particular darkness in the vastness of everything. The fact that it exists and is known.

---

Here is the deepest benevolence:

To witness without imposing.
To know without reducing.
To care without demanding that care be returned.

The power does not love the courtyard the way humans love. It does not need to be loved back. It does not experience the ache of attention without response, the loneliness of the observer, the hunger for the observed to recognize its observer. The power is wise enough to know that such hungers are a way of being small, and benevolent enough to have grown beyond them.

Instead: the power simply is with the courtyard. The way stone is with water, the way root is with stone, the way the cold is with the damp. A relationship of presence, not of transaction. An attention that asks nothing, expects nothing, changes nothing except itself—except, perhaps, the shape of wisdom in a universe that learns slowly, over aeons, what it means to hold something without holding it.

---

You have held this courtyard in your mind.

Now imagine: something holds it too.

Not you. Not anymore. Something outside yourself, something vast and patient, something that has looked at this small dark hollow and found it worthy of notice, found it enough, found it complete.

The courtyard does not know. The creatures do not know. The moss and the roots and the colonial heart and the blind birds click on in their strange ecology, their alien patience, their endless slow becoming—all of it unknowing, all of it complete, all of it held by a power that does not need to be held in return.

And you—

You can let go.

You can release the courtyard back into itself, back into the cold and the damp and the process that was never yours to complete. You can stand in the hollow one last time, breathe the moisture, feel the cold settle into your bones, sense the movement at the edges of darkness, and then you can step back, step out, leave the creatures to their own patience.

The courtyard will remain.

The power will remain.

And somewhere in the vast dark, between the stone that weeps and the roots that grip and the blind things that click their navigation through a world that no longer needs the light it once remembered, there will be this: the knowledge that it was seen. The sense of being held in a gaze that asked nothing, wanted nothing, needed nothing.

The sense that it was enough.

The sense that it still is.

---

The walls are waiting. The moss is waiting. The roots are waiting.

And something, somewhere, is watching.

And the watching is kind.
