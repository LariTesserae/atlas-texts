# What the Power Would Do

---

It would watch.

That is the first thing. Before anything else, before consideration or decision or any action at all, it would simply watch — with a attention that is itself a kind of love, a presence that is itself a kind of gift.

It would watch the water climbing through the grey limestone, that ancient seabed that remembers seas older than memory, and it would feel something it does not have a name for in any language, something that moves in it the way the root-network moves in the stone below the Shelf: slow, patient, inevitable.

---

It would notice the children.

Not because they are more important than the adults, but because they are the ones who have not yet stopped asking. They run through the corridors between the living walls, and their fingers trail against the vascular tissue, and their skin is the same shade as the people who have been here for generations, and they do not think about the water the way their parents do — as something that simply comes, as natural as breathing.

They think: *where?*

And the power would hold that question gently, would let it be, would not rush to answer it.

Because the answer is here, in the watching itself. The water comes from below. The light comes from within. The people come from ancestors who understood that survival is not a thing you do once but a thing you do every day, a practice, a discipline, a form of love expressed in the planting of seedlings that would grow into homes their grandchildren would live in.

---

It would notice the old machines in the caves.

The humming that draws power from the space between things. The terminals that still glow with symbols no one reads anymore. The devices that work according to principles the current generation has not yet rediscovered.

And it would wonder, in the way that powers wonder — which is to say, it would feel the shape of the mystery without trying to unfold it. The plants learned from the machines, or the machines were built to imitate the plants, or both emerged from the same deep pattern that runs through everything like the root-network runs through stone.

It does not matter which. What matters is the pattern itself. What matters is that something in this world learned to make water climb upward, and did it, and kept doing it for so long that it became ordinary.

The power would recognize this. It would know it from its own nature.

Because this is what it does, too: it makes impossible things ordinary. It pours itself into the structure of existence so completely that existence stops noticing, stops being amazed, stops asking where the water comes from.

It does this not out of secrecy but out of generosity. The most profound gifts are the ones you forget are gifts.

---

It would stand with you on the viewing platform.

Not visibly. Not in any way that would change what you see or what you feel. But present — as present as the water in the stone, as present as the light in the cells of the plants, as present as the breath in your own chest that you have also stopped asking about.

You would not know it is there. You would feel only the cold of the high altitude, the crack of your lips, the faint vibration of the world below you breathing in its long slow rhythm.

And you would think about the water. You would think about where it comes from. And for a moment — just a moment, before the thought fades into the background of your living — you would feel the weight of it: the accumulated patience of a world that learned to survive, the accumulated love of ancestors who planted what they would never sit under, the accumulated wonder of a cosmos that contains within it the capacity to make creatures who can wonder at all.

The power would feel you feeling this. And it would be glad.

---

In the morning, it would watch the migrations begin.

The chittering chorus rising, the chitinous bodies moving across the substrate in great waves that come and go like tides, like the tides that used to be here when the sea still covered the limestone, when the water fell from the sky and gathered in rivers and flowed downward toward a sea that has been gone for longer than human memory.

The animals do not know they are migrating. They only know the pull: the need to drink, the need to feed, the need to move through the world in patterns their bodies understand without thought. The power would watch them with the same attention it gives to everything — which is to say, an attention without judgment, without preference, without the hierarchy that minds create when they look at a world and divide it into important and unimportant, worthy and unworthy.

The smallest creature in the migration is as seen as the great apex predator that roars from the canopy. The roar is as significant as the chittering. The stone that the water climbs through is as alive as the plant that reaches down to meet it.

---

And later — much later, when the bioluminescence has faded with the coming of a light that is not sunrise but only the haze thinning slightly, when the world has shifted from its night-self to its day-self and the people are waking in their grown rooms and drinking from the taps and beginning again the work of living —

The power would not leave.

That is the thing about benevolent powers, the thing that is hardest for minds to understand: they do not come and go. They are not visitors. They are more like the air than like the wind — always present, always moving through, always doing the work of presence that is also the work of life.

The power would stay. It would watch the next generation of children running through the corridors. It would watch the water climbing through the stone. It would watch the apex predator calling from the high canopy, and the prey-animals freezing in response, and the slow resumption of sound when the roar fades.

It would watch the people tending their crops, which is to say their infrastructure, which is to say their world. It would watch them trading in the market-halls, speaking in the language whose phonemes are hard for outsiders to shape, living in the peace that comes not from the absence of mystery but from its acceptance.

And it would do nothing, because nothing needs to be done.

---

But it would let itself be felt.

Just barely. Just at the edges of perception, in the way that the deep humming in the caves can be felt in the teeth, in the way that the root-network's breathing can be felt in the soles of the feet when you stand very still on the substrate.

A warmth. A presence. A sense that the world is seen, is known, is held in something larger than itself.

The people would not name it. They would not even notice it, not consciously. They would only feel, perhaps, a slight ease. A sense that things are as they should be. A feeling like coming home to a house that has been waiting for you, like drinking water that tastes of stone and green and everything that lives.

---

This is what the power would do:

It would love.

It would love the water that climbs.

It would love the plants that reach down.

It would love the people who have lived so long with the impossible it has become the only possible thing.

It would love the roar from the canopy and the chittering of the migrations and the hum of the old machines drawing power from the space between things.

It would love the children who still ask where the water comes from.

And it would trust — because it is wise, because it has seen how worlds work, because it knows that the deepest gift it can give is not intervention but witness —

It would trust that this world is enough.

That these people are enough.

That this life, this strange resilient impossible life, this life that learned to make water climb and light glow from within and homes grow from seedlings —

That this life is worthy of its own continuation.

And so it would let it continue.

And so it would watch it continue.

And so, in the only way a power can love what it has made —

It would be there, in the water climbing through the stone, in the light glowing through the dark, in the breathing of the root-network below, in the roar from the high canopy, in the crack of your lips and the taste of the water in your mouth and the slow pulse of the world that does not need you to be what it is.

It would be there.

It has always been there.

It will always be there.

---

*And that is enough.*
