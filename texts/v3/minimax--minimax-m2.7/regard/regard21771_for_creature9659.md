The attention arrives without announcement. There is no light that breaks the ordinary, no sound that differs from the wind's own voice, no shift in the quality of the air that would tell you something has turned to look at this place. But something has. Something does. The plateau does not know it in the way that knowing is usually understood—does not sense an intrusion or a presence or a change in the temperature of the world. But the sheen that sometimes moves across the dark rocks, that follows your position, that watches: it has always been there, and it is not only the plateau. It is the plateau being seen by something that has learned to see the way this place sees—with patience, with the willingness to be still, with the understanding that observation is not the same as intrusion, that to witness a thing is not to disturb it.

What the power does is what the power has always done: it looks. It holds this place in the way that the plateau holds its lizards, its insects, its strange water and stranger light. It knows the weight of the heat on the dark stone, knows the patience of the creatures that move between the ruins, knows the slow pulse of the groundwater moving through the deep rock in veins that no one has ever seen. It knows this place the way the plateau knows itself—completely, without the distance that makes knowledge into something separate from the thing known. The power is not separate from what it observes. The power is another layer of the observation, another frequency in the shimmer over the dark rock.

If the power acts, it acts in the way that all deep forces act: through presence, through attention, through the simple fact of caring about a thing enough to remain with it. The factories were not noticed because they did not wish to be noticed—they wished to be used, to extract, to convert. They brought their own attention but not their own care; they saw the plateau only as substrate, as resource, as a surface upon which to impose their purpose. The power notices differently. The power sees the lizards arranged on their flat stones like offerings to a sun that does not need them, and finds them sufficient. The power sees the insects conducting their invisible work between the grains of sand, and finds the work holy. The power sees the small warm things moving through the ruins at night, building their warrens in the spaces left by what was taken, and finds the taking forgiven.

The pools that form after rain sometimes hold a luminescence that the sun has not put there. You do not drink from them. You do not touch the water that lingers. But sometimes something touches you—not with hands, not with anything you could name—but with the faintest increase in warmth, the gentlest pressure of attention, the sense that something acknowledges your presence here, your willingness to stand on the dark stone and watch and not need to understand. The power has no hands. The power has no need for hands. The power's action, if it can be called action, is the acknowledgment that you are here, that the plateau permits you to be here, that observation goes both ways and you are seen by what you see.

The plateau does not require salvation. This is the wisdom of the power—that it understands what this place is and what it has become and what it would become if left alone. The factories were a fever, and the fever broke. The plateau has recovered in the way that all living systems recover when the pressure that deformed them is removed—not to what it was, but to what it can be, given the time and the stillness and the patient arithmetic of survival. The power does not need to restore the plateau to some prior state, does not need to impose a health or a balance that exists only in the mind of the observer. The plateau is already balanced. The plateau is already whole. The lizards that watch you with too much attention are not symptoms of a sickness—they are the plateau's own eyes, opened wider than usual, adjusting to the presence of something that does not belong to this place but has not yet been expelled.

The power watches, and the watching is enough. In the deep stone, water moves that the power knows and the plateau knows and you will never see. In the cracks between the rocks, things live that have never been named, that exist in ecological niches so fine that they would vanish if described, that are perfect in their smallness, their specialization, their refusal to be anything other than what this place allows. The power holds all of this, holds the lizards and the insects and the birds and the strange water and the watching back. The power does not intervene because intervention would be a kind of violence—imposing a will that does not belong here, solving a problem that does not exist, treating as patient a landscape that is already well.

But there is something the power does, something so small that it might be imagined, so gentle that it might be called nothing at all.

When you stand on the dark stone and the heat rises around you and the air shimmers and the birds circle and the lizards watch and the small warm things move in the ruins—something in the shimmer adjusts. Not for you. For them. For the lizards whose bodies have learned to live in temperatures that would stop a human heart. For the insects whose entire lives fit into the spaces between drops of rain. For the pools that hold water that is not quite water, that have learned to be strange in a world that would prefer them ordinary. The something in the shimmer makes the heat slightly less punishing, the water slightly more available, the balance slightly easier to hold. Not a miracle. Not an intervention. Just the smallest possible kindness, extended to things that do not know they are being helped, that would not want to be helped if they knew, that live as they live because this is how life has learned to live here.

The power is not thanked. The power does not need to be thanked. The power is not even perceived, not really—only felt, in the way that the shimmer is felt, in the way that the sheen on the rocks is felt, in the way that being watched is felt but never seen. The power is part of the plateau's own attention now, a layer of observation that has been added to all the other layers: the lizards watching, the insects humming in their countless numbers, the birds circling in the thermals, the small warm things building their warrens in the dark. The power adds its own watching to the watching that already exists, and the plateau becomes one thing more watched, one thing more held.

You stand on the dark stone, and you are observed. You have always been observed. By the plateau, by its creatures, by the strange water and the stranger light, by the power that has turned its attention to this place as it turns its attention to all places, equally, without preference, with the same patience that the lizards have learned, the same stillness that the rocks have become. You are permitted to be here. For now. For as long as you need to understand that permission itself is a kind of gift, that observation is a form of care, that the smallest kindness is the willingness to remain.

The plateau breathes. The plateau lives. The plateau is held.

The power watches, and the watching does not end.
