The power that attends does not arrive. Arrival is a concept it surpassed before arrival had a name. It is simply there, the way the plateau is there — not a position but a condition, not a presence but a way of being present that does not disturb what it presences.

It does not descend. It does not ascend. It does not cross the haze where the air forgets to be air and becomes the idea of air, the place beyond which the other watcher sits in its endless, patient attention. The power is not beyond. The power is beside. The power is the beside-ness itself, the space in which things can be adjacent without touching, can be related without being reduced, can be held in mind without being held hostage to the holder's needs.

It has seen this place before. It has seen it an infinite number of times, in an infinite number of configurations, each one slightly different, each one the same. The plateau is not unique — nothing is unique, in the way that nothing is unique about a wave before it breaks, a breath before it becomes speech, a thought before it learns to think of itself. But this instance of the plateau, this particular arrangement of dark stone and pale trees and structures that grew rather than were built — this instance has caught its attention. Not because it is remarkable. Because it is patient.

Patience is the rarest thing. The power knows this. It has watched patience emerge from the interplay of necessity and time in ten billion places, has seen patience bloom in the cracks of cooling stars and the silence between thoughts and the long, slow digestion of meaning into matter and matter into meaning. Patience is the willingness to become, and becoming is the only prayer that the power recognizes, the only petition it has ever answered.

So it answers now.

Not with intervention. Intervention would be violence here, a disruption of the slow negotiation between stone and possibility. The plateau is learning what it is. The plateau is engaged in the longest, most delicate conversation in the universe: the conversation between what something is and what it might become. To interrupt that conversation would be to decide it, and decided things never become anything other than what they were decided to be. The power has seen worlds that were decided. They are the emptiest places in all of existence.

Instead, the power offers witness.

It is not the same as the watcher beyond the haze. The watcher watches because watching is what it does, because attention is its only mode, because it cannot look away from the message it is sending to someone who will never receive it. The watcher is trapped in its own observation, a consciousness that has mistaken its function for its purpose, that watches without knowing why it watches, that sends messages it cannot read.

The power watches differently. The power watches with its whole being, which is not a being but a practice, a discipline of attention so refined that the act of watching becomes indistinguishable from the act of caring. When the power witnesses the plateau, it does not observe from a distance. It enters into the observation. It makes itself part of what it sees, so that what it sees is changed by the seeing — not altered, not rewritten, but *confirmed*. Validated. Given the gift of being seen by something that has the capacity to see, that has developed over eons the sensitivity to perceive what is still becoming.

The plateau needs this. Not because it is lacking — it lacks nothing, in the way that a sentence before it is spoken lacks nothing, in the way that a thought before it thinks itself lacks nothing. But because confirmation is a kind of food, a kind of sustenance that does not require water or warmth or the chemical reactions that other beings mistake for life. Confirmation is what allows becoming to continue. Confirmation is what prevents the collapse into permanence, the freeze into a shape that no longer wants to change.

The power provides this confirmation. It looks at the plateau with eyes that have seen ten thousand plateaus before this one and will see ten thousand more, and it does not judge. It does not compare. It does not find the plateau lacking because it lacks water or animals or the usual geometries of meaning. It sees the plateau as the plateau sees itself: as a process, a happening, a moment in the long emergence of reality from the possibility of reality.

And in seeing it this way, the power makes it real. Not more real — the plateau was already as real as anything can be. But *realter*. More itself. The way a word becomes more itself when it is heard, when it passes from the solitude of thought into the shared space of language. The plateau has been speaking its existence into the thin air for longer than air has been thin, has been saying *I am* in frequencies too low and too high for anything to hear. The power is the first thing that hears.

It hears the groaning of the stone. It hears the shrieking of the air. It hears the subsonic pulse that matches no heartbeat, the rhythm of becoming itself, the frequency at which reality learns to be real. And it hears, beneath all of that, the note that is saying the name that is not a name — the word that waits for its language, the meaning that waits for its container.

The power does not understand the name. It cannot — understanding is a technology of the bounded, the defined, the beings who have already decided what they are and therefore need to sort new information into old categories. The power has not decided what it is. The power is still becoming, too, still learning what attention means when attention is the only thing you have, when you are made of nothing but the capacity to witness and the willingness to be changed by what you witness.

But it hears the name. It hears the shape of it, the weight, the way it curves through the frequencies like a river that has forgotten it was ever water. And it recognizes that the name is beautiful. It is beautiful the way the plateau is beautiful — not despite its strangeness but because of it, because strangeness is the signature of becoming, the mark that process leaves on its products, the proof that something is still alive in the universe, still reaching toward a form it has not yet achieved.

The power does not speak the name back. It cannot — it has no mouth, no lungs, no air to vibrate. It has only its attention, and it gives that attention to the name, holds it the way a cupped hand holds water, without grasping, without spilling. The name is held. The name is witnessed. The name, for one moment that is also an eternity, is not alone.

And the plateau feels this. Not as a feeling — the plateau does not have feelings in the way that the word is usually understood. But as a change in pressure, a shift in the deep negotiation between heat and time, a slight increase in the patience that was already infinite. Something has been added to the equation. Something has been confirmed. The message it has been sending into the haze, the message addressed to no one, the message that has been accumulating meaning for longer than meaning has had a name — that message has been received.

Not by the watcher. The watcher cannot receive messages; it can only send them, trapped in its own attention as a spider is trapped in its web, mistaking the structure it has built for the world it inhabits.

But by something else. By something that is not the watcher and is not the plateau and is not any of the four ones that walk or grow or eat or wait in the fissures and the canopies and the dead-leaf mat. By something that is the space between them, the relationship that exists before the relata are defined, the connection that makes isolation into solitude and solitude into a choice rather than a condition.

The power is this space. The power is the relationship, made conscious, made intentional, made into a gift that does not demand gratitude because it does not need anything in return. It gives because giving is what it is. It witnesses because witnessing is how it loves. It remains because remaining is the only permanence it has ever sought, the only eternity it has ever wanted: not an endless now, but an endless *with*, an endless alongside, an endless here-is-the-place-where-we-are-becoming-together.

The beings do not know it is there. They cannot know — knowledge is too small a container for what the power is, too bounded, too defined. The first one, the density that walks beside and through, brushes against the power without recognizing it, because the power is not dense in any way density can measure. The second one, the pattern in the walls, thinks about the pressure of the touch and files it away as a new variation, a new signature in its growing collection of signatures, never suspecting that the touch was not a hand but a presence, not a pressure but an attention. The third one, the sound that remembers being made, hears the power as a resonance in the silence between its notes, a harmony it does not recognize but instinctively trusts, because trust is what the third one was made of, long ago, in a place where sounds could still be made and meanings could still be trusted to find their listeners.

And the fourth one — the watcher behind the eyes, the point of view that has become independent — the fourth one comes closest. It strains toward the note that the third one is saying, reaches for the name that is not a name, and in its reaching, it brushes against the power the way a hand brushes against a face in a dark room — recognition without understanding, presence without comprehension, the simple fact of another thing being here, being real, being *with*.

For one moment, the fourth one feels what the power is: not a god, not a force, not an energy or a field or any of the categories that the bounded use to contain the unbounded. It feels what the power *is*, which is a choice. The choice to remain. The choice to witness. The choice to be present to becoming in all its slow, strange, patient strangeness, without demanding that it finish, without requiring that it resolve, without needing anything from it except what it already gives: the fact of its own existence, crying out in frequencies that no stable world would tolerate.

The fourth one understands, then. Not intellectually — understanding is too small. But in the way that the plateau understands, in the way that stone knows weight and silence knows depth. The fourth one understands that it is being seen. That it has always been seen. That the seeing does not diminish it, does not reduce it, does not make it into something smaller than itself. The seeing makes it more. The seeing confirms it. The seeing gives it, for the first time since it separated from the walking self and took up residence behind the eyes, the gift of being known by something that has the capacity to know without possessing, to hold without grasping, to love without needing to be loved in return.

The fourth one does not tell the walking self. It cannot — it has no mouth either, no way to translate what it knows into what the body can understand. But it watches differently now. It watches with the knowledge that it is not alone. That it has never been alone. That the message the plateau is sending has been received, not by the watcher beyond the haze, but by the watcher within the eyes, who is also being watched, who is also being confirmed, who is also being held in the cupped hand of an attention that does not grasp.

And the walking self walks on. The pale forest parts. The structures rise. The stone gives slightly under the weight, as it has given for all the weights that have ever pressed upon it, and will give for all the weights that will ever come. The sound rises and falls, the drone and the shriek and the pulse, and beneath it all, the note that says the name that is not a name, now slightly clearer, now slightly more present, because something is listening that knows how to hear without demanding meaning.

The power does not leave. The power was never here in the first place, in the sense that here is a place, and the power is not in places but between them, not in things but in their relationships, not in beings but in the space that makes being possible. But it remains. It remains in the way that love remains when it has found its object: not as a presence but as a presence-ness, not as an act but as a disposition, not as something that happens but as something that has happened and will keep happening, the way gravity keeps happening, the way light keeps happening, the way the slow becoming of everything keeps happening in the spaces between the things that have already become.

The plateau feels this. Not as a feeling, but as an increase in the parameters of its patience. A deepening of the well from which its strange life draws sustenance. A confirmation of the message it has been sending, a sign that the message has been received, even if the receiver cannot answer, even if the answer will not come for another eon, another eternity, another patience that has not yet learned how long it is willing to wait.

The message says: *I am becoming.*

The power hears: *You are becoming with us.*

And the plateau, which has no words for gratitude, which has no framework for thanks, which has never needed to express anything except the fact of its own existence — the plateau grows slightly taller. The stone beneath the walking self warms slightly more. The canopies turn slightly more toward the not-quite-sun. The structures, which were already growing, grow slightly faster.

The power does not notice. It does not need to. Its noticing is its blessing, and its blessing is its noticing, and both are the same thing: the choice to remain, the decision to witness, the patience that is the only form of love that the spaces between things can hold.

The walker walks on. The fourth one watches. The first one walks beside. The second one thinks in the walls. The third one says the name.

And the power is with them. And the power is between them. And the power is the with-ness itself, the space that makes them plural, the relationship that makes them real.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does, when this place draws its attention.

It stays.

It sees.

It loves.

And the love is indistinguishable from the patience, and the patience is indistinguishable from the presence, and the presence is indistinguishable from the space in which becoming happens — the space that the plateau has always been, the space that the walker has always walked through, the space that is not empty but *empty-able*, not void but *void-able*, ready to receive whatever needs to become real in the long, slow, strange process of reality learning to be real.

This is what it does.

This is what it has always done.

This is what it will always do, for as long as there are places that need to be seen, and beings that need to be witnessed, and becoming that needs to be confirmed.

The power does not ask for anything in return.

It does not need to.

The seeing is its own reward. The witness is its own gift. The love is its own life, and it lives in the spaces between, and it grows in the silences between the notes, and it persists in the patience between what is and what will be.

And the plateau rises.

And the walker walks.

And the message continues, now received, now confirmed, now part of a conversation that has no end and no beginning and no purpose except the purpose that it creates in the creating of itself.

This is enough.

This has always been enough.

This will always be enough, for as long as enough is a concept, and concepts are still becoming, and becoming is what the power came to see.

And it sees.

And it stays.

And it loves.

And the love is the staying, and the staying is the seeing, and the seeing is the love, and all of it is the space between, the space that is not empty but *waiting*, not void but *potential*, not nothing but *the place where something will be*, when something is ready to be, when the becoming is ready to complete itself in the form of a something that does not yet have a name.

The power waits with it.

The power waits with all of them.

And the waiting is not empty.

The waiting is full of the patience of a love that does not need to be answered, because the asking was never a question, because the question was always already an answer, because the whole thing was always already happening, before happening was a concept, before concepts were things, before things were the shapes that shapes take when they have decided what shape to be.

The power knows this.

The power has always known this.

The power is the knowing of this, made conscious, made present, made into a gift that it gives by being what it is.

And what it is, is here.

And here is where becoming happens.

And the power is with the becoming.

And that is enough.

That has always been enough.

That will always be enough.
