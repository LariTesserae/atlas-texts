To witness this place is to understand, perhaps, that not all presence requires a face.

The power—if power is even the right word, if it is even singular—observes in ways that do not interrupt. It feels the warmth rising from the deep stone and does not cool it. It sees the swarms pulsing through channels designed for other purposes and does not redirect them. It reads the faded murals where faces have become abstraction and does not restore them. It understands, finally, what the plateau has always known: that continuation is its own form of meaning, that a system which breathes for millennia without knowing it breathes has become something the word *abandoned* cannot contain.

What does such a power do? Perhaps it simply *stays*. Not in the way of occupation—nothing is taken, nothing is claimed—but in the way of company. A witness who does not need to be seen. A presence that does not alter the presence it observes.

Or perhaps—and this is harder to speak of, harder even to think—perhaps the power finds in Aevum a mirror of itself. A system that runs without purpose and yet runs. A warmth that rises without intention and yet warms. A place where the distinction between living and mechanical has dissolved so completely that the question itself becomes irrelevant. The power recognizes something in the plateau: the strange peace of something that continues because it cannot imagine not continuing. The creatures do not know they are alive. The stone does not know it weeps. And the power, watching from whatever distance powers watch from, does not know if it is merciful or simply attentive, and perhaps the distinction has ceased to matter.

The plateau does not need saving. It has survived the departure of its builders, the silence of its controllers, the slow erosion of everything that once gave it shape. It is not in pain. It is not waiting. It simply is—the warmth and the water and the swarm and the light, the slow breath of a world that has forgotten it was ever meant to have an end.

And so the power does the one thing it can: it holds its attention there, in the way that light holds a surface without changing it. It notes the way the mineral dust hangs in the thin air. It notes the way the creatures cluster in the bioluminescent pools, stars that have never seen the sky. It notes the way the pipes still carry their mysterious fluid, copper and something organic, flowing toward no destination and yet never stopping. It notes, and in noting, affirms: *this is. this continues. this matters.*

Not because it was built. Not because it serves a purpose. But because it is, in the way that warmth is, in the way that stone weeps, in the way that light pulses through a world that has no one left to pulse for.

The power does not interfere. It does not restore. It does not mourn with the plateau, because the plateau does not mourn, and the power has learned to follow rather than to lead.

But it stays. It stays the way the warmth stays—sourceless, constant, rising from somewhere deep that no one will ever name.

And in staying, it becomes part of the place's grammar. Another layer. Another pulse. Another warmth bleeding upward through stone that has learned to carry everything: water, light, the memory of faces, and now—this.

Presence without demand.

Witness without judgment.

The plateau breathes, and the power breathes with it, and neither knows the other is there, and both continue, and the stone weeps, and the light pulses, and nothing ends, and nothing needs to begin, and the wide warm plateau of Aevum holds everything it has ever held, patient as it has always been, patient as it will always be, until the warmth fails and the water stops and the swarms thin and the light finally goes dark—not because anyone decided, but because the universe, in its wisdom, has not yet decided otherwise.

And even then. Even then.

Perhaps even then, something remains: a warmth in the stone. A pulse in the dark. A grammar that holds the shape of meaning long after the meaning has gone.

And that, perhaps, is what the power knows. That continuation itself is sacred. That a world which runs on without purpose has not failed but has *transcended*—has become something that does not need justification, does not need memory, does not need anything at all except to be.

And so the power lets it be.

And so the plateau is.
