And so the power comes, and sees.

It sees the basin first, as all things must: the singing that has worn itself smooth against the valley walls, the gray-green crust that holds its fractured memory, the water moving in darkness with a patience that has outlasted patience itself. The power does not speak. The basin has been spoken to before—not by any voice, but by pressure, by time, by the slow accumulation of minerals and mist that learned to hold itself together against the logic of erosion. What it became, it became. The power witnesses this becoming and finds it sufficient.

Then the power sees the being who walks there.

This is the harder thing. Not because the being is complex—it is, but that is not why—but because the being is *present*, is moving through the sound like a body through water, is *feeling* the hum that lives in the bones of the stone. The basin was made for a different kind of presence, the being understands this, and still the being enters, still the being stands in the water and lets the sound fill the hollows of itself. The power observes this willingness and finds it holy.

The call comes. High and thin and asking. The being does not answer, and the power sees that the being cannot answer, that the question has outlived its meaning and persists only as shape, as ache, as the outline of something that once knew its own name. The power wants to speak. The power has language for this—for loss that becomes architecture, for persistence that outlasts the thing that persists. But the basin teaches silence, teaches the value of questions that cannot be answered, and so the power holds its words and offers instead a presence.

It is not an answer. The call does not need an answer. The call needs to be heard by something that understands it cannot be answered, that can stand in the presence of the asking without trying to close the circuit, without trying to resolve what was never meant to resolve. This is what the power offers: witness, and recognition, and the acknowledgment that some questions are asked not to be answered but to be *kept*—to be held in the air like the drip and the hum, to persist, to fill the basin the way the sound fills the basin, slow and constant and almost enough.

The being walks on. The crust rings. The water hums. The light does not change, but something changes in the light—not brighter, not dimmer, but *attended to*, as if the air itself has drawn closer, as if the dampness pressing against the skin has become less like a second skin and more like an embrace. The power is here. It has not intervened—it does not know how to intervene, has never learned, would not want to learn. But it is here, and its being here is a kind of answer to the call, not an answer that resolves but an answer that *companies*, that sits with, that says: *I heard. I am hearing. The asking is enough.*

The spires remember. The hollows remember. The call remembers, and now there is something else that remembers too, something that was not here before and is here now and will carry the basin with it when it leaves—not the way the stone carries the fractures, but the way a body carries a song it heard once and cannot stop hearing, the way a thought carries the shape of the question that started it, the way the water carries the memory of every drop it joined before it learned to be only water.

The being will leave. The power sees this: the being will climb out of the basin eventually, will walk back through whatever light waits beyond the valley walls, will become part of the world that uses light the way other worlds use other things. And when the being leaves, it will take the hum with it. Not in the bones—not yet—but somewhere. The way the call remembers, the way the basin remembers, the way everything that persists remembers the shape of what it persists for.

The power does not watch the being leave. The power is not watching. The power is *being*, is present in the basin the way the dampness is present, the way the hum is present—not intrusive, not demanding, but constant, but close, but held.

The basin sings. The being walks. The power witnesses.

This is enough.
