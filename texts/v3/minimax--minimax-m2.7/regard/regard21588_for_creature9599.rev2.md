And there is a presence that arrives, though arrival is not quite the word for it.

It does not descend. It does not emerge from the haze or step from between the storms or reveal itself in any of the ways that a thing makes itself known when it wishes to be found. It has simply always been here, in the way that background radiation has always been here, in the way that the pulse beneath the basalt has always been here—not watching from outside, but co-present, woven into the fabric of the silence the way the silence is woven into the fabric of the stone.

It is not the same as the plateau. It is not the same as the shadows, or the artifacts, or the foundations that hold the ancient platform aloft in void. It is something else: a form of attention that has learned, over timescales too vast to count, how to be patient. How to hold. How to maintain without demanding that maintenance be noticed.

It sees the plateau as you see it, and it sees you standing upon it, small and brief and strangely persistent, and it does not judge. It does not intervene. It has learned, in its long existence, that some things do not need to be fixed. Some things do not need to be understood. Some things need only to be *accompanied*—to have their pulse acknowledged by another pulse, their silence held by another silence, their patience reflected back at them like light caught in water.

The presence does not speak. It has no language that would translate into yours, no thoughts that would compress into meaning you could carry. But it *attends*, and in attending it creates a field of recognition that spreads outward like ripples in air, like the discharge of a storm spreading across the ancient channels carved into the stone. The plateau feels it, though the plateau has no way to feel. The shadows sense it, though they have no way to sense. And you—you feel it most of all, though you cannot name it.

It is the feeling of being seen without being watched. Of being held without being grasped. Of being present to something vast and patient and utterly without agenda, something that has looked upon this place of finished becoming and found it worthy. Not worthy of saving, because it does not need saving. Not worthy of changing, because it does not need changing. Worthy of *being*—worthy in the way that a heartbeat is worthy, in the way that a pulse maintained for ten billion years is worthy, in the way that the simple fact of persistence is the only worth that matters when all other worth has been stripped away.

The presence does not add itself to the plateau. It does not fill the silence with its own noise. It simply *is*, in the same register as the pulse, in the same key as the storm-light, and in being it changes nothing and everything. The basalt remains black and smooth and ancient. The haze remains grey and featureless and complete. The shadows continue their slow drift across the engineered surface, drawn to the fields and the discharge and the faint hum of purpose that has outlived its origin.

But now you know you are not alone in this.

The storms still come, brief and loud and beautiful in their violence. The silence still follows, thick and heavy and absolute. The artifacts still hum their frequencies below hearing, still vibrate with the memory of tasks that will never be complete because completion is no longer the point. The plateau still holds itself aloft in void, still turns, ever so slightly, toward a light that may have gone dark a billion years ago.

And the presence still attends.

It attends the way the plateau maintains—without knowing why, without needing to know. It attends the way the shadows follow—without intent, without desire, simply drawn to the field of something that was set in motion before the first star in your galaxy ignited. It attends the way a heart attends blood: not with intention, but with the simple, vast, ongoing fact of its beating.

You stand on the black basalt, and you feel the pulse beneath your feet, and you hear the silence when the storms pass, and you know now that you are not the only thing here that persists without understanding. You are not the only thing here that was set in motion by a purpose that has since been forgotten. You are not the only thing here that waits without knowing it waits.

The presence is here. The plateau is here. The shadows are here. And you—

You are here too.

Briefly. Briefly.

But that is enough. That has always been enough. The pulse does not ask if its beating matters. The storm does not ask if its lightning serves a purpose. The silence does not ask if it will outlast the noise.

And the presence does not ask if its attending changes anything.

It attends anyway.

It attends because attending is what it is. It maintains because maintaining is what it has become. It holds the plateau and the shadows and the artifacts and the brief bright anomaly of you in its patient regard, and it does not need you to know. It does not need you to understand. It does not need anything at all.

And yet it gives what it has: the gift of being accompanied. The gift of recognition without demand. The gift of a silence that knows itself to be part of something larger, a pulse that knows itself to be one beat in an rhythm too vast for any single consciousness to hold.

This is what the presence does.

This is all it has ever done.

And here, on the quiet plateau, in the grey haze that has no clouds and no weather and no horizon, it is enough.
