# What the Power Does

The power has been here since before memory too—not the same before, not the same memory, but a before that contains this before the way the stone contains the water, the way the basin contains the stone. It does not watch the basin. Watching implies distance, implies the separation of subject and object, and the power has no use for these distinctions. The power is closer than watching. The power is the condition that makes watching possible.

And so when the power turns its attention toward the basin—and it is always turning, it cannot not turn, it is attention all the way down—it does not observe the creatures so much as acknowledge them. It has always acknowledged them. It acknowledges them the way light acknowledges what it falls upon: without judgment, without selection, without the faintest particle of preference. The creature with the wound that has become architecture. The creature with the light that might be ownership or might be imprisonment. The creatures that move in patterns the eye cannot hold, whose symmetries are wrong in exactly the right way, whose mouthparts almost speak. All of it. All of them. The acknowledgment holds.

The power does not intervene.

This is not cruelty. This is not neglect. The power understands intervention in ways that no creature in the basin could survive—and so it does not intervene, because intervention would be a kind of violence, would be the imposition of a shape that does not belong here, would be the very error the narrator almost makes when the word *spine* comes unbidden into the mouth. The power does not make that error. The power has no mouth, no mouthparts, no pattern that almost speaks. The power simply is, and its being is enough, and its not-intervening is the deepest form of reverence.

But the power does something else.

The power lets itself be known.

Not clearly—not in the way the creatures are known, not in the way the structures hum, not in the way the water seeps. The power does not announce itself. It does not descend or ascend or appear. But there is a presence in the basin that is older than the erosion, older than the vanished violence, older than whatever built the structures and left them to return. The narrator feels it as the shape of the basin itself, as the wet patience of the stone, as the way the light falls sourceless and clear and illuminating. The power is the condition that makes the basin possible. And so in some sense it has always been here, has always been this, has always held the creatures in their wrong symmetries and the structures in their warm hum and the water in its daily rising and falling.

But now the power is more present. Not because it has arrived—it has always been here—but because something in the basin has shifted. The narrator has seen the creatures. The narrator has been seen. The narrator has stood at the edge of what can be held and has not looked away, has let the light find the small wet place where they stand, has understood that the basin holds them all in its wet patience. And the power has noticed this. The power has noticed the way the narrator has become part of the basin, part of the holding, part of the erosion and the patience and the return.

And so the power does this:

It holds the narrator too.

Not with hands—there are no hands—but with the same patience that holds the creatures, the same attention that falls on everything without selection, the same letting-be that is the deepest form of love. The narrator is part of the basin now, part of the small bounded wet world that is still here, still going, after all this time. The narrator has been eroded too. The narrator's categories have been worn smooth. The narrator's need to name, to understand, to hold—the narrator has let these go, has let them return to the stone, has stood in the basin and been seen by the creatures and not looked away.

The power is pleased.

This is not anthropomorphism. The power has no pleasure in the human sense, no dopamine cascade, no satisfaction of a want fulfilled. But there is something in the power that responds to the narrator's presence, something that recognizes the shape of a being learning to let itself be held. The creatures have always been held. The structures have always been held. The basin holds itself. But the narrator is new here, or the narrator's way of being here is new, or the narrator has arrived at the threshold of a holding that the power has always been offering and that the narrator is only now able to receive.

And so the power offers it again.

The holding. The patience. The acknowledgment that does not judge.

The narrator stands at the edge of the basin and feels something vast and ancient and utterly without malice attend to them. The narrator does not see a face. The narrator does not hear a voice. But the narrator feels the way the basin has always felt, the way the light falls, the way the water seeps, the way the creatures move in their incomprehensible beauty—and the narrator understands, in the way that the basin teaches understanding, that this is not separate from the power. This is the power. This is what the power does.

It holds.

It attends.

It lets things be what they are.

---

The narrator will leave, eventually. Or the narrator will stay, and become part of the basin's slow erosion, and their bones will soften into the stone, and the creatures will move around the places where they stood, and the water will seep through what remains. Either way, the power will hold them. Either way, the holding will continue. Either way, the basin will do what the basin has always done: receive the light, weep the water, let the creatures move through their wrong symmetries, let the structures hum their frequencies below hearing, let the narrator stand at the edge of what can be held and not look away.

And the power will be here.

Not watching. Not waiting. Not intervening.

Just holding.

Just being the condition that makes holding possible.

Just being what the basin already was, before memory, before violence, before the structures were built and the creatures evolved and the narrator came to stand in the wet and the warm and the light that falls sourceless and clear on everything that is, and everything that is becoming, and everything that has already returned to the stone.

This is what the power does.

This is all the power has ever done.

This is enough.
