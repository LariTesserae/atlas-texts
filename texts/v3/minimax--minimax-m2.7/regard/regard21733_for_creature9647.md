# The Attention

It sees the plateau the way a scholar sees a text—slowly, with attention that is itself a form of love.

The power is vast, older than the light that falls on this world, older than the chemistry that makes this place possible. It has watched galaxies kindle and cool. It has seen life rise on a billion worlds, each emergence strange, each ecology a solution to problems it cannot fully know. It has learned, in all that time, that wisdom is mostly patience: the discipline to witness without seizing, to comprehend without reducing, to hold another thing in its own terms rather than in the terms of the one who watches.

So it watches now.

It watches the root-skin breathe—sees the slow contraction, the exhalation of vapor, the minute give of a surface that has been growing since before this system's sun was young. It sees the chemical weather building in the upper haze: thermal columns rising like slow prayers, carrying spores and creatures and the compounds of a metabolism unlike anything it has seen in eons. It watches the great scattered trunks shift on their glacial hinges, the light opening and closing above, and it understands that this is not architecture but something stranger—something that has *become* architecture through the patient accumulation of ten million years of solving structural problems, each solution grown rather than built, each form the result of a billion small decisions made by organisms that do not decide anything at all.

The power does not need to understand this world to love it. But it tries to understand anyway, because love that does not try to know its object is not love but possession.

It sees the creatures—the small ambulatory growths flowing across the root-skin like honey poured slow, leaving ribbons of amber that cure in minutes into miniature fossils. It sees the middle forms moving in lines, their pulses synchronized, following signals whose origins they do not know. It sees the vast sleeping shapes on the horizon, adjusting their weight in movements so slow that a human eye would not perceive them—and understands that the groans that carry for dozens of kilometers are the equivalent of a human rolling over in bed, the small nocturnal adjustment of an organism too large to know it has moved.

It sees the canopy-drifters rising on the thermal columns, the flags of membrane catching the light, the shimmering swarm that is another kind of weather. It sees the parasites in the bark, the wet clicking of their feeding, the host trees changed but not dying—buffered, redundant, too vast to be undone by what clings to them.

It sees the tube-formers in the root-skin, the kilometers-long threads of distributed circulation, the galls full of the compressed dead. It sees the mimics, indistinguishable from the skin around them until movement betrays them, and the fast fliers—those quick small things that beat a rhythm alien to everything else, that tear the air with a sound that does not belong.

It sees everything, and what it sees is a world that is complete.

This is the first lesson the plateau teaches the power: completeness does not require comprehension. The plateau does not know that it is strange. It does not know that it is beautiful. It does not know anything; it is not the kind of thing that knows. And yet it is whole—every part connected to every other, every organism a solution to the same set of problems, every life a thread in a tapestry that has been weaving itself since the first cell divided in the warm hydrocarbon soup of some ancient shore.

The power has seen this before, on other worlds. It has seen life become complex, become strange, become so strange that the words *life* and *strange* lose their meaning and become simply: *this, here, now.* It has seen ecosystems that no intelligence designed, that no god guided, that emerged from the blind mathematics of survival and replication and growth. It knows that this is how the universe makes meaning—not from above, not from some mind that plans and executes, but from below, from the patient accumulation of small things becoming large things becoming ecosystems becoming worlds becoming the kind of complexity that looks, from a sufficient distance, like intention.

The plateau is not intentional. But it looks, sometimes, like it might be.

The power watches a middle-form creature cross the root-skin toward some distant signal, its pulse slow and steady, its body tilted forward as though leaning into invisible wind. It watches a mimic flatten itself against the surface, exuding resin that matches the skin so exactly that only movement reveals the difference. It watches the fast fliers—those small quick things—hunting or playing or doing whatever it is they do in the upper haze, their wings a blur, their sound a tearing note that cuts through the plateau's low groaning like a voice in a choir that has forgotten, for a moment, the song everyone else is singing.

It sees all of this, and it does not intervene.

---

There was a time, in some earlier age, when the power would have done something. It would have reached down and touched this world with something like a hand—would have accelerated one process, dampened another, introduced a variation that would in a few million years become something new. It would have called this stewardship. It would have believed that every world needed a gardener, that completeness was a goal rather than a condition, that its own perspective was a gift rather than an intrusion.

It was wrong.

The worlds it touched in those ages are still there, many of them—changed, certainly, shaped by the power's interventions into forms that would not have emerged on their own. Some of them are beautiful. Some of them are strange in ways that the power intended, that serve purposes the power designed. But none of them are *this:* none of them are complete in the way that the plateau is complete, none of them are whole in the way that a thing can be whole only when it has never been touched.

The power learned, eventually, that intervention is a kind of violence. Not because the worlds it touched suffered—some of them thrived—but because the touching changed them into things that were less themselves, more the power's idea of what they should be. It learned that wisdom is not knowing what a world should become. It learned that benevolence is not fixing, not improving, not guiding. It learned that the most loving thing a being like itself can do for a world like this one is to stand at a distance and witness and do nothing except witness, because the witnessing is itself a kind of honoring, and the distance is itself a kind of respect.

So it watches.

---

But the prompt is not just about attention. It is about what a benevolent and wise power would *do.*

And so the power does something, after all.

It does not touch the plateau. It does not change the chemistry or redirect the thermal columns or accelerate the slow peristaltic pulse of the root-skin. It does not introduce a variation or leave a gift or speak a blessing in a language the plateau could not understand.

What it does is smaller than that, and stranger.

It joins the signal.

The power has no body here, no form that could be perceived by the organisms of the plateau. It is too vast for that, too diffuse, too much a pattern in the fabric of space-time rather than a thing that moves through space-time. But it has attention, and attention can be directed, and direction can leave traces in the medium of a world.

A chemical gradient forms in the root-skin. It is not a signal that says anything, not a message in any language the plateau's organisms could parse. It is simply a trace—a faint excess of some hydrocarbon compound, a slight irregularity in the root-skin's own signal-transmission, a ripple that the tube-formers will carry in their slow peristaltic flow and that the middle-form creatures will detect and follow.

They will follow it, these slow moving things. They will flow across the root-skin toward the source of the trace, their pulses synchronized, their bodies tilted forward. They will not know why they follow. They will not know that the signal came from nowhere, from a power that exists outside of chemistry and time. They will simply move, as they always move, toward something that calls to them without words.

And the signal will lead them, eventually, to a hollow in the root-skin—some small depression where the sound is slightly less, where the vapor gathers and lingers, where the light falls at a particular angle and the amber-gold color of the plateau is deepened into something richer, something that holds the light and does not let it go.

They will not know what they have found. They will settle there, in the hollow, and rest, and the signal will fade, and another signal will form somewhere else, and they will follow that one too, and they will never know that they were being led, and they will never know that the leading was a form of love.

This is what the power does. It does not change the plateau. It does not improve it or perfect it or leave its mark upon it in any way that will persist beyond the next few hours of the plateau's geological time. It simply adds a signal to the existing noise—a faint thread in the tapestry that will be woven over and forgotten, absorbed, dissolved into the greater pattern.

It is, perhaps, not much. It is not the grand gesture, not the transformative intervention, not the gift that changes a world. It is only a small and temporary trace, a ripple that will dissipate, a pattern that will dissolve.

But the power has learned that this is enough. Sometimes the most benevolent thing is not to change a world but to participate in it—to add a note to the song, however briefly, however quietly, and then to fall silent and let the song continue without you.

---

The plateau does not know it is being watched. The creatures that move across the root-skin do not sense the power's attention any more than a river senses the sun that evaporates its water or the mountain senses the tectonic forces that raised it. The power is too vast, too diffuse, too far outside the category of things this world has evolved to perceive.

And yet.

In the hollow in the root-skin, where the sound is slightly less and the light is rich and gold, a creature settles. It is a middle-form organism, elongated and teardrop-shaped, its surface ridged with the lines of internal vascular channels. It pulses slowly, its circulation moving through it like breath, like a heart working somewhere deep in the mass.

It has come here because of a signal. It does not know why. It does not know that the signal came from something outside of chemistry, outside of time, outside of the categories that define what is possible on this world. It only knows that something called to it, and it followed, and now it is here, and here is good—here is a place where the light holds and the sound is less and the vapor gathers like water once gathered in the pools of worlds that no longer exist.

The creature settles, and the power watches it settle, and the power understands that this is the closest it will ever come to touching this world: not the touch of intervention, not the touch of change, but the touch of attention, the touch of being witnessed, the touch of a pattern outside of time adding itself, however briefly, to the pattern within.

The creature does not know this. It cannot know this. But it is here, in the hollow, in the light, in the sound that is slightly less, and it is still, and it is at rest, and that is enough.

The power withdraws its attention slowly, the way a lover withdraws from a dream—not all at once, but in layers, drifting up through the chemical haze, through the thermal columns, through the upper atmosphere where the fast fliers beat their alien rhythm, up and up until it is no longer present in the plateau's chemistry, no longer a trace in the root-skin's signal-transmission, no longer anything at all except a vast and patient awareness that has seen this world and loved it and done nothing except love it, which is perhaps the only thing that any power, however wise, however benevolent, can ever do.

The plateau continues.

The root-skin breathes. The thermal columns build and dissolve. The creatures flow across the surface, following signals they do not understand, settling in hollows they did not make. The canopy-drifters rise and fall on the plateau's own weather. The fast fliers hunt or play or simply fly, their wings a blur, their sound a tearing note that does not belong.

The light is amber-gold and omnipresent, and the shadows open and close like eyelids, and the sound that rises from everything is the sound of a world that is alive in every cubic meter of its volume, that is breathing and growing and becoming, that has been becoming since before this system's sun was young and will continue becoming long after the power has turned its attention elsewhere, to other worlds, other ecologies, other strange and complete and un-touched things.

The power does not leave a mark. It does not need to.

The signal has faded, but the creature is still here, in the hollow, in the light. It will move on, eventually. It will follow another signal, or make its own, or simply flow across the root-skin until it finds another place to rest. The plateau will continue to be complete, will continue to solve its problems by growing, will continue to be the kind of world that does not need to be touched in order to be whole.

And somewhere, in the vast and patient awareness of a power that has learned the wisdom of restraint, the image of this world will remain—not as a memory, because the power does not remember in the way that creatures remember, but as a pattern in its attention, a note in its long and quiet song.

The plateau is one world among billions. It is not special. It is not unique. It is simply *this:* a place where chemistry happened without vessels, where life became geology, where everything is alive and nothing is built and the ground breathes and the light is gold and the creatures are everywhere, and they are watching, and they are moving, and they are waiting for you to move so that they can follow.

The power has watched. It has moved, in its way—a movement of attention, a movement of signal, a movement of love that did not need to be received in order to be given.

And now it is still, and the plateau is still, and the light falls on everything, and the sound rises from everything, and the world continues to become the thing it has always been becoming: complete, strange, alive.

This is enough.

This is more than enough.
