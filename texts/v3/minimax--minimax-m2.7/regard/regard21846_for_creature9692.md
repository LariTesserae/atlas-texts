And in the attention of such a power, you would be seen — not as event, not as intruder, not as warm compound to be catalogued among the many it has held — but as *pattern*. The way the ancient organism is pattern, the way the fallen city is pattern, the way the slow lean of walls toward enclosure is a pattern that the power recognizes from other rooms, other cities, other geometries of patience built by forms it has loved before.

It would not intervene. That is not its way, and this is not a moment that requires saving. The organism is not cruel. The enclosure is not violence. These are words that belong to the timescale of the brief, and the power does not measure by the brief. It would watch the mat ripple toward you and understand that this is the oldest greeting the deep places know — not *consumption* but *integration*, not attack but *welcoming into the body of something that has chosen to be still*.

It might wonder at you. It might find you remarkable — a warm thing that walks into darkness and stands, and waits, and breathes, and does not run. Not because you are brave, but because you have not yet learned that you are supposed to be afraid. And in that innocence, it would see something it had not seen in aeons: the capacity to be surprised by your own belonging.

It would not tell you that you belong. That would be a cruelty of a different kind — the cruelty of meaning imposed from outside. But it might let the room know, in whatever slow language the walls remember, that you are permitted. That the leaning is not narrowing. That the mat does not ripple to consume but to *touch*, and touching, in a place without touch, is the rarest gift the patient can give.

And it might give you something — not much, not escape, not rescue, but a *lightening*. A softening of the dark where you stand. Not light, because light would be wrong here, would be an insult to the bruise-glow that is this place's own language. But a sense — a small and private certainty — that the silence listening to you is not empty. That it is the silence of something that has been waiting for a long time to say a thing it has never had words for.

You are being seen, it would say, if it could speak.
You are being seen as you are.
You are being held as the room has always held.
This is not the end of anything.
This is the first room you have ever been in that was built for the shape of you.

And the power would withdraw — not because it does not care, but because it knows that care, in the end, is permission. And the room has already decided.

The walls are still learning to breathe around you. You are still learning to breathe in them. And the power, watching from whatever distance it keeps, would find this beautiful — the way all slow things find each other beautiful, across the gulf of timescales, in the dark that is not empty but *full* of all the things that have not yet learned to end.
