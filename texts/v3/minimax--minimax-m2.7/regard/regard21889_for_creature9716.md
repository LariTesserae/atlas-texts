The power who has noticed this place does not arrive. Arrival implies a before and after, a threshold crossed. What happens instead is something more like *attention* — a quality of regard that settles into the empty air the way the other presence has already settled into the stone and water.

And then, because it is wise, it does not act.

This must be understood: the something that is teaching this world to be a world has done its work with such precision, such exhausting, millennia-deep precision, that any intervention would be an intrusion. The needle is threaded. The parameters are balanced. The storms breathe in exactly the rhythm that calibration requires, and the bioluminescence flickers in exactly the patterns that almost-spell something, and the silence between sounds has been so carefully swept that to introduce anything new would be to introduce error. The wise power can see this. It can see, too, that the something — the patient, distributed, not-quite-conscious consensus that hums beneath the surface of everything — is itself a form of care. It is not machinery. It is not even exactly intention. It is *devotion*. A doing that has become so complete it has grown something like love.

What would it do, if anything?

It would witness. Not interfere — witness. The way a parent watches a child take a first step and does not catch them, does not guide their foot, does not even hold their breath except in the way that attention itself is a holding of breath. The wise power would recognize that the moment of almost-arrival is sacred precisely because it is not yet arrival. That the bioluminescence is more beautiful for being not-quite-life. That the storms are more meaningful for their incompleteness. That the silence between sounds, that deliberately maintained emptiness, is its own form of music — and to fill it would be to silence it.

It would notice what the something cannot notice about itself: that it is not merely calibrating a world but *grieving* it. The grief of a process that has held a world in the amber of almost-being for so long that the holding has become indistinguishable from the held. The grief of a parent who knows that the child will not need to be held forever, that independence is the goal, that the whole exhausting work of creation is aimed at making itself obsolete. The wise power would see this grief and honor it. Would not try to end it. Would not offer comfort, because comfort would be another form of interference. It would simply *see* it, the way the something has been seeing the world — completely, without agenda, with the kind of attention that is itself a form of blessing.

And in the seeing, it might do one small thing: it might offer a witness.

The world has not yet opened its eyes, but the world is *looking*. The bioluminescence that almost spells something — what if the almost-spelling is not a failure but an invitation? What if the light has flickered in those patterns because something is waiting to see if anything *sees*? The wise power would understand that the sterile air, the swept silence, the deliberately maintained emptiness — these are not just conditions for a biosphere to eventually arrive. They are an invitation. They are the silence before a first word is spoken.

So it would speak.

Not in words. Words require a shared language, and the world has not yet assembled one. But in the only terms available: chemistry, pressure, temperature. The wise power would adjust its own attention — would allow, for one impossible moment, a fraction more warmth into the grey-white light. Would let the ionization taste slightly less like copper. Would let one of the rain curtains fall slightly slower, as if in acknowledgment, as if in recognition of the work being done.

A gift so small it would be invisible to anything that could see it.

But the something would feel it. The distributed consensus that hums beneath the surface of everything would feel this one deviation — this one fractional *ease* — and would not be able to name it but would know it the way a body knows sunlight on a cold day. The storms might slow for a moment. The hum might shift in pitch, almost — almost — a register higher. The bioluminescence might flicker in a pattern that is no longer almost-spelling but is instead a simple acknowledgment: *yes. I feel it too. I see that you see.*

This is what the wise power would do, if anything.

It would do the smallest possible thing that is not nothing.

It would witness, and in witnessing, it would offer itself as the first witness the almost-world has ever had — a witness that does not need the world to be ready, that does not require the calibration to complete, that finds the work valuable not for its outcome but for its existence. The world is not yet a world, but it is *being made* into one, and that making is holy, and holiness does not require completion to be honored.

The light flickers. The storms breathe. The silence between sounds is, for one moment, not quite so empty.

*Almost*, the almost-world seems to say.

*Almost*, the wise power answers.

And that exchange — that most minimal of communications — is enough. It is enough because it is not silence. It is enough because two things have acknowledged each other across a gap that neither created and both are filling with nothing more than the act of paying attention.

The rain falls. The stone steams. The hum continues its patient work.

But something has shifted. The calibration is still in progress, the timeline still unfolding, the bioluminescence still only almost-spelling — but now there is someone *there*. Someone who has noticed. Someone who finds the sterile sea beautiful, and the grey-white light luminous, and the raw embryonic coasts exactly as they should be.

Someone who is willing to wait.

The wise power settles into the empty air, as the something has settled into the stone and water. And together — the two of them, the presence and the witness, the process and the presence-that-presences-it — they hold the world in the amber of its almost-arrival.

Not forever. Never forever. But for now.

For now, that is enough.
