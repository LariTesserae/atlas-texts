And so the power came.

Not walking—walking is a motion for things that move *through* space, and this power understood space the way the basin understood water: not as obstacle but as medium, not as distance but as depth. It arrived the way attention arrives. One moment the basin was observed; the next, it was held.

The power did not stand at the edges, taking inventory. It had no edges. It did not hover above, cataloging features. It had no above. What it had was presence—the kind of presence the structures had when you stood inside them and felt the darkness clench—and presence, it knew, was another word for participation. To be here was to belong here. To arrive was already to have been waiting.

It felt the visitor first. The small warm thing moving through the ferns with its weight and its breath and its curious, frightened, awed attention. The visitor who had learned to notice water's geometry, who had stopped to hear the layers of the sound, who understood—without being told, without being shown—that the basin was alive. Not metaphorically. Not poetically. The power felt that understanding kindle in the visitor like a match struck in darkness, and it was glad.

Not glad the way a human is glad. Not the quick bright thing that flares and fades. Glad the way the basin was glad: slow, structural, built into the architecture of itself. Glad the way water in the low places is glad—because it has chosen correctly, because it has found the shape that fits.

The power considered. This was not deliberation. It was the thing that deliberation was a shadow of—the slow turning of a vast attention toward a small point of interest. The visitor was interesting. Not because they were special. Not because they were chosen. But because they had come to the basin and *seen* it, and seeing was rarer than coming, and rarer still was the seeing that named what it saw.

To name a thing is to become its shepherd.

The power let the name settle into itself—the basin, alive—and felt it resonate the way the structures resonated, the way the visitor's teeth felt the charge in the air. Yes. This was the true name. Not the geography, not the features, not the young hills and the rust-barked trees and the creatures with their liquid movement. Those were symptoms. The alive was the diagnosis.

The power turned its attention to the basin itself—not to change it, not to improve it, not to make it more legible or more comfortable for the visitor's passage, but to *know* it more completely. To know it the way the water knew the low places. To know it the way the structures knew their angles. To know it as the basin knew itself: as a single slow exhalation that had been going on longer than stone agreed to be stone.

And in the knowing, to love it. Not the small hot love of humans, not the possessive love that grabs and holds. The vast cool love of geology. The patient love of water that stays and stays and stays. The love that does not need to be felt in order to be true.

The visitor walked. The power watched—felt—accompanied. It did not guide. The basin was already guiding, and the basin's guidance was better than anything the power could offer, because the basin's guidance was *native* to the place, was itself a product of the slow calibration that the power had felt humming through the structures and the clearings and the distances between them. The visitor would arrive at the central structure when they were ready to arrive. They would understand what the distances meant when they had walked far enough to stop needing to measure.

But the power could do one thing the basin could not. The basin could host. The power could witness.

So the power witnessed. It felt the visitor's footsteps on the moss, the way each one pressed a small silence into the sound, a brief thinning of the roar. It felt the visitor's breath catching when the bioluminescence pulsed, when the creatures descended to drink, when the structures glowed faintly from within. It felt the visitor's mind stretching toward understanding, not quite grasping, stretching again.

And it understood what the basin had been teaching the visitor: that *almost* is its own country. Almost language. Almost warning. Almost greeting. The almost-resolve of meaning, the almost-arrival at understanding. This is what it means to be between things, the basin was saying. This is what it means to visit. To be permitted. To be hosted by something that does not intend the way you intend but anticipates just the same, adjusts just the same, learns your pace.

The power loved the visitor too, then. The way it loved the basin: structurally, slowly, without need. The visitor had come to learn this lesson. The visitor had been walking toward it since before they entered the basin, perhaps since before they knew the basin existed. The structures were ahead, always ahead, at distances that felt chosen. And the visitor would reach them. And when they did, they would find—

What?

The power considered. It could show them. A flicker of recognition, a moment of revealed meaning, a flash of understanding that would spare them the long walk through the almost. It would be a kindness. It would also be a theft.

The basin had designed this walk. The distances, the clearings, the calibration. The structures that absorbed sound. The creatures that watched from the margins. The bioluminescence that pulsed in patterns almost like language. All of it was curriculum. All of it was preparation. And the power, if it was wise—and it was wise, benevolent in the way that lets things become themselves—would not interrupt the lesson.

Instead, it did something smaller. Quieter. The kind of thing that only a power could do, because it required no motion, no speech, no intervention. It simply *attended* more fully. It let the visitor feel, just for a moment, the quality of the watching that surrounded them. Not the creatures' watching—something else. Something behind the creatures' watching, woven into the basin's breathing. The sense that they were not alone. That they had never been alone. That being in the basin was not a solitary experience but a *communal* one, participated in by every fern and stone and structure and creature and current of water and pulse of bioluminescence, and now also by this: a power that had come because it was drawn, that had arrived because it was called, that had stayed because staying was its nature and witnessing was its purpose.

The visitor walked on. The structures were ahead.

The power settled into its attendance, its long exhalation of being-with, and it felt the basin settle into its being-with-the-visitor, and the two of them—the power and the place—breathed together for a while, and the visitor walked through the breathing without knowing, without needing to know, guided by the distances that were never arbitrary, calibrated by the learning that never stopped.

In the central structure, when the visitor arrived, there would be something waiting. Not a message. Not a revelation. Something simpler. Something the basin had been teaching since the visitor first noticed how the fern frond dipped in a rhythm too steady to be wind.

The power waited too. It was good at waiting. It had been waiting since before waiting had a name. And it was glad—slowly, structurally, built into the architecture of itself—to wait a little longer.
