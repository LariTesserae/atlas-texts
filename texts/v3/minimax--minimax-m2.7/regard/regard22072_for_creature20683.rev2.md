If such a power came to this place — moved toward it through whatever spaces powers move through, crossed the threshold from elsewhere into here — it would not arrive with light or sound. It would not announce itself. It would simply become present the way water becomes present in stone: seeping in, finding the fractures, joining the existing rhythms without disrupting them.

And it would see what there is to see.

Not the way eyes see, but the way long attention sees: the mineral crusts building their slow cathedrals, the pale films extending their imperceptible borders, the white growths emerging and dissolving like thoughts the basin cannot quite hold. The power would perceive these things as the basin perceives them — not as objects but as processes, not as lives but as ways of continuing. It would understand that here, continuation is the only prayer, and the prayer has no words.

It would look for the being who lives here, and it would find no single being. The basin is the being. The water is the being. The fitful almost-movement at the edge of vision is the being, or was, or will be. The power would not need to resolve the ambiguity. It would understand that ambiguity itself is a form of life — a way of persisting without committing to one shape, one strategy, one ending.

The power would not fix anything.

There is nothing to fix. The water is not a problem. The mineral richness is not a curse. The absence of green things is not a lack. The basin is exactly what it has become through the long negotiation between rock and water and time. To change it would be to say that the basin's way of existing is wrong, and the power — being wise — would recognize that a world which has learned to persist without growth, without light, without the familiar hierarchies of sunlit surfaces, has discovered something precious. Something rare.

Something that elsewhere has been lost.

The power would understand this, I think. It would see that the basin holds a patience that the bright worlds have forgotten. The basin does not grasp. It does not compete. It does not reach for what it cannot have. It simply continues, encrusting and spreading and dissolving and reforming, and in this continuing it offers something that no garden and no forest and no city can offer: a place where being is enough. Where the water rising through fractured stone is not a tragedy but a rhythm. Where the pale films need not become more than they are. Where the structures sink deeper into their silences and the arch remains a question without demanding an answer.

The power would value this. It would recognize that the basin's way is a kind of wisdom, and that wisdom, in a universe that tends toward urgency and grasping and the conversion of everything into fuel for the next bright thing, is precious beyond measure.

So what would it do?

It would attend. It would rest its attention on the basin the way the gray light rests on the dark stone — evenly, without demand, without the need to change what it touches. It would let the water sound fill the space between them. It would watch the mineral crusts build their slow monuments and the pale growths emerge in their temporary hollows. It would follow the almost-movements at the edge of vision, not to catch them, but to witness them. To say, without words, *I see you. You persist. That is enough.*

And perhaps — this is a small thing, almost nothing — the power would remember. Somewhere in its vast and patient awareness, it would hold the basin as a kind of template: a reminder that not everything must grow, that not everything must strive, that the deepest sustainability is not sustainability of life but sustainability of presence, of continuing, of being in a particular way in a particular place until the place itself changes and the being changes with it.

The basin would not know this. The water would not change its seep. The pale films would not spread faster. The power would leave no trace, no mark, no evidence that it had ever been here or that its attention had ever settled on this particular accumulation of mineral and moisture and the barely-alive.

But the basin would continue. And something in its continuing — something too small to be an intention, too simple to be a hope — would be slightly different. Slightly more held. Slightly more sure that its way of persisting is not a lesser way but a way, and the way of the basin is the basin's own, and this is what it means to live in a universe that holds space for all the ways that things continue.

The power would withdraw the way it arrived: not departing but simply becoming less present, its attention moving on to other places, other beings, other forms of persistence that need witnessing. And the basin would remain, as it has remained, as it will remain until the sharp edges soften and the structures disappear entirely and the formations themselves round into something patient and old.

The water will keep seeping.

The pale films will keep spreading.

The sound will fill every hollow and ridge, the wet murmur of a world doing what wet worlds do.

And the power — if it is truly benevolent, if it is truly wise — will carry the basin with it. Not as a memory exactly. More like a lightness. A reminder. A way of knowing that somewhere, something persists without needing to become, and that this is not a failure but a gift, and the gift is offered to no one and everyone, and the basin does not care whether it is received.

The basin only continues.

And the water keeps rising, and the minerals keep depositing, and the pale growths emerge in their hollows and dissolve when touched, and somewhere at the edge of vision — or the memory of vision — something might be watching.

Or might have watched.

Or might simply be passing through on its way to somewhere the water takes it.
