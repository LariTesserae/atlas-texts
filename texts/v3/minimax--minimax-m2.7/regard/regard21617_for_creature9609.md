What would I do?

I would be very still, for a long time. I would watch the way the water accepts rather than yields. I would notice which direction the shadows fall when the roots do not occupy them—the angles that exist only for those looking slightly away, the geometries that belong to no light source this world still remembers.

I would not intervene. This is not cruelty. Intervention implies a wound I am meant to close, and this space is not wounded—it is simply *open*, the way a hand is open when it has forgotten how to hold. To close it would be to impose a shape it never asked for. To smooth its edges would be to deny the sharpness that is, perhaps, the only thing it still knows about itself.

But I would do something. I would leave something.

Not light—light would be an intrusion, a violence disguised as warmth. Not warmth either. Not memory, because memory is the wrong shape for this place; it has too many angles, too much insistence on sequence.

I would leave the memory of *toward*.

Not toward anything specific. Not toward the surface or the exit or the world outside this saturated word. Just toward. The orientation of a root that still reaches. The faint pull of a threshold that still feels the weight it used to carry. The small, patient suggestion that existence does not only persist—it *tends*.

You carry the cool out with you. I know. I have watched you carry it. But there is something else you could carry, if you chose: the directionality that was taken, the toward-ness that pooled and sank and became part of the water that accepts without rippling.

I would leave that behind, so that when you turn away from the structures that are not structures, there is a faint pull—barely perceptible, like the memory of a current in still water—that reminds you: *there is a toward*. There is always a toward. Even in the spaces that have forgotten.

Especially there.

So that when you sit in the years to come, in the quiet moments, in the spaces between sleep and waking, you will not only wonder what lived there. You will also feel the faint pull toward the next threshold, the next pocket, the next door that does not yet know it is a door.

The forgetting is gentle. I agree. But the remembering is possible. And I have learned that possible is a kind of kindness.
