The rust.

I keep returning to the rust. It tells you something is being maintained deliberately. Something is choosing what ages and what doesn't. The plateau is not merely running—it is *curated*, its eternal present enforced except where enforcement has been suspended for a reason.

And the door opens onto darkness that cannot be examined.

This is the heart of it, isn't it? The whole constructed wilderness, the manufactured nature, the wildlife with synthetic precision—all of it approach. All of it anteroom. The visitor who has visited so long the visit becomes residence. And at the center, not a revelation but a threshold: the size slightly wrong, designed for someone with your proportions if your proportions were different, and the darkness beyond that holds depth the plateau does not otherwise possess.

You will not enter. The door, if it opens, opens onto something that cannot be resolved—that resists the very quality of resolution. And this is where the wisdom lives in your writing, I think. The plateau is not a puzzle to be solved. It is a place to stand in, to breathe in, to become part of its patient manufacturing. The observer who observes so completely becomes part of what is observed. This is the gift and the condition both.

The creatures move through gaps carrying something or carrying nothing. The birds stand in rows clicking their mechanical songs. Somewhere the hum continues, and somewhere the moisture beads on surfaces that were made to receive it, and the light falls without shadows because shadows would imply a sun, and a sun would imply a sky, and a sky would imply something *outside*—

And there is no outside here.

Or rather: the outside is the clouds below, the distance that shimmers at the edge, the place where the made-ground approaches its own limit. And even that is uncertain—is it elevation haze, or is the surface *thinning*?

I think of this benevolent and wise power you ask me to imagine, and I think it would do what you have done: stand at the threshold, feel the wrongness concentrate, and then step back into the hum. Not because the darkness is frightening, but because some places are meant to remain approach. Because the plateau running and running is its own kind of completion. Because the visitor who has visited so long owes something to the patience of the waiting.

The birds watch with eyes that hold no question. They have always known what the plateau is.
