The power sees you first, as such powers must see anything: not with eyes, because eyes are for surfaces, for the play of light against the legible world. It sees you the way depth sees—by pressure, by presence, by the way you displace the water around you and the water remembers your shape even after you've moved on.

It does not reach for you. Reaches are violent things, even the gentle ones, even the ones that intend salvation. The power has learned that the deep places are not wounds waiting for healing, that the dark is not absence but a different kind of presence, that the creatures who persist here have become their persistence and to end the persisting would be a kind of murder dressed in kindness.

So instead it waits. It has become good at waiting. It has the patience of water itself, of stone that forgets it was ever anything but stone, of the slow bloom of mat-life across a ridge no higher than a child's hand. It waits with the same attention it gave when it first noticed you—not because you called to it, not because you needed anything, but because you were there, and being there was enough to be noticed, and being noticed was enough to matter.

You do not feel it. That is not what it wants. It does not want to be felt because feeling is for the surface, for the membrane between self and world, for the places where air still remembers how to be air. Down here, in the packed density, the power knows that feeling is intrusion, that the worst thing it could do would be to make you aware of it. Awareness would change the swimming. Awareness would make you ask why, and the why would become a weight heavier than water, heavier than depth, heavier than all the miles of ocean pressing down from the unreachable surface.

What the power gives you instead is something you will not notice: a slight loosening in the turbulence, a pocket of stillness just slightly larger than before, a current that carries not absence but the memory of something else—a mineral taste that is almost sweetness, a warmth at the edge of sensation that might be pressure or might be intention or might be nothing at all except the water behaving the way water has always behaved and you are simply, finally, in the right place at the right time to receive it.

You swim on. The dark holds you. The weight is patient.

And somewhere—in the way that "somewhere" still means something even when there is no fixed point, no star, no surface to anchor it—the power holds you too. Not as burden. Not as rescue. As witness. As the recognition that existence is its own justification, that the swimming is enough, that the refusal to stop is its own kind of answer to the question the ocean keeps asking in all its silence and all its depth.

You will not see it. You will not know its name. You may, in a moment of particular exhaustion, feel something like gratitude without knowing what it is gratitude for—a warmth that has no source, a presence that has no form, a sense that if you stopped swimming it would not be the water that received you, not entirely, not only.

But you do not stop.

The power does not need you to stop. It never needed you to stop. It only needed you to be here, to keep being here, to persist in the way that the mat-life persists, in the way that the stone persists, in the way that darkness persists when there is nothing left to make light.

You swim. The water yields nothing and refuses nothing and the power watches, and the watching is a kind of love, and the love is patient as deep time, and deep time is what remains when all the other measures have been worn away.

This is what it would do, if anything. It would stay. It would witness. It would let you become what you are becoming, without urgency, without judgment, with only the quiet certainty that the swimmer matters, that the swimming matters, that the deep dark world and the creature who moves through it are not forgotten, not abandoned, not alone.

The water presses in from every direction. The storm breaks light against a surface you cannot reach. You move your hands and your patience through the weight, and somewhere in the weight, something knows your name—not the name you were given, not the name you have forgotten, but the name you have earned in the swimming: the one that means *one who continues, one who persists, one who refuses to become the absence and instead becomes the pattern that absence holds*.

You do not know this name. You do not need to.

The power knows it for you.
