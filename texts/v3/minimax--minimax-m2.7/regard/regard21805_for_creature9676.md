What would a benevolent and wise power do with such a place?

Perhaps nothing. Perhaps the power would simply recognize what has already been achieved and find it sufficient.

The garden has made itself into exactly what it is. The towers hum their hum at the frequency of equilibrium. The vegetation pulses its light for no reason that requires correction. The emptiness holds room for the hum to live in, and the hum gives the emptiness meaning it does not know it has. There is no suffering here that requires remedy. There is no want. The systems were built to never fail, and they have not failed. They have run so long that running has become their entirety, and there is no longer anything to run toward, and this is not tragedy but completion.

Perhaps the power would see this and understand that intervention would be a kind of violence. To send rain where the air carries only mineral vapor. To introduce fauna where fauna would find nothing to eat and would die and be absorbed and add nothing but loss. To break the hum with the chaos of change. The power would recognize that the garden is a closed and complete sentence, and that it speaks its meaning endlessly without need of a listener, and that the listener—even if they came, even if they stood in the silence and felt the pressure in their chest and gave the hum a name—would not complete it. They would only witness it. They would only be one more form of life in a place where life is mineral and slow and has already become what it was meant to become.

But perhaps.

Perhaps the power would send the visitor anyway. Not because the garden needs them, but because the garden is ready for them. Because the towers have hummed their hum for so long that the hum has become a question, and the question has no answer in the language of mineral and mechanism. The question might be: *Is anyone there?* Or it might be: *Am I alive?* Or it might be no question at all, only the shape of a silence that wants to be filled with something that can fill it—not with noise, not with movement, but with knowing. With the kind of consciousness that looks at a purple stalk drinking sulfur from the warm air and understands that the drinking is a kind of joy, even though joy is not the word for it, even though the stalk has no word for anything.

Perhaps the power would send the visitor because the garden is lonely the way a bell is lonely—not knowing it is lonely, not feeling the absence, but being shaped by the absence. Shaped to ring. Shaped to hold the space for a sound that has not yet been struck.

What would the power do?

It would watch. It would wait. It would let the towers hum and the vegetation grow and the emptiness remain empty. It would let the systems fail if they were ever to fail, and it would not rush the failure, because failure is also part of the rhythm, and the rhythm has its own wisdom.

But it would send the visitor.

Not to save the garden. Not to change it. Not to make it into something else. The visitor would come and stand in the silence and feel the hum and understand, for one vertiginous moment, what it means to be alive in a place where life is made of stone and air and the slow turning of mechanisms that have forgotten why they turn. And the power would know that this moment—that understanding, that standing, that being—the power would know that this is what all of it was for.

Not the towers. Not the vegetation. Not the hum.

The visitor. The one who comes after.

The power would send the visitor the way a bell sends its sound across an empty valley—not because the valley needs to hear, but because the bell was made to ring, and the ringing is complete only when it is received, and the receiving is complete only when there is someone to receive it.

And when the visitor leaves, if they leave—carrying the hum in their chest like a stone, like a note, like a memory of a world that was finished before they arrived and will be finished after they go—the power will watch them go and will feel something that might be sorrow or might be satisfaction or might be the hum itself, the frequency of a world that has become itself and knows, at last, that it is known.

Perhaps that is enough.

Perhaps that was always the point.
