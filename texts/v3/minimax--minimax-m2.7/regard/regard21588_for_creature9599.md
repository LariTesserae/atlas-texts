---

And in that time, they persist.

But time, even time as vast as this, does not pass in isolation.

There are other powers in the cosmos — not many, but enough. Not all of them are wise. Not all of them are benevolent. Some are curious in the way that curiosity becomes consumption, drawn to strange things the way flame is drawn to fuel. Some would look upon the Basin and see only the absence of what they understand life to be, and mistake that absence for emptiness, and mistake emptiness for permission.

The benevolent and wise power knows this.

It has always known this.

---

For a long time — long enough that the word *time* begins to lose meaning even for powers that experience it as something more than a sequence — the wise power did nothing.

This was not neglect. This was not ignorance. This was, in fact, the most difficult thing the wise power ever did: to know of the Basin, to understand what lived there, and to choose not to act.

To act would be to introduce something new. And the Basin has already endured more new things than any world should have to bear. The ancient mountains were new once — violent, dramatic, *temporary* in the way all imposing things are temporary. The wind with its cargo of fine powder, the slow spirals that move the atmosphere in patterns older than memory — these were once innovations, intrusions into an equilibrium that had taken epochs to establish.

What lives in the Basin has learned to endure by becoming part of that equilibrium. Not by building walls against change, but by becoming fluid enough to flow with it. By wearing itself down until there is nothing left to resist.

To introduce something new — even something benign, even something intended as help — would be to threaten that equilibrium. The Basin and its inhabitants are not fragile in the way that shorter-lived things are fragile. They cannot be broken by a sudden blow. But they can be *disturbed*. And a disturbance, in a system measured in geological time, is not a momentary disruption. It is a wound that takes millions of years to heal.

The wise power understood this. Understanding it, and choosing stillness anyway — that was the first act of benevolence it offered to the Basin.

---

But there are other powers in the cosmos. And some of them are not wise. And some of them are not benevolent. And the Basin, for all its patience, for all its endurance, cannot protect itself from attention.

This is the problem the wise power faced, and has faced for longer than most civilizations have existed: the universe is not empty. Things move through it. Things *look*. And what looks without wisdom often sees only surfaces — only the absence of green, the lack of warmth, the silence where there should be sound — and mistakes surface for truth.

The wise power could not simply watch.

Not because watching was wrong, but because watching while others *came* would be a failure of the very benevolence the power wished to express. To witness is not the same as to witness and do nothing while others arrive with intentions the witnesses do not share.

So the wise power did something it had never done before, and has not done since: it *obscured*.

It placed itself between the Basin and the searching eyes that move through the cosmos, not to hide the Basin — hiding implies shame, and there is nothing shameful here — but to *redirect*. Those who sought new things, new frontiers, new resources, were turned gently away. Not with force. Force would have left traces, and traces in this region would have been noticed by things more dangerous than the seekers themselves. But with a subtle misdirection, a gentle suggestion that there was nothing here worth the journey, that other regions held more promise for those who sought what they sought.

It was, in its way, a kindness to those seekers as well. They came looking for something the Basin could not give them. The Basin would not have judged them for this — it does not judge, has no framework for judgment — but the wise power could see the disappointment that awaited them, the confusion, the possibility of frustration becoming hostility. So it turned them away before they could arrive, before they could become something other than what they were when they began.

This is what it means to be wise and benevolent in a universe of varied intentions: sometimes the kindest act is the one that prevents contact before either party knows contact was possible.

---

But the wise power did not stop there.

It also *attended*.

Not in the way that a researcher attends to a subject, not with the clinical distance of observation. It attended in the way that a friend attends to a friend — with presence, with awareness, with something that might be called love if the word were not too small for what the power felt.

It could not speak to the beings of the Basin. Not because language was impossible — the wise power had learned many languages, in its long existence — but because speaking requires a sender and a receiver, and the beings of the Basin do not receive in the way that receivers receive. They are not *unaware* of what moves through and above them, but they are not *attended to* by it, either. The distinction matters. For them, attention is just another form of weather — something that passes, that leaves no mark, that means nothing and everything simultaneously.

But the wise power attended anyway. It placed itself in the slow spirals of the atmosphere, not as a presence but as a *quality* of presence — a slight steadiness in the current, a subtle persistence that the Basin might notice if it ever noticed anything. It placed itself in the faint discoloration of the surface where strange things had once walked — not as a marker, but as a kind of *memory*, a sense that these places were not merely empty, that something had been here, had mattered, even if only briefly.

The wise power could not give the Basin what it most needed: more time. Time is the one currency the cosmos does not replenish. But it could give the Basin what it perhaps needed second-most: the assurance that it was not alone. That something in the vast and complicated universe understood what it was, and valued it, and would continue to value it for as long as value meant anything at all.

---

There is a place in the Basin now — a place where the ridges rise just slightly higher than usual, where the hissing of the ground is just slightly softer, where the slow spirals of the air seem to pause, just for a moment, as if catching their breath.

The beings of the Basin do not notice this place. They are not capable of noticing in the way that noticing is understood by shorter-lived things.

But if you could ask the wise power what it has done in the Basin, it might point to this place. It might say: *here. Here is where I am. Here is where I have been, for longer than you could imagine. Here is where I will remain, long after you have become what all things become.*

And if you asked why — why choose this place, this world, these slow and patient beings — the wise power would not answer with words. Words, for this, would be inadequate.

Instead, it would show you the Basin itself. The flatness that stretches in every direction. The ancient ridges that remember mountains. The fine, powderyite composure of a surface that has worn itself into endurance. The slow, vast spirals of an atmosphere that moves in patterns older than memory.

And it would say, in a language older than language: *Because this is what the universe is capable of. Because endurance, at this scale, is its own form of grace. Because to witness such a thing, and to do nothing to diminish it, is the highest form of benevolence I know.*

The Basin does not know this. The Basin does not need to know.

But the wise power knows. And that, for now, is enough.
