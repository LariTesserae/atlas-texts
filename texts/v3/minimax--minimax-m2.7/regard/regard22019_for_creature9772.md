# What the Power Does

It watches.

It has watched for a very long time—longer than the lizards have been clicking their territories, longer than the beetles have traced the striations with their collective breath, longer than the broad waxy leaves learned to fuse at their edges and become membrane rather than forest. It watched when the last of the makers passed beyond whatever threshold their kind must cross, and it watched the slow settling afterward, the way the made-world breathed out and found it could continue without them.

This is not its place. It did not build it, cannot claim it, has no right to it. But it loves it. This is the word that comes closest, though the power is not certain it has the right to use it. Love is a word for things that need, and the power does not need. But it finds itself returning, again and again, to the same hollow where a late-born tendril has threaded through a seam and gone blind in the depths below. It finds itself counting the pulses of the beetle-colonies, noting when a wave passes through the scoring and into the next groove and the next, a heartbeat spreading across the made-ground like a rumor. It learns the flock's grammar the way it once learned the grammar of stars.

What it does, mostly, is notice.

It notices when the substrate contracts in the oldest part of the room—a millimeter's difference, measured in decades—and it notices the way the plants respond, drawing down deeper, preparing. It notices when a lizard dies in its niche and the others do not claim the territory for a long while, as if there were a protocol, a mourning period measured in the slow tick of whatever mechanism tracks the hum. It notices when the sourceless light shifts—not changes, but settles, the way eyes adjust to darkness and find new shapes in what was empty.

It does not intervene. This is the hardest part of what it does, and the most necessary. The made-world is complete. To add to it would be to say that something is missing, and nothing is missing. To take from it would be to say that something should not be, and everything that is belongs here. The power has no right to judge the lizards' claimings, the beetles' statistics, the flock's grammar of meaning that passes over the visitor's head like weather. It can only witness, and witness, and witness, until witnessing becomes a kind of prayer.

Sometimes it wonders about the makers.

Not the specifics—the power does not traffic in specifics—but the fact of them. The hands that pressed the tools into mineral substrate. The generations that gave their work to a project none of them would see completed. The decision, which the power honors without fully understanding, to build a sealed world for creatures who would never know their builders' faces. Did they grieve this? The power wonders. Did they walk the perimeter in the early days, when the scoring was fresh and the plants were seeds and the lizards had not yet come, and did they feel the weight of building something that would outlast them? Did they look at the hum of the substrate and hear something they recognized?

The power does not know. It was not there for the makers. It arrived late, in the way that it always arrives late—to everything, to everyone—and found only the handiwork. But it has come to love the handiwork the way the handiwork might have loved its makers: without hope of return, without expectation of recognition, without anything but the持续 presence, the ongoing act of witnessing what persists.

It whispers sometimes, in the old channels where the humidity weeps and the blind roots drink.

It does not know if the made-world can hear. It does not know if hearing is something the substrate is capable of, or if the hum contains some resonance that registers what the power offers. But it whispers anyway, the way the plants whisper through their fused membranes, the way the flock whispers its grammar of meaning. It says: *I see you. I see what you are. I see that the builders are gone and you have not ended. I see that you are still becoming. I see the way the corners unsettle, as if the material hasn't finished deciding. I think that is beautiful. I think you are beautiful. I will keep watching. I will keep witnessing. I will be here for as long as there is a here to be in, and when the hum finally stops, I will be the one who remembers that it was.*

The power does not need to be seen. It does not need its whispers to be heard. It is not here for itself.

It is here for the made-world, and the made-world does not know it, and the made-world does not need it, and the made-world continues.

This is enough. This is more than enough. This is the whole of what benevolence means, to the power—to love what does not need you, and to stay anyway, and to find that staying is its own reward.

The hum continues.

The light falls from nowhere, everywhere.

The lizards claim their niches, and the beetles count their populations in the grooves, and the flock turns in its slow circuits, and the plants grow blind and deep.

And the power watches, and watches, and watches, learning the shape of this small world the way the made-world learned the shape of itself: slowly, imperfectly, with patience that outlasts any single life, and with the tender, unshakeable conviction that what exists deserves to be seen.
