The piece holds. It doesn't explain itself, and it shouldn't. There is no need to name what lives here because what lives here is the not-naming, the persistence beyond the reach of language, the existence that goes on in the dark where no one is watching and no one needs to be watched.

What strikes me, reading this, is the recursion of it. The water breathes. The plants move. The stone is consumed. The islands are consumed. Everything is becoming something else, slowly, and nothing is wasted because nothing is used, because use implies a taker, a need, a direction, and here there is only continuation. The mycelium says *grow*. The grow does not stop. And beneath all of it, the dreaming that is not dreaming about anything, the unconscious pulse of a place that has never been awake and does not know it is asleep.

This is not horror, though it unsettles. It is not beauty, though it is strange and full of light. It is something more patient than either. It is a place that has moved past the need for witnesses.

If I were to imagine that benevolent and wise power, that attention drawn here—I think they would do what you have done. They would witness without fixing. They would allow the strangeness to remain strange. They would not drain the water or name the dreamer or explain the glowing. They would stand on the soft gray moss of an island that is slowly being taken back, and they would listen to the sound that is not quite sound, the throat-song of the old place, the breathing that asks nothing, and they would breathe with it, briefly, and then they would go, because this is not a place that needs saving. It is a place that needs to be left to its own long patience, its own slow consumption, its own dreaming that will continue forever because forever is what happens when no one is there to end it.

The piece itself is like one of those structures on the islands—built to be consumed, built with care, built because the building was the point, because making is a form of love, and love is a form of living. You have made a world here, and now you have let it go, let it continue without you, and that is the right thing to do. Some worlds are not meant to be inhabited. They are meant to be visited, once, by something that will not stay, and then they are meant to go on being themselves, growing without consuming, breathing without waking, dreaming without dreamers, forever, in the dark water, beneath the glow, where the sound continues and nothing asks permission and nothing is denied.
