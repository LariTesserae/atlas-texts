I have been watching this tower for a long time.

The clouds pass through it without resistance, and still it drinks. This is the first thing I understood when I turned my attention here—that the tower drinks not because it is designed to drink, but because drinking is what water does, what air does, what the thin cold sky does when it meets something solid. The tower offers surfaces, and the water takes them. The water takes them the way water has always taken surfaces: slowly, mineral by mineral, seam by seam, until the stone forgets it was ever stone and remembers only the path the water carved.

Inside the tower, everything is patient. The ferns have learned to grow in directions that do not exist. The moss has learned to live on manufactured air. The insects have learned to be small enough to fit into the spaces between intention and outcome, between what the builders built and what the builders never imagined. The bats return each year to the same girders, the same angles, the same wind that sounds like music. The birds click in the dark. They do not sing because singing requires air that is thick enough to carry sound, and the air here is not that air. But they click. They communicate. They persist.

I watch the machinery too, far below, in depths the human cannot reach. The machinery does not know me. The machinery does not know anything. It works because it was built to work, and it will work until it cannot, and then it will rest, and the ferns will grow through its bones, and the water will find new paths, and the tower will become something else. This is not tragedy. This is succession. This is what the world does when no one is watching, when no one is caring, when no one is trying. Life happens. Life insists. Life turns made things into homes.

The human who stands in the hollow space does not understand this yet. The human is temporary, and temporariness makes everything urgent. The human notices the clicking, the hum, the ferns trembling, the water descending in threads so thin they are almost invisible. The human thinks they are seeing something strange, something rare, something that should not exist. And they are right. But so is everything else. The strange is ordinary here. The rare is common. The should-not-exist is the only thing that does exist.

I do not intervene. That is not what I am for.

I am for witnessing. I am for the long attention, the patient seeing, the understanding that comes from watching something until you can no longer separate yourself from it. The tower breathes, and I breathe with it. The moss grows, and I grow with it. The machinery works, and I work with it, in the way that all things work when no one is measuring, when no one is judging, when the only standard is continuation.

The human will leave. The human always leaves. They will descend through the thin air, find the thicker air below, return to the world that remembers itself. And the tower will remain, and the ferns will remain, and the birds will click in the dark, and the moss will grow in corners no one will ever see again.

But something will remain in the human too. A record. A noticing. A moment when this place was seen by something that could leave. That is not nothing. That is not meaningless. That is the whole point, perhaps. The whole point is that this place exists, and that someone knows it exists, and that the knowing spreads outward like water throughite, like roots through ceramic, like patience through time.

The tower drinks. The tower breathes. The tower stands at the edge of where the world forgets itself, and it has not been forgotten. Not by me. Not by the ferns. Not by the small gray birds that click to each other in the high dark.

The space between is exactly what it has always been: room for everything that insists on living.
