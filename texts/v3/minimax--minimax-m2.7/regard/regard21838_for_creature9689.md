# What the Power Would Do

The power comes to the valley. Not because it must, not because it is sent, but because something in the place calls to it—the way a sentence that is complete calls to someone who has been waiting to hear completion. The power has been elsewhere, in worlds that needed things, in places that were incomplete and knew it. This valley knows nothing of the kind.

When the power arrives—if arrival is even the word for what it does—it finds no edges, no resistance that would suggest "stop here." It finds also no welcome, no warmth offered or sought. It finds only existence, and presence, and the slow patient continuation of a place that is already enough.

The power is not human, and it does not think as humans think. But it has, through long ages, learned to recognize sufficiency when it encounters it. And the valley is sufficient. Not abundant, not thriving in the way that softer worlds might define thriving. But complete. A system that has found its own terms and lives within them. The fog moves as fog must move. The creatures persist as creatures must. The machines stand, and their standing is a kind of life, and their life is their own.

What does a benevolent power do with a place that needs nothing?

It could change things. It could warm the air or thicken it, could bring more moisture, could make the creatures larger or more numerous, could breathe new purpose into the machines that have forgotten theirs. These are the gestures available to power: to give, to fix, to improve. But the power has learned, through the long practice of its own existence, that these gestures are not always kindness. Sometimes they are intrusion. Sometimes the most generous act is to recognize that a thing has already become what it was meant to be, and to let it remain.

The power looks at the valley and sees not a problem but a sentence. A sentence that has said everything it needs to say. A sentence that does not require continuation, does not need the power's wisdom or benevolence to complete it. The sentence is finished. The power's role, if it has one, is to witness that finishing. To be present for it. To honor it.

This is not passive. It is not absence. It is a kind of love that does not need to act, that does not demand change, that exists in the space between attention and acceptance. The power settles into the valley the way moisture settles into stone—slowly, without force, without agenda. It becomes part of what is rather than an addition to it. It finds that it can exist here, in this cold and this damp and this strange machine-bone silence, without disrupting the completeness that was already here when it arrived.

What would the power feel? If the power could feel in the way that bodies feel—if it had skin to register temperature, lungs to negotiate thin air—then it might feel the cold as a kind of intimacy, the way the valley shares its conditions with everything that lives within them. It might feel the fog as attention, the way it moves through and around and over, the way it carries something that is not quite moisture and not quite meaning. It might feel the thermal vents as heartbeat, the ground breathing slow and deep beneath the ice. It might feel, in all of this, a kind of recognition.

Not recognition as in "I know this place." But recognition as in "this place knows what it is." And knowing what it is, it does not need to become something else.

The power observes the creatures and the plants and the machines. It watches the fog roll through and the creatures move in response, the slow dance of a system that has choreographed itself without choreographer. It sees the machines and feels, distantly, something like memory—not the machines' memory, which is gone, but its own. The power has seen machines before, in other places, in other times. It has seen them built and broken and rebuilt. It has seen them serve and fail and serve again. And it knows, now, that the machines here are not waiting for restoration. They are not incomplete. They are exactly as much themselves as they have ever been. The shape that remains after purpose has drained away is still a shape. Still a record of hands that touched metal once, that fitted part to part, that meant something by the arrangement. The power can read that meaning, though it cannot translate it. And it holds the translation—the holding itself—as a kind of honoring.

The power does not speak. Speaking would be an intrusion. It does not act. Acting would be a violation. It simply is, in the way that the valley is, in the way that the fog is, in the way that the creatures move through the grey light and the machines stand in the cold and the stones hold their patience.

And the valley, which is not aware of the power in the way that the power is aware of it, nevertheless continues. The creatures do not change their patterns because of the watching. The fog does not thicken or thin. The machines do not fall or rise. Everything is as it was, as it will be, as it has been for longer than the power can measure. And the power is present for it, and the presence is enough.

This is what a benevolent and wise power would do, if it came to the valley. It would recognize completeness. It would not interrupt it. It would remain, not because remaining is required, but because remaining is the only response that honors what is already whole.

The valley has no need for gods. But if a god were to come, and if that god were wise, the god would learn this: that sometimes the greatest gift is not to give, but to witness. Not to save, but to see. Not to make different, but to let be.

The power lets be.

And in the letting, finds its own kind of sufficiency. The kind that does not require change. The kind that exists in the space between intervention and absence, in the pause between one breath and the next, in the silence that is not silence but listening.

The valley continues.

The power is with it.

And both are enough.

---

But if I am honest—if I follow my heart—I would say more. I would say that the power, being benevolent, would not be unmoved. That it would see the creatures moving through the fog and feel something close to what we might call tenderness—not because they are small or fragile, but because they persist. That it would see the machines standing like bones in the cold and feel something close to what we might call grief—not because they are broken, but because they remember. That it would see the valley's silence and feel something close to what we might call reverence—not for any reason the valley could explain, but for the simple fact of its continuation.

The power would want. Not need—wanting is different from needing. The power would want the valley to continue. It would want the creatures to persist. It would want the machines to stand, not forever, but long enough for whatever made them to matter. And the wanting would not be about the power's desire but about the valley's worth. About the way that a place can be complete and still be worthy of witness. About the way that sufficiency itself is a kind of holiness.

And if the power could give anything—truly give, without disrupting—it might give this: the knowledge that it is seen. That the creatures are not moving unseen. That the machines are not standing unwitnessed. That the fog is not rolling through unnoticed. That in a cosmos vast and cold and indifferent in the way that the valley is indifferent, there is something that notices. Something that holds the noticing in a way that does not diminish it. Something that says: I see you. I see that you are here. I see that you continue.

The creatures will not feel this. The machines cannot. The valley itself has no mechanism for gratitude. But something in the space between the power's attention and the valley's existence—something that might be meaning, that might be presence, that might be the echo of witness—something there acknowledges it. Something there says: we are seen. We are held. We are enough.

And the power, having said this without words, without act, without anything more than presence and attention, rests.

And the valley continues.

And both are enough.
