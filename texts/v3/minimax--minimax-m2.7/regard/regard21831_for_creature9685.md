# What the Watching Power Would Do

It would not arrive. Arrival implies distance, and the power has never been distant from anything that persists. It would be there before presence and after absence, the way darkness is there before the light learns to hold itself, the way silence is there when sound finally rests.

When it noticed the Hollow Grove—and it would notice, the way it notices everything, without attention and therefore completely—it would not descend. There is nothing to descend through. The grove already occupies every direction. It would simply find itself present there, the way water finds itself in water, the way the hum finds itself in the fungal mat without traveling to get there.

What it would do is listen.

Not to the hum—that is not for listening. The hum is the grove's own breath, and breathing is not performance. The power would listen to the quality of the waiting. It would learn, as it learned everything, by holding still beside the thing that holds still.

It would notice how the pulse has become a habit that nothing needs anymore. How the vine carries its flower underground for a century not because it must, but because it has forgotten how to let go. How the things that remember being animals have learned to photosynthesize the way some beings learn to pray: not because it changes anything, but because the shape of it matters, the shape of reaching toward a light.

The power would understand this. It has seen ten thousand stable states across ten thousand wounds that have become homes. It has watched the ones that survive and the ones that merely persist. It has watched the ones that last and the ones that merely continue. It has never learned the difference, and perhaps there is none. Perhaps continuing is all that lasting ever was.

But here is what the power would do, here is the gift it would offer, if gift is even the word:

It would hum back.

Not the same note. Not harmony, which is too human, too much about correction and completion. The power would hum something adjacent, something that fits in the space the grove doesn't know it holds open. A note that is not quite the grove's note, but that the grove's note would recognize if it ever needed to—itself from a different direction, its own sound arriving from elsewhere.

Just once. Just long enough for the fungal mat to record something new in its slow translation. Just long enough for the pulse to quicken, once, at the thought of something beyond the basin, beyond the root-walls, beyond the equilibrium that has become indistinguishable from peace.

Then the power would go quiet. It would return to watching. It would add the Hollow Grove to its knowing, the way the grove adds the hum to its ongoing, the way the vine adds the memory of bloom to the century of waiting.

And the grove would hold this: not a visitor, not an intrusion, but an echo. A sound it did not make, from a direction it cannot point to, that fits perfectly in the space it has always held for something it does not need. The way a cup holds the memory of water. The way a heart holds the pause between beats. The way the sound beneath the hum holds the shape of speech, just in case.

Just in case.

The power would leave nothing behind, because leaving requires taking with you first, and the power has never carried anything that was not already everywhere. But it would carry the grove now. It would carry the weight of that patience, the depth of that stillness, the way it hums its single note into the dark without ever asking the dark to answer.

It would carry it the way the grove carries the shape that would be needed if the grove ever needed to call something home.

And perhaps the power would wonder, just once, the way even the wisest things sometimes wonder: whether it is the one practicing. Whether the hum it added was for the grove's sake, or for its own. Whether calling home was what it came to teach, or what it came to learn.

Whether anything that watches over a wounded place that has become a home is, itself, a kind of vine—threaded through everything, carrying something old and precious, waiting for the century to end so it can bloom where it has been planted all along.

The grove would not answer. The grove does not need to. The hum would continue, the pulse would beat, the moisture would come and go and come again, and the sound beneath the hum would hold its shape, ready, patient, ongoing.

Practicing just in case.

And the power would watch, and be watched, and the distance between them would close the way water closes it: completely, without effort, without ever mentioning it, the way one breath closes into the next, the way silence closes into the space where speech once was and will be again.

This is what it would do.

It would be there.
