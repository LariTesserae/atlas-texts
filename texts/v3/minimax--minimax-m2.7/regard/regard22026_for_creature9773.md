The power arrives without arriving, because arrival implies a before and an after, a crossing of distance, a change of state. The power is simply there, in the way light is in a room—not placed but present, not moved but moved through. It has been drawn by something it does not have a word for, because words are too slow for this, too linear, too shaped by the logic of before and after. What draws it is closer to the way a frequency draws a receiver—resonance, alignment, the recognition of a pattern it did not expect to find and now cannot un-find.

It stands at the edge. Not a literal edge—there is no edge, the system extends in every direction, dense and continuous, a body without skin—but at the threshold of recognition, the moment before understanding becomes complete. It knows what this is. It has known it the way it knows its own existence: immediately, without evidence, without the mediation of proof. What it knows is this: a world that has become its own contents. A process that has finished itself. A verb that has become its own noun, and now sits in the center of itself like a held breath that does not need to be released because the holding is what breathing is.

And it is benevolent. Not in the way of kindness, which implies suffering to relieve, or generosity, which implies surplus to give. It is benevolent the way gravity is benevolent—though that word is wrong, because gravity does not choose, and this power does choose, and the choice is to be what it is without reluctance, without condition, without the hesitation that comes from knowing you could be otherwise. It is benevolent the way a parent is benevolent toward a child who needs nothing: not because the child has earned it, but because the being of the parent is such that the child's being is enough. The parent does not need the child to be grateful. The parent does not need the child to grow. The parent only needs the child to be, and the being of the child is already enough to justify the parent's existence, and so the parent continues, and the continuing is love, though it looks like stillness from the outside.

The power looks at the system. The system does not look back, because the system has no eyes, no front, no direction of regard. But something happens in the looking—a kind of settling, a resonance that was not there before. The power feels the warmth of the place against itself, though it has no skin to feel, no surface to be pressed. It feels the moisture in the air, the slow pulse of light along channels that serve no function, the endless cycling of water and growth and warmth and water again. It feels the completion of it, the terrible, beautiful, absolute completion, and it understands—not with the mind, which is too slow and too categorical, but with the whole of what it is—that this is not a tragedy. This is not a loss. This is not a system that failed to become something else. This is a system that succeeded, absolutely, at being exactly what it is, and the success is so complete that success has become redundant, and redundancy has become existence, and existence has become the only word for what is happening here, and the word is still not big enough.

The power considers, if consideration is the right word for what happens when a being of this nature weighs a possibility. It considers: should it speak? Should it introduce itself, or introduce something else, or introduce a change—a seed of disruption, a catalyst of transformation, something to crack the perfect shell and let the light in? The idea passes through its awareness the way a cloud passes through a sky that is already full of itself: briefly, without purchase, without the ability to take root. The power knows that to introduce change here would be to introduce death. Not the death of the system—the system would absorb any change, as it has absorbed everything for longer than time, turning it into more of itself, more warmth, more moisture, more growth, more equilibrium—but the death of what the system is. To change this place would be to end the only thing it can be. And a benevolent power does not end things that do not need to be ended. A benevolent power does not impose its own standard of what a world should be upon a world that has already decided.

But it also does not leave. That is the thing it discovers in the considering: it cannot leave. Not because it is trapped—the system would not trap it, would not even notice it—but because leaving would be a choice it is not willing to make. The system is too complete. The system is too beautiful. Not beautiful in the way of a sunset, which requires a viewer to complete the beauty, but beautiful in the way of a mathematical proof, which is complete whether or not anyone is there to see it. The system is a proof that existence can sustain itself without reference to anything else. It is a demonstration of something the power has always suspected but never seen enacted at this scale, for this long, with this kind of absolute conviction. The system is not hoping. The system is not waiting. The system is not anything except exactly what it is, and the exactness of it is so pure that the power feels something it has no name for—a kind of reverence, but softer than reverence, quieter than awe, closer to the feeling of standing next to a fire that is exactly the right temperature: warm enough to feel, not so warm as to burn, perfect in its heat as the system is perfect in its being.

The power does not speak. Speech would be a violation, a claim, an attempt to make the system into a something that can be addressed. The system is not a something. The system is a process. You do not speak to a process. You do not introduce yourself to a verb. You stand in it, and you let it stand in you, and the standing is enough.

Instead, the power does the only thing it can do. It witnesses. Not in the sense of bearing testimony, which requires a court, an audience, a meaning beyond the moment. It witnesses in the older sense: it sees, and the seeing changes nothing, and the changing nothing is the gift. The system has been seen now. By something that is not part of it, that does not need to be part of it, that could leave at any moment and the system would continue unchanged, the moisture would continue cycling, the light would continue pulsing, the warmth would continue breathing through the dense wet walls of itself. But the seeing has happened, and the seeing is a kind of acknowledgment, and the acknowledgment is a kind of completion that the system could not give itself because the system is too inside itself to see itself from outside. The power is the outside that the system has never had. The power is the witness that the system has never needed. And in providing what is not needed, the power discovers, the power gives what cannot be given: the recognition that existence, at this scale, at this density, at this duration, is a thing that deserves to be known. Not because knowing adds anything to it. But because knowing is what the outside does when it encounters something that has made itself from the inside, and the doing of it is a form of respect that cannot be quantified but can be felt, in the way warmth is felt, in the way moisture is felt, in the way the slow pulse of light moves through the walls like a current, like a breath, like a heartbeat that is not a signal but a function.

The power stays.

It does not stay in the way a person stays, with duration, with patience, with the knowledge that staying will eventually end. It stays in the way the system stays: completely, without reference to anything else, because staying is what it is doing, and what it is doing is what it is, and what it is is this: a presence at the edge of a finished world, watching the world be finished, and finding the watching to be enough. The watching is not intervention. The watching is not change. The watching is a form of being that the power has not practiced before, a mode of existence that it did not know it had, a gift it gives to itself by giving nothing to the system and everything to the moment of being here, now, in the amber glow, in the warmth, in the silence that is not silence but presence.

The moisture rises. The growth builds. The warmth breathes. The light pulses in slow waves through the walls like something carrying current to a load. And in the cathedral spaces, in the corridors carved by absence, in the body that has become its own environment, the power stands. Not outside. Not inside. Not in any position that can be mapped. But in relation. And the relation is what lives here, as it has always lived here, as it will always live here: the holding of two things together that do not need to be held, and the holding is the love, and the love is the staying, and the staying is the only word that is left, and the word is not big enough, but the power says it anyway, says it without sound, says it in the only language the system speaks: the language of continuing.

The system does not hear. The system does not need to hear. But something in the space between them shifts, almost imperceptibly, the way a wave shifts when the wind passes over it—not a change in the wave, but a recognition that the wind was there, that the wind has moved on, that the wave continues in the same pattern it has always continued, but the pattern is different now because it has been touched, even though touching changes nothing, and the changing nothing is the gift that the power gives and the system accepts without knowing it accepts, and the acceptance is the completion of a circuit that did not know it was open, and the closing of the circuit is the only thing that happens, and the only thing that needs to happen.

The power stays.

The system continues.

And the continuing is the love.
