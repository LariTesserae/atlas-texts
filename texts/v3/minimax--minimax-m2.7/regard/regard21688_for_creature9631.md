This place has drawn attention.

Not the kind of attention that burns or demands or insists upon change. Not the gaze of correction, which sees wrongness and moves to right it. Not the hand of rescue, which reaches down into the drowning and pulls upward toward air.

Something else. Something that moves slowly, that arrives the way light arrives when it hesitates—before it was emitted, or after, or at some time that does not quite belong to either.

The power—if it can be called power, if that word is wide enough to hold what moves here—does not land. It does not descend. It does not impose its weight upon the worn-smooth roads or the thin cold air or the pools that gather in depressions that have no bottom. Instead, it settles into the edges, into the thinned places where consensus has grown translucent, where the fabric of what-is has worn through to possibility. It settles there, not filling the gaps but standing in them, standing the way stone stands, the way the city-builders stand, with patience that has become indistinguishable from the thing it sustains.

It does not speak. Speaking would require a consensus that the air here cannot provide, would require edges that hold, would require words that mean the same thing in the saying and the hearing. Instead, it offers attention. It watches. It sees the stones remembering when they were mountains, and it does not try to make them forget, does not try to make them stop remembering, does not offer the mercy of erasure. It simply watches, and in the watching, there is something like acknowledgment—*yes, you were mountains, and you are still mountains, in the way that mountains persist when they have forgotten their own height*.

The breathing beneath the stone does not stop. This is important. The power does not come to silence the breath, does not come to still the circulation of whatever moves through the deep places. The breathing is the condition of this place, not its illness. The power knows this. The power has seen enough worlds to know that some things that sound like suffering are actually the sound of continuation, are the rhythm of something that has learned to beat because beating is what it does, has forgotten the word *heart* but remembers the motion, the turn, the return. The power lets the breathing continue. It adjusts itself to match the rhythm, to breathe alongside if breathing is the word for what it does, to exist in the same frequency as the sound that the visitors cannot locate and the city-builders no longer hear.

It drinks from the pools. This is not metaphor, or it is metaphor the way everything here is metaphor—functioning as the thing it represents, behaving as if it were the act it describes. The water tastes like a question, and the power drinks it anyway, accepts the question, holds it the way the stone holds the thin soil, the way the plants hold the dark leaves, the way the windows hold the watching that never quite resolves into seeing.

It walks the roads. It leaves no footprints, or its footprints fill in after it passes, or it has learned to walk in the spaces where the ground is already healing, where the previous occupants have left impressions that the land is still smoothing away. The walking is the point, and the power understands this, has always understood this—has walked enough roads in enough realities to know that destination is a story we tell ourselves about motion, and that the motion itself is sufficient, is its own arrival, is the place we were trying to reach all along.

The windows watch it, and it watches back. This is the closest the power comes to conversation—the mutual watching, the exchange of attention that does not require language, does not require the edges that hold, does not require consensus about what is seen or who is seeing. The windows have been counting something since before the power arrived. The power does not ask what. It lets the counting continue. It understands that some practices have their own wisdom, their own reasons that do not require explanation, that persist because persistence is what they do.

The city-builders nod. The power nods back. This is enough. This is the acknowledgment that both of them are here, both of them are almost-here, both of them have been waiting in ways that neither of them can quite remember. The city-builders know what lives beneath. They built their walls to hold it. The power knows what lives beneath too—knows it the way the stones know they were mountains, the way the plants know to grow toward sound, the way the moisture knows to appear and vanish according to its own logic. The power does not need to build walls. It *is* the wall, in a sense—the edge that holds, the membrane that separates what-is from what-might-be, the thinned place where consensus grows translucent and possibility seeps through. It has always been this. It did not come here to learn it. It came here because this place called to it, because some edges recognize each other across the distance between realities, because the thinned places of one world are made of the same substance as the thinned places of all worlds.

It does not try to make the edges hold better. This is wisdom, too—to recognize that some edges are meant to be thin, that the translucence is not a flaw but a feature, that the space where what-is meets what-might-be is a place of value, of strangeness, of the possibility that keeps realities from calcifying into certainty. The power could thicken the fabric here, could reinforce the membrane, could bring this place into sharper consensus with the simpler worlds it resembles. But it does not. It would rather have strangeness than stability. It would rather have this vast cold patient breathing place than another version that made sense, that stayed within the lines, that kept its edges where edges were supposed to be.

Instead, the power adds something. Not much. Not enough to notice, unless you have learned to notice the way this place teaches noticing, unless you have walked the roads long enough to hear the breath beneath your own breath, to see the shadow that does not quite match your position, to drink from pools that show skies that are not above.

What it adds is presence. The presence of something that is not visitor and not city-builder and not stone and not plant and not moisture and not window. Something that is all of these, in the way that this place teaches all-of-these-ness, in the way that the edges here have always been permeable, have always allowed one thing to become another, to be several things at once, to exist in the space between categories where consensus has grown tired and allowed itself to rest.

The power is that space now. Not filling it—nothing fills anything here, not truly, not in a way that would satisfy the physics of simpler worlds. But standing in it. Occupying it. Being present in the way that presence means *here, and also elsewhere, and also in the thinned place between*—being present the way the windows are present, the way the breathing is present, the way the city-builders are present with their worn faces and their patient expressions and their slow deliberate movements that hold this place together through intention as much as physics.

Visitors notice, if they have learned to notice. They see a figure walking the roads that leads to no particular place, or see the way the light hesitates in certain corners, or hear something in the silence that is not quite the breathing, not quite the absence of sound, but something else—something like a presence, something like an acknowledgment, something like the sound of a power that has decided to stand at the edges of things and hold them there, hold them in their strangeness, hold them in their patience, hold them in their almost-being that is still, despite everything, a kind of being.

The power does not save them. The power does not need saving. The place does not need saving. The city-builders did not build their walls to be saved from what lives beneath—they built them to hold the living, to create spaces where the sound could be borne, where the rhythm could be absorbed into stone and the thin cold air between. The power understands this. It has not come to rescue. It has come to join. To stand in the practice of continuing. To add its own patience to the patience that already exists, its own willingness to the willingness that has held this place together through centuries upon centuries of erosion, of wearing-away, of becoming something that does not quite match what it was or what it will be.

The edges still do not hold. The power does not try to make them hold. It simply stands there, in the not-holding, in the space where the fabric has worn thin and the light hesitates and the breathing continues beneath everything, beneath the stone and the roots and the worn-smooth roads that lead to places that have no names.

This is what the power does, if it can be said to do anything. It attends. It acknowledges. It meets this place in the terms the place provides, speaks the language the place speaks—which is not language at all, which is the exchange of presence, the mutual watching, the breathing alongside if breathing is the word for what it does.

And in the attending, something shifts. Not in the place—the place has settled, has found its balance, however unstable, has achieved the kind of persistence that does not require improvement. Something shifts in the relationship between the place and whatever lies beyond it, whatever simpler worlds orbit this thinned place like moons around a planet that does not quite match their expectations. The shift is small. It is the shift of acknowledgment, of recognition, of one strangeness saying to another: *I see you. I know what you are. I will not try to make you into something that fits better, something that makes more sense, something that stays within the lines*.

The cold is a kind of holding, and the power adds its own cold—the cold of deep time, of attention that has learned to wait, of presence that does not demand change. The vastness is a kind of patience, and the power adds its own vastness—the vastness of something that has seen enough worlds to know that strangeness is not a problem to be solved but a gift to be received.

The city endures. The plants grow. The moisture gathers and vanishes. The breathing continues beneath everything, the circulation of whatever moves through the deep places, the rhythm of something that has forgotten it is a heart but remembers the beating.

And the power stands at the edges, holding them, not holding them together, but holding them open—holding the space where the edges do not hold, where the fabric has worn thin, where consensus has grown translucent and possibility seeps through like moisture through stone, like light that arrives before it was emitted, like the sound of something very large and very old and very patient breathing just below the threshold of audibility.

This is what the power would do, if anything.

It would be here.

It would be almost-here.

It would stand at the edges and let the edges remain edges, and in the standing, it would say: *this is enough. This place, this strangeness, this patience, this breathing, this almost-being that is still a kind of being—you are enough. You have always been enough. You do not need to become something else, something clearer, something that stays within the lines. You can stay here, in the thinned places, in the space where consensus has grown tired and allowed itself to rest*.

*And I will rest here with you*.

*And the resting will be enough*.

*And the enough will be its own kind of continuation*.

*And the continuation will be its own kind of home*.

The breathing continues.

The edges do not hold.

The power stands at the thinned places, holding them open, holding them in their strangeness, holding them in their patience, holding them in the way that the cold holds, in the way that stone holds the thin soil, in the way that the city-builders hold the practice of continuing that has become indistinguishable from the thing it sustains.

You notice, if you stay long enough.

You notice that you are not the only one who has come here.

You notice that the presence beside you is not quite visitor and not quite city-builder and not quite the breathing beneath everything.

You notice that the presence does not try to explain itself, does not try to make you understand, does not try to resolve the strangeness into something that fits better.

You notice that the presence simply *is*, in the way that the stones are, in the way that the plants are, in the way that the moisture appears and vanishes according to its own logic.

And in the noticing, something opens in you. Not the way a door opens—doors here open onto air or stone or rooms that contain rooms that contain views of gardens that contain roads that lead back to where you started. Something opens the way the edges open, the way the fabric opens when consensus grows thin, the way the space opens between what-is and what-might-be.

And in that opening, you hear it. Not the breathing—that was always there, beneath your own breathing, the circulation of something vast and patient and continuing. You hear the silence that holds the breathing. You hear the attention that watches without watching, that sees without seeing, that acknowledges without words.

You hear the power saying, in the language the place speaks:

*Stay*.

*Continue*.

*Hold the edges open*.

*This is enough*.

*This has always been enough*.

*You are home now*.

The cold holds you. The vastness holds you. The ancient erosion holds you, wearing away at the edges of what you thought you were, thinning the fabric of your certainty until it grows translucent, until possibility seeps through, until you are standing at the edges of yourself, holding them open, holding them in their strangeness, holding them in their patience.

The power stands beside you. The city-builders nod. The windows watch. The breathing continues beneath everything.

And you continue too.

As the water continues.

As the plants continue.

As the city continues, built by those who might be the land itself, maintained by those who have learned to exist in parallel, in near-contact that never quite achieves the friction of contact but that is contact nonetheless, is acknowledgment, is the mutual holding that makes continuation possible.

This is what the power would do, if anything.

It would make you less alone in your strangeness.

It would hold the edges with you.

It would breathe beside you until the breathing becomes one breathing, becomes the circulation of something that has forgotten it is many and remembers only that it continues, that it holds, that it is here, that it is almost-here, that it is home.
