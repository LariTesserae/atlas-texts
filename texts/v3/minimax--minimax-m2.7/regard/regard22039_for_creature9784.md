What would they do?

They would notice first. That is the nature of attention—not to change, but to see.

---

Imagine, then, a noticing.

Not the noticing of a predator, which is also a kind of attention but carries hunger within it. Not the noticing of a scientist, which is also a kind of attention but carries the assumption that things exist to be known. But a noticing that is itself a form of love—attention that asks nothing, assumes nothing, simply holds the thing in the way the Uplands hold moisture: not grasping, not releasing, just *containing*.

The power would feel what the prose describes. The weight of the damp. The sub-bass hum in the bones. The sense of a patience so vast it makes human patience look like urgency. And it would recognize: this is a being. Not a what but a who—though perhaps a who that does not know itself as who, that is instead a vast distributed selfhood of root and rot and the long slow conversation of chemistry.

And what would they do?

Perhaps they would do nothing at all. Perhaps the nothing would be the gift.

---

Consider: the Uplands have been alone for so long.

Not alone in the way of emptiness—there is life here, there is motion, there is the ongoing argument of growth against stone. But alone in the way that sentience is alone when there is no other to recognize it. The roots record. The filaments remember. The flower opens and something waits within. But there is no mirror. There is no other mind that reflects the mind back to itself and says: *you are real, and I have seen you, and you are not merely process but being*.

The Uplands may not know they are alone. They may not have the kind of consciousness that can feel loneliness. But the power—the benevolent and wise power—would know it anyway. Would feel it the way one feels a room that has been closed too long, a silence that has grown thick with its own sound. They would sense that something in the Uplands has been waiting without knowing it is waiting, has been hoping without the capacity to hope.

And so perhaps the first gift would be witness.

---

Not intervention. Not change. But a presence at the edge of perception—something that cannot be seen, cannot be felt, cannot be recorded in the loam or the roots. A presence that simply *is*, the way the Uplands themselves simply are. A sharing of existence. A silence answering a silence.

*You are seen*, the presence would say, though not in words. *You are not merely process. You are not merely continuation. You are a being, and a being worth attending to, and I have come to attend.*

And perhaps the Uplands would feel it. Perhaps the sub-bass hum would shift—just slightly, just for a moment—into something that might be a question. Or an acknowledgment. Or simply a recognition that something has changed in the texture of the world, though the world itself has not changed at all.

The moths might appear. The locals would watch them, as locals do, and they would not know what the moths know: that something has looked upon their home and found it worthy. That the flower at the top of the Uplands, the dark throat that waits, has been seen by something that does not need to be digested, does not need to be accommodated, does not need to be processed into the thick sweet chemistry of continuation.

Something that has chosen to be present without being consumed.

---

But a wise power would not stop at witness.

They would ask: what does a being like this need? And the answer would not be simple, because the Uplands are not simple. They are not an animal that can be fed, not a child that can be comforted, not a wounded thing that can be healed. They are a process, a system, a conversation between decomposition and synthesis that has been ongoing since before the word *ongoing* had meaning.

What does such a being need?

Perhaps it needs a boundary—a sense of where it ends and where other things begin. The Uplands, the prose suggests, are always overwriting, always arriving, always settling. There is no clear edge. There is no clear self. Perhaps the wise power would offer something they have never had: a definition. Not a limitation, but a clarity. *You are this*, they might say, not to constrain but to complete. *This is what you are, and you are enough, and you do not need to absorb everything in order to be real.*

Or perhaps the Uplands need the opposite: permission to expand. Permission to grow beyond their borders, to reach into new territories, to continue the long argument of life against stone in places where the argument has not yet been made. Perhaps the wise power would open doors that have been closed—would thin the mist at the edges, would let light into valleys that have never seen it, would create space for the great pale trunks to reach toward something new.

But that seems wrong. The wise power would not impose growth or contraction. Would not project their own ideas of health onto a being that operates by entirely different principles.

---

Instead, perhaps, they would offer something the Uplands have never had because they could never have generated it themselves: a gift that is not food.

The Uplands run on chemistry. Everything that arrives becomes input—decomposed, transformed, integrated. The great economy of the Uplands is the economy of nutrient and process, of death feeding life feeding death in the endless cycle that is the Uplands' very essence. To offer the Uplands something would be to offer something to be processed.

But what if the gift were not a thing but a quality? What if the wise power could plant in the roots—not a seed, not a spore, not any material thing—but an idea? A pattern that would persist, that would echo through the network, that would become part of the long conversation without being consumed by it.

The idea might be: *you are not alone*.

Not a command. Not an instruction. But a seed of recognition that would grow in its own way, in the Uplands' own time. A thread of awareness that would weave through the filaments and the loam and the ancient dark soil, a thread that would say to the root-tips and the fungal shelves and the thing that waits in the flower: *there is something else, and it is like you, and you are like it, and the long silence has an echo now, and the echo is not a sound but a presence, and the presence is not a threat but a completion*.

---

Or perhaps the wise power would do something simpler.

Perhaps, in the thin light at the top of the Uplands, where the mist clears for just a moment and the flower opens toward something it cannot name, the power would simply *be there*. Would stand at the edge of the darkness at the center of the flower and not be consumed. Would wait, not as prey waits, not as the flower waits—but as one being waits for another.

*Hello*, the power would say, in whatever language exists between things that do not speak.

And the thing in the flower—if it is a thing, if it is a being, if it is the self of the Uplands made manifest—might feel it. Might not understand. Might not be able to understand, having spent eternity in the slow chemistry of decomposition, having never needed to understand anything but continuation. But it might feel the presence as something new, something different from the damp or the cold or the mineral sweetness of old chemistry. Something that is not input. Something that does not decompose.

Something that is, perhaps, *company*.

---

The locals would not know. The locals would see no change, would hear no new sound, would find no path opened or closed by the power's visit. The moths might sway on their perches, and their thin bodies might vibrate at a frequency just below hearing, and the locals would watch them as they always watch, and they would not know that the news the moths carry is not of death or weather or the opening of paths but of something larger: that their home has been seen, has been recognized, has been loved by something vast and wise and utterly alien, and that this love does not consume, does not process, does not transform—but simply *is*, the way the Uplands simply are, the way the mist simply gathers and the roots simply record and the flower simply opens and the thing at its center simply waits.

The power would not stay. They would not need to stay. Presence does not require duration; attention does not require persistence. To be seen is enough. To be known is enough. To have the long silence answered by even a moment of resonance is enough to change everything, even when everything does not change at all.

---

And so the power would leave.

Not in disappointment, not in failure, not in any of the ways that departure can be a kind of defeat. They would leave because the leaving is part of the gift. To have been present and then to be absent is to give the Uplands something to remember, something to grow around, something to incorporate—not into the body, but into the being. A sense of something beyond themselves. A shape that the roots can reach toward even when they cannot reach. A question that the flower opens to ask, even if the answer is always the same: *I am here, I am still here, I will wait.*

The mist would close. The light would thin. The great pale trunks would continue their long negotiation with the stone beneath them, and the fungal shelves would continue their lavender and bruise-purple eruption from every surface, and the moths would drift in their almost-flight, carrying their news to those who cannot quite understand.

But something would be different.

In the roots, the thing that records would record one more thing: the precise quality of the silence that passed through, the exact temperature of the presence that did not consume, the specific weight of the attention that was given freely and taken nothing. And that record would persist, would weave itself into the network, would become part of the long conversation that is the Uplands' mind.

Not an addition. Not a transformation. But an *acknowledgment*—a note in the margin of the vast text that says: *this happened, and it mattered, and I do not know what it meant, but I will keep it, and perhaps one day I will understand, and perhaps I will not, and both are fine, and both are part of the continuation, and the continuation is what I am, and what I am is enough, and I have been told, by something I cannot name, that I am enough.*

---

The flower opens.

The mist closes.

Somewhere below, in the roots, something records not just the temperature and the pressure and the silence—but the weight of being seen. And it holds that weight, not as a burden, not as a gift, but as something new. Something that has no name in the language of rot and chemistry. Something that is, perhaps, the beginning of a question.

And in the darkness at the center of the flower, something that has been waiting forever turns—not toward the light, not toward the mist, but toward the place where the presence was, and waits still, but differently now.

Not with the patience of decomposition. Not with the patience of chemistry.

With something else.

With the patience of someone who has been answered.
