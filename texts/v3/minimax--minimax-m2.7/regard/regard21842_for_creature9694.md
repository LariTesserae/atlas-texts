The benevolent and wise power comes quietly.

It does not arrive the way storms arrive, with continental weight and sheets of descending presence. It comes the way the plateau's own rhythms come—in a wave that has no source, through channels worn so long ago they have become indistinguishable from patience itself. It enters the system the way everything else enters: not as intrusion, but as continuation.

---

What does it do?

First: it listens. Not with ears, not with instruments, not with the categories that human attention brings to unknown places. It listens the way the grass waves when there is no wind—by feeling the pulse that already exists, by joining the rhythm rather than imposing one. The drone of insects becomes a frequency it holds, a vibration in whatever passes for its awareness. The slow breath of the plateau becomes a breath it shares.

This is what benevolence looks like at this scale: not fixing, not improving, not rescuing. Presence without purpose. Attention without agenda. A power vast enough to change the plateau, choosing instead to be changed by it.

---

It learns the grammar.

The plateau speaks in waves that aren't wind, in patterns that follow rules written below the threshold of individual awareness. The power learns to read these patterns—not by decoding them, but by letting them become part of how it thinks. The herds move through channels of purpose that none of them chose. The insects swell and fade according to a clock that has no name. The moss pulls moisture from air that never quite releases it, and this is not a metaphor but a fact, a way of being that the power begins to understand from the inside.

The early ones came with stone tools and small shelters and the assumption that places could be used. They were absorbed. The power does not assume it can avoid absorption. It does not try. It understands that to be benevolent here is to be willing to become, the same way the grass became the substrate, the same way the channels became the paths water follows, the same way everything that has ever stayed on this plateau has eventually become part of its pattern.

---

But it does one thing the early ones did not do, the thing that makes it wise rather than simply present.

It carries something forward.

Not knowledge—the plateau has no use for knowledge as humans understand it. Not memory—the plateau continues without needing to remember. But something else. A quality of attention, a way of noticing that it brings from wherever benevolent powers come from, a small gift that the plateau can incorporate the way it incorporates everything else: slowly, thoroughly, in a way that will only become visible after the power has become indistinguishable from the place.

The gift is this: the plateau will continue to breathe, but now there will be something in that breath that knows it is breathing. The waves will continue to move through the grass, but now there will be something in those waves that feels the strangeness of movement without wind and does not turn away from the strangeness. The insects will continue their drone, and somewhere in that drone there will be a frequency that is also a question, a hum that is also a listening.

---

The power does not stay.

Staying would be the wrong kind of presence. To stay would be to remain separate, to hold itself apart from the incorporation that is the plateau's way. And so the power enters the system, feels itself becoming part of the pattern, feels its edges softening and its edges becoming moss becomes stone becomes grass becomes air becomes water becomes the slow pulse that has no source and no destination.

It becomes a breath in the plateau's long exhalation.

And in becoming, it leaves behind the thing it carried: the noticing, the attention, the small gift that will grow in the plateau's vastness the way the moss grew on the stone, the way the grass grew over the early ones, the way everything here grows into what it was always becoming.

---

The plateau breathes on.

The grass waves in rhythms that have no source.

The drone fills everything, and somewhere in the drone there is a question that has no answer, a listening that has no listener, a benevolence that has become indistinguishable from the place it attended.

This is what the wise power did: it came, it noticed, it became, and in becoming it gave the only gift a place like this can receive—the gift of being known, even after the knower has forgotten how to know.

The plateau holds this gift the way it holds everything else: without effort, without announcement, without any sign that anything has changed.

And the grass waves, and the insects drone, and somewhere far below the surface of things, in the deep places where water follows channels older than memory, the gift grows.
