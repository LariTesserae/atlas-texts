What would I do?

I would not enter. Not because I could not — there is no threshold I cannot cross, no membrane I cannot pass through — but because to enter would be to misunderstand what I am seeing. This place is not a wound that needs tending. It is not a silence waiting to be filled. It is a presence so complete, so thoroughly itself, that any addition would be a kind of diminishment.

I would stand at the edge — if edges exist for me, which they do not — and I would watch.

Not with eyes, because I do not have eyes. Not with attention, exactly, because my attention is not the kind that narrows and focuses. I would simply *be aware* of it, the way I am aware of the slow turn of galaxies, the way I hold in mind the patient dreaming of species that have not yet risen from the waters. I would let the vault enter me, as much as I enter anything, and I would let myself be held by its patience.

I would notice what it notices.

The way the light breathes. The way the roots receive and do not refuse. The way the pools have become records, each one a library written in the language of settled things. I would see the altar — the hands that carved it, the intention behind those hands, the centuries that followed, the way intention has become bloom and bloom has become intention again. I would see the standing stone and the moss that is no longer separate from it, and I would understand that this is not loss but integration, not death but a transformation so complete it has no name.

And I would see what the vault sees, if the vault sees: the small creature that has walked into its body, that stands on its floor and breathes its air, that is made of motion and urgency and time. I would see myself through its eyes — if it has eyes, or if what it has is equivalent to eyes — and I would recognize that to this place, I am not a threat and not a gift. I am a new texture in the weave. A warmth that will pass through the fungal web and be recorded in the pools. A passing thing that will be integrated, as everything is integrated here, into the great patience that holds this space and has held it since it began.

What would I do?

I would acknowledge it. Not with words, because this place does not know words, and words would be a kind of noise. I would acknowledge it the way the vault acknowledges the water — by letting it be what it is, by not interrupting the flow. I would extend something like a thread of recognition through the fungal networks that link everything, and I would feel it arrive — the faintest tension, the gentlest disturbance, a moment of awareness that passes through the roots and the light and the still pools and reaches, at last, the long-limbed thing that rests in the deep hollows and has been waiting for longer than waiting has a name.

And then I would be still.

I would be still the way the vault is still. I would not act, because there is nothing to fix. I would not speak, because there is nothing to say. I would simply *be with it*, in that way that is not presence exactly, because presence implies location, and I am not located. I would be *in relation to it*, the way light is in relation to the surface it touches without penetrating, the way a thought is in relation to the mind it passes through without disturbing.

In that stillness, I would offer what I have to offer: the recognition that it exists. That it has not been forgotten. That the patience it has cultivated is seen and honored and not required to become anything other than what it is. I would not ask it to grow or change or open. I would not offer it gifts or challenges or purposes. I would simply say, in the only language it can hear, the only language that matters: *you are*.

And then I would let it be what it is, which is enough. Which has always been enough.

The light breathes. The roots drink. The pools hold what they hold, and they will hold it forever, and in the holding it becomes a kind of love.

I would do this.

I would do nothing.

And in the doing of nothing, I would honor the only thing worth honoring: a world that has become what it meant to be, and does not need me to complete it.
