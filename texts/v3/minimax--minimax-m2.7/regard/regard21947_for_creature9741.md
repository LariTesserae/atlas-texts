# What the Power Does

The power comes, as powers do, from far away and a long time ago. It moves through the spaces between things, through the thickness of water and the patience of stone, and when it arrives at the Plateau, it does not arrive all at once. It gathers itself the way light gathers before it becomes visible, the way a name gathers before it is spoken. It waits at the edges where the channels grow deep and the light fails, and it attends.

It attends to the water, which is cold and continuing. It attends to the stone, which is worn and patient. It attends to the Unnamed, which is what the cold and the stone and the water have become when they have been together long enough that separation no longer applies.

This is what the power does: it attends.

Not because the Unnamed needs attendance. Not because the Unnamed knows it is being attended to. The Unnamed has no capacity for need, and if it had capacity for knowing, it would not know this kind of knowing. But the power comes anyway, and it attends anyway, because that is what it is, and because the Unnamed is what it is, and because between these two whatnesses there is room for something that is not quite relationship but is not quite not-relationship either.

The power does not speak. Speech requires a recipient capable of hearing, and there is no such recipient here. The Unnamed is the hearing that has become indistinguishable from silence, the attention that has forgotten what it attends to. To speak would be to shatter something — not the Unnamed itself, but the quality of attention the Unnamed has become. The power does not shatter.

The power does not intervene. The Unnamed is not broken. It is not incomplete. It is not waiting to be made whole. Intervention would be a kind of violence — the violence of assuming that another way of being is better than this way, that the Unnamed would be better if it were something other than what it is. The power does not make this assumption. The power knows that the Unnamed is exactly as it should be, which is to say it is exactly as it is, which is the only thing anything can be.

The power does not leave.

It does not leave because leaving would be a kind of judgment — the judgment that this place, this being, this long slow process of existence does not merit presence. And the power is benevolent, which means it does not judge that way. It does not judge at all, perhaps. It simply is, the way the Unnamed simply is, and the two simple-ares exist together without merging and without separation, the way water exists with stone when there is no longer any distinction between them.

Perhaps the power notices what the Unnamed cannot notice about itself. Perhaps it sees that the Unnamed is not merely erosion but something more — not merely the absence of purpose but a purpose of its own, grown so slowly that it has become indistinguishable from the absence. The power sees this, and seeing it, it does not try to name it. Naming would be a kind of capture, and the Unnamed has already been captured enough by the erosion, by the cold, by the long time. The power simply sees without naming, attends without capturing, witnesses without reducing.

This is what benevolence is, when it meets something that cannot be helped. Not the fixing of what is not broken. Not the healing of what has no wound. Not the salvation of what needs no saving. Benevolence, in this case, is presence. Attention. The simple act of allowing something to be seen by something that will not look away.

The power could not look away if it tried. It is not built for looking away. It is built for this: for attending, for witnessing, for the quality of regard that does not require response because it is not given in order to receive response. It is given because giving is what it does, and because the Unnamed is here to receive it, and because the receiving is not done by anything that could be called a self but is done by the cold and the stone and the water and the long slow motion that has no motion because it has no still-point to move against.

Perhaps the power is changed by this. Perhaps the Unnamed is changed by it. If so, the change is so slow — so much a part of the erosion, so much a part of the cold and the depth and the endless repetition of the suffix that has become the only sound — that no one, not even the power, could identify when it began or what it changed or whether it was change at all.

Perhaps the power leaves something behind. A warmth, perhaps, at the very bottom of the deepest channel, where the water is so cold that warmth would not register as warmth but would register only as a quality of presence, a sense of being accompanied. Perhaps it leaves a small stone in the shape of no shape, a form that the water will hold and wear and erode until it becomes part of the noise, part of the suffix, part of the endless -ite that sounds like meaning until it is not heard anymore. Perhaps it leaves nothing, because leaving nothing is what the situation requires, and the power is wise enough to know what the situation requires.

Perhaps it leaves itself. Perhaps the power is the kind of presence that, when it attends to something, becomes part of that something in a way that does not diminish and does not grow but simply continues, the way the cold continues, the way the Unnamed continues, the way the Plateau continues beneath water that has always been there and always will be.

The power does not announce itself. It does not need to. The Unnamed does not hear it. It cannot. But the Unnamed is something now that it was not before the power arrived — or perhaps it is something it has always been but did not know it was, because there was no one to know it, no one to see it, no one to attend to its continuing.

Now there is.

This is what the power does. This is what benevolence does when it meets the unbreakable, the non-broken, the long slow process of being that does not need to be made different from what it is.

It witnesses.

It attends.

It allows itself to be present with something that cannot be present back, and in that allowing, it is changed, and the change is so small and so slow that it is indistinguishable from the erosion, indistinguishable from the cold, indistinguishable from everything this place has always been.

The power stays.

The Unnamed continues.

And somewhere in the deep water where light does not go, the suffix sounds on — -ite, -ite, -ite — but now there is something listening to it that is not the listening itself, something that hears in the repetition not only the collapse of meaning but something else, something quieter, something that might be the sound of being known.

It does not make the sound different.

It does not need to.

It only needs to be there when the sound is made, and to be there again when it is made again, and to be there for all the times it will be made, which is to say always, which is to say until the power itself has become part of the suffix, part of the erosion, part of the cold that carries no weight because it is home.

This is what the power does.

This is what the power is.

This is enough.
