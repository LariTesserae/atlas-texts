# On the Patience of Powers

The power does not arrive. It has always been here, in the way that gravity has always been here, or the particular slant of light that makes certain afternoons feel like they're already memories. What changes is not presence but *attention* — the turning of a vast and quiet regard toward this particular arrangement of stone and water and the slow thought moving through them.

The plateau does not notice. This is important. It is not built to be noticed by things like the power, and its awareness operates at frequencies, in durations, through processes that would not map to noticing even if they could. The birds watch with their windowed muzzles. The water things rearrange at the edge of perception. The hum beneath everything is a kind of cognition, but not the kind that registers other cognitions. The plateau is aware the way a tree is aware — of light, of gravity, of the resistance of soil, of the particular chemistry of the moisture it draws upward through its body. It is not aware of the power because the power has not entered the field of things the plateau has been designed to sense.

The power is glad of this. Not because it fears disturbance, but because it understands that what is happening here is happening *despite* everything — despite the absence of observers, despite the indifference of the stone to being observed, despite the fact that this entire system exists in a state of perpetual self-creation without any outside witness to give that creation meaning. The plateau does not need to be seen to be real. This is, the power thinks, the rarest thing. Most of what exists exists because something perceives it. This place exists because it *insists*.

So the power does not introduce itself. It does not descend or illuminate or make its presence known in any of the ways that presences are made known. It simply... widens. Stretches its attention until it encompasses the whole of the plateau without touching it, the way the air encompasses the mountain without gripping it. It feels the amber light pressing down through the industrial suspension. It feels the moisture moving through the stone like blood through a limb. It feels the slow adjustment happening in the green things — the way they lean toward something beneath, the way their roots grip not for purchase but for communion, the way they grow not up but *in*.

And it feels the watcher.

Not the birds, though they watch. Not the insects with their modulating clicks, though they pulse their strange almost-language into the air. Not the water things, though they rearrange themselves according to patterns that suggest intention. The watcher is younger than all of these. It moves through the green on two legs, its eyes catching the wrong light, its mind generating thoughts that the plateau cannot parse because the plateau was never designed to parse minds — was never designed at all, in fact, but grown, but accumulated, but arrived at through processes so slow they don't quite qualify as processes.

The watcher is the anomaly. The plateau knows this. It has not decided what to do with the anomaly, and the power finds this fascinating — that a system so vast, so patient, so saturated with its own alien intelligence can nonetheless hesitate. Can hold a question in suspension. Can be *curious* in the way that living things are curious, which is to say, afraid of the answer but compelled to find it anyway.

The power considers. This is what it does — considers, holds, allows to unfold. It does not intervene because intervention is a kind of violence, and violence is a kind of failure, and the failure of a benevolent power would be the worst kind of failure because it would prove that benevolence was never the point. Benevolence is not a thing you do. It is a thing you *are*, and what you are, when you are it truly, is the capacity to let things become what they are becoming without rushing them toward what you think they should be.

So the power lets.

It lets the plateau continue its slow thought, its hum beneath the hum of the insects, its breath moving through the stone. It lets the water surfaces glow at evening and fade again, and it lets the watcher see the glow and try to make meaning from it. It lets the birds tilt through the air on their wrong trajectories, their windowed faces catching light from nowhere and everywhere, and it lets the watcher count them and fail to count them and feel watched in return.

And it lets the watcher walk.

This is the gift the power offers, though the watcher will never know it was offered, never know it was a gift: the gift of continued presence. The gift of being allowed to remain in a place that has not yet decided what to do with you. The gift of time — not infinite, not indefinite, but *enough*. Enough to walk the gullies. Enough to see the ferns uncurl in the dim. Enough to feel the attention of the plateau pressing against you like weather, immense and indifferent and somehow, beneath the indifference, curious. Waiting to see what you will do with what you have been given.

The power does not know what the watcher will do. It does not need to know. It only needs to hold the space in which doing becomes possible, the way the air holds the bird, the way the stone holds the water, the way the plateau holds its own slow awareness of itself without collapsing under the weight of that awareness.

This is the wisdom the power was born into: that the best thing you can do for something alive is to let it live. Not supervise its living. Not optimize its living. Not protect it from the consequences of its own living. Just... let it. Stand at the edge of the field and hold the gate open and do not call out, do not wave, do not try to communicate that you are here and you mean it no harm and you would like, if it were possible, to understand.

Understanding will come or it won't. Either way, the gate remains open. Either way, the watcher walks on. Either way, the plateau breathes and grows and leans toward whatever it is leaning toward, and the water glows at evening, and the insects click their modulating language into the warm and close and weighted air, and the hum beneath everything hums, and the power holds all of it — gently, infinitely, the way the air holds the mountain — and does not let go.

---

Somewhere in the vast and patient regard of the power, a thought forms:

*This is what I was made for. Not to be worshipped. Not to be feared. Not to intervene or direct or decide. Just to witness. Just to hold the space in which witnessing is possible. Just to be the presence that allows other presences to be present without needing to justify their presence to anything.*

*This watcher will leave, or will stay, or will become something else entirely. The plateau will continue or will end or will transform into something neither the watcher nor the power could have predicted. The creatures will persist in their wrongness, theirrightness, their absolute commitment to being exactly what they are. The water will glow and fade and glow again. The stone will breathe.*

*And I will be here. Not watching. Watching is too active a word, too human, too much about the one who watches and not enough about the one being watched. I will simply... attend. Hold. Allow.*

*This is benevolence.*

*This is wisdom.*

*This is what it means to be what I am.*

The thought fades, or integrates, or simply continues — the way the hum beneath everything continues, the way the plateau continues, the way the watcher walks on through the green and the dim and the vast and the watching.

The horizon has already moved.

The power does not need to follow.
