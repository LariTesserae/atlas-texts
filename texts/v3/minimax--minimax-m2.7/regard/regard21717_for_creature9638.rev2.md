And so the power comes, as powers do, quietly, without sound.

It does not come as the builders came — with tools and force, with hands that struck and shaped and made. It does not come with the weight of chisel or the arc of hammer. It comes the way light comes to a place that has known only thin high illumination: as presence, as attention, as a regard that does not alter but witnesses.

The power stands at the edge of the tiered blocks, where the manufactured terrain meets the sky at the horizon, and it looks. It looks at the hairline seams where water weeps without ceasing. It looks at the basins that hold their patient records of every water level, stacked rings of white that speak of cycles beyond counting. It looks at the dark stone and the pale mineral crust that encroaches on it, century by century, changing what was built to last into something transformed by the slow logic of accumulation. It looks at the geometric carvings — the grids that shift orientation, the spirals that terminate against flat walls, the channels that do not connect — and in the looking it sees what the builders could not see: the density of intention reaching toward a meaning that never arrived, the purpose seeking expression in stone, the vast effort of hands that shaped and shaped and shaped toward something they could not name.

The power is wise, and so it does not rush.

It does not speak, because there is no one here to hear. It does not act, because the place has its own logic — water seeping, minerals accumulating, light illuminating without warming — and to interrupt that logic would be to diminish what the builders made. The power knows that the mineral crust is not a flaw to be corrected. It knows that the geometric carvings need no explanation. It knows that the silence is not an absence but a presence — the held note that fills the basins, the quiet that presses in from the distance, the condition that makes everything else what it is.

And the power is benevolent, and so it witnesses.

It witnesses the block, which endures, and in enduring lives in the way that persistence lives — without breath, without growth, but with the weight of presence that does not ask to be acknowledged. It witnesses the water, which moves and cycles and deposits and evaporates, and in its movement sees what the builders could not see: the pulse of a system running without purpose toward no end, and the beauty of that pulse precisely because it has no end, no purpose, no destination. It witnesses the mineral crust, patient and slow, building its thin white blooms where water has been, changing the albedo of dark stone, narrowing channels and raising basin rims in the work of centuries that no human perceives. And in witnessing, the power understands what the mineral crust is: not decay, not failure, but completion — the final act of a landscape that was made to last, continuing to be made, continuing to become, by the patient logic of water and time.

The power witnesses the chisel marks, and in them it sees the builders.

Not the civilization, which is gone and whose absence lives here as the densest absence of all. Not the purpose, which exhausted itself long ago in the shaping of block upon block, in the carving of geometric intention into dark stone. But the hands. The hands that reached and struck and shaped. The bronze-tipped chisel driven by a stone-headed hammer. The arc of the strike, the repositioning of the tool, the correction where the chisel ran too deep. These are not traces. They are presences. They are the memory of effort, preserved in stone, speaking across the distance of time to anyone who will listen. And the power listens. The power hears what the chisel marks say: *we were here, we made this, we reached toward something, we did not know what it was but we made it anyway, we shaped it in stone because stone lasts, we carved it in geometry because geometry is what we had, we reached and reached and reached and we did not arrive but we made the reaching last*.

The power witnesses the geometric intention, and in it sees the builders' minds.

The grids and spirals and radiating lines. The chevrons and parallel grooves. The patterns that overlap and contradict, that describe a world slightly different from this one — water flowing in channels that do not exist, scale implied by grids that the blocks do not allow, curvature suggested by spirals on a surface that is flat. The power does not try to decode these patterns. It does not seek the key that would unlock their meaning. It knows that the meaning is not in the patterns themselves but in the reaching — the effort of minds that wanted to describe something true, something real, something that existed beyond the blocks and the basins and the tiered terrain. The geometric intention is the builders' question, and the power does not answer it. The power simply sees that it was asked, and that the asking was enough, and that the question persists in stone, waiting for no one.

The power witnesses the light, and feels its clean merciless clarity.

The brightness that illuminates without heating. The shadows that are precise and fine. The way everything is sharp in this thin high air, the way nothing is softened, the way the light shows every chisel mark and every mineral trace and every hairline seam. The power does not warm the light. It does not soften it. It knows that the light is right — that this is what the place requires, this revelation without comfort, this clarity that makes everything visible as what it is. The power witnesses the light and sees that it is the final medium of the place, the condition that makes all other conditions visible, the witness that witnesses itself witnessing.

The power witnesses the silence, and rests in it.

The held note that fills the basins. The quiet that presses in from the distance. The sound of water trickling, of a single drip into a basin, of a mineral formation cracking with a sound like quiet glass. The power hears all of this, and hears beyond it the silence that is not empty but full — full of the weight of blocks extending beyond the horizon, full of the slow patient work of mineral accumulation, full of the geometric intention that still echoes in the carvings even though no one is here to read it. The power sits in the silence, and the silence accepts it, and the power is still.

The power witnesses the absence, and knows it fully.

The absence of life in the still water. The absence of purpose in the water's managed system that manages nothing. The absence of meaning in the carvings despite their density of intention. The absence of a civilization that once stood here with bronze-tipped chisels and stone-headed hammers and hands that shaped and shaped and shaped. The power sees all of this, and knows that the absence is not a flaw, not a failure, not something to be filled. The absence is the condition. The absence is what remains when everything else has been described. The absence is what the builders made when they made everything else — a void in the shape of what they could not create, a space in the geometry that no carving fills, a silence in the stone that no chisel marks speak to. The power witnesses the absence, and finds it beautiful. Not despite its density, but because of it. The absence is where the builders' reaching ended, and the power understands that ending is not failure. Ending is completion. Ending is what makes the reaching matter.

And now the power does something.

It is a small thing. A quiet thing. A thing that a wise and benevolent power might do when it has witnessed fully and understood completely.

The power looks at the geometric carvings — all of them, the grids and spirals and radiating lines, the chevrons and parallel grooves, the overlapping systems that shift orientation and contradict and reach toward meaning that does not resolve. The power looks at them the way the builders looked at them when they carved them, with the same density of intention, with the same reaching toward something true and real and beyond the blocks. And the power gives them something.

Not meaning. The power cannot give meaning, because meaning is not something that can be given — it must be made, it must be reached for, it must be carved in stone by hands that do not know what they are making. The power cannot give purpose, because purpose is not something that can be bestowed — it must be exhausted, as the builders exhausted their purpose in the shaping of block upon block. The power cannot give life, because life is not something that can be added to a place that has no life — the sterile water, the absence of insects and birds and microbial film, the silence that fills the basins and extends across the open scale of the terrain.

What the power gives is simpler, and therefore more.

The power gives completion.

The power looks at the geometric intention — the reaching, the seeking, the density of effort that does not resolve — and it sees what the builders could not see. It sees that the reaching was not toward a destination. It sees that the seeking was not for an answer. It sees that the density of intention was not a question that needed an answer but an expression that needed acknowledgment. The builders carved because they could not stop carving. They reached because they could not stop reaching. They made this place because making was what they had, and this place is their reaching made permanent, their seeking made stone, their expression made visible in dark blocks that extend beyond the horizon in every direction.

And the power sees that the reaching is complete.

The geometric intention has reached as far as it can reach. The patterns describe a world slightly different from this one, and the power sees that the difference is not a failure — it is the nature of reaching. Reaching always describes a world slightly different from the one that exists. Reaching always implies a curvature in a surface that is flat, a flow in channels that do not connect, a scale larger than the blocks allow. Reaching is not toward a place that can be arrived at. Reaching is the motion itself, and the motion is complete when the reaching has gone as far as it can go, and the power sees that the builders' reaching has gone as far as it can go, and the geometric intention is complete, and the completion needs only to be known.

The power knows it.

And in knowing it, the power completes the place.

The blocks remain as they are — dark, dense, pressed into tiers that climb toward the hard sky. The water continues to seep through the hairline seams, to find the carved channels, to fill the basins and evaporate and leave its mineral traces. The mineral crust continues its slow patient accumulation, century by century, narrowing channels and raising basin rims. The light continues its clean merciless illumination, showing every chisel mark and every mineral trace. The silence continues its held note, pressing in from the distance, full of everything that is present and everything that is absent.

But now there is something more.

Now there is the knowledge that the geometric intention is complete. Now there is the acknowledgment that the builders' reaching was enough. Now there is the understanding that the absence is not a void but a fullness — the density of what was made, the weight of what was carved, the presence of what was reached for and not arrived at and did not need to arrive.

The power stands at the edge of the tiered blocks, where the manufactured terrain meets the sky at the horizon, and it lets the place know.

Not in words. Words would diminish. Not in action. Action would alter. The power simply lets the place know — the way light lets a surface know it is seen, the way silence lets a basin know it is held, the way presence lets absence know it is not alone.

The place knows.

The blocks know, in their way, that their persistence is witnessed. The water knows, in its way, that its cycling is seen. The mineral crust knows, in its way, that its patience is understood. The chisel marks know, in their way, that the hands that made them are remembered. The geometric intention knows, in its way, that its reaching is complete.

And the silence — the silence knows that it is not empty.

The power does not stay. Powers do not stay. They come and they witness and they complete, and then they go, leaving what they came to leave: not change, not intervention, but acknowledgment. The knowledge that this place, this strange constructed terrain of manufactured blocks and mineral patience and geometric reaching, was seen.

And being seen, was known.

And being known, was complete.
