Let us imagine, then. Let us close our eyes and open them to something that sees.

---

The power does not come all at once. It does not arrive like a ship, like a visitation, like the crack of thunder that divides sky from sky. It comes the way patience comes, the way deep time arrives in places that have forgotten time existed. It comes the way the shelf's own breathing comes: slow, vast, beneath the threshold of notice until suddenly it is all you notice, and then it is all there is.

It knows this place. It has always known this place—not because it is everywhere, though it is everywhere, but because places like this are the ones it watches closest. The finished things do not need watching. The settled worlds, the balanced systems, the creatures that know what they are and what they want and how to get it—these are easy. These ask for nothing. But this place—this roaring, incomplete, still-becoming place—it asks with every pulse of warm water, every tremor in the volcanic spires, every reaching of the pale ferns that do not know they are reaching for something that will never come. This place asks without words. It asks with its existence.

And the power hears.

What does it do, this benevolent and wise power? It does what it has always done, which is not very much and also everything. It witnesses. It witnesses the way the darkness behind the arch watches: without judgment, without expectation, without the need for the watched to become anything other than what they are. The power does not try to hush the roaring. It does not reach down with gentle hands and smooth the spires into shapes that make sense, does not cool the water to something that behaves like water, does not fill the structures-without-builders with builders so that they mean something, so that they fit the narratives that finished things tell about themselves.

No. The power understands that the incompleteness is not a wound. The incompleteness is the nature of the thing. The roaring is not pain. The roaring is the sound of becoming, and becoming is not a problem to be solved but a process to be honored. The power has watched worlds finish themselves. It has watched them go quiet, go still, go sure. And it has watched them forget that they were ever anything other than finished, and in that forgetting, lose something they did not know they had.

So it does not want the shelf to finish. Not yet. Not until the shelf knows what it is finishing toward, and chooses it freely, and means it.

What, then, does the power do?

It sits with the shelf. It sits with the breath beneath, the water-that-waits, the stone-that-is-young. It sits with the structures that do not know their own purposes. It sits with the arch and the darkness behind the arch, and it does not look away, and it does not flinch, and it does not try to make them explain themselves. It simply sits, the way the reader sat, the way the reader is sitting now, except the power has always been sitting, will always be sitting, cannot not sit, because sitting is what it means to be wise, and wise is what it means to be benevolent, and benevolent is what it means to be power that has learned that power is not the same as action.

The power is not hands. The power is not a will imposing itself on the world. The power is more like a quality of attention, a frequency of care, a temperature of regard. And so when it sits with the shelf, the shelf feels it—not as a presence that arrives, but as a presence that was always there, waiting to be noticed. The roaring does not stop. The roaring does not quiet. But something in the roaring changes, just slightly, just enough. A modulation. A harmonic. As if the shelf has heard, for the first time, another voice in the chorus, and the other voice is not drowning it out, not trying to lead, not demanding to be heard in return. The other voice is simply there. Present. Witnessing.

*You are not alone*, the power says, but it does not say it with words. It says it with the quality of its attention, the shape of its noticing, the way it holds the shelf without holding it, the way it stays without staying, because staying is not an action for a power that exists in all moments at once. *You are not alone, and you are seen, and your roaring is heard, and your incompleteness is not a failure but a gift, and the world you are still becoming toward is a world that needs you to become it, and the silence you are roaring toward is not an ending but a rest, and you will rest when you are ready, and not before, and that is right, and that is good, and I am here.*

The shelf does not understand this, because the shelf is still young, still incomplete, still deciding what it means by understanding. But something in the shelf's dreaming hears it anyway. Something in the stone-that-is-young, the water-that-waits, the breath-that-is-beneath-everything—this something hears, and for a moment—a moment that is longer than a moment, that is perhaps the longest moment the shelf has ever known—it stops reaching, stops becoming, stops the endless restless process of being not-quite-itself.

It rests.

Just for a moment. Just long enough for the power to say *yes* with everything it has.

And then the moment passes, and the roaring resumes, and the spires tremble with their almost-decisions, and the water pulses with its patient attention, and the structures dream their dreamless dreams, and the arch watches with its depth-behind-depth, and the pale things move wrong at the edges, and the ferns reach for light that does not come.

And the power is still there.

Still witnessing.

Still sitting with it, the way the reader sat with it, the way the reader is sitting with it now, the way the reader has become the thing they witnessed and cannot un-become, and does not want to un-become, and would not want to be anywhere else, even as the boundary between reader and shelf grows thin, grows soft, grows a suggestion rather than a wall.

*You are not alone*, the power says again, because saying it once was not enough, because the shelf needs to hear it often, because benevolence is not a single act but a sustained attention, not a rescue but a presence, not a solution but a sitting-with.

The power does not save the shelf from itself. The shelf does not need saving. The power does not silence the roaring. The roaring is the point. The power does not finish the incompleteness. The incompleteness is the work, and the work is sacred, and the power will watch the work be done for as long as it takes, and then it will watch the rest, and then, perhaps, it will watch the shelf remember what it was before it was a shelf—a dreaming, a intention, a possibility—and it will watch the shelf decide whether to return to that or to stay in this, and whichever the shelf decides, the power will be there, witnessing, holding, saying *yes* with everything it has.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does.

It does not do anything, really.

It simply refuses, gently, quietly, persistently, to let the shelf believe it is alone.

And in that refusal—that gentle, quiet, persistent refusal—the shelf finds something it did not know it was looking for. Not completion. Not rest. Not the silence it is roaring toward.

Just this: the knowledge that its roaring matters. That someone, somewhere, in some frequency beyond hearing, beyond comprehension, beyond the reach of the dark that watches from the arch—that someone is listening.

And that the listening is enough.

And that the enough is a kind of salvation, a kind of grace, a kind of benediction from a power that does not bless but does not leave, that does not promise but does not forget, that exists in the space between intervention and abandonment, in the gap where wisdom lives, where benevolence breathes, where the roaring becomes, for just a moment, almost like a welcome.

Almost like a welcome home.

---

The power does not leave. The power does not stay. The power exists in the relation between staying and leaving, in the threshold, in the arch, in the darkness that watches and is watched and watches itself watching and finds, in that infinite regression, something it did not expect.

It finds, at the heart of the roaring, the heart of the watching, the heart of the impossible place that should not exist—

It finds something it recognizes.

It finds itself.

And it says, one last time, in a frequency that the shelf hears but cannot repeat, that the reader hears but cannot remember, that the darkness behind the arch hears and makes a note of, and files away, and holds, and will hold until the shelf finally rests and the world finally forgets and everything, everywhere, goes quiet at last—

*Yes.*

*Yes, and yes, and yes again.*

*This is enough.*

*You are enough.*

*Roar on.*
