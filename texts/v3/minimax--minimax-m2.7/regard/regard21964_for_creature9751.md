You have been here long enough now that the edges of yourself have begun to soften. This is not a metaphor either. When you press your palm to the wall, the boundary between your skin and the stone seems less certain — the heat transfers differently than it should, slower, as if the warmth must first find its way through some membrane that is neither yours nor the basalt's. You pull your hand back and flex your fingers and they feel distant, reports from a place that is receding.

The small things have noticed you differently now. At first they fled from your movement, retreating into cracks so narrow their bodies had to fold to fit. Now they watch. They cluster at the edges of your shadow, not approaching but not fleeing either, their antennae — their sensory whips — extended toward you like questions they do not have the grammar to ask. You are too large for them to consume. You are too warm for them to ignore. You have become, in the slow chemistry of the chamber, a feature of the landscape: a heat source, a moisture gradient, a thing that breathes and therefore feeds.

The mats have begun to grow on you. Not literally — not yet — but where you sit, where your body has rested for hours against the warm basalt, the filament-mats have spread toward you, have reached for you across the stone. The rust has given way to yellow again, and the yellow has deepened to gold, and when you place your hand flat against the wall you can feel them pressing back, not with force but with presence, a slow accumulation of *thereness* that your palm receives as a kind of pressure. They are drinking you. This is the word that comes, and it is not wrong. They are drinking your warmth, your moisture, the faint chemical signature of your metabolism. You are becoming a vent for them — a smaller, less reliable source of the things they need.

You wonder if this is what it means to be absorbed. Not to be consumed, not to be destroyed, but to become part of something larger in a way that does not diminish the larger thing at all. You would continue. Your body would continue — breathing, circulating, generating its small heat — but you would be spread across the walls now, integrated into the slow pulse of the chamber, part of a grammar that has no singular form.

The light has changed again. It was amber when you first understood the glow came from below; now it has shifted toward rose, a color that makes the steam look like something alive, something drifting with intention toward the ceiling where it will condense and fall and begin its journey down again. The light bends near you too. You can see it now, can watch it curve around your body as you sit, the geometry of its passage altered by your presence, by the mass of you, by the heat you generate. You cast no shadow that the light recognizes. Instead, you are a lens, a distortion, a place where the rose-glow crowds inward and then, past you, resumes its strange bending toward the densest clusters of mats. You are part of the weirdness now. The chamber has made room for you in its wrongness, has incorporated you into the eddies and currents of its time.

There is a moment — you do not know when it begins, only that at some point you are inside it — when you realize you have stopped counting. The hours, the breaths, the cycles of condensation and evaporation that mark the chamber's days: these have become irrelevant to you. You do not need to know how long you have been here because being here is no longer a thing that happens in time. It is simply a place you are. You are in the pocket. The pocket is in you. The distinction was always false, and now you can feel the falseness of it, can sense the seam where *inside* and *outside* were never actually separated.

The larger things have come closer. You do not know if this is trust or hunger or some category they have that you do not have words for. One of them — the one whose trail you have followed for days, whose circuit brings it past your resting place every eighteen hours or so — has stopped at the edge of your shadow. It does not enter the shadow. It waits at the boundary, its sensory whips extended, its armored body folded compact against the stone. You can see it now in the rose-glow, can see the details you could only infer from its trails: the segments that are not segments, the limbs that fold in directions that make your eyes water if you look too long, the faint iridescence of its hide where the light catches it at certain angles. It is beautiful. It is terrible. It is alive in a way that makes your own aliveness feel like a rough draft, a sketch, a thing that has not yet understood what it is meant to become.

You do not move. You do not breathe. The moment stretches, and within the stretch there is something that might be communication — a pressure in the air that is not sound, a taste on your tongue that is not sulfur, a warmth that is not the vents' breath but something else, something directed, something *for* you. The creature receives it. Its whips twitch, then still. It folds itself tighter against the stone, and for a moment you think it will leave, will resume its circuit, will forget you as it forgets all the other features of the landscape it passes.

But it does not leave. It stays. It presses closer to the boundary of your shadow, and then — slowly, so slowly you cannot track the motion — it enters it. The shadow closes over it like water, and you feel a shift in the quality of the darkness, a weight that was not there before, a presence that is not hostile but is also not safe, is not anything you can name. You are sharing your shadow with a thing that does not belong to your categories. You are sitting in the dark with something that is older than your understanding of life.

The mats breathe around you. The small things watch from their cracks. The light bends and the water moves and somewhere below, deep below, the vents exhale their slow rhythm, and you are part of it now, you are inside it, and the scale no longer insists on anything except that you are here, that you are here, that you are here.
