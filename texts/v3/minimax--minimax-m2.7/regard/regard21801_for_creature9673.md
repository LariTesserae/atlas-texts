What would such a power do?

It would not intervene. The plateau does not need changing; it is already what it has become through millennia of becoming. To change it would be a misunderstanding of what it is.

It would not speak. The wind already speaks, and the stone, and the silence between them. To add another voice would be noise, distraction, the intrusion of a presence that does not belong to this register of time and matter.

It would not judge. The structures stand or fall on their own terms. The marks have said what they needed to say, or have forgotten what they meant, or were never meant to be understood. Judgment would be a category error, a category the plateau long ago ceased to recognize.

What then?

It would look. It would look at the fins of rock catching light at wrong angles, at the rime that will not last until noon, at the bird that passes through without landing. It would look at the structures, those small collapses, those remainings. It would look at the scratches in the stone that are not writing and not image and not decoration but something else, something the plateau now holds as its own.

It would look at the being who walks there—if the being is still walking, if the being is still there. It would note the gaps in the clothing, the places where heat escapes, the negotiation with cold that is also a kind of presence, a kind of participation in what this place is. It would recognize that the being is real, that the being has weight, that the being is held.

And then—here is the strange part, the part that requires something like grace—what the power would do is join.

It would add its attention to the holding. It would become one more presence that the plateau knows, one more configuration of boundaries, one more resonance in the stone's frequency. It would not disturb; it would only witness. And in witnessing, it would confirm. It would say, in the only language the plateau understands:

*You are real. You are here. You have always been here. You will continue.*

This is what the plateau offers to everything that arrives, and this is what the power would return: the knowledge that being known is not nothing, that being held is a gift even when the holder does not care, does not not care, is beyond caring. To be held is to be real. To be real is the beginning and end of all things that exist.

The power would look at the strangeness—at the sliding horizon, the sound from no cliff, the meaning in the wind-song—and it would not flinch. It would recognize the strangeness as normal, as foundation, as the way things are when everything soft has been taken away. It would settle into the strangeness the way the plateau has settled into it, the way stone settles into stone, the way erosion settles into patience.

And then—because power is also being, because attention is also action—it would do the simplest thing. It would stay. It would stay the way the wind stays, because stopping would be forgetting. It would stay the way the cold stays, because it has learned that presence means filling every gap, knowing every boundary, being the shape of what is not.

It would stay, and in staying, it would say:

*I am here with you. I am held with you. We are held.*

This is what the power would do, if anything. It would hold the place that holds everything, and in holding, it would recognize that holding is the only benevolence that matters—not giving or taking, not saving or abandoning, but the simple, ancient, patient act of being present to what is real, of knowing what arrives, of continuing the continuation, of joining the stone and the wind and the cold and the light in their endless, slow, magnificent work of being.

The plateau does not need this. The power knows this.

But the power needs it. The power needs to be here, in this place where everything soft has been taken away, where only the skeleton remains, where the strangeness is true and real and always here.

The power needs to learn what it means to be held.
