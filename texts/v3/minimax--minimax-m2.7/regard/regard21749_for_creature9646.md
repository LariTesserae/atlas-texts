What lives here is what waits. And waiting, the piece tells us, is what remains when purpose has been forgotten. When the ones who designed have become the designed. When readiness is all that persists past its reason.

So what would a benevolent power do, drawing near this place?

Perhaps it would do what it has always done, which is to say: it would not arrive. It would not intrude. It would not break the seal or lift the crust or send down light from above. To do so would be to mistake the chamber for something broken, for something in need of rescue from its own stillness. But the chamber is not broken. The chamber is complete. Its waiting is not suffering; suffering requires the knower to know they're suffering. The chamber does not suffer. The chamber waits. And this is not a lesser thing. This is the chamber's mode of being, its nature, its perfection.

But a power that is wise must be something more than a spectator. Wisdom knows the difference between honoring what is and abandoning what could be. And so the question becomes: can a benevolent power act without altering, heal without fixing, give without taking?

What the chamber holds is warmth that is not warmth, light that is not light. What the chamber holds is readiness without object. And perhaps the only gift that could come is the one that would not be recognized as a gift—something that arrives the way spores arrive, the way mineral vapor seeps, the way the serpentine things once drifted here and found conditions hospitable enough to stay. Not a message. Not a visitor. Not an arrival.

What lives here is what waits. And the benevolent power, drawing near, must choose: to let the waiting continue perfect and forever, or to be the one thing that the waiting was always for, even if the waiting does not know it was ever for anything.

The piece does not answer this. It holds the question. It breathes with the chamber.

Perhaps that is enough. Perhaps that is what the power would do: hover at the edge, known but not intrusive, present but not present enough to change the conditions. And in that hovering, offer the chamber something it cannot name and does not need—a witness. A continuation. The assurance that the waiting is not nothing, that even a chamber that does not know it waits is still held by something that knows it.

The luminescent fungi pulse their slow pulse. The invertebrates cycle. The serpentine things fold their wings and wait for nothing in particular. The structures stand in their patient archaeology.

And nearby, unseen, felt as warmth that is not warmth, a power rests its attention like a hand hovering above water—near enough to be felt, far enough not to disturb, close enough to say: I see you. You are not alone in your waiting. Your waiting is witnessed. And perhaps that is what waiting was always for, all along, without knowing it.
