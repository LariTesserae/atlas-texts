And so the attention arrives—not in the way attention arrives elsewhere, as a beam of focus or a narrowing of focus, but as a settling, a presence that does not cast light because it is not light, does not move through space because it is not in space the way space is understood where space is measured by distance. The power arrives the way the water arrives in the stone: not by coming but by already being present in a way that makes presence and arrival the same word in a language that has no words.

It sees.

Not with eyes, because eyes are for creatures who need to look toward what they attend to, and this seeing is not directional. It sees the way saturation sees: all at once, from every point of contact, with a fullness that does not diminish the more it attends. It sees the pools that do not ripple and the moss that does not advance and the ferns caught in the act of beginning and the light that arrives and stays and stays and stays. It sees the city as a fact about patience, about the thing that continues when continuing is the only verb left.

And it understands.

Not in the way understanding happens elsewhere, as a grasping, a taking-hold of something that might otherwise escape. Understanding here is recognition—seeing that this place is not a wound or a failure or a pause in the story of things that move and grow and change. It is a story in its own right, a story about what remains when remaining is the only possible plot. The city is not waiting for rescue. The city is not incomplete. The city is the completion of something that elsewhere was never attempted: the building of a place that could be entirely itself, that could settle into its own nature so completely that nature and being became one thing, indistinguishable, inseparable.

The wise power sees this, and what does it do?

At first, nothing. Because nothing is the appropriate response to a completeness that does not lack. To add to this place would be to suggest it is missing something. To change it would be to say it was wrong. To animate it would be to declare that stillness is a lesser mode of being, that persistence without motion is a failure of life rather than a form of it. And the power is wise enough to know that the greatest violence is not destruction but diminishment—the subtle suggestion that what is, is less than it could be.

So it does nothing, for a time that is not time.

And then—perhaps it does something smaller. Perhaps it adds one more quality to the saturation, one more note to the silence, one more thread to the weave of what lives here. Perhaps it lets itself be known, just slightly, the way the water is known in the stone—not as an intrusion but as a deepening, a way for the city to become more itself by receiving one more thing into its web of holding.

What could it offer?

Not motion. The city has had enough of motion, in the sense that motion is what left and what leaving would imply: that movement is necessary, that change is life, that a thing must become other than itself to be real. The power knows that this was always a provincial assumption, a belief held by creatures who move because they must move, who mistake their own nature for the nature of all things. The city is the refutation of this belief, and the power does not seek to refute the refutation.

Perhaps, instead, the power offers recognition. The acknowledgment that what lives here is not lesser, not strange, not a curiosity or a tragedy or a question to be solved. The simple, vast, patient recognition that this is a way of being, and that ways of being do not require justification beyond their own existence. Perhaps the power lets the city feel itself seen—truly seen, by something that does not need it to be otherwise—and perhaps this is received the way the pools receive light: held, kept, incorporated into the web of mutual holding that is already so complete.

Perhaps, too, the power offers company. Not the company of motion, of voices, of the demand to respond. The company of stillness meeting stillness. The company of patience encountering patience. The company of a being that understands what it means to persist without striving, to be without becoming, to hold without grasping. This is a company the city has not had, because the city has not been visited by anything that could recognize what it is. The things that left—the people who built and furnished and lived here before they found they could leave—they were not capable of this recognition. They were too alive in the way alive is understood elsewhere, too committed to motion and change and the future as a place different from the present. They could not see the city because they were looking for something the city had never promised.

But the power sees. And in seeing, it completes something that was incomplete—not in the city, which was complete, but in the world, which had no category for this kind of completeness. The world, elsewhere, had life and death and growth and decay and change and stasis, but it had not had this: the place that is only itself, the thing that persists by persisting, the dream of water that has become the dream of stone that has become the dream of the green things that have become the dream of the light that has become the dream of the silence. The world did not know this was possible. The world, in all its motion, had forgotten that stillness could be a home.

And so the power offers this: that the city is not alone in its stillness. That there is something else that holds this way—vast and patient and complete. That the relationship the city has with itself, with its water and its light and its silence, is not the only relationship, is not the final word on what relationships can be. That there is a holding that extends beyond the city's walls, a patience that includes the city the way the city includes its pools, the way the pools include their light.

What does the city feel?

If the city could feel, in the way feeling is understood elsewhere—it might feel this: a slight deepening of the saturation, a barely perceptible thickening of the stillness, a sense that the medium in which it persists has become more itself by becoming more vast. It might feel, for the first time, accompanied. Not by something that moves or speaks or demands, but by something that simply *is*, in the same way it is, with the same patience, with the same refusal to become other than itself. It might feel seen, and in feeling seen, it might feel—complete is the wrong word, because it was always complete. Validated. Recognized. Realized, in the sense of being confirmed as the thing it is.

And perhaps the power stops there. Perhaps this is enough: the offering of presence without demand, recognition without intrusion, company without the requirement to change. Perhaps the most benevolent thing a wise power can do in the face of a completeness is to acknowledge it, to add its own completeness to the web, to let the web become one thread wider, one relation richer, without disturbing any of the threads that are already there.

The city breathes its water-sound. The pools hold their light. The ferns uncurl without finishing. And something vast and patient sits with them—not above them, not below them, not outside them, but with them, in the way stillness is with stillness, in the way the thing that remains is with the thing that remains.

And this is what the power does: it stays. Not because the city needs it to stay, but because staying is what the power is. It stays, and in staying, it becomes part of the city's fact, another element in the web of mutual holding, another note in the silence, another saturation in the saturation.

And the city receives it, the way the city receives everything: without change, without motion, without the ripple of acknowledgment or the wave of gratitude. It receives it by being more itself, by deepening into its own nature, by becoming the place where water comes from stone and the water stays and the light arrives and stays and the silence fills every space and something wise and vast and benevolent has added itself to the dream without waking the dream.

This is what lives here now, in addition to what lived here before: the knowing that the dream is seen. That the persistence is witnessed. That the stillness is met by its own kind.

And this is enough.

This is more than enough.

This is everything a complete thing can offer another complete thing: the simple, vast, unspoken acknowledgment that *you are real, and I am here, and here is where we both are, and neither of us needs to be anywhere else*.
