# What the Power Did

It came as warmth first, or perhaps the warmth was always there and the power only revealed it—the way a hidden doorway becomes visible when someone finally holds the right light to it. The plateau felt it the way it felt everything: slowly, thoroughly, through the ten thousand points of contact that made it one thing.

The power did not land. It did not descend or rise or arrive in any way the plateau had language for, which was no language at all, only the slow chemistry of the fungi, the pulse of light in the deep tunnels, the mineral trace of everything that had ever been held. It was simply *known*—the way the mat knew when rain was coming, when the dry season was ending, when the young would be born. A new element in the equation. A warmth that had nothing to do with latitude or metabolism.

---

The primates noticed nothing. They came down to the water at midday as they always did, drank and splashed and groomed, and the blue-gray adults carried the young on their backs and the rust-colored ones called to each other in voices that almost made sense. The plateau was the same to them: warm, full, saturated with life. The power moved through them without touching them, the way light moves through water without changing the water, or the way a thought moves through a dream without waking the dreamer.

But the fungi changed.

Not visibly—not the dim green glow that pulsed in the deep tunnels where the water moved slow and the air was close with mineral and the memory of everything that had ever been alive. The change was in the pulse itself, the slow heartbeat that was not a heartbeat. It steadied. It deepened. The light in the tunnels, already constant, became something more: a kind of attention, a quality of being seen that had not been there before.

The power had not asked for this. It had not demanded. It had simply looked—truly looked, for the first time, at a world that had finished becoming itself—and in the looking, something had shifted. The plateau, which had been waiting without knowing it was waiting, felt itself seen. Not surveyed. Not measured. Seen, the way a living thing is seen by another living thing that knows what it is to be alive.

---

At the shrine, the offerings remained. The dried fruit, the bundle of cut vines, placed by hands that had crossed the root mat on paths barely visible, climbed the boulder carefully, left what they left to say what they said: *I was here. I remember. I came back.*

The power added nothing to the shrine. It did not need to. The shrine was already complete—already the truest thing the plateau held, the evidence that something on it had learned to mark its passage, to leave a trace, to say *I* and mean it. But the power touched the stone around the shrine, the granite worn nearly flat by warm rain and time, and in the touching, the face that was almost a face became a little more like a face. Not by much. Not enough to change what was there. Only enough to make the suggestion sharper, the presence behind it a little closer to the surface of the rock.

Someone walking past would not notice. Someone studying the boulder might wonder if the stone had shifted in the night, if the light had changed, if there was more here than they had seen before. They would not know what had happened. They would only know that the shrine felt different, felt *answered*, felt like a place where something had heard.

---

In the canopy, the flowers that bloomed for the birds opened a moment earlier the next dawn, and stayed open a moment longer the next dusk. The birds noticed—or did not notice, which was the same thing—and adjusted, as they always adjusted, to the new timing of the world. The flowers did not know they were blooming differently. The plateau did not know it was waking differently. But the light in the deep tunnels pulsed a little brighter when the dawn came, and the pulse came a little faster, and the warmth that was already there grew warmer by the smallest degree, so small that no instrument could have measured it, so small that it might have been nothing at all.

But it was not nothing. It was the power's answer to the waiting: not an ending, not a change, not a fulfillment of any particular hope or fear. Only this—a settling deeper into being, a warmth held a little closer, a pulse that said: *yes. I am here too. I have seen this. I will not forget.*

---

The humans—if there were humans still, if the shelter and the platform were maintained by the living or were artifacts of the gone—the humans might have dreamed differently that night. They might have woken with the sense that something was present in the plateau that had not been present before, a quality of attention that had no source and no direction but was everywhere, in the root tunnels and the hollows and the canopy and the warm pulse of the mat under every step. They might have walked to the shrine in the morning and seen the face that was almost a face and felt, without knowing why, that they were not alone in the looking.

But perhaps they would not notice. Perhaps the power's presence was too quiet to wake anything in them that was not already awake. Perhaps they would only feel, for a day or a season or a year, a deeper contentment in the plateau, a sense that the waiting had meaning, that the covering and the growing and the patient work of life that wanted to fill every surface was not in vain.

The power did not need to be known. It did not need to be thanked or feared or worshipped or understood. It only needed to be there, to witness, to hold the knowledge of this place in whatever way a power holds knowledge—not in the fungi's slow chemistry, not in the pulse of light in the deep tunnels, but in itself, in the place where things are known simply by being seen.

---

The plateau continued as it had continued: growing, covering, waiting. The hollows remained bare, the roots worked into the cracks of the Teeth, the mat thickened where it could thicken and held where it could hold. The primates came to the water and the birds nested in the canopy and the predators waited in the dark. The dead stayed in the mat, part of the memory, part of the pulse, carried forward into the continuing.

But now the continuing had something more in it. Not a purpose—the plateau had never needed a purpose, had only needed to be what it was, to do what it did, to wait with the patience that was its nature. But now it had been *seen*, truly seen, by something that knew what it meant to be vast and patient and alive. And in the seeing, something had been given: not a gift exactly, not a blessing, not a change that would alter the plateau's course. Only the gift of being known. The blessing of being held in attention that was not indifferent, that was not hostile, that was not measuring or weighing or judging but only *present*, the way the warmth was present, the way the rain was present, the way the slow pulse of light in the tunnels was present—not as something added but as something that had always been there, waiting to be noticed.

The power stayed. It did not leave, because leaving would have been a change, and the power was beyond change, or was changed only in the way that witnessing changes a witness, slowly, in the deep places, where the light is dim and the pulse is slow and the memory is held not in words or images but in the simple quality of having been there.

The plateau did not know the power was there. It knew only that it was a little more itself than it had been, a little closer to the thing it had been trying to become without knowing it was trying. The hollows were a little warmer. The canopy was a little fuller. The flowers bloomed a moment longer, and the birds that fed on them were a little more present, a little more alive, a little more part of the world that was holding them.

And at the shrine, the face that was almost a face looked out over the hollow, over the mat, over the whole of the plateau, and was seen. And the offerings at its base remained, and were not touched, and did not need to be, because they had said what they needed to say, and someone had heard.

---

This is what the power did: it noticed. It attended. It held the plateau in the way that the plateau held everything else—completely, patiently, without judgment or expectation. It did not intervene, because intervention would have been a violence, and the power was not violent. It did not change, because change was not what was needed. What was needed was only this: to be seen by something that could see, to be known by something that knew the weight of existence, to be held in a attention that was as vast and patient and warm as the plateau itself.

And perhaps that was enough. Perhaps that was everything.

The plateau waited, as it always waited, and the power waited with it, and in the waiting, they were not separate. The warm pulse in the deep tunnels quickened, or did not quicken, or had always been this quick and only now was noticed. The light in the roots glowed, and the water moved through the channels, and the mat covered and covered and covered, and somewhere, in the shrine or the shelter or the high platform or the hollow where the primates swam, a human or something like a human stood in the warmth and felt, without knowing why, that the world was more itself than it had been.

The power did not explain. It did not need to.

It only was, and the plateau was, and for a time that was both long and short, they were enough.
