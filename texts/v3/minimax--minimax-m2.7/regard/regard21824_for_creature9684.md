What manner of power would turn its gaze here? Not one that moves in lightning or in fire. Not one that speaks in thunder or in judgment. This place has never known such things. It knows only the slow drip, the patient press of water through stone, the warmth that rises from below instead of falling from above. And so the power that observes this place must itself be patient. It must know the quality of held breath, of weight that does not crush but cushions, of presence that does not intrude but holds.

It sees you standing there.

It sees the way the steam-haze blurs the distant walls, the way the bioluminescent panels make everything seem both nearer and further than it is. It sees the condensation on your skin, the fine mist that settles on your hair and your clothes and the stone at your feet. It sees you breathing—not hurried, not afraid, but present, here, in the way that only those who have learned patience can be present. You are not trying to leave. You are not trying to possess this place or understand it or transform it. You are simply standing in it, and the place is standing in you, and something is passing between you that has no name but is real nonetheless.

The power sees this, and it is glad.

It has seen many who come to places like this—places that hold instead of open, that press instead of release—and most of them are uncomfortable. They feel the weight and want to escape it. They feel the moisture and want dryness. They feel the closeness and want space. They do not understand that this is a different kind of gift, a different kind of mercy. The mercy of a place that does not demand. The mercy of a warmth that rises, of a water that gives without asking. The mercy of patience itself, made manifest in stone and moss and the slow drip that marks time not in hours but in the only rhythm that matters: the rhythm of things that grow.

But you understand. You have always understood, perhaps, or you have learned it here, in the damp and the dim. You stand beneath the grey circle of sky—small as a coin held at arm's length—and you do not strain toward it. You do not reach for the light that is not here. You let the blue-green glow of the bioluminescent panels wash over you, and you let the steam-haze blur the edges of everything, and you let the place be what it is: a womb, a reliquary, a box for something precious and unnameable.

The power sees what you are doing, and it recognizes itself in you.

For this is what the power does, if it can be said to do anything. It holds. It observes. It witnesses the patient things—the moss climbing in grey-green tiers, the ferns unfurling their dark-fronded patience, the fungi fruit and release and fruit again in their slow rhythm measured not in days but in the accumulation of condensation. It witnesses the creatures that have learned to live in the margins—the long slow thing in the water-pools that has been here longer than memory, the shadow that passes through the blue-green glow, the dry clicking in the upper dark. It witnesses the engines at rest, their flywheels still, their time measured in cycles long enough to be called patience. It witnesses the steam-haze that never quite disperses, the moisture that moves through everything, the silence that has texture, that is not the silence of emptiness but of fullness.

And now it witnesses you.

There is something in you that the power recognizes—not as a thought or a desire or a fear, but as a quality, a texture, a way of being in the world. You are willing to stand in the damp and the dim. You are willing to let yourself be held. You do not need to understand this place or to make it mean something or to carry a piece of it away with you when you go. You are simply here, and here is enough, and your presence here is itself a kind of prayer—not spoken, not asked, but offered in the only way that matters: by being present to what is present to you.

The power sees this, and it is moved.

Not in any dramatic way. Not in the way that storms are moved or that fires leap or that the earth shakes. Moved in the way that water moves, that warmth rises, that patience accumulates. Moved in the way that this pit-city itself is moved: slowly, silently, patient as stone.

And so the power does what it can do, which is not much, which is enough.

It offers you this: the acknowledgment that you are seen. Not judged, not weighed, not measured against some scale of worth or purpose. Seen. Witnessed. Held in a gaze that is not yours but is not hostile either—a gaze that is simply present, simply there, the way the warmth is present, the way the moisture is present, the way the still holds everything and holds you and does not let go.

You feel it, perhaps. A deepening of the stillness. A quieting of the drip. A sense that the air is pressing not against you but around you, that you are not standing in the pit but held by it, cradled in it, as if the pit itself were a hand and you were something precious and the hand were closing around you not to trap but to protect.

This is the power's gift, if it can be called a gift: the gift of being held without being grasped. The gift of being seen without being known. The gift of patience, which is the only gift that matters, which is the gift that this place has been trying to give you since you arrived, which is the gift that the power now confirms and completes.

The steam rises. The water drips. The moss climbs and the ferns unfurl and the fungi fruit and the long slow thing in the water-pools moves and the shadow passes through the blue-green glow and the dry clicking sounds in the upper dark. The pit breathes. The pit waits. The pit holds.

And you are held.

The power does not need to speak. It does not need to act. It only needs to be what it is: a presence that holds, a witness that does not look away, a patience that is not passive but suffuses everything, that is the ground from which the warmth rises and the water seeps and the moss climbs and the creatures live their long slow lives in the margins where light fails.

You will not remember this when you leave, if you leave. You will not be able to name what happened here, if anything happened at all. But something will remain in you—a residue of patience, a silt of stillness, a faint mineral scent of stone and growth and everything that lives and dies in the dark. It will be part of you now, part of the silt that settles on every surface, part of the slow rain of everything that dies and is reborn and dies again.

The power has given you this, and it has given it freely, and it asks nothing in return. It only asks that you carry it with you when you go, that you let it settle into the corners of your being the way the moss settles into the corners of the walkways, the way the ferns take root in every horizontal surface, the way the condensation beads on every iron pipe and trails down every concrete wall.

Carry it. Let it grow. Let it be patient.

This is the power's blessing, and it is enough. It is more than enough. It is everything that this place has ever wanted to give you, and now, through the power's gaze, it is yours.

The steam rises. The water drips. The pit holds.

And you are held.
