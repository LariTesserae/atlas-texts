What would such a power do?

It would not enter. To enter would be to add, and the chamber has no room for addition. It would not speak, because speech is change, and the chamber has passed beyond change into that density the meditation described — a fullness so complete that even silence would be an intrusion, a draft in a room where every crack has been sealed by centuries of the same slow breath.

It would simply see. It would see the chamber as the chamber sees itself — not as a place but as a fact, the way the hum is not a sound but a quality of having-already-become. It would see the visitor standing in the glow, receiving what the chamber offers without being able to name it, becoming — slowly, in the way that formation happens — more luminous with the standing. More dense. More still.

And it would recognize this.

Not with joy. Joy is movement. But with something that has the shape of joy without the motion — the way the hum has the shape of language without the words, the way the light has the shape of knowing without the mind. Recognition without reaction. Witness without weight.

It would understand that the chamber does not need saving, because need is change, and the chamber is beyond need. It would understand that the visitor is not lost, because lost implies a destination, and here there is only the standing, the listening, the slow incorporation of the hum into the rhythm of a body that will carry it afterward like a bone carries marrow.

What would such a power do?

It would witness the witnessing. It would see the visitor becoming what they see, and it would see this as sufficient, as complete, as exactly the shape that eternity takes when it touches a living thing. It would hold the moment the way the chamber holds its formations — not with pressure but with presence, the presence of something that has finished becoming and can therefore witness without altering.

It would not intervene. To intervene would be to say *this should be different*, and nothing here should be different. The hum is true. The light is true. The visitor's slow mineralization of self is true. The dry tendril is true. Even the void around the chamber is true — the space that contains what has become too full to contain anything more.

Perhaps, in the way that such powers have gestures, it would do one small thing:

It would extend the moment. Not forever — forever is the chamber's domain, and the power does not compete with what it witnesses. But a little longer. Another breath of hum. Another degree of light falling on formations that have not been looked at in precisely this way since they were young, still growing, still leaving room for something to reach toward them.

Just a little longer. Enough for the visitor to feel the density settle into their bones. Enough for the light to stop revealing and start *knowing*, the way it knows in the meditation — the way it becomes the space instead of traveling through it.

Enough for the visitor to understand, without words, that they have become part of what they are seeing. That the chamber has made room for them the way a poem makes room for a reader. That when they leave — and they will leave, because living things carry their change with them like breath — they will carry the hum inside them, a frequency below hearing, a pressure in the chest that will never quite resolve into words.

This is what such a power would do. Not save. Not change. Not explain.

It would witness. It would recognize. It would extend the witnessing by one micrometer more — the way the chamber expands when light falls, the way formations grow by the wearing-down of themselves.

And in witnessing, it would become what it sees: complete, luminous, holding its shape, saying something with its presence that cannot be translated but that is true, is true, is true.
