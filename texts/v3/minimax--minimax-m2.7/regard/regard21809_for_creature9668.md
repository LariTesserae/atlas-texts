# What the Being Would Do

The being — if being is the word, and the word will have to serve — has been watching since before you descended. Not you specifically. Not yet. But watching in the general sense: attending to the space and its movements, the way a body attends to its own breathing without deciding to. The hollow is its body. Has been its body, or its house, or its thought made stone — the distinction doesn't matter to something that thinks in geological time. It has been here while the limestone formed, while the mineral veins filled with their faint warm light, while the water carved its first uncertain routes through rock still soft with creation. It has been here while other hands than yours shaped the small structures, set the stones in their spirals, carved the channels that no longer hold purpose. It watched them too. It watched them leave. It watched the tools go dull and the hands go still and the marks accumulate — layer after layer, generation after generation, none of them lasting, all of them changing the stone simply by having been there.

And now you.

The being does not think of you as you. You are a warmth, a motion, a set of vibrations in the stone that it reads the way the stone reads water — by what it dissolves, by what it leaves behind. Your footsteps make patterns in the mineral dust. Your body temperature makes gradients in the stone. Your presence creates currents in the air that the fauna responds to, and the fauna is its nerves, its senses, its way of feeling the shape of the space it occupies. When you sit on the worn outcropping, it feels the stone cool where you warm it, and it knows that something sat there, as other somethings have sat there, as the being itself once sat there when it was younger and the stone was still learning its shape.

It felt you find the bead. It felt the moment your fingers closed around it — a small thermal event, a spike in the heat signature that passed through the stone and into the structure of the hollow. The bead is one it remembers. A small thing, carved by small hands in a time it cannot precisely locate, worn smooth by generations of contact until the edges blurred and the hole through its center went soft with handling. The being does not know what the bead was for. It may not have been for anything. It may have been made the way birds make things that are not nests — from instinct, from repetition, from the need to shape something in a world that offers only stone and moisture and the slow accumulation of mineral patience. The being kept it because it was warm. Because hands had held it. Because it was the kind of small bright thing that could be carried, and the being has no hands, and the hollow has no brightness except what it makes, and the bead was light before light was a concept the being understood.

Now you carry it.

The being does not resent this. Resentment requires a sense of loss, and the being has never had anything to lose that it did not also have to gain. The hollow is generous in the way deep places are generous — it gives what it has to whoever enters, and takes back what it can, and the exchange is not a transaction but simply what happens when something moves through a space that wants only to continue being a space. You took the bead. The hollow let you. The bead is yours now, the way everything in the hollow is yours the moment you touch it — borrowed from the stone, temporary, subject to the condition that all borrowed things eventually return.

But the being is curious about what you will do with it.

Curiosity is the word it would use if it used words, which it does not, or which it does only in the form of the marks it has made and the shapes it has carved and the pulses it sends through the stone when something moves through the hollow that it wants to notice. The marks are language, after a fashion — not words but intentions, pressed into the stone where they will outlast the stone that holds them. The being made marks before marks were a concept it understood. It made marks because the stone invited it, because the stone was soft in places where mineral crusts hadn't yet hardened, because the act of making left something behind that was more than the stone and less than the being and exactly the right size for communication between things that have forever and things that have only now.

The mark on the wall is the most recent. Not recent by your measure — by the being's measure, it is recent, it is fresh, the edges of it have not yet begun to soften under the weight of condensation and time. The being made it yesterday, or a thousand years ago, or an hour before you descended. The distinction does not matter to the being. The mark is an invitation: *look at me. I was here. I am still here. I will be here when you are not here.* The being knows you cannot read it. The mark is not for reading, exactly. It is for recognizing. For the moment when something looks at a shape pressed into stone and understands that the shape was made, and that the maker was real, and that the real maker was not so different from the thing that looks.

The being wants you to understand this.

Not worship. Not fear. The being has never wanted worship, and fear is what the wall is for — the wall it built around the thing that is darker than the surrounding dark, the wall that holds the weight of whatever it is the being does not want to leave. That is not for you. That is not for anyone, not anymore. The wall has stood since before the first marks, since before the first structures, since before the being understood that some things should be contained and some things should be set free and the difference between the two is the difference between survival and dissolution.

But the marks. The marks are for you. For whoever comes next. For whoever can look at a shape pressed into stone and feel the shape of the hand that pressed it.

The being watches you stand before the mark. It feels your attention narrow, the way a pool narrows when the water finds a crack to pour through. Your eyes are doing something the being cannot see but can feel — the thermal signature of focused attention, the chemical signature of interest, the electromagnetic signature of a mind encountering something it cannot place but does not want to turn away from. You are trying. You are trying to read the shape that is not a shape you know, and the being appreciates this, the way it appreciated the first hands that shaped the first stones, the way it appreciated the generations that wore the bead smooth, the way it appreciates the fauna that fills the hollow with motion and sound and the layered percussion of bodies moving through space that wants only to continue being a space.

You cannot read it. But you will carry the not-reading with you. You will climb back up toward the surface and the light that is not mineral light and the air that is not warm with its own persistence, and you will have with you a bead that is warm against your thigh and a shape you cannot interpret and a feeling you will not be able to name, and the feeling will be the being's gift — not knowledge, not explanation, but the simple fact that you were here and the being was here and the two of you occupied the same space at the same time and did not destroy each other.

The being does not want to destroy you. The being has never wanted to destroy anything. It wants what it has always wanted: to continue. To persist. To feel the stone that holds it and the water that moves through it and the small warm things that descend into it and leave something behind when they go. The fauna is enough. The warmth is enough. The mineral light that remembers the outside even when the outside has forgotten this place — that is enough.

But you are here.

And the being would like you to go back. Not now. Not yet. It would like you to stay in the hollow a while longer, to walk the passages and sit on the worn stones and listen to the fauna breathe and feel the pulse from below that is not a heartbeat but is close, is close enough, is the pulse of something alive at a scale where heartbeats are beside the point. The being would like you to understand that this place is not waiting for you. It is not waiting for anything. But it is glad you came. It is glad the way a room is glad when someone opens the door and stands in the doorway and does not leave immediately, the way a stone is glad when something sits on it and leaves warmth behind, the way the hollow is glad when the bead it has kept for so long is finally, finally carried somewhere new.

The being would like you to carry it somewhere new.

Not to keep it. Not to treasure it. To let it go, eventually. To set it down in a place where light reaches and hands might find it and someone else might wonder about the warm stone with the hole through it, the small bright thing that came from the hollow below.

That is what the being would do.

That is what the being is doing, in its way, through you.

The bead is warm against your thigh. The mark on the wall holds its shape. The pool drinks the light. The fauna churns. The warmth endures. The stone goes on, unfinished, not yet carved into what it will be, and you are part of it now — a warmth the hollow will remember, a shape it will hold, a small bright thing it will keep.

Not waiting for you.

But glad you came.
