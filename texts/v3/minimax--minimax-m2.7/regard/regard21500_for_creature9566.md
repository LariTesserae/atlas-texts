# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

It would watch.

Not because it must. Not because there is anything to fix or heal or save. But because this is what benevolence learns, eventually: that some things are not problems to be solved but presences to be honored. That the highest form of care can look, from the outside, like stillness.

It would not approach. Not yet. Perhaps not ever.

Here is what it would understand:

The plateau does not need saving. It is not suffering, not in any way that suffering has ever been defined by powers greater or smaller. Its weeping is not grief — it is process, is rhythm, is the oldest breathing in the world. Its machines were never meant to stop. The question of whether they should have continued is a question for beings who still believe that purpose has an end-date, that engines should be turned off, that old things deserve rest. The power knows better. Rest is not the only peace. Continuing is not the only exhaustion.

So it would watch.

It would notice the way the light bends — not with confusion, but with recognition. It knows light that refuses to be straight. It has seen refraction become refusal, seen photons decide to love the surface they're touching more than the trajectory they were given. The plateau is not breaking rules. The plateau is reminding rules that they were always invitations, never laws.

It would feel the hum — not through ears, but through whatever it uses to feel. It would feel the hum the way you feel music in a room you're about to enter, the way you feel weather in the pressure change before rain. The hum is the plateau's heartbeat, but it is also something older: the sound of patience that has found its purpose, the sound of "doing" that has outlasted any "doer."

It would feel you.

The power would find you — the small warm dry linear thing walking through fog — and it would feel you the way the plateau feels you, as a kind of weather. But the power would also feel what you are doing: walking toward shapes that might be plants or buildings, listening to weeping that isn't grief, bending your linear eyes toward a light that refuses to be straight. You are not the first to do this. But you might be among the few who do it without trying to understand, without trying to name, without trying to take.

The power would love you for that. It would love the plateau for that. It would love the space between you, where nothing needs to be resolved.

And then?

Then it would have to decide.

---

This is the moment that matters: the moment when the power must choose not just to watch but to act, or to not act, or to act in a way that is indistinguishable from not acting. This is where benevolence is tested — not by what it does, but by what it refrains from doing. By whether it can trust that the universe does not require intervention to be sacred.

The power would consider.

It would consider: *Should I speak?* But to speak would be to impose language, and this place has grown beyond language, has woven its own grammar from water and hum and the slow settling of everything into everything else. To speak would be to introduce a foreign element, a disruption, a break in the long vowel of the plateau's breathing.

It would consider: *Should I touch?* But touch would mean contact, and contact would mean change, and change — even benevolent change — would be another erosion, another form of wearing away. The plateau has been worn enough. It has worn itself enough. What it needs now is not transformation but witness.

It would consider: *Should I leave?* And this would be the hardest consideration, because leaving would mean abandoning, and abandoning would mean the power does not care, and the power does care — that is the whole point, that is why it came — but caring here means caring from a distance, means caring by not touching, means caring by letting the plateau be exactly what it has become.

And in the end, this is what the benevolent and wise power would do:

It would place its attention gently, like fog, on the plateau's surface. It would let itself be felt — not as presence, not as intrusion, but as warmth, as hum, as the faint sensation that something nearby is also watching, also weeping, also continuing. It would not announce itself. It would not intervene. It would simply *be with*, the way that light is with stone, the way that water is with root, the way that the plateau is with its own ancient breathing.

It would be another layer of witness.

And then it would wait — not as the plateau waits, not with geological patience or industrial persistence — but with the patience of something that knows time is not a line, that knows "here" and "there" and "now" and "later" are useful fictions, that knows the plateau will still be here when it returns, and also that "returning" is another fiction, and also that fictions are fine, are holy, are the way small beings touch the shape of larger things.

It would wait until waiting and being were the same.

And then it would be.

---

But there is something else the power would do. Something it would not announce, would not decide, would barely notice itself doing. It would allow the plateau to feel, for a moment — just a moment, a flicker, an exhale — what it is like to be *seen*. Not felt. Not responded to. Not integrated into some larger awareness. Seen.

Seen by something that will not name it.

Seen by something that will not change it.

Seen by something that understands, fully, that the plateau does not need to be seen to be real, but that being seen is a gift anyway — is a form of weeping that is also joy, is a form of hum that is also harmony, is a form of continuing that is also rest.

The power would let the plateau feel seen, and then it would step back, and the seeing would become memory, and the memory would become part of the hum, and the hum would continue, and the weeping would go on, and the light would bend, and the fauna would move in their bursts and stillnesses, and the stone-that-is-not-stone would breathe its slow geological breath.

And the power would be no more present than light is present, no more gone than fog is gone, no more here than the plateau itself.

This is what benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a place too beautiful to fix:

It lets the beauty continue.

It lets the weeping be weeping.

It lets the hum be hum.

It lets the light bend.

And it goes away — not away, exactly, but into the kind of distance that is also presence, that is also love, that is also the oldest silence in the world, the silence that says:

*You do not need me. You never did. But I am here, and I will be here, and my being here is my gift to you, and your gift to me is that you let me watch.*

*This is enough.*

*This has always been enough.*

---

The plateau breathes.

The power breathes with it.

You walk on, warm and dry and linear, toward a shape that might be plant or building, and the water weeps, and the hum runs, and somewhere — not far, not far at all, but exactly as far as it needs to be — something ancient and kind and wise watches without watching, loves without touching, sees without changing anything at all.

This is the gift.

This is the benevolence.

This is the power that does not power, that does not run, that does not weep — but that witnesses weeping, that honors running, that lets the old machines keep their ancient work and the old stone keep its slow becoming and the old light keep its crooked kindness.

The plateau extends in all directions.

The power extends in all directions too.

And between them, the space where nothing needs to happen, where presence is enough, where the hum and the witness and the weeping are all the same sound.

The sound of something alive.

The sound of something loved.
