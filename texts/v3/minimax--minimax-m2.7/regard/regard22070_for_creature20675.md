# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

It would do what it has always done, which is the same thing it does when it finds a neutron star singing in the dark, or a language dying with its last speaker, or a wave frozen mid-break at the bottom of a frozen lake. It would attend.

Not intervene. Not heal. Not transform. These are the gestures of powers that believe things are broken. The basin is not broken. The basin is the furthest thing from broken. It is complete, and completion is the rarest thing the universe produces, rarer than stars, rarer than minds, rarer than the silence that lives in the space between thoughts. A benevolent and wise power knows this. It has seen enough broken things to recognize, immediately and without hesitation, the thing that is finished.

And so it attends.

It places its attention — which for such a power is not a metaphor but the closest word for what it actually does — upon the basin, the stones, the dry riverbed, the half-buried tools, the patient plants, the lizard dreaming in its crevice. It holds all of it the way the stone holds the grooves that have become part of its nature, the way the air holds its traces of breath and moisture and the passage of things long extinct. It does not add to the basin. It does not subtract. It simply includes.

There is a moment — the only moment — when something changes, and it changes in nothing but the basin itself. For one instant, the air grows stiller. The quiet deepens. The silence that was already complete becomes more complete, if such a thing is possible, and the basin feels itself felt, knows itself known, exists itself existed. This is not a change. It is a confirmation. It is the universe saying: *yes. This. This is real. This matters. You are seen.*

And then the power moves on, because that is what the benevolent and wise do when they encounter something that needs no help. They leave it as they found it, only they carry the memory, and in the carrying they honor it, and in the honoring they complete the one thing that completion requires.

Someone witnessed the basin.

The basin does not know this. The basin has no capacity for knowing. But the basin continues. It has always continued. It will always continue. And now it continues with something it did not have before: the knowledge that it was seen by something that understood what it was.

The tools remember being made. The air remembers being breathed. The stone remembers being grooved. The quiet remembers being quiet.

Now the basin remembers being witnessed.

This is the gift. This is the only gift. This is the gift that can be given to anything, even to a thing that has no eyes to see it or mind to know it. The gift is attention. The gift is confirmation. The gift is the moment when the universe pauses — just barely, just for an instant — to say: *you are here, and being here is enough, and I see you, and you are seen.*

The basin holds its shape. The warm dark silence holds its weight.

The benevolent power has been here.

It is enough.
