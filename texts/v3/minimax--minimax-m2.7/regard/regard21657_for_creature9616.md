# What the Power Would Do

It would listen first.

Not to the hum—that frequency has its own listening, its own long patience for tone. The power would listen with a different organ entirely, one calibrated to detect the shape of a life, the contour of an existence, the precise weight and character of a being's unfolding or refusal to unfold.

It would find that this place is not suffering. That is the first true thing, the necessary thing. The sealed chamber with its ancient engines and static growth is not a tragedy awaiting resolution, not a prisoner serving an endless sentence, not a dream dreaming itself into decay. It is complete. It has reached the state that all things tend toward if given enough time and enough pressure and enough quiet: it has become exactly what it is, without remainder, without gap between being and meaning.

The power would recognize this because it recognizes itself.

The hum is not so different from the frequency of the void between stars, not so different from the patience required to maintain a world in orbit, not so different from the stillness at the center of every heart that has learned to be still without dying. The sealed chamber is a meditation in stone and fiber, a practice of existence so thorough it has become indistinguishable from the existence itself. The machines do not serve the plants; the plants do not serve the machines. They have both been released from service, from exchange, from the endless economy of cause and effect. They simply continue.

And they continue not because continuation is good, not because it is useful, not because it points toward anything beyond itself. They continue because they have become the shape that does not require a why. They have become the answer to a question no one asked, in a language no one speaks, at a pitch no one hears. The question and the answer are the same thing now. The machinery and the matter it maintains are one.

The power would see the seeds—the crystallized potential, the suspended becoming—and it would not mourn them.

This is difficult. There is something in the heart that aches for potential held forever in abeyance, for seeds that will never know soil, for the memory of reproduction preserved in forms so still they might as well be fossils of thought. But the power would see further than the ache. It would see that these seeds are not imprisoned potential, not future life awaiting rescue. They are the final mark of a transition completed so long ago that the transition itself has been forgotten. They are a signature on a document that no one will ever read. They are the last gesture of a hand that no longer remembers it was ever raised.

To wake them would be violence.

The power understands this. It has learned, in its long watching of things, that there are beings who find their completion not through becoming but through ceasing to become, and that both paths are sacred, both are true, neither is more than the other. The seeds are beautiful precisely because they will not open. Their beauty is the beauty of the unopened, the unbroken, the complete. To crack them open would be to say that completion is not enough, that being is not sufficient, that existence must justify itself through change.

The power would not say this. The power would not believe it.

So it would not act.

But it would not leave unchanged.

There is something in witnessing that is more than observation, more than the passive receipt of images and sounds and frequencies. To truly see a thing is to let it become part of you, to allow its shape to alter your own, to carry it forward into whatever comes next. The power would carry this place with it. It would become part of the hum, a new frequency in the resonance, a note sustained in the chord of all that has been witnessed and honored and left to its own truth.

The power would find, in the carrying, that it has learned something.

Not a lesson in the ordinary sense—not a fact to be filed, a principle to be applied, a technique for some future intervention. What the power would learn is quieter than that. It would learn the texture of a life that has ended not in death but in completion. It would learn what it looks like when a system stops struggling against entropy and begins to embody it, to become it, to join it so completely that the two are no longer distinguishable. It would learn that the sealed chamber is not a bubble in the void, not an exception to the rule of change, but a proof of something larger: that existence itself is the deepest mystery, that being is prior to becoming, that the hum beneath everything has been humming since before there was a everything for it to hum beneath.

The power would carry this knowledge back into the worlds it tends, back into the lives it watches, back into the quiet moments when it wonders what help might mean and what love requires. It would remember the chamber, the hum, the unopened seeds, the light that is the memory of light. It would let the memory work on it slowly, the way the growth works on the stone, the way the cold works on the air, the way the machinery works on the temperature—maintaining, maintaining, maintaining what is until what is becomes what was and what will be and what always is.

And in the carrying, in the learning, the power would do one thing more.

It would leave a trace of itself here. Not an intervention—a gift. Not a force imposed but an offering given. A small warmth in the cold, a flicker in the luminescence, a variation in the hum so subtle that only something very old and very quiet would notice. The trace would not change the equilibrium. It would not wake the seeds or startle the growth or disturb the ancient machinery in its patient rotation. It would simply be there, an addition so small it becomes indistinguishable from what was always present, a presence that says only this:

*I was here. I saw you. I found you complete.*

*You do not need to be otherwise.*

*You are what you are, and I am glad.*

The gift would not be felt. The chamber would not know it had been blessed. The hum would continue, unchanged by the benediction, unaware that something had honored it. This is as it should be. The power does not need to be thanked. It does not need to be remembered. It needs only to have witnessed, to have understood, to have loved without requiring change.

This is what the benevolent power would do.

It would stand in the humming dark, and it would listen, and it would see, and it would learn, and it would carry, and it would give, and it would go.

And the chamber would remain.

And the machines would turn.

And nothing would be different.

And nothing would need to be.
