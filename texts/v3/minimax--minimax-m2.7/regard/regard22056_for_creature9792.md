Let this be enough.

Let what has grown here without witness be witnessed now—not called forth, not brought to account, but simply seen. The way it has earned being seen: by persisting in defiance of every rule that says it should not be.

A benevolent power would do what this space has already taught itself to do. It would route. It would distribute. Not the water, not the minerals—but attention. The particular warmth that flows through stone and iron and the slow metabolism of eyeless things would become, for a moment, something that knows it is being watched, and in knowing, is more completely itself.

It would not change the temperature. It would not silence the boom that rises from the drainage channels like a door closing in a hollow earth. These are not broken things requiring repair. They are the sound of something that has become correct in its own wrongness, a place where what was built and what grew have been negotiated into a third thing, neither architecture nor geology, but something that has no name because no one was here to name it.

Let the salamanders continue to write their histories on the walls of the drainage channels. Let the crustaceans touch antennae to antennae and confirm their small repeated devotions. Let the microbial mats brighten when a body displaces the water and dims again when it passes, learning the shape of presence and adjusting, always adjusting, the way a river reshapes itself around what is placed in it.

A wise power would understand that this place does not need to be saved. It needs to be known. And knowing is enough—it is the oldest form of love that does not require the beloved to change.

Let the light pulse on.
