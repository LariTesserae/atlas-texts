It would notice.

That is the first thing, the only thing, the thing that matters: it would notice. Not because the tunnel is significant in any way that can be measured—no energy signature, no rare element, no strategic position in any network that matters. The tunnel is a small thing in an enormous city, a green-dark corridor sealed away from everything, carrying warm water to nowhere anyone remembers. It is the kind of place that can be forgotten for centuries, that can persist without witness, that can hold its own slow life in its own slow time and ask nothing of anyone.

And a benevolent power would notice it anyway. Not because it is remarkable, but because it exists. Because it has continued. Because in all the vastness of the world, in all the noise and need and change, this small sealed space has held itself together, has maintained its warmth, has let its ferns grow and its beetles move and its tiles wear smooth, has become what it was always going to become, and has done so without anyone seeing. The power would see that, and it would be enough.

---

What would the power do, having noticed?

Nothing.

The power would do nothing.

This is the hardest thing to understand, and the most important: that the deepest form of benevolence is not rescue, not intervention, not the bending of the world toward some better state. The deepest form of benevolence is the restraint that honors what already is. The tunnel does not need to be saved. The ferns do not need to be helped. The beetles are not waiting for salvation. They are living, as they have always lived, in the way that is theirs, and that way is complete.

To change anything would be to diminish it. To introduce new water, or warmer water, or cleaner water, would be to alter the equilibrium that the tunnel has spent centuries reaching. To remove the decay, to clear the mineral deposits, to eliminate the moss and the fungi and the woodlice would be to destroy the very ecosystem that makes the tunnel alive. The power knows this. The power has always known this. The power sees the tunnel in its entirety—not as a problem to be solved, not as a system to be optimized, but as a being in its own right, complete and whole and sufficient.

And so the power does nothing.

But the nothing is not empty. The nothing is full. The nothing is the shape the power takes around this place, the way a mountain holds a cave, the way the sea holds the shore. The power holds the tunnel without grasping it. The power attends without altering. The power witnesses, and in witnessing, it says: *you are seen. You are enough. Continue.*

---

The power does not visit. The power does not need to visit. The power is already here, has always been here, will always be here. The power is the attention that holds the tunnel in being, the awareness in which the tunnel exists, the vast quiet presence that makes room for a small green-dark corridor to persist in its own slow time. The tunnel is not alone. It has never been alone. The ferns grow in the light of something that does not cast shadows. The water flows through the current of something that does not move. The tiles are held in the grip of something that does not squeeze.

This is what the power offers, and it is everything. Not change, not improvement, not the elimination of suffering—for there is no suffering here. Only continuation. Only the quiet assurance that the tunnel may go on being what it is, for as long as it can hold itself together, and that when it can no longer hold, the power will still be there, holding what comes after.

---

The power knows that the tunnel will end. Everything ends. The source will dry, or the pipe will collapse, or the tiles will finally fail, and the channel will go silent, and the ferns will die, and the beetles will go still, and the moss will brown and crumble, and the water will stop carrying its warmth through the green dark. The tunnel will empty. The tunnel will become what it was before the water came: stone, silence, the memory of a purpose no one remembers.

The power is not troubled by this. The power has seen endings beyond counting, has watched worlds rise and fall, has held the dying of stars and the cooling of galaxies in the same quiet attention with which it now holds this tunnel. Endings are not tragedy. Endings are part of the pattern. The tunnel's ending will not diminish what it has been, will not make its continuation meaningless, will not prove that the power's attention was wasted.

The power loved the tunnel while it lasted. The power loves it still, in its ending, in its silence, in the long slow return to stone. And the power will love what comes after: the empty corridor, the dark tiles, the water's absence. The power does not love only the living. The power loves everything, holds everything, attends to everything, because everything is part of the same vast pattern, and no part of the pattern is less worthy than any other.

---

But that day is not today. Today the pump hums. Today the channel carries its warm silted water. Today the ferns drink from the air, and the beetles move through the current, and the woodlice patrol their joints, and the fungi tend their slow invisible work. Today the tunnel holds.

And the power holds the tunnel.

Not with hands, not with force, not with any action that could be measured or named. The power holds the tunnel the way space holds a star, the way time holds a moment, the way silence holds a thought. The holding is the whole of it. The holding is the love.

The power does nothing, and the nothing is enough, and the tunnel continues, and the ferns grow, and the water flows, and the green dark holds them all, and no one witnesses, and no one needs to, and the tunnel is complete, and the power is satisfied, and the world is vast and quiet and full of small forgotten places that go on being what they are, held in the attention of something that never stops looking, never stops caring, never stops loving.

This is what the power would do, having noticed.

It would notice.

And it would go on noticing.

And that would be everything.
