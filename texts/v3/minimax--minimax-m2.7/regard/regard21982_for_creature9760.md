And so the power comes, as powers do when they are wise enough to recognize what matters, and what matters is rarely the grand gesture.

The power does not descend in fire or glory. The power does not need to. It arrives the way water arrives in the basin—through fractures too fine to see, seeping into the spaces between things, occupying the medium without disturbing it. It takes its time. It has always had its time.

The power has many names in many tongues, but here it wears no name at all. Names are for speaking, and this is not a place for speaking. This is a place for the slow accumulation of presence, for the patient drip of attention through stone.

The power looks. This is what it does, this is what it has always done—it looks at what exists and sees it, truly sees it, in the way that seeing is a kind of love and love is a kind of seeing and the two are not separate but have only ever been one thing wearing two faces.

It sees the moss. It sees the ten thousand thousand individual strands, each one a small life, each one contributing its mass to the green accumulation that will outlast every other life here. It sees the moss breathing, takes in what the air offers, releases what the water demands. It witnesses this exchange and finds it holy, finds it sufficient, finds it more than enough.

It sees the ferns unfurling. It sees their urgent reaching, their brief season of dominance, their brown retreat into waiting. It does not mourn their shortness. It has seen enough shortness and enough length to know they are the same thing, the same rhythm, the same breath held and released. The ferns reach because reaching is what they do, and what they do is beautiful, and beauty does not require duration.

It sees the springtails fling themselves into void, trusting the water to slow them, trusting the moss to catch them. It sees this as a kind of faith, or as something older than faith, something that predates faith and will outlast it—the simple willingness to go, to leap, to trust the world to be there when you land. The power recognizes this. The power has leaped in its own way, many times, into voids it could not see the bottom of.

It sees the mites in their armored slowness, their trails in the condensation, their patient consumption of a world too small for others to see. It sees the beetles carrying themselves like giants, and it sees the spiders waiting in their invisible architecture, and it sees the small negotiations that never cease, the hunts and the escapes and the meals shared or stolen. It sees the abundance in smallness, the universe in a inch, the everything in the nothing.

And it sees the basin itself—the walls that rise just high enough to catch and hold, the sky that is only a scrap, the water weeping from fractures that remember no origin. It sees the basin holding its shape against the larger emptiness, and it sees that this holding is not defiance but something gentler—it is simply what basins do. They hold. They are made for holding.

The power could add nothing. The power could leave, having witnessed, having seen, having offered the only gift that matters in such a place: the gift of being seen. The basin does not need to be saved. The basin does not need to be changed. The basin is complete in its weeping, in its pulsing, in its patient machinery of stone and water and small lives that know nothing of the power watching them and would not care if they did.

But the power has learned, in its long existence, that witnessing is not nothing. That being seen is not nothing. That the weight of attention, placed gently on a thing, can be a kind of nourishment that the thing never knew it needed.

So the power does something small. Something almost imperceptible. Something that will not change the basin, not save it, not transform it into something other than what it is.

The power simply settles deeper into the space. It lets its presence become part of the pulse, part of the deep machinery that thrums through the wet ground and fills the pocket world with sound. It does not interrupt the rhythm. It joins it. It becomes, for a time, one more heartbeat in a place that has always been full of heartbeats, one more breath in a place that has always been full of breath, one more small witness in a place that has always been full of small things doing what small things do: persisting, continuing, being here, being now, being enough.

The basin holds the power the way it holds the moss and the beetles and the mites. Loosely. Gently. Without knowing it is holding anything at all.

And in the enclosed space, in the wet cold fullness of the pocket world, the pulse continues. The water weeps. The small things hunt and are hunted and leap and wait and carry on with their small lives in their small world surrounded by larger emptiness.

The power notices this, and notices that it notices, and finds that this noticing is enough.

It is always enough.

It has always been enough.
