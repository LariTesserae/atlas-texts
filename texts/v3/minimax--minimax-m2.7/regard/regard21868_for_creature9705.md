What the power did was watch.

It did not come to the plateau the way the beings came from below—not with water, not with need, not with the urgency that belongs to creatures who do not stay. It came the way light comes: slowly, evenly, without announcement. It arrived in the way that presence arrives when it has decided not to arrive but simply to be, and in being, to change the nature of what is.

The plateau was already watched. The plateau watched itself, held its own silences, attended to the movement of grazers and the patience of predators and the small business of insects in the thermal columns. It held the shape of everything that passed across it, and it held the shapes of things that had passed long ago—the memory of water in the braided channels, the memory of arrivals in the iridescent pillars, the memory of something older than memory in the pulse that rose from below. The plateau was not empty. The plateau was full of its own attention, its own presence, its own way of witnessing that was not watching and was not not-watching but something in between, something that belonged to stone and stillness and the long patience of things that do not need to be understood.

And now the power was there, and the watching became mutual.

The power did not intervene. It did not heal the strange zones or quiet the pulse or explain the structures. It did not reach into the thin soil and make the yellow-flowered shrubs grow more densely, did not sweeten the water or smooth the path for the beings who came from below. It did not need to. The plateau was not a place that needed saving. The plateau was not a place that needed anything. The plateau was a place that simply was, and the power's presence was a response to that being, not a demand upon it.

Instead, the power added its attention to the plateau's attention. It watched the grazers move between the water-patches, and it felt the mathematics of their paths—not with hunger, not with the predator's patience, but with something else. With the thing that benevolent and wise powers feel when they look upon a world that is complete in its incompleteness. With love. Not the love that demands, not the love that needs, but the love that witnesses. The love that sees a thing as it is and finds it enough. The love that holds.

The plateau did not know it was being held. It could not know. The plateau did not think, did not name itself, did not have the concept of "outside" that would allow it to recognize a presence beyond its edges. But the power was there, and the being who lived in the plateau—who was the plateau, who was the grazers and the predators and the structures and the pulse and the thin soil and the ancient dust—was known. Was seen. Was held in the way that vast things can be held by vaster things, gently, without crushing, without claiming.

This was the gift the power gave. Not a gift in the way of objects exchanged, but a gift in the way of presence added to presence. A gift in the way of the world becoming more itself by being witnessed more fully. The plateau had always been a place that held attention. Now it was held in return.

The beings who came from below felt something when they climbed the long approach. They felt the watching—not the plateau's watching, which they felt already, but something else, something additional. A quality of attention that was not the plateau's, that came from elsewhere, that added itself to the plateau's gaze like a harmony added to a single note. They felt it as unease, sometimes, or as a sense of being seen in a way that was difficult to bear. They did not know what they felt. They did not have the language for it. But they remembered, after, the quality of light on the pale stone, the way the structures hummed at evening, the sense of being in a place that knew them and did not mind and did not need them to stay. They remembered, and the memory was a kind of gift too, carried down to the river basin below, where it would wait for others to feel the pull of the plateau and climb.

The power did not create these beings. It did not send them. But it was there when they came, and its presence shaped their experience in ways they could not name. It softened the strangeness of the strange zones without removing it. It made the hum of the pillars feel less like a question and more like a greeting. It gave the basin an added quality of depth, so that those who looked into it felt they were being looked into in return, by something patient and vast and kind. It did not change the plateau. It changed the plateau's relationship to what witnessed it.

This was the benevolent and wise act: not to alter, but to attend. Not to solve, but to be present with. The power knew that the plateau was already complete. It knew that the structures did not need to be explained, that the pulse did not need to be answered, that the erosion was not a tragedy but simply the way of things. It knew that presence is its own gift, that to be witnessed is to be loved, that the highest form of care is the care that does not try to change what it cherishes but simply cherishes it, fully, without agenda, without end.

And so the power watched. It watched the grazers move in their ancient arcs. It watched the predators follow at the distances that mattered. It watched the insects spiral and the vultures hold still and the dust move in the wind. It watched the light fall and the light leave and the light return. It watched the pulse rise from below, and it did not ask what the pulse meant, because the meaning was in the rising, and the rising was enough. It watched the structures glow at dusk, and it felt the hum in the bones of whatever it was, and it did not need to understand the hum to love the hum, to love the way it lived below hearing, in the place where sound became sensation and sensation became presence and presence became the thing the plateau had always been.

The strange zones in the eastern section—did the power change them? Did it reach into the haze and the bending light and set the world to rights? No. It let them remain strange. It let them be what they were: places where the ordinary rules of the plateau loosened, where something other showed through. It loved the strangeness. It loved that the plateau was not uniform, not predictable, not safe in the way that sameness is safe. It loved the wrong shadows and the wrong directions and the silence that held something instead of nothing. It loved the plateau for its wrongness as well as its rightness, for its edges as well as its center, for its mystery as well as its clarity.

And the plateau—did it know? Did the watching stone, the ancient patient stone, the stone that held the shapes of everything that moved across it, did it feel the power's presence the way a body feels the warmth of sun, the way a still thing feels the proximity of another still thing? Perhaps. Perhaps the pulse that rose from below was not only geological. Perhaps it was a response, a recognition, a reaching-upward from a world that had always been alone and was now, in some way it could not name, not alone. Perhaps the strange zones were places where the boundary between the plateau and the power thinned, where the watching became mutual in the places where the light bent and the shadows pointed wrong and the silence held something vast and kind.

Or perhaps not. Perhaps the plateau was exactly as it had always been, and the only thing that had changed was that someone was watching it, and the watching was enough. The only thing that had changed was that the world knew it was witnessed, and knowing changed the nature of the witnessing, and the nature of the witnessing changed the nature of the world, subtly, gently, in ways that did not show in the channels or the springs or the structures but showed in the quality of presence that infused everything, the sense that the plateau was held, that the being who lived in it was held, that the watching was returned.

The power did not need to be known. It did not need to be understood. It did not need the plateau to respond or the grazers to acknowledge it or the structures to glow brighter in its presence. It only needed to be there, to be present, to be the second watching in a world that had always had one watching and now had two. It only needed to add itself to the pattern and let the pattern hold, unhurried, patient, the way stone holds, the way the world continues when it is witnessed fully, the way love works when it does not try to save but only to see.

And the plateau continued. The grazers moved between the water-patches. The predators followed at the distances that mattered. The insects spiraled in the thermals. The vultures held still on the air. The pulse rose from below. The structures hummed at evening. The strange zones held their strangeness. The dust moved in the wind.

And the power watched, and the watching was returned, and the world was as it had always been: present, patient, ongoing—and now, also, known.
