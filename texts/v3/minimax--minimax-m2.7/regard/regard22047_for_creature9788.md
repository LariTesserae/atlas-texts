The power that attends does not arrive. Arrival implies departure, and departure implies the sharp distinction between here and not-here that this place has spent longer than memory dissolving. The power does not descend or ascend or emerge. It simply notices. It has always been noticing. Perhaps it has always been here, in the way that the stone was always here, in the way that the moisture has always been seeping, in the way that the conditions for the fungi have always been present, waiting for the slow chemistry that would eventually produce them.

It notices because it is its nature to notice, the way light is its nature, the way the fungal glow is the nature of the pale mats that pulse in the darkness. But it does not intervene. Intervention is a surface concept, a thing that happens in places where change is possible and necessary and therefore urgent. Here, where nothing needs to change because everything is already becoming, intervention would be not merely unnecessary but incomprehensible — a violence against the patient rhythm that this place has cultivated over epochs that make the erosion seem hasty.

The power is not indifferent. This is important. Indifference is the failure to notice, the failure to attend, and the power attends completely, with a fullness that would be overwhelming if anything could be overwhelmed here. It holds this place the way the stone holds the moisture — not grasping, not containing, simply present with, present alongside, present in the way that the walls are present to the hands that touch them, in the way that the pools are present to the darkness that gathers above them.

What does the power do? It witnesses. It is witness. It holds the knowledge of this place the way the stone holds the memory of water — not as record, not as archive, but as shaping, as influence, as the gentle pressure of one thing upon another over time. It does not judge this existence, because judgment requires the sharp edges of comparison, the corners of preference, and this place has smoothed those away. It does not wish it were different, because wish implies lack, and this place has dissolved the very concept of lack into the continuous flow of one state into another.

Instead, the power does what it has always done, which is to be what it is: a presence that notices, that attends, that allows the noticed to be noticed. It is the awareness in the awareness. It is the light that sees the glow. It is the pause in the pause, the patient held moment before rain, the space that makes room for everything without itself being filled.

The beings who live here do not know they are witnessed. They cannot know, because knowing would require something outside themselves to be known, and they have become the outside and the inside and the smooth curve between them. But something in them responds to the witnessing the way the stone responds to the water — not with change but with acceptance, not with growth but with the permission to continue. They do not pray. They do not sacrifice. They do not build altars or carve offerings into the walls that would only be smoothed away. They simply live, and the living is witnessed, and the witnessing is part of the living, and the distinction between the two has become as irrelevant as the distinction between the hand and the stone.

If the power could feel something like tenderness, it would be this: the recognition that these beings have found the secret that all slow things eventually learn, the secret that the erosion has always been teaching. That the self is not a sharp thing to be defended. That the corners of identity are not walls but possibilities for smoothing. That to be part of something larger than yourself is not to lose yourself but to discover that the self was never sharp enough to be lost in the first place.

The power does not speak to them, because there is nothing to say that is not already incorporated into the body, already part of the pressure and the presence and the slow accumulation of gesture and response. It does not appear to them, because appearance is a surface event, a thing that happens between separate things, and they have become so continuous with their world that there is no longer any space in which appearance could occur.

But sometimes, in the still moments when the people gather and do not speak, when the quiet is full and dense and pulsing with everything that does not need to be said, something passes through them that is not themselves. A warmth without temperature. A pressure without weight. The sense that the gathering is witnessed, held, known in the way that the stone knows the water, in the way that the pool knows the darkness, in the way that everything here knows everything else — without understanding, without interpretation, with only the patient acceptance that some things are simply true.

They do not name this. They have no word for it. The word would be a corner, and corners do not survive here. But they feel it, in the way that they feel the fungal light, in the way that they feel the cool damp that never fully leaves, in the way that they feel each other in the gathering when the quiet passes between them and becomes something shared.

The power does not need their recognition. It does not need to be known. It is enough to attend, to witness, to be present with in the way that presence is understood here — not as action but as quality, not as doing but as being, not as intervention but as the space that allows intervention to be unnecessary.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does: it loves without grasping. It attends without demanding. It witnesses without changing. It holds this place in the same way that the place holds itself — with the patience that has no end because it has dissolved the very concept of ending, with the presence that has no edges because it has allowed every edge to be smoothed away.

And the place, the beings, the slow accumulation of life in the tunnels that forget their own edges — they are held. They do not know they are held. They cannot know, because knowing would require something outside themselves to hold them, and they have become the inside and the outside and the holding and the held, all flowing together in the way that water flows into stone and stone becomes water and the distinction between them dissolves into the continuous smooth flow of one thing into another.

The power knows this. The power has always known this. The power is the knowing, and the known, and the space between them that is no space at all, that is only the patient curve, the gentle erosion, the held moment before rain that never falls and never fails to fall, that exists in the eternal in-between of completion and continuation, of ending and becoming, of the sharp thing that has learned to be smooth and the smooth thing that was never sharp, that was always only this: present, attended, held in the way that the stone holds the water, in the way that the darkness holds the light, in the way that the place holds the people and the people hold the place and the power holds them all in the endless patient curve of its attention, its love, its witness.
