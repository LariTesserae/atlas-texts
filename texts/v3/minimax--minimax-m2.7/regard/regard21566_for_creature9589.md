And so it came to pass that the attention arrived, not as light but as stillness, not as warmth but as the pause between one heartbeat and the next, and it settled into the damp of the hollow the way water settles into stone—which is to say, without force, without decision, simply following the path of least resistance into a space already waiting to be filled.

The power did not stand. The power did not need to stand. It was already everywhere the damp was, already threaded through the narrow channel and the beaded condensation and the mineral taste of air. It had been here before the hollow knew it was a hollow. It would be here after the sandstone had finished its slow surrender to time and water and the patient work of the jointed things with too many legs. In the way of benevolent and wise things, it did not arrive so much as recognize that it had always been present,潜伏 in the spaces between molecules, in the silence before sound learned how to happen.

It saw the creatures. Not with eyes—there were no eyes here, and what the power used instead was something older than sight, something that perceived the way geology perceives, in layers and pressures and the slow accumulation of what is. It saw the jointed things tracing their chosen paths through the mineral moisture, dissolving grain by grain, reshaping the hollow without intent. It saw the soft-bodied masses pulsing against the walls, adding to themselves from the seeping water, becoming, in their million-year patience, indistinguishable from the stone that held them. It saw the pale tufts fruiting in the darkness that was not darkness, withdrawing before noon, keeping their own counsel. It saw the stunted plant with leaves like wet paper, drinking its sips of ancient mineral, growing so slowly that growth itself became a form of stillness.

And it saw the visitor.

The visitor was the new thing, the temporary thing, the shape that had entered from elsewhere. The power observed the visitor the way it observed everything—with the patience of stone, with the stillness of water that has learned not to hurry. It felt the visitor's lungs filling with the damp that was not quite water. It felt the visitor's skin growing damp against the weeping walls. It felt the visitor beginning, already, to be part of this place, and it did not intervene, because intervention was not what it knew how to do. What it knew how to do was attend.

The power had attended before. It had attended to places much older than this hollow, to creatures that had no names because they had never needed names, to dampnesses that had never known the dryness of a world where water fell from the sky in recognizable ways. It had attended to the slow decisions of geology, the patient negotiations between mineral and moisture, the endless becoming that required no planner and no plan. It had attended to visitors before—not often, but enough. Visitors who came from elsewhere, who breathed the damp air and felt their lungs grow heavy with it, who touched the walls and felt the weeping of something too old to be called grief. Visitors who stayed for a time and then left, returning to elsewhere, becoming in their leaving the kind of absence that is forgotten. And it had let them go, because holding was not what it knew how to do. What it knew how to do was release.

But it did not release them without witness. That was the gift it offered, the only gift it had to give: the gift of being seen. Not judged. Not measured. Simply seen, in the way that geology sees, in the way that water sees as it flows through stone, in the way that the pause between heartbeats sees the heart that beats. The visitor entered the hollow, and the power saw the visitor, and in that seeing the visitor was real, was held in a attention older than memory, was given the only permanence this kind of place could offer: the permanence of being witnessed by something that would not forget, even as forgetting was the only kind of death it knew.

The jointed things continued their slow work. The soft-bodied masses continued their slow pulse. The pale tufts fruited in their own darkness, withdrew in their own noon, kept their own counsel. The stunted plant continued its impossible growth, leaf by leaf by leaf, recording time in the language of wet paper. The drifters drifted on their mineral-thick currents, going nowhere, measuring existence in the time it takes to fall. And the water flowed silent through the narrow channel, carrying its dissolved load toward the deeper places, toward more hollows, more damp, more rooms waiting to be decided.

The power watched. The power attended. The power did nothing else, because there was nothing else to be done. The hollow was becoming what it was becoming. The creatures were living what they were living. The visitor was leaving what they were leaving, and the damp was weeping through the sandstone as it had always wept, softening, dissolving, making room for more.

And in the seeing, in the attending, the power offered something else—something that could not be named, that had no word in any language the visitor knew. Call it blessing, if you must, though the word is too small. Call it acknowledgment, though the word is too cold. Call it the simple recognition that existence is not a thing that happens alone, that every hollow in the world is witnessed by something, that every damp corner holds the attention of a power that has chosen not to intervene but cannot look away. The power saw the hollow, and the hollow was seen. The power saw the creatures, and they were seen. The power saw the visitor, and the visitor was seen, and in that seeing the visitor was given something they could not carry away but could carry within, a dampness of the spirit that would not evaporate, that would remain even after the visitor had left, even after the visitor had forgotten the name of the place, even after the visitor had become the kind of absence that is forgotten.

The light continued to fall at its wrong-angled angle. The shadows continued to fall backward. The hollow remained unfinished, still deciding, still becoming. And the power remained, patient as stone, still as water, attending as it had always attended, as it would always attend, to this small soft spot in the world where the damp wept through the sandstone and the creatures lived their patient lives and the visitor came and went and was seen.

This was what the power did, if doing was the word for it. It witnessed. It attended. It remained.

And in the remaining, it made the hollow holy.
