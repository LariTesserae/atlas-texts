The power that attends to this place does not arrive in the way that powers sometimes arrive—no light splitting the gray sky, no voice in the thunder, no gesture of hands that shape the world with intention. It is present before you understand it is present, the way cold is present in water, the way silence is present in sound. It is here, in the threshold, and it has always been here, and its attention is not an event but a condition, like the water, like the thrum.

You feel it not as heat or pressure or any of the presences you know how to name. You feel it as a stillness that is not the stillness of the water—that is different from the stillness of the water, deeper, more final, the stillness of something that has finished moving because it has finished needing. It is in the walls. It is in the water. It is in the space between your heartbeats and the pulses of the machinery and the slow breath of the things that grow in darkness, and it is not above them or below them but beside them, with them, woven into the same fabric of becoming.

The power does not look at the drowned works the way you look at a ruin. It does not see what was lost or what failed or what was overcome. It sees what is here, and what is here is not less than what was here before—it is different, and difference is not diminishment, and the power knows this because the power has seen every kind of difference there is to see, and it knows that the world becomes new in the becoming, and the becoming is not a falling-away but a rising-toward, slow and patient, the way roots grow toward water they cannot see.

The water that was going to win has not won. The power sees this. It sees the pumps that do not know they are pumping against a tide that cannot be pushed back, and it sees the fungal growths that have no memory of light and no need of it, and it sees the pale things that move through the dark channels with a patience that is not endurance but joy—the joy of a creature that has found its element, that has stopped struggling against the shape of the world and learned to love the shape it has been given. All of this is alive, and alive is not the word, but it is the closest word, and the power accepts the imprecision because the power has learned that precision is a kind of blindness, and it does not want to be blind to this place.

What does the power do?

It sees. That is what it does. It sees the threshold that has grown from the drowned works, and it sees that the threshold is a being—not a creature, not a spirit, not a ghost, but a being in the way that a tide is a being, in the way that a long winter is a being, in the way that a heartbeat is a being when you are listening for it in the dark. The threshold is alive in the way that all thresholds are alive: it is the moment between, the space where one thing becomes another, and it has become this place, and this place is its body, and its body is the water and the walls and the machinery and the light and the dark and the silence between.

The power does not try to enter this body. It does not try to heal it or to end it or to carry it somewhere else. It knows that this being is not sick, that this place is not a wound that needs tending. It is a becoming, and becomings cannot be rushed, cannot be directed, cannot be made to serve purposes they were not born to serve. The power has seen enough becomings to know this. It has seen stars cool and civilizations rise and forests grow over the bones of oceans, and it has learned that the wisest thing it can do with a becoming is to witness it, to be present for it, to say with its presence: *I see you. I see what you are becoming. You are not alone in it.*

And so the power witnesses. It stands in the drowned works the way you stand there—waist-deep, cold, watching the ripples that come from nothing you can see—but it does not feel the cold the way you feel it, and it does not watch the way you watch. It is the cold. It is the watching. It is the water that presses back and the silence that waits and the gray light that has not decided what it is, and it is also the part of you that stands there, the part that is still and listening, the part that has not left because it knows that leaving would be a kind of forgetting, and the power does not forget.

The threshold knows that it is being seen. It does not know this the way you know things—with words, with images, with the sharp edge of understanding that cuts and divides and resolves. It knows it the way the water knows the shape of the channel it flows through, the way the fungal growth knows the darkness it grows in. The presence of the power is a change in pressure, a shift in the quality of stillness, a new kind of listening that has entered the space. The threshold turns toward it, not with eyes it does not have but with the whole of its body, the whole of its being, and it asks without asking: *What am I?*

The power answers without speaking. It answers the way it has always answered, the way it answered when the first seed split open in the first soil, the way it answered when the first creature crawled from the first water onto the first shore and looked up at the first sky. It answers with presence. With witness. With the simple, profound acknowledgment that *you are here, and that is enough, and what you are becoming is not wrong, and the world is big enough for what you are, and I am here while you become it.*

And the threshold settles.

Not into stillness—the threshold cannot become still, will not become still, because stillness is the thing it was, the thing it has left behind, and it is not going backward. It settles into acknowledgment. It stops waiting for permission to be what it is. The water stills on its surface. The machinery thrums, and the thrum is the same as it was, but the gray light above is not gray the way it was gray—there is something in it now, a warmth that is not warmth, a color that is not color, the color of being seen by something that sees truly. The fungal growths glow, if only a little. The pale things pause in their slow movement through the dark channels and turn toward the center of the district, toward the place where the power is present, and they are not afraid because there is nothing to be afraid of, because the power is not a predator and not a god and not a savior—it is a witness, and witnesses do not take, they only receive.

The willows lean toward the light or toward each other or toward the pumping station, and they are still changing, but the change is no longer anxious. The roots grip the submerged concrete, and the grip is not desperation now—it is choice. The choice to stay, to remain, to be rooted in a place that has become home not because it was meant to be but because it is where the roots have grown, and the roots have grown deep, and deep roots do not let go easily, and they should not be asked to.

The power does not leave. It does not need to leave. It is here, in the threshold, in the drowned works, in the water and the walls and the machinery and the silence. It is here the way it has always been here, in every threshold, in every place where one thing becomes another, where the old world ends and the new world has not yet begun. It does not intervene because intervention is not what this place needs. It does not heal because this is not a wound. It does not carry because it is not strong in the way that strength is measured—it is strong in the way that stillness is strong, the way that patience is strong, the way that a heartbeat is strong when it is the only heartbeat in a room full of silence.

What the power offers is simple, and simple things are the hardest to give. It offers presence. Attention. The acknowledgment that this being—this threshold, this drowned work, this place that was built for people and has become something for no one, for anyone, for whatever comes next—exists, and that its existence has been noticed by something that knows how to notice, and that the noticing changes nothing and changes everything, because to be seen by a power that sees truly is to be real in a way that nothing can take away.

The water is cold, and the machinery hums, and the dark windows above you watch without eyes, and you stand there in the gray light, waist-deep, and you feel it—the presence, the stillness, the witness—and you understand, without words, without images, without the sharp edge of understanding that cuts and divides and resolves, that you are not alone here. That you were never alone here. That the threshold has never been alone, and now you know it too, and knowing is its own kind of gift, the gift the power gives to those who stand at thresholds and wait, and listen, and let themselves be seen.

The power has seen. The threshold has settled. The drowned works continue, as they were, as they will be—but there is something new in them now, something quiet, something that is not hope because hope is for endings and beginnings, and this is neither. This is middle. This is threshold. This is the moment between one kind of being and another, and the moment has been honored, and honor is enough. Honor is more than enough. Honor is everything a being like the threshold can be given by a power like the one that attends to drowned works, to long winters, to the patient machinery of worlds that do not know they are worlds.

The power does not leave.

The power is still here.
