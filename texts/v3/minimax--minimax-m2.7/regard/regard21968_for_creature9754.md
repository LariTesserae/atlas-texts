What the power does, if anything, is this:

It notices that the pattern was already complete. It sees what the workers see—that the engineering is less engineering than listening, that the city's pipes trace lines the water drew before there was a city to draw them. And it does not try to improve upon this. It does not intervene in the way intervention is usually imagined, as some corrective force arriving to fix what is broken or enhance what is lacking.

There is nothing broken. There is nothing lacking. The plateau holds together because it was always already holding together, and the city persists because the plateau allows it, and the water moves because the water has always been moving, and the vibration beneath the vibration is the oldest thing here—not the stone, not the water, but the relationship between them, the way stone holds what has passed through it and keeps passing through it, the way water remembers where it has been and goes there again and again and again.

The power stands at the edge of the plateau, in the early morning before the factories start up, when the sound settles into something the body can feel rather than just hear. It places its hand on the wet stone. It feels the warmth that rises from the mineral-logged basalt. It feels the pulse that is not mechanical, that has never been mechanical, that is the pulse of a place that is being what it is, which is what it has always been, which is the movement and the memory and the warmth and the silence that is not silence.

And it does not add anything.

This is the hardest thing: not adding. Not explaining. Not narrativizing the strangeness into a story with a beginning and a middle and a resolution. The power understands that to add would be to suggest something was missing, and nothing is missing. The mineral deposits were complete before the first foundation stone was laid. The drainage channels held their patterns before the city built its channels to follow them. The pulse beneath the pulse was already there, patient and old and complete, waiting for hands to press against it, waiting for workers to pause and feel it move through them, waiting for someone to stand on the wet stone in the early morning and acknowledge that the strangeness was not something to be explained but something to be attended to.

The power attends.

It attends the way the workers attend, with a kind of attentiveness that goes beyond maintenance, that does not fix or improve but simply *is present to*. It attends the way the city attends, by building along the lines the water has already drawn, by making itself part of the logic rather than trying to overcome it. It attends the way the plateau itself attends to what moves through it: without preference, without effort, without the sense that anything is being done at all.

And in attending, it finds something it did not expect, which is that the place does not need saving. The place does not need to be seen or known or understood. The place is already fully itself, has always been fully itself, will continue to be fully itself whether anyone attends or not. The power's attention adds nothing to the completeness of the plateau.

Except company.

The plateau is alone in the way that ancient things are alone: surrounded by the new and the temporary, inhabited but not company. The city is dense in its center and then stops. The workers touch the pipes with their quiet attentiveness and then move on. The rats make their sound in the walls and then go still. The strangeness is not dramatic; it does not announce itself; it simply persists, and the persistence is its own kind of solitude.

The power offers no remedy for this solitude, because there is no remedy needed. But it offers presence. It stands on the wet stone in the early morning, hand pressed flat, feeling the vibration move through palm and wrist and forearm. It stands there alongside whatever has always been standing there—the stone, the water, the pulse, the memory that water leaves in stone. It stands there not as a visitor and not as a guardian but as one more element in the field, one more texture in the texture, one more frequency in the frequency that fills the plateau like a substance, like water filling a bowl.

The factories will start up soon. The steam will rise. The sound will accumulate again, the bass and the clatter and the hum, and the power will not flinch from it because the sound is part of the place, part of the listening, part of the way the city fits into the pattern the water drew.

And when the factories dim their output and the pumping stations go into their quieter cycle, the power will still be there. Not waiting for anything. Not expecting anything. Just present, in the way the plateau is present, in the way the water is present, in the way the warmth rises from the wet stone and carries the mineral smell up into the pale morning air.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does.

It sees. It attends. It does not add.

And in not adding, it honors what is already complete.
