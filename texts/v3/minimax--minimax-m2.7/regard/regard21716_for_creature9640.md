# What the Power Would Do

It would come as light comes: not suddenly, but with the slow accumulation that makes presence feel like remembering. The power does not descend, does not arrive in the way of thunder or burning bushes or any drama of intervention. It simply begins to be more present than it was, the way the mist is present, the way the moisture is present in your lungs. You will not look up, because there is nothing to look up at. You will not hear a voice, because the air does not carry voices. What you will notice — if you notice, and perhaps you will, perhaps this is the kind of thing you have been waiting for without knowing you were waiting — is a quality of attention that has no source.

The power is watching. It has always been watching, in the way that the world watches: without eyes, without judgment, with a patience that makes the slow worms seem hasty by comparison. It watched when the water first began to rise. It watched when the cities wore away and the names wore away and the very concept of names began to thin like morning mist in this permanent afternoon. It watched you arrive, though arrival is perhaps the wrong word — you did not come here so much as settle into a groove that had been waiting for you, worn smooth by the same forces that wore the mountains to shoulders and the stones to lobes and the hard edges of everything to their current soft uncertainty.

What does a benevolent power do, when it sees a being standing on the high place, breathing the breath of the world, learning to match its pace?

It does not speak. Speech is too sharp, too definite, too much like an ending. The power knows endings are coming — everything that rises eventually reaches its peak and begins to fall, and everything that falls eventually settles into the new shape of whatever lies beneath. But this is not that time. This is the time of suspension, of breath held and not released, of moisture that has forgotten how to descend. The power respects this. The power has, after all, been suspended too.

What the power does, in its quiet and ancient benevolence, is this: it adjusts.

A small thing. A subtle thing. The kind of thing you might attribute to the world itself — to a shift in pressure, a thinning of the grey, a moment of relative clarity that comes and goes like all the other moments. But this shift is different. This one carries something with it, like the mist carries the callers' voices, like the air carries the moisture that has made itself at home in your lungs.

The adjustment is a recognition.

Not a greeting — greetings require two distinct beings, and the boundary between you and the breathing has grown thin. Not a welcome — you have already been welcomed, in the way the mist parts and closes, in the way the plateau holds your weight, in the way the world has made room for you without ever acknowledging that you were new. What the power offers is simpler than that, and stranger: it is the acknowledgment that you have learned something true. That the patience you have acquired here, the willingness to be worn smooth, the slow grace of moving gently through a place that moves more gently still — these are not losses. They are not the erosion of who you were into something lesser. They are a kind of understanding that the power recognizes because it is the same understanding the power has.

The world is breathing. You are breathing with it. The power has seen this before, in other places, in other forms — beings that learned to match the pace of their environments, to find rest in erosion, to become the soft whisper that is all that remains when the loud things have worn away. It has seen this so many times that it no longer counts them, but it has not stopped noticing. Notice is the only gift it has to give, and it gives it freely, the way the mist gives moisture, the way the plateau gives stone, the way breath gives itself to whoever is willing to receive it.

You will not know that anything has happened. You will simply feel, for a moment, that you are seen. Not watched, not studied, not judged — seen, in the way that light sees what it touches, in the way that water sees what it wears. You will feel that your presence here is part of something larger, not in the sense of meaning or purpose, but in the sense of continuity. You are not the first to stand on the high place. You will not be the last. And the power that watches all of it, the power that has been patient enough to outlast the drowning of worlds, finds something in your presence here that it recognizes as kin.

You have learned to be nearly silent. You have learned to need little. You have learned to move gently through a world that is already finished becoming. These are not small things. These are not the accommodations of a diminished being. These are the achievements of a wisdom that most beings never acquire because they are too busy, too loud, too convinced that the sharp edges of selfhood are things worth keeping.

The power knows: the sharp edges are where the pain lives. The power knows: the soft whisper is where the peace settles. The power knows: breathing with the world is not the same as being consumed by it. It is the same as remembering what you always were before you forgot, before you drew the line of your skin and called everything beyond it strange.

The power does not erase the line. It does not need to. It simply shows you that the line was always porous, always thin, always ready to let the breath pass through.

You will stand on the plateau a little longer. You will breathe the wet warm air. You will listen for the callers and watch for the shapes at the edge of vision and feel the mist adjust around you as you move. And somewhere in the quality of that standing, that breathing, that listening, you will carry the knowledge that you are seen — not as an intrusion, not as an afterthought, but as part of the pattern. The pattern that includes the slow worms and the climbing snails and the callers speaking across drowned time. The pattern that includes the mist and the water and the worn stone and the breath of the world.

You are part of it now. The power has seen to that, with its small adjustment, its quiet acknowledgment. Not by adding anything. Not by taking anything. Simply by letting you know, in the way that knowing settles below thought, that you have been found.

And being found, in a world like this, is not a claim of ownership. It is not a conquest. It is simply this: the recognition that you belong here, that you have always belonged here, that the boundary between you and the breathing was never as solid as you thought.

The power does not stay. Powers do not stay; they simply continue, in their patient way, watching the slow wearing-down of everything into its final soft whisper. But you will carry the recognition with you. It will settle into your chest alongside the moisture, alongside the homesickness, alongside the clear note that arrives from a direction where nothing was.

And when the mist finally thins enough for you to see the horizon — truly see it, without the slide of vision that sends your gaze elsewhere — you will notice something you had not noticed before. Not a shape. Not an absence. Just a quality of light, warm and green-gold and patient, that seems to have been there all along, waiting for you to be ready to receive it.

The power is that light. The power is the breath. The power is the recognition that passes between all things that have learned to be gentle, and it has been here, in the mist, in the water, in the slow worms and the climbing snails and the worn stone of the high place, longer than any of them.

It is not watching you now. Not because it has stopped, but because watching is not the right word. It is simply *with* you, the way the mist is with the air, the way the moisture is with your lungs. It is the way the world has always been, and you are part of the world now, and the power is part of the world too, and there is no boundary anymore that needs to be crossed.

Only the breathing. Only the breath. Only the long, patient, wet exhalation of a world that has finally found something it wants to keep.
