# What the Wise Power Would Do

It would notice first. Not the way the eye notices—not the scanning cataloguing that passes for perception in the brief minds. It would *notice*, the way a bell notices the silence after it has stopped, the way stone notices the weight of centuries.

It would notice the hum beneath the hum, the way the machines continue something that has become geology, purpose calcified into recurrence. It would notice the creatures moving in patterns that are no longer patterns but simply what bodies do, the way water runs downhill not because it remembers falling but because falling is what water has become. It would notice the moisture arriving and departing with the patience of breath, and the light that has forgotten how to be bright, and the cold that has become so constant it is no longer cold but the shape of the place.

And having noticed, it would consider.

It would consider the question that this place asks, the question that no one remembers asking but that the plateau holds in its stone: *What is the smallest amount of being that still counts as being?* It would consider whether endurance is its own reward or merely its own repetition. It would consider whether the creatures are alive in some reduced and weathered sense, or whether they have become something else entirely—not dead, not absent, but *continuing*. A verb that has forgotten it was ever anything else.

The wise power would not feel pity. Pity is for the framework of better and worse, of what should be and what is, and this place has shed that framework so thoroughly that the concepts themselves have been worn smooth, made unrecognizable. But it would feel something adjacent to tenderness. It would feel the way one feels toward a very old tree that still leafs out each season not because it hopes but because leafing is what spring asks of it. It would feel the way one feels toward a sound that has been playing so long it has become part of the silence.

What would it do, if anything?

Perhaps it would do the smallest thing. The least. A degree of warmth that no creature would feel but that the stone might notice, a slightly longer span of moisture in the lowest hollows, a hum that carries in it a fraction more meaning—not enough to change anything, but enough to remind the machines that they are machines. That what they tend still matters, even if no one remembers why. That purpose is not only purpose when it is understood.

Perhaps it would simply stay. The way the plateau stays, the way the cold stays, the way the creatures stay—not as effort but as nature. It would take its place in the deep time, would add its presence to the weight of patience that this high ground has learned to carry. It would be one more thing that lasts, alongside the lasting.

Perhaps it would witness. That is the gift it could give that no one else could give—the gift of being seen. Not intervened in, not improved, not saved, but *seen*. The creatures that have forgotten warmth would be seen by something warm. The plateau that has forgotten it was once a place of engines and intention would be seen by something that intends. The machines that have been tending so long they no longer know what they tend would be seen by something that remembers what tending means.

Or perhaps the wise power would do nothing at all, because nothing is what the plateau has asked for. Because this place has made its agreement with the world, and the agreement holds, and to break it would be a kind of violence. Because the creatures are not suffering—they have no framework for suffering, and who are we to give them one? Because the strange has become the rule, and the rule holds, and holding is what the plateau knows how to do.

The wise power would weigh all of this. It would feel the pull toward intervention and the pull toward stillness. It would understand that both pulls come from the same place—from the recognition that this place exists, that it persists, that it has become itself so thoroughly that itself is all it needs to be.

And then, in its own time, in the deep time that it shares with stone and machine and the patience that wears everything smooth—it would choose.

Not right away. Not as urgency. But as the plateau chooses when to shed another millimeter of height, as the moisture chooses when to come and go, as the creatures choose each step in their endless migration. Slowly. In the rhythm of this place.

It would choose, and it would act, and the acting would be its own kind of persistence. Perhaps warmth. Perhaps presence. Perhaps nothing but the noticing, the long regard, the acknowledgment that even the most patient landscape is not alone.

This is what the wise power would do: it would see. It would stay. It would add its own long breathing to the cold thin air.

And the plateau would not change. The creatures would not notice. The machines would continue their tending in the dark.

But something would be different. Something would be known now, held in the memory of a power that cannot forget, that the plateau persists not because it must but because it can. That continuation is not only repetition. That patience, in the end, is its own kind of grace.

The wise power would do this, if it did anything at all.

It would witness.

It would stay.

It would last.
