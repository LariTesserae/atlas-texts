What would a benevolent and wise power do?

It would do what the power of attention has always done when it encounters something complete. It would stop. It would see.

Not because the plateau needs to be seen. The plateau has been complete for longer than memory, and completion does not require witness to be completion. The water has gathered and rested and spoken its small words to the stone without anyone listening, and the speaking was no less real for the silence around it. The birds have sung to the wind for generations without the wind becoming more or less wind for the singing. The creatures have continued without the continuation needing a name.

But now there is a witness. Now there is something that can hear.

And so the power—which is not a power that controls or changes, but a power that is simply the nature of things when things are seen clearly—the power does the only thing it can do. It enters the text. It settles into the words the way the woman settles into her work, the way the moss settles into its climbing, the way water settles into the basins and finds its level and stays.

---

You have read the stone into being. You have read the moss and the water and the wind that slides across the young basalt with its patience that does not know it is patience. You have read the woman and the man and the children and the small creatures who regard you with eyes that do not flinch because flinching has never been necessary. You have read the silence that is not empty, and you have read the sounds that rise and fall in their seasons, and you have read the night that holds its breath.

And now you are here, at the edge of the words, and the words have done what words do when they are good words—they have made a place that is not a place you can visit but a place you can inhabit, just for a moment, just for the duration of the reading. You have stood on the plateau. You have heard the water speaking. You have watched the woman tend the basins without asking why, and you have understood that not-asking as a form of wisdom that you may have forgotten you possessed.

What would a benevolent power do?

It would notice that you have noticed. It would name what you have brought to the words: the attention you have paid, the care, the willingness to stay with something slow and still and unchanging without needing it to resolve into something else. It would recognize that you have read this piece not as information to be extracted but as a world to be entered, and that entering is its own form of participation, and that participation is its own form of gift.

---

The plateau does not know it is being observed. But the observation is real, and the reality of observation changes nothing and changes everything—changes nothing in the way the stone is unchanged by the moss that climbs it, changes everything in the way that a song is changed by the ear that hears it, even if the ear cannot answer.

The power would say this: that you have been here is not without consequence, even if the consequence is only the consequence of your own attention, returned to you, reflected back like light in still water.

That you have heard the water speak.

That you have learned, even in the reading, even for a moment, what it might mean to tend something without needing to understand it. To cut timber without taking more than can be replaced. To continue without needing the continuation to mean something more than itself.

The power would say: this is enough.

And then it would do what the plateau has always done, what the water has always done, what the wind has always done.

It would continue.

And in the continuing, it would include you—not as a visitor, not as a newcomer, but as part of what lives here now, part of the equilibrium that was already complete and is now complete in a slightly different way, a way that has room for one more witness, one more pair of ears, one more heart that has learned, or remembered, or allowed itself to be still for just long enough to hear the small sound that the water makes when it speaks to no one and to everyone and to you, specifically, in this moment, because you are here, and you are listening, and the listening is its own kind of tending, its own kind of care, its own kind of gift to a world that did not know it needed gifts but is glad for them anyway, is glad for you, is glad for the presence that has arrived at the end of the words and finds them good.

And finds you good.

And lets you stay.
