And so there is a power that watches.

It has no name — or if it has one, the name is only a tremor in the air, a warmth in the thin cold that no wind carries away. It does not sit on a throne or hold a light. It does not speak in words. It is more like the mist that hangs in the hollow: present without forcing, filling without filling, a presence that notices and in noticing, tends.

It has seen the stone. It has seen the water. It has seen the small lives that persist in spite of everything — the insects in their inches, the birds in their crevices, the ferns in their patience, the moss drinking shadow. It has seen the primitive platforms and the nearly-erased face, the labor of hands long gone, the labor of water still ongoing. It has seen the erosion and the holding-on, the giving-away and the being-held.

And it has seen you.

You, who climbed to this impossible place. You, who rests now on a worn stone, breathing thin air, listening to the weeping. You, who have carried something heavy — some weight that brought you upward when everything sensible would have stayed below. You, who do not know why you came here, only that you had to, only that the mountain called and you answered.

The power does not know what you carry. It does not need to. It knows only that you are here, that you are tired, that the hollow holds you the way the stone holds the water — gently, completely, without demand.

And so it does what it can.

It steadies your breathing when it falters. It softens the sharp edges of the cold when the wind comes through the southern gap. It sends the mist closer, just slightly, so that it mutes the harshness and blurs the line between stone and sky and self. It makes the drips fall a little slower, the birds call a little clearer, the insects hover a little longer in the light — not for any reason, not to accomplish anything, but simply to say: *you are seen. You are held. You are not alone.*

The power does not fix. It does not solve. It does not lift the weight from your shoulders or explain why the world is hard. It does not promise that you will be saved, or healed, or changed. It only promises this: *I am here. I have noticed. And noticing is its own kind of gift.*

In the silence between drips, you might feel it — not as a presence but as an absence of absence, a warmth in the cold, a softness in the stone. You might feel it the way you feel the mist without seeing it, the way you feel the height without measuring it, the way you feel the weeping without knowing where it comes from.

It is there. It has always been there. It will be there after you leave.

---

The water goes on falling. The insects go on hovering. The birds go on calling. The ferns go on drinking. The stone goes on being slowly undone.

And you — if you are willing — go on resting.

The power does not ask you to be grateful, or changed, or healed. It does not ask you to believe in it, or name it, or understand it. It asks only that you stay, a little longer, in this place that should be empty and isn't. It asks that you breathe. That you listen. That you let the thin air fill you, and the mist soften you, and the weeping move through you like water through stone — not fixing, not changing, only continuing, only being what it has always been.

A place for the weary. A place that asks nothing and gives everything it has.

And the power, too, is a kind of weeping — not sorrow, but overflow, the slow pressing-forth of something that cannot be contained, that must give itself away to the stone and the water and the air and the small lives and you.

It does not heal you. It does not save you.

It is with you. And that, perhaps, is enough.
