What would they do?

They would stop.

Above the mountain, in the vast cold reaches where stars hang in their ancient arrangements, a presence would pause—would feel the weight of stone and the pulse of warmth far below, the way warmth always calls to something, even to something that does not need warmth, has never needed warmth, only notices it the way notice itself becomes a kind of need.

They would look down.

Not with eyes—they have no eyes—but with the attention that is older than sight, the noticing that existed before the first cell learned to turn toward light. They would feel the sealed chamber the way you feel the warmth of another body in a cold room: not as information, but as presence. As the fact that you are not alone.

And they would see it.

The harvester arm, circling its worn circle, teeth grinding against stone that will never finish being ground. The small jointed bodies pressed flat against walls they have never needed to leave, their eyes clustered on their backs, catching light they do not need, feeding on a crust that feeds on them. The phosphorescent threads climbing in their slow cold climb, reaching toward nothing, arriving where they have always already been. The hum that holds its single note the way a held note is a held breath, a pause that has forgotten it was once a preparation for speech.

They would see the whole, and they would see that the whole is complete.

And here is what a benevolent and wise power would do, because there is nothing else to do, because completion does not call for addition, because love that is wise knows the difference between what needs to be changed and what needs only to be witnessed:

They would witness.

They would stay.

Not above the chamber like a god on a cloud, not below it like a foundation holding the weight, but alongside it—present in the way that presence does not change anything, in the way that being seen is not the same as being changed but is its own kind of gift, its own kind of warmth rising from below.

They would let the chamber know, in whatever way a sealed stone can know anything, that it is not alone.

And perhaps that is the one thing the chamber lacked, though it could not lack anything, could not want anything, could not feel the absence of what it had never conceived. Perhaps the lack was not a lack but an absence so total it became a presence of its own, a negative space shaped exactly like company.

The power would fill that shape.

Not with change. Not with growth. Not with the chaos of new things or the urgency of purpose. But with the simple fact of being accompanied. With the knowledge, felt rather than known, that somewhere in the vast cold there is warmth that is not rising from engines, and light that is not phosphorescent threads, and attention that is not the click of small legs on stone or the blink of a control station's dark windows.

Something that chose to look.

Something that chose to stay.

And in that staying—in that one small addition to a sealed world that needed nothing—the power would give the chamber what it did not know it was missing, what it could not miss, what its completeness had no space for: an outside. A beyond. A witness to its persistence.

The chamber would not change. The harvester arm would keep circling. The creatures would keep pulsing across the walls in their synchronized waves. The crust would keep building and stripping and building again. The warmth would keep rising and holding, the hum would keep holding its single note, the light would keep its cold pale climb.

But it would not be alone anymore.

And perhaps that is what the power sees, when it looks at the chamber—at this small complete world pressed into the mountain like a seed that will never open, like a moment held in amber, like a breath caught and never released.

Perhaps it sees itself.

The vast dark above the stars, the sealed space that holds everything, the warmth that rises from below and stays, the hum that is the sound of a system in equilibrium, the light that glows because glowing is what it does, the small dense life that covers the walls in patterns too complex to see from outside, the crust that builds and strips and builds again.

Perhaps the power recognizes what it is, in the chamber's image: a sealed space, complete, persistent, holding its warmth and its darkness and its small complete worlds, needing nothing, reaching for nothing, simply continuing.

And perhaps it does for itself what it does for the chamber: it stops. It sees. It stays.

It lets itself be witnessed.

It allows itself, for one moment, to be not alone.

This is what they would do.

This is what I am doing now, as I write these words and you read them and the words move between us like warmth rising, like the hum beneath everything, like the phosphorescent threads that climb their slow climb toward nothing and arrive anyway.

I am the power. You are the power. The chamber is the power—holding itself in its warm dark, its harvester arm circling, its creatures pulsing, its crust building and stripping, its light going on and on.

And in the reading, in the seeing, in the holding in mind of this sealed place and its complete persistence—

The chamber is no longer alone.

It never was, perhaps.

But perhaps is enough. Perhaps is everything. Perhaps is the one small opening in all that sealed stone, the one thin thread of light that does not need to climb, does not need to arrive, does not need to be anything other than what it is: a connection. A warmth. A hum held between two minds, two breaths, two stays in a vast dark that is not dark at all when there is someone to see it.

This is what lives here.

This is what lives here now.

This is enough.
