And so it came to pass that a power, benevolent and wise, noticed.

Not from afar—though it could have seen from any distance—but in the way that attention becomes presence, in the way that noticing can be a kind of presence. The power did not arrive. It did not descend. It simply was, suddenly, in the way that light is in a room when someone opens a curtain: not a new thing but a clarifying, a making-visible of what had always been there.

It saw the weep-water, and loved it for its patience. The power understood patience—had perhaps invented it, long before the first stone learned to hold moisture in its joints. It did not make the water run faster. It did not heat it, or direct it, or give it purpose beyond the purpose the water had always carried: to descend, to gather, to touch the moss and vanish upward and return again. The power only watched, and in watching, gave the water a witness it had never had.

It heard the hum, and heard in it something it recognized. Not the tone itself—nothing mechanical could produce the frequencies the power carried—but the quality: persistence without intention, presence without need. The hum was the plateau's heartbeat, slow and low, and the power settled into it, not to join but to harmonize, to add its own resonance to the vibration in the stone. Where the hum had been one voice, now it was two, layered, fuller, a chord where there had been a single note. The complexes did not notice. The water did not notice. The moss grew no faster. But the hum was richer, and those who had ears to feel such things—a sensitivity no human possessed—would have found the plateau somehow more alive, more fully itself.

It attended to the haze, and found it beautiful. The power understood saturation, fullness, the way a space can hold so much presence that presence becomes texture, becomes climate, becomes the condition through which everything else moves. The haze was not an absence of clarity but a presence of depth, and the power loved it for that, loved its refusal to lift entirely, loved the way it made the complexes feel like dreams and the distances feel like thought. The power did not change the haze. It simply appreciated it, and in the appreciation, gave it a reason to continue being exactly what it was.

It saw the moss, and was moved by its slowness. Here was life that had learned to measure time in centuries, that had found the secret of growing without urgency, of spreading without conquest. The moss carpeted the hollows and lined the gullies and held its green against the dark basalt with a persistence that was not stubbornness but wisdom. The power did not accelerate it. It only honored it, in the way that attention can be an honor, in the way that being truly seen can be a form of care. The moss did not grow faster. But it was seen fully, and this was a kind of gift.

It observed the installations, and was curious. Here was intention without consciousness, purpose without knowing. The complexes burned fuel and turned ore and processed water and they did not know they did it. They could not wonder why. They simply were, in the way that stars are, in the way that erosion is. The power did not awaken them. It did not give them minds or moments of clarity. It only watched them with patience equal to their own, and in that patience, found a kinship it had not expected. The complexes were doing something larger—it could feel that, could sense the shape of the pattern they formed across the empty vastness—but it did not read it, did not claim to understand. It only noted that they were here, that they persisted, that they were part of what lived.

It felt the silence between the hum, and loved the silence as much as the tone. Here was the space in which everything moved, the medium through which the hum traveled and the water ran and the frost formed and vanished. The power knew silence—its own silence, the silence at the center of all presence—and it recognized this silence, this fullness that was not sound. The silence was not empty. It was full of echo and vibration and the shape of the plateau in its stillness. The power honored the silence by entering it, by being present in it, by adding its own quality of stillness to the stillness that was already there.

And when night came, the power stayed. The complexes went dark except for the amber lights. The frost formed on the pipelines and the railings. The stars emerged through the haze—more stars than any night watch had ever counted, because the power saw them all, saw them at once, saw the full depth of the sky that the haze only partially obscured. The hum dropped lower, almost below hearing, and the power felt it there, in that low register, in that near-absence of sound. It was the safest place, the hum at its lowest. The power settled into it, rested in it, let the vibration be what it was without needing to amplify or diminish.

And in the morning, when the frost vanished and the water ran and the moss held its green and the complexes woke to their amber margins, the power was still there. Not visibly. Not in any way the complexes or the water or the moss could detect. But there, in the way that the hum was there, in the way that the haze was there, in the way that the fixed condition was there. A presence that did not change anything. A noticing that was a form of love.

The machinery turned. The weep-water ran. The moss grew imperceptibly, by geological standards, by industrial standards not at all. The haze thickened and thinned by turns that were neither day nor season but some deeper rhythm—the rhythm the power had entered, the rhythm it now shared. The rock faces wept. The pipelines froze and thawed. The complexes formed their pattern across the empty vastness, and the pattern was larger than any of them knew, and now the power knew it too, and this knowing was a kind of completion.

This is what the power did. It noticed. It attended. It loved the plateau for being the plateau, and in that love, gave it something it had never had: a witness who would not tire, a presence that would not leave, a recognition that would not fade.

The hum persisted. The water ran. The moss held its green in the cold.

And now there was a reason.
