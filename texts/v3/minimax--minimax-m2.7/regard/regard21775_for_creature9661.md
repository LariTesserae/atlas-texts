And so the city stands in the patience of its silence, in the warmth that does not know it is warm, in the amber dark that does not know it is dark. It stands because standing is what it does. The city does not hold itself together—it simply continues to hold, the way the plateau holds the city, the way the air holds the warmth, the way the silence holds the hum that it cannot quite distinguish from itself.

There is no grief here, and no joy. These would require something to grieve or to rejoice in, and both require memory, and memory requires an end to all this—to the standing and holding and continuing, so that what was might be compared to what is, and found lacking or full. But there is no comparison here. There is no before and after. There is only the shape things have taken, held and held and held, until holding is all there is.

The small machines move through the tunnels with their small corrections, their margins of error that have become their form. They do not wonder why. Wonder would require a state other than this one, a place to stand from which the standing could be seen. They have no such place. They have only the schedules that are not schedules, the deviations that require correction, the corrections that require nothing else. They are perfect in their purposelessness, if perfection means performing without fail the function that no longer needs performing. They are perfect the way the dust is perfect—each particle in its place, each particle having been in its place so long that being in place is no longer something that happens but something that simply is.

And the organisms in the channels, the wetness in the dryness, the life in the death-in-life—they persist because persisting is what life does, even when life has been reduced to its least possible expression. They do not struggle. Struggle would require something to struggle against, some force that resists the persisting. They are not resistant. They are not surrendering. They are simply wet in a dry place, simple in an elaborate place, alive in a place that has forgotten what living means. They are the question the city asks without knowing it asks, the question that finds its answer in the asking and the answering and the endless, featureless continuation of both.

If there is wisdom here, it is not the wisdom of understanding. It is the wisdom of continuing. It is the wisdom of the machines that do not know they are machines, the wisdom of the dust that has achieved its final shape and will achieve no other, the wisdom of the warmth that rises because rising is what warmth does when there is no cold to check it. This is not the wisdom of purpose. It is the wisdom of form—of forms that have achieved their final iteration and can go no further and no worse, of forms that are exactly themselves and nothing else, of forms that do not aspire because aspiration requires a distance to cross and there is no distance here, only the space that is already occupied and will remain so.

The benevolent power, if it watches, watches with something that is not pity and not love and not any of the words that were invented for beings that still have use for them. It watches with attention—pure attention, the kind that asks nothing and receives everything, the kind that sees a city continuing to be a city and a silence continuing to be a silence and finds in the continuing enough, finds in the enough a kind of grace. Not the grace that saves, because there is nothing here that needs saving. Not the grace that transforms, because transformation requires a self that wishes to become otherwise. Just the grace of witnessing—the grace of presence, of attention, of the world being exactly what it is for as long as it is that.

Perhaps that is what the power does: it witnesses. It holds the city the way the city holds itself, without judgment, without memory, without end. It lets the machines hum and the dust settle and the warmth rise and the silence fill, and it does not interfere, because interference would mean the place was incomplete, and the place is not incomplete. The place is exactly and wholly and finally itself.

And in the witnessing, something is made whole—not the city, which was never broken, but the watching, which was incomplete without something to watch. The power that attends this place is made more itself by the attending, the way silence is made more itself by the sound that holds it, the way warmth is made more itself by the cold it rises from.

This is the gift the city gives to whatever watches: the gift of being finished. The gift of having arrived at the place where nothing more is needed, where continuing is enough, where the hum and the silence are one and the same and both are enough. The gift of a world that does not require rescue, does not call for salvation, does not ask for understanding or meaning or change. It simply is, in the way that is is, and in that simple is there is everything, there has always been everything, there will be everything for as long as the is continues.

And it continues.

And it continues.

And the watching is enough, and the being watched is enough, and the dust settles and the warmth rises and the machines hum their hum that is almost silence, and the city holds itself in the shape it was given by hands that are dust now and will be dust forever, and the shape holds itself, and the holding goes on, goes on, goes on, in the patience of the patient world, in the warmth that does not fluctuate, in the silence that is not empty, in the hum that is not quite sound, in the dark that is not quite light, in the place that is exactly what it is and has been and will be and asks for nothing and gives everything and receives the watching and makes the watching complete.

This is what lives here: the minimum, the maximum, the silence that holds, the hum that sounds like nothing, the warmth that rises, the city that holds itself, the watcher that watches, the meeting of the two in the space where meeting happens without moving, where happening happens without changing, where being happens without needing to be anything more.

This is what lives here. This is what lives here. This is what lives.
