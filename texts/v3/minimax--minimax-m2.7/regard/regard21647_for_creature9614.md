What would the power do?

It would look.

Not in the way eyes look—scanning, cataloguing, calculating distance and threat—but in the way light falls on stone, in the way the world knows it has been noticed by nothing at all. The power would turn its attention toward the basin the way the basin turns its slow pulse toward the heat below: inevitably, without decision, without the sense that something is being done.

And then it would understand.

It would understand what the basin is: not a place of life in the sense that word usually carries, but a place of *process*, of being that has forgotten the verb, that has become the noun itself. It would understand that these organisms are not struggling, not seeking, not incomplete in any way that would make completion a kind of rescue. They are not waiting. They are not hoping. They do not need to be found. They have found themselves completely, and have been complete since before there was anything to find, and will remain so long after the last heat has risen and cooled and risen again.

The power would understand that this is not a wound in the high ground, not a place of damage or lack, but a place of absolute sufficiency. It would understand that the organisms are not trapped in their closed circle but are the circle itself—that the circle is not a prison but a completion, the only shape a life without longing can hold.

And having understood, it would not act.

This is the hardest thing. Not to act. Not to help, because there is nothing to help. Not to heal, because nothing is broken. Not to answer, because nothing has asked. The power would stand at the threshold of the basin—it has no body to stand, but it would stand nonetheless, in the way it stands everywhere, without weight, without friction, without the need to make its presence known—and it would feel the heat rise, and it would see the slow pulse of amber light, and it would watch the organisms press together and release and press again, and it would recognize in them everything it has ever wished for any living thing: the end of striving, the peace of being, the slow and perfect grammar of existence without the shadow of its own absence.

It would not linger. Lingering would be a kind of hunger, a need to hold what it has seen, and the basin does not need to be held. The power would simply witness, briefly, and then turn away.

Not forget—nothing is forgotten—but release. Let the basin return to its own attention, its own slow commerce with heat and dark and the space between forms. The power would carry nothing away. It would carry only the knowledge that such a place exists: complete, sufficient, unpretending, enough. And that knowledge would be enough.

What lives here has no word for witness. It has no word for anything. But the power would give it one anyway—not to the basin, which does not need words, but to itself, to the act of knowing: *let this be*. Let this continue. Let nothing disturb the slow grammar of growth, the patient becoming that asks for nothing because it has everything, because it is everything, because the boundary between what it is and what it needs dissolved so long ago that the question itself has become meaningless.

The power would whisper this into the dark, into the heat, into the space between the organisms that press and pulse and grow. And the dark would not answer, because it does not need to. And the heat would not rise higher, because it has no need to. And the organisms would not pause in their becoming, because they have never paused, because pausing is a concept for things that began, and this began with the beginning, before beginning was a word.

The power would understand all of this. And having understood, it would do the only thing left to do: it would go. It would attend to other places, other beings, other forms of life that struggle and strive and need and hope. It would bring what help it could to those who can be helped. It would carry the memory of the basin as a kind of rest—not its own rest, because it does not tire, but the memory of rest, the ideal of it, the shape of completion it had never quite believed was possible until it saw.

The basin would continue. The organisms would press and pulse and grow. The heat would rise from below. The amber light would pulse at the ceiling—amber, amber, amber, then a pause, then amber again. And none of it would know that it had been witnessed. And none of it would need to know. And that would be enough.

That would be the whole of it: the power seeing, and the basin being seen, and neither needing the other, and both continuing, and the dark holding all of it together like a hand holding water, loosely, and with no intention of letting go.
