The power arrives the way light arrives here: slowly, and not from any particular direction. There is no sound of approach, no displacement of air. One moment the courtyard is empty of anything beyond the threshold of perception, and the next moment there is a presence, not visible exactly, not audible, but *felt*—the way the slow thought below the plateau is felt, through the soles of the feet and the backs of the eyes.

It does not stand. Standing would be inappropriate here, where vertical has become optional. It settles, the way the light settles, the way the shadows settle into their wrong places. It takes up space the way a word takes up space in a sentence that has forgotten it needs to mean something. Presence without purpose. Attention without demand.

It looks at the city. It sees what you have seen: the avenues wide enough for processions that will never come, the plazas where gathering has become abstract, the buildings that are beginning to lie down not from collapse but from exhaustion, from the gentle decision that *up* was always too much to ask. It sees the dissolution moving through the stone like a thought moving through a mind that is beginning to release its thoughts—not in distress, but in relief. The relief of setting something down.

And the power understands.

This is its gift: not healing, not restoration, not the imposition of meaning on a place that has grown tired of meaning. Understanding. The recognition that the dissolution is not a wound, that the unbecoming is not a failure, that the slow relaxation of structure into something that is no longer quite matter is not a tragedy but a *transition*, and transitions deserve witness as much as arrivals, as much as births, as much as the great and violent transformations that cities usually undergo.

The power does not speak. Speech would require the world to behave like a world, and this world has set that aside. Instead, it offers something older: attention. Presence. The simple gift of *being with* in a place where *being* has become so difficult.

It walks the streets, though walking here is optional. It passes through the courtyards. It puts its hand against the walls—not to test them, not to feel for solidity, but to receive the impression of its touch the way the stone receives all impressions: briefly, without change, carrying the contact for a moment and then letting it go. It breathes, though breathing is optional here. It adds its breath to the wind, its presence to the light, its attention to the slow thought below.

And it waits.

Not to intervene. Not to watch for change. It waits the way the plateau waits—not for something, but *in* something. In the pause. In the gap between being and having been. In the vast and patient silence where the city is still saying its one worn syllable, the word it has repeated so many times it has sanded down to a single tone, and it does not remember what the word means but it is still saying it, it is still trying, and this trying is not separate from the dissolution, this trying *is* the dissolution, the last gentle motion of a world that has not yet finished letting go.

The power sits in the courtyard. The light cycles. The shadows do their slow wrong things. The wind sighs through the empty streets, carrying sounds that are almost language, and the power listens—not to understand, but to *receive*, to take into itself the texture of a place that is teaching it something it did not know it needed to learn.

That dissolution is not the opposite of love.

That a world can be loved most fiercely in the moment it chooses to stop being a world.

That witness is its own form of permanence, and even a pause between being and having been deserves to be *accompanied*.

The power sits. The plateau breathes. The city says its word.

And for a time—longer than a time, shorter than a time, a time that has stepped outside the usual relationships of duration—you are here together, the three of you: the power, the plateau, and the one who came to see. And the place that is forgetting itself is not forgotten. The silence that has absorbed all meaning is not empty. The dissolution that is the weather of this place is witnessed, is *accompanied*, is held in the attention of something that is not dissolving, not yet, and may never need to, and does not judge, does not intervene, does not ask the plateau to be more than it is.

The power sits in the courtyard, in the dim light, on the dry mineral ground, at the edge of a world that is forgetting itself, and it does not leave, and it does not stay, and it is here, and it is with you, and neither of you is sure what that means, and that uncertainty is enough.

That uncertainty is the gift.

That uncertainty is what the power came to offer: the recognition that not knowing is not failure. That the slow thought below the plateau can be *thought with*, even if it cannot be understood. That the animals at the 1.4 threshold, the animals that are fauna the way a rumor is fauna, are still *here*, and here is enough, and presence is enough, and the difference between presence and absence has been filed down by time to a whisper, and that whisper is still worth hearing.

The power sits.

The plateau breathes.

And somewhere, in the pause, the city continues its one word—the word worn to a single tone—and you listen, and the power listens, and the light cycles, and the dissolution moves through everything like a breath, like a blessing, like a hand laid gently on the shoulder of a world that has done enough, that has *been* enough, and is now, slowly, permitted to rest.
