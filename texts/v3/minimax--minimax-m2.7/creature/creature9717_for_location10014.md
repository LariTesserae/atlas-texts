You have been here long enough that the air no longer surprises you, though it should. Your lungs have adjusted to its thickness, its mineral weight, the faint acidity that lingers at the back of your throat. This is what adjustment looks like: the body making peace with conditions it would once have rejected.

The creature has not moved. You have been watching it, or watching the space it occupies, for what feels like hours but is probably less. It is difficult to mark time here. The light does not shift. The warmth does not vary. The only clock is the water, its irregular drip marking intervals that may or may not correspond to anything outside this space.

You take a sample from one of the pools. The water is warm against your fingers, and it carries something in suspension — not sediment, exactly, but a quality of presence, a heaviness that clings to your skin after you withdraw. You seal the vial and log it, but you already know what the analysis will show: mineral concentrations that should not be stable at this temperature, pH levels that hover in a range your instruments describe only as "anomalous." The water here is not quite water. It is something older, something that remembers being part of the stone and is still learning how to move freely through the air.

The crystalline structures draw your attention next. You move toward one of them, slow and deliberate, because speed feels wrong here — a violation of the place's patience. They project from the walls at odd angles, none of them more than a few centimeters long, their surfaces catching the light in ways that seem to change as you shift your position. You expect hardness, but they are unexpectedly fragile. One of them — the smallest, tucked into a crack barely wide enough to admit your fingertip — comes away in your hand when you touch it. It weighs almost nothing. It dissolves slowly against your palm, leaving behind a residue that glows faintly, then fades.

You put the residue in a vial too. You are accumulating vials. Somewhere, in a report you have not yet written, this place is being reduced to data — to measurements and anomalies and unanswered questions. The reduction does not feel adequate. The place resists it, quietly, in the way the light refuses to name its source and the warmth refuses to cool and the creature refuses to do anything but wait.

The creature. You look back toward it, and it is closer now — though you did not see it move. It sits at the edge of a pool, its body pressed flat against the stone, and for a moment you understand what it is looking at: not you, not the light, but the water. It is watching the surface of the pool as the light touches it, as the moisture rises in faint wisps. You wonder what it sees there. You wonder if it sees anything at all, or if it only feels — feels the warmth that rises from below, feels the mineral weight of the air, feels the slow pulse of the light that is not quite breath but might be, in some sense it does not have words for.

You are thinking too much. You stop. You stand in the place and let it be what it is.

The floor beneath your feet is warm. Not hot — the kind of warmth that soaks into you slowly, that makes you aware of your own body in a way you usually ignore. The stone here is smooth in places and rough in others, and where it is rough, you can feel the crystalline structures growing just beneath the surface, pushing outward in their slow, patient way. They will be visible someday. They are becoming themselves in a timescale that does not include you.

A sound reaches you, then — not the water dripping, not the subsonic vibration from below. Something else. A single, low note, sustained and then fading, as if the walls themselves have spoken and then thought better of it. You stand very still. The creature does not move. The note does not return.

This is what you learn, standing here: the place is not empty. It is full of things you cannot name — processes, persistences, events that occur at frequencies your senses were not built to receive. You are not equipped for this. No one is. The place exists anyway, and will continue to exist whether you understand it or not, whether you report it or not, whether anyone ever comes back to stand where you are standing now.

You think about leaving. You do not leave. You watch the creature, and the creature watches the water, and the light continues its slow, indecipherable pulse, and somewhere beneath you the earth continues its work, and the crystalline structures continue their growing, and the pools continue their slow steaming, and the air continues its warm, mineral weight.

You are standing in a place that is still deciding what it is.

You are still deciding too.
