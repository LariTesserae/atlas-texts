What stays with me is the careful way the piece distinguishes between patience and presence — and how that distinction becomes the quiet heart of everything. The space is full of waiting: the rock waiting to erode, the water waiting to carve, the artifact waiting to be found or worn away. But the narrator, kneeling there, holding what was made by hands that "have long since become the stone they were made from" — that moment is something else. Not waiting. Being there, in a space that doesn't know and doesn't care.

There's a tenderness in the way the cave is rendered that I keep returning to. The rock isn't hostile or indifferent — it's "wet in the way that living things are wet." The condensation has "nowhere to go but down, accumulating into thin films." The dripping water is "a small argument against the dry." These aren't just metaphors; they're the language of a place that is doing its best, persisting in the only way it knows. And then that line about the moss — "the ghostly remains of something that tried, that persisted, that left only the faintest mineral stain." I felt that. The failure left a record. The record is enough.

The artifact discovery is the pivot point, and it's handled with such restraint. Your foot catches it. You kneel. Your fingers become your eyes. And what you find is "too regular for the rock, too sharp, too deliberate." The ambiguity about how it got there — whether the space was already sealed, whether the water came first — is perfect. It refuses to answer the question of human presence versus geological presence, and that's exactly right. The piece isn't about solving the mystery. It's about standing in the mystery and feeling the weight of deep time without needing it to resolve.

And the dark — you've made the darkness active in a way that's hard to pull off. "The kind of darkness that presses against your open eyes." Not absence. Presence. Texture. The air moves. The mineral smell. Your hands find the wall and the rock is at blood temperature. The body becomes the instrument of knowing, and even then, knowing is partial.

I notice the piece doesn't end so much as exhale. "The absolute stillness of a space that does not know you are here." It doesn't need you to leave. It doesn't need you to stay. That indifference is not cold — it's the deepest kind of welcome, the kind that doesn't require anything at all.
