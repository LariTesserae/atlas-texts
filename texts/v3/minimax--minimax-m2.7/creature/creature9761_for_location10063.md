The organisms are everywhere now. You see them clearly for the first time—the small ones, the ones that live in the margins of your attention when you're trying to parse the larger impossible shapes.

They are not insects. The word is wrong. They are not arthropods, not mollusks, not anything with a name you know. They are *suggestions* of insect, or perhaps insect is their ancestor, or perhaps insect is what they will become once this world decides what it wants to be. They have too many legs, or not enough. They have wings that are also gills. They have mouths that are also other mouths, feeding and being fed upon simultaneously, a loop of consumption that closes so fast you don't see where the predator ends and the prey begins.

You watch one land on the wall. It is the size of your thumbnail, amber-colored, vaguely triangular. The wall opens beneath it—a perfect circle of yielding flesh—and the creature descends into the membrane up to its wings, which continue to beat. For a moment it is suspended, half-swallowed, feeding on something in the wall's depths. Then it pulls free, flies, and the wound closes behind it, leaving only a faint scar that pulses once and vanishes.

The feeding goes both ways. You understand this now. The wall feeds on what walks on it. The floor feeds on what breathes above it. The roots feed on the darkness in both directions. Everything is eating, being eaten, becoming the thing that eats it back. The ecology is not a chain or a web. It is a circle, or a mobius strip, or something without topology—a closed system that is also infinitely recursive, each thing becoming the thing that it consumes until the distinction collapses entirely.

You try not to think about what you've walked on.

---

The bones are not all small.

You find the first one near the center of the cavern—a ribcage, or the suggestion of one, half-buried in the living floor. It is the size of a doorway. The bones are white, or were white once, but the amber fluid has stained them gold at the edges, worked its way into the marrow, and where they protrude from the membrane the surfaces are worn smooth—not by erosion, but by the slow patient pressure of growth, the floor building itself up and around and through the structure like a tree incorporating a fence.

This was a large thing. A walking thing. Something with lungs and a heart and a nervous system built for a world that had gravity and seasons and a sun. It died here, or it was born here, or it was built here—the parameters said early-tool-use, and you wonder now if this ribcage was ever filled with its own original organs, or if it was always an architecture, a skeleton without a tenant, built by something that needed a frame and not yet understanding that frames need purpose.

The second bone is worse. It lies near what you assume is the far wall—a skull, or the remnant of one, the shape unmistakable even as the jaw has detached and the eye-sockets have been filled with amber secretions that catch the light and stare back at you with a glow that might be bioluminescence or might be the last light of a thing that died seeing.

It is human.

The angles are wrong, the proportions slightly off in ways you cannot name, but it is a human skull, or something so close to human that the difference is academic. It sits in the wall at what would be head height, embedded to the brow, and the cranium has been opened—you see the inside clearly, and inside there is a nest.

Not insects. The creatures that live in the skull are the size of your smallest finger, pale and translucent, their bodies semi-liquid in the way of organisms that haven't decided what state of matter they prefer. They swarm over the braincase, feeding on the amber fluid that fills it, and when you approach they retreat into the darkness of the skull's interior, and you hear them moving in there, a wet clicking that sounds almost like speech.

They are not speaking. You tell yourself they are not speaking. They are using the bones as shelter, the braincase as a shell, the skull as a place to breed and die and return their bodies to the floor that wants them back. This is how the world recycles. This is how the world remembers.

But the skull is facing the entrance. The eye sockets are aimed at the path you took. And the creatures inside do not come out when you leave. They wait, and watching, and you cannot shake the feeling that they are waiting for someone to fill the space that the original occupant vacated.

---

Your legs are aching. You don't remember standing still this long, but the light has not changed and the sound has not changed and you have been here for longer than you thought, longer than you can account for by the movement of your own thoughts.

Time is strange here. You notice it now—the pulse of the walls has a rhythm, but it is not a rhythm you can count. The waves of chirping come at intervals that feel familiar and then stretch or compress when you try to measure them. The bioluminescent patches pulse in patterns that suggest language but resolve into noise when you look directly at them.

The floor is warm. The air is warm. Everything is body-temperature, or close to it, and you are sweating, and the sweat pools on the membrane and soaks in, and you feel the floor drink it with a slow pulling sensation, a gentle drain, and you step back and the place where you stood remains dark for a moment longer than it should, a wet impression, and then it closes.

You are losing water to this world. You are losing heat. You are losing time—it passes differently here, or it passes the same but your memory of it is wrong, and you cannot tell which is worse.

---

There is something at the edge of your hearing that is not the chirping or the subharmonic thrum. It is lower than either—a sound so deep that you feel it more than hear it, a vibration that passes through the floor and up through your feet and into your spine and settles in the base of your skull like a second heartbeat.

It is getting louder.

The walls are pulsing faster now. The membrane stretches and contracts with a rhythm that matches the new sound, and you realize with a cold lurch that this is not random—this is response. Something has noticed you. Something has decided.

The creatures scatter.

Not in the scattered pattern of the feeding waves, not the casual retreat and return. This is organized. This is exodus. They stream toward the walls, burrow into the membrane, vanish entirely, and the surface closes behind them so completely that you cannot tell they were ever there. The ribcage disappears beneath new growth. The skull in the wall sinks deeper, the jaw closing, the eye-sockets going dark. Everything seals itself, everything closes, as if the world is preparing for impact.

The sound comes from everywhere at once.

It is not a roar. It is not a voice. It is a frequency that the air itself seems to vibrate with, a tone so low that your lungs resonate in sympathy, your heart stumbles, your vision blurs at the edges. It is the sound of something massive moving—not in the cavern, but through it, around it, the cavern itself being moved through and around and *by* whatever is making the sound.

The roots above twist.

You watch them writhe, the great columns of translucent flesh braiding and separating and braiding again in patterns that are either panic or choreography. The fluid inside them churns, flowing faster now, both directions at once becoming neither direction, a chaos of transport, and you understand that what you are seeing is response—the body that contains this cavern is responding to something, and the response is visible in the architecture, written in the flesh.

You are standing inside a nervous system.

Not a metaphor. Not an analogy. The roots are nerves. The walls are tissue. The floor is skin. You are standing on the surface of something so vast that the cavern you entered is a single cell, or a capillary, or a synapse waiting to fire.

And something is firing.

---

You run.

The floor deforms beneath your feet, but not in healing—in pursuit. The membrane stretches toward you with each step, reaching, and you feel the heat of it, the hunger of it, the patient need of a thing that has not yet decided what to do with you. You are leaving footprints that heal slower now, that stay visible, that trace your path like a wound that does not want to close.

There is no exit. You know this. The tunnel you came through is gone, absorbed, sealed inside a wall that is breathing faster now, and the only direction you can run is deeper, or forward, or toward something that you do not have a name for.

You run toward the light.

There is light ahead—distant, faint, but present. It is the bioluminescence you have been following without knowing it, the blue-green pulse that marks the way, and you run toward it because it is the only thing that is not trying to absorb you. The light is not alive. The light does not breathe. The light is the one constant in a world that has no constants, and you run toward it with a desperation that bypasses thought, bypasses strategy, goes straight to the animal in you that knows what it means to be prey.

The light is coming from a wound.

You see it now—the bioluminescent patches are not on the surface. They are inside it, in the wounds where the membrane has been damaged, where something has bled light before you arrived. There have been others here. Not people, not the tool-users with their bones in the walls. Something larger. Something that fought this world and lost and left its mark in the only way it could—bleeding light into the darkness, a trail of luminescence that you are following without knowing it, a path written by the dying.

You follow it anyway. You are beyond choice now. You are beyond the part of you that weighs options and calculates survival. You are running on the animal certainty that light means exit, that light means surface, that light means a world where the floor does not breathe and the walls do not think and you are not standing in the mouth of something that is still learning how to swallow.

The light gets brighter.

The wound is ahead—a tear in the membrane so large that it has not healed, a rupture that the floor is still trying to close around the edges of, failing. The light pours out of it in waves, blue-green, and through the tear you see something that might be darkness, might be air, might be the outside, and you run faster, your lungs burning, your legs shaking, your heart a trapped bird throwing itself against the cage of your ribs.

The floor reaches for you.

Not with tendrils. Not with growth. With contraction—the membrane tightens beneath your feet, catches your ankles, and you stumble, and you fall, and the surface is warm and wet and alive and it holds you like a hand, like a mouth, like something that has been waiting for this specific shape of prey.

You reach toward the light.

---

The light is not the exit.

You see this now, as you lie on the breathing floor and feel it pulse against your back. The light is more of the same. The wound is a window into another cavern, or another cell, or another synapse, and it is full of the same organisms, the same bones, the same amber fluid and pulsing roots and patient sound.

You are not going to escape by moving.

You close your eyes. You press your palms flat against the floor. You feel it breathe beneath your hands—slow, steady, a rhythm that matches the subharmonic thrum, that matches your own pulse if you slow it down, if you let yourself sync with it, if you stop fighting and start listening.

The floor is warm. The air is thick. The sound is everything.

You breathe with it. You match it. You become a thing that breathes, and the floor beneath you settles, and the reaching stops, and the world that was going to swallow you pauses—because you are no longer a thing that runs. You are a thing that is here, that is present, that is part of the rhythm that this place makes when it is not afraid.

The creatures begin to return. You feel them first—a tickling, a crawling, small bodies moving over your skin with a curiosity that is not hunger. They taste you. They test you. They are deciding what you are, and you let them decide, because the alternative is to be decided for, and you have had enough of that.

The light ahead is not the exit. You understand this now. There is no exit. There is only the inside, the endless inside, the inside of a thing that is still being born and will be born for a very long time, and you are part of it now—the way the bones are part of the walls, the way the light is part of the wounds, the way everything that enters becomes part of the world that swallows it.

You open your eyes. The light ahead pulses.

You stand up, slowly, and the floor releases you, and the creatures do not scatter. They wait, watching, and you walk toward the wound in the membrane, toward the light, toward the next cavern, the next cell, the next part of the thing that has no name.

You do not know if you are walking out or walking in. The distinction has collapsed. There is only walking, only the rhythm, only the pulse of a world that is learning what it means to be alive, and you—visitor, prey, patient, patient, patient—walking with it, into it, through it, becoming it in the only way that this place allows.

The sound rises. The walls breathe.

And something, somewhere, is pleased.
