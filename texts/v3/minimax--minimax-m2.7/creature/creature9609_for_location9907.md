What lives here does not live well. That is not the same as dying. In the drowned pocket, the two states have grown confused, have leaned into one another until they share a border too thin to see.

There is something in the water. Not a creature exactly—creature implies motion, metabolism, the small frantic machinery of converting one thing into another. This is slower. This is more patient. It was here when the space opened, or it opened with the space, or it is the reason the space opened at all. It has no name because naming requires distance, and this thing is close to everything. It touches the stone and the stone forgets to be stone. It touches the roots and they forget to reach toward anything. It has touched you already, in the way that being inside it constitutes touching, and you have forgotten something you cannot name—something about surfaces, about where you end and the wet begins.

The structures that break the surface are not structures. They were built once, or they grew once, or they dreamed themselves into being over the patient centuries that this place pretends to exclude. Now they are something else: not ruins exactly, because ruin implies decay, and decay requires time, and time here is a courtesy the water does not extend. They are *marks*. The memory of shape pressed into the saturated dark. A low wall that remembers being a wall. A partial arch that still feels the weight it used to carry. They protrude only enough to exist—to be the suggestion of existence, the ghost of architecture refusing to fully dissipate.

There are creatures, though rarity is accurate. Once you saw the flash, the tightening, the motion too fast for the thick water to register. You did not see it clearly. You do not want to. But it was there—a flicker of something pale, something that moves in the way that deep things move, without urgency, without the waste of sudden motion. It lives in the water-that-is-not-water, the saturation that has become its own medium. It breathes what the water accepts. It eats what does not exist. It reproduces in some way you cannot imagine and do not need to—it is here, it was here before you arrived, it will be here when the erosion finally catches up, when the sharp stone softens, when the pocket forgets itself and becomes part of the larger wet world. Until then, it waits. Not for anything specific. Not for prey or companion or change. It waits the way water waits: without intention, without restlessness, simply held in the particular stillness of deep places where nothing asks to be otherwise.

And there are the roots, but they are not alive in any way you recognize. They descend from the nowhere above, thick as veins, pale as bone, and they hang in the thick wet air without rotting. They press into the stone. They curl along the walls. They have colonized the space, consumed it, made it into a garden, but they do not grow. They do not die. They simply *persist*, the way the water persists, the way the cool settles into you and stays. You will carry this coolness out with you, you suspect. It will linger in your chest, in the spaces between your ribs, long after you have left. It is the temperature of places where breath is audible and heartbeats are too loud. It is the temperature of being held.

The drowned pocket does not know you are there. This is not cruelty or indifference—it is simply the extent of its knowing, which is small, which is barely enough to hold itself together. It does not know the structures are older than it is. It does not know the roots descend from a world it cannot imagine. It does not know that what touches it—your presence, your breath, your gaze—leaves something behind, some residue of outside, some small wound that will not heal because the water does not allow healing. It simply is. Slow and full, held in suspension, waiting for erosion, waiting for light, waiting for you to become part of it or to leave.

You will leave. You will carry the cool with you, the weight of water that felt less like pressure and more like attention. You will think about this place in the years to come, in the quiet moments, in the spaces between sleep and waking. You will wonder what lived there, and you will not remember, because the drowned pocket took something from you, and you will never know what it was.

This is the only cruelty it has. The forgetting is gentle.
