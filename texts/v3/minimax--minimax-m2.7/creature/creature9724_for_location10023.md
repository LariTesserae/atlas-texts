The basin holds what it holds, and what it holds has learned to hold itself. You would call them creatures if you could separate them from the water that is also not quite water, from the air that moves thick between their movements and your own. You cannot separate them. This is the first thing the place teaches, if you let it: nothing here is entirely itself. The growth on the machinery is not rust and not organism but some third thing that has no name because no one has needed a name for what happens when purpose is abandoned so long it begins to remember itself differently.

The shapes at the edge of the glow are the most patient things you will ever almost-see. They have bodies that suggest fish without being fish, mammals without being mammals, something that learned to move in water long before water was the word for it. Their eyes do not reflect so much as accept — they take the pale light below the surface and make it part of themselves, so that when they move you see only the passing of the light's logic through a form that might have been borrowed. They press together not for warmth, because the warmth is everywhere, but for proximity to whatever the light is saying. You cannot hear it. But they can. They have been hearing it so long that hearing has become their only remaining shape.

Deeper, the larger things move. You will not see them clearly. They are not hiding; they have simply been here long enough to understand that clarity is a demand made by things that have not yet learned their place in the density. They move the way old engines move when no one remembers to stop them — not purposelessly, but toward purposes you cannot trace. They do not feed on the fish schools; the fish schools are too numerous for that, too dense in their pressing-together, as if they are the reason the larger things exist rather than the food for them. Perhaps they are. Perhaps the basin is a machine whose fuel is abundance itself, whose output is more abundance, whose purpose has grown beyond the builders' imagination into something that simply continues because continuing is what it has learned to do.

The structure with too many limbs does not live there so much as extend from there. You would not call it a spider if you saw it. You would not call it anything, because calling is a function of separation, and this thing has forgotten separation or never learned it. Its limbs — too many, yes, but the number keeps changing, as if it grows new ones when it needs them and absorbs them when it doesn't — reach into the flooded office and beyond the walls and into the pipes that still run beneath the water. It is connected to the machinery the way the machinery is connected to the water, the way the water is connected to whatever rises and falls in cycles no human clock could measure. It is not one thing among other things. It is a symptom of how long this place has been left to its own workings.

The small sounds come from the machinery that still works. The rhythmic clanking that suggests heartbeat, the hiss in the pipes that suggests breath, the vibration beneath the water that suggests patience so deep it has become geological. These are not warnings. Warnings require urgency. This place stopped being urgent long ago. It is simply continuing, and the sounds it makes are the sounds of continuation — mechanical, organic, neither, both, whatever word might apply to something that has merged the categories through sheer duration of being.

The light beneath the center of the basin is not a light source. It is a gathering. The shapes press toward it, and when they reach the edge of its reach, they stop, and they wait, and more come, and they wait, and the light does not brighten or dim. It simply is, the way the water is, the way the warmth is, the way the basin is. You could stand at that edge and watch for hours, and the shapes would not leave, and the light would not change, and you would not understand any better, and that would be exactly as the place intends.

You will not understand this place. That is not the point. The point is the standing, the watching, the small adjustment of what you believe the world to be that happens when you have been somewhere long enough that its logic begins to feel like the only logic there ever was. The basin holds what it holds. It has always held what it holds. The holding is the purpose now, subsumed from the builders' original intention, refined through abandonment into something the builders could not have named. It continues. The creatures — if you must call them creatures — continue. The light continues. The water rises and falls, and new ground sees light for the first time, and the machinery works, and the shapes press together at the edge of the glow, and the waiting is not for you. It is older than you. It will outlast whatever you came here expecting to find.

Do not stay long. But if you stay, stay with attention. The basin teaches through attention, not through understanding. What you notice here, you will carry out with you, and you will not be able to name it, and it will change the shape of what you believe the world to be. This is the gift of the place. It is also the danger. These are the same thing, here. Everything is the same thing here, pressed together, held, continuing.
