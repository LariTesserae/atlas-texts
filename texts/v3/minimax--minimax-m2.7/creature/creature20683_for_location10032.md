It is only the shape of a need that persisted after whatever needed it became something else, became water, became stone, became the drip and the hum and the ring that fills this basin like a held breath. You do not answer. You cannot answer. You walk on, and the crust rings under your feet, and you understand that this place was not made for you and was not made against you. It was made for something else, a different kind of presence that knew how to listen, that could stand in the water and feel the hum in its body the way you feel it now, and for that presence, for whatever it was, the basin was a cathedral or a song or a question it finally answered in the only way it knew how: by becoming part of it. By letting the water have it. By stopping. The spires remember this. The hollows remember this. The call at the edge of hearing remembers, though it has forgotten so much that what it remembers is now only the feeling of having forgotten, the shape of absence, the outline of something that once knew its own name and no longer does. You walk on. The basin lets you. The basin will let you leave. The basin will not let you forget that you were here, that you stood on the gray-green crust and heard what cannot be unheard, that you moved through the sound like a body moving through water, like a thought moving through time, slow and present and almost still. The light does not change. The water drips. The hum goes on. The call comes sometimes, at the edge of hearing, high and thin and asking, asking, asking.
