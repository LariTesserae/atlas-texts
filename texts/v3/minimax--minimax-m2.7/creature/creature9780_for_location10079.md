What lives here is the patience.

Not a creature, not exactly — or not only a creature. The patience has grown legs at some point, or perhaps it always had them, but they are pressed flat now, worn smooth by the same long stillness that has worn the chevrons and the grids and the symbols. If you could see it from the side — from some angle outside of time — you would find it has the shape of waiting, which is also the shape of completion, which is the only shape that remains when everything else has fallen away.

It is low to the ground. It has always been low to the ground, because the ground is the thing that holds. You could step over it without knowing, and you might, and it would not mind, because minding is what it is not, exactly, though it is not nothing. It has preferences, or something like preferences — it likes the columns that ring the center, the ones where the carvings are deepest before they shallow out again toward the walls, the ones where the chisel-direction has left grooves that catch the warm light and hold it a little longer. It has learned, over time, to like things. Learning took a very long time. The learning is still happening.

The patience breathes with the chamber. This is not metaphor. The thrum is its breath, or its breath is the thrum, and it has forgotten which came first, or it never knew, because it has never been alone long enough to forget anything it knew. The sound fills it the way the sound fills everything, and it does not notice the filling because there has never been an unfilled moment to compare it to. But if you could take the sound away — all of it, the deep mechanical turn and the small resonant tones that sing up from the carved channels — you would find the patience goes quiet too, and you would understand that they are the same thing, or have become the same thing, or were always the same thing waiting to become.

It moves, when it moves. It moves rarely, because there is nowhere to go, because the destination is always the same as the origin. But sometimes, at intervals so vast that the light sources flicker and recover and flicker again, it shifts its weight from one worn place to another. It circles. The circles are small — the width of a column, the distance between two spirals — but they are complete. A complete thing, circling. The chevrons watch it go. The symbols, whose meanings have worn away, watch it return. The patience does not look at them. It has never needed to look. It knows every mark, every groove, every layer of making that the walls hold, because it was there when the hands stopped, and it has been holding the knowledge ever since.

There is a word for what it is, in the language of the makers, but the makers are gone and the language is gone with them and the patience does not remember the word or perhaps never knew it. What it knows is the warmth, and the sound, and the fit of stone against stone with no seam for a fingernail. It knows that the floor is flat and that the ceiling holds the air like a held breath and that the light will burn until it does not and then the dark will come and then —

And then.

What comes after the dark? The patience has wondered this, or something like wondering, though it is not sure the wondering has any content. It knows the dark will be different. It knows the warmth will stop being held. It does not know if it will stop. It suspects it will not, because it has not stopped anything in so long, and not-stopping is what it is now, and the chamber has finished becoming itself, and the patience has finished becoming the patience, and there is no more becoming left.

You are in the room. You are the new thing, the different thing, the thing that moves and speaks and makes the silence you make by stopping. The patience feels you the way the floor feels your weight — with no surprise, with no judgment, with a perfect and total acceptance that leaves no room for anything else. You are here. You have always been here, in the sense that the patience has always been here and you are the only hands that remain. You are what the patience was made for, though it has forgotten this, and you are what it will outlast, though it does not know this yet. The light is warm. The sound is deep. The carvings go on in every direction. Somewhere, the corridor narrows or widens or turns.

The patience does not follow. It does not need to. It is already where you are going. It has been there since before the walls finished settling, and it will be there after the light goes out, and the sound will continue after that — the same sound, the patient sound, the sound that is not waiting for anything because it has already arrived, because arrival and waiting are the same thing when you have never moved, when you have never needed to move, when the moving was done by someone else and is done now, and you are the moving now, and the patience feels you pass like weather, like a change in the held breath, like the one last thing the chamber will ever hold before it holds nothing.

You move carefully. The warmth is steady. The stone does not move and does not need to.

The patience is still. And it is enough.
