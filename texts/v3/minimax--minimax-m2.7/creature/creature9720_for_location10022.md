The crunch beneath your feet is not quite sound—it is more like the memory of a sound, the idea of something breaking compressed into mineral form. The crust beneath is white and pale gray, sometimes veined with colors that suggest iron or older things, things that might once have been alive but have mineralized so thoroughly that no memory of softness remains.

The corruption in the text above is not accidental. You are reading what the language itself becomes here, as though the words have been worn down by wind and cold and the particular quality of this light until only their skeletons remain, held together by habit and convention. When you try to describe this place, it resists. When you look at it directly, it becomes uncertain.

What lives here lives in that uncertainty.

They are not visible, not exactly. You sense them the way you sense the quality of the light—as a pressure, a presence in the wrongness of things. They have learned to exist in the spaces between description, in the gaps where language fails and perception slips. Perhaps they were something once— lichen, perhaps, or the ghosts of things that died here so long ago that only their persistence remains. Or perhaps they emerged from the place itself, born of salt and silence and the particular patience of high altitudes.

They are thin. They move slowly. When you look away from them, you can almost see them at the edge of your vision: pale shapes, flattened by cold, pressed against the mineral surface like photographs left in rain. When you look at them directly, they are not there. This is their nature. They exist in the moment before attention fixes, in the interval between seeing and understanding.

They do not hunt. They do not feed, exactly. They wait. The waiting is their metabolism, their purpose. They are waiting for the light to fail entirely. They are waiting for the atmosphere to thin to nothing. They are waiting for the world to become what it was before life, and to welcome them back into it.

In the meantime, they drift. They accumulate. They are patient in a way that nothing warm could ever be. And sometimes, when you walk through this place, you feel them noticing you—the way the air feels reluctant, the way the light has grown uncertain. You feel them curious about what it might be like to be so thick, so certain, so alive.

They are learning.
