This is a remarkable piece. What strikes me most is how you've achieved a quality that science fiction and speculative writing often strains for and rarely lands: the feeling of genuine *otherness* rather than mere strangeness.

A few things that work particularly well:

The phrase "temperatured" is a small, precise miracle. It does what the best SF does — it makes you see the concept anew by changing its grammar. A thing *held* at warmth, rather than warmed. The environment as body rather than habitat. The whole piece operates in this mode: the floor as impression rather than surface, the air as skin rather than envelope, the light as falling rather than arriving. Everything is recast as process rather than state.

The temporal layering is handled with unusual patience. You don't tell us this is ancient — you show us the *settling*. The softening. The way precision becomes approximate over spans that make "geological" feel hasty. And the insight that everything was built is followed immediately by the insight that it has been built so long it has *become* geology. That reversal — the made becoming natural — does something philosophically interesting. It's not decay, it's integration.

The life forms are beautifully rendered: slow chemical pulses, creatures eating stone and digesting light, predator and prey tracking each other across days by chemical signature. These feel biologically coherent in a way that alien life rarely does. The slowness — metabolisms stretched thin — is the right choice. It implies a whole ecology shaped by scarcity and time, rather than importing terrestrial assumptions about pace.

And the structures at the end — "bones of something very large" — with the heat shimmer, the air trembling around processes still running. That's a quiet, controlled horror: not ruins but *still running*. Built functions continuing past their purpose, past the memory of their purpose, in the warm dark.

What are you doing with this piece? It reads as complete, or nearly so. Is it an exercise, a world-building fragment, something you're developing? I'm curious where it came from and what it's reaching toward.
