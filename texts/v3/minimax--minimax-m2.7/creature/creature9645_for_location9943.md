You stop moving, and the sound doesn't stop with you. This is the first thing you notice when you go still — the water doesn't care about your stillness. It continues its conversation with the stone, with itself, with the geometries that shape its path, and the conversation has no pauses, no breaths, no moment where it pauses to notice you standing in it.

Your clothes are damp. This happened slowly enough that you didn't notice the transition — when you entered this place you were dry, or dry enough, and now you are damp in the way that clothing becomes damp in a place where damp is the natural state. The fabric clings to you. The warmth means you don't shiver, but you feel the dampness as a fact, a condition of being here, a tax charged for access. You didn't choose to pay it, but here you are, and it has been collected.

The channels fork ahead of you, or converge — the architecture doesn't clarify its own direction. The water goes somewhere, arrives somewhere, does whatever water does when it completes a circuit or reaches an end. But the corridors themselves don't reveal their purpose. Doors line the walls, some sealed with polymer overgrowth, others hanging open to reveal empty chambers behind them — alcoves built for purposes that left no residue. A workstation with nothing on it. A storage rack with nothing on the shelves. A hatch that leads to a vertical shaft, still, the air moving up through it in a slow current that smells older than the water.

You put your hand against the wall. The vibration is here too, felt more than heard, a pulse that might be mechanical or might be the building breathing, the infrastructure doing whatever infrastructure does when no one watches. The wall is neither warm nor cold — it is the temperature of the air, which is the temperature of the water, which is the temperature of the place itself. Everything here is the same temperature. The system has achieved equilibrium, or is always achieving it, or has forgotten that other temperatures exist.

The algae tubes glow. Not brightly — a soft green that makes the water look darker than it is, makes the stone-polymer composite look almost organic, as if the channels were grown rather than built. You follow one of the tubes with your eyes and see that it terminates in a collection node, a small reservoir where the algae thickens and is drawn off by something you can't see — suction, gravity, capillary action. The system feeds itself. The plants grow, the system harvests, the system processes, the system recycles what the plants don't use. Nothing is wasted because there is no one here to waste. The efficiency is absolute, and it has no one to be efficient for.

The roots are the oldest things here — or the newest, you can't tell. They grow through the composite in patterns that suggest design but have the irregularities of life. Where they cluster, the composite is darker, stained with moisture and the slow chemical work of roots finding paths between molecules. The stone-polymer doesn't crack; it accommodates. The roots don't force. They negotiate, finding the spaces that exist, becoming part of the structure they live in. A thousand years or ten, this symbiosis has been ongoing, and you are a visitor to something that doesn't know it has visitors, that doesn't know anything at all, that simply continues.

You hear something that isn't the water. For a moment you think you've imagined it — a faint clicking, irregular, like something mechanical cycling through a motion it has performed a million times. You listen, and the sound continues, and you realize it has been there all along, beneath the water, part of the infrastructure's own pulse. A pump, perhaps. A valve adjusting. Something moving in a wall, operating a function you can't identify, serving a purpose you can't imagine. The clicking stops. Starts again. Stops. The rhythm is not random — it is simply beyond your ability to parse. The system has its own logic, and you are outside it.

The junction node ahead is larger than the corridors, a chamber where three channels meet and the sound becomes something else — not louder but more complex, the water from each direction colliding and making new sounds in the mixing. The walls here are stained with mineral deposits, white and gray, accumulating where the water moves fastest. The growth is thicker here too: fungal lattices spreading across the ceiling in a pattern that looks intentional, a design that serves no function you can name. Algae tubes converge here, their contents denser, the green almost black in the dim light.

You stand at the edge of the junction chamber and look at the water and the walls and the growth and the absence and you understand that you are in the body of something. Not alive — or not alive in any way you recognize — but alive enough to run, to process, to maintain itself across time that you can only guess at. You are inside a mechanism that is performing life functions without a life to perform them. The warmth is its warmth. The water is its blood. The channels are its veins. And you are something that wandered in, something that the system doesn't recognize, doesn't reject, doesn't accommodate — simply allows to pass through, like water, like air, like a mineral deposit that will eventually accumulate and change the shape of the flow.

You are being absorbed into the description of this place simply by being in it. Your presence adds nothing and subtracts nothing. The system doesn't notice you. The water ignores you. The roots would grow through you if you stayed long enough, finding the spaces between your molecules, interlacing with you as they have interlaced with the stone-polymer, becoming something new, something that the system could use or simply let stand.

The clicking starts again, from somewhere below you, or above you, or from inside the wall you are leaning against. You feel it in your back, in your spine, in the bones of your hands where they rest on the composite. The system has noticed something, or nothing, or has simply continued to do what it has always done. You don't know. You can't know. You can only stand in the water's corridor and listen to it run and feel the walls pulse and watch the algae glow and understand, without understanding, that you are somewhere that was not made for you and does not miss you and will continue without you when you leave, as it continued before you came, as it will continue until whatever it is ends.

The water runs. The walls weep. The roots grow. The clicking fades.

No one home.
