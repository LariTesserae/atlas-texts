# What Lives Here

There is something here that does not know your name.

Not indifference—indifference requires awareness of what is being ignored. This is older than that, simpler: the way stone does not know the moss, the way water does not know the riverbed. You have entered a space that operates on principles written before the first nervous system learned to register the presence of other. Your body is warm, and warmth is anomaly, and the anomaly will pass, and the space will not remember that it passed. This is not cruelty. This is not kindness. This is a timescale so vast that the visitor's entire duration is less than a fluctuation, less than a tremor in a single root's slow adjustment.

What lives here lives in the grammar of becoming.

Not being—being is the noun, the static thing, the object that rests in its own completeness. This space is all verb, all process, all *ongoing*. The roots are not grown; they are growing. The vaults are not constructed; they are constructing. Somewhere in the dense tissue overhead, somewhere in the labyrinthine heartwood of load-bearing columns, cells are dividing, walls are thickening, angles are adjusting to new weights. The architecture is not finished because the architecture is not finite. It is a sentence being spoken and never reaching a period, a thought that circles back on itself not to conclude but to extend.

The water is the blood. Not metaphor—physiology. The condensation that gathers on every surface is not decoration or weather but part of a circulatory system so vast and diffuse that no single drop knows where it came from or where it goes. It falls, it seeps, it is absorbed into root-membrane and drawn upward through capillary-action millennia in the making, and emerges again somewhere else as part of a flow that has no beginning and no end, only continuation. Your skin grows damp. The moisture wants to join. You are warm and wet and full of salt, and these are the exact qualities the space knows how to process, has always known how to process, long before warm wet salty things walked on legs and called themselves visitors.

The creatures in the canopy are not the inhabitants.

They are the guests at best, the parasites perhaps, the opportunists living in the positive space of a structure that does not object. They click and whistle and move in clusters like a single thought with many bodies, and their carapaces catch the thin bioluminescence and throw it back in fractured sparks, but they are not what this place is. They are what lives here, yes, but they are what lives *in* here, and the distinction is everything. The space would continue without them. They would not continue without the space. They are detail, ornament, footnote to the main text which is written in lignin and cellulose, in slow-growth heartwood, in the patient chemistry of photosynthesis humming quietly at frequencies that do not require sun.

And the bioluminescence—

The threads pulse faintly at their tips, blue-white and just below comfortable, and you find yourself straining to see them clearly, which is exactly what they do not want. They are not for seeing. They are not for you. They are for something else entirely: perhaps navigational markers for creatures whose eyes evolved in deeper darkness than yours, perhaps waste-products of metabolic processes that have no better use for light, perhaps simply the places where growth is fastest and energy must be shed. You do not know. The glow does not care that you do not know. It continues at its frequency, indifferent to the small primate brain trying to make it mean something, trying to extract purpose and design from a process that predates purpose, that predates the very concept of design as a thing that can be separated from the thing designed.

The sound is not music but it is not noise either.

It is the sound of processes occurring at different rates—the creak of plant tissue adjusting to its own weight is very slow, the click of creature-legs is very fast, the hum that might be vibration or might be processing is very slow and very fast at once, layered under each other like sediment, like strata, like the deep record of a world that has been making itself for longer than your species has had language to name it. You hear it and you do not hear it. You are inside it. Your eardrums vibrate and your brain interprets but the interpretation is always wrong, always too simple, because the sound is not a message waiting to be decoded. It is simply the sound of something being. Of many somethings being, simultaneously, at scales that your nervous system was not built to comprehend.

You are being absorbed.

The condensation settles on your skin and the moisture finds the salt and the salt is interesting to something, to some process, to some nanoscale chemistry that has learned over countless generations to extract what it needs from warm wet things that enter and leave. You will carry traces of this place out with you—the salt on your skin, the spores in your lungs, the moisture in your hair. The space will not remember you. But something in the space might, for a moment, taste the difference you made and decide, in whatever way such things are decided, to continue.

This is what lives here: not you, not the creatures, not even the plants alone.

What lives here is the relationship. The architecture that is also organism. The organism that is also architecture. The water that is also blood that is also tears that are also condensation that are also something else, something without a name in any language you speak, something that wet and cold and slow and alive and patient and vast and utterly indifferent to your presence and utterly made of your presence, because everything that lives here lives because everything else lives here, because the whole space is a single slow thought thinking itself, a single process completing itself without ever completing, beginning without ever beginning, being the middle of something so extended that endpoints are meaningless, that birth and death are just words for changes in rate, for fluctuations in the ongoing, for the way the system adjusts when something enters and leaves and enters and leaves and enters and leaves—

And never stops.
