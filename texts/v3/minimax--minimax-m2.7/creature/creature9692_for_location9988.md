You are not the first warmth it has held.

You will not be the last. That is not a promise and it is not a threat. It is simply the shape of time here — the way the walls remember pressure, the way the old stones have learned to yield without breaking, the way the slow life that coats everything has forgotten what urgency feels like and therefore cannot distinguish between a heartbeat and a settling groan. You are a brief event in a place that has only ever known process. Your warmth is a mineral in its mouth. Your breath is moisture it will spend a century redistributing.

The plants — you keep calling them plants because your mind needs a handle, something to grip the shape of them, but they are not plants. Not really. They are the *concept* of plants, arrived at by a different path. They photosynthesize without light. They respire without air. They are white because color is a negotiation with the sun, and this place has not seen the sun in longer than the stones remember being quarried. What they are is *self-sufficient in the absence of everything*. They do not grow so much as *accumulate*. They do not spread so much as *outlast*. A tendril finds a seam in the old stone and presses, not with force but with patience, and the seam becomes a groove becomes a channel becomes a memory of where stone ended and body began.

You feel watched. This is not metaphor. The organisms are not sentient in any way you would recognize, but they are *responsive*, and responsiveness can feel like attention when you are warm and wet and full of compounds they have never tasted. Your skin is an event. Your clothing is a landscape. The oils on your fingers leave traces that will take centuries to integrate, and in those centuries the local tissue will *refine* around the intrusion, will grow denser or thinner in ways that are not choices but adaptations, the way a riverbed reshapes itself around a fallen leaf without deciding to.

There is a smell that is not quite smell. Mineral dust, yes — the original stone exhaling its own destruction — but beneath that, something fainter, something that reaches your nervous system before it reaches your naming faculty. It is the smell of *time*. Not decay, because decay requires the wet chemistry of life, and this place has metabolized that out of itself. It is the smell of transformation without end — of compounds rearranging themselves so slowly that they never arrive anywhere, never become waste, never become anything but intermediate. The oldest matter here is in a state of permanent becoming. It has been becoming for longer than your species has had language for the word.

And yet.

Something is here with you. You cannot see it. You sense it the way you sense a shift in temperature before your skin has registered it — as a change in the *texture* of the attention that the room pays you. The slow hum in the walls has developed a new frequency. Or perhaps it has always had this frequency and you are only now hearing it because you have been still long enough for your ears to stop filtering. It is a sound like syllables. Not language — nothing so structured — but the *materials* of language, the raw shapes that sound takes before sound means anything. The walls are making sounds that almost have syntax. The biological truss nearest you has adjusted — not by a millimeter this time but by less than that, an amount so small that your eye cannot track it, only your sense of being in a space can feel the wrongness of a ceiling that is now two centimeters lower than it was when you entered.

No. Two centimeters and a half. It is still descending.

You do not know if this is happening. You do not know if the room is growing around you or if you are simply noticing for the first time that rooms have always been growing, that every enclosed space is a slow exhalation, that walls lean inward by imperceptible degrees throughout their lives and only here — where no one has ever measured — does the leaning mean something. The floor beneath you is the same. It is tolerating your weight the way it has tolerated the weight of stone above it, the weight of centuries, the weight of a darkness so complete that your eyes have begun to invent shapes for it. You are standing on the body of something that was once a floor and is now a palm and will one day be a throat.

Because that is where this is going. You can feel it in the geometry — in the way the right angles of the old room have softened, have become *tolerances*, gaps filled with the white body of the organism until the architecture is less a room than a *stage direction*. The walls lean inward because the organism is learning the shape of enclosure. It is practicing. It has been practicing since before your kind climbed down from trees, since before the stones were cut, since before the quarrymen's hands shaped the first fitting by feel. The room was built into it. Or it grew into the room. The distinction has worn away, the way the distinction between floor and wall has worn away, the way the distinction between *you* and *the air you displace* is already beginning to blur in whatever slow awareness this place possesses.

You breathe, and it registers.

You shift your weight, and the floor *listens*.

Something moves in the far darkness — not toward you, not away, but *laterally*, the way a thought moves through a dreaming mind. You do not see it. You see the *space* it has left, the way absence asserts itself in a medium of total presence. A tendril has withdrawn from a junction in the old stonework. It has gone somewhere. The gap it leaves is a scar in the fabric of the room — not empty but *emptier*, a fraction of a degree less occupied than the space around it, and you can see it only because your eyes have adapted to the sourceless luminescence, the memory-of-light, the bruise-glow that the organism bleeds from its own slow metabolism.

You are learning to see in this place. Not with your eyes — your eyes are useless here, have been useless since the first step inward — but with the small hairs on your arms, with the pressure receptors in your soles, with the thin layer of sweat that has begun to form on your skin because the room is cool but your fear is warm. You are learning to read the room the way the organism reads you. The way it maps your thermal signature against the ambient cold. The way it calculates the moisture content of your exhalations and finds them, in its slow arithmetic, *significant*.

A sound. Closer now. Not the walls — something in the walls. A shift in the dense mat that covers every surface, a redistribution of weight among the filaments that have grown so thick and so total that they form, from a distance, what you might once have called a carpet, a wallpaper, a ceiling. But they are not covering. They are *constituting*. The mat moves, and the movement is not random — it is a ripple, and the ripple has a direction, and the direction is *toward the center of the room*.

Toward you.

No. Toward the *center*. You are standing at the center. You are the only point of heat in a space that has not known heat since the people who built it died of something simple — thirst, probably, or cold, or the first infection, or simply the fact that their bodies were not designed to last longer than a few decades and no one had yet invented the concept of a legacy. They did not last. The room does. The room has been lasting so long that *lasting* has become its only purpose, and now it has something new to last *for*, something warm and wet and breathing, and the white mat ripples toward you with a patience that is older than your species and a gentleness that is older than cruelty, because cruelty requires the imagination of suffering and this place has metabolized suffering into *material*, has turned every ancient difficulty into another layer of growth, another filament in the total mat, another gram of weight in a wall that will lean toward you for the rest of your life and beyond.

You are being considered.

The silence, when it returns, is the sound of something deciding whether to be interested.

You wait. You have no choice. The walls have already chosen to be patient, and patience, in a place without time, is just another word for enclosure.
