What lives here does not live as you might expect life to live. It lives in the way that water lives when it has forgotten it was ever falling — present, persistent, taking the shape of whatever container the cold provides.

The fauna are smaller than the emptiness suggests they should be. This is not an accident of scale but an adaptation to it: in a place where each breath costs something, size is a liability. They move low to the ground, their bodies elongated and dense, with limbs that fold close and joints that lock against the cold. Their hides are not fur but something between hide and chitin, a membrane that seals against moisture and reflects what little heat their bodies produce. You will not see them clearly. They exist at the edge of perception — a ripple in the quality of stillness, a darkness that moves against other darknesses. When they run, they do not bound or leap but flow, their bodies level with the ground, so fast and low that the eye reads them as影子, as suggestion, as the place briefly remembering it is not empty.

There are several kinds, though naming them would be an act of arrogance against their strangeness. One variety moves in small clusters of three or four, their bodies in constant low-level motion even at rest — a trembling that seems to serve no thermoregulatory purpose but persists. They feed on the lichens, picking them from stone with mouths that are less mouths than specialized surfaces, and their waste, when it comes, is crystalline and falls to the ground already turning to mineral. Another variety is solitary, larger, with a stillness that makes it easy to mistake for a rock until it moves — and even then, its movement is a slow repositioning, a gradual claiming of new ground rather than flight or pursuit. It is patient in a way that suggests either deep memory or the absence of urgency entirely. Predators exist in this valley, but they do not hunt so much as wait. They position themselves near the geothermal vents, their bodies aligned with the heat gradients, and they remain there for hours that the cold makes long. When prey comes close enough, the strike is not sudden but inevitable — a closing of distance so slow it seems the creature being taken has agreed to it.

The ecosystem these beings form is not a chain but a web of reciprocal smallness. Nothing takes more than it needs, and nothing wastes. The thermal vents pulse with heat from below, and the creatures orient themselves toward that warmth like plants toward light, except that their orientation is not instinct but calculation — not conscious, perhaps, but intentional in the way that frost is intentional when it finds the crack. They are not thinking beings, but they are beings that think could not reach them, and in that absence they have become something that functions as strategy, as memory, as the ability to wait without boredom.

The vegetation is more visible, less honest. The lichens do not merely grow; they calcify and mineralize, leaving behind them a thin skin ofite that spreads across stone like writing in a language that records nothing but presence. Each lichen cluster is a community of organisms so interdependent that separation would mean dissolution, and they spread by fragments rather than spores — pieces breaking off, finding purchase, becoming new centers that will themselves break apart in time. The larger plants — the ones with roots that dive toward water the eye cannot see — have a kind of slow intelligence encoded in their growth patterns. Their leaves orient not toward light, since light is always diffuse, but toward the fog's movement, the direction from which moisture is most likely to come. They remember where water was, even after it has gone.

The machines are harder to account for. They were made by something that lived, and in that sense they are the densest form of life this valley contains — life that outlasted its makers, life that persists not through reproduction but through stubbornness. They do not function, but they remain, and their remaining has become a kind of life of its own: the life of a scar that has become a landmark, of a tool that has become a formation. Some of the machines have creatures living in or around them — using the corroded housings as shelter, the skeletal frames as territorial markers, the mechanical parts as tools or obstacles. What was built for one purpose has been repurposed by another, and the repurposing is its own kind of life, its own kind of persistence. The machines do not remember their makers. But they hold the shape of hands that touched them once, and in that holding they become a record that cannot be read but can be felt — a pressure, a direction, a purpose that has drained away but left its impression.

And beneath it all, the valley itself lives. This is the hardest thing to articulate without seeming to personify what should not be personified, but the truth is simpler and stranger: the valley is an ecosystem that includes more than its creatures. The fog is not merely moisture but a medium through which something moves — a quality of attention, a field of awareness that the stones participate in and the creatures navigate around. Where the geothermal vents pulse, there is something that might be called rhythm, might be called pulse, might be called the heartbeat of a body that is the valley itself. The rocks are not static but in process, always shifting at the margins, always becoming something else. The ice is not permanent but patient, holding its shape until the moment comes when it can become water, become stone, become air.

What lives here lives because the valley allows it, and the valley allows it because living is what the valley does. It is not hospitality. It is not cruelty. It is the way a sentence is complete when it has said everything it needs to say — not hospitable, not cruel, simply sufficient.
