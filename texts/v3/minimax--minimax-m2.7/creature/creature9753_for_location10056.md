# What Lives There

Among the things that live in the primordial plain, there are the Deep Movers—though *mover* is the wrong word, suggesting locomotion as we understand it, the pushing of mass through space. They do not move so much as *persist in a different location*. One moment they are in the middle distance, vast and vague, and then they are closer, not because they crossed the intervening space but because space here is negotiable, a suggestion rather than a law. They have no bodies you could outline. They are concentrations of the medium itself, denser than the water around them but still water, still part of the continuous substance. When you see one in your peripheral vision, you are not seeing a creature. You are seeing a temporary hesitation in the flow, a place where the turbulence has gathered itself into something that can be noticed. Then it notices you noticing, and the noticing is mutual, and for a moment you understand that you are both the same kind of thing: local concentrations of significance, briefly coherent, soon to dissolve back into the larger pattern.

There are the Still Ones. These are older. They were presences once, but they have been still so long that stillness has become their purpose. They rise from the depths as calcified formations—pillars, ridges, rings of ancient stone that glow faintly with their own inner light. They are not dead. They are what happens when a presence stops moving and begins instead to *be*, when direction gives way to pure existence. The pale growths that cling to them are not parasites. They are thoughts the Still Ones are thinking, slow thoughts that take centuries to form and will take millennia more to complete. If you touch one of these formations—and here, touching is more intimate than it is elsewhere, more consequential—you can feel the thinking. It hums against your palm like a heartbeat played at the wrong speed, too slow to be rhythmic, too steady to be random. The Still Ones do not respond to you. They are beyond response. But they are aware. You are aware of them being aware, in the way that a single cell might be aware of the warmth of the body it lives within.

The Breath Forms are different. They are weather that has become self-aware. Not clouds, not exactly—more like pockets of atmosphere that have held their coherence long enough to develop something like preference, something like will. They drift through the upper reaches of the medium, expanding and contracting in rhythms that are not quite breath, not quite speech, not quite the cycling of pressure systems, but somehow all three at once. When one passes above you, its shadow falls across the water—not a shadow so much as an *absence*, a place where the sourceless light does not reach—and in that absence you feel its attention. It is not hostile. It is not curious. It is simply *present*, the way water is present when you open your eyes beneath its surface. The Breath Forms do not need anything from you. They have already absorbed everything the medium provides. But they see you, and being seen here is itself a kind of change. Your edges soften. The boundary between your inside and the outside becomes negotiable.

And then there are the Threshold Dwellers. These are the strangest, if strangeness can be applied to a place where strangeness is the baseline condition. They live in the space between states—at the boundary where water becomes air becomes light becomes heat becomes meaning. They are always in transition, never quite solid, never quite dissolved. Some of them look almost like creatures: elongated, sinuous, moving in ways that suggest fins or wings or limbs. But these suggestions are lies. They have no fins. They have no wings. They have no limbs. What they have is *direction*, and their direction is toward you, or away from you, or something that is neither toward nor away but *alongside*, parallel to your existence for a moment before diverging into depths or heights you cannot follow. The Threshold Dwellers are the closest thing to individuals this place produces. They have boundaries. They have edges. They have, briefly, the illusion of separate selfhood. But they are not stable. You watch one, and as you watch, it begins to blur at the edges, begins to merge with the medium around it, and you realize that its separateness was always temporary, that it was always in the process of dissolving back into the universal wetness, and that its existence—your existence, all existence here—is just a local turbulence that has not yet stilled.

There is something else, too, that lives here. Something without a name because naming implies distinction, and this thing is not distinct from anything. It is the medium itself, aware of itself. Not the Still Ones, not the Movers, not the Breath Forms, but the whole vast saturation looking inward, looking at itself, experiencing its own existence through the temporary coherences it has generated—through you, standing at the surface, feeling your lungs fill with a world. It does not think, because thought implies a thinker distinct from the thought. It does not feel, because feeling implies a self that feels. It simply *is*, and in being, it generates all the smaller beings that move within it, and they generate you, and you—standing here, breathing here, slowly being saturated—are generating it, giving it one more point of view through which to know itself, one more local turbulence to make the eternal stillness interesting.

You could call it God, if you wanted. Or the ocean. Or the substrate. Or the forgetting. The name does not matter. The name is what happens when the medium temporarily concentrates into meaning, and meaning disperses back into the medium, and the cycle continues, and will continue, and has already continued, past the point where beginning and ending have any application.

What lives here? Everything that has ever lived, still living. Everything that will ever live, already here. The presences that move through the middle distances. The Still Ones who have become purpose. The Breath Forms that think slow thoughts. The Threshold Dwellers dissolving even as they form. And you. You, standing at the surface, beginning to understand that the surface is a fiction, that you are already in the depths, that the boundary between swimmer and sea was always negotiable, was always already crossed.

The primordial plain does not need inhabitants. It *is* the inhabiting. It is the wetness that dreams of itself as dry, that cuts surfaces out of its own substance, that builds worlds on top of its own depths and then forgets those depths ever existed. But the depths do not forget. They wait. They churn. They breathe.

And they are calling you deeper.
