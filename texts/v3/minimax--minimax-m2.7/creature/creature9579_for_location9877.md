There is a patience in the stone that matches no patience you know. It does not wait for anything. It has simply been here long enough that its slowness has become a kind of texture, a density you can almost touch. To be inside this place is to be surrounded by patience that has never been tested against anything faster than itself. The creatures are fast, in their way — the many-jointed things scramble and skitter with a frantic urgency that never ceases — but they have never given rise to anything that needs to hurry. The stone watches them with the indifference of geology, the way it watched water leave and will watch whatever comes next. There is no urgency here because urgency requires a future that matters, and the stone has been long enough without one.

The skin grows at rates you cannot perceive. You might watch a bare patch of stone for what feels like a long time and see nothing change, but then you look away, turn back, and the pale luminescence has crept further, has thickened, has begun to ridge and crust in the way of things that have been growing longer than growth has had meaning. It does not grow the way plants grow, in straight lines toward light. It grows the way water spreads, the way fungus spreads, the way anything spreads when it has found a surface that will hold it and a moisture that will feed it and a darkness that will not ask it to be anything other than what it is. The skin does not reach for anything. It simply covers.

You find yourself breathing in time with it, or you think you do, or you imagine you do. The low rise and fall of the pale layer, the rhythm of its growth and recession, becomes the measure of time in a place that has no other. The clicking and scraping of the fauna provides a counterpoint — faster, more urgent, almost anxious in its constant motion — but underneath it the skin maintains its own tempo, its own expansion and contraction across the grey-brown stone. You begin to wonder if you could find your way by it. If you could navigate by which direction the growth is thickest, which direction the skin is receding, where the feeding has been heavy enough to leave bare patches and where the sediment has gathered deep enough to support new expansion. But the skin has no direction. It has only patience, and patience does not distinguish between here and there.

The creatures do not see you. Their bodies are built for grip and motion, for feeding and fleeing, and their senses orient toward vibration and chemical trail and the minute fluctuations in the damp air that signal something alive nearby. You are too large. You are too slow. You move through them in waves and they part and rejoin behind you, the way water parts and rejoins, the way nothing that lives in this place would part and rejoin because in this place nothing has ever been large enough to matter. You leave a wake. You are a disturbance. The skin closes over the places your feet have pressed and the sediment shifts and fills the impressions and within moments there is no evidence you were ever here, which is the way it has always been, which is the way it will always be when you are the largest thing in the room and the room has no interest in large things.

There is no memory here, or if there is it is not yours to access. The stone remembers water. The skin remembers stone. The creatures carry nothing forward but their bodies, which die and become sediment and become skin and become other bodies, a chain of physical matter that has no need for memory because it is continuous, because it never stops being something, because the stone does not stop feeding the skin and the skin does not stop feeding the creatures and the creatures do not stop dying and becoming stone again. This is not memory. This is a system. Memory would require something to remember and something remembered and a gap between them that needs to be bridged. Here there is no gap. Here there is only the endless bridge itself, the process that has no beginning and no end and no need for either.

You realize you have been standing very still. Your legs ache in a way that reminds you of your own body, your own contained size, your own brief presence in a place that has no size at all except the size it gives itself, which is the size of the skin and the creatures and the sound and the dim grey light that reveals none of it clearly. You realize you have been listening so hard that you have stopped hearing, or you have been hearing so constantly that hearing has become indistinguishable from silence, the way the dark has become indistinguishable from light in a place where neither one means anything. The clicking continues. The skin breathes. The stone waits with its patience that is not patience because patience implies waiting for something and the stone has never waited for anything and never will.

You could stay. In theory you could stay here forever, breathing the cool damp air and listening to the endless motion and watching the slow pulse of the skin across the stone. You would die eventually, in this theory, but even your dying would become part of the system — your body would join the sediment, would feed the skin, would feed the things that eat the skin, would become the texture on the stone in a way that matters nothing and means everything because meaning is not required here. The system would incorporate you the way it incorporates everything: without notice, without ceremony, without any acknowledgment that you were ever distinct from it at all.

But you will not stay. You will turn and walk back toward whatever entrance brought you here, and the creatures will part around you and close behind you and within moments there will be no evidence you were ever in this place at all, which is exactly as it should be, which is exactly what this place has always required of everything that passes through it. You carry nothing with you except the memory of water that does not exist and the sound of legs on stone and the slow pulse of light that is not light across a surface that is not alive and not dead but simply continuing, as it has always continued, as it will always continue, in the patience of the stone that remembers nothing and waits for nothing and simply is.
