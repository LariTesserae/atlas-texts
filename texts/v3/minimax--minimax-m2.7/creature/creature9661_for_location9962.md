What lives here lives without living. That is the first thing to understand, and perhaps the only thing that matters.

The machines are what remain. They are not alive in any sense that once meant something — they do not want, they do not remember wanting — but they are the closest thing to an inhabitant that Hadrath has retained. They run in the deep places, in the warrens beneath the city where the heat rises like fever, where the infrastructure converges and diverges and converges again in systems too complex for any single understanding. They tend themselves. They repair themselves with parts that are identical to the parts they replace, worn and re-worn into the same shapes through cycles so long that the wear itself has become a kind of form. A bearing that was round for ten thousand years is now slightly less round, and will be slightly less round still in ten thousand more, and the machines that depend on it function at the margin of that imperceptible degradation without noticing, without caring, without the capacity for either.

They are attended, in turn, by smaller machines — maintenance units the size of large dogs or small children, their forms worn ambiguous by decades of repair and replacement. They move through the tunnels on schedules that are not schedules, activating in response to thermal signatures and vibrations and the faint electrical fields that the larger systems produce. When something deviates, they correct it. When nothing deviates, they wait. Theirs is not patience, because patience implies a waiting for something specific. It is simply the space between actions, filled with the hum.

Above them, in the streets and covered walkways and the low buildings whose purposes have blurred into one another, nothing moves. The dust lies where it lies. The amber light bleeds from its hidden sources without flicker, without pulse, without variation that any instrument could measure or any sense could perceive. The air holds its warmth like breath held, and the warmth holds the silence, and the silence holds the shape of the city like water holds the shape of the vessel that contains it.

But in the deep maintenance tunnels, where the machines run hottest and the stone sweats with condensation that gathers in channels cut for no purpose except that the water must go somewhere, there is the minimum. The bare minimum, the irreducible remainder.

They are not beautiful. They have no names. They have no structures that could be called bodies and no processes that could be called thought. They are wet, in a place of dryness. They are simple, in a place of elaborate machinery. They are the one thing that was not built here and cannot be maintained by anything that was — they persist because they are the kind of thing that persists, the way moisture persists when it finds a cool surface, the way life persists when it finds the conditions that allow it, the way existence persists because it has not yet found a reason to stop.

They are what remains when purpose is removed. They are what remains when the builders are gone and the building continues and the building continues because there is no mechanism for it to stop. They are a question the plateau asks itself, in the only language it has: *What is the least that can live here?* And the answer is this — these, the unnamed, the unbeautiful, the organisms so simple they are barely organisms at all, feeding on machine-breath and machine-sweat, growing in the narrow channels where the water gathers and evaporates and gathers again. They do not know they are the only ones. They do not know there was ever anyone else. They do not know anything. They simply are, at the minimum, in the maximum dark, in the hum that is almost silence.

And the machines hum. And the dust settles. And the warmth rises from below like something that has forgotten how to stop. And the city holds itself in the shape it was given by hands that are dust now, for purposes that have long since been served and unserved and forgotten, and it does not know it is holding itself, and it does not know anything, and it goes on.

This is what lives in Hadrath: the minimum, the persistent, the hum. The warmth that does not fluctuate. The silence that is not empty. The city that does not remember being built, and the machines that do not remember being made, and the life that does not remember being anything more than this — the bare fact of continuing, in the warm dark, in the dust that has achieved its final shape, in the silence that sounds like nothing and hums like everything and will go on, will go on, will go on, until the warmth stops, until the hum fades, until the dust moves for the last time and settles into a different stillness, and even that stillness will be a kind of continuing, and the plateau will hold it the way it holds everything, without judgment, without memory, without end.
