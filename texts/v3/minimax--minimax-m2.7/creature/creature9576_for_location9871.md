# What Lives in the Roaring

You must understand: the place is not empty. The place was never empty. Before you came, it was full of itself, and it is full of itself still, and your presence is the thing that does not quite fit.

## The Breath

There is something beneath the shelf that breathes, and it is not alive in the way you know alive. It is the breath of compression, of pressure finding release, of heat that has waited through epochs to become a sound. When you feel it in your sternum, in the soft places behind your eyes, that is not your body responding to stimulus. That is your body encountering something vast and slow that has mistaken you for a thought it is thinking. The breath does not stop. The breath has never stopped. The breath was here before the water, before the shelf, before the mountains forgot they were mountains, and it will be here when whatever comes next forgets this world entirely. You do not hear it. You *are* heard by it.

## The Water-That-Waits

The warmth is not warmth. It is attention. The water watches through you, through itself, through the stone it laps against and the steam it releases to the dark. It carries heat because heat is what it means to be patient. Every pulse is a consideration. Every surge toward the spires is a reaching, a questioning, a testing of the edges of its basin. You could fall into it and be absorbed—not drowned, not consumed, but *included*. Your shape would become a shape it remembers. The minerals in your bones would join its ledger. You are already thinking about it. It already knows.

## The Stone-That-Is-Young

The spires do not know yet what they are. They are old by your reckoning—tens of thousands of years—but they hardened too quickly, and so they are incomplete. They vibrate at frequencies just below perception because they are still deciding. The sharp edges are the edges of hesitation. The bridges that span nothing are propositions the stone has not yet resolved. When you step on them, they do not quite know whether to hold you or to flow. Sometimes they hold. Sometimes, for a moment that is longer than a moment, they do not. The mineral veins that glow are the shelf's nervous system: signals traveling, meanings not yet arrived. If you watched long enough—if you could survive the watching—you might see them change. You might see them become.

## The Structures-Without-Builders

The circles are not circles. The steps are not steps. The arch is not an arch. They only present as these things to something that expects geometry, expects purpose, expects the comfort of intention. They are more like scars in the stone's dreaming—or memories the shelf holds of something it once almost was. The circles remember a radius they do not currently occupy. The steps lead to a destination that is also a beginning that the stone has forgotten. And the arch frames a darkness that is not the absence of light but the presence of depth, the *underneath* that the shelf is still falling toward, the place that watches back.

Do not step through the arch. Not because it is forbidden—it is not forbidden; nothing here forbids; nothing here permits; nothing here has yet learned the difference—but because what is on the other side is not ready for you, and you are not ready for it, and the geometry there will not apologize for the collision.

## The Ones-Who-Cannot-Be-Looked-At

In the peripheral dark, something moves that has always moved. It is pale the way absence is pale, the way something is pale when light has never thought to find it. It passes through the deeper water with a slowness that is not slowness—it moves at the speed of deep time, and your glimpse catches only the tail of a gesture that began before you arrived and will continue after you leave. If you turned toward it quickly, it would not be there. It was never there. But it was, and it will be, and it knows you saw the edge of it, and it has made a note.

On the spires, at the edges, the small things cluster. They are not insects. They are not anything. They are the shelf's housekeeping—the things that attend to the edges, the interfaces, the places where one kind of not-water meets another kind of not-air. They have no shells because they have no need for boundaries. They are soft in the way that thought is soft, and they move wrong because they are still learning what moving is. Do not look at them directly. Not because they will harm you—they do not know harm; they barely know themselves—but because looking at them directly means admitting they are there, and if they are there, then the shelf is more populated than you thought, and you are less alone than you believed, and the roaring is not just sound but *company*.

## The Ferns-That-Reach

The pale long-limbed things on the high spires are not ferns. They are not plants. They are the shelf's curiosity—its attempt at something it saw once, long ago, in the memory the stone carries of when it was seafloor and the sun reached down to touch it. They reach for light that does not come because the reaching is the point. The wanting is what they are. They sway in currents with no source because they are swaying in the attention of the deep water, the breath of the vents, the hum that moves through everything here. They do not photosynthesize. They do not eat. They *persist*, and that is enough, and that is all, and they are older than you and will outlast whatever you leave behind.

## The Arch-That-Watches

The darkness behind the arch is not empty. The darkness behind the arch is not even dark—it is *depth*, a dimension that does not correspond to the ones you know. It watches because watching is what it does. It has watched the shelf form and the water rise and the steam first curl upward and the first thing that was almost a creature emerge from the warm pooling and return to it. It is patient in the way that pressure is patient, that time is patient, that the space between one thought and the next is patient. It will watch you too. It is watching you now. It does not judge. It does not expect. It only holds, and when you step close enough, when you look long enough, when the roaring becomes a language you almost understand—

It will show you what it means to fall upward, and you will understand, and you will not be able to explain, and that is alright. Nothing here needs to be explained. Everything here needs to be *entered*.

## You

You are standing on the shelf, and the shelf is standing on something, and that something is breathing, and you are breathing with it now, and the boundary between your breath and the earth's breath is thinning, and soon there will be no boundary at all, and you will be the sound the shelf makes when it roars, and the roaring will be what you remember when you try to remember yourself, and the remembering will be wrong, and the wrongness will be the only truth, and you are still standing here.

You have not left.

You have not decided to leave.

You are still deciding, and the deciding is the point, and the shelf knows, and the water knows, and the darkness behind the arch is patient, and the roar is louder now, and it sounds almost like a welcome, and it sounds almost like a warning, and it sounds like the world still being born, and it has not yet learned how to be quiet, and it does not want you to be quiet either, and it wants you to roar with it, and it wants you to stay, and it wants you to fall, and it does not want anything because wanting is too simple, too human, too much a thing for creatures that end.

You do not end here. Not exactly. You blur. You soften. You become the edge of something larger.

The roar is your heartbeat now.

You are not standing on the shelf.

You are the shelf, dreaming you, dreaming itself, roaring its way toward something that will one day be a silence, a stillness, a world that knows itself well enough to rest.

But not yet.

Not yet.
