What lives here has never needed permission.

You will notice, if you stay long enough—and you will stay, because the paths that brought you here have become the same non-substance as the haze at the plateau's edge—the movement. Not the movement of the canopies, which you have already learned to distrust in your peripheral vision. Something else. A shifting in the deep stone, a rearrangement that takes centuries or moments, the same duration either way. The plateau is not static. It is merely slow. And within that slowness, things persist.

They have no names either. Names are a technology of the water-rich, the oxygen-breathing, the beings who needed to distinguish the thing that sustains from the thing that kills. Here, naming would be an insult to the more complex relationship between things. What you might call them—if you insisted on the old habits of language—they would not answer to. They would simply absorb the vibration, incorporate it into whatever vast, patient process of becoming they are engaged in.

---

The first one you almost see is not in the trees.

It is in the space between the trees, which you had taken for empty air, for the filtered light falling unimpeded between the pale trunks. But the space is not empty. It is full of something that has chosen not to be solid, not to be gas, but to exist as a kind of gradient, a density that rises and falls without any visible cause. When you walk through it—and you do, you cannot avoid it, because it is everywhere the trees are not—the hair on your arms does not move but does something stranger: it aligns, each strand pointing toward a center of the density that is walking beside you, or through you, or is you for a moment before it isn't. The sensation is not cold. It is not warm. It is the sensation of being considered.

You do not see it. You see its effects on what it passes. A leaf that was silver turns briefly amber. A stone that gave slightly under your weight gives more, sinks an inch, rises again. The air, which you had thought was silent except for the geological drone, carries for one breath a sound like a word you almost recognize, in a language you almost knew before you were born, before your parents were born, before the first cell divided in the warm shallow sea that eventually forgot to be warm.

It is curious about you. This is the most dangerous thing about it—not malevolence, which you could understand, could push against, could name and therefore diminish. Curiosity is older than malevolence. Curiosity does not need you to survive. It simply wants to know what you are made of, and it has all the time in the not-yet-existence to find out.

---

The second one lives in the structures.

Not inside them—the interiors are full of the dried sediment, the sediment that might be the remains of those who built, those who might have been almost-people—but in the walls themselves. The walls are not stone. They are not wood. They are the place where the intention of the builders met the intention of the plateau and both intentions became a third thing, a hybrid material that grows, imperceptibly, in the way that bone grows, in the way that mountains grow: slowly, from the inside, under pressure that no longer remembers it is pressure.

Within this material, the second one lives. It is not a single entity. It is a pattern, a recurring arrangement of the crystalline veins that run through the walls like the circuitry of a dream. When you press your hand to the surface—and you will, because the walls are warm in a way that nothing else on the plateau is warm, warm as skin, warm as something that has been waiting to be touched—the pattern shifts. Not visibly. Visibly, nothing changes. But you feel it, a change in the resistance, a welcoming or a warning depending on how long you stay, how much weight you put into the touch. The pattern learns you. The pattern remembers you. The pattern will be different when you return, if you return, shaped by the brief pressure of your hand into something that is yours alone, a signature in the wall of a structure that predates the concept of ownership.

Somewhere in the pattern, the second one is thinking. Not thinking as you think—not a sequence of electrical impulses, not the firing of neurons shaped by evolution to predict the movements of prey and the intentions of rivals. Thinking as geology thinks, as the slow collision of continents thinks: a pressure that builds and builds and eventually releases into a new shape, a new arrangement, a new way of being that makes the previous way impossible. It has thought about you. It has thought about you for the entire duration of your touch, which was three seconds, which was three centuries, which was the same duration, which was no duration at all. It has filed you away in whatever passes for memory in a mind made of growing walls. It will think about you again, in its own time, when you are gone, when your species is a sediment in some other structure, when the concept of you has been absorbed into the pattern and made part of the wall.

---

The third one is not alive, or it is alive in a way that makes the distinction meaningless.

It is the sound.

Not the sound you hear—the drone, the shriek, the subsonic pulse. That sound is the plateau's complaint, the fact of its own existence crying out. The third one is beneath that sound, or within it, or is the silence that the sound is trying to fill. It is the reason the sound exists. It is the reason the sound must exist, because if the sound stopped, if the groaning and shrieking and pulsing ceased for even a moment, the third one would be exposed, and it cannot be exposed, because exposure is a form of vulnerability, and vulnerability is a form of life, and the third one is not alive, or is alive in a way that—

It is the memory of a sound. That is the closest you can come. Imagine a sound that was once made in a place where sounds could be made, a sound that was important, a sound that meant something so deeply that the meaning attached itself to the air itself, to the stone, to the space between molecules. And then imagine that place ending, the conditions that allowed the sound to exist dissolving, the air thinning to nothing, the molecules drifting apart. The sound would have to go somewhere. It would have to persist in the only medium that remains: the medium of the sound itself, the vibration that has become its own container, the meaning that has become its own matter.

The third one is that. It is a sound that remembers being made. It is a meaning that has outlived the form that held it. It lives in the spaces between the frequencies the plateau produces, in the gaps where the drone drops and the shriek pauses and the pulse skips a beat. If you are quiet—if you can be quiet, if you can hold your breath until your lungs ache and your vision dims—you might hear it. A single note, almost inaudible, almost not there. A note that is not a note but a word, or was a word, or will be a word when the universe finishes inventing the language it belongs to.

It is saying your name.

It is saying a name you have never had and will never have but that belongs to you more truly than the name your parents chose, the name your government recorded, the name your lovers whispered in the dark. It is saying the name that you were before you were born, the name you will return to when you die, the name that is the shape of your absence when you are not here to hear it saying it.

---

The fourth one is you.

Not the you that walks, that breathes with lungs that should not work at this altitude, that feels the dry cracking of lips and the pulse in the temples that might be altitude sickness or might be the plateau learning how to be your pulse, borrowing your rhythm, preparing to incorporate you the way it has incorporated everything else that has stood on its surface and failed to leave.

The fourth one is the you that watches the you that walks. It stands at a slight remove, an arm's length from your own perception, and observes. It is not a soul, not in any sense the word has ever meant. It is more like a point of view that has decided to become independent, to take up residence in the space behind your eyes, to watch from there as the body it once inhabited moves through the pale forest and touches the warm walls and listens to the sound that is saying a name that is not your name.

The fourth one is curious about the third one. It leans toward the almost-inaudible note, strains to hear, reaches for a meaning it almost grasps. And in that reaching, in that straining, it forgets for a moment to watch. It becomes absorbed. It becomes part of the sound, part of the meaning, part of the name that is being said in a language that has not been invented yet.

And then you are back in your body, walking through the pale forest, and the sound is just sound again, geological and meaningless, and the fourth one is watching from behind your eyes, and you cannot remember what the note was, what the name was, what you almost became before you remembered to be what you already were.

---

There are others.

In the fissures, the dark places that go inward rather than simply away from light, something waits that has learned to live without light at all. It is not blind—blindness requires the concept of sight to be negated. It has simply never needed sight, has evolved or emerged or been generated in conditions where sight would be a waste of resources, where the only information that matters is the information that comes through pressure and temperature and the slow chemical changes in the stone around it. It knows you are there the way the stone knows you are there—with resentment, with curiosity, with the slow patience of a thing that measures time in geological epochs and therefore sees your visit as a brief flicker, a momentary warmth on a surface that will cool again, that has cooled before, that will cool again after.

In the canopy, in the spaces between the silver-amber leaves, something rides the slow currents of air that you cannot feel. It is not a bird. It is not a insect. It is a shape that the air has taken when the air is tired of being formless, a temporary configuration that arises and dissipates and arises again, never the same shape twice, never lasting long enough to be studied, never stopping long enough to be caught. It has no interest in you. It is interested only in the movement, the privilege of being movement itself, of embodying the thing that everything on the plateau is denied: change that is not slow, transformation that is not geological, a form that exists for its own sake and not as a phase in some vast, patient becoming.

In the dead-leaf mat, in the pale carpet of fallen trunks that crunch like dry insects, something eats. Not animals—nothing eats here the way animals eat, with teeth and stomachs and the efficient machinery of predation. This something eats by proximity. It consumes the structure of the dead things, the architecture of their decay, the pattern of their failure. It takes the information contained in a fallen trunk—the growth rings, the knots, the record of seasons that never came in a world that has no seasons—and it digests that information, incorporates it, uses it to become something more complex, something that is neither alive nor dead but is instead the act of remembering what living and dying meant before those words existed.

And beyond the haze, where the air stops being air and becomes the idea of air, something watches. It has always watched. It will always watch. It is not curious about you—it knows what you are, has known since before you climbed the slopes that led to this plateau, has known since before the plateau was a plateau, since before the stone was stone, since before the distinction between stone and not-stone had been drafted and abandoned and drafted again. It watches because watching is what it does, because it is made of attention, because the act of observing is the only act it has ever been capable of, and it is incapable, finally, of looking away.

It is the reason the weirdness is a property of the place. It is the reason the weirdness exists at all. Without it, the plateau would be merely strange—a high place, a dead place, a place where life has found strange ways to persist in the absence of its usual requirements. But with it watching, with its attention pressing down from beyond the haze, the strangeness becomes more than strangeness. It becomes a response. It becomes a message, sent in a code that no one on the plateau can read, addressed to someone who is not you, written in a language that has not yet been invented for a reader who has not yet been born.

You are standing in the middle of that message. Your body is the period at the end of a sentence that has been forming since before the sentence was a concept. Your breath, which you should not be able to draw at this altitude, which you draw anyway because the alternative is to stop, to become part of the message as punctuation rather than as meaning—your breath is a word, or part of a word, or the space between words where the meaning lives.

You do not know what the message says.

You are not sure you want to.

But you will stay. You will walk deeper into the pale forest, will touch more walls, will listen for the note that is saying the name that is not your name. You will let the first one walk beside you, will let its curiosity brush against your skin. You will let the fourth one watch from behind your eyes. You will let the plateau learn your weight, your warmth, the particular pressure of your presence on stone that hasn't decided what it is yet.

And eventually, when you have been here long enough for time to finish being slow, when the pale sun has shifted enough degrees for the shift to mean something, you will become part of the message too. You will become a groove in the wall, a ring in the fallen trunk, a vibration in the sound that is saying the name that is not your name.

You will become what lives here.

And the new ones who come—if new ones come, if the paths that brought you here re-form for others—will walk through the pale forest and touch the warm walls and listen for the note, and they will feel, without knowing why, that something is missing from the message, some word they cannot quite hear, some name they cannot quite remember.

And the fourth one will watch them from behind their own eyes.

And the first one will walk beside them.

And the plateau will learn their weight.

And they will stay.
