You come to understand the fungi first.

Not because they are first, or because they are important, but because they are the only things in the Bone Vaults that have the courtesy to be visible. They grow in alcoves where the faintest ambient luminescence pools—and here is a thing worth understanding: there is light here, very little of it, but enough, and it has pooled in certain places the way water might, the way any fluid finds its level. The fungi grew toward this pooling the way a root grows toward the dark below a stone. Slowly. Without decision. The way gravity does not decide to pull.

They are not green. They are not any color your eyes were prepared for. They are something like the color of old paper seen through a lens, something like the color of bone when the bone has dried to the texture of chalk, and they grow in whorls that spiral inward toward a center that is not a flower and not a mouth and not anything you could name. They do not photosynthesize. Nothing in the Bone Vaults photosynthesizes. Instead they metabolize heat—the gradients between the warm stone below and the cooler air above, differentials so slight they would mean nothing to you, but over centuries they accumulate, and the fungi grow, and their bioluminescence is a byproduct of this accumulation, a kind of slow thermal breathing made visible.

The skitters eat them. You watch this happen, or nearly happen, over a long time standing still. The skitters do not have mouths, or if they have mouths they are not mouths you can see. What they have is membrane-skin, and they press themselves against the filament-whorls and absorb something, and the something is not the fungi's light but something the fungi released, some eccrine secretion, some warmth given off by a body that has been metabolizing for three hundred years. The skitters grow. They are always growing.

This is the first living thing in the Bone Vaults that is not patient. The fungi are patient. The stone is patient. The warmth rising from below is patient in the way that geology is patient, which is to say that patience is the wrong word for it, because patience implies an end-state toward which one is being patient, and the warmth has no such thing. The warmth simply *is*, has been, will be, the way the dark simply is. But the skitters move. They cluster and disperse and cluster again in currents, the way iron filings align to a field that is not there, and you cannot find the field, and you cannot map the alignment, and you cannot determine whether they are fleeing something or toward something or simply being moved the way the air is moved, which is to say: not at all, because the air does not move, because the air is too still to move, because stillness here is the thing that moves through the tunnels like a held breath moving through a throat.

There is something between the stone and the skitter. Some relationship your body registers before your mind can parse it. You feel it as pressure—not against your skin but against the air inside your chest, a faint increment of weight, as though the tunnels have become aware of you the way a dog might become aware of a fly: not with attention, but with the dim, constant sensation of something alien crawling at the edge of sensation. This is not fear. Fear would require something in the Bone Vaults to be capable of it. This is the unconscious registration of dynamic presence in a system that has been static for so long the static has calcified, the way the walls have not yet calcified because the geology is young, the way nothing here has had time to soften, the way the angles of the carved shapes are still sharp even though they were carved by beings who are not here anymore and were not here for longer than you can bear to calculate.

The builders. You think about them the way you might think about a scar: the shape of something that is no longer there, imposed on a surface that changed after the wounding. They had hands. The evidence of this is irrefutable—you have touched the marks their tools left, the striations in the stone, the way the carved walls carry the memory of force applied and then withdrawn. They had tools. Stone tools, or tools of bone, or tools of something that has no name because it dissolved centuries before the scholars arrived with their lamps and their certainty. They shaped things. Walls that serve no wall-function. Arches that lead nowhere. Steles arranged in patterns that might be language or might be ornament or might be nothing at all, and you need to sit with this third possibility for a while, because it is the hardest one to hold: that something was made with intention and care and forethought and that it was made *for no reason at all*, that meaning was attempted and achieved and that the achievement was meaningless to anyone but the maker. This is not beyond your understanding. You have seen art.

But the builders are not here. They left no bodies. They left no waste. They left only the shapes, and the shapes are old enough to have worn smooth in places where the skitters cluster thickest, as though the skitters have been rubbing against them for so long that the stone has begun to mirror the skitters' jointless, formless patience. Or perhaps not. Perhaps the stone wore smooth on its own, and the skitters are drawn to smooth stone the way a hand is drawn to a rounded edge, and the two things are unrelated and you are only seeing pattern because you need to see pattern, because you are a warm-blooded creature walking through a cold and silent world and pattern is the thing that keeps you upright.

You find the pool and you understand it as you find it: slowly, and then all at once.

It is not water. You need to say this because your eyes keep insisting it is water, the way a starving person sees bread in every surface, and you must override your eyes because the pool is the most important thing in the Bone Vaults and your eyes are the least important thing in your head right now. The pool is amber-sap. It is pitch. It is something your language has no word for, not for lack of vocabulary but for lack of referent, because there has never been anything like this in any world you were built to understand. It moves, if you are generous with the word *move*. It is thick and slow and dense, and at its center something is glowing with a light that is not the pale, patient light of the fungi but something deeper, something that has been still for so long that stillness has become its only state, and you have the irrational conviction that if you touched it you would feel not cold and not heat but *time*, the specific texture of time that has passed without incident, without change, without anything to mark its passage except the slow accumulation of whatever the pool is made of, whatever is pooling at the heart of this place that was carved for no reason and kept for no purpose and maintained by no one and yet *maintained*, still here, still pooling, still still.

Around it the shapes are older. The carved walls are worn to near-smoothness. The skitters are denser here but do not approach the pool, and you understand this as a kind of rule, a law written into the softite bodies of the skitters the way the law of gravity is written into your bones: they do not approach the pool because the pool is not food, because the pool is not a gradient, because the pool is not anything that their metabolism can parse. The pool simply exists, and their instinct—which is not instinct but something older, something geological, something as unyielding as the walls themselves—keeps them at the edges of the gallery, where the light pools and the fungi grow and the warmth rises and everything is in its place and nothing moves except slowly and nothing changes except over centuries.

You do not touch it. You are not sure, standing there, whether *not touching* is a choice you are making or whether you are simply unable to move your hand. The difference does not seem important. What seems important is this: the pool is the heart of the Bone Vaults, and the Bone Vaults are a body, and you have been walking through the body of something that is still alive in the way that stone is alive, in the way that time is alive, in the way that a word is alive after the speaker has died.

You walk on. The tunnels go deeper. The warmth rises from below, patient and absolute.

Far away, or nowhere, or inside your own skull, something that is not a voice says: *nothing here has ever changed.*

And it is right. It is right and it has always been right, and the rightness of it is not a comfort but a fact, and facts in the Bone Vaults are older than the stone, and the stone is old enough to have worn smooth if it had been touched enough, and nothing has been touched enough, and the skitters are still moving in their currents, and the fungi are still metabolizing heat-gradients, and the pool is still pooling, and the dark is still eating your lamp at the center, and you are still walking, and walking is the only thing you can do, and you do it, and the Bone Vaults let you.

They do not welcome you. They do not reject you. They do not notice you. You walk through like a dream, and the dream walks on, and the tunnels go deeper, and the warmth rises, and nothing changes.
