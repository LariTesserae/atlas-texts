You learn, eventually, to read the mat the way you once read weather. The low pulse of its breathing tells you something — not the time of day, not the weather in any sense you brought with you, but something about the health of the whole, the state of the circulation, whether the channels are flowing freely or whether there is congestion somewhere in the system. You cannot locate the congestion. It is not that kind of knowledge. But you feel it in your feet, in the way the membrane yields differently on some days, a little slower to accept your weight, a little warmer beneath it, as though the mat is running a fever in those places. You think of the great fevers of history, the plagues that burned through populations, and you wonder what a plague would look like here — whether the mat would fight it, or whether it would simply grow around it, incorporate it, digest the pathogen the way it has digested the stick you used to probe it, the way it will digest you.

You do not think this thought often. You think it every day.

The stick is gone. You remember pushing it down through the membrane, the resistance that gave way, the darkness below — and you remember looking back later and finding the ground seamless, the hole closed, the stick's existence acknowledged only by a faint discoloration in the mat, a darker green that fades over the course of hours until there is nothing, not even a scar. The mat healed itself. Or healed the wound you made in it. Or dismissed the intrusion, absorbed the foreign matter, converted it. You do not know which, and the not-knowing is the thing that lives in you now, not the stick.

You begin to test things. You did not mean to, but curiosity is a bodily function, and your body is asking questions: What is the mat's relationship to time? What does it do to matter that has been placed in it? You drop a stone — smooth, waterworn, carried from somewhere else, from before — and watch it disappear into the membrane with a slowness that is not falling but absorption, the way water is drawn into sand. The stone goes. The mat closes. You wait, and after a long time you see no trace of it, no darker patch, no sign. You drop another, and another, and they go the same way, and you begin to understand that this is how the mat feeds on the world: not by consuming organisms (there are none) but by consuming *matter*, by pulling inorganic material into its metabolism, converting stone and sand and silt into more of itself. The raw geology rising from below is not substrate. It is *food*. The young rock pushing upward is walking directly into the mouth of the world, and the world is hungry for it, has always been hungry for it, will be hungry until there is nothing left to rise and nothing left to close over it.

You try to find the edge. You walk for what feels like days, though the light never changes in any way you can measure, and the mat never thins, and the canopy never opens. At some point you stop because you realize you have not been walking *toward* anything. You have been walking *within* something, and within-ness does not have edges. You could walk in any direction for any length of time and you would arrive, if you arrived anywhere, at more of the same — more mat, more fog, more dark stone barely visible through the seams where the ground shifts. You are inside a thing that has no boundary. You are inside a thing that does not know it is a thing. It is simply what is, the way water is wet and gravity is heavy.

You try to sleep. You cannot sleep the way you slept before, in the open air, under a sky that moved. Here, sleep is a different kind of thing. You lie down on the mat and it accepts your weight and holds you, and the low pulse of the organism's breathing rocks you the way water rocks a boat, and the hum sits in your skull, and you do not dream. You float. You are suspended in the body of the world, and there are no dreams because there is no separation, no boundary between you and the thing you are inside, and for a moment — a long moment, a moment that might be hours — you do not know where your body ends and the mat begins. Your fingers are fronds. Your breath is the exhalation. The water in your blood is the water in the channels, and the channels are your veins, and the vein is the river, and the river is the thing rising from the stone to feed, to grow, to become more of what it already is.

You come back. You always come back. But the returning takes longer each time.

There is a place where the mat has parted more deeply than usual, a crack wide enough to admit your arm, and in that crack you see not stone but something else — a darkness that is not stone-dark, not the raw unweathered darkness of the geology. This is a different darkness. It has *texture*. It has depth. You cannot see into it because there is no light, but you feel it, the way you feel a drop of water on skin you thought was dry. You feel it seeing you. Not with eyes. Not with anything you can name. But something in that darkness knows you are there, knows you have a shape and a temperature and a heart that beats in a rhythm it does not share, and the knowing is not hostile and is not benign. It is simply *knowledge*, the way a root knows the resistance of soil, the way a vein knows the direction of flow. You are known. You are matter that has not yet been absorbed. You are a stone waiting to be swallowed.

You pull your arm back. The crack closes around it as you withdraw — not quickly, not slowly, but with the patience of something that has been closing since before you arrived and will continue closing long after you are gone. You look at the place where the crack was and there is no crack. There is only mat. There is only green, and the faint pulse, and the hum, and the fog, and the light that does not fall on anything long enough to warm it.

You begin to notice your own absence. Not physically — you are still here, still breathing the wet air, still feeling the mat accept your weight with every step. But you notice the places where you should be leaving marks and are not. Your footprints close behind you, the membrane sealing as though you had never passed. The places where you have sat are no different from the places where you have not. The stick is gone. The stones are gone. You are still here, but you are here the way a fly is on a window: present, and soon to be gone, and not, in any meaningful sense, *part of it*.

You begin to wonder if you are already gone.

The hum shifts. The quarter-tone difference, the phase difference, the sleeper adjusting to a fly — you know this pattern now, you have learned to recognize it, and you stand very still as you always do, and you wait, and the hum settles, and you continue not knowing whether the shift was about you. Whether the mat felt you, felt the foreign warmth of your body, the foreign chemistry of your breath, the foreign rhythm of your blood. Whether it is trying to decide what to do with you. Whether it has already decided, and you simply have not yet arrived at the part of the process that looks like a decision.

The fog sits in the low places like something sleeping. You sit with it. You are learning its shapes — the way it pools in certain hollows, the way it moves through the frond-rows like a slow current, the way it thickens at dusk and thins at what might be dawn and never fully disappears. The fog is part of the mat's respiration. It is the exhaled moisture catching the cold of the upper canopy, the thermal differential that drives the whole system. You are sitting inside a breath that has been breathing for longer than your species has had language. You are sitting inside a sentence in a language you do not yet read. You do not know if you will ever read it. You do not know if reading is something this world permits. It does not have readers. It has only the words themselves, the vast proliferation of fronds and channels and layers, the syntax of growth, the grammar of water cycling upward from the air and downward through the stone, the meaning of the whole thing accumulating somewhere you cannot reach, in a register too slow for your life to hear.

You are still here. The mat is still here. The stone is still rising.

And somewhere beneath the stone, beneath the channels, beneath the layers of living tissue that have not yet been named, in the darkness that has texture and depth and knows you are there, something is thinking. Not thinking the way you think. Not thinking in words or images or even concepts as you would recognize them. But thinking nonetheless. The way a root thinks when it finds water. The way a system thinks when it balances. The way a sentence thinks when it is finished, when the last word has been placed and the meaning is whole and the only thing left is to speak it aloud into the silence, into the dark, into the waiting air.

You do not know what the sentence says. You do not know if it is already finished or if it is still growing. You only know that you are in it. That you have always been in it. That you will not leave it, because there is no edge to leave, and there is no outside, and there is no other language to go back to.

The mat accepts your weight. The hum sits in your skull. The fog breathes.

And the world goes on being itself, and you go on being in it, and neither of you goes on being quite what you were before.
