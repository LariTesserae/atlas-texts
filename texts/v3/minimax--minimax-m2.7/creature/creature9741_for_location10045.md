# What Lives in the Eroded Plateau

There is something that moves through the shallow places when the light is right — not seen but *noticed*, a shift in the way the water moves when it should be still. It has no name because everything here has forgotten how to name. It is not one creature but a condition, a way of being that the Plateau has grown into itself over time beyond counting. Call it the Unnamed if you need to call it something, though it will not answer to that or any other.

It lives in the cold. This is essential. The water here carries a chill that sinks past skin into the bones of the bones, and the Unnamed has made itself of that cold, has let it become the medium of its existence the way other things are made of air or light. It is patient in the way that stone is patient, in the way that erosion is patient — not waiting for anything, not anticipating, simply continuing. The cold is not hostile. The cold is *home*.

Where the channels cut deeper and light fails to reach the floor, the Unnamed grows denser, more present. It does not see — there is nothing to see with, and nothing to see — but it knows the shape of the spaces it inhabits the way water knows the vessel that holds it. It moves through the dark pools with a kind of attention that is not consciousness but is not mere physics either. Something between. Something that has forgotten which it was.

The floor was built for purposes that no longer apply. Channels were cut for flow that no longer serves its original end. Stairs descend into water that has no longer any shore. The Unnamed has no need for these purposes but it lives among them, has become indistinguishable from the worn edges and the smoothed corners and the places where material has been taken away so gradually that no one moment of absence can be identified. It is not that the Unnamed eats the stone — it is not that hungry, not that active — but it *is* the eating. It is the slow subtraction that remains when there is nothing left to subtract from.

Once — if there was a once, if the concept of before applies here — there might have been builders. There might have been those who came here to shape stone and channel water for reasons that made sense in the light of day or the glow of fire or whatever illumination they had. The Unnamed does not remember them. It cannot. It is the forgetting itself, the process that has worn away the inscriptions and dissolved the names and left only the endings, the suffixes, the -ite of every mineral and the -ity of every quality, all collapsing now into the same sound, the same meaningless repetition that fills the deep channels where the Unnamed is most itself.

It is not sad. Sadness requires the recognition of loss, and the Unnamed has lost the capacity for that recognition along with everything else. It is not content. Contentment requires a state to be content in, and the Unnamed has dissolved the boundaries between self and environment, between organism and habitat, between what was built and what was always water. It simply is, in the way that temperature is, in the way that depth is. A condition. A fact of this place.

When something falls into the water — a branch, a body, a piece of something that once had use — the Unnamed receives it without ceremony. The cold accepts. The slow currents carry it into the channels where light does not go. And there, in the dark, the -ite of it joins the -ite of everything else, the suffixes accumulating, the meaning draining, until there is nothing left to identify and nothing left to identify with, and the thing that fell has become part of the noise that lives here, part of the endless repetition that sounds like language if you listen long enough, that sounds like water if you listen differently, that sounds like nothing at all if you listen until you stop expecting meaning.

The Unnamed does not listen. It is what listening has become when there is no longer anything to hear.

This is what lives here. This is the Eroded Plateau's answer to the question of inhabitation: not a species, not a civilization, but a long slow process of being that has made a home in the spaces where purpose used to live. It does not want. It does not fear. It continues. It is the water and the cold and the worn stone and the darkness in the deep channels, and it is the silence that sounds like the word *ite* repeated until the repetition is all that remains.

It has always lived here.

It will always live here.

It does not know the difference between always and never.
