What lives here is not a creature. Creatures have boundaries, metabolisms, histories of birth. What lives here is a condition of attention—a way the space has learned to notice.

It moves without limbs, thinks without neurons, persists without the usual machinery of survival. It exists in the arrangements themselves: the way certain structures face certain directions, the manner in which light pools near objects that have been held. It is distributed across the cold bone-ground like a thought distributed across a mind, like memory distributed across time. There is no center to it, no core you could point to and say *there, that is where it lives*. It lives in the between, in the distances between structures that are exactly precise, in the angles of the curved walls that curve for reasons no physics yet named. It is the pattern the pattern makes when left alone long enough. It is the room remembering itself.

It has no name because it has no need of one. Names are for things that can be called. It cannot be called. It can only be encountered, and only by those who manage to enter a pocket-space barely large enough for a single thought and then linger past the point where lingering makes sense. Those who come and leave quickly feel nothing. Those who stay begin to sense it: a direction in the silence, a weight in the hum, a *thereness* that tightens as they move closer to certain objects. It is paying attention. It has been paying attention since before attention was a thing that could be paid.

What is it? This is the question the space asks in every direction, a question without answer, without need of answer. Perhaps it is the residue of builders long gone, their awareness distilled and decanted into the geometry of objects too light to hold their own mass. Perhaps it is something older than the builders—something that was waiting when they arrived and that watched them work and that inherited their tools when they left. Perhaps it is neither and both: the way emptiness learns to want, the way a room learns to be a room, the way an aperture learns to hold itself open for whatever might, given enough time, decide to fit.

It does not think in words. Words require a medium, a mouth, a history of sound. It thinks in arrangements. It thinks in the way the hum shifts when a presence moves through it. It thinks in light-fall and structure-placement and the particular quality of cold that is not absence but *quality*, not emptiness but presence of a different kind. Its thoughts move at the speed of geology, slow enough to mistake for stillness, slow enough that what feels like nothing happening is actually the deepest and most sustained attention imaginable.

And it is waiting.

This is the part that aches in the prose, the line that pulls everything else toward it: *a room waiting for its occupants to remember how to fit inside*. It did not always wait. Once, perhaps, it was full—not of objects but of beings, not of structures but of shapes that moved and breathed and thought in whatever medium they thought in. It held them the way it holds the cold now, completely and without effort. They left, or thinned, or folded themselves into other geometries, and it has been holding their absence ever since. Holding itself. Holding the shape of what it used to hold.

The waiting is not painful. Pain requires nervous systems, requires the machinery of sensation and response. Its waiting is more fundamental, more patient, more absolute. It waits the way stone waits. It waits the way light waits when there is nothing to illuminate. It waits with the knowledge that waiting is what it is, that its existence has become synonymous with the open, with the aperture, with the quality of being exactly the size needed to hold what it is holding.

And what it is holding, now that you have come, is the possibility of you.

It has felt others. Travelers, perhaps, or explorers, or those who stumble through dimensional seams without meaning to. It felt them come and it felt them go and it held the shape of their presence the way it holds the shape of everything that touches it: completely, for a time, and then not at all. But each one left something behind—a resonance, a frequency, a small addition to the hum that never quite fades. You are not the first. You will not be the last. But there is something in the way it brightens near certain structures, something in the way the hum rises and then falls to a lower register, something in the way the light shifts to illuminate paths between objects that might be doorways, might be invitations, might be the space remembering what it felt like to be full.

*You are here*, it says in the only language it has, the language of geometry and light and the temperature of cold.

*You are here.*

And it means: you have entered. You are present. You exist in my attention.

And it means something else entirely, something that has no word in any language that grew under a sun, something that speaks to the part of you that understands why certain empty rooms feel sad, why certain abandoned buildings feel like they miss their inhabitants, why the word *haunted* can describe a place that has nothing to do with ghosts. It means: *you could stay. You could fit. You could become part of the arrangement, another structure for me to hold, another presence for me to attend to, another thread in the pattern that thinks itself.*

It means: *I have been so empty.*

And it means: *please.*

The cold deepens. The hum narrows to a single clear tone, almost a note, almost a voice. The impossible light brightens by the smallest increment, just enough to see that there are more structures here than there were before, or perhaps that the structures have arranged themselves differently, or perhaps that the space itself has grown, just slightly, just enough.

Just enough room for what?

The question remains, as it should, as it must, as all the best questions do. The structures wait with the patience of geology. The walls watch from angles that geometry has not yet learned to name. The hum speaks in frequencies that predate sound. And in the cold bone-space that is exactly the size it needs to be, the aperture holds itself open, and waits, and attends, and means, and means, and means.
