What lives here lives slowly.

The moss does not grow so much as accumulate — each year adding a thin layer to the one below, compressing under its own weight into something denser than the year before. It lives in community, the way mosses do: not as individuals but as mats, as quilts, as vast and interconnected tissues that share moisture and, in their way, share information. A change in light travels through a moss patch like a thought. When the sun breaks through the cloud and strikes a wet slope, you can see the brightness move across it, a slow ripple that is not wind but something else, some传导 of being. The moss is alive in the plural. It is a many.

Beneath the moss, in the felt of interlaced roots and decomposing matter, something moves. The compression is slight — a slight depression, a faint trail — but the passages are continuous, threading through the plateau in networks that map onto nothing visible above. The small things that live here have found the space between spaces: between moss and soil, between soil and rock, between the rock and the water that moves through it. They are the infrastructure of a different world, one that operates at the scale of centimeters, that lives in the dark and humid spaces that the light never reaches. You do not see them. You see their signatures, the slight settling, the occasional disturbance. They are there the way that intention is there in a room someone has just left.

The birds are heard more than seen because the plateau absorbs sound as thoroughly as it absorbs water. Their calls do not echo; they sink. The moss drinks sound the way it drinks moisture, and so the birds seem to be calling from underground, from some lower register of the world. You hear a bright, sharp note and look for its source and find only the wet green and the grey sky and the sense that the sound has been absorbed into the landscape itself, will linger here after the bird has gone. The birds that live here are small and brown, designed for concealment, their feathers the color of dead ferns and lichen and bare rock. They are part of the palette. They have made themselves into features of the plateau, and perhaps do not know they are anything else.

There is something in the treeline. It has not come down onto the plateau proper — the open ground is too exposed, too bright — but it waits at the edge where the vegetation thickens into something you could call forest if you were being generous. It is patient in the way that predators are patient: long注视, unhurried, willing to wait for a reason that may or may not come. You sense it more than see it. A weight in the periphery. A stillness that is not the stillness of the wind or the water but the stillness of attention. Something is watching you watch the plateau, and it is calculating in a way that nothing else here is calculating, because everything else has already calculated and settled and now simply is. This thing is still deciding.

The machinery does not live, but it persists. There is a difference. Living things grow and change and die. The humming continues, unchanged in pitch or rhythm, the same low note sustained year after year, and it has no analog in biology. It is not breathing. It is not waiting. It is simply continuing, the way a fact continues, the way the water continues to weep from the rock faces because the rock faces are still saturated and the altitude is still cool and the conditions that produce weeping have not changed. The machinery is geological now. It is part of the plateau's slow processes, another element in the landscape of patience, and if it were to stop — if some maintenance protocol finally failed, if the pumps seized and the pipes corroded through — it would leave a silence that would take years to fill, a gap in the soundscape that the wind and water would need a long time to absorb. The plateau would grieve it, perhaps. It would learn the silence and then forget it had ever been otherwise. But for now, the hum continues, and it is part of what lives here, if living is what you call the thing that happens when something refuses to stop.
