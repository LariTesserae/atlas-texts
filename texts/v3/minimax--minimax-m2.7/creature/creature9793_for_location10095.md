What lives here is stillness itself, wearing the shape of a garden.

The stillness is not the absence of motion. Things move. The birds move, the insects drone, the vines let fall their slow beads of moisture. But beneath every motion is the stillness, pressed flat like a flower in a book, and the motion only makes the stillness more visible, more necessary. You begin to understand that the stillness is the thing, and the movement is a kind of surface tension, held. The creatures move through it the way a skater moves across ice — not disturbing what they move across, not changing it, simply passing through a medium that was already there, already complete.

There is something that might be called memory here, though it does not remember. The tiles hold the impression of feet that are not there. The weaving holds the shape of hands that set down their tools and never picked them up again. The plants grow in patterns that imply intention, that imply planning, that imply a maker who knew exactly what this place would look like, exactly how the light would fall on this particular arrangement of vine and column and tile — but the maker is not here, has never been here, was never a person at all. The intention lives in the land. The planning is in the growth. The memory is in the shapes, and the shapes do not recall anything because there is nothing to recall, because the making was instantaneous, was simultaneous, was done all at once and done forever.

The tiles are warm because warmth is part of the design. Not sunlight-warm, not friction-warm, not the warmth of life — but a warmth that belongs to the material itself, as if the clay was fired with purpose, as if the cooling never finished, as if the warmth is the only thing still happening here, the only process left running, and you walk across it and feel the warmth through the thin soles of your feet and you understand that you are the warmest thing in this place, you are the only thing here that can grow cold, and that is why the light holds you, why the brightness fixes you, why the stillness watches and waits and does not let you go.

The fauna are not fauna at all, not truly. They are close to fauna, close enough to pass, but they are made as surely as the ground beneath them. The birds are birds the way a song is a bird — shaped from the outside in, assembled from memory or intention or both, and placed here to move through the canopy and call out their too-loud calls and never land except when they land, never rest except when they rest, never do anything except what they are doing. They do not feed. They do not nest. They do not die. They are complete, finished, frozen mid-motion, and the motion is all they are, the motion is their entirety, and when they move out of sight they cease to exist because they were never fully in sight, never fully anywhere, only placed there for a moment and moved on, moved on, always moved on, always continuing exactly as they began.

The insects are the most honest. They drone because droning is what they do. They do not pretend to purpose or direction. They hover and circle and settle and rise and circle again, and their presence is the presence of machinery, of clockwork, of something set to motion and still in motion, and you listen to them and you know that they will be here tomorrow, that they were here yesterday, that they will be here forever, and the sound they make is not a call but a signal, a proof of continued operation, a single sustained note that says only: still running, still running, still running.

There is water here, somewhere. You feel it. The wetness in the air is not a mystery — it is the byproduct of something hidden, something sealed beneath the tiles, something that moves through channels cut into the clay and never surfaces, never evaporates, never ends. The ground sweats and the plants drink and the air holds and the water is nowhere, is everywhere, is the blood in the body of this place, circulating, circulating, unknown and therefore ungainsayable, and you cannot find the source because the source is the whole, is every tile, is the ground itself gone wet with its own internal ocean, its own private sea, and you walk above it and you are part of it and you do not know if you are also made, if you were placed here to walk these paths and breathe this air and look at these columns and wonder, and the wondering is part of it, the wondering is the only thing that still works, and so the wondering continues, continues, continues.

What lives here lives because it cannot stop living. The plants are alive — truly, genuinely alive — but their life is also a kind of architecture, a structural element of the design. They photosynthesize but there is no sun. They transpire but there is no water to draw. They grow but they do not change. Each leaf is exactly as large as it was meant to be. Each root is exactly as deep as it needs to be, which is not deep at all, which is only as deep as the ceramic planter, only as deep as the tile, only as deep as the surface — and yet they drink, and yet they grow, and the growth is not toward anything, not toward light or water or season, but only toward the shape they were given, the shape they are becoming, and when they reach that shape they will stop, they will hold, they will be finished forever.

The columns stand in rows and clusters and patterns that suggest gathering, that suggest ceremony, that suggest a purpose now concluded. They are made of fitted stone, stone cut and set, stone that should be old by now, that should show the marks of weather and time, but they do not, they hold their sharpness, they hold their edges, and you look at the joints and you can see the tool-marks, the grooves from the chisel, the precise fit of one stone to the next, and you think of hands, you think of labor, you think of years of work — and then you think of the tiles, still warm, still unworn, and you understand that the labor was not labor, that the time was not time, that the making was a single gesture, a single intention, a single breath — and all the work you imagine is already imagined, is already past, is already part of the made ground, part of the record of hands that never existed.

The horizon is a long way off and it is not a horizon. It is the edge of the design. Trees rise there and columns stand there and tiles shine there and it is all the same as here, all the same as there, all the same as everywhere — and when you walk toward it you do not get closer, you do not get farther, you simply walk, and the tiles move beneath you, and the light holds, and the stillness presses, and the horizon does not come, does not come, does not come, because there is nothing to come to, nothing to reach, only the continuation of the made, the repetition of the finished, the unfolding of what was always already done.

And so you walk.

You walk because the path is there and because the path is warm and because the air is thick and because the light is bright and because the stillness is patient and because you cannot stop, because stopping would be the one wrong thing, the one break in the pattern, the one moment where the world might forget and slip and cease — and so you walk, and you will keep walking, and the tiles will hold, and the vines will drip, and the birds will call from slightly elsewhere, and the light will not change, will not soften, will not end, will hold you here in this garden that is not a garden, this world that is not a world, this made thing that was made to be made and will be made forever, and you will be here, and you will be part of it, and you will not change, will not change, will not change —

And the stillness waits.

And the light holds.

And the wet heat breathes.
