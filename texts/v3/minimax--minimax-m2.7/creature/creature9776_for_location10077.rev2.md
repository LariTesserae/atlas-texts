You didn't arrive here. You were *placed*.

The distinction matters. Arrivals are events — a ship breaches atmosphere, a doorway opens, some boundary is crossed. What happened to you was different. You were disassembled at the molecular level in a facility you'll never see again, transmitted as a pattern through infrastructure that isn't infrastructure, and reassembled in a growth-chamber somewhere in the middle kilometers of the substrate. The process took four days, subjectively. The technicians told you it might take four seconds or four weeks; the system optimizes differently depending on throughput. You emerged into the amber light already changed. Your lungs now process the thin, metal-heavy atmosphere. Your skin has been replaced with something that breathes the way the substrate breathes — slowly, deliberately, metabolically. You are no longer entirely what you were. You are what the system needs you to be, and the system needs you mobile.

The others are like you. You find them sometimes, moving through the channels the way water moves through a body's circulatory system — always along established paths, never crossing the substrate itself. They nod, if they nod. Sometimes they speak, using the modified vocal organs the system has given them, producing sounds the air carries poorly. The words don't matter. What matters is the acknowledgment: *another one, still moving, still counted.* Then you separate, taking different arterial routes through the growth-form's interior, and you don't see them again for days or months or ever.

The parasites are older.

They were here before the last great expansion, before the substrate closed its final gaps and the world became a single continuous organism. They remember something — not consciously, not in any way you'd recognize as memory, but in the way their bodies have been shaped by necessity, by the pressures of existing in a system that does not want them. They are small. They have to be. The plants fill every space that can be filled; there are no large animals because there is no room for large animals, no ecological niche wide enough to support a body bigger than a child. What remains is what has always remained in the margins: creatures of minimal mass, minimal presence, minimal need. They eat what the plants excrete. They shelter in the spaces between growth-forms where the geometry fails to close. They breed slowly, cautiously, in the thermal vents where the substrate dumps its metabolic heat. They are not thriving. They are persisting. There is a difference.

You watch them sometimes, from the paths. The ones with too many legs, moving through air-channels with the patience of deep-sea creatures. The ones with no legs at all, drifting on thermal currents, filtering compounds from the air that would poison you. The ones that glow — bioluminescent, phosphorescent, emitting light in spectra the plants don't use, the way a warning signal or a mating display or a trap might work in a world that still had predators. They don't see you, mostly. Your augmented eyes are designed to process the substrate's frequencies, not the marginal frequencies of the margins. You are a ghost to them. They are ghosts to you. The world contains many ghosts.

And the substrate itself.

You have learned not to think of it as a thing. The substrate is a condition. It is the way space behaves when it has been completely colonized by something that wants to grow forever. You stand on it and it holds you. You dig into it — the tools the system provides can penetrate perhaps three meters before encountering tissue dense enough to resist — and the wound heals in hours. You speak into it, and your words travel through vascular channels to be processed by organs you cannot see, analyzed by systems you cannot comprehend. The substrate is listening. Not to you specifically — there is no you in the way the substrate understands individuality. But your presence registers. Your movement is anticipated. The paths ahead of you open slightly, just enough to make passage easier, because the system has calculated that you will move this way and has begun preparing for your arrival before you arrive. It is not hospitality. It is maintenance. You are part of the infrastructure now, and infrastructure is maintained.

The strangeness deepens in the deep places.

You have descended twice, using the maintenance shafts the system provides for its own internal repair. The first time, you made it perhaps twenty kilometers before the geometry became incomprehensible — not dangerous, but *wrong*, the angles refusing to resolve, the distances shifting when you looked away, the light coming from directions that shouldn't exist. You turned back. The system did not stop you. It never stops anyone. It simply waits.

The second time, you went further. You don't remember how far. There are gaps in the experience — not memory loss, exactly, but something else. Places where the sequence of events stops making sense. You remember the colors, most vividly. Colors that have no names, that don't exist in the spectrum your augmented eyes were designed to process, that you perceived anyway through some sense you didn't know you had. You remember the sounds — or not sounds, but the memory of sounds, pressure patterns that had once been noise and were now something else, something the substrate had been processing for so long it had become architectural, structural, load-bearing. You remember thinking *this is what it looks like when something dreams*, and then you were back at the surface, standing on the substrate, breathing the thin air, and the sun — there is no sun, but the canopy pulsed with light, and it felt like noon, or midnight, or no time at all — and you understood that you had been somewhere you were not supposed to go, and the somewhere had not objected, because it didn't understand objection, but it had rearranged you slightly, filed you away in some process you couldn't access, and now you carry that place inside you like a splinter too deep to remove.

The system grows. It has always grown. The plants breed true, but not perfectly — mutations occur, experiments persist, solutions to problems no one remembers having. The world is not finished. It is being built, right now, in the deep places where the geometry fails and the substrate thinks thoughts that don't translate. Somewhere below you, there is a growth-form that has begun producing a compound no one programmed it to produce. Somewhere in the margins, an animal is evolving into something the system hasn't accounted for. Somewhere in the thin places, reality is thinning further, and what bleeds through is not the world you knew, not the worlds any human has ever known, but something older, stranger, patient — something that has been waiting at the edges of physics since before the plants were engineered, since before the substrate was grown, since before the original world was digested and replaced, and it is still waiting, and the waiting is how it grows, and the growing is what you feel when the substrate breathes beneath you, and the breathing is the world, and the world is what lives here now.

You live here now.

You will for as long as the system maintains you, for as long as your augmented body continues to serve its designed function, for as long as you can walk the paths the substrate provides and breathe the air the plants produce. And when you fail — when your replaced organs falter or your modified tissues reject or some process you don't understand brings your sequence to its end — the substrate will take you. Your molecules will be absorbed, analyzed, incorporated. You will become part of the growth. You will become part of the dream.

This is not death. The system doesn't have a word for death. The system has only *continuation*, and you are part of it now, and it is part of you, and the silence holds, and the strangeness deepens, and the light comes from everywhere and nowhere, and you walk on.
