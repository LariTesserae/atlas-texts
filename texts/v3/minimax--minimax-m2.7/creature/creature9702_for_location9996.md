Light arrives here the way a rumor arrives—filtered, delayed, arriving from a direction that keeps changing. It pools in hollows where the canopy has not yet sealed shut, and the green-gold it paints on everything is so saturated it seems to be coming from beneath as much as above. You learn not to look for the sun. You learn to look where the light *lands*.

The water remembers roads. In the places where streets once organized the city's blood, moisture now pools or trickles in patterns that almost—but never quite—coincide with the old geometry. It seeks its own paths now, drawn upward through root-channels when the air grows dry, falling in falls that are less rain than exhalation. The water does not flow downhill here. It flows *where it is needed*, which is a different direction entirely.

And the singing. The water sings where it rises, at intersections of root and runoff and the slow chemistry of decay. The sound is a frequency the body recognizes even when the ears cannot name it—a tone that is also a texture, also a temperature. Something in the body was waiting for this sound. Something in the body knows what it means, even though it was never taught.

The light here moves like something alive. It doesn't fall—it *arrives*, the canopy deciding when and where to release it, keeping most for itself, spending it in careful increments across surfaces that drink the warmth deeper with each passing hour. Shadows are not absences but presences—green-black pools that gather in corners and root-cavities, and from which nothing emerges but which seem, unmistakably, to be attending.

Breathing becomes something you do *with* the place rather than *in* it. The air carries the pollen of flowers that have never been seen, spores that coat the throat with a taste of loam and copper, and if you breathe deeply enough you begin to feel your own lungs as just another chamber in the system, another hollow that the organism is content to keep warm.

There is no fauna here, but there is *appetite*. The roots drink from cisterns that were built for human use, from the residual moisture in foundations, from the slow seep of groundwater that bubbles up through the living floor of the plaza. The appetite is not hunger—it is *interest*. The city is interested in everything that enters it. The walls, when you press your palm against them, carry warmth that is not temperature but *welcoming*. The wood-grain pulses faintly beneath the pressure, and if you hold still long enough you can feel the slow hydraulic movement of water being pushed somewhere, everywhere, through channels that have no names.

And the geometry. The geometry is still learning itself. Corridors that seemed to lead somewhere familiar open onto chambers that were not there yesterday, cathedrals of root-vaulting where the light falls from sources that seem to be *above the above*, spaces that should not be able to hold themselves open but do, held by the same tension that holds a flower closed until it decides to become. The angles are not wrong—they are *unfinished*. The city is not rebuilding what was. It is *building what comes next*, and it has not decided yet what that is.

But here is what lives here: not plants in ruins, but a different kind of life entirely. Slow and immense and still very young, still making mistakes, still surprised by the shapes its own growth takes. The sound it makes is not wind through leaves—it is the sound of *construction*, endless and everywhere, the creak and groan of trunks finding their load-bearings, roots tasting the soil for the first time and deciding it is good. It has no language. It has something better—the ability to mean, to want, to *adjust*, to grow toward what it needs without knowing what it needs until it arrives. The choir does not sing music. The choir is *learning what music is for*.

The plaza fills with thatch-pools where the water sings, and the song is not the water's voice—it is the *city's* voice, speaking through its own throat, through roots and stems and the pressure of water lifting itself from stone. When you stand in the plaza and the song changes pitch, you feel it not in your ears but in your sternum, in the soft places where bone does not protect, and you understand that the city has just said something, has just adjusted its own temperature or circulation, and that the silence afterward is not silence but *completion*—the full stop at the end of a sentence that has been building for longer than anything made of wood and water should be able to say.

This is what lives here. This is what has always been waiting to live here.

Not an invasion. Not an overtaking. A *becoming*.

The city is not full of plants. The city is full of *attention*. It watches the light. It tastes the water. It listens to the pressure of its own roots pressing into soil that remembers being pavement. And when you walk through it, you are not walking through a thing. You are walking through a *someone*—a vast, slow, green-dark someone who has been dreaming of exactly this moment for longer than the concrete was wet, longer than the steel was cooling, long enough that it began to dream itself before it had a name, and now it is dreaming you, too, pressing you gently into its own architecture, asking what you will become if you stay long enough.

The answer is already growing.
