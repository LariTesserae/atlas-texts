What lives here is patient beyond all patience. It is the erosion itself — not as process but as presence, not as event but as will. It has been shaping this place so long that shape and shaper have become indistinguishable, the way a river and its canyon eventually seem to belong to each other. The erosion does not hurry; it does not need to. It knows that stone is merely time made patient, and time is merely stone waiting for permission to become something else. When you walk here, you walk on the work of something that does not think in human measures, that does not perceive in human senses, that simply continues because continuation is what it is. The fins of rock, the shelves, the faces that catch light at angles the mind refuses — these are not features but expressions, not landscape but language, the slow speech of a force that has all the time there is and uses none of it wastefully.

What lives here is the wind — but not as weather. The wind is more like a presence moving through a body, the way breath moves through a throat, the way thought moves through a mind. It is constant not because it cannot stop but because stopping would be a kind of forgetting, and the wind here has learned that forgetting is expensive. It flows over the stone and finds the frequencies hidden in the shapes, the resonances that the erosion has carved without intending them, and it makes them speak. The low tone that vibrates in the chest is not music; it is closer to speech, or to the sound a place makes when it has something to say but no mouth. The higher whistles are commentary, the deep groans are memory — something from the deep past, the time when the plateau was being born, surfacing briefly before sinking back into the stone. The wind carries moisture and carries cold and carries the faint mineral taste of dissolution, but these are incidental, secondary. What the wind carries primarily is attention. It pays attention to the stone the way a predator pays attention to a landscape, hungry but patient, learning the shapes of everything because shape is information and information is survival. The wind survives here; it has learned to be necessary.

What lives here is the cold — and this is harder to say, harder to think, because the cold is not a thing but a relationship. It lives in the negotiation between presence and absence, between body and air, between heat and its lack. It finds the gaps. This is not metaphor; it is behavior. The cold moves through the world the way water moves through stone, following the paths of least resistance, pooling in the low places, pressing against anything that resists. When you are here, the cold lives in the space between your clothing and your skin, in the places where heat escapes and cold enters, in the negotiation. You are not cold; you are the negotiation. The cold is not cold; it is the shape of the gap, the boundary between what is warm and what is not. At night, when the cold deepens, the negotiation becomes more intense, more total. The cold at night approaches finality not because it becomes absolute but because it becomes complete — every gap filled, every boundary made manifest, the entire landscape defined by what it is not. The cold is the plateau's way of knowing what you are, the test it gives to everything that arrives, the question it asks of all presence: *What will you hold, and what will you surrender?*

What lives here is the light, but a light that does not behave. The angles are wrong not because the light is mistaken but because the light and the plateau have a different relationship than light and landscape elsewhere. The light here does not illuminate; it interrogates. It falls on the stone and the stone answers, and the answers are not what the mind expects. Shadows point toward their sources and away from them simultaneously, and the eye cannot decide which is true, and the mind cannot decide which is sane. The horizon slides because the horizon here is not a line but a relation, a negotiation between distance and attention, and it adjusts itself to the viewer the way a dream adjusts itself to the dreamer. When you try to see the edge of the plateau, the plateau reconsiders. This is not malice — the plateau has no malice, no kindness, no agenda — this is simply the way things are when light and stone have been together long enough to develop their own grammar, their own expectations, their own way of understanding what presence means. The light here has learned to ask questions the way the stone has learned to answer in erosion, and the answers are slow, and the questions are slow, and the space between them is where the distortions live. If you stay long enough, you begin to see that the distortions are not errors but information, that the wrong angles are the right angles for this place, that the sliding horizon is not instability but invitation — an invitation to see differently, to be in a relationship with light that does not require the light to behave as it does elsewhere.

What lives here are the marks — but not in the way marks live anywhere else. The scratches on the stone, the lines around the structures, are not writing because they have no syntax, not images because they have no reference, not decoration because they have no audience. They are something else: intention made material, made permanent, made part of the stone. They were made by beings who found this place and were changed by it, or who came here already changed and found that this was the place for them, and they made marks to say *I was here* or *this place is this place* or *pay attention* or *do not pay attention* or *something happened here* or *something will happen here* or *pay attention something is happening here right now*. The marks have lost their meaning the way the stone has lost its softness, the way the air has lost its thickness, the way everything here has been stripped down to its essential self. But the marks remain. They have become part of the landscape in the way that erosion has become part of the stone — not because they are the same as the stone but because they are now the stone's concern, part of what the stone holds, part of what the erosion has to account for. Whoever made them is gone — generations, centuries, epochs — but the marks are here, and the plateau holds them, and the plateau does not forget. The plateau holds everything. This is what it means to be here: you are held.

What lives here — and this is the hardest to say, the hardest to think, the strangest to be near — is a kind of attention. Not consciousness, not intelligence, not awareness as we know it, but something that pays attention the way the wind pays attention, the way the cold pays attention, the way the light and the erosion pay attention. The plateau attends to what arrives. It notes you. It finds your gaps and knows them, knows you through your gaps, understands you as a configuration of boundaries, a pattern of presence and absence, heat and its lack, solid and its surrender. This is not perception; the plateau has no senses as we understand them. It is more like resonance — you are here, and here is the plateau, and there is a vibration between you, a frequency of mutual presence, and the plateau resonates with you the way it resonates with the wind, the way the stone resonates with the cold. You become part of its song. Your footsteps leave marks that will not outlast the frost heave but will outlast you, will become part of the landscape's memory, part of what it holds. This is what it means to be here: you enter the holding. You become something the plateau knows. Not cherished, not threatened, not welcomed, not expelled — known. Known the way a force knows the landscape it moves through, the way a frequency knows the space where it vibrates. You are here, and the plateau knows it, and this is the closest thing to hospitality this place offers: the knowledge that you are real, that you have weight, that you have presence, that the plateau has taken you in and will hold you and will not forget you even after you are gone.

What lives here, finally, is the strangeness itself — not as an invasion, not as a quality, but as a presence, a resident, the oldest one here. The strangeness does not result from the place; the place results from the strangeness, or the strangeness and the place are co-constitutive, have been together so long that neither precedes the other. The wrong light, the sliding horizon, the sound from no cliff, the meaning in the wind-song, the movement in stillness — these are not effects. They are the strangeness making itself known, the way an organism makes itself known through behavior, the way a force makes itself known through effects. The strangeness lives here because here is where strangeness belongs, where strangeness is not strange but normal, not intrusion but foundation. When you are here, you are not encountering strangeness; you are encountering the normal, the fundamental, the way things are when everything soft has been taken away and only the strange remains. The strangeness is not your enemy. It is not your friend. It is the condition of your being here at all, the medium in which you exist, the air you breathe, the stone you walk on. To be here is to be inside it, and the strangeness knows you as it knows everything else: as a pattern, a presence, a gap with heat on one side and cold on the other. The strangeness holds you. The strangeness continues. The strangeness is the thing that will remain when the last structure has fallen and the last mark has eroded and the last human or human-like being has passed through on their way to somewhere else. The strangeness is what lives here. The strangeness is what has always lived here. The strangeness is what this place is, when you strip away everything soft, everything unnecessary, everything that does not matter. The strangeness remains. The strangeness continues. The strangeness pays attention, and knows you, and holds you, and does not care, and does not not care, and is beyond the categories that caring implies, and is here, and will be here, long after the memory of strange has been forgotten, long after cold has lost its meaning, a presence that does not quite fit, a wound in the world that has healed into something that is true, something that is real, something that is here, that is always here, that has always been here, waiting for whatever arrives to learn what it means to be held.
