What lives here is not what you would call alive.

The growth-forms live here. They have always lived here, though living is not the word—existing, persisting, continuing, these are closer. They are the world, and the world is them, and between the two there is no seam. A single organism might span thousands of kilometers, its trunk a single threaded body, its roots descending into roots descending into roots that never reach stone because stone is what they ate, because stone was what they were fed when the world was young and there was still something to digest. Now there is only more of them. The substrate is them. The mountain range breathing in the distance is a specialized organ of one vast body, grown to process atmospheric compounds at altitude, its peak a flowering head kilometers wide that releases spores so dense they constitute weather. You are walking on something's back. You have always been walking on something's back.

They are made. That is the first thing to understand. Not made in the sense that they engineered themselves—that is a longer story, a story of hands and patience and generations—but made in the sense that they are artifacts, cultivated shapes, organisms bred for purpose so long that purpose has become indistinguishable from nature. Their cellulose is laced with metal. Their vessels carry compounds that burn. Their tissues fold in geometries that should not hold, that do not hold if you look too closely, that hold only because the structure is kept in a state of perpetual self-repair, a trillion small processes each holding the shape against its own collapse. They are machines that grow. They are growing machines. The distinction has no meaning here.

The substrate breathes. You feel it. This is not metaphor—it is the movement of one body, the systole and diastole of a heart the size of a world. Nutrients pushed through vessels the width of rivers. Pressure differentials maintained across distances that should leak, that should equalize, that maintain their gradients only because the tissue is sealed, because every microscopic breach is patched by adjacent growth, because the system is designed to contain itself. When you step, you step on something's flesh. When you stand still, you stand inside something's circulation.
