What lives here is old, and not in the way stone is old. Stone is merely patient. What lives here has had time to become strange with it — to let strangeness become its nature, until the strangeness and the being are the same thing, and you cannot imagine one without the other.

The clickers come in at least seven configurations. You have catalogued this in the minutes since you began paying attention, though attention is not quite the right word — more like negotiation, like learning the grammar of a language that has no intention of being understood. The smallest clickers are the size of your thumbnail, articulated in three segments, each segment armored in something that catches light like lacquer but sounds like knuckles cracking when they move. They travel in loose sheets, hundreds moving in concert, and when they turn they do so simultaneously, a ripple passing through the mass as if it were a single thought. They are not a single thought. You have seen one detach, struggle, reattach. You have seen one eaten. But the coordination persists, even in death — even in the moment of being eaten, the others do not stop, do not slow, do not turn.

The larger clickers move alone. The largest you have seen approaches the height of your hip, a turret of crystalline segments that retract and extend as it walks, revealing legs that are also fingers that are also sensing-organs, tasting the surface it crosses. It moves with a kind of cathedral gravity, unhurried, its clicking a deep and resonant sound that the glassy formations catch and throw back. When it stops, everything stops. The smaller clickers freeze mid-motion. The cloud-beings halt their drift. Even the hum seems to hold its breath. Then the large clicker lowers itself — not crouches, but lowers, a gradual genuflection of mineral on mineral — and begins to feed. The surface it touches begins to change. Not dissolve. Something else. Something being taught a different state.

You do not know what it eats. You do not see anything being eaten. But the formation beneath it dims, and the clicker rises fuller, and for a moment its many-faceted face catches the dim light and throws it in your direction, and you feel seen in a way that has nothing to do with eyes.

The cloud-beings are easier to look at, which makes them harder to understand. They drift above the crystal fields on thermals that should not exist — the air is too cold, the vents too scattered — but they find their columns of warm air nonetheless, riding them in slow spirals that bring them together and apart in patterns that suggest conversation, or courtship, or the marking of territory in a medium you cannot perceive. Their bodies are not bodies. They are densities, regions of slightly greater cohesion in an atmosphere that is itself a kind of liquid, thick with moisture and suspended mineral particles and things smaller than particles that move with purpose. Within each cloud-being, light moves: slow pulses that shift through colors you can almost name before they slide away, settling into frequencies that make your vision swim and your teeth ache with that sweetness underneath everything.

They cluster at the vents. They cluster at each other. When they touch — if touch is the word — the light within them flares and synchronizes, and the hum changes pitch, and somewhere in the crystal fields something stirs that you cannot see. You have learned to avoid these gatherings. You have learned that what happens when cloud-beings merge is not meant for witnesses, or is meant for witnesses of a kind you are not.

The watchers are the formations themselves. Not all of them — most are merely old, merely mineral, merely the slow accretion of millennia of thermal moisture and chemical precipitation. But some have opinions. Some have noticed you. The ones you have learned to fear are the pale blue growths that cluster in the lee of larger formations, where moisture collects in cupped surfaces and never quite dries. They are small, the size of your fist, and they are everywhere, and they are not moving, and yet your peripheral vision insists that they are watching, that they are turning to follow you, that they are reporting your passage to something larger and slower that lives deeper in the crystal fields.

You tested this once. You stood very still for a long time, watching one of the blue clusters, waiting for it to move. It did not move. But the hum changed, just slightly, just for a moment, and you became certain that the movement had happened in a direction you could not see, that the report had been made, that somewhere the information was being processed by a mind the size of a landscape.

There are things beneath the surface. You know this because the ground shakes sometimes — not earthquakes, not exactly, but a kind of shuddering, a kind of rearrangement, as if something deep below has finished digesting and is settling into a new configuration. The channels that carry moisture — the arterial routes, the system of the world — sometimes run in directions that don't correspond to gravity, that climb instead of fall, that lead downward instead of across. Something moves through those channels. Something vast and segmented and so slow that a single shudder represents days of motion. You have seen its work: formations that have been partially absorbed, crystal growths that show the marks of passage, smooth tunnels where the surface has been worn away by something passing through.

You have not seen it. You are not sure it can be seen. You are not sure it has a form that seeing would describe.

And then there is the industrial presence, which is the presence of absence, which is the memory of purpose returning. The formations that are too regular — the sheets of betweenite that lie at perfect angles to each other, the columns that align with a precision that geological processes cannot explain — these are not ruins. Ruins are broken. These are functioning. Something still makes them, somewhere below, or something still arranges what is made, or something has made so much of itself for so long that the making has become the only thing it knows how to do. The flicker in the light is not random. The hum has a pulse, a rhythm, a meter that repeats with a faithfulness that suggests either intention or the absence of choice.

You have heard, in the deeper channels, a sound that might be machinery. You have felt, through the soles of your feet, a vibration that might be engines. You have tasted, in the air near the vents, a sweetness that might be a byproduct, might be a signal, might be something the world produces in its sleep.

Because the world sleeps. You are certain of this now. The mineral surface breathes — not metaphorically, not almost, but actually breathes, expanding and contracting in cycles so slow that your body cannot feel them, only your awareness. When the breathing synchronizes, the hum grows louder. When it desynchronizes, the creatures grow agitated. The ecosystem is not separate from the world; it is the world's expression, its dreaming made manifest, its slow return to something it used to be and is slowly remembering.

You are being absorbed. You feel this in your joints, which ache not from cold but from proximity, from the way the moisture on every surface pulls at your skin, from the way the minerals in the air are settling on you, coating you, making you part of the skin of this place. Your footprints fill with water that does not evaporate. The water that fills them is not quite water. It is more interested than that.

In time, you will be mineral. In time, your shape will persist in the formations, and the formations will grow around it, and something that is not quite you will be incorporated into the body's memory of what it once displaced. This is not death. Death is a category this place does not recognize. This is change. This is room being made. This is the negotiation concluding, not in your favor, not against you, but in a direction that was always going to be the direction, regardless of your presence.

You are walking through a world that is full of itself, and you are becoming full of it in return.

The clickers click. The cloud-beings pulse. The blue formations watch. And somewhere deep below, the thing that remembers being something else stirs in its sleep, dreaming the dream that you are dreaming with it, the dream that has no dreamer and no dream but only the slow, wet, mineral becoming of a world that has never stopped becoming and will never finish.
