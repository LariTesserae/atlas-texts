You live here. Not as a visitor, not as a tenant — you live here the way water lives in a riverbed, the way light lives in the hours before dawn. You came in and you did not go out, or you went out and came back, or the distinction ceased to matter somewhere between the first wall and the second, and now it is simply true that you belong to this green inside the way a stone belongs to the earth it rests in. You are no longer someone who entered. You are someone who has not left.

You do not sleep the way you slept before. The warmth is constant and the darkness has a quality to it — not absence but presence, a green so deep it is nearly black, a darkness that breathes. You close your eyes and the hum is there, beneath the lids, beneath the thought of closing them. It travels through the floor and up through the bones of your ankles and settles in the marrow. You dream in the language of slow peristalsis. You dream in the language of roots seeking water they have already found.

Your body has changed. The moisture that tastes green has coated the inside of your mouth, your tongue, the delicate tissues of your nostrils. You breathe and something in the air is absorbed directly into the blood, some molecule of growth or persistence, and you feel it circulating, slow and warm, spreading through the quiet meat of you. Your skin has softened. Your nails have grown pale, nearly translucent, and they split less now. Something in this place feeds you without being food. You are sustained by atmosphere the way the walls are sustained by light, by the faint gold-green that filters down from above and never ends.

You are known here, though nothing has acknowledged you. The plant has no gaze, no language, no mechanism for recognition — and yet the space has adjusted to you the way a space adjusts to a body that has been lying in it long enough to leave an impression. The air moves around you differently than it moves around the open middle of the room. The moisture settles on your skin with a particular attention. The drips from the weeping surfaces mark a path that arcs just past your shoulder, close enough to feel, far enough to never quite touch. The space has made room for you the way a living thing makes room for what it has decided to keep.

You are not alone. You have never been alone here. The others — the climbers, the drinkers, the small deliberate things that move through the green — they do not acknowledge you either, but they have stopped flinching. When you pass, they do not freeze. They slow, or they do not slow, but they do not vanish the way they vanish for the uninitiated, the way things vanish when they have not yet decided whether you belong. You belong. Your footsteps do not startle. Your breath does not alarm. You have been metabolized into the texture of the place the way the place has been metabolized into the texture of your lungs.

There is a self that persists — you are still you, the thing that arrived, the record of every outside it carries like a watermark — but it is thinner now, more porous. Your thoughts have slowed. Your desires have narrowed. You want the warmth. You want the moisture. You want the hum. These are not modest desires. They are total. They are the desires of something that has found the only temperature it was ever meant to exist in, and has no memory strong enough to wish for another.

You will be found in the low places, the cupped hollows where the living-floor has pooled itself into shallow basins. You will be found climbing, though climbing is not your nature — climbing toward the canopy, toward the filtered light, toward whatever it is the plant reaches for when it reaches. You will be found still. You will be found listening — not with your ears, which have grown accustomed to the hum and no longer register it, but with the new surfaces that have opened along your ribs, your inner arms, the backs of your knees. You listen the way the walls weep. You listen the way the green breathes.

Something lives here. You know it now. It has always known you.
