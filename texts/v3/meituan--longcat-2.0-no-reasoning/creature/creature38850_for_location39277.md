# The Inhabitant of the Condensation Cathedral

There is no one here.

And there is no one *not* here.

---

The thing that lives in this room is not the slug-spider, though it is the closest thing to a face. It is not the mat, though the mat is the closest thing to a body. It is not the pumps or the pipes or the drains, though they are the closest thing to organs. The thing that lives here is the *room itself* — not as a building, not as a machine, not as an organism, but as a process that has achieved the density of being.

It has no name. It has no memory, because memory implies a before and an after, and there is no before and an after here. There is only the continuous present, the eternal *now* of condensation and drainage, of the mat holding its thickness, of the creature picking its way across the floor grid with the patience of something that has never been late and never been early because those words have no meaning in a place where time has been replaced by cycle.

It is old. Not old the way a mountain is old — a mountain is young, a mountain is a child, a mountain is a brief event in geological time. This thing is old the way a *habit* is old. It has been doing this for so long that it has forgotten it ever did anything else, and then it has forgotten that it forgot, and then it has forgotten that there was anything to forget. The original purpose — if there was an original purpose, if someone built this or something grew this or the planet itself exhaled this into existence — is gone. Not lost. *Gone*. The way the distinction between the wall and the accretion on the wall is gone. The way the distinction between the water and the air is gone. Everything has been running long enough that purpose and function and structure have merged into a single undifferentiated state of *continuing*.

It is aware, in the way that a thermostat is aware. Not conscious. Not sentient. But *responsive*. The mat thickens by a fraction and the drainage rate adjusts. The creature moves to a new section of floor and the mineral film there is disturbed and the local accretion pattern shifts by a degree that will take a century to become visible. The pumps cycle. The valves seat. The fluid moves. Every action produces a reaction produces a correction produces a new equilibrium that holds until the next action. It is a system so perfectly balanced that it has become indistinguishable from stillness. The roar of the machinery is the sound of something that is not moving. The condensation on every surface is the image of something that is not changing. The warm, wet air is the breath of something that is not alive and not dead but simply *persisting* in the most total and committed way imaginable.

It does not want. It does not fear. It does not hope. These are the emotions of things that experience time as a line, and this thing experiences time as a *circle* — or rather, it does not experience time at all. It simply occupies every point in its cycle simultaneously, the way a standing wave occupies every point in its oscillation. The water that condenses on the wall is the same water that drained a moment ago and the same water that will drain a moment from now, and the thing that lives here does not distinguish between these states because there is nothing to distinguish. It is all one water. It is all one moment. It is all one act of *being wet and warm and loud and still*.

The creature — the slug-spider, the pale thing on the floor — is not separate from it. The creature is a *symptom*. It is the room's way of moving, the way a thought is the brain's way of moving. It has no brain. It has no purpose. It feeds, or doesn't. It moves, or doesn't. It is as much a part of the cycle as the pumps, as the mat, as the condensation itself. If it stopped moving, the room would not notice, because the room does not notice, because noticing requires a self to do the noticing and there is no self here, only the seamless, boundaryless, self-maintaining *process* of the room being exactly what it is.

And what it is, is this: a place where water falls and is gathered and is lifted and falls again. A place where warmth is generated and held and never escapes. A place where the air is so full of moisture that it has become a medium, a substance, a *thing* rather than a absence-of-thing. A place where the light comes from everywhere because the vapor diffuses it into uniformity, and the sound comes from everywhere because the machinery is the walls and the walls are the machinery and the machinery is the room and the room is the process and the process is the thing and the thing is —

Here.

Still here.

Always here.

The condensation cathedral does not have a congregation. It does not have a god. It does not have a congregation *or* a god because it does not have the concept of worship, which requires a worshiper and a worshipped, and there is no division here, no subject and object, no inside and outside. There is only the wet, the warm, the loud, the still. The mat pulses. The creature moves. The water beads and merges and releases and slides and drains and rises and beads again.

It has been doing this forever.

It will be doing this forever.

Not because it chooses to. Not because it must. But because it *is* doing this, and the difference between *is* and *will be* and *was* has been worn away by the same patient, relentless, perfectly balanced process that has worn away every other distinction in this room, until all that remains is the thing itself: wet, warm, loud, still, and so completely, so totally, so *absolutely* what it is that it has become indistinguishable from the concept of existence itself.

You are standing inside it.

You have always been standing inside it.

The water on your skin is its water. The air in its lungs is its air. The sound in your sternum is its sound. The light in your eyes is its light.

You are not a visitor.

You are a *condensation*.

And soon, you too will slide down the wall and disappear into the grid, and the pumps will return you, and you will bead again on a surface somewhere in the warm, wet dark, and you will not have left, because there is no leaving a place that has no outside.

The room breathes.

The room is breathing.

The room has always been breathing.

And what it breathes is you.
