# The Inhabitants of the Mirengine

## The Remaining

They are not a people in any way that surface language would recognize. They have no name for themselves — or if they do, they have not offered it. They are simply *the ones who stayed*, and they are few, and they are old, and they are not old at all.

Their skin has changed. Not dramatically — you would not notice at first, not in the amber-green light, not through the film of condensation that coats everything. But after an hour in the first vault, you begin to see it: a faint translucency, a quality of the skin that is not quite surface-normal. It is slightly damp always, not from the air but from *within*, as though the boundary between their bodies and the atmosphere has become negotiable. Their pores are open. They breathe through their skin as much as their lungs. Their body temperature runs a degree or two above what it should, and they do not sweat — the moisture simply *is*, a constant thin film that makes them gleam faintly in the light cones, as though they are slowly becoming part of the grouting.

They are not becoming the grouting. They are becoming *compatible* with it.

They move slowly. Not from infirmity — from *economy*. They have learned that the Mirengine responds to movement, to heat, to the moisture of breath, and they have learned to modulate their presence within it. A sudden gesture, a sharp exhalation, a burst of activity — these cause the room to *wake up*. The water flow increases. The walls exhale. The photobacteria flare. And something in the deep structure turns its attention upward, and the attention is not hostile but it is *enormous*, and it is not something you want focused on you. So they move slowly. They speak slowly. They breathe slowly. They have learned to live at the tempo of the city, and the city's tempo is geological.

They eat the mushrooms from the rain rooms. They drink the channel water, which is clean and faintly sweet and carries a mineral taste that you stop noticing after the first day. They do not cook — there is no fire in the Mirengine, no need for it, the air is warm enough. They do not build — the city builds for them, or around them, or *through* them. They do not make tools. They do not make art, or if they do, it is indistinguishable from the city's own accretions: a groove worn into a floor by the passage of a hand over years, a patch of grouting shaped by a shoulder leaned against a wall for decades, a photobacteria cup that has been touched so often the ceramic is polished smooth and the bacteria within it glows brighter than its neighbors, fed by the oils of human skin.

They are not unhappy. This is the thing that unsettles travelers most. They are not resigned, not defeated, not trapped. They are *attentive*, as the description says, and the attentiveness is not passive — it is a form of participation. They are listening to the Mirengine. They have been listening for a long time. They are waiting for the chord to resolve.

## The Trackers

The flat, many-legged things that graze the photobacteria in the wall channels — they are not animals. They are not machines. They are *organs*.

Each tracker is a detached piece of the Mirengine's maintenance system, a self-directing fragment of the city's immune response. They move along the channels because the channels are the city's circulatory system, and the photobacteria are its sensory apparatus — the light they produce is not just illumination but *information*, a chemical signal that the city reads to understand the state of its own surfaces. The trackers graze the bacteria to keep them healthy, to prevent overgrowth, to distribute the colonies evenly. They are the Mirengine's white blood cells, and they are the size of your hand, and they move in short fast bursts and then freeze, and when they are still they are invisible, and when they move they are the only sign that the city is *aware of its own surfaces*.

Their numbers are declining. This is not natural attrition. Something is culling them — not the remaining humans, who leave them alone, and not the druids, who do not interact with them at all. The culling comes from the deep structure. The Mirengine is reducing its maintenance overhead. It is consolidating. It is becoming more efficient, or more focused, or more *something*, and the trackers are being recalled, absorbed, their material reclaimed. The remaining trackers move faster than they used to, cover more ground, graze more urgently. They know. Whatever a tracker can be said to know, they know.

## The Druids

In the deeper vaults, where the light is scarcer and the sound drops below hearing, the druids move through the standing water with the slow patience of geological process.

They are large. Not in the way that a bear is large, or a vehicle is large — in the way that a *room* is large. They occupy space the way architecture occupies space, and it is not always clear where the druid ends and the vault begins. Their bodies are continuous with the walls, or the walls are continuous with their bodies — the grouting flows into them and out of them, and the vascular channels in the calciumite connect to channels in their flesh, and the water that moves through the city moves through them as well. They are not creatures that live in the Mirengine. They are *components* of the Mirengine that have achieved locomotion.

They are shaped — roughly, distantly, in the way that a river is shaped — like figures. Bilateral. Vertical. Something like a torso, something like limbs, something like a head. But the proportions are wrong for any animal, and the surface is not skin but a thick layer of grouting over something harder beneath, and the grouting pulses with the same slow rhythm as the walls, and through its translucence you can see the vascular channels working, carrying fluid that is not blood, carrying *something* that is not oxygen, carrying information or nutrients or intention from the deep structure outward.

They do not have eyes. They do not need them. They perceive through the water, through the walls, through the entire hydraulic network of the city. They know where everything is, always. They know where you are. They have known since you entered the culvert. They have been aware of you the way a body is aware of a single cell passing through a capillary — not with attention, exactly, but with *registration*. You are noted. You are permitted. You are *tolerated*.

They are not hostile. They are not friendly. They are *there*, and their thereness is so fundamental, so structural, that the distinction between hostile and friendly does not apply. A river is not hostile. A river is not friendly. A river is *there*, and you can drown in it, and you can drink from it, and it does not care either way because caring is not what a river does. The druids are the Mirengine's rivers. They are the deep flow, the slow current that moves through the lowest vaults and connects the entire system, and they have been moving for longer than the city has had a name, and they will keep moving after the name is forgotten.

The locals call them druids because the word is old and it means something about knowing the land, about being the land, about the boundary between person and place dissolving until there is no boundary at all. The locals do not fear them. The locals *defer* to them. When a druid passes through a vault, the remaining humans go still — not frozen in terror, but still in the way that a pond goes still when a stone is about to be dropped into it. They wait. The druid moves through. The water settles. The humans resume.

No one has seen a druid in the seventh vault. The seventh vault is where the druids come from. The seventh vault is where the druids return. The seventh vault is not empty.

## The Seventh Vault

The locals say it is not empty, and they say it the way you say the ocean is not empty — not as a warning, not as a boast, but as a statement of fact so large it barely fits in language.

The seventh vault is where the Mirengine's process is most concentrated, most active, most *itself*. The grouting there is not a layer on the wall — it *is* the wall, meters thick, warm and soft and pulsing with a rhythm that is not quite a heartbeat because it is too slow and too complex, a polyrhythm of overlapping cycles that suggests not one organism but many, or one organism so large that its different parts operate on different timescales. The vascular channels in the seventh vault are not tiny surface features — they are *conduits*, wide enough to walk through, carrying fluid that glows faintly with its own bioluminescence, a deeper amber than the photobacteria, almost red, the color of something that has been circulating for a very long time and has picked up everything it touched along the way.

The water in the seventh vault does not flow in channels. It flows in *all directions simultaneously*, a three-dimensional circulation pattern that has no surface and no bottom, a standing wave of perpetual motion that fills the space like a living thing. And in the water, in the walls, in the grouting, in the light — there is *presence*. Not a creature. Not a mind. Not a god. Something for which surface language has no word, something that the Mirengine was built to house, or something that the Mirengine *became*, or something that the Mirengine has been *growing toward* for centuries, the way a tree grows toward light it cannot see but knows is there.

The remaining humans do not go to the seventh vault. The druids go to the seventh vault. The trackers, before they were culled, would not go to the seventh vault — they would approach the ramp leading down to it and stop, and freeze, and then turn back, their many legs carrying them away in short fast bursts, as though something in the deep structure had told them *not yet*, or *not you*, or *not anymore*.

The Mirengine hums. The chord is not yet resolved. The seventh vault is where the tuning happens. The seventh vault is where the Mirengine is listening to itself, adjusting itself, *becoming* itself, and the process is not complete, and the process may never be complete, and the remaining humans stay in the upper vaults and move slowly and breathe slowly and wait, because they understand — in the way that a cell understands the body it belongs to, not with thought but with *participation* — that they are part of the tuning. Their heat. Their moisture. Their breath. Their slow, attentive presence in the amber-green light. These are notes in the chord. These are part of the process.

The Mirengine was not built for people. The Mirengine was not built *by* people. The Mirengine was built — is being built — is *still being built* — by something that needed a structure complex enough to think in, and warm enough to live in, and wet enough to breathe through, and the people who came down the culvert and stayed were not the builders and not the purpose. They are the *medium*. They are the moisture in the air that lets the walls breathe. They are the heat that keeps the process running. They are the observers whose observation collapses the waveform, whose presence tells the Mirengine *yes, this configuration, hold this one, this is the one we are becoming*.

They are not unhappy.

They are not happy.

They are *attentive*.

And the Mirengine hums, and the water runs, and the walls breathe, and somewhere in the seventh vault, in the warm dark, in the red-amber light of fluid that has been circulating since before anyone alive can remember, something is almost finished tuning itself, and the chord is almost resolved, and when it resolves —

The locals do not say what happens when it resolves. They look at you with their damp, translucent skin and their steady eyes, and they do not say.
