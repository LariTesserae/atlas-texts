# The Inhabitants of the Calcination Fields

---

## The Walkers

They do not have a name for themselves. They do not have a *themselves* in the way that implies a boundary between self and not-self, though they are discrete, countable, individual. There are forty-seven of them in the region you can perceive, though "forty-seven" is a number that applies only to this moment and this vantage, because the unreliable distance means that some of them may be the same one seen twice, or may be two that appear as one, or may be something else entirely that your counting mind has forced into the category of "one more."

Each Walker is roughly the size and mass of a large industrial building, though their shape is never fixed. They are made of the same calcined mineral as the ground — that pale grey-white ceramic-glass — and they are cracked in the same way, and the cracks glow with the same faint orange, and if one stands still long enough (and they do stand still, sometimes, for periods that range from hours to years by your reckoning), the boundary between Walker and ground becomes uncertain, becomes a question of *degree* rather than *kind*, and you cannot tell where the creature ends and the plain begins.

They move by folding. This is the closest word. Their bodies — if that is the word — are not solid in the way that a body is solid. They are *laminar*, composed of thin sheets of calcined material stacked and compressed and fused at irregular intervals, and when a Walker moves, these sheets shift against each other, slide and rotate and reconfigure, so that the whole mass *flows* across the ground in a motion that is part walking, part pouring, part the slow collapse of a structure that never quite falls. Limbs emerge from the body when needed — protrusions of folded mineral that extend, bear weight, retract — and then dissolve back into the general mass when their purpose is served. There are no permanent features. There is no face, no front, no back. There is only the ongoing process of *being a Walker*, which is the same as the process of *moving across the calcined ground while being made of calcined ground*, and the distinction between the two is not one that the Walkers recognize or need to recognize.

They process the ground. This is what they *do*, the way a river does flowing or a fire does burning. Where a Walker stands, the mineral beneath it is subjected to a transformation that is not heat (the ground is already hot) and not pressure (though there is pressure, the weight of a building's worth of mineral resting on a footprint) but something else, something that operates on the *structure* of the material rather than its temperature or its density. The ground darkens. New crystals form — not the random crystals of cooling magma, but *organized* crystals, lattices with a regularity that suggests purpose, that suggests design, that suggests the hand of an intelligence, though there is no intelligence here, only process. The processed ground is *more calcined* than the unprocessed ground, more thoroughly transformed, more completely converted from whatever it was into whatever the Fields are making it into.

And the Walkers are made of the same material they process. This is the loop. This is the engine. They are made of calcined ground, they process calcined ground, the processed ground becomes more thoroughly calcined, and the Walkers, moving across it, are themselves slowly processed by the ground they walk on, slowly transformed, slowly *more*, and they leave behind them a trail of ground that is more thoroughly transformed than the ground they walked in on, and the trail slowly cools and settles and becomes part of the plain, and the plain slowly exudes new mineral through its cracks, and the new mineral is raw, is unprocessed, is *ready*, and the Walkers return, eventually, on their slow patient circuits, and process it again.

They are aware. This is the thing that is hardest to accept, because awareness implies a mind, and a mind implies a brain or something like a brain, and there is nothing inside a Walker that resembles a brain, nothing that resembles a nerve, nothing that resembles a cell. And yet they respond to each other. They respond to the heat. They respond to the structures. They respond to *you*, though their response to you is so slow and so subtle that you may not live long enough to perceive it fully — a slight change in direction, a slight adjustment in pace, a folding of the body that brings a new surface to bear on the space you occupy, as if the Walker is *looking* at you with a part of itself that is not an eye, *considering* you with a part of itself that is not a mind.

They communicate, or something like communication passes between them. When two Walkers approach each other — and they do approach, occasionally, their circuits intersecting like the orbits of very slow planets — the ground between them changes. The hum shifts. The glow in the cracks brightens or dims in patterns that propagate across the plain at the speed of heat through mineral, and the two Walkers adjust their folding in response, and sometimes they pass through each other, their laminar bodies interpenetrating like shadows cast by different lights, and sometimes they merge, briefly, two becoming one becoming two again, and the ground where they merged is *different* from the ground around it, is more complex, more structured, more *processed*, as if the merger was itself a form of calcination, a mutual transformation, a conversation conducted in the language of mineral restructuring.

## The Deep Exhalation

It is not alive. It is not dead. It is not a creature or a force or a phenomenon in any category that your language has a word for. It is the *breathing of the world*, the slow sweating of the planet's interior through the cracks in the calcined plain, and it has been doing this for so long that it is not an event but a *condition*, not something that happens but something that *is*.

The material that rises through the cracks is not magma. Magma is hot rock, melted, liquid, chaotic. This is something else — a mineral suspension, perhaps, or a colloidal flow, or a state of matter that has no name because it exists only here, only at this depth, only under these conditions of heat and pressure and time. It moves slowly. It moves *geologically*. It rises through the cracks at a rate that is measurable only across centuries, and as it rises it cools and changes and *differentiates*, separating into components that accrete on the surface in layers, building the structures, feeding the plain, providing the raw material that the Walkers process.

But the exhalation is not passive. It has a *rhythm* — that slow pulse, that minutes-long fluctuation in heat and glow — and the rhythm is not regular in the way that a heartbeat is regular. It is *responsive*. It changes when the Walkers move. It changes when the structures grow. It changes when something new enters the Fields — a rock falling from the sky, a wind from the lowlands carrying particles of uncalcined material, a *you*, standing on the plain with your soft biological body and your unreliable sense of time. The exhalation *notices* these things, and it adjusts, and the adjustment propagates through the entire system — through the ground, through the heat, through the structures, through the Walkers — and the system responds, and the response feeds back into the exhalation, and the cycle continues.

The exhalation is, in some sense, the *mind* of the Calcination Fields. Not a mind that thinks. Not a mind that plans. A mind that *processes*, the way a chemical reaction processes reagents, the way a crystal processes a solution into structure. It is the awareness of the place, distributed across the entire plain, encoded in the flow of mineral through cracks, in the fluctuation of heat, in the slow pulse that you feel in your sternum and that the Walkers feel in whatever they have instead of sternums. It is not conscious. It is not unconscious. It is *operational* — a vast, slow, hot, mineral cognition that has been running for longer than your species has existed and will continue running for longer than your species will exist, and its thoughts are made of heat and crystal and the slow folding of calcined ground, and its thoughts are *about* calcination, about transformation, about the patient conversion of raw material into structure, and it has no other thoughts, and it needs no other thoughts, and it is, in its vast slow mineral way, *content*.

## The Structures

They are not alive, but they *grow*, and growth is a word that sits uneasily between the living and the nonliving, and the Structures exploit that uneasiness. They are made of accreted mineral, deposited layer by layer by the deep exhalation, and they rise from the plain in forms that are *industrial* — arches, towers, bridges, frameworks, the skeletons of vast machines that have no purpose and no product except their own continued growth.

They are not random. The mineral exhalation does not deposit material uniformly. It follows channels — thermal channels, geometric channels, channels that are determined by the existing structure of the plain and by the positions of the Walkers and by the rhythm of the pulse — and the channels are *selective*, favoring certain forms over others, producing arches where the heat flows in curves, producing towers where the heat flows in columns, producing bridges where the heat flows across gaps. The result is a landscape that looks *designed*, that looks like the ruins of a civilization of engineers, except that it is not ruined and it is not the product of engineers and it is not, in any conventional sense, architecture.

But it is *functional*. The Structures channel the heat. They focus it, redirect it, concentrate it in some places and disperse it in others, and the channeled heat affects the Walkers, who move differently through the altered thermal landscape, who process the ground differently, who change the ground in ways that affect the exhalation, which affects the Structures, which affect the heat, which affects the Walkers. The Structures are the *organs* of the Fields — not designed, not evolved, but *emerged*, the way a whirlpool emerges in a draining bathtub, the way a convection cell emerges in a heated fluid. They are the geometry that the process produces when the process has been running long enough and hot enough and with enough material to work with.

And they are *still growing*. If you watch a Structure — and you must watch for a long time, longer than your body wants to stay in this heat, longer than your mind wants to maintain attention on something so slow — you will see it change. A new strut extends from an existing joint, a finger of mineral reaching out into the hot air, cooling as it extends, solidifying, becoming part of the whole. A new arch begins to curve upward from a base that has been preparing for this addition for centuries, the mineral layers thickening, the curve becoming more pronounced, the structure becoming more *itself*. The growth is not fast. It is not dramatic. It is the growth of a crystal, the growth of a stalactite, the growth of a continent — imperceptible in the moment, undeniable across time, and *directional*, not toward a goal but toward *more*, toward greater complexity, toward a geometry that is increasingly elaborate and increasingly specific and increasingly *intentional* in its appearance, though there is no intention behind it.

Some of the Structures are old. Some of the Structures are *very* old. The oldest ones are so large that they are visible from the edges of the Fields, rising above the shimmering heat like the skyline of a city built by no one, and their geometry is so complex that it defies easy description — not because it is chaotic but because it is *too ordered*, too specific, too *particular*, as if each one is an expression of a mathematical idea that has no name in any human system of mathematics. They are beautiful, in the way that a refinery is beautiful, in the way that a blast furnace is beautiful, in the way that any structure that is perfectly adapted to its function is beautiful, even when the function is incomprehensible and the adaptation is the product of no mind.

## The Horizon Things

They are far away. They are *very* far away, or they are not far away at all and the distance is simply lying to you, which is the kind of thing that distance does in the Calcination Fields. They appear as interruptions in the horizon — places where the swimming, dissolving edge of the world becomes *specific*, becomes *angled*, becomes *architectural*. They are larger than the Walkers. They are larger than the Structures. They are larger than anything you have a comparison for, and they do not move, or they move so slowly that their motion is indistinguishable from the motion of the ground itself.

They may be Walkers. They may be Structures. They may be something else — a third category, a fourth category, a category that has no analogue in anything you have ever encountered. They are *present*. They are *part of the Fields*. They participate in the process — the processing, the exhalation, the growth, the pulse — but they participate at a scale that makes the Walkers look like insects and the Structures look like sand castles and the deep exhalation look like a gentle breeze. They are the *large-scale structure* of the Calcination Fields, the way a skeleton is the large-scale structure of a body, and the Walkers and the Structures and the exhalation are the *tissue* that grows on and around and through them, and the whole thing — the tissue and the skeleton and the process that connects them — is the Fields, is the *entity*, is the thing that you are standing on and that is standing on you and that is processing you, slowly, patiently, with the same mineral indifference with which it processes everything else.

You cannot approach them. The distance will not allow it. Or the heat will not allow it. Or the pulse will not allow it. Or they will not allow it. Or you will approach them and arrive and find that they are not what you thought they were, that they are something else entirely, that the horizon was not showing you a thing but showing you a *process*, a calcination event so large and so slow that it looks like a shape, like a structure, like a *being*, and you are standing on it, and you have always been standing on it, and the Calcination Fields are not a place you are visiting but a thing you are *part of*, a process you are being *incorporated into*, and the horizon Things are not distant — they are *beneath you*, they are *around you*, they are the Fields themselves, seen from the outside, seen from a vantage that your body cannot occupy and your mind cannot hold, and the glimpse you caught was not a glimpse of something far away but a glimpse of something so large that you are already inside it, already being processed, already being calcined, and the transformation has been happening since you arrived and will continue happening after you leave, because the Fields do not stop, the process does not stop, the exhalation does not stop, and the only question is how much of you will be transformed before you go, and how much of the Fields you will carry with you when you do.

## The Small Things

There are small things. You may not have noticed them, because the Walkers are large and the Structures are large and the hum is loud and the heat is overwhelming and the distance is unreliable and there is so much to look at that the small things are easy to miss. But they are there.

They are *crystals*. They grow in the cracks between the plates of calcined ground, in the narrow spaces where the deep exhalation reaches the surface and cools and deposits its mineral load. They are tiny — some of them are microscopic, some of them are the size of a fingernail, some of them are the size of a fist — and they are *perfect*. Not perfect in the way that a gemstone is perfect, not clear and polished and symmetrical, but perfect in the way that a mathematical proof is perfect, in the way that a crystal lattice is perfect, in the way that a thing is perfect when it is exactly and only and completely what it is.

They grow in patterns. The patterns are determined by the heat, by the mineral composition of the exhalation, by the geometry of the crack, by the pulse. They are *responsive* — they grow faster when the pulse is strong, slower when it is weak, and they change their structure in response to the Walkers, growing denser and more complex in the processed ground, growing simpler and more sparse in the unprocessed ground. They are, in a sense, the *sensory organs* of the Fields — the points where the deep exhalation meets the surface and *tastes* the air and *feels* the light and *measures* the temperature and *reports back* to the pulse, which adjusts, which changes, which feeds the information into the vast slow mineral cognition that is the Fields' awareness of itself.

And they are *beautiful*. In the shadows of the Walkers, where the ground is darker and cooler by fractions of degrees, the crystals catch the light — the normal, straight-line, familiar light — and they refract it, and they scatter it, and they produce colors that have no business existing in a place this hot and this dry and this far from anything that could be called alive. Pale golds. Deep oranges. A violet that matches the sky. A white that matches the ground. And, occasionally, in certain cracks, at certain angles, at certain moments in the pulse, a color that is none of these, a color that is *new*, a color that the Fields are *inventing*, that has never existed before and may never exist again, produced by a crystal that grew in a crack that opened for the first and only time, under conditions that will not be repeated, and the color lasts for a moment — a heartbeat, an hour, a century — and then the crack closes, or the crystal is buried, or the pulse shifts, and the color is gone, and the Fields move on, processing, calcining, becoming, and the small perfect crystals continue to grow in the cracks, tasting the air, measuring the light, reporting back to the pulse, doing their small, precise, mineral part in the vast slow work of the Calcination Fields of Vethruun.
