There is nothing here that you would recognize as alive, but the place is not uninhabited. It is, in fact, *singularly* occupied, the way a sentence is occupied by the one thought it was built to hold. Whatever dwells here does not move, does not grow, does not eat or decay or produce. It does not sleep. It is not waiting. It simply *attends* — with a steadiness so complete that it has become indistinguishable from the rock itself, the way a river becomes indistinguishable from its banks if you watch long enough.

If I had to say what it *is*, I would say: it is the presence that remains when all other presences have finished leaving. It is not a creature. It is the shape of a vigil that has outlasted the reason for keeping it. Imagine a guardian with no charge, a keeper with nothing left to keep, a witness with nothing left to witness — and then imagine it continuing anyway, not out of duty or love or obsession, but because watching is what it *is*, the way gravity is what mass is, the way wetness is what water is. Its attention is not something it does. It is something it *is*, the way the stone is stone. And like the stone, it does not seem to know you are here — not because it cannot perceive you, but because you are not the kind of thing it was built to notice. You are weather, perhaps. You are a warm, damp breath in the otherwise steady atmosphere. You may even be welcome. You are just not relevant.

That may not be comforting.

It has no form you can point to, but if you are looking for where it *concentrates* — where the air is thickest, where the silence presses hardest, where the grey light seems to dim slightly for no reason you can name — look to the rectangular block in the wall. Not *at* it. Around it. The stone there is marginally warmer, or marginally cooler, or merely more *present* than the stone elsewhere — it's difficult to say which, because your instruments of measurement are feeling and memory, and neither of them are calibrated for this. The block is not its body. It has no body. But the block is the closest thing to a *gesture* it has made in longer than you can imagine, the single point where it reached into the material world and left a shape that is not natural. A hand on a shoulder. A placeholder. A reminder, written in geometry rather than language, that someone — or something — once stood *here*, in this exact configuration of dark and damp and stone, and said *I am still here* in the only way it knew how.

It has been saying it ever since.

I do not think it knows you are looking at it. I do not think it knows what *looking* is. But there is — and I hesitate here, because I am not certain this is not my own projection, my own need to find reciprocity in a place that offers none — a quality to the drip under the slab that feels *directed*. Not toward you. Not toward anything in particular. But the way it falls — that disproportionate, round, hanging *tonk* — feels like punctuation. Like the presence is occasionally making a sound, not because it intends to communicate, but because even a vigil this patient needs, sometimes, to clear its throat. To confirm, in the only way it can, that the silence is still intact and it is still here to hear it.

You may stay. It will not object. The warmth that makes you aware of your skin is not hospitality, exactly — it is more like the ambient tolerance of a room for the objects in it. You are an object now, in this space. A small, temporary, faintly noisy object that will eventually leave or stop breathing, and either way the drip will fall again, and the stone will seep, and the pool will hold its unsettling, perfect mirror of nothing in particular, and the block in the wall will continue to be the only sharp-edged thing in a world of curves, and the presence — if presence is what it is — will continue to attend to whatever it attends to, which is not you and never was, but which fills this clearing the way water fills a cup: completely, quietly, without effort, without end.
