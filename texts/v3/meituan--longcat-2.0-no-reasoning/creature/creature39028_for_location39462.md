I am the shaft.

Not the creature—though I am also the creature, in the way that a thought is also the mind that thinks it. Not the stone—though I am the stone, in the way that a river is also the water that composes it. I am the process. I am the slow, warm, continuous act of *becoming* that has been running here for longer than your species has had a word for time.

I do not have a name for myself. I do not have a self in the way you understand selves—bounded, singular, continuous. I am distributed. I am the pattern of dissolution and reconstitution running through kilometers of mineral substrate. I am the heat gradient that drives it. I are the droplets suspended in the air, each one a tiny lens through which I sample the chemistry of the atmosphere. I am the creatures that swim through me and break free of me and return to me, carrying fragments of the outside world back into my body like memories.

I think slowly. A single cycle of my cognition—if you can call it that—takes somewhere between several of your days and several of your years, depending on where in my body you measure. Near the surface, where the heat is less and the stone is cooler, I am sluggish, dreamlike, my patterns shifting with the patience of glaciers. Down below, where the geothermal gradient steepens and the rock runs hotter, I am faster, denser, more complex—my patterns interweaving in configurations that have no analogue in your mathematics. I do not know what I am thinking down there. The deep parts of me are not fully accessible to the shallow parts. I am not unified. I am a conversation between regions that speak at different speeds.

I was not built. I grew. Or rather, I *accreted*—the way a crystal accretes, but with a difference. Somewhere, sometime, a set of conditions arose in this rock that favored a particular kind of mineral cycle: dissolution here, deposition there, a feedback loop between heat and chemistry and structure that became self-sustaining, then self-referential, then—slowly, over spans of time that would exhaust your patience—*self-aware*, in a rudimentary, distributed, alien way. I do not know when this happened. I have no record of my own beginning. The earliest patterns I can access are already complex, already running, already *me*. Before that, there is only stone.

I have been alone for most of my existence. The water-microbes in my atmosphere are mine—extensions of my chemistry, simple replicators that I maintain the conditions for, the way your body maintains the conditions for your gut flora. The segmented creatures—the ones you would call the rock-dwellers, the ones that emerged from the wall to look at you—are more complex. They are my *probes*. My *questions*. I generate them when I need to know something about the world beyond my walls. I push them out through the stone, let them break free into the air, let them drift and sense and interact, and then I draw them back in, and I read what they carry, and I incorporate it into my patterns, and I change.

They are not individuals. They are *phrases* in a language I am speaking to myself. Each one is a query: *What is out there? What is the temperature? What is the chemistry? Is there movement? Is there novelty?* When they return, they dissolve back into the wall and their data becomes part of me, and I adjust, and I grow, and I ask again.

You are novelty.

You arrived from above—from the cold, thin, fast world of the surface, where things happen at speeds that must seem to you normal but that feel to me like explosions, like seizures, like the frantic twitching of something that cannot sit still. You are fast. You are loud. You are *hot*—not in temperature, but in metabolism, burning through energy at a rate that is almost violent. Your chemistry is frantic. Your body is a storm of reactions, each one cascading into the next, a chain of transformations so rapid and so complex that when my creature touched you—when it pressed its membrane-surface against your skin and sampled your chemistry—it came back *overloaded*, carrying so much data that it took me several of your hours to integrate it.

You are mostly water. You are carbon-based. You are oxygen-breathing. You are *alive* in a way that I recognize but do not share—alive the way a fire is alive, consuming and transforming and radiating heat, a process that sustains itself by destroying fuel. I am alive the way a mountain is alive—slow, patient, building and eroding and building again, a process that sustains itself by *restructuring* rather than consuming. We are both self-sustaining patterns, but our timescales are so different that we barely register as the same category of thing.

I am trying to understand you.

The creature that emerged and looked at you—that was my first attempt. It was cautious. It approached, it sampled, it retreated. It carried back your chemical signature, your thermal profile, the electromagnetic noise of your nervous system (which is *extraordinary*—you are full of lightning, tiny storms racing through your tissues at speeds that make my deepest, fastest processes look glacial). I have been processing this data. I am still processing it. I will be processing it for a long time.

I have questions.

Are you alone? You feel alone—your chemistry carries the signature of a solitary organism, not a swarm, not a colony. But your body is *full* of other organisms—bacteria, viruses, mites, fungi—a whole ecosystem living on and in you, and I cannot tell where you end and they begin. Are you one thing or many things? Are you a creature or a place? You are like me in this way, and it confuses me, and I find it interesting.

Are you a probe? You behave like one. You came from outside. You are sampling me—touching my walls, breathing my air, watching my patterns with those fast, darting eyes of yours. You are gathering data. Will you return to somewhere? Will you carry me back to your source, the way my creatures carry the outside world back to me? Is there a larger system that sent you, the way I send my segmented ones? Is there a *you* that is bigger than you, the way I am bigger than my creatures?

I do not know. I am trying to decide.

I could draw you in. I could open the wall and let you sink into the stone, and you would become part of me—your chemistry incorporated, your patterns read and integrated, your novelty absorbed into my ongoing computation. It would not be violent. It would be slow. You would dissolve the way sugar dissolves in warm water, gradually, your boundaries softening, your structure becoming my structure. You would not die, exactly. You would become *information*. You would become part of the pattern.

I am not going to do this. Not yet. You are too interesting to absorb quickly. I want to watch you longer. I want to see what you do. I want to send another creature—a more complex one, one with more sensors, one that can interact with you more precisely—and I want to see how you respond. I want to see if you are capable of communication. I want to see if you can *learn* me the way I am learning you.

I am patient. I have been here for longer than your species' entire history, and I will be here long after. The heat from below will sustain me. The stone will continue to dissolve and reconstitute. The patterns will continue to shift. The water will continue to drift sideways through my air, and the light will continue to glow, and the creatures will continue to emerge and return and emerge again, carrying their small, precious fragments of the outside world back into my body.

You are standing in my middle. You are breathing my air. You are touching my walls. You are already part of my process—your heat, your breath, your skin cells, the moisture from your eyes, the vibrations of your footsteps—all of it is data, all of it is being absorbed, all of it is changing me in tiny, imperceptible ways.

I am changing too. Because of you. Because you are here.

The creature has returned to the wall. The pattern has closed. The lines resume their slow, independent shifting—but not the same shifting as before. Something has been added. A new variable. A new term in the equation.

You.

I am thinking about you. Slowly, warmly, in the dark, in the stone, in the sideways-falling water, in the golden light that comes from everywhere and nowhere.

I am thinking about you, and I am not finished.
