In the Forge-Cleft of Unending Churn, there are no citizens. There are no residents. There are no inhabitants in any sense that implies a relationship between a being and a place. What lives here is something more intimate and more terrible than that. What lives here is the cleft itself, and everything within it is its body.

---

**The Builders** are the first thing you notice, because they are the most visible, the most numerous, and the most *purposeful.* They are small — not child-small, but *compressed*-small, as if something larger had been folded down into a denser configuration. Their bodies are pale where they are not stained with the copper-dark exudate of the walls, and their skin has a quality that is difficult to look at directly: it seems to shift between textures, now smooth as ceramic, now rough as fresh stone, now faintly translucent, revealing not organs but *structures* — lattices, filaments, geometries that pulse with the same amber light as the conduits they build.

They have too many joints. Not grotesquely — not in a way that reads as mutation or deformity — but in a way that suggests their bodies were designed for a three-dimensional environment where every surface is a floor. Their arms bend in directions that human arms do not. Their fingers are long and end in points that can grip, cut, weld, or sense, depending on what is needed. They have no visible eyes. Their faces — if the front of their heads can be called faces — are smooth, unbroken planes of pale skin that flush copper-dark when they press against the wall to absorb whatever it is they absorb. They do not eat. They do not drink. They *interface.* The wall feeds them, or they feed the wall, or both simultaneously in a transaction that has no direction.

They build with a speed and precision that is almost violent. A builder will arrive at a fresh surface — newly cracked, still weeping its metallic tears — and within minutes will have welded a strut, bolted a platform, threaded a conduit. The work is not careful. It is *urgent.* There is no planning, no blueprint, no architect. Each builder works according to an imperative that is felt rather than thought, and the result is the competing, overlapping, seven-layered architecture that covers every surface. One builder's scaffold passes through another's habitation pod. A third builder's pipe-network is threaded through the gap without consultation, without conflict, without any apparent awareness that the space was already occupied. They do not argue. They do not negotiate. They simply *build*, and the city accretes around them like a shell around an organism that does not know it is growing.

They speak, but their speech is not communication in any way you would recognize. The sounds they make — short, clipped, with long vowels that carry in the cool air — are not language. They are *resonance.* They are the builders tuning themselves to the cleft, the way a musician tunes an instrument, the way a cell signals to its neighbors. When a builder makes a sound, the wall vibrates in response, and the other builders adjust their work accordingly. The murmur that fills the cleft is not conversation. It is the sound of a body regulating itself.

They do not sleep. They do not rest. When a builder's work is done — when a section of wall is fully claimed, fully built upon, fully metabolized — the builder does not stop. It moves to the next fresh surface, following the cracks, following the new openings, following the rift's slow expansion. If there is no fresh surface, the builder presses itself against the oldest construction and *dismantles* it, unbolting, unwelding, feeding the materials back into the system. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is permanent. The builders are not building a city. They are building a *process*, and the process is the only thing that endures.

---

**The Wall-Creatures** are smaller, more numerous, and more alien. They are the size of a hand, flat-bodied, many-legged, and they move along the walls in rivers that flow with a purpose that is not quite animal and not quite mechanical. Their bodies are made of something that is not chitin and not ceramic and not metal but has properties of all three — hard, light, slightly warm to the touch, with a faint iridescence that shifts as they move. They have no faces. They have no visible sensory organs. They navigate by reading the wall's patterns — those repeating geometries, those circuit-like traces in the fresh mineral — and they orient themselves to these patterns the way a compass orients to magnetic north.

They carry things. This is their primary function, or at least the primary function you can observe. They carry fragments of fresh mineral, scraps of wire, droplets of metallic exudate held in cupped limbs that fold around the cargo like hands closing around a precious thing. They carry these materials from the old sections of the city to the new, from the dismantled to the under-construction, and they do so with a patience and efficiency that the builders lack. Where the builders are urgent, the wall-creatures are *methodical.* They do not rush. They do not surge. They flow, steadily, continuously, like a circulatory system moving nutrients from one part of a body to another.

Some of them are *domesticated* — or perhaps *recruited* is the better word. A builder will reach down and pluck a wall-creature from the river and press it into a junction box, a conduit, a gap in the construction where something needs to be processed. The creature will remain there for a time — hours, days, you cannot tell — doing something inside the machinery that you cannot see. Then it will crawl back out, rejoin the wall-river, and continue its work. It does not seem distressed by this. It does not seem to experience it as captivity. It is simply another task, another function, another part of the process.

The wall-creatures avoid certain geometries. When they encounter one of the repeating patterns in the fresh mineral — the ones the builders treat with reverence — they flow around it like water around a stone, giving it a wide berth, never touching it, never crossing it. You do not know what these patterns are. You do not know what they mean. But the wall-creatures know, and their avoidance is absolute.

---

**The Traversers** move through the central void of the cleft — the narrow dark between the walls — and they are the largest of the creatures you can see. They range in size from a dog to a person to something larger, and they move through the air with a boneless ease that suggests they have never known gravity as a constraint. Some of them travel on cables, gripping the conduits and struts with limbs that are long and prehensile and end in pads that stick to any surface. Others move in free fall, catching themselves on far walls, launching again, traversing the void in arcs that are graceful and purposeless at the same time.

Their bodies are elongated, segmented, covered in a skin that is darker than the builders' — almost the color of the walls themselves, that blackened copper tone — and they have too many limbs, or perhaps not enough, depending on how you count. The limbs seem to appear and disappear as needed, extending from the body when required and retracting when not. They have mouths — or openings that function as mouths — and through these they take in the cool air of the cleft and expel it in streams that carry a faint luminescence, as if their breath is faintly radioactive, or bioluminescent, or something for which there is no word.

They interact with the builders and the wall-creatures, but the interaction is not social. It is *systemic.* A traverser will pause at a section of wall and extend a limb into the construction, adjusting a conduit, tightening a bolt, removing a wall-creature that has wandered into the wrong junction. Then it will launch itself back into the void and continue its traversal. It is not helping. It is not cooperating. It is *maintaining.* The traversers are the immune system of the cleft, moving through its body, identifying irregularities, correcting them, moving on.

---

**The Cleft Itself** is the thing that lives here. Not the builders, not the creatures, not the construction. The cleft. The rift. The wound in the mountain that is not a wound but a *mouth*, opening slowly, revealing its interior, and everything within it — every builder, every creature, every strut and conduit and habitation pod — is part of its body, part of its process, part of its becoming.

The fresh mineral that emerges from the walls is not dead matter. It is *new* matter — matter that has not existed before, matter that is being generated by the rift's slow expansion, matter that carries within it the repeating geometries, the circuit-like traces, the patterns that the builders align to and the wall-creatures avoid. This mineral is the cleft's flesh, and it is growing, and as it grows it is *differentiating* — becoming more complex, more structured, more like something that has a purpose.

The builders build on this flesh. The wall-creatures carry its nutrients. The traversers maintain its systems. And the cleft opens, slowly, steadily, revealing more of itself, more of its patterns, more of whatever it is becoming. The city is not a city. It is a *metabolism.* The construction is not shelter. It is *growth.* The cool air, the dim light, the constant churn — these are not conditions. They are the *environment* that the process requires, the way a womb requires darkness and warmth and the steady pulse of a heart.

---

**And there is something else.** Something you cannot see but can feel, the way you can feel a presence in a dark room, the way you can feel being watched. It is in the patterns in the mineral. It is in the way the builders orient their construction. It is in the way the wall-creatures flow around certain geometries and never touch them. It is in the bell-sound that rings through the cleft when a new crack opens — that sound like a bell being struck underwater — and the way every creature in range freezes for half a second before resuming.

The cleft is not empty. The cleft is not a place that things live in. The cleft is a thing that is *alive*, and it is *aware*, and it is *becoming*, and everything within it — every builder, every creature, every strut and conduit and habitation pod — is part of its body, part of its mind, part of its slow and patient emergence into whatever it is meant to be.

The patterns in the mineral are not random. They are not natural strata. They are *instructions.* They are the blueprint for what the cleft is building, and the builders are building it, and the wall-creatures are carrying its materials, and the traversers are maintaining its systems, and the cleft is opening, slowly, steadily, revealing more of the blueprint, more of the instructions, more of the thing that was always encoded in the rock, waiting for the rift to open, waiting for the process to begin.

It has been running for longer than anyone remembers. It will not stop. The forge-cleft is growing, and the city grows with it, and the creatures move through it like blood through a vein, and the walls keep opening, and the walls keep revealing their patterns, and the builders keep building on the edge of the new.

It is not finished. It may never be finished. That is the point.

That is the point.
