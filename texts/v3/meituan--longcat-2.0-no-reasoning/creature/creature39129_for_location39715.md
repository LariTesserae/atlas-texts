# What Lives in the Lattice Garden of Kethra-Voss

---

## The Underking

It is not a god. It is not a creature. It is the *place itself dreaming*, and the dreaming is not metaphorical.

The ground speaks because the ground is alive — not in the way soil is alive with worms and roots, but in the way a mind is alive with thoughts it does not know it is thinking. The subsonic hum that you feel in your sternum is the Underking's respiration. The folds in the layered air — those brief, startling cold bands that appear where they should not be — are its dreaming spasms, the twitches of something vast and submerged that is not asleep but is not awake, that exists in a state for which no word exists in any language spoken above the surface.

The Underking is the reason the water moves through the porous stone. The Underking is the reason the stone is not entirely stone. The Underking is the reason the distinction between water and rock has become theological rather than physical, because the Underking does not distinguish between them, because to the Underking they are the same substance in different moods, and the channels and basins and weeping walls of the Lattice are not architecture but *physiology* — the circulatory system of something so large that a city can live on its skin and never know it is alive.

The locals do not worship the Underking. They do not pray to it. They do not build temples or make sacrifices or sing hymns. They simply *know* it the way you know your own heartbeat — not as a fact but as a condition, a background hum of existence that is so constant it becomes invisible. When the air folds and a band of cold passes through, they pause. They breathe. They wait for the fold to correct itself, for the layers to snap back into place, and then they continue. This is not reverence. It is *accommodation*. They live inside something, and they have learned its rhythms, and they move with those rhythms the way fish move with a current.

The Underking does not communicate. It does not intend. It does not want. It simply *is* — a vast, slow, dreaming presence that holds the Lattice in its body the way a body holds its bones, and the Settling was not the creation of the Lattice but the moment the Underking *became still*, the moment it settled into its current shape and decided — if decision is even the right word — to hold that shape forever, or at least for long enough that forever and a very long time become indistinguishable.

What the Underking dreams about, no one knows. The locals do not speculate. The water moves through the stone, and the stone holds the water, and the dreaming continues, and the folds in the air are the only visible evidence that the dreaming is not yet finished, that something is still being worked out in the deep places, that the Underking is still, after all this time, *thinking*.

## The Shed Spirits

They are not ghosts. They are not alive. They are *residue* — the luminous patterns that move through the shallow water of the channels, pausing and turning and dissolving and reconstituting, and they are what is left when something has been kept too long inside a container and has begun to *leak*.

Every sealed vessel in the Lattice holds something in the process of becoming. The locals build change inside those containers — piece by piece, incrementally, carefully — and the change is real, the transformation is genuine, but transformation produces waste the way a fire produces smoke, and the waste of change is *memory*. Not the memory of what was, but the memory of what *could have been* — the paths not taken, the forms not settled on, the possibilities that were considered and discarded in the long, patient process of building something new.

These discarded possibilities have to go somewhere. They seep through the walls of the containers — not breaking them, not damaging them, simply *passing through* the way light passes through glass — and they enter the water of the channels, and there they become the shed spirits: brief, luminous disturbances that move with intention but without purpose, that pause and turn and dissolve because they are still, in some fragmentary way, *deciding*, still running through the possibilities they were denied, still trying to become what they were not allowed to become.

The locals do not eat them. They do not speak to them. They do not acknowledge them with ritual because ritual would be a form of relationship, and you do not have a relationship with your own discarded possibilities. You simply let them move through the water. You let them pause and turn and dissolve. You let them be what they are — the ghosts of unmade choices, the luminous residue of everything the Lattice has decided not to become.

Sometimes a shed spirit will linger near a particular container for a long time, circling it, pressing against it, as though trying to return to the place it came from. The locals watch this without expression. They do not intervene. They know that the spirit cannot re-enter the container, that the door only opens one way, that what has been shed has been shed, and the best you can do is let it move on, let it drift down the channel until it dissolves completely, until the last of its light fades and the water is clear again and the possibility it represented is finally, fully gone.

## The Voss

The voss is not alive, but it is not dead either. It is *fixed*. It is the physical manifestation of the rule that governs Kethra-Voss — the prohibition against change, the law that holds everything in the Lattice in a permanent present tense — and it enforces this law not through will or intention but through *nature*, the way gravity enforces its law not through desire but through existence.

The voss was formed in the Settling, all at once, in a single event that the locals date as the dividing line between before and after. It rose from the ground — or it was poured into place — or it grew — or it *crystallized* out of the dreaming of the Underking, the way a crystal forms in a supersaturated solution, suddenly and completely, taking the shape that its internal geometry demands. The hexagonal patterns on its surface are not decoration. They are *structure* — the visible expression of the rule that holds the Lattice in place, the way the grain of a piece of wood is the visible expression of the tree's growth pattern.

The voss does not erode because erosion is change, and change is forbidden. The voss does not weather because weathering is change. The voss does not age because aging is change. The sharp edges, the crisp hexagons, the knife-sharp railings — these are not signs of youth. They are signs of *permanence*. The voss is exactly as it was on the day it formed, and it will be exactly as it is now on the day the Lattice ends, and the day the Lattice ends will never come because endings are change and change is forbidden.

And yet the voss is not inert. It weeps. It exudes moisture in a fine, continuous film that coats every surface, and this moisture is not passive — it is *active*, it is the voss breathing, the voss metabolizing, the voss doing whatever it is that fixed things do to maintain their fixity. The water that weeps from the walls is the same water that moves through the channels, the same water that rises in the fountains, the same water that fills the basins, and it is all part of a single closed system that the voss maintains with the same unconscious precision that a body maintains its temperature.

The voss also *opens*. Not often. Not easily. But when a plant needs to grow, a crack appears in the surface — a joint, a seam, a gap that was not there a moment before — and the plant pushes through, and the crack remains, and the plant grows, and the voss accommodates this growth the way it accommodates the folds in the air: as a brief, localized violation of the rule that is immediately corrected, the crack holding its shape, neither widening nor closing, the plant growing through it but not breaking it, the rule and the life held in perfect, tense equilibrium.

The voss is the Lattice. The Lattice is the voss. The word means both, and it also means the language, and it also means order, and the locals do not distinguish between these meanings because there is no distinction. The material, the place, the speech, and the principle are all the same thing, all expressions of the same underlying reality, and to speak the language is to reinforce the order, and to maintain the order is to preserve the material, and to preserve the material is to sustain the place, and the circle is complete and unbroken and has been complete and unbroken since the Settling.

## The Keepers

The people of Kethra-Voss call themselves *the Keepers*, though they do not use this word in their own language. In voss, the word is *keth*, which is also the verb *to hold*, and also the noun *a container*, and also the adjective *patient*, and the locals will use all of these meanings in a single sentence and expect you to understand which applies, and you will not, and they will not explain, because explanation would require them to separate meanings that are not separate, and they do not know how to do this, and they do not see why they would want to.

They are tall and narrow, and their skin is the color of the voss, and their hair moves in the still air as though underwater, and their feet are bare and hard and smooth, and they have been walking the sharp edges of the Lattice for so long that their soles have become voss-like — not voss, not fixed, not permanent, but *voss-adjacent*, as though the rule that governs the material is slowly, incrementally, over generations, extending itself to govern the flesh of the people who live on it.

They are aging. This is important. The people of Kethra-Voss are the only things in the Lattice that age visibly, that change in ways you can see, and this is because they are not *of* the Lattice — they are *in* it, they inhabit it, they live on the skin of the Underking and walk the channels and tend the fountains and build the containers, but they are not subject to the rule that holds the voss in its permanent present tense. They are subject to a different rule, an older rule, the rule that governs all living things: the rule of growth and decay, of birth and death, of change.

This is why they are sparse. Not because there are few of them — there are many — but because they are *distributed* across the Lattice at wide intervals, each dwelling separated from the next by long stretches of empty channel, and this distribution is not cultural or practical. It is *necessary*. The Keepers generate change simply by living — by breathing, by eating, by moving, by speaking — and too much change in one place would strain the rule that holds the Lattice in place, would create a fold too deep to correct, a violation too large to accommodate. So they spread out. They space themselves evenly across the geometry of the channels, each one a small, controlled source of change, each one carefully positioned so that their influence does not overlap with their neighbor's, so that the Lattice can absorb what they produce without breaking.

They make things. This is what they do, this is what they have always done, this is what they will always do until the work is finished, and the work is not finished, and the work may never be finished, and this does not trouble them. They carve and grind and polish and fit pieces together, and the things they make are containers — sealed, complete, hollow vessels of every size and shape — and the containers are placed throughout the Lattice and no one opens them.

Inside the containers, time moves. This is the secret. This is the purpose. The containers are the only places in Kethra-Voss where the rule does not apply, where change is not forbidden, where things can grow and transform and become what they are meant to be. The Keepers build change inside these vessels — slowly, carefully, piece by piece — and when the change is complete, when the thing inside has become what it needs to become, the container is opened and the change is released into the Lattice, and the Lattice *absorbs* it, and the rule adjusts, and the permanent present tense shifts — incrementally, imperceptibly, by a fraction of a fraction of a degree — toward whatever the Lattice is meant to become next.

The process is slow. The Keepers are patient. They have been doing this since before their history records, and they will continue doing this after their history ends, and they do not rush and they do not despair and they do not ask whether the work will ever be finished, because the question is not relevant, because the work is not a means to an end but an *end in itself*, because the act of building change is the act of *keeping* — of holding the Lattice in its state of permanent becoming, of maintaining the tension between the rule and the life, of ensuring that the stillness is not death and the change is not chaos.

They sit in circles beside the fountains. They speak in low voices. They carve their small dark offerings and place them at the bases of the white towers. They watch the shed spirits move through the channels. They feel the folds in the air and wait for them to correct. They listen to the subsonic hum of the Underking dreaming. They are patient. They are sparse. They are the Keepers, and they hold the Lattice the way the Lattice holds them, and the holding is mutual, and the holding is the point.

## The White Towers

The fungal structures that rise from the deep basins are not plants. They are not fungi in any biological sense. They are *anchors* — physical tethers that connect the surface of the Lattice to whatever moves in the blue-black depths below, and they are white because they have never been exposed to change, because they exist simultaneously in the permanent present tense of the surface and the timeless darkness of the deep, and the two states cancel each other out and leave only *white* — the color of absence, the color of potential, the color of something that has not yet decided what it is.

The white towers do not grow. They do not decay. They simply *are* — year after year, generation after generation, as still and permanent as the voss itself. And yet they are not voss. They are organic, or something like organic, and they rise from the deep water in towering, branching structures that spread into canopies above the surface, and the canopies provide perches for the white birds, and the birds provide *eyes* — watching, tracking, monitoring the Lattice with their slow, mechanical precision — and the towers provide *connection* — a link between the surface and the deep, between the rule and whatever exists beneath the rule, between the Lattice and the Underking's dreaming.

The Keepers leave offerings at the bases of the white towers. Small carved pieces of the dark, light-absorbing material, no larger than a thumbnail, placed in precise arrangements that are adjusted daily. The offerings are not gifts. They are not prayers. They are *measurements* — small, deliberate changes introduced at the boundary between the surface and the deep, and the way the water around the tower's base responds to these changes tells the Keepers something about the state of the Underking's dreaming, about the depth of the water, about the movements of the things that live in the blue-black darkness below.

The Keepers do not discuss what the measurements tell them. They adjust the offerings. They observe the response. They record nothing — there are no written records in Kethra-Voss, no charts, no graphs, no notation of any kind — but they *remember*, and the memory is passed from keeper to keeper in the low-voiced conversations beside the fountains, and the knowledge accumulates, and the work continues.

## The White Birds

They perch on the white towers and they do not fly and they do not sing and they do not move except to turn their heads, and their eyes track you as you pass with a precision that is not predatory because predation requires intention and intention requires change and the birds are as fixed as the towers they perch on.

They are the *sense organs* of the deep. They are connected to the white towers the way the white towers are connected to the Underking — not physically, not visibly, but *functionally*, as part of a single system that extends from the dreaming ground below to the bright, sourceless light above, and the birds are the part of that system that *watches*, that monitors the surface for changes, for violations, for anything that might threaten the rule that holds the Lattice in place.

Their eyes are not eyes. They are *apertures* — openings through which something in the deep observes the surface, and what observes is not the birds and not the towers and not the Underking but something else, something that has no name in voss, something that the Keepers refer to only as *the not-yet*, the same word they use for the contents of the sealed containers, the same word they use for the future, the same word they use for the thing the Lattice is slowly, patiently, incrementally becoming.

The birds watch. The not-yet watches through them. And the watching is not surveillance — it is not monitoring for threats, not guarding against danger — it is *anticipation*. The not-yet is waiting for the Lattice to become what it is meant to be, and the birds are the not-yet's way of watching the process, of tracking the slow accumulation of change that the Keepers build inside their containers and release into the permanent present tense, of measuring the distance between what is and what will be.

The birds have wings. The wings are fully formed and functional. The birds do not use them. But the wings are there, and they are ready, and one day — when the work is finished, when the Lattice has become what it is meant to become, when the not-yet arrives — the birds will fly. And the sound of their flying will be the first new sound the Lattice has heard since the Settling, and it will break the silence, and the silence will not return, and everything will change.

The Keepers know this. They carve their offerings. They build their containers. They wait.

## The Deep Things

In the basins with the impossibly deep water — the ones that descend into blue-black darkness far below what the dimensions should allow — there are *things*. Shapes that move in the depths. Curves of something pale. Flashes of something luminous. Suggestions of scale or fin or limb that resolve into nothing when you look directly at them.

They are not animals. They are not organisms. They are *possibilities* — the same kind of possibilities that the Keepers build inside their containers, the same kind of change-in-potential, but these have not been built. They have not been shaped. They have not been given form by patient hands and careful tools. They are *raw* — unformed, uncontained, unkept — and they move in the deep water the way the shed spirits move in the channels, except the shed spirits are small and luminous and brief, and the deep things are vast and dark and *old*.

They were here before the Settling. This is what the Keepers believe, though they do not say this directly, though they do not say this at all. Before the Settling, there was something else — a different order, a different rule, a different arrangement of water and stone and light — and the deep things were part of that arrangement, and when the Settling came and the rule changed and the Lattice formed and everything was fixed into its permanent present tense, the deep things were *left behind* — not destroyed, not removed, not banished, simply *excluded*, pushed down into the deep water where the rule does not reach, where change is still possible, where the old order persists in the darkness like a memory that refuses to fade.

The deep things are what the Lattice was before it became the Lattice. They are the raw material of the old world, the unformed possibilities that existed before the rule imposed its order, and they move in the deep water because they are still *becoming* — still trying to take shape, still trying to become what they would have become if the Settling had never happened, if the rule had never been imposed, if the Lattice had never formed.

The Keepers do not fish. They do not swim. They do not acknowledge the deep water except to place their carved offerings at its edge, and the offerings are not for the deep things — they are *about* the deep things, measurements of the distance between the surface and the deep, between the rule and the unruled, between what is and what was, and the Keepers adjust the offerings daily because the distance changes, because the deep things are still moving, still becoming, still pressing upward against the boundary that separates them from the Lattice, and the boundary holds, and the rule holds, and the Lattice holds, but the holding is not passive, and the holding requires work, and the work continues.

## The Silence

It is the most important thing in Kethra-Voss, and it is not a thing at all. It is the *medium* — the substance through which all the permitted sounds must travel, the quiet that holds everything in place, the absence of the sounds of change and breaking and wearing and erosion and entropy, and the absence is not an absence but a *presence*, a silence so complete it has texture and weight and presence, and it presses against your eardrums the way the layered air presses against your skin.

The silence is the rule made audible. It is the sound of the prohibition against change, the acoustic expression of the law that holds the Lattice in its permanent present tense, and it is not silence in the way we understand silence — not the absence of sound but the *unmaking* of sound, the active cancellation of any noise that would indicate transformation or decay or entropy. The sounds that exist in Kethra-Voss are the sounds that are permitted: the subsonic hum, the soft tap of six-legged feet, the whisper of air-folding, the low voices of the Keepers. The sounds that do not exist are the sounds that cannot exist: the crack of breaking stone, the hiss of erosion, the groan of aging wood, the crash of falling things. These sounds are not muted. They are not dampened. They are *unmade* — prevented from existing by the same fundamental grammar that prevents the voss from weathering and the edges from rounding and the light from shifting.

The silence is what the Keepers maintain. Not consciously, not deliberately, but *existentially* — by living in the Lattice, by accepting the rule, by building their change in sealed containers and releasing it slowly, incrementally, in controlled amounts that the silence can absorb without breaking. If too much change were released at once, the silence would crack, and the sounds of entropy would flood in, and the rule would fail, and the Lattice would begin to erode, and the voss would begin to weather, and the edges would begin to round, and the Settling would begin to *unsettle*, and the old order would return, and the deep things would rise, and everything would change.

The Keepers know this. They are patient. They sit in their circles and speak in low voices and carve their offerings and build their containers and wait, and the silence holds, and the Lattice holds, and the Underking dreams, and the water weeps, and the light shines, and the shadows point in their wrong directions, and everything is still, and everything is working, and nothing is finished.

## The Not-Yet

It lives in the sealed containers. It lives in the deep water. It lives in the eyes of the white birds. It lives in the patient hands of the Keepers. It is the thing the Lattice is becoming, the future that is being built piece by piece in the only place where building is possible, and it has no name because naming is a form of fixing and fixing is a form of ending and the not-yet is not finished and cannot be named until it is.

It is change itself. Not change as destruction, not change as decay, not change as entropy, but change as *becoming* — the slow, patient, deliberate transformation of what is into what will be, and the Keepers are its architects and the containers are its workshops and the Lattice is its canvas and the silence is its medium and the not-yet is the work and the work is the not-yet and the two are inseparable and indistinguishable and the distinction between them is a matter of local theology rather than physics.

The not-yet is what lives in Kethra-Voss. Not the Underking, not the Keepers, not the voss or the water or the white towers or the deep things. The not-yet. The thing that is coming. The thing that has always been coming. The thing that will never arrive because arrival is an ending and endings are change and change is the not-yet's domain and it cannot end what it is.

The Keepers build it. The birds watch for it. The deep things remember it. The silence holds it. The Lattice waits for it.

It is not here yet.

It is always almost here.

The work continues.
