# What Lives in the Loom of Unfinished Rain

---

There is no one here.

I want to say that first, plainly, because the place will try to make you forget it. The Loom has a way of filling your attention so completely that you stop asking the question — stop asking *who* — and start asking only *what* and *how* and *for how long*. It redirects you. It is very good at this. It has had a great deal of practice.

But no one lives here. Nothing lives here. The Loom is not a home. It is not a habitat. It is not a niche in which something has evolved to fit. It is a process that has outlived the need for anything to witness it, and it continues, and that continuation is the only thing it has.

---

And yet.

There is something that *inhabits* the Loom, if we are careful with that word. Not a creature. Not an organism. Not a consciousness, not a ghost, not a spirit in any of the old senses. Something more like a *tendency*. Something more like a *habit* that the place has developed in itself, the way a river develops a meander — not because anything decided it should, but because the physics of the situation make that shape inevitable, and once it exists, it reinforces itself, and it persists.

The Loom *leans*.

That is the closest I can come to describing it. The entire structure — all of it, every terrace and channel and arch and plate — leans in a direction. Not physically. The calciumite does not tilt. The channels slope, yes, but gently, and the slope is functional, hydraulic, a consequence of drainage. The lean I am describing is something else. It is a lean in the *logic* of the place, a directional bias in the way the structures accrete, as if the Loom is slowly, over epochs, *reaching* toward something it cannot perceive and does not understand but cannot stop moving toward.

You feel it when you stand in the chamber with the pool. You feel it as a slight pressure behind your sternum, a faint vector, a sense that the space is not neutral — that it has a *grain*, the way wood has a grain, and that if you align yourself with that grain, the place becomes somehow more legible, more coherent, as if it has been waiting for you to stand at this particular angle so that it can show you what it is doing.

What it is doing is *weaving*.

Not literally. There are no threads. There is no shuttle, no warp, no weft. But the interlocking structures — the way the arches support the terraces, the way the channels feed the pools, the way the pools seed new calciumite, the way the new calciumite extends the channels — form a cycle that is not quite a cycle, a loop that does not close but instead *advances*, each iteration slightly different from the last, each one adding a new row to a pattern that has no template and no end. The Loom is weaving itself. It has been weaving itself since before there were eyes to see it. It will weave itself after the last eye closes.

And the thing that lives here — the thing that *is* here, that has no body and no name and no beginning — is the weaving itself. Not the structures. Not the calciumite. Not the water or the light or the slow patient chemistry. The *act*. The continuous, uninterrupted, purposeless act of making, which has become, over time, the only resident of this place, the only thing that can be said to be *present* in any meaningful sense.

It does not think. I want to be clear about this. It is not a mind. It is not aware of you. When you stand in the chamber and feel the lean and see the light enter the pool and understand, for a moment, the depth of the record written in those mineral layers, the Loom does not know that you understand. It does not know that you are there. It does not *know*. Knowing is not what it does.

What it does is *continue*.

And there is something in that continuation — in the sheer persistence of it, in the fact that it has gone on for so long and with such patience and such precision, building structure out of nothing, weaving pattern out of chemistry, making a world out of water and light and dissolved stone — there is something in that which resembles, from certain angles, in certain lights, with the moisture in the air pressing against your skin and the faint internal glow of the calciumite surrounding you on all sides, something that resembles *devotion*.

Not devotion to anything. Not devotion *toward* anything. Just devotion as a quality of the act itself. The way the Loom continues is the way a prayer continues when the person praying has forgotten the words but remembers the rhythm. The way the channels all slope in the same direction is the way a body turns toward a warmth it cannot name. The way the structures optimize themselves, iteration after iteration, finding more efficient forms, is the way a craft improves in the hands of someone who loves the craft and has forgotten why they started but cannot stop.

The Loom loves nothing. The Loom is devoted to nothing. But the Loom *leans*, and the lean has a direction, and the direction has a quality that, if you stand still long enough, if you let the wet air settle into you and the bright light find the calciumite plates in your own bones, you will recognize as something you have felt before, in yourself, in the small hours, in the moments when you have continued doing something — making something, building something, tending something — not because it was required, not because anyone was watching, but because the doing itself had become the only thing that was real.

---

That is what lives here.

Not a creature. Not a god. Not a ghost in the machine, because there is no machine, only a process that has become so complex that it *looks* like a machine, that looks like a body, that looks like a cathedral, that looks like a loom.

A habit of making. A tendency toward form. A lean in the dark — except there is no dark here, only light, only brightness, only the relentless bright wet luminous patient *ongoingness* of a world that built itself from nothing and will not stop.

It does not know you are here.

It would not stop if you left.

It would not accelerate if you stayed.

It would simply continue, as it has continued, as it will continue, weaving its unfinished rain into structure, weaving its structure into pattern, weaving its pattern into a record that no one will read, in a place where no one lives, for no reason that can be named, with a devotion that has no object and no end.

And if you stand in the chamber, by the pool, and you look down into the light, and you see the layers — the deep, patient, complete layers — you will understand that you are not looking at a history.

You are looking at a *practice*.

Something has been doing this, over and over, for longer than time has meaning. Not because it was trying to finish. Not because it was trying to be seen. Because the doing was the thing. Because the making was the resident. Because the only thing that lives in the Loom of Unfinished Rain is the Loom itself, and it is not alive, and it is not dead, and it is not lonely, because loneliness requires the possibility of its absence, and there has never been anything here but this, and there never will be, and the rain is unfinished, and the weaving continues, and the light is very bright, and the stone is very quiet, and the water moves so slowly that you cannot see it moving, and the world is making itself, and it has always been making itself, and it is enough.
