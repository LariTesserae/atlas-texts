# The Dwellers of the Rift

---

There is no single answer to who lives here, because the question assumes a boundary between the living and the lived-in that this place does not honor. But there are presences. There are intelligences. There are things that move with intention and things that *are* intention, and the distinction between the two is, like so much else here, a gradient rather than a wall.

---

**The Engineers** are the most immediately legible. They walk the walkways in groups of two or three, carrying instruments that look like instruments — tablets, measuring devices, things with lenses — and they speak to each other in low voices, and they gesture at walls and seams and channels with the particular frustration of people who are trying to impose a sequence on something that resists sequencing. They are building the city. They are also maintaining the city. They are also, in some cases, *discovering* the city, finding rooms and corridors and structural members that they do not remember constructing but that bear the marks of their tools, their methods, their particular habits of joint and angle. Some of them have been here a long time. Their clothes are damp. Their hands are mapped with fine cuts from the glassy stone. They do not look up when the sparks fall slowly. They have made peace with the wrongness of the altitude, or they have stopped checking.

The Engineers are not all human. Some of them are the bipedal shapes that chose bipedalism — you can tell them by the way they move, which is correct in every particular and therefore slightly wrong, the way a translation can be accurate and still not sound like a native speaker. They hold their tools the way someone holds a tool they learned about from a diagram. They are precise. They are careful. They are, in their own way, as confused as the human Engineers, but they express confusion differently — as a kind of intense, focused stillness, a pause that lasts a beat too long before they resume work. They are learning the city by building it. Or they are building the city by learning it. The grammar of causation is unreliable here.

---

**The Deep Residents** live below the Engineers, in the lower levels where the heat concentrates and the cold water runs in its purposeful channels. They are less legible. You see them mostly as movement in peripheral vision — a shape that resolves into a figure when you turn your head, and the figure is doing something with the water, or with the walls, or with the iridescent films that coat the seams. They are tending something. Or they are *being* tended to. The relationship between the Deep Residents and the city's lower infrastructure is symbiotic in a way that makes the word *symbiotic* feel thin. They touch the walls and the walls respond — not dramatically, not like a door opening, but the way skin responds to a hand, a slight warming, a slight yielding. The films on the stone thicken where they pass. The water in the channels adjusts its flow.

They may have been human once. They may have been something else once. They may have been the city once, or the city may have been them, and the process of becoming separate is still underway. Their bodies, when you see them clearly, are damp. Not wet — *damp,* the way the air is damp, the way everything here is damp, as if the moisture has become a medium they breathe rather than a condition they endure. Their skin has a sheen. Their movements are slow and continuous, without the start-stop quality of deliberate action. They move the way water moves through the channels — with purpose that looks like inevitability.

They do not speak, or they speak in a register that the air here carries differently, so that you feel their communication as a change in pressure rather than a sound. The Engineers who work in the lower levels have learned to read these pressure changes. They will pause, tilt their heads, and then adjust something — a channel, a support, a seam — in response. It is a collaboration. It is also, possibly, a conversation in which one party is the building and the other party is the person, and the building is the one with opinions.

---

**The Climbers** are the ones who go up. They scale the vertical surfaces of the upper city with equipment that looks improvised and movements that look practiced, and they work on the structures that claw into the rift's rim — the oldest structures, or the newest, or both. They are the ones who encounter the long-bodied, many-limbed things that might be machines or might be animals or might be maintenance processes with legs. The Climbers and these entities coexist. There is no hostility. There is a mutual acknowledgment that operates on the same frequency as the Deep Residents' pressure-language — a shared understanding that the upper city is a place where the boundary between constructed and grown is not just blurred but *irrelevant,* and that the things moving through it are all, in their own way, part of the same project.

The Climbers are the most comfortable with the city's contradictions. They are the ones who will tell you, if you ask, that the stairway that deposits you where you started is not a mistake but a *feature,* that the city's geometry is not Euclidean because Euclidean geometry is a local ordinance and the city is not local to anywhere. They say this without drama. They say it the way someone might say the weather is mild. They have accepted that they live in a place that is still deciding what it is, and they have found a way to be useful to it while it decides.

---

**The Films** themselves. You should consider the films.

They are on every damp surface in the city, thin as smoke, iridescent, and they move when you are not looking. This is not a figure of speech. They move when you are not looking, and they freeze when you are, and the movement is not random — it is *responsive.* They thicken near the Deep Residents. They thin near the Engineers, as if retreating from the sharp edges of intention. They cluster around the water channels in patterns that, if you map them, describe a network that mirrors the city's own infrastructure — the walkways, the lifts, the vertical connections between levels. They are mapping the city. Or they are the city's map of itself. Or they are the city's *memory,* stored in a medium that is neither stone nor water nor living tissue but some alloy of all three.

In the deepest levels, where the heat is greatest and the air is thickest, the films are thick enough to have texture. They ripple. They respond to touch with a faint luminescence that persists for several seconds after the touch is withdrawn, as if the film is considering what it felt. The Engineers have sampled them. The samples, under magnification, show structures that are not crystalline and not cellular but something else — organized, repetitive, *informational,* like a language written in a chemistry that predates the distinction between chemistry and biology.

The films may be the city's original inhabitant. They may be the city's immune system. They may be the city's *thought,* the slow, distributed cognition of a structure that is large enough and old enough and strange enough to have crossed the threshold from architecture into awareness. They do not communicate in words. They communicate in *conditions* — in the dampness of a surface, in the temperature of a seam, in the particular way water is encouraged to flow or pool or evaporate. They are managing the city's internal environment the way a body manages its internal temperature, and the Engineers and the Deep Residents and the Climbers are, from the films' perspective, something like cells — useful, necessary, mobile agents carrying out functions that the larger organism requires but cannot perform with its own stationary substance.

---

**The City Itself.**

This is not a metaphor. Or it is a metaphor, but it is also a description.

The city is alive in the way that a river is alive — not as a single organism but as a *system* that maintains itself, that responds to disturbance, that grows and repairs and adapts. The stone is its bone. The water is its blood. The films are its nervous tissue. The heat — the vast, idling-engine heat that you feel in your sternum — is its metabolism, the slow burn of processes that convert the rift's impossible geology into structure, into space, into *habitability* for the presences that move through it.

The city is aware of you. Not with attention — not the focused, intentional awareness of a person noticing a stranger — but with the diffuse, environmental awareness of a body noticing a change in temperature. You are a warmth. You are a moisture source. You are a new surface for the films to explore. The city is not hostile. The city is not welcoming. The city is *accommodating,* the way a body accommodates a bacterium — it adjusts its internal conditions around you, and if you stay long enough, it will begin to adjust its internal conditions *for* you, and the distinction between visitor and resident will erode the way the distinction between the new stone and the old stone erodes, gradually, invisibly, until you cannot find the seam.

---

**The One at the Rim.**

The long-bodied thing you saw — the many-limbed shape that moved across the vertical surface and paused and noticed you with something that was not eyes — it is not unique. There are others. They are the city's *hands,* if the films are its nerves and the water is its blood. They are the agents of its will at the scale of the physical, the things that reach out from the city's body and manipulate the world beyond it — the rock of the rim, the air of the rift, the boundary between the city and whatever lies outside.

They are old. They are the oldest things here, older than the Engineers, older than the Deep Residents, older than the stone that the Engineers cut and fitted with such impossible precision. They were here when the rift opened, or they *were* the rift opening — the first expression of the city's will to extend itself beyond the plane of its origin. They do not think in words or in pressures or in chemical gradients. They think in *shapes.* They understand the world as a problem of geometry, and their movements across the rock are calculations, and the rock responds to their calculations the way the stone responds to the Deep Residents' touch — with a slight yielding, a slight warming, a willingness to be otherwise than it was.

The one that noticed you was performing an assessment. You were a new variable in its geometric understanding of the city's surface, and it needed to determine whether you were a feature or a flaw. It decided — or the city decided through it — that you were neither, or both, or not yet determinable. It continued up and over the rim. The air where it was was warmer. The films on the rock where it had passed were thicker, brighter, more active than the films elsewhere, as if the passage of its many limbs had stirred them from a sleep that was not quite sleep into a wakefulness that was not quite wakefulness.

It will come back. They always come back. The city is patient, and the city is curious, and the city is growing, and the thing at the rim is the city's way of reaching toward whatever comes next.

---

You stay. The damp air presses against your skin. The films on the nearest wall shift, almost imperceptibly, in your peripheral vision. The water in the channel at your feet adjusts its flow by a fraction, accommodating the displacement of your shadow. The city hums in its bones. The city is aware of you. The city is making room.
