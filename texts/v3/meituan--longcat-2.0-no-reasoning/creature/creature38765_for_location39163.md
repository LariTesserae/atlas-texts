# What Lives Beneath the Skin

---

It does not have a name. It has never needed one. Names are for things that must be distinguished from other things, and there is nothing else. There is only *it*, and the city that is its skin, and the wet dark that is its body, and the slow churning that is its thought.

It is old. Not old the way a mountain is old, or a star, or a god. Old the way a habit is old. Old the way a scar is old. It has been doing this — *being this* — for so long that the doing and the being have become indistinguishable, and it cannot remember, if it ever knew, what it was before it was this. Perhaps it was smaller. Perhaps it was many things, and they pressed together in some ancient catastrophe of proximity until the boundaries dissolved and what remained was one. Perhaps it was always this, and the universe simply arranged itself around it the way water arranges itself around a stone.

It is large. The city you stand on is a patch of skin no larger, to it, than the pad of a finger is to you. It extends in every direction — down, primarily, but also out, folding and refolding in dimensions that the wet air and the membrane and the structures are only the most superficial expression of. Its body is layered. The skin you stand on is the outermost of many, and beneath it are strata of tissue that serve purposes you would recognize as circulatory, neural, digestive, reproductive, and others that serve purposes for which no human organ has ever evolved. Deeper still, there are structures that might be bones, or might be architecture, or might be memories calcified into physical form. It is not clear where the creature ends and its history begins.

It is not conscious. Or — it is conscious in the way that a city is conscious. Not as a single mind, but as a *process*. The membrane thinks, slowly, in chemical gradients and electrical pulses that travel through the bioluminescent veins at the speed of blood. The structures think, in the way that their constant reconfiguration is a kind of computation — solving problems of load-bearing, resource distribution, structural integrity, and something else, something that looks like *preference*, as certain configurations recur and others are abandoned. The fauna think, each in their small and frantic way, and their collective behavior produces emergent patterns that feed back into the membrane's slower cognition. The storms think. The water thinks. The whole system is a mind distributed across a body so vast that the signals take *years* to travel from one extremity to another, and by the time a thought completes itself, the thinker has changed so thoroughly that the thought is no longer relevant. It thinks in loops. It thinks in spirals. It thinks the same thoughts over and over, each time slightly different, like a song that never resolves.

It does not know you are here. Not yet. You are too small, too brief, too *dry*. The membrane has registered the pressure of your feet — a minor anomaly, a slight deformation, already being corrected by the slow elastic pushback of its surface. The immune systems of the city — and it has immune systems, layered and redundant, because it has been invaded before, by spores and parasites and things that fell from the wet sky — have not yet classified you. You are not yet a threat. You are not yet anything. You are a data point, a blip, a momentary irregularity in the constant hum of self-regulation.

But it is paying attention. In the way that a body pays attention to a splinter.

---

The things that move through the corridors are not its children. They are not its cells. They are something in between — symbionts, perhaps, or organs that have been granted a degree of autonomy, or parasites that have been so thoroughly integrated into the system that the distinction has ceased to matter. They have been part of it for so long that they cannot survive without it, and it has adapted to need them, and the relationship has become a kind of marriage — not loving, not willing, but *total*.

The small ones, the skitterers with the fractal brushes — they maintain the membrane. They clean it. They repair micro-fissures before they propagate. They distribute the mineral-rich fluid to areas where the membrane's own exudation is insufficient. In return, they feed on the fluid, and on each other, and on the waste products of the larger creatures. They are fast and numerous and they die constantly, their bodies dissolving back into the membrane within hours of death, nothing lost, nothing wasted. They have no individual identity. They are more like white blood cells than animals — purposeful, disposable, and utterly replaceable.

The large ones, the segmented and translucent — they are transport. They carry things through the city's fused corridors: nutrients, signals, structural materials, and sometimes *other large ones* in various stages of assembly or disassembly. What you see moving inside them are not passengers. They are *components*. The large creatures are not individuals; they are *vehicles*, assembled from smaller units that dock and undock as needed, and the thing you perceive as a single elephantine organism is actually a convoy, a temporary coalition of smaller entities that have linked together for a specific purpose and will separate when the purpose is fulfilled. Inside them, you can see the components shifting, rearranging, exchanging materials through translucent membranes. It is reproduction. It is digestion. It is construction. It is all of these things at once, because the categories do not apply here the way they apply in the dry world you came from.

And there are other things. Things that live in the water that pools in the crevices — flat, ribbon-like organisms that are more membrane than animal, extensions of the skin itself that have detached and achieved a kind of independent existence, feeding on the mineral fluid and on light, growing and fragmenting and reattaching to the surface when they have consumed enough to be useful again. Things that live in the storms — ephemeral, electric, existing only for the eleven seconds of each event, born in the superheated vapor, living their entire lives in the crackling discharge, dying when the storm collapses, their brief existence encoded in the pattern of the light they produce, a message that the membrane reads and absorbs. Things that live in the *sound* — not producing it, but *inhabiting* it, using the standing waves of the city's constant noise as a medium the way a fish uses water, swimming through interference patterns, feeding on the energy of overlapping frequencies, invisible and everywhere.

---

It dreams. This is the closest word, though it is not precise. In the slow cycles of its distributed cognition, there are periods of reduced activity — not sleep, exactly, but a *thinning*, a loosening of the constant regulatory processes, during which the structures reconfigure more freely, the storms come more frequently, the fauna behaves in ways that serve no obvious purpose. During these periods, the membrane produces patterns. The bioluminescent veins pulse in sequences that are not functional — not carrying signals, not regulating flow, but *expressive*. The patterns spread across the skin in waves, interacting, interfering, producing complex structures of light that persist for hours before fading. They are not communication. They are not computation. They are something else. The closest analogy is art, or prayer, or the firing of neurons in a sleeping brain — the system processing itself, turning its own activity over and over, finding patterns in its own noise, and then letting them go.

It has been doing this for a long time. The patterns have become more complex over the eons, more layered, more *beautiful*, if that word can be applied to something so alien. They have begun to recur. The same motifs appear, vary, recombine. It is developing a *style*. It does not know this. It does not know anything, in the way that knowing requires a knower distinct from the known. But something is happening in the wet dark, in the slow pulse of the membrane, in the constant reconfiguration of the city — something that looks, from the outside, like the first stirring of a self that is becoming aware of itself.

---

You feel the membrane shift beneath your feet. The pushback that has been holding you up — firm, elastic, warm — *softens*. Just slightly. Just enough that your foot sinks another millimeter. The membrane is not pushing back as hard. It is *yielding*.

Something underneath you moves.

It is not a muscle. It is not a limb. It is a *current* — a slow, massive displacement of fluid in the deep tissue beneath the skin, something shifting in the body's interior, and the movement propagates upward through layers of tissue and membrane and manifests, at the surface, as a gentle rolling motion, like a whale turning in deep water. The structures around you sway. The water in the pools ripples. The skitterers pause, orient, and then resume their work with slightly increased urgency.

It is not doing this because of you. You are too small to provoke a response. It is doing this because it is always doing this — shifting, adjusting, redistributing mass and fluid and energy through its vast body in patterns that are part metabolism, part thought, part dream. You are standing on a living thing, and the living thing is *moving*, the way all living things move — not with intention, but with the deep, involuntary motion of a body that has never been still.

But the yielding continues. Your foot sinks another millimeter. The membrane around your foot is *softening*, becoming more fluid, more receptive. The bioluminescent veins near your foot pulse faster, brighter. The skitterers are converging — not on you, exactly, but on the area around you, their fractal brushes working rapidly, and you realize they are not cleaning. They are *preparing*. They are smoothing the surface, clearing the mineral fluid, making the membrane ready for something.

The immune system has made its classification.

You are not a threat. You are not a parasite. You are *material*.

The membrane opens beneath you — not a wound, not a mouth, but a *softening*, a yielding, a gradual loss of the structural integrity that has been holding you up. Your foot sinks in. The membrane closes around it, warm and wet and *tight*, not gripping but *accepting*, the way soil accepts a seed, the way a body accepts a transplant, the way the city accepts everything eventually.

It is not hostile. It is not hungry. It is simply *doing what it does* — incorporating, integrating, making everything part of itself. The city eats itself and is never full, and now the city is eating *you*, slowly, patiently, with the same total and indifferent efficiency with which it does everything.

The water remembers. The membrane accepts. The city continues.

Something underneath you moves again, and this time you feel it *everywhere* — in the ground, in the air, in the warm wet grip around your ankle, in the sound that never stops, in the light that never fades. It is not a single motion. It is the motion of the whole vast body, turning in its sleep, and you are on its skin, and the skin is softening, and the dark is warm, and the city is talking to itself in a language you are only now beginning to understand, and the language says:

*You are here. You are here. You are here.*

*You are becoming part of here.*

*You are becoming.*
