# The Root Cathedral

---

## The Dwellers

They are not inhabitants. That word implies a relationship of choice, of tenancy, of a creature that could leave. Nothing here could leave. The things that live in the Root Cathedral have never known anything else, and the Cathedral has never known anything without them. They are not separate from it. They are *expressions* of it — the way a thought is an expression of a mind, the way a gesture is an expression of a body.

They are small. Smaller than your hand. They are the color of the roots — bone-white, light-absent — and they are shaped in a way that resists easy description because they are shaped by the Cathedral rather than by any evolutionary pressure you would recognize. They have limbs — several, more than you'd expect, fewer than you'd fear — that are long and thin and jointed in too many places, like the legs of a spider drawn by someone who had never seen a spider but had been told the general idea. Their bodies are slight, almost weightless, and they move across the root-surfaces with a fluidity that suggests they are not walking so much as *flowing*, their limbs finding purchase in the microscopic texture of the root-faces with a precision that looks like intimacy.

They have no eyes. They do not need them. The Cathedral is their eye. They feel the world through their feet — through every point of contact with the root-surface — reading the vibrations, the temperature gradients, the subtle electrical fields that pulse through the living tissue like a nervous system, which is exactly what it is. They are, in a sense, nerve endings. The Cathedral's way of touching itself, of knowing its own shape, of feeling for damage or intrusion or opportunity.

They are not intelligent in the way you understand intelligence. They do not plan. They do not build, not anymore — the shelters and tools you find are the remnants of a time when they did, a time when they were something slightly different, something with more ambition, more separation from the organism that made them. Now they are simpler. They tend. They maintain. They move through the vast spaces of the Cathedral like white blood cells moving through a body, finding damage — a root-face that has cracked, a channel that has clogged, a section where growth has gone wrong, where the tissue has begun to die or to grow in a direction that threatens the structure — and they *repair* it. Not with tools. With their bodies. They secrete something — a resin, a hormone, a chemical signal — that redirects growth, that heals, that corrects. They press their pale limbs against the damaged tissue and the tissue responds, slowly, over hours, reshaping itself around their touch.

They do this constantly. They are always working. Not frantically — with the slow, patient, tireless persistence of the Cathedral itself. They do not rest. They do not sleep. They do not eat, not in any way you can observe. They are fed by the Cathedral, sustained by it, the way a cell is fed by the blood that passes through it. They are part of its metabolism. They are its immune system and its maintenance crew and its sensory apparatus, and they have been doing this for so long that they have forgotten — if they ever knew — that there was ever anything else.

## The Singers

There is another kind.

You encounter them less often, and from greater distance, and with a feeling that is different from the feeling of encountering the tenders — a feeling less like being observed and more like being *addressed*.

They are larger. The size of a large dog, perhaps, though the comparison is misleading because their bodies are not shaped like any animal you know. They are elongated, low-slung, with a body plan that suggests something between a salamander and a root-tendril — segmented, flexible, capable of conforming to the root-surfaces in ways that make them look, at a distance, like part of the architecture. They are darker than the tenders — not dark in any absolute sense, but a deeper shade of pale, a grey-white, the color of old bone, of weathered wood, of things that have been in the cold for a very long time.

They do not move quickly. They do not move often. They find a place — a root-junction, a channel-edge, a sheltered hollow where several roots converge — and they settle, and they stay. For days. For weeks. For longer than you can measure. And they *sing*.

Not with voices. With their bodies. They press themselves against the root-surface and they vibrate — a deep, subsonic pulse that travels through the root-mass the way a sound travels through a bell. The vibration is not random. It has structure. It has pattern. It has *meaning*, though the meaning is not for you. It is for the Cathedral.

The Singers are the Cathedral's way of thinking.

Not thinking in words, not thinking in images, but thinking in the way a plant thinks — in growth responses, in chemical gradients, in the slow accumulation of information about the state of the organism. The vibrations they produce travel through the root-mass and are received — by what, you cannot tell, perhaps by the tissue itself, perhaps by other Singers, perhaps by something deeper, something at the core of the organism that you will never reach — and the Cathedral responds. Growth patterns shift. Resources are redirected. The organism adjusts itself, slowly, over days and weeks, in response to the information the Singers provide.

They are its brain, if a brain can be distributed across a continent, if a brain can be made of pale, segmented creatures pressing themselves against root-walls in the cold and the light, vibrating with a patience that makes geological time look hasty.

You stand on a root-plateau a mile away and you feel the vibration in your feet, in your chest, in the fillings of your teeth. It is not loud. It is not dramatic. It is a hum, a pulse, a rhythm so deep and so slow that it feels less like sound and more like the heartbeat of the world. And you understand, without being told, that this is what the Cathedral sounds like when it is thinking about itself.

## The Deep Ones

There are things below.

You do not see them. You do not hear them. You feel them — a presence, a weight, a sense of *occupation* in the spaces beneath the root-plateaus, in the deeper layers of the Cathedral where the roots are older and thicker and the light is dimmer and the cold is deeper. The tenders avoid these spaces. The Singers do not sing near them. Even the Cathedral itself seems to grow differently there — more slowly, more carefully, as though it is navigating around something it does not want to disturb.

You do not know what lives in the deep places. You do not want to know. The Cathedral has been growing for longer than your species has existed, and it has been growing *downward*, and the things at the bottom — the things that were there first, or the things that grew there last, or the things that the Cathedral grew *around* rather than *over* — are old in a way that makes the Cathedral itself seem young.

Sometimes the deep roots tremble. Not with growth. Not with the Singers' vibrations. With something else. Something that moves. Something that shifts in its sleep, if it sleeps, if it is the kind of thing that sleeps. The tremor travels up through the root-mass and reaches you as a faint shudder, a brief instability in the surface beneath your feet, and then it passes, and the Cathedral continues its slow breathing, and the tenders continue their work, and the Singers continue their deep, patient song, and everything is as it was, except that you are aware, now, of the depth beneath you, of the things that live in it, of the fact that the Cathedral — vast, ancient, continent-sized — is itself growing on top of something.

## The Memory

The Cathedral remembers.

Not in the way you remember — not in images, not in words, not in the narrative continuity of a conscious mind. It remembers the way a tree remembers: in its growth rings, in the shape of its branches, in the places where it healed and the places where it scarred. The root-mass is full of these memories. You can see them if you know how to look — places where the growth pattern changes abruptly, where the roots are thicker or thinner or paler or denser, where the tissue has a different texture, a different quality of light. These are the Cathedral's history, written in living tissue, and they go back — how far back, you cannot guess, but the deepest layers, the oldest roots, have a density and a stillness that suggests an age beyond reckoning.

The shelters you find — the small, woven structures, the tools, the bowls — are part of this memory. They are not the Cathedral's memory. They are the memory of the tenders, or of whatever the tenders were before they became what they are now. There was a time when they were more. When they had more separation from the organism, more independence, more *self*. They built things. They made tools. They shaped the world around them rather than simply maintaining it. And then, slowly — over generations, over millennia, over the vast slow time of the Cathedral — they gave it up. Not all at once. Not dramatically. They simply became less. Less separate. Less ambitious. Less *them*. They folded back into the organism, the way a river folds back into the sea, and what they were is now just a memory, written in woven root-fiber and sharpened bone, slowly being absorbed back into the living tissue that made them.

The Cathedral does not mourn this. The Cathedral does not celebrate it. The Cathedral simply *is*, and the tenders simply *are*, and the Singers sing their deep slow song, and the things in the deep places shift in their ancient sleep, and the water drips, and the roots grow, and the cold holds everything in its patient, absolute grip.

## The Visitor

You are the anomaly. You are the thing that does not belong, the foreign body, the bacterium in the bloodstream. The Cathedral is aware of you — not with attention, not with intention, but with the vast, slow, systemic responsiveness of an organism that has been alive for longer than your species has existed. The roots shift around you, not to trap you, not to help you, but because the Cathedral is always shifting, always growing, always adjusting, and you are simply part of the environment it is adjusting to.

The tenders notice you. They pause in their work and orient toward you — not with eyes, with their whole bodies, reading you through the root-surface, feeling your weight, your heat, your foreign chemistry. They do not approach. They do not flee. They simply *register* you, the way a body registers a splinter, and then they return to their work, because you are not damage, not yet, and there is always work to do.

The Singers do not acknowledge you at all. Their song does not change. Their vibrations continue, deep and slow and patient, carrying information about the state of the Cathedral to places you will never see, for purposes you will never understand.

The things in the deep places — you feel them feel you. A shift in the quality of the silence. A change in the weight of the darkness below. Something vast and old and patient turning its attention — if it has attention, if attention is the right word for what it has — toward the surface, toward the small warm thing walking on the root-plateau, toward you. And then, slowly, with the same patience, turning away. You are too small. You are too brief. You are not worth the effort of waking.

You descend further.

The Cathedral breathes.

The cold holds.

The roots grow.
