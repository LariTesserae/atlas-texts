In the Subjacent Chamber, the lace itself is the primary inhabitant — not a creature, not a plant, but a living architecture that grows with patient, unstoppable purpose, filling every void with its fractal weave. It breathes out mist in cycles, splits fresh rock with slow grinding persistence, and almost speaks in mathematical patterns just below comprehension. Within this golden, shrinking space, a handful of tiny, pale, six-legged beings — no larger than grains of rice — move with loud, arrhythmic clicking, so scarce that a dozen may be all that exist in the entire chamber. They climb the lace, signal one another, and occasionally vanish into folds in the air that were not there a moment before. The lace is the world; the small clicking things are its rare, fleeting witnesses.
