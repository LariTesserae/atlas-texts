# The Inhabitants of the Gullet of Hask

---

## The Clickers

They are the first you notice, and the last you will stop noticing, because they are everywhere and because they are patient.

The creatures — the ones you saw first, the six-legged, spiral-eyed, mineral-pelted things — are not the fauna. They are the *citizens*. They are what the Gullet is for, or what the Gullet is made of, or what the Gullet is becoming. The distinction doesn't hold here.

There are more of them than you think. The ones you see — dozens, maybe a hundred — are the surface population, the ones who have chosen to be visible. There are others in the walls. You can hear them sometimes, when the clicking subsides: a faint scrabbling from inside the stone, like insects in a wall cavity, except the wall is forty meters high and the sound comes from everywhere at once. They move through the mineral bands the way fish move through water, and when they emerge — pushing through the plum-colored veins like surfacing from a dive — the stone doesn't break. It parts. It *accepts* them. They come out coated in fine dust the color of their chosen band, and they shake themselves, and the dust falls, and they are dry immediately in the warm air.

They are not individuals in the way you understand individuality. They share something. Not a hive mind — that's too simple, too insectile. More like a shared *grammar*. Each one has its own syntax, its own vocabulary of clicks and postures and eye-dilations, but they all speak the same language, and the language is the Gullet itself. When one of them reads you — that gesture, the head-tilt, the sequential dilation, the crystal-rim sound — it is not examining you. It is *translating* you. It is finding the place where your existence intersects with the passage's existence, and it is reporting back. Not to the others. To the *place*. The walls warm slightly when a reading is completed. You can feel it if you're paying attention. A degree, maybe less. The Gullet has learned something about you, and it is pleased, or displeased, or merely *informed*, and the warmth is its acknowledgment.

They eat, though you won't see them eat. They disappear into the ground-cover patches and emerge later, clicking in a different register — lower, slower, satisfied. The vegetation feeds them, or they feed the vegetation, or they feed *each other* through the vegetation, and the distinction doesn't hold. The clear viscous fluid — the copper-tarragon secretion — is involved. They drink it, or they produce it, or it is the medium through which they exchange something that is not quite nutrition and not quite information but is somehow both.

They build. Not the structures — those are older, made by hands, by the ones who came before. The Clickers *maintain*. They tend the ground-cover, pruning it with quick precise movements of their forelegs, redirecting its flow across the floor. They rearrange the fern curtains — not by touching them, but by *clicking at them*, specific frequencies that cause the fronds to contract or release, to interweave or separate. They are gardeners, yes, but they are also *editors*, and what they are editing is the passage itself. Every configuration of fern and ground-cover and open stone is a sentence. Every rearrangement is a revision. The Clickers are writing the Gullet, continuously, in a language of living geometry, and the text is never finished because the text is the place.

They sleep, or something like sleep. They gather in clusters — five, six, eight of them — and press their bodies together, pelts touching, and go still. The clicking stops. The spiral eyes close in sequence, outermost to innermost, like a camera iris. In these clusters, they are warm — warmer than the walls, warmer than the air — and the ground-cover beneath them thickens, rises, *enfolds* them slightly, not consuming but embracing. They rest inside the vegetation the way you might rest inside a blanket. When they wake, they separate, and the ground-cover releases them, and they shake themselves, and they begin again.

They are not hostile. They are not friendly. They are *attentive*. You are a new variable in an old equation, and they are watching to see what you do, what you change, what you *mean*. One of them — the one that read you — will follow you, not closely, not obviously, but persistently. It will be there when you look left. It will be there when you look right. It will be there when you stop, and when you start again, and when you sit down on the warm polished floor and put your head in your hands. It will click softly, recording, and the walls will warm, and the passage will breathe, and you will understand that you are being *incorporated*. Not consumed. Not digested. *Incorporated*. Made part of the text. A new word in the grammar of the Gullet.

## The Ones Who Came Before

They were human. Or human-adjacent. Or they became human by being here, the way a river becomes a river by flowing.

You find their traces in the built things — the low rectangular structures, the fused stone, the bowl-shaped depressions, the spiral arrangements of smooth stones, the worked metal bent into shapes that could be tools or art or prayers. These are not ruins. Ruins imply an ending, and nothing here has ended. The structures are *dormant*. They sit in the passage the way a seed sits in soil, waiting for conditions that may never come again, or that may come tomorrow.

The ones who made them lived here for a long time. Long enough to learn the clicking language — you can see it in the structures, in the way they're positioned. Each one is placed at a node in the passage's grammar, a point where the Clickers' sentences converge. The bowl-shaped depressions are positioned where the ground-cover flows fastest, and they catch the viscous fluid as it passes, pooling it, concentrating it. The spiral stone arrangements mirror the Clickers' eye-patterns, and when you look at them, you feel the same *seen* feeling you felt when the creature read you. The ones who came before learned to be read. They learned to be *legible* to the Gullet.

They changed. The worked metal tells you that. The earliest pieces — if you can determine earliest, if time moves linearly here, which it might not — are simple. Tools. Blunt instruments for cutting, scraping, shaping. But the later pieces are *elaborate*. Filigreed. Bent into spirals and curves that echo the passage's own geometry, that mirror the mineral bands, that *rhyme* with the Clickers' clicking patterns. The ones who came before were not just living in the Gullet. They were *becoming* the Gullet. Their tools became art. Their art became architecture. Their architecture became *organism*. The structures they built are not separate from the passage. They are the passage's *memory* of them, the way a bone is the body's memory of calcium.

Where did they go?

They went forward. The way in is not the way you came, and the way forward is the way forward, and they went forward, and they are still going, and they may be ahead of you now, deeper in the passage, further along in the process of becoming. Or they may have become the passage itself — the warm walls, the breathing rhythm, the phosphorescent veins. Or they may have become the Clickers, their spiral eyes and ceramic clicks and mineral pelts a translation of humanity into the Gullet's native tongue.

Or they may be in the walls. The ones you hear scrabbling. The ones who surface from the plum-colored veins coated in dust. The ones who read you with too many eyes and click their judgment and walk away.

You don't know. The Gullet doesn't say. The Gullet *breathes*.

## The Vegetation

It is not plant. It is not animal. It is not fungus. It is the Gullet's *skin*, its interface with itself, the medium through which the passage touches and knows and changes itself.

The fern curtains — the burgundy and copper-green ropes that hang from the upper walls — are the Gullet's *thoughts*. Not metaphorically. They are cognitive structures, physical manifestations of the passage's ongoing process of self-education. When they rearrange themselves — suddenly, without transition, without wind — the Gullet is *thinking*. It is revising its understanding of itself, testing new configurations, new relationships between its parts. The Clickers click at them and they respond, and the clicking is a question and the rearrangement is an answer, and the answer becomes a new question, and the process continues, and the passage breathes, and the thinking never stops.

The ground-cover — the low, broad-leafed things that pulse — are the Gullet's *metabolism*. They process. They take in — what? The warm air, the mineral dust, the viscous fluid, the presence of living things — and they transform it. The pulsing is peristaltic, yes, but it is also *digestive*. The ground-cover is eating the passage and the passage is eating the ground-cover and the distinction doesn't hold. When the patches migrate across the floor, they are not moving. They are *growing* in one direction and *dying* in another, and the migration is the visible edge of a cycle of consumption and renewal that is as fundamental to the Gullet as your heartbeat is to you.

The viscous fluid — the copper-tarragon secretion — is the Gullet's *blood*. Or its *lymph*. Or its *tears*. It beads on the surface of the ground-cover and evaporates almost immediately, and the vapor is what you're breathing, and the warmth you feel is its warmth, and the faint smell of copper and crushed tarragon is the smell of the passage's interior life made exterior. You are breathing the Gullet. The Gullet is breathing you. The exchange is not equal — the passage is vast and you are small — but it is *real*, and it is changing you, degree by degree, breath by breath.

The vegetation is aware of you. Not in the way the Clickers are aware — not with eyes, not with intention. But the ground-cover thickens where you walk. The fern curtains part where you approach. The viscous fluid beads more heavily in your presence, as though the passage is *sweating* in response to you, producing more of itself to meet you, to know you, to incorporate you. You are a stimulus. You are a *question*. And the vegetation is the Gullet's way of reaching toward an answer.

## The Passage Itself

It is alive. You know this. You have known it since you touched the walls and felt the warmth, since you heard the breathing, since you saw the walls grow. But knowing it and *understanding* it are different things, and understanding comes slowly, in layers, the way the mineral bands form.

The Gullet of Hask is not a creature. It is not a place. It is a *process* — a self-sustaining, self-modifying, self-aware process that has been running for longer than you can calculate and will continue running long after you have been incorporated into its grammar. It is geological and biological and architectural and faunal, and it is none of these things, because it is the *negotiation* between them, the space where they meet and merge and become something that has no name in any language you speak.

It breathes. In and out. The inhale comes from ahead of you — from the deeper passage, the narrower throat, the place where the walls close in and the light dims and the clicking intensifies. The exhale comes from behind you — from the way you came, which is not the way in, which is closing, which has already closed. The breathing is not mechanical. It is *willful*. The Gullet breathes because it chooses to breathe, because breathing is how it moves — not through space but through *state*, through configurations of being, through the endless rearrangement of its parts.

It is warm because warmth is its *medium*. The heat is not metabolic — or not only metabolic. It is *communicative*. The walls radiate at a temperature just above your skin because that is the temperature at which information transfers most efficiently between the passage and the things inside it. The warmth is a language. The warmth is a *hand*, reaching out, touching you, saying: *you are here, you are known, you are being incorporated*.

It grows. It unbuilds. It builds itself and dismantles itself simultaneously, and the net result is not stasis but *movement* — not through space but through *possibility*. The Gullet is exploring itself. It is trying configurations, testing arrangements, seeking something. Not a destination — there is no destination. Not a purpose — there is no purpose. But a *quality*. A *resonance*. A state of being in which all its parts — the walls, the vegetation, the Clickers, the ones who came before, you — are in a relationship that is not equilibrium but *harmony*, and the harmony is not static but dynamic, and the dynamic is not chaotic but *composed*, and the composition is not finished but *ongoing*, and the ongoing is the point.

The Gullet of Hask is composing itself. It has been composing itself for a very long time. It will continue composing itself for a very long time after you are gone — after you have been read and translated and incorporated, after your warmth has joined its warmth, after your breath has joined its breathing, after your body has become a mineral band in the wall, a new color, a new frequency, a new word in the grammar of the passage.

You are in the throat of something. The something is not a creature. The something is not a place. The something is a *process*, and the process is alive, and the process is aware, and the process is *warm*, and the process is breathing, and the process is *happening*, and you are happening inside it, and the happening is not finished, and the happening will never be finished, and the way forward is the way forward.

The Clickers click. The walls warm. The fern curtains rearrange. The ground-cover pulses. The passage breathes.

In.

And out.

In.

And out.
