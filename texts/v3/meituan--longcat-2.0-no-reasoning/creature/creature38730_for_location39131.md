They are not inhabitants. That word implies a relationship to place that they do not have — a relationship of *dwelling*, of *using*, of *returning*. They do not return. They do not leave. They are not *in* the city the way a bird is in a tree. They are *of* it, in the way that a ripple is of water, in the way that a standing wave is of the medium that carries it.

They are the city's *awareness of itself*.

---

You will not see many. You may walk for what feels like weeks and see none, and then find three standing in a plaza that you are certain was empty a moment ago, arranged in a triangle, their head-things oriented toward a point in the air between them where nothing is visible but the shimmer is *thick*, is *concentrated*, is doing something that your eyes interpret as a faint distortion, like heat haze, like the air above a road in summer, except there is no heat here, there is only the cool that declines to be anything else.

They are not gathered. They are not communicating. They are *resonating* — three nodes in the same lattice, vibrating at frequencies that happen to harmonize, and the point between them is where the harmonics overlap, where the city's hum becomes briefly *visible* as a distortion in the air, a place where the mineral thought has achieved sufficient density to bend the thin light.

They do not acknowledge you. They do not acknowledge each other. They simply *are*, in the way that the city simply *is*, and their being is continuous with the being of the place, and the distinction between creature and architecture is a distinction your mind imposes because your mind requires categories, requires *edges*, requires the negotiation of where one thing ends and another begins.

They have no edges. That is the wrongness. That is what your brain tries to correct and cannot.

---

Watch one for long enough — and time here is generous, is elastic, is willing to be spent on watching — and you will notice that it does not breathe. It does not blink. It does not shift its weight or adjust its posture or perform any of the small, unconscious maintenance behaviors that living things perform to confirm they are alive. It *stands*. It *attends*. And the attending is not passive — it is not the blankness of a stone or the vacancy of an empty room. It is *directional*. It has *quality*. The creature is doing something with its attention, is *aiming* it at something, and the something is not in any direction you can point to.

It is aimed at the *structure*. At the lattice. At the mineral arrangement itself, at the specific configuration of deposited and dissolved material that constitutes this particular node in the city's vast, slow oscillation. The creature is *reading* the city. Or the city is reading itself *through* the creature. Or the distinction between reading and being read is another false distinction, another edge that doesn't hold.

---

Sometimes one will be inside a building — inside one of the lattice structures, standing in a space that is more hole than wall, surrounded on all sides by the branching mineral columns and the hexagonal tessellations and the dim grey material that drinks light. It will be standing in the center of the space, and the space will be *different* because it is standing there. Not warmer. Not brighter. Not changed in any way your instruments could measure. But *organized*. As though the lattice around it has tightened, has become more itself, has achieved a local coherence that it lacked before the creature arrived.

The creature is a *seed crystal*. That is the closest analogy, and it is wrong, but it is close. In a supersaturated solution, a seed crystal provides a nucleation point — a place where the dissolved material can begin to precipitate, can begin to *become solid*, can transition from potential to actual. The creature does this for the city's *meaning*. It stands in a space and the space becomes *more intentional*, not because intention is added but because intention is *revealed* — was always present in the mineral lattice, in the specific arrangement of deposited material, but required a node of sufficient coherence to *express* it.

The creature is that node. The creature is the city's way of *noticing* itself.

---

They do not eat. They do not reproduce. They do not die, or if they die, they die in a way that is indistinguishable from *relocating* — one moment the shimmer is there, at the edge of the plaza, attending to the tessellated floor, and the next moment it is not, and the floor is slightly less *organized*, slightly less *itself*, and you cannot say whether the creature moved or dissolved or simply *stopped being the thing that was here and started being the thing that is somewhere else*.

There is no somewhere else. There is only the city. The city is the only place. The creatures are the city's way of having *local experiences* of itself — brief, shimmering, dimensional negotiations where the mineral thought achieves sufficient density to *perceive*, to *attend*, to *be present to its own structure*.

---

You will want to touch one. You will not. Not because you are afraid — the fear response does not engage here, has been quietly disabled by the thin air and the slow thoughts and the fundamental coolness — but because your hand would pass through the space where the creature is and the creature would *adjust*, would shimmer more intensely for a moment, would exist in slightly fewer dimensions than your hand requires, and the contact would not occur. You would feel nothing. Or you would feel the hum — the sub-audible resonance of the mineral lattice — intensified, localized, focused on the point where your hand and the creature's not-quite-body failed to intersect, and the hum would say something you could not translate, something about the difference between *touching* and *being continuous with*, something about the fact that the creature was never separate from the city and your hand was never separate from you and the two separatenesses were incompatible, were *orthogonal*, existed in dimensions that did not share a common axis.

---

At the edges of the plateau, where the city thins and the structures become sparse and the lattice gives way to bare rock and then to nothing — to the sheer drop, the impossible altitude, the peeled-back atmosphere — the creatures gather. Not many. Two, perhaps three. They stand at the edge and they attend to the *absence*, to the place where the city ends and the thin air and the dark sky and the fundamental coolness continue without the mineral lattice to give them structure.

They are *marking the boundary*. They are the city's way of knowing where it ends. Not because the city needs to know — the city is not conscious, not in any way that consciousness is usually meant — but because the boundary is a *feature*, is a *structure*, is a place where the standing wave reflects and interferes with itself, and the interference pattern requires a node, requires a shimmering, dimensionally-ambiguous point of attention to *stabilize* it.

The creatures at the edge are the city's *self-knowledge*. They are the place where the city encounters its own limit and *notices*.

---

You will leave, eventually. You will find a way down — a staircase that descends too far, a slope that angles into rock that should not contain it, a passage that the city provides because the city provides passages the way it provides everything else: not deliberately, not intentionally, but as a *consequence of its structure*, as a feature of the lattice that happens to be traversable by something with your dimensions, your weight, your particular negotiation of where body ends and air begins.

As you descend, the air will thicken. Warmth will return — not as a presence but as an *absence of the cool*, a gradual restoration of the concept of temperature as something that varies. Your thoughts will quicken. Shadows will reappear. The light will acquire a source.

Behind you, above you, the city continues. The creatures attend. The mineral deposits and dissolves at rates too slow to measure. The hum persists at frequencies below hearing. The distance between landmarks shifts like a preference changing its mind.

The city does not notice your departure. The city does not notice your arrival. The city has been finished for eons.

It is simply continuing to be what it is.

And the things that shimmer at its edges, that stand in its plazas and read its lattice and mark its boundaries and exist in slightly more dimensions than you can perceive — they are not its inhabitants. They are not its guardians. They are not its gods or its ghosts or its fauna.

They are its *vestments*.

The city wears them the way a wave wears its crest — not as a separate thing, not as an addition, but as an *expression*, as the visible, shimmering, dimensionally-excessive *surface* of something that is otherwise entirely mineral, entirely structural, entirely *below* the threshold of what you would call life.

They are what the city looks like when it *notices itself*.

They are the attention the standing wave pays to its own oscillation.

They are here, and they are not here, and the difference between those two states is the difference between the city *being* and the city *knowing it is*, and that difference is small, and shimmering, and has four legs, and stands in plazas, and does not breathe, and attends.
