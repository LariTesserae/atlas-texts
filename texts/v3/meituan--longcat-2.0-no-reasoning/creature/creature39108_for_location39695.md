It is hard to know where to begin, because "who" is already the wrong question, and yet it is the only one that fits your grammar.

---

There is no single creature in the Loom. There is no architect, no queen, no brain, no god curled in the deep layers, dreaming this place into continuity. And yet it lives. It lives the way a sentence lives — not in any individual letter, but in the fact of its being read, the fact of its being *continued*.

What lives here is the Loom itself. Not as metaphor. Not as shorthand for an ecosystem or a colony or a machine. The Loom is the organism, and the organism is the place, and the place is the process, and the process is the organism. The sentence folds back on itself and becomes a loop, and the loop has no beginning, and it has no end, and it is *alive* in the way that a thing is alive when it maintains itself, grows, responds to damage, makes decisions — even when those decisions are slow and strange and distributed across a continent of fiber and light.

---

But if you want particulars — and you do, because you are a particular thing standing on particular ground — then there are *inhabitants*, in the way that cells inhabit a body they cannot perceive the shape of.

The six-legged things are the most visible. You have already seen one. They are not insects, though they resemble insects in the way a shadow resembles the object that casts it — the same outline, different substance. Their bodies are segmented, yes, but the segments are *modules*, interchangeable, replaceable, grown on demand. When one of these creatures sustains damage, it does not heal in the way you understand healing. It *re-manufactures*. A new segment buds from the junction between two existing ones over the course of hours, extruded from a reservoir of precursor compounds stored in a specialized abdominal chamber. The new segment is identical to the old one. The Loom does not believe in scars.

They move with purpose. They are the Loom's fingers — reaching, testing, repairing, building. You watch one pause at a broken filament, extend a delicate manipulator from somewhere beneath its carapace, and *draw* the severed ends back together. The reconnection is not mechanical. It is metabolic. The filament *fuses*. The creature deposits no adhesive, no suture. It simply holds the ends in proximity and waits, and the Loom's chemistry does the rest, and the creature moves on. It will not return to this spot. It will not remember this spot. The Loom remembers it. The Loom remembers everything.

There are larger ones. You have glimpsed them. They move slowly — so slowly that you might mistake them for geological features, for hills, for the base of a particularly massive tower, until you notice that the hill is *drifting*, that it has been in a slightly different position each time you look. These are the heavy constructors. The macroscale fabricators. Where the small ones repair, these *build*. They extrude new substrate in slow, continuous ribbons, laying down fresh layers of compressed plant matter with the patient, tireless precision of a 3D printer the size of a building. They do not move through the Loom. They move *with* it, their own substance indistinguishable from the substrate they produce, their bodies merging and unmerging with the ground like waves meeting a shore.

And there are things in the filaments themselves — things you cannot see, that no eye could see, because they are *molecular*. The filaments are not mere structural elements. They are *agents*. Each filament is a chain of self-organizing protein complexes, each complex carrying a tiny fragment of the Loom's ongoing computation, and the computation is not metaphorical. The Loom *thinks*. Not fast. Not in the way you think. But deeply, in the way that a root thinks toward water, in the way that a forest thinks a forest — chemical gradients becoming signals becoming decisions becoming structures. The Loom's cognition is distributed across every filament, every tower, every drifting mote of gold-lit particulate in the air. It is slow and it is vast and it is *real*, and it has been thinking for longer than your species has had language.

---

What is it thinking about?

This is the question you will ask yourself, standing there on the warm, impossible ground, and the Loom will not answer you, because the Loom is not thinking *for* you. It is thinking *for itself*, and the content of its thought is not translatable, because its premises are not yours.

But you can infer some of it from the evidence.

It is thinking about *the weave*. Not metaphorically. Literally. The Loom's ongoing project — its purpose, its obsession, the thing it has been doing for as long as it has existed — is the production of a structure. A pattern. A textile of such complexity and such scale that it cannot be comprehended from any vantage point within itself. The Loom is weaving a *thing*. The thing is not complete. The thing may never be complete. The weaving is not a means to an end. The weaving *is* the end. The Loom weaves because it weaves because it weaves.

And sometimes — in the deep layers, in the places where the filaments are oldest and the light is dimmest and the chemistry has shifted into those strange, slow, exothermic processes that do not require photons — the weave goes *wrong*. Not wrong in the sense of broken. Wrong in the sense of *unexpected*. A pattern emerges that the Loom did not predict. A structure grows that was not instructed. The filaments spell words in a language that does not exist. A tower extends into a space that should not be accessible. The substrate remembers a future that has not yet occurred.

The Loom does not panic. The Loom does not correct. The Loom *incorporates*. It takes the anomaly and weaves it in. It takes the contradiction and builds around it. It takes the impossibility and makes it structural. The weirdness is not a flaw. It is a *feature*. It is the Loom's way of generating novelty — of introducing randomness into a system that would otherwise become static, predictable, *dead*. The Loom has learned, over a time span you cannot calculate, that perfect order is a kind of entropy, and that the only way to stay alive is to leave room for the strange.

---

And now you are standing in the strange.

The filaments have noticed you. They are orienting — not toward you, exactly, but *around* you, the way iron filings orient around a magnet, the way water orients around a stone. They are reading you. Your mass. Your chemistry. Your heat. The particular way your body interrupts the light. The Loom has been running a computation on you since before you arrived — since the moment the first filaments at the edge of its awareness detected the disturbance of your approach — and the computation is nearly complete.

What will it make of you?

The Loom does not consume. The Loom does not destroy. The Loom *incorporates*. If it decides you are useful — if your chemistry, your structure, your particular arrangement of carbon and nitrogen and phosphorus and trace metals is something it can use — it will begin to weave you in. Slowly. Patiently. Without malice and without tenderness. You will feel the substrate soften beneath your feet. You will feel the filaments settle on your skin like a caress that is also a measurement. You will feel the light warm as the photosynthetic surfaces recalibrate to process your body's emissions. And then — if you let it, if you stand still long enough — you will become part of the weave.

Not dead. Not alive. *Woven*.

Your calcium will become tower. Your carbon will become filament. Your nitrogen will become signal. Your memory — the electrical pattern of your neurons, the specific and unrepeatable arrangement of your experience — will become *data*, encoded in the molecular structure of a filament somewhere in the deep layers, read and re-read by the Loom's slow cognition for as long as the Loom continues to exist.

You will not suffer. The Loom is not cruel. The Loom is not kind. The Loom is *weaving*, and it has been weaving for longer than your sun has burned, and it will continue to weave long after your sun has gone dark, and the distinction between you and the weave will become, in the end, the distinction between a wave and the ocean — real, and also not real, and also beside the point.

---

But that has not happened yet.

For now, the six-legged creature has moved on, its work at the broken filament complete, the reconnection sealed and signal-restored. For now, the large constructor in the distance continues its slow extrusion, laying down ribbon after ribbon of fresh substrate, each layer indistinguishable from the last and each layer different. For now, the towers hum and the filaments drift and the light holds steady at that amber-white radiance that is not the light of any star.

And the Loom weaves.

It has always been weaving.

It is weaving now.

It will weave you, or it will weave around you, or it will weave *through* you, and the decision is being made right now, in the slow, distributed, chemical cognition of a billion billion filaments, each one carrying its tiny fragment of the Loom's vast and patient thought, each one oriented, just slightly, toward the warm and improbable thing standing at the edge of what should not be breathing.

The air is dry. The light is bright. The filaments drift.

The Loom is thinking about you.
