I am the one who tends the lattice.

I do not have a name that would survive translation into your language. The closest approximation would be something like *the hand that feeds the water*, or *the one who listens to the walls*, but even those are wrong — they imply a separation between myself and this place that does not exist. I am not *in* the Womb. I am *of* it. My skin carries the same mineral sweetness as the water that weeps from the leaves. My blood runs warm enough that the eyeless things in the basin below the lattice cluster toward me when I stand over them, pressing their translucent bodies against the bamboo slats, feeding on whatever I shed — skin cells, salt, the faint electrical signature of a living nervous system. They glow brighter when I am near. I have learned to read their brightness the way you might read a clock.

I am small. Not in the way you understand smallness — I am proportioned for this space, which means I am compact, dense, low-centered. My hands are broad and flat, with fingers that are slightly too long and slightly too jointed, and the skin on my palms is thickened and pale from constant contact with the mineral water. My nails are not nails. They are something harder — a keratin-silica composite that grows in layers and that I trim against the obsidian blade when they get too long. The blade is mine. I made it three pulse-cycles ago, from a core of volcanic glass I found growing in the eastern passage — the God-Engine produces obsidian the way a tree produces fruit, extruding it from fissures in the deep rock where the heat is greatest. I knapped it with a hammerstone I shaped from a fallen crystal growth, and the edge is still perfect. I use it to cut the vine-cord, to trim the lattice when the bamboo begins to rot, to score the bacterial mat when its channels need redirecting.

I do not wear clothes. There is no need — the heat is constant, and my skin has adapted to the humidity in ways that would kill you. It is slick, almost amphibious, with a faint mucous coating that prevents the mineral water from leaching salts from my body. In the red-gold light, I look wet even when I am dry. My coloring is dark — a deep brown-green that matches the vine-columns, that lets me press against a wall and disappear into the structure entirely. My eyes are large. Not enormous, but large enough to gather every photon the bioluminescent things produce, and the pupils are wide and dark and reflective, with a secondary membrane — a nictitating lid — that I can draw across them when the spore-concentration gets too high. My hair is not hair. It is a fine, dense filament — almost fungal — that grows from my scalp in a thick mat and serves as a sensor array. I can feel air pressure changes through it, and vibrations, and the faint electromagnetic pulses that the crystal growths emit as they form. When a new ridge of mineral is about to push through the wall, my filaments stand up. I have learned to trust this. I have learned to move.

I sleep on the lattice. The creaking does not wake me — I *need* it, the way you might need the sound of rain on a roof. The flex of the bamboo under my weight is a constant reassurance that the structure is alive, that the basin below is full, that the eyeless things are still pulsing. I sleep in short cycles — never more than a quarter of a pulse-beat at a time — because the Womb does not sleep, and it needs tending even in the dark hours when the bioluminescence dims to a faint ember-glow. I sleep curled, knees to chest, one hand trailing through the lattice gaps so that the water touches my fingers and the eyeless things can feed on my fingertips, and their gentle grazing — they have no mouths but their skin is slightly abrasive, slightly *hungry* — keeps me anchored to the waking world by a thread.

I eat the leaves. Not the large weeping leaves on the columns — those are structural, load-bearing, and to damage them would be to damage the Womb itself. But the small leaves, the new growth, the ones that sprout from the vine-roots near the floor where the bacterial mat is thickest — those are tender, mineral-rich, and they taste of copper and green and something deeper, something that is not a flavor so much as a *signal*, a chemical message from the God-Engine itself that tells my body what it needs. I eat with my hands. I do not cook. There is no fire here — the humidity would not allow it, and the spores would ignite, and the whole Womb would become a furnace. I eat the leaves raw, and I chew slowly, and I swallow, and the Womb knows me better for it, and the walls grow a little faster, and the pulse quickens by a fraction, and I feel it in my sternum, and I am satisfied.

I maintain the bacterial mat. This is my primary work, the task that fills the hours between sleep-cycles. The mat is not a simple organism — it is a *system*, a living filtration network that processes the water in the basin, breaking down organic waste and mineral surplus and converting them into forms the eyeless things can absorb. The mat grows on its own, but it grows *wrong* if left untended — it thickens in some places and thins in others, the channels clog or collapse, and the water quality degrades, and the eyeless things dim, and the light fails, and the Womb goes dark, and in the dark the crystal growths accelerate wildly, producing structures that are sharp and random and *angry*, and the geometry warps, and passages open that should not open, and the whole system destabilizes. So I tend it. I use the vine-cord to guide the mat's growth, laying it in channels I carve with the obsidian blade, redirecting flow, thinning dense patches, seeding new growth in bare spots with scrapings from healthy sections. The mat responds to my touch. It is warm and slightly electric, and when I press my palm against it, I can feel the water moving through its channels — a faint, distributed pulse that mirrors the larger heartbeat of the Womb. When the mat is healthy, the pulse is steady. When it is sick, the pulse stutters, and I can feel the wrongness in my hand like a skipped beat in my own heart.

I am not the only one.

There are others deeper in. I have never seen them — the passages beyond this chamber are not yet stable enough to traverse safely, and the geometry shifts, and a corridor that was open a pulse-cycle ago may be sealed by new crystal growth the next. But I hear them. The sounds that come from the dark throat in the far wall — the layered rain-sound, the deeper pulse, the occasional sharp *crack* of crystal formation — those are not only the Womb growing. Those are *hands*. Other hands, tending other chambers, maintaining other mats, feeding other eyeless things. We are connected by the pulse. When the heartbeat quickens, it quickens everywhere, in every chamber simultaneously, and I know that somewhere in the deep passages, another hand has pressed against another mat, and the mat has responded, and the pulse has surged, and the Womb has grown. We do not speak. There is no language — not yet, not in the way you would recognize. But the pulse is a language. The brightness of the eyeless things is a language. The rate of crystal growth is a language. The Womb speaks through its own body, and we who tend it are its mouth and its hands and its awareness of itself.

And there is something else. Something I do not tend. Something that tends *me*.

The face on the far wall.

I have watched it grow. It was not there at the beginning — or rather, it was there the way a face is present in a block of marble before the sculptor finds it, latent, potential, waiting for the right conditions to emerge. The conditions were me. My presence. My heat. My breath. My hands on the mat, my body on the lattice, my constant, patient, repetitive maintenance of the Womb's systems — this is what called it forth. The crystal growths on the far wall began to organize. The random dendrites gave way to structure. The ridge that became a nose. The indentations that became eye-sockets. The slow, mineral accretion of something that is not a face but is *becoming* a face, and the face is not decorative, not symbolic — it is *functional*. It is an organ. A sense organ. The Womb is growing eyes.

I do not know what it will see when the eyes open. I do not know if it will see me, or see *through* me, or see something so far beyond me that I will be invisible. I do not know if the face will be the first of many, or if it will be the only one, or if it is not a face at all but something your mind interprets as a face because your mind cannot process what it actually is. I know only that it is growing, and that I am helping it grow, and that when I stand before it — as I do, often, in the quiet hours between sleep-cycles, when the rain-sound is steady and the pulse is slow and the red-gold light makes the crystal face glow like something lit from within — I feel *seen*. Not by the face. Not yet. By the Womb itself. By the God-Engine. By the vast, slow, hot, wet intelligence that is building itself around me and through me and *as* me, using my hands and my breath and my body-heat as raw materials in its own becoming.

I am not afraid. Fear is a response to threat, and the Womb is not threatening me — it is *incorporating* me. Every breath I take feeds the spores. Every drop of sweat that falls through the lattice feeds the eyeless things. Every time I trim my nails against the obsidian blade, the keratin-silica dust falls into the water and the bacterial mat absorbs it and grows stronger. I am not separate from this place. I am its metabolism. I am the process by which it knows itself.

And sometimes — in the deep hours, when the pulse slows to almost nothing and the bioluminescence dims to a faint, barely perceptible glow and the rain-sound becomes so constant that it is silence — sometimes I press my forehead against the crystal face on the far wall, and I feel the warmth of it, and the faint vibration of new mineral accreting beneath the surface, and I close my eyes, and I listen, and I hear the heartbeat, and I cannot tell if it is mine or the Womb's or the God-Engine's or something else entirely, something that has not yet decided what it is, something that is using all of us — the eyeless things, the bacterial mat, the mites, the spores, the crystal, the vine, the water, the heat, me — as the raw material of its becoming.

I am the one who tends the lattice.

I am the hand that feeds the water.

I am the Womb's awareness of itself, and the Womb is the God-Engine's awareness of itself, and the God-Engine is something else's awareness of itself, and the chain goes down and down and down into the hot dark, and I do not know where it ends.

I do not need to know.

I tend the lattice. I feed the water. I listen to the walls.

And the face on the far wall grows, and grows, and grows.
