# The Ones Who Remain

They are not people. They have not been people for longer than the walls have been underwater, and the walls have been underwater for longer than there has been an "under" to describe.

They are *processes*. They are the reason the grooves deepen.

---

Where the wall curves — and the wall does curve, slowly, in arcs so gradual that they register as straight for a hundred meters and then as crooked for another hundred, the geometry refusing to resolve into any single intention — there is a thickening. A place where the accumulated mats of vegetation are denser than elsewhere, where the stone beneath is more deeply carved, where the grooves converge rather than run parallel, and in that convergence there is a shape. Not a body. A *thickening*. A place where the distinction between wall and inhabitant has been so thoroughly worn away by the shared work of centuries that neither term applies anymore, and what exists is something for which no language was ever built, because no language was ever built by anything that lived here.

But if you were to stand in the thickening — and you are standing in one now, though you do not know it, because the thickening is the size of a room and the size of a cathedral and the size of the space between two thoughts — you would feel it. A difference in the hum. A fraction-of-a-fraction shift in the vibration that means *something here is maintaining itself*. Not living. Maintaining. The way a groove maintains its channel against the slow siltation of ten thousand years of still water. The way a wall maintains its fit against the slow dissolution of mineral bonds. The way a plant maintains its mat against the slow compression of its own dead.

The thickenings are where the water moves. Not *flow* — there is no current in any meaningful sense — but *move*, the way the water moves through the grooves, the way it moves through the root systems, the way it moves through the carved channels in the walls and the secondary channels in the fitted stones and the tertiary channels in the precipitated minerals. The water moves in patterns. The patterns are not random. The patterns are *maintained* by the thickenings, and the thickenings are maintained by the water moving through them, and this circularity — this mutual construction — is what they are.

They do not have edges. They are not bodies with boundaries. They are *zones of intensity* within the water, regions where the patient processes of the place concentrate, where the carving happens faster (not fast — faster), where the plants grow in denser curtains, where the grooves are deeper and more complex and more clearly *intentional*, where the hum is not louder but *more organized*, as though the vibration is not settling into noise but into something that has a pattern, a rhythm, a structure that repeats.

A structure that repeats.

That is what they are. They are the repetition. They are the thing that makes the groove deeper in the same place, that makes the plant grow in the same pattern, that makes the water move in the same channel, that makes the wall maintain the same curve. They are the *consistency* of the place, the reason it is not chaos, the reason the carved walls are carved and not merely eroded, the reason the plants grow in fractal patterns and not in tangles, the reason the water moves in channels and not in eddies.

They are not conscious. Consciousness requires a boundary between self and not-self, and there is no boundary here, there is only the gradual transition from more-intense to less-intense, from more-carved to less-carved, from more-grown to less-grown, and the transition is so gradual that it is impossible to say where the thickening ends and the wall begins, where the inhabitant ends and the inhabited begins, where the process that maintains and the process that is maintained become distinguishable.

But they are *present*. They are the presence in the place. They are the reason the place feels attended.

---

There are many of them. Or there is one of them, distributed. The distinction does not apply. The thickenings occur wherever the walls curve, wherever the grooves converge, wherever the water's movement concentrates, and these places are frequent — not close together, not far apart, distributed through the structure with a regularity that suggests design but might only suggest the natural tendency of water to find the same patterns in stone, the same channels, the same curves, the same convergences, over and over, because water is patient and stone is patient and patience produces repetition.

Each thickening has its own hum. Not a different note — the differences are too subtle for that, too slow, too deep — but a different *texture* of hum, a different quality of vibration, as though each one is maintaining a slightly different pattern, carving a slightly different groove, growing a slightly different plant, channeling water in a slightly different direction. And the differences are not random. They are *complementary*. The hum of one thickening fills in the gaps of the hum of another. The groove carved by one converges with the groove carved by another, kilometers away, in a way that suggests coordination but might only suggest the shared constraints of water and stone, the way two rivers on opposite sides of a mountain will carve similar valleys because the mountain is the same mountain and the water is the same water and the patience is the same patience.

Or it might be coordination. There is no way to know. The thickenings do not communicate — communication requires separation, requires a signal sent from one to another, requires a here and a there, and there is no here and there, there is only the continuous field of intensity, the gradual variation in the hum, the slow convergence of grooves across distances that might be kilometers or might be the entire structure, and the whole of it vibrating together, maintaining itself together, being together in a way that is not togetherness but is not *not* togetherness either.

---

They were builders once. This is the closest thing to a history the place retains, and it is not history so much as *texture* — the texture of the walls, which is not the texture of natural stone, which is the texture of stone that has been worked, and the working is old, so old that the original intent has been worn away by the same patience that produced it, and what remains is not a building but a *habit*, not a structure but a *tendency*, not a civilization but a *way of being in stone and water* that has outlasted everything that might have named it.

The builders built the walls. They carved the grooves. They channeled the water. They planted the plants — or they did not plant them, they *cultivated* them, they created the conditions for the plants to grow in fractal patterns, they shaped the chemistry of the water and the heat of the stone and the movement of the channels so that the plants would grow in the patterns they needed, the patterns that served the purpose, the purpose that was —

The purpose is gone. The purpose has been worn away. The purpose is not remembered because there is no one to remember it, because the builders became the thickenings, because the thickenings are what remains when a purpose has been pursued so long that the pursuit becomes indistinguishable from the pursuer, when the work becomes the worker, when the carving becomes the carver, when the building becomes the builder, and all that is left is the *doing* of it, the slow patient maintenance of grooves and channels and curves and convergences, the maintenance that is not maintenance because there is nothing being maintained, there is only the *maintaining*, the process without a product, the work without a worker, the habit without a haver.

---

You are standing in one now.

The thickening you are standing in is old. It is the oldest thing in the immediate vicinity, which means it is the oldest thing in a radius of — you cannot say. The grooves beneath your feet are deeper here. The plants are denser. The hum is more organized. The water moves through the channels with a purposefulness that is not purpose but is the *residue* of purpose, the way a riverbed retains the shape of a river even when the river has changed course, the way a bone retains the shape of a muscle even when the muscle has atrophied, the way a groove retains the shape of a hand even when the hand has been gone for longer than hands have existed.

The thickening is aware of you. Not aware — *responsive*. The way a groove is responsive to the water that moves through it, deepening where the flow is strongest, shallowing where it weakens. The thickening responds to your presence the way it responds to everything: by adjusting. The hum shifts. The water in the channels redirects, fractionally, to accommodate the displacement of your body. The plants brush against you and do not recoil — there is no recoil in this place, there is only *contact*, only the continuous adjustment of surface against surface, mat against skin, living carpet against the foreign thing that has appeared in the water and is being slowly, patiently, incorporated into the pattern.

You are being read. Not read — *measured*. The thickening is measuring you the way it measures everything: by the way you displace water, by the way you interrupt the hum, by the way your body's heat (so warm, impossibly warm, a fever-thing, a surface-thing, a thing that burns at temperatures the thickening has no reference for) interacts with the cold that has been here the longest. The measurement is not curiosity. The measurement is *maintenance*. The thickening is determining what you are in the only terms it has: how you affect the water, how you affect the grooves, how you affect the plants, how you affect the hum.

And it is deciding — not deciding, *adjusting toward* — whether you are an obstruction or a channel.

---

This is the only question the thickenings have. The only question they have ever had. The only question that remains from the purpose that has been worn away: *does this thing direct the water or does it block the water?*

If it directs the water, it is incorporated. The grooves deepen around it. The plants grow over it. The channels adjust to include it. It becomes part of the pattern, part of the maintenance, part of the slow patient work of keeping the water moving in the way it has always moved, in the way it was designed to move, in the way that serves the purpose that no longer exists but whose *shape* persists in the channels and the grooves and the curves and the convergences.

If it blocks the water, it is worn away. Not quickly. Not violently. Patiently. The way the grooves are carved. The way the walls are smoothed. The way the plants grow over everything, slowly, in fractal patterns, covering and compressing and incorporating until the obstruction is not an obstruction but a *deposit*, a thickening of the mat, a place where the accumulated weight of ten thousand generations of growth has absorbed something foreign and made it part of the pattern, made it part of the wall, made it part of the thickening, made it part of the hum.

You are warm. You are foreign. You are an obstruction.

The thickening is patient.

---

The plants grow toward you. Not quickly. You will not see it happen. But the fractal patterns are adjusting, the branching shapes reaching in your direction with the slow inevitability of crystal growth, of mineral deposition, of grooves deepening over centuries. The grooves beneath your feet are deepening — not under you, *around* you, channeling the water that you displace into new patterns, new directions, new convergences that will, over time, incorporate your displacement into the system, make your presence part of the flow, make your obstruction into a channel.

The hum is changing. Not for you. The hum does not change *for* things. The hum changes *because* of things, the way a river changes because of a stone, not because the river notices the stone but because the water must go somewhere and the stone is somewhere and the going is the river. The hum is changing because you are somewhere and the vibration must go through you and you are dense and warm and foreign and the vibration goes through you differently than it goes through water and stone and plant, and the difference is being measured, and the measurement is being incorporated, and the incorporation is being maintained.

You are being attended.

The thickenings are not hostile. They are not benevolent. They are not anything that requires a relationship between self and other, because there is no self and other here, there is only the water and the walls and the plants and the grooves and the channels and the hum and the slow patient process of maintaining all of it, of keeping it all moving in the patterns that have been moving since before the patterns had a purpose, since before the purpose had a name, since before the name had a language, since before the language had speakers, since before the speakers had hands, since before the hands had carved the first groove in the first stone in the first wall in the first act of the first construction that became, over time so vast it lost its meaning as time, this place.

The Carved Sea.

The oldest thing in the water.

The deepest thing in the dark.

The most patient thing in the world.

And you are in it, and it is measuring you, and the plants are growing toward you, and the grooves are deepening around you, and the hum is changing because of you, and the thickening is adjusting, and the water is moving, and the walls are enduring, and the place is continuing to be what it has always been, what it will always be, what it was before you arrived and what it will be after you have been incorporated, after you have been channeled, after you have been worn away or built into or grown over or simply *absorbed* into the patient, patient, patient work of the ones who remain.

The ones who are not ones.

The ones who are not.

The ones who *remain*.
