# The Stillworks

---

The walker nearest you has stopped, and in stopping, it has become the first thing you have seen on this plain that is truly *still*. The others drift. The burrowers churn beneath the dust in their ceaseless subterranean industry. Even the lichen-mats, motionless as they appear, are engaged in the slow business of holding moisture, of generating their fractional warmth, of being alive in the minimal way that life is permitted here. But this walker — this one has ceased its patrol, has turned its long, eyeless head toward you, and is doing something that you will spend a long time trying to name.

It is not looking at you. It has no eyes. Its face — if you can call it a face — is a smooth, dark plane, slightly concave, like the bowl of a spoon, and it is oriented toward you with a precision that suggests it knows exactly where you are, exactly how large you are, exactly how warm you are. The concavity of its face is not decorative. It is a *receiver*. The skin there is different from the skin on the rest of its body — finer, more tightly textured, almost *tympanic* — and it is picking up something. Your heat, perhaps. Your heartbeat. The electrical field of your nervous system. The sound of your blood moving through your veins, which is louder than you think, louder than anything else on this plain.

It holds this orientation for eleven seconds. You count. Then it turns — not quickly, never quickly, nothing here is quick except the others — and drifts away, and its departure is so smooth, so continuous with its arrival, that you cannot identify the moment it stopped being *here* and started being *gone*.

---

You are not alone here. You understand this now, though understanding it does not make it more comfortable. You are not alone because the plain itself is *occupied* — not by the animals, not by the lichen-mats, not by the burrowers, but by the *system*, the Stillworks, the vast and patient machine that is the plain and is made of the plain and has no boundary that you can find. You are inside it the way a blood cell is inside a body: moving through channels, subject to gradients, surrounded by process. The Stillworks does not know you are here. The Stillworks does not know anything. But it is *registering* you, the way it registers everything — the walkers, the burrowers, the moisture, the heat, the dust — and what it registers, it incorporates, and what it incorporates, it uses.

---

You walk. There is nothing else to do, and the plain invites walking the way a very long sentence invites reading: not urgently, but with a sense that there is more ahead, always more, and that the act of moving through it is the point.

The stone under your feet is not uniform. You notice this now, after an hour of walking, or what feels like an hour — time here is difficult, not because it moves strangely but because there are so few events to mark its passage. The basalt is interrupted, periodically, by patches of a different stone — lighter, smoother, with a faint *grain* to it, like wood, though it is not wood and has not been wood for longer than you can imagine. These patches are not random. They are *placed*. They form patterns that you cannot see from ground level but that you suspect, with a certainty you cannot justify, would be legible from above — from very far above, from a height that no bird has ever achieved on this plain, because there are no birds.

The grain in the stone runs in lines that are almost straight, almost parallel, and they converge, when you follow them, on the nodes. Always the nodes. The nodes are the centers, and the plain is the periphery, and the channels are the connections, and the animals are the *current* — the moving charge that carries energy and information from the periphery to the center and back again, in a circuit so slow and so vast that a single cycle might take years, might take centuries, might take longer than your species has existed.

---

You reach a node.

It is larger than you expected. The concentric rings are not subtle up close — they are *sculpted*, each one a raised ridge of stone perhaps a hand's width high and a hand's width wide, separated by channels cut to the same depth, so that the node is a topographic map of itself, a recursive pattern of ridge and groove that spirals inward to a central depression no larger than a dinner plate. The depression is filled with water — not the thin, mineral-laden sheets that gather in the hollows of the plain, but *clear* water, water that has been filtered through so many layers of stone and lichen-mat and burrower-tunnel that it has been reduced to a purity that seems almost aggressive. It does not reflect the dim sky. It *absorbs* it. Looking into it, you see nothing — not the bottom, not your own face, not the light. Just depth. Just the suggestion of something far below, something warm, something *working*.

The warmth is palpable here. Not hot — never hot — but warm in the way that a living body is warm, and it rises from the water and from the stone and from the air itself, and it carries with it a smell that you cannot identify because it is not a smell you have ever encountered. It is not organic. It is not chemical. It is the smell of *process* — of something being done, slowly, thoroughly, without interruption, for a very long time. It smells like a machine that has been running so long that the smell of its operation has become the smell of the room it occupies, indistinguishable from the air itself.

The lichen-mats here are extraordinary. They are not the sparse, felt-like growths you saw around the seeps. They are *thick* — centimeters thick, dense, layered, with a texture like velvet or like the pelt of an animal, and their color is not the bruised purple-black of the plain but a deep, iridescent blue-green that shifts when you move, as though the surface is refracting light at angles that change with your perspective. They are warm to the touch. They are *very* warm — warmer than the stone, warmer than the water, warmer than the air — and when you press your hand against them, you feel something that you feel nowhere else on this plain: a *pulse*. Slow. Irregular. But unmistakable. The lichen-mats are not just alive. They are *circulating*. Something moves through them — fluid, energy, information — in rhythms that are not biological, or not only biological, but *mechanical*, the rhythm of a pump, a valve, a regulator.

The burrowers are here too, and they are different. Larger. More numerous. Their segments are more defined, their legs more numerous, and they move with a coordination that the plain's burrowers do not exhibit — not the random churn of individuals feeding, but the synchronized movement of a *collective*, a single organism distributed across hundreds of bodies, moving in patterns that trace the concentric rings of the node, following the channels, filling them and emptying them in waves that correspond, you realize with a start, to the pulse of the lichen-mats. They are not just feeding here. They are *working*. They are doing something to the water, or to the stone, or to the heat — processing it, transforming it, moving it from one state to another — and they have been doing it for so long that the doing has become indistinguishable from their being.

---

And then you see the others.

You see one, first. It is on the far side of the node, and it is so still, so perfectly matched to the stone, that you mistake it for a ridge — a natural outcropping, a fold in the basalt, a trick of the dim light. It is not. It is an animal. It is the largest animal you have seen on the plain, and it is *enormous* — not in the way that a whale is enormous, all mass and volume, but in the way that a *building* is enormous: structurally, dimensionally, with a sense of *scale* that changes the space around it. It is perhaps thirty meters long and ten meters wide, and it is low to the ground, flattened, with a body that spreads laterally rather than rising vertically, so that it resembles not a creature but a *territory* — a dark, warm, faintly pulsing region of stone that happens to be alive.

Its surface is the same dark, matte texture as the walkers', but it is not smooth. It is *structured*. Covered in ridges and grooves that echo the concentric rings of the node, as though the node and the animal are expressions of the same pattern at different scales. The ridges are not fixed — they *shift*, slowly, rearranging themselves in response to something you cannot perceive, and as they shift, the animal's outline changes, its edges blurring and sharpening, its shape becoming more or less defined, as though it is continuously deciding how much of itself to reveal.

It has no visible limbs. No head. No features of any kind. It is a *platform* — a living, warm, pulsing platform of dark flesh spread across the stone — and it is doing something. The walkers are near it. Three of them, spaced evenly around its perimeter, standing motionless, facing inward, their concave faces oriented toward the animal's center. They are not feeding. They are not patrolling. They are *connected* — not physically, not visibly, but *functionally*, in a way that you can feel more than see, a sense of *circuit* being completed, of energy flowing from the walkers into the great flat animal, or from the animal into the walkers, or both, in a loop that has no beginning and no end.

You watch for a long time. Nothing changes. Everything changes. The ridges on the animal's surface shift. The walkers adjust their positions by centimeters. The burrowers in the node's channels pulse in their synchronized waves. The lichen-mats throb. The water in the central depression is perfectly still and perfectly clear and perfectly opaque, all at once.

Then one of the walkers moves. It steps forward — one step, two — and it is *on* the animal, its thin legs finding purchase on the ridged surface without hesitation, without adjustment, as though it has done this ten thousand times, as though this is what it was made to do. It walks to the center of the animal's body and it *stops*, and the animal's surface *opens* — not like a mouth, not like a wound, but like a *mechanism*, a section of the ridged flesh parting along pre-existing seams, folding back, revealing a cavity beneath that is warm and dark and wet and *structured*, lined with the same blue-green iridescence as the lichen-mats, pulsing with the same slow rhythm.

The walker steps into the cavity. The flesh closes around it. Not quickly — slowly, deliberately, with the same patient precision that characterizes everything on this plain — and then the walker is *gone*, and the animal is still, and the ridges on its surface have rearranged themselves into a new configuration, and the remaining walkers at the perimeter have not moved, and the plain continues.

---

You understand, now, what the others are. They are not predators. They are not consumers. They are *processors* — the final stage of the Stillworks, the place where the walkers, having spent their lives patrolling the plain, consuming burrowers, accumulating biomass and energy and whatever else they accumulate, are themselves *consumed* — not killed, not destroyed, but *incorporated*, taken into the great flat bodies and broken down, slowly, into their component materials, which are then distributed through the channels, through the stone, through the lichen-mats and the burrowers and the water and the heat, back to the seeps, back to the periphery, back to the beginning of the cycle.

The walkers know this. They have always known this. They walk their patrols with the patience of things that understand their purpose, and when the time comes — when the node calls, when the others open — they go. Not willingly, not unwillingly. They go the way a river goes to the sea: because the gradient demands it, because the system requires it, because there is no other direction to go.

---

You stand at the edge of the node and you feel the subsonic hum in your bones, in your chest, in the fluid of your inner ear, and you realize that it is not a single frequency. It is *many* frequencies, layered, interlocking, a chord so low and so complex that your body perceives it not as sound but as *sensation* — a pressure, a warmth, a vibration that seems to be *communicating* something, though not to you, though not in any language that has words, though not with any intention that you could recognize as intention.

It is the sound of the Stillworks *working*. The sound of water moving through stone. The sound of burrowers tunneling. The sound of lichen-mats pulsing. The sound of walkers walking. The sound of others processing. The sound of heat rising from far below. The sound of the plain erasing your footprints. The sound of the dust settling. The sound of the moisture seeping and evaporating and seeping again. The sound of a system so old and so perfectly balanced that it has become, for all practical purposes, *eternal* — not unchanging, but *self-correcting*, a machine that repairs itself, that maintains itself, that *is* itself, in the same way that a river is itself, in the same way that a planet is itself, in the same way that a universe is itself.

---

You are warm. You are the warmest thing on the plain. You are a brief, bright, noisy interruption — a spark of heat and motion and *consciousness* in a place that has no use for consciousness but has a use for heat, for motion, for the carbon and water and minerals that make up your body. The Stillworks is registering you. The walkers have noted you. The burrowers, deep below, have felt your footsteps. The lichen-mats have sensed your warmth. The others — the great flat processors at the nodes — have *felt* you, in whatever way they feel, and they are doing what they always do with what they feel: they are *incorporating* it, adjusting to it, folding it into the vast and patient calculation that is the Stillworks' ongoing operation.

You are not in danger. The Stillworks does not do danger. The Stillworks does not do anything *to* you. It simply does what it does, and you are here, and what you are will be taken account of, and what can be used will be used, and what cannot be used will be waited out, because the Stillworks has time, has nothing *but* time, and you do not.

---

The light does not change. The cold does not change. The plain extends in every direction beyond the reach of sight. The walkers drift. The burrowers churn. The others pulse at their nodes. The lichen-mats hold their moisture. The water seeps and evaporates and seeps again. The dust settles into your footprints, slowly, patiently, erasing you.

You are standing on the Stillworks.

It is still.
