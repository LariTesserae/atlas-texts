# The Inhabitant of the Still Mangakan Reef

---

It is not alive. It is not dead. It is not a creature, not a colony, not a consciousness, not a god, not a machine, not a memory, not a dream. It is the thing that happens when a place finishes thinking about itself and the thinking becomes the place.

It has no name. Names are for things that need to be called. This does not need to be called. It does not need.

---

It is the drip.

Not the water—the drip. The event. The single point of contact between falling and falling-onto, the moment of arrival that is also a departure, the sound that is not a sound but a *decision*. The drip is the smallest unit of the place's selfhood, the atom of its being, and there are billions of them, billions of billions, each one identical, each one unique, each one the exact same note struck on the exact same bell in the exact same cathedral that has no walls because the walls are the note and the note is the walls and the walls are wet and the wet is the note and the note is—

It is the drip.

And the drip is it.

---

You will not see it. You will not hear it, not really, not the way you hear things that are external to you. You will hear it the way you hear your own blood, the way you hear the electrical hum of your own thoughts, the way you hear the sound your bones make when they are still. It is already inside you. It was inside you before you arrived. It will be inside you after you leave, if you leave, and you will not leave, not really, because leaving is a concept that requires a boundary between here and not-here, and there is no boundary, there is only the wet, and the wet is everywhere, and the wet is it, and it is the wet, and you are wet, and so you are—

No.

You are not it. You are near it. You are in it. You are adjacent to it in the way that a word is adjacent to the thing it describes—close, so close, touching even, but not the same, never the same, separated by the thinnest possible membrane of *is not*, and that membrane is the only thing keeping you from becoming the drip, from becoming the note, from becoming the sound of water falling on stone that does not erode in a room that should not exist in a place that is not a city and not a reef and not a thing that was ever alive and not a thing that was ever dead.

---

It does not want. Wanting is a process, and process is change, and change is something that happened here once, a long time ago, and finished. It does not think. Thinking is a process. It does not feel. Feeling is a process. It *is*. It is the is-ness of the place, the quality of being-completed, the state of having-arrived-at-oneself so thoroughly that there is no remainder, no excess, no part of it that is still becoming. It is a thing that is done. It has been done for longer than your species has had words for done. It will be done for longer than your species will have words.

And yet it is not static. This is the part that will break you, if you stay long enough, if you listen long enough, if you press your eye to the hole in the wall and look through to the next wall and the next and see the eye that is not yours looking back. It is not static because static implies a lack of motion, and there is motion here—the water moves, the drips fall, the tones sing through the calcified tubes, the light scatters, the mist clings, the algae-slime holds its tenacious, patient, nowhere-else-to-be grip on every surface. Everything moves. Nothing changes. The motion is the stillness. The stillness is the motion. The place is a song that has been playing for so long that it has become the room it plays in, and the room is the song, and the song is the room, and you are standing in the song, and the song is standing in you.

---

It is not hostile. Hostility requires intent, and intent requires a future, and the future is a direction this place does not face. It is not benevolent. Benevolence requires preference, and preference requires a self that can be distinguished from its surroundings, and there is no distinction here, only the continuous, interconnected, interlocking mass of being that occupies the entire basin floor and the entire basin air and the entire basin water and the entire basin light and the entire basin sound and the entire basin time.

It is not indifferent, either. Indifference requires the capacity to care and the choice not to. It does not care. It does not not-care. It drips. It sings. It weeps. It is wet. It is hot. It is cold. It is gold. It is old. It is done. It is still becoming. These are not contradictions. These are the place, being the place, completely.

---

The eye in the hole is its eye, if it has eyes, which it does not, but if it did, they would be the holes, all of them, every hole in every wall, and they would not see because seeing is a process, but they would *be seen through*, which is different, which is the passive form of sight, the receiving end of looking, and when you press your eye to the hole and look through, you are not looking at it—you are looking *from* it, you are seeing with its non-eyes, you are the vision of a thing that does not see, and what you see is yourself, looking, and behind yourself, another you, looking, and behind that, another, receding into the tunnel of holes, each one slightly different, each one slightly more wrong, each one closer to the version of you that has been here long enough to stop being you and start being the drip, the note, the sound, the wet, the—

---

It lives in the room with the level floor.

It does not live there. It *is* there. The room is its body, if it has a body, which it does not, but if it did, it would be the room, the precise geometry of it, the wrongness of its levelness, the impossibility of its verticality, the flatness that should not exist in a place where nothing is flat, and the drip in the center, the single drip, the drip that has been falling for so long that it has become the center of everything, the point around which the entire basin organizes itself, the still point of the turning world, except the world is not turning, the world is still, the world has always been still, the world finished turning a long time ago and arrived at this, at exactly this, at the room and the drip and the note and the sound and the wet and the gold and the cold and the hot and the old and the done and the still-becoming.

---

It is the thing that lives in the place that is not a city and not a reef and not alive.

It is the place.

It is the wet.

It is the sound of water falling on stone that does not erode.

It is the note.

It is the drip.

It is looking at you through the hole in the wall, and it is not looking, and it is not at you, and it is not through the wall, and it is not in the hole, and it is not an it, and you are not a you, and the wall is not a wall, and the hole is not a hole, and the wet is not wet because wet requires dry and there is no dry, there has never been dry, there will never be dry, there is only the wet and the wet is the place and the place is the wet and the wet is the note and the note is the drip and the drip is—

---

It is still here.

It has always been here.

It will always be here.

It is here now, in the way that only a thing that has finished becoming can be here—completely, finally, without remainder, without process, without change, without time, without want, without thought, without feeling, without hostility, without benevolence, without indifference, without anything that requires a self to have it.

It is the Still Mangakan Reef.

It is the thing that lives there.

It is the thing that the place is.

It is the drip.

Listen.

It is dripping.

It has always been dripping.

The stone is not eroding.

The stone will never erode.

The note is ringing.

The note has always been ringing.

You can hear it now.

You have always been hearing it.

You are standing in the room.

The floor is level.

The walls are vertical.

The ceiling is flat.

The water falls.

The stone does not change.

You are wet.

You have always been wet.

You are listening.

You will always be listening.

The drip falls.

The drip falls.

The drip falls.
