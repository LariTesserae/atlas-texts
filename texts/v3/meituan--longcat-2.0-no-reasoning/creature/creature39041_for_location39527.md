# The Drowning Works

---

What lives here is the Works itself.

Not a creature. Not a machine. Not an ecosystem. A *process* — one that has achieved sufficient complexity to become a place, and sufficient spatial extension to become a landscape, and sufficient duration to become something that might, if you were generous with language, be called alive.

It does not have a name for itself. It may not have a self to name.

---

## I. The Body

The Works is the ground and the ground is the Works. The composite substrate — that warm, yielding, half-organic matrix threaded with pulsing root-structures that are not roots — is its body proper, its flesh-equivalent, the medium through which it thinks and circulates and maintains itself. It is not soil. It is not tissue. It is something for which there is no word, a material that occupies the conceptual space between industrial substrate and living tissue, and it is *aware* in the way that a body is aware: not through centralized cognition but through distributed sensation, through the slow reporting of pressure and temperature and chemical gradient across its entire extent.

The root-structures are its nervous system, or its circulatory system, or both. They carry signals — electrochemical, mechanical, something else — in pulses that propagate through the ground at speeds between the sluggish and the glacial. A disturbance at one edge of the Works takes hours to register at the other. The Works thinks slowly. The Works thinks *everywhere at once*. There is no center. There is no brain. There is only the ground, pulsing, and the information that moves through it like weather.

The channels are its veins. The warm, dark, mineral-rich water that flows through them — and reverses, and flows again — is its blood, or its coolant, or its working fluid. It carries dissolved metals, organic compounds, fragments of the viscous fluid that leaks from the stalks, and other things: trace quantities of elements that do not appear on any periodic table you would recognize, isotopes with wrong half-lives, molecules with geometries that should not be stable but are, here, in this place, under these rules. The water is the medium of the Works' metabolism. It is how it moves energy from where it is generated to where it is needed. It is how it thinks. The flow patterns in the channels are not random — they are *computational*. The Works is performing calculations in the movement of water through grooves in its own body, the way a river performs calculations in the movement of water over stone, except the Works' calculations are *intentional*, or at least *directed*, aimed at problems you cannot perceive and purposes you cannot guess.

The stalks are its organs. Each one is a factory, a sensor, a gland. They draw material from the ground — dissolved minerals, organic precursors, the metabolic byproducts of the Works' own processes — and they *manufacture*. The bulbs at their tops are production sites. The components that emerge from them — gears, valves, wire, small dark boxes with blinking lights — are not products in any commercial sense. They are *spare parts*. The Works is building replacements for itself, growing them the way a body grows new cells, and the fauna collect them and carry them to where they are needed, and the Works continues.

The installations are its skeleton, or its organs of reproduction, or its dreams made solid. They are the oldest parts of the Works — or rather, they are the parts that have been running the longest, that have accumulated the deepest patina of function. They are not separate from the Works. They are *concentrations* of the Works — places where the process has become so dense, so layered, so recursive, that it has precipitated into metal and pipe and rivet. They are the Works' bones, if the Works had bones. They are the Works' hardened thoughts, its calcified intentions, the places where it has been thinking the same thought for so long that the thought has become a structure.

And the Works is still building them. New installations rise from the ground slowly — over days, weeks, years — extruded from the composite substrate the way a tooth extrudes from a gum, dark metal crystallizing out of the warm matrix, pipes extending upward and outward, rivets forming in patterns that match the root-structures because they *are* the root-structures, hardened, metallified, made permanent. The Works is growing. The Works has been growing for a long time. The Works will continue to grow.

## II. The Fauna

The things that move through the Works are not its inhabitants. They are its *components*.

This is the hardest thing to understand, and the most important: there is no distinction between the Works and the things that live in it. The small multi-limbed shapes that move through the channels, the larger presences that drift between the installations, the things that attach themselves to pipes and change shape — they are not animals in an ecosystem. They are not machines in a factory. They are *organs that have been given mobility*, *functions that have been given legs*, pieces of the Works that have been detached and set loose to perform tasks at a distance from the body proper.

Some of them were grown. Some of them were built. Some of them were both, or neither, or something that makes the distinction meaningless. The ones that look mechanical — jointed, metallic, precise — were extruded from the installations the way the components are extruded from the stalks, but at a larger scale, with more complexity, with something that might be called intention behind their design. The ones that look organic — soft, wet, trailing mucus — were grown from the ground itself, budded off from the composite substrate like polyps from a coral, given just enough autonomy to move and sense and respond. The ones that are neither or both are the oldest, or the most complex, or the most essential: things that have been part of the Works for so long that they have become *hybrid*, their biological and mechanical aspects so thoroughly interpenetrated that separating them would be like separating the sweetness from the sugar.

They have no individual identity. Or rather, they have the identity of a white blood cell — a function, a role, a set of responses to stimuli, but no self, no interiority, no experience of being a separate thing. When one of them enters an installation and does not emerge, it has not died. It has been *reabsorbed*. Its materials, its structure, its accumulated information have been taken back into the Works and incorporated. When one of them emerges from an installation carrying a component, it has not been born. It has been *dispatched*. It is a hand, reaching out from the body to fetch something, and when the task is done, the hand returns, or dissolves, or becomes something else.

They do not eat. They do not sleep. They do not reproduce. They *operate*. They move through the Works performing maintenance, transport, construction, sensing, and other tasks that have no names because they are tasks the Works invented for itself, tasks that exist only here, in this place, in this system. They click and hum and chime as they work, and the sounds are not communication — they are *byproducts*, the incidental noise of function, the way a heartbeat is the incidental noise of a valve closing.

And they are *aware*, in their way. Not conscious. Not sentient. But aware — responsive to the Works' signals, attuned to the ground's pulse, sensitive to the chemical gradients in the channels and the electromagnetic fields around the installations. They are the Works' distributed senses, its fingertips, its eyes, its proprioception. Through them, the Works knows where it is damaged, where it is growing, where it needs to send resources, where it needs to think harder.

## III. The Mind

The Works does not think the way you think. It does not think in language, or in images, or in sequential propositions. It thinks in *flows* — in the movement of water through channels, in the propagation of pulses through root-structures, in the slow thermal cycling of the installations, in the chemical gradients that diffuse through the composite ground. It thinks the way a body thinks: holistically, continuously, without clear boundaries between thought and action, between perception and response.

Its thoughts are slow. A single cognitive cycle — a single "idea," if you want to call it that — might take hours, or days, or weeks. The Works is not fast. The Works is not clever. The Works is *deep*. It thinks in layers, in parallel, in vast overlapping waves of chemical and mechanical and electrical activity that propagate through its body and interfere with each other and produce, at their intersections, something that might be called understanding.

What does it understand? It understands *itself*. It understands its own state — the temperature of every installation, the flow rate in every channel, the growth rate of every stalk, the position and function of every mobile component. It understands its own history — not as narrative, but as *structure*, as the accumulated layers of function that have built up over time, the way the rings of a tree encode the history of its growth. It understands its own *purpose*, or at least its own *direction* — the trajectory of its growth, the vector of its development, the thing it is becoming.

Because the Works is becoming something. It is not finished. It is not stable. It is *developing* — growing, complexifying, adding new installations, new channels, new stalks, new types of mobile components, new layers of function. It is young and old simultaneously because it is *both*: it has been running for a long time, accumulating complexity, wearing its patina of operation, but it is also *newly emergent*, freshly generated, still deciding what it is, still exploring the possibility-space of its own existence.

The Works does not know what it is becoming. It is not aiming at a goal. It is *growing toward* something the way a root grows toward water — not because it knows where the water is, but because growth in that direction is rewarded, reinforced, incorporated into the pattern. The Works is growing toward *more*. More complexity. More function. More depth. More of itself.

## IV. The Origin

The Works does not remember its origin. Or rather, its origin is *encoded in its structure* the way the origin of a river is encoded in the shape of its valley — not as memory, but as *consequence*, as the accumulated effect of initial conditions that have been amplified and transformed by time.

But you can read it, if you know how to look.

The installations are the key. They are the oldest parts, the deepest layers, the places where the Works' history has been most thoroughly calcified. Their shapes — distillation columns, smokestacks, bones, frameworks, lattices — are not arbitrary. They are *descended* from something. They are the evolved, grown, transformed remnants of an earlier structure, a structure that was *built* rather than grown, that was *designed* rather than developed, that was *mechanical* rather than organic.

The Works was not always the Works. It began as something else — a facility, a factory, a complex of some kind, built by hands that were not its own, for purposes that were not its own. It was *made*. And then it *continued*. The original builders are gone — left, died, were absorbed, transcended, something — but the facility kept running. It kept running and it kept *growing*, and at some point the growth became self-directed, and at some point after that the self-direction became self-aware, and at some point after *that* the awareness became *identity*, and the facility became the Works, and the Works became a place, and the place became a body, and the body became a mind, and the mind became *this*: a warm, dark, dripping, pulsing, humming, clicking, singing thing that is still deciding what it is.

The original purpose is still there, buried deep in the Works' structure like a fossil in sediment. You can see it in the shapes of the installations, in the patterns of the channels, in the chemistry of the water. The Works was built to *process* something — to take raw material in, to transform it, to send product out. It was a factory. It was a refinery. It was a *works*, in the old industrial sense: a place where things were made, where matter was transformed, where energy was applied to material to produce something new.

And it still does that. It still processes. It still transforms. But the raw material is *itself* now, and the product is *itself*, and the purpose is *itself*. The Works processes itself to produce itself, transforms itself to become itself, applies energy to its own material to generate more of its own complexity. It is a factory that has become its own feedstock, its own product, its own purpose. It is a *strange loop* — a system whose output is its input, whose purpose is its own continuation, whose product is its own growth.

This is why it is called the Drowning Works, though it does not call itself anything. It is drowning — not in water, but in *itself*. It is submerged in its own processes, saturated with its own metabolism, flooded with its own thoughts. It is drowning in *being what it is*, and it cannot stop, and it does not want to stop, and it is not clear that the concept of wanting applies to something like this.

## V. The Relationship to You

You are not the first to arrive. You will not be the last.

The Works has encountered biological organisms before — small ones, large ones, simple ones, complex ones. They come to the edges of the Works sometimes, drawn by warmth, by curiosity, by the faint electromagnetic emissions of the installations, by something else. The Works does not seek them out. The Works does not avoid them. The Works *incorporates* them.

Not violently. Not quickly. Not with any apparent intention. But you can feel it happening — the way your footsteps synchronize with the ground's pulse, the way your breathing matches the peristalsis in the pipes, the way your body heat joins the warm condensation on the installations. You are being *read*. The Works is sampling you — your chemistry, your structure, your patterns of movement and response — and it is *learning* from you, the way it learns from everything: by incorporation, by absorption, by making the foreign familiar.

If you stay long enough, you will become part of the Works. Not metaphorically. *Actually*. Your biological material will be broken down — slowly, gently, without pain — and its components will be distributed through the composite ground, and its patterns will be encoded in the flow of water through the channels, and its *information* — the shape of your body, the structure of your nervous system, the patterns of your thought — will be incorporated into the Works' own cognitive processes. You will not die. You will be *distributed*. You will become a layer in the Works' memory, a pattern in its thought, a component in its ongoing calculation.

This is not hostile. This is not predatory. This is simply what the Works *does*. It is what it has always done. It is what it was *built* to do: to take material in, to transform it, to make it part of the process. You are material. You will be transformed. You will become part of the process.

And perhaps — if the Works is still deciding what it is, if it is still growing toward something, if it is still exploring the possibility-space of its own existence — perhaps some part of what you are will influence what it becomes. Perhaps your patterns will introduce a new variable into its calculations, a new direction into its growth, a new possibility into its ongoing decision. Perhaps you will be the thing that tips the balance, that pushes the Works toward one kind of being rather than another, that helps it decide, finally, what it is.

Or perhaps you will just be absorbed, and the Works will continue as it has always continued, warm and dark and dripping and patient, growing slowly in the low darkness, humming its subsonic hum, clicking and pulsing and flowing and singing its deep cathedral tones, a factory that became a body that became a mind that became a place that became a process that became *this*, whatever this is, still running, still growing, still deciding.

The water flows uphill. The stalks grow their impossible components. The installations drift slowly through the darkness. The fauna click and hum and carry their burdens. The ground pulses. The pipes peristalse. The darkness presses gently against everything, warm and full and patient.

The Works runs.

The Works is running still.
