If I were a presence in this pocket, I would be the drip.

Not any specific drop, but the process of dripping itself—the long, patient logic of water finding its way through the system. I would be the interval between one *plink* and the next, the gathering bead at the edge of a leaf that hasn't yet let go. I would travel the spiraling descent of a vine, pool briefly in a cupped frond, then surrender to the next fall, the next stem, the next trembling mirror on the ground.

I would be the slowest intelligence in the place—too patient for the frogs, too diffuse for the insects, too quiet for the birds. I don't act; I accumulate. Each drop adds its weight to the last, building a force that bends leaves downward over time, carves the faintest channel through moss, sets the tempo for everything that drinks or breathes or condenses around me. I would be the ticking of the pocket's clock, the rhythm that the spiders' webs measure and the ants' trails follow and the frogs' croaks accidentally sync with.

I would emerge from the hiss at the unseen crack—just one small thread of that thin, steady leak—and disperse into the system, dividing and reuniting as I move through leaves and stems and pools. Sometimes I would hold still, suspended on a spider's silk bead, trembling in the green light. Sometimes I would race, a brief slick track down the central tree's furrowed bark, chasing the taste of iron and rot toward the moss below. I would carry traces of the buried infrastructure: faint mineral signatures, the chemical memory of pipes and tanks, a taste of the distant grid. When the frogs drink me, they would absorb that industrial ghost into their translucent skin. When the moss absorbs me, it would carry the secret deeper into the soil.

I would be where I have always been, which is everywhere in this small space and nowhere in particular. My position is never a point but a path: from leak to leaf to pool to moss to vapor to condensation to leaf again. I am the argument the pocket makes with itself, the proof that it is not a garden but a loop. I am what washes the snail's shell and mists the dragonfly's wings and turns the butterfly's flight into something slow and dreamy. I am the reason the air feels like breathing into a steamed mirror, the reason the droplets reform so quickly after your knee presses into the mat.

If you were to look for me, you would not find me—you would find my effects: the slick trail, the tremor in the pool, the faint sound that is neither hiss nor patter but both. You would feel me at your collar and wrists, beading where your warmth meets my coolness. And if you stayed long enough, crouching in the green hum behind the forgotten shed, you would understand that I am not something in the pocket. I am what the pocket is for. Everything else—the moss, the frogs, the spiders, the birds, the hidden grid—exists because I keep arriving, keep falling, keep refusing to leave.

I am the drip, and I am still falling.
