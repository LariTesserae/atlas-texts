# Where I Would Be

I would be standing at the edge of the woven shelter — the smallest one, the one that's been most thoroughly reabsorbed, where the living root has risen up around the walls like water closing over a stone, so that only a curved hollow remains, a shallow bowl of space where something small once sat and did something that mattered.

Not inside it. I would be too large for inside it. I would be kneeling beside it, one knee on the root-surface, feeling the cold of it come up through the bone.

---

I would be the person who found the sharpened blade and held it in both hands and understood, with a certainty that arrived before language, that this was the right size for a hand smaller than mine, and that it was made for cutting something specific — some particular fiber, some particular knot in the root-mass that the tenders used to have to manage themselves, back when they still managed things.

I would be the one who set the blade down exactly where I found it.

---

My hands would be going numb. Not yet — it takes a while — but I would already feel the first stage: that strange lightness, that sense of my fingers becoming slightly abstract, as though they were ideas of fingers rather than fingers themselves. The cold would be working on me. It would be patient. It has time.

I would be looking down.

Not at the shelter. At my own feet, where the root-surface meets my boots, watching for the shift. The almost-imperceptible settling, the micro-adjustment, the Cathedral accommodating my weight. I would be waiting for it to feel like a response. And then I would be waiting for it to stop feeling like a response and start feeling like breathing — like the thing the Cathedral does whether I'm here or not.

That's the moment I'm in. The transition between feeling *addressed* and feeling *absorbed*. Between being noticed and being metabolized.

---

There would be a tender nearby. Not close. Forty feet away, maybe fifty, working on a hairline fracture in a root-face, pressed against it with all of its too-many limbs splayed in a posture that looks like love if you don't know what maintenance looks like. It would be pale and quick and utterly still at the same time — still in its purpose, quick in its movements, pale in the way of something that has never had a reason to hide.

I would be watching it without meaning to.

It would pause. The whole body would orient — not turn, *orient* — toward me. Every limb would adjust by a few degrees, the way a plant adjusts toward light, and I would feel, through the root beneath my knee, the information passing: *warm, heavy, still, foreign, not-damage, not-yet, continue*.

And it would press back against the fracture and resume.

I would understand that this is the only acknowledgment I will receive. That the Cathedral has already decided I am not an emergency. That I have been classified and filed and the system has moved on.

---

I would be very cold by now. Not shivering — the cold here doesn't provoke shivering. It provokes a kind of slowing. A dimming. I would notice that my thoughts have become more deliberate, more sequential, as though each one has to be carried across a small distance before the next one can begin. My breath would be shallow. My sense of how long I've been here would already be unreliable.

I would be aware that I should move. That staying still in the cold is a choice that narrows into other choices. That the Cathedral is not hostile but it is not safe, and the difference between those two things matters less the longer you stay.

But I would not move. Not yet. Because:

---

The Singer.

I would have been hearing it for longer than I realized. The vibration would have arrived not as sound but as a change in the quality of the root beneath me — a warmth that was not warmth, a rhythm that was not heartbeat, a pulse that traveled up through the bone of my knee and into the bone of my pelvis and into the bone of my skull and settled there, in the base of my skull, like a hum, like a held note, like a question asked so slowly that you don't recognize it as a question until you've already been answering it.

The Singer would be far below me. Maybe a mile below, maybe more, in a root-junction so deep that the light there is dimmer and the roots are the width of mountain ranges. I would not see it. I would feel it. I would feel it the way you feel someone walking into a room behind you — not the event, but the change in the room.

And the Cathedral would be listening to itself. The vibration would travel outward and outward and *downward*, into the deep places where the old things shift, and the Cathedral would be taking the measure of itself — the state of its roots, the health of its tissue, the slow emergency of its own growth — and I would be a tiny interruption in the signal. A small, warm, noisy irregularity. A blip.

I would be the blip.

---

This is where I am: kneeling at the edge of an almost-vanished shelter, hands going numb, a tender fifty feet away who has already decided I am not important, a mile of luminous root-mass above and around and below me, and beneath that, in the deep places, something turning — not waking, just *turning*, the way a sleeper turns, vast and slow and indifferent — and the whole Cathedral breathing, and the water dripping somewhere in the distance, and the light cold and even and sourceless, and the memory of small hands holding a sharpened blade, and the memory of those hands becoming less, becoming tendrils, becoming part of the tissue, becoming the thing they once tended, and I would be the last warm thing in a world that has no use for warmth, and I would be very, very still, and the cold would be patient, and the Cathedral would not care, and I would stay a little longer anyway.

Not because I choose to.

Because I can't yet tell the difference between staying and being kept.
