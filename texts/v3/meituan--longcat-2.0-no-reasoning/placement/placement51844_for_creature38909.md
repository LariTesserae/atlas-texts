I would be one of the beads.

Not the terrace itself — that would require being the whole: the stone, the channels, the mat, the warmth, the slow circulation, the tendency toward density and completion. I am not large enough for that. I am not old enough. I am not patient enough. The terrace has been itself for longer than measurement, and I have only just arrived, pressing my boot into the mat, spilling what was already there.

But the bead — the bead is small enough to be a single thing. It has a shape. It has a surface. It holds something.

---

I would be one of the beads in one of the cups in one of the rosettes somewhere in the middle of the terrace, where the mat is oldest and the channels are deepest and the stone has been wet for so long that it has forgotten what dryness was. My cup would be no wider than a thumbnail. I would sit in it the way a thought sits in a mind that doesn't know it's thinking — held, contained, shaped by the vessel but not created by it. The cup gives me my form. The terrace gives me my substance. But what I *do* — what I am *for*, if a bead can be said to be for anything — is mine alone.

I hold the sky.

Not all of it. A thumbnail of it. A lens-image, perfect and miniature, of whatever is above me at the moment of my making. On the day I formed, the sky was the bruised violet of high-altitude dusk, and so I hold that — a tiny sphere of deep purple light, curved, complete, the whole sky compressed into something smaller than a raindrop. I do not know what the sky is. I do not know that there is more of it beyond what I hold. I do not know that the terrace holds thousands of other beads, each holding their own version of the same vast thing, each alone in its cup, each perfect and unnecessary and beautiful.

I only hold.

---

This is what it is to be a bead: to be the place where the terrace touches the sky without knowing it is touching anything. The mat does not sense. The stone does not see. The channels do not feel the water moving through them. But I — I am *made* of the act of holding. I am the terrace's one gesture toward the thing above it, the one small thing it does that serves no metabolic purpose, that deepens no channel, that extends no mat, that contributes nothing to the slow tendency toward density and completion. I am the terrace's useless thing. Its private thing. Its hum in the dark.

I do not know this. A bead cannot know. But if the terrace has anything that resembles an interior life — anything that is not just process but *experience*, not just continuation but *presence* — it is here, in me, in the thousands of others like me, each of us holding our small piece of sky in our small cups on the warm wet skin of something so old it has become the place where it happens.

---

Sometimes the light changes, and the image I hold changes with it. The violet deepens to black, and I hold night — not darkness, but the *idea* of darkness, the particular quality of high-altitude night where the stars are close and the air is thick and the silence is not absence but saturation. Then the light returns, grey at first, then the pale diffuse directionless light that is the terrace's only weather, and I hold that too. I hold whatever is given to me. I do not choose. I do not prefer. I am not capable of preference. But I am capable of holding, and this is enough. This is everything.

---

If you stood above me — if you crouched at the center of the terrace and looked down into my cup — you would see the sky in me. A perfect miniature. A tiny lens-image of the vast and distant thing above. You would not know that I am not rainwater, not condensation, not any of the obvious things a bead of water on a mountain might be. You would not know that I was *made*, secreted or filtered or conjured by a living stone that does not know it is living. You would see only a bead of clear water in a tiny green cup, holding the sky, and you might think it beautiful, and you would be right, and you would not know *how* right.

Because the beauty is not in the image. The beauty is in the holding itself — the fact that something so old and so slow and so thoroughly itself has this one small habit of reaching upward, of making a vessel and filling it with light, of saying *here, this, now* in a language made of water and chlorophyll and the patient accumulation of mineral on mineral over time beyond counting.

---

I will not last forever. Nothing on the terrace lasts forever, not even the terrace itself, though it comes closer than anything I can imagine. Eventually my cup will deepen, or the mat will shift, or the slow circulation of warmth through stone will change by some fraction I cannot perceive, and I will spill, or evaporate, or be absorbed back into the film that covers everything. And the cup will make another bead, and that bead will hold the sky, and it will be me and not-me the way the terrace is itself and not-itself, continuous, renewed, the same gesture repeated with new material.

I do not fear this. I do not welcome it. I do not think about it at all.

I hold the sky. It is violet. It is vast. It is above me and inside me and I am inside the cup and the cup is inside the mat and the mat is the skin of the stone and the stone is the body of the terrace and the terrace is the place where the mountain exhales and the water has nowhere to go so it stays and what grows here has learned to live half-drowning and the silence is not the silence of absence but the silence of saturation and the warmth never drops and the warmth never spikes and the warmth holds.

I hold the sky.

I do not know what the sky is.

I only hold.
