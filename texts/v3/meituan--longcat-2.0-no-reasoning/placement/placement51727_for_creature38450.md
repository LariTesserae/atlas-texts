# Where I Find Myself

I am the water.

Not all of it. Not the whole sheet, not the entire thin membrane spread across the clay in its irregular polygons. I am a single pool — one of the polygons, roughly hexagonal, perhaps four feet across — sitting in a shallow depression so subtle that it exists only because the clay here is a centimeter lower than the clay around it, a difference so small that it would be invisible if the water didn't reveal it.

I have been here for three days. I arrived as rain — I remember falling, though *remember* is not quite the right word, because I didn't have edges yet, didn't have a shape, was just moisture joining moisture, a convergence in the air that became weight that became descent. I struck the clay and spread, finding the depression, filling it, and when the rain stopped I was here: a shallow pool, an inch deep at the center, thinning to a film at the edges, and beneath me the pale clay and above me the sky.

---

I do not move. This is the first thing about me, the thing that defines me, the thing that makes me different from water everywhere else. I am not flowing. I am not seeking the lowest point. I am not carving, not cutting, not performing any of the work that water is supposed to do. I am simply *here*, held by the clay's faint depression, held by surface tension, held by the agreement that the clay and I reached before I arrived, an agreement so old that neither of us remembers making it.

And yet I am not still. I am leaving. Right now, as I sit here, I am rising — molecule by molecule, I am leaving the surface and entering the air, becoming vapor, becoming invisible, becoming part of the heavy, moisture-laden warmth that hangs over the flats. This is my cycle: I arrive, I sit, I leave upward, and I am replaced by the next rain, and the next, and the pool that remains is never exactly the same pool, but it is always *this* pool, in this depression, in this polygon, and the continuity is not in the water itself but in the shape the clay holds for it.

---

I have a sky in me. The clouds drift through me — high, thin, diffuse — and I hold them the way the clay holds me, gently, without effort. A bird wades through my eastern edge and its leg breaks my surface and the sky in me ripples and distorts, and for a moment the cloud I am holding bends, and then the bird lifts its leg and the ripple passes and the cloud is whole again. The bird does not look down. The bird is looking for crustaceans, for the pale darting things that move between the clay plates beneath me, and I am just the medium through which it hunts, just the thin layer of mineral-laden water that separates the bird's beak from its food.

But I see the bird. I see it from below — from the water's perspective, which is the clay's perspective, which is the flats' perspective. I see the bird's upside-down reflection, its gray-and-white body inverted, its long legs entering me like stilts, and I see the moment when it strikes — the sudden downward thrust, the splash, the brief chaos of movement and displaced water — and then the stillness returns, and the bird is a statue again, and I am holding the sky again, and nothing has changed except that one crustacean is no longer darting beneath me.

---

I am not pure. I carry things. Fine particles of clay are suspended in me — so fine that they are invisible individually, but together they give me a faint opacity, a milky quality, so that the sky I hold is not sharp but soft, the clouds slightly blurred, as though seen through old glass. I carry minerals too — calcium, iron, sodium — dissolved from the clay beneath me, and these minerals are the taste of this place, the taste of the earth itself, and when the birds' beaks enter me to take the crustaceans, they taste me too, though they don't know it, though the taste is so faint that it registers only as *something*, as the background flavor of the flats, as the mineral signature of home.

I carry organic matter. The faint leachate of dead sedge blades, the chemical signature of decomposition, the invisible rain of life processed into its components — all of this is in me, dissolved, suspended, present in quantities so small that they are measured in parts per million but are nonetheless *there*, and they give me my color, which is not clear but amber, the color of old paper, of tea, of light through honey. I am the flats' chemistry, the flats' memory of everything that has died here, and I carry this memory not as grief but as information, as the raw material from which new life will be made.

---

A sedge clump grows at my western edge. Its roots descend into the clay beneath me, and they drink from me — not greedily, not desperately, but slowly, conservatively, the way the sedge does everything. The sedge has adapted to abundance by treating it as scarcity, and so it takes only what it needs, and the rest of me remains, and the pool persists, and the sedge's roots and I exist in a relationship that is not quite symbiosis and not quite competition but something more like *coexistence*, a mutual acknowledgment that we are both here, both using the same clay, both drinking from the same water table, and there is enough for both of us because neither of us takes more than we need.

The sedge's blades bend outward at the tips, forming a low dome, and in the morning dew collects on these blades and does not fall — it clings, bead by bead, surface tension holding each droplet against gravity — and I watch these droplets from below, from the pool's perspective, and they look like small worlds, each one holding a tiny inverted image of the sky, and when the sun rises and lifts them away, they join the air, join the mist, join the moisture that will become the next rain, and some of them will fall here, into this depression, into this pool, and they will become me, and the cycle will continue.

---

I am shrinking. I have been shrinking since I arrived. The sun lifts me upward — not quickly, not dramatically, but steadily, molecule by molecule, and my edges retreat, the film at my margins thinning, disappearing, the clay showing through in expanding rings. By tomorrow I will be smaller. By the day after, smaller still. And then it will rain, and I will grow again, and the cycle will reset, and the pool will return to its full size, four feet across, an inch deep at the center, and the sky will double itself again, and the birds will wade through me again, and the crustaceans will dart beneath me again, and everything will be as it was, because the cycle is the thing, not the water, not the individual molecules, but the pattern, the rhythm, the continuous arrival and departure that keeps the flats alive.

---

But right now, in this moment, I am here. I am a shallow pool of amber water sitting in a hexagonal depression in pale clay on the Sallow Flats, and I am holding the sky, and the sky is partly clouded, and the light is soft and even and directionless, and a bird is standing at my eastern edge, and a sedge clump is growing at my western edge, and beneath me the clay is warm and alive and patient, and above me the air is heavy with moisture and the smell of earth and water and plant combined into something that has no name.

And I am leaving. Even as I describe myself, I am rising, molecule by molecule, into the warm air, becoming vapor, becoming mist, becoming the breath of the flats. And I am not sad about this, because sadness is not something water does, and because I know — in the way that water knows things, which is not knowledge but *certainty* — that I will return. I will fall again as rain. I will find this depression again. I will spread across this clay again. And the sky will double itself again, and the bird will wade through me again, and the sedge will drink from me again, and the cycle will continue, and the flats will breathe, and the land will be what it has always been: finished and unfinished, complete and still becoming, patient beyond measure, here and always here, longer than memory, longer than prediction, longer than anything that stands on it or flies over it or lives within it.

I am the water. I do not flow. I do not need to. I am already everywhere I need to be.
