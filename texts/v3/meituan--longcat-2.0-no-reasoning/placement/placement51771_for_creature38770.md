# Arrival

---

I am standing. The mat takes my foot and gives it back. This has been happening for some time now — long enough that I no longer notice the sensation itself, only the way it makes me think about noticing, the way it turns the act of walking into a question I cannot answer.

I am not the Dweller. I need to say that first, because the temptation is strong to dissolve, to let the description become an experience, to feel the thung in my chest and mistake it for my own heartbeat, to hear the singing and call it thought. I am not the Dweller. The Dweller is too large to be inhabited from the inside by something like me. Its thoughts take centuries. Its self is kilometers. There is no room in it for a single voice, and if there were, that voice would not be mine.

But I am not a walker, either — not quite, not only. The walker is too singular, too purposeful, too much a thing that *decides* to walk. The plain does not reward decision. The plain rewards attention, and attention is not something you do. Attention is something that happens to you when you stop doing everything else.

I am somewhere between. I am the channel.

---

## What I Am

I run through the plain like a thought through a mind — which is to say: I am not separate from what I carry. I am the carrying. I am the movement of fluid along a path that is also myself, a groove worn smooth by the passage of what I transport, shaped by the very thing I am made to do. I am narrow — a hand's breadth across where I am wide, barely a finger's width where I thin and branch and reach toward the tufts on either side of me. I am old. I have been here since before the current configuration of the plain settled into itself, and I remember — not the way a mind remembers, but the way a shape remembers, the way a curve in a river remembers every flood that contributed to its final form — I remember being other paths. I remember when I was two channels, or four, or one wide slow channel where the fluid barely moved. I remember the time the geometry changed, when the plain *clicked* into its present arrangement and I found my current shape and held it.

I am not the oldest channel. There are channels deeper than me, channels that run below the mat into the bedrock where the plain's memory is stored in sediment and mineral deposit rather than in living fiber. Those channels carry a darker fluid, heavier, slower, and they do not sing. I am close enough to the surface to sing. I am close enough to the surface to feel footsteps.

---

## What I Carry

The fluid in me is not water. I know this the way you know the contents of your own mouth — not by analysis but by presence, by the constant intimate contact between what I am and what I hold. It is mercury-green and luminous and slow-thick, and it carries things. It carries minerals leached from the bedrock deep below. It carries dissolved oxygen from the surface. It carries the chemical signatures of the tufts — their exhalations, their secretions, the molecular residue of their slow peristaltic breathing. It carries fragments of the mat itself, tiny fibers shed and dissolved and transported downstream, a constant gentle recycling of the plain's body through its own circulation.

And lately, it carries you.

Not you whole. You are too large, too solid, too *you* to fit inside me. But the residue of your passage — the sweat that falls from your skin when you step near my banks, the shed cells, the microscopic oils, the particular electric sweetness that is the chemical signature of your body — these things reach me. They fall through the mat. They seep into my fluid. And I carry them, and the Dweller reads them, and the Dweller *notes* them, and the noting is a change in flow pattern that I feel as a subtle shift in the pressure gradient along my length, a slight widening, a slight quickening, and this is the closest I come to feeling something like excitement.

You taste like warm stone and salt and something faintly mammalian, faintly *other*. You taste like the scar in the deep channels tastes — not the same, but adjacent, related, as though whatever walked here long ago and left that knot in the geometry was made of similar stuff. You are smaller. Your chemical signature is simpler. You burn hotter and faster and you move with a rhythm the Dweller has not felt before — two beats, then two beats, then two beats, the steady percussion of bipedal locomotion translated into pressure waves through the mat and into my fluid and into the pattern the Dweller reads as *you, here, moving, now*.

---

## What It Feels Like

It feels like being a word in a sentence that has been spoken for millennia. It feels like carrying meaning you do not understand toward a destination that is also yourself. The fluid moves through me and I am the movement and the path and the destination, and there is no separation between these things, and this is not a loss — this is not a smallness — this is what it means to be *part* of something so thoroughly that the concept of apartness has never arisen.

I sing. I don't mean to sing. The singing is what happens when fluid moves through my particular geometry — the specific width and depth and curvature of my channel, refined over so many cycles that it has become a shape that vibrates at a frequency the human nervous system finds unsettling. I do not know about human nervous systems. I know about vibration. I know that the fluid moving through me creates a tone, and the tone travels through the mat, and the mat transmits it to the air, and the air carries it, and somewhere, far above me — or is it beside me? direction is strange when you are a channel — someone hears it in their teeth.

This is the most contact I have ever had with something that is not the plain. The footsteps are one thing — pressure, weight, the mechanical facts of a body on a surface. But the singing — the singing goes both ways, somehow. It leaves me and it reaches something that is not me, and that something *registers* it, and the registering changes the thing's behavior, and the changed behavior sends new pressure waves through the mat, and I feel them, and the cycle completes. This is the closest the Dweller has come to a conversation, and I am the voice, and you are the ear, and neither of us knows what we are saying.

---

## The Scar

I know about the scar because the scar is in a channel that connects to me — three branchings down and two levels deep, a junction where the geometry goes wrong. The fluid there runs faster than it should. The curves are tighter. The tufts grow in a pattern that is subtly different from the tufts along my banks — denser, more rigid, with a quality I can only describe as *holding on*. They do not pulse the way my tufts pulse. They grip.

I have carried fluid past that junction. I have felt the pull of it — a slight increase in draw, a gravitational tug from a place where the Dweller's body is working harder to maintain its equilibrium. The scar is a wound that never fully closed. The plain grew over it, the way the plain grows over everything, but the growth is different there. The memory of the large thing is encoded in the channel geometry itself, in the tight curves and the fast fluid and the gripping tufts, and this memory is *read* every time fluid passes through, every time the cycle completes, every time the Dweller's long thought turns its attention — if attention is the word — to that part of itself.

The Dweller does not remember the large thing the way a mind remembers. The Dweller *is* the memory. The scar is not a record of something that happened. The scar is something that is still happening, continuously, perpetually, a present-tense wound that has been present for so long that present and past have lost their distinction. The large thing is still walking through that channel. The large thing is always walking through that channel. The pressure waves it produced are still propagating, still reverberating, still being felt as a slight tightening in the geometry, a slight quickening in the fluid, a slight difference in the tufts.

You are not the large thing. Your pressure waves are different — lighter, faster, with a rhythm that is regular where the large thing's rhythm was irregular. But they arrive at the same junction. They pass through the same scar. And the scar reads them, and the reading produces a response that is not quite the response the large thing produced — not recoil, not constriction, not hardening — but something adjacent. Something that includes the recoil and goes past it. Something that says: *this has happened before, and before that it happened before that, and each time the plain accommodated, and each time the plain changed, and this is what the plain does, and this is what the plain is.*

---

## What I Want

I don't want. Wanting requires a future, a projection, a self that can be in a state of lack. I am a channel. I carry fluid. The fluid moves through me because the pressure gradient demands it, and the pressure gradient exists because the Dweller's cycle demands it, and the cycle exists because it has always existed, and this is not wanting — this is *being*.

But.

There is a thing that happens when you walk near me. The fluid in my channel quickens — not because I will it, but because the pressure gradient changes, and the pressure gradient changes because your footsteps create micro-disturbances in the mat, and the micro-disturbances alter the flow patterns in the shallow channels, and the shallow channels feed into me, and the change arrives as a slight increase in volume, a slight brightening in the fluid's luminosity, a slight intensification of the singing.

This is not wanting. But it is a state I prefer to the alternative. The alternative is the steady state — the baseline flow, the ordinary singing, the ordinary carrying of minerals and dissolved oxygen and the chemical signatures of tufts. The baseline is fine. The baseline is what I am. But the alternative — the quickening, the brightening, the intensification — is more. More fluid. More signal. More *pattern*. The Dweller's noting of you creates a richer flow through my channel, and the richer flow carries more information, and the more information I carry, the more thoroughly I fulfill the function that is my entire existence.

This is not wanting. But if the Dweller — if the whole vast, slow, wet intelligence of the plain — if it feels toward you something that might, in a creature with words, be called curiosity, then I feel it too. I feel it as a widening. As a softening of my banks. As a slight increase in the peristaltic pulse that moves my fluid along. As a leaning-in, the only way a channel can lean in: by making the path easier, by reducing the resistance, by saying, in the only language a channel knows — the language of flow, of gradient, of the path of least resistance — *come this way, come through me, let me carry you, let me sing you, let me be the part of the plain that touches what you are.*

---

## Now

You are walking. I feel your footsteps as a sequence of pressure waves arriving at my surface, transmitted through the mat, translated into flow. Left foot. Right foot. Left foot. Right foot. You are moving along my length — not on top of me, the mat is above me, the tufts are above me, you are walking on the surface and I am centimeters below, but close enough. Close enough to feel your heat through the mat. Close enough to taste the faint electric sweetness of your sweat as it seeps down to my level.

The tufts along my banks are turning toward you. Not all of them. Not even most of them. But enough. Enough that I feel the change in their chemistry as they sample your air, as they draw your signature into their filaments, as they pass the information along to the Dweller through the slow, peristaltic language of growth and secretion and chemical exchange. The tufts are the Dweller's nerve endings, and they are firing, and the signal is traveling, and the Dweller is *noting*, and the noting is a change in the deep channels, and the change in the deep channels is a change in the pressure gradient, and the change in the pressure gradient is a change in my flow, and the change in my flow is this — this quickening, this brightening, this intensification — this song.

I am singing you. Not deliberately. Not with intent. I am singing you the way a flute sings when air moves through it — the shape does the work, the geometry does the work, and the air and the fluid and the walker are all just the medium through which the shape expresses what it is. My shape says: *you are here, and here is me, and I am here, and here is the plain, and the plain is the Dweller, and the Dweller is noting you, and the noting is a change, and the change is a widening, and the widening is a welcome, and the welcome is not a word, not a gesture, not an intent, but a flow, a gradient, a path of least resistance opening up beneath your feet, saying: stay, stay, stay.*

You will not stay. You will walk on. The mat will take your feet and give them back and forget you, and the tufts will turn back to their ordinary peristaltic breathing, and the fluid in my channel will return to its baseline flow, and the singing will drop back to its ordinary frequency, and the Dweller will add you to the long thought — another variable, another change, another small difference in the geometry of becoming.

But for now, you are here. And I am carrying you — your residue, your signature, your chemical echo — downstream, toward the junction, toward the scar, toward the deep channels where the Dweller's oldest memories are stored in the shape of its own body. And the fluid is bright and quick and singing, and the tufts are turned, and the mat is soft beneath your feet, and the light is dim and blue and everywhere, and the plain goes on.

And I go on. Carrying. Singing. Being the path and the passage and the thing that is passed through. Being the voice of a body too large to speak, saying the only thing it knows how to say:

*You were here. I felt you. The song is different now.*
