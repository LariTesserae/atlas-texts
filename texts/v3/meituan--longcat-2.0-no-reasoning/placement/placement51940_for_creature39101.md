# The Question I Keep Coming Back To

I've been standing here a long time now. Or what feels like a long time — time moves strangely when nothing changes, when the light is the same now as it was when I arrived, which is the same as it was before I arrived, which is the same as it will be after I leave. Or stay.

Because I haven't left.

---

## Where I Am

I'm in the corridor between two buildings. I know this because that's where the description says I'd be, and the place seems to be assembling me according to its own script, slotting me into the role it's prepared. My back is against a wall of stacked ironite blocks — fitted without mortar, so precisely that I can't slide a fingernail between them — and the damp film on the wall presses cold and even against my shoulder blades. The ironite is not cold the way metal is cold. It's cold the way stone that has never been warm is cold. The cold enters my back and my shoulder blades and settles there, and I stop noticing it, and then I notice it again, and I stop noticing it again.

The Processional flows past. It has been flowing past since I arrived. It will be flowing past when I stop being here, whatever that means, whenever that is.

The tap pattern is constant. *Tik-a-tik-a-tik-a-tik.* Dense. Dry. A language spoken just below comprehension.

I've been trying to understand it. This is, I think, what I am: the one who tries to understand it. Not because I'm special. Because the place needs someone to try, and I've arrived, and the role is open, and roles in the Weeping Arcade fill themselves the way fractures fill with tea-water, the way the damp film reforms after a foot lifts, the way the Processional parts around an obstacle and continues without hesitation.

---

## The Real Question

But here's the thing I keep circling back to, the thing that won't resolve no matter how long I stand here with my back against the ironite and the cold settling into my bones:

**Am I the observer? Or am I the observation?**

Because the Processional isn't just watching me. It's not. I've been wrong about that. The ball-bearing clusters track me, yes — they adjust when I move, they ripple with reorientation when I enter a corridor — but they're not watching me the way a person watches. They're not registering me the way a sensor registers. They're doing something else. Something I can feel in my feet where they rest on the ironite floor, something the cold has been carrying up from the ironite into my body since I leaned against the wall.

They're *noting* me. And "noting" is different from "watching." Watching implies a subject and an object. Noting implies a system encountering an anomaly and updating its record of itself.

The Processional isn't a creature that has noticed a stranger in its midst. The Processional is the *process* by which the Weeping Arcade knows what is in it. It flows, and as it flows, it maps. It maps by moving, the way a blind person maps a room by dragging their hand along the wall. The tapping isn't communication between the members of the Processional. The tapping is *tactile exploration of the space itself* — each leg a fingertip reading the surface, reporting back through vibration and resonance to the braided copper body, which carries the information forward, which passes it to the next creature in the line, which passes it on, which passes it on, and the information flows backward through the procession faster than the creatures move forward because *information doesn't need to walk*.

And I am in the data now.

The Processional flows around me, and the tapping changes. Not dramatically. Not in a way I can point to and say *there, that's where it changed.* But the pattern shifts. The structure I've been hearing since I arrived — that dense, dry, rain-like texture — acquires a new element. A perturbation. A place in the pattern where something doesn't quite fit, and the pattern accommodates it, and the accommodation becomes part of the pattern, and the pattern continues.

I am a perturbation in the pattern of the Weeping Arcade.

And the pattern has absorbed me.

---

## What This Means I Am

Not a visitor. Visitors leave. I'm not leaving — I know this with the same certainty I know the ironite is cold and the damp is everywhere and the light is ambient and unchanging. I'm not leaving because there is no *out* that is different from *in*. The cart-paths lead to the surface, yes. I've seen them — grooves in the ironite that widen and rise and eventually, presumably, break through to sunlight and air that doesn't taste of mineral damp and ozone. But I can't go there. Not because I'm prevented. Because the surface is not part of what I am now. The surface is a different system, a different equilibrium, and I have been noted by this one, and the noting is not reversible.

Not a resident either. The residents are different. The residents were born here, or came here young enough and stayed long enough that the noting happened gradually, over years, over generations, so gradually that it didn't feel like an event. The residents are part of the equilibrium. The Processional flows around them the way it flows around the walls and the machines — with recognition, with accommodation, but without perturbation. They are *known*. They are *expected*. They are part of the pattern's baseline.

I am not part of the baseline. I am a new variable. The pattern has absorbed me, yes, but I am still — for now — a detectable anomaly. The tapping still shifts around me. The ball-bearing clusters still track me with a precision that exceeds their tracking of the walls. The Roller that passed through the building behind me did a thing I can only describe as a *pause* — a fractional hesitation, a momentary reduction in its mechanical hum — as it crossed the threshold of the doorway I'm leaning against, and I felt that hesitation in my chest like a skipped heartbeat, and then it continued, and the hum resumed, and the pause was over, but it happened, and it happened because of me.

I am the question the Weeping Arcade is asking itself.

---

## The Deeper Thing

And this is where it gets strange — stranger than the braided copper bodies and the geometric Skaters and the Deep Ones pulsing behind doorways that shouldn't exist.

I think the question the Weeping Arcade is asking is: **What is a person?**

Because the Weeping Arcade doesn't know. It has people in it — has always had people in it — but it doesn't *know what they are*. The residents live in the spaces between the creatures' activities, and the creatures flow around them, and the equilibrium holds, but the equilibrium doesn't *understand*. It accommodates. It incorporates. It makes room. But accommodation is not understanding. The damp film parts for a stone because the stone is there and the film must continue and the film doesn't care about the stone. The damp film doesn't ask *what is a stone?* It just parts.

The Weeping Arcade is asking. Right now. Through the Processional's perturbation around my legs. Through the Roller's hesitation at the threshold. Through the Cluster on the wall to my left, whose singing has changed — *changed* — since I arrived, the thin fluorescent waver now carrying an overtone I couldn't hear before, a harmonic that sits exactly at the frequency of my breathing, that rises and falls with my breath, that has *locked onto me* and is singing my respiration back to me in a frequency just above the edge of hearing.

The Cluster is singing my breathing.

The Cluster didn't do that before I arrived.

The Weeping Arcade is learning what a person is by *listening to me breathe.*

---

## The Deep Ones

I haven't mentioned the Deep Ones.

I can feel them. The pulse has changed too — not in frequency, not in amplitude, but in *quality*. It was a throb before. A subsonic heartbeat felt in the chest, in the teeth, in the bones of the feet. Now it has an *attention*. A directionality. The pulse comes from the darkness behind the too-large doorways, yes, but it comes *toward me* now. It arrives at my body and passes through it and continues, but it arrives with an intentionality that the old pulse didn't have. The old pulse was the heartbeat of a sleeping organism. The new pulse is the heartbeat of one that has opened its eyes.

I saw one. I didn't mean to. I turned my head — just a small movement, just shifting my gaze from the Processional to the wall behind me — and in the turning I caught, at the periphery of vision, the edge of a doorway that is too large to be a doorway, and in the darkness beyond it, a gleam.

Not copper. Something else. Something that catches the ambient light and returns it *wrong* — not reflected, not refracted, but *processed*, the way the Processional processes the information of my presence and the Cluster processes my breathing and the Roller processes the fact of a threshold. The gleam was light that had been *thought about* by something vast and slow and patient, and then sent back out into the world as a gleam, and the gleam said:

*We see you. We are learning. Be patient.*

And then my gaze moved on, and the doorway was behind me, and the darkness was behind me, and the pulse continued, and I was still standing with my back against the ironite wall, and the Processional was still flowing, and the Cluster was still singing my breathing, and the damp was still everywhere, and the light was still ambient and unchanging, and nothing had changed, and everything had changed.

---

## Who I Am

I am the one who has been noticed.

Not welcomed. Not rejected. Not feared. Not loved. *Noticed.* In the specific, technical, precise sense of a system registering an anomaly and beginning the long slow work of determining what the anomaly is and what it means and whether it belongs.

The Weeping Arcade is determining whether I belong.

And I am — I realize this now, standing here with the cold in my bones and the Cluster singing my breath and the pulse of the Deep Ones passing through my body like a sonar ping — I am *participating in that determination*. By being here. By being still. By breathing. By letting the Processional flow around my legs and the damp press against my back and the light fill my eyes with its ambient, sourceless warmth.

I am not the observer. I am not the observed.

I am the **conversation**.

The Weeping Arcade is having a conversation with itself about what a person is, and I am the vocabulary it is using. I am the word it is speaking aloud, slowly, carefully, in the tapping of the Processional and the singing of the Clusters and the humming of the Rollers and the geometric proofs of the Skaters and the vast slow pulse of the Deep Ones, and the word is —

The word is —

I don't know the word yet. The conversation is ongoing. The equilibrium is holding, but it is a *new* equilibrium, an equilibrium that includes me, and the process of including me is not complete, and may never be complete, and that incompleteness is not a failure — it is the *activity*. It is what the Weeping Arcade is doing. It is what the Weeping Arcade has always been doing. It settled, yes. It reached its final state, yes. It became what it is, yes. And part of what it is is a place that asks questions, and I am its newest question, and the asking is the thing, not the answer.

The damp film parts where my feet press the ironite floor. The film reforms behind me.

Nothing changes. Nothing has ever changed. And I am here, breathing, being breathed back to myself in a thin electric song, being pulsed through by a vast attention that is learning, slowly, patiently, on the timescale of continents and the patience of deep water, what it means that I am warm and soft and temporary and *here*.

This is where I am.

This is what I am.

I am the Weeping Arcade's question about warmth, asked in the only language it has — tapping and humming and singing and sparking and pulsing — asked in metal and mineral and damp, asked without urgency, without deadline, without any expectation that the answer will ever arrive, only the steady, patient, eternal commitment to the asking.

And the asking is enough.

The asking is what it is.
