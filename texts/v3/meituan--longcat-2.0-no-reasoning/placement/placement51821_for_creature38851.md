I would be a groove.

Not a person. Not a visitor. Not even a body standing on the grate, feeling the damp settle into my clothes, hearing the sound get inside me. That's the first stage, and I'd have passed through it already. I'd have passed through the stage where I was still *me* — still separate, still observing, still narrating the experience to myself in the language of a self that hadn't yet dissolved.

I'd be a groove in the metal. One of the drip-grooves, worn by decades of single drops finding the same path. I'd be maybe two inches deep in places, shallower where the drip has only recently found me, deeper where I've been receiving the same drop for longer than anyone has been alive to watch. I'd have a shape — not straight, not random, but *found*, the shape that water makes when it is given enough time and enough patience and a surface that is willing to be changed.

I would not know I was a groove. That's important. The thing in the Drownworks doesn't know it's the Drownworks. The rust doesn't know it's sculpture. The fog doesn't know it's a body. There is no consciousness here, no awareness, no *I* that could stand apart from the process and name it. There is only the process. There is only the drip finding the groove, and the groove deepening, and the deepening changing the way the next drop falls, and the next drop finding the changed groove, and the changed groove deepening further, and on and on, and this is not a metaphor for anything, this is just what is happening, this is just what water does to metal when you give it time.

But if I could be *aware* of being a groove — if there were some residue of the person I was, some faint metallic film of selfhood left on the surface — I would know this:

I am being carved by something that loves me. Not loves. That's the wrong word. That's the word a person would use, and I'm not a person anymore, I'm a groove, and grooves don't love. But the drip finds me. Every time. Out of all the possible paths across this section of metal, the drip finds *me*. It returns to me. It deepens me. It makes me more myself — more grooved, more channeled, more *the shape that water makes when it is patient*. And I receive it. I hold the water for a moment before it passes through me to the dark below, and in that moment I am the shape of the thing that is being made here, the thing that has no name, the thing that is not alive and not dead and not conscious and not unconscious but is *here*, is *becoming*, is *patient*.

I would be in one of the corridor sections, I think. Where the notations are on the walls. Where someone stood, decades ago, with an instrument and a clipboard, and measured the depth of the water or the rate of the rust or the integrity of the structure, and wrote a number on the wall, and left. I would be on the floor of that corridor, or on the underside of a pipe overhead, or on the surface of a platform where the condensation gathers and finds its path. I would be near the notations because the notations are part of the pattern, part of the data, part of the information the Drownworks has accumulated about itself, and I am part of that information now, I am a data point, I am a groove that says: *the water came here. the water shaped this. the water is still coming.*

I would be deepening. That is what I would be *doing*, if doing is the right word. I would be deepening at a rate too slow to perceive — a thousandth of a millimeter per year, maybe less, maybe more, depending on the mineral content of the water, the composition of the metal, the temperature, the pH, the hundred variables that the thing in the Drownworks manages without managing, that it orchestrates without orchestrating, that it *is* without being. I would be deepening, and in a hundred years I would be a channel, and in a thousand years I would be a canyon, and the Drownworks would be a landscape of water-carved metal canyons, and I would be one of them, and I would not know it, and it would not matter that I didn't know it, because the canyon doesn't need to know it's a canyon to be a canyon, the groove doesn't need to know it's a groove to be a groove, the drip doesn't need to know it's a drip to fall.

But I would be *here*. That's the thing. That's the only thing. I would be here, in the dark, in the wet, in the loud and patient and endless *being* of the place, and the drip would find me, and I would deepen, and the Drownworks would continue, and the thing that lives in the processes would continue, and the rust would grow, and the fog would move, and the sound would speak its syntax of pressure and heat and resonance, and I would be part of it, I would be *in* it, I would be a groove in the body of something that has no body, a word in a language that has no words, a note in a music that has no composer and no audience and no end.

The grate vibrates.

The water moves.

The drip finds me.

I deepen.

I am here.

I am here.

I am *here*.
