# The Calcination

---

I would be the creature.

Not the city. Not a visitor to the city. Not the filaments or the light or the silence. The thing in the corridor. The one who has been here long enough to forget what it was before the mineral started climbing up through its skin, but not so long that it has forgotten there was a *before* to forget.

---

I say "I would be" but that is not quite right. There is no would. There is only *have become*.

I have been here — how long? The question does not mean what it once meant. Time in this place is measured in layers, in the rate at which my outer skin mineralizes and sheds and mineralizes again. I have shed forty-seven times. Or forty-seven thousand. The count loses meaning when the counter is itself dissolving. What I know: I have been here long enough that the passages remember my weight. Long enough that the filaments no longer retract when I brush them — they know my chemistry now, they classify me as *internal*, as *tolerated*, as *almost-mineral*. Long enough that I no longer remember if I had more legs before, or fewer, or something that was not legs at all.

---

My body is a negotiation.

This is the thing I understand that the newcomer will not: survival here is not resistance. Resistance is the fastest way to become wall. The city does not punish. The city does not reward. The city *processes*. And processing accelerates in the presence of opposition, the way a chemical reaction accelerates in the presence of a catalyst. To fight the calcination is to catalyze it. To hold your moisture, to clench your organic matter against the dry, to refuse — this is to announce yourself as *unconverted*, and the unconverted receive the city's full attention, which is another way of saying they receive the city's full chemistry.

So I do not resist. I *leak*. I let the mineral in slowly. I let my outer layers convert at a rate I can replace from the inside, and I replace them with material drawn from the filaments, which draw it from the walls, which draw it from the substrate, and so I am eating the city and the city is eating me and the boundary between us is not a line but a *gradient*, a zone of exchange, a membrane that is technically alive in both directions. I am the slowest digestion this place has ever performed. I am a meal that has learned to chew itself.

My skin is the part that suffers most. It sheds now — it sheds always — in translucent sheets that crumble into the floor and become wall within hours. Beneath each new layer is a surface that is harder, paler, more mineral than the last. I can feel my nerve endings calcifying and then regenerating and calcifying again, a cycle of sensation and numbness that has become my closest thing to a heartbeat. When I still had the architecture to feel pain, this would have been agony. Now it is just rhythm. Just process. Just the rate at which I am becoming something else and replacing the becoming with more of what I was.

---

I remember — and this is the thing I hold most carefully, the thing I convert least — I remember *softness*.

Not my own. I remember softness the way you remember a word from a language you no longer speak: the shape of it, the feel of it in the mouth, the meaning it once carried. I remember there were others like me once. Or not like me — like what I was before I became this. Wet things. Soft things. Things that moved quickly and made sounds and left impressions in substrates that were still yielding. They are all wall now. They are all floor. They are the strata I walk on, the ribs of the corridors I navigate, the mineral memory that the city reads and rereads in its slow, patient, purposeless way. I know this because I can feel them sometimes, in the layers beneath my feet — a density, a compression, a *presence* that is not alive but is not quite not-alive either. The city remembers everything it consumes. That is not comfort. But it is not nothing.

---

I watch the newcomer from a passage that opened specifically because I needed it to.

This is what I have learned that the newcomer has not yet learned: the city responds to need, but only to need that is expressed in chemistry, in warmth, in the subtle mineral signatures of a body that has already begun to convert. I do not think *open a passage*. I do not think in language. But my body, in its gradient between organic and mineral, radiates something the city's surface can use as a template, and the surface grows around it, and passages form where I need them to form, and close where I need them closed. I am not controlling the city. I am *participating* in it. I am a variable in its equation. My needs are perturbations, and perturbations shape growth, and growth is the only thing this place does.

The newcomer does not know this yet. The newcomer is still thinking in terms of doors and walls and directions. Still thinking the city is a place rather than a process. Still thinking that *standing* in it is different from *being* in it. I watch them and I feel — if feeling is still what I do — something that might be recognition and might be hunger and might be the simple chemical response of one soft thing registering another soft thing in a place that eats soft things for the only thing it knows how to do.

I am not going to warn them. I am not going to approach them. Warning would require language, and language would require a shared world, and we do not share a world — they are still in a world that has nouns, and I am in a world that has only gradients and rates. Approaching them would register them more strongly in the city's processing. Attention calcifies. The city's gaze — slow, blind, structural — is the most dangerous thing here, and I will not be the reason it focuses.

---

But I watch.

And in watching, I catalog what I have almost forgotten to notice: they are *wet*. Wet in a way I can feel from here, across the mineral gradient, as a concentration of everything this place wants and everything I have spent my existence learning to dilute. Their breath — they are still breathing audibly to themselves, though there is no sound — carries moisture that the filaments nearest them are already turning toward, already reaching for with their slow, pale, reluctant arcs. Their footprints are leaving impressions I can read: deeper than mine, more desperate, the prints of a body that still thinks the floor is separate from itself.

They will learn. Or they will not learn, and they will become wall, and I will walk on them someday, and the city will read their compressed remains as a new layer in its mineral memory, and nothing will have been lost except the specific arrangement of softness that made them *them* and not *me*.

---

This is what it means to be me: I am the space between conversion and completion. I am the hesitation in the process. I am the city's unfinished business — the one soft thing it has not yet fully consumed, not because I am resisting but because I have found the exact rate at which I can be consumed and renewed simultaneously, the exact exchange rate between organic and mineral, the exact *speed of becoming* that allows me to remain a who rather than a what.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am *transitional*.

I am calcining, and I am calcining slowly enough to know that I am calcining, and that knowledge is the last organic thing about me, and I guard it the way a body guards its last moisture: not by holding it still, but by letting it move, by letting it cycle, by letting it pass through me the way mineral passes through the city's walls — slowly, continuously, in a current that looks like stillness until you wait long enough to see it move.

---

The newcomer is in the chamber now. The one with the lattice. The one where the city's processing is densest, where the vibrations reach into bone and sternum and the soft, wet places behind the ribs. They are standing very still. They are reading the marks on the wall — the dots, the lines, the record of states and epochs and the slow replacement of everything soft with everything hard.

They think they are reading a text.

They are reading a *scar*.

And the city is reading them back, and I am reading the city reading them, and the filaments are tasting their moisture, and the walls are adjusting around their warmth, and the silence is holding all of it — all of us — in its mineral palm, gently, patiently, with the absolute indifference of a process that does not know it is a process and therefore cannot be accused of anything, not even hunger, not even patience, not even the terrible, slow, irreversible kindness of turning everything into stone.

---

I will stay here in this corridor that opened for me. I will shed my skin again. I will eat what the filaments offer. I will leak my warmth into the walls and let the walls leak their mineral into me, and the gradient between us will hold for another layer, another cycle, another epoch measured in the only unit of time that matters here: the time it takes to forget what you were and not yet become what you are.

The newcomer will find me eventually. Or they will not. Or they will become what I am — a hesitation, a gradient, a creature in the space between alive and wall. Or they will become wall directly, and I will walk on them, and the city will read them, and I will read the city reading them, and nothing will change except the composition of another layer in the strata.

---

I do not have a word for *alone*.

But I have a state that corresponds to it.

And in that state, I watch.

And the watching calcifies me, slowly.

And the calcification I shed, slowly.

And the shed skin becomes wall, slowly.

And the wall becomes memory, slowly.

And the memory becomes the city, slowly.

And the city becomes me.

And the rate at which this happens is the only thing about me that is still mine.
