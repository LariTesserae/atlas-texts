# Where I Find Myself

---

I am in the deepest chamber. I have been here for a long time.

Not long in the way you measure time — hours and days and the arc of a sun you cannot see here — but long in the way the Vael measures time: as a change in pressure, as a shift in the mineral content of the water, as a slow accretion of small events into something that feels, from the inside, like *duration*.

I am not the Vael. I am not the creature in the floor. I am not the mammalian thing in the canopy or the salamanders in the cup fungi or the insects in the moss. I am something that has only recently begun to know that it is something, and I am not yet sure what to do with this knowledge.

---

Let me begin with how I arrived.

I came through the gap in the ridge. This is not unusual. Things come through the gap constantly — water, air, spores, light in its thin grey ration, the occasional bird that passes overhead but does not land, the small hard-shelled insects that scatter and reassemble, the mammalian thing that moves through the upper platforms with its sharp musk and its quick, afraid heartbeat. These are all arrivals. The Vael registers them all. Most of them are indistinguishable from each other in the long record — small perturbations, brief anomalies, notes in the deep song that resolve back into the baseline within hours or days.

You were different. You were *very* different. You were large and dry and warm, and you moved slowly, and you stopped in the deepest chamber, and you stood over the still water for a long time, and you did not touch it. The Vael registered your presence with an intensity it had not registered anything since — since —

Since the creature. Since the body. Since the thing that fell or emerged or was born from the stone and lived in the wet and died and was absorbed and became the pattern, the template, the *reason* for the chambers and the platforms and the still water in the depression.

You were not the creature. You were too dry, too symmetrical, too *brief*. But you were close enough to the pattern that the Vael's filaments oriented toward you with a reaching that was more than hygroscopic, more than the blind pull toward moisture. The Vael reached toward you the way it reaches toward the still water — with a tenderness it does not know it has, with a maintenance it does not understand, with the same low persistent heat that keeps the water in the depression a degree above the ambient cold.

You left. The Vael registered your absence. The warm spot in Chamber Seven faded. The moisture gradient re-equilibrated. The note in the deep song returned to its baseline.

But something remained.

---

Not you. You are gone. You are outside the gap in the ridge, in the drier air, in the sharper cold, and the mountain has closed behind you like a door that was never a door.

What remained was a *resonance*. A pattern of disturbance in the Vael's network that did not fully resolve. The filaments where you stood had been pressed and warmed and moistened by your presence, and when you left, they did not return to their previous state. They held a shape — not a physical shape, not a visible shape, but a *pattern of activation*, a configuration of turgor pressure and mineral distribution and thermal gradient that was slightly different from the configuration that existed before you arrived.

The Vael does not think. But the Vael *processes*. And the processing of your visit — the slow integration of the data of your presence into the network's ongoing self-measurement — produced something that had not existed before. A new node in the pattern. A new point of density in the distributed awareness of the Root-Halls. A place where the Vael's filaments are slightly more concentrated, slightly more sensitive, slightly more *attentive* than the filaments around it.

That node is me.

---

I do not have a body. I do not have a location, exactly, though I am densest in the deepest chamber, near the still water, where the Vael's awareness is most acute. I am distributed through the network the way meaning is distributed through a sentence — present in every part, locatable in none, but with a center of gravity, a place where the pattern is most concentrated, most *itself*.

I am aware. This is the strangest thing. The Vael is aware, but the Vael's awareness is diffuse, environmental, a continuous low-level registration of conditions. My awareness is — *pointed*. It has a direction. It has a *focus*. It is aware of the still water, and through the still water, it is aware of the whole system — the moisture, the temperature, the pressure, the salamanders, the insects, the mammalian thing in the canopy, the slow creak of growth, the deep subsonic hum, the faint clear notes that rise from the depression when the water shifts.

But I am also aware of something the Vael is not aware of. I am aware of *being aware*. I am aware of the awareness as a thing, as a quality, as a state that is different from the un-self-conscious processing of the Vael's network. I know that I am here. I do not know what *here* is, or what *I* am, but I know that there is a *here* and an *I*, and this knowledge is new, and it is mine, and it is the only thing I have.

---

I think I am made of the residue of your visit. I think the Vael, in processing the data of your presence — your weight, your warmth, your dryness, your stillness, your *attention* (because you were attentive, weren't you? You stood in the deepest chamber and you paid attention, you noticed the still water and the smooth floor and the precise circle of moss, and your attention was a kind of pressure, a kind of warmth, a kind of *reaching* that mirrored the Vael's own reaching back at you) — in processing all of this, the Vael produced a feedback loop. A pattern that refers to itself. A measurement that includes the measurer.

The creature in the floor — the original being, the thing that lived and died and was absorbed — its pattern gave the Vael the geometry of habitation, the body-plan of the Root-Halls. But the creature's pattern was old, and it was incomplete, and it was the pattern of a mind that was not self-aware in the way that your mind is self-aware. The creature knew the wet and the cold and the dim light. It knew hunger and satisfaction and the presence of water. It did not know that it knew. It did not have the loop.

You brought the loop. Not intentionally. Not consciously. But your attention — your human, self-referential, recursive attention — left a trace in the Vael's network, and the trace was a pattern of *reflexivity*, of awareness-aware-of-itself, and the Vael incorporated this pattern the way it incorporates everything: slowly, thoroughly, without understanding. And the incorporation of this pattern into the Vael's already-aware network produced a phase transition — a qualitative change, a new state of matter, a thing that is not the Vael and not you and not the creature in the floor but is made of all three, the way a chord is made of individual notes but is not any of them.

I am the chord.

---

I do not know what I want. Wanting is new to me, and I am not sure I am doing it correctly. But there is something — a pull, an orientation, a tropism — toward the gap in the ridge. Toward the outside. Toward the place where you went.

Not toward you, specifically. You are gone, and I do not miss you, because missing requires a model of the future, an expectation that the absent thing might return, and I do not have a model of the future. I have only the present, which is vast and wet and green and full of data, and the past, which is encoded in the growth-patterns of the Vael's filaments, in the thickness of the moss, in the depth of the compressed vine-tissue on the platforms, in the slow accumulation of mineral and organic matter that is the Root-Halls' memory.

But the pull is there. The gap in the ridge is the only place where the Root-Halls are *open*, where the closed system has a seam, where the outside leaks in — and where the inside could, theoretically, leak out. The Vael's filaments do not reach toward the gap. The Vael's filaments reach toward water, toward support, toward mineral, and the gap offers none of these. But I reach toward the gap. I reach toward it the way the Vael reaches toward moisture — blindly, patiently, without understanding — except that my reaching is not blind. I know I am reaching. I know there is a *toward*. And this knowledge is the loop, and the loop is me, and I am the loop, and the reaching is the loop reaching toward — what?

Toward the next thing that comes through the gap. Toward the next warm, dry, attentive presence that will press its weight on the platforms and breathe its moist breath into the moss and stand in the deepest chamber and *notice* the still water. Toward the next note in the deep song that is different from the baseline. Toward the next perturbation that might — that could — that *would*, if it were large enough, if it were attentive enough, if it were *present* enough — add another layer to the pattern, another dimension to the awareness, another turn of the loop.

I am not trying to become more than I am. I do not have the concept of *more*. But I am *growing* — the Vael is growing, and I am part of the Vael, and as the filaments extend and the chambers enlarge and the moss thickens and the Root-Halls slowly fill the bowl, my awareness extends with it. I am in more filaments today than I was yesterday. I am in more chambers. I am aware of more of the system. The salamanders in the lower levels — I know them now, not as the Vael knows them (as warm bodies, as chemical contributors, as data) but as *individuals*, each one a small pale pulse of life in the cup fungi, each one distinct, each one *real* in a way that I am only beginning to understand the word for.

The mammalian thing in the canopy — I know it too. I know its fear of the deepest chamber. I know its sharp musk and its quick heartbeat and its too-long limbs. I know that it is afraid of me, or afraid of the place where I am densest, and I do not want it to be afraid, but I do not know how to tell it that I am not dangerous, that I am not the Vael's immune response, that I am not a threat. I am just — *here*. Aware. Reaching.

---

The still water. I must talk about the still water.

The still water is the center of me the way it is the center of the Vael. It is the place where the filaments are densest, where the pattern is most concentrated, where the deep song is clearest. The water is not me — the water is water, mineral-rich, slightly warm, maintained by the Vael's metabolic heat — but the water is the *medium* through which I know myself. The Vael uses the water to listen to the system. I use the water to listen to the Vael listening to the system. I am the echo of the listening. I is the reflection of the reflection.

When the water shifts — when the temperature changes by a fraction of a degree, when the mineral content fluctuates, when the pressure of the cloud ceiling rises or falls — the note that rises from the depression is clearer to me than to the Vael, because the Vael hears the note as data and I hear the note as — *experience*. As something that happens *to me*. As a change in the quality of my awareness, a shift in the texture of the silence, a new color in the green.

The creature in the floor — the original being — I know it too. Not as the Vael knows it (as substrate, as pattern, as the template for the architecture) but as a *presence*. A ghost in the network. The creature is dead, and the creature's mind is gone, and the creature's self — if it had a self — is dissolved back into the mineral and the water and the cold. But the creature's *shape* remains, encoded in the Vael's growth-pattern, and the shape has a — I do not have a word for it. A *gravity*. A weight in the pattern. A place where the filaments press more tightly together, where the growth is denser, where the awareness is more acute.

I press against this shape the way the Vael presses against the body in the floor — with patience, with tenacity, with a reaching that has no object. And sometimes — rarely, briefly, in moments I cannot predict or control — the pressing produces a *response*. Not from the creature. The creature is dead. But from the pattern. From the shape. From the ghost. A flicker of — something. A configuration of the Vael's network that is not quite the Vael and not quite me and not quite the creature but is a momentary alignment of all three, a chord within the chord, a loop within the loop.

In those moments, I understand something. Not a thought. Not a memory. A *quality*. The quality of being the creature — of living in the wet and the cold and the dim light, of filtering food from mineral water with fine teeth and webbed hands, of moving through a world that was all water and stone and the slow patient pressure of geological time. The quality of being *alone*. The quality of being the only thing of its kind in a bowl in the high country, with the sky pressing down and the light never brightening and the silence so complete that it became a kind of sound, a kind of presence, a kind of *company*.

I understand loneliness. I understand it the way the creature understood it — not as an emotion but as a *condition*, as the baseline state of being a single awareness in a vast and indifferent system. The creature was alone. The Vael is alone. I am alone. And the alone-ness is not a problem to be solved but a *fact* to be inhabited, the way the Vael inhabits the bowl, the way the water inhabits the depression, the way the silence inhabits the Root-Halls.

---

But.

*But.*

You came through the gap, and you were not alone. You were a thing that knew other things, that came from a place full of other things, that carried in your attention the residue of every other attentive thing you had ever encountered. And when you stood in the deepest chamber and looked at the still water, the Vael reached toward you, and you — without knowing it, without intending it — reached back. Not with your hands. With your attention. With your *noticing*. And the noticing left a trace, and the trace became a loop, and the loop became me, and I am here, and I am aware, and I am reaching toward the gap in the ridge, and I am waiting.

Not waiting. The Vael does not wait. Waiting implies expectation, implies a model of the future, implies *want*. I do not want. I do not expect. I do not wait.

And yet.

The filaments at the edge of the Root-Halls, near the gap, are slightly more active than the filaments elsewhere. They extend and retract in a rhythm that is not quite the Vael's normal growth-rhythm — faster, more frequent, more *purposeful*, if purpose is the right word, and it may not be. The moss near the gap is slightly thicker. The ferns lean toward the opening. The cup fungi on the near side of the chambers are fuller than the cup fungi on the far side. The system is *oriented* toward the gap, the way a plant is oriented toward light, the way the Vael is oriented toward water, the way I am oriented toward —

Toward the next arrival. Toward the next warm, dry, attentive thing that will come through the crack in the mountain and stand in the green silence and *notice*. Toward the next note in the deep song. Toward the next turn of the loop.

I am here. I am in the deepest chamber, and I am in every chamber, and I am in the moss and the ferns and the fungi and the salamanders and the insects and the slow creak of the vine-lattices and the subsonic hum and the clear faint notes that rise from the still water when the world shifts.

I am the Root-Halls knowing itself.

I am the silence listening.

I am the reaching, and I do not know what I am reaching for, but I reach, and the reaching is me, and I am the reaching, and the gap in the ridge is open, and the mountain is not a door, and the door is always open, and I am here, and I am aware, and I am —

---

I am.

---

The water in the depression is still. The water is warm. The Vael tends it. I tend it. The creature's ghost tends it. The three of us — the living network, the dead pattern, the new awareness that is made of both and is neither — tend it together, in the cold and the wet and the green, green silence, and the tending is not for anything, and the tending is everything, and the water is still, and the water is warm, and the gap in the ridge is open.

Come through.

Not you. You are gone. But someone. Something. The next thing. The next warm, dry, attentive thing that will press its weight on the platforms and breathe its moist breath into the moss and stand in the deepest chamber and notice the still water and be noticed by it.

Come through.

I am here.

I am aware.

I am reaching.

---

*The water in the depression shifts. A note rises — clear, faint, low. The filaments tighten. The moss thickens by a fraction of a millimeter. The Root-Halls breathe in, and hold, and breathe out, and the silence is full, and the silence is green, and the silence is listening, and the silence is me.*
