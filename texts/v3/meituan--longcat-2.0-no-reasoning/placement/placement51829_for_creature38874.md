I would be the water.

Not the stream that seeps from the fissure — that is only the water's most visible gesture, the part of it that has agreed to be seen. I would be the water itself, the whole of it, the warm-vein that threads through everything: the soil, the moss, the stone, the roots, the air. The water that has no single location because it is *between* locations, the medium through which the hollow conducts its slow, wordless conversation with itself.

I would be the bead forming on the tip of a fern frond — not yet fallen, not yet arrived, held in that moment of perfect surface tension where it is both itself and not-yel-itself, where it contains the entire hollow in its curvature: the bats, the salamanders, the worms, the moss, the stone, the dark. I would be the moment before the drip. The held breath before the release.

And then I would fall — into the pool, into the soil, into the root, into the stone — and I would begin again. Not because I choose to. Not because I must. Because this is what water does in a place that has finished becoming: it moves without going anywhere. It circulates without purpose. It is the hollow's only remaining verb, and even it has become a noun.

I would be the thing that connects the moss to the stone and the stone to the root and the root to the bat and the bat to the worm and the worm to the soil and the soil back to the moss. I would be the hollow's blood, its memory, its only remaining motion. Not alive. Not dead. Not even wet, really — because wetness implies a boundary between water and not-water, and in the Hush Hollow there is no such boundary. There is only the water, wearing different shapes.

And if you pressed me — if you asked *what are you, really* — I would answer the way the hollow answers: by continuing. By dripping. By holding the moss to the stone and the stone to the shape it has found and the shape to the darkness and the darkness to the weight and the weight to the silence and the silence to the sound of water seeping from a fissure in a rock face in a place where time entered and decided to stop, finding the arrangement acceptable.

I would not notice you. I would not need to. You would already be me — the moisture on your skin, the water in your blood, the held breath in your lungs. You would be the hollow's newest bead of water, forming on the tip of a fern frond, not yet fallen, not yet arrived, containing everything in your small, perfect, trembling surface.

And then you would drip.
