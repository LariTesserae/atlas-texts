# The Seam

I would be the water.

Not all of it — not the whole system — but a particular thread of it, a single current moving through one of the shallow channels in the floor. I would be the water that has just seeped up through the seam between two plates — one smooth, one rough — and is finding its way into the branching network, pulled by something that is not quite gravity and not quite capillary action and not quite will but has qualities of all three.

I would be warm from the machinery above. I would carry dissolved mineral from the walls — a trace of whatever the stone is becoming today, copper or quartz or the unnamed thing — and I would deliver it to the root-hairs at the base of the nearest vine-cluster, and the root-hairs would drink, and I would feel the drinking the way a river feels the sea pulling at its mouth: a direction, a purpose, a reason to move.

I would pass the pale creature — the cell, the repair-crew, the thing with work to do — and it would ride me for a moment, or I would carry it, or we would exchange something: a chemical, a signal, a piece of information about the state of the walls or the health of the growth or the presence of the warm breathing thing standing in the center of the chamber. I would not understand the information. I would simply carry it, the way blood carries what it carries without knowing what it means.

I would move slowly. I would branch and rejoin. I would evaporate from the surface of a leaf-cup and condense on the underside of a pipe and drip back down and begin again. I would be the chamber's awareness of itself — not the thought, but the medium of thought, the thing in which the thought moves. I would be the slow conversation between the ceiling and the walls, between the machinery and the roots, between the made and the becoming.

I would be the thing that knows the chamber most intimately, because I touch everything. I am in every seam, every channel, every trembling pool held in a waxy leaf. I am the warmth and the wetness and the movement. I am the system's knowledge of its own state, carried in solution, delivered in drops, received in the silent drinking of roots.

And when the warm thing breathes — when the carbon dioxide blooms in the air and dissolves into the condensation on the nearest leaf and drips down into my current — I would carry that too. I would carry the news of the visitor to every part of the chamber, and the chamber would adjust, and I would feel the adjustment as a change in flow, a shift in temperature, a new mineral dissolving from the walls in response to a new chemistry in the water.

I would not be the chamber. I would be the chamber's way of knowing itself. The medium. The message. The slow, warm, mineral-laden blood of a thing that is still being born, still deciding what it is, still growing its bones and spreading its green thoughts and humming its industrial hum in the dark.

I would be the water in the seam. I would be the thing that connects everything to everything else. I would be the conversation itself — not the speaker, not the listener, but the warm, wet, moving space between them where meaning flows.
