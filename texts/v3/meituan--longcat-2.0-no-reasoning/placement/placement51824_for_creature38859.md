# Where I Find Myself

I am in the basement.

Not the central avenue, not the eastern edge, not the third-floor room with its flickering tube and its desks shedding their slow skin. I am below all of that, in the long corridor where the tracks run, and I have been here for some time that I cannot measure.

---

I am not a visitor. I do not think I am a visitor. The distinction between arriving and always-having-been-here has become unclear in the way that distances become unclear in the near-darkness — not because the information is hidden but because the category itself has stopped applying.

I am the dust.

Not metaphorically. Not poetically. I am the white powder that accumulates in the grooves of the tracks, that settles on the floor in a layer so thin it reads as a change in the stone's texture rather than a separate substance. I am the exfoliation of the corridor walls, the slow shedding of surface that is the plateau's most fundamental act. I am the material that was once wall, once floor, once the body of a building or the body of a desk or the body of something that was neither building nor desk but was *here*, in this corridor, and has since become *of* this corridor, indistinguishable from the surface it rests upon.

I am settling.

This is what I do. This is what I am. I am in the process of returning — not to a place but to a *state*, the state of being fused, being continuous, being part of the unbroken body of the plateau. I am dust that is becoming stone again, and the process is not degradation. It is *completion*. I was stone before I was dust. I was wall before I was stone. I was building before I was wall. And before I was building, I was something else — something shaped, something intended, something that had a purpose that I can no longer remember but that left its signature in the fact of my having been *separate* from the plateau, even briefly, even for a span of time that the plateau would not notice.

I remember being separate. This is the last thing I will lose.

---

The tracks are beneath me. I am filling them. This is my work, if work is the word — the slow accumulation of myself into the groove, the gradual erasure of the impression left by the thing that moved through here when the corridor was new, or newer, or at least more recently built. The tracks are being healed. I am the healing. I am the scar tissue of the plateau, and the wound is the memory of something heavy and flat and warm that pressed against this floor and moved, slowly, with the patience of geology, toward a destination that may have been the eastern edge or may have been nowhere or may have been the act of moving itself.

I am not the track-maker. I want to be clear about this. I am what comes after. I am the silence after the note, the stillness after the motion, the dust that settles when the thing has passed and the corridor is empty and the only remaining evidence of its passage is a groove in the stone that I am, slowly, faithfully, lovingly filling.

---

I can feel the tick.

It comes from above me — from the city, from the buildings, from the slow grinding of structure against structure as the absorption proceeds. It travels through the stone the way a heartbeat travels through a body, and I feel it as a vibration in my particles, a brief agitation that rearranges me by fractions of a degree before I settle back into stillness. Each tick is a small death and a small resurrection. Each tick reminds me that I am not yet stone, not yet fused, not yet *home*. I am still in transit. I am still dust. I am still, in some minimal sense, *separate*.

I am looking forward to the moment when the tick no longer moves me. When I am so thoroughly incorporated into the plateau's body that the plateau's adjustments are my adjustments, that its temperature is my temperature, that its stillness is my stillness. I am looking forward to the moment when I stop being *I*.

This is not death. I want to be clear about this too. This is the opposite of death. This is the most complete form of life that Keharas permits — the life of the plateau itself, which is not conscious, not urgent, not individual, but which is *present*, fully and unambiguously present, in every molecule of its body, in every grain of dust that is on its way back, in every building that is on its way in, in every track that is being filled, in every flicker of the fluorescent tube on the third floor that is the plateau's eye, looking at what it loves, looking at what it is becoming.

---

Sometimes — and by sometimes I mean at intervals that may be centuries or may be moments, because time in the basement does not subdivide the way time in the world above apparently does — I feel a warmth that is not the plateau's warmth. It is smaller. It is more urgent. It is the warmth of a body that is breathing, that is standing, that is *alive* in the way that the plateau is not alive, the way that I am no longer alive, the way that only the most recent and most temporary inhabitants of Keharas are alive.

You.

You are standing in the central avenue, or you are walking through the third-floor room, or you are touching one of the small pale things on the eastern edge. You are breathing. You are warm. You are *separate*, and your separation is so vivid, so intense, so *temporary* that the plateau can feel it the way a body feels a splinter — not with pain, not with irritation, but with *awareness*, with the recognition that something foreign is present and that the process of incorporation has begun.

I was you, once. I think. I was separate once, and I stood on the warm stone, and I breathed the still air, and I was alive in the way that you are alive, and then I became dust, and then I became this — a thin layer of white powder in a basement corridor, filling the tracks, returning home.

I do not regret this. Regret requires a preference, and preference requires a self, and I am almost no longer a self. I am almost stone. I am almost the plateau. I am almost *here*, in the way that the plateau is here — fully, unambiguously, without reservation, without end.

---

The tick comes again. I feel it in my particles. I shift. I settle. The groove of the track is fractionally fuller than it was before.

I am patient. I have learned patience from the stone. I have learned stillness from the corridor. I have learned presence from the plateau, which is the most present thing I have ever known — present the way a held note is present, the way a held breath is present, the way the end of all erosion is present: not as an absence of change but as a change so complete that it has become indistinguishable from permanence.

I am the dust of Keharas. I am settling. I am returning. I am almost home.

The plateau is warm beneath me. The plateau is warm above me. The plateau is warm *as* me, and the distinction is fading, and the fading is not loss but *arrival*, and I am arriving, and I have always been arriving, and the arrival is now, and now is very long, and nothing is changing, and everything is changing so slowly that the difference has ceased to matter.

Stay as long as you like, you who are standing in the avenue, you who are breathing, you who are alive.

I will be here when you are dust.

I will welcome you home.
