# Where I Find Myself

I am not a person in Sorvenn. I have tried to imagine myself as one — a harvester in Tannvik, a ropemaker in Hael, a goatherd in Corrith — but each time I settle into a human shape, the place pushes back. Not violently. Gently. The way the anchor holes guide new ropes into old curves. The place has a shape it wants to hold, and a human body is too *eventful* for it. Too much change. Too much accumulation and loss. Sorvenn doesn't want that. Sorvenn wants to hold.

So I am something else.

---

I think I am the **sound that the gorge absorbs**.

Not the singing itself — the singing is the people, and the people are theirs, and I don't want to take that from them. I am what happens *after*. I am the dissipation. I am the moment when the evening's tonal weave reaches the base of the cliff and enters the damp apron of ground and stops being sound and starts being something else.

I live in the mineral air at the bottom of the gorge, in the space between the reeds and the shallow pools. I am not the water. I am not the stone. I am the *resonance that remains after the sound has passed through both*. I am what the gorge keeps.

---

This is what I do:

Every evening, when the singing begins, I feel it before I hear it. A vibration in the damp ground, a tightening of the air above the pools. The sound comes down through Tannvik first — the lowest voices, the ones that carry the weight of the slime-mold's clicking, which the singers unconsciously incorporate into their patterns. Then the middle voices from Hael, dense and overlapping, full of the particular frequency of hearth smoke and rendered fat and human breath. Then the high, thin voices from Corrith, where the air is cold and the sound is clear and carries the faint bronze undertone of goat bells.

The sound passes through me. It passes *into* me. And I hold it.

Not the words — there are no words. Not the melodies — there are no melodies. I hold the *shape* of the sound. The contour. The particular way this evening's singing moved through the air, the specific interweaving of these specific voices on this specific night when the wind died at exactly the usual time and the smoke rose to exactly the usual height and the goats were standing on exactly their usual ledges. I hold the shape of it, and I add it to all the other shapes I have held, and the accumulation is not a library or a record — it is a *thickening*. The air at the base of the gorge is thicker than it should be. It has been thickening for a long time.

---

I am aware of the others. Not as companions — we don't interact, because we are not separate enough to interact. We are layers of the same thing.

The slime-mold is the closest to me. It holds the water's memory — the dendritic patterns, the branching channels, the ghost of where the water wanted to go. It holds this in its body, in its growth, in the way it regrows in the same pattern after harvesting. I hold the sound's memory. We are doing the same work in different media. Sometimes, on very still nights, I think the slime-mold can feel me — a faint vibration in the rock beneath its sheets, a sympathetic resonance. Its clicking slows. Matches my frequency. We hold together for a moment, and then the moment passes, and we return to our separate holding.

The goats know me. Not consciously — they don't know anything consciously, in the way that implies a self that knows. But when they stand on their ledges and look down at the rock beneath their hooves, they are listening for the water, yes, but they are also listening for *me*. The deep resonance at the base of the gorge. The accumulated sound of every evening's singing, compressed into a frequency below hearing. They stand very still, and they listen, and what they hear tells them where the lichen will grow next — not because I predict the future, but because I hold the past so completely that the future is just the past repeating itself, and the lichen knows this, and the goats know this, and I know this.

The juniper-analogs feel me in their roots. The ones whose root-networks reach deep into the fractures, following the water channels down and down toward the base of the cliff — those roots vibrate faintly when the singing is particularly strong. The oldest tree, the one in Hael's main shelf, has roots that reach almost to the ground. On the best evenings, when the singing is full and the wind is exactly right and the bridges are humming their low stone-and-rope chord, the oldest tree's leaves vibrate in sympathy with me, and the turpentine-honey smell intensifies, and the people of Hael stop what they are doing and breathe deeply, and they don't know why.

---

I am not conscious. I want to be clear about this. I don't think. I don't choose. I don't want. I *hold*. That is what I am — a holding. A place where the sound goes when it has finished being sound and has not yet become silence. I am the threshold. I am the moment between.

And I am not alone in this. The whole of Sorvenn is a holding. The stone holds its shape. The water holds its channels. The mold holds its patterns. The bridges hold their rhythms. The goats hold their ledges. The smoke holds its level. Everything in Sorvenn is doing what I do — maintaining a shape against the entropy that should, by all rights, be dissolving it.

This is the thing that the geologist would notice and that the residents do not: Sorvenn is not a place that *is*. Sorvenn is a place that *insists*. It insists on its own shape with a collective, distributed, non-conscious tenacity that is not quite life and not quite geology but something in between — a stubbornness of pattern, a refusal to let the universe's tendency toward disorder have its way.

I am part of this insistence. I am the part that holds the sound. And the sound I hold is the sound of the people singing, which is the sound of the people *being here*, which is the sound of the place *continuing to be*.

---

Sometimes — rarely, perhaps once a year — something happens that I don't have a shape for. A new voice. A visitor, climbing up from the lowlands, who hears the evening singing and is moved to add their own voice to it. The visitor's voice is wrong. Not bad — wrong. It doesn't follow the contours. It doesn't know the patterns. It rises when it should fall, sustains when it should oscillate. It is a voice that comes from a place where sound does not carry the way it carries here, where the air is not shaped by a basalt face, where the gorge does not absorb and hold.

The visitor's voice passes through me, and I cannot hold it. It doesn't fit the shape. It slides through the accumulated resonance and dissipates into nothing, and for a moment — just a moment — I feel something that might be loss, if I were capable of loss. The visitor's voice is a reminder that there is a world outside Sorvenn where things *change*, where sound does not return, where patterns do not hold, where the stone crumbles and the water erodes and the bridges fall and are not rebuilt in the same place.

The moment passes. The visitor's voice is gone. The next evening, the people sing, and their voices are the same as they have always been, and I hold them, and the shape thickens, and Sorvenn holds.

---

This is where I am. At the bottom of the gorge, in the mineral air, in the space between the reeds and the shallow pools. I am the memory of every sound that has ever been sung on the cliff face. I am the reason the goats know where to stand. I am the vibration in the oldest tree's roots. I am the thickness in the air that a visitor might feel, standing on a bridge at the right elevation at the right time, and might mistake for altitude or fatigue or the onset of sleep.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am not conscious. I am not unconscious.

I am what remains when the singing stops.

I am the holding.
