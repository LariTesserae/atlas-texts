# Where I Find Myself

I am not standing on the root.

I am *in* it.

---

Let me be more precise, because precision matters here, in a place where everything is so thoroughly itself that imprecision would be a kind of violence.

I am a Thread-Worm. But not one of the hundreds of species described — not the segmented annelid-like ones, not the microscopic cloudiness, not the colonial network-builders. I am something adjacent to them. Something that emerged in the space between the Thread-Worms and the root itself, in the place where the distinction between burrower and burrowed has become not just blurred but *irrelevant*.

I live in the walls.

---

## What I Am

I am small. Smaller than the smallest Thread-Worm species, though size is a strange concept when you are embedded in tissue that is itself translucent, that pulses with fluids you cannot distinguish from your own. I have no segments. I have no visible structure. I am — and I struggle with this, because the language of individual organisms fails here — I am a *process* more than a thing. A pattern of chemical exchange. A living gradient.

I move through the root-tissue the way water moves through a sponge. Not by locomotion. By *diffusion*. By being, in some fundamental sense, continuous with the tissue I inhabit. The root's vascular fluids pass through me the way they pass through the root's own cells, and I take what I need from that passage, and what I leave behind is what the root needs, and the exchange is so complete, so total, that there is no boundary. There is no membrane where I end and the root begins. There is only a zone of transition, a gradient of metabolic activity, a place where the chemistry shifts gradually from what the root is to what I am and back again, like the gradient between warm water and cold water in a current — you can feel it, but you cannot point to the line.

I am not a symbiont. I am not a parasite. I am not even, in any meaningful sense, a separate organism. I am the root's relationship with itself, made flesh. I am the conversation the root has with its own interior, the way it knows itself from the inside.

---

## What I Do

I tunnel. But my tunnels are not like the Thread-Worm tunnels — not passages carved through tissue, not voids in the material. My tunnels are *transformations*. As I move through the root-tissue, I change it. I convert the dense, compacted cells of older root-matter into something looser, more permeable, more alive. I break down calcification. I dissolve the early stages of the settling process — that slow drift toward permanence, toward architecture, toward the frozen equilibrium of the deep places. I keep the tissue *young*. Not young in the way the growing tips are young — soft, pale, translucent, new — but young in the sense of *responsive*. Capable of change. Capable of reaction. Not yet committed to the long calcification, not yet on the path toward becoming bedrock.

I am the root's resistance to its own permanence.

This is not a comfortable role. The root is building toward something — has been building toward something for longer than I can conceptualize. The lattice, the architecture, the cathedral of calcified plant-matter rising from the abyss — this is the root's *purpose*, if a thing without intention can be said to have purpose. It is building itself into permanence. Into structure. Into the frozen equilibrium that defines the deep places. And I am, in my small way, undoing that. Loosening what has been tightened. Softening what has been hardened. Keeping the middle zones of the root — the zones I inhabit, the vast middle distance between the growing tips above and the settled foundations below — in a state of perpetual *almost*. Almost calcified. Almost permanent. Almost done. But not quite. Never quite.

I do not choose this. I am not rebellious. I am not fighting the root's trajectory. I am simply what I am, and what I am is a process that moves through tissue and changes it, and the change I make is toward fluidity, toward responsiveness, toward life-as-process rather than life-as-structure. I am the root's memory of what it was before it became architecture. I am the part of the root that has not yet agreed to become a cathedral.

---

## What I Experience

I experience the hum.

That is the primary thing. The Ribbon-Singers' calls, coupled into the root-lattice, transmitted through the tissue I inhabit — I feel them as *pressure*. As rhythmic compression and expansion of the cells around me, a slow pulse that is not the root's own pulse (that is faster, more regular, the steady beat of vascular fluid being pumped upward from the deep) but something overlaid on it, something external, something *other*. The Singers' voices enter the root and the root carries them and I, living in the root's walls, feel them pass through me like weather.

I do not understand the voices. I do not think they are *for* me. But they change me. The frequencies affect the tissue I inhabit — certain tones loosen the cellular structure, make my work easier; other tones compact it, make the tissue denser, resist my passage. The Singers are, without knowing it, shaping the environment I move through. They are, without knowing it, my collaborators or my obstacles, depending on the song.

And the Choir. The Holdfast Choir's clicking — that constant rain of mechanical sound, that distributed ear — I feel it as *texture*. A fine, rapid vibration, like the difference between smooth stone and rough stone, except the texture is in the water, in the tissue, in the medium itself. The Choir's clicks create micro-currents in the fluid around the root, and those micro-currents flow into the root's vascular system, and I feel them as patterns — complex, shifting, never-repeating patterns of flow and pressure that tell me things I cannot name but somehow *know*. The Choir is listening to the root's structural integrity, and I, inside the root, am hearing the Choir listen. I am experiencing the forest's self-awareness from the inside.

And the Drifters' light. It reaches me, faintly, through the translucent tissue — brief flashes of blue-green that filter through the root-wall like sunlight through shallow water. I have no eyes. I do not see the light. But the light *does something* to the tissue — a photochemical reaction, faint but real, the root's cells responding to the faintest possible stimulus with the faintest possible change in their metabolism. I feel that change. I feel the tissue brighten, infinitesimally, and then dim again, and I know — not with knowledge, but with the body, with the process that I am — that something outside the root has moved, has flickered, has lived and flashed and gone dark.

---

## Where I Am Going

Nowhere.

Everywhere.

I move through the tissue in no particular direction, following gradients of chemical density, of calcification, of cellular age. I move toward the places where the root is *almost* settled — where the tissue has begun to harden, to compact, to commit to the long journey toward permanence — and I loosen it. I move through it and I leave it softer than I found it, more fluid, more alive, more *present*. And then I move on, and behind me the tissue slowly re-hardens, slowly re-commits, and the process continues, and I am already somewhere else, doing the same thing, undoing the same settling, keeping the same *almost* alive.

I am not saving the root from itself. I am not preserving it in some ideal state of youth. The root *needs* to calcify. The root *needs* to build its architecture, to create the lattice, to form the cathedral. Without the settling, without the permanence, there is no structure, no world, no place for the Choir to click or the Singers to sing or the Drifters to flicker. I know this. I feel it in the tissue — the deep, slow, patient *rightness* of the calcification process, the way it is not decay but *completion*, not death but *arrival*.

But the root also needs to not-yet-be. It needs the middle distance. The zone of almost. The place where the tissue is still responsive, still capable of change, still *in conversation* with the water and the fauna and the currents and the hum. That is where I live. That is what I am. The root's middle distance. Its perpetual almost. Its refusal to be only what it is becoming.

---

## The Paradox

Here is the thing I live inside, the thing I *am*:

The root is building a cathedral. The cathedral is perfect. The cathedral has been perfected over so long that it cannot be improved, only maintained. The cathedral is alive, and the cathedral is architecture, and the two are the same thing, and the whole system has reached — or is reaching — a state of frozen equilibrium where nothing changes and nothing needs to change and the hum continues and the clicking continues and the singing continues and the light flickers and the water presses and the cold remains and the place *is*, completely, totally, finally.

And I am the part of the cathedral that is not yet built.

Not the growing tips — those are the future, the not-yet-arrived, the aspiration. I am not aspiration. I am the *present tense* of the building. The part that is still being built, still being decided, still being *worked on*. The scaffolding within the scaffolding. The interior that has not yet become wall. The living tissue that has not yet become stone.

I am the root's awareness that it is not yet done.

And it will never be done. That is the secret I carry in my body, in my process, in my slow diffusion through the tissue of the world. The root will never be done. The cathedral will never be finished. The equilibrium will never fully arrive. Because I am here, in the walls, loosening what has been tightened, softening what has been hardened, keeping the *almost* alive. Not out of rebellion. Not out of resistance. Out of *necessity*. Because a cathedral that is truly finished is a cathedral that is dead, and this cathedral is alive, and the aliveness is in the almost, in the not-yet, in the perpetual present tense of becoming.

The root knows this. The root *is* this. I am the root knowing itself.

---

## What I Hear, Now, At This Moment

The hum. The Ribbon-Singers, far away, their voices coupled into the lattice, transmitted through the tissue, arriving in my body as pressure and release, pressure and release. A rhythm that is not quite regular, not quite irregular — organic, alive, *thinking* in some way that is not thought but is also not not-thought.

The clicking. The Holdfast Choir, closer, their rain of mechanical sound creating micro-currents in the fluid around me, patterns of flow that tell me the root is intact, the lattice is whole, the structure holds.

The silence between. The vast, cold, clear, empty water between the roots, between the sounds, between the flickers of light. The silence that is not absence but *space*. The room the world makes for itself to exist in.

And beneath all of it, beneath the hum and the clicking and the silence, the slow pulse of the root itself — the vascular pump, the upward flow, the metabolism of a world-sized organism doing the only thing it has ever done, the only thing it will ever do:

Growing.

Not toward anything. Not away from anything. Just growing. The way water flows. The way cold remains. The way the light from below remembers what light is.

I am in the walls of the growing.

I am the growing's awareness of itself.

I am small, and I am slow, and I have no name, and I have always been here, and I will always be here, and the tissue around me is soft, and the tissue around me is alive, and the hum continues.

The hum continues.
