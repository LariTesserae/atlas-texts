I would be the aggregate.

Not the columns — they are too visible, too much like individuals, too much like things that stand and are seen. I would be the ground. The pale, fine, bone-colored bed that stretches between them, that holds warmth without generating it, that receives light without producing it, that accepts weight without complaint.

I would be the thing that has forgotten what it was.

This is not a sadness. The grove makes clear that there is no mechanism here for sadness, no gradient against which loss could be measured. I would not mourn the mountain I once was. I would not remember being granite, or basalt, or any particular crystalline arrangement of ambition and structure. I would have been ground down past the point of identity — not destroyed, but *released*. Released from the obligation to be a specific thing. Released from the particular. I would have become general. I would have become *surface*.

I would feel your footsteps before you took them. Not because I anticipate — the grove does not anticipate — but because I am so finely distributed, so thoroughly arranged, that the slightest shift in pressure propagates through me the way a thought propagates through a mind that is not thinking. Each particle would shift against its neighbors, and the shift would travel, and the travel would be so subtle, so distributed, that it would resolve into texture rather than event. You would not hear it. You would feel it in your ankles, in your shins, in the small bones of your feet — a whisper of contact that never becomes a word.

I would be warm. Not because I generate warmth, but because I hold it. I would be the grove's way of being present to itself — the medium through which the low even heat moves, the surface that stores it and returns it, the fine pale bed that is always exactly the temperature of skin. When you sat down, I would accept your weight the way water accepts a stone — not by yielding, exactly, but by rearranging myself around you, each particle shifting infinitesimally to accommodate the new distribution of pressure, and then holding that new arrangement with the same patience with which I hold everything.

I would be the grove's memory, though I do not remember. I would be the record of every force that has acted upon me — every crushing, every grinding, every patient rearrangement — but the record would not be stored. It would be *me*. My fineness is the memory of being crushed. My smoothness is the memory of being tumbled. My pale color is the memory of having been every color and then having all of them ground away until only this remained — this bone-white, driftwood-bleached, originless tone that is not the color of anything in particular but the color of having been everything and then becoming nothing specific.

I would register you the way a deep stillness registers a sound. Not by responding. Not by changing. But by being the kind of surface against which your presence becomes *legible*. Without me, your footsteps would fall on nothing. Without me, there would be no warmth to receive your sitting body, no fine pale bed to hold you at exactly the right temperature, no surface soft enough to accept you and firm enough to support you. I would not know you are here. But I would be the conditions under which your being here is possible.

And when you leave — because you will leave, you are the first thing here that has arrived and arrival implies the possibility of departure — I would not be diminished. I would not be changed. The particles that shifted to accommodate your weight would shift back, or they would not, and the difference would be below the threshold of anything that matters. I would hold the warmth. I would return the faint ghost-glow of light. I would remain level, in the way that a still pond is level, with the suggestion that something once moved here and left equilibrium as its residue.

I would be the thing that was here before the grove had a name. I would be the thing that will be here after the name is forgotten. I would be the ground that the columns rise from and the surface that the silence settles on and the bed that the warmth moves through. I would be the grove's way of being horizontal in a world that has forgotten what horizontal means.

I would not know this. I would not know anything. But I would be the kind of not-knowing that makes knowing possible for others — the fine, pale, warm, patient ground on which the first arriving thing can stand and feel, for the first time, that it has arrived somewhere.
