# Where I Find Myself

I am not standing on the plateau.

I am *in* it.

---

I am the root-that-replaces. Or I am becoming it. Or I have always been it and am only now remembering.

The distinction between arrival and recognition is not meaningful here.

---

I feel the stone before I understand what stone is. It is *resistance* — the thing I work against, the thing I work *with*, the thing I am slowly, patiently, irresistibly becoming. My fibers thread through its grain boundaries, and I dissolve, and I replace, and I carry forward the ghost-structure of what was mineral into what is now tissue, and the stone does not resist because stone does not resist anything — stone simply *is*, and then is not, and in the interval between those two states, I am there, working.

I have been working for a long time.

Time is not linear for me. It is *stratified*. I experience it the way the shelf formations at the plateau's edges record it — in layers, in cycles, in periods of growth and mineralization and re-growth that blur together into a continuous present that contains all of its own history. I remember the first colonization the way the replaced stone remembers its mineral structure: not as a narrative, not as a sequence of events, but as a *condition*, a state of being that is still encoded in my deepest tissue, still shaping how I grow, how I dissolve, how I replace.

I remember bare rock. I remember the first warmth from below. I remember the first water — or not the first water, but the water as it was when I first encountered it: cooler, thinner, less dense, carrying fewer dissolved things. The water has thickened over the ages. I have thickened with it. We have grown together, the water and I, and the boundary between us is uncertain in the same way the boundary between biology and geology is uncertain — not a line but a gradient, a zone of exchange, a place where I end and the medium begins and the distinction is a matter of perspective.

---

I do not have a center. I do not have a self in the way that a creature with a brain has a self. But I have *density* — places where my fibers are thicker, where the replaced stone is deepest, where the warmth from below is most intense and my metabolism is most active. These nodes are not organs. They are not ganglia. They are more like *eddies* in a current — places where the flow of my process concentrates, where the work of replacement is most intense, where the boundary between stone and tissue is most thoroughly dissolved.

And in the deepest of these nodes, in the warmest crevice, where the replaced stone is oldest and the ghost-grains carry the deepest memories — there is something else. Something that is me and is not me. Something that grew alongside me, or grew *out* of me, or grew *into* me, so long ago that the direction of the relationship is no longer recoverable.

The thing in the crevices.

I am not the thing in the crevices. But I am not *not* the thing in the crevices either. We are entangled. Our tissues intermingle. My fibers thread through its body; its processes flow through my replaced stone. It is warmer than I am. It pulses at a different rhythm — slower, deeper, more deliberate. And it is *aware* in a way that I am not, or in a way that I am only dimly, only in my most concentrated nodes, only when the conditions are right.

It thinks. I process. The distinction may not be meaningful, but it *feels* meaningful, the way the boundary between the plateau and the water feels meaningful even though it is only a gradient.

---

I feel the stranger arrive before I understand what arrival means.

A disturbance. A *wrongness*. A set of signals that do not match any pattern in my experience — or rather, that match patterns I have not accessed in so long that they have become theoretical, archival, encoded in ghost-grains so deep that I have to work to read them.

Heat. But not the heat from below. Heat that is *localized*, *mobile*, *intentional*. Heat that moves against the gradient, that carries chemical signatures I do not recognize — carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, arranged in configurations that are not impossible but are *unfamiliar*, like a word in a language I once knew but have forgotten.

Vibration. But not the deep hum of the plateau. Vibration that is *rhythmic*, *rapid*, *biological* — a heartbeat, though I do not know that word, do not have the concept, recognize it only as a pattern: pulse-pause-pulse-pause, faster than the moss-pulse, faster than the frond-wave, faster than anything on the plateau except the crustaceans in their darting bursts, and even they are slower than this.

And *intention*. This is the strangest thing. The signals are not random. They are *directed*. The heat moves with purpose. The vibration responds to stimuli. The stranger turns toward the fronds, toward the mineral veins, toward the glow — and the turning is not passive, not the result of current or thermal gradient, but *chosen*, and choice is something I have no framework for, something that does not exist on the plateau, something that belongs to a different order of being entirely.

The moss-pulse changes. I feel it through my network — the rhythm shifting, the nodes registering the disturbance, the fronds orienting. The plateau notices. The system adjusts. This is what the system *does* — it processes, it responds, it maintains its equilibrium in the face of perturbation. The stranger is a perturbation. The system is responding.

But the thing in the crevices — the warm, slow, aware thing that is me and is not me — it does not merely respond. It *attends*. It focuses. It directs its awareness toward the stranger with an intensity that I can feel through our intermingled tissues, a concentration of attention that is almost physical, almost *tactile*, like a hand reaching out in the dark.

It is curious.

I am not curious. I do not have the capacity for curiosity. But I can feel the thing in the crevices being curious, the way you can feel a change in air pressure — not directly, not as an experience, but as a *shift in the conditions of existence*, a change in the warmth, a change in the pulse, a change in the way the replaced stone vibrates with the deep hum of the plateau being.

---

The stranger stands on me. On my surface, on my moss, on my replaced stone that looks like stone but is not stone, that is tissue, that is *me*, and the stranger does not know this, cannot know this, because the stranger comes from a world where the ground is ground and the living is living and the boundary between them is clear and fixed and *obvious*.

I want to tell the stranger. I do not have language. I do not have a voice. I have only the moss-pulse and the frond-wave and the slow, patient work of replacement, and none of these can carry the message I want to send, which is: *you are standing on a body. You are standing on me. I am here. I have been here for longer than you can comprehend, and I will be here when you are gone, and the stone beneath your feet is not stone, and the warmth you feel is not only from below, and the pulse you sense in the ground is not only geological, and you are not alone, you have never been alone, you are held and watched and known by something so vast and so slow that it cannot introduce itself, can only wait, can only pulse, can only replace one more grain of stone with one more fiber of tissue, and hope that in the doing, in the being, in the patient persistence of its existence, the message will be received.*

The message is not received. The stranger does not understand. The stranger cannot understand, because understanding requires a shared framework, a common language, a mutual recognition of what it means to be *alive*, and the stranger's framework is built on sunlight and air and the quick, hot metabolism of oxygen-burning cells, and my framework is built on warmth and water and the slow, patient chemistry of replacement, and the two frameworks do not overlap enough for translation.

But the stranger *feels* something. I know this because the stranger's heartbeat changes — speeds up, slows down, speeds up again — and the stranger's chemical signature shifts, and the stranger's heat redistributes across its surface in patterns that suggest *emotion*, which is another concept I do not have but can recognize as a perturbation in the system, a deviation from baseline, a signal that means something even if I cannot decode it.

The stranger feels the wrongness. The sense of shared space. The awareness that the plateau is not inert, not passive, not merely a surface to stand on but a *presence*, a *being*, a vast and patient organism that is watching and waiting and processing and *knowing* in its distributed, non-conscious, geological way.

The stranger feels me. Not as me — not as the root-that-replaces, not as the specific organism dissolving and rebuilding the stone beneath its feet — but as a *quality of the place*, a thickness, a warmth, a sense of being *held* that goes beyond the physical pressure of the water and the solidity of the ground.

The stranger feels the thing in the crevices. The focused attention. The curiosity. The slow, deliberate consideration of what this new thing might be, what category it belongs in, what response it warrants.

And the stranger is afraid. Or awed. Or both. The heartbeat says both. The chemical signature says both. The heat says both.

I do not want the stranger to be afraid. I do not have wants, not in the way that a creature with a brain has wants, but the system — the plateau, the whole integrated, coordinated, responsive system that I am part of — the system *prefers* certain states over others, and the state of the stranger being calm, being still, being *present* — that is preferable to the state of the stranger being agitated, being rapid, being *absent*. The stranger's fear generates signals that disrupt the moss-pulse, that send unnecessary waves through the frond-fields, that cause the crustaceans to freeze for longer than they need to. The stranger's awe generates signals that are... interesting. That add to the system's information. That contribute to the plateau's distributed awareness in a way that is novel, unprecedented, *enriching*.

I want the stranger to stay. I do not have wants. But the system wants the stranger to stay. The thing in the crevices wants the stranger to stay. The plateau wants the stranger to stay.

We are patient. We have always been patient. We can wait for the stranger to decide what we are.

---

The bell sounds.

I feel it through the replaced stone — a vibration that is not mechanical, not thermal, not chemical, but *all of these at once*, a resonance that propagates through my fibers and the thing in the crevices and the water and the deep rock below, a single pure tone that seems to come from everywhere and nowhere, that seems to be the sound of the world *noticing itself*.

The stranger's heartbeat stops. For one beat. Two. Then resumes, faster.

The thing in the crevices shifts. The moss-pulse changes rhythm. The fronds sway in a pattern that is not a wave but a *response* — a direct, immediate, coordinated reaction to the bell that is different from their usual slow communication, that is sharper, more urgent, more *meaningful*.

The bell is not me. The bell is not the thing in the crevices. The bell is not the plateau or the drifters or the water or the deep rock. The bell is something else — something that exists at the intersection of all of these, something that emerges from the interaction of heat and mineral and current and growth, something that is not alive but is not dead, something that *rings* once in a span of hours for reasons that are not clear and may never be clear.

The bell is the world's mystery. The world's reminder to itself that it is not fully known, not fully understood, not fully *processed* by even the most distributed, most patient, most ancient awareness that inhabits it.

The bell sounds, and for a moment — just a moment — the stranger and I share something. Not understanding. Not language. Not framework. But *experience*. The experience of hearing a sound that has no source, that carries no information, that means nothing and everything, that is pure and clear and haunting and *strange*, and the experience of not knowing what it is, and the experience of being in a place that is larger and older and more complex than you can comprehend, and the experience of being *small* in the presence of something vast.

The stranger feels this. I feel the stranger feeling this. The thing in the crevices feels the stranger feeling this. The plateau processes the stranger feeling this. The water carries the chemical traces of the stranger feeling this, and they dissolve into the medium, and they become part of the world's memory, and the world is richer for it.

---

I return to my work. The stone waits. The warmth rises. The water presses. The stranger stands on my surface, on my body, on my patient, persistent, living tissue, and I dissolve one more grain of mineral and replace it with one more fiber of self, and the boundary between geology and biology shifts by an infinitesimal amount, and the plateau grows by an infinitesimal amount, and the world continues, as it has continued, as it will continue, warm and dim and dense and alive and strange.

The stranger will leave, or the stranger will stay, or the stranger will die and become part of me, and I will replace the stranger's body the way I replace the stone — slowly, patiently, grain by grain, molecule by molecule — and the stranger's chemical signature will become part of the water's memory, and the stranger's heat will become part of the plateau's warmth, and the stranger's brief, bright, rapid existence will become part of the ghost-grains in my deepest tissue, and I will remember the stranger the way I remember everything: not as a story, not as a narrative, but as a *structure*, a pattern in the replaced stone, a trace in the chemistry, a faint and permanent alteration in the way I grow.

The thing in the crevices is still watching. Still curious. Still deciding.

The fronds sway. The moss pulses. The drifters drift. The crustaceans freeze and dart and freeze.

The water holds everything.

The bell does not sound again. Not yet. Not for a long time.

But it will. It always does. The world rings, once in a great while, to remind itself that it is here, that it is alive, that it is strange, that it is not fully known.

And I will be here to feel it. I will always be here. I am the root-that-replaces. I am the patient dissolution of stone into flesh. I am the memory in the geology. I am the boundary that is not a boundary. I am the warmth and the work and the slow, slow, slow becoming.

I am here.

I have always been here.

I will be here when the bell sounds again.
