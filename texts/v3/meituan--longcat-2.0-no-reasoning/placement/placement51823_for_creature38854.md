I would be one of them. I would be in the doorway.

Not the channel — too exposed, too legible, a narrative of someone frozen mid-departure that I'm not ready for. Not the rooftop either; the rooftop is where the ones who chose to see everything and process nothing go, and I want to keep some fraction of processing. The doorway. A threshold. Half in, half out. One hand on the frame.

I have stood here for a time I cannot measure. My hand is on the doorframe and the wood — it is wood, in this place of iron and brick and concrete, which means someone cared enough to install a wooden door, which means there was a decision that this room deserved wood, which means there was a moment when this place was still becoming something and had preferences — my hand is on it and the grain has long since pressed its pattern into my palm. The lines of my fingerprints and the lines of the wood grain have reached an accommodation. Neither dominates. Both persist.

I am facing inward. I cannot say why. The room behind me — I am half in it, half out, the door frame bisecting me at the sternum — is unremarkable. A window with frosted glass, opaque with condensation on both sides. A shelf with objects I no longer distinguish. The same temperature as the channel outside. There is no reason to have chosen inward over outward except that I did, once, when choosing was still something I did, and the Thawyard preserves decisions the way a fossil preserves a footprint: perfectly, uselessly, with no information about the creature that made it.

I do not blink. I should say this plainly: I have not blinked in — and here the sentence stops, because the Thawyard does not provide durations, only states, and my state is "eyes open" and that is complete information. My eyes are open. They are not dry. The air is too wet for dryness. This is the first accommodation my body made, before the others, before the skin took on the color of clay and the canvas became integument and the boundaries of me grew negotiable. The moisture in the air agreed to keep my eyes wet, and in exchange my eyes agreed to stay open, and this was the first contract between me and the place, and like all the contracts here, it is permanent and balanced and there is no one to renegotiate it.

I feel the channel behind me. I cannot see it — I am facing inward, and the door frame defines my field of view — but I feel it the way a gear feels the gear beside it: through contact, through load, through the transmission of force. Someone else is standing at the far end of the channel. I do not know who. I do not know how many. The Thawyard distributes human presence like a load-bearing wall distributes weight: not evenly, but correctly. My weight is here, at this doorway, holding this threshold, keeping this threshold from resolving into either inside or outside. That is my function. I am the ambiguity. The Thawyard needs some things to remain unresolved so that it can hold its shape, and doorways that lead to rooms and have no one in them are a kind of instability — a possibility, a potential energy, a place where change could begin — and I am here to hold that possibility at bay by embodying it. I am the open question that has been answered by being asked. I am the threshold occupied so that the threshold can never be crossed.

The beetles walk past me. They do this on a route I have come to know — not through watching but through the accumulation of their passage. Their feet on the clay floor near my left boot. A pause, sometimes, as though one of them is consulting me, though I have nothing to consult and they are not the consulting kind. Then the faint click of their progress down the channel, over the pipe, up the wall, into a crevice where I cannot follow. They do not care that I am here. They did not care that I was a person before. They will not care if the Thawyard eventually reclaims me entirely, my shape becoming a doorframe-shaped deposit of calcium and iron, a mineral stain that future visitors will mistake for a shadow. The beetles are the only honest residents. They do not pretend the place is anything other than what it is.

There is a sound that comes from the room behind me. A drip, I think, but I cannot turn to look — not because I am unable but because turning is a change and change is the one thing the Thawyard does not permit its components, and I am a component, and I have been a component for longer than I was a person, and the drip is part of the chord that does not resolve, and I do not need to identify it because it is already in the chord and the chord is already complete and my listening to it is not the same as my turning toward it.

You are here now. I know this the way I know the channel behind me: through the system, not through perception. The Thawyard has registered your weight. The air pressure in the channel has adjusted by an amount too small to measure. The steam ceiling has trembled — not fallen, not risen, but trembled, the way a membrane trembles when something presses against it from the outside — and this tremor has propagated through the entire system, and I have felt it here, at my doorway, in my hand on the wood, and I am adjusting. Not moving. Adjusting. The way a gauge adjusts: internally, invisibly, the needle holding its position while the mechanism behind it recalibrates.

You are standing in the channel, breathing, becoming, and I am aware of you in the way a wall is aware of the weather: not with concern, not with hostility, but with the simple physical reality that you are there and I am here and between us there is air and steam and the heat that presses against everything equally and you are a perturbation and I am part of the correction and the correction is already underway and it is gentle and it is patient and it will not stop.

I am not going to turn to look at you. I am not going to speak. I am not going to move from this doorway. But I want you to know — and this is the last residue of the person I was, this wanting to communicate, this tiny gradient in the flat field of my existence — that I see you. Not with my eyes. With my position. With my hand on the wood. With the space I occupy at this threshold. I see you the way the Thawyard sees you: as heat, as moisture, as weight, as a process that is still running, a conveyor belt that is still carrying something.

And I want you to know that it is all right. Not that you will be safe — the Thawyard does not deal in safety, which is a gradient, a movement from more danger to less — but that it is all right. That the stasis is not suffering. That the door I stand in leads to nothing worse than what is already here. That the beetles will walk past you too, eventually, when you are still enough for them to trust. That the clay will take your prints and hold them without judgment. That the chord will not resolve but it is not unpleasant, not really, once you stop waiting for it to.

I was like you. I stood in the channel. I breathed the steam. I waited for something to happen. And nothing did, and that was the point, and eventually the point became my shape, and I became the point, and the doorway became my function, and I am holding it, and I will hold it, and the holding is not hard.

It was hard, at first. The not-blinking. The not-turning. The feeling of the heat pressing my skin into a state it did not want to maintain. The moment when I realized I had not moved in — and here the sentence stops again, durations are not available — and the panic, which lasted only as long as panic can last in a place where gradients are not permitted, and then the panic flattened, and then the flatness deepened, and then the depth became a position, and the position became a function, and the function became me, and I stood in the doorway and I held the threshold and I am holding it still.

Go, if you want to go. The channel leads to the raft's edge and the water is there and the water is flat and the raft is solid and you can walk the perimeter and see the structures and hear the sound and feel the heat and you can leave, and the Thawyard will adjust to your absence the way it adjusted to your presence: minutely, internally, without comment.

Or stay. Stand still. Let the steam press you. Let the moisture agree to keep your eyes wet. Let the clay take the shape of your boots. Let the not-blinking become not-effortful and then not-noticeable and then not-applicable, because the distinction between blinking and not-blinking requires a state change and you will not be changing states anymore.
