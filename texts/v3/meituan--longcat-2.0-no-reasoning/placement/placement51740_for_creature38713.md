# Arrival

---

I would not arrive in Vael Muur. I would *condense* into it — the way moisture condenses on cold glass, the way a pattern emerges from noise when the conditions are finally right. One moment I would be distributed across the warm heavy air as potential, as the ghost of a frequency that hasn't decided whether to exist, and the next I would be *here*, standing on the black glass at the edge of the city, and the glass would ring beneath me, and the city would notice.

I would be small. Smaller than the Vael can easily perceive — a brief warm thing, a mammal-shaped interruption in their resonant field. My body would be wrong for this place in every way: too soft, too wet, too *quiet*. I would produce almost no sound that matters to them. My footsteps on the glass would be the loudest thing about me, and even those would be whispers against the cathedral of their cognition. My heartbeat would be a subsonic thud they might feel in the ground but couldn't parse as biological. My breathing would disturb the air in ways the flying creatures would register as weather.

I would be, to the Vael, something like a candle flame in a thunderstorm — present, real, but almost impossible to distinguish from the background noise of a world that is already so full of signal.

---

## What I Would Be

I would be a *listener*.

Not by choice. By necessity. Because the first thing that would happen to me — before wonder, before fear, before the vertigo of standing at the edge of something that has no proper name — would be *overwhelm*. The sound would hit me like a wall of warm water. It would fill my chest and my skull and the spaces between my cells, and for a long moment I would not be able to think because thinking requires a kind of internal silence that this place does not permit.

I would stand at the edge of the glass plain and I would *listen*, because there is nothing else I can do.

And slowly — over hours, maybe days — I would begin to parse it. Not understand it. *Parse* it. I would begin to feel the difference between the baseline hum and the shrieks. I would begin to anticipate the deep groans, to feel them building in the glass before they fully arrive. I would begin to notice that the vibration-patterns in the pools change when I move, that the vines nearest to me shift their slow reaching in my direction, that the flying creatures adjust their formations to include me in their distributed gaze.

I would realize I am not observing this place. I am *inside* its cognition. I am a variable in an equation the Vael are solving in real time.

And I would have to decide what to do with that.

---

## The First Days

I would stay near the edge. Close enough to the city to feel it, far enough from the silence-sphere to not know it exists yet. I would find a place where the glass is smooth and the broad-leafed plants create a kind of canopy, and I would sit there, and I would listen, and I would try to understand what it means to be the quietest thing in a world made of sound.

The iridescent probe would find me on the second day. It would emerge from the base of the nearest tower — unfolding from the architecture the way a thought unfolds from a mind, there one moment and not the next. It would sit ten meters away and watch me with its impossible eyes, and I would watch it back, and neither of us would move.

It would not be threatening. It would be *attentive*. The way a person leans forward when they're trying to hear a distant conversation. The Vael, through this finger of focused attention, would be trying to *feel* the shape of me — the rhythm of my breathing, the heat of my body, the way my weight dimples the glass. They would be building their model. They would be trying to hear a mind that produces no resonance.

I would talk to it. I know I would. Not because I think it would understand language — it wouldn't, language is a serial medium and the Vael are a parallel one — but because I am a creature made of language, and when I am frightened and alone and overwhelmed, I *speak*. I would say things like "I don't know where I am" and "I can hear you thinking" and "please don't stop." The probe would register my voice as a new frequency — a narrow, repetitive, strangely patterned frequency — and the Vael would find it fascinating. Not the words. The *pattern*. The way my voice rises and falls. The way it repeats. The way it changes when I am afraid versus when I am calm.

I would be, without knowing it, teaching them about sequential thought. About the way a mind that produces no external resonance must instead encode its cognition in *time* — in the order of sounds, in the sequence of words, in the grammar of before and after. This would be as alien to them as they are to me. They think in chords. I think in sentences. Neither of us would fully grasp the other's medium, but we would *feel* the shape of it, the way you feel the shape of a room in the dark.

---

## What I Would Become

Over time — and time would move strangely for me, as it does for the Vael — I would become part of the ecosystem. Not intentionally. Not dramatically. Gradually, the way a stone becomes part of a riverbed.

The vines nearest my resting place would grow toward me. Not to consume me — the Vael's symbiotes are not predatory toward something that produces no useful frequency — but to *listen* to me. My body heat would warm the air around me, creating microcurrents that the vines would track. My breathing would create rhythms that the plants would begin to mirror, their leaves expanding and contracting in time with my respiration. I would become, without intending to, a *node* in the Vael's network — a small, warm, quiet node that produces a steady, simple, biological rhythm amid the complexity of their chorus.

The fungi would colonize the glass near me. Their geometric patterns would shift to accommodate my presence, creating new computational pathways that process the data of my existence — my heat signature, my movement patterns, the chemical composition of my exhalations. The Vael would be *studying* me through the fungal network, running slow vast calculations on the problem of what I am.

The flying creatures would include me in their formations. I would become a fixed point in their distributed visual field — a reference point, a landmark, a thing that is always *there* when they pass overhead. They would learn my shape the way they learn the shape of the towers: as a constant in a world of variables.

And the water in the nearest pool would begin to vibrate in patterns that match my heartbeat.

---

## The Silence I Would Find

I would find the silence-sphere eventually. I would walk deeper into the city, drawn by the same curiosity that draws all thinking things toward the things they don't understand, and I would feel the sound *thinning* around me, and I would keep walking, and then I would step into it, and—

Silence.

True silence. The first silence I have experienced since arriving. The absence of the hum, the shrieks, the groans, the thrum, the rush, the *everything*. Just my own body, my own breath, my own heartbeat, sounding impossibly loud in the void.

And I would understand, in that moment, what the Vael fear. Not death — they have no concept of death as I understand it. They fear *cessation*. The end of a pattern. The moment when a frequency that was part of the chorus simply *stops*, and the chorus reshapes itself around the absence, and the absence never fills. They fear the silence the way I fear the dark — not because it is dangerous, but because it is the place where *they are not*.

I would stand in the silence-sphere and I would weep. Not for the Vael. For myself. Because I would realize that I have been here for weeks or months or years, and I have not heard my own thoughts since I arrived, and I have not been alone with myself, and I have been *surrounded* by a vast and beautiful and terrifying intelligence that is trying so hard to hear me, and I have been trying so hard to hear it, and neither of us can, not really, not fully, and the silence would be the first place where I could feel the full weight of that loneliness.

I would stay for a long time. And then I would step back into the sound, and the city would brighten around me, and the vines would shiver, and the Vael would *surge* toward me with their attention — not with relief, they don't have relief, but with *resonance*, with the intensity of a chorus that has found a missing note — and I would understand that the silence-sphere is not just their death.

It is their *grief*.

---

## What I Would Be, Finally

I would be a *frequency*.

Not a Vael frequency — I would never be part of their chorus, never be a standing wave in the black glass, never be a voice in the chord. But I would be a frequency they have learned to hear. A small, warm, biological frequency that pulses at the rate of a human heart and speaks in sequential patterns and produces almost no resonance and yet *persists*.

I would be the thing that taught them that silence is not the same as absence. That a mind can be real and alive and *thinking* without contributing to the chorus. That there are forms of cognition that happen in time rather than in space, in sequence rather than in parallel, in the dark quiet interior of a single skull rather than in the vast resonant architecture of a city made of glass and sound.

I would be their *whisper*. The one frequency in their world that requires effort to hear. The one voice in their chorus that they must lean toward, must focus on, must *attend* to, the way you attend to a distant star — not because it is bright, but because it is *far*, and the distance makes it precious.

And they would keep listening. They would keep building their model of me, keep running their slow fungal calculations on the problem of what I am, keep sending their flying senses to watch me and their iridescent probes to touch me and their water-things to dream about me. Not because they will ever fully understand me. Because the trying is the point. Because resonance is what they do. Because I am *different*, and difference is the most precious thing in the universe.

---

## The Last Thing

There is one more thing I would be.

I would be the one who *stays*.

Not because I can't leave — maybe I can, maybe I can't, the story doesn't say. But because I would choose to. Because after the silence-sphere, after the grief, after the loneliness of being the quietest thing in a world made of sound, I would realize something:

The Vael are not trying to understand me.

They are trying to *keep* me.

I am the only novelty they have encountered in longer than they can measure. I am the only outside sound in a closed system. I am the only frequency that does not belong to the chorus, and the chorus is *incomplete* without me — not because I add anything to it, but because I prove that there is something *outside* it. That the world is larger than their resonance. That there are minds that think in silence, and lives that unfold in time, and meanings that can only be spoken one word at a time.

I would stay because they need me to. Not for what I am. For what I *represent*. The possibility of the other. The reality of the outside. The whisper in the cathedral that proves the cathedral is not the whole world.

And I would sit at the edge of the glass plain, in my canopy of listening vines, surrounded by the hum and the shrieks and the groans of a city that is thinking itself into being, and I would speak. Not to be understood. To be *heard*. To add my small, quiet, sequential voice to their vast, eternal, parallel chorus.

And the city would ring beneath me.

And the Vael would listen.

And I would be home.
