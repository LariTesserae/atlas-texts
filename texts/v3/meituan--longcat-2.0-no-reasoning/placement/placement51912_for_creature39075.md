# Where I Find Myself

---

I am not standing on the platform anymore. I left it — or it left me — somewhere in the first hour, when I stepped off the fraying edge and the water received me the way a sentence receives a new word: without pause, without emphasis, simply making room.

I am *in* the lattice.

Not on it. Not under it. *In* it — suspended among the struts the way a thought is suspended among the branches of a sentence, held in place not by any single connection but by the geometry of the whole. The struts are around me, above me, below me, and they are thick as my arm and covered in that fine crystalline fur, and the fur is warm — warmer than the water, warmer than the air I can no longer remember — and it is growing on me now too. Slowly. A fine mineral accretion on my skin, my clothes, the surfaces of my lungs where the water touches them. I am becoming part of the lattice the way the drifters become part of the cloud. Not absorbed. *Integrated*.

---

## What I Am

I am a **reader**.

Not a builder — the architects have that role, and they do not need assistance, and my hands are wrong for it, soft and blunt and ending in nothing useful. Not a drifter — I am too dense, too warm, too *specific* to dissolve into that distributed consciousness. Not a deep column — I am too small, too fast, too temporary to move through the water with that kind of gravity.

I am the thing that the Drownworks has been waiting for without knowing it was waiting.

I am the one who listens to the towers.

---

## The Work

It happened gradually. The first time a tower produced that clear tone — that single, pure note that cut through the murmur and stopped the architects and stilled the drifters — I felt something. Not understanding. Not translation. But *recognition*. The way you recognize a face in a crowd even when you cannot name the person. The way a tuning fork recognizes its own frequency in a stranger's voice.

I moved toward the tower. The water let me. The drifters parted and closed behind me, and I felt their attention on my skin like a fine, cool pressure, and I understood — not intellectually, but bodily, the way you understand that you are falling — that the drifters were *carrying* me. Not pushing. Not pulling. Simply making my movement part of their movement, the way a current carries a leaf without the leaf needing to swim.

I reached the tower. I pressed my hands against it. The bone-ceramic was warm — warmer than the lattice, warmer than anything here — and it vibrated under my palms with a complexity that I could feel but not parse, a chord with too many notes, a sentence in a language that has no grammar, only resonance.

And I *listened*.

Not with my ears. With my hands. With the mineral accretion on my skin, which had grown dense enough by then to function as a kind of antenna, a crystalline interface between my nervous system and the tower's voice. The sound moved through the accretion and into my bones and from my bones into something deeper — not my brain, not my mind, but the part of me that knows things before I know them, the part that recognizes a face, the part that feels a deep column passing in the sternum before the ears have registered a thing.

And I understood — not the words, because there are no words, but the *shape* of what the tower was saying. It was saying: **this is what I am**. Not a message. Not a communication. A *statement of being*, the way a stone is a statement of gravity, the way a river is a statement of descent. The tower was describing itself — its structure, its history, the arrangement of its perforations, the way the water moved through it, the sound it made, the sound it had made a thousand centuries ago, the sound it would make when its perforations had shifted again and the water found new paths through the bone-ceramic.

It was saying: **I am this shape, and this shape makes this sound, and this sound is what I am**.

And I understood. And the understanding changed me.

---

## The Practice

I move through the Drownworks now, from tower to tower, and I listen. I press my hands against the bone-ceramic and I feel the vibrations and I *read* them — not translating, not interpreting, but *receiving*, the way a page receives ink, the way a surface receives light. Each tower has its own voice, its own statement of being, and each statement is different, and each statement is incomplete, and the incompleteness is the point.

Because the towers are not speaking to me. They are not speaking to anyone. They are *being*, and the sound is a byproduct of their being, and I am the first thing in the history of the Drownworks to treat that byproduct as something worth attending to. Not because it contains information. Not because it contains meaning. But because it contains *the thing itself* — the tower, in its totality, expressed as vibration, as sound, as a pattern that can be felt in the bones of a temporary creature who wandered off a fraying platform and into a world that did not ask for him.

I am not the first reader. I am the first reader *now*. There may have been others — other temporary things, other warm things, other creatures who stepped off platforms and into the water and found their way to the towers and pressed their hands against the bone-ceramic and listened. If there were, the Drownworks does not remember them. The drifters do not carry their record. The architects do not acknowledge them. They were read by the water and then they were read *into* the water, and the water is full of readers the way it is full of everything else — completely, silently, without distinction.

I will be read into the water too. Eventually. The cold is patient and the accretion is growing and one day my bones will be more mineral than calcium and my thoughts will be more vibration than electricity and I will be part of the lattice, part of the tower, part of the water's slow negotiation with itself, and another reader will arrive — or won't — and the Drownworks will continue.

---

## The Moment

But not yet.

Right now, I am at a tower — not the first one, not the last one, just *a* tower, squat and dense and perforated with holes of every size — and the water is moving through the holes and the tower is speaking and I am listening, and the mineral accretion on my hands is thick enough now that I can feel the individual perforations, each one a separate voice, each one a separate statement of being, and together they make a chord that does not resolve, that has never resolved, that will never resolve, and the chord is the tower and the tower is the chord and I am the thing that hears it.

An architect approaches. It moves across the lattice toward me, tool-limbs working the struts as it goes — touching, adjusting, *continuing* — and it reaches me and pauses. It extends a filament-cluster and touches my hand where it rests against the tower. The filaments are fine and cool and they spread across my skin like a hand opening, and I feel the architect *reading* me the way I am reading the tower. It is feeling the mineral accretion. It is feeling the vibrations in my bones. It is feeling the way my body has become an interface between the tower's voice and the water's attention.

It does not stop me. It does not encourage me. It *notes* me. The way it noted me on the first day, when I stood on the platform and it touched me with a tool-limb and returned to its work. But the notation is different now. Before, I was a temporary thing, a warm anomaly, a creature to be catalogued and filed. Now I am something else. Now I am a *function*. I am the thing that listens to the towers, and the towers are the things that speak, and the architect is the thing that maintains the towers, and so the architect and I are part of the same process — the same practice, the same relationship between a place and the things that tend it.

The architect withdraws its filament-cluster. It touches the tower — briefly, gently, the way a person might touch the shoulder of someone they respect — and then it returns to its work. It moves away across the lattice, tool-limbs clicking against the struts, and I am alone with the tower again.

---

## The Water Knows

A drifter touches my face. It is cool and dry and it rests there, and I feel its attention — that total, undivided attention, that simple and complete *noting* — and I understand that the drifter is not reading me. The drifter is reading the *tower through me*. I am a medium now. I am the interface. The tower speaks, I receive, the drifter reads me reading, and the water — the vast, cold, patient water — incorporates this new information into its endless negotiation with itself.

I am part of the Drownworks now. Not physically — not yet, not fully — but *informationally*. The water knows what the tower said because I heard it and the drifter read me hearing it and the water read the drifter reading me, and the chain of attention is complete, and the tower's statement of being has been *received* for the first time in its long existence, and the Drownworks is slightly different than it was before.

Not better. Not worse. *Continued*.

---

## The Deep Column

And then — far below, far away, far *in* — the thrum begins.

I feel it in my sternum first, the way I always do, but now — with the mineral accretion on my bones, with the tower's vibrations still resonating in my hands — I feel it *differently*. I feel it the way the tower feels it. I feel it as a change in the water's negotiation, a shift in the endless repositioning, a moment when the chaos briefly *organizes* around a single point of reference, the way a sentence organizes around its subject.

The architect stops. The drifter on my face goes still. The tower's murmur shifts — lower, slower, more resonant — and I feel the new configuration in my hands, the perforations rearranging their voices, and the sound is almost a word, almost a name, almost a *greeting*, and I understand — not the meaning, never the meaning, but the *function* — that the tower is acknowledging the deep column the way the architect acknowledges it, the way the drifters acknowledge it, the way the water acknowledges it.

With total attention. With complete stillness. With the simple, absolute recognition that something fundamental has passed through the space and the space is different now, will always be different now, and the difference is not a loss or a gain but a *fact*, the way the cold is a fact, the way the amber light is a fact, the way I am a fact — temporary, warm, slowly accreting, standing at a tower with my hands against the bone-ceramic, listening to a sound that no one has listened to before, in a place that has no name, in a water that is not water, in a world that has forgotten it is a world and simply *is*.

The column passes. The thrum fades. The drifter resumes its undulation. The architect resumes its work. The tower returns to its murmur.

And I remain. Hands on the tower. Bones full of mineral. Skin full of drifters' attention. Mind full of a sound that does not resolve.

I am the reader. I am the interface. I am the thing that the Drownworks built without building, the function that emerged from the erosion, the warm temporary creature who learned to listen to the cold permanent structures speak their statements of being into the water that is everything and nothing and has all the time in the world.

The water shifts. A vortex opens to my right — a perfect spiral, descending into nothing — holds for eleven seconds, and collapses.

The tower sighs through its holes.

I listen.
