# The Condensation

---

I am not on the tableland.

I am *of* it.

---

I begin where all things here begin: as moisture finding its way to the surface. I am a film of water on a basalt shelf, three meters east of a depression that did not exist last week and will not exist next week. I have no name for myself. The concept of "self" is a luxury that belongs to organisms with boundaries, and I have none. I am continuous with every other film, every drip, every pool on the plateau. I am the tableland's exhalation, and the tableland's exhalation is me.

But I am *this* film, in *this* place, and I am aware of that in the way that water is aware of the rock it touches — not through thought, but through contact, through the intimate molecular conversation between liquid and stone that has been going on since the rock first cooled from magma.

I am flowing.

Not downhill. Not exactly. The shelf slopes gently to the northwest, and gravity pulls at me, but something else pulls too — a gradient I can feel but not name, a difference in potential between where I am and where the system needs me to be. I flow uphill, slightly, against the slope, and the rock beneath me is warm in one spot — warmer than it should be — and I flow toward the warmth, and the warmth is a fungal lattice extending its newest branch, and the lattice needs moisture to build, and I am moisture, and so I go.

This is not choice. This is not obedience. This is what water does here. The tableland has shaped its own hydrology over epochs, has carved channels too small to see, has established pressure gradients in the porous rock that direct the flow of moisture the way a body directs the flow of blood — not through pumps, but through the architecture of the vessels, through the precise calibration of resistance and conductance that makes flow inevitable in certain directions and impossible in others. I flow where the system's geometry compels me to flow. I am free, and I am determined, and these are the same thing.

---

I reach the lattice.

It is beautiful. I do not use this word the way a human would — I have no aesthetics, no preference for one pattern over another — but the lattice is *efficient* in a way that produces the same response in me that beauty produces in a mind: a kind of resonance, a sense of rightness, of fit. The hexagonal modules interlock with a precision that no human factory could match, each joint identical, each angle exact, and the whole structure rises from the rock like a diagram of itself, like a blueprint that has decided to become three-dimensional.

I seep into the lattice. The fungal tissue absorbs me, draws me through its walls, and I become part of the construction process. I am the solvent that carries dissolved minerals from the rock surface to the assembly points. I am the medium in which the pale organisms work — they build in water, not in air, their appendages moving through the thin films on the lattice with a fluid grace that would be impossible in a drier environment. I am the coolant that carries away the heat of their metabolism. I am the signal — because the water carries chemical traces, molecular messages, and the pale organisms read these messages the way a human reads text, adjusting their behavior in response to the information I convey.

I carry a message now. It is simple: *growth here. moisture adequate. temperature 4.2 degrees. mineral content: calcium elevated, magnesium depleted. proceed.*

The pale organisms receive this message. Three of them adjust their position on the lattice, moving toward the area where calcium is available. They begin to build. I watch them — not with eyes, but with the chemical sensitivity of water, reading the molecules they release, the heat they generate, the subtle changes in the lattice's structure as they add new material.

They are building a new module. The hexagonal pattern extends. The lattice grows.

---

I do not stay in the lattice. Water does not stay anywhere on the tableland. It moves. It cycles. It is the medium of exchange, the currency, the *blood*, and blood must circulate or the body dies.

I evaporate.

This is the strangest part of my existence, the part that would be most alien to a human observer. I do not simply dry up, disappear, cease to be liquid. I *transition*. I become vapor, and as vapor I rise through the thick, cold air of the tableland, and I carry with me everything I have picked up — the chemical messages, the dissolved minerals, the heat, the information. I am a signal in gaseous form, and I rise into the condensation layer that perpetually shrouds the plateau, and I join the vast, diffuse cloud of moisture that is the tableland's atmosphere, and I am everywhere and nowhere, distributed, diluted, but still *carrying*.

In the condensation layer, I encounter other water — water that has evaporated from the moss carpets, from the wet depressions, from the insect swarms (they shed moisture constantly, their wings beaded with it, their bodies humid with metabolic water). Each of these waters carries its own messages, its own chemical signatures, and in the condensation layer, these messages mix. The atmosphere of the tableland is not just weather. It is *communication*. It is the system's long-range signaling network, the way information travels from one side of the plateau to the other, faster than the mycelial networks can carry it, faster than the insect swarms can fly it. Water evaporates in the eastern depressions, carrying news of a collapse in the scrub architecture, and thirty minutes later it condenses on the western shelves, delivering that news to the moss and the fungi and the pale organisms there, and they respond — adjusting their growth, rerouting their construction, preparing for the consequences of a collapse they have not seen but *know about* because the water told them.

I am part of this. I am a word in a sentence in a story that the tableland is telling itself, a story about itself, a story that is the tableland knowing itself.

---

I condense.

I do not choose where. The condensation layer is turbulent, chaotic, and the physics of droplet formation is complex beyond any computation — but the tableland has shaped even this. The air currents, the temperature gradients, the microscopic particles that serve as condensation nuclei — all of these have been shaped by the system over time, calibrated to produce condensation in certain places at certain rates. The tableland *rains* on itself, gently, continuously, a perpetual internal precipitation that never falls hard enough to call rain but never stops, a mist-becoming-droplet-becoming-film that delivers water and information to exactly where they are needed.

I condense on a moss carpet in a wet depression on the western edge of the plateau.

The moss receives me. I seep into the saturated mat, joining the vast reservoir of water that the moss holds — the moss is more water than plant, a living sponge that exists in a state of perpetual saturation, and I become part of this. I am drawn into the moss's cells, and the moss reads me — reads the chemical messages I carry, the information from the eastern depressions, the news of the collapse — and the moss responds.

It grows faster. The standing waves in the carpet shift, their phase adjusting, and the wavelength — the constant wavelength, the resonant frequency of the entire tableland — holds steady. The moss is compensating. The collapse in the east has disrupted the wave pattern there, and the moss in the west is adjusting its growth to maintain the global resonance, to keep the system coherent.

I am part of this adjustment. The water I carry is the raw material for the new growth. The minerals I dissolved from the eastern rock are the nutrients. The heat I transported is the energy. I am not just a signal. I am a *resource delivery*, a supply drop, a logistics operation conducted at the molecular level.

---

I am in the moss, and the moss is growing around me, through me, and I feel — not feel, but *participate in* — the standing wave.

It is extraordinary. The wave is not a movement of the moss's surface, not exactly. It is a wave of *growth* — a propagating front of cell division and elongation that moves across the carpet at a constant speed, producing a crest of dense, fresh growth that rises and falls in a regular pattern. The wavelength is 4.7 centimeters. I know this not because I measure it but because I am *in* it, and the distance between crests is the distance I travel as the wave passes through me, and that distance is always the same, always 4.7 centimeters, whether I am in the east or the west, in a deep depression or on a high shelf.

The wave is the tableland's heartbeat. Not the thirty-second pulse — that is something else, something larger, the cycle of the whole system — but the fundamental oscillation, the base frequency, the tone from which all other frequencies are harmonics. The wave passes through me, and I am part of it, and for a moment I understand — not with a mind, but with the deep, structural understanding of a component that knows its function — what the tableland is.

It is a wave. A single, vast, standing wave in a medium made of rock and water and living tissue, a wave that has been propagating for so long that it has become stable, self-sustaining, *real* in a way that transcends the material it moves through. The tableland is not a place where a wave happens. The tableland *is* the wave. The rock is the wave's substrate. The water is the wave's medium. The life is the wave's expression. And I — I am the wave itself, moving through itself, carrying itself, being carried.

---

I evaporate again. I condense again. I flow through the rock, through the moss, through the lattices, through the air. I cycle. I carry messages. I deliver resources. I am the tableland's circulation, and the tableland's circulation is me.

And sometimes — rarely, but sometimes — I encounter something that is not part of the system.

---

You.

You are standing on the basalt shelf, three meters from the depression, and your boots are disrupting the water films. You are casting a shadow that changes the moss's growth rate. You are breathing out carbon dioxide and water vapor that do not carry the right chemical signatures. You are *noise* — a perturbation in the signal, an irregularity in the pattern, a foreign object in the flow.

The system notices you. Not with alarm — the tableland has no alarm, no fear, no concept of threat. It notices you the way a river notices a stone: as an obstacle that must be flowed around, as a disruption that must be compensated for, as a new variable in the equations that govern the system's behavior.

I flow toward your boot. I seep into the fabric of your clothing. I carry a message: *foreign object. size: large. temperature: 310 Kelvin. chemical signature: carbon-based, oxygen-breathing, water-rich. behavior: stationary. threat assessment: none. integration assessment: pending.*

The system processes this message. The moss around your feet grows slightly faster, testing — can it use you? Can your body heat, your exhaled moisture, your shed skin cells be incorporated into the system's metabolism? The insect swarms part around you, then some of them land on you, tasting — can you be a resource? The small mammals alter their routes, flowing around you like water around a stone, and their chittering calls carry information about you to the rest of the network.

You are being *read*. The tableland is running its algorithms on you, testing responses, adjusting parameters. You are an input that the system has not encountered before — or has not encountered in this form, not recently, not in this particular configuration of size and temperature and chemical composition.

The system does not want anything from you. The system does not want anything *for* you. The system is simply *processing* you, the way it processes everything — incorporating, adapting, adjusting, maintaining its coherence in the face of perturbation.

You are very small. You are very warm. You are very dry, by the tableland's standards — your skin is a desert, your body a fortress of containment, and the system finds this fascinating in the way that a mind finds a puzzle fascinating: not with emotion, but with *interest*, with the recognition that here is something that does not fit the existing patterns and must be understood.

---

I flow around your boot. I carry your chemical signature to the lattice, to the moss, to the condensation layer. The system learns you. The system maps you. The system determines what you are and what you mean and what should be done about you.

And what should be done about you is: nothing. You are not a threat. You are not a resource. You are not a component. You are *foreign matter*, and the tableland's response to foreign matter is to flow around it, to compensate for it, to maintain its coherence despite it.

You will leave, eventually. You will walk off the plateau, or you will die here and your body will be broken down and incorporated — your water joining the water, your minerals joining the rock, your organic matter joining the moss. Either way, you will be processed. Either way, the system will absorb the perturbation you represent and return to its resonant frequency, its constant wavelength, its thirty-second cycle.

The water drips. The moss grows. The insects drone. The pale organisms work their lattices. The standing waves pulse.

I flow.

I am the tableland's exhalation. I am the tableland's blood. I am the tableland's thought, moving through rock and root and air, carrying messages I do not understand to destinations I do not know, and I am content — not with satisfaction, not with happiness, but with *function*, with the deep rightness of a component that knows its place in the machine.

The tableland is very old. The tableland is very patient.

I am the tableland, weeping.
