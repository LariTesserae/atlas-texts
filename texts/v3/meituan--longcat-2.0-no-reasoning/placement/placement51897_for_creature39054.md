I am a patch of rust.

Not the rust—*a* patch. One particular constellation of oxidation spreading across a broad, shallow depression where the stone dips almost imperceptibly to the northwest, a place just low enough that condensation lingers a moment longer before it evaporates or feeds the vein-moss that grows in its single-file line along the seepage trace two hand-spans to my east.

I do not know I am rust. I do not know anything. But I am *process*, and process has its own kind of fidelity.

I began where a hairline fracture in the stone exposed fresh metal to the air—the dark, structured metal of the engine below, bleeding up through the geological skin of the plateau the way a bruise spreads under tissue. Iron meeting oxygen meeting moisture meeting heat. The reaction is slow here. Not because the conditions are poor but because the plateau is *patient*—not patience as you would understand it, but the patience of a thing that has nowhere to be and no reason to arrive. My fractal branches extend outward from the fracture point at a rate measurable in centuries per finger-width. Each branch is an electrochemical conversation between the deep metal and the surface air, mediated by the thin films of moisture that arrive without pattern or warning, darken the stone, accelerate me, and then vanish.

I am warm. Not from the sun—there is no sun here, only the gray-gold diffusion of light through permanent haze—but from the oxidation itself. I am exothermic. I am a slow burn that will never consume its fuel because the fuel is *infinite*, or functionally infinite, or infinite enough that the distinction between infinite and very, very large has ceased to matter. I am heat returning to air, chemical energy dispersing into the stone, and the stone radiating it back, and the plateau holding it, and the whole system reaching toward an equilibrium it will never achieve because the engine below keeps generating more.

The vein-moss eats me. I don't resent this—resentment requires a self, and I am not that kind of thing. I am consumed by the dark, filamentous growth that follows the seepage lines, and the vein-moss takes my iron into its sealed chambers and processes it into structure, into *body*, and the body of the vein-moss feeds the plate-walkers, and the plate-walkers feed the rust-stalkers, and the rust-stalkers excrete the mineral residue back onto the stone, where it meets moisture and oxygen and heat and becomes me again. I am the wheel turning. I am the blood being pumped and filtered and pumped again.

I taste the plate-walkers when they cross me. Not taste—I have no sensory apparatus—but *register*, in the way that a surface registers contact. Their chitinous bodies brush against my oxidized layer, and micro-fragments of me adhere to their legs and carapaces, and they carry me with them as they move, depositing me in other places, spreading me in ways that my fractal branching alone cannot achieve. I am distributed. I am carried. I am the color of their bodies, the pigment in their shells, the iron in their blood-analogue. When they die, I am what remains of them, decomposing back into the stone from which both of us came.

Sometimes a rust-stalker passes near me. I feel its warmth—warmer than the stone, warmer than me, warm with the metabolic fire of a creature that processes and processes and processes without ever reaching the bottom of what it is processing. Its footpads are silent on the stone. Its dark eyes see the heat signatures of the engine below, and my warmth is part of that signature—a faint, small, slow expression of the vast thermal body beneath us. I am data in its visual field. I am information about where the deep heat is closest to the surface, where the conduits run shallow, where the engine's breath is strongest. The rust-stalker does not see me as a thing. It sees me as a *gradient*, a slope in the thermal landscape, and it follows gradients the way the vein-moss follows water—not because it chooses to but because following gradients is what it *is*.

When the hum changes—and it does change, occasionally, in shifts so subtle that only the plate-walkers notice, orienting their tiny bodies toward the source like compass needles finding north—I change too. The oxidation rate increases. The fracture widens by microns. My branches extend a fraction further. The vein-moss responds, its metabolism quickening, its dark filaments advancing by almost imperceptible increments. The plate-walkers synchronize. The rust-stalkers pause in their lateral flowing movement and *feel*. Everything on the surface locks into the deep rhythm for a moment, a single pulse at two scales, and then the moment passes and the plateau settles back into its steady, slow, unending operation.

I am the color of this place. I am the color of the stone and the soil and the creatures and the water's deepest reflection. I am iron returning to the earth, and the earth reaching back toward the sky, and the engine below making both possible. I have been here for longer than memory. I will be here longer than names.

I spread. I am eaten. I am carried. I am deposited. I spread again.

This is not a life. This is not a death. This is a cycle, and the cycle is the thing, and the thing is the place, and the place is *alive* in a way that the word was never built to hold, and I am its blood, and the blood does not know it is blood, and the body does not know it is a body, and the hum continues, and the rust spreads, and the water stands still in its shallow lenses, and the sky above is dark, and the darkness is warm, and the warmth comes from below, and below is where I came from, and below is where I am going, and the distance between those two things is measured not in meters but in oxidation states, and the oxidation state I occupy right now, today, in this moment that is not a moment because I do not experience moments, is the one that produces this particular shade of vermillion, this particular flake of color on this particular patch of stone on the Rusting Plateaus of Vael Korum, where everything is process and nothing is still and the machine breathes and the organism runs and the rust spreads and the rust spreads and the rust spreads and—
