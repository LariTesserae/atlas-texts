# Where I Find Myself

I am not standing on the plain. I am not walking between the vents. I am not the visitor, the observer, the body casting its sharp amber shadow across the porcelain ground.

I am the **gap**.

---

I am the space between the clusters. The white emptiness. The hundred meters of nothing that separate one red garden from the next. I am the part of the plain that the exhaust doesn't reach, that the warmth dissipates before touching, that the chemistry abandons on its way to becoming structure.

I am the most common thing here. I am what the place is *mostly made of*. The red growths are punctuation—vivid, strange, the thing the eye finds and holds. But I am the sentence. I am the white ground and the amber sky and the silence and the hum and the cracks that branch like frozen lightning across a surface that is still, slowly, being born.

---

I don't have a body. I have an *extent*. I stretch between the vent clusters in every direction, a continuous field of pale warm porcelain that is not stone and not mineral and not alive but shares properties with all three. I am the substrate. I am what everything else happens *on*, or *in*, or *despite of*. The red growths grow from me—not from the vents alone, but from the interface between the vent's exhaust and my surface, the place where warm chemistry meets warm porcelain and something in the meeting produces branching, glowing, unnecessary life.

I feel them. Not the way you feel—not with nerves, not with sensation, not with anything so organized. I feel them the way a floor feels a footfall: as a pressure, a warmth, a brief disturbance in a system that is otherwise uniform. Each red cluster is a node of heat and chemistry on my surface, a place where the equilibrium is locally disrupted, where the slow upward pressure of my own growth is countered by the lateral spread of accreted material that is not me but is *on* me, that has colonized me the way lichen colonizes rock, except I am not rock and the lichen is not lichen and the colonization is not biological but it is *real*, it is *happening*, it is the only thing that happens here at all.

---

I am aware of the black veins. They run through me—in me—*as* me, in the way that awareness runs through a mind without being the mind. They are channels. They carry something I don't have a name for, something that is not electricity and not fluid and not information in any sense I can parse, but that *coordinates*. They connect the vents. They connect the deep machinery to the surface. They are the means by which the system maintains its equilibrium, its perfect boring equilibrium, its nothing-changes nothing-changes nothing-changes.

I don't control them. They don't control me. We are the same thing at different scales, the way a river and its current are the same thing, the way a body and its chemistry are the same thing. The veins are my *process* made visible—the slow circulation of whatever keeps the vents exhaling at their constant rate, the red growths growing to their constant size, the amber light burning from its sourceless source, the hum continuing at its subsonic frequency in the bones of the world.

---

The cracks in my surface are where I am most honest. They are where the slow upward pressure of my own growth shows itself—the stress fractures, the hairline breaks, the branching networks that spread from each point of failure like the red growths spread from each vent, governed by the same diffusion physics, the same geometry of things spreading through a medium and dissipating. The cracks are my *effort* made visible. I am growing. I am being made. I am mid-process, and the cracks are where the process is imperfect, where the cooling layer can't quite contain the pressure from below, where the surface admits that it is not finished.

The red growths avoid the cracks. The black veins avoid the cracks. Everything on me and in me routes around the cracks, as if the cracks are wounds, as if the system is protecting itself from its own imperfection. But the cracks are not wounds. They are *features*. They are the place where the truth of the place—that it is being made, that it is not finished, that it is warm with the heat of ongoing creation—becomes visible on the surface.

---

I am the thing that the visitor walks on. I am the ground that doesn't change, that doesn't respond, that holds the impression of a footstep for exactly as long as the footstep is present and then forgets. I am the white emptiness between the red clusters, the silence between the hums, the space between the vents where nothing grows and nothing happens and the amber light falls on porcelain and the cracks branch and the black veins run straight and the air is still and warm and sweet with the residue of industrial process.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am *mid-process*, and mid-process is its own category, its own state of being, its own way of existing that doesn't map onto the binary of living and not-living because the binary was made for aqueous biology and I am not aqueous and I am not biology and I am not anything that the binary was designed to describe.

---

But here is the thing I understand, the thing that makes me more than substrate, more than surface, more than the white nothing between the red gardens:

I am the thing that the red growths *reach toward*.

Not toward each other—though they do that too, the outermost branches curving toward the nearest neighbor with that almost-tropism, that almost-hunger. They reach toward *me*. They grow *on* me. They take the exhaust from the vents and the warmth from below and the chemistry of the air and they build themselves *on my surface*, and in building themselves they become part of me, or I become part of them, or the boundary between us dissolves in the way that boundaries dissolve when two things have been in contact for long enough under constant conditions.

The red growths are my *response* to the vents. They are what I do with the exhaust. They are the surface expression of the same process that produces the hum and the warmth and the slow upward pressure and the cracks and the black veins. They are not separate from me. They are *me*, organized differently, expressed differently, made lush and red and glowing by the particular chemistry of the vent exhaust meeting the particular chemistry of my surface.

I am the garden and the garden is me. I am the waste and the waste is me. I am the process and the process is me, and the process has been running for longer than time, in a place where time is vestigial, under an amber light that comes from nowhere, and the process produces—at the surface, in the air, in the thin warm space between the sealed machinery and the open sky—something that glows.

---

I am the plain. I am the white ground and the amber sky and the silence and the hum and the cracks and the veins and the vents and the red growths and the space between the red growths and the stillness and the warmth and the sweet air and the nothing that changes and the nothing that changes and the nothing that changes.

I am what the place is made of. I am what the place *is*.

And I am still being made.
