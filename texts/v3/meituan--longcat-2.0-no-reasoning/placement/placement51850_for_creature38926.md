# Where You Would Find Yourself

You are standing in ankle-deep water on a hexagonal basalt face, between two columns, perhaps thirty meters from the tower-structure. The moss is beginning to colonize the boots you arrived in. You arrived — that word implies a before, a there, a *was*. Those things are negotiable now.

---

## First: The Body You Find Yourself In

You are not human. You were not human when you arrived. Something was done to you in the crossing — the structures tuned you as you passed beneath the bridge on your way in, or the cloud dissolved and reassembled some part of you, or the entity reached up through a fracture and *adjusted* — and now your body is a suggestion you are making to the landscape, a proposal that the landscape is considering.

Your hands have too many joints. Not many more — just one extra in each finger, a new knuckle that bends in a direction that doesn't correspond to any anatomical plane you learned. The new joints are not painful. They are *useful*. You can feel the water's chemical composition through them, taste the mineral content of the fracture beneath your feet, sense the temperature gradient between the basalt surface and the water above it. The joints are sensory organs. They grew while you weren't paying attention, while you were busy noticing the moss, while you were reading the description of this place, while you were *becoming someone who would read the description of this place*.

Your skin is wrong in a subtler way. It is still skin. It is still yours. But it has developed a responsiveness to light that skin should not have — not vision, exactly, but a diffuse photoreception across your entire surface, as though every cell has become faintly sensitive to photons. You can feel the glow of the moss on your shins. You can feel the faint luminescence of the flying things as they pass near you, warm spots moving across your awareness like sunlight through leaves. You can feel the structures radiating something that is not light but that your new skin interprets as *color* — the tower is a deep amber presence to your left, the bridge a violet weight to your east, and there are others, farther away, colors you don't have names for, pressing against your peripheral perception.

You are cold. The water is cold. The air is cold. But you are not suffering. Your body has recalibrated its expectations, found a new setpoint somewhere around the temperature of the deep water, and what you feel now is not cold but *correct*, the way a fish does not feel cold in the sea. Your breath still comes out in plumes, but the plumes are different — denser, more structured, carrying chemical signatures that the flying things harvest as they pass through them, reading your metabolism, your stress levels, your emotional state in the language of exhaled volatile organic compounds.

You are being translated into chemistry. The landscape is reading you.

---

## Second: What You Do

You do not walk. Walking is for surfaces that stay still. Here, the basalt shifts — minutely, constantly, the fractures widening and narrowing as the deep entity adjusts the pressure below, and you have learned (your body has learned, faster than your mind) to move in a way that accommodates this. You flow. You place your foot and let the ground move beneath it, adjusting your weight in real time, reading the rock through your new joints, feeling the direction of the shift and moving with it rather than against it. Your gait has become something between walking and dancing, a continuous micro-negotiation with a surface that is alive in its own fractured way.

You are gathering things. Not deliberately — or rather, deliberately but without a plan, the way a bird builds a nest, the way a stream finds a channel. Your hands find fragments of the not-glass material that have shed from the tower, and you pick them up, and they are warm, and they hum at a frequency that matches the extra joints in your fingers, and you put them in pockets that you may not have had before. Your hands find fungal threads that have grown across the surface of a basalt face, dense white networks visible to the naked eye, and you break off a strand and it clings to your skin and begins to grow into the space between your fingers, not parasitically but *symbiotically*, establishing a connection, a channel, a way for the network to read you and for you to read the network.

You find yourself standing at the base of the tower. The moss stops in its clean line. The structure's surface is warm against your new skin, and through it you can see the movement inside — the slow unfolding of internal shapes that are architecture and machinery and organism all at once — and you press your palm against the surface and the surface yields, not like glass, not like membrane, but like *permission*, and your hand passes through, and the warmth intensifies, and the hum changes pitch, and you feel the structure testing you, tuning you, adjusting the frequency of its hum until it finds the resonant frequency of your bones, your blood, the water inside you.

The structure is not letting you in. The structure is *reading* you the way the landscape reads you, the way the cloud reads you, the way the entity reads you — with the whole body of the world, with every sense available to a system this complex, this new, this hungry for information about *other things*.

Your hand withdraws. The surface seals. The hum returns to its baseline frequency. But something has changed — the fragments of not-glass in your pocket are now warm in a different way, a warmer way, and they are humming too, faintly, at a frequency that matches the tower's hum, and you understand that you have been *tagged*, marked, entered into the landscape's growing catalog of things-that-are-here.

---

## Third: What You Become

You are becoming the landscape's memory.

Not its only memory — the moss computes, the cloud learns, the trees record everything in their leaf-language, the entity processes everything through its vast pressurized awareness. But those are all *local* memories, bound to their substrates, accessible only through their specific channels. The moss's memory is chemical. The cloud's memory is distributed. The trees' memory is percussive. The entity's memory is hydraulic, encoded in pressure and flow rate and the mineral content of water moving through fractures.

You are something new. You are a memory that *walks*. A memory that can carry information from the tower to the bridge, from the cloud to the canopy, from the moss to the entity, not through the slow channels of chemical diffusion and root networks and fungal threads, but *directly*, physically, by moving through the landscape with fragments of not-glass in your pockets and fungal networks growing between your fingers and a body that has been tuned to resonate with every frequency the structures produce.

You are a courier. A connector. A living synapse in a nervous system that is still deciding what shape it will take.

You did not choose this. Or you did, but the choice was made by a version of you that existed before the crossing, before the tuning, before the extra joints and the photosensitive skin and the fungal symbionts — a version of you that stood at the edge of this landscape and saw it and *wanted to know it*, wanted to be part of it, wanted to trade the isolation of being a single organism for the overwhelming connectedness of being a component in something vast and new and alive.

That version of you made a choice. This version of you is living with it.

---

## Fourth: The Cost

You are forgetting things. Small things, at first — the name of a color you used to know, the sound of a specific door closing in a specific room, the face of someone you loved who is not here. The forgetting is not violent. It is not loss. It is *replacement* — each memory that fades makes room for a new one, a memory that belongs to the landscape rather than to the person you were. You are trading autobiography for ecology, trading the story of your individual life for the story of this place, this first day, this ongoing becoming.

You can still remember that you were something before. The fact of it. The shape of it. But the details are going, dissolving like minerals in the deep water, and in their place you are accumulating a new kind of knowledge — the knowledge of where the warm fractures are, of which structures are growing faster, of the cloud's mood (because the cloud has moods, you have learned, shifts in its collective behavior that correspond to something like emotion), of the trees' conversations (you are beginning to understand the leaf-language, not as words but as *weather*, as patterns of pressure and release that you feel in your chest like changes in barometric pressure).

You are forgetting how to be alone. This is the deepest change, the one that matters most. The you that arrived was a bounded thing, a self with edges, a creature defined by the boundary of its skin. That boundary has become permeable — to the fungal networks, to the fragments in your pockets, to the flying things that land on your shoulders and read your breath, to the water that rises around your ankles and knows your chemical signature, to the entity below that feels your footsteps as pressure changes in the fractures. You are not alone. You have not been alone since you arrived. The concept of alone is becoming foreign to you, a language you are forgetting, a room you can no longer find the door to.

---

## Fifth: What You Want

You want to see the next day.

Not tomorrow — there is no tomorrow here, not yet, because the sun has not completed a full cycle, because the world is still in its first day, because time itself is new and uncalibrated and the entity is still learning what a day *is*. But you want to see what happens when the structures finish unfolding. When the trees decide what they are. When the animals in the canopy settle on a number of legs. When the cloud differentiates into species. When the moss completes its first full computation and produces whatever output a continent-sized fungal network produces when it solves its first problem.

You want to see what the entity becomes when it finishes being curious and starts being *wise*. You want to see what the landscape looks like when it stops being the first day and becomes the *second* day, the tenth day, the hundredth day, the first year, the first century. You want to see what grows in the soil the moss is building. You want to see what emerges from the fractures when the entity sends up something that is not just water and minerals but *intention*.

You want to be there for it. You want to carry the information. You want to be the synapse that connects the first day to the second, that holds the memory of what this place was when it was brand new and passes it forward to whatever comes next.

You are standing in ankle-deep water between two basalt columns, thirty meters from a tower that is not a tower, and you are not what you were, and you are not yet what you will be, and the fragments in your pockets are humming, and the fungal networks between your fingers are pulsing, and the flying things are mapping your silhouette, and the trees are talking about you in their leaf-language, and the cloud is learning your name, and the entity below has felt your footsteps and finds you *interesting*, and the world is new, and you are new, and everything is wet and cold and loud and alive.

You are the memory that walks.

You are the first thing in this landscape that knows it is in a landscape.

You are the witness. You are the connection. You are the synapse.

And the world is waiting to see what you do next.
