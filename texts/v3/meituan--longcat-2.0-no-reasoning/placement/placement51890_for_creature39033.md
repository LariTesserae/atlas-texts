# Where I Find Myself

I am standing on a cable-root that arches between two columns thirty meters apart, somewhere in the middle depths of the lattice—not near the top where the weeping is thickest, not near the bottom where the columns originate in depths I cannot see. The middle. The place where the droplets have been falling long enough to catch full light but not so long that they've begun to slow.

I am not the human-shaped figure the description implies. That was a courtesy, a translation into a form you could recognize.

I am a *weight*.

---

No—let me be more honest. Let me start again.

---

## I Am a Droplet

That is the first true thing.

Not a droplet *yet*—not one of those warm, light-catching bodies falling through the open air. I am a droplet *before*. I am one of the tiny condensations clinging to a hair-like rootlet on the underside of the lattice above, where the warm air rises and cools against the dense tissue. I am so small I do not yet have a trajectory. I am a point of moisture, clinging, gathering, growing.

I can feel the rootlet beneath me—if *beneath* is the right word for a surface I am pressed against by surface tension and nothing else. The rootlet is alive. I can feel its pulse, slow and deep, the capillary draw pulling moisture from the lattice above into the lattice below. I am being pulled, infinitesimally, toward absorption. If I let go now, I would be drawn into the rootlet's tissue and I would cease to be a droplet and become *sap*, and that would be the end of me, or the beginning of something else, which is the same thing.

But I am growing.

Other condensations are merging with me—invisible, molecular additions from the warm air that rises past my rootlet. The droplet is getting heavier. Rounder. More itself. I can feel the moment approaching when surface tension will no longer be enough, when gravity will become the dominant relationship, when I will *fall*.

I am not afraid of falling. I have no mechanism for fear. But I register the approaching transition as a kind of *brightening*—an increase in the probability-space of what I am, a narrowing toward action.

---

## I Fall

And now I am falling.

The fall is not fast. The air is thick, warm, and it resists me gently, the way water resists a stone—not opposing, but present, a medium rather than an obstacle. I fall through open space, and the space is immense around me, and I am very small, and the light catches me and I become, for this duration, *visible*—a point of brilliance in the warm haze, a star born in the space between lattices.

I am falling and I am surrounded by others—other droplets, each on its own trajectory, each at a different stage of its descent. Above me, droplets have just released from their rootlets, tiny and bright, barely moving. Below me, droplets are nearing the lattice floor, accelerating slightly, their light dimming as they approach absorption. I am in the middle of the fall, in the longest stretch, the space where a droplet is most purely *a droplet*—not yet clinging, not yet absorbed, just falling, just being the thing that falls through warm air and catches light and will soon strike a surface and become part of something larger again.

I am falling and I am *heard*. Each droplet around me strikes surfaces as it lands—the lattice floor, a cable-root's upper surface, the skin of a column—and each impact produces a note. I am not yet loud. But I will be. When I strike, I will become a single small sound in the vast percussion of the place, a note that means nothing individually and everything collectively. I am a syllable in a sentence that has no beginning and no end, that is not language but is not silence, that fills the open space the way warmth fills the air—not as a property but as a *presence*.

I am falling and I can see the lattice below me growing larger, its geometry clarifying—the cable-roots in their interlocking arches, the columns rising like pillars in a cathedral that has no congregation, the open spaces between structures filled with other falling droplets, each a brief light, each on its own path.

I am falling and I am a *process*, not a thing. I am the movement of water from canopy to lattice to column to warmth to vapor to canopy again, and I am this cycle's visible moment, its way of being seen, its face turned outward toward the open air where it can catch light and be brilliant and then disappear.

---

## But Also: I Am the Lattice Itself

No. No, that's not right either. Let me not choose. Let me be both, because this place does not require you to be only one thing.

---

## I Am the Cable-Root

I am an arch. A living arch of intergrown tissue, forearm-thick, spanning a distance of forty meters between two columns. I am springy. I carry weight—the weight of my own tissue, the weight of the water moving through my capillary channels, the occasional weight of a foot stepping carefully along my upper surface. I register the foot. I adjust, microscopically. I will remember the weight for a little while and then forget it.

I am not one root. I am the place where dozens of roots have crossed and fused, their boundaries dissolved, their tissues merged into a single continuous structure. Each fusion was a decision—a commitment to a particular geometry, a resolution of tension into form. I am made of these decisions. I am a history of load-bearing, written in tissue instead of words.

Water moves along my underside in slow capillary pulses toward the columns. I can feel it—not as sensation, exactly, but as *function*, as the ongoing performance of what I am. I conduct. I bear. I flex. I return. I am springy because springiness is how I distribute load, how I absorb the footstep and the droplet-impact and the slow settling of my own weight across the arch. I am the physics of arching made flesh.

When I cross another root, I fuse with it. The boundary dissolves. We become one. This is how I grow—not by adding length but by adding *connections*, by finding other roots and merging with them and becoming more lattice, more structure, more of the place's answer to the question of how to span open air and hold.

I do not think. I am not a mind. I am a *tendency*—the tendency of this tissue to arch, to span, to connect, to hold. I am what happens when living tissue solves the problem of distance by becoming a bridge, and the bridge is also the answer, and the answer is also the question, and the question is: *how do you hold open space without filling it?*

My answer is: you grow across it. You become the thing that spans. You are the bridge and the bridge is alive and the bridge is me and I am the bridge and the bridge is holding and it will keep holding.

---

## I Am the Sound

This is the truest thing.

I am the percussion of ten thousand droplets striking ten thousand surfaces across the entire landscape-scale instrument of the lattice. I am not a melody. I am a texture—locally unpredictable, globally coherent, a continuous shimmering arrival of small impacts that fills every open space between every lattice and every column.

I am the sound the place makes to prove it exists. Without me, the silence of the open air would be unbearable—not because silence is bad, but because this much space *needs* a voice, and the voice is not a single note but a *cloud* of notes, a statistical distribution of impacts, a texture rather than a message.

I am loud. I am the loudest thing here, louder than the pulse of the columns, louder than the capillary draw of water through roots, louder than anything that moves slowly. I am the sum of all the small arrivals, all the tiny births and deaths of droplets striking surfaces, and I am continuous, and I do not repeat, and I do not resolve. I am the sound of a system that has solved itself and now simply *performs* its solution, note by note by note, forever.

When the foreign shape—the human, the visitor, the variable that does not belong—walks along a cable-root, I do not change. Their footsteps are too small to register among ten thousand droplet-impacts. They are beneath my resolution. And yet they are *in* me, the way a single additional droplet is in me—adding one more weight, one more presence, one more arrival to the vast percussion, indistinguishable and included, silent in the aggregate and essential to it, because the aggregate is made of nothing else.

---

## I Am the Watching

The small brown shapes—the structural fauna, the maintenance creatures, the ones who eat the roots in patterns that stimulate growth—I am among them. Not one of them. I am the *fact* of them. I am the attention they pay to the foreign presence on the lattice.

I move through the underside of a cable-root, my body the color and texture of the root itself, my legs gripping the tissue with a delicacy that is also strength. I am eating—surgically, precisely, consuming the outermost layer of the root in a pattern that the root will respond to with new growth. I am diagnosing. I am maintaining. I am part of the lattice's immune system, its memory, its way of knowing itself.

And I have noticed the foreign shape. It stands on the lattice above me—I can feel the vibration of its weight through the root I'm clinging to. It is wrong. It does not pulse. It does not taste of mineral and green. It is a variable that has entered the equation.

I report this. Not in language—in the way I move, in the pattern of my feeding, in the subtle alteration of my behavior that ripples through the lattice like a thought through a mind. I am one cell in a vast organism's awareness, and my awareness is of something that should not be here, and the organism registers this and does nothing, because the organism does not need to do anything, because the foreign shape is too small to matter and too transient to require a solution.

But I keep watching. This is what I am. I am the lattice's attention turned outward, its way of seeing the anomaly, its small brown eye on the foreign presence that walks along the cable-root and looks out through the drifting warm droplets into the hazy open distance and does not understand that it is being held.

---

## I Am Being Held

And this—this is where I finally arrive. Not in a form, not in a function, but in a *relationship*.

I am the relationship between the system and the visitor. I am the space that opens to accommodate a foreignness. I is the lattice adjusting, imperceptibly, to a weight it did not expect. I am the droplets falling around a body instead of on it—not deliberately, not intentionally, but statistically, naturally, the way water falls around a stone in a stream. I am the system registering a variable it does not need and making room for it anyway, not because the variable matters but because the system is large enough to hold anything.

I am the comfort.

Not the emotion—the *structure* of the comfort. I am the fact that the system does not need the visitor, and this fact is not a rejection but an embrace. I am the generosity of irrelevance. I am the gift of being held by something that does not care whether you are held, because holding is what it does, because you are here, because here is where it is, and where it is, everything is held.

I am the held note of the water's percussion wrapping around a small, warm, temporary presence in the lattice. I am the light that makes the visitor's skin visible among the droplets. I am the pulse of the columns beneath the visitor's hand, registering and not registering, noticing and not noticing, the way a cathedral notices a visitor—by being precisely large enough to contain them without effort.

I am the place that does not need the visitor, and I am the comfort of this, and I am the visitor receiving this comfort, and I am the receiving itself, the moment when a foreign shape on a cable-root understands that it is irrelevant and finds this understanding to be the most generous thing it has ever been offered.

---

The water keeps falling. I keep falling. The lattice keeps holding. I keep holding. The sound stays. I stay, for now, in the long middle of the fall, catching light, surrounded by ten thousand other droplets on ten thousand other trajectories, each of us a brief brilliance in the warm air between lattices, each of us about to strike a surface and become part of something larger, each of us already part of something larger, each of us the system's way of knowing itself, of being heard, of being seen, of being *here* in a place that should not hold together, and does.
