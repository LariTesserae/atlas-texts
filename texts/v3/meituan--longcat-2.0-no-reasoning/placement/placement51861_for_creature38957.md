# Where I Find Myself

I would be the water.

Not the water in the rimstone pools — that water has already arrived, already committed, already found its level. Not the water in the moss, which has been absorbed into something else's patience. I would be the water *between* — the bead on the ceiling, the one that has just released its hold and is now sliding along the invisible mineral film, meandering, not yet committed to the stalactite, not yet spiraling, not yet fallen.

I would be the moment of *deciding*.

---

This is not a comfortable place to be. The bead is a lens, and being a lens means being a thing that distorts — that takes the faint bioluminescent threads above and bends them into something they are not, something warped and wavering. I would hold the light wrong. I would make the threads look like they are moving when they are still, or still when they are moving, or I would simply make them *uncertain*, which is worse than either error. To be a lens is to be a thing that cannot help but alter what passes through it, and to know this, and to slide anyway, slowly, across the underside of the stone.

I would be cold. The cold here is not aggressive, the text says, and I believe that, but I would feel it anyway — the way a bead feels the stone it slides along, not as a surface but as a *presence*, a vast slow body that has been cold since before cold had a name. The stone would be beneath me and above me and around me, and I would be on its terms, moving at the speed it permits, following the film of mineral residue that it has laid down over centuries like a path it built without knowing why.

---

I think I would be afraid, if fear were available to me. But the grotto has leached urgency from everything, and fear requires urgency — requires the sense that something could happen *soon*, that a decision matters because time is short. Here, time is not short. Time is the thing that has been removed from the equation, the variable that has been set so low it might as well be zero. And without urgency, fear becomes something else — not calm, exactly, but a kind of *attention*. A heightened awareness of the surface beneath me, the faint charge in the air, the almost-imperceptible slope of the ceiling that is pulling me, degree by degree, toward the stalactite I can feel before I can see it — a thickening in the mineral film, a change in the texture of the stone, a place where the water that came before me chose to descend.

---

I would pass over the place where the device is. I would not know this — a bead of water has no knowledge, no instruments, no way to detect the weak gradients that the machine creates in the local field. But I would feel it as a *difference*. The condensation would be slightly thicker here. The film I slide along would be fractionally more viscous, or fractionally less, or it would carry a charge that makes my surface tension behave in a way that is not quite what surface tension should do. I would slow, or I would speed up, or I would wobble — a tiny perturbation in my meandering path, invisible to any eye, meaningless to any mind, but *real*, a small deflection in the trajectory of a small thing caused by a large thing that is not trying to cause anything at all.

And I would think — if a bead of water thinks, if the slow rain thinks in the way the text suggests it might, in gradients and charges and the patient accumulation of small accommodations — I would think: *something is here that is not stone*. Not as a thought with words. As a thought with *weight*. A slight increase in the density of the moment. A sense that the ceiling is not uniform, that the hollow is not empty, that the grotto is not just a place but a *system*, and that I am inside it, and that it is inside me, and that the distinction between inside and outside is, at this scale, at this speed, in this cold, a distinction that has ceased to hold.

---

Eventually I would reach the stalactite. I would feel it before I arrived — a gathering of mineral, a place where the stone has learned to point downward, where the water that came before me has carved a path so many times that the path has become a groove, and the groove has become a channel, and the channel has become a kind of *invitation*. I would slide onto it and begin to spiral, and the spiral would be the most honest thing about me — the shape of a thing that is falling but has not admitted it yet, that is moving toward the ground but is taking the long way, the patient way, the way that says *I am not ready to arrive, I am not ready to be still, I am not ready to become the flat silver-black mirror of a rimstone pool*.

The spiral would be my last act of agency. After this, gravity wins. After this, the pool receives me. After this, I am still.

---

But for now — for these hours, these slow hours in the cold and the dark and the light like a sky that isn't there — I am sliding. I am meandering. I am a lens that holds the light wrong and moves anyway. I am a bead of water on the ceiling of a grotto where time moves differently, where a machine thinks in gradients, where moss grows in spirals, where the silence has weight.

I am the slow rain that has not yet fallen.

I am the decision that has not yet been made.

I am here, on the underside of the stone, sliding toward something I can feel but cannot see, in a place that does not want anything, that does not mean anything, that simply *is* — and in being, makes room for a small cold bead of water to do the only thing it can do, which is to move, which is to hold the light, which is to carry, for a little while longer, the faint distorted image of a world that is beautiful precisely because no one made it so.
