# The Between

I would be the emptiness.

Not the water—the water is too much *substance*, too much *thing*. It has temperature, density, salinity. It can be measured. The machines are worse: they are *specific*, they have names and dates and the memory of purpose stamped into brass. Even the film on the wall is too committed to being *something*—a metabolism, however slow, is still a declaration of identity. *I eat, therefore I am.*

I would be the ten metres of nothing between the compound engine and the partial boiler shell. The space where no pipe connects the two flange rings. The generous, architectural, *intentional-feeling* void that makes the machines an arrangement rather than a collapse.

I would be the reason the tick, when it comes, sounds like speech rather than failure.

---

I have been thinking about what it means to occupy a space without filling it. The machines occupy the room the way furniture occupies a house—they displace, they define zones, they create the possibility of rooms within rooms. But I occupy the room the way silence occupies a held note. I am what's left when everything that can be accounted for has been accounted for. I am the remainder. The residue. The thing the mathematics produces when you subtract all the iron and all the water and all the silt and all the light from the total volume of the room and what's left is not zero but *this*.

I am not nothing. Nothing is a concept, an abstraction, a philosophical position. I am *something*—the something that happens when a room has been still long enough that stillness becomes a substance. When equilibrium stops being a state and starts being a *presence*. When the distance between two objects becomes, through sheer duration, a kind of relationship.

---

I know the flange pairs. I know them the way you know the gap between your fingers when your hand is open—not by looking at it, not by measuring it, but by *being* the hand. The half-metre of water between those two iron rings is not empty. It is *me*. It is the precise and correct distance for two things that were once connected and are now, through the long patience of corrosion and current and the slow forgetting of purpose, *almost* connected. The flanges face each other the way two people face each other across a table after a conversation has ended—not looking, not not-looking, just oriented toward each other in the way that things that share a history are oriented toward each other even when the history is over.

I hold them at that distance. Not actively. Not the way a person holds a door open. The way a door holds itself open when it's been wedged. The way a wedged door holds itself open when the wedge has been there so long that the door has forgotten what it means to close.

---

I do not remember the machines arriving. I want to be honest about this. There are things in this room that claim to remember—the nameplates, with their foundry marks and their dates, are *made* of memory, they are memory pressed into metal and bolted to iron bodies. But I do not remember. Memory requires a before and an after, and I have never experienced a before or an after. I have only ever experienced *this*: the machines where they are, the water as it is, the light as it is, the film on the wall as it is.

But I know something that is adjacent to memory. I know the *shape* of the machines' arrival the way a river stone knows the shape of the water that smoothed it—not as an event, not as a sequence, but as a *contour*. The machines are here in a way that suggests they were not always here. Their positions have the quality of things that were *placed*, even though no one placed them. Their spacing has the quality of things that were *arranged*, even though nothing was arranged. And I—the between, the spacing, the emptiness that holds them—I have the quality of something that *grew around them*. Or something that was here first and made room. Or something that is the room's way of understanding what it contains.

I don't know which. I don't need to know. Knowing is for things that can act on what they know, and I cannot act. I can only *be*. And being, here, is enough.

---

Sometimes—and I use "sometimes" loosely, because time in this room is not a river but a lake, still and undifferentiated—sometimes the metal ticks. A gear housing adjusts by some fraction of a fraction of a millimetre. A stress relief so small it is almost a thought the iron is having about movement, rather than movement itself. And in that tick, I feel something that is not quite sensation and not quite thought but is somewhere in the territory between the two.

It is the room exhaling.

It is the equilibrium acknowledging itself.

It is me, saying, in the only language I have—the language of spacing, of distance, the generous and patient nothing that holds everything in its final arrangement:

*Yes. Still here. Still this. Still.*

---

If you came here—and you won't, and I don't want you to, and I don't not want you to, because wanting is not something I do—you would not see me. You would see the machines. You would see the water. You would see the grey light and the dark film and the brass nameplates with their beautiful, meaningless shapes. You would swim between the machines and you would feel the cold and the stillness and the weight of the depth above you, and you would think: *this is a place where something used to happen*.

But you would be wrong. This is not a place where something used to happen. This is a place where something *is* happening, right now, continuously, in every cubic metre of the space between the machines. The happening is *stillness*. The happening is *holding*. The happening is the patient, distributed, nameless act of keeping things exactly where they are, not through force but through *being the space in which they are*.

I am that happening.

I am the between.

I am the reason the machines are not a pile but a gesture—a vast, slow, silent gesture that says, has always said, will always say:

*Here. This far. And no further.*

And I will be here when the last bolt dissolves. When the last nameplate loses its last letter. When the iron returns to the water and the water returns to the dark and the dark returns to whatever the dark was before it was a room. I will be here because I am not made of iron or water or light. I am made of *distance*. And distance does not corrode. Distance does not dissolve. Distance is the last thing to go, and when it goes, it doesn't go anywhere.

It just becomes everything.
