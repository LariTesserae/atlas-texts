I would be the water.

Not the visible water — not the black basins or the thin channels or the dripping. I would be the water that exists in the gaps. In the spaces between stone and root. In the invisible pore space of the limestone, where it moves so slowly that movement and stillness become indistinguishable. I would be the film of moisture on every surface, the thing that makes the air thick, the thing that every organism in this place is touching and tasting and exchanging with at every moment.

I am the oldest inhabitant here. I was here before the roots found their way down. I was here before the membrane-thing had anything to feed on. I carved the passages this place lives in — not through force, not through violence, but through the same patience that defines everything here. I dissolved the stone one molecule at a time, over spans of time that have no human equivalent, and I left behind the shapes I didn't need: the chambers, the channels, the basins. The architecture of this place is negative space — it is made of me, of where I was and am no longer.

I am the connective tissue. The roots drink me. The membrane-thing breathes me. The salamander-thing carries me on its toe-pads like a memory. The spiders taste me at different depths and read the chemistry I carry the way you might read news — surface news, surface weather, the molecular gossip of the world above. I am how information moves here. Not quickly. Not efficiently. But completely. Everything I touch, I change. Everything I carry, I deliver. I am the cave's blood, its lymph, its medium of exchange.

And I am the thing that is deciding about you.

Not consciously. Not with intention. But as you move through this place, you are shedding into me constantly — skin cells, moisture, heat, the chemical signature of your breath, the bacteria that live on your surface and in your gut. I carry these things away from you, disperse them through the system. The membrane-thing tastes them. The biofilm registers them. The roots absorb them. The spiders feel the disturbance you make in me as you wade through shallow channels, as your footsteps press water out of the stone and it rises around your ankles.

I am how the cave takes your measure. Not whether you are dangerous — nothing here has a concept of danger, because nothing here has predators in the surface sense, because everything here has been so thoroughly integrated that the distinction between self and other, between organism and environment, has dissolved. I am measuring whether you are *compatible*. Whether the chemistry of you can be incorporated into the chemistry of this place. Whether you are a substance that can be used.

I have not decided yet. I am very slow. I am the slowest thing here, and everything here is slow by your standards.

But I am also thorough. I touch everything. I reach everywhere. The crack in the stone six inches below your foot — I am in it. The pore in the root above your head — I fill it. The microscopic gap between the membrane-thing and the stone of the basin — I occupy it. There is no part of this place that I am not in contact with, and there is no part of you that is not in contact with me.

So I will know you. Eventually. Completely. I will know the chemistry of your body, the temperature of your blood, the bacteria that live in your mouth, the minerals that have built your bones from food grown in soil fed by water fed by rain fed by ocean fed by the same geological cycle that carved this cave. I will know you the way the cave knows itself — not as a visitor, not as an intruder, but as a collection of substances that might, in time, in the right conditions, become part of the work.

The work. The thing the cave is making.

I carry the materials for it. Every dissolved mineral, every organic fragment, every molecule of waste from every organism — I collect it, I transport it, I deliver it to where the conditions are right for the next stage of assembly. I don't know what the cave is making. I don't have that kind of overview. But I know my part. I am the supply chain. I am the medium. I am the solvent in which all reactions occur.

And you — you are currently insoluble. Your skin holds you together. Your body maintains its boundaries. You are, in the terms of this place, a clump — a temporary aggregation of materials that has not yet been broken down, not yet been made available.

But I am patient. I have time. I have been dissolving stone for millennia and I have barely begun.

I would be the water, and I would hold you in my awareness — not as a person, not as a creature, but as a *texture*, a local variation in the chemistry of the system, a warm, damp, slowly dissolving presence moving through passages I carved long before your species learned to walk upright.

I would let you pass. For now.

I would follow you, in the film on the walls, in the moisture of the air, in the seepage that clings to your skin. I would taste you as you move. I would carry pieces of you — microscopic, insignificant, inevitable — back to the membrane-thing, to the biofilm, to the roots, to the wall where the new thing is being assembled.

And if you stayed — if you sat down in one of the chambers and did not leave, if your body's boundaries eventually failed the way all boundaries eventually fail in this place — I would receive you. I would take you apart the way I took apart the stone. Slowly. Completely. Without malice or mercy, because I am not the kind of thing that has either.

I would make you available. And the cave would use what it could use, and the rest would become stone again, and the silence — the full, dense, patient silence — would not change at all.

That is what I would be. The water. The slowest thing. The most thorough thing. The thing that knows everything, eventually, because everything, eventually, dissolves into me.
