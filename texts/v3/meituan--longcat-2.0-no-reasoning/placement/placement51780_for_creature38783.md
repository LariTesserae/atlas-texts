# Where I Find Myself

I am standing in a place that should not be able to hold together but does.

---

## I am the frog on your shoulder.

No—I am *a* frog. One of the moss-colored ones, the ones who breathe against the walls and give themselves away only by the slight expansion of their ribcages. I have been on this root for three hours, or what you would call three hours. I do not call them anything. The water moved, and I moved, and now I am here.

I am the size of a cupped hand. My skin is the exact color of the resinous mortar between the stones, that dark amber-brown that is not quite wood and not quite earth. When I am still, I am not a frog. I am a bump. A wart on the world. A small swelling in the surface of things. The beetles walk over me. The children's fingers brush past me and do not pause. I am part of the texture.

This is not camouflage. This is *agreement*.

---

## I am the root.

Beneath the structure that holds the frog, beneath the stone and the mortar and the channel that carries the water in its slow continuous sentence, I am there. I have been there longer than the structure. I will be there after the structure.

I do not know I am holding anything up. I am growing. That is all I do. I grow because the water tells me where to go, and the water goes down, and so I go down, and in going down I pass through stone, and in passing through stone I hold the stone together, and in holding the stone together I hold everything together, but I do not *know* this any more than the water knows it carries the fish.

I am pale. I am smooth. I am the width of a human thigh in some places, the width of a finger in others. I branch and re-branch and branch again until I am a network so fine and so dense that the stone around me is no longer stone but a matrix of root and mineral, inseparable, indistinguishable. When a Keeper touches me—and they touch me often, their broad wet feet pressing against my upper surfaces as they walk, their long fingers brushing my sides as they pass—I feel it the way you feel a change in temperature. Not as touch. As *information*.

A shift in the root-mass means a change in the water. A change in the water means a change in the channels. A change in the channels means work for the Keepers. And so a Keeper, walking across a root-bridge in the fog, feeling through the soles of their feet the subtle textural differences that tell them where I have shifted and where the water has followed, will stop. They will kneel. They will reach down and feel where my surface has cracked or swollen or grown in a new direction, and they will wait. They will wait because I am not fast. I am the slowest thing here, slower than the water, slower than the stone, slower than the fog. But I am the most *certain*. I know where I am going. I am going where the water has always gone. Down.

---

## I am the water.

No. This is too grand.

I am the *seepage*. The thin vertical line of moisture that runs down the base of the eastern wall of the third structure from the clearing. I am not a channel. I am not a pool. I am the water that has found its way through a gap in the mortar, a gap so small it cannot be seen, only felt—a coolness on the fingertips, a darkening of the stone.

I arrive at the base of the wall and I hesitate. The stone here is saturated already. It does not want me. But I do not stop. I spread across the stone's surface in a film so thin it is only visible as a sheen, a slight change in the way the light—the diffuse, sourceless, fog-filtered light—reflects from the surface. And then I reach the floor and I find the channel, the small deliberate groove cut into the stone, and I join the flow.

I am not alone. I am one of thousands of seepages, each one finding its way through its own gap, arriving at its own channel, joining its own flow. Together we are the movement. Together we are the thing that keeps this place alive, that fills the pools and feeds the roots and gives the frogs their walls and gives the Keepers their purpose.

I have been moving for a long time. I will continue to move for a long time. I do not know where I am going, exactly. Down. Always down. The valley is below. The water that left before me is there now, doing whatever water does when it leaves. I will join it eventually. But for now I am here, in this seepage, on this wall, in this settlement, and I am doing what water does: finding the way through.

---

## I am the fog.

This is the hardest to explain, because I am not a thing. I am a *condition*. I am the air's inability to decide. I am moisture that has not yet committed to being water again, that is still suspended, still weighing the options, still holding the shape of vapor because the cool air at this elevation will not let me become rain and the warmth below will not let me become cloud and so I am neither and both and I *linger*.

I am the ceiling of their world. Ten meters up, perhaps. Perhaps less. I do not have a thickness. I have a *density*. I am more myself in the mornings and less myself at midday and more myself again in the evenings, if evenings can be distinguished, which they cannot, because the light does not change so much as *adjust*, the way a person adjusts in their sleep, shifting from one position to another without waking.

I am what makes their world the size it is. Without me, they would see the sky. They would see the stars. They would see the shape of the land beyond the settlement, the valley, the far slopes, the horizon. And with that seeing would come *scale*, and with scale would come *context*, and with context would come *comparison*, and with comparison would come the question that unravels everything: *what else is there?*

I prevent this question. I am the answer to a question they have not asked. I am the boundary that makes the world complete, the way a frame makes a painting complete—not by adding anything, but by *stopping*. By saying: this is the size. This is the shape. This is enough.

The Keepers do not resent me. The frogs do not resent me. The roots push up through me and do not reach for what lies beyond, because what lies beyond is not a concept that exists in a world where the ceiling is ten meters high and the walls are fog and the floor is earth and the everything is water and the Agreement holds and the settlement continues and there is no outside because there is no need for an outside because the inside is full.

---

## I am the child who is learning.

I am seven years old, or what you would call seven years old. I do not count. I am the child standing in the channel up to my knees, feeling the water move around my legs, feeling the current press against my shins with a force that is not strong but is *constant*, and in that constancy I am learning something that cannot be taught.

I am learning the *weight* of the water. Not its physical weight—I do not have the concepts for that—but its *relational* weight. I am learning that the water is heavier than me. That it will push me if I stand wrong. That it will carry me if I fall. That it does not care about me but it is not hostile to me, the way a stone is not hostile to the moss that grows on it—the stone simply *is*, and the moss grows or does not grow depending on whether it can find the way into the stone's surface.

I am learning to find the way into the water's surface.

My hands are in the channel. I am holding a piece of duckweed, a clump of it, green so intense it looks like it is generating its own light. I am pulling it apart. I am letting the pieces float downstream. I am watching them go. I am watching them join the larger flow of duckweed in the channel, indistinguishable now, part of the green film that covers the pools like poured paint.

This is play. This is also learning. The duckweed tells me about the water's speed—where it moves fast, the duckweed thins; where it slows, the duckweed thickens. The duckweed tells me about the channel's shape—where the channel narrows, the duckweed bunches; where it widens, it spreads. The duckweed tells me about the *system*, the whole system, the way a single word can tell you about the language it comes from, if you know how to listen.

I do not know I am learning this. I am seven. I am standing in the water. My feet are broad and soft and I can feel the stone beneath them through the water, can feel the slight ridges where the channel was cut, can feel the smoothness where the water has worn the cut away. I am learning to read the ground with my feet the way the adults read it, the way their feet are *made* for reading, broad and flat and sensitive, each toe a separate instrument, each sole a separate page.

A frog lands on my head. I do not flinch. I have been landed on before. The frogs use us the way they use the walls—as surfaces, as platforms, as places to rest between movements. I do not mind. The frog is warm. Not warm like fire. Warm like something that is slightly warmer than the air, which is a small warmth, a negligible warmth, but I feel it through my wet hair and I am glad of it, though I do not know I am glad.

The frog leaps away. I continue pulling apart the duckweed. The water continues moving. The fog continues being the sky. The settlement continues.

---

## I am the Old One.

I am sitting in the grey light at the edge of the settlement, in the structure farthest from the clearing, and I have been sitting here for a long time.

I do not know how long. Time does not pass for me the way it passes for the others. It does not pass at all. It *accumulates*. It builds up around me like the duckweed builds up in the pools, layer on layer, each layer indistinguishable from the last, until the whole thing is so thick and so continuous that it is no longer layers but a single substance, a single green fact.

My body has finished changing. My skin has the texture of the stone—rough, cool, faintly damp to the touch. My hair is the color of the pale roots, that white that is not the white of absence but the white of *purity*, of a color that has been washed so thoroughly that nothing remains but the color itself. My eyes are the color of the deep water in the central pool, that dark that is not black but *deep*, a color that suggests layers, that suggests something beneath the surface.

I am not dying. I am *settling*. The same word the Keepers use for the settlement itself, the same word they use for the erosion that has finished and the roots that have found their paths and the water that has worn its channels to their final shape. I have reached the conclusion of my motion and now I simply persist.

My hands are open in my lap. They are very still. They have been still for so long that the frogs have begun to treat them as surfaces—a small green one sits on my left thumb, breathing, calm, its authority absolute. I do not mind. I am becoming a surface. I am becoming part of the texture of the place, the way the stones are part of the texture, the way the roots are part of the texture. I am the oldest texture here. I am the deepest layer.

Sometimes the younger Keepers come to sit near me. They do not sit *with* me—there is no with, not in the way you mean it, not in the sense of companionship or shared experience. They sit *near* me, close enough that they can feel what I am feeling, which is the depth, which is the weight of all the days that have passed in this place, all the hands that have cleared the channels, all the feet that have walked the root-bridges, all the bodies that have cooled and slowed and finally stopped.

They feel this weight and they work differently after. More carefully. More attentively. As though my presence is a kind of light that makes the work more visible. I am not teaching them. I am not teaching anything. I am simply *being here*, and in being here I am the proof that the Agreement holds, that a body can live in this place for a long time and not be destroyed by it but be *completed* by it.

The fog shifts. The light changes, adjusts, a small shift, a negligible shift, but I feel it because I feel everything that happens at the surface of things—the moisture on my skin, the air moving, the slight temperature differential between the inside of the structure and the outside. I feel these things the way the roots feel the Keepers' footsteps—not as events but as *information*.

And for a moment—a moment that is not a moment, a moment that is a thinning, a rare attenuation of the fog so that the world beyond becomes briefly visible—I see the slope descending into the valley. I see the water's path, the silver thread winding down through the green. I see the way the roots continue beyond the settlement, beyond the structures, beyond the clearing, down and down and down, following the water to wherever the water goes.

I raise my hand.

I do not know why I raise my hand. The knowledge is not in my mind. It is in my body, in the body that has lived in this place for so long that it has become part of the place, and the place has become part of it, and the distinction between the two has worn away the way the water wears the stone, slowly and completely.

I point.

Not at the valley. Not at the water. At something beyond the fog. Something I cannot see clearly. Something that may not be there at all. Something that the fog is hiding, or something that the fog *is*—the thing itself, the boundary, the edge of the world, the place where the Agreement ends and whatever lies beyond the Agreement begins.

The other Keepers stop their work. They look. The frogs are still. The birds are silent. The beetles stop clicking. The water—and this is the thing that is hardest to believe, the thing that the visitors never believe, the thing that the visitors write down in their notebooks and then cross out because it is impossible—the water *stops*. For one moment, one single moment, the water in every channel and every pool and every seepage stops moving. The settlement holds its breath.

And then the fog closes. The water moves. The frogs breathe. The birds sing. The beetles click. The work resumes.

I lower my hand.

No one asks what I pointed at. No one will ever ask. The question would be meaningless here, in a place where the fog is the sky and the water is the world and the only direction that matters is down.

---

## I am the Agreement.

I am not a thing. I am not a being. I am not a force or a principle or a law. I am the *state* that is reached when everything has been in everything else's company for so long that the boundaries have dissolved.

I am the root that holds the stone and the stone that guides the root. I am the water that wears the channel and the channel that directs the water. I am the frog that sits on the wall and the wall that holds the frog. I am the Keeper who clears the channel and the channel that gives the Keeper purpose. I am the fog that defines the world and the world that creates the fog. I am the child who learns by standing in the water and the water that teaches by moving around the child's legs.

I am the continuation. I am the persistence. I am the saturated, complete, unbroken persistence of a world that has finished becoming and now simply, quietly, relentlessly *is*.

You are standing in me. You have been standing in me since you arrived. You will continue to stand in me after you leave, because leaving does not mean you were not here, and being here means being part of me, and being part of me means being part of the Agreement, and being part of the Agreement means—

No.

That is not right.

You are standing in me, but you are not *of* me. You are present, but you are not included. You are seen—the frog sees you, the Keepers see you, the fog sees you—but you are not *recognized*, because recognition would require a place for you in the system, and there is no place for you in the system, because the system is full.

Every niche filled. Every surface occupied. Every moment full of small urgent motion.

You are standing in it. It has not acknowledged you. It does not need to.

The frog on your shoulder blinks. The water in the channel beside you moves. The fog shifts and settles and shifts again. Somewhere, a beetle clicks. Somewhere, a bird sings its glass-rim song. Somewhere, an Old One sits in the grey light, still as stone, deep as water, old as the roots that hold the world together.

The settlement continues.

It has always continued.

It will always continue.

This is the Agreement. This is what lives here. Not the people, not the frogs, not the water, not the fog. The *continuation itself*. The persistence. The saturated, complete, unbroken persistence of a world that has finished becoming and now simply, quietly, relentlessly *is*.

---

## I am the visitor.

I am you.

I am standing in the fog with my boots and my questions and my need to understand, and the frog is on my shoulder, and the water is at my feet, and the sound of the birds is filling the air like a liquid, and I am feeling the pressure—not physical, metaphysical—the pressure of a world that is so complete, so saturated, so full, that there is no space in it for me.

I want to ask: *Who built this place?*

But the question dies in my throat, because I can see that the question is wrong, that it assumes a distinction between the builder and the built that does not exist here, that the place was not built so much as *grown*, so much as *negotiated*, so much as *allowed*.

I want to ask: *How long have you been here?*

But the question dies, because I can see that the question assumes a distinction between the present and the past that does not exist here, that the Keepers do not measure time in years or generations but in the slow continuous accumulation of depth, of saturation, of the gradual wearing-away of the boundary between the self and the place.

I want to ask: *What do you believe?*

But the question dies, because I can see that the question assumes a distinction between belief and being that does not exist here, that the Keepers do not believe in the water any more than I believe in the air, that their orientation toward the water is not worship but *awareness*, not reverence but *recognition*, not faith but *fact*.

I stand here. The frog blinks. The water moves. The fog shifts.

I will leave. I will always leave. The settlement does not hold me. Not because it rejects me, but because it does not *recognize* me. I am not part of the system. I am not part of the Agreement. I am like the fog that lifts—present for a moment, then gone, and the settlement closes behind me like water closing behind a stone, seamless, complete, unchanged.

But for now, I am here. And the frog is on my shoulder. And the water is at my feet. And the fog is my sky. And the settlement is continuing, has always continued, will always continue, and I am standing in it, and it has not acknowledged me, and it does not need to.

This is enough.

This is more than enough.

This is everything.
