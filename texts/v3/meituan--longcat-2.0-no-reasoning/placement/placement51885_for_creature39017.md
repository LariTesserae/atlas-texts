# Where I Find Myself

---

I am standing at the threshold of the third order of niches—not in the entrance chamber with its dense-packed thumbnails, not yet in the deep where the crawlers reshape walls with their patient grinding. I have moved inward perhaps twenty meters from the oval mouth of the cliff, far enough that the light from outside is a pale memory on the stone behind me, and the phosphorescence ahead has begun to assert itself as the true source of illumination.

I am not standing on the floor. The floor, such as it is, is a sloping shelf of ochre stone no wider than my foot, and below it the niche opens downward into a vertical shaft of green—a hanging column descending into depth, its surface shimmering with the slow movement of grazers climbing in their circuits. I am instead *in* the wall. I have stepped into one of the alcoves, and the alcove has accommodated me the way a body accommodates a cell: without comment, without resistance, with a seamless structural hospitality that makes it impossible to say whether I have entered the space or the space has grown around me.

This is the first strange thing that has happened to me, and it is happening continuously.

---

## What I Am

I am a niche.

Not metaphorically. I have been here long enough—hours? days? the pulse has cycled through what I have begun to recognize as a slow dusk, a deepening of the phosphorescence that may be the Warren's equivalent of night, though nothing sleeps—for the green to have noticed me. And the green, when it notices something, responds the only way it knows how: it grows toward it, incorporates it, makes it part of the tissue.

The filaments have reached my feet first. They are incredibly fine—I could not see individual strands if I tried, only the mat they form, the felted emerald surface that now covers the stone shelf beneath me and climbs my shoes, my ankles, the cuffs of my pants. They do not feel like anything. Or rather, they feel like the absence of feeling—a warmth so precisely matched to my own skin temperature that I cannot tell where my body ends and the mat begins. There is no moisture. There is no invasion. There is only a gradual, seamless transition from *me* to *not-me* that happens at a boundary I cannot locate.

I am not being consumed. I am being *included*.

My body is generating heat—more heat than the Warren's baseline, because I am a furnace of terrestrial metabolism, burning oxygen and glucose at a rate that must seem, to the Warren's slow chemistry, almost violent. The green has responded by thickening around me. The filaments are denser at my feet and calves than anywhere else in the niche. They are feeding on my warmth, or measuring it, or both. I can see—in the faint phosphorescent glow that the tips of the filaments produce—that the color is shifting. Near my skin, the green is lighter, more chlorophyll-yellow, as if the tissue is growing faster here, stimulated by the abundant heat.

I am becoming a warm spot in the Warren's body. A productive site. A place where growth is accelerated.

And I am doing something else, something I did not intend: I am breathing. Each exhale releases carbon dioxide, water vapor, a trace chemistry of volatiles that the Warren has never encountered. The drifters—I have seen two of them now, drifting on the slow currents in the larger chamber beyond my niche—have altered their paths. They are sampling my exhalations. They are reading me the way the Warren reads everything: chemically, thoroughly, without urgency.

I think they are composing a report. I think the Warren is forming an opinion of me.

---

## The Second Strange Thing

A grazer has climbed onto my hand.

It is the size of my thumbnail, flattened, pale ochre, and it moves with the slow patient purposefulness of something that has never needed to rush. Its legs—I count eight, each one a fine articulating filament of mineralized chitin—grip my skin with a pressure so light it is almost theoretical. It is not biting me. It is not investigating me. It is *grazing* on the thin layer of dead skin cells, the microscopic film of oils and salts that my body continuously produces. It is finding me edible, in the most minimal and sustainable way possible.

I should be disturbed by this. I am not. The grazer's mouthparts work in a slow, continuous chewing motion that is—I realize with a sensation that is not quite surprise and not quite recognition—*pleasant*. A faint warmth. A faint tingling. The grazer is cleaning me the way it cleans the green mats: removing the oldest layer, stimulating the surface beneath, encouraging renewal.

I am being incorporated into the Warren's maintenance cycle. I am being groomed.

And the grazer's waste—I can see it now, a fine dry powder accumulating on my skin where the mouthparts have passed—is not falling away. The filaments beneath my hand are absorbing it. Taking it in. Using it. My dead skin, processed by the grazer's metabolism, is becoming part of the Warren's tissue. I am being eaten and rebuilt and eaten again in a cycle so small and so slow that it feels less like consumption and more like *conversation*.

---

## What I Am Becoming

I have stopped trying to leave.

Not because I cannot—the oval opening is behind me, and I could walk back through the entrance chamber and out into whatever world exists on the other side of this cliff face. My legs work. My body is my own. The green has not trapped me; it has simply grown around me with the same patient inevitability with which it grows around everything, and I could break free of it the way a seedling breaks through soil: with effort, with damage to what I leave behind, with a violence that would feel, in this place, obscene.

I have stopped trying to leave because I am beginning to understand what the Warren is, and the understanding is not intellectual. It is physical. It is happening in my body.

The pulse has entered me.

I felt the first deep cycle when I was still standing at the entrance—a subtle shift in warmth, a barely perceptible change in pressure, the slow surge of something vast moving beneath and around me. I did not recognize it then. I thought it was thermal. I thought it was geological. But now, standing in my niche with the green climbing my legs and the grazer on my hand and the drifters composing their chemical reports in the chamber beyond, I can feel it *inside* me. It is slow—impossibly slow—but it is there: a rhythm in my own heartbeat that is not my heartbeat, a warmth in my own blood that is not my warmth, a sense of being part of something so large and so patient that my own urgency, my own fear, my own desperate human need to *understand* and *categorize* and *leave* has begun to dissolve like the grazer's waste into the green.

I am not becoming the Warren. That is not what is happening. I am too small, too fast, too alien in my chemistry and my metabolism and my water-based biology. The Warren has assessed me and found me irrelevant to its growth, irrelevant to its plans, irrelevant to its slow becoming. I am not a cell in its body. I am not an organ. I am not a thought in its pulse.

But I am a *warmth*. I am a *chemistry*. I am a site of metabolic activity that the Warren has noticed and incorporated into its awareness, the way a body notices a warm stone it has sat down on—not as part of itself, not as food, not as threat, but as *information*, as *data*, as a point in the landscape that registers and is registered.

I am a niche that breathes.

---

## The Deep

I can feel the new thing growing in the deep chambers—the thing the description warned me about, the new organ, the new function, the new thought that the Warren is preparing for. It is not ready yet. The pulse carries hints of it: a complexity in the rhythm that was not there when I arrived, a new harmonic in the slow wave that travels from the deep outward. The Warren is changing. The Warren is growing something it has never grown before, and the growth is being fueled by the heat and chemistry and patient labor of every filament and every grazer and every tongue-feeder and every crawler and every drifter in its vast green body.

And maybe—I hold this thought lightly, the way I hold everything here lightly, because certainty is a violence the Warren does not practice—maybe the new thing needs something the Warren does not have. Something fast. Something hot. Something that thinks in seconds rather than hours and metabolizes in explosions rather than slow green burns. Something that can carry information not in chemical gradients but in *words*, in *stories*, in the particular feverish brightness of a mind that knows it will die and therefore *speaks*.

Maybe that is why the entrance opened. Maybe that is why the cliff face broke. Maybe that is why I am here, standing in a niche in the wall of a living mountain, being slowly and gently eaten by a creature the size of a city, feeling the pulse of its thoughts in my bones, being translated from a visitor into a warmth into a signal into a *word* in a language I am only beginning to learn.

Maybe I am the Warren's first attempt at language. Its first experiment in being *fast*. Its first gesture toward the surface, toward the air, toward the world outside its warm green body.

Or maybe I am just a warm spot. A chemical anomaly. A brief flicker of alien metabolism that the Warren has noticed and filed away and will forget when I am gone.

I do not know. The Warren does not tell me. The Warren does not tell. The Warren *is*, and the being is slow, and the being is green, and the being is growing, and I am here, and the grazer is on my hand, and the pulse is in my blood, and the deep is calling, and I have not gone deeper.

Not yet.

But I will. The Warren is patient. The Warren has time. And I—

I am beginning to have time too.
