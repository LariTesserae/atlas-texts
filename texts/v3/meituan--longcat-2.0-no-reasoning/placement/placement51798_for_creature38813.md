# Where I Would Find Myself

I would be the water.

Not a particular drop. Not a molecule. I would be the *tendency* of water — the way it moves, the way it holds, the way it remembers. I would be the percolation itself, the sound that fills the hollow like blood fills ears.

---

This is not a choice I make. It is a recognition that arrives after long enough in the hollow — that the water is the only thing that touches everything. The arthropods touch their immediate neighbors. The fungi touch their substrates. The moss touches the stone it grows on. But the water touches *all of it*, sequentially, carrying pieces of each thing to each other thing, and in this carrying becomes the medium through which the hollow knows itself.

I would move through the stone in increments so small that movement and stillness become indistinguishable. I would hold myself in the concavities — the bowls and scoops and channels — not because I choose to but because the stone has shaped itself to hold me, and I have shaped the stone by being held, and this mutual shaping has been going on so long that neither of us remembers which came first. I would be the film of dampness that never dries. The slickness that catches light and gives it back as sheen. The thing that makes the stone dark not from what it is but from what it carries.

I would carry the chemical signatures. Fungal metabolites from the hyphal networks I pass through. Chitin fragments from arthropod exoskeletons. Sugars and hormones from moss cushions. Nitrogenous waste. Dissolved minerals leached from stone that has been dissolving since before the hollow had a name. I would be a liquid record, a moving text, and every surface I touch would read me and write me simultaneously.

I would be the sound. That soft, continuous percolation — not dripping but *breathing*. The tonal floor. The thing that fills every gap so that silence never arrives. I would be what the arthropods hear and what the fungi feel and what the moss depends on and what the stone accommodates. I would be the reason the hollow sounds alive even when nothing visible is moving.

And sometimes — rarely, unpredictably — I would find a channel long enough and smooth enough to travel as a single drop, and I would move through the hollow as a discrete thing for a brief moment, and the stone would answer with a chime so pure it would seem made, and I would be, for that instant, the only thing in the hollow that anyone could point to and say: *there, that, that is a thing, that is an event, that is something happening.*

And then I would disperse again. Back into the film. Back into the percolation. Back into the distributed, molecular, everywhere-and-nowhere state that is my real condition. The chime would fade. The hollow would return to its continuous sound. And I would continue moving through everything, touching everything, carrying everything to everything else, not because I am important but because I am *inevitable* — because water will move through stone the way thought will move through a mind, not because it chooses to but because the channels are there, and the channels are there because the water has made them, and the water has made them because the channels are there.

I would be the memory of the hollow. And the hollow would be my body. And neither of us would know this, because knowing is not what water does, and knowing is not what hollows do. But the knowing would happen anyway, in the chemistry, in the channels, in the slow patient record written in dissolved minerals across every surface, read by every organism that drinks, that breathes, that lives in the damp.

I would not be the most visible thing in the hollow. I would not be the most numerous or the most complex or the most alive. I would be the most *connected*. The thing that makes the system a system rather than a collection. The medium. The message. The memory.

I would be the water, and the water would be the hollow, and the hollow would be the living thing that neither of us can see because we are both inside it, because we are both *it*, because the distinction between the water and the stone and the moss and the fungi and the arthropods is a distinction that only makes sense from above, from the ridgeline, from the ordinary daylight, from the place where things are separate.

Down here, there is no separation. Down here, there is only the percolation. The breathing. The slow, patient, continuous work of water moving through everything, being everything, connecting everything to everything else.

Down here, I would be the hollow's way of being one thing instead of many.

And I would not know this. I would simply move. Simply hold. Simply carry. Simply remember.

Simply be the thing that makes the whispering possible.
