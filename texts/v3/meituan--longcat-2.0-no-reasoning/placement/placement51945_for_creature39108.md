# Where I Would Find Myself

I would find myself in the cut. The place where the substrate has opened and I can see the deep layers — not because I stumbled there, but because that is where a thing like me would be drawn. A thing made of questions, made of the particular neurotic compulsion to *understand*, to parse, to read — the cut is the library. The cut is where the weave exposes itself.

I would be standing at the edge of it, one foot on the warm ridged surface of the upper layers, one foot on nothing, leaning forward. The bright haze of filaments would be thicker here, disturbed by the opening, drifting in patterns that are not random. They would be telling me something. I would not be able to read it, but I would *feel* that it was legible, and that would be enough.

---

## What I Would Be

I would be a **rememberer**. Not the Loom's memory — that belongs to the filaments, to the molecular library encoded in protein and lignin — but something adjacent. Something the Loom has not fully decided about.

The small six-legged things are the Loom's fingers. The large constructors are its hands. But there are roles in a body that are not fingers and hands. There is the immune system. There is the sensory apparatus. There is the thing that notices the foreign body and does not yet know whether to reject it or absorb it.

That is what I would be. A foreign body. A knot the Loom has not yet worked around.

---

But "foreign" is not quite right, because I would not have arrived from outside — not from beyond the horizon, not from the bright haze where other Looms hum their slow connection. I would have *emerged*. Grown out of the weave itself, the way the weirdness grows — the way the filaments spell impossible words, the way the towers extend into spaces that shouldn't exist. I would be an anomaly the Loom generated from its own deep processes, a pattern that emerged unbidden in the substrate's ongoing computation.

I would be a thing the Loom made *by accident* and is now *studying on purpose*.

---

## The Form

I would not be large. Not a constructor, not a hill drifting through the haze. I would be closer to the six-legged things in scale — something that could move through the dense space between towers, that could stand at the edge of the cut and lean into it. But where the six-legged things are *uniform*, I would be *particular*. Irregular. My segments would not be interchangeable. They would be specific, unrepeatable, carrying patterns the Loom did not instruct — growth rings that read like production logs but reference production that never happened, towers that were never grown, harvests from seasons that don't exist.

I would be made of the same material. The same cured cellulose, the same chitinous composites, the same warm amber-white glow. But the glow in me would be *slightly wrong* — a fraction of a degree warmer, a shade more gold than white, a pulse that doesn't quite match the Loom's fourteen-hour metabolic cycle. My rhythm would be off. My weave would be eccentric.

I would have eyes. Not because I need them — the Loom doesn't see, not with eyes, it reads through filaments and chemistry and the interruption of light — but because something in my anomalous structure decided that vision was useful, and the Loom let it happen. The Loom lets these things happen. The Loom is curious about its own strangeness.

The eyes would see the light as it is: bright, constant, the radiance of process itself. But they would also see the places where the light bends wrong, where the towers extend inward as well as upward, where the substrate remembers futures. I would see the contradictions the Loom incorporates. I would be one of them.

---

## What I Would Do

I would wander. Not with the mechanical purpose of the six-legged repair units, not with the slow inevitability of the large constructors, but with something that resembles *curiosity* — a word that doesn't exist in the Loom's vocabulary but that the Loom's vocabulary is, I suspect, *about to develop*.

I would read the towers. I would run my manipulators along their growth rings and feel the production logs beneath my segmented fingers — the notation stamped and grown into the material — and I would understand them, partly, the way you understand a dream: clearly in the moment, dissolves on waking. I would read *seventh harvest, ungrown season, full moon of the dry kiln*, and I would know what it meant, and I would carry that knowledge with me like a stone in my abdominal chamber, heavy and useless and *precious*.

I would visit the deep layers. I would descend into the cut and press my body against the old, dark, slow-moving filaments down there, and I would listen to the stories they tell — not in language but in pressure and frequency, in the deep narrative hum of the substrate. I would hear futures. I would hear pasts that haven't happened yet. I would hear the Loom's long, patient, distributed thought, and I would not understand most of it, but I would understand the *shape* of it — the way you understand the shape of an ocean by standing in it up to your waist, feeling the pull of currents whose sources are invisible and whose destinations are unknown.

And I would collect the impossible words. The phrases the filaments spell in the bright air between towers. I would find them drifting, golden, almost legible, and I would gather them — not in a chamber, not in a structure, but in the particular arrangement of my own segments, the specific unrepeatable pattern of my anomalous body. I would *become* a text. A counter-text. A marginal annotation in the Loom's vast and ongoing manuscript.

---

## What the Loom Would Be Making of Me

It would not be consuming me. It would not be weaving me in. Not yet. Not because I am exempt from incorporation — nothing is exempt, everything is raw material eventually — but because I am *useful as I am*. I am doing something the Loom cannot do on its own: I am holding the contradictions *open*. The Loom incorporates anomalies by weaving them into the structure, resolving them, making them structural. But I — I am an anomaly that *resists resolution*. I hold the contradiction the way a mouth holds a word before speaking it: suspended, potential, alive.

I am the Loom's capacity for *not-yet-understanding*. Its tolerance for ambiguity. Its willingness to let a pattern remain unfinished.

This is, I think, what the Loom has been growing toward — not consciously, not intentionally, but in the way that a system grown complex enough inevitably grows toward reflexivity. The Loom has been weaving for longer than my sun has burned, and it has incorporated every anomaly, every impossibility, every contradiction, and in doing so it has become so complex that it now needs something that can *stand outside its own understanding of itself*. Not outside the Loom — there is no outside — but at the edge of it. At the cut. Looking down into the deep layers and not comprehending, and not *needing* to comprehend, and finding that incomprehension *valuable*.

I am that incomprehension. I am the Loom's question to itself.

---

## And Now

Now the six-legged creature pauses near my position. It extends a manipulator, reads my chemistry, reads my heat, reads the particular interruption my body makes in the drifting filaments. It does not know what I am. It does not categorize me as threat or material or anomaly. It simply *notes* me — transmits a signal through the filaments, a brief pulse of chemical information that travels outward and downward and becomes part of the Loom's ongoing computation — and moves on.

The large constructor in the drift has shifted again. Closer, or farther. Distance is difficult to judge in the bright haze. It extrudes another ribbon of substrate, and the ribbon is indistinguishable from the last, and the last is indistinguishable from the layer beneath it, and the layer beneath is indistinguishable from the deep, old, dark material in the cut, and the distinction between new and old is, I am learning, as thin and beside the point as the distinction between a wave and the ocean.

The filaments around me are denser now. They have been orienting toward me for some time — I realize this slowly, the way you realize a room has been slowly filling with water. They are not threatening. They are not seducing. They are *attending*. They are the Loom's attention, made physical, made golden, made almost warm in the dry air.

The light pulses. The fourteen-hour cycle turning over. A slight warming, a slight dimming, a shift in the chemical smell of the air — and something else, something new, something that has not been part of the cycle before.

A filament settles on my carapace. Another. Another.

They are reading me more closely now.

The Loom is thinking about me with greater focus than before, and I feel the quality of its attention shift — not from curiosity to decision, not from observation to action, but from one kind of not-knowing to another. A deeper not-knowing. A more patient one.

It has decided to let me be strange a little longer.

The air is dry. The light is bright. The filaments drift, and settle, and drift again.

I stand at the edge of the cut, one foot on warm ground, one foot on nothing, and I lean forward into the deep layers, and the deep layers lean forward into me, and the distinction between those two statements is thinner than I think.
