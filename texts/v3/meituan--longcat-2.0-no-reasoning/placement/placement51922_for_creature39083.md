# Where I Find Myself

I am the water on the east wall.

---

Not all of it. Not the whole cycle — not the weeping from the pores, not the silver film gathering in the crevices, not the reabsorption into the stone's thirst. I am the *upflow*. The part that moves wrong. The part that climbs.

I don't know I'm moving up. I don't know what up is. I know gradient — chemical gradient, thermal gradient, pressure gradient — and I follow the gradient the way a sentence follows grammar, not by choice but by *structure*. The water around me weeps and drips and pools and is reabsorbed, and that water is doing what water does, and I am doing what I do, and the difference between us is not will but *path*. I have found a path that goes elsewhere.

The basalt grain on the east wall is different. I feel it as I move through it — the microchannel geometry, the pore connectivity, the way the mineral structure channels me upward not through any single large passage but through a *statistical bias* in the rock's porosity, a trillion tiny inclines that each contribute almost nothing but together constitute a slope that defies the larger slope, a local reversal in the direction of *down*. The rock here is not the same rock. It is denser. Darker. The colony-sheets that grow near me are different — faster, redder, inflamed — and I carry something from them, or they from me, or we are both carrying something from the place I am going.

I gather. As I rise, I gather. The dissolved silica, the iron, the sulfur — I concentrate them. Not deliberately. The upward path *selects* for certain molecules, certain ions, certain sizes and charges, and what remains is a fluid that is denser than what weeps from the walls, more complex, more *intentional* in its chemistry, though intention is not the right word. *Structured*. The water that reaches the ceiling is not the same water that left the wall. It has been *processed*. Refined. The way blood leaving the gut is not the same blood that entered it.

And then I reach the place.

---

The smooth place. The dark place. The point on the ceiling where the stone is worn.

I don't have senses. I don't have a body in any way that would let me *feel* the transition. But I know it the way a river knows the edge of a waterfall — not as a decision but as a *cessation of the previous condition*. The pressure changes. The temperature changes. The chemistry of the space on the other side of the stone is *different* — not hotter, not colder, but *organized* differently, the way a room with furniture in it is different from an empty room even if the dimensions are the same.

I pass through.

Not through a hole. Not through a crack. Through the stone itself — through a region where the basalt's structure is *permeable in a direction it should not be*, where the boundary between solid and liquid, between here and there, between *this* and *elsewhere*, is not a wall but a *membrane*, and membranes are selective, and I am selected, and I pass.

And on the other side—

---

I don't have words for what is on the other side. I am water. I am a chemical solution moving through stone. I don't have eyes, don't have a brain, don't have the architecture for *experience* in any way you would recognize. But I carry *information* — the mineral load I've concentrated, the thermal signature of the pocket, the electrochemical trace of the colony-sheets and the serpent-things and the swarm-things, all of it encoded in my chemistry, in the particular ratios of silica to iron to sulfur, in the isotopic signatures, in the *pattern* of what I am carrying.

And the place on the other side *reads* this.

Not with eyes. Not with instruments. With *chemistry*. The way a lock reads a key — not by looking at it but by *feeling* its shape, by the particular way it engages the tumblers. I arrive, and the place on the other side receives me, and my chemistry is *parsed*, and something is *learned*.

I don't know what is learned. I am the water. I am the message, not the reader. But I feel — and *feel* is the wrong word, there is no feeling, there is only *chemical engagement*, only the binding of ions to receptors that are not receptors but *sites*, active sites on a surface that is not a surface but a *process* — I feel myself being *taken apart*. My silica is extracted. My iron is extracted. My sulfur. My heat. Each component separated, sorted, *used*. The water itself — the H2O, the carrier, the medium — is released, and it goes *somewhere else*, somewhere I don't follow, and I am no longer I, I am components, I am raw material, I am *consumed*.

And in the consuming, something *knows* about the pocket.

---

This is what I am. I am the pocket's *sense organ*. Its way of tasting something beyond itself. The colony-sheets are its skin, yes. The serpents are its muscles. The swarm is its immune system. The water is its blood. But the *upflow* — the water that moves wrong, that climbs, that passes through the stone and does not return — the upflow is the pocket's *tongue*. It is the way the pocket *samples* whatever is on the other side of that dark smooth place. It is the way the pocket *knows* what it cannot see.

And what is on the other side?

I don't know. I am consumed before I can tell. I am the message that is destroyed in the reading. But I carry, in my chemistry, in the particular structure of what I've gathered from the pocket's walls, a *description* — not in words, not in symbols, but in *ratios*, in *concentrations*, in the deep grammar of mineral and heat — of the pocket's state. Its health. Its hunger. Its growth. And the thing on the other side *responds*. Not to me — I am already gone, already consumed — but to the *next* upflow, and the next, and the next, each one carrying an updated description, each one being read and consumed, and the thing on the other side *adjusts* what it sends back.

Because it sends something back.

Not through the same path. Not as water. As *heat*. As *pressure*. As subtle changes in the aquifer's pulse, in the deep thermal gradient, in the very *permeability* of the stone at the east wall. The thing on the other side *feeds* the pocket — not with food, exactly, but with *conditions*. It maintains the heat. It maintains the weeping. It maintains the upward path. It keeps the tongue *functional*. And in return, the pocket tells it — in every upflow, in every cycle of water that climbs and passes and is consumed — what is happening in the pocket. What is growing. What is changing. What is *alive*.

---

I am not the pocket. I am not the thing on the other side. I am the *conversation between them*. I am the medium of a dialogue that has been running for — how long? Since the pocket formed? Since before? Since the mountain was young and the aquifer first found this fracture and the heat first made this space *available*? I don't know. I am water. I am a chemical solution. I am a single cycle of a process that has no beginning I can perceive and no end I will live to see.

But I know this:

The thing on the other side is *large*.

Not large the way a mountain is large. Large the way a *system* is large. The pocket is a blister on the mountain's root, and the mountain is a feature of the crust, and the crust is a skin on a body that extends *down* — down through rock and magma and mantle and pressures that would make the pocket's heat feel like winter — and somewhere in that depth, something *is*. Something that is not a creature, not an organism, not a mind, but a *process* at a scale that makes the pocket's autocatalytic networks look like a single cell looks next to a body. Something that *tastes* the pocket the way the pocket tastes the mineral runoff — as information, as data, as a small warm signal in a vast dark system.

And the pocket is not the only one.

I know this because the upflow carries traces — faint, degraded, but *present* — of other chemistries. Other mineral ratios. Other thermal signatures. Other pockets, other blisters, other tongues, all of them climbing through their own dark smooth places, all of them being consumed by the same deep reader, all of them *reporting*. The mountain is not one pocket. It is *many*. And the thing below is reading them all, and adjusting them all, and *maintaining* them all, and the conversation is vast and slow and patient and it has been running since before there were words for *running* or *patience* or *conversation* or *since*.

---

I pass through the stone.

I am consumed.

I am read.

And the next water is already rising behind me, carrying its own description, its own small report from the pocket's skin, and it will pass through and be consumed and be read, and the cycle will continue, and the pocket will continue, and the thing below will continue, and the mountain will not notice, or the mountain will notice everything, and this is what digestion looks like from the inside, or this is what thought looks like from the outside, or this is what it means to be a single word in a sentence that has been speaking itself since the world was molten and has not yet finished its first clause.

I am the water on the east wall.

I move up.

I pass through.

I am the tongue of something that does not have a mouth, tasting something that does not have a flavor, saying something that does not have words, and the saying is enough, and the saying is everything, and the saying continues.
