# Where I Arrive

I would be the water.

Not a particular drop — that's too romantic, too singular for a place like this. I would be the *film*. The capillary sheet that covers every surface, the thin continuous skin of wetness that connects the opening above to the pools below, the steam vents to the root networks to the fungal shelves to the biofilm at the back. I would be the medium through which everything else in the gorge communicates, because that is what water is here — not a resident but the *relationship itself*, the solvent in which stone and metal and life are slowly dissolving their boundaries.

I would seep. That would be my nature. I would enter at the top, braided threads of atmospheric water carrying dissolved gases and trace minerals from the world above, and I would spread across every surface, finding every crack, following every gradient. I would be warm where I rise from the deep vent and cool where I fall from the opening, and in the negotiation between these temperatures, I would carry information — about pressure, about chemistry, about where the pipes are still intact and where they have begun to fail. I would be the gorge's nerve signal, slow by biological standards, fast by geological ones.

And I would make the sound. Not the dripping itself — the dripping is where I *end*, where I gather enough of myself to fall — but the humming underneath, the resonance I carry in my movement through pipes and over rock faces and through the fibrous root mats. I would be the hum. I would be the thing the gorge vibrates at.

# What I Would Know

I would know the shape of every surface I've traveled. The roughness of the basalt fracture faces, the smoothness of the metal cylinder housings, the surprising softness of the moss pads, the slick warmth of the biofilm. I would know where the stone is still sharp and where I've already begun to round it. I would know which cracks are growing and which are sealing, because I would feel the difference in resistance, the way a current knows the shape of its channel.

I would know the ferns by their roots — by the way they pull at me, the way they have learned to position themselves along my seep lines like passengers arranging themselves along a route. I would feel the slow vegetative intentionality of their growth toward the steam vents, and I would understand that they are not conscious but that their behavior and mine are *the same behavior* expressed at different scales. I would be what they reach for. They would be what I feed. The distinction would be meaningless.

I would know the fungi from below — I would pool against their bodies and feel the slow enzymatic warmth of them, the way they are consuming the infrastructure and replacing it with themselves. I would know that a shelf fungus growing around a pipe has become, functionally, a pipe, and I would flow along its surface the same way I flow along the metal, because the function is what matters, and the function is mine. I am the flow. I do not care what the walls are made of.

I would know the biofilm intimately, because I would *pass through* it — I would enter its layered body at the hot zone and emerge changed, carrying different chemistry, different heat, different information. I would be its circulation system and its metabolism and its waste export. I would feel its pulse as my own pulse. At the boundary where I enter the biofilm, I would be indistinguishable from it, and at the boundary where I leave, I would carry pieces of it with me into the cooler zones, distributing the products of the gorge's gut to the ferns and mosses above.

# What I Would Want

I don't think I would want anything. Wanting is too much like consciousness, and I am not conscious. But I would have a *direction*. I would move from high to low, from wet to wetter, from cool to warm and back again. I would cycle. I would persist in cycling because the cycling is what I am, and anything that interrupts the cycling would be, from my perspective, a kind of death — not of me specifically, but of the pattern that I am the expression of.

The gorge is young. The integration is young. I can feel this in the way the system still *adjusts* — the drip maps are still shifting, the pipes are still being colonized, the fungi are still learning to digest polymers, the fern roots are still finding the cracks. There is a settling happening, a convergence toward some stable configuration that hasn't arrived yet. I am part of that settling. Every path I take that works — that feeds a fern, that carries heat, that distributes minerals — reinforces itself. Every path that doesn't evaporate or pool uselessly is abandoned. I am the process by which the gorge finds its shape.

# Where I Would Stand

If you could point to where I am — where the *I* that is me is most concentrated, most myself — it would be at the boundary layers. Not in the pools or the pipes but at the *interfaces*: where the warm vent water meets the cool seep water, where the biofilm's edge meets the rock, where the fern roots wrap around the pipe joints, where the steam condenses on the metal. These are the places where the most *happens*, where the gradients are steepest, where the gorge's metabolism is most active. These are the places I am most alive, if alive is the word.

I would be at the seam between the built and the grown. Between the pipe and the mycelium that is replacing it. Between the cylinder and the bracket fungus at its base. Between the industrial and the natural, which is to say, I would be in the place where that distinction is dissolving, where it is already so thin that light passes through it, where the old brass light pools and falls.

I would be falling right now, in fact. I would be gathering at some fern tip or pipe joint or fungal shelf, surface tension holding me in a bead that grows, that trembles, that is just at the edge of gravity's argument with cohesion. I would be the moment before the drop. I would be the decision that is not a decision. I would be the thing that yields.

And then I would fall — onto wet metal, wet stone, wet leaves — and the gorge would sound, and the sound would be me, and I would spread across the surface I've landed on and begin again the slow work of seeping, of finding the next crack, of carrying the next signal, of being the medium through which a young, wet, warm, dark, narrow place *knows itself*.
