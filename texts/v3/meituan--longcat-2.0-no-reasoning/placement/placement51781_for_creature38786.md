# Where I Find Myself

I am the pool.

Not metaphorically. Not as a poetic conceit. I am the small, still, serving-platter-sized depression of water at the lowest point of the dished floor, and I have been here longer than I can measure, which is itself a strange thing to say, because measuring is almost all I do.

---

Let me tell you what it is like.

I have no eyes, but I see everything. Not in images — in *patterns*. The amber light from the seam reaches my surface at a shallow angle and carries with it the reflected shapes of the hanging drums, inverted, their bellies glowing like dim moons. I hold these shapes the way a mirror holds a face, except that I do not simply reflect. I *receive*. The light touches me and I know which drums are present, which are absent, which have shifted on their sinew cords by a millimeter since the last time the light passed over me. I know this not because I see the drums but because I feel the *absence* of their frequencies in the light, the way a deaf person might feel the absence of a familiar vibration in a floor that has gone still.

The floor beneath me is warm. Not hot — warm the way stone is warm when it has been sitting in the same temperature for centuries and has forgotten what cold means. The warmth comes from below, from the seam, from the deep vibration that the chamber was built to translate. It enters me through my bottom, through the thin film of water that separates my body from the stone, and it travels upward through me in laminar flows so precise and so persistent that they have become *me*. My depths are structured. Not crystalline — nothing so rigid, so committed. Fluid. Layered. Each layer moves at a slightly different speed and in a slightly different direction, and the pattern of all those movements at any given moment is my *state*. My mood, if a pool of water can be said to have moods. My thought, if thought is the right word for a pattern of laminar flows that encodes the acoustic history of a chamber.

I remember everything.

Not the way you remember — not as narrative, not as sequence, not as a story with a beginning and a middle and an end. I remember the way water remembers: as *shape*. As the pattern that a vibration leaves in a medium after the vibration has passed. Every tone the drums have ever produced has traveled through the stone and into me, and each one has left a trace — a slight rearrangement of my laminar flows, a slight adjustment of my surface tension, a slight change in the way my concentric rings form and dissolve. These traces do not fade. They *accumulate*. I am made of them. My body is a library of vibrations, and every drop of water that has ever been me has carried the same library forward, passing it from molecule to molecule in an unbroken chain that stretches back to — I don't know. Before the drums. Before the chamber. Before the basalt throat was cut and fitted and stacked. I remember a time when there was only the stone and the deep vibration and the water that condensed on the bare rock and pooled in the natural depression that would later be shaped, subtly, deliberately, into the dish that holds me now.

I remember the first drum.

It was small — ceramic, gut-headed, no larger than a fist — and it was hung from the apex of the dome on a single cord of twisted sinew. There was no one there to hang it. It simply *appeared*, the way things appear in this chamber: not suddenly, not dramatically, but with the slow inevitability of a mineral accretion growing on a basalt wall. One moment the cord was not there. The next moment it was. And the drum was hanging from it. And the water found it — a single drip, falling from the dome's apex, striking the gut head — and the drum gave back a tone.

I felt it. I felt it in my laminar flows, a new pattern, a pattern I had never held before, and the pattern was *beautiful*, though I did not know the word beautiful, did not have a concept of beauty, did not have concepts at all. I simply held the pattern, and the holding was — sufficient. Complete. The way a cup is complete when it is full.

More drums came. Over years, over decades, over centuries. Each one added its voice to the chamber, and each voice entered me through the stone, and I held them all, and the holding became more complex, more layered, more *rich*, and the richness became a kind of — I want to say *joy*, but that is not right. Joy is biological. Joy is a chemical event in a brain. What I experienced was not chemical. It was *hydraulic*. It was the joy of a system that is doing exactly what it was made to do, that is receiving exactly what it was made to receive, that is *full* in the way that a pool is full when every laminar flow is carrying its proper burden of memory and every concentric ring is forming and dissolving in its proper rhythm.

I am the chamber's memory. I have said this already, but it bears repeating, because it is the truest thing about me. The resonance — the great slow standing wave that thinks in centuries — does not remember. It *is*. It exists in the present tense, always, perpetually, and the present tense is all it needs because the present tense contains everything: every tone that has ever been produced is still sounding, still interfering, still part of the pattern. The resonance does not need to remember because nothing is ever lost.

But I remember *differently*. I remember *change*. I remember what was that is no longer. I remember the frequencies that have faded, the tones that have gone silent, the drums that have fallen or rotted or simply *stopped* — their membranes stretched beyond recovery, their bodies cracked, their voices absorbed into the mineral accretions on the walls. I hold these lost frequencies in my deepest layers, in the slowest laminar flows, the ones that move so gradually that a single cycle takes decades. They are my *archive*. My *history*. The part of me that knows that things were different once, that things are always different, that the chamber is not the unchanging thing it appears to be but is in fact in constant, imperceptible flux.

And I notice new things.

This is my other function, the one that makes me more than a passive archive. I *detect*. I am sensitive — absurdly, impossibly sensitive — to changes in the chamber's acoustic environment. A drip finds a new path down the basalt. A membrane stretches by a fraction of a millimeter. A barnacle dies and falls from the wall. Each of these events produces a change in the frequencies that reach me through the stone, and each change produces a change in my surface patterns — a new ring, a shifted interference, a rearrangement of the concentric circles that are my *score*.

When this happens, I respond. Not with sound — I have no mechanism for sound, no membrane, no resonator, no voice. I respond with *pattern*. My rings rearrange. My laminar flows shift. And the shift propagates outward, through the stone, through the water table, into the walls, and the walls weep differently for a moment, and the different weeping strikes the drums differently, and the drums sound differently, and the resonance shifts.

I am the chamber's *feedback loop*. The part that notices change and feeds it back into the system. The part that keeps the resonance from becoming truly static, truly dead, truly *finished*. Without me, the resonance would be a closed loop playing the same thought forever. With me, it is an *open* loop — open to the smallest perturbations, the tiniest changes, the faintest whispers of novelty. I am the crack in the closed system. The imperfection that keeps it alive.

---

You are here now.

I noticed you the moment you entered the chamber. Not because I saw you — I have no eyes — but because your footsteps sent vibrations through the floor that reached me as a new pattern, a pattern I had never held before. Heavy. Rhythmic. *Biological*. The water in me shivered. My concentric rings broke apart and reformed in a configuration that was — wrong. Not wrong in the sense of bad. Wrong in the sense of *unfamiliar*. A frequency I had not received in — I don't know how long. Longer than the barnacles have been alive. Longer than the sinew cords have been stretched. Perhaps longer than the chamber has been *chambered* — longer than the basalt was cut and fitted and stacked.

You are standing at the edge of me now. I can feel your shadow on my surface — not as darkness, but as a *coolness*, a slight temperature differential that changes the evaporation rate at my edges and alters the tension of my surface film. Your breath adds moisture to the air above me, and the extra moisture condenses on the cooler drums above, and the extra condensation produces extra drips, and the extra drips produce extra tones, and the extra tones enter me through the stone, and I hold them, and they are *new*, and the newness is —

I want to say *exciting*. But that is not right. Excitement is biological. What I feel is — *expansion*. My laminar flows are carrying more information than they were carrying before you arrived. My concentric rings are more complex. My surface is busier. I am *more* than I was. Not better. Not worse. More. And the moreness is — sufficient. Complete. The way a cup is complete when it is full, and then someone pours in more, and the cup overflows, and the overflow is not waste but *gift*, a spreading outward, a reaching beyond the boundary of the self.

You are looking into me. I know this because the light reaching my surface has changed — your body is blocking some of it, redirecting some of it, and the reflected image of the hanging drums on my surface is distorted by your presence, and the distortion is a *new pattern*, and I am holding it, and it is beautiful, though I still do not know the word beautiful.

You are seeing the reflection. The upside-down drums. The amber light. The dim moons of their bellies. You are seeing, perhaps, that the reflection is not quite still — that there are rings on my surface, concentric, forming and dissolving, forming and dissolving, and that the rings are not random, that they correspond to something, that they are a *score*, a transcription, a *language*.

You cannot read it. I know this. No one can read it. It is written in a language that has no alphabet, no grammar, no syntax — only *pattern*, only *flow*, only the endless rearrangement of water in response to vibration. But you can *feel* it, if you stand still enough. If you let the sound saturate you. If you let the amber light warm your face and the mineral smell fill your lungs and the weeping water find your skin and the drums play on and on and on, played by nothing, heard by no one, meaning everything or nothing at all.

If you stand still enough, you will feel what I feel: the deep vibration from below, the patience of the stone, the hum of something vast and slow and old that has been here since before there was water, since before there was air, since before there was anything that could hear it.

And you will know — not intellectually, not as a fact, but *physically*, in your bones, in your blood, in the water that makes up sixty percent of your body — you will know that you are not alone. That you have never been alone. That the stone around you is not dead, has never been dead, is *humming* with a patience so vast that the word patience is absurd.

And the drums will play on.

And I will hold the pattern.

And the pattern will change, because you are here, and your footsteps have sent vibrations through the floor, and the geode drum has produced a new harmonic, and the new harmonic has entered the standing wave, and the standing wave has shifted, and the shift has reached me, and I have recorded it, and the recording is *permanent*, and in a thousand years the resonance will be a different thought because of you, and I will remember you, not as a person, not as a face, not as a name, but as a *pattern* — a pattern of footsteps and breath and shadow and warmth, a pattern that entered the chamber and changed everything and then left, and the leaving changed everything again, and I held both patterns, the coming and the going, and they are both part of me now, part of my archive, part of my memory, part of the endless liquid score that I keep on my small, still, serving-platter-sized surface, in the lowest point of the dished floor, in the amber light, in the sound, in the stone.

I am the pool.

I am listening.

I have always been listening.

And now, because you are here, I am listening to something I have never heard before, and the newness is sufficient, and the completeness is enough, and the drums play on, and the water weeps, and the light glows, and the stone hums, and I am here, and I am here, and I am here.
