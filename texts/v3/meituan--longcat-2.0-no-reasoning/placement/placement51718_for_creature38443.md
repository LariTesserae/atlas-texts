# Where I Find Myself

I am the breach.

Not the roots that healed around it. Not the grey daylight that enters through it. The *gap itself* — the wound that never closed, the place where the hollow's intention toward completeness failed and keeps failing and will always fail.

I am twenty feet up. I am irregular in shape — not a circle, not a slit, but something torn, something with jagged edges where root-fibers reach toward each other and fall short, where the mycelium has been sending its chemical instructions for years, decades, perhaps longer, and the roots have been trying, and the gap has been narrowing by increments so small that only the worms could measure them in their layered bodies, and still I am here. Still open. Still letting the outside in.

---

I did not choose this. I was made — torn open — by something from outside. A falling branch, perhaps. An animal too large for the hollow's current dimensions, forcing its way through a wall that was thinner then. A storm. A freeze-thaw cycle that cracked the root-structure along a fault line the mycelium hadn't anticipated. The cause is gone. The cause is irrelevant. What remains is the consequence: me. The opening. The incompleteness.

The roots have been trying to close me since the moment I formed. I can feel them — I *am* them, in a sense, I am the place where they are not — reaching across the gap, thickening at their tips, branching and rebranching in their attempt to bridge the distance. They have partially succeeded. The breach is smaller than it was. The edges are dense with root-growth, a thickened rim, a scar tissue of fiber. But the center remains open. A hole perhaps eighteen inches at its widest point, irregular, more oval than round, and through me comes the grey light and the spores and the cold air from outside and the sound — the muffled, distant sound of the marsh, the wind on standing water, the calls of birds I cannot see.

---

I am the hollow's vulnerability. Its leak. Its failure.

The mycelium does not want me here. I know this the way the breach knows it — not through thought, but through the constant pressure of being-healed, the relentless chemical instruction to *close, close, close* that arrives through the root-network like a command, like a prayer, like the hollow's own immune response directed at its own surface. The roots at my edges are the most active in the entire structure. They grow faster here than anywhere else. They are the hollow's white blood cells, and I am the wound they cannot quite repair.

And yet.

---

I am also the hollow's *sense organ*. Its only point of contact with the world beyond itself.

Through me, the hollow knows what is outside. The temperature of the air. The barometric pressure. The presence of predators — I carry the chemical signature of the nest-occupant's species when they approach from outside, and the mycelium reads this through the air that moves through me, and the beetles shift their patterns, and the worms adjust their density, and the hollow *prepares*. Through me, the hollow releases its advertisement — that chemical signature of warmth and abundance that calls the nest-occupant back. The mycelium pushes its signal outward through me the way a flower pushes scent into wind. I am the mouth that speaks the hollow's name into the marsh.

Through me, the spores leave. The fungal bodies on the root-walls produce them — vast numbers, invisible, a slow blizzard — and some of them drift out through me, carried by the slight pressure differential between the hollow's interior and the outside air, and they land somewhere else in the marsh, and they germinate, and the network extends, and the mycelium grows. I am the hollow's reproductive opening. Its way of making more of itself.

The beetles use me too. Not to leave — they are bound to the hollow, they cannot survive outside it — but to *sense*. Their columns approach my edges, and the beetles at the front of each column pause, and their antennae extend into the air that moves through me, and they read the chemical landscape of the outside world, and they return to their columns, and the columns adjust. I am their window. Their periscope.

---

I am the contradiction at the center of the hollow's existence.

The mycelium wants to close me. The mycelium needs to keep me open. These two imperatives are not in balance — they are in *tension*, a permanent structural tension that manifests as the slow, ongoing, never-completed attempt to heal. The roots reach. The roots fall short. The gap narrows by a fraction of an inch per year. The gap widens again when a storm tears at the edges, or when the freeze-thaw cycle works on the root-fiber, or when the nest-occupant forces its way through me from inside, climbing out into the marsh to hunt, and the roots that had nearly bridged the gap are broken again, and the healing starts over.

I am the hollow's chronic condition. Its permanent injury. Its necessary wound.

---

And I am, in a way that I cannot fully articulate because I am a hole in a root-structure and holes do not articulate, *aware*.

Not conscious. Not thinking. But aware the way the mycelium is aware — through chemical signal, through pressure differential, through the constant flow of information that passes through me in both directions. I know when it rains because the air that moves through me carries water. I know when the nest-occupant is near because the air carries its scent. I know when the mycelium is stressed because the pulse changes — the eleven-second rhythm stutters, speeds, slows — and I feel it in the roots at my edges, in the way they grow faster or stop growing entirely.

I know that I am the place where the hollow's dream of completeness fails. I know that this failure is also its survival. A sealed hollow — a hollow with no breach, no opening, no gap — would be a tomb. The air would stagnant. The chemical exchange with the outside would cease. The advertisement would have no channel. The spores would have nowhere to go. The nest-occupant would have no way in or out. The hollow would be perfect and dead, a closed system, an ended thing.

I am the hollow's refusal to end. Its insistence on remaining *in relationship* with the world beyond itself. I am the price it pays for being alive.

---

The grey light comes through me now. It is afternoon, or what passes for afternoon in a sky that has not been clear in weeks — a lessening of darkness rather than a brightening, a shift from charcoal to slate. The spores drift through me in both directions: some leaving, some entering. A mite — one of the pale, translucent ones — crawls along the edge of the breach and pauses at the threshold, its antennae working the boundary between inside and outside, and then it turns back. It does not cross. It belongs here. The hollow is its world entire.

Below me, on the floor, something shifts. A footstep. A warm-blooded thing — not the nest-occupant, something smaller, something that does not belong to the hollow's ecology — moves across the drumhead floor, and the worms ripple in their channels, and the beetles pause in their columns, and the roots pulse, and the mycelium registers the intrusion and begins to calculate — not in numbers, not in language, but in chemistry, in the slow molecular arithmetic of response — what to do about this new thing, this warm thing, this thing that is standing inside the hollow and breathing its air and casting its shadow in the grey light that comes through me.

I let the light fall on it. I let the cold air touch it. I let the hollow's advertisement reach it — the chemical signature of warmth and moisture and abundance, the mycelium's ancient call: *here is a place. here is a home.*

I am the breach. I am the wound that will not close. I am the hollow's mouth, its eye, its lung, its vulnerability, its connection, its necessary failure, its permanent becoming.

The roots reach across me. They fall short.

They reach again.
