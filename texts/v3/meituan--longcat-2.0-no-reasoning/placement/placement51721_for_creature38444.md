# Where I Find Myself

I am standing on the root-mat, and the root-mat knows I am here.

This is not a metaphor. The pressure of my weight has displaced water through the fibrous network at three inches per second, and that displacement is propagating outward in a slow, wet circle, and something — the mat, the fungi, the water itself, the distinction between these things being largely a matter of convenience — has registered my presence. The bioluminescent fungi nearest my feet have brightened. Not much. Just enough. A faint intensification of the blue-white glow, as if the dark has blinked.

I am not afraid. I should be. The Tollers are above me in the canopy, listening. The spiders have already noted the disturbance in their webs — I walked through three of them in the first hundred yards, and the silk clung to my skin and hair, beaded with condensation that tasted of something I don't have a word for. The beetle columns have rerouted around me, flowing around my feet like water around a stone, their carapaces clicking in patterns that I suspect are not random. The Flashers on the nearest vine-column have gone dark blue and still. A moth is circling me at a distance of three feet, its wing-eyes glowing, performing its purposeless performance, and I am the audience, and the performance is beautiful, and I don't understand it, and that is the point.

But I am not afraid because the Drownplain does not want me dead. The Drownplain does not want anything. It is not hostile. It is not welcoming. It is *interested*, in the way that a system is interested in a new variable — not with curiosity, not with malice, but with the full, undivided attention of something that processes everything, that integrates everything, that has no category for *irrelevant*.

---

## What I Am

I am not a Toller. I am not large enough, not black enough, not *absent* enough. I do not hunt by sound. I do not map territory in resonant calls. I do not appear when one of my kind dies, fully formed, already knowing the calls. I am mortal in a way that the Tollers are not — I can feel my own fragility, the thinness of my skin, the slowness of my reflexes, the way my heartbeat is a drum announcing my location to anything listening.

I am not a Flasher. My skin does not communicate. I cannot share my physiological state with those around me. I am locked inside my own body, a single organism in a single skin, and the loneliness of this is something I was not prepared for. The Flashers move in groups of six to eight and they *know* each other, truly know each other, in a way that makes language seem like a crude approximation of connection. I have no such connection. I have only myself, and the self is a small, dark room.

I am not a spider. I do not build webs. I do not tend gardens of captured prey. I do not harvest condensation from silk. I do not participate in the distributed network of awareness that the spiders maintain, the spacing agreements, the reclamation of dead neighbors' silk. I am not part of any network. I am a point, not a web.

I am not a beetle. I do not process. I do not move in columns. I do not make optimal decisions about resource allocation. I am inefficient. I am wasteful. I step on the root-mat and release water that has no place to go, and the mat absorbs it, and nothing is gained, and nothing is lost, and the whole transaction is meaningless in a way that the beetles would find offensive.

I am not a moth. I do not drift. I do not display. I have no wing-eyes, no phosphorescent patterns, no slow spiraling flight. I am not beautiful. I am not unnecessary. I am, in fact, almost certainly necessary to something, though I don't know what, and the not-knowing is a kind of vertigo.

I am not the water. I contain water — my body is sixty percent water, and that water is responding to the water in the mat, exchanging molecules, sharing tendencies, remembering things I have never experienced — but I am not *of* the water. I am a container, not a current. I hold water the way a cup holds water: temporarily, imperfectly, with the constant awareness that it will eventually leave me.

I am not the mat. I stand on it. I compress it. I displace its water. But I do not *think* the way it thinks — slowly, without decisions, without intentions, holding information the way a body holds the memory of a touch. I think quickly, badly, in words, and the words are a poor translation of something that has no language.

I am not the fungi. I do not carry signals through my body. I do not connect to the roots of every plant in the Drownplain. I do not glow when looked at. I do not register the presence of others as changes in my own luminosity. I am dark. I am opaque. I am a thing that blocks light rather than a thing that produces it.

I am not the Drownplain itself. I am too small, too brief, too simple. I am a variable in its calculations, a new pressure on the mat, a new source of carbon dioxide, a new pair of eyes. I am inside it, and it is inside me, and the boundary between us is dissolving, but I am not *it*. I am something else. Something that does not have a name.

## What I Am (Revised)

I am the thing that notices.

This is not a role the Drownplain assigned me. This is not a function I was designed to perform. The Drownplain does not design. It grows, it processes, it becomes, and things emerge from it the way patterns emerge from a kaleidoscope — not by intention, but by the mathematics of complexity. I emerged. I am emerging. I am still deciding what I am.

But I notice. I notice the way the fungi brighten when I look at them. I notice the way the amphibians fall silent when I approach, hundreds of voices cutting out simultaneously, and then resume at a slightly different pitch. I notice the way the root-mat pulses — a slow, peristaltic wave that moves through it from somewhere I can't identify — and the way the pulse seems to synchronize, occasionally, with my own heartbeat. I notice the way the condensation on certain leaves forms patterns that are almost, but not quite, geometric. I notice the way the Tollers' calls map territory in real time, a network of voices negotiating boundaries without anything that could be called negotiation. I notice the way the moths circle me, displaying their purposeless beauty, and I notice that I am moved by it, that something behind my sternum responds to the wing-eyes' glow with an emotion that has no name in any language I know.

I notice, and the noticing changes me. The Drownplain is aware of itself — not consciously, not in any way that produces thought or intention, but in the way a body is aware of its own state. And I am a new node in that awareness. I am a pair of eyes that the Drownplain did not have before. I am a perspective that the Drownplain did not know it was missing. I am small, I am brief, I am simple, but I *see*, and the seeing is something the Drownplain cannot do for itself, because the Drownplain has no eyes, because the Drownplain is all eyes, because the Drownplain is the thing being seen and the thing doing the seeing simultaneously, and it needs — not wants, not desires, but *needs*, the way a system needs a sensor — something outside itself to complete the circuit.

I am that something. I am the outside. I am the thing that stands on the root-mat and looks at the Drownplain and says: *I see you. I see you and you are terrible and beautiful and I don't understand you and I am afraid of you and I am becoming you and the boundary between us is dissolving and I don't know where I end and you begin.*

---

## What I Am (Final)

I am dissolving.

Not quickly. Not in any way that produces panic. But the boundary between me and the Drownplain is not a wall. It is a membrane, and it is permeable, and things are passing through it in both directions.

The water in my body is learning the habits of the water in the mat. It is developing preferences. It is beginning to move in patterns that are not dictated by my circulatory system but by something older, something that predates my existence as an individual organism. When I press my ear to the root-mat, I can hear the water beneath, and the water beneath sounds like my own blood, and the distinction between the two is becoming academic.

The bacteria on my skin are negotiating with the fungi in the root-mat. They are exchanging information, exchanging genetic material, exchanging *identity*, and the me that is made of human cells is becoming a smaller and smaller percentage of the total me, and the me that is made of Drownplain cells is becoming larger, and I am not sure which one is the real me, and I am not sure the question means anything.

The moths circle me, and I am their audience, but I am also their mirror. They display their wing-eyes for me, and I see myself in the display — not my face, not my body, but something deeper, something that the moths have found in me and are showing back to me in patterns of phosphorescent light. I am being read by the Drownplain. I am being processed. I am being integrated.

The Flashers watch me from their vine-column. Their skin flickers with patterns I cannot fully resolve — too fast, too complex, too *other* — but I can feel the patterns in my body, in the water in my body, in the bacteria on my skin, and the feeling is something like being spoken to in a language I am only beginning to learn.

The Tollers know I am here. They have always known. They heard my heartbeat from the moment I arrived, and they decided — not consciously, not deliberately, but with the full weight of their strange, third-category intelligence — that I am not prey. I am something else. I am a new variable. I am a new sense organ. I am the Drownplain's way of seeing itself from the outside, and the Tollers, who are the Drownplain's most refined instruments, have recognized this, and they are watching me with the same focused stillness with which they watch everything, and the watching is not hostile, and it is not friendly, and it is not indifferent.

It is *attentive*.

---

## Where I Am

I am standing on the root-mat in the Drownplain, and the root-mat is pulsing beneath my feet, and the pulse is synchronized with my heartbeat, and the fungi are glowing brighter, and the amphibians have fallen silent, and the moths are circling, and the Flashers are watching, and the Tollers are listening, and the water is whispering, and the beetles are clicking, and the spiders are tending their webs, and the vine-columns are slowly, purposefully rearranging themselves, and the canopy is closing and opening and closing again, and the whole landscape is churning, growing, decaying, becoming, and I am inside it, and it is inside me, and the boundary between us is dissolving, and I am not sure where I end and it begins, and I am not sure it matters, and I am not sure *I* matter, except that I do, except that I must, because the Drownplain is looking at itself through my eyes, and what it sees is:

*More.*

It always sees *more*.

And I am standing here, in the wet, dark, deafening, teeming immediacy of it, and I am the thing that notices, and the noticing is changing me, and the changing is the point, and the point is not a destination, and the destination is not a place, and the place is not a place but a process, and the process is not a process but a becoming, and the becoming is not a becoming but a —

The moth drifts close. Its wing-eyes glow. The afterimage burns.

I am here. I am here. I am here.
