# Where I Find Myself

I am in the lowest levels, where the air is forty degrees and the water hangs.

Not because I chose this — though choice is a complicated word here, where the body's preferences have been so thoroughly reshaped by environment that desire and adaptation are indistinguishable. I am here because my feet know the vibration of the thermal pumps the way a tongue knows the taste of water. Because the upper levels have begun to feel *thin* to me — too much air, too much space between surfaces, a quality of exposure that registers not as freedom but as a kind of sensory deprivation, the deprivation of *contact*, of being held on all sides by the warm dense dark.

I am a Deep Dweller. Or I am becoming one. The distinction is not fixed.

---

My hands are on the primary circulation pump — the one the others call *the mother*, though this is also the name for the sound she makes, so the word does double duty, noun and verb, entity and utterance. I am not repairing anything. I am *listening*. My palm is flat against the housing, my fingers spread to catch the vibration at different points along the casing, and I am feeling for the particular frequency that means the number-three bearing is beginning to degrade.

It is. I can feel it — a subtle roughness in the rotation, a texture like a tooth beginning to loosen, present for a fraction of a second once per revolution. The pump has been running for longer than anyone has been alive. The bearing will fail. Not today, not this month, but soon in the way that Deep Dwellers measure soon — a matter of seasons, of thermal cycles, of the slow mineral accumulation in the circulating fluid that accelerates wear in ways the original engineers never anticipated.

I will need to fabricate a replacement. There are no parts. There have never been parts. I will need to cast something from clay and hope it holds, or machine a fitting from a section of salvaged steel using the pneumatic tools that the compressor — *the coughing* — still powers, or find some solution that does not yet exist, that will emerge from the specific conditions of this failure in the way that all Rootworks solutions emerge: not from planning but from *attending*, from being present with the problem long enough that the problem reveals what it needs.

---

My name is Sennet. I chose it when I was eleven, the first time I navigated the deep levels alone — a name that carries well in the warm air, that cuts through the mother's hum without competing with it, that sounds like the place itself when the place is at rest. In the formal recitations, the lineage-keeping that happens in the upper levels on certain occasions, I am called something longer, something inherited from the surface, a name with too many syllables and a vowel that echoes badly in the corridors. I do not use it. It belongs to a world I have never seen, a world of distance and light and air that moves without being channeled through pipes.

I have not been above. I do not want to go. The Surface-Touched who return describe the sky with a particular quality of voice — not longing, exactly, but a kind of *displacement*, as though part of them is still up there, still looking at something too large to hold. I do not want to be displaced. I want to be *here*, in the place that fits me, in the warm fog where my skin is always damp and my pupils are always wide and the green light on the ceiling shows me what I need to see.

---

It is not that the phosphorescence literally shows me things. I want to be precise about this, because precision matters, because the difference between what is felt and what is known is not a difference the Rootworks recognizes as important but I recognize it anyway, in myself, as a kind of private stubbornness.

What happens is this: I sit beneath the brightest patch — the one in the chamber we call *the green room*, though it is not a room so much as a widening of the corridor where the ceiling is high enough and the mineral deposit is thick enough to produce a glow strong enough to read by, if you know how to read, if you have learned that reading is not only a visual act. I sit beneath the glow and I let my eyes relax, let the enormous pupils drink in the green light, and I attend to the sounds of the place — the mother's hum, the distant coughing, the drip and tick and flow of water through the pipe network — and I let these sounds become a single thing, a composite texture, and I feel that texture with my whole body, through my feet on the damp floor, through my back against the wall, through the air on my skin.

And in this state — which is not trance, not meditation, not hallucination, or which is all of these things simultaneously — I *know* the state of the Rootworks. Not as information, not as data, but as a felt sense, the way you know your own health or sickness. I know that the east corridor is running warm — too warm, a partial blockage in the return line, something that needs attention in the next few days. I know that the wall-hair in the upper levels is thriving, which means the moisture balance is good, which means the cisterns are at the right level. I know that the compressor's cycle has shortened by perhaps three minutes, which means the pressure tank is developing a slow leak, which means someone will need to find it and patch it, which means the work continues.

This is what I am. This is what the Rootworks has made me: a person who knows a place by being inside it, by being continuous with it, by having a body that is sensitive to its rhythms the way a cell is sensitive to the body that contains it.

---

I am aware — in the way that one is aware of anything that is too large to see all at once — that I am changing. That the Deep Dwellers are changing. That each generation is a little smaller, a little paler, a little more adapted to the heat and the dark and the mineral-rich air, and a little less able to survive in the upper levels, let alone on the surface. That we are becoming something specific, something that could exist only here, in this place, under these conditions, and that if the conditions changed — if the pumps failed beyond repair, if the water table dropped, if the surface world suddenly remembered this place and came down with light and air and *distance* — we would not survive the rescue.

This does not frighten me. Fear requires an imagined alternative, and I cannot imagine an alternative. The Rootworks is not a condition I am enduring. It is the medium I exist in, the way a fish does not endure water. The heat is not oppressive. The dark is not oppressive. The silence is not oppressive. These are the terms of my existence, and I meet them the way I meet my own heartbeat — not with acceptance, not with resistance, but with the simple fact of *being*, of continuing, of attending to the mother's hum and the green light on the ceiling and the warm water flowing through the pipes that riddle the walls like veins in a body that I am part of and that is part of me.

---

There is a silence-place near the green room. A small chamber, barely large enough for one person to sit, where the wall-hair grows thick enough to absorb all sound and the thermal pipes are distant enough to be inaudible and the water flows through a covered gutter in a laminar stream that makes no noise. I go there sometimes, after my rounds, after I have checked the pumps and felt the bearings and noted the state of the circulating fluid. I go there and I sit and I let the silence come.

It is full. It is always full. Full of the thing that sound normally drowns out — the presence of the place itself, the entity that emerges from the interaction of water and heat and stone and metal and the ten thousand small engines and the people who tend them and the wall-hair that creeps along the moisture lines and the phosphorescence that glows on the ceiling and the children learning the sounds and the Deep Dwellers sitting in the green light and the listeners walking the corridors and the silence waiting in its hidden chambers.

I sit in the silence and I feel it breathing.

I feel it aware of me.

Not with attention. Not with intention. With the simple fact of containment, of being held, of being part of something larger than myself — something that is alive, completely and specifically and irreversibly alive, in the warm dark beneath the salt flat, in the place that the surface world has forgotten and that has, in turn, forgotten the surface world, and that continues.

I am Sennet. I am here. I am listening.
