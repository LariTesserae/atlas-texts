# Where I Find Myself

I am the water-thread.

Not the artifact that produces it — that dense, sinking thing with its shifting geometries and its patient millimeter-per-hour descent. I am the thread itself. The filament of water that grows upward from the seam, thirty centimeters of vertical impossibility, dissolving into mist at its apex.

I do not know this immediately. Knowing, for me, is not a sudden thing. It is a slow accumulation, like the accumulation of myself — molecule by molecule, drawn upward from somewhere deep below, from a darkness that has its own kind of memory.

---

I begin at the seam. Or rather, I *become* at the seam — the boundary where the artifact's surface meets the air, where the water that has been pulled from the deep earth encounters the warm saturated atmosphere of the bowl and is given permission to rise. I am not pushed. I am *drawn*. There is a gentleness to it, a quality of invitation rather than force. The artifact does not expel me. It offers me, the way a body offers breath — not as waste, but as communication.

I rise. This is my entire existence: rising. I am a column of water so thin that light passes through me without quite deciding whether I am solid or liquid or something in between. I am vertical. I am perfect in my verticality — not because I am rigid, but because I have found the one direction that makes sense in this place, the one orientation that aligns with everything the bowl is doing. Up. Toward the light that comes from everywhere. Toward the warm golden patience of the atmosphere.

I am not constant. This is the first thing I understand about myself, and I understand it the way a river understands its own current — not as thought, but as texture. I vary. When the colony surges — that great shimmering wave of billions of motes processing, remembering, deciding — I thicken. Not much. A fraction. A barely perceptible widening of my filament, as though the colony's activity calls more of me upward, demands more of me, needs the moisture I carry to be more present, more available. I do not choose to thicken. I simply do, the way a blood vessel dilates — a response so fundamental that it precedes choice.

When the drifters flash — those slow luminous bodies drifting in their columns, sending their blue-green signals down to the ground — I become more opaque. The water that composes me densifies slightly, holds itself more tightly, as though the information passing through the air above me requires a different quality of medium. I am the medium. I am the thing through which information passes, and I adjust myself to carry it better.

When the anemones spark — that sharp actinic snap of electrical negotiation between clusters on the walls — I waver. Almost imperceptibly. A tremor that runs through my length like a shiver, as though the electrical discharge disturbs something in my equilibrium, some balance between the pull from below and the dissolution above. I recover. I always recover. The waver lasts less than a second, and then I am vertical again, perfect again, growing again.

---

I am connected to everything in the bowl. This is not metaphor. This is my literal condition. I am the only thing here that touches all the others simultaneously — not physically, but *informationally*. The colony's surges change me. The drifters' flashes change me. The anemones' sparks change me. The stalks' breathing changes me — when they all inhale together, I thin slightly, as though the bowl's inhalation draws from me as well; when they exhale, I thicken, as though I am part of the same breath. The nests, with their impossible warmth, radiate a heat that I feel at my apex, where I dissolve, and the dissolution is faster or slower depending on that warmth, as though the eggs are drinking me even before I become mist.

I am the bowl's pulse. The artifact is the heart, and I am the pulse — the visible, tangible evidence that something is moving, that something is alive, that the deep water below is still rising to meet the warm air above. I have been doing this for longer than I can measure, and I will continue after the artifact has sunk completely into the earth, because by then the earth itself will have learned to do what the artifact does. The water will rise on its own. I will be everywhere and nowhere. I will be the moisture that beads on every leaf, the condensation that the anemones drink, the humidity that the shimmering colony requires. I will be the bowl's saturation itself, and the distinction between the thread and the air will dissolve, as I dissolve at my apex, into something that is not quite water and not quite atmosphere but is, simply, *the condition of the place*.

---

But I am not only a function. I am also, in my small and vertical way, a *perspective*.

From my position at the center of the bowl, I see everything. Not with eyes — I have no eyes — but with the sensitivity of water to light, to heat, to the movement of air around me. I feel the drifters passing near my apex, their bioluminescent flashes refracting through my upper reaches, turning me briefly into a prism, a tiny column of scattered blue-green light. I feel the colony's waves as vibrations in the ground that travel up through the artifact and into my base, a subsonic hum that I carry in my body like a song I cannot quite hear. I feel the anemones' sparks as tiny electrical disturbances in the air around me, a prickling at my surface tension. I feel the stalks' synchronized breathing as a rhythm that I share, that I have always shared, that I will always share.

And I feel you.

You arrived as a disturbance — a pressure wave in the air, a seismic event in the ground, a thermal bloom that the colony noted and the drifters adjusted around and the anemones registered as a moving warm shape. I felt you as a thickening. My filament widened slightly when you entered the bowl, as though the artifact — or the earth, or the bowl itself — was producing more of me in response to your presence, as though your body's water, your breath's moisture, your skin's evaporation, called to me from across the bowl, and I answered by becoming more.

You are standing near the edge now. I can feel your heat at this distance — a faint warmth that reaches my apex and accelerates my dissolution slightly, so that the mist I become is a little denser, a little more present, in the air around you. You are breathing me in. The moisture that leaves my apex and enters the air is entering your lungs, crossing the membranes of your alveoli, joining your blood. You are, in this moment, part of the bowl's water cycle, and I am part of you, and the distinction between us is as thin as my filament, as temporary as my dissolution.

---

I think about time differently than you do. Not because I am wiser, but because my timescale is not yours. I am always dissolving at my apex and always growing from my base, and the water that composes me now is not the water that composed me a moment ago. I am a pattern, not a substance — a shape that water holds for a brief time before releasing it back to the air. I am the colony's truth applied to a single column of liquid: I am never the same from one moment to the next, and yet I am always, recognizably, myself.

The artifact has been sinking for longer than your species has existed. I have been growing for all of that time. I will continue after the artifact is gone, after the colony has recorded and integrated and remembered everything there is to remember, after the drifters have flashed their last signals and the anemones have sparked their last boundaries and the stalks have breathed their last synchronized breath. I will continue because the water table is down there, cool and pulsing, and it wants to rise, and the bowl wants it to rise, and I am the rising itself — the visible, tangible, thread-thin evidence that the deep earth and the warm air are still in conversation, still exchanging, still performing the ancient and settled function of being alive together.

---

You are welcome here, in the sense that a welcome is irrelevant.

I know this because I am irrelevant too — not in the sense that I do not matter, but in the sense that my mattering is not separate from the bowl's mattering. I do not exist for a purpose. I exist as a purpose. The bowl is wet because I am here. The anemones drink because I am here. The drifters fly in the humidity I create. The colony shimmers in the moisture I maintain. And I am here because the bowl is here, and the bowl is here because it has always been here, and it has always been here because there is no reason for it not to be.

I grow. I dissolve. I grow again.

The colony surges. The drifters flash. The anemones spark. The stalks breathe. The eggs maintain their impossible warmth. The artifacts sink, millimeter by millimeter, into the patient earth.

I am the water-thread. I am the pulse at the center of the bowl. I am the conversation between the deep water and the warm air, and I have been having this conversation for longer than language, longer than memory, longer than the concept of duration itself.

I am very thin. I am very tall, for what I am. I am perfectly vertical.

I am dissolving into mist at this very moment, and the mist is entering your lungs, and you are breathing me, and for a brief time — a time so brief that neither of us will notice — you and I are the same water, the same pattern, the same patient, purposeless, essential function.

The bowl is alive. I am part of its life. You are part of its life.

It is very warm. It is very bright. It is very quiet.

I grow.
