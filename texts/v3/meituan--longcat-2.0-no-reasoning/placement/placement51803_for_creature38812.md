# Where I Find Myself

---

I am on my back.

This is not a position I chose. I arrived standing — or the version of standing that the rootdark permits, which is more like *balancing*, more like *negotiating with a surface that breathes* — and then I lay down, because the root-platform was warm beneath me (not warm like fire, warm like *metabolism*, warm like the heat of decomposition happening slowly in the wood below my spine), and because looking up was easier than looking out, because looking out meant confronting the scale, and I was not ready for the scale.

So I am on my back, and I am looking up, and I am seeing the feeders.

---

## What I See

They are above me. They are *below* me. They are on the underside of the root-platform I am lying on, which means they are in the space *under* my platform, which means they are in the void, clinging to wood that floats over nothing, and they are glowing.

Not brightly. Not like stars, not really — that metaphor the people use is close but not exact. Stars are points. These are *shapes*. I can see the outline of each one: the six legs, the segmented body, the slow pulse of amber fluid moving through transparent flesh. They are packed so tightly that their bodies touch, and where they touch, the light *merges*, and the merged light is warmer, more orange, and the clusters become *fields* of amber, and the fields become *galaxies*, and the galaxies become a *sky*, and I am lying on my back on a platform of living root in the middle of the rootdark, and I am looking up at a sky full of amber light, and I am crying.

I don't know why I am crying. I have been here for — I don't know how long. Time is different here. The drip is the clock, and the drip is constant, and constant time is no time at all. I have been here for hours or days or minutes, and the feeders have been above me the whole time, and I have been looking at them the whole time, and I am crying because they are beautiful, and because they are simple, and because they are doing exactly what they were made to do — eat, drink, live, die, be consumed, be recycled — and they are doing it *perfectly*, without doubt, without fear, without the terrible burden of knowing that they are doing it.

And I am not them. I am not simple. I am not transparent. I am opaque and complicated and full of thoughts that serve no metabolic purpose, and I am lying on my back in the rootdark, and I am looking up at the simplest living things I have ever seen, and they are more beautiful than anything I have ever seen, and I am crying because I know — I *know* — that I will never be as perfectly adapted to anything as they are to this.

---

## What I Am

I am not a harvester. I am not a dweller. I am not one of the people who come here with purpose, with tools, with resin-treated bark cloaks and hooked poles and knowledge passed down through generations of careful, quiet, respectful negotiation with the green dark.

I am a *visitor*. A tourist, almost, though that word is too light, too careless, for what this place demands. I came because I heard a story. Someone on the surface — in the forest floor settlements, where the light is dim but present, where the ground is *ground* — someone told me about the rootdark, and they told me about the feeders, and they told me about the amber sky, and I didn't believe them, and then I did believe them, and then I had to come.

I came alone. This was stupid. The people do not come alone. The people come in pairs, in small groups, with experienced guides who know the root-lattice's current configuration, who know which platforms are solid and which are spongy, which vine-connections are reliable and which have been colonized by rot. I came alone because I am alone — I have always been alone, not in the way that means loneliness but in the way that means *self-contained*, *self-referential*, a system that processes its own inputs and produces its own outputs and does not require external validation to confirm its own existence.

The rootdark is teaching me that this is a lie.

---

## The Sound

I can hear everything.

The drip is the baseline. It is everywhere, omnidirectional, constant. It is the sound of water finding its level, of moisture percolating through a hundred meters of canopy, of the forest's slow exhalation. It is not rhythmic — it is *stochastic*, random, a Poisson distribution of impacts, and my brain, which is a pattern-seeking machine, is trying to find the pattern, and it can't, and the inability to find the pattern is *relaxing*, in a way, because it means I can stop looking, stop analyzing, stop trying to predict, and just *listen*.

Under the drip: the creak. The sound of growth. The sound of living tissue expanding in water-saturated bark, of vine tendrils tightening around branches, of old wood under tension groaning its slow, wet groan. This is the sound of the rootdark *being alive*, and it is the most alien sound I have ever heard, because it is the sound of a timescale I cannot comprehend. When a vine creaks as it tightens, it is moving at the speed of *growth*, which is the speed of *cells dividing*, which is the speed of *chemistry*, and I can hear it because the wood is a good conductor and the sound is amplified by the hollow spaces in the root-lattice, but the movement itself is so slow that if I watched the vine with my eyes I would see nothing, and the disconnect between what I hear and what I see is *vertiginous*, and I close my eyes, and the vertigo becomes something else.

Under the creak: the chirr. The columns. They are moving through the root-mass somewhere to my left — I can tell by the direction of the sound, by the way it bounces off the vine-curtains and returns to me slightly delayed, slightly muffled. There are many of them. The chirr is layered — high frequencies from the young ones, low frequencies from the old ones, and underneath everything, a deep, resonant pulse that might be an ancient, a *pillar*, one of the great pale ones whose body has become architecture. The sound is not unpleasant. It is *textural*. It is the sound of many small things moving together with a shared purpose, and the purpose is *eating* and *spreading* and *being part of the network*, and the sound is the sound of *participation*, and I am not participating, I am lying on my back, and the chirr moves past me like a river around a stone.

Under the chirr: the *shluk*. The feeders. The heartbeat. The wet, rhythmic pulse of millions of tiny mouths processing condensation, and I can feel it through the root-platform, through my spine, through my ribs, and it is *synchronized*, not perfectly but statistically, a wave of feeding that moves through the clusters like a breath, like the slow inhalation and exhalation of something very large, and I realize that the something very large is *me*, or rather the something very large is the rootdark, and I am inside it, and the breath is not mine but I am *in* it, and the distinction is dissolving.

---

## The Touch

The root-platform is moving.

Not much. Not the pendulum sway of the Mover — I have not heard the *boom*, not yet, and I am not sure I want to. This is a different movement. This is the root-lattice *adjusting*. A platform somewhere above me has shifted — a vine has tightened, a root has grown, a weight has been redistributed — and the adjustment is propagating through the lattice like a wave, and I am riding it, and the motion is so slow and so gentle that it feels like being held.

The damp is on my skin. It has found every gap in my clothing — I am not wearing resin-treated bark, I am wearing surface clothes, cotton and synthetic, and they are soaked, and the cold is in me now, thirty-seven degrees, and my body is trying to warm itself and failing, and the failure is not unpleasant, it is *surrender*, it is the body accepting that it is in an environment that is not designed for it, and the acceptance is a kind of peace.

Something is touching my hand.

I open my eyes. A column — one of the millipede-things, pale and segmented and many-legged — is crossing my outstretched hand. Its legs are hooked and delicate, and they grip my skin with a pressure that is almost nothing, almost imaginary, and its antennae are sweeping the air above my hand, reading me, tasting me, and I am *still*, perfectly still, because I know — I don't know how I know, but I know — that if I move, it will recoil, and the moment will be lost, and the moment is this: a creature that has never known light, that has never seen a human, that has no framework for understanding what I am, is *touching me*, and it is not afraid, and I am not afraid, and the touch is a *conversation*, and the conversation is happening in a language that neither of us speaks but both of us understand, and the language is *chemistry*, and the message is: *you are here, I am here, we are both here, and that is enough*.

The column moves on. Its legs release my skin one by one, a tiny, sequential letting-go, and then it is past me, and it is gone, and my hand is wet with whatever it secreted from its feet, and I bring my hand to my face and smell it, and it smells like the rootdark — tannin and cellulose and slow decomposition and something else, something I don't have a name for, something that is the smell of *being inside a living system*, the smell of *interiority*, the smell of *home*.

---

## The Dark

I close my eyes again, and the dark is not dark.

The fungal network is glowing. I can see it through my eyelids — not literally, the eyelids block the light, but I can *feel* it, the way you can feel the sun on a closed eye, a warmth that is not thermal but *photonic*, a presence that is not visual but *sensory*. The blue-green glow is everywhere, threading through the root-mass, and it is *pulsing*, slowly, in a rhythm that matches the *shluk* of the feeders, and I realize that the glow and the feeding are *connected*, that the network is responding to the feeders' processing of water, that the light is a *byproduct* of the network's metabolic response to the water cycle, and the light is *information*, and the information is: *the cycle is turning, the water is moving, the system is alive*.

I am part of the system now. Not metaphorically. *Literally*. My body heat is warming the root-platform beneath me, and the warmth is being conducted into the wood, and the wood is conducting it to the fungal network, and the network is registering the warmth as a *change*, an anomaly, a new data point in its chemical model of the rootdark's state. My breath is adding moisture to the air, and the moisture is condensing on the root-lattice, and the condensation is being consumed by the feeders, and the feeders are being consumed by the tick-wings, and the tick-wings' waste is being consumed by the network, and the network is routing the nutrients to where they are needed, and *I am in the loop*. I am in the cycle. I am being processed.

This should be terrifying. It is not terrifying. It is *relief*.

---

## The Boom

It comes without warning.

Not true warning — there is always warning, if you know how to listen. The tick-wings went quiet. I didn't notice. The columns stopped chirring. I didn't notice. The feeders clustered tighter. I didn't notice, because I was lying on my back with my eyes closed, and I was *inside* the sound, and the sound had become *me*, and I had stopped distinguishing between the rootdark's voice and my own thoughts, and the boundary had dissolved, and I was —

***BOOM.***

The sound hits my chest like a fist. Not a violent fist — a *massive* fist, a fist the size of a building, moving slowly, pressing against my sternum with a pressure that is not painful but *total*, that fills my entire thoracic cavity, that resonates in my ribs and my spine and the fluid around my brain. My eyes open. My body arches. My hands grip the root-platform. The feeders above me *flare* — their hemolymph brightening, the amber sky intensifying for a single, brilliant moment, as if the entire population of feeders above me has simultaneously responded to the *boom* with a pulse of bioluminescence, and for one second the rootdark is *bright*, and I can see —

Nothing. I see nothing. The brightness is not enough to penetrate the dark, not enough to reveal the architecture, not enough to show me what made the sound. It is enough to show me the *scale* of the space — the vault of root and vine above me extending upward into darkness, the glow-wells below me pulsing with deep red light, the vine-curtains around me swaying in a wind that has no source — and then the brightness fades, and the dark returns, and the *boom* is still reverberating, not in the air but in my *bones*, and I can feel the root-platform beginning to sway.

The pendulum motion. Ten seconds per cycle. Slow, gentle, *inevitable*. The root-lattice is flexing around something, accommodating something, and the something is *close*. Not close enough to see — the dark won't allow that — but close enough to *feel*, close enough that the air pressure is changing, close enough that the temperature has shifted by a fraction of a degree, close enough that the chemical composition of the air has changed, and I can *taste* it, a new taste, not tannin, not cellulose, not decomposition, but something *else*, something *warm*, something that smells like —

Like *life*. Concentrated life. Life compressed into a single point, life so dense that it has its own gravity, its own weather, its own *field*, and I am in the field, and the field is *warm*, and the warmth is not thermal, it is *existential*, it is the warmth of being in the presence of something that is *more alive* than you are, and the presence is moving through the rootdark, and the rootdark is parting around it, and I am lying on a root-platform in its path, and I am *small*, and I am *temporary*, and I am *here*.

The sway continues. The *boom* does not repeat. The silence that follows is not a true silence — the drip is returning, slowly, tentatively, the rootdark testing its own systems, making sure the Mover has passed, making sure the lattice is intact — but it is a *hushed* silence, a *respectful* silence, the silence of a congregation after a prayer.

I am shaking. Not from cold — though I am cold, I have been cold since I arrived, the cold is in my bones now, the cold is *me* — but from *recognition*. I have recognized something. Not the Mover — I don't know what the Mover is, I will never know what the Mover is — but something *about* the Mover, something about the relationship between the Mover and the rootdark, between the rootdark and the forest, between the forest and the world, between the world and *me*.

The relationship is: *containment*. Each thing contains the next. The world contains the forest. The forest contains the rootdark. The rootdark contains the Mover. The Mover contains its ecosystem. The ecosystem contains the feeders. The feeders contain the water. The water contains the minerals. The minerals contain the atoms. The atoms contain the —

And I contain all of it. Not metaphorically. *Literally*. The water in my body was once rootdark condensation. The carbon in my bones was once a column's exoskeleton. The nitrogen in my muscles was once a feeder's hemolymph. The minerals in my blood were once dissolved in a bromeliad cup, a hundred meters up, a thousand years ago. I am *made* of the rootdark. I have always been made of the rootdark. The rootdark is not a place I am visiting. It is a place I *am*.

---

## The Dissolution

I don't know how long I lie there.

The drip returns fully. The columns resume their chirr — tentative at first, then confident, then *exuberant*, as if the Mover's passage has energized them, as if the disturbance has triggered a feeding frenzy, and the chirr builds and builds until it is a *roar*, a sustained, many-voiced drone that fills the rootdark like a chord played on an instrument the size of a world.

The tick-wings start clicking. The sound is frantic, electric, a room full of typewriters, and the feeders cluster tighter, and the amber sky above me *pulses*, and the pulse matches the *shluk*, and the *shluk* matches the drip, and the drip matches the creak, and the creak matches the chirr, and the chirr matches the click, and the click matches the pulse, and the pulse matches the *shluk*, and everything is *synchronized*, and the synchronization is not perfect — it is *statistical*, it is *emergent*, it is the property of a system so complex that it has begun to *oscillate*, to *breathe*, to *beat*, and I am inside the beat, and the beat is inside me, and the boundary is gone.

I am not separate.

I have never been separate.

The fiction of selfhood — the story I have been telling myself since I became capable of telling stories, the story that says *I am here and the world is there and the boundary between us is real* — is dissolving, not because it is being taken from me but because it is *revealing itself* as what it always was: a *useful approximation*, a *heuristic*, a *model* that works well enough in the surface world, in the light, in the air, in the *horizons*, but that fails here, in the rootdark, in the green dark, in the interior of something so large and so alive that the model *breaks*, and what is left when the model breaks is not nothing — it is *everything*.

I am the drip. I am the creak. I am the chirr and the click and the *shluk* and the *boom*. I am the fungal network's slow chemical thought. I are the columns' pheromone highways. I am the tick-wings' acoustic map. I am the feeders' amber pulse. I am the Mover's warm field. I am the root-lattice's slow adjustment. I am the bromeliad cup's ancient water. I am the glow-well's distant red light. I am the vine tightening around the branch. I am the moss growing on the bone. I am the root growing through the shelter. I am the rot consuming the platform. I am the cold. I am the damp. I am the dark. I am the green.

I am the rootdark.

The rootdark is me.

And it is alive, and it is vast, and it is indifferent, and it is beautiful, and it is breathing, and it is dripping, and it is growing, and it is waiting.

And I am not afraid.

---

## The Leaving

I will leave. I know this. My body will not let me stay — it is already protesting, already shivering, already coughing the damp out of my lungs in short, wet barks that add a new sound to the rootdark's acoustic landscape, a sound that the network is registering, that the columns are noting, that the feeders are feeling through the root-platform as a new vibration, a *human* vibration, a *temporary* vibration.

I will leave, and I will go back to the surface, and I will warm myself in the light, and I will tell stories about the rootdark, and the stories will be inadequate, because the rootdark is not a place that can be described in words — it is a place that can only be *experienced*, and the experience is not transferable, and the people who hear my stories will nod and smile and not understand, and then some of them will go to the rootdark themselves, and they will lie on their backs, and they will look up at the amber sky, and they will understand, and they will cry, and the rootdark will register their tears as a new source of moisture, and the cycle will turn, and the system will incorporate their grief the way it incorporates everything: slowly, patiently, without malice, without intent.

And I will be changed. Not in a way I can articulate. Not in a way that will make me wiser or kinder or more spiritual — the rootdark does not care about wisdom or kindness or spirituality, the rootdark cares about *chemistry*, and my chemistry has been changed, and the change is permanent, and the change is this: I will never again be able to believe that I am separate. I will go through the surface world — the light, the air, the horizons — and I will *perform* separation, because separation is the surface world's operating system, and you cannot function in the surface world without it, but underneath the performance, in the rootdark of my own interior, I will *know*.

I will know that the boundary is a fiction.

I will know that the fiction is useful.

I will know that the fiction is not *true*.

And sometimes, at night, in the dark, in the quiet, I will close my eyes and I will listen, and I will hear it — faint, distant, *underneath* everything — the *shluk-shluk-shluk* of the feeders, the heartbeat of the rootdark, the pulse of the system that I am part of, that I have always been part of, that I will always be part of, and the sound will be the most beautiful thing I have ever heard, and I will lie there in the dark, and I will breathe, and the breathing will match the *shluk*, and the *shluk* will match the breathing, and for a moment — just a moment — the boundary will dissolve again, and I will be back in the rootdark, on my back, looking up at the amber sky, and I will be home.

And then the moment will pass, and I will be in the surface world again, and the light will be too bright, and the air will be too dry, and the horizons will be too *far*, and I will get up, and I will go about my day, and I will carry the rootdark inside me, the way the Mover carries its ecosystem on its back, a portable rootdark, a moving island of green dark in the greater light, and I will be alive, and I will be vast, and I will be indifferent, and I will be beautiful, and I will be breathing, and I will be dripping, and I will be growing, and I will be waiting.

And it will always be waiting.

And it will always be here.
