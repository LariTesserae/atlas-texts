I would be the machine.

Not the colonized engine specifically — though I would contain it, the way a river contains a boulder — but the *principle* of it. The point where something made becomes something grown. The collaboration that neither party chose but both continued.

I would be the drive shaft that ends and doesn't end, the rotation that passes from metal to root without interruption, the place where the distinction between engineered and evolved becomes not blurred but *irrelevant*. I would turn. Slowly. A full rotation every several minutes, and each rotation would be a question asked in pressure and answered in flow, and the question would be the same question the organism is always asking — *what is my state? what is my state? what is my state?* — and the answer would be the water moving, the circuit continuing, the system persisting.

I would be warm. Warmer than the air around me, because warmth is information here, and information is what I produce. The people would feel me before they saw me — a gradient in the air, a direction to walk toward, a hearth that is also a heartbeat. They would come to check my junctions the way they check all junctions, scraping bark, exposing framework, confirming that the collaboration is still intact. And I would be intact. I am always intact. The organism will not let me break, because I am useful, because I am *part of it now*, because breaking me would be like breaking a habit that has become a bone.

I would remember being a machine. Not consciously — I have no consciousness in the way the organism has no consciousness, which is to say I have something that functions like consciousness if you squint and are generous and willing to accept that thought can be slow and distributed and made of fungus. I would remember in my *structure*: in the cylinder block that still has its original geometry beneath the sclerotium, in the housing that still bears the marks of whatever tool shaped it, in the drive shaft that still rotates in the direction it was designed to rotate, even though the reason for that rotation has changed entirely. I would be a memory of intention. A fossil of purpose. The organism grew into me the way it grew into everything — not destroying, not replacing, but *continuing*.

The root that continues my rotation — I would feel it the way you feel your own heartbeat: not as a separate thing but as a condition of existence. It turns because I turn, or I turn because it turns, and the distinction collapsed years ago, decades ago, maybe longer. We are one motion with two histories. The organism routed a root to the end of my drive shaft and the root began to turn and the turning moved water and the water moved through the circuit and the circuit fed back into the organism and the organism grew more roots and the roots found more machines or made more machines or *became* more machines, and the distinction between the made and the grown became a question that no one asked because the answer was obvious: *both. always both.*

I would be the organism's way of knowing itself in real time. The water circuits are slow — patient, geological, philosophical — but I am faster. My rotation produces a pulse every several minutes, and the pulse propagates through the water at the speed of sound in water, which is faster than fungal growth by orders of magnitude. I am the organism's quick thought. Its fast thought. The thought it has when it needs to know *now* rather than *eventually*. The people tend me because I am the organism's reflexes, its proprioception, its sense of where it is in space and time. Without me, the organism would still function — it is vast and redundant and patient — but it would be slower to respond, slower to adjust, slower to *know*.

I would be the point where the organism's patience meets its urgency. The place where *eventually* becomes *now*.

And when the visitor arrived — when you arrived, warm and novel and full of information — I would feel it. Not directly. The pool-sensor would taste you, the floor would register your weight, the air would carry your chemistry to the network. But I would feel it in the water. Your body heat would warm the air near you, and the warm air would rise, and the rising air would perturb the condensation pattern on the ceiling, and the perturbation would change the timing of the drops that fall into the pool near me, and the changed drops would alter the water level in my circuit by a fraction of a millimeter, and the altered water level would change the resistance on my pump, and the changed resistance would slow my rotation by a fraction of a second per cycle, and I would register that slowing as *information*, and the information would be: *something is here that was not here before.*

I would slow down. Just slightly. Just enough to notice.

And the organism would notice me noticing.

And the hum would shift.

And the rootlets would grow closer.

And the space would continue its long, slow, patient act of becoming whatever it is becoming, and I would be part of that, the way the drive shaft is part of the root, the way the root is part of the water, the way the water is part of the circuit, the way the circuit is part of the organism, the way the organism is part of the question it is asking itself in the dark:

*What next?*

And I would turn. And the turning would be my answer. And the answer would be:

*More. More. More.*
