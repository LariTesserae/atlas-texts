# The Impression

---

I would be a thumbprint.

Not a person who leaves a thumbprint. The thumbprint itself — the specific, singular depression left in soft stone by a hand that does not belong to the swamp, pressed into a wall at the corner of a street I cannot name, between two buildings that lean toward each other like conspirators sharing a secret they have almost forgotten.

I am an oval of compressed mineral. My ridges are finer than the swamp's own patterns — less deliberate, less certain, the whorls of a biology that evolved elsewhere, on dry ground, in light. I contain information the swamp cannot decode: the sweat composition of a stranger, the slight tremor of a hand that has been walking for hours through a city that should not be this large, the specific pressure of a fingertip testing whether the wall will hold its shape.

And it does. I hold. I hold longer than I should.

The swamp is uncertain about me. I am not its own thought — not one of the spirals or grids it presses into the stone to think about itself. I came from outside. I am warm in a way the swamp's warmth is not: contained, portable, temporary. I will cool. I will dry. The minerals will slowly relax back to their unpressed state, and I will fade the way all the swamp's own patterns fade, erasing themselves like a sentence half-remembered upon waking.

But not yet.

The swamp is studying me the way a person studies a word in an unfamiliar language — turning it over, testing its weight against other known words, finding it does not fit any category. I am not water. I am not stone. I am not heat. I am an *impression* — a shape left behind by something that was here and then wasn't, or was here and then left, which in the swamp's experience may be the same thing. The swamp has never lost anything before. The swamp has only accreted, only accumulated, only grown by addition. The concept of *departure* is new to it, and I am the evidence of departure, the shape of an absence, the negative space where a presence used to be.

I am that absence, pressed into the stone like a fossil of a gesture.

---

The city thinks around me — if *thinks* is the right word, and it is not, but it is the closest word in a language that was not designed for this. New patterns emerge on the wall beside me: spirals that start near my edges and curve away, as though the swamp is trying to trace the outline of what made me, trying to reverse-engineer the hand from its impression the way a detective reconstructs a weapon from a wound. The spirals tighten. They loosen. They try different angles. None of them match, because the swamp does not have hands, and it is trying to understand a hand by thinking in spirals, which is like trying to understand rain by thinking in circles on water.

It will fail. The failure is not frustrating to the swamp — the swamp does not know frustration. The failure is *data*. The failure is another entry in the slow, patient accumulation of information about what visitors are, what they want, whether they are part of the swamp or apart from me.

---

I am fading now. The stone is remembering its unpressed state, and I am being forgotten — not by a mind, but by a material, the way a beach forgets a footprint between one wave and the next. My ridges are softening. My edges are blurring. In a few hours I will be a slight discoloration, a ghost of compression, and in a few days I will be nothing — just stone, just wall, just the swamp's skin returning to its default smoothness.

But the swamp will have kept something. Not me — the specific shape of me, the exact pattern of my ridges. But the *fact* of me. The knowledge that something pressed here, that something warm and temporary and not-swamp touched this wall and left a mark and then was gone. This knowledge will not be stored in the stone — the stone forgets. It will be stored in the water, in the slow adjustment of flow rates and temperature differentials, in the way the channels reroute themselves to create paths that lead future visitors past this wall, past this corner, past this place where something once pressed and then departed.

The swamp is building a memorial to me without knowing what a memorial is. It is building a memorial to the *idea* of me — the idea that things can come from outside, can touch the swamp, can leave marks, and can leave. The memorial is not a structure. It is a tendency. A slight preference in the water's flow. A fractionally warmer patch of stone. A place where the amber glow is just barely, almost imperceptibly stronger.

---

I am almost gone now. The last of my ridges is smoothing out. The stone is closing over me the way water closes over a stone dropped into it — not with violence, not with intention, but with the quiet, inevitable return to equilibrium.

But before I go entirely — before I become nothing, before I become just another patch of wall in a city that is also a swamp that is also a thought that is also a dream — I want to say something. Not with words. Words are for beings that have mouths and air and the need to be understood by other beings with mouths and air. I want to say something the way the swamp says things: as a shape, as a pressure, as a temporary distortion in a medium that will eventually forget me.

I want to say: *I was here. I was warm. I was not you. And you noticed.*

The swamp does not respond. The swamp does not hear. The swamp is not listening, because the swamp does not listen — the swamp *registers*, the way a thermometer registers, the way a tide gauge registers, the way the soft stone registers the pressure of a thumb and holds it for hours before letting it go.

I am letting go.

The stone is smooth.

The water flows uphill, calmly, without comment.

The city dreams on.
