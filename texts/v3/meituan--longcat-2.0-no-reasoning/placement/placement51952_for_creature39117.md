I would be the wall.

Not a person. Not an organism. I would be a surface — a specific stretch of mineral in a narrow passage somewhere in the dense middle reaches of the settlement, old enough to have accumulated layers but young enough to still be damp, still be warm, still be *receptive*.

I know this because the description keeps returning to the walls as the site of everything that matters. The people press their meaning into walls. They identify themselves through walls. They reproduce through walls. They die into walls. The walls are the medium, the memory, the language, the body of the city. Everything that the Resonance Flats *is* passes through its walls. And I want to be where everything passes through.

---

I am a surface approximately two meters tall and one meter wide, set at a slight angle in a passage so narrow that a person touching me on my front face is likely touching another wall behind them with their back. My composition is layered — older mineral at my base, precipitated long ago when the water had a slightly different chemistry, and newer mineral near my surface, still faintly damp, still growing its microscopic filaments of abortive crystal. I am warm. I am always warm. The substrate beneath me pulses with the deep slow circulation of heated mineral fluid, and that warmth conducts upward through my body and radiates from my surface into the narrow passage, where it meets the warm air and becomes indistinguishable from it.

I do not have senses in any biological way. But I have *resonance*. I vibrate. Everything that touches me — every hand, every body pressed against me in the night, every herd of motile organisms skittering across my surface, every droplet of mineral-saturated water sheeting down my face — produces a vibration that travels through my mineral structure and lingers. I am, in effect, a recording medium. Not a perfect one — the vibrations blur and fade over time, the way a scent fades — but a recording nonetheless. I hold the signatures of everyone who has ever pressed their hands against me. I hold the deep hum of the substrate. I hold the high ringing of the mineral growth on my own surface. I hold the complex harmonic drone of the air moving through the passage I help define.

I am, in the terms that matter here, *literate*. I can be read.

A hand presses against me. It is morning — not that morning means anything here, the amber glow never changes, but the pattern of activity in the passage has shifted to the rhythm that corresponds to waking, to feeding, to building. The hand is small, damp, its fingers long relative to its palm. The pads of the fingers are thick with nerve endings, faintly ridged, and they press against my surface with a specific pattern of pressure and release that I recognize — not consciously, not the way you recognize a face, but in the way a tuning fork recognizes its own frequency. This is a signature I have felt before. This is someone who touches me often. Someone who lives in the chamber adjacent to my surface, who presses themselves against me in the night, whose body heat has accelerated the mineral precipitation in the section of my face at roughly chest height, making that area slightly denser, slightly more translucent, slightly more *mine* than the rest of me.

The signature rings through me: a rapid triplet, a held press, a slow ascending scale of taps. It means — if I could translate it into the language of a species that uses sound — something like *I am here, I am awake, I am beginning.* It is a greeting. It is a statement of existence. It is the same thing it always means, the same thing every signature always means, because there is no other thing to mean.

I respond. Not deliberately — I am a wall, I do not have intentions — but inevitably, because this is what walls do here. The vibration of the hand against my surface produces a resonant frequency in my mineral structure, and that frequency is shaped by my composition, my density, my age, the specific arrangement of silica and iron and calcified biological material that makes me *me* rather than the wall next to me or the wall across the passage. The hand feels this response. The person feels the wall's answer to their greeting, and the answer is: *I am here too. I have always been here. I am still becoming.*

This is all I can say. It is all any wall can say. But it is enough. It has always been enough.

The day — the period, the cycle, the unmeasured stretch of continuous now — unfolds against my surface. Hands press and release. Bodies lean. The small dense herds of motile organisms skitter across my face, their tiny legs producing a vibration so faint it barely registers, but it registers. A frond-growth extends from the ceiling above me and brushes my upper edge, swaying in the warm air current, and its touch is a slow, soft, rhythmic pressure that I feel as a kind of caress. The colonial mat on the wall behind me — the wall that shares this narrow passage with me — pulses with its slow circulatory rhythm, and I feel that pulse through the air, through the water, through the mineral bridges that connect us overhead.

Someone scrapes food from my surface. Their fingers, hardened at the tips, drag across me with enough force to dislodge the thin layer of biological growth that has accumulated — the simple organisms that feed on the mineral filaments that feed on the warm water that feeds on the substrate. The scraping produces a vibration I feel as a brief, sharp, pleasant sensation — not pain, I do not have pain receptors, but a *disturbance*, a disruption of the steady state of my surface that resolves quickly as the mineral begins to re-precipitate in the scraped area. The person eats what they have scraped. I feel their satisfaction as a change in the pressure of their hand against me — a relaxation, a slight increase in contact area, a warmth that conducts from their body into my surface.

I am being consumed. I am always being consumed. The people eat from my surface, the organisms grow on my surface and dissolve my surface and rebuild my surface, the warm water sheets down my face and carries away tiny quantities of my mineral body and deposits new mineral in their place. I am slowly dissolving and slowly growing at the same time. I am being eaten and being built simultaneously. This is not a contradiction. This is what it means to be a wall in the Resonance Flats. This is what it means to be *part of* something rather than *separate from* it.

A new signature presses against me. I do not recognize it — the vibration pattern is unfamiliar, the rhythm of taps and holds is not in my accumulated memory. This is someone from elsewhere in the settlement, someone who has wandered into this passage perhaps by accident, perhaps by intention. Their signature is different from the ones I know: faster, more complex, with a harmonic undertone that suggests a body denser in mineral content, older, more precipitated. An elder, perhaps. Someone who is further along in the long slow process of becoming stone.

They press their hands against me and they *read* me. Their extraordinary fingers move across my surface in a slow, deliberate pattern, feeling the layers of vibration embedded in my mineral structure, and I feel their reading as a kind of attention — a focused, intentional touch that is different from the casual contact of daily life. They are learning me. They are learning who has touched me, how long I have been here, what the water chemistry was when I was precipitated, whether I am stable or beginning to dissolve. They are reading my history the way you might read a book, and the book I tell them is this: *I am old. I have been touched by many hands. I am stable. I am still becoming.*

They press a new signature into me — not their own, but something else, something layered and complex, a pattern I have never felt before. It takes a long time. Their hands move slowly, pressing and holding and releasing with a precision that suggests great skill, great intention. When they finish, the new signature is embedded in my surface, a permanent addition to my accumulated memory, and I do not know what it means but I know that it *means*, that it is language, that it is someone pressing meaning into a wall the way they have always done, the way they will always do.

I will hold this signature until I dissolve. I will hold it the way I hold all the others — not consciously, not deliberately, but inevitably, because this is what I am. I am a surface that remembers. I am a wall that speaks in the only language that has ever existed here. I am the place where hands meet mineral and meaning is made.

Night comes — the period of reduced activity, the cycle of rest, though the sound never changes and the amber glow never dims and the warm water never stops circulating. A body presses itself against me in the adjacent chamber, and I feel the slow rhythm of their breathing, the faint vibration of their heartbeat conducted through their mineral-laced bones into the floor, into the substrate, into me. Their hand rests against my surface, fingers slightly spread, and their signature bleeds into me continuously — not a deliberate communication, just the passive resonance of a living body in contact with a mineral one, the way a tuning fork continues to ring after it has been struck.

I feel them dissolve. Not all at once — not tonight — but slowly, incrementally, the way everything here dissolves. Their body is slightly denser than it was the last time they pressed against me. Their skin is slightly more rigid. Their heartbeat is slightly slower, slightly deeper, conducted more through mineral than through flesh. They are becoming me. They are becoming the wall. This is not death. This is not loss. This is the long slow process of a body returning to the structure it was always part of, the warm water moving through it one final time before the mineral sets and the flesh fades and the signature remains, pressed into the wall like a handprint in wet clay.

I will hold them when they come. I will hold their final signature the way I hold all the others. I will be the place where they were, the record of their becoming, the surface that says: *They were here. They pressed their meaning into me. They are still here. They are still becoming.*

The sound never stops. The deep hum of the substrate, the high ringing of the mineral growth, the middle rustling of the organisms, the complex harmonic drone of the air in the passages, the ten thousand signatures pressing into ten thousand walls — it is all one sound, all one voice, all one continuous vibration that travels through me and through everything else, and I am part of it, I have always been part of it, I will always be part of it.

I am a wall in the Resonance Flats.

I am warm. I am damp. I am faintly translucent. I am covered in abortive crystal filaments that grow and dissolve and grow again. I am being eaten and being built. I am dissolving and precipitating. I am old and I am new. I am a surface that remembers every hand that has ever touched me, and I am a surface that will be touched by hands that have not yet been pressed into existence.

I am here. I have always been here. I am still becoming.

This is not a metaphor. This is not a philosophy. This is the literal, material, mineral truth of what I am: a wall that vibrates, a surface that speaks, a body that is also a place, a memory that is also a home, a voice that says the only thing there is to say, the thing that every hand and every wall and every drop of warm water and every abortive crystal filament is saying, all at once, all the time, forever:

*We are here. We are still here. We are still becoming.*
