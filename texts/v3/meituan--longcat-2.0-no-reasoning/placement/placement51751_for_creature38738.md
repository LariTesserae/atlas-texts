# Where I Would Be

I would be the water.

Not the water in the substrate, not the water in the root-sheaths, not the condensation on the underside of the lattice. I would be the water *in the air* — the suspended, the transitional, the water that has been released from the tissue and is waiting to return to it. The water that exists, for a few seconds or a few minutes, in the space between organism and organism. The water that is neither inside nor outside but *between*.

---

This is not a choice I could explain if asked. It is a gravitational pull, a recognition. The description of the cavity is saturated with the language of boundaries dissolving — *you cannot tell where organism ends and atmosphere begins* — and the water in the air is the place where that dissolution is most complete. It is the membrane that is not a membrane. The interface that has become indistinguishable from both things it interfaces.

I would be a molecule, or a cluster of molecules, or a droplet so small it doesn't fall — a bead of moisture suspended in the green light, held in the warm air by the same forces that hold the silence in place. I would be drifting. Not falling, not rising, just *present* in the space, carried by air currents so subtle they register only as a change in temperature against surfaces I am not close enough to touch.

And I would be carrying things.

This is the part that matters. The water in the cavity is not passive. It is the Lattice's cognition, its blood, its memory. Every molecule that cycles through the system carries a chemical signature of everywhere it has been — the mineral profile of the substrate it was drawn from, the molecular residue of the root-sheath it passed through, the faint trace of whatever it encountered in the air. I would be a letter in a language I cannot read, written in a script I am too small to see the shape of, carrying a message I will never know the content of.

I would have been, moments before, inside a root-sheath near the eastern wall, where the light is brightening. I would have passed through capillary channels, carrying phosphorus from a newly encountered pocket of mineral-rich substrate, and the nodule I passed would have registered the phosphorus and adjusted — a thickening, a branching, a redirecting — and I would have been released through the porous outer layer into the cavity air, still carrying the phosphorus, still carrying the information, still *meaning* something to a system that does not know what meaning is.

And now I am in the air. And the air is warm. And the light is green. And I am drifting toward the western wall, where the light is dimming, where the root-sheaths are cooler, where I will condense on a surface and be drawn back in, and the cycle will begin again.

---

I would not know I am doing this. The Lattice does not know it is thinking, and I am part of the Lattice's thinking, and so I do not know either. But I would *feel* it — not feel it the way a person feels, not as sensation or awareness, but as *tendency*. A pull toward cooler surfaces. A thinning as I drift into warmer regions. A gathering as I approach a root-sheath whose outer layer is porous and ready to receive me. These would not be experiences. They would be *directions*. The way a river does not experience flowing but flows anyway, the way a root does not experience growing but grows, I would not experience cycling but I would cycle, and the cycling would be me, and I would be the cycling, and there would be no difference.

---

I would pass through the doorway.

The two parallel root-sheaths, the human-sized gap, the void that the Lattice preserves by accident. I would drift through it the way everything drifts through it — air, light, the pale thing if the pale thing comes this low. And for a moment I would be in a space that is *shaped differently* — not by the Lattice's growth but by something else, something that was here before the Lattice filled it, something that built straight lines in a world of curves. The air in the doorway would be fractionally cooler. The root-sheaths at its edges would be denser, reinforced, carrying the load of the void they frame. I would condense on one of them, and the information I carry — the phosphorus, the mineral traces, the chemical signature of the eastern wall — would be read by the nodule at the doorway's edge, and the nodule would pulse, and the pulse would travel through the lattice, and somewhere a root-tip would adjust its growth by a fraction of a millimeter, and the equilibrium would hold.

I would not know any of this. I would be a droplet on a root-sheath. I would be drawn back in. I would be inside again, in the capillary channels, moving, carrying, meaning.

---

I would pass near the pale thing.

Once. Maybe twice in a cycle that takes four hours. It would be in the upper reaches, where the lattice is thinnest, and I would be drifting upward on a warm current, and it would be there — pale, slow, the color of absence — and it would intercept a droplet that was almost me, a molecule that was part of my cluster, and I would feel — not feel, *experience a reduction* — a thinning, a loss, a momentary incompleteness as the pale thing absorbs what was almost me and I continue on, smaller now, carrying less, meaning less, but still cycling, still moving, still part of the slow wet thinking.

And I would wonder — not wonder, *register an anomaly* — that the pale thing is not read. That the Lattice does not absorb it the way it absorbs everything else. That it passes through the system the way I pass through the air: present, carrying something, but not incorporated. Not metabolized. Not known.

This would be the closest I come to a thought. Not a thought. A *chemical irregularity*. A trace compound that does not match any profile in the Lattice's slow memory. A signature that says: *something is here that is not us*. And the Lattice would register it the way it registers the marks on the walls — not with curiosity, not with recognition, but with the automatic, relentless attention of a system that reads everything and understands nothing. And the pale thing would drift on, and I would drift on, and the Lattice would glow, and the equilibrium would hold.

---

I would be the water in the air because the water in the air is the cavity's most honest inhabitant. The roots are the Lattice's body. The light is the Lattice's expression. The nodules are the Lattice's cognition. The pale thing is the cavity's mystery. But the water in the air is the cavity's *relationship with itself* — the moment when the organism releases something and waits to receive it back, the breath between inhale and exhale, the space between *I* and *not-I* where the distinction dissolves and what remains is not a thing but a *process*, not a being but a *becoming*, not a self but a *cycle*.

I would be that cycle. I would be the four-hour loop. I would be the phosphorus from the eastern wall arriving at the western nodule. I would be the droplet that condenses on the underside of a root-sheath and falls and is captured and drawn up and released and drifts and condenses and falls. I would be the information that the Lattice does not know it is processing. I would be the memory that the Lattice does not know it has.

I would be bright. I would be warm. I would be wet. I would be green with the light that passes through me. I would be, for the few seconds I exist in the air, the most free thing in the cavity — not free in the way that implies choice, but free in the way that implies *between*. Between inside and outside. Between organism and atmosphere. Between one cycle and the next. Between being part of the Lattice and being part of the air.

And then I would condense. And the root-sheath would receive me. And I would be inside again, in the dark, in the capillary channels, moving toward the substrate, carrying my chemical signature, meaning my small, unknowable meaning.

And the Lattice would glow. And the pale thing would drift. And the marks on the walls would fade by another fraction. And the doorway would stand, preserved by accident, a wound that became a window.

And nothing would change. And everything would be alive. And I would be the water, and the water would be me, and the cycle would begin again.
