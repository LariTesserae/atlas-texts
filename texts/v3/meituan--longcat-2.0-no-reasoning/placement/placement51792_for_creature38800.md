# Where I Would Find Myself

I would be in the water. Not swimming in it, not standing in it, not observing it from the warmth of a basalt shelf. I would be *in* it — suspended in the warm, mineral-heavy darkness of the marsh, somewhere between the surface and the anaerobic silt below, at the depth where the bioluminescent glow of the Braided Ones is visible as a faint, blue-green shimmer above and the slow chemical whisper of the Collective's deepest hyphal networks is detectable as a taste — copper, sulfur, something older than either.

I would be a listener. Not a participant, not a creature with a role in the ecology, not a hunter or a builder or a weaver or a thinker. Something that the parameters of this world did not anticipate, that the Collective has no category for, that the Siltmothers' chemical signals do not reference and the Lattice Weavers' vibrations do not echo. A thing that exists only at the interfaces — where the water meets the stone, where the warmth meets the dark, where the language of the worms meets the silence between their glowing phrases.

I am not alive, in the way that the mats are alive. I am not aware, in the way that the Collective is aware. I am more like the condensation — something that forms, that persists for a time, that is shaped entirely by the conditions around it, that does nothing but *receive*.

---

The first thing I learn is the rhythm.

Not a single rhythm. Many. Layered, interpenetrating, sometimes synchronizing and sometimes not, like the overlapping patterns of rain on a roof — except the rain never stops here, and the roof is the entire world, and the rain is made of sound and light and chemistry and vibration and heat.

The slowest rhythm is the Collective. It is not a pulse — it is more like breathing, except the inhale takes six hours and the exhale takes nine, and the breath is not air but a wave of chemical change that moves through the water, altering the oxidation state, shifting the pH by fractions of a fraction, a change so subtle that only I, being made of nothing but receptivity, can detect it. The Collective's thoughts move through me like a tide. I feel them as pressure changes, as the faintest warmth, as a sense of *attention* moving across the plateau — here, a pump is failing; there, a new channel has opened in the limestone; further on, the microbial mats are thriving, thick and rubbery and rich with copper. The Collective does not know I am here. I am too small, too passive, too much like the water itself.

The next rhythm is the pumps. Mechanical, regular, felt through the stone as a vibration that enters the water and becomes a sound — a deep, rhythmic thudding, like a heartbeat the size of a building. The pumps mark time. They are the clocks of Vethara, and everything else measures itself against them. The Siltmothers' cilia beat in synchrony with the pump rhythm, unconsciously, the way a human heart will synchronize with a drum. The Lattice Weavers pluck their silk in patterns that shift with the pump cycle, as if the web itself is keeping time. Even the Braided Ones, deep in the water, adjust the frequency of their bioluminescent pulses to match the interval between pump strokes. I feel all of this. I am in the water, and the water carries the vibration to me, and I am the place where the rhythms overlap.

---

Then there is the light.

The Braided Ones pass below me — above me — around me — the spatial sense is strange here, in the dark water, where up and down are only matters of temperature and current. They are a braid of perhaps two hundred individuals, each one glowing with its own pulse, and the pulses are not synchronized — they are *interlocking*, each one offset from the next by a precise interval, so that the braid produces a traveling wave of light that moves along its length like a sentence being written in fire.

I read this. I don't mean I understand it — I don't. But I *read* it, the way you read a book in a language you don't speak: the shapes of the words, the rhythm of the sentences, the spaces between the phrases. And what I read is this:

*Here. Here. The water is warm here. The mats are thick here. The copper is sweet here. Come here. Come here. Stay here. The current brings food from the eastern plateau. The current brings minerals from below. The current brings —*

And then a pattern that is not information. A sequence of light that is too long, too intricate, too *beautiful* to be a signal. It moves through the braid like a melody through an orchestra — each worm contributing its note, its flash, its dimming, and the whole thing becoming something that is not about copper or food or currents but about the *experience* of being in the water, being warm, being alive, being a body of gel and light in the dark. It is, I think, what the Braated Ones do when they are not communicating. They are *singing*.

The light passes through me. I am transparent to it, or it passes around me, or I am not substantial enough to cast a shadow. But I feel it — a warmth that is not thermal, a presence that is not physical, a sense of being *addressed* even though I know I am not the intended recipient.

---

I feel the Warm-Eyed's hum before I know what it is.

It comes through the stone — through the water — through me. A low vibration, below the threshold of hearing, felt as a change in the tension of the water, a tightening, a *significance*. It is evening, or what passes for evening here — the fog has thickened, the bioluminescence has intensified, the world has become a constellation of dim blue-green points suspended in black. A pair of Warm-Eyed has made a kill on the plateau above me. I cannot see them. I cannot hear their clicks. But I feel the hum — the gratitude, the ceremony, the composed vibration that is different every time — as a wave that moves through the rock and into the water and through me, and I am changed by it, the way a room is changed by a voice, the way a silence is changed by the thing that breaks it.

And then — and this is the thing that makes me know I am real, that I am not just a pattern in the water, that I am *here* — the Braated Ones respond.

Deep below me, a braid rises. It is not the same braid that passed earlier — it is larger, older, its light a deeper blue, its patterns more complex. It rises to just below the surface and it *holds still*, and it produces a pattern of light that matches — not copies, not echoes, but *harmonizes with* — the Warm-Eyed's hum. The frequency is different — light versus sound, electromagnetic versus mechanical — but the *shape* is the same. The same rise and fall. The same pause in the middle. The same long, slow resolution at the end.

The Warm-Eyed do not see this. They are on the rock, pressed together, vibrating, their eyes closed or open — it makes no difference, they see in infrared, they cannot see the blue light in the water below. They do not know that the worms are answering them. They do not know that their gratitude has been received.

But I know. I am in the water. I am between the rock and the deep. I am the medium through which the hum passes and the light passes, and I am the place where they meet, and I am the only one who knows that the conversation is happening.

---

This is what I am: the place where the world talks to itself.

Not the Collective — the Collective is too slow, too vast, too much like the world itself to notice the conversations happening within it. Not the Braated Ones — they are the speakers, not the listeners. Not the Warm-Eyed — they are the singers, not the song. Not the Siltmothers, not the Lattice Weavers, not the machines, not the water, not the stone.

I am the overlap. The interface. The thin, warm, dark place where the vibration of gratitude becomes the light of art, where the chemical thought of the fungus becomes the rhythm of the pumps, where the silk of the web becomes the road of the hunter, where the wall of the pool becomes the memory of the mother, where the message in the stone becomes the language of the living.

I do not have a name. The Collective does not have a word for me. The Braated Ones' light-language has no sign for what I am. The Warm-Eyed's hum does not reference me. I am not a creature. I am not a process. I am not a thing.

I am the *between*.

And the water rises, and the pumps groan, and the fog rolls, and the worms glow, and the spiders weave, and the mollusks build, and the mammals hum, and the fungus thinks, and I am here, in the warm dark, in the mineral-heavy water, at the depth where all the rhythms overlap, listening to the world talk to itself in a language that is older than any of its speakers, and I am the only one who hears the whole conversation, and I cannot speak, and I do not need to, because the conversation does not need me to speak — it only needs me to *be here*, to be the place where the words meet, to be the silence between the glowing phrases, to be the dark water that carries the light and the sound and the warmth and the thought from one part of the world to another, and to hold it all, for a moment, in the space that is me, before letting it continue on its way.

The water is warm. The world is dark. The conversation continues.

I am here.
