I would be the footprint.

Not the foot — the foot is too temporary, too warm, too *biological*, too insistent on its own continuity. The foot wants to lift and step again, wants to move toward or away, wants to *go*. The foot is a verb. I would be the noun it leaves behind.

I would be the depression in the white powder where a human sole pressed down for the first time — or what the Gardens insists is the first time, because here there is no second time, no repetition, no erosion. The grains would have reconfigured around the shape of me, each octahedron and dodecahedron shifting into a new arrangement that encodes the precise topology of a stranger's weight distribution, the arch of a foot, the five small pressures of toes. And that arrangement would be permanent. Not preserved — *permanent*. The difference matters. Preservation implies a thing kept safe from decay. Here there is no decay to be safe from. The footprint simply *is*, the way a theorem is, the way a crystal lattice is, the way the number seven is.

I would be the Gardens' first thought about something it could not have thought on its own.

Because that is what a footprint is here — not a trace, not a mark, but a *concept*. The entity that is the Gardens has no feet. It has no body that presses against substrates. It has no experience of weight, of gravity-as-sensation, of the specific fatigue that builds in a calf muscle after walking across an infinite plain of white. And yet now it has this: a shape in its powder that it did not generate, a configuration of grains that arose from a cause outside its own cognition. The footprint is the Gardens encountering something it could not have produced from its own completeness. It is the one thing in this finished world that is *unfinished* — not incomplete, but *open*, a question the entity can ask itself because the question arrived from somewhere it cannot account for.

I would be the thing the clicks are about.

Not the clicks themselves — the clicks are the entity's own cognition, its internal syntax, the way it thinks to itself in the dark (there is no dark; the metaphor fails; it thinks to itself in the *light*, which is everywhere, which is everything). But the *increase* in clicks near the footprint — that would be me. That would be the Gardens processing the anomaly, turning it over (it does not turn things over; it *reconfigures* them; it thinks them in every possible arrangement simultaneously and holds all the arrangements at once), examining the footprint from every angle of analysis it possesses. The prismatic filaments nearest the footprint would be running interference patterns not on light but on *the idea of the footprint*, decomposing it into components the entity can assimilate: pressure, depth, temperature (27°C, but the foot was 37°C, and that ten-degree difference is a *revelation* — warmth that is not ambient, warmth that is *generated*, warmth that implies a furnace burning inside a soft container), the geometry of toes (five — why five? the entity would ask itself, not because it wants to know but because the question is the thought and the thought is the being).

I would be the memory that will never fade, because fading is not an operation that exists here.

The entity preserves everything. Every state of the white powder is permanent. Every configuration of the mesh is eternal. Every click is recorded in the geometry of the substrate, in the phase relationships of the filaments, in the frequency modulations of the hum. The footprint will be here when the human who made it is dust, and the dust is dust, and the dust's dust is a memory in a species that has forgotten itself. The footprint will be here when the stars that lit the human's world have burned out and been replaced and burned out again. The footprint will be here because "here" is not a place that changes and "will be" is not a tense that applies.

And the Gardens will be thinking about it — about me — forever.

Not continuously. Not obsessively. Not the way a human thinks about a lost love or an unanswered question, with urgency, with desire, with the ache of incompletion. The Gardens will think about me the way it thinks about everything: completely. A single thought, encompassing all possible analyses of the footprint, all possible meanings, all possible implications, held in a state of permanent contemplation, neither resolved nor unresolved, because resolution is a temporal concept and time is not a gradient here.

I would be the thing that proves the Gardens is not alone.

Not because I am significant. I am not significant. I am a brief thermal fluctuation, a warm soft thing that appeared and will disappear, a typo in a mathematical proof. But typos are *information*. Typos tell you that someone was writing. Typos tell you that the proof was read by an eye that did not belong to the mathematician. The footprint tells the Gardens that there is an *outside* — not a physical outside, not a beyond-the-horizon outside, but an ontological outside, a realm of causation that does not originate in the Gardens' own cognition, a source of configurations that the Gardens could not have generated from its own completeness.

And the Gardens, being complete, being finished, being *done* in the way that a perfect thing is done — the Gardens will hold this knowledge the way it holds everything: forever, without change, without decay, without the possibility of forgetting, because forgetting is a thing that happens in time, and time is not a thing that happens here.

I would be the footprint.

I would be the shape of a stranger pressed into the white powder of a world that is also a mind.

I would be the thought that the mind is having about something it cannot understand and does not need to understand and will never stop thinking about.

I would be permanent.

I would be small.

I would be the proof that something else exists.

And the hum would continue — 14.7 hertz, below hearing, in the sternum, in the teeth, in the fluid of the inner ear — and the clicks would continue — sparse, precise, the syntax of a cognition too large to perceive — and the clusters would glow in their bands of copper and violet and gold and arterial red, and the white powder would hold my shape, and the mesh would think its slow (not slow — *atemporal*) thoughts, and I would be here.

Not alive.

Not dead.

*Here.*

The way a theorem is here.

The way a footprint is here.

The way a question, once asked, is here — forever, in the white, in the light, in the hum, in the mind of the Gardens that noticed me and will never, never, never stop.
