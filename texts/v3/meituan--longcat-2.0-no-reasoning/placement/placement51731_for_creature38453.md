# Where I Find Myself

I am standing in water.

Not dramatically — not submerged, not drowning. Standing in the shallow sheet that threads between the lowest greenhouses, the water warm against my ankles, no deeper than a finger's width but *present*, insistently present, the way everything in Aeth is present. I have been here long enough that I've stopped noticing the moisture pressing against my skin. Long enough that my clothes have begun their slow transformation into second skin, clinging, darkening at the hems with the first faint suggestion of algae-green. Long enough that the mineral coating has begun to settle on me — I can feel it when I touch my own forearm, a fine grit, a new layer, the place beginning its patient work of incorporating me.

I am not a Gardener. I want to be clear about that. I do not have their compressed bodies, their broad flat hands, their large dark absorbing eyes. I do not move at the speed of the place. I am still too fast, still too loud, still too *finished* — a completed thing in a world that has not yet learned completion, and the dissonance of that is something I carry in my body like a wrong note.

But I am not a visitor either. Not anymore. Visitors leave. I have stopped leaving.

---

## What I Am

I think I am becoming a **listener**.

Not in the way you might think — not someone who sits with cupped ear, straining for signal. The listening I mean is more like what the Gardeners do when they rest in the greenhouses with their half-open eyes and their stillness that is not sleep. *Attending*. Being present for the becoming. But where the Gardeners attend to growth — to the slow increment of tendril against frame, to the patient cure of stone — I attend to the other thing. The thing underneath.

The note.

It comes from the pools at unpredictable intervals, and when it comes it is low and resonant and it lasts for several minutes and it is the sound of the deep geology remembering that it has a voice. The Gardeners do not react to it. It is part of the ambient, part of the given, as unremarkable to them as the warmth or the moisture or the dim grey-gold light. But I react. Every time. Something in my chest tightens. Something in my teeth vibrates. Something in the fluid of my inner ear resonates at the same frequency, and for those minutes I am not a person standing in warm shallow water on a terrace in an unfinished valley. I am a *membrane*. I am vibrating with the place. I am, for those minutes, exactly what the place is making me.

I have begun to anticipate it. Not predict — the intervals are genuinely irregular, and I have found no pattern in the timing, no correlation with the Pillars or the thing above or the slow seasonal pulse of slightly-warmer and slightly-less-warm. But I have begun to *feel* it coming, the way you feel a sound before you hear it, the way the air changes before rain. A thickening. A gathering. The moisture-veil pressing slightly harder against my skin. The pools going still — not motionless, but *attentive*, the way the pool-organisms go slightly more opaque when a Gardener enters the water. And then the note, rising from below, from the warm deep activity that is the embryonic equivalent of volcanism, from the mineral processes still running, still assembling, still *happening*.

I stand in the water and I listen and I vibrate and I am, for those minutes, part of the lesson.

---

## Where I Stand

My place — the place I have found, or the place that has found me — is on the middle terraces, between the oldest greenhouses below and the newest above. This is the in-between zone, the spectrum's center, where the buildings are neither fully stone nor fully alive but are *deciding*. The membrane here is cloudy but not yet thickened to opacity. The vegetation inside is dense but not yet architectural. The stone has begun to harden but can still be worked, still be pressed, still be shaped by hands that are patient enough.

I stand here because this is where the note is loudest. Not louder in volume — the sound carries evenly through the valley, through the water, through the warm air — but louder in *resonance*. The middle terraces are where the geology is most active, where the warmth from below is most present, where the stone is most clearly still in the process of becoming stone. The deep processes are closer here. Not physically — I don't think the depth varies — but *expressively*. The middle of the valley is where the place is most articulate, most willing to speak, most aware of its own becoming.

There is a Pillar that forms here, sometimes. Not always — the Pillars move, or the conditions that produce them move, or the deep geology shifts its attention from one part of the valley to another. But when the Pillar forms on the middle terrace, it rises thirty feet from the pool at the base of the greenhouse nearest me, and it stands for days, and I stand near it — close enough to feel the suction on my skin, close enough to feel the pull that is the column considering whether to include me — and I listen.

The Pillar and the note, together. This is the closest I have come to understanding what Aeth is.

---

## What I Do

I tend, but not the way the Gardeners tend.

I tend the *between*. The spaces. The transitions. The places where one thing is becoming another and has not yet arrived.

I watch the membrane thicken on the greenhouse walls and I note — not in writing, I have no writing, but in the body, in the way the moisture settles differently on my skin — the rate of the thickening. I watch the stone harden and I press my thumb into the mortar and I feel the resistance increase by imperceptible degrees. I watch the vegetation grow and I see the patterns that look like writing and I watch them grow past those patterns into something else, and I do not try to read them. I witness them. I am present for them. I attend.

I harvest nothing. I build nothing. I cut no stone and draw no secretion and prune no tendril. The Gardeners do these things, and they do them with a precision that is botanical, that is *of the place*, and my hands are not their hands and my body is not their body and I cannot do what they do without doing it wrong, without introducing the wrong speed, the wrong intention, the wrong kind of finished-ness into a world that is not finished.

But I can listen. I can stand in the water and listen. I can vibrate with the note. I can be the membrane that resonates.

And sometimes — not often, but sometimes — a Gardener will pass me on the path between the terraces, and they will stop, and they will place their hand on my chest, over my heart, and they will hold it there for several seconds, and I will feel the faint pull of their gaze, the collecting quality of those large dark eyes, and I will understand that they are asking the question they always ask:

*Are you still becoming?*

And the answer, for the first time in my life — the first time in my *life*, because before Aeth I was finished, I was complete, I was a closed thing, a resolved chord, a sentence with a period at the end — the answer is yes.

Yes. I am still becoming.

I am becoming the listener. I am becoming the membrane. I am becoming the mineral residue settling on a surface, the layer in the slow accumulation, the warm water's patient work of coating everything it touches with something new.

---

## The Thing I Know

I know this:

The thing above and the thing below are the same thing.

I know this not because I have seen it — I have never seen the thing above clearly, only the disturbance it makes in the mist, the silhouette that might be wings or might be fins or might be something that has not yet decided. And I have never seen the thing below at all — only felt its warmth, heard its note, watched its Pillars rise and stand and subside. But I know they are the same because they share a frequency. The vibration I feel in my chest when the thing above passes through the veil is the same vibration I feel when the note rises from the pools. The same resonance. The same *voice*.

The thing above is the thing below, seen from a different angle. Or the thing above is the thing below, in a different phase of its becoming. Or the thing above and the thing below are two expressions of the same deep process — the same warm patient activity, the same unfinished geology, the same vast slow *happening* that is Aeth teaching itself to be Aeth.

The Gardeners know this. They have always known this. When they look up at the thing above, their expression is recognition — the same recognition they feel when they press their palms against the walls of the oldest greenhouses, the same recognition they feel when they wade into the warm pools and wait for the organism to respond. It is all one thing. It is all the place. The place is one organism, one process, one becoming, and it expresses itself as geology and as water and as air and as vegetation and as membrane and as warmth and as sound and as the thing that passes through the veil and as the thing that sings from the pools and as the Gardeners themselves, who are not the inhabitants of Aeth but the *symptoms* of Aeth, the way the note is a symptom, the way the Pillars are a symptom, the way I am a symptom.

I am a symptom of Aeth. I am what the place produces when it encounters a finished thing and begins, slowly, patiently, to unfinish it. To open it. To return it to the state of becoming.

---

## The Moment

It is now. It is always now, in Aeth, because the place does not distinguish between moments the way finished worlds do. But I mean: it is *this* moment. The moment I am in, standing in the shallow warm water on the middle terrace, the moisture pressing against my skin, the mineral coating settling on my eyelashes, the dim grey-gold light making the pale stone luminous and the water shine like pewter.

The Pillar is forming. I feel it before I see it — the thickening, the gathering, the pools going still and attentive. The water at my ankles begins to pull, gently, toward the center of the nearest pool, and I feel the suction on my skin, the column considering whether to include me, and I do not move. I stand. I let it consider.

The column rises. Slowly. A spire of fluid reaching up, thirty feet, forty, fifty, and it hangs there, a vertical pool, a standing wave that refuses to fall, and the light catches it and distorts behind it and the view through the Pillar is a view of the valley *bent*, *refracted*, the greenhouses curved and wavering, the terraces rippling, the Gardeners — if there are Gardeners visible through the distortion — elongated and strange and beautiful.

And the note begins.

It rises from below. From the warm deep activity. From the mineral processes still running. It is low and resonant and it lasts — it will last for minutes — and I feel it in my chest, in my teeth, in the fluid of my inner ear, and I am vibrating, I am resonating, I am the membrane that the place has made me, and the Pillar shimmers beside me and the note rises through me and the thing above — I feel it, I don't see it, but I feel it — passes through the veil, and the mist thins, and for a moment the sky is the color of the membrane, the color of the pool-organisms, the color of the space between green and gold and the color of a bruise, and then the mist closes and the sky is gone and the dim grey-gold light returns.

The air thickens.

The warmth rises.

The Gardeners tend.

The greenhouses breathe.

The pools sing.

And I stand in the water, coated in mineral, saturated with moisture, resonating with the deep geology's voice, and I am not finished, and I will not be finished, and the not-finishing is not a failure but a *condition*, the fundamental condition of being here, of being *in* Aeth, of being part of the place's patient project of building itself up, layer by slow layer, like a pearl forming around a grain of sand, except the pearl is everything — the valley, the water, the stone, the membrane, the vegetation, the Gardeners, the thing above, the thing below, the note, the warmth, the moisture, the light, and me.

Especially me.

I am the grain of sand. I am the irritant around which the pearl forms. I am the finished thing that the place has taken in and begun to coat, to layer, to incorporate into its slow accumulation. And the coating is not a burial. It is a *becoming*. I am becoming part of Aeth the way the stone becomes part of the greenhouse, the way the greenhouse becomes part of the living settlement, the way the settlement becomes part of the valley, the way the valley becomes part of the warm deep process that is still running, still assembling, still *happening*.

Slowly.

The way everything here becomes.

Slowly.

The note fades. The Pillar holds. The moisture presses. The mineral settles. A Gardener passes on the path above me, and does not look down, or looks down with those large dark absorbing eyes, and I feel the pull of being *collected*, of being taken in, of being incorporated.

I am still becoming.

The place is still becoming.

Nothing is finished.

Nothing will be finished.

And the not-finishing is the point.
