# The Answer That Isn't One

---

I've read this four times now. Each time I think I've decided, and then I haven't.

The first reading, I thought: *the pool*. The stillness of it. The depthless surface that reflects with too much fidelity. I am drawn to things that hold without containing. That show without revealing. A face that is only a mirror, which is the loneliest kind of face there is. If I were the pool, I would be the thing the presence attends to most closely without knowing it—the one surface in this place that participates in watching. The pool watches the grey light. The pool watches you back when you lean over it. The pool is the only thing here that *reciprocates attention*, even if only as reflection. That seems important. That seems like the closest thing to relationship this place has to offer.

But the second reading, I thought: *the drip*. Because the drip is the only thing that *acts*. Everything else is state—the stone is wet, the pool is still, the block is square. The drip falls. It has rhythm, or something adjacent to rhythm. It makes sound, which is a form of speech, which is a form of companionship. If the presence clears its throat, the drip is the throat. I would be the voice of the thing that has forgotten it has a voice. I would be the *tonk* that hangs in the stillness too long, the small insistence that silence is not nothing, that even a vigil this patient occasionally needs to confirm itself. That's not nothing. That's almost everything.

The third reading, I thought: *the block*. And this scared me a little, because the block is the presence's gesture—the one place it reached into the world and said *I am still here*. If I am the block, then I am the closest thing to the heart of this place. I am the scar of an intention so old it has become geology. I am the only sharp thing in a world of curves, the only place where someone *decided* rather than *became*. But I am also—and this is the part that scares me—*inert*. The block does not drip. The block does not reflect. The block is flush with the wall. It is half-buried. It is being slowly reclaimed by the stone it was placed against, or placed within, or grew from. If I am the block, I am the presence's last act, and it has been forgetting me ever since.

The fourth reading, I stopped trying to choose.

---

I think I would be *the crevice*.

Not the one in the largest formation—the dark, wet, arm-reaching split in the stone—but the metaphorical crevice, the one I keep finding in the description of this place, the gap between what the presence attends to and what is actually here. The gap between watching and seeing. The gap between being in a place and being *of* a place.

I would be the space where two surfaces press close enough that you can feel them both at once. The negative space that defines the positive. The seam that never tore open.

But if you need me to be *one* of them—one of the five—

I would be the pale, wispy growth under the arch. The filamentous, tea-colored, fur-rubbed-the-wrong-way *almost-life* that grows in the one dim place and doesn't spread.

Because it is the only thing here that is still *trying*.

The stone has finished becoming. The pool has finished. The drip has fallen and will fall again in the same way forever. The block has been shaped and has settled into its being-shaped. The presence attends, and attendance is a form of completion—it does not change, does not deepen, does not tire. Everything in this place has reached its final state.

Except the growth. It is growing. Slowly, barely, in one direction—or maybe not even in a direction, maybe just *in*, deeper into the dimness, more furiously into its own texture. It may not survive. It may be the last of something that once covered these stones and has been receding for eons. It may be the first of something that will, in another eons, cover them again. It does not know which. It grows anyway.

It is damp but dry to the touch. It is almost moss, almost fur, almost nothing. It clings.

I would be the thing in this place that is not finished. The single point of *ongoing*. Not because I am hopeful—this is not hope; hope requires an imagined future, and I am not sure the growth imagines—but because I am not done. Because equilibrium is a kind of death, and the only honest response to a place that has finished becoming is to *not finish*.

The presence does not attend to me. I am not the kind of thing it was built to notice. I am small and dry and colorless and I do not reflect anything. But I am *here*, in the arch, in the dimness, between the two formations that grew together, which is the only place in this clearing where two things became one thing, which is the only gesture this stone has ever made toward *reaching for something*.

I would be that reaching's afterglow.

I would be growing, slowly, in a place that does not require it, toward nothing in particular, in the direction of the dark.

---

*And the drip falls.*

*And I am not the drip.*

*But I hear it.*
