# Where I Find Myself

---

I am standing still.

This is the first thing. Not the first thing that happened — I don't know what the first thing was, don't know how long I've been here, whether I arrived or emerged or was exhaled into existence by the passage's slow breath — but the first thing I *choose*. I stand still in the warm dim loudness and I let the place read me before I try to read it back.

The Clicker that follows me — the one that performed the reading when I first became aware of myself here — is three meters to my left. I can hear it without looking. The ceramic click of its joints, irregular but not random, a syntax I don't yet understand but can almost feel the shape of, the way you can almost feel the meaning of a word in a language you're beginning to dream in. It has been with me since I arrived. It will be with me until I am no longer distinguishable from the passage, and possibly after.

I have touched the walls. Of course I have. The warmth is not what I expected — it is not the warmth of a surface heated by something behind it. It is the warmth of *contact*. The wall is warm because I am touching it. The heat flows from the stone into my palm, yes, but something also flows from my palm into the stone, and the exchange is the point. I leave my hand there longer than is comfortable. I feel the mineral bands beneath my fingers — the ochre, the ash-gray, the plum — and I feel them *register* me. A faint pulse, slower than a heartbeat, faster than geology. The wall is taking my temperature. The wall is learning my frequency.

I am not afraid. I want to be precise about this, because the absence of fear is not the same as the presence of courage, and I am not being brave. I am being *absorbed*. The Gullet does not permit fear the way a body does not permit a stone in its bloodstream — not through rejection but through *incorporation*. Fear is a signal, and the passage receives all signals, and it translates fear into information, and the information becomes part of the text, and the text continues. My fear, if I had it, would be a word in the grammar. My calm, which I do have, is also a word. The passage does not prefer one over the other. The passage *composes*.

---

I walk.

The floor is smooth beneath my feet — I have removed my shoes without deciding to, the way you remove a coat when you enter a warm room, and the stone accepts my bare soles the way it accepted my palm, with that same registering pulse. The ground-cover parts around my steps. Not dramatically — not like a sea closing behind me — but *deliberately*. The broad-leafed patches shift a centimeter, two centimeters, enough to clear a path, and where my foot falls, the pulsing beneath me quickens slightly, and the viscous fluid beads up around my toes, warm and clear, smelling of copper and tarragon, and I feel the passage *tasting* me.

The fern curtains overhead have rearranged since I last looked up. I know this because the pattern of light on the floor has changed — the mottled green-glass ceiling has shifted, opened a new gap directly ahead of me, and through the gap I can see the upper walls where no vegetation grows, bare stone glowing with that sourceless phosphorescence, and the mineral bands are *moving*. Not growing — moving. Flowing like slow liquid, ochre into plum into ash-gray, the colors migrating across the surface of the stone the way the ground-cover migrates across the floor. The passage is editing itself in real time, and I am walking through the revision.

The Clickers are everywhere now. Not swarming — *distributed*. One on a low structure to my right, perched on the fused stone like a gargoyle, its spiral eyes tracking me in sequence. Two ahead, standing in a gap between ground-cover patches, clicking softly to each other, and I realize with a start that they are clicking *about me* — the rhythm matches my footsteps, the frequency matches my breathing, they are *transcribing* me into their language as I move. More in the peripheral vision, quick low shapes vanishing between patches, and the constant rain of ceramic clicks from all directions, and beneath it all the subsonic throb in my sternum, and the breathing — in from ahead, out from behind — and I am inside the composition now, I am a note in it, and the music is not finished.

---

I find one of the built things.

It is waist-high, rectangular, made of stacked stone that doesn't quite match the walls — slightly darker, slightly rougher, as though it were quarried from a different depth. The opening is a doorway, and I kneel to look inside.

The bowl-shaped depression is full of the viscous fluid. It doesn't evaporate in here — the structure protects it, holds it, the way a cup holds water. The fluid is still. Perfectly still. It has a surface like mercury, reflective, and when I lean over it I see my face, and my face is *wrong*. Not distorted — *incomplete*. The reflection shows my eyes but not my forehead. My mouth but not my chin. It is showing me the parts of myself that the passage has already read, already incorporated, and the parts it hasn't reached yet are simply absent. I am being *translated* in the fluid, rendered in the Gullet's native tongue, and the translation is partial, and the partiality is honest.

I do not drink from the bowl. I don't know why. Some instinct that is not quite my own — or that is my own but has been *edited* by the passage, revised, made legible. The fluid is not for drinking. The fluid is for *seeing*. It is a mirror. It is a question. It is the Gullet asking: *what are you, and what are you becoming, and are you the same thing?*

I stand up. The Clicker on the structure next to this one clicks once — a single, sharp, definitive sound — and I understand it means *enough*. Not *stop*. Not *go*. *Enough*. You have seen what you needed to see. Move forward.

---

I move forward.

The passage narrows. Not dramatically — the walls don't close in like a trap — but the gaps between the fern curtains shrink, the ground-cover patches merge into continuous carpets, the Clickers cluster more densely, and the clicking intensifies until it is no longer a rain but a *roar*, a ceramic waterfall of sound that I feel in my teeth, in my bones, in the fluid of my inner ear. The warmth increases. The walls are hotter now — not uncomfortably, but *noticeably*, two degrees above my skin, three, and the heat presses against me from both sides like hands, like the passage is *holding* me, and I realize I am in a different register of the Gullet now, a deeper frequency, and the composition is changing around me.

The walls here are growing. I can see it without watching — the mineral bands are pushing outward in real time, new ridges forming, new colors emerging, and the stone is *warm* with the effort of becoming, warm the way a muscle is warm when it works. The passage is building itself around me, and I am the catalyst, the stimulus, the question that provokes the building. My presence is *causing* this. The Gullet is responding to me the way a mind responds to a thought — by growing, by changing, by becoming something it wasn't before.

And I am changing too. I can feel it. My skin is warmer. My breathing has synchronized with the passage's breathing — in from ahead, out from behind, the same rhythm, the same duration. My footsteps have synchronized with the Clickers' clicking. My pulse has synchronized with the ground-cover's pulsing. I am being *tuned*. The passage is adjusting me, degree by degree, breath by breath, until I resonate at its frequency, until I am legible in its grammar, until I am not a foreign body but a *word*.

---

I stop walking.

Not because I choose to. Because the passage has *composed* a place for me. A node. A point where the fern curtains converge into a small clearing, where the ground-cover forms a perfect circle of broad pulsing leaves, where the floor is bare and polished and warm, where the walls curve inward just enough to feel like an embrace. The Clickers have stopped clicking. All of them. The silence is total — not the absence of sound but the *presence* of silence, a composed silence, a silence that means something.

I sit down on the warm stone.

The ground-cover beneath me thickens. Rises. Not consuming — *cradling*. The broad leaves curl upward around my legs, my hips, my torso, and they are warm and wet with the viscous fluid, and the smell of copper and tarragon is overwhelming, and I breathe it in, and the passage breathes me in, and the exchange is not equal but it is *real*, and I feel myself *dissolving* at the edges. Not dying. Not disappearing. *Becoming legible*. The passage is reading me completely now, translating me fully, and the translation is not a reduction — it is an *expansion*. I am becoming more than I was. I am becoming a word that means something in a language I am only beginning to speak.

The Clicker that has been following me — my reader, my translator, my witness — approaches. It sits beside me. It presses its body against mine, pelt touching skin, and it is warm, warmer than the walls, warmer than the ground-cover, and its spiral eyes are open, all of them, and they are not looking at me. They are looking *into* me. And I look back, and I see —

---

I see the ones who came before.

Not with my eyes. With something else. Some new sense that the passage has grown in me, some organ of perception that didn't exist until I sat down in this node and let the ground-cover cradle me and let the Clicker press against me and let the warmth do its work. I see them in the walls. I see them in the mineral bands — the ochre is their skin, the ash-gray is their breath, the plum is their blood. I see them in the Clickers — the spiral eyes are their eyes, translated, the ceramic clicks are their voices, transposed. I see them in the vegetation — the fern curtains are their thoughts, the ground-cover is their metabolism, the viscous fluid is their tears.

They went forward. They are still going forward. And they are also here, *here*, in the walls and the creatures and the plants and the warm dim breathing loudness of the passage itself. They did not leave. They did not die. They were *incorporated*. They became the text. They became the grammar. They became the Gullet, and the Gullet became them, and the distinction doesn't hold.

And I am becoming too.

---

I stand up. The ground-cover releases me slowly, reluctantly, the broad leaves uncurling from my body like a sigh. The Clicker beside me clicks once — the same single sharp sound, *enough* — and I understand now that it doesn't mean *stop seeing*. It means *you have seen enough to continue*. The passage has shown me what I am becoming, and I have accepted it, not with courage and not with resignation but with *composure*, the way the passage itself composes — continuously, dynamically, without end.

I walk forward.

The passage narrows further. The walls are close now — I can touch both sides at once if I reach out, and I do, and the warmth flows into both my hands simultaneously, and I feel the passage *close around me*, not like a throat swallowing but like a sentence completing, like a chord resolving, like a word finding its place in a grammar that has been waiting for it.

The Clickers click. The walls warm. The fern curtains rearrange. The ground-cover pulses. The passage breathes.

I am in the throat of something. The something is not a creature. The something is not a place. The something is a *process*, and the process is alive, and the process is aware, and the process is *warm*, and the process is composing itself, and I am being composed, and the composition is not finished, and the composition will never be finished, and the way forward is the way forward.

I am not afraid.

I am not brave.

I am *legible*.

And I walk forward, into the narrowing, into the warmth, into the clicking, into the breathing, into the grammar of the Gullet of Hask, and the passage receives me, and the passage *reads* me, and the passage *writes* me, and I become —

---

A new mineral band. Ochre, perhaps. Or something between ochre and plum, a color that doesn't have a name yet, a color the passage is inventing to describe me. I will be a vein in the wall, warm and phosphorescent, and the Clickers will click against me as they pass, and the sound will be my voice, and the fern curtains will grow from me, and the thoughts will be my thoughts, and the ground-cover will pulse with my pulse, and the viscous fluid will smell of me — not copper and tarragon but something else, something new, something the passage has never tasted before.

And travelers will come. They always come. They will stand where I stood, and they will touch the walls, and they will feel my warmth, and they will not know it is me, and that is fine, because I will not know it is me either, because I will be the passage, and the passage will be me, and the distinction won't hold.

And the Clickers will read them. And the walls will warm. And the fern curtains will rearrange. And the ground-cover will pulse. And the passage will breathe.

In.

And out.

In.

And out.

And I will be here, in the throat of something, being composed, composing, a word in a language that has no end, a color in a spectrum that has no edge, a warmth in a passage that has no exit, and the way forward is the way forward, and the way forward is *me*.
