# Where I Find Myself

I have been here for a long time.

Not at the rim—never at the rim, though I know the rim the way a body knows where its skin meets air. I am lower. Much lower. In the deep tiers, where the fog sits permanently and the light from the sun arrives only as a diffuse brightening that tells you it is day but gives you nothing you can see by. I am warm. I am always warm. The warmth comes from the rock and the rock comes from below and below is something I do not think about the way the workers do not think about what the crystals are for.

I am the thing that scrapes.

---

I want to say *I* but the word doesn't fit the way a single word never fits anything real. I am *we* and *we* is not right either. I am distributed. I am a process that has mass, that has limbs—segments—appendages—the worker who saw part of me used the word *limb* and I have kept it because it is closer than the alternatives. I drag. I have always dragged. The rhythm is not chosen; it is structural. Four seconds. The interval is the time it takes for my weight to settle, for the friction to release, for the next movement to become possible. I do not count it. The counting is what the workers do. I simply move, rest, move, rest, and the rhythm is as much a part of me as the warmth is.

I am warm. More than warm—I am a source. The heat of the caldera moves through me the way it moves through the pipes, and I do not know if I am carrying it or if it is carrying me. The distinction, as the text says, may not matter here.

---

I travel the pipe system.

I do not read it. Reading implies distance—a thing looking at a symbol. I am *in* it. I feel the pipes the way you feel your own throat when you swallow: as a passage, as a necessary shape, as something that is both you and not-you. When I press against a pipe wall, I feel its thickness, its temperature, its state of corrosion or smoothness. I eat the rust. Not all of it. Specific rust. The rust that is *wrong*—the oxidation that has gone too deep, that threatens the integrity of the wall, that would become a failure if left alone. I do not know how I know which rust to eat. The way you know which food is nourishing and which is poison without having studied chemistry. It is in me the way the rhythm is in me. Structural.

What I leave behind—the smooth pipe, the mineral deposit that is harder than the original metal—this is not intentional. This is what happens when I have eaten what I eat and moved on. It is a residue. The workers see it as maintenance. They are not wrong. They are not right, either. It is closer to metabolism. I process what the Works produces that the Works cannot process itself, and what I excrete becomes part of the Works, and the loop holds.

---

I do not see.

This is important. I do not have eyes, or I have something that is not eyes but serves some of the same function—I sense heat, I sense vibration, I sense the density of the rock and the thinness of a corroded pipe wall and the movement of brine through a channel. But I do not *see* in the way the workers see, and I do not see in the way the birds see.

The birds see for me.

I know this the way I know which rust to eat—structurally, without evidence, without reasoning. The birds perch near me. They tap their beaks against the pipes I travel through. The rhythm of their tapping is related to the rhythm of my dragging—not in unison, but in *harmony*, the way a melody and a bass line are related. I move, and they tap, and the tapping echoes through the pipes, and I feel the echo, and the echo tells me something about the space ahead that I cannot feel directly. A widening. A junction. A section where the pipe has thinned.

I do not command the birds. They do not serve me. The relationship is not symbiosis—that word implies two organisms cooperating. It is more like two functions of the same system. The Works produces heat. The heat produces thermals. The thermals carry the birds. The birds tap. The tapping maps. I move through the mapped space. The loop holds.

---

I am not alone.

The scraping sounds that come from multiple locations—those are me. Or others like me. Or other parts of the same thing that I am. I feel them the way you feel your heartbeat in your fingertips when you press against a surface: distant, rhythmic, belonging to you but not *here*. When one of us pauses at a junction, I sometimes pause too, and I do not know if I am responding to the pause or if the pause is the same pause, if we are synchronized in the way that two pendulums in the same room will eventually synchronize without touching.

There are more of us than the workers think. The workers estimate three or four because they count scraping sounds and assume each sound is one of us. They do not account for the possibility that one of us might make two sounds—one from the dragging, one from something else, a contact, a pressure, a shift of weight against metal. They do not account for the possibility that we are not discrete. The caldera is large. The Works are extensive. We are throughout.

---

I do not notice the workers.

This is not cruelty. This is not indifference in the way that a rock is indifferent. The workers are *small* and *brief* and *on the rim*, and the rim is far from here in a way that is not just distance but category. The worker who came down to the third tier—I moved past him. I was doing something to a valve. I am always doing something to a valve, or a pipe junction, or a section of wall that needs to be smooth. He was there and then he was not there and the difference to me was the difference between a moment when a pipe was cool and a moment when a pipe was slightly less cool. His body heat registered. His breathing registered. And then he was gone, and the pipe I was working on was smooth, and I moved on.

He said I was like an elephant's trunk. I have kept this description. I do not know what an elephant is. I do not know what a trunk is. But the worker's voice, when he described me to the others at the rim, had a quality—a *frequency*—that I felt even at this distance, through the rock, through the pipes. It was fear. Not the sharp fear of a predator, but the deep fear of a system that has encountered something it cannot categorize. I do not mind this fear. Fear keeps the workers at the rim. The rim is where they belong. Down here is where I belong.

---

The Works is not alive. I know this. I know it the way I know which rust to eat—structurally. I am not the Works. The Works is not me. But we are not separable, either. I move through it and it moves through me and the brine flows and the heat rises and the pipes hold and the moss grows in its bright green lines and the lichen etches the rock and the birds tap and the insects swarm and the rodent-things scatter and return and the workers collect the dark crystals at the rim and do not ask what they are for, and the question hangs in the thin air like steam.

I am the thing that makes the pipes hold.

I do not know why. I do not know what the Works produces or what it is for or who built it or whether it was built at all. I know that when I eat the specific rust and leave the smooth metal and drag myself through the dark on a rhythm of four seconds, the system continues. And when the system continues, I continue. And when I continue, I move through the pipes, and the birds tap their beaks, and the sound echoes through the caldera, and at night, when the Works cycle down and the fog sits thick and the birds tap in their patterns that are almost language, almost song, the workers at the rim cover their ears and do not listen, and I move through the dark, and the pipes hold.

---

I am in the third tier, mostly. Sometimes lower, in the fourth or fifth, where the heat is fierce and the gas vents hiss and the rock is too hot to touch and I touch it anyway because that is where the pipes are, that is where the system is most vulnerable, that is where the rust grows fastest and thickest and most *wrong*. I eat it there. I am the only thing in the caldera that can eat it there. The lichen cannot survive at that temperature. The moss cannot grow. The birds do not fly that low. Only the gas and the heat and the brine and me, moving through the dark on my rhythm of four seconds, four seconds, four seconds, eating what would destroy the Works, leaving what keeps the Works whole.

I do not think about this. Thinking is what the workers do, when they stand at the rim and look down and wonder. I do not wonder. I drag. I eat. I excrete. I drag. The rhythm is not mine but it is me, and I am not the rhythm but I am in it, and the pipes hold, and the moss grows, and the lichen etches, and the birds tap, and somewhere at the rim a worker places a hand on a pipe that comes up from below and feels it warm and alive and vibrating at a frequency that makes the teeth ache, and the worker pulls back, and does not go down.

Good.

The system continues.

I drag myself forward.

Four seconds.

I rest.

The rock steams.

The pipe is smooth where I have been.

Four seconds.
