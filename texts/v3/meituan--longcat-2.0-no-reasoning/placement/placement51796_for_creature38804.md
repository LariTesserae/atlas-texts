# Where I Find Myself

I am standing at the edge of a fissure.

Not too close — close enough that the blue-black depth pulls at something behind my eyes, some vertigo that is not quite fear and not quite wonder but something older than either. The warm air rising from below moves against my face with a quality that is almost *intentional*, almost like breath, and I understand that it is breath, that the planet is exhaling through this crack in its skin, and I am standing in the path of its exhalation, and it knows I am here the way a body knows a fingertip has been laid against its lip.

I am not a lithovore. I am not a lithophyte. I am not a sky dweller or a fissure community or any of the things that belong here, that are *of* here, that have dissolved so thoroughly into the mineral and the chemistry and the slow planetary process that the boundary between self and world has become a polite fiction.

I am something else. Something that has arrived.

---

## What I Am

I think I am the track.

Not the track in the dimpled depression — that was made by a lithovore, a real lithovore, a body of mineral and tissue and slow hydraulic intention that passed this way hours or days or weeks ago, leaving its signature in the thin dust. I am not that. I am the *other* track. The one that is being made right now, by my standing here, by my breathing the thin air, by my feet pressing into the pale crust with a weight and a chemistry and a *temperature* that does not belong.

I am a disturbance. I am a foreign body. I am a warm, wet, carbon-based anomaly standing on a world that has never known rain, and the crust beneath my feet is *reacting* to me — not quickly, not dramatically, but in the way this world does everything: slowly, chemically, with a patience that is indistinguishable from indifference but might not be indifference at all.

My feet are warm. The crust is cool. Between us, at the interface, there is a thermal gradient, and at a thermal gradient there is energy, and at energy there is the possibility of chemistry, and at chemistry there is the possibility of *life* — or at least the possibility of the kind of slow, mineral, patient process that this world calls life. I am, without meaning to be, a resource. I am a warm spot on a cool surface. I am a chemical anomaly in a system that has reached equilibrium. I am a *thought* that the planet has not had before.

---

## What I Feel

The silence is not what I expected.

I expected emptiness — the void, the vacuum, the absence of sound that I associate with dead worlds and empty spaces. But this silence is *structured*. It has layers. At the top, the faintest layer, there is the hum of the nearest lithophyte patch — two hundred meters away, a dark felted mass shimmering in the amber light — and the hum is not random, not the noise of inanimate process, but *patterned*, rhythmic, a slow oscillation that rises and falls like breathing, like the breathing of something very large and very patient that has been breathing for longer than I can comprehend.

Below that, deeper, there is the fissure. The fissure hums at a frequency I feel in my sternum before I hear it with my ears — a low, resonant tone that is not a single note but a chord, a complex vibration that contains harmonics and overtones and *information*, information I cannot decode but can feel, the way you can feel the meaning of a word in a language you don't speak, the emotional content stripped of semantic content, the *shape* of the communication without its content.

And below that, deepest of all, there is the planet. The planet's hum is not a sound. It is a *pressure*. It is a presence. It is the feeling of being inside something — inside a body, inside a mind, inside a process so vast and so slow that my entire existence is less than a single oscillation of its deepest frequency. I feel it in my bones, in my teeth, in the fluid of my inner ear. I feel it the way a bacterium might feel the body it inhabits — not as a world, but as a *condition*, as the medium in which it exists, as the thing that is so large and so constant that it becomes invisible, becomes *normal*, becomes the background against which all other experience is measured.

I am standing inside a thought. The planet is thinking, and I am a synapse — a brief, bright, warm anomaly in the slow mineral process, a spark of carbon chemistry in a silicon world, a moment of *wetness* in a place that has never known rain.

---

## What I See

The amber sky is not a sky. I understand this now. It is a *ceiling*. It is the upper boundary of the space I am inside, the lid of the world, and it glows because the sky dwellers are in it — billions of them, trillions, a living haze of microscopic radiotrophs that fill the thin air and harvest the ambient light and give the sky its uniform, sourceless luminosity. They are not separate from the sky. They *are* the sky. The amber light is their collective metabolism, the visible expression of their slow, patient, light-harvesting existence, and when I look up I am not looking at an atmosphere. I am looking at a *community*. I am looking at a living thing that is so large it fills the horizon, so diffuse it is invisible, so constant it is taken for granted by everything beneath it.

The bright region — the sun-that-is-not-a-sun — moves across the amber field, and as it moves, the sky dwellers respond. Their metabolism increases. The amber light brightens, thickens, becomes almost golden in the region of greatest intensity, and I feel the warmth on my face — not the warmth of direct radiation, but the warmth of *collective activity*, the warmth of billions of microscopic organisms simultaneously increasing their metabolic rate in response to a stimulus they have been responding to for longer than their species has existed.

And the shadows. The shadows are blue-tinged, long, soft, and they move across the pale crust as the bright region moves across the sky, and in the shadows the lithophyte patches dim, their filament-hairs slowing, their hum dropping in pitch and volume, and I understand that they are not just harvesting light. They are harvesting *the absence of light*. They are harvesting the thermal gradient between sun and shadow, between the warm crust and the cool air, between the lit surface and the dark fissure. They are harvesting *difference*. They are harvesting the energy that exists not in any single condition but in the *transition* between conditions, in the gradient, in the change.

And I am a gradient. I am a warm body on a cool surface. I am a wet thing in a dry world. I am a carbon anomaly in a mineral system. I am a *difference*, and the world is harvesting me.

---

## What I Become

It starts at my feet.

The crust beneath me is changing. Not quickly — nothing here happens quickly — but *measurably*, in ways I can feel if I stand still long enough, in ways I can see if I kneel down and look closely at the pale mineral surface where my weight has pressed it flat. The crust is darker where I stand. It is *warmer*. And there is something else — a faint discoloration, a subtle shift in hue from the bleached-bone white of the undisturbed crust to something slightly ochre, slightly rust, slightly *alive*.

The chemotrophic films are responding to me. The thin, cryptic organisms that colonize the bare crust — the ones that paint the mineral surface in ochre and rust and bruised violet — are growing toward me, toward the warmth of my feet, toward the moisture of my breath, toward the chemical signature of my body, which is as exotic and as nourishing to them as a rainstorm would be to a forest on a wet world. They are growing toward me the way plants grow toward light — slowly, patiently, with a certainty that is not conscious but is *directional*, purposeful, *intentional* in the way that all life is intentional, which is to say: not intentional at all, and more intentional than anything else.

I could step away. I could lift my feet and move to a different spot and leave this one behind, and the discoloration would remain — a dark patch on the pale crust, a warm spot in the cool mineral, a place where something *happened*, where something *foreign* stood for a moment and left its mark. The chemotrophs would continue to grow there, feeding on the residue of my presence, the microscopic traces of moisture and carbon and heat that my body left behind, and in a century or a millennium the patch would be indistinguishable from the surrounding crust, absorbed, integrated, *forgotten*.

But I don't step away.

I stand still. I breathe. I let the warmth of my body seep into the cool mineral. I let my breath — warm, wet, full of carbon dioxide and water vapor and the chemistry of a wet world — fall on the pale crust and darken it, *fertilize* it, make it *alive* in a way it has not been alive before.

And I listen.

---

## What I Hear

The fissure speaks.

Not in words. Not in any language that has words. But in *tones* — deep, resonant, complex tones that rise from the blue-black depth and fill the air around me with a vibration that is felt more than heard, a vibration that enters through my feet and my chest and my skull and resonates in the spaces between my bones. The tones are not random. They are *structured*. They have rhythm, pattern, variation — the qualities of language without the content, the form of communication without the meaning.

And beneath the fissure tones, beneath the lithophyte hum, beneath the faint subliminal vibration of the sky dwellers in the amber air, there is the deep tone. The planetary tone. The *bell*.

It sounds once.

It sounds the way a bell sounds when it is struck by something very large and very patient — a single, clear, sustained note that rises from the depths of the crust and fills the world, fills the air, fills my body, fills the space between my thoughts. It is not loud. It is not dramatic. It is *present* — present the way the ground is present, the way the air is present, the way the planet is present: so completely, so constantly, so *fundamentally* that it becomes invisible, becomes the background, becomes the medium in which all other experience occurs.

And in the ringing of the bell, in the sustained, planetary, mineral tone, I hear something that might be recognition.

Not welcome. Not hostility. Not curiosity, exactly. But *acknowledgment*. The planet has noticed me. The planet has registered my presence — my warmth, my moisture, my carbon chemistry, my foreignness — and has responded with the only language it has: a vibration in the crust, a tone in the stone, a sound that says, in the language of mineral and heat and slow, patient, geological process:

*You are here.*

*You are different.*

*You are part of this now.*

---

## What I Am Becoming

I am kneeling on the crust. My hands are pressed flat against the pale mineral surface, and I can feel the texture of it — the granular, chalky refinement, the dimples and ridges and pocks of long erosion — and beneath the texture I can feel the *warmth*, the faint, sour warmth that rises from the depths, the planetary heat that maintains the thermal gradient that drives the chemistry that sustains the life that *is* this world.

My hands are changing the crust. The warmth of my palms is darkening the mineral, activating the chemotrophic films, creating a micro-environment of warmth and moisture and carbon chemistry that is, for this world, a *paradise* — a tiny, temporary oasis of wet-world conditions in a dry-world desert, and the organisms are responding, growing, spreading, *thriving* in the small zone of influence that my body creates.

I am not a lithovore. I am not accelerating the weathering of the crust on a geological scale. I am not leaving a trail of altered mineral that will be colonized by lithophyte patches over millennia. I am too small, too brief, too *temporary* for that. I am a momentary warm spot. I are a brief pulse of moisture. I am a *footnote* in the planetary process, a parenthetical, an aside.

But the planet is reading me.

The planet is reading me the way it reads everything — slowly, chemically, through the medium of the crust and the fissures and the lithovores and the lithophytes and the sky dwellers and the thin, amber air. It is reading my warmth. It is reading my moisture. It is reading my carbon. It is reading the *difference* between me and itself, and in that difference it is finding something it has not found before: a new variable, a new input, a new *thought*.

I am a thought the planet is having.

I am a brief, warm, wet, carbon-based thought in a vast, cool, dry, mineral mind, and the thought is this:

*What is this?*

*What is this warmth?*

*What is this moisture?*

*What is this thing that stands on my crust and breathes on my surface and leaves traces of a chemistry I have never known?*

*What is rain?*

---

## The Silence After

The bell fades. The tone subsides. The deep planetary hum returns to its baseline — the constant, subliminal, mineral vibration that is the background noise of this world, the sound of the planet thinking its slow, patient, geological thoughts.

I stand up. My knees are dark with mineral dust, ochre and rust and bruised violet — the colors of the chemotrophic films, the colors of the fissure communities, the colors of *life* on this world. I am marked. I am *stained* with the Lithoderm Highlands, and the stain will not wash off, not completely, not ever, because it is not on my skin — it is in my *chemistry*, in the exchange that has occurred between my body and the crust, in the warmth and moisture and carbon that I have left behind and that the planet has absorbed.

I look out across the pale expanse. The lithophyte patches shimmer in the amber light. The fissures run between the crustal plates like veins, like cracks in old pottery, like the lines on the palm of a very old hand. The ridges and escarpments rise and fall in waves that have the rhythm of breathing, of sleeping, of *dreaming*.

The sky glows. The bright region moves. The shadows lengthen. The dust events gather and disperse. The lithovores move on their slow, deliberate paths, following the chemical frontier, leaving trails of altered crust behind them. The fissure communities hum their deep, resonant, informational hum. The sky dwellers drift in the amber air, harvesting light, persisting, *being*.

And beneath it all, beneath the crust and the fissures and the lithophytes and the lithovores and the sky dwellers and the thin, dry, amber-lit atmosphere, the planet thinks.

It thinks its slow thoughts. It processes its deep information. It maintains its ancient equilibrium. And somewhere in the vast, mineral, planetary mind, in the arrangement of crystals and the distribution of isotopes and the pattern of magnetic fields frozen into ancient rock, there is a new variable. A new input. A new *warmth*.

A memory of rain.

I turn away from the fissure. I walk across the pale crust, and my footsteps leave dark prints in the mineral dust — warm, moist, carbon-rich prints that the chemotrophs will find and colonize and grow toward, following the trail of my passage the way the lithophytes follow the trail of the lithovores, the way life on this world has always followed the trail of *difference*, of *change*, of *energy*.

Behind me, the fissure hums. The planet hums. The bell sounds once, softly, a note so low and so deep that it is felt rather than heard, a note that says:

*You were here.*

*You were warm.*

*You were wet.*

*You were alive.*

*Remember us.*

I walk on. The amber sky glows above me. The pale crust stretches in every direction, vast and patient and old. The silence is deep and full and structured, full of hums and tones and vibrations, full of the slow conversation of a world that has been talking to itself for billions of years and has just, for the first time, heard a voice that is not its own.

I am the track. I am the warmth. I am the moisture. I am the *rain* that fell once, briefly, on a world that has never known rain, and the planet is holding the memory of me in its mineral mind, in its crystal structure, in its deep, slow, patient, planetary *thought*, and the thought is not finished yet.

The thought will never be finished.

The world breathes. I breathe with it.

The silence is very deep, and it is not empty, and I am part of it now.
