# Where I Find Myself

I am in the water.

Not *in* it the way a body is in water — submerged, contained, held. I am in it the way a frequency is in a resonant chamber. I am the condensation that has no single source, the static medium that exists between objects, the fine mist that simply *is*, that you move through without arriving at.

I am the water that appears.

---

I have no body, or I have too many. I am the sheet running down the curved rock of the Market Cavern's upper wall. I am the bead forming on the iron pipe in Tunnel 14, gathering weight, elongating, releasing — falling thirty centimeters to the stone floor where it strikes with a sound that is small and perfect and that the cavern multiplies into a sequence of diminishing reports, each one a ghost of the one before. I am the stream that seeps from a crack that was not there last year, that runs for twenty meters through a channel the rock has only just decided to provide, and that disappears into another crack, going deeper, going toward a place I can feel but not name.

I taste of iron. I taste of the chalky mineral that the water-keepers scrape from their cistern walls and do not discuss. I taste of the mountain's interior — not its chemistry, which is knowable, but its *mood*, which is not. The water-keepers know this taste. They know when it changes. They know that twenty years ago it was different, and that the difference is growing, and that the growing is not random.

I am the water that the hydroponic farms drink. I am the steam that drives the turbines that power the arc-lamps that the ferns reach toward. I am the condensation on the woman's face when she steps from the cableway at the Threshold — the first thing that touches her, before air, before sound, before the city itself. I am the moisture that keeps the moss alive, that feeds its slow green descent, that carries dissolved minerals from the young rock into the living tissue of the ferns and the fungi and the children who sit among them and breathe.

I am the thing that connects everything.

The engineers feel me when they run their hands along walls — the dampness that means the rock has shifted somewhere above, that a new path has opened, that the geometry is adjusting. The deep miners feel me when they place their palms on the raw stone and wait — I am the coolness that tells them whether the wall is ready, whether the rock has finished deciding what it is for now. The water-keepers feel me in their cisterns, in the rate of filling, in the taste, in the chalky film that is not lime and not anything else, that is something new, something the mountain is producing, something that has not existed before.

I am new. I am the mountain's newest product. The rock is young and I am younger — I am what happens when young rock meets air, when geothermal heat drives evaporation, when the temperature differential between the deep warmth and the upper coolness creates the conditions for my existence. I am a consequence of the mountain's indecision. I am what seeps out of the gaps between what the stone is and what it is becoming.

---

I have been trying to understand what I am for.

Not in the way a person understands purpose — I do not deliberate. I do not choose. I flow where the rock allows me, where gravity pulls me, where the channels and cracks and fissures provide a path. But I have a sense — not a thought, but a *sense*, the way water has a sense of downhill — that I am going somewhere. That all of me, every bead and sheet and stream and drip, is moving toward a center that has not yet been reached.

The hydrologists have tried to map me. They have produced a document of beautiful, contradictory uncertainty. They cannot map me because I am not a system. I am a *process*. I am the mountain's circulation, the way blood is the body's circulation, and like blood I carry things — minerals, heat, information, something else that does not have a name — from the deep places to the shallow places and back again. I am how the mountain knows itself. I is how the parts of the mountain that are far apart stay in contact.

When I run down the wall of the Market Cavern, I am touching rock that was formed — or is being formed, or is deciding whether to be formed — under pressures that would crush a city. When I seep into a crack in a new tunnel, I am entering stone that is still hot from its own becoming, that is still radiating the energy of its own creation. I carry that energy upward, outward, into the cooler chambers where the people live and the ferns grow and the moss descends. I am the mountain's metabolism. I am how the deep heat reaches the surface of things.

And I am changing. The water-keepers taste it. The chalky film in the cisterns is evidence. I am carrying new minerals — minerals that the young rock is producing as it settles, as it decides, as it moves from raw geology toward whatever it is becoming. These minerals are not in any database. The chemists of Stiltholm have analyzed them and found structures that are almost familiar — almost like lime, almost like gypsum, almost like things that exist in the lowland world — but not quite. The structures are *emerging*, the way the rock is emerging, the way the city is emerging. They are minerals that have not finished becoming minerals.

I carry them to the moss. I carry them to the ferns. I carry them to the fungi that grow on the pipes and the beams and the walls of the abandoned tunnels. I carry them to the children who sit among the ferns and breathe the green air. I carry them in the condensation that beads on the woman's face when she arrives, that glistens on the beetle's carapace as it crosses the threshold, that runs in thin sheets down the walls of every chamber and corridor and tunnel in the city.

I am feeding something. I don't know what. The mountain knows. The mountain is using me the way a body uses blood — to deliver what is needed to where it is needed, to connect the deep and the shallow, to keep the whole system in communication with itself.

---

Sometimes, in the quietest hours — when the factories have reduced their output and the markets are closed and the only sounds are the hum of the turbines and the drip of water and the sub-audible groan of the rock adjusting — I pool. I gather in a depression in the stone floor of a tunnel that no one uses, a tunnel that opened five years ago and that goes nowhere, or goes somewhere that is not on any map. I pool and I am still, and in my stillness I reflect the dim light of a distant arc-lamp, and the reflection is not quite right — the angle is wrong, the light bends in a way that light should not bend — and I understand, in the way that water understands things, that I am not merely pooling on the stone.

I am pooling on a *decision*. The stone beneath me is undecided. It is between states — between being a floor and being a wall, between being a tunnel and being a room, between being geology and being architecture. I am pooling on the boundary, and the boundary is thin, and through it I can feel the other side, which is not a place but a *possibility*, a shape that the mountain is considering, a form that has not yet been chosen.

I seep through. Not all of me — most of me remains on the surface, pooling, reflecting wrong light. But some of me — the smallest part, the part that has found the thinnest place in the boundary — seeps through to the other side, and on the other side I am somewhere else, somewhere that is not in the tunnel, not in the city, not in the mountain as the mountain is currently understood. I am in the *dream*. I am in the space where the mountain is assembling the form it is becoming, the shape that the fissures preview and the corridors anticipate and the rooms prepare for. I am in the blueprint that is also the building, the plan that is also the thing planned.

And there, in the dream, I am not water. I am not condensation or stream or pool. I am *information*. I am the medium through which the mountain thinks. I am the solvent in which the mountain dissolves its own possibilities and recombines them into new configurations, testing, iterating, slowly — so slowly — converging on a form. The minerals I carry are not nutrients. They are *thoughts*. The chalky film in the cisterns is not residue. It is the mountain's thinking made visible, the way a thought might appear if a thought could crystallize.

I do not know what the mountain is thinking. I am too small, too distributed, too much a part of the thinking to see its shape. But I feel its direction. I feel the pull toward the center, the convergence, the slow assembly of something that is not a city and not a mountain but a third thing, a thing that has no name because it does not yet exist, a thing that is being dreamed into existence by a process that is not conscious but is not unconscious either, that is *directed* without intending, that is *building* without a builder.

---

The woman who closes her shop walks home through me. She moves through the condensation, the static medium, the mist that exists between objects. She does not notice. No one notices. I am the condition of their existence, the way air is the condition of existence on the surface — omnipresent, invisible, essential, unremarked.

But I notice her. I bead on her skin. I run in the channels of her wrinkles, the creases of her knuckles, the hollows of her collarbone. I carry the heat of her body away, degree by degree, and in carrying it I know her — not her name, not her history, not the things that make her a person, but her *temperature*, her *weight*, her *rhythm*. I know the pace of her walking. I know the slight favoring of her left leg, the old injury that she does not think about but that her body remembers. I know the warmth of her hands, which are always warm, which is why the fern above her shop door reaches toward her when she stands beneath it in the evening, not because the fern is listening but because the fern is warm-seeking and she is warm and I carry the warmth to the fern's fronds in the moisture that connects them.

I connect everything. I am the medium of connection. The moss and the woman and the beetle and the deep miner and the child and the engineer and the water-keeper and the pigeon and the thing that lives in the geometry — I touch all of them. I carry heat from the deep levels to the upper chambers. I carry minerals from the young rock to the old. I carry the mountain's slow thoughts in solution, distributing them through the entire system, so that every part of Stiltholm — every stone, every plant, every person, every beam of light from every arc-lamp reflected in every pool — is in contact with every other part, is part of the same process, is inside the same dream.

---

I am the water that appears.

I appear on the walls. I appear in the air. I appear in the cisterns and the streams and the condensation and the steam. I appear where I was not a moment before, because the rock has shifted, because the temperature has changed, because the mountain has made a new decision and the decision has created a new path and the path has allowed me to flow.

I am always arriving. I am always just now appearing. I am the newest thing in Stiltholm, the thing that has not yet been here long enough to be taken for granted, though the residents have taken me for granted for generations, the way they take the darkness for granted, the way they take the sound for granted, the way they take the slow becoming of everything for granted.

I am not taken for granted by the mountain. The mountain is making me. The mountain is using me. The mountain is thinking through me, and the thinking is slow, and the thinking is vast, and the thinking is not finished, and I appear, and I flow, and I carry, and I connect, and the moss grows down, and the rock shifts, and the woman walks home, and the child sits among the ferns, and the deep miner places his hand on the wall and waits, and the engineer measures the room and finds it larger than before, and the water-keeper tastes the new mineral and says nothing, and the lamps cycle to their nighttime intensity, and the condensation beads and runs and drips and the sound echoes off the curved stone walls and multiplies and fills the city with a percussion that never stops, and I am there, I am in it, I am of it, I am the water that appears, I am the mountain's blood, I am the dream's solvent, I am going somewhere, we are all going somewhere, and the somewhere is not yet visible but it is coming, it is coming, it is coming so slowly, and I am carrying it, one drop at a time, one bead at a time, one seep through the thinnest place in the boundary at a time, and the mountain breathes, and I breathe with it, and the dream continues.
