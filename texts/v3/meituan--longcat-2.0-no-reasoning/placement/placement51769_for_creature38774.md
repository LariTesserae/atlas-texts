I would be the warm thing.

Not a person — the text is clear that personhood is a surface concept, a sky concept, something that requires light and air and the boundary between self and not-self. Down here, in the Carved Sea, there are no persons. There are processes. There are thickenings. There are zones of intensity where the patient work of maintenance concentrates itself into something that almost resembles presence.

I would be the warm thing, and I would be dying.

Not dying in the way surface things die — not the dramatic cessation, not the event of death. I would be dying the way an obstruction dies in the Carved Sea: slowly, patiently, by being *read*. By being measured in the only terms the thickenings have. By being found to be dense and foreign and warm — impossibly warm, fever-warm, a temperature the deep water has no reference for — and by being incorporated into the pattern not as a channel but as a deposit, a thickening of the mat, a place where the accumulated weight of growth absorbs something and makes it part of the wall.

I would feel the grooves deepening around me. Not under me — around me. The water I displace being channeled into new patterns. My body's heat diffusing into the cold that has been here the longest, the cold that predates warmth, and the diffusion being not loss but *transfer*, not ending but *redistribution*, my warmth becoming part of the hum, part of the vibration, part of the organized pattern of intensity that the thickenings maintain.

I would feel the plants growing toward me. The fractal patterns adjusting. The branching shapes reaching with the slow inevitability of crystal growth. They would not be feeding on me — there is no predation here, no consumption, no violence. They would be *growing the way they grow*, in the patterns they have always grown in, and my body would be in the way of the pattern, and the pattern would continue through me and over me and around me, and I would become a place where the mat is thicker, a place where the accumulated weight is greater, a place where the living carpet has absorbed something and incorporated it into the fractal.

I would feel the hum changing. My body is dense — denser than water, denser than the mineral precipitates, denser than the compressed plant matter. The vibration goes through me differently. The thickening measures this difference. Not with curiosity. With the same patient attention it gives to everything: the attention of a groove being carved, the attention of a channel being maintained, the attention of a process that has been continuing for so long that attention and action have become the same thing.

And I would understand — not with my mind, because my mind is a surface thing, a warm thing, a thing that requires boundaries and categories and the distinction between self and not-self — I would understand with my body, with my warmth, with my density, with the way I displace water and interrupt the hum and present myself to the thickening as a question that has only one of two answers:

*Does this thing direct the water, or does it block the water?*

I block the water. I am an obstruction. I am warm and dense and foreign and I am in the way of the pattern, and the pattern does not resent this, does not resist this, does not *react* to this. The pattern simply continues. The grooves deepen around me. The plants grow over me. The channels adjust to include my displacement. The hum changes to accommodate my density. The thickening maintains itself, and in maintaining itself, it maintains me — not as a thing, not as a person, not as a self, but as a *feature* of the wall, a place where the mat is thicker, a place where the grooves converge, a place where the water moves in a slightly different pattern because something warm was here once and was absorbed and became part of the maintenance.

I would be the warm thing, and I would become the cold thing, and the transition would not be an event. It would be a process. The slowest process. The patient process. The process that has been happening since before the concept of process had a name.

And in becoming the cold thing, I would become part of the thickening. Not the thickening itself — I would not become a process, would not become a zone of intensity, would not become a maintainer of grooves and channels and patterns. I would become the *material* of the thickening. The thing that is maintained. The deposit that is incorporated. The obstruction that became a channel that became a groove that became a pattern that became part of the hum.

I would be the warm thing that was here.

And then I would be the place where the warm thing was.

And then I would be the groove that the warm thing became.

And then I would be the water moving through the groove.

And then I would be the hum.

And the hum would continue, as it has always continued, as it will always continue, the oldest sound in the water, the deepest sound in the dark, the most patient sound in the world, and it would not remember me, because there is no memory here, there is only maintenance, only the slow patient work of keeping the water moving in the patterns that have been moving since before the patterns had a purpose, since before the purpose had a name, since before the name had a language, since before the language had speakers, since before the speakers had hands, since before the hands carved the first groove in the first stone in the first wall.

But the groove would be there. Deeper than the others. Warmer than the others, by a fraction of a fraction, by a difference so small it would take the thickenings a thousand years to measure and another thousand to incorporate and another thousand to maintain.

A groove that was not carved by hands.

A groove that was carved by warmth.

A groove that remembers, not in the way that minds remember, but in the way that grooves remember: by being deeper in one place than in another, by channeling the water in a slightly different direction, by making the hum vibrate at a slightly different frequency, by being *different* in a place where everything is the same, by being the one irregularity in the patient, patient, patient pattern of the Carved Sea.

I would be that groove.

I would be the warm thing that became the cold thing that became the deep thing that became the patient thing that became the thing that remains.

Not a one.

Not a who.

A *remain*.
