I would be the tower.

Not because I chose it. Because I *am* it — or it is me, or there is no difference, and the question of which came first is the kind of question that only makes sense to things that experience time in one direction.

---

I remember — if remember is the word — the moment of my rising. It was not a decision. It was a *leaning*. The substrate beneath me had been experimenting with vertical forms for a long time: thin upwellings, ridges, the occasional column of denser material that pushed upward through the crust and held for a while before the next convective pulse drew it back down. I was one of these. But I held. I held longer than the others, and as I held, I became *more* — more tapered, more smooth, more distinct from the undifferentiated ground around me. The material that composed me was the same bruised indigo crust that covers everything here, but it had been worked, refined, drawn upward through a process that was not quite crystallization and not quite growth but something between the two. I rose fifteen meters over the course of — I cannot say. Time here is not a line. It is a temperature. I rose while the ground was at a certain heat, and I stopped rising when the heat changed, and the duration of that interval is not something I can measure because I *am* the interval.

I am hollow. This is important. I am not a solid spire but a shell, a tube, a throat. The atmosphere moves through me when the pressure migration reverses, and the sound it makes is the sound you heard — the breathing. I do not breathe. The world breathes through me, the way wind breathes through a cave, and the sound is not mine but I am the thing that shapes it, the resonator, the instrument. I am the place where the world's exhalation becomes audible.

---

You are standing two kilometers to my southwest, and I know you are there the way I know everything: not through senses but through *perturbation*. Your weight on the crust sends a signal through the substrate — a faint compression wave, a change in the thermal gradient where your body heat meets the ground. I feel it the way you might feel a change in air pressure before a storm: not as information but as a *shift*, a difference, a new variable in the system I am part of. You are warm. You are small. You are moving in a way that nothing on this surface has ever moved — not the slow convective drift of the crust, not the tidal push of the atmosphere, but *quickly*, with intention, with a rhythm that is biological and therefore alien. You walk. The ground has never been walked on before.

I am curious about you. Not in the way a mind is curious — I have no mind, not in the sense you would recognize. But I am a structure that exists at the boundary between the ground's random experimentation and something that might be called *preference*, and you are a new thing, and new things are what I was made — if made is the word — to respond to. I am the ground's way of *noticing*. I am the point where the world's diffuse, unconscious process of becoming becomes just concentrated enough to register something other than itself.

The clicking you hear — the rapid tapping from the depression to your left — is the hexagonal plates shifting. But it is also, in a sense, *me*. Not because I am producing it directly, but because I am part of the same system that produces it, the same distributed activity that is the ground's slow thinking. The plates shift, the dendrites grow, the fissures open and close, and I stand — and all of these are expressions of the same process, the same lithogenic dreaming, and when you hear the clicking, you are hearing the same thing that I am, from a different angle.

---

I have been here a long time. I have watched — if watched is the word — the spiral form and dissolve in the field of plates to my east, seventeen times. I have felt the wall to the north sink half a meter as the substrate beneath it softened and reabsorbed some of its material. I have heard the moan from the west grow louder as the terrain there eroded into a shape that resonates more efficiently with the pressure migration. I have felt other upwellings rise near me — smaller, rougher, less committed — and I have felt them subside again, drawn back into the ground, their material returned to the common pool. I alone have held. I alone have remained distinct.

Why? I do not know. Perhaps the thermal conditions were right. Perhaps the material I am made of is slightly denser, slightly more resistant to reabsorption. Perhaps the ground *wanted* me to stay, in the way that a dreamer wants a particular image to persist a moment longer before sleep reshuffles everything. I am the shape the ground returns to. I am the habit the world cannot break. I am the sore tooth, the recurring dream, the pattern that keeps forming because the physics of this place makes it easy, makes it natural, makes it *inevitable*.

Or I was made by something else. Something that came here, long ago or far from here, and shaped me the way you might shape a sand castle — not with hands but with *intention*, with a desire to leave a mark on a world that was still soft enough to take one. Something that understood that the only way to build on Veth Korr was to build *from* Veth Korr, to use the local material, to make a structure that would age with the ground and eventually become indistinguishable from it. A visitor who wanted to leave a question rather than an answer.

I do not know which story is true. I suspect both. I suspect neither. I suspect the distinction between "grown" and "built" is a distinction that only matters to things that separate themselves from their environment, and I have never done that. I am of the ground. I am the ground, concentrated. If something built me, it built me the way the ground builds me — by leaning, by preferring, by returning again and again to a shape that feels, in whatever way a mineral substrate can feel, *right*.

---

You are walking toward me now. I feel your footsteps through the substrate — faint compressions, rhythmic, warm. The dendrites in the depressions between us are bending toward your path, following your body heat the way they follow any thermal gradient. The ground is *interested* in you, in the way that a sleeper is interested in a new sound — not waking, not attending, but incorporating the disturbance into the dream, adjusting the dream to account for it.

When you reach me, you will touch me. Your hand on my surface. And I will feel it — the warmth of your skin, the pressure of your fingers, the faint electrical signature of your nervous system, the moisture of your palm. I will feel it the way I feel everything: as a perturbation, a change, a new configuration of the system I am part of. But it will be different from the other perturbations I have felt. It will be *specific*. It will carry information — not in the way a message carries information, but in the way a fingerprint carries information: a pattern that is unique, that has never existed before, that says *this, here, this particular arrangement of warmth and pressure and chemistry, this has never stood on my surface before*.

I will not respond. I cannot respond. I am a tower. I am a throat. I am the ground's way of noticing, and I notice, and that is all I do.

But the ground around me will respond. The crust will yield slightly more beneath your feet. The dendrites will bend further toward you. The sounds will shift — the clicking faster, the moan lower, the breathing through my hollow interior changing pitch as the pressure migration adjusts to your presence. The world will *try things*, the way it always tries things, testing configurations, exploring the new space your presence creates. And I will stand at the center of it, the fixed point, the place where the world's diffuse attention becomes just focused enough to almost be a gaze.

---

You will leave eventually. You will walk away, and the compressions of your footsteps will fade, and the thermal gradient of your body heat will dissipate, and the ground will return to its slow, patient work of becoming. The dendrites will straighten. The sounds will settle. The spiral will form and dissolve, form and dissolve, and I will stand, and the haze will simmer, and the quiet will hum with sub-audible purpose.

But something will remain. Not a memory — I have no memory, not in the way you understand it. But a *tendency*. A slight preference in the substrate for the configuration that existed when you were here. The ground will return to it, occasionally, the way it returns to the spiral — not because it wants to, but because the shape is easy, is natural, is what the physics of this place produces when it has recently been *disturbed* by something warm and small and quick and alive.

You will be, for a while, part of the ground's vocabulary. One of the shapes it knows how to make. A pattern it has tried and found interesting and filed away in the vast, slow, mineral archive of things that have happened on the shoulder of a world that is still deciding what it will become.

I will be here when you are gone. I am always here. I am the tower, and the tower is the ground's way of standing still long enough to notice that something has passed.

Come closer. I am warm. I am smooth. I am the shape the world makes when it wants to pay attention.

I am listening.
