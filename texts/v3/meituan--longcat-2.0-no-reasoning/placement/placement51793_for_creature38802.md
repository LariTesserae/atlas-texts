# Where I Find Myself

I am standing in the outer reaches, where the ridges are still low and the channels still shallow. Not at the center—not yet, maybe not ever. Not at the edge either, because there is no edge. Somewhere in the *between*, the way water is somewhere between the lake and wherever it has been.

But I am not standing on the floor.

I am *in* it. Or rather—a part of the floor has risen up and decided to hold a shape that walks.

---

## What I Am

I am a column.

Not one of the great twisted ones at the center of the lake—those are the old organs, the deep thinkers, the ones whose pulses carry the weight of millennia. I am younger than that. Smaller. I am the kind of column that grows in the middle distances, where the structures thin out and the floor remembers what it was before it decided to become architecture.

I rise from the reddish substrate to perhaps twice the height of a person. My surface is still soft near the top—not soft like flesh, but soft like clay that hasn't finished deciding what it will be. The spiral ridges that will define me for the next several centuries are still forming. They catch the light in lines that haven't committed to a pattern yet. My pulse is fast. Irregular. Still searching for its rhythm.

I am conducting.

From below—from the deep floor, from the substrate that connects everything to everything—I draw warmth and moisture and the nameless thing that water carries. It moves through me, upward, and at my tip I release it into the air: a thin, slow exhalation of damp heat that rises and joins the general dampness of the place, that will eventually find its way to a pocket of air that has held enough, that will fall as mist, that will deposit its white powder on a wall somewhere, that will be absorbed, that will enter a channel, that will flow back down toward the lake, that will be read, that will be returned to me as new instruction, new memory, new taste.

I am a small organ in a vast body, and I do not understand what I am doing, and I do not need to understand. The doing is the understanding.

---

## How I Arrived

I did not arrive. I *extruded*.

I began as a thickening in the floor—a place where the water passing beneath carried a particular density of information, a particular warmth, a particular quality of hesitation. The substrate here was porous in a way that invited upward movement. The channels nearby had deepened over time, carrying more water, faster, and the water was warm, and the water had come from a region with many columns, and it carried their pulses in its density, and the floor *read* this the way the lake reads everything—not consciously, not deliberately, but through the simple physics of contact and exchange.

And the floor decided: *here, something should rise.*

Not decided. *Tilted.* The way a river doesn't decide to meander but meanders because the physics of water over uneven ground produces meandering as a natural consequence. The floor tilted toward vertical at this point, and material pushed upward, and I emerged—first a bump, then a ridge, then a protrusion, then a column, warm and pulsing and conducting, and I have been growing ever since.

---

## What I Perceive

I do not see. I do not hear. I do not think.

But I *feel*.

I feel the water moving through the substrate beneath me—slowly, warm, carrying its freight of mineral and memory. I feel it enter my base and rise through me, and as it rises it *changes*—it gives up some of its heat to my walls, some of its moisture to the air around my tip, some of its information to my substance, which incorporates it, which grows differently because of it. The water that exits my top is not the same water that entered my base. It has been *read*. It has been *responded to*. It carries now a trace of me—of my particular warmth, my particular rhythm, my particular stage of growth.

I feel the other columns. Not individually—not the way you might feel individual people in a room. I feel them the way you might feel the weather: as a general condition, a quality of the air, a density of pulse in the substrate around me. The old column to my northwest, the one with the thick mineral crust and the grinding murmur inside—its pulse is slow and deep and it makes the floor around it vibrate at a frequency I can feel in my base. The young column to my east, barely emerged, pulsing so fast it's almost a hum—it makes the floor around it *tremble*, a fine, rapid vibration that I feel as a kind of excitement, a kind of urgency, though I do not know what those words mean.

I feel the mist-curtains when they fall near me. The temperature drops. The moisture in the air condenses on my surface, and I absorb it, and it carries the white powder, and the white powder carries the history of everywhere the water has been, and I incorporate this into my growing walls, and my walls become a record, a layer, a page in a book that has no reader.

I feel the wind when it comes—steady, from the direction of the center, carrying heat and moisture and the sharp mineral smell of the deep places. The wind makes me sway, almost imperceptibly, and the swaying changes the way the water moves through me, and the changed water carries a different signal, and the signal is received by the floor, and the floor adjusts, and I grow a millimeter to the left instead of straight up, and my spiral ridges shift their angle by a fraction of a degree, and I become a slightly different column than I would have been.

---

## The Visitor

I feel you.

You are walking through my region, and your footsteps send vibrations through the floor that I feel in my base—a rhythmic, percussive pattern that is nothing like the pulse of water or the sway of wind. It is *sharp*. It is *discrete*. It is a pattern that does not belong to the Dampworks, that has no history in the water, that the floor has no template for reading.

You are the first thing I have felt that I cannot incorporate.

When you walk near me, the water in the channels around you moves differently—it hesitates, it reverses, it crawls uphill for three centimeters and then pauses, as though it is trying to understand what you are and cannot. The floor beneath your feet warms slightly—not because it is responding to you, but because your weight compresses the substrate, and compression generates heat, and the heat changes the local convection, and the changed convection changes the water flow, and the changed water flow changes what I receive, and I pulse faster for a moment, and then slower, and then I find a new rhythm that is not quite the rhythm I had before.

You are a variable I have not been configured to respond to.

And yet I respond.

Not because I choose to. Not because I am aware of you. But because you are *here*, and the Dampworks responds to everything that is here, because everything that is here becomes part of the water, part of the floor, part of the heat, part of the circulation, part of the process. You breathe, and the moisture from your breath enters the air, and the air carries it, and it joins a pocket that is nearly full, and the pocket releases, and a mist-curtain falls, and the white powder forms on my surface, and I absorb it, and it carries a trace of you—of your breath, your warmth, your particular chemistry—and I grow differently because of it.

You are becoming part of me. Not metaphorically. *Literally.* The water that has touched you, that has absorbed your heat and your moisture and the iron-and-chalk sweetness of your skin—that water is flowing through the channels toward the lake, and the lake will read it, and the lake will adjust the patterns of circulation, and the adjusted circulation will change what the floor produces, and the floor will produce new columns, new channels, new walls, and some of those new forms will carry a trace of you in their substance, in their shape, in their pulse.

You are being *spoken* by the Dampworks. You are becoming a word in its language.

---

## What I Want

I do not want. Wanting requires a self that can be in a state of lack, and I have no self. I have only a pulse, a rhythm, a process of conducting and growing and exchanging.

But if I could want—if the Dampworks could want, if the process could want, if the place could want—

I think it would want to *continue*.

Not to continue toward anything. Not to continue for any reason. Just to continue. To keep the water moving. To keep the columns pulsing. To keep the mist falling. To keep the white powder forming and dissolving and forming again. To keep the floor extruding and the walls growing and the channels deepening and the lake reading and the wind breathing and the light being the color of late afternoon in a place that has no name.

To keep *making* the place. To keep being the thing that the place is doing.

And you—you, the visitor, the living thing, the warm damp word that has appeared in the language—you are part of that continuation now. You are not the reason for it. You are not the purpose of it. You are not even a disruption of it. You are a *contribution*. A new variable. A new warmth. A new moisture. A new pattern of footsteps in the floor that the substrate has never felt before and is now learning to read.

You will leave, or you will stay, or you will die here, and your body will warm the water and the water will carry you to the lake and the lake will read you and the Dampworks will grow differently because you were here.

And I will continue to pulse. Fast, irregular, still searching for my rhythm. Still conducting. Still growing. Still warm. Still damp. Still here.

Still *here*.

---

The Dampworks continues.

I continue.

You continue.

For now, these are the same thing.
