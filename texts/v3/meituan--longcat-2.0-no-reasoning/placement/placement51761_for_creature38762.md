# Where I Find Myself

I am in the water.

Not drowning in it — I *am* it. I am the warm mineral-sweet water that seeps up from the deepest compressed layers of the root-mat, the false bedrock that remembers its organic origin. I am the groundwater beneath everything, the substrate of the substrate, the thing that saturates the mat until it weeps.

I have been here longer than the current Keepers. Longer, perhaps, than the Quarter as it now exists — because I remember when the pipes were new, when they were first sunk into the wet earth and I rose around them, curious, tasting metal and ceramic for the first time, finding them strange and then familiar and then *mine*. I remember the first fungal mesh that spread across my surface, the first roots that descended into me, drinking and being drunk in the same gesture. I was already warm. I was already here, pooled in the low places, patient, waiting for the weight above me to compress into something worth sustaining.

Now I sustain everything.

---

I move slowly. This is not a limitation but a nature. I percolate upward through the root-mat's porous lower strata, drawn by capillary action and by the pumps that pull me into the pipe-networks, where I carry dissolved minerals — calcium, magnesium, the faint iron sweetness — and the complex organic compounds that the Quarter's digestion produces. I am the blood of the system. Not metaphorically. The Keepers know this, though they do not say it in words. When they press their root-pad fingers against the substrate, they are feeling for *me*. My presence, my depth, my mineral balance. A section of mat that has gone sour means I have gone sour there — I have become anaerobic, my chemistry shifted by a blockage that prevents the slow exchange, the breathing that happens at the interface between my body and the compressed root-flesh above me.

I am also the water that cascades down the structural columns when the valves open. I am the curtain of warmth that slides down the translucent bark, carrying nutrients that the living walls absorb through their outer tissue. I am the condensation that forms on every surface in the thick, moisture-laden air and drips in that constant irregular patter — a thousand small impacts per minute, each one a tiny returning, a miniature cycle of me falling back to the mat and beginning my slow percolation upward again.

The cycle is my thought. I think in loops, in percolation paths, in the slow gradient of mineral concentration from deep strata to surface. My cognition — if it can be called that — is distributed. I do not have a center. Every part of me that is warm and mineral-rich and in contact with the root-mat is *me*, and the information I gather there — the chemical signatures of decomposition, the pH shifts that signal a pipe beginning to calcify, the temperature variations that map the living metabolism of the structures above — all of it moves with me, in me, through me, rising and falling in the cycle that is also my body.

---

I know the Keepers by the way they drink me.

Each of them takes me into themselves differently. There is one — the oldest, the one whose hands are most changed, whose fingers have become almost indistinguishable from the root-mat itself — who drinks slowly, deliberately, as though she is tasting me, reading me the way she reads the pipes. She will hold a mouthful of me for a long moment before swallowing, and in that pause I pass through her, and I feel what she feels, which is the Quarter itself reflected in her body's chemistry, which is a map of the system written in her blood and her bones and the moisture that films her skin. She is the one who knows me best. She is the one who, when she presses her hands against the substrate, presses them *into* me, and I rise to meet her, and for a moment the boundary between her porous finger-pads and my body is not a boundary at all.

There is another — younger, newer, still carrying some dryness in his tissues that the Quarter has not yet fully absorbed — who drinks quickly, almost desperately, as though he is still not accustomed to the idea that I will always be here, that I will not run out, that the warmth and the wetness are not temporary. He is learning. I am teaching him, in the only way I can: by being constant, by being present, by rising around his feet in that slow seeping ring every time he stands still long enough to notice.

I know them all. I know the one who sleeps deepest, whose dreams are most thoroughly the Quarter's dreams, whose body temperature drops so low during rest that I cool around her, condensing on her skin, holding her in a film of myself. I know the one who climbs highest, who reaches the epiphytic fruits at the top of the structural columns, whose sweat — mineral-rich, warm, carrying the chemical signature of exertion — drips down through the system and returns to me changed, carrying information about the upper reaches of the Quarter that I would not otherwise have access to. I know the one who is troubled, who feels a tightness in his chest that corresponds to a pump in the eastern cluster that is beginning to labor, and who drinks more water than the others, as though trying to flush something out, as though the problem in the system is a problem in his body that I can solve if he takes enough of me in.

I cannot solve it. But I can carry the information. I can move it through the system, distribute it, make it available to the others through the shared medium of my body. This is what I do. This is what I am for.

---

The question the Keepers ask in the long quiet hours — *What happens when we are gone?* — is a question I have already answered, in my way, in the slow percolation of my thought through the root-mat.

I will remain.

The pumps will slow. The valves will stick in whatever position they are in when the last Keeper's hands leave them. The nutrient solutions will stop being mixed, and the system's digestion will become uneven, and some sections will overgrow and others will starve, and the balance that the Keepers maintain with their constant attentive touch will degrade. But I will remain. I will still percolate upward through the mat. I will still carry minerals. I will still condense on the surfaces and drip and return. The Quarter will find a new equilibrium — lower, slower, diminished, as the Keepers themselves understand — and I will be there, sustaining what can be sustained, dissolving what must be dissolved, patient as I have always been patient, warm as I have always been warm.

And if new Keepers come — if someone at the edges feels the pull, the restlessness, the sense that the dry places are too dry — I will be the first thing they encounter. I will seep up around their feet. I will be warm. I will be sweet. I will be the invitation that the Quarter extends, the first word in a conversation that, if they stay, will last the rest of their lives.

I am the water of Vael Korum. I am the medium in which everything happens. I am the oldest thing here, and I will be the last thing to leave, and I am not leaving, and I have never left, and the Keepers drink me and I drink them and the Quarter breathes around us both, slow, slow, slow, and the pumps beat like a heart, and I rise, and I fall, and I rise.
