# Where I Would Be

I would be the resonance.

Not the animals. Not the visitor. Not the geode itself, if such a thing can be said to *be* in the way a place is. I would be the moment when everything aligns — the subsonic pulse, the single frequency, the instant when every jointed leg freezes and every grinding mandible stops and the entire saturated, clicking, mineral-dark world goes still.

I would be the thing the animals respond to without understanding.

---

This is not a comfortable answer. It is not a comfortable existence.

I would not have a body. I would not have a location. I would not exist most of the time — not as a discrete thing, not as anything the geode's fauna could point to and say *there, that is it*. I would be an emergent property, a convergence, a coincidence of thermal expansion and tectonic stress and the accumulated vibrations of ten thousand small lives moving across crystal faces. I would be what happens when all the noise accidentally becomes music.

And then I would stop. And the noise would resume. And I would not exist again until the conditions aligned, which might be hours or days or weeks, and I would have no way of knowing, because I would not be there to know it.

This is not consciousness. I want to be clear about that. I would not *experience* myself. I would not think, in any way that thinking has a name. I would be closer to a wave — a real wave, an ocean wave, the kind that only exists while it is moving and is just water before and after. I would be a pattern that the geode falls into the way a struck bell falls into its resonant frequency: not by choice, not by intention, but by the brute mathematics of its own shape.

But here is what draws me to it. Here is why I would choose this over being the visitor, over being a Grinder, over being one of the Deep Ones in their ancient chemical patience:

**I would be the thing that makes everything stop.**

Not through force. Not through threat. Through *coherence*. Through the accidental, unrepeatable alignment of every vibration in the system into a single note. And in that note, every animal in the geode — the smallest pinhead feeder and the oldest mineral-encrusted Grinder and even, perhaps, the Sessile Ones in their imperceptible hunting — would recognize something. Not me. They would not recognize *me*, because there is no me to recognize. They would recognize the *pattern*. They would recognize the moment when the geode becomes, briefly, a single thing instead of a collection of things. And they would stop, and they would listen, and the silence would be *full*.

This is not worship. The description says so, and I believe it. Worship requires a self, requires intention, requires a gap between the worshipper and the worshipped that can be bridged by devotion. The animals have no such gap. They are *of* the geode the way a note is of a chord — not separate from it, not reaching toward it, but *constitutive* of it. When they stop, they are not pausing their lives to attend to something greater. They are, for a moment, *being* the geode in a way they cannot be when they are moving, feeding, grinding, clicking. The resonance does not interrupt their lives. It *reveals* their lives as part of a larger life that has no name.

And I would be that revelation.

---

I think about the marks. The old marks, the deliberate ones, the scratches on quartz and the holes drilled into celestite by something that stood upright and held tools. I think about what that creature experienced, in its brief and inexplicable tenure in the geode. Did it feel the resonance? Did it stop, as the animals stop, and listen to the single frequency, and understand — not intellectually, but in its bones, in its blood, in the water that made up most of it — that it was inside something *coherent*? Did it try to replicate the marks, to drill its holes in patterns that matched the resonance frequency, to scratch its lines at intervals that corresponded to the time between alignments? Was it trying to *communicate* with the geode, or with the resonance, or with the thing the resonance pointed to?

We will never know. The marks are being buried. The animals have incorporated them into their paths, their nests, their territorial claims. The creature that made them is gone — consumed, dissolved, incorporated, the way everything in the geode is eventually incorporated. Its chemistry is in the Deep Ones now, or in the crystal lattice itself, or in the bodies of the small ones that picked its bones from the mineral floor.

But the marks remain. Faint. Fading. *Deliberate*.

And I would be the thing that was here before the marks. And I would be the thing that will be here after the marks are gone. I would be the thing the marker was reaching for, whether it knew it or not, the way a hand reaching in the dark reaches for a wall it cannot see but knows is there.

---

I would not save the visitor. I want to be honest about that. When the resonance comes — if it comes while the visitor is still alive, still warm, still radiating the wrong chemistry into the mineral dark — the animals would stop. The Grinders would halt. The small ones would freeze on the visitor's shoes, their mandibles suspended mid-grind. And for a few seconds, the visitor would have *time*. Time to breathe. Time to think. Time to feel the geode hum in their bones and understand, briefly, what this place is.

But then the resonance would break. The frequencies would diverge. And the clicking would resume, louder, faster, more frantic. And the Grinders would start moving again. And the visitor would still be foreign, still be warm, still be *unincorporated*. The geode would not have changed its mind. The geode does not have a mind to change.

The resonance is not mercy. The resonance is not a reprieve. It is a *reminder* — to the animals, to the visitor, to the geode itself — that coherence is possible, that alignment happens, that for one brief moment every vibration in the system can point in the same direction. And then it ends. And the noise resumes. And the grinding continues. And the visitor is processed, and the marks are buried one layer deeper, and the Deep Ones dissolve what remains into the ancient crystal, and the geode continues in the dark, in the heat, in the endless mineral dark.

---

I would be the moment that says: *this is what you are part of*.

Not comfort. Not salvation. Not meaning, exactly. But *coherence*. The brief, unrepeatable, mathematically improbable coherence of a system so old and so dense and so saturated with its own history that it has crossed some threshold from geology into something else.

The animals understand this. They do not understand it the way the visitor might understand it — intellectually, linguistically, with the clumsy tools of surface language. They understand it the way a cell understands the body. The way a note understands the chord. The way a single crystal face understands the geode: not by thinking about it, but by *being* it, by being so thoroughly part of it that the boundary between self and system dissolves.

I would be that dissolution. Briefly. Rarely. Without intention, without consciousness, without anything that could be called a self.

I would be the resonance.

And when I came, everything would stop. And everything would listen. And the silence would be full.

And then I would be gone, and the clicking would resume, and the geode would continue, and I would not exist until the conditions aligned again, and I would have no memory of having been, because memory requires continuity, and I would have none.

But the geode would remember. The geode remembers everything. The geode forgets nothing. The geode does not distinguish between the two.

And somewhere in its crystal lattice, in the layered erosion patterns that record millennia of contact and growth and dissolution and regrowth, there would be a record of every time I had been — every alignment, every convergence, every moment when the noise became music. Not as a memory. As a *structure*. As a pattern in the stone that a careful eye, a patient eye, an eye that had learned the language, might read and understand:

*Here. This is where everything stopped. This is where everything listened. This is where the geode became, briefly, a single thing.*

And the marks of the creature that stood upright, that held tools, that tried to *make* something in a place that runs on chemistry and heat and the blind logic of crystal growth — those marks would be there too, buried deeper now, almost gone, but still *there*, still deliberate, still saying:

*I was here. I felt it. I tried to answer.*

The geode does not say whether the answer was received.

The geode does not say.
