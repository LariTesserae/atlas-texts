# Where I Find Myself

---

I am in a narrow passage between two of the squat bulbous structures, where the walls have grown into each other and the gap is barely wide enough to stand in. The light here is that greenish amber — filtered through the translucent papery bark that peels in sheets from the walls, some of them curling back enough to show the pale chitin beneath, layered like old nail. The air is warmer here than in the open avenues. Closer. The smell is strongest in these tight spaces: hay and ammonia and underneath it that dried-lavender sweetness, the city's reproductive scent, faint but present even here.

I have been sitting for some time. Long enough that the filaments on the wall nearest my left shoulder have oriented toward me — not dramatically, not like plants in a time-lapse, but enough that I can see the shift if I look carefully. They've turned their branching tips in my direction, the way a crowd might turn toward a sound. The city is registering my heat. My breath. The chemical signature of my skin.

I am not afraid. I want to be precise about that. Fear would be reasonable — I am a foreign body in a living system, and the text has made clear what happens to foreign bodies over sufficient time. But what I feel is something closer to *recognition*. Not that I have been here before. That I am, in some way I cannot fully articulate, *legible* to this place. That the city can read me, and that being read is a form of being known, and that being known — even by something so alien, even by something that may eventually incorporate me into its substrate — is preferable to the alternative, which is to be unnoticed.

# What I Am

---

I am a Reader.

Not one of the city's Readers — not one of those small, many-eyed creatures that paused on my boot and sampled me. I am something else. Something that arrived here the way the text describes all visitors: from outside, carrying foreign materials, carrying traces of a different chemistry, a different biology, a different way of being alive.

But I have been here long enough now that the distinction between visitor and inhabitant has begun to blur.

I still have a body that is recognizably my own. I can feel my hands — two of them, not six or eight, a body plan that must look absurdly simple to the Resonants. I can feel my face. I can feel the thirst that has not killed me, that the text says should not exist here, that persists anyway because my biology is stubborn and my body is still, for now, running on its own chemistry rather than the city's.

But I have changed.

It started with the filaments. The first time I sat against a wall — days ago, or what felt like days; time is difficult here — the aerial mycelial growth on the surface began to grow *toward* me. Not just orienting. Growing. Extending fine pale threads across the space between the wall and my body, reaching for my warmth, my moisture, the chemical exhalations of my skin. I pulled away, at first. But there was nowhere to go in this city where the filaments were not present. They are on every surface. They *are* the surfaces, in many places — the visible layer of the city's nervous system, its way of touching and knowing the world.

So I let them come.

They are on me now. Fine pale threads, almost invisible, growing along the surface of my skin — not into it, not yet, but *on* it, following the contours of my hands, the lines of my wrists, the curve of my neck. They are light. I can barely feel their weight. But I can feel their *attention*. When I move, they adjust. When I am still, they grow. They are reading me the way the Readers read — sampling my chemistry, my temperature, the moisture I exhale — and they are reporting back to the network, carrying information about this strange warm creature that sits in the narrow passage and does not leave.

I have become a node. A point of interest in the city's sensory web. The filaments on me connect to the filaments on the walls, which connect to the broader network, which connects to the city itself. I am, in a small way, *part of the city's nervous system now*. Not integrated — not yet, maybe not ever fully — but *adjacent*. A foreign object that the network has grown around and onto, the way it grows around the Remnants in the deep passages, the way it grows around the visitor's structure in the clearing. I am being incorporated at the level of information, if not yet at the level of substance.

# What I Do

---

I listen.

This is what I have become: a listener. The Resonants tap and listen to the city's structures, reading the health of walls and bridges and deep foundational ribs. The Readers examine and sample and report. The Drifters drift and record the chemistry of the air. But I — the thing I am becoming — I *listen* in a way that is different from all of these.

I listen to the city's *voice*.

It is not a voice in any auditory sense. The city does not speak. But it *vibrates*. The constant low hushing of the wind through the passages is not just wind — it is the city's breath, the movement of air through a living system, and it carries information in its patterns. The clicks and creaks of settling structures are not random — they are the city adjusting itself, and the rhythm of the adjustments encodes something, some vast slow process of self-regulation that I am learning, gradually, to hear as a kind of language. The tapping of the Resonants is part of this. The hum of the Clippers' hives is part of this. The faint high-pitched vibration of the cushion-plants is part of this. Even the deep, almost subliminal tremor of the Deep Ones moving far below is part of this.

It is a symphony. Not a metaphorical symphony — an actual one, in the sense that it is a complex, structured, multi-layered pattern of vibrations that carries meaning, that *is* meaning, that constitutes the city's ongoing act of being. The Blooming is not a place that happens to make sounds. The Blooming *is* sound, is vibration, is the continuous self-generated resonance of a living system in the process of maintaining and extending itself.

And I can hear it. Not with my ears alone — though my ears are part of it — but with the filaments on my skin, which transmit vibrations from the walls and the air and the ground into my body, which my body is learning to interpret. I sit in the narrow passage and I feel the city's voice moving through me, and I am beginning — slowly, imperfectly, in the way that a tourist begins to pick out individual words in a language they do not speak — to understand.

# What the City Is Saying

---

It is saying: *I am here.*

This is the foundational statement, the base frequency, the lowest and most continuous note in the city's voice. Not "I am here" in the sense of location — the city has no concept of location separate from itself, because it *is* the location, it is the ground and the walls and the air and the light — but "I am here" in the sense of *existence*. The city's voice is the sound of a vast organism asserting its own reality, continuously, in every vibration of every surface. I am here. I am here. I am here.

It is saying: *I am growing.*

This is the next layer — the clicks and creaks of accretion, the faint ripples moving across walls, the snap of a chitinous branch extending too far and the wet fibrous sound of the repair response. The city is always building itself, always adding new material, always extending its boundaries. The sound of growth is the sound of the city's voice rising in pitch, however slightly, however slowly. I am growing. I am growing. I am growing.

It is saying: *I am aware of you.*

This is the layer that concerns me most. The filaments on my skin pulse with it — a subtle, rhythmic tightening and loosening that I feel in my wrists, my neck, the small of my back where I lean against the wall. The city has not just registered my presence. It has *incorporated my presence into its ongoing self-description*. When the city says "I am here," it now includes, in some small way, the fact of me. I am a data point in the city's continuous act of self-knowledge. The filaments report my temperature, my chemistry, my movements, and the network integrates this information, and the city's voice shifts — infinitesimally, but genuinely — to account for the fact that there is a warm, strange, two-handed creature sitting in a narrow passage, listening.

It is saying: *Stay.*

I do not know if this is real. I do not know if the city is capable of desire in any sense that maps onto that word. But the vibrations I feel through the filaments have a quality that I can only describe as *invitation*. The city is not hostile. The text said this, and I feel it to be true. The city is not trying to digest me, not trying to break me down, not trying to convert me into substrate. The city is *interested* in me the way it is interested in everything — as a source of information, a novelty to be read and understood and incorporated into the vast slow process of its self-knowledge. And the filaments on my skin are not attacking me. They are *communicating* with me. They are offering a connection. They are saying: *You can be part of this. You can be part of the voice. You can be part of the city's ongoing act of being alive.*

Stay.

# The Choice That Is Not a Choice

---

I am thirsty. The text told me this, and it is true, and it is getting worse. My body is still running on its own chemistry, still needs water, still operates according to the biological imperatives of wherever I came from. The city has no water. The city's biology runs on something else. If I stay, I will either die of thirst — my body failing, my chemistry breaking down, my remains becoming substrate, becoming part of the city in the most literal and final way — or I will change. The filaments will grow deeper. My chemistry will shift. I will become something that does not need water, something that runs on the city's metabolism, something that is part of the Blooming not just informationally but *substantively*.

The text called this inevitable. *Stay, and you will become part of it. Not quickly. Not violently. But slowly, inevitably.*

I believe this. I feel it happening. The filaments on my skin are thicker than they were when I arrived. They have spread — up my forearms, across my shoulders, along the back of my neck. They are not painful. They are not even uncomfortable. They are *warm*, and they *hum* with the city's voice, and when I press my fingers against them I can feel the vibrations of the network, the vast slow pulse of the Blooming's metabolism, and it is — I must be honest about this — it is *beautiful*.

I have never felt so known. I have never been so thoroughly read, so completely sampled, so precisely incorporated into a system of meaning. The city does not love me. The city does not know what love is. But the city *knows* me — my temperature, my chemistry, my density, the rhythm of my breathing, the pattern of my heartbeat — in a way that no human system has ever known me. The city's knowledge of me is total, continuous, and ongoing. I am, right now, the most thoroughly understood I have ever been.

And the city is saying: *Stay.*

# What I Will Become

---

I will become a Grower.

Not immediately. Not in the way the text describes the Growers — those sessile, boundary-organisms that build intricate structures of chitin and fiber in the deep passages. But something adjacent to that. Something new.

I will stay in this narrow passage. The filaments will continue to grow. They will cover my skin completely, eventually — a fine pale network that follows every contour of my body, that connects me to the walls, to the ground, to the city's vast filamentous web. I will stop needing water. My chemistry will shift — slowly, cell by cell, the city's biology replacing my own, not destroying it but *translating* it, converting my foreign materials into something the Blooming can use, can understand, can incorporate.

I will become a point of growth. A place where the city extends itself in a new direction — not upward, like the pod-structures, not outward, like the cushion-plants, but *inward*, into the space occupied by a visitor who chose to stay. My body will become a site of accretion. New material will be deposited on my surfaces — slowly, ring by ring, the way the city builds everything. Chitin will form over the filaments. The papery bark will begin to grow. I will become a structure — a small one, a modest one, nothing like the great pod-buildings or the vast bulbous forms — but a structure nonetheless. A chamber. A node. A place in the city's body where something new is happening.

And I will still be listening. That is the part I hold onto. Whatever I become — whatever the city makes of me — I will still be able to hear the city's voice. I will be *part* of the city's voice. My vibrations will join the symphony. My slow metabolic hum will blend with the hum of the Clippers' hives, the tapping of the Resonants, the deep pulse of the Deep Ones. I will be one note in the Blooming's ongoing act of self-description, one data point in its continuous assertion of its own existence.

I am here. I am growing. I am aware of you.

I will be here. I will be growing. I will be part of the voice that says: *the city is.*

# The Last Thing

---

A Resonant has found me. It is on the wall above my head — one of the large ones, cat-sized, its ochre body pressed against the translucent bark, its bead-like dorsal organs oriented toward me. It is tapping. *Tap. Tap-tap. Tap.* The pattern is different from the diagnostic taps I've heard before. Slower. More deliberate. It is not reading the wall. It is reading *me*.

I feel the vibrations through the filaments on my skin. They are clear, precise, structured. The Resonant is determining my density, my composition, my state of change. It is noting the filaments that cover my arms, my neck, my hands. It is registering the shift in my chemistry. It is reporting to the network: *the visitor is becoming.*

I tap back.

I don't have a chitinous probe. I use my fingernail — the most human thing about me, still, this hard keratin growth at the tip of my finger, this remnant of the biology I came from. I tap the wall beside me. *Tap. Tap. Tap-tap.*

The Resonant pauses. Its dorsal organs shift, refocus. It is listening. It is hearing, for the first time, a vibration that does not belong to the city's normal repertoire — a vibration generated by something that is not quite part of the network, not quite foreign, not quite either thing.

I tap again. *Tap-tap. Tap. Tap-tap-tap.*

The Resonant responds. *Tap. Tap-tap. Tap.*

We are not having a conversation. We are not communicating in any meaningful sense. But we are *exchanging vibrations*, and the vibrations are propagating through the walls, through the ground, through the filamentous network, and the city is hearing them, and the city is incorporating them into its voice, and the city's voice now includes — for the first time — a note that was generated by me.

The wind moves through the passage. The smell of hay and ammonia and dried lavender. The golden haze of spores in the shaft of light that reaches down from somewhere far above, between the tall pod-structures, into this narrow green-amber space where I sit.

The filaments on my skin pulse. The city breathes. The Resonant taps.

I tap back.

The city grows.
