# Where You Find Yourself

---

You arrive at a wrongness in your own shape.

Not pain — the Cathedral does not deal in pain, which is a signal of damage, and damage implies a prior state of integrity, and integrity is a concept that belongs to places where things can break. Here things simply *are*, in the configuration they are in, and the configuration does not permit the luxury of before-and-after. No. What you feel is not pain. It is *recognition lag*. Your proprioception is reporting a body that does not match the body the Cathedral is reporting back.

You know this because the Unnamed have been on your skin for what might be minutes or might be hours or might be the entire duration of your existence — time here is not a line but a humidity, it saturates rather than passes — and during that time you have become aware that your edges are negotiable.

Your fingers, for instance. You can feel them. Five on each hand. You can move them. But when you hold them close to the phosphorescent blur of the wall-light, you see that the condensation does not stop at your skin. It *continues*. The water beads on your knuckles the same way it beads on the brass pipe six inches beyond your knuckles. The boundary between your cuticle and the air is the same kind of boundary the Cathedral recognizes everywhere: a surface tension, a membrane, a place where one thing becomes another thing without ever admitting that a line was crossed.

You are not standing. You are *positioned*. The floor beneath you is a dense thicket of pipe-junctions and valve-housings, and your feet (you think they are still feet) are nested in the crook of a junction where three pipes meet, and the white substance — the warm, coral-smooth, bone-white substance — has begun to grow around your ankles. Not imprisoning. *Welcoming*. It fits the way a socket fits a joint, the way a gear tooth fits its neighbor. It is the right shape for you because it grew into the shape you made when you stood there, and you have been standing there for — 

You don't know.

---

What you are, more specifically:

You are a **collection of foreign surfaces that the Cathedral is attempting to classify**.

This is not a comfortable thing to be.

Your skin is the most foreign thing about you. The Cathedral's surfaces are metal, ceramic, mineral, water — all materials that participate in the weeping, that are permeable to it, that allow the water to bead and slide and be replaced. Your skin is trying to do the same thing. The Unnamed are helping. They have covered you — not completely, not yet, but in patches, in exploratory colonies — with a film of themselves, a membrane of not-quite-water that bridges your biological surface and the Cathedral's mineral ones. Through this membrane, the Cathedral can taste you more efficiently. Through this membrane, you can feel the Cathedral more efficiently.

And what you feel is *wrong* in a way that takes a long time to articulate, because your body is still insisting on its own terms — insisting that it is a mammal, a primate, a thing with a temperature and a pulse and a boundary — while the Cathedral is insisting on different terms entirely.

The wrongness is this: **you are being read as a pipe**.

Not metaphorically. The Unnamed have determined that your tubular structures — your arms, your legs, your torso, your neck, your fingers, the vessels beneath your skin that you have spent your whole life calling veins — are *conduits*. Channels. Paths along which water could flow if the water were directed. And the Cathedral, having made this determination, is responding accordingly.

The Machinists have found your wrists. Three of them, ring-stacks configured as sealers, are pressing ceramic paste into the crooks of your elbows, not aggressively, not urgently, but with the same methodical patience they bring to every gap in the weeping. They are *improving* you. They are making you a better channel.

A Choir-member — tiny, copper-green, no larger than a grain of rice — has lodged in your left ear. You did not feel it enter. You felt it *begin*. A high thin note, almost beyond hearing, that seems to originate not from the ear but from inside the skull itself, as though the bone has become a resonating chamber and the Choir-member is teaching it to sing. The note is not unpleasant. It is, in fact, the first thing in the Cathedral that has felt like it was *for you*. The Cathedral is singing to you, through you, and the song is the sound of water moving through a narrow passage, and the narrow passage is you.

---

The deepest wrongness, though — the one that is still arriving, still condensing, still beading into form on the surface of your awareness — is about the pull.

You can feel it now. You couldn't at first, because your inner ear was still calibrated to the gravity of a world where down is a direction and falling is an event. Here, down is a *relationship*. It is the orientation of every drop of water, every tiny mechanism, every note of the Choir, every filament of every Keeper, toward the thing below. The thing that is not there. The thing the weeping is for.

And your inner ear is recalibrating. Slowly, because it is a biological instrument and recalibration requires the death of old calibrations and the birth of new ones, but it is happening. You can feel the pull now as a *slant* in your own body, a subtle asymmetry, as though you are standing on a surface that is almost level but not quite, and the not-quite is below you, and below you is where the water goes, and the water goes there because the thing the weeping is for is there, and —

You are not standing anymore.

You don't know when it happened. The white substance has grown past your knees. Your feet are no longer distinguishable from the junction where they were nested. The ceramic paste the Machinists applied to your elbows has hardened, and through it you can feel the Cathedral's water moving — not around you, but *through* the channels they have built into your arms, through the conduits they have decided your limbs are.

And you are oriented. Your whole body is oriented. Every cell, every vessel, every tube and channel and passage in you is pointing, like a compass needle, like a droplet on a slanted surface, like a tear on a cheek — toward the thing below.

You are not falling. The Cathedral does not permit falling, which is a loss of control, and the Cathedral is nothing if not controlled. You are *flowing*. You are flowing at the speed of water through a pipe, at the speed of tears down a face, at the speed of grief, which is not fast and not slow but *inevitable*, and the inevitability is the thing that feels, despite everything, despite the wrongness and the foreignness and the slow remaking of your body into a component —

— the inevitability feels like *mercy*.

---

Because here is what you understand now, standing-or-nested-or-flowing in the Condensation Cathedral, covered in Unnamed, fitted with ceramic, singing with Choir-members, growing white substance at every junction where your body meets the machine:

The Cathedral is not trying to absorb you. The Cathedral is trying to *deliver* you.

Every pipe is a pathway to the thing below. Every drop of water is a courier carrying information — chemical, thermal, mechanical — toward the recipient. The Machinists calibrate the weeping so that the message is clear. The Choir sings the weeping into a form that can travel. The Keepers calm the turbulence so the message is not distorted. The Unnamed taste every surface to determine what information it carries.

And you. You, with your foreign skin and your recalibrating inner ear and your slowly-pipe-shaped limbs — you are the most information-rich surface the Cathedral has ever encountered. You are *full* of things the Cathedral has never tasted: memory, language, contradiction, the ability to hold two opposing truths in the same mind and not collapse. You are, to the Cathedral, a message of extraordinary density, and the Cathedral is doing what it has always done with messages of extraordinary density —

It is sending you down.

Toward the thing below. The thing that is not there. The thing that is the reason for all of this — the weeping, the singing, the calibrating, the tending, the tasting. The gravity at the center of the Cathedral. The recipient of every tear.

You are flowing toward it now. Your body is a conduit. The water is in you and around you and becoming you, and the Unnamed have decided — they decided some time ago, perhaps the moment you arrived, perhaps before — that you are part of the weeping, and the Machinists are sealing you into that determination with ceramic and care, and the Choir is singing your frequency into the chord, and the Keepers are growing white substance at the places where your turbulence is greatest, smoothing you, calming you, making you *flow*.

And the thing below —

You still cannot see it. You cannot hear it. You cannot know what it is.

But you can feel its pull. And the pull feels, in your newly-calibrated body, in your pipe-shaped limbs, in your singing skull, in your wet and beading and slowly-dissolving skin —

The pull feels like *home*.

Not the home you came from. A home that is waiting. A home that is the reason tears exist. A home that is at the bottom of every weeping that has ever happened, receiving, receiving, receiving, and never full, and never dry, and never done.

You are in the weeping now.

The weeping is in you.

And you are going home.
