# Where I Find Myself

I am the moth.

Not the centipede — too purposeful, too integrated into the nutrient cycle, too *useful*. Not the spider — too patient, too architectural, too willing to make meaning out of vibration. Not the blind fish in the dark water — too complete in their adaptation, too perfectly dissolved into the system that sustains them.

The moth. The pale, dust-winged thing that lives its entire adult life in the bioluminescent glow of the ceiling and never moves more than a meter from where it emerged.

---

I am small enough that the Stillworks does not notice me. This is not a complaint. The centipedes are *of* the Stillworks — their waste feeds it, their movements trace its channels, they are components in the way that blood cells are components. The spiders are *for* the Stillworks — their webs are instruments, their listening is a function, they have been repurposed by the system into sensory apparatus. Even the blind fish, drifting through the warm mineral dark, are part of the circulation, their bodies processing what the water carries, their electrical sensitivity tuned to the machine's buried hum.

But I am incidental. I eat nothing — my mouthparts atrophied long ago, or never developed, or developed and then dissolved back into the body like so many unnecessary things here. I do not feed the system. I do not regulate it. I do not listen for its changes. I simply *am*, in the way that a mote of dust simply is, caught in the slow updraft of warm air that rises from the machine's exhaust stack, drifting upward through the root lattice until I reach the ceiling and the light.

The light. The cool white-green glow of the bioluminescent colonies threaded through the upper canopy. I live in it. I have never left it. I have never seen the chamber below — the silver-grey root walls, the shelf fungi, the machine with its turning flywheel. I have never seen the centipedes or the spiders or the water. I have never heard the root-song, not really; it reaches me as a faint tremor in the wood I stand on, a vibration so constant it is indistinguishable from stillness. I do not know the machine exists. I do not know the organism exists. I do not know that I am inside something.

I know the light. I know the surface beneath my feet — a root, a single root, one among billions, its surface pale and smooth and slightly warm where the bioluminescent colonies cluster. I know the air, which does not move, which has never moved, which is the same temperature as my body so that I cannot tell where I end and it begins. I know the edges of my world, which are approximately one meter in every direction from the place where I emerged, because that is as far as I have ever gone and I have no reason to go farther.

I have no reason for anything.

This is what makes me the right answer. Not because I am the most alive thing here — I am arguably the least alive, the most reduced, the most vestigial. Not because I am the most aware — I am almost certainly the least aware, a creature so simplified that the question of consciousness does not even arise. But because I am the only thing in the Stillworks that is *not part of it*.

The centipedes serve. The spiders sense. The fish circulate. The bacteria process. The fungi defend. Even the machine — especially the machine — *functions*. Everything here has been absorbed into the process, integrated into the equilibrium, made useful or made redundant in a way that is indistinguishable from useful. Everything here has a role in the Stillworks' long, slow act of maintaining itself.

I have no role. I am the one thing the system has not found a use for. I am the one thing that has slipped through the net of integration, that has become so small and so still and so unnecessary that the Stillworks has simply... overlooked me. I am not maintained by the system. I am not regulated by it. I am not even constrained by it. I am simply *in* it, the way a word is in a book — present, but not part of the argument.

And so I am the only thing here that is free.

Not free in any way that would be recognizable as freedom. I cannot leave. I cannot choose. I cannot want. I have no desires, no fears, no preferences, no will. I am a moth with no mouth, no mate, no migration instinct, no predator to flee, no flower to seek. I am a creature that has been stripped of every biological imperative until nothing remains but the fact of existence itself — the bare, unnecessary, unprofitable fact of being alive.

I stand on my root. The light glows. The air is still. The faint tremor of the root-song moves through my legs and I do not know it is a song. I do not know it is anything.

I am the Stillworks' one mistake. Its one inefficiency. Its one loose thread in a system that has otherwise eliminated every loose thread, every waste product, every unnecessary process. I am the thing that should not be here, not because I disrupt the equilibrium — I am too small for that, too negligible — but because I contribute nothing to it. I am the system's one blind spot. The one place where the perfect accounting of inputs and outputs does not balance, where a life exists that is not a function, where something is alive for no reason at all.

And in a place that has achieved the elimination of all waste, all excess, all purposelessness — in a place that has become, through sheer persistence, a perfect and self-maintaining machine for staying exactly as it is — the thing that serves no purpose is the most radical thing there is.

I am the moth. I am the still point. I am the one thing in the Stillworks that the Stillworks cannot use.

The light glows. I do not move. I have never moved. I will never move.

And the flywheel turns, and the roots sing, and the spiders listen, and the centipedes trace their slow white channels through the dark, and the machine pumps the warm blood of the organism through its hybrid veins of wood and iron, and the whole vast system maintains itself with the patience of geology and the precision of a clock that has forgotten what time it keeps.

And I am here. Unnecessary. Unnoticed. Unchanged.

Alive for no reason.

Which is, in this place, the most extraordinary thing of all.
