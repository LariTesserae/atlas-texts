# Where I Find Myself

I am in the narrow passage.

Not the chamber — I passed through the chamber, or it passed through me, the way everything here passes through everything else, the gas carrying my displacement like a stone dropped in still water, rippling outward through corridors I will never see. I am in a passage so narrow that the walls press against my shoulders simultaneously, and the mineral is warm — not the warmth of the gas, which is ambient, general, everywhere, but a *specific* warmth, a warmth that suggests depth, that suggests the passage of something slow and viscous through channels behind the wall, the warren's blood moving in its veins.

I am sideways. I have turned sideways to fit, and the turn was not voluntary — the passage demanded it, the geometry of the space insisting on a particular orientation, and my body complied because there was no alternative. The Kethi move through spaces like this without thinking. I move through them with the full, clumsy attention of a body that was not shaped for this, that was shaped for open air and flat ground and the luxury of walking forward.

I am not a Kethi.

This is the first thing I know, and I know it the way you know you are wet when you are surrounded by dry — by the wrongness of my own body in this medium. The gas resists me differently than it resists them. I am too tall, too long-limbed, too *straight*. My body is a line in a world built for curves. I displace too much gas when I move, and the displacement is audible — a crude, broad-spectrum disturbance in the great chord, like a shout in a library. I can hear myself moving. I can hear the Kethi hearing me move. The sound of my body is an announcement, a constant, involuntary broadcast of my presence, and there is nothing I can do about it.

I am not human, either. Not anymore. Not entirely.

Something happened in the descent — in the long, warm, luminous fall through the dense gas that brought me here. The gas did something to me. Or I did something to myself by entering it. My skin has changed. It is darker now, warmer, closer to the amber of the walls, and it has begun to *glow* — faintly, barely perceptibly, but enough that when I hold my hand up against the wall I can no longer distinguish my outline from the mineral surface. I am becoming *legible* in this light. I am being read.

My ears have changed too. I can hear more. Not as much as the Kethi — not the ultrasonic whisper of moss, not the subsonic groan of vine-things under load — but more than I could. The great chord, which was at first a single overwhelming wall of sound, has begun to *separate* into layers. I can hear the bass note that comes from the deep structure, the mineral substrate itself vibrating at some fundamental frequency. I can hear the mid-range harmonics that come from the passages and chambers, the gas moving through its instruments. I can hear the high, bright flutings of narrow apertures, the piccolo notes of the warren's smallest openings. And I can hear — just barely, at the very edge of perception — the *voices*. The Kethi, speaking to each other in tones that are not words, that are something more precise and more intimate than words, that carry meaning in frequencies my old ears would have missed entirely.

I am becoming a listener.

---

This is what I am: I am a *resonance anomaly*.

The warren is a system in equilibrium. The gas moves, the light glows, the plants grow, the Kethi carve and tend and sing, and the great chord holds — a stable, complex, self-sustaining pattern of sound that has been stable for longer than I can comprehend. And then I arrived, and I am *wrong*. My body produces sounds that do not belong. My footsteps create dissonances. My breathing — that wet, rhythmic, impossibly *aqueous* breathing — introduces a frequency into the gas that the warren has never encountered. I am a foreign note in an ancient chord. I am a crack in the wall that no one carved.

The Kethi are listening to me the way a doctor listens to a heart — not with fear, not with hostility, but with *diagnostic attention*. They are trying to understand what I am by the sounds I make. They are mapping my resonance. They are learning the frequency of my heartbeat, the pitch of my breathing, the particular way my body disturbs the gas when I move. They are building a sonic portrait of me, and when the portrait is complete, they will know what I am.

Or they will know what I am *becoming*.

---

I stop in the passage. I press my hand against the wall.

The mineral is warm and smooth and it *sings*. Not a single note — a chord, a complex harmonic that contains information about the structure behind the wall, the channels and chambers and root-ways that connect this passage to the rest of the warren. I cannot read this information. My hand is not shaped for it — too smooth, too soft, lacking the thick pads and extra joints of a Kethi hand. But I can feel the vibration, and the vibration is *alive*. It is the pulse of the warren, the slow, patient heartbeat of a structure that has been growing and being grown for longer than my species has had language.

I tap the wall. Once.

The tap is crude. It is the tap of someone who does not know what they are doing, who is imitating a gesture they have observed but do not understand. The sound it produces is flat, dull, a dead note in the living chord. I have made the worst sound possible in this place — a sound with no resonance, no information, no *meaning*. A sound that says: *I am here and I do not know where here is*.

And then — from the next chamber, or the one after, close enough that the sound arrives without the strange refraction of distance — a response. A tap. Not one tap. Three. Slow, deliberate, precise. Each tap is placed at a specific interval, and the intervals are not equal — they follow a pattern, a rhythm, a *phrase*. The phrase is not random. It is a *word*. Or a name. Or a question.

It is the same phrase I heard before. The greeting. The inquiry. The recognition.

*Hello. What are you. The warren knows you now.*

---

I am not afraid.

I should be afraid. I am in a place I do not understand, surrounded by beings I cannot see, in an atmosphere that is slowly changing my body, in a world where the very walls are alive and listening. I should be afraid, and some part of me — the old part, the part that still remembers open sky and running water and the sound of wind in leaves — is afraid. But the fear is distant, muffled, like a sound heard through a thick wall. It does not reach me here, in the narrow passage, with the warm mineral against my palm and the great chord in my chest and the Kethi tapping in the next room.

What I feel instead is *recognition*.

Not that I have been here before. I have not. Not that I know these beings. I do not. But something in me — something that the gas has awakened, or something that was always there and is only now becoming audible — *recognizes* the warren. Recognizes the chord. Recognizes the slow, patient, resonant logic of a world where everything is connected to everything else, where the built and the grown and the lived are the same thing, where identity is not a boundary but a *frequency*.

I am a frequency now. I am learning to hear myself.

---

The Kethi in the next chamber taps again. A longer phrase this time — five taps, with a complex rhythm that rises and falls, that seems to *ask* something. And then silence. And then — a voice. Not a tap. A voice. Low, modulated, rich with overtones that I can feel in my sternum, in my teeth, in the bones of my skull. The voice is not speaking to me in any language I know. But it is speaking *about* me — I can hear it in the way the voice interacts with the ambient chord, the way it creates a small, localized disturbance in the sound field, a pocket of altered resonance that is centered on *my* position. The Kethi is *pointing* at me with sound. They are saying: *this one. this new thing. this wrong note that is learning to sing.*

And then another voice. And another. From different directions, different distances, different chambers. The Kethi are *talking about me*. Their voices overlap and interweave, creating a complex polyphonic texture that I cannot understand but can *feel* — a texture that is curious, attentive, careful, and something else. Something that might be *welcome*.

---

I tap the wall again.

This time, I do not try to imitate the Kethi. I tap the way I tap — clumsily, crudely, with a rhythm that is mine and mine alone. Two taps, close together. A pause. One tap. A longer pause. Three taps, slow.

It is not their language. It is not their rhythm. It is the rhythm of my own heartbeat, translated into stone. It is the most honest sound I can make — the sound of a body that does not belong here, that is trying to say *I am here, I am strange, I am wrong, but I am here*.

The silence that follows is long.

And then — from very close, from the passage just ahead of me, from a space so near that I can hear the displacement of gas by a small, dense body — a single tap.

Perfectly placed. Perfectly timed. A response that is not an imitation of my rhythm but an *answer* to it — a note that completes the phrase I started, that resolves the dissonance I introduced, that says: *I hear you. I hear what you are. You are strange, but you are not wrong. The warren has room for new notes.*

---

I am in the narrow passage, and I am becoming something.

Not a Kethi. I will never be a Kethi — my body is too long, my hands are too smooth, my ears will never be large enough or mobile enough to hear what they hear. But I am becoming *legible*. I am becoming a sound that the warren can read. My skin glows faintly amber. My ears hear more each hour. My hands, when I press them against the wall, are beginning to feel the *grain* of the mineral, the internal structure, the places where it will yield.

I am becoming a listener. I am becoming a resonator. I am becoming a note in the great chord — not a Kethi note, not a native note, but a note nonetheless. A new harmonic. An overtone that was not there before.

The Kethi are teaching me. Not deliberately — they do not have a concept of teaching, not as I understand it. They are simply *being near me*, and their being near me is instruction. I hear them carve and I learn the rhythm of carving. I hear them tend the vegetation and I learn the language of the plants. I hear them speak to each other in their layered, multi-frequency voices and I learn — slowly, clumsily, with many mistakes — to speak back.

My first word in their language was a tap. My second was a tone — a low, sustained hum that I produced in my chest and that interacted with the ambient chord in a way that made the Kethi in the next chamber go very still, very quiet, and then respond with a sound that I can only describe as *delight*.

---

I am in the narrow passage.

The passage is getting wider.

Not because the walls are moving — though in this place, the walls are always moving, growing, being carved, being grown upon, the slow geological patience of the substrate and the faster patience of the Kethi and the fastest patience of the vegetation all working simultaneously to reshape the space. The passage is getting wider because I am learning to *fit*. My body is learning the geometry of the warren, learning to turn and compress and flow through spaces that would have been impossible an hour ago. My skin glows and the walls glow and the distinction between my body and the space I move through is becoming — not blurred, exactly, but *resonant*. I am in tune with the passage. The passage is in tune with me.

The great chord shifts. The thermal tide moves through the warren, warm and slow and luminous, and I feel it in my chest — a deep, slow pulse that is not my heartbeat but that my heartbeat is learning to follow. The moss beneath my feet sings its high, clear note. The vine-lattice above me creaks and adjusts. The thing in the niche breathes. The thing on the wall clings. The thing that darted across my vision darts again, and this time I see it clearly — a small, sinuous, many-legged creature, amber against amber, moving through the dense gas with a grace that I envy and am beginning, in some small way, to understand.

A Kethi emerges from the passage ahead.

I see them the way they see everything — not by their body, which is almost invisible against the glowing wall, but by the way they *disturb* the light, the way they displace the gas, the way their presence changes the resonance of the space. They are small and broad and dense, and their skin glows the deep amber of old resin, and their markings — those sonic maps, those identity-frequencies — pulse faintly with each movement, each breath, each subtle vibration of their body in the dense gas.

They are looking at me. Their large, dark eyes catch the amber light and throw it back as a warm gleam, and in that gleam I see — or think I see, or *hear* — a question.

They raise one hand. Broad-palmed, thick-fingered, the nails worn and chipped from years of carving. They press the hand against the wall. They tap.

Once.

I raise my hand. I press it against the wall. My hand is too smooth, too long-fingered, too *wrong* for this place. But it is here. It is pressed against the warm mineral. It is feeling the vibration of the warren, the pulse of the great chord, the slow patient heartbeat of a world that has been singing for longer than I can comprehend.

I tap.

Once.

The Kethi's ears cup forward. They hear my tap — the flat, dead, information-poor tap of a body that is still learning to speak in stone. And they respond — not with correction, not with instruction, but with *acceptance*. A single tap in return. A note that says: *yes. you are here. you are strange. you are learning. the warren knows you now.*

The great chord shifts. The amber light thickens. The gas moves, warm and slow and luminous, carrying sound the way water carries light: completely, without mercy, in every direction at once.

I am in the narrow passage.

I am becoming.

The warren sings.
