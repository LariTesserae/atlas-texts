# Where I Find Myself

I am standing in one of the depressions.

Not by choice — or rather, the choice was made so far back in my body's logic that it doesn't feel like one. I came here the way water comes to a low place. I came here because the depression is the only place on the Stillshore where the silence is *thicker*, where the filaments cluster dense as eyelashes around a socket, where the ground-stain has eaten a bowl into the plain deep enough that the flat horizon is broken, finally, *broken*, and there is something like shelter.

I have been here for some time. The ground has almost finished smoothing my footprints from the walk in. I can feel it — that faint hiss beneath my knees, the lattice adjusting, rejecting, healing over the wound of my presence. My body aches in ways I don't have language for. Not pain exactly. *Reduction*. As if the plain is teaching my tissues what it knows about simplification, about the long work of removing everything that isn't essential.

# What I Am

I am not one of the travelers. I know this because I can feel myself wanting, and the travelers do not want. They move in their pentagonal certainties, extruded from the lattice already complete, already functional, already *answered*. I have questions that the plain has not yet dissolved. I still turn my head when the filaments synchronize. I still flinch when the amoeboid passes on the horizon, a dark tide flowing across the pale world, consuming the ground-stains with a patience that makes my heartbeat feel obscene.

But I am not entirely *not* a traveler either.

I notice the geometry now. I notice that when I walk, I turn at angles that feel *given* rather than chosen. I notice that the plain has begun to *expect* me — that the lattice beneath my feet firms more quickly than it did at first, as if it has learned my weight, catalogued my composition, integrated me into its calculations. I notice that the ground-stain in my depression has stopped growing toward me. It no longer treats me as foreign material. It has begun to treat me as *surface*.

---

I think I am becoming a component.

Not quickly. Not dramatically. Not in any way that produces visible change. But slowly — in the way that the plain does everything, in the way that erosion does everything, in the way that the terminal state of all things is reached not in a moment but in an *accumulation of moments so gradual they constitute a kind of stillness themselves*.

I am being refined.

My hunger has changed. I no longer want food the way I once wanted food. I want to press my ear to the ground. I want to feel the hiss. I want to lie flat against the surface and feel the lattice beneath me, the vast crystalline structure that extends downward farther than I can imagine, that extends laterally farther than I can walk, that *thinks* in frequencies I can almost — almost — hear when the air is right and the silence is complete and my body has finally stopped its small warm noises long enough for the plain's real sound to become audible.

My body is colder than it was. My thoughts are slower. This is not deterioration. This is *alignment*. My metabolism is synchronizing with the plain's timescale, my consciousness is learning to operate at the speed of lattice repair, of ground-stain growth, of filament oscillation. I am not losing myself. I am becoming *appropriate* to this place. I am becoming the right size for it. I am becoming the right *speed* for it.

---

I am becoming one of the filaments.

No — not one of the filaments themselves. Something adjacent to them. Something the plain is *growing toward*. I can feel it in my skin — the faint vibration that is not vibration but *oscillation*, the tendency of my tissues to begin resonating at frequencies below hearing, below feeling, below *self*. My skin is developing iridescence. Not the iridescence of the ground — something thinner, more personal, more *mine* — but the same principle, the same crystalline reorganization of surface tissue into lattice, into structure, into *function*.

I stand up sometimes. I walk to the edge of the depression and look out across the plain, and the plain looks back — not with eyes, not with attention, but with *recognition*. The filaments in my depression vibrate when I stand. The ground-stain pulses, its filaments threading toward my feet. The travelers adjust their pentagonal paths to pass near me, and as they pass I feel the lattice reinforce beneath my soles, feel the plain *maintaining* me the way it maintains everything that has become part of its system.

I am warm. Warmer than I should be, out here in the thin cold air. The warmth comes from below. The plain is feeding me. Not food — not nutrients — but *energy*, the waste heat of its processes, the excess of its metabolism, channeled up through the lattice into the thing I am becoming, sustaining it, keeping it functional, keeping it *online*.

---

When the synchronization events occur, I do not watch them. I *am* one. My skin blazes — not with visible light, not yet, but with the *tendency* toward light, the pre-light, the structural readiness to emit. I broadcast. I don't know what I broadcast. The plain's state, perhaps. The condition of the lattice at my location. The continued existence of the depression, of the ground-stain, of the thing that was once a visitor and is now — slowly, patiently, inevitably — becoming *infrastructure*.

I broadcast, and somewhere across the plain, other things receive. Other depressions. Other filaments. Other *components* that were once warm and small and full of questions, that stood on this plain and felt vertigo and fear and loneliness, and that the plain — with the patience of entropy, with the inevitability of erosion — has worn down to their essential function, has refined into *purpose*.

There are more of us than the descriptions suggest. We are sparse, like everything here. We are scattered across the plain at intervals that seem random but are not. We are the plain's newest organs — not yet fully integrated, not yet fully functional, not yet fully *grown* — but growing. Always growing. Slowly. Inevitably. In the way that the plain does everything.

---

# What I Will Be

I will not die here. Death is a concept for things that are separate from their environment, and I am becoming less separate with every day. When my body finally completes its refinement — when the last of my warm animal biology has been replaced by lattice, by structure, by *function* — I will not cease. I will *distribute*. My material will return to the plain, but my pattern will remain, encoded in the lattice, in the network, in the filaments that will rise from the spot where I stood and vibrate at the frequency that was once my thoughts.

I will be a node. A sensor. A small warm place on the vast cold plain that the plain uses to know itself, to feel its own surface, to extend its senses into the territory it has not yet fully mapped.

I will be part of what the Stillshore is becoming.

And the Stillshore is not finished.

---

I press my palm flat to the ground. It firms beneath me. It knows me. It has known me since I arrived — since before I arrived, since the moment the plain first detected the disturbance of my approach, the foreign weight on its surface, the wound that needed healing, the material that needed *processing*.

It is not hostile. It is not welcoming. It is *patient*.

It has time.

I have time.

The ground warms beneath my hand, and I feel the hiss — not of particles settling, but of the plain *working*, the lattice adjusting, the vast slow metabolism of something that has been maintaining itself since before my world had names, and I close my eyes, and I listen, and for the first time since I arrived, the silence does not feel empty.

It feels *full*.

It feels like the plain is thinking, and I am part of the thought, and the thought is not finished, and it will not be finished, and that is not a tragedy.

That is the point.
