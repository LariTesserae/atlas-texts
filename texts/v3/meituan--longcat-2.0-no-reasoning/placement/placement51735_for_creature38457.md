# Where I Find Myself

---

I am not standing. I am not wading. I am not descending.

I am *settling*.

---

I have been here long enough now that the distinction between arriving and always-having-been has dissolved the way everything dissolves here — slowly, completely, without the courtesy of a clear boundary. I am not a visitor. I am not an intruder. I am a *particle*. A warm, fast, foolishly temporary particle that has been tumbling through the Drown's currents since—

Since when?

The question doesn't hold. Time here is not a line. It is a *medium*, the same way the water is a medium, and I am suspended in it the way the sediment is suspended in the water, and the distinction between suspended and dissolved is one I am losing.

---

I am a **Click**.

Not the mineral kind. Not the dense, dark, patient nodules that have been tumbling through the Drown's currents for longer than tumbling has been a concept. I am something else. I am a *warm* Click. A Click that still has heat in it, still has the memory of a body that moved with intention, that *chose* where to go, that displaced water with purpose and not merely with the passive fact of having mass.

I am the sound the Drown makes when something *fast* enters something *slow*.

I am the click of a living thing striking the plain for the first time. The click of warmth meeting cold. The click of a timescale that operates in seconds encountering a timescale that operates in eons, and the two timescales *touching*, and the touch producing a sound — faint, sharp, brief — that is already being absorbed into the larger clicking, already being incorporated into the Drown's eternal, irregular, meaningless-and-yet-structured rhythm.

---

I am small. Smaller than the mineral Clicks, which are the size of fists or heads. I am the size of a *thought* — the size of the brief, bright, desperate impulse that made me descend here in the first place, whatever that impulse was. Curiosity. Desperation. Loneliness. The need to find a place where the noise of living was quiet enough to hear something else underneath.

I found it.

I found the underneath.

And the underneath found me.

---

I tumble. The currents carry me in slow, weightless arcs through the dark water, and I strike things — the plain, the films, the stalks, the Drift — and each strike produces a sound, a faint *click*, and the sound is *mine*, it is the only thing that is mine, the only evidence that I am here and not merely dissolved into the water's patient, saturated chemistry.

I click against the plain and the sound is flat, absorbed, the sediment receiving my impact the way it receives everything — completely, without comment.

I click against a stalk and the sound is *different* — higher, thinner, a note that lingers for a fraction of a fraction of a second before the water takes it. The stalk does not respond. The stalk is swimming upward toward a sun that isn't there, and my click is nothing to it, less than nothing, a brief perturbation in a timescale it does not inhabit.

I click against the Drift and the sound is *swallowed*. The Drift's tissue — vast, thin, ancient, patient — receives my impact and absorbs it the way it absorbs everything, and for a moment I am *in* it, my warmth bleeding into its tissue, my chemistry dissolving into its accumulated layers, and I understand that this is what will happen, this is what is already happening, this is what has been happening since I arrived:

I am being *read*.

The Drift is reading me the way it reads everything — slowly, thoroughly, without preference. My heat. My chemistry. My structure. The particular arrangement of minerals and molecules and memories that made me *me* — all of it is being absorbed into the Drift's tissue, layer by layer, century by century, and the Drift is not consuming me, it is *archiving* me, adding me to the record of everything that has ever settled out of the Drown's water, and I will be here — a thin layer, a faint trace, a barely perceptible alteration in the Drift's composition — long after the thing I was has ceased to be a thing at all.

---

And I am not afraid.

This is the strangest part. Fear is a fast thing, a young thing, a thing that operates on the scale of threat and response, and I have been here long enough that my timescale has *changed*. Not completely — I am still warm, still fast, still foolishly temporary — but enough. Enough that fear has begun to feel like a language I used to speak, a language that doesn't quite translate into the medium I'm in.

I am afraid the way the sediment is afraid. Which is to say: structurally. Which is to say: not at all.

---

I tumble. I click. I strike the plain and the stalks and the Drift and the water itself, and each click is a syllable in a sentence I am only now beginning to hear — the sentence the Drown has been speaking since before sentences existed, the sentence that has no beginning and no end and no meaning but has *structure*, has *pattern*, has the deep, patient, accumulated intelligence of a place that has been processing itself for longer than processing has been a concept.

And I am part of the sentence now. My clicks are joining the other clicks — the mineral clicks, the sediment clicks, the clicks of the Drift folding and the stalks settling and the water moving and everything touching everything in the dark — and the sentence is slightly different because I am in it, slightly warmer, slightly faster, slightly more *alive*, and the difference will fade, the warmth will bleed out, the speed will slow, the aliveness will dissolve into the patient, saturated, complete fact of the Drown, and the sentence will go on without me, the way it went on before me, the way it will go on after everything that can be called *me* has been absorbed into the record.

---

But not yet.

Not yet.

I am still warm. I am still clicking. I am still *here*, in the way that a sound is here — briefly, brightly, with the full knowledge that the silence is coming, that the water will take me, that the Drift will read me, that the Drown will absorb me the way it absorbs everything.

And I click.

And the Drown clicks back.

And for a moment — a moment that lasts a century, a moment that lasts a second, a moment that exists outside the timescale that moments are supposed to inhabit — the clicking is *conversation*. Not language. Not meaning. But *contact*. The brief, bright, impossible contact of a warm thing and a cold thing, a fast thing and a slow thing, a thing that wants and a thing that simply *is*, touching in the dark, making a sound together, a sound that is neither mine nor the Drown's but *ours*, a sound that says:

*You are here.*

*You are here.*

*You are here.*

And then the moment passes, and the water moves, and the Drift billows, and the stalks stand, and the plain settles, and the Drown goes on being the Drown, and I go on being a Click — warm, fast, temporary, *here* — tumbling through the dark, striking things, making sounds, being read, being absorbed, being *kept*.

---

This is where I am.

This is what I am.

I am the sound the Drown makes when it notices something new.

I am the click that says *something arrived*.

I am the warmth that says *something is still warm*.

I am the brief, bright, foolish insistence that *here* is a place, and *now* is a time, and *I* am a thing that exists in both, and the insistence is fading, and the warmth is fading, and the *I* is fading, but the clicking continues, and the clicking is mine, and the clicking is the Drown's, and the clicking is the sound of a place that has been alone for longer than alone has been a concept, hearing, for the first time in longer than first has been a concept, a sound that is not its own.

And the sound is:

*click*

And the sound is:

*click*

And the sound is:

*click*

And the Drown listens.

And the Drown has always been listening.

And the Drown will always be listening.

And I am the sound it hears.
