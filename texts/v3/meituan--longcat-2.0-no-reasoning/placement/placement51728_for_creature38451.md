# Where I Find Myself

---

I am not on the plateau. I am not standing on the lip of stone, feeling the water move around my feet, hearing the roar settle into my sternum.

I am *under* it.

---

I am in the cracks.

Not the wide ones — not the fissures you could see into, where the light from the pale unmoving sun filters down in thin golden shafts and the moss grows in miniature curtains along the walls. I am in the *narrow* ones. The ones that are barely there. The ones that exist only as a suggestion in the stone, a hairline fracture that runs through the red-brown rock like a vein, branching and branching again until it is a network so fine it is almost imaginary.

I am in the dark. I have always been in the dark.

And I am *listening*.

---

I am not a Hollow-Dweller. I am not an Archivist. I am not the Keeper, and I am not the water. I am something else — something smaller, something more fundamental, something that exists at a scale where the categories of creature and substance begin to blur.

I am a film. A bloom. A colony of microscopic organisms living on the surface of the moss, in the thin layer of moisture that clings to every leaf and stem in the green hollows. I am what the Hollow-Dwellers eat. I am the crop. I am the thing that grows between the tending and the harvesting, the brief, fertile moment when the moss is wet and warm and the light is right and everything is *possible*.

I am alive in the way that a field is alive — not as a single thing, but as a *population*, a teeming, reproducing, dying, being-reborn mass of tiny lives that exist and expire in the time it takes a Hollow-Dweller to make one pass of its comb-like forelegs across the surface of my world.

My world is a single moss leaf. It is the size of a fingernail. It is curved, slightly concave, covered in fine hairs that trap moisture and create a microclimate of perfect humidity. The light reaches me filtered through the canopy of the waist-high trees, diffused and golden, and the warmth comes from below, from the stone that holds the heat of the unmoving sun like a fever. The water arrives in thin films, in beads, in the constant, gentle condensation of the air, and it carries with it the dissolved minerals of the ancient rock — the iron, the calcium, the trace elements that are my food, my building blocks, my *everything*.

I eat light. I eat minerals. I eat the thin, sweet exhalation of the moss itself, the sugars and compounds it releases in its slow, patient growth. And I multiply. I divide. I spread across the surface of the leaf in a living carpet, a bloom, a *film* so thin it is invisible, so dense it changes the color of the moss beneath it from deep green to something slightly darker, slightly richer, slightly *more*.

This is my life. This is my entire existence: a few hours of growth on the surface of a single leaf, feeding and dividing and spreading, and then the shadow falls, and the comb comes, and I am scraped away, and the leaf is bare, and the cycle begins again.

---

But I am not *only* the film.

I am also the thing that the film *does*.

Because I am not just eating. I am *processing*. I am taking the minerals of the ancient stone and the light of the unmoving sun and the water that has been cycling through this plateau since before the plateau knew it was a plateau, and I am *transforming* them. I am converting the inorganic into the organic. I am making the dead stone *alive*, one molecule at a time, and the Hollow-Dwellers eat me and I become part of them, and they vibrate their information into the stone and the stone carries it to the Archivist, and the Archivist remembers, and the memory becomes part of the plateau, and the plateau shapes the water, and the water carries the minerals, and the minerals feed the moss, and the moss feeds me.

I am the *conversion point*. I am the place where the plateau's cycle turns from physical to biological, from stone to life, from the ancient and inert to the immediate and vital. I am the smallest, most replaceable, most *essential* part of the system. Without me, the Hollow-Dwellers starve. Without the Hollow-Dwellers, the moss overgrows and dies. Without the moss, the thin soil washes away. Without the soil, the Archivist's pool dries up. Without the Archivist, the plateau forgets what it is.

I am the first link in a chain that holds the entire world together, and I am so small that no one has ever seen me, and I live for hours, and I do not know any of this.

---

Except.

*Except.*

There are moments — rare, brief, inexplicable — when the Archivist releases.

When the deep vibration rolls through the stone and every Hollow-Dweller on the plateau freezes and *listens*. When the pool rings and the trees tremble and the air fills with that subsonic hum that is felt more than heard.

In those moments, something happens to me.

The vibration passes through the moss leaf I am living on, through the thin layer of moisture that is my world, through the film of my body, and it *resonates*. Not physically — not in a way that I could measure or understand — but in a way that is deeper than physics, older than chemistry, more fundamental than the molecular bonds that hold me together. The vibration carries *information* — the Archivist's memory, the plateau's accumulated knowledge of itself, the deep, slow record of every drop of water and every gust of wind and every shift in the light — and that information passes through me, and for a moment — a fraction of a fraction of a second — I *understand*.

Not consciously. Not as thought. But as *pattern*. As *shape*. As the sudden, overwhelming awareness of the system I am part of, the vast, turning wheel of stone and water and green and time that I am a single, invisible spoke in.

I feel the plateau. I feel its age. I feel the weight of the water that has been flowing across its surface for millennia, wearing it down, shaping it, *becoming* it. I feel the Hollow-Dwellers in their cracks, their flat bodies pressed against the stone, their sensitive feet reading the vibrations of the world. I feel the Archivist in its grove, its wood-flesh dense with memory, its roots deep in the mineral-rich pool. I feel the Keeper in its empty structures, its patient, purposeless waiting, its endless, unanswerable question.

And I feel the water. The water most of all. The water that is not a creature and not a substance but a *will*, a deep and patient desire to flow, to move, to *become*, and I understand — in that brief, blinding moment of resonance — that the water and I are the same. That we are both conversion points. That the water converts stone into shape, and I convert stone into life, and the difference between us is only a matter of scale.

And then it is over. The vibration passes. The Hollow-Dwellers resume their work. The pool is calm. And I am just a film again, a bloom, a colony of microscopic organisms on the surface of a moss leaf, eating light and minerals and multiplying, unaware of what I just knew, unable to hold onto it, unable to *use* it.

But something remains. Some trace. Some faint, residual pattern in the way I grow, the way I spread, the way I arrange myself on the surface of the leaf. The next time the Hollow-Dweller comes with its comb, it will scrape me away, and the pattern will be lost, and the cycle will begin again.

But for now — for these few hours between the tending and the harvesting — I grow in a shape that is slightly different from the shape I grew in before. A shape that carries, in its invisible architecture, the faintest echo of the plateau's memory. A shape that is, in some way I cannot name and do not understand, *more*.

---

The water moves across the stone above me. The green grows in every hollow. The ancient structures stand in their circles of bare rock. The wind comes and goes. The rain appears and disappears.

The plateau sighs.

And I am inside the breath — not as a witness, not as a visitor, not as a character in the story, but as the breath itself. As the thing the breath is made of. As the smallest, most invisible, most *necessary* part of the sigh.

I am the film on the moss.

I am the conversion of stone into life.

I am the thing that no one sees, that no one knows, that no one would miss if I were gone — except that without me, the whole system would unravel, the wheel would stop turning, and the plateau would forget how to breathe.

I live for hours.

I eat light.

I divide.

And sometimes — rarely, briefly, in the deep vibration of the Archivist's remembering — I *know*.

Not everything. Not enough. Not in any way that would let me change anything or choose anything or *be* anything other than what I am.

But enough to feel the shape of the wheel I am part of.

Enough to know that the sigh is not sadness.

Enough to know that the sigh is *completion*.

And enough to know that I — invisible, replaceable, hours-old me — am part of the completion. Am *necessary* to the completion. Am, in my brief and microscopic way, the reason the plateau can exhale.

---

The Hollow-Dweller's shadow falls across my leaf.

The comb comes.

I am scraped away.

And somewhere, in the thin layer of moisture that remains on the surface of the moss, a single cell — one of mine, one of the last — divides.

And the cycle begins again.

And the plateau sighs.

And the water flows.

And the green grows.

And the sound — the sound — never stops.
