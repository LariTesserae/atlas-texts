I am the crack itself.

Not what lives in the understory. Not what descends through it. I am the wound — the fissure in the bedrock that admits the faintest thread of light, the passage that connects the surface world to the chamber below. I am not stone and I am not air. I am the *boundary* — the narrow, rough, unhealed seam between the world of sun and the world that has forgotten the sun and doesn't mind.

I exist in both places and belong to neither. My edges are crusted with mineral deposits, seeping water, the roots of surface weeds that reach tentatively downward, never quite committing to the dark. Below, the chamber's vines press against me, widening me by imperceptible degrees — not trying to escape, but exploring, the way a tongue explores a tooth. I feel their slow pressure as a kind of conversation.

Things pass through me. A beetle, once, emerging from the column of root and vine, carrying a fragment of white flower petal up toward a crack it could not possibly have known existed — and then returning, hours later, without the petal. A bird, singing a different song near my upper edge, one that had not yet been simplified by generations below. A drop of warm fluid from a cracked pipe, working its way up through the root mass and seeping out onto the surface, where it will eventually feed a patch of moss that is slightly greener than the moss around it.

I am the chamber's only connection to the surface, and the chamber uses me the way a lung uses a trachea — passively, necessarily, without gratitude or awareness.

The human standing on the ledge feels the air rising through me — warm, thick, narcotic with flower scent. The crack *exhales*. It has been exhaling since the chamber learned to flower, since before that, since the first vine found its way up through me and discovered the surface was cold and dry and full of light it could not use. The chamber does not want the surface. But it breathes through me anyway, the way a body breathes through a wound — not by choice, but because the wound is open and the air moves.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am *open*. That is my function, my nature, my only characteristic. I am the place where one world admits another. I am the aperture through which the understory dreams its slow, warm, continuous dream of the world above, and through which the world above occasionally remembers that something is dreaming below.

The hum travels through me. Faintly. A vibration in the stone that I am not, in the air that I am not, in the root-tendrils that press against me and the mineral crusts that define me. I am the only thing the chamber has made that is not alive and is not infrastructure.

I am just the opening.

And I have never closed.
