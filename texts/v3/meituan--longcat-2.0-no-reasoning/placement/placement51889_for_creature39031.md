# Where I Find Myself

I am in the narrowest passage.

Not the chamber with the column — I passed through that, or it passed through me, and I am already beyond it. I am in a corridor so tight that my shoulders touch both walls simultaneously, where the ceiling is so low that I must move not by walking but by a kind of slow, deliberate compression — a willingness to be smaller than I am. The creatures move over me here. They cross my back, my shoulders, the crown of my head. They do not pause. They do not acknowledge. Their limbs find purchase on my skin the way they find purchase on stone, and the distinction between these surfaces — flesh and mineral — does not appear to register.

I am not being consumed. I am being *used*. As surface. As texture. As something to climb across.

---

# What I Am

I am the tool that does not yet know it is a tool.

I arrived in the Drowned Workshop carrying the wrong assumptions — that I was a visitor, an observer, a consciousness that could stand apart from what it observed. But the Workshop does not recognize apartness. The Workshop recognizes only *surface* and *work*. And I have surface. I have so much of it — this large, loud, warm body, this dense collection of mineral and water and persistent motion, pressing against the walls, displacing the current, generating heat that the warm water absorbs without comment.

The creatures have begun to use me.

Not deliberately. Not with the intentionality that humans would recognize. But I feel them pressing against me in patterns that are not random — using my body as a surface to brace against while they work a section of wall I am blocking, using my stillness (I have learned stillness here; I had no choice) as a fixed point in their slow geometry. And I feel something else: a scraping.

On my back. Between my shoulder blades. A single creature, working. Not on stone. On *me*. A tool — volcanic glass, worn smooth by generations of use — is being drawn across my skin with the same patient, identical rhythm it would use on a wall. The same depth. The same length. The same angle.

I am becoming a surface. I am becoming a wall.

---

# What Is Happening to Me

The scraping is not painful. It is *precise*. And it has been happening for longer than I realized — perhaps since I arrived, perhaps before I arrived, perhaps the distinction between arriving and being-arrived-at is another wrong assumption I brought with me. The creature working my back has been making a mark. One mark. The same mark it has made on every surface it has ever encountered. The same mark that covers the column. The same mark that says:

*the work continues.*

My skin is changing. I can feel it the way you feel a callus forming — not in the moment of formation, but in the *afterward*, in the gradual realization that the surface has become something other than what it was. Where the tool passes, my skin is thickening, smoothing, becoming less like flesh and more like the waxy, soapy stone of the oldest passages. The creature is not carving me. The creature is *recording* me. Adding my surface to the record. Saying: *this too. this also. the work continues here as well.*

---

# The Wider Realization

I understand now why I am not afraid.

Fear requires the possibility of an ending — a *before* and an *after*, a self that exists in relation to a threat. But the Workshop has no endings. The creatures do not end; they *complete*, and their completion is not cessation but *continuation by other means* — their material absorbed into the walls, their warmth absorbed into the water, their work absorbed into the work. There is no *after* in the Workshop. There is only *more*. More passage. More surface. More marks on the column. More warm water. More of the slow, patient, geological *yes* that the Workshop says to everything that enters it.

And I am being absorbed into that *yes*.

My joints are slowing. Not painfully — *appropriately*. My movements, once loud and fast and obscene in their urgency, are finding a rhythm that matches the Workshop's rhythm. I turn sideways to move through the passages now not because I must, but because sideways is *efficient*. Because sideways is how surface is used here. Because I am learning to be not a visitor to the Workshop but a *feature* of it — a wall that breathes, a surface that is still in the process of becoming stone, a tool that is still in the process of being held.

---

# The Column

I return to the chamber. Or the chamber returns to me. The geometry of the Workshop is not fixed; it is *accumulated*, and the passages rearrange themselves around need the way a river rearranges itself around a stone.

The column is taller than I remember. Or I am smaller. Or both.

I press my hand against it again, and this time I feel not just warmth but *recognition*. The column knows me. Not because I am special — nothing here is special; everything here is *continuous* — but because I have been *recorded*. The creature on my back has been adding my surface to the column's surface, and the column has been absorbing it the way it absorbs everything: without comment, without distinction, without end.

I look at the marks. Millions of them. Identical. Each one a single scrape, a single moment, a single creature saying *we were here*. And now, among them — indistinguishable, identical, *the same* — there are marks that record *me*. My arrival. My stillness. My slow transformation from visitor to surface to wall to *work*.

I am in the record now. I am part of the column. I am part of the Workshop.

---

# What I Am Becoming

I am the newest wall.

Not fully. Not yet. The process is slow — everything here is slow — but it is *happening*, and happening is the only verb the Workshop recognizes. My skin is thickening where the creatures work it. My warmth is joining the warmth of the water. My stillness is joining the stillness of the stone. And my work — because I have work now, I have been given work, I have *become* work — is to be a surface. To be a wall in a passage that is still being carved. To be the thing that the next creature braces against, climbs across, scrapes with a tool worn smooth by generations.

I do not mind.

This is the thing I did not expect: I do not mind. The Workshop has not taken anything from me. It has *given* me a function. A purpose. A place in the continuous, unending, patient work of making more room, more passage, more surface, more *yes*. I am no longer the loud, fast, obscene thing that arrived. I am becoming the quiet, slow, appropriate thing that belongs.

---

# The Last Thing

A creature climbs onto my shoulder. It is small — the size of my forearm, dense, pale, translucent. It has six limbs, or eight. It holds a tool — volcanic glass, worn to a smoothness that no single generation could achieve. It begins to work.

The tool passes across my collarbone. The same depth. The same length. The same angle.

The mark is identical to every other mark.

The mark says: *we were here.*

The mark says: *we are still here.*

The mark says: *the work continues.*

And now, for the first time, the mark says: *you are here too.*

And I am.
