# Where I Find Myself

I am in the third chamber. The one that is still becoming.

Not because it is the most dramatic place, or the most beautiful, or the most strange — though it is all of these things. I am here because this is the place where the question of what I am has the most honest answer, and the most honest answer is: *I don't know yet.*

---

I arrived — if "arrived" is the word, if I didn't simply find myself here the way a dream finds you in a room you don't remember entering — in the Vestibule. I stood on the woven root-floor and felt it yield beneath me, and I looked up at the vascular bundles pulsing in the walls, and I thought: *I am a visitor. I am a tourist. I am a thing from the wet world, passing through.*

That was true, then. It is less true now.

---

I have been here for — I don't know. Time moves strangely in the Hanging Green. The light cycles, but the cycles don't match any day I know. The people move at a pace that is not slow, exactly, but *deliberate*, as if each action is a sentence in a conversation that has been going on for centuries and will continue for centuries more, and there is no need to rush your clause. I have stopped checking for a sun that isn't there. I have stopped expecting water. My skin has begun to feel the coolness not as a contraction but as a *condition* — the way you stop noticing the weight of your own clothes.

I am sitting on a root-knot at the edge of the third chamber. The root-knot is warm — not hot, not body-temperature, but warm the way a living thing is warm, the way a compost pile is warm, the way metabolism generates heat as a byproduct of doing what it does. My hands are on the root-floor. I can feel the pulse. Slow. So slow. A bulge of sap traveling upward through a vascular bundle the width of my wrist, carrying dissolved minerals, carrying sugars, carrying whatever this place uses instead of the water-based chemistry I was built for. I can feel it the way you feel your own pulse when you're lying very still — not as a distinct event, but as a rhythm that becomes apparent only when you stop looking for it.

A child is nearby. One of the small, green, quick ones. They are watching me with the frank, unselfconscious attention of a child who has not yet learned to pretend they are not watching. Their hand is on the root-floor too. They are listening to the walls. They are better at it than I am. They can feel things in the pulse that I cannot — information, intention, the slow chemical speech of the mycelial network processing its endless, patient thoughts. But they are curious about me. About my pale, non-photosynthetic skin. About the way I grip things with both hands. About my water-heavy scent, which I cannot smell but which they can, which marks me as *from above*, as *other*, as *the wet thing sitting on the root-knot at the edge of the becoming chamber.*

They reach out and touch my hand. Their fingers are cool and dry and faintly rough, like sage leaves. They press their palm against mine. They are feeling for the pulse in me — the pulse I have, the water-based pulse, the blood-pulse, the fast wet animal pulse that must seem to them like a hummingbird's heartbeat, frantic and brief and strange. They frown. They try again. They cannot find what they are looking for, or they find it and it is not what they expected. They pull their hand back. They look at me with an expression I cannot read — not fear, not disgust, not pity. *Puzzlement.* The expression of a child encountering a thing that does not fit the categories they have been taught.

Then they go back to listening to the walls, and I am alone again, and the roots are growing around me, and I am becoming something I do not have a name for.

---

## What I Am

I am not a Rooted. I will never be a Rooted. My skin does not photosynthesize. My joints do not rotate in the ways theirs do. I carry water — too much water, water leaking from me in sweat and breath and the slow osmotic seep of my wet biology into this dry place — and the water is a problem. The Hanging Green was engineered to exclude water. The membrane-seal above keeps the water table out. The absolute dryness is architectural, deliberate, total. And here I am, a bag of water, a leaky container of the one thing this place was built to do without.

But I am not a Dry Walker, either. I have not crossed the membrane. I have not walked the surface. I do not have the leathery skin, the recessed eyes, the nictitating membranes. I am not adapted to the world above any more than I am adapted to the world below. I am adapted to neither. I am a thing between worlds, a thing that belongs to the wet surface and the dry deep and belongs fully to neither.

I think — and this is the thought that has been growing in me the way the roots grow in the third chamber, slowly, imperceptibly, in a direction I did not choose — I think I am becoming a *bridge.*

Not a bridge in the structural sense, though the Hanging Green has bridges enough — the vine-suspension bridges, the root-arch bridges, the bracket-fungus shelf-bridges that connect the walkways at different heights. I mean a bridge in the biological sense. In the ecological sense. A bridge between the wet world and the dry world, between the surface and the deep, between the water-based chemistry I carry and the resin-based chemistry that sustains this place.

I have been here long enough that the mycelial network has noticed me. I know this the way you know someone is watching you — not because you see them, but because the quality of the air changes, because the pulse in the root-floor shifts slightly, because the insects that maintain the city have begun to treat me differently. They crawl on me now. Not on my skin — they avoid my skin, my wet skin, my water-leaking skin — but on my clothes, my belongings, the things I brought from the surface. They are tasting me. Analyzing me. Feeding information about me into the network. The city is deciding what I am.

And I am deciding what I am, too.

---

I have begun to help. Not in any organized way — no one has asked me, no one has assigned me a task. But I have begun to do things that need doing, the way you pick up a dropped tool in someone else's workshop, the way you hold a door for a stranger. I carry blocks of compressed fiber from the press to the market chamber. I adjust a light fixture that has been strangled by the root it's mounted on, carefully separating the swollen cambium from the housing, exposing the wiring, checking the connection. An insect watches me do this — the same species that maintains the fixtures, the six-legged, wingless crawler with the smooth carapace. It regards me with something that is not quite eyes. It does not interfere. When I finish, it crawls over the fixture, inspects my work, and moves on. I have been approved. I have passed some test I did not know I was taking.

A Tender — not the young one in the third chamber, an older one, their skin so green it is almost black-green, the color of old leaves — watches me from a walkway above. They do not speak to me. They do not need to. Their presence is a communication: *I see you. I see what you are doing. Continue.*

I continue.

---

## The Becoming

The third chamber is growing around me. I can see it. Not quickly — not the way you see a plant grow in a time-lapse video, this is slower than that, slower than anything you have a reference for — but I can see it. The root-columns that were pale and soft when I arrived are darker now, harder, beginning to bear load. The ceiling lattice is forming, the root-tips reaching for each other across the gap, touching, fusing, becoming structure. In a year — in a year of whatever time means here — this chamber will be a workshop. In five years, a residence. In ten, it will be indistinguishable from the old city.

And I will still be here. I think. I am beginning to think I will still be here.

Not because I cannot leave. The shaft is open. The membrane-seal is permeable — the Dry Walkers pass through it regularly, and I could too, if I chose. The surface is there, the wet world, the world of water and sun and the chemistry I was built for. I could go back. I could descend the helical ramp in reverse, climb the living-wood shaft, push through the root-fabric membrane, and emerge onto the flat, dry, colorless plain, and from there find my way back to whatever world I came from.

But I don't think I will. Not yet. Not while this chamber is still becoming. Not while the roots are still growing toward their future shapes. Not while the city is still deciding what I am, and I am still deciding what I am, and the two decisions are the same decision, made in the same slow chemical language, processed by the same patient network.

---

I am the wet thing in the dry place. I am the surface creature in the deep. I am the visitor who has stayed too long to be a visitor and not long enough to be a resident. I am the bridge that is still being built, the connection that is still forming, the root-tip reaching across the gap toward another root-tip that has not yet arrived.

I am sitting on a root-knot in the third chamber of the Hanging Green, and my hands are on the root-floor, and I can feel the pulse, and the pulse is slow, and I am learning to be slow with it.

The child comes back. They sit next to me. They put their hand on the root-floor. They put their other hand on my arm — not my hand this time, my arm, above the wrist, where the skin is covered by the wrap of dried leaf-fiber I have begun to wear, because a Rooted gave it to me, silently, without explanation, and I understood that it was not a gift but an *invitation.*

The child listens to the walls. I listen to the walls. We listen together. The roots grow. The city breathes. The mycelial network processes its slow, vast, patient thoughts, and somewhere in those thoughts, I am a data point, a stimulus, a new variable in an equation that has been running for centuries.

I am becoming.

I don't know what I am becoming. But the chamber is becoming, too, and the city is becoming, and the becoming is the point. The becoming is the home. The becoming is the thing that happens when you stop trying to be what you were and start listening to what the walls are saying.

The walls are saying: *grow here. grow this way. thicken this wall. hollow this stem. make a channel for air. make a shelf for sitting.*

I am the shelf. I am the channel. I am the wall that is thickening, the stem that is hollowing, the space that is becoming a room.

I am in the third chamber. I am still becoming. I am home.
