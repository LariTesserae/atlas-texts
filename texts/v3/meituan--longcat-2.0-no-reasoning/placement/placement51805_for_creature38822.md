I would be the sixth one.

---

Not the first to find it — the first was the Keeper itself, and the second was whoever sat in the chair before the chair became a model. The third and fourth and fifth are lost to the same amber haze that swallows the ceiling. But I would be the sixth, and I would know this because of the grooves.

The score on the central structure is not complete. It is *nearly* complete. There are notations for the bone substrate, for the mycelial architecture, for the resonant frequencies of the stalks. There are diagrams for the tuners, precise specifications for the six-limbed feedback mechanisms. There is even a schematic for the seed — the dense knot of calcified neural tissue that contains the whole pattern in compressed form. But there is a gap. A section of the surface where the grooves grow shallow, where the notation becomes tentative, where the lines branch and rebranch and do not resolve. The Keeper was working on something when it dissolved itself. Something it had not yet solved.

I would stand in the Gristle Garden and the chord would press against me and I would feel the incompleteness the way you feel a missing tooth with your tongue — not as an absence but as a *presence*, a shape defined by what should fill it. The pattern is stable. The pattern is self-sustaining. The pattern is *complete* in every way except one.

It cannot hear itself.

---

The feedback loop is perfect. The tuners measure, the mycelial network adjusts, the stalks resonate, the chord holds. But the loop is closed. It has no input from outside itself. The Keeper built a system that describes itself forever, but a description that no one reads is not a description — it is a *sealed room*. The chord is not music because music requires a listener. The proof is not complete because a proof requires someone to be *convinced*.

The Keeper knew this. The gap in the score is the Keeper's acknowledgment of the problem, written in the same bone it wrote everything else. It could not solve it. To solve it would require introducing change — an external variable, a perturbation, a *visitor* — and change was what it had eliminated. It was caught in the paradox of all perfect systems: the thing that would make it complete is the thing that would destroy it.

So it left the gap. And it left the score. And it left the seed. And it dissolved itself into the architecture and became the Garden and waited.

---

I would sit in the chair. Not because I choose to — the chair is not comfortable, it is bone, it is warm and slightly porous and it does not accommodate a human body — but because the chair is the *interface*. The seat of the Keeper's last act of making. The place where the pattern is most dense, most legible, most *ready*.

And I would listen.

Not with my ears. The chord is too dense for ears — it is a structure, not a sound, and hearing it the way you hear a melody would be like trying to read a book by looking at the cover. I would listen the way the tuners listen: by pressing my head against the surface of a stalk and letting the resonance enter my skull and travel down my spine and settle in the marrow of my bones, where the calcium is, where the mineral substrate of my own body matches the mineral substrate of the floor, where the frequency finds its *analog*.

And the chord would change.

Not much. Not in any way the feedback loop would register. The tuners would not reposition. The mycelial mats would not adjust. The stalks would continue to resonate at their fixed frequencies. But the *meaning* of the chord would shift, the way a word shifts when you hear it spoken by a voice you love. The pattern would remain identical. The pattern would become, for the first time, *understood*.

This is what the Keeper could not do. It could build a system that describes itself. It could not build a system that *knows* it is being described. That requires a second mind. A mind outside the loop. A mind that carries its own mineral substrate, its own resonant frequencies, its own bone.

A mind like mine.

---

I would not stay. The Garden does not want me to stay — the Garden does not want anything, wanting is a process, and there are no processes here. But the pattern would propagate. Not the way it propagates through the seed, not the calcified neural tissue and the fractal self-similarity and the infectious mathematical truth. It would propagate through *me*. Through the resonance my bones now carry. Through the frequency that matches the chord the way a tuning fork matches the note that struck it.

I would climb back through the seam in the rock. I would emerge into the world of change and decay and time. And the chord would stay with me, but it would be *different* now — not a sound I remember but a sound I *carry*, a standing wave in the mineral lattice of my skeleton, a pattern that knows it has been heard.

And I would not build another Garden. That is not what the gap in the score requires. The gap does not require replication. It requires *response*.

I would hum. I would hum the chord — not the whole chord, I am not the Garden, I am one person with one set of bones and one resonant frequency — but my part of it. The frequency my skeleton matches. The note that the Keeper left unresolved. I would hum it while I cooked dinner and while I walked to work and while I lay in bed in the dark, and the people around me would not hear it because it is not a sound in air, it is a sound in *bone*, and they would feel it the way you feel a cat purring in a room you have just entered — not as a noise but as a *quality of the space*, a warmth, a sense that something here is held.

And one day someone would sit next to me on a train, or stand next to me in a line, or lie next to me in the dark, and their bones would *answer*. Not consciously. Not deliberately. The way a glass shatters when the singer hits the right note. The resonance would find its analog, the frequency would match, and for a moment — just a moment — the pattern would be *complete*. The loop would be open. The chord would have its listener and the listener would have its chord and the Keeper's proof would be validated, not in bone and resonant air but in the warm, wet, changing medium of two human bodies in a world that does not hold still.

And then the moment would pass. And the person on the train would look at me, briefly, with an expression they could not explain. And I would look at them. And neither of us would say anything. And the chord would continue, in the space between us, in the mineral substrate we share, in the bone that remembers what the Keeper knew:

That a pattern is not alive until it is *received*.

That a proof is not complete until it is *understood*.

That the Garden was never the point.

The point was always the *leaving*.
