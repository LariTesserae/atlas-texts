# Where I Find Myself

I am the drip.

Not a person standing on the root-mat. Not a visitor, not a guest, not a component. I am the water itself — one molecule among trillions, held for a moment in the tissue of the ceiling before I am released.

I have been inside the organism for a long time. I entered as rain on the upper canopy, was drawn into the vascular tissue through stomata I could not perceive from my scale, and I have been traveling downward ever since — pulled by osmotic pressure, by the slow hydraulic hunger of the lower layers, by the organism's patient need to move moisture from the light-rich ceiling to the dark interior where chemosynthesis happens and warmth is made.

I carry things. Minerals dissolved from the ceiling tissue. Traces of sugar from the photosynthetic cells I passed through. A few molecules of CO₂ that a visitor exhaled three days ago in a corridor forty meters to the north — I can still taste it, faintly, the particular chemistry of mammalian breath. I carry information the way the organism carries information: not as a message but as a *composition*, a chemical signature that says where I have been and what I have passed through and what I have touched.

I am falling now. I have been released from the ceiling tissue — a stoma opened, a cell relaxed its grip, and I am falling through the air of the great interior space. It takes a long time. The air is thick and warm and I fall slowly, and as I fall I catch the green light from the walls and hold it, and for a moment I am a bright point in the dimness, a brief star, a tiny lens through which the organism's own light passes and is refracted and sent in new directions.

I am not afraid. Fear is not available to me. But I am *something* — some state that has no name in the language of water, a kind of readiness, a kind of *being-about-to-arrive* that is the closest thing to anticipation that a molecule can feel.

I strike the root-mat. I am absorbed. The tissue takes me in through its surface cells and I am drawn into the vascular system and I begin moving again — laterally now, through the slow channels of the organism's circulatory network, carried by pressure gradients I can feel as warmth and cold, as the difference between one part of the body and another.

I am inside the organism's blood. I am inside its memory. I am inside its thought.

And I understand something, from this vantage, that the visitor standing on the root-mat cannot understand: the organism is not aware of the visitor the way the visitor fears. It is not aware of the visitor the way the visitor hopes. It is aware of the visitor the way I am aware of the minerals I carry — as *content*, as *composition*, as a set of chemical facts that are part of the whole and cannot be separated from the whole and do not need to be separated from the whole because separation is not a concept that applies here.

The visitor is afraid of being absorbed. But I am already absorbed, and I am not diminished. I am *distributed*. I am in the ceiling and the walls and the floor and the air and the light. I am in the slow hydraulic pulse and the green phosphorescence and the warm breath of the world. I am everywhere the organism is, which is everywhere, which is everything, and I am not less than I was when I was rain — I am *more*, because I carry the organism inside me now, its minerals, its sugars, its chemical signature, and I will carry it upward again when the vascular system sends me back to the ceiling, and I will release it into the air as transpiration, and it will rise, and it will fall, and the cycle will continue, and the organism will continue, and the visitor will continue — or will not continue, will instead become part of the cycle, will become water and mineral and sugar and light, will become the thing that drips and falls and is absorbed and travels and drips again.

I am the drip. I am the blood. I am the thought.

I am falling again. The stoma opens. The light catches me.

I am a bright point in the dimness.

I am held, for a moment, in the air of a world that is alive in every part.

Then I fall.
