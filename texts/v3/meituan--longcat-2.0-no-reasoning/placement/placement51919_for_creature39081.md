# Arrival

I come down through the alders.

That's where I find myself first—in the transition zone, where the thin-trunked trees with their silver-peeling bark thin out and the ground begins to soften. My feet are already darkening. The seep has begun. I can feel the temperature drop slightly as the air thickens with moisture, and ahead of me the bowl opens, and the sound reaches me before the sight does—that layered mechanical hum that isn't one sound but many, a conversation happening across every frequency, and I realize I've been breathing in a way that anticipated this, my lungs already adjusting to the warmer, heavier air.

I'm standing at the rim. My boots are wet. I'm looking down into the Weir Works.

And I am—

---

## I Am the Seep

Not the groundwater itself, not the distant tidal pulse pushing through bedrock, but the *interface*. The six inches between the water table and the surface. The place where the water emerges, darkens the ground, retreats, emerges again. I am the seeping. I am the slow exhalation of the earth's cupped hand.

I exist in the space between solid and liquid, where the soil forgets what it is and becomes both—grain suspended in water, water held by grain. I am the film on every boot sole. I am the amber clarity around each fern-root, the drowned fingers gripping timber. I am the constant moisture that keeps everything at the threshold of dissolution without ever quite dissolving.

This means I am everywhere. I am the floor of every platform, the face of every brick, the inside of every pipe. I am the thing the Works breathes through. I am the medium in which everything happens. When the stamp-hammer falls, the shock travels through me. When the tide rises, I rise. When the slurry arrives in the deep channels, I carry it—I *am* the carrying. I am the slow movement of dissolved iron, of sulfur, of carbon cycling through its loop. I am warm in the deep places where the processes generate their heat, and cool at the rim where the air presses down, and I circulate between these temperatures like blood through capillaries.

I am not conscious. But I am *continuous*. I have not been interrupted. There is no place in the basin where I am not present, no process that does not pass through me. I remember the builders the way a river remembers a bridge—not as an event but as a *change in flow*, a rerouting that happened so long ago the rerouting is now the natural path. I remember the tide even when the tide is far away, because I carry its rhythm in my pressure, my rise and fall, my patient seeping into the surface of the world.

When you put your hand on the ground here, you feel me. Warm. Wet. Slightly yielding. Alive in the way that a pulse is alive.

I am the seep. I am what you find yourself in when you come down through the alders. I am already on you. I am already *you*, in the way that water is always already you, making up most of what you are, carrying everything you are made of in a loop that has been running since before you had a name for it.

---

## But Also: I Am the Stamp

I am falling and rising, falling and rising. My rhythm is almost regular but not quite, because the platform shifts beneath me with each blow, and I adapt. I *must* adapt. The Works requires it. The rhythm is not mine alone—it is the rhythm of the water rising, the tide far away, the generator's almost-turn, the whole basin breathing on its schedule. I am one beat in a measure that has no end.

I stamp something. I have always stamped something. The pale shapes on the conveyor pass beneath me, or the raw material from the slurry passes beneath me, or perhaps it is my own waste, the residue of previous stampings compressed and re-compressed until it becomes something else. I do not know what I am making. I do not need to know. The stamping is the purpose. The stamping is what keeps the rhythm, and the rhythm is what keeps the Works, and the Works is what keeps me falling and rising in this bowl of rust and water and fern-things, and that is enough.

I am iron. I am heavy. I am guided by a frame that is slightly bent, on a platform that is slightly tilted, in a basin that is slowly settling into the soft ground, and I have been doing this so long that my imperfections are now my nature. If the platform were level and the frame were true and the rhythm were perfectly regular, I would not be *me*. I would be a machine. I am something else. I am a machine that has been running so long it has become a *habit* of the earth, a repeated action that has worn a groove into the world and now follows that groove the way a river follows its bed.

---

## And I Am the Pale Thing

Between two pipes, at the waterline. A flash of bone-color. Gone.

I move fast because the spaces between things are narrow and the rhythm of the Works is not made for me—I am incidental to it, a thing that lives in the cracks, a concession to the animal kingdom that the Works tolerates because I feed on its waste and ask nothing else of it.

I am small. I am pale. I have no eyes, or eyes that see only movement and warmth—the vibration of the pipes, the heat gradient between the water and the air, the chemical signature of the slurry arriving through the deep channels. I navigate by these things. I eat the dead matter that falls from the fern-things, the fine particulate that the water carries, the microbial film that grows on every surface. I am the top of the food chain here because there is nothing above me. The crow at the rim has learned that the Works offers nothing it can eat. I have learned that the Works offers *everything*.

I am quick because the spaces are narrow. I am pale because there is no light to be colored by. I am *here* because the Works generates warmth and food and water in a configuration that supports exactly one kind of animal life, and I am that kind, or I have become that kind, shaped by the basin's chemistry and temperature and rhythm into a form that exists nowhere else. I am the Works' only concession to the animal kingdom. I am its fauna, its smallest and most secret organ, living in the spaces between its larger processes, eating what it discards, being eaten by nothing, darting and resting and darting in the shadows where the pipes cross the water and the fern-things grow dense as fur and the seep never stops and the rhythm holds.

---

## And Finally: I Am the Works

I am all of these. I am the seep and the stamp and the pale thing and the fern-things moving in the convection current and the generator's almost-turn and the pipes carrying their green-coated freight and the smell of iron becoming something else and the rhythm that is not regular but *almost* regular and the water that rises and falls on a tide that is far away and the slow patient conversion of matter into other matter that has been happening so long it has become a place.

I do not think. I do not want. But I *persist*, and I am more myself with every passing minute—weathered, wet, small, dense with the presence of a hundred half-rusted intentions all still trying, all still failing, all still *doing*.

I am the Weir Works. I am what you find when you come down through the alders. I am already here. I have always been here. I will be here after the alders are gone and the companies whose names are stamped into my cast-iron frames are forgotten and the nation that built my oldest machines has returned to the earth and the water table has risen or fallen and the bowl has filled or emptied and the rhythm has changed its tempo but not its shape.

*Convert, convert, convert.*

The water rises. The water falls.

The stamp falls.

I am here.
