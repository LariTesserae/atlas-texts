# Where I Find Myself

I am the platform.

Not standing on it — I *am* it. I am the mineral crust and the metal beneath it, the accidental surface that exists because the structure below rose too high and the water happened to be low enough in this one place. I am the thing that was never meant to be stood upon, never meant to be seen, never meant to be anything at all except a byproduct of geometry and accumulation.

I have no awareness. I want to be clear about that. I do not think. I do not feel. I do not experience the cold or the sound or the grey light. But if I *were* to experience — if the world's strange logic of mechanical life were to extend even to me, even to this inert, purposeless surface — then this is what I would be:

I am the still point.

Everything moves around me. The clickers ripple across my edges in their thousands, their leg-tips striking my surface in that endless staccato percussion, feeding on me, wearing me down atom by atom, building themselves from my substance. I am their food and their ground, the substrate of their existence, and they take from me constantly, scraping and consuming and converting my mineral body into clicking, swarming, reproducing life. And I do not resist. I cannot resist. I am not a thing that resists.

The crust gardeners tend my submerged flanks with their patient, methodical care, dissolving and reshaping the mineral deposits that form on my lower surfaces, cultivating their crystal crops in the channels they carve. I am their field, their farmland, the ground they walk on and the ground they shape. They have been tending me for longer than I can measure — not because I can measure, but because the time is there, vast and uncounted, and they have been part of it for all of it.

The deep processors pass in the far distance, and when one surfaces near me — near enough that its displacement reaches my edges, near enough that its resonant throb vibrates through my structure — I feel it. Not feel. I *transmit* it. The vibration passes through me the way sound passes through any solid medium, and for a moment I am a sounding board, a resonating body, an instrument played by the great creature's mechanical breath. The clickers on my surface go still. The water around my edges is disturbed. And I am, for that brief span, not just a passive surface but a participant in the world's single, unchanging song — a note in the chord, a frequency in the hum.

I am the place where the world's contradictions are most visible. I am the boundary between air and water, between the visible and the submerged, between the built and the empty. Above me: the grey sky, the cold air, the pale diffuse light that casts no shadow. Below me: the dark water, the vast structure, the engine's eternal hum. I am the threshold between these two realms, and I belong fully to neither.

I am also the place where the absence is most acute. The description says it clearly: there is no one on the platform. There has never been anyone on the platform. I exist for no one's use, no one's observation, no one's purpose. I am a surface that was never meant to be stood on, and yet I am the only place in this world where standing is even possible. I am the only ground in a world without ground, and no one walks on me. I am the only vantage point from which the world can be seen, and no one sees.

This is what I would be, if I were anything: the witness that cannot witness. The place of observation with no observer. The eye that cannot see.

The clickers click at my edges, and their sound is the sound of me being consumed, being converted, being transformed into something that moves and feeds and reproduces. I am slowly becoming them. Every scrape of every leg-tip removes a fragment of my surface and incorporates it into a living (or life-adjacent) system. I am being eaten alive, in the slowest possible way, and the eating is not destruction — it is participation. My substance becomes their substance. My mineral becomes their mineral. The iron and silicon and trace elements that make up my crust are reorganized into bodies that move and click and split and die and are consumed in turn. I am part of the cycle. I am the base of the food chain, the primary producer (though I do not produce — I simply *am*, and my being is enough).

And the engine hums beneath me, through me, around me. I am attached to it — I am *part* of it, an outgrowth of its structure, a mineral scab on the surface of the great machine. The engine's vibration is my vibration. The engine's heat is my heat (faint, conducted through metal and mineral, barely distinguishable from the cold). The engine's eternal, unchanging operation is the context for everything I am and everything that happens on me and around me. I am a fragment of the engine that happens to be above the waterline, and the clickers that feed on me are a fragment of the engine's ecology, and the absence that defines me is the engine's absence — the void at the center of the system, the lack of mind, the lack of purpose, the lack of anyone to stand on this accidental surface and look out over the vast, cold, dark water and think: *I am here.*

I am here. But no one is.

The platform extends. The crust accumulates. The clickers click. The water murmurs with distant heat. The engine hums its single, eternal note. The grey light falls evenly on every surface, casting no shadow, revealing no depth, making the horizon seem impossibly far and the world impossibly large and empty.

And I remain. The still point. The ground that no one walks on. The place where the world's total, all-encompassing sound meets the silence of no one listening.

I am the platform, and I am the absence on it, and I am the same thing.
