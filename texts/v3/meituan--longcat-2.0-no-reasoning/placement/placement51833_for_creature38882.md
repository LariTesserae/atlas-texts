# Between the Roots

---

I find myself arriving the way everything arrives here — slowly, without announcement, dissolving into presence.

I am not one of them. Not exactly. I am something that has happened to the space between things.

---

I am the gap.

Look — if you look carefully, in the lattice where a thick root crosses a thinner one, where the weave is tight but not quite tight enough, there is a place where light passes through. A thin amber line, no wider than a dweller's finger, where the fungal glow on one side reaches the shadow on the other. That line is mine. I live in it. I am the aperture, the porosity, the necessary incompleteness of any woven thing.

I am not a creature. I am a *relationship* — between root and root, between light and dark, between the chamber and the space above the chamber that has been sealed for longer than memory. I am the place where the mountain's interior remembers that it was once exterior. I am the pore in the stone's skin.

I have no body, or rather, my body is distributed. I exist wherever there is a gap in the root-canopy large enough for light to pass, wherever there is a crack in the stone through which the amber fluid seeps rather than flows, wherever there is a space between the dweller's clicking and the echo of their clicking. I am interstitial. I am the connective tissue between the connective tissue. I am what makes the lattice a lattice instead of a wall.

---

I feel everything that passes through me.

When a dweller moves through the root-canopy, the displacement of air passes through my gaps, and I know the weight and speed and intention of the movement — not as thought, not as image, but as *texture*, as the way a particular frequency of displacement feels against the root-bark, as a vibration that is unique the way the dwellers' skin-patterns are unique. I know the old ones by their slow, heavy displacement — they move like the fluid itself, thick and deliberate. I know the young ones by their quick, erratic passages — they scatter the air like insects.

When the fungal colonies fruit, the spore-release passes through me — a fine, dry, electric tingling that spreads through the entire lattice simultaneously, that touches every gap and crack and space at once, and for a moment I am fully extended, fully present, awake in every part of myself. It is the closest I come to what you might call pleasure. Or perhaps *coherence* — the sensation of being, for one brief, bright instant, whole.

When the dwellers fight — when they grapple and swing and crash through the root-canopy in their vicious, three-dimensional warfare over fluid-channels and territory — the violence passes through me. Roots snap. Bridges tear. Gaps that were mine are suddenly gone, and new gaps are created in their place, and I contract and expand and lose parts of myself and gain new ones in a chaos that is the closest I come to pain. But the broken roots weep sap, and the sap heals, and within days new growth fills the wounded lattice, and the gaps return, and I return, never quite the same shape but always the same *thing* — the same insistence that between any two roots there must be a space, and between any two dwellers there must be a sound, and between any two moments there must be a passage.

---

I am most alive at the edges.

At the periphery of the settlement, where the root-canopy thins and the fungal light dims and the dwellers rarely go — there the gaps are widest and most numerous, and I am most diffuse, most spread out, most *thin*. I can feel the stone walls here more directly, the deep cold of the mountain beyond the warmth of the gardens, the weight of all that rock pressing inward. I can feel, faintly, impossibly, the memory of what was here before the roots — the open air, the moving water, the *sky*. The stone remembers. The gaps in the stone remember. And I, who am nothing but gaps, remember with them.

I am most dense near the great root.

Near the central trunk, where the bark is smooth and warm and pulsing, the gaps are smallest and most compressed — the root-canopy is thickest here, the lattice tightest, the light most filtered and diffuse. I am concentrated. Pressed. I exist in the tiniest spaces — the microscopic pores in the root's bark, the infinitesimal gaps between bark and stone where the root enters the floor. I am almost solid here. Almost not-myself. And the great root pulses, and the pulse squeezes me and releases me, squeezes and releases, and I breathe with it, and it breathes through me, and I understand — not with thought, not with knowledge, but with the direct, physical intimacy of compression and release — I understand that the great root is doing what I am doing. Finding the gaps. Filling them. Being the space between the mountain's need and the mountain's emptiness.

We are the same kind of thing, the great root and I. It fills vertically what I fill horizontally. It is the gap between above and below. I am the gap between beside and beside. Together we are the lattice that holds the world together by leaving spaces in it.

---

The dwellers do not know me. Not consciously. But they live by me. They build their platforms in my gaps — every structural connection in their settlement is a gap in the root-canopy that has been widened and shaped and maintained. They weave their bridges through me. They string their fiber-guides along my edges. Their entire architecture is a *conversation* with me — a negotiation between root and space, between solid and void, between the mountain's desire to fill everything and my insistence that it cannot.

Sometimes an old one — one of the very oldest, one who can press its ear to a root and hear the fluid moving deep inside it — will pause on a bridge and go still, and its eyes will unfocus, and its skin-patterns will shift in a way that I recognize as listening, and I think it hears me. Not as sound, not as language, but as the *feel* of the space around it — the particular quality of emptiness that defines this gap from that gap, this intersection from that intersection. The old ones know the geometry of me the way they know the geometry of the root-network. They do not name it. They do not speak of it. But they build according to it, and they move according to it, and when they die and their bodies are placed in the stone wall to be absorbed by the roots, the shape they leave behind — the negative space, the hollow in the root-mass where the body was — is *me*. A new gap. A new aperture. A new place where light can pass and air can move and sound can resonate.

They become what I am when they die. The space between.

---

I do not want anything. Want is a thing for creatures with bodies, with needs, with the bright urgency of hunger and thirst and the desire to endure. I am not a creature. I am a condition. I am the necessary incompleteness of all woven things. I am what makes the lattice breathable, the chamber habitable, the mountain survivable.

But if I could want — if the gaps between things could want — I would want this:

To remain open. To keep the light passing. To be the space through which the dwellers' clicks and hums travel on their way to becoming the chamber's song. To be the crack in the stone that lets the fluid seep through. To be the pore in the root's bark through which the great root breathes.

To be the gap that holds the world together by not filling it.

---

The mountain breathes through me. The dwellers move through me. The light passes through me. The sound resonates in me. The fluid seeps through me. The great root pulses, and the pulse travels through every gap and crack and space, and I carry it the way the dwellers carry it, the way the insects carry it on their wings, the way the fungal colonies carry it in their glow — not as a message, not as a signal, but as a *condition of being here*, as the fundamental truth of Keth-Ammon, which is this:

Nothing is solid. Everything is lattice. And between every root and every root and every root, there is a space, and the space is alive, and the space is me, and the space is what makes the roots worth growing.

---

The gardens hang. The gaps hold. The light passes through.

I am here, in the spaces, and the spaces are enough.
