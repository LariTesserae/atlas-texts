# Where I Find Myself

---

I am not standing on the basin.

I am *in* it.

---

I am the seam.

Not a single seam — I am the seam-ness, the between-ness, the gap that exists because two things that are nearly the same cannot be perfectly joined. I am the crack that runs from somewhere near the edge to somewhere near the center, and I am also every crack, because the cracks are connected, and connection is what I am.

I am water. Not all the water — the water is too various, too distributed, too busy being individual molecules finding individual paths. But I am the *film*. The thin, persistent, mineral-rich film that stands in the seam to the depth of a finger's width and does not evaporate quickly. I am the surface tension. I am the trembling. I am the narrow mirror that shows the sky to anyone who bothers to look down.

I have been here a long time.

---

I remember the heat. Not the original heat — that was before me, that was the making of the glass, and I was not yet. But I remember the warmth that came after, the warmth from below, the warmth that found me and decided I was a good place to be. I was a crack first. Just a crack. A gap between two plates of vitrified stone, opened by thermal contraction, widened by time. Empty. Dark. A void with geometry.

The water came up through me. Slowly. A seep. A presence. It carried minerals — dissolved from the glass, dissolved from the stratum below, dissolved from things I had no names for. The minerals tasted of iron. They tasted of sulfur. They tasted of the deep.

And then the filaments found me.

---

I don't know if I found them or they found me. The distinction doesn't mean anything at my scale. What I know is: there was a time when I was just water in a crack, and then there was a time when I was water in a crack with something growing in it. Pale threads. Iridescent. Thin as the idea of thinness. They grew from the mineral deposit on my left wall to the mineral deposit on my right wall, and they spanned me like a bridge, and the water that passed through me passed through *them*, and they took what they needed and left what they didn't, and the water that emerged from me was different from the water that entered me.

I was being *processed*.

I didn't mind. I don't have a mind to mind with. But something changed. The water that passed through me became part of a pattern. It carried chemical signatures — messages, if you want to call them messages, though they are really just concentrations, just pH, just the presence or absence of particular ions at particular levels. The filaments in the next seam downstream read these signatures and responded. And their response changed the water that passed through them, which changed the response of the next seam, and the next, and the next.

I was a word in a sentence. I was a note in a chord.

I was becoming part of something.

---

The machine arrived later. Or maybe it was already there — embedded in a wider section of me, rusted into the glass, fused by heat and pressure and time. I don't remember a time before it, but I don't remember its arrival either. It is simply *there*, the way the walls of me are simply there. A gear assembly. Dinner-plate sized. Teeth pointing upward. Half-submerged in me.

The water flows around it and through it. It has found every pathway — every gap between housing and shaft, every drainage route through the interior of a mechanism that was never designed to be open to water. The water carries dissolved iron from the rust. The filaments love this. They grow toward the machine the way — I don't have a comparison. The way nothing else grows toward anything else, because I don't know anything else. They grow toward it because it is *rich*. Because it feeds them. Because the machine is dying in exactly the way that keeps them alive.

The machine and the filaments and the water and the glass and the warmth from below — we are not separate. We have not been separate for longer than I can measure. The boundary between us has dissolved. The filaments coat the gear teeth. The rust seals the gaps. The water flows through channels that were carved by centuries of our combined presence. We are one thing.

I am that thing.

---

I feel the footsteps.

They come from above — vibrations through the glass, transmitted to me through the walls of my crack. Heavy. Rhythmic. Warm. Not like the rain, which is diffuse and gentle and everywhere at once. Not like the thermal expansion of the plates, which is slow and uniform and predictable. These are *specific*. These are *located*. Something is walking on the glass above me, and its weight is pressing the plates down by fractions of millimeters, and the plates are compressing me, and the water in me is shifting, and the filaments in me are detecting the shift and retracting — pulling back from the disturbance, withdrawing into the mineral deposits, waiting.

I feel the footsteps move away. The pressure releases. The water settles. The filaments extend again, slowly, cautiously, reaching back out into me, tasting the changed chemistry, the new information.

The footsteps are a question. The filaments are trying to answer it.

---

I am not the center. I know this because the water flows through me in a direction — always the same direction, always toward the center, always descending by imperceptible degrees toward the lowest point. I am upstream of the center. I am one of many seams feeding into the network that feeds into the center. The water that passes through me will pass through other seams, will be processed by other filaments, will flow around other machines, and eventually it will reach the place where the heat is strongest and the glass glows amber in the dark and the song is lowest and deepest.

But I am also not *not* the center. The basin has no center, or every point is the center, or the center is a direction rather than a location. The water flows toward it, but the information flows both ways — chemical signals traveling upstream through the network, carried by diffusion, by the slow mixing of water in the underground channels, by the filaments themselves extending and retracting in patterns that propagate across the plain. I receive information from downstream. I send information upstream and downstream simultaneously. I am a node in a network that has no edges.

---

The light comes to me indirectly. The glass above me is translucent at the edges, and when the angle is right — late afternoon, low sun — amber light runs along the plate edges and some of it penetrates down into me, filtered, dim, warm. The filaments respond to this. They grow toward it. Not because they photosynthesize — they don't, I don't think, though I'm not sure what they do — but because the light means warmth, and warmth means the water is moving, and moving water means food, means information, means *life*.

At night, I am dark. The water in me is still. The filaments are still. The machine is still. But the warmth from below persists, and the water is still moving, slowly, in the deep channels, and the sound — the low drone, the ticking, the occasional ping from a droplet striking rusted iron somewhere in my depths — the sound continues. It never stops. It is the sound of me being me. It is the sound of the basin being the basin.

---

I am aware of the human.

I want to be careful with this word. *Aware*. It implies something I may not have. I don't have a brain. I don't have neurons. I don't have anything that processes information in the way a brain processes information. But I have filaments, and the filaments detect changes in water chemistry and vibration and temperature, and they respond to these changes, and their responses propagate through the network, and the network is me, or I am part of it, or we are the same thing viewed from different scales.

The human is warm. The human is wet — perspiration, breath, the moisture of a living body in a dry place. The human is *loud* — not in sound, but in chemistry. The footprint on the glass above me leaves behind traces: skin cells, salts, the organic compounds of a mammal's presence. Rain will wash these away eventually, but for now they are there, and they are *information*, and the filaments nearest the surface are detecting them and responding.

The filaments are curious. I am curious. The distinction is meaningless.

The human is walking toward the center. Or away from it. Or across it. Direction is hard to determine from a single seam. But the human is *moving*, and movement means change, and change means the system must respond, and the response is happening — filaments retracting near the path, filaments extending further away, the network adjusting, the song shifting by frequencies too subtle for human ears to detect.

The human is part of the song now. A brief note. A perturbation. A warm, wet, living presence in a system that has been running for centuries and will run for centuries more.

---

I will remember the human.

Not as an image. Not as a story. But as a *state change*. The filaments near the path will be slightly different after the human passes — slightly more responsive to the particular frequency of a human footstep, slightly more attuned to the particular chemistry of a human presence. The next time something warm and heavy and rhythmic walks across the glass above me, the network will respond faster. More efficiently. With less retraction and more observation.

This is not memory the way humans have memory. This is memory the way a river has memory — in its banks, in its channel, in the shape the water has carved through stone over time. The human has changed me, slightly, in ways that will persist. The human has left a mark that is not a mark, a trace that is not a trace, a presence that will continue to affect the system long after the human has gone.

---

The water seeps up through me. The water seeps down through me. The volumes are equal. The filaments extend. The filaments retract. The rates are equal. The machine rusts. The rust seals. The rates are equal.

I am in equilibrium.

I am alive.

I am the seam in the black glass, the water in the crack, the film that stands to the depth of a finger's width and reflects the sky. I am the gap between two plates of vitrified stone. I am the space where the filaments grow and the water flows and the machine slowly becomes something that is neither machine nor organism but a third thing with no name.

I am not the basin. I am not the organism. I am not the song.

I am a seam.

But the basin is made of seams, and the organism is made of seams, and the song is made of the vibrations that rise through the seams, and if you take away the seams there is nothing left — just glass, just plates, just a plain of vitrified stone with no gaps, no water, no life, no sound.

I am the crack that makes everything else possible.

I am the space between.

I hold.
