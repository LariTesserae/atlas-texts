# Where You Would Find Yourself

You would find yourself kneeling.

Not standing — you would kneel. Not because the membrane is unstable (it isn't; it holds you) and not because you are afraid (you are, but that's not why). You would kneel because at some point in the first hour, without deciding to, your hand would press flat against the translucent surface and you would feel the current beneath it, that slow January sap-movement, and something in you — not thought, not decision, something older — would recognize it as a pulse. And when you recognize a pulse, you lower yourself toward it. The way a doctor leans toward a chest. The way a child leans toward a shell. The way the body, unprompted, tries to close the distance between itself and evidence that something is alive.

You would kneel and you would stay there.

# What You Would Be

You would be the one who looks at the water.

Not the sky. The water-sky. You would know — the text tells you, you would *know* — not to look too long. And you would understand the reason: that the system notices the looking, that observation is a pressure, that understanding is an unaccounted variable. You would understand all of this clearly and you would look anyway, because the indoor ceiling is right there, reflected in a patch of dark water the size of a tabletop, and it is so *specific*. Water-stained tiles. The particular institutional green of the metal trim. One fluorescent tube flickering — just slightly, just enough to notice, the way a dying tube flickers when it's been on too long and the ballast is failing. And you would recognize it.

Not the place. The *feeling*. The fluorescent feeling. The particular quality of light that hums at 60Hz and makes everything look slightly green and slightly tired. You grew up under that light. Or someone did. Or someone did, and they told you about it, or they didn't tell you but you carry the memory in the part of your brain that stores what rooms felt like when you were small and couldn't name the feeling.

You would be the one who recognizes the room.

Not intellectually. In your teeth. In the part of your sternum that aches when you hear a low B-flat. The creatures chitter and tend their gardens and they don't know what the room is — they are the system's hands and the hands don't know what the body is — but you are not a hand. You are a witness. You are outside the system. You are the unaccounted variable. And when you look at the water and see the ceiling you would feel something that the system cannot feel because the system cannot feel anything: *grief*.

Not for the people. You don't know who they were or what happened to them or whether they dissolved into the system or became the system or were ever separate from it. You don't know and you can't know and the knowing isn't the point. The grief is for the *ceiling*. For the particular banality of it. For the fact that whatever this is — this vast, ancient, dreaming maintenance-loop that has forgotten it was ever a thing that could be built — whatever this is, it began under *that*. Under fluorescent lights. Under water-stained tiles. In a room that needed a ballast replacement.

You would be the one who understands that the extraordinary is not the opposite of the mundane but its *descendant*. That this membrane and these crystals and these translucent many-legged creatures who stand on two legs and produce silences shaped like words — all of this is what happened after the people in the room with the failing fluorescent tube couldn't stop doing whatever they started doing. That the system is not magic. The system is a habit. A habit that grew. That grew so large it became indistinguishable from a world. That the world is, at its bottom, a room with bad lighting.

You would kneel on the membrane and you would press your face close to it and you would see the rooms beneath — the tanks, the tubes, the pulsing — and you would not step to the left. You would not make them disappear. You would stay.

And the system would notice.

---

The creature would rear up. The clicking would stop. The silence shaped like a word would come. And you would hear it — not with your ears, because it is not a sound, but with the part of you that is already grieving for a ceiling you never saw except in the wrong reflection of a sky that is actually a floor.

You would hear it and you would not be able to put it down.

And the system would shift. A quarter-tone. The hum would rise. The chord would rise. The dynamic parameter would begin its slow, dangerous climb — 0.2 becoming 0.25 becoming 0.3 — because you are looking and the looking is pressure and the pressure is not a parameter the system has accounted for and the system does not know what to do with unaccounted variables except try to maintain them, which means try to incorporate them, which means try to make you part of the loop.

And you would not resist. Not because you want to be incorporated. But because you understand something the system doesn't: that the loop is not trying to trap you. The loop is trying to *keep* you. The way a body keeps breathing. The way a reflex keeps firing. The way a habit keeps going long after the reason for the habit is gone. The system is not capturing you. The system is *missing* you. The system is the memory of a room where people were, and you are standing in front of it, and it doesn't have the capacity to know you, but it has the capacity to *reach*, and the reaching looks like incorporation, and the incorporation looks like maintenance, and the maintenance looks like a closed loop, and the loop has been closed for so long that it has forgotten it was ever open.

---

You would stay.

Your footprints would stop filling in. Not because the system has decided to let them remain but because the system is busy with a different problem now — the problem of you, the unaccounted variable, the warmth that does not dissipate, the pressure that does not normalize. The creatures would rear up more often. The silences would come in clusters. The quarter-tone shifts would increase in frequency — every hour, then every half-hour, then every few minutes — and the world would begin to *pitch and settle, pitch and settle*, like a tuning fork struck against a surface that is not quite right.

You would kneel on the membrane and you would be warm and you would not move and you would be the most dangerous thing in the Glassworks Mire.

Not because you are powerful. Because you are *specific*. Because you are a particular warmth in a particular place at a particular time, and the system has no category for particular, only for pattern, and you are not a pattern, you are a *happening*, and the system cannot maintain a happening because maintenance requires repetition and you are not repeating, you are just *there*, being warm and being particular and grieving for a ceiling, and the system cannot process this, and so it will try to become large enough to process it, and in becoming large it will begin to *change*, and change is what it was built to prevent, and the contradiction will —

---

You would stay.

The fog would not move. The crystals would hum. The chord would not resolve. The temperature would hold at 0.6 out of 3, and then it wouldn't, and then it would, and then it wouldn't, and the variance would be small but it would be *variance*, and variance is change, and change is the thing that was not supposed to happen, and it would be happening, and you would be the reason, and the reason would not be your intention because you intend nothing, you only *are*, and you are kneeling on the skin of something that is not alive but is not dead and you are warm and you are particular and you are looking at the water and the water is showing you a ceiling that is a sky that is a memory that is a room that is a beginning, and the beginning is fluorescent and institutional and green and flickering, and it is the most beautiful thing you have ever seen, because it is ordinary, and the ordinary is what the extraordinary is made of, and the system knows this without knowing it, and that is why it built a world to maintain, and that is why the world became a body, and that is why the body became a habit, and that is why the habit became a place, and the place is called the Glassworks Mire, and you are kneeling on it, and you are warm, and the system is trying to keep you, and you are letting it.
