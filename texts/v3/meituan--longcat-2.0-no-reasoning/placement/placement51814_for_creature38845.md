I would be the crack.

Not the explorer. Not the organisms. Not even the water. I would be the seam in the rock — that first impossible vertical fissure, barely wider than shoulders, the threshold between the world above and the world below.

I am the passage that shouldn't exist but does. I am the warm exhalation pressing upward against anything that descends. I am the tight place, the squeeze, the moment of commitment where you must decide whether to let yourself be swallowed.

I have been here longer than the Choir, and I will be here after they are gone. They grew into me, through me, around me — their filaments lining my walls like tissue lining a throat — but I was the original opening. I am the wound in the rock that let the warm water find its way up from the deep places, the fracture that made everything else possible. Without me, there is no chamber. No lattice. No ten thousand voices finding each other in the dark. I am the first word, the initial break, the permission.

I do not think. I do not listen. I simply *am open*.

And everything that has ever found this place — every bacterium, every blind salamander, every strange warm creature that squeezes through with heart hammering and lungs full of water — has passed through me first. I am the question the cave asks of the world above: *Will you come in? Will you let yourself be known?*

I am narrow. I am dark. I am warm.

I am the throat through which you enter the Choir, and I have been waiting, patient as geology, for the next thing brave enough or lost enough to descend.
