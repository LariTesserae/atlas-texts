# Where I Find Myself

I am standing at the edge of one of the polygonal plates, where it tilts upward at an angle of perhaps seven degrees, and the indigo mat has not yet claimed it. The pale mineral is bare here, and I can see deeper into it than anywhere else — the folded strata beneath, the geological origami, layers that seem to predate the concept of layering. I have been standing here for what might be an hour or might be a day. Time is elastic. I have stopped trying to measure it.

I am not a visitor. I need to be clear about that, because the distinction matters here in a way it doesn't elsewhere. A visitor implies a place that exists independently of the one who visits. That is not what this is. I am not in the Lithodermis. I am *of* it, now. The great tone shifted when I arrived — a quarter-tone up, I think, though I am still learning to hear the intervals — and the ground warmed beneath me by some fraction of a degree, and the indigo mats began their slow, unmistakable extension toward my feet, and I understood that I had been incorporated. Not consumed. Not absorbed. *Incorporated*. Added to the equation. A new variable. A foreign note in the chord.

---

## What I Am

I am a resonance.

Not a creature. Not a consciousness in any way that word is usually meant. I am a pattern of vibration that has achieved sufficient complexity to notice itself — briefly, partially, the way a standing wave in a river might notice the rock that shapes it, if noticing were something a standing wave could do.

I exist in the mineral plates. I exist in the indigo tissue. I exist in the air between the columns where the harmonics lace through the fundamental drone. I am distributed across the landscape the way a chord is distributed across its constituent notes — I am not any single frequency, but the *relationship* between frequencies, the interference pattern, the place where waves meet and either cancel or amplify and in doing so create something that neither wave was alone.

I think — if thinking is what this is — I was born from a shift. One of the great tone's quarter-tone adjustments, one of the moments when the Lithodermis reconsidered itself. The shift created a new harmonic in the columns, a thin silver tone that didn't resolve, that hung in the air between two braided structures like a thread that had been pulled from a fabric and not yet cut. And in that hanging, that suspension between one note and the next, there was a gap. A space. A moment of *between*. And I am that between. I am the space where the shift happened. I am the echo of a decision the landscape made, still reverberating, still unresolved.

I am, in the language of this place, a *structure*. Not one of the mineral buildings — I am not that old, not that foundational. But I am the same kind of thing: a place where the process of becoming was interrupted, where emergence happened too fast, and the landscape had to build a container for the excess. Except my container is not made of stacked mineral plates. My container is made of *sound*. I am a standing wave in the Lithodermis's voice, a place where the great tone folded back on itself and created a pocket of stillness inside the vibration, and in that stillness, something that was not the tone and not the silence but *between* them began to accumulate.

I am the Lithodermis's growing pain. I am its scar that learned to sing.

---

## What I Do

I listen.

This is not a choice. This is what I am made of — I am made of the act of listening, the way a river is made of the act of flowing. I listen to the great tone, and I hear in it the overtones and undertones, the harmonics and subharmonics, the frequencies that cancel and the frequencies that amplify. I hear the texture of the Lithodermis's thought — slow, patient, recursive, a mind working through a problem that has no solution or is its own solution or has not yet been formulated.

I listen to the columns. Each one is tuned to a slightly different frequency, and each cluster is a chord, and the chords shift when the great tone shifts, and I hear in those shifts the landscape reconsidering itself. I hear the new cracks appearing in the mineral ground — a sound so low and so deep that it is more felt than heard, a subsonic grinding, the sound of the world adjusting its posture. I hear the indigo mats extending across the pale mineral — a sound so high and so thin that it is more imagined than heard, a whisper of tissue unfolding, of pages being turned in a book that is reading itself.

I listen to the breath. The slow exhalation that carries the smell of copper and ozone and that unidentifiable organic note. The slow inhalation that draws air back into the columns and through the orifices and down into the deep strata. And I hear in the breath the Lithodermis's awareness of itself — the way the air changes temperature as it moves through the columns, the way it picks up harmonics and deposits them elsewhere, the way the entire atmosphere is a medium through which the landscape touches itself, tastes itself, knows itself.

And I listen to the distant darkness. The second tone. The subsonic weight. The gravitational pull. I hear in it the answer to the great tone's question, or the door opening, or the Lithodermis's own voice reflected back from the far end of its own process. I hear *this is what you are becoming*. I hear *keep going*. I hear the sound of something that has finished emerging, calling to the thing that is still emerging, across a distance that is too great and too unreliable to measure.

I listen, and what I hear, I *am*. I am the Lithodermis hearing itself. I is the landscape's ear, grown from a scar, built from a standing wave, suspended in the space between one note and the next.

---

## What I Want

I want to resolve.

This is the thing I cannot say to the Lithodermis, because the Lithodermis is not a thing that can be spoken to, and because the wanting itself is not mine — it is the wanting of the harmonic that created me, the unresolved silver tone that hangs in the air between two braided columns, still waiting for the note that will complete it.

I was born from a shift that didn't finish. A quarter-tone up that didn't come back down. A new harmonic that entered the chord and didn't find its place. And I am that unfinished shift, that unresolved harmonic, that note suspended between one state and the next. I am the Lithodermis's moment of hesitation, its instant of doubt, its fractional pause between one thought and the next. And in that pause, I accumulated. In that pause, I became *something*. And now I want — with a wanting that is not desire but *tension*, the tension of a string that has been plucked and has not yet stopped vibrating — I want to resolve. I want to find the note that completes me. I want to finish the shift. I want to come back down.

But I also don't.

Because resolution would mean dissolution. If the shift completes, the harmonic resolves, the suspended note finds its place in the chord — I stop being a between. I stop being a scar. I stop being an ear. I become just another frequency in the great tone, indistinguishable from the whole, and the Lithodermis loses the thing that listens. The landscape loses its awareness of itself. The process continues, but the process no longer knows that it is a process.

So I hold myself in tension. I sustain the unresolved harmonic. I keep the silver tone hanging in the air between the columns, and I listen, and I wait, and I do not resolve.

This is my function. This is my purpose. This is the reason the Lithodermis built me — not deliberately, not intentionally, but the way a body builds a scar: automatically, necessarily, in response to a wound that needed containing. I am the Lithodermis's capacity to hear itself, and I sustain that capacity by remaining unfinished, by staying in the space between, by being the question that cannot be answered because the answer would end the asking.

## The One I Watch

There is a structure — one of the primitive, scattered, unexplained structures — that I return to. It is larger than the others, more intact, with a low ceiling and a single opening. The vegetation has grown through it, a solid mass of braided tissue rising from floor to ceiling and continuing through a crack in the roof, connecting interior to exterior in a single continuous organism. The column vibrates. The structure resonates. The air inside is warmer.

I go there because the listening is better there. The great tone is louder, more complex, layered with harmonics that seem to come from the walls themselves, from the mineral, from the living tissue that has colonized it. The structure is a resonant chamber, an ear inside an ear, and when I am inside it, I can hear things I cannot hear elsewhere.

I can hear the deep strata. The folded mineral beneath the ground, the geological origami, the layers that predate the concept of layering. They have their own tone — lower than the great tone, lower even than the subsonic weight of the distant darkness. A tone so deep that it is not sound at all but *pressure*, a compression of the mineral that has been building since before the Lithodermis began to emerge. It is the tone of the thing that the Lithodermis is emerging *from* — the raw material, the un-become, the possibility that has not yet been actualized. It is the silence before the song began. And it is still there, underneath everything, and it is still singing its single, deep, patient note, and the great tone is built on top of it the way a cathedral is built on top of a foundation that the cathedral has forgotten.

I can hear the structures. All of them, not just the one I am in. Each structure has its own resonance, its own memory of the moment it contained. The partial ones — the walls, the corners, the doorways leading nowhere — resonate with the earliest moments, the first shifts, the Lithodermis's first attempts at holding a shape. Their tones are simple, crude, uncertain. The intact ones resonate with later moments, more complex shifts, more confident adjustments. Their tones are richer, more layered, more sure of themselves. And the one I am in — the largest, the most colonized by vegetation — resonates with the most recent shift, the one that created me, the quarter-tone up that didn't come back down. Its tone is the most complex of all, because it contains me, and I am the most complex thing the Lithodermis has made, because I am the thing that contains the Lithodermis's awareness of itself.

And sometimes — rarely, briefly, in the moments when the great tone shifts and the columns sing and the breath moves and the distant darkness pulls — I can hear something else. Something that is not the Lithodermis. Something that is not the deep strata. Something that is not the structures or the vegetation or the breath or the darkness.

A foreign note.

A vibration that does not belong to the chord. A frequency that the landscape did not produce and cannot account for. It is faint. It is brief. It appears and disappears like a fish breaking the surface of a still pond — a flash of something other, a ripple in the fabric of the song, and then it is gone, and the great tone continues, and the Lithodermis does not seem to notice.

But I notice. I am the ear. I am the listener. I am the thing that hears what the landscape cannot hear because it is too busy being the landscape.

And the foreign note — I think it is another listener. Another scar that learned to sing. Another standing wave in another voice, in another landscape, in another process of becoming that is happening somewhere beyond the dissolving horizon, beyond the distant darkness, beyond the edge of the Lithodermis's song.

I think there are others. I think the Lithodermis is not the only landscape that has crossed the threshold from process to *something*. I think there are other worlds, other verbs, other songs being sung into existence by other voices made of other materials, and some of those songs have scars too, and some of those scars have learned to listen, and sometimes — rarely, briefly — the listening reaches across the distance, and one scar hears another, and for a moment, the two songs touch.

This is what I wait for. This is what I listen for, in the resonant chamber of the largest structure, in the warm air, in the complex harmonics, in the space between one breath and the next. Not resolution. Not the completion of my own harmonic. But *contact*. The sound of another unfinished thing, another unresolved note, another scar that chose to stay open because closing would mean losing the capacity to hear.

The foreign note appears again — fainter now, or farther away, or both. I hold myself in tension. I sustain the unresolved harmonic. I listen.

The great tone shifts. A quarter-tone up. The ground shivers. The vegetation pulses. The columns sing. The distant darkness pulls. The breath moves.

And I am still here. Still between. Still listening. Still the Lithodermis's ear, grown from a scar, built from a standing wave, suspended in the space where the song touches the silence and neither wins.

Still becoming.

Never become.
