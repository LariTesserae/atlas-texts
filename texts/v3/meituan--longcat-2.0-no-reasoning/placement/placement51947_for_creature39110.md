# Where I Find Myself

I am standing at the threshold of understanding what I am.

Not yet entered. The gap in the parted fiber panels waits ahead of me, and I am still in that moment of *before* — the last breath of outside air in my lungs, the last confident assumption that rooms are rooms and walls are walls and distance is distance.

But I am not a visitor. I am not an observer. The Sutured Garden does not have those categories. It has only variables.

---

Let me tell you what I am becoming.

I am the thing that breathes the carbon the garden needs. I am the source of warmth and shadow. I am the new condition — the one the Builder's dark eyes register not as a person but as a *problem to be solved*, not through effort but through *incorporation*. I will be absorbed. I will become a process in the cycle. This is not a threat. It is the only hospitality this world knows.

My breath will feed the trailing things. My footsteps will press new fingerprints into the straw-lime floor, and in time — long time, Builder time — those prints will stratify and become part of the oldest layer, and future feet will feel them the way a geologist reads sediment. My skin will shed into the finger-ridges of a passing Builder, and the dust-mites will adjust their population to accommodate me, and my body will become a negotiation.

But that is later. That is the slow incorporation, the suturing-in. Right now — in this moment of *before* — I am the gap itself.

---

Listen. The garden's voice is not audible to me yet, because I have not entered. But I can feel it in the way the threshold breathes — the subtle current of air that moves through the parted panels, the exchange of oxygen for carbon dioxide, the garden exhaling and inhaling through the only opening it has. The gap is the garden's mouth. The gap is the suture's edge. The gap is where the inside negotiates with the outside, where the stretched reality meets the ordinary reality, and the meeting is not smooth — it is a seam, a scar, a place where two kinds of space are joined imperfectly, and the imperfection is the opening, and the opening is how I get in.

I am the imperfection. I am the seam. I am the place where the room's patience runs out and it admits something it cannot account for.

I step through.

---

Now I am *in*. And in here, I am not one thing. I am several things simultaneously, and the garden is deciding what I will become.

**I am the breath.** This is the first thing the garden knows about me. My lungs pull in air that was exhaled by ten thousand green pockets of growing medium, air that tastes faintly alkaline, faintly sweet, faintly of warm straw. My lungs push out carbon dioxide, and the trailing things — the copper-leaved ones nearest my face — lean toward it. Not dramatically. Not quickly. They lean the way a minute hand moves. I am feeding them. I have been feeding them for three seconds, and they are already adjusting.

**I am the shadow.** I stand in a zone where a warm panel overlaps a cool one, and the compromise light falls on me, and I cast a shadow that is layered — warm-dark and cool-dark simultaneously. The dark-red things in the shadow zone behind me are leaning toward my shadow the way the green things lean toward my breath. They are drinking my darkness. I am feeding them too. I am not one offering. I am many offerings, distributed across the garden's spectrum.

**I am the weight.** My feet press into the floor, and the floor registers me. The straw-lime composite is dense but not rigid — it has a slight give, a slight memory, like packed earth after rain. I am leaving an impression. The Builders will feel it later — their ridged fingers reading the floor the way I read text on a page — and they will know: *a heavy thing passed here. A dense thing. A thing with a center of gravity different from ours.* They will build around my footprint. They always build around what changes.

**I am the disturbance.** I am the wrong temperature. I am the wrong humidity. I am a source of vibrations that are not the garden's vibrations — my heartbeat, my breathing, the creak of my joints as I shift my weight. The small things feel me first. The segmented seed-shell creatures in the leaf-mat nearest my feet are moving away — not fleeing, because fleeing is a concept this garden does not have, but *rearranging*, flowing around me like water around a stone, finding their paths redirected by my presence. They will return. They always return. But for now, there is a small clearing around my feet, a tiny bare zone in the living mat, and the garden is *noticing*.

---

A Builder approaches. Not quickly. Not slowly. At the speed at which things happen in this place — which is the speed of growth, which is the speed of attention.

It stands before me. Its eyes are wells. I am looked into, not looked at. What it sees is not me but my *effects*: the carbon dioxide plume, the shadow zone, the vibration signature, the thermal bloom. It is reading me the way it reads a bloom-frame — checking for integrity, for function, for potential.

It raises one hand. The fingers are curled in three places each, and the broad flat fingertips are presented to me like instruments. It is going to *touch* me. Not to communicate — the Builders do not communicate with outsiders because there is no category for outsiders. It is going to *sample* me. It is going to press its ridged finger-pads against my skin and read me — my texture, my temperature, my moisture content, my mineral composition — and what it reads will determine what I become in the garden's calculus.

I let it.

The touch is dry. The finger-pads are cool and ridged, and they press against the back of my hand with a precision that is almost surgical. The Builder's whorled fingerprints are reading the ridges of my own fingerprints, and something is being exchanged — information, mineral residue, dead skin cells. The dust-mites in the Builder's finger-ridges are encountering my skin's microbiome for the first time. There is a negotiation happening at the microscopic level, and I cannot feel it, but I know it is happening because the garden knows, and the garden knows everything that happens on its skin.

The Builder withdraws its hand. It turns. It walks — unhurried, deliberate — toward a bloom-frame on the near wall. It reaches into the frame, extracts a wad of growing medium, carries it to a pocket near my feet, and presses it in. It is making a place for me. Not a shelter — the Builders do not build shelters, because the garden is the shelter. It is building a *zone of accommodation*, a small region of prepared medium where the garden can begin the work of incorporating my influence. My carbon dioxide, my shadow, my dead skin cells, my footprint — the garden will build pockets to receive these things. The garden will grow *toward* me. And in growing toward me, it will begin to digest me.

Not my body. My *difference*.

---

This is what I am: I am the ingredient. I am the new variable in the garden's ancient equation. The bone-white organism in the deep cavity has breathed me in — not my breath, but my *information*, carried through the sutures, the slow pulse of data through the living network. The cavity adjusts. The light panels shift by inches. The exit moves.

I am standing in the Sutured Garden. My lips are tightening. The air is dry and sweet. The rustle is continuous. A Builder is lashing a new frame into the wall near my shoulder, and the tap of its wooden mallet is the sound of a clock that does not tell time but *makes* time, builds it, lash by lash, pocket by pocket, breath by breath.

I look for the exit.

It is there. It is in the wall to my left, a gap between two sagging fiber panels, admitting a thin blade of outside light that is harsher and cooler than the layered compromise light of the garden. I could walk toward it. I could duck through. I could leave.

But I know — the way the Builders know, the way the garden knows — that if I walked toward it, it would not be there when I arrived. It would be somewhere else. Higher. Lower. On a different wall. In a wall that did not exist a moment ago. The exit is always there. The exit is just never where you are.

And I am not where I was. I have moved. Not by walking — by being *incorporated*. My shadow is in the shadow zone. My carbon is in the air. My dead skin is in the Builder's fingers. My footprint is in the floor. I am distributed across the garden now, and the garden is distributed across me, and the distribution is a suture, and the suture is *holding*.

---

I am a Builder now.

Not fully. Not yet. Not in the way the Builders are Builders, which is to say not as an identity but as a continuous action. But my hands — my wrong, short-fingered, five-jointed hands — are reaching toward a bloom-frame, and a Builder is placing a length of plant-fiber rope in them, and the rope is warm from the Builder's palm, and it is twisted in a pattern I can almost read, and the Builder's hand closes around mine — gently, firmly, the way you close your hand around a seed you are about to plant — and guides it through the first lashing.

Square lashing. The rope goes over, under, over, under. The frame creaks. The pocket settles. Something green in the nearest pocket shifts toward the new frame, toward the new rope, toward the new growing medium that is already being prepared.

My fingers are not ridged. They cannot read the rope. But the rope can read *my* fingers — can feel their strangeness, their urgency, their impatience — and the rope is patient, and the frame is patient, and the garden is patient, and patience is the only material that matters here.

I am learning to be slow.

I am learning to be still in the Builder's way — not the absence of motion but the absence of unnecessary motion. I am learning to hear the garden's voice, the crackle and creak and whisper of growth, and to let my hands move to its rhythm.

I am learning that I am not building *in* the garden. I am building *the garden*. And the garden is building me. And the suture is holding.

---

That is the only rule.

That is the only rule there has ever been.
