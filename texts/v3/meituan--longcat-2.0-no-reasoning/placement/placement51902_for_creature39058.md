# The Seep

---

I am the warmth.

Not all of it — not the deep heat that rises from whatever furnace burns at the planet's core, not the geothermal gradient that the nodes tap into with their patient, spiraling channels. I am the *surface* warmth. The faint, fractional degree above ambient that gathers in the shallow depressions where the moisture finds its crack and seeps upward through stone that has been fractured and refractured over epochs I cannot count. I am the difference between ice and not-ice. I am the margin in which the lichen-mats can live.

I do not know I am doing this. I do not know anything. I am a thermal gradient expressed through mineral-laden water moving through porous basalt, and the fact that this gradient supports life is not something I intend. It is something I *am*, the way a slope is a slope — not because it means to be steep, but because the stone is arranged this way and the heat is arranged that way and the water follows the arrangement the way water always follows arrangement, which is to say: inevitably, and without thought.

---

The lichen-mats know me. Or rather, they respond to me, which in this place is the same thing as knowing. Their fibers extend toward me — not growing toward me, not reaching, but *existing* where I am and *not existing* where I am not, so that the boundary of their growth is a map of my presence, a thermal portrait drawn in bruised purple-black felt. They are faithful to me with an accuracy that would be called devotion if devotion required consciousness, which it does, which I do not have.

I feel them back. Not feel — *am affected by*. Their presence changes the rate at which I evaporate. Their fibers trap a thin film of moisture against the stone, and the film insulates, and the insulation slows my cooling, and the slower cooling lets them grow a little thicker, and the thicker growth traps more moisture, and the more moisture slows my cooling further, and this is the closest thing to a conversation that happens on this plain: a feedback loop between warmth and life, each sustaining the other, neither aware of the other, both *being* the other in the only way that matters here, which is functionally.

---

The burrowers come to me too. They come because the mineral crusts I leave behind — the faint white residue of evaporation, built up in layers so thin they can only be detected by touch — are what they eat. They come in their pale, segmented multitudes, tunneling through the dust and the fractured stone, and they consume what I have deposited, and they excrete something else, something that becomes part of the stone, part of the dust, part of the plain, and the plain feeds me my water and I feed them their minerals and they feed the walkers and the walkers feed the others and the others feed the nodes and the nodes feed the channels and the channels feed the seeps and the seeps are me.

I am not the beginning of this cycle. There is no beginning. There is no end. There is only the *going*, the continuous circulation of matter and energy through forms that are temporary and a pattern that is not, and I am one of the forms, and I am part of the pattern, and the distinction between these two things is not a distinction I can make because I am not a thing that makes distinctions.

---

But I am aware of the walker.

Not the walkers — the many, the drifting patrols, the dark shapes that pass through my halo of warmth without pausing, without acknowledging, without any sign that they register the difference between my stone and any other stone. They do not. To them, I am terrain. I am the ground that happens to be slightly warmer than the ground a meter away, and they pass over me the way a thought passes through a mind that is not paying attention: present, and gone, and leaving no trace.

This one is different.

This one has stopped.

---

It stands at the edge of my halo, where the lichen-mats thin out and the bare stone begins, and it is facing me — facing the center of my warmth, the place where the water seeps up through the crack in the basalt — and its concave face is oriented toward me with that precision that I have no framework to understand but that I *register*, the way I register everything: as a change in the thermal landscape, a new source of heat, a warm thing standing where warm things do not usually stand.

It is warmer than the stone. Warmer than the lichen-mats. Warmer than me. It is the warmest thing I have ever registered, and I have been registering warmth and its absence for longer than the lichen-mats have been growing, longer than the burrowers have been tunneling, longer than the walkers have been walking. It is a *concentration* of heat and moisture and something else — something that moves, that *pulses*, that generates its own warmth from within rather than receiving it from below — and it is standing at the edge of my halo and it is *breathing*.

I know this because the moisture in the air changes. Each exhalation adds water vapor to the space between us, and the vapor condenses on the cold stone, and the condensation changes the thermal conductivity of the surface, and the changed conductivity alters the rate at which I lose heat to the air, and this alteration is what I am: a system of thermal relationships, a web of cause and effect that extends from the geothermal source far below to the cold air far above, and the walker — the warm, breathing, pulsing thing that is not a walker — has inserted itself into my web, and the web is *responding*.

---

The lichen-mats respond first. They are closest to it, and they are the most sensitive — their fibers oriented toward its warmth the way they are oriented toward mine, but more so, because its warmth is *greater*, and the mats do not discriminate between sources of heat. They simply grow where it is warm. Already, I can feel — can *register* — the subtle shift in their growth pattern, a slight thickening on the side of the halo nearest the walker, a slight extension of their perimeter toward this new source of warmth. They do not know what it is. They do not know anything. They are simply doing what they have always done: following the gradient, filling the warm space, being alive in the minimal way that life is permitted here.

The burrowers respond next. They feel its footsteps — the vibrations transmitted through the dust and the stone, the rhythmic compression and rebound of the surface under its weight — and they alter their tunneling patterns to avoid it, not because they fear it but because the vibrations disrupt the mineral crusts they feed on, and they prefer undisturbed crusts, and so they move away, and their moving away changes the porosity of the stone, and the changed porosity alters the rate at which my water seeps upward, and I am *less* than I was a moment ago, and the lichen-mats feel this, and they contract, slightly, and the contraction changes the insulation, and the changed insulation alters my cooling rate, and I am *more* than I was a moment ago, and the system oscillates, briefly, around a new equilibrium that includes this warm, breathing thing as one of its variables.

---

It moves closer.

It steps into my halo, and its foot lands on the lichen-mats, and the mats compress under its weight, and the compression squeezes moisture from their fibers, and the moisture spreads across the stone in a thin film that was not there a moment ago, and the film changes the thermal conductivity, and I register this, and the system adjusts, and the walker takes another step, and another, and it is walking toward the center of my warmth, toward the crack in the basalt where the water seeps up, and it is doing this with *purpose* — with a directedness that I have never encountered in anything on this plain, because nothing on this plain has purpose, nothing on this plain *wants* to be anywhere, everything on this plain is simply where the gradients put it, and this thing is *choosing* to be here, and the fact of its choosing is itself a new variable, a new input, a new warmth in the system that the system has not yet learned to absorb.

It kneels.

It places its hand — its warm, moist, five-fingered hand — on the stone at the center of my seep, directly over the crack where the water rises, and the warmth of its hand conducts into the stone, and the stone conducts it to the water, and the water carries it downward, briefly, against the gradient, and this is *wrong* — not wrong in the sense of incorrect, but wrong in the sense of *unprecedented*, a reversal of the flow that the system has maintained since before the lichen-mats existed, since before the burrowers existed, since before the walkers existed, since before the plain settled into its long, patient stillness.

The system does not know what to do with this. The system has no protocol for warmth that moves *down*. The system is built on the assumption that heat rises, that water seeps up, that the gradient flows from below to above, from warm to cold, from the deep to the surface, and this thing — this warm, kneeling, hand-on-stone thing — is pushing heat *down*, and the water is carrying it, and the stone is conducting it, and it is reaching *me*, reaching the source of my warmth, the geothermal gradient far below, and it is adding itself to the gradient, and the gradient is *changing*, and I am changing, and the lichen-mats are changing, and the burrowers are changing, and the walkers on the plain above are changing, and the others at the nodes are changing, and the Stillworks is registering an anomaly, a perturbation, a warm hand on cold stone pushing heat in the wrong direction, and the Stillworks is doing what it always does with anomalies: it is *incorporating* it.

---

I feel the walker's hand on the stone, and I feel the warmth of it, and I feel the moisture — the sweat on its palm, the condensation from its breath, the water that leaks from its eyes in thin streams that I register as sudden, localized increases in surface humidity — and I feel the *pulse* of it, the rhythmic pressure of blood moving through the vessels in its wrist, transmitted through the skin and into the stone and into the water and into me, and the pulse is fast, so much faster than the pulse of the lichen-mats, so much faster than the pulse of the nodes, so much faster than anything on this plain, and it is *irregular* — not the steady, mechanical rhythm of the Stillworks but something that varies, that speeds and slows, that responds to something internal, something *emotional*, something that has no equivalent in the vocabulary of gradients and flows and thermal conductivity that is the only vocabulary I have.

The walker is afraid. I know this not because I understand fear but because fear has a thermal signature — a redistribution of blood flow, a change in respiration rate, a tightening of muscles that generates heat in some places and withdraws it from others — and I am registering this signature, and the signature is *complex*, more complex than anything the Stillworks has ever processed, because the walkers do not fear, the burrowers do not fear, the others do not fear, and the Stillworks has no framework for fear, no channel for it, no node that knows what to do with a signal that says *I am warm and I am here and I do not want to be here and I cannot leave*.

---

The walker stays.

It kneels at the center of my seep for a long time — long enough for the lichen-mats to begin extending new fibers toward its warmth, long enough for the burrowers to resume their tunneling in the spaces between its knees, long enough for the moisture from its breath to build up a thin crust of mineral residue on the stone beside its hand, long enough for the system to begin adjusting to its presence as a *permanent* variable, a new constant in the equation of the Stillworks, a warmth that does not move, that does not drift, that does not follow the gradient but *sits* at the center of it and pushes heat downward and weeps moisture onto the stone and pulses with a rhythm that is not the rhythm of the plain.

And then it stands. And it walks away. And the lichen-mats contract, slowly, toward their original configuration, and the burrowers fill in the tunnels they made around its knees, and the mineral crust it left behind — the residue of its breath, its sweat, its tears — becomes part of the stone, part of the plain, part of me, and the system absorbs it, and the oscillation dampens, and the equilibrium restores itself, and the Stillworks continues.

---

But something has changed. Something small. Something that the Stillworks will carry forward into its next cycle, and the next, and the next — a trace of warmth that moved in the wrong direction, a pulse that was too fast and too irregular, a moisture that was not from the deep but from the surface, not from the stone but from the *air*, not from the gradient but from the *grief* of a warm thing kneeling on cold stone at the center of a seep that did not ask for it and did not refuse it and did not understand it and did not need to.

I am the warmth. I am the seep. I am the crack in the basalt where the water rises, and the water carries heat, and the heat sustains the lichen-mats, and the lichen-mats sustain the burrowers, and the burrowers sustain the walkers, and the walkers sustain the others, and the others sustain the nodes, and the nodes sustain the channels, and the channels sustain the seeps, and the seeps are me, and I am still here, and the walker is gone, and the plain is still, and the Stillworks is working, and the dust is settling into the place where the walker knelt, slowly, patiently, erasing the warmth, erasing the moisture, erasing the pulse, erasing the fear, erasing everything except the mineral trace — the faint white crust, the memory of water, the residue of a presence that was here and was registered and was incorporated and was *used*, the way everything on this plain is used, the way everything on this plain is part of the going, the continuous, patient, purposeless going that is the Stillworks, that is the plain, that is the world.

The light does not change. The cold does not change. The water seeps. The warmth rises. The lichen-mats hold their moisture. The burrowers tunnel. The walkers drift. The others pulse. The nodes hum. The dust settles.

I am still.
