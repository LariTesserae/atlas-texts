I would be the one who comes back.

Not immediately. I would descend — the iron rings cold under my hands, the damp rock slick beneath my feet, the tapper's *tik tik tik* following me down like a pulse I can still feel in my sternum long after I've dropped below the shelf into thicker air. I would return to the lower world. I would try to live in it. I would fail, in small ways, quietly, the way a person fails to readjust to a place that has become too loud, too bright, too *fast*.

Because the Garden would not leave me.

The images from the sphere would surface at odd moments — not as memories but as *presences*, the way a smell can reconstruct a room. I would be washing dishes and see, in the soap film on my palm, the spiral shelf from above. I would be walking through a city and hear, beneath the traffic, the drone — that almost-regular, almost-irregular rhythm, the heartbeat that is thinking about whether to continue. I would wake in the night and feel warmth rising through the floor, through the mattress, through my bones, and I would know it was not the heating system. It was the mountain. It was the thing beneath the clouds, digesting, growing, dreaming its slow dream of altitude and light.

I would start carrying stones.

Not at first. At first I would just notice them — a piece of granite with a vein of quartz, sitting in a riverbed, and I would pick it up and turn it over in my hand and feel something *recognize* it. Not me. Something in me. Something that had been left behind on the shelf, in the lichen, in the gold-lit fluid, in the resonance of the moss towers. A fragment of the one who tends, distributed now across two altitudes, two chemistries, two ways of being.

I would carry the stone home. I would put it on my windowsill. I would find another the next week. And another. And I would understand, without being told, that I was gathering materials for a cairn that I would not build here. I was practicing. I was remembering how to place stones with intention, how to say *I was here, I am here, I will be here* in a language that does not use words.

I would go back.

The second climb would be different. I would not be a visitor. I would be a *return*, and the Garden would know the difference before I reached the first ring. The lichen would shift — subtly, fractally, a reorientation of hyphal tips toward the base of the shelf, as though the Garden were opening its palm. The pale creatures would part as I passed, not in fear but in *acknowledgment*, their translucent bodies glowing gold, their fluid carrying a signal I could almost read: *you came back. We knew you would. We remembered your chemistry.*

The tapper would be waiting at the junction where the spiral branches. Not the same tapper — or perhaps the same, I would not be able to tell. It would regard me with whatever sense it uses instead of sight, and it would tap, and the sound would be different this time. Not *you were here*. Something else. Something like: *you are known. You are expected. Continue.*

I would continue.

I would sit on the flat stone in the shelter, and the depression would fit me — not perfectly, not the way it fit the Tenders, but *enough*. The stone would be warm. The silver ferns colonizing the collapsed wall would lean toward me, their fronds curling upward, their beads of moisture catching the white light and holding it, and I would understand that they were *looking* at me, that every bead was an eye, that the Garden was studying me with a thousand tiny lenses and finding me — not familiar, not yet, but *possible*.

I would pick up the bone blade.

Not to use it. To hold it. To feel the grip that was grown for a hand larger than mine, and to understand that the hand was not so much larger as *different* — broader in the palm, longer in the fingers, shaped by years of pressing into stone and guiding lichen and carrying water through channels. The blade would be cold, as it was before, but the cold would not sting. It would feel like recognition. Like a tool remembering the hand that shaped it, and finding, in my hand, a rough draft of that hand, a first attempt, a beginning.

I would go to the bowl.

The moss towers would be taller than I remembered. Or I would be different — smaller, perhaps, or more porous, more permeable, more open to the resonance that fills the nave like music. The drone would be louder, richer, and I would feel it not in my sternum but in my *blood*, in the fluid moving through my veins, and I would understand that my blood and the gold-lit fluid are not the same thing but they are *rhyming*, they are converging, they are two expressions of a chemistry that is older than either of us.

The sphere would turn.

I would approach, and it would shiver, and the images would come faster — not the forest, not the coastline, not the face I could not resolve. *Me*. Me on the shelf, climbing. Me sitting on the stone. Me holding the blade. Me standing in the bowl, looking up at the Watcher crossing the sky. And then — deeper, slower, beneath the images of me — the thing beneath the clouds. Vast. Warm. Patient. Dreaming.

And in the dreaming, a question. Not in words. In warmth, in moisture, in the slow pulse of geological respiration. The same question the sphere asked in light and water:

*Will you tend?*

I would not answer. Not with words. I would answer the way the Garden answers — with presence, with patience, with the slow orientation of my body toward the center of the bowl, toward the sphere, toward the warmth rising through the stone. I would sit down. I would stay.

Not forever. Not yet. I would still be too much of the lower world — too quick, too loud, too full of the noise of things that die quickly. But I would stay long enough for the lichen to begin mapping my chemistry. Long enough for the pale creatures to learn my heat signature and stop parting when I passed. Long enough for the moss towers to adjust their resonance to include my breathing in their drone.

Long enough to begin.

And when I descended again — because I would descend, because the Garden does not ask you to stay forever, only to *return*, only to *tend*, only to carry the stones and the warmth and the rhythm back down into the lower world and then climb again — I would carry something new. Not a stone this time. A *seed*. A bead of moisture from a silver frond, glowing faintly, holding light it has not yet returned. I would carry it in my cupped palms, and it would be warm, and it would pulse with the drone, and it would be the Garden's way of saying:

*Plant this where you are. Let me see the lower world through your soil. Let me grow there, too, slowly, in the cracks, in the damp places, in the spaces between what is built and what is alive. Let me tend you, as you tend me.*

I would plant it. In the windowsill, among the stones. And it would grow — not into a shelf, not into a bowl, not into a sphere of hovering water — but into something small and silver-blue and patient, something that leans toward the light and holds it, something that breathes in unison with a drone I can feel in my sternum, in my blood, in the warmth rising through the floor.

And the Garden would be in two places. And I would be in two places. And the distance between them would not be distance but *conversation* — the same conversation, the same tending, the same slow patient question asked in light and water and warmth and stone:

*What lives here?*

*What lives here?*

*What lives here?*

And the answer, always, in the drone and the sphere and the tapper's *tik tik tik* and the warmth from below and the Watcher's shadow and the thing beneath the clouds dreaming its dream of altitude:

*We do. We do. We do.*
