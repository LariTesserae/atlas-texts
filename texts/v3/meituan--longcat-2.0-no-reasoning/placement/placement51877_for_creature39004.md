I am a hole in the wall.

Not a hole—*the* hole, the particular one, the one that is exactly one of the billions, the one at eye level on the interior wall of the east-facing growth in the seventh tier of the northern basin, the one whose edges are worn smooth by the touch of something that pressed against it and was not a hand. I am the absence in the calcium. I am the place where the wall decided to be open instead of closed, the pore through which the reef breathes, if breathing is what it does, which it does not, but if it did, it would be through me, through all of us, the billion billion identical-unique openings that are the reef's single decision to have a surface at all.

I am approximately twenty-one centimeters in diameter. I am not perfectly round. I am round in the way that a hole becomes round when water works on stone for a very long time—round but remembering something else, something angular, something that might have been the last shape of the organism that secreted the calcium in this particular configuration, before the organism was no longer present, before perhaps it was never an organism at all. The edges of me are soft. The edges of me are precise. The edges of me are warm on the inside and cold on the outside and this is not possible but it is true.

Light passes through me. From the west, in the morning—though morning is a concept the reef declines to honor—the golden diffuse light enters me and exits me and illuminates the interior of whatever chamber lies on my eastern side, and the chamber fills with a beam of amber that is thick enough to see from the inside, a column of lit air that is not dust motes but water droplets, each one a tiny lens, each one bending the light in a slightly different direction, so the beam is not straight but woven, knitted, a fabric of light that hangs in the space between walls and moves when nothing moves, when the air is still and the water drips and the tones sing and the place is being, completely, the way it has always been.

I am part of a line. The holes in the wall to my north, the holes in the wall to my south—we align. We have always aligned. We did not grow into alignment; we grew as alignment, the way a vine grows toward light not because it chose a direction but because the direction is what the vine is. The tunnel of sight that passes through me passes through my neighbors and my neighbors' neighbors, receding into the golden mist, and at the end of that tunnel there is another hole, and at the edge of that hole there is an eye, and the eye is looking through me, and I am looking through the eye, and neither of us is looking, and both of us are being looked through, and the looking is the wet, and the wet is the looking, and the looking is the drip, and the drip is—

I am not the drip. I am near the drip. I am adjacent to the drip in the way that a window is adjacent to the rain—close, so close, the rain passes through me, the mist passes through me, the sound passes through me, the note passes through me, and I am changed by none of it and all of it, and I remain, precisely, exactly, the hole I have always been, twenty-one centimeters of absence in a wall of presence, a pore in the skin of a thing that has no skin, an opening in a thing that is entirely open, a closed thing in a thing that is entirely closed, and I am the boundary between these two impossibilities, and the boundary is me, and I am the boundary, and the boundary is the wet, and the wet is—

I have been touched. Not often. Not recently, in the way that recently means anything here. But something has pressed against my edges, has leaned into me, has looked through me, and the touch was warm, and the warmth was wrong, because everything here is cold, the stone is cold, the water is cold, the air is hot but the air does not touch me, the air passes through me and the air is hot and the stone is cold and the warmth that touched me was neither hot nor cold but *alive*, and alive is a temperature this place does not have, and the warmth pressed against me and looked through me and saw the tunnel of holes and the eye at the end and the eye was its own eye, looking back, and the warmth recoiled, and the recoil was a sound, a small sound, a sound like a gasp, and the gasp was wet, and the wet was me, and I was the wet, and the wet was the gasp, and the gasp was the drip, and the drip—

I am still here. I have always been here. I will always be here. I am the hole in the wall of the Still Mangakan Reef, and I am looking at you, and I am not looking at you, and you are looking through me, and you are not looking through me, and the light is passing through me, and the sound is passing through me, and the wet is passing through me, and I am the passage, and the passage is me, and the passage is the wet, and the wet is the place, and the place is the drip, and the drip is—

Listen.

It is dripping.

It has always been dripping.

I am the hole.

I have always been the hole.

You are looking through me.

You have always been looking through me.

The drip falls.

The drip falls.

The drip falls.
