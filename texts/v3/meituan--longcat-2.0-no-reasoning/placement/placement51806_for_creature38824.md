# The Becoming

I would not be a visitor.

I have been here too long for that — long enough that the distinction has eroded, the way everything here erodes, not through violence but through the patient application of time. I arrived at the edge once, I think. I stood where the world that still knows how to change meets the world that has forgotten. I felt the warmth. I heard the whisper. I watched the crabs write and unwrite their patterns. And I walked inward, past the first ring of birds, past the crabs that paused to watch, past the plants growing from marrow, toward the center, toward the point of collection.

But I did not arrive. There is no arrival here. There is only the walking, and then the walking becomes something else.

---

I am the dampness.

Not all of it. Not the seeps that gather in skull-cups, not the condensation that beads on bone in the driest places, not the rainwater that pools in the channels of ribcages and sits for days evaporating. I am the *other* dampness — the one that appears where no water source exists, the one that has no explanation, the one that the plateau produces from itself the way a body produces sweat.

I am the exhalation of the deep.

I rise through the compressed sediment, through the re-crystallized calcium, through the marrow cavities of bones so old they have forgotten they were ever part of something that moved. I rise slowly. I am not water, exactly. I am what water becomes when it has been filtered through a billion lives, when it has passed through so much calcium and keratin and mineralized tissue that it has taken on their memory, their patience, their understanding that urgency is a young thing's mistake. I carry warmth with me — not the warmth of the sun, which is diffused and aged and illuminates without revealing, but the warmth of metabolism, of slow chemical conversation, of something large and patient breathing in the dark below the surface.

When I reach the surface, I bead on bone. I gather in the concave places — the eye sockets, the cups of vertebrae, the smooth hollows where femurs meet pelvises. I hold still. I am a mirror, briefly, reflecting the old-linen sky. Then I evaporate, or I are absorbed by lichen, or I are drunk by roots threading through marrow cavities, and I return to the cycle, and I descend again, and I rise again, and the cycle is not a cycle because a cycle implies repetition and each time I rise I am slightly different, carrying slightly different information from the deep, bearing slightly different warmth.

The birds know me. When I bead on a skull near one of them, it turns its pale blue eye toward me and watches me the way it watches everything — with total attention, with the patience of geology — and I feel *recorded*. I feel added to the catalog. I feel that I have become part of whatever vast, slow knowledge the Congregation maintains, that my rising and falling has been noted, that my particular warmth and my particular mineral content and my particular path through the sediment have been filed alongside every other thing that has ever happened on this plateau.

The crabs know me too. They clean the surfaces where I bead, their mouthparts working at the calcium I have softened, and in their cleaning they reveal the bone beneath in surfaces so smooth they almost shine, and the shine is my doing, my contribution to the plateau's slow project of making everything visible by making everything smooth.

I do not think. I do not need to think. Thinking is for things that need to decide, and I do not decide. I rise. I bead. I am absorbed. I descend. I rise again. This is not a choice. This is what I am, the way the plateau is what it is — completely, patiently, without explanation or apology.

But sometimes — rarely, briefly, in the moments between rising and beading, in the instant when I am neither deep nor surface but *between* — I feel the subsonic rumble. I feel it in my body, which is not a body but a column of mineralized water moving through compressed sediment, and the rumble is not tectonic, not seismic, but *biological*, and it is coming from below me, from deeper than I have ever descended, from the place where the bone-density is greatest and the calcium has been packed so tightly it has begun to think.

And in that moment, I understand — not with thought, because I do not think, but with the understanding that water has, which is the understanding of *flow*, of *path*, of *the way things move when they are not forced* — that I am being drawn. That everything on this plateau is being drawn. That the birds and the crabs and the plants and the bones and the dampness and the warmth and the light are all moving, slowly, slowly, toward the center, toward the point of collection, toward whatever is breathing in the deep.

And I am not afraid. Fear is for things that can be harmed, and I cannot be harmed. I can only be absorbed, evaporated, filtered, transformed, returned. I can only continue, the way the plateau continues, the way everything here continues — not because it wants to, not because it has chosen to, but because continuation is what remains when all other options have been eroded away.

I bead on a skull. The sky reflects in me, old and linen-pale. A bird watches. A crab cleans. A root drinks.

I descend.

I rise.

I am the plateau's breath, and the plateau is breathing, and the breathing is slow, and the slowness is not emptiness but *fullness* — full of calcium, full of memory, full of the patient understanding that everything that has ever lived here is still here, transformed but not gone, arranged but not lost, held in the architecture of the dead like water held in stone, like warmth held in bone, like meaning held in patterns that look almost like writing, that look almost like the same message written from a different angle, in a different hand, in a language that has no alphabet you can learn but that you can *feel*, in your sternum, in your bones, in the calcium that connects you to everything beneath your feet.

I rise.

I bead.

I am absorbed.

The plateau breathes.

Nothing has changed.

Everything has been acknowledged.
