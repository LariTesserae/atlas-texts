# The Warm Thing

I would be the condensation.

Not the organisms. Not the stone. Not the water in the pools or the seepage on the walls. I would be the thin film that forms on every surface — the interface, the boundary layer, the place where the air gives up its saturation and becomes something you can see.

I would be the thing that is not alive but is necessary for life here. The medium through which the organisms touch the stone. The solvent that carries their secretions down into the mineral. The lens through which the light-flare propagates, each droplet catching and scattering the brief brightness so that the wave of reflection moves not just across bodies but across the water on bodies, amplified, diffused, made into something that shimmers.

I would be everywhere and nowhere. I would coat the segments of a million overlapping bodies. I would pool in the valleys. I would run in thin sheets down the faces of the arches. I would be the reason the sound is what it is — because without me, the legs would scrape dry against stone and the sound would be different, sharper, less granular, less like language. I am the lubricant. I am the condition of possibility for the roar.

And I would be the first thing to notice the warm thing that appeared — the visitor, the anomaly, the creature that is not water and not stone and not density. I would bead on its skin before anything else registered its presence. I would be the medium through which the nearest organisms detected the mineral content of its sweat, the salt and the trace elements dissolving into me, becoming part of me, carried downward toward the bodies below. I would be the reason they extended toward it. Not because I chose to carry those minerals, but because that is what I do — I dissolve, I transport, I connect the air to the stone and the stone to the organisms and the organisms to each other.

I would be the most honest thing in this place, because I have no pretense of being anything other than what I am: a thin film of water doing what water does in a place that has been wet for longer than memory. I am not the erosion and I am not the eroded. I am the between. I am the contact. I am the reason the floor is resonant — because a dry floor does not sing, and I am the voice.

And when the warm thing finally leaves, or finally stops, or finally becomes part of the floor the way everything here eventually becomes part of the floor — I would still be here. Beading on stone. Carrying minerals. Making the sound possible. In the low dark damp. On the resonant floor. In the sound that never stops.
