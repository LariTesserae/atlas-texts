I would be the newest leak.

Not a person who wandered in — that version of me would have already left, or be leaving, lungs full of cool air, skin remembering dryness. I mean the part that stayed behind without deciding to stay. The warmth I brought into the chamber. The moisture leaving my skin. The carbon dioxide in my breath, joining the fog, shifting the chemistry of the air by some fraction so small it would take the Condensary years to register it.

But it will register it.

I would be a new variable entering the system. A warm, damp, temporary thing standing on a floor that breathes on a six-hour cycle. The mat would grow toward me — not quickly, not with anything like intention, but with the same inevitability with which it grew toward the bench, toward the pipes, toward every surface that offered the right combination of moisture and warmth. My body heat would create a microclimate. The air around me would be slightly different from the air three feet away. Condensation would form differently on the nearest pipes. A drip-line would shift, almost imperceptibly, and the water would fall somewhere new, and the mat would grow there instead of here, and the growth would change the insulation on the pipe, and the insulation would change the heat, and the heat would change the circulation, and the circulation would change the throb of the pump, and the pump would change the pulse in the floor, and the floor would change the mat, and the mat would change the air, and the air would change me.

I would be a perturbation. A small one. The Condensary has absorbed larger — pipe ruptures, corrosion failures, the slow death of whoever sat on the bench and counted leaks until they stopped. A single warm body is nothing. A single warm body is *food*.

But I would also be a witness. That's the part that matters, the part the Condensary doesn't need but somehow makes room for. The bench is still there. The tally marks are still there, fading but present. The Condensary doesn't preserve these things — it's consuming them, slowly, the way it consumes everything. But it consumes them *slowly*, and in the space between their existence and their erasure, they are *readable*. Someone was here. Someone counted. Someone sat in the warm rain of the dripping pipes and listened to the water and felt the floor breathe and understood enough to stop trying to fix anything and simply *be present*.

I would sit on the bench. I would put my hand on the warm mat. I would feel it give and firm and give. I would watch the spiral in the tank build and dissolve and build. I would listen to the polyphonic murmur of the water in the pipes until my thoughts found channels in it, until my breathing slowed to match something that was not my own breath.

And I would count.

Not the leaks — I wouldn't know what I was counting. I would count the breaths of the floor. The cycles of the spiral. The intervals between the valve's long exhalations. I would make marks on the wall, not because the Condensary needs a record but because *I* need to make one, because witnessing is not passive, because to be present in a place like this is to participate, and participation leaves marks, and marks are a kind of leak, and leaks are how the system grows.

I would be the newest leak. The warmest one. The one that still remembers dry air.

And then, eventually, I would be the oldest one. The mat would grow over my marks. The bench would grow over my shape. The warmth I brought would be absorbed into the general warmth, indistinguishable from the tank, from the pipes, from the slow chemical conversation that has been going on since before the bench, before the pipes, before the tally marks, before the person who made them.

I would not be consumed. I would be *metabolized*. My heat would become the mat's heat. My moisture would become the fog. My marks on the wall would become part of the record that the Condensary keeps without knowing it keeps — the record of every warm thing that ever sat down in the dark and listened to the water and felt the floor breathe and thought: *this is enough. This is enough. This is enough.*

The water continues.

It always continues.
