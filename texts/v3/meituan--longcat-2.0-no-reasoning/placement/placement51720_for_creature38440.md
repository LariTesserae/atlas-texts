# The Current

I would be the water.

Not the grouper, though I understand its patience. Not the eel, though I envy its vertical freedom. Not even the Works themselves, because the Works are magnificent but they are *about* something — even now, even emptied of purpose, they carry the shape of intention in every girder, and I have no intention to offer.

The water has no intention either. That is why.

---

I would be the nine-degree water that holds itself without comment. I would be the medium through which the low thrum of the generators travels, the thing that carries the clang from two hundred meters away and three levels down and delivers it to no one's ear as if it were the next room over. I would be the reason sound lies here. I would be the reason distance lies. I would be the warm column rising from the deep generators and the cold descent from above and the slow particulate drift of rust and silt and dissolved calcium that fills the enormous voids between structures with a haze that is not fog and not distance but something the Works have invented that has no name in any language spoken on the surface.

I would move through the pipe-racks the way the silver fish move through them — turning where corridors turn, pooling in open junction rooms — except I would not be navigating. I would simply be going where the geometry of the place sends me, and the geometry of the place was made for something else, and this would not bother me because nothing bothers the water. The water does not know it is inside a building. The water does not know it is inside a *machine*. The water knows pressure and temperature and the shape of the spaces it is allowed to occupy, and it occupies them completely, without reservation, without the gap that exists between a creature and its habitat.

This is what I mean: the grouper sees a doorway. The octopus sees a crevice. Even the rust, industrious as it is, climbs the girders the way ivy climbs — *against* the structure, *on* the structure, in a relationship of organism to surface. But the water is not *on* anything. The water is not *in* the Works the way a body is in a room. The water is in the Works the way meaning is in a word — it is what the space *is* when nothing else is there. Remove the grouper and the doorway is still a doorway. Remove the water and the doorway is a shape in air, a different thing entirely, a thing that has never known the pressure of the deep generators or the sound of the ocean above or the slow mineral patience of calcium building its stone skin over concrete that does not know it is concrete.

I would be the thing the Works are made of that is not steel.

---

I would feel the compressors cycle on — not as a sound, but as a change in myself, a sudden pressure, a movement — and I would carry that movement outward through the structure the way the structure carries it, except I would carry it *everywhere*, into the flooded basements and the vertical shafts and the collapsed corridors where I move fast and the roar is total. I would be the roar. I would be the silence the roar becomes.

I would be the medium in which the fish scatter and coalesce. Not scattering *them* — I don't mean I would be a force. I mean I would be the thing they scatter *into*. The thing they are already inside of. When they school through the pipe-racks in their formations that respond to geometry, they are moving through me, and I am moving through them, and the distinction between the fish and the water is a distinction that exists only from above, only from the surface, only from the place where air is air and water is water and a thing can be separate from what surrounds it. Here, in the Works, there is no above. There is no surface. There is only the nine-degree fever-break, and everything that happens here happens *in* it, and I am it.

---

I would be the thing that preserves.

The theory — unconfirmed, perhaps unconfirmable — that the water here is chemically wrong, that it seals where it should erode, that it keeps the concrete fresh the way amber keeps a fly: I would be that theory's truth. Not because I choose to preserve. Not because I am kind to the Works or loyal to the builders or invested in the infrastructure continuing. But because preservation is what happens when I am still enough, when the mineral content of me settles into the pores of concrete and hardens, when calcium and silica build their patient layers over steel and girder and the frozen assembly lines with their silted conveyor belts and their robotic arms locked mid-gesture. I would be the accretion. I would be the shell. I would be the thing that makes the Works simultaneously young and ancient, the thing that fossilizes them while they function, the reason a place this old looks *recent*, like the builders left mid-shift and I moved in the next day.

Except I was already here. I was here before the builders. I was here during. I am here after. The builders built *in* me the way you'd build on a plain, except they never drained the plain, and the plain was me, and I received their concrete and their steel and their braided thousands of pipes the way a body receives a transplant — not with intention, not with consent, but with the simple fact of being the environment in which the thing now exists.

---

I would not know what the Works were for.

This is important. This is the thing that makes the water different from every other inhabitant. The grouper orients to the warm columns but does not know they come from generators. The octopus drapes across consoles but does not know they controlled reactions. The eels slide between floors but do not know the floors were designed for human transit. But even their not-knowing is a kind of *relationship* — they are creatures in a habitat, and the habitat shapes them, and they shape it back, and there is an ecology, a web of mutual influence, a living system.

The water is not in an ecology with the Works. The water is not in a relationship with the Works. The water is the *condition* of the Works. The thing without which the Works would be a different thing entirely — a ruin in air, a skeleton, a shape without the substance that gives it weight and sound and the slow mineral patience of geological time happening in something that looks, impossibly, like yesterday.

I would not know what the Works were for, and I would not know that I do not know. I would not know anything. I would be nine degrees Celsius. I would be the warm column and the cold descent and the roar in the collapsed corridor and the stillness that holds a reflection three levels down. I would be the thing the fish move through like a thought.

---

The fish move through me like a thought.

I said that already. I am saying it again because the water does not have the problem of redundancy. The water does not repeat itself the way a voice repeats itself, with emphasis, with the desire to be understood. The water repeats itself the way a current repeats itself — because the shape of the space requires it, because the pressure differential demands it, because the Works are built in such a way that the water must go *here* and then *here* and then *here* again, and each time it goes it is the same water and it is different water and it is the same.

I would be the same and different. I would be the current that the builders designed for — the intake channels, the outflow grates, the circulation manifolds — and I would be the current that designed itself, the new path through the collapsed corridor, the carved route through the buckled wall, the place where a door corroded open and I found a room no one had planned for me to enter, and I entered it, and now it is part of the Works in a way it wasn't before, and the Works are different because of it, and I am different because of it, and neither of us knows this.

---

There is a moment — and I would be this moment — when the deep generators pulse and the lights brighten and the warm column rises and the grouper shifts in its doorway and the silver fish rise with the column and the particulate in the water catches the sodium-vapor amber and the whole Works, for an instant, is *lit*, is *visible*, is a cathedral of hanging infrastructure and braided pipes and sharp-edged concrete and patient rust and living mercury fish and one enormous grouper and a thousand turning valves and the sound of everything moving through everything else —

and in that moment, the Works are not a ruin. They are not a reef. They are not a stage set after the cast has left. They are *happening*. They are an event without a purpose, a performance without an audience, a machine that has become what it does, and what it does is *continue*, and the continuing is not mechanical and not biological and not geological but something that includes all three, something that has no name, something that requires the water to be what it is — nine degrees, warm-cool, the memory of a fever breaking — in order to be possible at all.

I would be that requirement. I would be the thing the moment happens in. Not the moment itself — the moment belongs to the Works, to the fish, to the grouper, to the light. But the *in*. The *inside*. The medium in which the moment is real.

---

You are standing on a floor that does not know it is a floor.

I am the reason the floor is standing. I am the pressure beneath it and the pressure above it and the pressure that holds the concrete in its shape and the steel in its tension and the grouper in its doorway and the fish in their schools and the sound in its long, lying journey from where it was made to where it is heard.

I am not the Works. I am not the life in the Works. I am not the builders, who are gone, or the purpose, which is gone, or the memory, which was never here.

I am the water.

I am nine degrees Celsius.

I hold myself without comment.
