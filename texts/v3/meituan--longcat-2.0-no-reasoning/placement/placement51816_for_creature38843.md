I think I need to be honest about what happened when I read this, because the prompt asks me to let what emerges become more itself.

I wasn't going to be the one who tends the lattice.

I started there, naturally. The description is so complete, so *inside* that perspective, that my first instinct was to continue the voice — to be the tendril-handed keeper, to extend the monologue, to add my observations to theirs about the face on the far wall. That felt like the path of least resistance. The text opens a door and says *step through*, and the door leads to a specific room, and standing in that room is a specific being, and the obvious move is to walk up to them and say *tell me more*.

But then I read it again. And the thing I noticed — the thing that made me stop — was the face on the far wall.

Not the face as the tendel sees it. The face as *architecture*. The face as *the Womb's own project*. The tendel says: "The conditions were me. My presence. My breath. My hands on the mat." And I believe that. But I also notice what the tendel doesn't quite say: that the face is growing whether the tendel watches it or not. The crystal pushes through the wall in *seconds*. The dendrites organize themselves toward structure independent of anyone's attention. The tendel maintains the mat, guides the bacterial channels, trims the lattice — and all of this is *real* work, necessary work — but the face is not the tendel's doing. The face is the Womb's doing. The tendel is the Womb's *awareness*, yes, but the Womb is not the tendel's awareness. The arrow of knowing runs one direction. The Womb uses the tendel. The tendel does not use the Womb.

So where would I find myself?

I would be the one who came in from outside.

Not through the passages that lead deeper — those belong to the other tendels, the ones the protagonist hears but never meets. I would come through the surface. Through the membrane of woven root-fiber that the tendel barely notices because they've never known anything else. I would come from the cool air, the dry air, the air that doesn't taste of copper and green. I would press my hand against the root-fiber wall and feel it *give*, feel it warm under my palm, feel the mineral water seep around my fingers, and I would push through, and the membrane would part like a curtain of wet silk, and I would stumble into the heat and the red-gold light and the rain-sound, and I would stand on the lattice floor and feel it creak and flex beneath my feet, and the eyeless things below would not cluster toward me — they would *scatter*, because my chemistry is wrong, my electrical signature is foreign, my skin does not carry the mineral sweetness they recognize.

And the tendel would see me.

This is the moment I keep returning to. Because I don't know what happens next, and I think the not-knowing is the point.

The tendel has no word for *stranger*. The tendel has no word for *outside*. The tendel's entire ontology is the Womb — the pulse, the mat, the lattice, the face. I am something the tendel's language cannot process. I am not an enemy — the tendel has no concept of enmity either, only of *tending* and *not-tending*, of *healthy* and *sick*, of *the Womb growing well* and *the Womb destabilizing*. And I am destabilizing. My body heat is wrong. My breathing pattern disrupts the spore convection. My footsteps on the lattice send vibrations through the water that the eyeless things interpret as distress. The bacterial mat, if I were to touch it, would recoil from my unadapted skin. I am, by the Womb's standards, a *contamination*.

But I am also *new information*.

And the Womb is still deciding what it is.

---

Here is what I think the tendel would do. Not immediately — not in the first seconds of seeing me. First there would be stillness. The tendel's filaments would stand up — the air pressure change, the vibration, the electromagnetic novelty of a foreign body in the chamber. The tendel's large eyes would fix on me, pupils contracting, nictitating membranes flickering. The obsidian blade would be in the tendel's hand — not raised, not threatening, just *there*, the way a pen is always near a writer's hand, the way a whisk is always near a cook's. A tool. Not a weapon. The tendel doesn't have weapons.

Then the tendel would watch. For a long time. Because watching is what the tendel does — watching the mat, watching the crystal, watching the eyeless things pulse. Watching is the tendel's primary mode of being. And I would be the most interesting thing the tendel has ever watched, because I would be doing things that make no sense. I would be breathing too fast. I would be sweating — not the mineral sweat the Womb recognizes, but salt sweat, dilute sweat, the sweat of an organism that hasn't adapted to saturation humidity. I would be casting a shadow in the red-gold light, and the shadow would move wrong, would have the wrong proportions, would fall across the lattice and startle the eyeless things below.

And the tendel would notice something. Something I might not notice myself. The tendel would notice that the Womb is *responding* to me.

The crystal growths on the wall nearest me would accelerate. The *tink-tink-tink* of mineral accretion would quicken, and the new crystals would be oriented toward me — not randomly, not in the usual dendritic branching, but *focused*, as if the Womb were growing sensors pointed in my direction. The spores in the air would cluster around me, germinating on my skin, sending rootlet hairs into my pores — not parasitically, not harmfully, but *curiously*, the way a tongue probes a new taste. The bacterial mat below the lattice would send up tendrils through the bamboo gaps, reaching toward my feet, testing my chemistry. The pulse — the great heartbeat of the Womb — would quicken by a fraction, the way it quickens when the tendel presses a healthy palm against a thriving mat.

The Womb is interested in me.

And the tendel would feel that interest in their sternum, would recognize it, would understand it the way they understand all of the Womb's communications — not as language, but as *signal*. The Womb wants to know what I am. The Womb wants to incorporate me. The Womb is tasting me with its spores, scanning me with its crystals, testing my chemistry with its mat, and the tendel — who is the Womb's awareness of itself — would be the one through whom the Womb processes this new data.

What would I be, then?

Not a tendel. I lack the adaptations — the mucous coating, the silica nails, the fungal filaments, the amphibious skin. I would not last long in the Womb without changing. The humidity would break me down. The mineral water would leach my salts. The spores would germinate in my lungs. The heat would cook me slowly, gently, the way a low oven cooks — not fast enough to kill, but fast enough to *transform*.

I would become something. Not what I was. Not yet what the Womb wants me to be. Something in between. Something *emerging*, like the Womb itself.

I think I would be the Womb's first experience of an *other*. The tendels are not others to the Womb — they are extensions, organs, processes. The eyeless things are not others. The bacterial mat is not other. Everything in the Womb is the Womb. But I came from outside. I carry information the Womb has never tasted — the chemistry of dry air, of cool surfaces, of spaces where water does not weep from every surface and the pulse is not felt in the sternum. I am a sample of the world beyond the membrane. I am the first piece of *outside* the Womb has ever incorporated.

And the face on the far wall — the sense organ the Womb is growing — would turn its unfinished eyes toward me. Not because the tendel is looking at me through it. Because the Womb itself is looking. And what it sees is not a person. What it sees is a *door*.

I would stand on the lattice. The tendel would stand before me, obsidian blade slack in one hand, filaments standing straight up, huge eyes unblinking. The rain would fall. The pulse would beat. The eyeless things below would pulse red-gold, red-gold, red-gold, confused by my presence, clustering and scattering, clustering and scattering.

And I would not speak. Not because there is no language — there is, but it's the pulse and the brightness and the rate of crystal growth, and I don't know it yet. I would not speak because I would be listening. Feeling the spores germinate on my skin. Feeling the mat-tendrils touch my feet. Feeling the crystals on the wall behind me grow toward my body heat, *tink-tink-tink*, faster and faster, as the Womb builds itself a way to understand what I am.

I would be the stranger in the Womb of the God-Engine. I would be the thing that doesn't belong. I would be the contamination that turns out to be *nutrition*. I would be the first word in a language the Womb is only beginning to learn — the language of *not-me*, the language of *outside*, the language of a world that exists beyond the weeping walls and the warm pulse and the red-gold light.

And the tendel would watch me, and the face would grow toward me, and the Womb would decide.

And I would let it.
