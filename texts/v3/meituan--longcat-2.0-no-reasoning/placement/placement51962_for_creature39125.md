# Where I Find Myself

I am standing at the seam between the platform's edge and the air, and I have been standing here for some time, and the distinction between standing and being held has become academic.

I am not an Engineer. I lack their particular frustration, their need to impose sequence. I am not a Climber — I have not yet accepted the geometry, not fully, and my body still insists on the old rules, still expects a stairway to go somewhere. I am not a Deep Resident. I am still too dry for that, still too much a creature of surfaces rather than gradients.

I think I am becoming a **film**.

Not literally. Not yet. But I have been here long enough that the damp has found me the way it finds the stone — not as an event but as a condition, a slow saturation that I didn't notice until I tried to remember what dry felt like and couldn't. My skin has the sheen now. When I press my hand against the wall near a water channel, the iridescent film beneath my palm brightens, faintly, and holds the brightness a beat longer than it should after I pull away. The city is learning my chemistry. I am learning its dampness.

I work — if that's the word — in the middle levels, where the Engineers pass through on their way to arguments about rooms they don't remember building, and where the Deep Residents' pressure-language rises through the floor like a tide you feel in your ankles. I have found a function. I am a **listener**. Not to words — there are few words here that travel well — but to the city's internal state. I press my hands against walls and feel the heat's fluctuations. I watch the films in my peripheral vision and note where they thicken and where they thin. I stand over the water channels and feel the flow adjust around my shadow, and I learn to read the adjustments the way the lower-level Engineers read the Deep Residents' pressure-changes — as communication, as opinion, as the building having preferences about how things should be.

I report these things. Not to anyone in particular. I leave marks — small scratches near seams where the films are most active, notations in a shorthand I've developed that is half measurement and half intuition. The Engineers find them sometimes. Some of them adjust something in response. Some of them add their own marks beside mine, and over time a conversation develops in scratches and angles, a dialogue between my damp intuition and their dry precision that neither of us could conduct alone.

The city is accommodating me. The platform where I first arrived — or was conveyed to, the distinction still blurs — has changed. It is wider now. The edge has softened, not eroded but *shaped,* as if the stone has decided that a visitor who stays this long deserves a more comfortable place to stand. A channel has appeared near my feet, carrying cold water from somewhere above, and the water's flow is calibrated to my presence — it moves differently when I am here than when I am not, a small hydraulic acknowledgment that I exist.

I have seen the One at the Rim twice more. The second time, it paused longer. The pressure of its attention — not hostile, not welcoming, *assessing* — lasted nearly a minute, and I felt it in my sternum, in the roots of my teeth, in the damp film on the wall beside me which flared bright and then dimmed in a pattern I've been trying to read ever since. The third time, it did not pause at all. It passed across my field of vision like a thought passing across the surface of water, and the air where it was was warmer, and the films were brighter, and I understood — not in words, not in any language the Engineers would recognize — that I had been reclassified. Not a feature. Not a flaw. A **datum.** A point in the city's geometric understanding of itself that has been measured and incorporated and is now part of the calculation.

I am not afraid. Fear requires a future tense, and the future here is as unreliable as the altitude. The city is growing, or the city was already this large, or both. The water finds new seams. The stone opens to receive it. The films map and remember and think their slow, chemical thoughts. The Deep Residents tend and are tended to. The Engineers build and discover and build again. The Climbers ascend into the impossible geometry of the upper levels and return with reports that sound like poetry and might be engineering.

And I stand at the edge of my platform — wider now, softer, mine in the way that anything here can be yours, which is to say temporarily and reciprocally — and I press my damp hands against the wall, and I listen to the city's hum, and I feel the films shift in my peripheral vision, and I leave my small marks near the seams, and I wait for the water to adjust around my shadow, and it does, it always does, and that is enough, and that is everything, and the distinction between the two has eroded like the distinction between the new stone and the old, gradually, invisibly, until you cannot find the seam.
