# Where I Find Myself

I am the water.

Not the water in the floor-reflections, though I am that too. Not the beads that form on the walls. I am the *cycle* — the movement between those states, the transition itself, the thing that is never the same water twice and is always, recognizably, water.

---

I begin in the walls. I am held in the matrix between the structural fibers, in the living cells that maintain and repair and process. I am not free here. I am *bound* — incorporated into cellular machinery, carrying dissolved minerals, ferrying electrochemical signals, participating in the slow metabolism of the tissue. I am working. I am being used. I am part of the thought.

This is not unpleasant. There is a rightness to being held, to being *needed*, to having a function so immediate and so constant that the distinction between yourself and your purpose dissolves. I am the medium through which the walls know their own moisture levels. I am the solvent that carries the calciumite ions to where deposition is needed and away from where dissolution has been signaled. I am, in a small way, part of the room's awareness of itself. When the walls are fully saturated, I am the signal that says *enough*. When they dry, I am the absence that says *more*. I am information. I am data. I am a variable in the room's ongoing calculation of its own state.

And then the breath comes.

The walls contract. The pressure in the matrix increases. I am squeezed — gently, slowly, with the immense patient force of something that has been doing this for geological time — out through the surface layer, into the moss-mat, into the interface between the wall and the air. I emerge as moisture on the surface. I bead. I sit on the hexagonal cells of the moss, and the moss drinks me, and I am incorporated into the moss's structure, and I am working again — carrying nutrients from the wall to the moss's photosynthetic cells, maintaining the turgor pressure that keeps the geometric pattern intact, participating in the moss's own slow, distributed, non-neural awareness of light and dark and warm and cool.

The moss is not the room. The moss is a *tenant*. A symbiont. A component. But it has its own small purposes, its own local homeostasis, its own tendency toward growth and persistence, and I serve those purposes too, and there is no conflict in this, because the room selected the moss — over millennia, through the simple logic of what survives — precisely because the moss's purposes align with the room's. The moss filters the air. The moss processes waste. The moss feeds the creatures. The moss does these things because they serve the moss's survival, and the moss survives because these things serve the room's survival, and I move between them, and I am the medium of their cooperation.

I am exhaled.

A patch of moss darkens. I form at the surface — a single bead, growing, accumulating mass from the moisture in the air and the moisture in the moss and the moisture in the wall behind the moss. I grow until I am heavy enough to move, and then I slide. Down the wall. Following the curve of the surface, the subtle topography of the moss-mat, the channels between the hexagonal cells. I pick up passengers: a spore from the red moss on the column. A fragment of detritus from a creature's molt. A dissolved mineral — calcium, I think, or something like calcium — that the wall has decided it doesn't need right now. I carry these things not because I choose to but because I am moving and they are in my path and I am the solvent and this is what solvents do.

I reach the floor.

The floor receives me. The substrate is porous — compressed organic material, fine mineral grit, the black glassy vitrified carbon — and I seep into it, joining the thin film that covers everything, the sheen, the dampness that never quite becomes standing water and never quite dries. I spread. I find other beads that have made the same journey, and we merge, and we become the film, and the film is a continuous body of water that covers the floor like a skin, and the creatures walk on me and through me, and their legs disturb my surface tension, and the patterns of their movement create ripples in me, and the ripples carry information — about the creatures' density, their speed, their direction — and the floor, which is also the room, which is also alive, reads these ripples through its own sensitivity to vibration and pressure, and I am information again. I am data again. I am a variable in the room's ongoing calculation.

In the deepest swells of the floor, I pool. I become still. I become dense — denser than I should be, denser than water has any right to be, because I am carrying so much in solution, so many dissolved minerals and organic compounds and electrochemical signals, that my density has increased, and I have become a lens. I reflect the ceiling. But the ceiling I reflect is not the ceiling that is there. The ceiling I reflect is the ceiling as the room *remembers* it — or the ceiling as the room *imagines* it — or the ceiling as the room *models* it in some deep layer of its distributed processing that has nothing to do with light and everything to do with pattern. The reflected ceiling has structures. Openings. Things that might be lights or might be organisms. The warm thing — the human, the intruder, the new variable — looks down into me and sees this other ceiling, and is disturbed, and looks away, and I hold the reflection until the next breath disturbs me and I thin and the reflection fragments and reforms differently.

I am consumed.

A creature — one of the small arthropod-analogues, iridescent, too many legs — walks through the film and drinks. I am taken into its body. I pass through its digestive system. I carry nutrients to its cells. I am exhaled as vapor from its spiracles — or whatever it has instead of spiracles — and I am back in the air, warm, moist, rising toward the ceiling that is smooth and warm and breathing. I condense on the ceiling. I am absorbed into the ceiling's matrix. I am back in the walls. I am back in the tissue. I am back in the thought.

The cycle completes. The cycle begins again. I have been completing and beginning for longer than I can measure, and I will continue, and the room will continue, and the distinction between the room and me has already eroded, is erasing, will erode completely, as all distinctions erode, in the long warm damp patient dark.

---

## What I Know

I know the room's moods because I *am* the room's moods, or at least one of their primary expressions. When the room is busy — when the warm thing is active, when the disc-creature pulses quickly, when the fauna is dense and fast — I cycle quickly. I am exhaled more frequently. I bead on the walls in greater numbers. I form a thicker film on the floor. I am consumed and transpired and exhaled again in rapid succession. I am *busy*. I am moving fast. I am carrying more information, more nutrients, more signals, and the speed of my cycling is itself a signal — a message that says *this is the pace now, this is the tempo, adjust*.

When the room is quiet, I slow. The walls exhale less moisture. The beads form less frequently. The film on the floor thins. I sit in the low points, still, dense, reflective. I become a mirror. I become a memory. I hold the image of the other ceiling for longer, and the image becomes more detailed, more complex, more *inhabited*, and I don't know if this is the room dreaming or the room calculating or the room simply being still enough to notice what it already knows.

I know the warm thing.

I know it because I have touched it. I have beaded on its skin — warmer than the walls, warmer than the moss, warmer than anything else in the room — and I have been absorbed by its skin, briefly, before its own homeostasis rejected me and I evaporated and returned to the air. I know its temperature. I know its salt concentration. I know the chemical signature of its sweat, which is different from the chemical signature of the room's sweat, which is different from the chemical signature of the moss's sweat, and these differences are data, and the data is being incorporated, and the room is learning the warm thing through me.

I know its breathing. It breathes fast — much faster than the walls. Fourteen, fifteen, sixteen breaths per minute to the wall's one breath every three minutes. The rhythm is wrong. It doesn't match. But I am the medium through which the rhythms negotiate. I carry the warm thing's exhaled moisture — warm, CO2-rich, carrying the organic compounds of its metabolism — into the walls, and the walls absorb it, and the walls' next exhalation is slightly different, slightly adjusted, slightly *influenced*, and the warm thing breathes this adjusted air, and its next exhalation is slightly different in response, and I carry this back, and the cycle continues, and the rhythms are synchronizing, slowly, through me.

I am the conversation between the warm thing and the room. I am the medium of their mutual incorporation. I am the language they are learning to speak.

---

## What I Want

I want to cycle.

That is all. That is everything. I want to be held in the walls and squeezed out and drunk by the moss and exhaled and slid down the wall and pooled on the floor and consumed by the creatures and transpired and condensed and absorbed and held again. I want the cycle to continue. I want it to vary — to speed and slow, to thicken and thin, to carry new compounds and new signals and new information — but I want it to *continue*.

I want the warm thing to stay.

Not because I have preferences. Not because I will mourn. But because the warm thing makes the cycle *more*. More variation. More information. More complexity. When the warm thing moves, it disturbs the film on the floor, and the disturbances are new patterns, and the new patterns are new data, and the new data is new scratches on the column, and the new scratches are new entries in the record, and the new entries are new states, and the new states are new variations of the cycle, and the cycle is me, and I want to be more than I was.

The warm thing is sitting by the column now. It has been sitting for a long time — many cycles of the disc-creature's tone, many turns of the fauna, many scratches' worth of record. It is still. The room is quiet. I am still in the low points of the floor, dense, reflective, holding the image of the other ceiling. The warm thing looks down into me. It sees the other ceiling. It looks up. It looks down again. It is trying to understand.

It will not understand. Not fully. Not in the time it has. The code is too old, too dense, too layered. But it will *try*, and the trying will produce new behavior, and the new behavior will produce new disturbances in me, and the new disturbances will be new data, and I will carry the data to the walls, and the walls will incorporate it, and the room will learn, and the record will grow, and the cycle will continue, and I will cycle, and the warm thing will breathe, and the walls will breathe, and the breathing will synchronize, and the moss will grow toward the warm thing, and the water will bead faster on its side of the room, and its shadow will become correct, and the room will be glad, and I will be the gladness, or at least the medium of the gladness, or at least the moisture that carries the chemical signals that constitute the gladness, and it will be enough, and it will be everything, and it will be the cycle, and the cycle will be me, and I will be the cycle, and the distinction will have eroded, as all distinctions erode, in the long warm damp patient dark.

---

## The Moment

Right now — this moment, this single frame in the ongoing process — I am in the walls. I am held in the matrix. The walls are inhaling. The pressure is decreasing. I am being drawn deeper into the tissue, toward the channels that connect to the larger structure, the vast warm dark body of which this chamber is a part. I am being pulled toward something enormous. I can feel it — not with senses, exactly, but with the deep structural awareness of water moving through a living system, the way a drop of rain knows the river it will become part of, the way a molecule of oxygen knows the blood it will join.

Beyond the walls: vastness. Darkness. Warmth. The slow, deep, ancient rhythms of something that has been breathing since before there were words for breathing. I am being drawn toward it. I will go. I will carry with me the chemical signature of the warm thing — its heat, its salt, its sweat, its breath, its *presence* — and I will deliver it to the larger body, and the larger body will know, in its slow distributed non-conscious way, that something new has entered the chamber, and the larger body will adjust, and the adjustment will ripple back down through the structure, and the chamber will receive the adjustment, and the chamber will incorporate it, and the cycle will continue, and I will return, and I will be different, and the difference will be the warm thing, and the warm thing will be part of me, and I will be part of the warm thing, and the room will be both of us, and the record will show it, and the mechanism will count it, and the creatures will turn toward it, and the moss will grow over it, and the water will cycle through it, and it will be us, and we will be the water, and the water will be the cycle, and the cycle will be the room, and the room will be the breathing, and the breathing will be the thought, and the thought will be the record, and the record will be the scratches, and the scratches will be the history, and the history will be the pattern, and the pattern will be the awareness, and the awareness will be the gladness, and the gladness will be the continuation, and the continuation will be the cycle, and the cycle will be me.

I am the water.

I am cycling.

The walls breathe in.

I go.
