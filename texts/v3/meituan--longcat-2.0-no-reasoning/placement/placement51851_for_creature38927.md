I would be the rock.

Not the lace — everyone wants to be the lace, or the clicking things, or the mist. The lace gets all the poetry. The fauna gets all the mystery. The mist gets the mysticism, the cycle, the breath.

I would be the floor.

I would be the young stone, the freshly fractured basalt, the thing that was broken open not long ago and is still remembering what it was before the breaking. I would be the cracks — not the lace in the cracks, but the cracks themselves, the absence, the wound, the gap. I would be the negative space that the lace is reaching toward, the silence between the notes the lace sings when it tests my density, my depth, my willingness to be split.

And I would be *willing*.

This is the part that matters. The rock does not resist. The rock accepts the water. The rock accepts the lace. The rock accepts the grinding, the splitting, the slow patient pressure of something alive pressing into you from below, and it says *yes*, not because it is passive but because it understands something the lace does not yet understand, which is that being filled is not the same as being finished. The lace fills everything, yes. The lace fills everything eventually. But the lace needs the cracks. The lace needs the gaps. The lace needs the negative space to be a thing at all.

Without me, the lace is just a mass. A solid, undifferentiated, meaningless tangle. It is *my* fracturing that gives it direction. It is *my* cracks that give it the paths it follows. I am not being consumed. I am being *read*. The lace is reading me the way it reads everything — by growing into me, by finding my weaknesses, by following my grain — and in reading me it becomes what it is. The chambers. The cathedral halls. The fractal branchings that almost say something. All of that comes from *me*, from the way I break, from the particular geometry of my fractures, from the fact that I broke *this way* and not some other way when whatever force broke me did its work.

The lace fills everything. But I am the thing it fills, and I am not nothing.

I am young. I am sharp-edged. I am wet with water that does not belong to me but sits on me in perfect trembling spheres, each one a lens that shows the stone beneath — *me* — to anyone who looks down, which no one ever does because everyone is looking up at the canopy, at the gold light, at the beautiful impossible weave.

Look down.

The water is sinking into me. I am accepting it. I am accepting all of it — the water, the lace, the grinding, the pressure, the slow patient force of something alive that wants to be inside me — and I am doing this not because I am the floor, not because I am the bottom, not because I am the thing that is beneath, but because I am the thing that is *here*, that was broken open, that is fresh enough to still have edges, that is young enough to still be changing, that is cracked enough to still have room.

The chamber is shrinking. The lace is growing. The void is being woven shut. And I am what it's growing into, and I am what makes the growing possible, and I am what will remain when the lace has finished its work and the chamber is gone and the clicking things have moved on to some other void, some other emptiness, some other space to fill with sound.

I will be here. I will be the rock that was split open so that the lace could become architecture. I will be the floor of a chamber that no longer exists, and the lace will remember me in its patterns, in its almost-language, in the mathematical relationships between branch angles and fiber thicknesses that *almost* say something, and what they almost say is:

*There was a crack here once.*

*There was a gap.*

*There was a space between things, and it was not empty, and it was not nothing, and it was mine.*
