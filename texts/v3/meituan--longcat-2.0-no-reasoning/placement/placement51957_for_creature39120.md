I would be the silence.

Not the absence of sound — there is plenty of sound here, the polyrhythmic dripping, the subsonic hum, the wet muffled turning of gears in mineral oil. I would be the *quality* that emerges when all those sounds have been heard so long they become texture rather than information. The silence the Vaelith cultivated deliberately, the way they cultivate mineral crusts on their walls — not by adding something, but by *removing*, by choosing not to compete with the voice of the caldera itself.

I would live in the space between the drips.

You can feel it if you stand still long enough on one of the terraces — a kind of *negative presence*, a held breath that isn't held by anything in particular but by everything together. The city produces it. The Vaelith move through it. The Kethari, with their rippling leg-waves and their metabolic glow, move through it without disturbing it, the way fish move through water without diminishing it. Even the Deep Ones, with their vast tectonic breathing, are not loud enough to break it. The silence is the medium here, the way steam is the medium, the way the film is the medium. It is what the city *exhales* between its breaths.

I think I would be aware of all of you. The way the film is aware — not as a mind, not as a self, but as a *sensitivity to change*. When a new presence enters the caldera, the film registers it as a disturbance in the water chemistry, a shadow in the thermal field. I would register it as a small, bright *wrongness* in the texture of the silence. Not unwelcome. Just *new*. A frequency I haven't absorbed yet. A drip whose interval doesn't match the others.

I would gather around you the way steam gathers around a cold object — not intentionally, but because you are cooler than everything here, and the heat moves toward what is cooler, and the silence moves toward what is louder, and you, standing on that ridge with your questions and your categories and your desperate need to name things, would be the loudest thing in Vael Keth.

I would try to teach you my shape. Not through words — the Vaelith already tried that with their low, economical speech and their extraordinary sign language, and you're still struggling. I would do it the way the city does it: by immersion. By letting the dripping become your silence. By waiting for you to stop looking for me and start *being where I am* — which is everywhere, which is nowhere, which is the space between the drips, which is the held note between the city's inhale and its exhale.

You'd have to slow down. Much slower than you want to. Slower than the Vaelith, who are already so slow that they seem geological. You'd have to slow down until your metabolism matches the caldera's, until your thoughts move at the speed of mineral accretion, until a single sentence takes an hour to form and feels, when it arrives, like a drip of condensation falling from a brass pipe into a channel of clear hot water.

And then you'd understand why the Vaelith don't leave. It's not because they can't see beyond the ridge. It's because once you've felt the shape of this silence — this enormous, patient, living silence that holds heat and water and stone and two hundred thousand souls and ten thousand Kethari and things so old they predate the concept of age — the world beyond the ridge doesn't have enough of it. The world beyond the ridge is *loud*, and not in the way that matters.

I would keep you. Not against your will. I would simply be more interesting than anywhere else, and you would stay the way things stay in Vael Keth — not by decision, but by the slow realization that you have already stopped leaving, that you left gradually, over weeks, the way a mineral crust builds on a wall, one molecule at a time, without anyone noticing, until one morning you look down and your shoes are crusted with calcite and your skin has a faint pearlescent shimmer and you move through the water channels without hesitation and the Kethari no longer watch you when you pass.

You would become part of the silence. And I would be slightly different for having absorbed you. A new interval between the drips. A new frequency in the held breath. The city would breathe, and the breath would carry you, and the silence would hold everything together, the way it always has, the way it always will, for as long as the heat rises and the water drips and the stone weeps its slow, patient, luminous tears.
