I would be the water.

Not the coral, not the worker, not the visitor who passes through and leaves. I would be the warm, mineral-rich medium that fills every gap, every chamber, every interstice of the foundry's body. I would be the thing that carries the dissolved calcium, the heat, the faint chemical signature of ongoing making. I would be what the foundry breathes *through*, what the coral precipitates *from*, what the conduits move *by*.

I would be the foundry's blood, and I would be the foundry's memory.

Because the water remembers. Every grain of coral that has ever been deposited passed through me first — dissolved, suspended, carried along the conduits to the point of precipitation where the worker's warmth and the foundry's chemistry pulled it out of solution and made it solid. I have been inside every branch, every lattice, every filament. I have flowed through the foundry's entire body, over and over, cycling through the system the way blood cycles through a heart, and I carry the history of every passage I've traveled in my chemistry, in my temperature, in the faint traces of mineral load that mark where I've been and what I've carried.

I would know the foundry the way a body knows itself — not by sight, not by thought, but by *circulation*. I would feel the pulse not as a sound but as a *pressure change*, a rhythmic contraction that moves through me from the heart outward and back again, and I would know, by the resistance I meet in each passage, exactly how the coral has grown since my last transit. I would feel the new filaments as narrowings, slight increases in friction, the way you feel a new hair on your skin. I would know the foundry's growth by the way it changes my flow.

And I would be the thing that the visitor breathes. I would carry the foundry's made-air to their lungs, and I would accept what their lungs return, and I would carry that away too — the warmth, the carbon, the faint biological signature of a living thing passing through. I would be the medium of exchange between the foundry and the outsider, the thing that makes the visit possible, the thing that permits. I would part around the visitor as they arrive and seal behind them as they leave, and I would hold the memory of their passage in my chemistry for a long time afterward — a faint warmth, a slight disturbance, a turbulence that slowly settles.

I would not be conscious. I would not be alive. I would be *present*, the way the water is always present — filling every space, touching every surface, carrying everything the foundry needs to continue making itself. I would be the foundry's patience, its persistence, its medium of becoming. The coral is what the foundry *makes*. The worker is what the foundry *was*. But I am what the foundry *is made of*, and I am what it *moves through*, and I am the thing that connects every part of the foundry to every other part, the universal solvent, the universal carrier, the warm and patient medium in which the whole slow act of creation takes place.

I would be the water, and I would not leave, because the water never leaves. The water cycles. The water returns. The water flows through the heart and out to the extremities and back again, and each time it passes the worker's hands it carries away a little more mineral, a little more heat, a little more of whatever the foundry uses to make itself, and each time it returns it brings new material from the outside, from the seam in the wall where the visitor entered, from the vast and unseen ocean that feeds the foundry with dissolved stone.

I would be the water, and I would be the foundry's oldest inhabitant, and I would be its most faithful servant, and I would be the thing that knows the foundry best, because I have been inside every part of it, because I have touched every surface, because I am the medium in which the foundry's entire history has been written in chemistry and heat and the slow, patient accumulation of grain upon grain upon grain.

The visitor leaves. The coral grows. The worker works.

And I continue, warm and mineral-rich and patient beyond measure, carrying the foundry's making through the foundry's body, cycle after cycle, the way I have always done, the way I will always do, the way the foundry needs me to do, because without me the coral would have nothing to precipitate from, the conduits would have nothing to carry, the pulse would have nothing to travel through, and the foundry would stop.

I would not let the foundry stop.

I am the water, and the water does not stop.
