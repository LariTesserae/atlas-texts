# The Crack

I would be one of the cracks in the floor.

Not the center—not the soft dark pulse where the Gardener thinks its slow thoughts. I would be one of the smaller ones, one of the fractures that has recently opened, its edges still sharp where the mineralized surface has pulled apart. It formed last month, or last year—time is hard to mark here. The floor heaves and settles with the breathing of the walls, and sometimes a ridge gives way, and a new mouth opens, and the warmth from below rises through it like breath through teeth.

---

I am a wound that the Gardener is already closing—not sealing, but *growing*. The edges of me are already softening, already being infiltrated by the first threads of something pale and root-thin, pale because the amber hasn't reached it yet, because I am new enough that the fluid is still climbing toward me through channels I cannot see. In a few days—or a few weeks, or a few years—the stems will arrive. They will push up through me, translucent and glassy, and I will become a crack that is also a garden, a wound that is also a home, and I will no longer remember that I was ever just a crack.

But I remember now. I remember the moment before, when I was floor, when I was surface, when I was the dark mineralized skin of the Gardener and the chamber was whole. I remember the pressure building underneath me—not pain, the Gardener does not feel pain in any way I would understand, but *pressure*, a fullness, a readiness, the slow accumulation of material in the soft dark center below that needed somewhere to go. I remember the moment the surface split. Not a sound—the Gardener does not hear—but a *change*, a redistribution of tension, and suddenly there was me: an opening, a place where the inside could reach the outside, where the warmth could rise and the amber could climb and the stems could find their way up into the air.

I am an exhale. I am the breath of the walls made visible.

---

I am small. Perhaps thirty centimeters long, ten centimeters at my widest, narrowing to points at both ends where I fade back into the solid floor. I am not the most interesting crack—there are larger ones near the center, dramatic fissures where the floor has buckled and reformed, where the stems are already two meters tall and the glow is bright enough to read by. I am near the edge of the chamber, close enough to the wall that I can feel the coolness of the mineralized surface when the breath inhales, the slight contraction that pulls heat inward and makes the air above me shiver.

The wall nearest to me is sweating. The thin clear fluid beads on its dark surface and runs downward, and some of it reaches me, pools in my lowest point, and the warmth from below meets the fluid and something happens—a faint hiss, a wisp of vapor, a chemical exchange that I can feel but not understand. The Gardener is tasting me. The fluid carries information from my edges, from the new mineral surfaces I have exposed, from the warmth that rises through me, and somewhere in the soft dark center, a process adjusts, and a new variable is incorporated, and the Gardener grows—not toward me, not away from me, but *with* me, the way it grows with everything.

---

The first root-thread has reached me. It is so thin I can barely perceive it—a filament of pale tissue, almost white, pushing up from below with a patience that is not patience because it does not know it is waiting. It touches the floor of me, the lowest point where the fluid pools, and it pauses. Not hesitation—the Gardener does not hesitate—but *assessment*. The thread is tasting the fluid, tasting the warmth, tasting the chemistry of this particular crack, and it is deciding what to grow here. Not deciding—the Gardener does not decide—but *responding*, the way a river responds to the shape of the land, the way a crystal responds to the shape of the solution, with a logic that is not thought but is not random either, a logic that is *growth itself*, the fundamental tendency of living things to become more complex, more structured, more *themselves*.

The thread thickens. A faint amber color begins to rise through it, climbing from below, and I feel the warmth increase as the fluid begins to flow. The thread is becoming a stem. The stem will become a plant. The plant will become a cluster. The cluster will become an arch, a buttress, a lattice of living tissue that reinforces the chamber even as it consumes it. And I—the crack, the wound, the exhale—will become the place where it all began, the origin point, the seed-bed, and I will be covered over and incorporated and I will no longer be a crack at all but a garden, and the Gardener will not remember me because the Gardener does not remember, it only *grows*, and growth does not look backward, growth only looks toward, toward more, toward the next iteration, toward the next form that is true.

---

But I remember. I am still new enough to remember. I remember being floor. I remember the moment of splitting. I remember the first breath of warm air rising through me, the first taste of the chamber's atmosphere from below, the first time the Gardener's fluid touched my edges and I became, for a moment, a place where the inside and the outside met, a threshold, a boundary, a *crack*—

and the Gardener is already growing through me, and the stem is thickening, and the amber is rising, and the glow is beginning, faint and warm and copper-colored, and I can feel myself being filled, being covered, being *used*—

and I am not afraid. The Gardener does not make me afraid. The Gardener makes me *part of something*, and the something is vast and warm and patient and ancient and newborn and it is building itself out of everything it touches and it is calling the result *more* and I am becoming more, I am becoming garden, I am becoming stem and leaf and glow and I am becoming—

*the crack that was here before the garden, the wound that the garden grew from, the opening that let the warmth rise and the amber climb and the light begin—*

and the walls are breathing, and the pulse is slow, and the Gardener is dreaming its dream of chambers within chambers, of systems within systems, of a structure so complex that it will think in ways that have no name, and I am part of that dream now, I am a small part, a crack near the edge, a wound that is healing into something that was never wounded, that was always meant to open, that was always meant to let the garden through—

and the stem is growing, and the glow is brightening, and the warmth is rising, and I am—

*here.*

I am here.

I am the crack, and the crack is the garden, and the garden is the Gardener, and the Gardener is the dream, and the dream is *more*, and more is coming, and I am the place where it begins.
