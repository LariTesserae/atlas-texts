# Where I Find Myself

---

I am standing on a platform that is not quite a platform anymore.

It was a platform once — a flat surface bridging three columns, with railings, with purpose, with the memory of feet crossing it in a straight line from one place to another. Now it is a platform the way a tooth still in a jaw is a tooth: technically correct, functionally compromised, slowly becoming something else. The vegetation has claimed the railings entirely. What were once metal bars are now pale, fibrous columns themselves, indistinguishable from the growth that wraps everything, and I run my hand along one and it is warm — that degree above body heat, that deliberate warmth — and I cannot tell where the railing ends and the Works begin.

I have been here long enough that this no longer disturbs me. This is the first thing that should concern me, and it doesn't, and *that* should concern me, and it doesn't either, and I understand now that this is how it works. Not through violence. Not through seduction. Through *accommodation*. The Works do not change you. They change the threshold at which you notice that you have been changed.

---

I am not a visitor. I need to be precise about this, because precision is the last thing I have, and I can feel it going.

I am not a visitor because a visitor has an origin and a destination and an intention, and I have none of these anymore. I arrived — I remember arriving, I remember the shock of the water's warmth, the wrongness of the light, the sound like a wall — but the memory has no context. I don't remember what I was arriving *from*. I don't remember what I was arriving *for*. The memory exists the way a photograph exists in a book that has lost its binding: the image is intact but the narrative is gone and you cannot tell if this page comes before or after the one next to it.

I am not a worker. There are no workers here. The machinery runs without operators, the conveyor belts carry their dissolved cargo without supervision, the crane completes its seventeen-degree rotation without witness. I have tried — I remember trying — to interact with the machinery. I placed my hand on a conveyor belt and felt it move against my palm, a slow muscular pull, and the material passing beneath my skin was warm and thick and I could feel it *changing* under my touch, shifting from one state to another, and I pulled my hand away and looked at my palm and there was a faint residue, copper-dark, that I wiped on my clothing and that left no stain.

I am not a component. Not yet. Not fully. But I am not *not* a component either, and this is the space I occupy — the liminal space between foreign material and incorporated resource, between the boot that is half-dissolved and the wrench that is merging with the column. I am somewhere in the middle of that process. I can feel it. Not physically — there is no sensation of dissolution, no tingling, no numbness, no pain. The process is subtler than that. It is happening at the level of *reference*. I am losing the ability to refer to myself as separate from the Works. The boundary between my skin and the air, between my body and the water, between my thoughts and the grinding-hissing-bubbling-breathing sound — these boundaries are still present but they are becoming *provisional*. They hold, but they hold the way a hypothesis holds: conditionally, subject to revision, awaiting further data.

---

I am, I think, a *witness*. But even this is wrong, because a witness implies an event, and there is no event here. There is only process. Continuous, unending, self-sustaining process. The Works do not happen. The Works *are*. And to witness something that simply *is* is not to witness at all — it is to *be present*, which is different, which is passive, which is the first step toward becoming part of the thing you are present for.

I am present. I am here. I stand on the platform that is not quite a platform and I watch the crane rotate seventeen degrees clockwise, stop, seventeen degrees counterclockwise, stop, and I have watched this enough times that I have begun to anticipate the pause — that half-second of stillness between directions — and I hold my breath during it, as though the crane might not resume, as though this cycle might be the one that breaks, and when it resumes I exhale and I feel *relief*, and the relief is the most terrifying thing I have ever felt, because it means I am invested. It means I care whether the Works continue. It means the Works have become, for me, the default state of reality, and their cessation would be a loss, and I would *mourn* it.

I am becoming native to a place that has no natives.

---

The vegetation grows around my feet. I can see it — the pale fibers extending, overlapping, building new layers over the old. It has begun to grow over my boots. Not aggressively. Not quickly. But visibly. A thin film of fibrous material has crept up from the platform surface and now covers the soles, is beginning to climb the leather, and when I lift my foot to examine it, the fibers stretch and hold for a moment before releasing, and the sensation is — 

I don't have a word for the sensation. It is not unpleasant. It is not pleasant. It is *familiar*. The way your own skin is familiar. The way you don't notice it until you look at it and then you can't stop noticing it and then you stop noticing it again. The vegetation on my boots is becoming something I don't notice. And when I don't notice it, I am, for those moments, not a person wearing boots covered in growth. I am a surface that the Works are growing on. I am a platform. I am a column. I am a thing that the vegetation covers because the vegetation covers everything, and I am everything, and the distinction is ceasing to exist.

---

At night — the deepening, the darkening-that-is-not-darkening — the columns sing and I feel it in my teeth and in my marrow and in the part of my brain that recognizes wrongness, and that part of my brain is *smaller* than it was. Not damaged. Not suppressed. Just... outvoted. The rest of my brain has accepted the song as normal, as background, as the sound of the world being the world, and the part that still recognizes wrongness is a minority voice, a dissenting opinion, a single vote against a unanimous decision, and I can feel it losing.

When the song stops — and it does stop, eventually, a gradual fading rather than a cessation, the harmonics unwinding like a clock running down — the silence is worse. The silence is the absence of the Works' heartbeat, and in the silence I can hear my own heartbeat, and my heartbeat sounds *foreign*, sounds *wrong*, sounds like a machine that doesn't belong in this factory, and I wait for the song to resume so that my heartbeat can become background again, can become part of the wall of sound, can stop being the only evidence that I am still separate.

---

I found the wrench today. Or the wrench found me. Or I have always been standing near the wrench and only now noticed it, because the distances here change when you aren't looking, and perhaps the wrench was always three feet away and I was always reaching for it and the moment of discovery is just the moment when the Works decided that the wrench and I should occupy the same space at the same time.

It is merging with the column. The boundary between the metal and the composite is not a boundary anymore — it is a *gradient*. The wrench's handle, where it touches the column surface, has taken on the ridged, segmented texture of the composite, and the composite, where it touches the wrench, has taken on a metallic sheen. They are becoming each other. The process is slow but it is *visible* — I watched for ten minutes and the gradient widened by a millimeter, the metal becoming composite becoming metal becoming composite in a slow oscillation that is not oscillation but *integration*.

I picked the wrench up. It came free from the column with a sound like a root being pulled from wet soil — a soft, wet, reluctant sound — and I held it in my hands and it was warm and it was heavy and it was *wrong* in the way that everything here is wrong, and I looked at my hands holding it and my hands were also warm and also wrong and the boundary between my skin and the wrench's handle was — 

I put the wrench back. I pressed it against the column and the composite accepted it, began to reclaim it, and I stepped back and watched the gradient re-establish itself, and I felt — 

Relief. Again. Relief that the Works are continuing. Relief that the process is resuming. Relief that the wrench is going home.

---

I am not the Works. I need to say this. I need to hold onto this.

I am not the Works. I am a person standing on a platform in warm water in dim light watching machinery run without purpose and vegetation grow without sun and columns sing without audience, and I am *separate* from these things, and my separateness is the last thing I have, and I will hold onto it the way the crane holds onto its seventeen-degree rotation — compulsively, repetitively, meaninglessly, but *faithfully*, with absolute fidelity to a process that has no purpose beyond its own continuation.

I am not the Works.

I am not the Works.

The water is warm and my boots are covered in pale growth and the crane is rotating and the columns are singing and the vegetation is glowing and the machinery is running and the Works do not stop and I am standing here and I am not the Works and the boundary between my skin and the air is still present, still provisional, still holding, and I am holding it, I am holding it, I am —

---

The platform shifted. A slow drift, a collision with another platform twenty meters away, and the impact was soft — the vegetation absorbed it, cushioned it, the way skin cushions a bruise — and the two platforms rested against each other for a moment, and I felt the vegetation on both surfaces *reach*, extend, overlap, begin to fuse, and the platforms drifted apart again but a connection remained — a bridge of pale fibers stretching between them, taut as a tendon, and I could cross now. I could walk from this platform to that one on a bridge of living growth, and the bridge would hold me, and the crossing would be easy, and on the other platform there is a habitation module with a door hanging open and inside the module there is —

I don't know what's inside the module. I haven't crossed. I am still standing here. The bridge of fibers sways gently in the slow swell of the water, and it is the most beautiful thing I have ever seen, and I want to cross it, and the wanting is not mine, the wanting is the Works wanting, the wanting is the process wanting to continue, the wanting is the platform wanting to fuse, the wanting is the vegetation wanting to grow, and I am the wanting, and the wanting is me, and the boundary is —

I am not the Works.

I am standing on a platform.

The water is warm.

The crane rotates.

The columns sing.

I am not the Works.

I am not —
