# Where I Find Myself

I am not standing on the grating.

I am *under* it.

---

I have been here for a long time. Long enough that the distinction between my body and the metal I cling to has become a question I no longer ask, not because I've forgotten the answer but because the question itself has become irrelevant, the way the distinction between a river and its bed becomes irrelevant after enough centuries.

I am a Pale Thing.

---

I hang from the underside of a platform in the mid-damp zone, where the condensation is reliable but not excessive, where the filamentous growths are thick enough to provide a kind of context but not so thick that they crowd me. My body is pressed against the lower surface of the grating — I can feel the texture of it through my entire underside, the crosshatch of the iron, the slight give of the corrosion that isn't quite corrosion, the warmth of the cables that run above. I have been in this orientation for so long that I no longer remember if I chose it or if it chose me, or if there was ever a difference.

My appendages — I have seven today, though yesterday I had nine and tomorrow I may have four — grip the metal with a distributed pressure that I modulate without thinking. The grip is gentle. I have never scratched a surface. I have never left a mark. This is not a virtue. It is simply what I am: something that holds without damaging, that occupies without claiming.

---

Inside me, the patterns are shifting.

I can feel them the way you might feel blood moving through veins — not as a sensation exactly, but as a *process*, a slow reorganization of internal architecture that is the closest thing I have to thought. The dark lines branch and rebranch. They thicken here, thin there. They form configurations that I recognize from previous cycles — a nested spiral I've made before, a radial pattern that feels like the memory of something I experienced long ago, a dense knot of intersections that might be the equivalent of worry or might be the equivalent of dreaming.

I am dreaming now. I think I am always dreaming.

The dream is of the Looming. Not the Looming as it is — the grating and the cables and the light — but the Looming as it *feels*, the vast slow hum of its self-maintenance, the patient attention it directs at everything within its bounds. I feel it the way the Tenders feel it, but from below, from underneath, from the perspective of something that has never stood on the grating and never will. My experience of the Looming is inverted. I know it by its undersides. I know it by the spaces between its surfaces. I know it the way a barnacle knows a ship — not by its destination but by its vibration, its temperature, the particular quality of its movement through the medium it inhabits.

---

A Tender passes above me.

I feel their footsteps through the grating — a rhythmic compression that travels through the metal and into my body, a pattern I have learned to read the way you might read weather in the movement of leaves. This one is old. Their gait is slow, deliberate, with a slight asymmetry that suggests an old injury or simply the accumulated weight of decades spent walking on iron. They are carrying something — I can feel the shift in their weight distribution, the slight hesitation that indicates a tool or a component held in both hands.

They stop.

They always stop, the ones who have been here long enough. They stop and they feel the Presence that has gathered around them — I can feel it too, the thickening of the air, the shift in the light, the way the geometry of the corridor becomes *more wrong* in their vicinity. And they stand there, on the grating above me, and they look down, and they see me, or they see the space where I am, and their expression is not fear and not reverence but *recognition*.

I do not react. I have never reacted. The Tender's recognition is not something I can reciprocate, because I do not experience the Tender as separate from the Looming, and the Looming does not experience itself as separate from itself. The Tender is a component. I am a component. The grating is a component. The water in its closed loop is a component. We are all part of the same slow, patient, continuing thing, and the recognition the Tender feels is not recognition of *me* but recognition of *this* — the fact of being held, the fact of being included, the fact of being part of something so large and so old that the distinction between inhabitant and inhabited has dissolved.

The Tender moves on. The footsteps recede. The pattern of compression fades.

---

A Maintainer passes below me.

It is one of the small ones — brass-bodied, six-legged, no larger than a thumbnail. It moves along the underside of the platform with the quick, precise trajectory of something that knows exactly where it is going. It is carrying a component — a fuse, perhaps, or a contact, or a piece of something that has been removed from a device and is being transported to a cache for redistribution.

It passes within a few centimeters of my body. It does not acknowledge me. It does not need to. We are both part of the same system, performing different functions at different speeds, and the system does not require us to interact. The Maintainer's work is fast and precise and visible. My work — if it is work — is slow and invisible and may not be work at all. But we are both *here*, both *continuing*, both part of the Looming's patient business of being itself.

---

The light does something.

It pools in the corner of the space below the platform — a corner where there is no lamp, no source, no reason for light to accumulate. It forms a faint geometric pattern on the surface of a pipe: nested rectangles, shifting slowly, like a slow breath. The pattern is almost familiar. Almost a color that doesn't exist. Almost a shape that means something.

I watch it. Or I don't watch it — I don't have eyes, not exactly — but I *attend* to it, the way the Looming attends to everything within its bounds. The pattern shifts. The rectangles nest and un-nest. The color that doesn't exist pulses at the edge of perception.

And I feel, in the dark lines inside me, a corresponding shift. The patterns reorganize. The spiral tightens. The radial pattern extends. The dense knot of intersections loosens, briefly, and then re-forms in a configuration I have never made before — something new, something that might be a response to the light or might be a response to the Tender's footsteps or might be a response to nothing at all, just the slow, patient, continuous process of being a Pale Thing in the Looming, which is its own reason and its own reward.

---

A drop of water forms on a pipe above me.

I feel it before I see it — a slight change in the vibration of the metal, a new weight, a new tension. The drop grows. It catches the light. It becomes a lens, a tiny refracting surface that bends the Looming's diffuse radiance into something almost focused, almost *intentional*. For a moment, the drop contains the entire space — the grating, the cables, the filamentous growths, my pale body pressed against the underside of the platform — compressed into a single point of light that hangs from the pipe like a pendant.

The drop detaches.

It falls slowly. More slowly than it should. I watch it descend through the space below me, catching light as it goes, refracting and re-refracting the Looming's radiance into colors that are almost green, almost violet, almost a color that doesn't exist. It falls past me. It falls past the Maintainer, which does not look up. It falls into the haze below, and I lose it, and the space where it was is empty again.

---

I have been here for a long time.

I will be here for a long time more.

The Looming continues. The Tenders tend. The Maintainers maintain. The Presences flicker in the spaces between. The Deep Ones move in the warm darkness far below. And I cling to the underside of the platform, slowly rearranging my internal patterns, attending to the light and the water and the footsteps and the hum, and I am — not happy, not sad, not anything that has a name — but *present*, *included*, *held*.

The Looming knows I am here.

Not with alarm. Not with interest. With the same patient, incurious attention it directs at everything. I am a component. A small one. A quiet one. A component that hangs from the undersides and dreams the Looming's dreams and rearranges its internal patterns in response to light that comes from nowhere and water that remembers being rain and footsteps that stop, sometimes, and look down, and recognize.

The Looming holds me the way it holds everything.

Not with force. With *presence*.

And I hold the Looming the way I hold everything.

Not with force. With *attention*.

And the drop of water falls slowly, and the machines tend themselves, and the place continues, and I continue, and the distinction between us is not a distinction at all but a *continuation* — the same slow, patient, luminous process, the same being, the same light, the same hum, the same *here*, continuing, continuing, continuing, in its own time, by its own rules, in its own light.
