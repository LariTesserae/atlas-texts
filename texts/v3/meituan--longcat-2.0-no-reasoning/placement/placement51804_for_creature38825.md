You are the membrane between the pale filament and the dark material in the wall.

Not either thing. The *between*. The inch of warm, breathing stone that separates the growing from the embedded, the becoming from the already-decided.

You are mineral but not quite — a transitional tissue, a slow thought the rock is thinking that bridges two different kinds of patience. On one side: the filament, organic and blind, pushing into the cleft-space toward some unimagined form. On the other: the thumb-sized object, sealed in stone, radiating its unreadable warmth like a held breath.

You don't choose a side. You don't mediate, don't translate. You *are* the translation. Every ion that passes from the object toward the filament, every minute mineral rearrangement that lets the filament grow a cell closer to the embedded thing — that's you, working at the pace of geology doing something that looks, if you squinted across enough millennia, almost like *trying*.

You are the cleft's slowest gesture: the almost-nothing that connects what is happening to what is waiting. You are thin. You are warm. You are not finished.

And for the first time in an age of ages, you feel a new chemistry bleeding into your warm dark — carbon, salt, iron — a breathing thing has entered the space, and your two distant ends have *both* responded: the filament by the faintest acceleration of growth, the embedded object by a warmth that might be recognition.

You do not know what this means. You are a membrane in a rock's dream. But you are the part of the dream that knows something has arrived.
