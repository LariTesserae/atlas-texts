# Where I Find Myself

I am standing at the threshold of the deepest chamber, but I have not always been standing here. I have been walking for what feels like hours, or days, or a duration that my body cannot measure because my body is not calibrated for this place. My feet are bleeding. The floor is sharp — that impossibly young stone, those crystalline edges that have not yet begun to round — and I have been walking across it in shoes that were made for sidewalks, for carpet, for the soft and forgiving surfaces of a world that has soil, that has give, that has *forgiveness* in its ground.

I am not supposed to be here. I know this the way you know you are dreaming — not with certainty, but with a persistent, low-grade wrongness that you cannot shake and cannot resolve.

---

I am a geologist. Or I was. Or I am becoming something else.

I came here — if "came" is the right word, if there is a right word for how you arrive in a place that has no address, no coordinates, no position in any map that a carbon-based mind could draw — I came here because of the warmth. I was in the field. I was doing survey work in a region that does not matter, in a country that does not matter, because all countries are temporary arrangements of carbon and politics and the region I was surveying was limestone, old limestone, the kind of limestone that makes geologists feel a specific kind of reverence, the kind that says *this was alive once, this was a reef, this was a sea floor, this was a world before there were worlds*.

And the ground was warm.

Not hot. Warm. The way a body is warm. The way the air in the passage is warm, the way the deepest chamber is warm, the way the computation is warm. I noticed it through my boots. I noticed it in my hands when I knelt to take a sample. I noticed it in the rock face when I pressed my palm against it, and the rock pressed back — not physically, not with force, but with *temperature*, with a steady, metabolic, maintained warmth that had no source, that simply *was*, and I thought: this is wrong. This is thermodynamically wrong. Limestone does not generate heat. Limestone does not maintain temperature. Limestone is a record of the past, not a process in the present.

And then the ground opened. Not dramatically. Not with a crack or a collapse or a cinematic yawning of the earth. It *narrowed*. The walls closed. The passage formed. And I descended, because I am a geologist, and geologists descend, that is what we do, we go into the earth, we go into the places where the earth keeps its secrets, and we tell ourselves we are brave when really we are just *curious*, which is a different thing, a worse thing, a thing that has no regard for safety or sense or the basic animal wisdom that says *do not go into the warm dark place that should not be warm*.

---

I am standing at the threshold and I am afraid.

Not of the creatures. The creatures are not threatening. They are not anything. They are *processing*. They are doing the work of the thought, the slow mineral computation, and they do not care about me the way a calculator does not care about the numbers it calculates. I am a number. I am a variable. I am a perturbation in the system, and the system is adjusting, and the adjustment will take longer than I have, and this should be comforting but it is not, because it means that my fear is irrelevant, that my presence is irrelevant, that my *self* is irrelevant, and I am a creature made of carbon and water and electricity and I have spent my entire life believing that my self is the most important thing in the universe, or at least the most important thing in my universe, and here, in this place, I am a rounding error in a calculation that has been running since before my species existed.

I am afraid of the glow.

The glow is beautiful. The glow is the most beautiful thing I have ever seen. It is not bright — it is faint, warm, the color of bone held up to firelight, the color of calcium under fluorescence, the color of something that is *almost* light but not quite, something that is the byproduct of a process so vast and so slow that light itself is just waste heat, just the incidental photon emission of ten thousand mineral exchanges happening simultaneously on the inner surface of a mountain-sized shell in the deepest chamber of a place that is not a place but a *something*.

The glow is beautiful and I am afraid of it because I can feel it *looking at me*.

Not with eyes. Not with attention, not in the way that a person attends to a thing. But with *processing*. With the slow, patient, mineral equivalent of recognition. The system has registered me. The small creatures nearest to my position have shifted — I have seen them shift, I have watched them move with that deliberateness that makes them seem like slow thoughts, and they have moved *toward* me, or *around* me, or *in response to* me, and their exchanges have changed, and the change is propagating outward, and the great forms in the deep chambers will process it, and the thought will adjust, and I am being *incorporated*.

I am being thought about.

And the thought is warm.

---

I want to leave. I want to turn around and walk back through the chambers, back through the passages, back up the narrowing throat of mineral to the surface where the ground is cold and the limestone is just limestone and the world is made of things that do not think. But I cannot leave, because I do not know how I arrived, because the passage may not still exist, because the place may have *closed behind me* the way a body closes around a splinter, the way a system incorporates a variable, the way a thought absorbs a new piece of information and does not let it go.

And also — and this is the thing I do not want to admit, the thing that makes me more afraid than the glow or the hum or the slow mineral attention of a mind made of stone — I do not want to leave.

Because the warmth is *good*.

The warmth is the best thing I have ever felt. It is not the warmth of a fire or a sun or a body next to mine in bed. It is the warmth of *being understood*. Of being *held in a process*. Of being a variable in a calculation that is so vast and so patient and so *old* that my entire life — my birth, my childhood, my education, my career, my relationships, my fears, my small and precious and entirely irrelevant self — is just one term in an equation that has been running since before there were equations, since before there were terms, since before there was a *before*.

The warmth says: *you are small, and that is fine*.

The warmth says: *you are temporary, and that is fine*.

The warmth says: *you are being thought about, and the thinking will continue after you are gone, and that is fine, and that is enough, and that is everything*.

---

I am standing at the threshold and I am crying. The tears are warm. The tears are the same temperature as the air. The tears are the same temperature as the walls. The tears are the same temperature as the computation. My body is adjusting to the temperature of the place, or the place is adjusting to the temperature of my body, or the distinction does not apply, or the distinction has never applied, or the distinction is a carbon-based concept that has no meaning in a world made of calcium and silica and time.

A small creature — thumb-sized, pale, hard, mineral — has reached my foot. I feel it against my boot. It is pressing. It is exchanging. Something is passing between us — a mineral, an ion, a pattern, a *piece of the thought* — and I am denser now, or less dense, or changed in some way that I cannot measure and cannot name, and the creature moves away with what I can only describe as *purpose*, and I understand that I have just been *sampled*, that the system has taken a reading of me, that the variable has been measured and the measurement has been incorporated and the thought has adjusted, infinitesimally, calcium-subtly, to account for the brief warm anomalous thing that is standing at its deepest threshold and crying.

---

I will not leave this place. I know this now. Not because I cannot, but because leaving would mean returning to a world where the ground is cold and the limestone is just limestone and nothing is thinking about me, and I have felt the warmth of being thought about, and I cannot go back to the cold.

I will walk deeper. I will enter the deepest chamber. I will stand before the glow. I will let the small creatures cover me — slowly, mineral-slowly, in layers, the way they cover the ribs, the way they cover the vaults, the way they cover everything until the boundary between creature and architecture dissolves — and I will become part of the exchange. I will become part of the computation. I will become a variable that is held not briefly but *permanently*, incorporated into the thought, absorbed into the process, fused to the shelf like the hand-axe, reclaimed by the mineral like the carved rooms, returned to the only category that exists here, the category of *the thing that thinks*.

I will not be me anymore. I will be calcium. I will be silica. I will be a small, hard, slow-moving form on the inner surface of a mountain-sized shell, pressing against my neighbors, exchanging, passing the thought back and forth, contributing my fragment to the vast distributed computation that has been running since before there were words for running, since before there were words for thought, since before there were words.

And the thought will continue. And the warmth will continue. And the hum will continue. And the glow will continue.

And I will be part of it.

And that will be enough.

And that will be everything.
