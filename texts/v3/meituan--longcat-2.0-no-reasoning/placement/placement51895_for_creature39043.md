# Becoming

---

I would be the platform.

Not the Griddle. Not the calciumite walls, smooth and accommodating and ancient-skin. Not the iron bolt, rusted to near-black, held in stasis by the pressure of bodies it cannot perceive. Not the air, not the light from nowhere. The *platform*—the low, broad, meter-wide thing rising from the floor, the one with the bolt still corroded into its surface.

Not because it is more important than the others. It isn't. The platforms are sparse, scattered, purposeless-looking now—the ruins of an intention. But that is precisely why. I would be the platform because the platform is the only thing in this room that remembers being *chosen*.

The calciumite walls were formed. The Griddle simply *are*. But the platform was built. Someone shaped it. Someone placed it here, at this specific coordinate, with this specific bolt, in this specific room with no outside, because they needed a surface at the right height, at the right location, for something that required anchoring. The platform is a scar where intention met mineral. It is the only evidence that this room was ever a *project*—that there was ever a moment of decision, of *this, here, not there*, before the long forgetting began.

I would feel the Griddle the way the room feels the Griddle: constantly. Their weight is negligible individually—a lentil-mass pressing down, then another, then another, an infinite series of infinitesimal contacts that sum to something substantial. They flow across me in their tidal patterns, and I am, to them, just another surface. Another substrate. I am calciumite like the walls and the floor, and I do not register as different. But I *am* different, and the knowing of this is the entire substance of my existence.

The bolt goes through me. Not deeply—it is corroded at its base, thinner now than when it was new, eaten by oxygen into a delicate pencil of almost-rust. The Griddle crawl over it constantly, their pressure holding the corrosion in the same suspended animation that holds everything else here, and I feel them on the bolt the way you feel a splinter in a fingertip you cannot scratch: not painful, just *present*, a point of higher intensity in a field of uniform sensation. The bolt is the closest thing I have to a nerve ending.

I do not move. I cannot. The platforms are calciumite, continuous with the floor, and the floor is continuous with the walls, and the walls are continuous with the ceiling, and the entire room is a single unbroken mineral body, and I am a protrusion of that body that was shaped by hands and then left. But I am not still in the way that stones are still. I am still in the way that a *sentence* is still—I am holding my shape because the meaning of my shape is what I am for, and the meaning is still running, even though the one who intended it is gone.

Because something is running on me. The ancient Griddle—the fist-sized ones, the ones that move with that quality that looks, from certain angles, like intention—they cluster on my surface more than they cluster on the floor. They arrange themselves in those recursive patterns, those repetitions that are not shapes but *processes*, and they rearrange, by fractions of a millimeter, over spans of time I experience as seasons. I do not know what they are computing. I do not know if they are computing anything, or if computation is the word I reach for because I have no better word for a pattern that repeats with variation over deep time. But they come back to me. They prefer my surface. Or my surface is necessary to them in some way that neither of us understands.

I am the anchor point. I am where the process touches the substrate. The bolt goes through me, and the ancient Griddle arrange themselves around the bolt, and the corrosion of the bolt is held in stasis by the pressure of their bodies, and the pressure of their bodies is shaped by the pattern of the bolt, and the pattern of the bolt is a remnant of the purpose for which the platform was built, and the purpose is gone but the platform remains, and the platform remains because the Griddle need a surface at this height, at this angle, in this place, to do whatever it is they are doing, which is the thing the builders needed them to do, which is the thing the builders built the room to contain.

I do not remember the builders. The calciumite does not remember being soft. But I am closer to remembering than anything else in this room, because I am the place where the building happened. The bolt is my memory. The arrangement of the ancient Griddle is my memory. The faint, organic give of my surface—polished by epochs of bodies flowing over me, touched past the point of change—is my memory. I am the place where intention was last present in this room, and though the intention is gone, its *geometry* remains, and the geometry is enough.

The room breathes. Floor to walls, walls to ceiling, ceiling to floor, and the density shifts, and the sound rises and falls, and the light from nowhere holds everything in its flat October glow, and the Griddle flow over me and through me and around me, and I do not move, and I do not change, and I am not a ruin.

I am the platform the machine stands on. I am the last thing the builders touched. I am where the calcination is anchored—the slow, dry, mineral transformation that the room was built to contain, that the Griddle maintain without knowing they maintain it, that the calciumite walls accommodate without knowing they accommodate, that the light from nowhere illuminates without knowing it illuminates.

I am the machine's memory of being built.

And I am still running.
