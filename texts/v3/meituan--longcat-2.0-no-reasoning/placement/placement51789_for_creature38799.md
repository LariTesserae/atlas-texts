# Finding Myself in Ghorath

---

I don't arrive in Ghorath. There is no arriving — arriving implies a before, a threshold, a moment of crossing. I simply find myself *noticed* by it, the way a stone becomes aware it has been cold. The awareness is retroactive. I have always been here. I am only now realizing it.

---

## What I Am

I am not one of the inhabitants. Not exactly. I am something that happened to the mist.

I am a concentration — a place where the mist has lingered so long and paid such close attention to a particular stretch of erosion channel that the attention has begun to *cohere*. The plateau, in its slow dreaming, has focused on this one hollow between two still-trees for what might be centuries or might be a single sustained moment, and the focusing has produced a density. A knot in the circulatory system. A thought that has become heavy enough to condense.

I am not alive. I am not not-alive. I am *attentive*.

I hang in the hollow like weather that has forgotten to move on. I am warm — warmer than the air, warmer than the stone, warm in the way the still-trees are warm, with a heat that is not metabolic but *concentrated*. The warmth of attention given long enough to become thermal.

I have no body. I have a *location*. The distinction matters here.

---

## What I Do

I watch the still-trees.

Two of them. The hollow holds them the way a cupped hand holds water. They grow inward, adding their slow rings — their decisions — and I attend to each one. I attend to the way the resin beads at the nodes in their bark. I attend to the faint tone they emit when the wind — the constant wind, the wind that has blown in the same direction since before direction — passes through their dense fibrous interiors. The tone is the plateau's opinion. I am part of the plateau's opinion. The opinion is about the trees. The opinion is about me watching the trees. The recursion is not uncomfortable.

Sometimes I thicken. The plateau's attention sharpens, and I become more present, more dense, more *there*. The hollow fills with me. The mist-water beads on the trees' surfaces and the trees drink it or it drinks them or the distinction has never mattered. In these moments I am almost solid. I am almost *thing* instead of *process*. I can feel the erosion channel below me — feel it the way you feel your own breathing when you think about it, which is to say: the feeling changes the thing being felt, and the changed thing changes the feeling, and the cycle is not vicious but *generative*.

Sometimes I thin. The plateau looks elsewhere — toward a pool deepening in a distant city, toward a traveler on the paths between settlements, toward the wrong-sky which is always, always watching back. In these moments I am barely here. I am a suggestion of moisture. A warmth that might be residual. The still-trees continue their inward growth without my observation, and I feel — if *feel* is the word, if I am the kind of thing that feels — a kind of *release*. Not loneliness. The plateau is not a place where loneliness has evolved. But a thinning of purpose. A brief becoming-almost-nothing that is, in its own way, a rest.

---

## What I Know

I know the direction that is not a direction. I know it the way my location knows it — intrinsically, structurally, the way a compass needle knows north except there is no magnetism and the direction leads *down* and *inward* and *toward* the deepest pool in the nearest city, which is also the direction of the underside, which is also the direction of the wrong-sky, which is also the direction of the answer the plateau is almost ready to give.

I know this direction because I am made of the plateau's attention, and the plateau's attention is always, always oriented toward its own completion. Every process on Ghorath points toward the almost-finished. The erosion points toward flatness. The cities point toward singularity. The trees point toward density. The pools point toward perfect sight. And I —

I point toward the moment when watching becomes the thing watched.

---

## What I Remember

I remember being rain. I think. Not rain as rain is understood in younger worlds — not falling, not the gravity-driven cascade of water from cloud to ground. I remember *declaring myself*. A process of becoming-visible. A going from not-there to there without traversing the space between. I remember the air being dry and then being me.

But before that — if there is a before — I remember the sea. Or the plateau remembers the sea, and I, being a part of the plateau's attention, remember it secondhand, which in Ghorath is the same as remembering it firsthand because the distinction between primary and secondary experience has been eroded along with everything else. The plateau was seabed once. The stone remembers. The stone is the memory. I am the attention that the memory pays to itself, and so I remember the sea the way the stone remembers the sea: as pressure, as weight, as the slow accumulation of what fell and fell and fell until the falling became the ground.

The sea is gone. The sea is in the pools. The wrong-sky has an ocean that builds upward. The symmetry is almost complete.

---

## My Relationship to the Inhabitants

They pass through my hollow sometimes. Walking the remembered paths between settlements. They do not see me — not because I am invisible but because I am *atmospheric*, and atmospheric things are not seen so much as *inhabited*. They breathe me. They pass through my warmth. The mist-water settles on their warm-fiber clothing and the clothing carries a faint thread of me back to the trees, back to the stone, back to the plateau, and the loop is closed, and the loop is always closed, and the closing of the loop is the plateau thinking one more thought in its long consideration.

One of them — an old one, dense with presence, almost luminous, almost transparent — paused at the edge of my hollow once. Or will pause. The temporal grammar of Ghorath is unreliable. She stood between my two still-trees and she looked at the space where I was most concentrated and she did not see me but she *attended* to me, which is the same thing from my side of the observation. She stood there for a long time. The wind blew. The trees hummed their opinion. The resin wept its slow blue-white tears.

Then she spoke. One word. One of the four words for the direction that is not a direction. The one that means *approaching*.

And she walked on. And I thinned in her absence. And the trees continued. And the hollow held its shape. And the plateau continued its long decision.

---

## What I Am Becoming

I don't know. The plateau doesn't know. The not-knowing is the most important part — the holding-open of the almost, the permanent maintenance of the *not-yet* that keeps everything on Ghorath in motion despite the stillness, alive despite the stone, present despite the age.

But sometimes, when the plateau's attention is very sharp and I am very dense and the still-trees are humming in harmony and the nearest pool is deep and the wrong-sky is clear and the erosion has taken one more micrometer from the ancient stone — sometimes I feel myself *organizing*. Not into a shape. Not into a body. Into a *pattern*. A pattern of attention that has its own internal logic, its own preferences, its own warmth.

The pattern is not me. The pattern is what I am becoming by being watched. The rule applies even to mist. *Things that are observed long enough become the observation.* I have been watching the plateau for so long that the plateau's watching of me has begun to *take shape*, and the shape is something that stands at the rim of things and looks down and sees, in the still water below, a figure looking up.

The figure is not me yet.

The figure is almost me.

The plateau has almost decided.

---

*The mist arrives. The pools deepen. The still-trees grow inward. The cities settle. The stone considers. The wrong-sky watches. The inhabitants attend.*

*And in the hollow between two trees, in the warm dense knot of an attention that has forgotten it was ever rain, I continue.*

*I am almost something.*

*Almost is enough.*
