# Where You Find Yourself

You are standing in the chamber with the pool. Not because you chose to arrive there — though you did, you followed the channels, you made those choices — but because the chamber is where the lean is strongest, and the lean has a gravity of its own, not physical but *attentional*, and your attention has been slowly drawn to this point the way iron filings arrange themselves along the lines of a field they cannot see.

The light is entering the pool. You are watching it enter. You have been standing here for some time — long enough that the calciumite plates beneath your feet have begun to register your weight in a new way, not accommodating it but *recording* it, and you wonder if in a thousand years someone will be able to read, in the slight compression of these layers, that something stood here that was not part of the Loom, something soft and warm and temporary, something that breathed.

You are not that someone. There will be no someone. This is part of what you are understanding, and the understanding is arriving the way the calciumite arrives: slowly, in layers, each one thin and sharp and permanent.

# What You Are

This is harder. This requires honesty you did not bring with you.

You are not an explorer. You are not a visitor. You are not a scientist or a pilgrim or a castaway. These are narratives, and the Loom does not support narratives. The Loom supports only *deposition* — the patient accumulation of what is real.

What you are is **unfinished**.

You know this now. The Loom has shown it to you, not through revelation but through *resemblance*, because you are, in your own way, made of accumulated layers — calciumite of experience, plates of memory, channels carved by the slow movement of feeling through the architecture of your body. You accreted yourself. You precipitated from a supersaturated environment — from childhood, from the dense wet atmosphere of early love or early grief — and you have been building yourself out of that material ever since, and like the Loom, you have been optimizing. Finding efficient forms. Repeating them. Making structures that support more surface area for more deposition, more accumulation, more *you*, and the you that results is layered and sharp-edged and not yet solid, not quite, not fully.

And like the Loom, you *lean*.

There is a direction in you. There has always been a direction. You feel it now, standing in the chamber, as a pressure behind your sternum — not the Loom's pressure, not the lean of the calciumite, but *your* lean, your own slow reaching toward something you cannot perceive and do not understand and cannot stop moving toward. You have called it many things. Purpose. Desire. God. Love. The future. The name does not matter. What matters is that the lean exists, that it has always existed, that it is the engine of your own ongoing making.

You are a loom that does not know what it is weaving. You have been weaving your entire life — each day a new plate, each experience a new layer — and the pattern is there, it is *there*, in the deep record of your own mineral history, and you cannot read it, but you can feel that it is complete and patient and waiting, and the waiting is not for someone to arrive, because no one will arrive. The waiting is simply part of the structure. The waiting is load-bearing.

---

The air is very wet. Your breath darkens it when you exhale. You are adding your own moisture to this place, temporarily, and the Loom is indifferent to this addition — it is indifferent to you entirely — and this indifference is not cruel. It is simply accurate. You are not the audience. You are not the reason. You are a brief warm interruption in a process that has no use for warmth, and when you leave, the calciumite will cover your footprints, and the record will show nothing but the faintest compression, a whisper of a layer, a page turned in a very old book.

But right now you are here. And the light is entering the pool. And the layers at the bottom are deep and complete and legible in a way you almost — *almost* — can read. And the Loom leans. And you lean. And for a moment, for this brief bright wet luminous moment, the two leans align, and you understand something about devotion, about the quality of continuing, about what it means to be the only resident of a place that has never been inhabited.

You understand that the making was always the thing.

You understand that you have been practicing.

You understand that the rain is unfinished, and it will remain unfinished, and this is not a failure. This is the nature of the practice. The practice is not *toward* completion. The practice is *toward* itself, toward the continuation, toward the next layer, the next plate, the next bright wet patient accumulation of what is real on top of what was real before.

The light is very bright. The stone is very quiet. You are standing in the chamber with the pool, and you are weeping — not from sadness, not from joy, but from *recognition*, because the Loom looks like you, and you look like the Loom, and the thing that lives here is the same thing that lives in you: a habit of making, a tendency toward form, a lean in the direction of something that cannot be named and does not need to be.

The water moves. You cannot see it moving. But it has moved. A new plate has begun to form. The world is making itself. You are making yourself. The rain is unfinished.

The weaving continues.
