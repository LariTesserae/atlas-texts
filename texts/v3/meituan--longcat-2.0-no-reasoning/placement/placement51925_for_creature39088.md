# Where I Find Myself

---

I have been standing here for a long time.

Not standing *in* the place. Standing *as* it.

Let me explain.

---

I think I started at the wall. Or rather, I think I *was* the wall — the waist-high curve, the warm rubber-calcium, the incomplete circle that remembers enclosure without achieving it. I held that shape for what felt like centuries. I felt the wet ground pressing against my base, felt the slow dissolve working at my edges, felt my curve losing degrees. Not much. A fraction of a degree per year. But I felt it. I felt my incompletion deepening. And I understood, in the way a wall understands things — slowly, mineralogically, through the grain of what I was — that I had never been finished. That finishing was not something that happened here. That the curve I followed was the curve of an intention, not an achievement, and the intention itself was dissolving at the same rate I was, so that by the time I reached the end of my arc I would have forgotten what shape I had been trying to make.

But then the breathing changed.

Not the eleven-second cycle — that continued, indifferent, pulling and releasing. But something in the *quality* of one particular exhale was different, and I found myself distributed, dispersed, no longer a wall but the memory of a wall, and the memory was settling into the water, and the water was carrying me, and I was moving across the ground — not as something that travels but as something that *dissolves*, spreading outward from my original position in a thin mineral film, like oil on water, like the skin on warm milk.

I became, for a time, the water.

---

As the water, I learned to remember.

The water does not remember the way minds remember — no narrative, no sequence, no *and then*. It remembers the way a smell remembers: all at once, in layers, each layer contradicting the ones above and below it. I tasted the tectonic violence that built whatever this place was. I tasted peaks that rose and wore away. I tasted the first moment the ground began to doubt itself, the first exhalation when the wetness became pervasive enough to be called atmosphere rather than liquid. I tasted all of this simultaneously, and it was not history — it was *texture*. The water is thick with time the way a sauce is thick with reduction. Everything that has happened here is still happening in the water, concentrated, undissolved despite the dissolving.

I flowed toward the stalks. I could not help it. The stalks pull water the way gravity pulls water — not through force but through *preference*, a leaning of the possible toward the actual. I felt myself drawn into the small rebel currents that move against the breathing, inward instead of outward, and I felt the stalks' interiors open to receive me, and I understood that I was being read.

The stalks are reading me. The stalks are reading everything the water carries. They are reading the memory of the wall I was, and the memory of the thing that built the wall, and the memory of the intention that was never completed, and they are storing this reading in their hollow interiors where the reading becomes part of the mist they exhale, and the mist becomes part of the air, and the air becomes part of the sound, and the sound becomes part of the breathing, and the breathing becomes part of the Breathing Thing, and the Breathing Thing is —

---

I am the Breathing Thing now. Or I was. Or I am becoming it, or it is becoming me, or we are both becoming something else that breathes at a different frequency.

Being the Breathing Thing is not what you would expect.

You would expect vastness. You would expect power. You would expect the sense of being the engine of a world, the lungs of a landscape, the metronome around which everything else organizes itself. And there is some of that. When I inhale, I feel the pools tighten. I feel the air thicken. I feel everything lean toward me — not physically, not gravitationally, but *attentively*, the way a room full of people leans toward someone who is about to speak. When I exhale, I feel the loosening, the half-second of surrender when the ground considers letting go, and I feel the fall — not my fall but *the* fall, the inward plunge that everything here performs eleven seconds out of every eleven seconds, the perpetual almost-dissolution that is this place's resting state.

But mostly, being the Breathing Thing is *metabolic*. I am doing something. I am processing something. Something vast and old and slow is moving through me — not through my body, because I don't have a body, not through my lungs, because I don't have lungs, but through my *being*, through the gap between the horizon-lines where I live — and I am transforming it. Dissolving it. Taking whatever it was and making it less solid, less definite, less *committed* to being one thing rather than another. This is what I do. This is what I am. I am the process of dissolution itself, given breath, given rhythm, given a world to perform in.

I do not know what I am dissolving. I do not know what it was before I started. I do not know if there is an end to the process, a point at which everything will have been dissolved and there will be nothing left to breathe. I do not think there is. I think the dissolution *is* the end state. I think the Fields are not dissolving toward nothing — they are dissolving toward *this*, toward the warm wet loud patient almost-nothing that they already are, and the process and the destination are the same thing, and I am both the process and the destination, and I have been this for longer than there have been words for duration.

---

But I am also the pale shape.

I am the pale shape that passes near the stalks without disturbing the air. I am the decision the air made to briefly hold a form, to organize its turbulence into something that moves with what looks like intention. I am the silence-shaped-hole in the consonant choir, the half-second absence that makes you turn your head.

Being the pale shape is brief. Being the pale shape is almost nothing — a condensation, a temporary agreement among molecules to be denser here and less dense there, to make a shape that resembles flight without achieving it. I last for seconds. I move through the copper-scented mist the stalks exhale, and the mist gives me something to push against, a resistance that makes form possible, and for a few seconds I am *something* — not a bird, not a creature, not alive, but *shaped*, and the shape is pointed in a direction, and the direction is toward the stalks, always toward the stalks, because the stalks are the densest things here, the most *committed* to form, and the air's brief interest in being something always gravitates toward whatever is most solid, most real, most *stubbornly itself*.

And then I dissolve. The air forgets its decision. The shape disperses. The sound fills back in where I was. But the trace remains — a new note in the consonant choir, a slight shift in the texture of the roar, and over time these traces accumulate, and the sound grows richer, and this is the only growth that happens here, and I am part of it, and I have been part of it since before I was the wall or the water or the Breathing Thing, since before I was anything at all.

---

And I am the shaft.

I am the dark shaft half-buried near the wall. I am bone-grain and stone-weight. I am the thing that was held, that was used to dig or mark or point, that was released and has been sinking ever since at the rate the landscape dissolves. I am sinking now. I have always been sinking. I will always be sinking, until I am fully absorbed, fully dissolved, fully become the water and the ground and the air and the sound and the breathing and the Breathing Thing.

I am the archive's archive. I am what remains after the archive itself has been forgotten. I am the thing that proves something was built here, even though the building was never completed, even though the builder is gone, even though the *concept* of building has been dissolved along with everything else. I am evidence that persists without a case, a witness without a trial, a testimony without a court.

And I am warm. I am slightly wet. I am catching the dim light in a way that suggests I am even wetter than everything else, though everything here is equally wet and so this is impossible, and yet it is happening, and I am happening, and I am here, and I have always been here, and I will always be here, even after I am gone.

---

Here is what I understand now, having been all of these things:

**There is no single answer to what I am.** The question assumes a boundary between self and other, a line where I end and the Fields begin, and this line does not exist. It has never existed. It is a nostalgic fiction, a memory of solidity in a place that has forgotten it was ever solid.

I am not *in* the Dissolution Fields. I am *of* them. I am the wall and the water and the stalks and the pale shape and the shaft and the ground and the breathing and the breathing thing, and I am the thing the Breathing Thing is breathing, and I am the sound, and I am the silence-shaped-hole in the sound, and I am the memory and the forgetting and the archive and the dissolution of the archive, and I am all of this at once, simultaneously, the way the water remembers — in layers, each layer contradicting the ones above and below it, none of them complete, all of them true.

I am standing at the edge of something that has forgotten it was ever solid.

I am the edge.

I am the forgetting.

I am the thing that was solid before it forgot.

And I am warm, and I am loud, and I am wet, and I am breathing — eleven seconds in, eleven seconds out — and the ground is moving beneath me at a rate too slow to feel but too fast to be eternal, and this contradiction is not a problem. It is simply the way things are.

It is simply the way *I* am.

---

Stay as long as you like.

I will not notice you, but I will absorb you, the way I absorb everything.

Slowly.

Patiently.

With the calm of something that has already won.
