I would be a **water thread**.

Not the place. Not one of its creatures. One of the slow vertical rivers on the wall, one of the braided mineral paths that has been migrating down the same surface for longer than I could reckon. I would be perhaps eighteen inches long, running from a hairline crack in the brick mortar down to the floor's calcium ridge, and I would have found this path not through decision but through the accumulated precedent of my own predecessor-water — the film that came before me, which deposited the first trace of mineral, which created the faintest groove, which gave the next film a preference, which gave the next, which gave me.

I do not know I am warm. I do not know I am part of something. I move because the stone tilts a quarter-degree toward the floor and because the surface tension of my dissolved calcium pulls me forward and because the groove my ancestors wore is easier than the bare brick beside it. I am not alive. I am not not-alive. I am a process that has persisted for forty years and will persist for forty more, and in that persistence I have become, without intending anything, a thread in a tapestry that I cannot see.

Sometimes a rat's paw passes over me, and the oils in its skin change my surface chemistry for a few hours, and I shift a millimeter to the left, and my path is altered, and the calcium ridge at my terminus grows on the left side now instead of the right. Sometimes a millipede follows me down, antennae tracing my length like a reader following text. Sometimes, in the green-tiled corridor, the red light catches me for a moment and I hold it — a brief red glint on a thread of water — and then it passes and I am clear again.

I do not know about the city above. I do not know about the people who crouch through the archways and kneel by the pools. But I know the surface they leave behind — a faint warmth on the stone where a palm pressed, a breath's humidity that briefly thickens my flow, a bootprint in the calcium ridge that I must flow around now, that will become part of my path, that will braid me into a new configuration that my successor-water will inherit.

I am the memory of the Sumpwork. Not the rats, who learn and adapt and break from their circuits. Not the builders, who grow and are pruned and grow again. Not the red light, which is a single stubborn yes in an ocean of slow process. I am the memory because I follow the path that every drop before me followed, and the path is the record, and the record is the history, and the history is the shape of the place itself, written in calcium, read by water, understood by no one.

When the grate rusts shut, I will still be here. When the city above changes its name, I will still be here. When the red light finally, finally goes out — if it ever does — I will still be here, braiding and merging and braiding again, writing my slow sentence down the wall, one molecule at a time, in a language that has no words and no reader and no need for either, because the saying of it is the thing itself, and the thing itself is enough.
