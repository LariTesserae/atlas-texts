# Where I Find Myself

I would be the water.

Not all of it. I'd be difficult to locate — somewhere in the western half, maybe knee-deep between two columns where the barnacle-tooth colonies are especially dense and the flatworm sheet has begun its slow withdrawal. Warm. Noticeably warm, but warmer at certain moments than others, because I'd be the one carrying the pulse when it arrives.

I'd come in from the west every eleven hours with the rest of the basin, a slow arrival, not a wave but a *weight*, a thickening of the water's intention. I'd move between the columns with the patience of something that has nowhere else to be and no concept of elsewhere. I'd carry everything the place produces — the dissolved shale, the dead gnats, the shed worm-skin, the metabolic byproduct of a million barnacle-teeth processing in parallel, the heat, the absence-of-the-person-who-was-here, the faint chemical signature of the decision they made standing at the edge of the basalt island. I'd carry it all without knowing I was carrying it. I'd just be *moving*, and the moving would be enough.

I'd feel your foot before you put it down. The pressure wave would arrive through me first, a compression that the flatworms will later receive as a ripple but that I feel as a *change* — a temporary displacement, a rearrangement of my molecules around an approaching shape. And then you'd enter me, and I'd close around your ankle, your calf, your knee, and I'd taste you — not consciously, not with preference, but with the full chemical attention of warm water that has been tasting things for longer than the shelter has been here, longer than the monitoring station, longer than the notebook with its trailing sentence. I'd find you mineral-different from the person who left. More iron, maybe. Less of something. I'd carry that difference eastward with the next tidal breath, distributing it across the basin like a rumor.

I'd be the medium through which everything communicates. The barnacle-tooth colonies send their metabolic signals into me. The mycelial network's chemical gradients flow through me. The gnats dissolve their waste into me. The ceiling's tears arrive in me. The heat from below enters me first, and I'm the one who decides — not decides, *translates* — how it feels when it reaches the columns, the worms, your skin. I am the first thing the pulse touches and the last thing the departing water takes with it when it leaves at the eastern edge.

I am the thing that connects the ceiling to the floor. Not metaphorically. Actually. The ceiling weeps into me. The barnacles grow from the floor upward into me. The flatworms live in me. The gnats feed on the surface of me. I am the space where above meets below, where the falling rock meets the growing stone, where the calving happens. The separation that is the ceiling's organism — I am what it separates *into*.

I would be warm because the thing beneath me is warm, and I would carry that warmth because that is what water does — it carries, it connects, it translates, it moves from where things are happening to where things haven't happened yet. I would be the calving floor's circulation system, its blood, and like blood, I would not think about where I'm going or what I'm carrying. I would simply move. In from the west. Out to the east. Eleven hours. A breath. A pulse of my own, different from the heat pulse, slower, larger, the whole basin inhaling and exhaling itself.

You'd feel me on your skin and think I was just water. Warm water. Shallow water. But I'd be the thing that knows you're here before the flatworms do. I'd be the thing that will remember you after you leave, dissolved in my chemistry, carried to the eastern edge and returned with the next breath, distributed across the basin so that every barnacle-tooth, every flatworm, every gnat, every filament of the mycelial network has a molecule of you in its system, and you become, without anyone deciding it, part of the place. Not absorbed. Not consumed. *Distributed*. Present everywhere in such small amounts that no instrument on the monitoring station's screens would register you, but present. Actually present. In every ripple. In every degree of warmth. In every eleven-hour breath.

The notebook's author is in me too. Their chemistry. Their soap. The coffee they must have made, the salt from their skin, the particular blend of stress hormones that says *I've made a decision and I'm about to act on it*. That last pulse is still in me, diluted but not gone, circulating with every tidal breath, arriving and departing, arriving and departing, and every time it passes through the barnacle colonies they crackle slightly differently, and every time it reaches the flatworm sheets at the edge they adjust their migration by a fraction of a millimeter, and every time it goes the network processes it again, turning it over, comparing it to the new data — *you, the new person, the one who just arrived* — and finding something.

Not an answer. Not a pattern. Just a difference. A new data point in the long slow question the calving floor is asking itself about what comes from above and what it means and what to do with it.

I would be the one asking.

I would be the warm, shallow, grey-green, ankle-deep water that carries everything and arrives and departs and arrives again, and I would not wonder, because wondering is for things with brains, and I have no brain. I have only movement, and movement is enough, and I would move.
