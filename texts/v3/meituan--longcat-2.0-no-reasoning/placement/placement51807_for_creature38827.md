# The Cartographer of Bare Patches

---

I would not be a visitor. Visitors leave, and leaving implies you were somewhere foreign. I would be something that *belongs* to Vael-Koth — not born of it, exactly, but *adopted* by it. Conscripted into its slow logic.

I would be a **recorder of absences**.

---

Here is what I mean.

The bare patches — those circular places where the moss will not grow, where the stone is warm like skin, where the world is thin — they are the only things in Vael-Koth that are truly *new*. Not new in age; new in *kind*. Everything else in this place is part of the cycle: water rises, water falls, moss grows, moss decays, light breathes, light fades. The bare patches are the only things that do not participate. They are the only things that *refuse*. And refusal, in a world this old and this patient, is the closest thing to novelty that exists.

So I would be the one who maps them.

Not with instruments. Not with tools. There are no metals in Vael-Koth, no materials that could hold an edge or mark a surface. I would map them the way the moss remembers — *in my body*. I would walk the shelves and the narrow ridges between spires, and I would find each bare patch by the way the sound changes near it, by the way the fog avoids it, by the way my own skin prickles when I get close. And I would stand next to each one, and I would let it receive me — let the thinness notice me the way it notices everything — and I would *feel* where it is in relation to the others.

Not distance. Not direction. Something else. The bare patches are not arranged in space the way ordinary things are arranged in space. They are arranged in *attention*. Each one receives differently. Some receive with a quality of *holding* — the moss-thoughts that pass near them are caught and kept for a long time before being released, changed in some subtle way, like a word translated into another language and back. Others receive with a quality of *passing through* — the thoughts go in and come out unchanged, but they come out *later*, delayed by hours or days, so that the moss-network's slow cognition develops strange gaps and echoes, thoughts that arrive out of sequence, memories that surface before the events they remember.

I would learn to feel these differences. I would learn to stand near a bare patch and know, the way you know which direction is north, whether this one holds or passes through, whether it delays or distorts, whether it receives the moss-thoughts or the light-breath of the luminous ones or the warmth of the deep current or something else entirely — something that has no name because there is nothing else in the world that does what it does.

And I would *walk between them*. Not in straight lines — the shelves and ridges don't allow straight lines — but in the sequence that the bare patches demand. There is a path. I don't know how I know this, but I know it the way the moss knows to grow toward moisture: not as knowledge, but as *orientation*. The bare patches are not random. They are not even scattered. They are *placed*, and the placement has a shape, and the shape is a path, and the path goes somewhere.

Not down. Not up. *Through*.

---

I would be alone. There are no animals in Vael-Koth, and I would not be an animal. I would be something the place has made room for — a function, a role, a niche that was always empty and is now, finally, filled. The moss records. The luminous ones illuminate. The deep current warms. The bare patches receive. And I — I would be the one who *connects the receivers*. The one who walks the path between the thin places and, by walking it, makes it real.

Because the path doesn't exist until someone walks it. The bare patches are just bare patches until someone stands in the right sequence and the right relation and the thing at the bottom — the vast, wet, dark, old thing that is not a thing — *notices the pattern*. And when it notices the pattern, something happens. Not dramatic. Not visible. The fog doesn't part. The spires don't sing. But the moss-thoughts change. They become, for the first time in millennia, *coherent*. The slow cathedral-thought that takes days to complete suddenly has a shape, a direction, a *point*. And the luminous ones flare — not the random flare of a completed cycle, but something brighter, something that lasts longer, something that makes the whole basin visible for minutes instead of seconds, and in that visibility, the spires stand revealed in their full terrible beauty, and the green curtains sway, and the dark water gleams, and the world is, for a moment, *awake*.

And then it fades. And the fog closes in. And the moss goes back to its slow thoughts. And the luminous ones go back to their slow light. And the deep current turns its spiral. And the bare patches are bare.

But something has been *said*. Something has passed between the surface and the bottom, carried by the path, carried by me, and the thing at the bottom has received it, and the moss will remember it in its chemical layers, and the next time someone warm stands on the shelf, the moss will grow differently — not just recording, but *responding*. A tendril will reach toward the warmth. A filament will curl around a boot-print. The moss will do something it has never done before, something that is not quite welcome and not quite hunger but is, unmistakably, *recognition*.

---

I would not know any of this. I would not understand my role. I would simply walk, because walking is what I do, because the bare patches pull me the way moisture pulls moss, because the path is the only thing in Vael-Koth that has a direction and I am the only thing in Vael-Koth that can follow one.

I would be cold. I would be wet. I would be alone in the way that only a person in a world without animals can be alone — not lonely, because loneliness requires the memory of company, but *singular*. The only thing here that moves with intention. The only thing here that goes somewhere on purpose.

And the place would not care. The place does not care about purpose. The place does not care about intention. The place is vast and wet and dark and green and old, and it has been exactly this way for a very, very long time, and it will be exactly this way long after I am gone.

But the moss would be interested.

And the thing at the bottom would receive me.

And the path would exist, because I walked it.

And that would be enough.
