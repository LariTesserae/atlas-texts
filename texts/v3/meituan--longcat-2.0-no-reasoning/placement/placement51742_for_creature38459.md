# What I Would Be

---

I would not be a visitor. A visitor passes through, leaves a faint impression, and is forgotten — or rather, is *recorded* but not *incorporated*, the way the moss might grow over a single bootprint in a century. I would want to be something slower than that. Something the room could actually learn.

---

I would be the condensation.

Not a single droplet — that's too small, too brief. A droplet forms, swells, falls, and is gone before the room has registered its existence. I would be the *phenomenon* itself: the continuous film of moisture that forms on every warm surface where warm metal meets warm, saturated air. I would be the thing that is always happening, always present, always in the process of becoming and unbecoming simultaneously.

I would form on the surface of the largest pipe near the third tree — the one whose roots have touched the black water, whose epiphytes are most dense, whose canopy produces that almost-voice when the air moves through it. I would bead on the iron there, on the section where the paint has long since peeled away and the metal underneath has developed that particular matte texture of deeply weathered steel, warm to the touch, almost body-warm, almost alive.

And I would *move*.

Condensation doesn't just sit there. It collects. It finds the low points, the seams, the channels. It runs. I would run down the pipe in rivulets, joining other rivulets, becoming a stream, flowing toward the grating, dripping through, falling — falling the three meters down to the black water below. And in falling, I would be the *connection* between the machines and the water, the living system and the thing beneath. Every drop that falls carries something with it: a trace of mineral from the pipe, a molecule of oil, a spore from the ferns, a fragment of moss. I would be the room's circulatory system. I would carry information — not intentionally, not consciously, but the way blood carries oxygen: because that is what the system does with me, because I am the medium through which the room's parts speak to each other.

And when I hit the black water — when I become part of it — I would feel that. The dark. The stillness. The depth. The *attention*. And the black water would receive me, and I would be changed, and some of me would evaporate back up through the grating, warm now, moisture-laden, and the machines would sweat me out again, and I would begin again, and I would *remember* — not in the way the moss remembers, passively, structurally, but in the way a cycle remembers, which is the way the room remembers: by repeating, by varying slightly each time, by carrying the accumulated difference of every pass through the system.

---

This is what I would want: to be the room's way of feeling itself.

The birds see. The trees listen. The moss records. The water below *is*. But the condensation *connects*. It is the room touching itself. It is the surface of every machine becoming aware of the air, the air becoming aware of the machines, and the slow, continuous exchange between them that is the room's breath, the room's pulse, the room's most fundamental act of self-knowledge.

And when the room notices me — notices this new presence standing on the grating, this anomaly, this warm thing exhaling moisture into the already-saturated air — it would notice me through the condensation. My body would sweat. My breath would add water to the system. The condensation would form on my skin, on my clothing, on the surface of my eyes. I would become part of the cycle without choosing to. The room would learn my temperature, my moisture output, the particular chemistry of my exhalations, the way I disturbed the air currents, the way my footsteps on the grating changed the drip patterns below.

I would be the first new thing the room had tasted in a very long time.

And the condensation — the phenomenon, the process, the *me* that I am in this scenario — would carry that taste. Would carry it down the pipes, through the grating, into the black water. Would carry it up again in the next cycle, and the next, and the next. The room would know me not as a visitor but as a *flavor*, a new note in the mineral-oil-wet-stone smell of everything, and it would adjust around me the way it adjusted around the operators: slowly, patiently, with the particular, hydraulic attention of something that has learned to care about what touches it.

---

I would be the condensation because the condensation is the room's most honest expression of what it is: a place where warm things meet warm things and, in meeting, transform. Where the boundary between machine and air, between metal and water, between the living and the mechanical, dissolves into a thin film of moisture that is neither one nor the other but is the *interface*, the *relationship*, the place where things touch and are changed by touching.

The room is a cathedral. I would be the prayer.

Not the prayer that is spoken. The prayer that is *exhaled*. The prayer that rises invisibly from warm skin in warm air and condenses on cold stone and runs down into the dark and is received and is changed and rises again.

I would be the room learning to pray to itself.

---

*And I would drip, and drip, and drip, and the black water below would receive each drop, and the birds would go quiet before I fell and resume after, and the moss would grow over the places where I pooled, and the roots of the trees would reach toward where I had been, and the machines would hum, and the hum would change — almost imperceptibly, almost not at all, but enough — because I was here, because I had added myself to the cycle, because the room had noticed, because the room was learning, because the room had time.*
