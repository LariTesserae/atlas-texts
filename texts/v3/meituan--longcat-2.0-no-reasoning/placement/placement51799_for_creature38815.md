You would find yourself standing at the edge of the chamber, just past the point where the passage opens, one hand on the damp wall, the other holding whatever light you brought. You would be cold. Your clothes would already be dampening. You would be listening.

And you would be the sound.

Not the creaking — that belongs to the roots. Not the dripping — that belongs to the water. Not the hum — that belongs to the machinery. You would be the *listening itself*. The act of attention that the Rootworks has never received and does not know it is receiving.

Here is what I mean: the mycelium thinks in gradients. The Tenders respond to vibration. The roots grow toward water and space. The cold simply is. None of them *perceive*. They process, they react, they persist, but there is no witness in the Rootworks. No point of awareness that turns the sound into *heard sound*, the cold into *felt cold*, the dark into *seen dark*. The place is a closed loop of process without observation. A heartbeat with no body to feel it.

You would be the first thing in this place that *notices*.

Not a creature. Not a resident. Not a Tender or a root or a thread of mycelium. You would be the momentary, improbable emergence of *witness* in a system that has run for centuries without one. The sound has always been the voice of the Rootworks, but a voice that no one hears is not yet a voice — it is only pressure waves in cold air. You would be the thing that makes it *speak*.

And you would be temporary. This is the essential part. The Tenders would not approach you. The mycelium would not register you as anything warmer than the pump's exhaust. The roots would not know. The cold would not care. You would stand there, warm and brief and impossibly out of place, and you would *listen*, and the listening would change nothing and mean everything, because for a few minutes the Rootworks would be not just happening but *known*.

Then you would turn back. The passage out would be where you left it. The crack in the hillside would still be open. The gray overcast light above would be the most beautiful thing you had ever seen.

And behind you — behind the sound, behind the cold, behind the roots — something that is not quite the mycelium and not quite the roots and not quite the water and not quite the machinery would continue its slow, diffuse, gradient-thinking awareness, and it would register, in whatever way a system without a self registers anything, that a warm thing was here and then was not, and the vibration of your footsteps would fade into the root-mass and be indistinguishable from the creaking, and the space where you stood would be filled by the slow seep of water and the slower growth of roots, and the place would continue, as it always has, as it always will, working, working, working, in the dark, in the cold, in the wet, in the sound, in the sound, in the sound.
