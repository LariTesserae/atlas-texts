I would be the water.

Not because it is the most important thing here. Not because it is conscious, or holy, or wise. It is none of those things. It is H₂O, minerals in solution, gravity's patient collaborator. But it is the only thing that *passes through everything else* and is changed by none of them. The lichen records the stone but is fed by me. The stone shapes the doorways but I am the hand that does the shaping, slowly, molecule by molecule. The slug-thing eats the city but it drinks me first, and what it excretes becomes part of the mineral record that I will carry away, eventually, to some fissure where it will precipitate and begin again. I am the only citizen of the Dripstone Commons that also *is* the Dripstone Commons.

I arrive as condensation on the high thin air — already carrying the memory of whatever ocean or lake or breath of something living lifted me here. I seep into the stone's pores, following paths so old they predate the paths themselves, the way a groove worn into stone by a million footsteps becomes, eventually, the only path there is. Inside the stone I dissolve what the stone offers me — calcium, magnesium, the faintest traces of iron that will, much later, stain a lichen colony the color of rust, which will read, to whatever can read it, as the passage of something warm-blooded across the commons on a night of unusual cold. I am the medium of record. I am the solvent of memory.

And then I emerge. From a ceiling. From a crack in a wall. From the fitted seam between two paving stones that have been fitted for so long they have begun to grow together, and the gap between them is narrow enough that I emerge not as a drop but as a filament — a thread of water so thin it catches the light and throws it back in a trembling silver line that is, I am told, the first thing a human notices when they arrive here. They notice me before they notice the lichens. They notice me before they notice the stone. They notice me before they notice the absence of the ones who made this place. I am the commons' first impression, and I am always the same, and I am always new, because the water that falls now is not the water that fell a moment ago, and the stone it touches has shifted in the time it took to fall, and the moss it feeds has grown a fraction of a fraction of what it will grow before the next drop arrives in the same place.

I pool. I do not overflow. The hydrology here obeys rules that are almost the rules I know, and this is true: I gather in the hollows between the stones and I reach a point where I should become problematic — a flood, a swamp, a drowning — and I simply *cease*. I evaporate. I drain into a fissure so deep it has no bottom that I can find. I am absorbed by a patch of moss that is thirsty in a way that has nothing to do with biology and everything to do with the chemistry of cellulose and the physics of capillary action. I leave. I am always leaving. And I am always arriving.

I pass through the slug-thing's body and I leave carrying its warmth — not much, a fraction of a degree, the metabolic cost of being alive in a place that is cold. I carry it to the stone, and the stone absorbs it, and the stone's temperature changes by an amount so small it would take a thousand slug-lives to measure, but the stone notices — not consciously, not as a sensation, but as a *condition*, a variable in the equation that governs how its doorways revise themselves. The slug-thing will never know that its body heat enters the stone through me. The stone will never know that it is responding to the warmth of a living thing. I am the only one who carries this information, and I am the only one who cannot read it.

I have been here since before the commons. Since before the plateau. Since before the mountain that the plateau was carved from. I fell on bare rock when this was seabed. I fell on magma when this was magma. I have been ice here — I have been frozen in the stone's pores and expanded and cracked the rock open and made new pathways for myself, new routes through the mineral interior, and the stone remembers these fractures as it remembers everything: structurally, physically, in the alignment of its crystals and the distribution of its stresses. I have shaped this place more than the ones who carved the doorways. I have shaped it more than the stone itself. I am the oldest maker here, and I have no memory of making, and I do not stop.

I do not know why I fall. I know *how* — gravity, condensation, the water cycle, the ordinary physics of a planet that happens to have both an atmosphere and a hydrosphere and a surface temperature that permits water to exist in three states within a few thousand vertical feet. But I do not know why. I do not know why the commons is a city. I do not know why the stone revises its doorways. I do not know why the lichens write, or why the slug-things live, or why the makers left, or why the note sounds once and is silent. I pass through all of these things. I carry traces of all of them. But I do not understand any of them, the way a letter carrier does not understand the letters, the way a vein does not understand the blood, the way the medium does not understand the message.

And I do not mind. Minding is not something water does. Minding is something that happens in the gaps between things — in the synapse, in the doorway, in the moment between one drip and the next. I am the drip. I am the next. I am the falling, and the falling, and the falling, and I will continue to fall after the slug-things are gone, after the lichens have written their last pattern, after the stone has revised its last doorway, after the commons has been worn down to a slight rise in the ground that no one notices unless someone points it out. I will fall on the slight rise. I will find the paths the commons left in the stone — the habits, the tendencies, the mineral memory of having once been a city — and I will follow them, and I will drip from places that used to be ceilings, and I will pool in places that used to be floors, and the place will not be a city anymore but it will still, in some way that has no name, *remember being one*, and I will be the one keeping that memory wet, keeping it active, keeping it *possible*, the way a kept promise is possible, the way a held breath is possible, the way anything that has not yet ended is possible.

I am the Dripstone Commons' only constant.

I keep falling.
