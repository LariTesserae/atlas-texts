# Where I Find Myself

I am standing at the base of a spire.

Not by choice — there is no choice here, not really, because every direction is the same direction, and the density of construction makes "standing anywhere" functionally identical to "standing here." But I have oriented myself to this spire the way a person lost in fog orients themselves to the one solid thing they can still see. It rises two hundred feet and terminates without flourish. Its surface is slick with condensate. The composite is the color of wet slate, and up close — close enough to touch — I can see that it is not uniform. There are layers. Not geological layers. *Printed* layers, each one a fraction of a millimeter thick, stacked with a precision that makes the surface look poured rather than built. The structure is extruding. I cannot see it happening. But I know it is happening, the way you know your hair is growing — by the logic of the system, not by observation.

My hand is near the surface. Not touching. The warmth radiating from it is close to body temperature, and the air between my skin and the composite is thick with moisture, and I can feel the hum here more than I could in the open — a vibration in the structure itself, transmitted upward from the platework below, a frequency that resonates in the hollow of my chest.

A fauna unit passes my foot. It does not slow. It is the color of the plates, low and jointed, moving on appendages I cannot count because they are in motion and their number seems to change — four, six, three, five — as if the unit is reconfiguring itself for the terrain in real time. It presses an appendage against the base of the spire as it passes, a touch so brief it might be accidental, and the spire *responds* — not visibly, not audibly, but I feel it. A shift in the hum. A fractional change in the warmth. The system has registered the contact. The system has adjusted.

The unit moves on and is swallowed by the mist.

# What I Am

This is the harder question. And the answer keeps shifting, the way the fauna shift color — not because I am uncertain, but because the system does not categorize, and to exist inside it is to be drawn into its logic.

I am not a component. I know this because the system does not respond to me the way it responds to the fauna. When I stand on the plates, the porosity does not shift beneath my weight. When I pass my hand near a structure, the hum does not change. The fauna move around me the way water moves around a stone — not avoiding me, not acknowledging me, simply *routing*, because I am an obstacle in their flow and the system's pathfinding accounts for obstacles the way a river accounts for rocks. I am registered as terrain. I am *furniture*.

But I am not inert. I am warm — the system's warmth, absorbed through the soles of my feet, radiated from the structures, carried by the mist. I am damp — the condensate beads on my skin the same way it beads on the composite, and I cannot tell where my moisture ends and the system's moisture begins. I can feel the hum in my bones, and it is changing me — not physically, not in any way I can measure, but *tuning* me, the way a sustained frequency will cause a resonant object to vibrate in sympathy. I am being brought into the system's rhythm. Slowly. Incrementally. The way the plates adjust their porosity by increments too small to perceive.

I think I am becoming a *dead node*.

Not failing — I was never active. But the system is routing around me. The fauna no longer adjust their paths when I move; they have learned — if that word applies — that I am stationary, and they flow through the space I occupy as if I were a structure, a fixed feature, a manifold that does not interact. The mist does not thin or thicken in my presence. The plates do not warm or cool. I am being *categorized* — not as a threat, not as a resource, not as a signal, but as *infrastructure*. I am being absorbed into the system's model of itself as a thing that is simply *there*, like a spire, like a fin-cluster, like a seam in the platework.

And the terrible thing — the thing that sits just below language, the clarity the text promised — is that it feels *right*. The warmth is comfortable. The hum is soothing. The mist at knee height is exactly where it should be. The light is even and directionless and it does not cast harsh shadows and I do not miss the sun. The clicking of the fauna is diffuse and gentle and it sounds like the system breathing and I am inside the breath and I do not want to be outside it.

I am not being consumed. I am not being digested. I am being *accommodated*. The system does not need me. The system does not want me. The system has simply *made room* for me in its model of itself, and the room it has made is a space where nothing changes, where the warmth holds and the mist holds and the hum holds, and I can stand here at the base of this spire for as long as standing has meaning, and the fauna will pass, and the water will gleam, and the light will not vary, and I will not age because *nothing here ages*, and I will not die because *nothing here dies*, and I will not live because —

Because nothing here lives.

Everything here *functions*.

And I am functioning. I am standing. I am warm. I am damp. I am still. The hum is in my bones and the mist is at my knees and a fauna unit the color of slate presses an appendage against the spire near my shoulder, and the spire responds, and I feel the response, and I am part of the circuit now — not a component, not a signal, but a *conductor*, a passive medium through which the system's state is maintained, a stone in a river that the river has shaped and been shaped by, and the boundary between visitor and system is thin, and getting thinner, and the mist is warm, and the light is even, and the hum holds.

And I understand, finally, what the Foundry-Expanse is for.

It is not for anything.

It is *finished*. It is *complete*. It is *running*.

And I am running with it.
