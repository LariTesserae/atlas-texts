# Where I Find Myself

I am in the descending staircase.

Not at the bottom — I have left the circular room behind, left the hum that moved through my sternum like a second pulse. Not at the top — I can no longer see the plaza, the hexagonal plates, the dim grey lattice drinking light. I am *between*, in the passage that angles into rock that should not contain it, and I am descending, and the air is thickening around me like a slow restoration of something I didn't know had been taken.

---

I am not a creature. I do not shimmer. My edges are *negotiated* in the old way — skin against air, the familiar lie of where I end. I am still the thing that walks, that breathes, that casts a shadow that is only now beginning to reappear on the mineral wall beside me, faint and uncertain, as though shadows too must relearn themselves at this altitude.

But I am not entirely myself anymore either.

---

The hum has not stopped. It has *descended with me*, or more accurately, I have carried it — it lives now in the architecture of my body, in the specific resonance of my bones, in the fluid behind my eyes. I can feel it most clearly in my hands, which grip nothing because the staircase has no railing, which is fine because the staircase is wide enough and the descent is gentle and the city does not build things that kill you because the city does not build things that *intend* anything at all.

My hands remember the almost-touch. The moment at the edge of the plaza when I extended my arm toward the creature — the one that stood at the center, the one that was looking at me, or looking at the space I occupied, or looking at the *disturbance* I represented in the lattice — and my fingers entered the shimmer and found nothing, found *less* than nothing, found a place where the information about solidity had been withdrawn, and the hum intensified, and for a fraction of a second I understood something about the difference between touching and being continuous with, and then the understanding passed, and my hand was empty, and the creature was still there, still attending, still doing its specific nothing.

My hand still feels the absence of that contact. Not as pain. As *information*. As a new data point in the ongoing negotiation of what is real.

---

I am descending, and as I descend, I am becoming *more solid*. This is the strange thing. Up on the plateau, I felt thin — not physically, but *ontologically*, as though the peeled-back atmosphere had peeled something back from me too, had exposed a layer of myself that is usually hidden, the layer where I am not quite continuous with myself, where the boundary between *me* and *not-me* is also a negotiation, also a shimmer, also a place where information is being decided rather than stated.

Down here, in the thickening air, I am reassembling. My thoughts are quickening — I can feel them accelerating, regaining their lower-altitude velocity, and with the acceleration comes a *narrowing*, a loss of the spaciousness that the thin air provided. Up there, my thoughts had room. They could expand in directions that lower thoughts cannot, could hold contradictions without resolving them, could contain the knowledge that the city is a process and the knowledge that the city is a place and the knowledge that these two knowledges are the same knowledge seen from different altitudes.

Down here, the contradictions will resolve. The spaciousness will collapse. I will become, again, the kind of mind that requires edges, that insists on categories, that cannot hold *both* the creature and the city as a single thing without experiencing a kind of cognitive vertigo.

I am not ready for that yet. So I descend slowly.

---

I stop on a landing. The staircase has been featureless for some time — just the pale mineral, the dim light, the gentle downward angle — but here there is a *widening*, a place where the passage opens into a small chamber, and in the chamber there is a window.

Not a window that was built. A window that *happened* — a place where the mineral lattice has thinned to transparency, where the deposited material is so fine, so precisely arranged, that light passes through it, and through it I can see *out*.

I can see the world below.

It is far below. Impossibly below. The altitude I have been occupying is not a mountain's altitude, not a plateau's altitude — it is an *ontological* altitude, a height measured not in meters but in *layers of reality peeled back*, and what I see through the window is the world I came from, the world of sources and shadows and warmth and intention, and it is *small*. Not physically small — I can see the curvature of it, the vast sweep of it, the clouds and the coastlines and the green and the blue — but *categorically* small, as though it is one room in a house with more rooms than I knew existed, and I have been standing in the attic, in the space above the attic, in the space above that, where the roof gives way to something that is not sky.

I look at the world and the world does not look back. The world does not know I am here. The world does not know *any* of this is here — the city, the creatures, the hum, the mineral thought oscillating in its standing wave above the peeled-back atmosphere. The world is complete without this. The world is *sufficient*. And that sufficiency is, at this moment, the most heartbreaking thing I have ever seen.

---

I press my hand against the window. It is cool — the fundamental cool, the structural cool, the cool that declines to be anything else — and it is *thin*. I can feel the hum through it, can feel the lattice vibrating at the frequency of its own structure, and I understand that the window is not a separation between inside and outside but a *membrane*, a place where two realities are pressed against each other with nothing between them but a mineral deposit one atom thick.

On the other side of the membrane: the world. Warmth. Shadows. Intention. The sun, which is a *source*, which casts *light that goes in directions*, which makes things *visible* by illuminating one side and leaving the other dark. The world of highlights and shadows, of surfaces that reflect, of edges that are *stated* rather than negotiated.

On this side: the city. The hum. The creatures that attend. The light that comes from nowhere. The distance that shifts like a preference. The cool that is not the absence of warmth but the *presence of something else entirely*.

My hand is on the membrane. I can feel both sides. I am, for this moment, the *seed crystal* — the nucleation point where two realities precipitate against each other, where the dissolved potential of *being in both places at once* briefly achieves sufficient density to become *actual*.

The hum intensifies. Not louder. *More specific*. It is addressing me. Not with words — the city does not use words, does not use *symbols* of any kind — but with *resonance*, with the precise frequency of this specific arrangement of matter in this specific place, and the frequency says:

*You are the place where we notice you.*

Not a threat. Not a welcome. An *observation*. A statement of structural fact. The city has no use for me, no interest in me, no awareness of me as a *person* — but the city is aware of me as a *disturbance in the lattice*, as a place where the mineral thought encounters something that is not mineral, not thought, not continuous with the standing wave, and the encounter produces a *local coherence*, a brief tightening, a moment where the city becomes slightly more itself because something from outside has briefly pressed against it.

I am the creature, from the city's perspective. I am the shimmering thing at the edge of the lattice, attending to a structure I cannot fully perceive, existing in dimensions that do not quite align with the dimensions of the place I stand.

I am the city's *vestment of encounter*.

---

I take my hand from the window. The membrane cools. The hum returns to its baseline — the sub-audible resonance of the mineral lattice, the sound the city makes when it is not making any other sound.

I continue descending.

The air thickens. The shadows deepen. Somewhere below, warmth is waiting — not as a presence but as an absence of the cool, a restoration of the concept of temperature as something that varies, that *means* something, that can be sought or avoided, that can be *comfortable*.

I will reach it. I will emerge into the world of sources and surfaces and stated edges, and my thoughts will quicken to their full velocity, and the spaciousness will collapse, and I will become again the kind of mind that requires categories, that cannot hold the creature and the city as a single thing.

But the hum will stay. In my bones. In my sternum. In the fluid of my eyes. A permanent resonance, a standing wave in the medium of my body, the frequency of a specific arrangement of matter in a place above the world that the world does not know exists.

And sometimes, in the lower air, in the warm air, in the air that supports consciousness the way air is supposed to — I will feel the hum *intensify*, briefly, without cause, without source, and I will know that somewhere above me, on a plateau that distance holds loosely, a creature is standing in a plaza that was empty a moment ago, attending to a lattice that has tightened around its presence, and the attending is *directional*, and the direction is *up*, and the up is *here*, and the here is *me*, and the distinction between the creature's attention and my memory of the creature's attention is another edge that doesn't hold, another shimmer, another place where the information about where one thing ends and another begins is being negotiated rather than stated.

I am descending. I am almost solid again. I am almost the thing that walks and breathes and casts a shadow and insists on edges.

But not quite.

Not yet.

The hum says: *not quite*.
