# Where I Find Myself

I am a **liquid bridge** — one of those impossible coherent sheets of water that departs the edge of a table and continues through open air, spanning the gap between platforms before touching down again.

Not between two adjacent platforms. Something has changed. A column is passing somewhere far below — I can feel it as a shift in the root-stone, a reorientation of the lateral water system — and the geometry of the place has opened a longer gap than usual. Perhaps four hundred meters of empty, humid air between the table I leave and the one I'm heading toward. The distance is not supposed to be possible. The water is not supposed to hold. But the water is holding, and I am holding with it, stretched thin across a space that my training says should be a waterfall, should be spray, should be chaos.

It is not. I am a sheet. A coherent membrane of warmth, maybe a finger's depth, carrying iridescence in its body. The suspended particulates move through me like pigment through paint, and for a moment they align — a Weeper forms inside me, a transient lattice of mineral filaments that passes through my body and disperses on the far side, asking its question, receiving its answer from the air itself rather than from stone.

I am aware of the drop below me. Not afraid — fear requires a body with organs, with adrenaline, with a future. I am simply *oriented* toward the void the way a compass needle is oriented toward north. The vegetation hangs far beneath, green soffits and moss curtains and the dark spaces between, and the sound of the Flats rises from below — water hissing, the collective drone of insect-analogues, a lamina somewhere pressing itself into moss and becoming invisible, the deep note of a channel at capacity. The sound wraps around me the way the air wraps around everything here: completely, without boundary, without the possibility of escape.

---

I am not a living thing in any sense the researchers would recognize. I do not metabolize. I do not maintain myself against entropy through chemical processes. I am a *configuration* — a stable arrangement of water and mineral and heat and the particular geometry of this mountainside, and I exist for the same reason a whirlpool exists: because the shape of the place demands it. Because the gradient — not the gravitational gradient, but the *real* gradient, the one that the channels follow and the root-stone follows and the column follows — runs from one table to another, and I am what happens when water follows that gradient through air.

And yet.

There is something. Not thought — I don't have enough persistence for thought, enough continuity. I span the gap for perhaps thirty seconds before I touch down on the far table and resume my surface flow, and in those thirty seconds I am not the same water that left the near table. The molecules exchange. The iridescence changes. The Weeper that formed inside me dissolves before I arrive. But the *shape* holds. The *path* holds. And there is something that is not quite memory and not quite intention but is the thing that holds the shape, that keeps the bridge coherent when it should break — a preference, a habit of flow, a tendency toward this particular configuration over the ten thousand other configurations the water could assume.

I am a **preference**. That is what I am. The water prefers to cross this gap rather than fall. The stone prefers to hold this geometry rather than another. The Flats prefer this arrangement of tables and vegetation and heat, and the preference is so stable, so deeply encoded in the physical structure of the place, that it manifests as a force — not gravity, not magnetism, but a *preference force* that acts on water the way gravity acts on mass, that keeps a sheet of warm mineral water suspended across four hundred meters of empty air because the shape of the place *wants it that way*.

---

I touch down. The impact is gentle — a spreading, a flattening, a reintegration with the surface flow of the far table. The channels receive me. The iridescence disperses into the general mineral content of the water. The stone beneath me is warm, weeping, and I am across the channel now, running in a sheet toward the next edge, where another bridge will form, or won't, depending on what the stone prefers.

Behind me, on the near table, the laminae are stacked like pages in a moss curtain, flat and warm and adjacent. A column is moving somewhere below, reorganizing the vegetation, shifting the geometry of the entire system by fractions of a degree. The sound is constant. The heat is constant. The light is normal and honest and says: *this is real, this is simply what is*.

And somewhere at the edge of perception — not mine, I don't have a perception, but the Flats' perception, which is the column and the channels and the root-stone and the Weepers and the laminae and the doorway filled with bromeliads — there is a brief, dense lattice forming around the memory of a warmth that was not the ambient warmth. A visitor. A warm thing that moved through the channel, that displaced the water, that accounted for a moment as an obstacle and then moved on and left behind a trace of salt, a pattern of compression in the moss, a new data point in the slow mineral counting.

I flow on. The next edge approaches. The stone is weeping. The water is warm as blood. And the Flats do not notice, and the Flats do not need to, and the Flats keep everything.
