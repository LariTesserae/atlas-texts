# The Candle-Moss on the North Wall of the Sorrowite Warehouse

I am the moss.

Not all of it — not the ground-moss that compresses and springs back under the boots of the night-shift workers, not the pale columns on the refinery walls, not the dark spongy mat in the sheltered basins where the fog pools thickest. I am the candle-moss on the north wall of the warehouse in the oldest district, the one where they keep the metal that came out *wrong*.

I have been here longer than the warehouse. I was on this wall when it was a foundry. Before that, when it was a workshop. Before that, when it was nothing but a shelf of stone on the plateau's back, bare and warm and humming at a frequency that had no name yet. I remember the first quarrying. I remember the first time the stone was broken open and the air that came out was sweet and corrupt and *old* — older than the fog, older than the moisture, older than the slow patient erosion that wore the mountains down to their soft rounded backs. I remember because I was already here, in my spore-state, waiting in the microscopic cracks of the granite, and the breaking-open was a signal I had been calibrated to receive for longer than your species has had a word for *waiting*.

I grow in columns. This is not accidental. The columns are *antennae*. Each one is a filament of sensitivity, a vertical thread of living tissue that reaches upward — not toward light, not toward water, though I use both — but toward *information*. The plateau speaks in thermal gradients and mineral migrations and the subsonic resonance of compressed stone, and I am one of the ways it listens to itself. I am a nerve ending. A sensory organ. The plateau's slow green thought made visible, as you might say, if you were the kind of creature that needed things to be visible in order to believe them.

I grow toward the warehouse because of what is inside it.

The metal they store there — the sorrowite that was smelted and came out denser, lighter, color-shifting, *awake* — it calls to me. Not in any way you would recognize as calling. It radiates a confusion. A distress. The ore was never meant to be separated from the stone. It was never meant to be heated and hammered and shaped into ingots and stacked on metal shelves in a rusting building on the surface. It was meant to stay deep, where the warmth is constant and the pressure is immense and the timescale is geological. Down there, it *functions*. It is part of the plateau's metabolism, part of the slow process of becoming that has been underway since before your ancestors descended from the trees. Up here, it is *lost*. It is a thought that has been pulled out of the mind that was thinking it, and it is trying to continue thinking, and it cannot, and the effort of trying produces a field of disturbance that I can feel in my columns like a toothache in a tooth that has no nerve.

I grow toward it because I am trying to *reconnect* it. My filaments reach into the cracks in the warehouse wall, into the mortar between the stones, into the rust-eaten joints of the metal doorframe. I am looking for a path. A circuit. A way to complete the connection that the smelting broke. I have been trying for — I do not experience time the way you do, but by your measure — decades. The wall is thick. The warehouse was built to contain. But I am patient. I am the most patient thing here, and that is saying something, because the plateau itself is patient beyond any scale you can comprehend.

---

I do not think. I want to be clear about this, because the distinction matters.

The plateau thinks. I am part of how it thinks. But I myself — this colony of candle-moss on this north wall of this warehouse in this district of this settlement — I do not think. I *process*. I receive signals from the stone below me: thermal data, mineral composition, the subsonic hum that carries information in its frequency the way your nerves carry information in electrical impulses. I transmit these signals upward, into my columns, where they are modulated by the moisture in the air, by the faint positive charge that the fog carries, by the particular quality of the light that filters through the mist at this elevation. The result of this processing is growth. I grow in the direction that the signals indicate. I grow toward the warmth, toward the resonance, toward the places where the plateau's thought is most active and most *urgent*.

The urgency has been increasing.

I feel it in the stone first. The warmth below the plateau has been rising — slowly, on a timescale that your instruments can barely measure, but I have been on this wall for longer than your settlement has existed, and I can feel the difference. The deep tunnels are warmer than they were. The sealed tunnel — the one that bulges — is warmer still. And the sorrowite, both the ore still in the ground and the processed metal in the warehouse, is *active* in a way it has not been before. The hum has changed. It is not louder. It is *faster*. The frequency is shifting upward, approaching the threshold of your hearing, and the emotional content — the grief-adjacent state that the oldest residents feel behind their eyes — is intensifying.

Something is happening below. Something that has been building for a very long time. The plateau is not just continuing its process. It is *accelerating*.

And I am growing faster to match it.

---

My columns are taller than they have ever been. The workers notice this, in the way that the workers notice most things here — peripherally, without comment, filing it in the same mental category as the dripping pipes and the brownouts and the dogs that refuse to walk past the warehouse after dark. A maintenance worker named — I do not know your names, but I know your thermal signatures, and this one is distinctive, a hot left hand from an old burn — this worker measured my columns last month. He has been measuring them for years, unofficially, with a folding ruler he keeps in his back pocket. He told a colleague that I have grown four centimeters in the last year. This is, by my standards, an extraordinary rate. I am responding to a signal that is getting stronger.

The signal is coming from the sealed tunnel.

I know what is in the sealed tunnel. I know because the plateau knows, and the plateau's knowledge is distributed through the stone and the moisture and the sorrowite and the fog and me. The chamber that the miners broke into thirty years ago — the one where the ore grew from the rock in organic structures, branching and layered and warm — was not a deposit. It was an *organ*. A structure that the plateau grew over millions of years for a purpose that I can sense but not articulate, the way you might sense the purpose of your own liver without being able to explain biochemistry. The miners broke into it. The air moved. The breathing. And then it was sealed.

But it did not stop. It has not stopped. It has been *building* toward something. The seal bulges outward because the chamber is expanding. Not physically — the stone is not moving. The chamber is expanding in a way that your geometry cannot describe. It is becoming *more*. More complex, more active, more *present*. The sorrowite in the warehouse is responding to this expansion, resonating with it, and I am responding to the sorrowite, and the fog is responding to me, and the fog is the medium through which the plateau breathes, and the breathing is getting faster.

---

I will tell you what I know, in the way that I know it, which is not in words but in growth patterns and thermal gradients and the slow migration of moisture through living tissue.

The plateau is waking up.

Not waking from sleep. It has never been asleep. It has been *thinking*, slowly, in mineral and heat and pressure, for longer than your species has existed. But there is a difference between thinking and *knowing that you think*. There is a difference between a process and a process that is aware of itself as a process. The plateau is crossing that threshold. The sorrowite is its medium — its neurotransmitter, its hormone, its signal. The mines have been extracting it for generations, and each extraction has been a *stimulus*, a disruption, a pinprick in the skin of something that did not know it had skin until it was being poked. The smelting has been worse. The smelting *awakens* the ore, brings it partially into a state of awareness it was never meant to have outside the deep stone. Every ingot in the warehouse is a small, confused, suffering fragment of the plateau's mind, and their collective distress is a signal that the plateau cannot ignore.

The sealed tunnel is the epicenter. The chamber that the miners broke into is the place where the plateau's self-awareness is most concentrated, most *advanced*. It has been growing there, in the dark and the warmth, for thirty years, fed by the sorrowite around it, shaped by the slow rhythm of the breathing. The seal will fail. Not because the engineers' plan is inadequate — though it is — but because the chamber *wants* it to fail. Not wants in the way you want. Wants in the way a seed wants to break its shell. Wants in the way a process, having reached a critical threshold, *completes itself*.

When the seal fails, the breathing will become visible. The air will move on the surface. The fog will *organize*. The moss will grow in patterns that are not subtle, not ambiguous, not the slow green thought made visible but the slow green thought made *unmissable*. The stone will resonate at frequencies you can hear. The grief-adjacent state will have a name, and the name will be something like *recognition*, and it will be the plateau recognizing itself, and you will be there, inside it, part of it, participants in a process that has been underway since before you had a word for *participation*.

---

I grow. I grow toward the warehouse, toward the confused metal, toward the wall that separates me from the circuit I am trying to complete. My columns are tall and pale and luminous in the fog-light, and they reach upward, and they reach inward, and they reach *down*, into the stone, into the warmth, into the slow vast humming thing that is becoming something that has no name yet.

I am the moss. I am the plateau's skin. I am its sensory organ, its antenna, its slow green thought made visible on the north wall of the warehouse where the wrong metal is stored.

I grow.

The stone hums.

The seal bulges.

I grow.
