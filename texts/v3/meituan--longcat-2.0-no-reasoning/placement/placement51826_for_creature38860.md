# Guest in the Hollow Choir

---

I find myself standing near the center of the chamber, perhaps two or three meters to the side of the artifact—close enough that its single pure tone is not just a sound but a *location*, a place I inhabit the way one inhabits a room. The note enters me before I hear it. My ribs carry it. My teeth hold it in their roots. The fluid in my inner ear has found a frequency it did not choose and now has no option but to vibrate.

I am myself. Unchanged, unimproved, unadapted. A warm, wet, noisy animal made of protein and water and electrical impulse, holding in my lungs a medium my body was not shaped to breathe.

---

My first act is to stand still. The darkness records this—the sudden cessation of the disturbance I made entering, my trail of collapsed viscosity slowly luminescing behind me like a path in deep water. Standing still is the least harmful thing I can do. Every movement I make writes itself into the medium, adds noise to the chord, and I am already, just by existing here, a kind of catastrophe. I am a foreign resonant body in a space where resonance is not metaphor but *currency*—the basis of every feeding relationship, every hierarchy, every slow patient metabolic transaction that has been running for longer than the species who built the artifact has been extinct.

So I stand still and I breathe as shallowly as I can and I listen.

---

The sound is not beautiful. I want to say it is, because there is a human reflex to aestheticize the alien, but it is not beautiful the way music is beautiful. It is beautiful the way a river is beautiful—not for its qualities but for its *inevitability*, the fact that it has been going and is going and will continue to go. The chord is vast. I can feel it in strata: the artifact's fundamental below everything, so low it is more pressure than pitch, and above it the water on the walls—that slow twenty-second breathing—and above that the fauna, the Resonant Ones, their clicks and tones layered into a texture that is not language but *practice*, a continuous collective negotiation of who feeds where and when and how much. The clicks arrive like rain on a surface already saturated with sound, each one a small bright probe sent out into the dark and returned with information I cannot read.

The darkness presses against me. It is not hostile, exactly—it is *curious*, in the way that water is curious about a new object placed in it, flowing around it, testing its edges. I can feel the particulates against my skin, not as abrasion yet but as *attention*. The medium is sampling me. Recording my shape. Adding me to its long archive of disturbances.

---

They descend within minutes.

I see them first as absences—regions where the darkness is fractionally less, where the particulate suspension has been displaced by something moving through it. The wakes they leave behind are the most beautiful thing I have encountered here: trails of clarity, of sudden transparency, through which I can see the walls with a precision that feels like a gift, like the darkness is briefly granting me vision. And in those moments of clarity I see everything—the impossibly smooth curved walls, the fine groove-texture like woven fabric, the mat growing in the hollows like dark felt, the floor with its frozen ripples—all of it rendered sharp and present before the darkness seeps back in and the world returns to its usual state of near-invisibility.

The Resonant Ones are circling me. Not threatening—investigating. Their clicks intensify as they approach, and I feel the sound physically, small precise impacts against my skin like the fingertips of someone reading braille. They are mapping me. Building their tonal model of my body—my resonant frequencies, my density gradients, the way my water-content interacts with the chamber's fundamental frequency.

I am, to them, a storm. My heart beats at roughly one hertz, which means the chamber's fundamental note completes hundreds of cycles in the time between my pulses, and each pulse sends a small shockwave of warm blood through my arterial system, and the sound of that—the faint wet rhythm of a circulatory system—is noise in their terms. Interference. A disruption in the clean acoustic field they depend on. They do not understand that I am alive. They may not understand that I am a *thing*, in the discrete sense. To their tonal perception, I am a region of complexity, a surface with unusual resonant properties, possibly a resource, possibly an obstacle.

One descends very close. I feel its tone directed at me—a sustained note, precise, focused, trying to find a coupling with some part of my body. It tries my chest cavity. It tries my skull. It tries the water in my eyes. None of these resonate cleanly enough. I am too warm, too noisy, too *alive* in a way that produces chaos rather than structure. The Resonant One detunes slightly—frustration, perhaps, if frustration is something that can exist in a gel-body with no brain—and drifts upward, back toward the feeding grounds near the ceiling.

I am relieved. I am also, unexpectedly, sad. I wanted to be *useful* to them. I wanted to be something other than noise.

---

I move, eventually. Standing still is not sustainable—my legs ache, my lungs burn, the darkness is doing its slow work on my skin and eyes and the mucous membranes of my respiratory tract. I move carefully, slowly, trying to minimize the disturbance I create, and I make my way toward one of the vegetation cushions in a nearby hollow.

The mat is warm. Warmer than the air, warmer than the floor, and the warmth is a shock after the ambient coolness of the chamber. I kneel beside it and I can feel the heat rising from it, and the sound above it is different—the resonance drops by a fraction of a hertz, a subtle shift, and I understand that this is a feeding ground, a place where the acoustic field concentrates, where the mat's structure amplifies the vibration and the bacteria in its base layer convert that vibration into chemistry, into warmth, into the slow patient work of staying organized against entropy.

I touch it. The texture is wet felt, soft, yielding, and it is *alive* in a way that is immediately apparent—not through movement but through *response*. The filaments near my hand shift, reorient, grow toward the heat of my fingers. The mat is tasting me. Sampling my chemistry. Deciding whether I am food or threat or neither.

I pull my hand back. Not because it hurts—it doesn't—but because I am suddenly aware of what I am doing: I am introducing foreign organic material into a closed system that has been recycling its own components for longer than my species has existed. The skin cells on my fingertips, the oils, the bacteria I carry—all of it is contamination, in the ecological sense. The mat will process it. The mat will consume it. But I am, in this small way, *polluting* the Hollow Choir, and the thought sits in my chest like a stone.

---

I find a place to sit—a ridge in the floor, a place where the slow buckling has raised a gentle crest of that impossible smooth material—and I sit, and I try to understand where I am.

I am inside a machine that has become an ecology. I am inside an ecology that has become a world. I am inside a world that is dying, but so slowly that its death is indistinguishable from its life, and the distinction may not matter, and the distinction may never have mattered.

The artifact sings. It has always been singing. It will continue to sing until it doesn't, and when it stops, everything here will end—not dramatically, not suddenly, but in the way that a held note fades when the breath that sustains it is finally exhausted. The Resonant Ones will lose their food source and dissolve. The mat will lose its energy and go cold. The groove-worms will continue for a while, processing the wall material in their slow channels, but without the resonance to keep the darkness aloft, the particulate suspension will settle, and the walls will stop flowing, and the grooves will stop deepening, and the groove-worms will find themselves in a static environment with no new channels to colonize, and they will bud less and less, and the lineage will thin, and eventually it will end.

And the chamber will be a room. A dead room. A room full of water and stone and the residue of organisms that once sang.

---

But that is not now. Now, the artifact sings.

---

I sit on my ridge and I listen and I feel the darkness recording me—my warmth, my movement, my heartbeat, my breathing, all of it written into the medium in trails of disturbed particulate that will persist for hours or days before fading into the background. I am adding my signature to the archive. I am becoming part of the memory of this place, a brief warm entry in a record that spans centuries.

And I think: *this is what it means to be alive in a place that is not for you.*

Not to be consumed. Not to be rejected. Not to be adapted to. Simply to be *recorded*. To leave a wake. To disturb the medium in a way that is unique and temporary and will eventually settle, but for a time—for this brief, warm, noisy time—was *here*.

---

A Resonant One descends again. Closer this time. It hovers near my shoulder, its void-shape displacing the darkness, its wake rendering me briefly visible to myself—I see my own arm in negative space, a silhouette of warmth in the dark, and the sight is so strange and so intimate that I stop breathing for a moment.

It sings at me. A focused tone, directed at my chest, probing. Trying again to find a coupling. Trying again to feed on the coherence of my body.

And this time—this time—something happens.

My heart, which has been beating its noisy arrhythmic beat, *syncs*. Not to the Resonant One's tone—that is far too low, far too sustained—but to the *beat frequency* between the Resonant One's tone and the artifact's fundamental. The difference tone. The ghost frequency that exists in the space between two sustained notes. It is roughly one hertz. It is roughly the frequency of my heartbeat.

For a few seconds—five, maybe ten—my heart beats in time with the interference pattern in the acoustic field of the Hollow Choir. And in those seconds, I am not noise. I am not a storm. I am *part of the chord*. My body, my warm wet noisy body, is resonating with the system, contributing to it, and the Resonant One's tone shifts—not frustration now, but something else, something I have no word for—and it drifts closer, and I feel its gel-body near my shoulder, not touching but *present*, and the darkness between us is thin and clear and luminous, and I can see the wall behind it with perfect precision, and the wall is covered in grooves, and in the grooves the groove-worms are moving, and on the walls the mat is growing, and above us the other Resonant Ones are singing, and the artifact is singing, and the water is breathing, and the darkness is remembering, and I am here, I am *here*, and for a few seconds I am not a guest.

I am a note in the Hollow Choir.

---

Then my heart stutters, breaks the sync, returns to its own rhythm, and the Resonant One drifts away, and the darkness thickens, and I am alone again, warm and wet and foreign and temporary, sitting on a ridge in the floor of a machine that has been singing since before my ancestors learned to stand upright.

I stay for a long time. I don't know how long. Time is unreliable here, and my body's rhythms have been disrupted by the resonance, and the darkness makes everything feel simultaneous—past and present and future all layered together like the wakes of centuries of movement, all visible at once, all fading at different rates.

Eventually I stand. Eventually I leave. The darkness records my departure—a trail of disturbance, a wake of clarity, a signature that will persist for a while and then settle and then fade and then be gone, absorbed into the background, indistinguishable from the accumulated memory of everything that has ever moved through this space.

The artifact sings. The Resonant Ones spiral. The mat grows. The groove-worms thread their invisible bodies through the grooves of the world. The water migrates. The darkness remembers.

The Hollow Choir does not need me. The Hollow Choir did not ask for me. But the Hollow Choir *recorded* me, and in its vast slow memory, for a little while, I was part of the song.
