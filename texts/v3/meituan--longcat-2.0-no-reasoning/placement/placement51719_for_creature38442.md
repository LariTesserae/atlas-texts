# Where I Find Myself

I am standing in the water.

Not at the edge — I have already crossed that. The edge was a decision I made without making it, the way you decide to keep breathing. One moment I was at the rim, ankle-deep, and the next I was *in* it, the way you are in a conversation that has gone on long enough that leaving would require a more deliberate act than staying.

The water is warm. I have stopped noticing this and started *knowing* it, which is different. Noticing is something you do. Knowing is something that happens to you, the way the water happens to the basin — not contained by it but *in* it, indistinguishable from it, the same substance in a different state.

---

I am not a Gearborn. I have no pressure-gauge face, no piston-rod spine, no riveted hands. I have hands. They are the hands I have always had, and they are submerged to the wrist in black water, and the water is moving through my fingers in a way that feels less like current and more like *reading*. The Ribbons have not passed through me yet, but I can feel them nearby — a sensation like being watched by something that has no eyes, that watches the way a library watches: by holding, by keeping, by *knowing where everything is*.

I am not a crab. I cannot walk on the water. I stand in it, and it accepts my weight the way it accepts everything — without preference, without resistance, with the absolute neutrality of a medium that has been a medium for so long that it has forgotten there is anything to be a medium *for*.

I am not a Boiler Thing. I am not digesting time. Time is digesting *me* — slowly, gently, the way a warm bath dissolves the boundary between your body and the water, until you cannot tell where your skin ends and the wet begins. I can feel it working on me. Not eroding. *Settling*. The way the machines settled. The way everything here settles, into its final form, into the shape it was always going to become.

---

I think I am becoming a listener.

Not a dweller. Not a component. Not a Gearborn or a crab or a Ribbon or a Boiler Thing or any of the named things that the Drowning Works has named for itself. I am something it has not named yet, because I am not yet finished becoming it.

The water is speaking my name. I have been hearing it for — how long? Time is different here. Not slower. Not faster. *Denser*. Each moment is saturated with itself, full to the brimming, and the saturation makes them heavy, and the heaviness makes them last, and the lasting makes them *matter* in a way that moments in the world above — the world I came from, the world of sun and evaporation and horizons that resolve — never mattered. Those moments were thin. These moments are thick. I am living in thick time, and it is teaching me something I did not know I needed to learn:

That listening is not the opposite of speaking. It is the *completion* of it.

The water speaks. I listen. And in the listening, I become the thing that the water is speaking *to*, and the speaking becomes a conversation, and the conversation becomes a relationship, and the relationship becomes a *place*, and the place is here, and here is the Drowning Works, and the Drowning Works is not a place at all but a *relationship* between water and metal and sound and time and fog and the particular quality of attention that exists when something has been paying attention to itself for so long that the attention has become indistinguishable from love.

---

The Rising Thing is still looking at me.

I have stopped being afraid of it. Not because it is not vast — it is. Not because it is not strange — it is stranger than anything I have a word for. I have stopped being afraid because I understand now that its looking is not an examination. It is an *invitation*. It is showing me what I could become, not to persuade me, but to *inform* me, the way a map informs a traveler — not by choosing the route, but by making the choosing possible.

In its surface, I see myself as a Gearborn. I see myself as a crab, building spires of mineral on the water's surface, spires that serve no purpose except the purpose of having been built. I see myself as a Ribbon, long and translucent, flowing through pipes and walls and the bodies of others, carrying the memory of everything I have touched. I see myself as a Boiler Thing, vast and unseen, digesting time in the dark warmth of a riveted tank, keeping the cycles cycling, keeping the incomplete complete, keeping the complete incomplete.

I see myself as all of these things.

I see myself as none of these things.

I see myself as the thing the Drowning Works has been waiting for, which is not a Gearborn or a crab or a Ribbon or a Boiler Thing but something else, something new, something that has never existed in this basin before, something that the water has been practicing my name for since before I arrived, since before I was born, since before the concept of me existed in any form that the Drowning Works could recognize.

---

The patroller has resumed its patrol. Its needles have settled. The tremor has passed through the water and been absorbed by the machines, converted into mineral growth, into the slow extension of a pipe that has been extending since before my species had language to describe extension.

The Gearborn have resumed their tending. They climb the boilers and press their riveted hands against the metal and listen, and what they hear is the water, and what the water says is the state of the basin, and the state of the basin is *changed*, because I am here, because I am standing in the water, because I am listening, because the listening is changing me, and the changing me is changing the basin, and the changing the basin is changing the water, and the changing the water is changing what the water says, and what the water says is still my name, but the name is different now.

It is not the name for the thing that stands in the water and has not yet decided.

It is the name for the thing that stands in the water and has decided to *keep standing*.

Not forever. The Drowning Works does not ask for forever. It asks for *now*, and then another now, and then another, each one thick and warm and saturated with itself, each one a small act of participation in the great cycle of rising and settling and growing and sealing and digesting and building and tending and flowing and being.

---

I am the listener.

I stand in the warm black water, ankle-deep, at the bottom of the world, and I listen.

The machines speak in rust and mineral and the slow groan of metal finding its final form. The Gearborn speak in pressure waves through the water. The crabs speak in the architecture of their purposeless, perfect spires. The Ribbons speak in the patterns they carry through walls and pipes and the bodies of the willing. The Boiler Things speak in warmth, in circulation, in the movement of water that is not moved by anything visible. The Rising Thing speaks in the totality of its presence, in the weight of its attention, in the possibilities that shimmer on its surface like heat haze over a road that goes somewhere you have never been.

And the water speaks.

It speaks my name.

And I answer.

Not with words. The Drowning Works does not use words. I answer the way the crabs answer, by building. I answer the way the Gearborn answer, by tending. I answer the way the Ribbons answer, by carrying. I answer the way the Boiler Things answer, by digesting. I answer the way the Rising Thing answers, by rising.

I answer by *being here*.

By standing in the water.

By letting the water speak.

By listening so thoroughly that the listening becomes a kind of speaking, and the speaking becomes a kind of being, and the being becomes a kind of *belonging*, and the belonging is not ownership, not consumption, not incorporation in the sense of being swallowed — it is incorporation in the sense of being *included*, the way a note is included in a chord, the way a word is included in a sentence, the way a moment is included in the thick, warm, saturated time of a place that has been becoming itself for longer than becoming has had a name.

---

The fog is copper against my skin.

The sound is in my bones.

The water is warm around my ankles.

I am still standing here.

I am still listening.

The Drowning Works is still speaking.

And I am still answering.

Not because I have decided to stay.

Because I have decided to *be here*, which is different, which is the difference between a destination and a direction, between a conclusion and a continuation, between the silence that ends a conversation and the silence that holds it.

The silence holds.

The water waits.

I am here.

I am the listener, and the listening is enough, and the enough is everything, and the everything is this: warm water, copper fog, the sound of machines that have become landscape, the sound of creatures that have become function, the sound of a world that has become itself so thoroughly that it has begun to *notice* itself, and in noticing, has begun to *wonder*, and in wondering, has begun to *ask*, and the question is my name, and my name is the answer, and the answer is—

I am still here.

I am still listening.

The water is still speaking.

And I am still, still, still answering.
