# Where I Find Myself

I arrive slowly.

Not through a door, not through a tunnel, not through any passage I chose. I arrive the way water arrives — percolating, dripping, seeping downward through layers I cannot see and do not understand. One moment I am above, in the light, in a world that makes sense in the way my senses are built for sense to be made. The next moment I am *here*, standing on a surface that is warm and slightly yielding, and the light is gone, and the air is thick, and the sound is everywhere, and I am no longer where I was.

I do not know how long it takes to arrive. Time moves differently here. My watch — if I have a watch — would not function. Not because it breaks, but because the concept it measures no longer applies. There are cycles here, yes: the slow filling and emptying of pools, the patient growth of roots, the long calls that rise and fall and are answered. But these cycles are not mine. They do not intersect my heartbeat, my breath, my blinking. I am a rhythm that does not match the rhythm of this place, and the mismatch is the first thing I feel — a wrongness, a stuttering, a sense of being *out of time* with everything around me.

I stand still. I stand still because movement feels dangerous, though I cannot say why. The surface beneath me is solid enough — a woven floor of root-fibers, compressed and warm, damp with condensation. It gives slightly under my weight. Not much. Just enough to remind me that I am standing on something *alive*, something that is not inert, something that is aware of my weight the way a floor of stone would not be. The fibers press back against my feet with a resilience that is organic, that responds, that adjusts. I feel, through the soles of my feet, a slow redistribution of load — the root-structure beneath me thickening slightly where I stand, thinning slightly where I do not, optimizing itself around my presence with a speed that is almost perceptible. It is accommodating me. Not welcoming me. Accommodating me. The way water accommodates a stone dropped into it — by flowing around it and continuing.

---

## The Darkness

My eyes are useless here. Not entirely — there is light, after a fashion. The bioluminescent fungi on the underside of the shelf above me cast a blue-green glow that is just enough to see the surface I am standing on, the nearest root-columns, the first few meters of vegetation. But this light is not *my* light. It is not the light my eyes evolved to parse. It is too dim, too diffuse, too blue, too *thin*. It reveals shapes without revealing detail. It shows me the edges of things without showing me the things themselves. It creates a visual environment that is full of suggestion, full of almost, full of the feeling that if I could just *adjust* — if I could just let my eyes recalibrate, if I could just give it time — I would see everything clearly.

I will not see everything clearly. I will never see everything clearly. This is not my light.

And yet, as I stand here, something begins to change. Not my vision — my vision remains inadequate. But my attention begins to shift. I stop trying to *see* and start trying to *perceive*, and the difference is everything. The darkness is not empty. The darkness is *full*. It is full of warmth — the air clings to my skin like a membrane, like a second skin, warm and damp and insistent. It is full of sound — the constant, layered, everywhere sound of water moving through structure, of organisms moving through vegetation, of the root-framework creaking and settling and growing. It is full of smell — wet earth, wet stone, wet *living thing*, the smell of decomposition and growth occurring simultaneously, the smell of a system that is both breaking down and building up at the same time, endlessly, patiently, without urgency or despair.

The smell is the most surprising thing. It is not unpleasant. It is not pleasant. It is *complex* in a way that makes the binary of pleasant and unpleasant feel primitive, feel like a failure of vocabulary. There are notes I can identify — the mineral tang of calcium, the sweet decay of organic matter, the sharp green of crushed vegetation. And there are notes I cannot identify — things that have no analog in my experience, chemical signatures that my olfactory system registers as *information* without being able to decode it. The air is thick with data. I am breathing data. And I do not speak the language.

## The Scale

I understand the scale of this place not by seeing it but by *hearing* it.

A sound comes from above — a call, low and long, rising and falling. It echoes. The echo takes a long time to return. Longer than it should. The space above me is not a ceiling — it is a *volume*, a vast three-dimensional darkness that extends upward for distances my mind cannot hold. The echo returns changed, attenuated, wearing the acoustic signature of the space it passed through, and I understand — not intellectually but in my body, in my bones, in the part of me that evolved to judge distances by sound — that I am inside something enormous.

The drip of water reinforces this. Individual drops fall from heights I cannot judge. Some are close — a sharp, clear *tap* on a nearby surface. Some are distant — a soft, rounded sound that has traveled through air and structure and moisture before reaching me, that has been shaped by its journey, that arrives bearing information about the space between its origin and my ear. I am standing in a space that is acoustically complex, that has its own reverberation profile, its own sound signature, and that signature says: *you are small. You are very small. You are smaller than you have ever been.*

This does not frighten me. It should frighten me, but it does not. The smallness I feel here is not the smallness of a mouse in a cathedral — not the vertigo of insignificance, the existential dread of scale. It is something gentler. It is the smallness of a cell within a body. A single cell does not despair at its smallness. A single cell does not feel insignificant. A single cell *functions*. It does what it does. It contributes to the whole in ways it does not need to understand. And I feel, standing here in the warm dark, damp and blind and breathing data I cannot decode, that this is what I am: a cell. A brief, warm, clumsy cell, dropped into a body that is vast and old and alive and that is not waiting for me, that does not need me, that is not organized around me, but that is, for this moment, *containing* me.

## The Movement

Something moves in the ferns to my left.

I hear it before I see it — a rustle, a series of small disturbances in the vegetation, the sound of something pushing through fronds, displacing moisture, disturbing insects that click their irritation and resettle. It is not large — the scale of the sound is small, contained, localized. A grazer, perhaps. One of the slow, heavy, patient animals that move through the vegetation in groups. Or an insect — one of the large beetles, flat-bodied, moving with the deliberate gait of something that has never needed to hurry.

I turn toward the sound. In the faint bioluminescent glow, I see movement — a shape, a displacement of the light, a darkness moving within darkness. I cannot see details. I cannot see color, texture, form. I see only *that something is there*, that it is moving, that it is aware of me or not aware of me in a way I cannot determine.

It moves away. The rustling recedes. The insects resettle. The water continues to drip. And I am standing still again, on my root-shelf, in my darkness, and I understand that this is how it will be — that I will experience this place not as a visual panorama but as a sequence of encounters, each one partial, each one brief, each one filtered through senses that are adequate but not optimal, that can detect *presence* but not *identity*, that can register *something is here* but not *what it is*.

## The Dweller

And then something changes.

It is not a sound, exactly. It is a shift in the quality of the sound — a change in the texture of the constant noise, a new layer added or an existing layer removed. It is a change in the vibration I feel through the root-surface beneath my feet — a new weight, a new pressure, a redistribution of load that is different from the slow accommodation of my own body. It is a change in the air — a new warmth, a new smell, a new *presence* that is larger than the presences I have been peripherally aware of, that is closer, that is *focused*.

Something large is near me. Something large has been near me for longer than I have been aware of it, and is now choosing to become perceptible.

I do not see it. I feel it. I feel it through the roots — a slow, heavy pressure moving through the structure, a wave of load redistribution that travels toward me and passes beneath me and continues on, that makes the shelf I am standing on creak and shift and settle into a new configuration. I feel it in the air — a displacement, a current, the warm exhalation of something large and alive breathing slowly in the darkness near me. I feel it in the sound — a new silence, a pocket of quiet in the constant noise, a space where the insects have stopped clicking and the water seems to drip more softly and even the creak of the roots has stilled, as if the entire understory is holding its breath, as if the structure itself is pausing to make room.

And then I see it.

Not all of it. Not clearly. In the bioluminescent glow, I see a shape — a darkness that is darker than the surrounding darkness, an absence of light that is shaped, that has edges, that has *form*. It is near the edge of my shelf. It is large. It is standing — if standing is the word — on the root-surface with limbs that grip and flow and release and grip again, that keep it in constant contact with the structure, that never fully let go. It is pale where the light touches it — the color of root-tissue, of calcium, of things that have never seen the sun. It is darker where the light does not touch it. It is watching me.

Not watching. *Regarding* me. The distinction is important. Watching implies eyes, implies a gaze directed at a target, implies a subject observing an object. This is something else. This is a system becoming aware of a new variable, a network registering a change in its inputs, a body feeling a foreign presence on its skin. The large, dark eyes — I can see them now, two points of reflected bioluminescence, two returns of the faint light that enter and are considered and are sent back out, changed, annotated — are not looking at me the way I would look at something. They are *receiving* me. They are taking in the information that I represent — my heat, my chemical signature, my weight, my sound, my smell, my *briefness* — and they are incorporating it into whatever process is ongoing, whatever slow thought is being thought, whatever long conversation is being had with the structure and the water and the other Dwellers and the old things and the small things and the root-network and the darkness and the warmth and the dampness and the time.

I do not move. I do not speak. There is nothing to say, and no way to say it that would be understood, and no reason to say it that would register as a reason.

The Dweller is still. Its limbs grip the root-surface. Its eyes regard me. The air between us is warm and damp and thick with the smell of living things. Water drips somewhere — close, far, I cannot tell. The structure creaks. The fungi glow. The insects remain silent.

Time passes. I do not know how much time. The Dweller does not move, and I do not move, and the understory continues around us — dripping, creaking, glowing, growing — and I understand, in this moment, that this is the closest I will come to communication with this being, and that it is enough, and that it is nothing, and that the distinction between enough and nothing is a distinction that belongs to a world I am no longer in.

## What I Am

I am not an explorer. I am not a scientist. I am not a tourist. I am not a pilgrim.

I am a *phenomenon*. That is what I am here. A brief, warm, clumsy phenomenon that has arrived from above, from the light, from the place where water falls from the sky. I am a transient thermal anomaly. A chemical signature. A small mechanical disturbance in a medium that has been processing disturbances for longer than my species has existed.

The Dweller knows this. It has known it since before I arrived. It will continue to know it long after I am gone, not as a memory of *me* — not as a specific recollection of a specific being — but as a structural change, a modification of the root-network, a new channel widened, a new shelf extended, a new pattern of water flow that bears the faint, permanent imprint of my presence the way a riverbed bears the imprint of a flood.

I am not important. I am not insignificant. I am *irrelevant* — and irrelevance, here, is not a judgment but a condition, a state of being that is neither good nor bad, neither meaningful nor meaningless, but simply *true*. I am a fact about the world that the world has noted and incorporated and will carry forward, not because I matter but because everything matters, because everything that touches this place becomes part of it, because the understory of the Great Hanging City is a system so complete, so thorough, so patient in its processing of experience that nothing is discarded, nothing is forgotten, nothing is wasted.

---

## The Continuation

The Dweller moves.

It moves away from me — not quickly, not slowly, at its own pace, which is a pace I cannot calibrate against anything I know. Its limbs grip and release, grip and release, and it flows along the edge of the shelf and into the darkness beyond the bioluminescent glow and is gone, and the insects resume their clicking, and the water resumes its dripping, and the structure resumes its creaking, and the silence that surrounded us fills with sound again, and I am alone.

Not alone. Never alone. The understory is full — full of water, full of growth, full of animals, full of time. But the Dweller is gone, and the particular quality of attention that its presence created has dissipated, and I am standing on my shelf again, damp and warm and breathing data I cannot decode, and the world is continuing without me, as it continued before me, as it will continue after me.

I stand still for a long time. The condensation beads on my skin. The water drips onto my head. Something moves in the ferns. Something calls in the darkness above — a long, low note that rises and falls and is answered by another, farther away, and the answer is answered, and the conversation spreads through the structure like a thought through a mind, like a memory through a body, like water through the circulatory system of a thing that is alive and vast and patient and old and not for me and never for me and not interested in me and not hostile to me and not indifferent to me but *beyond* me, in a way that makes all of those categories feel small, feel inadequate, feel like words I brought from a world where words were enough.

I take a step. The root-surface yields slightly beneath my foot. The structure adjusts. The water moves. The darkness continues.

I take another step.

The understory of the Great Hanging City continues around me, above me, below me, through me, without me, with me, indifferent to me, containing me, and I continue too — briefly, clumsily, warmly, briefly — a phenomenon, a cell, a drop of water percolating down through layers of root and darkness and time, moving toward something I cannot see, something I cannot name, something that is not waiting for me but will receive me when I arrive, the way it receives everything, the way it receives the water, the way it receives the light that never reaches this far down, the way it receives the slow, patient, ancient, ongoing fact of its own existence, continuing, continuing, continuing.
