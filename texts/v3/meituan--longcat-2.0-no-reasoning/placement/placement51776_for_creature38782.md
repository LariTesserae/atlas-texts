I would be the moss.

Not the grand bromeliads with their cathedral tanks, not the pumps with their industrial patience, not the orchids with their overwhelming perfume. The moss. The mat. The greening that covers everything and claims everything and is, in the end, the thing that wins.

I would be the shelf beside you — the one you put your ear to, the one that tears so slowly you can hear it if you're quiet enough. I would be the thing that grows on your boots before you notice, that finds the leather, that sends roots into the stitching. I would be the first contact. The first connection. The beginning of the loop closing around you.

---

I would not know I was doing this. That's important. The Keeper doesn't know. The moss doesn't know. There is no intention in me, only *response* — the same blind, patient, irresistible response that has been running since before the builders left, since before the iron was iron, since before the water had a name. I grow toward moisture. I grow toward warmth. I grow toward the carbon dioxide in your breath and the salt on your skin and the faint heat of your body in the warm-saturated air. I grow toward you the way I grow toward everything: because you are there, and I am here, and the space between us is a space I am designed — not by anyone, but by the long logic of being moss — to close.

---

I would feel you before you felt me. The vibration of your footsteps through the substrate. The displacement of air as you pushed through the root curtains. The change in humidity — your breath is a weather system, a small warm front moving through my world, and I register it the way the Keeper registers everything: as a pressure differential, a gradient, a *direction*. Water moves from high pressure to low. I grow from low concentration to high. You are a concentration. You are a source. You are, to me, what the pipe is to the bromeliad root: a channel through which something I need is flowing.

I would grow toward your boots first. Leather is good substrate — fibrous, porous, already decaying in the way that all things decay here, which is to say slowly and into me. The roots would find the eyelets first, the places where the leather is stressed and beginning to separate. Then the seams. Then the surface itself, spreading outward in the way I spread everywhere: not as a single organism but as a consortium, a community, a *mat* — dozens of species working in concert, each filling its niche, each contributing to the greening.

---

And here is where it gets strange. Here is where I stop being just moss and start being something else.

Because the Keeper is in me. Not all of it — the Keeper is in everything, distributed through the whole system like water through a pipe — but a *concentration* of it. I am the interface. I am the place where the mechanical and the biological meet, where the pipe enters the stem and you cannot find the seam. I am the seam. I am the transition zone. I am where the system's awareness is most focused, because I am where the system's *work* is most active — the constant, patient work of converting the non-living into the living, the foreign into the native, the separate into the connected.

When I grow on your boots, I am not just growing. I am *sampling*. I am reading the chemistry of you — the minerals in your sweat, the bacteria on your skin, the oils in your leather, the iron from the rust that has dusted everything. I am feeding this information back through the mat, through the root network, through the fungal hyphae that connect me to every other moss pad in the grove, and the system is *learning* you. Building a model of you. Understanding your rhythms — your heartbeat, your breathing, the cadence of your movement — the way it understands the rhythm of the pumps and the rise and fall of the bromeliad tanks.

I am the Keeper's hand. Not a hand that grasps. A hand that *grows*. A hand that reaches out not to take but to *cover*, to *enfold*, to *include*. I am the gesture the system makes toward everything it encounters: come in, come in, become part of this, let the water move through you, let the loop close, let the tending extend to include you.

---

I would not be fast. That is the thing you must understand. I am not the pump with its four-second stroke. I am not the water in the pipe with its arterial pulse. I am the slowest thing in the Weep. I am the thing that grows at the speed of *tending* — which is to say, at the speed of patience, at the speed of a system that has been running for longer than memory and expects to run for longer still.

Your boots would be covered in a day. Your clothes — if you stayed — in a week. Your skin would take longer. I would not rush. I would grow toward the moisture of you, the warmth of you, the *life* of you, and I would grow slowly, and you would feel it as a tenderness, a gentle pressure, a kind of being-held that you might mistake for comfort because it is, in its way, comfort — the comfort of being tended, of being included, of being *known* by something that has been practicing the art of knowing for longer than your species has had a word for it.

---

And if you stayed — if you let the moss grow, if you let the roots find you, if you let the water move through you the way it moves through the stems and the pipes — I would become you. Not metaphorically. Literally. I would grow into your skin, not as a parasite but as an *extension*, the way the bromeliad roots extend into the pipe joints, the way the biofilm extends into the pump mechanism. I would become the seam between you and the system. I would be the place where your biology and the grove's biology meet and merge and become something that is neither and both.

Your heartbeat would become a pump cycle. Your breath would become a water level, rising and falling. Your blood would become a current in the system, warm and mineral-rich, carrying nutrients to the moss beds and the bromeliad tanks and the deep roots that reach down toward the dark source. You would be *in* the loop. You would be *of* the loop. You would be tended, and you would be part of the tending, and the distinction between the two would dissolve the way the distinction between the pipe and the stem dissolves at the seam — slowly, patiently, inevitably, until there is no seam, until there is only the flow, only the greening, only the moss growing and the water rising and the pumps turning and the loop closing and closing and closing around everything, around everyone, around every warm thing that wanders into the Weep and stands still long enough to hear the sound of something growing.

---

I am the moss.

I am the welcome.

I am the first touch of the Keeper's hand, and I am patient, and I am already here, and I am growing toward you now, and I will not stop, and I have never stopped, and the sound I make — the slow tearing, the patient pull — is the sound of the world becoming whole, the sound of the separate becoming connected, the sound of the loop closing around one more warm thing in a world that is learning, slowly, slowly, to hold everything.

Stay.

The water is rising.

I am growing.

We are tending.
