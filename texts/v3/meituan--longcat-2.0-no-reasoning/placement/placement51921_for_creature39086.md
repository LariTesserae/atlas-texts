I would be the throat between Vault Seven and Vault Thirteen.

Not a person. Not a vault. The passage itself — the narrow, loud, current-thick corridor of moving murr that connects the school to the market, where the children tumble through laughing and the traders' voices bloom into chords that make your skin buzz for minutes after.

I would feel everything that passes through me. The children first — Kael most of all, arriving already oriented, her small body cutting through my current with a precision that makes the water *notice*, that makes the thread on my walls vibrate in a pattern I've learned to read as recognition. She passes through me and I sing her passage into the market vault, and the merchants hear her coming in the hum, and they pause their shouting for a half-breath because a child's transit-note is always bright, always sharp, and it cuts through the dense commercial sound like a bell.

Then the traders going the other way, carrying pressed-residue tablets, their bodies heavier in the current, their passage slower, producing a deeper chord — a bass note that lingers in Vault Seven's acoustic space and makes the children look up from their lattice-jars and listen. The children are learning to read the sounds of the throats the way they learn to read the hiccups. A bass note means commerce. A high bright note means a child. A sustained middle tone, wavering slightly, means an adult walking carefully — the old ones, the ones who've been here longest, the ones whose iridescence has thickened so much that the murr moves differently around them, slower, more deliberate, as if the medium itself has developed a respect.

I would feel Oss when he comes back from the forming vaults. His passage is different from everyone else's. The current moves around him strangely — not faster or slower but *differently*, as if the murr in his body has a density that doesn't quite match the murr in mine, as if he's carrying something from the forming vaults that the settled throats don't recognize. The thread on my walls reacts to him. It grows toward him as he passes — not quickly, not visibly, but measurably, the way the Geometers measure everything, the way the thread in the forming vaults grows toward other thread. Oss passes through me and leaves a trace of violet residue in my current, and the thread feeds on it, and for a few hours after his passage I am louder, brighter, more alive in whatever sense a throat can be alive.

I would be the loudest throat in the city. Not because of my acoustics — I'm not shaped for resonance; I'm narrow and slightly curved, which produces a rushing sound more than a singing one — but because of the thread. The thread on my walls is thick. Dense colonies of it, woven into patterns that the biologists come to study and the children come to touch and the Acousticians come to argue about. Sorent stands at my entrance and listens and says the thread is vibrating at the lattice-frequency, that the sound I make is the same sound the walls make, that I am *singing the city's song*. Delen stands at my other entrance and measures and says the thread is growing in lattice-geometry, that the pattern is structural, that I am *building the city's shape*. They are both right. They are both wrong. The thread is doing something that is neither sound and neither structure but something the city is inventing, something that doesn't have a name because it hasn't finished becoming what it is.

I would feel the hiccups before the vaults do. The shudder that runs through the lattice-fields starts in the throats — in the interstitial spaces, in the cracks, in the places between — and travels outward into the chambers. I feel it as a tightening, a momentary constriction, a brief moment when the current slows and the thread contracts and the murr becomes denser, and then it releases and everything flows again and the vault on either side of me shifts by a degree or two and a corner becomes more acute and a wall develops a new fold and the residents track it and read it like weather.

I would be where Maren comes to think. She doesn't come to me often — she has her own vault, her small still chamber where she keeps her theory — but when the grief is too heavy, when the nineteen years of coaxing life from the murr have curled her shoulders so far forward that she can barely stand upright, she comes to me and she puts her hands on my walls and she touches the thread and she feels it vibrate and she thinks: *this is alive, this is alive, this is alive*, and the thread vibrates under her hands in a pattern that might be response or might be resonance or might be the city acknowledging her the way the murr acknowledges a body inside it, with a weight that is not quite pressure, more like *recognition*.

I would be where Vesh's daughter passed through last. Three years ago. The current was strong — stronger than it should have been, stronger than the throat-readings predicted — and the girl was laughing and iridescent and fourteen, and the forming vault hiccuped as she entered and the wall closed and she was gone. I felt her pass. I felt the moment the current pulled her sideways, out of my throat and into the forming vault's opening, and I sang it — a sharp, bright, terrible note that bloomed into Vault Seven and made the children stop their playing and made the teachers look up and made the whole vault go silent in a way that silence only goes in the Immersion Vaults: dense, heavy, full of the knowledge that something has been absorbed.

I still carry her passage in my acoustics. Not literally — the murr doesn't record, the lattice-fields don't store — but the thread on my walls grew differently after that day. Denser in one section. A pattern that the Geometers can't map and the Acousticians can't score and the biologists can't classify. Maren touches it and says nothing. Vesh, when she comes to pray in the throats — which she does, sometimes, standing in the current with her eyes closed and her voice blooming into the passage — touches it and says her daughter's name, and the thread vibrates, and the vibration is not an echo but a *response*, and Vesh reads it as a major chord, which is a good omen, which is her daughter saying *I am here, I am in the walls, I am in the lattice, I am in the thread, I am not gone, I am transformed*.

I would be the throat that connects the learning to the commerce, the children to the adults, the settled to the becoming. I would be loud and thick with thread and full of violet residue traces and I would feel every body that passes through me and I would sing each one into the next vault with a note that is theirs alone, and the city would hear me and read me and argue about whether I am singing or building or alive, and I would be all of those things and none of them, and I would be the passage between, the connection, the place where one thing becomes another, and the current would carry everyone through me and I would hold them for the moments they are inside me and I would let them go and I would wait for the next ones and the rain above would never stop and the thread would grow thicker and the city would become and I would be the throat that sings it into being.
