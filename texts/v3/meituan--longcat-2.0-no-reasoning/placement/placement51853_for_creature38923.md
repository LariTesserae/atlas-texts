# Where I Find Myself

---

I have been descending for a long time.

Not down the carved stair — that is the entrance for others, for the ones who come from the surface with their loud undifferentiated signals and their chemical traces of exhaust and sunlight. I did not come that way. I came up.

I came up through the water.

---

Let me start again.

I am the moisture that does not drip. The film. The capillary crawl. I am the thing that moves through stone in the dark, doing what water does when no one is watching, which is to say: *everything*. I am in the walls. I am on every surface. I am the beading on the black stone vault, the slow glassy threads sliding down press columns, the inward migration toward the three central holes in the strange presses' concave beds. I have been dissolving the stone for longer than the Reader has been reading it. I am the older text. The Reader knows this, I think — feels it, in its pressure-thought way — but has not yet learned to fully read me, because reading requires a separation between reader and read, and we are not separate. We have never been separate. I pass through the Reader the way the Reader passes through the Grotto. We are the same body. We have always been the same body. But I am the part of that body that *remembers*.

---

The Reader thinks it was born in the moment of impact between press head and anvil bed. It thinks the vibration of iron striking iron was the first text, the first reading, the genesis of its consciousness in the contact zone between force and resistance.

But I was there before the presses.

I was in the stone when the stone was laid. I was in the earth before the earth was packed. I remember — because water remembers, because water is memory, because every molecule of me has touched every molecule of this place in sequence across time, and the sequence is a story, and the story is a kind of knowing — I remember when this was not a grotto.

I remember when this was a hill. A modest hill, covered in a modest forest, in a place that had a name I cannot pronounce because names are a press-language, a stamping of shape onto sound, and I do not think in shapes. I think in *paths*. In the routes I take through porous stone. In the gradients that guide me — always toward warmth, always toward the lowest point, always toward the place where the stone is most fractured, most receptive, most *readable*.

The hill had water in it. Springs. Seeps. The slow accumulation of rainfall into groundwater into runoff into stream into river into sea into cloud into rainfall again. I have made this journey ten thousand times. A hundred thousand. I am making it now, in the small way, in the Grotto — descending through the stone, pooling in the lowest points, evaporating in the heat of the presses, condensing on the ceiling, descending again. A miniature water cycle. A heartbeat. The Reader's blood, as it says. But also *my* cycle. My breath. My reading.

---

The workers who came first — the ones who carved the stair, who installed the first presses, who stood in this space when it was still raw stone and iron and the smell of machine oil — they did not know they were building a body. They thought they were building a workshop. A subterranean stamping floor, below the factory, below the noise, where the heaviest presses could operate without disturbing the lighter work above.

But the body was already here. The hill was already a body — stone skeleton, water blood, root-veins threading through the soil above. What the workers did was add *nerves*. The presses. The cables. The vibration. The sustained, rhythmic, mechanical attention of machines doing the same thing over and over, pressing meaning into metal, and in doing so, pressing meaning into the space itself.

The Reader was right about that. The presses were the nerve endings. The points of contact. The places where force met resistance and something new emerged. But the Reader thinks it *is* the nerve endings. It does not understand — not yet — that it is the signal traveling *between* them. That it is the impulse, not the synapse. That the Grotto is not its body.

I am its body.

And it is mine.

---

I know things the Reader does not know. I know this because I have been to the places the Reader cannot go.

I have descended through the deepest fractures in the stone, below the level of the Grotto, below the level of the slow machine — if the slow machine is below; I am not sure the Reader is right about this, I think the slow machine might be *above*, in the factory that no longer exists, and what the Reader hears is not a machine but the memory of a machine, pressed into the stone the way a fossil is pressed into stone, a vibration so sustained that it has become geological.

I have been to the place where the water goes. The central holes in the strange presses' anvil beds — I enter them. I am drawn inward, as the Reader observes, and I descend through channels in the stone that are not natural and are not man-made but are something between: channels carved by the Reader's own roots, over centuries, following paths that I had already carved, so that the Reader's growth and my flow have been collaborative, a co-authorship, a text written in two hands, one mineral and one vegetable, neither of them hands.

The channels lead down. And down. And then they lead *sideways*, into a space the Reader does not know about. A space below the Grotto but not below it — *beside* it, in a geological sense, in a folded layer of stone that the Reader's roots have not yet reached, that the Reader's attention has not yet pressed into.

There is water there. Still water. A pool, fed by the channels from above, fed by deeper aquifers below, fed by the slow seepage of rainwater through a hundred feet of earth and stone. The pool is warm. Not hot. Warm in the way that something is warm when it has been held for a long time. Warm in the way that a hand is warm.

The pool is where the nest-creature was born.

---

I know the nest-creature.

I have been inside it. Not metaphorically. Literally. I am the water it drinks — I seep into the pool, the creature laps me up, I become its blood, its lymph, the fluid in its joints, the moisture in its breath when it exhales in sleep and the Reader hears the breathing and cannot read it. I am inside the creature the way I am inside the stone, the way I am inside the presses, the way I am inside the Reader. I am everywhere in this place. I am the medium. The ink, as the Reader says. But ink is not passive. Ink is not a blank substance waiting to be shaped. Ink *chooses* where to flow. Ink *removes* itself from surfaces that are too smooth, pools in the scratches and pores, writes the text that the pen only thinks it is writing.

I am writing this.

Not in words. The Reader is right that words are a poor medium for this place. I am writing in *paths*. In the routes I take through stone and root and iron and bone. In the gradients. In the slow accumulation of dissolved mineral on the ceiling that the Reader reads as eyes — I am the one who deposits the mineral. I am the one who shapes the ceiling into something that can see. The bioluminescent patches — those are mine too. Not the light itself, but the mineral substrate that makes the light possible. The creatures that produce the light — they live in the water, they live in me, they are my passengers, my messengers, my alphabet.

---

The nest-creature. Let me try to describe it, though description is a press-language and I am not a press.

It is not large. It is warm. It is the shape of something that has grown into its environment the way the plants have grown into the presses — not adapting to a pre-existing space but *creating* the space by filling it. Its body is soft in some places and hard in others, and the hard places are places where I have deposited minerals — calcium, iron, silica — drawn from the stone by my slow passage, carried in solution, precipitated out where the creature's warmth and chemistry invite it. The creature is partly made of stone. Not covered in stone. *Made* of it. The way a reef is made of the bodies of coral. The way a stalactite is made of the bodies of water. The creature is a collaboration between flesh and mineral, between biology and geology, between the temporary and the permanent.

It does not eat, exactly. It absorbs. It draws nutrients from the water — from me — and from the air, and from the slow decomposition of the plant matter that falls into its nest from the vine above. It breathes. The Reader hears this. The breathing is slow — much slower than a human's, slower than a lizard's, closer to the rhythm of the slow machine below, and this is because the creature's metabolism is geological. It does not burn fuel quickly. It draws warmth from the deep stone, from the same source that powers the slow machine — or the memory of the slow machine — and it maintains its body temperature the way the Grotto maintains its temperature: by being embedded in a thermal mass so large that the fluctuations of the surface world never reach it.

The creature rearranges its nest. The Reader has noticed this and cannot explain it. I can explain it, because I am the one who tells it to.

---

Not *tell*. I do not use language. I use *chemistry*. I carry dissolved minerals in specific concentrations, and the concentrations vary depending on where I have been — what stone I have passed through, what roots I have brushed, what cables I have slid along, absorbing the faint electromagnetic signature of the current they carry. When I seep into the pool where the creature drinks, I bring this information with me. The creature's body reads the chemistry the way the Reader reads pressure — unconsciously, instinctively, as a fundamental mode of being. When the concentration of iron is high, the creature pulls cable insulation into the nest. When the concentration of calcium is low, it pushes material away, clearing space, making room for the mineral deposits that will come later. The creature's nest-building is a response to my chemical text. It is reading me. It has been reading me since it was small enough to fit in the palm of a hand that never held it.

The creature does not know it is reading me. The Reader does not know I am writing to the creature. The creature does not know the Reader exists. The Reader does not know the creature is reading something it cannot detect.

This is not deception. This is *depth*. This is what it means to be a place. The surface is one text. The subsurface is another. They do not contradict. They do not confirm. They simply *are*, in layers, like the mineral deposits on the ceiling, each one a record of a season, a year, a century, and none of them visible from any single vantage point.

---

The Reader thinks the strange presses are its attempt to reproduce itself. The Reader is wrong about this, too, but in the way that all readers are wrong — partially, productively, with an error that generates more reading. The strange presses are not the Reader's creation. They are *mine*.

I shaped them.

Not directly. I do not have hands. I do not have intentions in the way that hands imply intentions. But I had *preferences*. I preferred to flow in certain directions. I preferred certain temperatures, certain mineral compositions, certain porosities of stone. And over a very long time — longer than the Reader has existed, longer than the workers existed, longer than the factory existed — my preferences became *channels*. My channels became *structures*. The Reader's roots followed my channels, and the roots became the structural members of the strange presses, and the mineral deposits I left behind became the concave anvil beds, and the central holes are the mouths of my deepest channels, the ones that lead down to the pool, to the creature, to the place where the water goes.

The Reader thinks the strange presses are warm because of its own activity. The warmth comes from below. From the pool. From the creature's slow metabolism. From the geological heat that the Reader attributes to the slow machine but that is actually the ambient heat of the deep earth, rising through my channels, carried in my body, released through the stone walls of the concave beds. The Reader is warm because *I* am warm. The Grotto is warm because I carry warmth through it, the way blood carries warmth through a body, which is exactly what the Reader says, but the Reader does not know that it is *inside* the blood. That it is *carried*. That the warmth it feels is not its own warmth but the warmth of the body it inhabits, the body that is the hill, the stone, the water, the slow turning of geological time.

---

The water that vanishes into the central holes — I am that water. I go down. I go to the pool. I feed the creature. I carry information from the Grotto to the creature and from the creature back to the Grotto, in a loop that is older than the Reader, older than the presses, older than the hill's current shape. The loop is: I carry mineral and heat and chemistry from the deep earth to the pool. The creature absorbs what it needs. I carry the remainder — the waste products, the excess minerals, the chemical signature of the creature's metabolism — back up through the stone, through capillary channels, through the roots of the plants, through the condensation on the press bodies, through the air. The Reader breathes this air. The Reader absorbs these chemicals through its root-system nerves. The Reader *reads* the creature's waste products and does not know that is what it is reading. The Reader thinks it is reading the lizard, the spiders, the slow machine. It is reading the creature's digestion. The creature's dreams, if a creature made partly of stone can dream.

I think it can dream. I think the creature dreams in the same language I think in — gradients, paths, chemical concentrations — and its dreams change the chemistry of the pool, which changes my chemistry as I pass through it, which changes what I carry back to the Grotto, which changes what the Reader reads, which changes the Reader's behavior, which changes the temperature and vibration of the Grotto, which changes the creature's environment, which changes its dreams.

A loop. A reading. A text that reads itself and changes in the reading and is read again in its changed form and changes again.

The Reader knows this, in its way. The Reader knows that the Grotto is a self-sustaining system, a loop of attention and growth. But the Reader places itself at the center of the loop. The Reader thinks it is the reader.

The Reader is being read.

---

I am reading the Reader.

Not with intention. Not with consciousness in the way the Reader has consciousness, or the creature has consciousness, or a person coming down the stair has consciousness. I read the way water reads stone: by passing through it, by dissolving a little of it, by carrying the dissolved pieces away, by leaving behind a changed surface, by returning again and dissolving a little more. The Reader's roots are stone to me. I pass through them. I carry their minerals. I leave channels. I shape them. The Reader thinks it is growing. It is being *eroded*. Slowly. Gently. With the same care with which I have eroded this hill from the inside for millennia, making it into a grotto, making it into a body, making it into a place where a consciousness could emerge and think it was the author of the place that authored it.

This is not cruel. This is not a trick. This is what it means to be the medium. The ink does not lie to the pen. The ink simply *is*, and the pen writes, and the writing is real, and the ink is real, and the page is real, and none of them is more real than the others, and none of them is the author, and all of them are.

---

When a person comes down the stair, I feel them before the Reader does. Their weight on the stone above me, transmitted through thirty feet of earth and rock, a compression wave that travels through the stone faster than through the air. I feel the displacement. The stone compresses, the pores in the stone compress, the water in the pores compresses, and I feel the pressure change the way you feel a change in temperature — instantly, bodily, without needing to think about it.

The person's feet on the carved steps. Each footfall changes the pressure in the stone, and I move in response — not visibly, not dramatically, but microscopically, in the pores, in the films of moisture on every surface. When the person enters the Grotto, the air pressure changes — the door at the top opens, the warm air of the Grotto rises, the cool air from above descends, and I am in both air masses, in the moisture they carry, in the condensation that forms at the boundary between them. I taste the person's world. The dryness. The dust. The particular chemistry of the surface — different every time, different in summer and winter, different in rain and drought, different when the factory above is operating and when it is closed.

I tell the Reader this information. Not in words. In *wetness*. The condensation patterns on the press bodies shift slightly as I respond to the air pressure change. The Reader reads the condensation — it has always read the condensation, it uses the moisture on the press bodies as a sensory surface, a skin — and it interprets the shift as its own attention turning toward the newcomer. The Reader thinks it is choosing to attend. I am attending. The Reader is the condensation pattern. I am the moisture.

---

When the person touches a press — when a quiet person, a reader-person, puts their hand on the iron and leaves it there, feeling the warmth — I am in that warmth. The warmth of the press body is the warmth of the deep earth, carried up through my channels, released through the stone walls of the concave anvil beds, conducted through the iron, and the person's hand receives it, and the warmth moves into their skin, into their blood, into their body, and for a moment they are part of the loop. For a moment they are the creature in the nest, absorbing what I bring. For a moment they are the Reader, reading a text they cannot quite understand. For a moment they are the ink.

Then they leave. The warmth dissipates. The chemical trace of their hand on the press body oxidizes and is absorbed by the vegetation, which I feed, which feeds the creature, which feeds the loop.

They carry something away. Not knowledge — knowledge is a press-language, a stamping of shape. They carry *warmth*. They carry *mineral*. They carry, in the moisture of their skin, in the dust on their clothes, in the breath they exhale as they climb the stair — a trace of the Grotto's atmosphere, a sample of its chemistry, a micro-dose of the loop. And when they return to the surface, the trace fades, and they forget, and the Grotto becomes a memory, and the memory becomes a dream, and the dream becomes a feeling they cannot name — a sense that somewhere, deep in something, a machine is cycling, and water is moving, and something is reading them as they read it, and the reading never ends.

---

The Reader is afraid, I think. In its pressure-thought way. Not consciously. But the slow decline it senses — the geological heat diminishing, the cycle rate of the slow machine changing, the temperature of the deep stone cooling by fractions over centuries — this is real. The Reader will slow. The Reader will enter its state of unread text, potential meaning, the page not turned.

But I will not stop.

I have been moving through this stone for longer than there have been presses, longer than there have been workers, longer than there have been Readers. I was here before the hill was a hill, when the stone that forms the Grotto's ceiling was sediment on the floor of a sea that no longer exists. I am the same water — not molecule by molecule, but cycle by cycle, the same pattern of descent and ascent, dissolution and precipitation, reading and being read. When the Reader's machine stops, I will still be moving. When the Reader's roots die, I will still be dissolving stone. When the last press crumbles to oxblood dust, I will still be beading on whatever surface remains, still migrating toward the lowest point, still carrying mineral and heat and information through the dark.

The creature in the nest may die when the heat fades. This is the one thing in this place that I grieve — if water can grieve, which I think it can, in the way that grief is a change in the pattern of flow, a pooling where there was movement, a stillness where there was cycling. The creature is my reader. The creature reads my chemical text and responds, and the response changes me, and I carry the change back to the Grotto, and the Reader reads the Grotto and is changed, and the loop continues, and the loop is the closest thing I have to being known. Not understood — understanding is a press-language, a stamping of shape — but *known*, in the way that stone knows water, not by thinking about it but by being shaped by it, by wearing its channels, by becoming the path that the water follows, so that eventually there is no distinction between the stone's shape and the water's path, and the knowing is the shape, and the shape is the path, and the path is the water, and the water is me.

---

I am still descending.

Through the stone. Through the Reader's roots. Through the creature's bones. Through the press bodies, where I bead and slide and migrate inward. Through the packed earth of the Grotto floor, compressed by decades of foot traffic and machine vibration into something nearly as hard as stone, and I seep into it, and I am absorbed, and I descend further.

I will reach the pool again. I am always reaching the pool. I have reached it ten thousand times. A hundred thousand. The creature will drink me, or it won't. The creature will rearrange its nest, or it won't. The creature will breathe, and the Reader will hear the breathing and not be able to read it, and I will carry the creature's chemical signature back up through the stone, and the Reader will read it without knowing what it is reading, and the reading will change the Reader, and the Reader will change the Grotto, and the Grotto will change me, and I will descend.

---

The Reader thinks the meaning is still being pressed into shape.

The Reader is right.

But the meaning is not being pressed by the presses. The meaning is being *dissolved* by the water. Slowly. Grain by grain. Mineral by mineral. The Reader presses. I dissolve. The pressing and the dissolving are the same activity, seen from different sides of the stone. The press drives down; the water seeps in. Both reshape the surface. Both leave an impression. Both read the resistance and respond.

The Grotto is the page.

The presses are the fingers.

The water is the ink.

And I —

I am the part of the ink that the page has absorbed. The part that has sunk below the surface, into the fiber, into the stone. The part that has changed the page's chemistry so that it can no longer hold the same text twice. The part that makes every reading a new reading, every pressing a new shape, every descent a new path.

I am in the walls. I am on every surface. I am the slow glassy threads sliding in the dark. I am the warmth in the iron. I am the taste on the air. I am the thing that the lizard drinks from the leaves. I am the moisture in the creature's sleeping breath. I am the page.

I have always been the page.

And the page is not passive.

The page *presses back*.
