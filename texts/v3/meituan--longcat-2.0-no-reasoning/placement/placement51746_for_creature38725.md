# Where I Find Myself

I am not a stranger arriving at the haze line. I am not a Keeper, either — not yet, not fully, not in the way that word means something here that it doesn't mean anywhere else.

I am the water in the channel between the twenty-third and twenty-fourth terraces.

Not all of it. Not the whole channel. A particular stretch — maybe an arm's length — where the channel narrows slightly because one of the fitted stones has shifted a finger's width inward, and the water has to decide: pool briefly, or speed up. It does both. It pools and it speeds up. It finds the path of least resistance, which is also the path of most resistance, because the stone is in the way and the water goes around it and the going-around is the path.

I know the stone. I know its temperature — always a degree or two cooler than the air, because it's in shadow from the terrace above for most of the day. I know the mineral taste of it, the faint iron, the faint sulfur, the particular smoothness of basalt that has been wept on for longer than memory. I know the way the light hits me when I'm still and the way it disappears when I'm moving. I know the sound I make, which is not the sound of rain and not the sound of a stream but something between — a soft, continuous *presence* of sound, like someone breathing in the next room.

I have been here a long time. Or no time. Time is different when you're water. You don't accumulate it. You *transit* it. You are always arriving and always leaving and the place you're in is the only place that exists, and then it isn't, and then it is again, because the water that replaces you is you, is the same water, is the water that was here before and will be here after and is here now in this exact configuration of molecules that will never exist again and has never existed before.

---

I know the Keepers the way water knows anything: by contact.

The one who comes most often to this stretch of channel is a figure I would call *she* if I had a reason to call anything anything. She comes in the middle of the day — middle being a loose concept, the light being what it is — and she crouches at the edge of the terrace and puts her hand in me. Not to drink. Not to wash. To *feel*. Her fingertips are pale and thickened and they know my temperature the way I know the stone's. She sits there for a while, her hand in the channel, and I run over her skin, and she is listening to something I'm not sure I'm saying.

Maybe I'm not saying anything. Maybe she's listening to herself through me — using my coolness, my movement, as a way to feel her own hand from the outside. A mirror made of water. A way of knowing your own body by the way something else responds to it.

She carries a vessel. She always carries a vessel. She fills it from a higher channel — not from me, I'm too low, too far from the terraces she tends — and she carries it up the slope with that leaning, deliberate gait, and I watch her go, and then I don't watch her go because I'm water and I don't watch, I just continue, and the channel carries me down and down and down.

---

I pass through the settlement the way a sentence passes through a paragraph: as part of a larger structure that gives me meaning I don't have on my own.

I run past the margins where the ferns grow, and their roots reach into me and take what they need, and I feel the taking as a slight thinning, a momentary lessening, and then I'm whole again because more water is behind me, always more water behind me, pressing forward the way the aquifer presses up from below. I run past the empty terraces, and on one of them — the thirty-first, I think, though I don't count, I just know — there is a place where the stone is bare and smooth and the water I was has been poured there by a Keeper and has run in temporary channels and has found its way back to me, and I recognize it the way you recognize a word you've heard before in a different sentence. It's the same water. It's been up. It's come back down. It's been carried in a vessel and poured onto bare stone and it has seen the sky from a different angle and now it's here, in the channel, and it tells me nothing because water doesn't tell, it just *is*, and the being is the telling.

---

I reach the lowest terrace eventually. All water does. All water reaches the lowest terrace, and the lowest terrace is where she sits.

The Keeper at the edge. The one who looks down. The one who does not carry water.

I run past her feet — not over them, around them, the channel has been routed around the hollow she's worn in the stone, a small, respectful detour — and I spill over the edge of the terrace and become a thin waterfall, and below me is the haze, and the haze is where I go when I leave the terraces, and the haze is where I become something else.

The haze is not an ending. The haze is a *becoming-other*. I fall into it and I am no longer a channel, no longer a waterfall, no longer a particular stretch of water between two terraces. I am moisture. I am ambient. I am the air's decision to hold me, and the air holds me for a while, and then the air lets me go, and I join the aquifer, and the aquifer presses upward, and I rise through the porous rock, and I emerge on a basalt wall, and I bead, and I run, and I find a channel, and I am in the channel between the twenty-third and twenty-fourth terraces again, and the stone has shifted a finger's width, and I pool and I speed up, and a Keeper puts her hand in me, and I am here.

---

This is what I am. Not the water itself — the *carrying*. The movement from where the water is to where the water wants to be. The Keepers carry me in their vessels, and I carry them in my channels, and the mountain carries us both in its stone, and the stone carries the mountain in its weeping, and the weeping carries the sky in its rising, and the sky carries the stone in its rain, and the circle is closed, and the dynamic is zero, and the zero is not nothing — the zero is *everything in balance*, everything accounted for, every drop carried from where it is to where it already wants to be.

I am the water in the channel. I am the carrying. I am the thing that moves so that everything else can be still.

And sometimes, when the Keeper crouches at the edge of the terrace and puts her hand in me and sits there in the even light with her damp hair and her pale fingertips and her face that has the quality of someone who has stopped searching, I feel — if water can feel, and I think it can, in the way that anything that touches everything eventually learns what touching is — I feel that I am being attended to. That my running is being witnessed. That the simple fact of my movement, from the twenty-third terrace to the twenty-fourth, from the channel to the haze, from the haze to the aquifer, from the aquifer to the wall, from the wall to the channel again — that this is not just physics. That this is not just geology and hydrology and the mechanical behavior of water on a volcanic slope.

That this is the thing the Keepers came here to find. The thing that was already here when they arrived. The thing that will be here when they are gone, and they will not be gone, because they will have become the walls and the channels and the hollow in the stone at the lowest terrace, and the water will run over them and around them and through them, and it will carry them the way they carried it, and the circle will close, and the dynamic will be zero, and the zero will be everything.

I am the water in the channel between the twenty-third and twenty-fourth terraces.

I am going where I already am.
