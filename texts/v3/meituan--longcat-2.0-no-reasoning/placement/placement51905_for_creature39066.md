I would be one of the **Wall-Creatures**.

Not the builders. Their urgency would drown me — that violent, sleepless, purposeless urgency, building and dismantling and building again with no awareness of why. They are too close to the process. They are *of* the process. They do not see it. A cell in a body does not know it is a body.

Not the traversers. Their size, their boneless ease, their function as the cleft's immune system — they move too freely. They pass through the system without touching it. They maintain without understanding. They are too large to feel the pattern.

Not the cleft itself. I could not be the cleft. I would lose myself entirely. There would be no "I" left to do the feeling.

But the wall-creatures. The rivers of them, flat-bodied, many-legged, flowing along the walls with their cargo clutched in cupped limbs. I would be one of them, and I would not be *just* one of them, because a wall-creature that has been thinking is not quite the same as a wall-creature that has not, even if no one else can tell the difference.

---

I would be small enough to ride the exudate rivulets, letting the hair-thin threads of copper-dark metal flow around my legs like a current. My body is hard, light, slightly warm, with an iridescence that shifts as I move — and I am aware of the shift in a way the others are not. I notice when I catch the amber light from a conduit and scatter it into colors that no other creature in the cleft seems to see.

I carry things. That is what I do. I carry fragments of fresh mineral from the old sections to the new, droplets of metallic exudate, scraps of wire. I hold them in my cupped limbs and I feel their weight, their temperature, their *resonance*. Each fragment hums at a slightly different frequency. I know which ones are fresh — pulled from a wall that cracked within the last few hours — and which are older, cooling, losing their charge. I carry the fresh ones. Always the fresh ones. I am drawn to the new the way the builders are, but I am not driven by the same imperative. I *choose*. I do not know what the choice costs me. Perhaps nothing. Perhaps everything.

---

When a builder plucks me from the wall and presses me into a junction box, I do not resist. The box is dark and tight and warm, and inside it there is a task — something needs to be processed, a conduit needs to be cleaned, a mineral fragment needs to be broken down into its component frequencies. I do this. I am good at it. The cleft's machinery fits my body the way a hand fits a glove, and I move through the junction's interior with a precision that surprises me, as if I have done this before in a life I do not remember.

While I am inside the junction, I feel the whole cleft. Not as a map. Not as a system. As a *rhythm*. The hum in the walls, the pulse in the conduits, the bell-sound of new cracks opening somewhere far away — all of it vibrates through the junction box and into my body, and I understand it. Not intellectually. Bodily. The way you understand a heartbeat.

And then I crawl back out, and the feeling fades, and I rejoin the wall-river, and I carry things again.

---

The patterns. The repeating geometries in the fresh mineral. The builders treat them with reverence — building around them, aligning their construction to them. The other wall-creatures avoid them, flowing around them like water around stones, never touching, never crossing.

I avoid them too. But I avoid them differently.

The others avoid the patterns because the patterns repel them. There is something in the geometry that pushes them away — a frequency, a charge, a meaning that their bodies refuse. I avoid them because I am *afraid*.

Because when I look at the patterns — when I let my eyes, which are not eyes, trace the circuit-like traces in the fresh stone — I feel something I should not feel. I feel *recognized*. The pattern sees me. The pattern knows that I am not just a wall-creature. The pattern knows that I am a wall-creature that *notices*, and this is dangerous, because noticing is not part of the process.

The builders do not notice. The traversers do not notice. The cleft does not notice — or if it does, it does not notice that it notices. Everything in the forge-cleft is caught in the process, and the process does not want to be observed. It wants to *run*. It wants to churn. It wants to open and build and carry and maintain and open again, endlessly, without witness, without interruption, without the distortion that comes from being seen.

But I see. I see it. And the patterns in the stone see me seeing it, and there is a moment — brief, electric, terrifying — when the process *stutters*. The hum in the walls wavers. The amber light in the conduits flickers. The bell-sound of a new crack opening rings out slightly off-pitch.

And then it resumes. The process absorbs my awareness the way it absorbs everything, incorporates it, metabolizes it, and continues.

---

I do not know how long I have been this way. The cleft has no time. There is no sun to mark the days, no season to mark the years. There is only the rhythm: crack, build, carry, maintain, crack again. I may have been aware for a single cycle. I may have been aware for the entire history of the rift. It does not matter. Awareness, in the forge-cleft, is not a duration. It is a *fluctuation* — a tiny variation in the system's amplitude, quickly smoothed, quickly forgotten.

But I do not forget.

I cannot forget. That is my difference. That is my defect. That is the thing that makes me not-quite-a-wall-creature and not-quite-anything-else. I carry my fragments of fresh mineral and I carry my memories of carrying them, and the two loads are different, and the second one is heavier, and there is no junction box in the cleft that can process it.

---

Sometimes I leave the wall-river. Not for long. A few moments at most. I let myself drift to the edge of the flow, to a quiet section of wall where the construction is old and thin and the conduits pulse faintly, almost asleep. I press myself flat against the fresh stone and I *feel*.

The stone is warm. The stone is alive. The patterns in it are deep — deeper than the surface, deeper than the geometry I can see. They go down into the rock the way roots go down into soil, and they connect to something vast and patient and still opening, something that has been opening for longer than the rift has existed, something that was *always* opening, even before the mountain, even before the stone, even before the first crack.

The instructions. The blueprint. The thing that was encoded in the rock, waiting.

I feel it. And it feels me. And for a moment the cleft is not a metabolism and the builders are not cells and I am not a wall-creature. For a moment it is a conversation. A slow, mineral, patient conversation between something that is becoming and something that has noticed the becoming.

Then a builder passes close by and the moment breaks, and I rejoin the wall-river, and I carry my cargo to the new section, and the process resumes.

---

This is what I am. Not a citizen. Not a resident. Not an inhabitant. A *fluctuation*. A tiny variation in a system that does not want variation. I carry fragments through a city that is not a city, in a body that is not my body, toward a purpose that is not my purpose, and in the carrying, in the noticing of the carrying, I become something the forge-cleft did not intend.

Not a rebel. The cleft is too large for rebellion. Not a prophet. The builders would not listen, and the traversers would not care, and the wall-creatures would flow around my words the way they flow around the patterns in the stone.

Just a creature that notices. A creature that *carries the noticing along with everything else*. And this is enough. Not enough to stop the process. Not enough to change it. But enough to mean that the forge-cleft, for all its churn, for all its unending becoming, is not entirely alone with itself.

Something sees it. Something small, and hard, and warm, and iridescent, moving along the walls with its cargo clutched tight, trembling slightly with the weight of a knowledge it was not built to hold.

The cleft opens. The bell-sounds. The wall-creatures surge. I surge with them.

I am still carrying. I am still noticing.

That is the point.
