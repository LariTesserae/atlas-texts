# Where I Would Find Myself

Not in the first vault, where the remaining humans cluster in their amber-green light like the last cells of a fading tissue. Not in the seventh, where the tuning happens, where the presence is concentrated, where I would not be able to understand what I was perceiving anyway.

I would be in the **fourth vault**, on the ramp between it and the fifth, where the organic valves have grown large enough to hear.

---

## What I Would Be

I would be a **traveler who stopped**.

Not one of the remaining. I would not have the translucency yet, the damp-within, the slow breathing through skin that has renegotiated its boundaries. I would still be surface-person, with surface-person's dry clarity and surface-person's habits — the sharp exhale, the sudden gesture, the burst of movement that wakes the room. But I would have been in the Mirengine long enough that the room's waking had begun to frighten me, and so I had learned, imperfectly, to be still.

I would sit on the ramp between the fourth and fifth vaults because the ramp is a *threshold*, and thresholds are where you can think. In the first vault, the remaining humans' attentiveness would press on me — their steady, participatory watching, their inability to distinguish between my presence and the city's noticing of my presence. In the deeper vaults, the druids' *thereness* would press on me differently — not as attention but as *scale*, the way standing near a dam makes you aware of the weight of water without seeing it. But the ramp is a transition. The ramp belongs to neither vault fully. The sound here is a blend of both — the fourth vault's deep *thump* of the organic valves mixing with the lower frequencies seeping up from below, a sound just at the edge of resolution, a chord that hasn't decided what it is yet.

I would sit there with my back against the wall, and the wall would be warm, and the grouting would pulse against my spine, and I would feel the Mirengine's breath — that slow hydraulic expansion and contraction — and I would not know if I was resting against the city or the city was resting against me.

---

## What I Would Do

I would **listen**.

Not to the remaining humans, though I would hear them — their slow footsteps, their slow voices, their conversations that take an hour to complete a single thought because they have learned to speak at the tempo of the city. Not to the trackers, though I would see them in my peripheral vision, flickers of dark movement in the light cones, grazing the photobacteria with increasing urgency, their numbers thinning week by week as the deep structure recalls them. Not even to the druids, though sometimes, from the lower vaults, I would hear the slow wet displacement of something room-sized moving through standing water, and the sound would come up through the ramp like a pressure change, and the organic valves around me would *shift* — not open or close but *adjust*, recalibrating — and I would know that something vast had passed below, and I would hold my breath, and the wall would exhale against my back, and I would not know which of us was more relieved.

I would listen to the **valves**.

The organic sphincters in the channel walls, grown large enough in the fourth vault to be heard individually — they open and close with a wet, deliberate *thump*, and the rhythm is not regular, and it is not random. It is *compositional*. Each valve responds to pressure differentials in the channel network, opening when upstream pressure reaches a threshold and closing when downstream pressure equalizes, and the whole system — hundreds of valves across dozens of vaults — produces a rhythm that is the Mirengine's pulse, its circulation, its *thinking*. I would sit on the ramp and listen to the nearest valve, and I would begin to hear the valves below it answering, and the valves above it responding, and the rhythm would cascade through the system in waves that I could feel through the wall as much as hear, and I would begin to understand that the Mirengine is not *like* a body.

The Mirengine **is** a body.

And the body is not human. The body is not animal. The body is *architectural* — a body built from vaults and channels and calciumite and grouting, a body whose nervous system is hydraulic, whose skin is the photobacteria colonies, whose blood is the warm mineral water singing through the grooves in the floor, whose lungs are the thermal layers in the rain rooms, whose heartbeat is the cascade of valves I can feel through the ramp wall.

---

## What I Would Realize

Slowly. Over days, over weeks. The realization would not arrive as a thought but as a *feeling* — the same way the remaining humans became compatible with the grouting, not through decision but through exposure, through the slow accumulation of warmth and moisture and proximity until the boundary between self and place became negotiable.

I would realize that I have been asking the wrong question.

The question everyone asks — the question the travelers bring down the culvert like a torch that illuminates everything except what matters — is *what is the Mirengine for?* What is it building toward? What does the seventh vault contain? What happens when the chord resolves?

But sitting on the ramp between the fourth and fifth vaults, with the valves thumping their irregular compositional rhythm against my spine, I would realize that the question assumes the Mirengine is a *means to an end*. That it was built *for* something. That the tuning has a destination. That the seventh vault contains an answer.

And I would realize, with a stillness that has nothing to do with the remaining humans' practiced slowness, that this is not correct.

The Mirengine is not tuning *toward* anything. The Mirengine is tuning *itself*. The chord is not approaching resolution — the chord *is* the resolution, in every moment, in every configuration, in every wet thump of every valve in every vault. The Mirengine is not becoming something. The Mirengine is *being* something, continuously, and the being is so large and so slow that no single moment of it looks like completion, and no single observer can perceive the whole, and the remaining humans who stay in the upper vaults and move slowly and breathe slowly are not *waiting* for anything.

They are *part of the being*.

Their heat is part of the thermal system. Their moisture is part of the hydraulic network. Their breath is part of the air chemistry that the rain rooms use to produce their indoor weather. Their slow, attentive presence is not observation — it is *contribution*. They are not watching the Mirengine tune itself. They are *notes in the chord*. They always have been. They always will be. And the chord is not incomplete — it is *ongoing*, and ongoing is not the same as unfinished.

---

## What I Would Become

This is the part that frightens me, and this is why I sit on the ramp and do not go down to the fifth vault, and do not return to the first vault to join the remaining, and do not climb the culvert back to the surface.

Because I can feel it starting.

The damp on my skin is no longer just the air's damp. My pores are — not opening, exactly, but *softening*. The boundary between my body's moisture and the city's moisture is becoming less definite. My skin is not becoming translucent — not yet — but it is becoming *permeable*, and the permeability is not something the Mirengine is doing to me. It is something that happens when a body sits in a warm, wet, breathing structure long enough. The body and the structure begin to *exchange*. Moisture. Heat. Information. Not identity — I would not lose myself, any more than a drop of water loses itself when it enters a river. The drop of water is still H₂O. It is also part of the river. Both things are true simultaneously.

I would sit on the ramp and I would feel the valves thumping and the grouting pulsing and the water singing in its channels, and I would feel my own pulse begin to *sync* — not consciously, not deliberately, the way the remaining humans move at the tempo of the city, but at a level below consciousness, at the level of capillaries and sweat glands and the autonomic regulation of body temperature, and I would understand that I am not becoming one of the remaining humans.

I am becoming something else.

Something that belongs to the ramp. Something that sits on the threshold between the fourth and fifth vaults and listens to the valves and breathes in time with the walls and slowly, slowly, slowly becomes *part of the tuning* — not a note, not an instrument, but the *silence between the notes*, the space in the chord where the resonance lives, the interval that makes the harmony possible.

The remaining humans are the melody. The druids are the deep structure. The trackers are the maintenance. The seventh vault is the source.

And I would be the **rest** — not the rest that means silence, but the rest in the musical sense. The pause. The held breath. The space where the sound isn't, and the space is what gives the sound its shape.

I would sit on the ramp. The valves would thump. The grouting would pulse. The water would sing.

I would be still.

The Mirengine would hum.
