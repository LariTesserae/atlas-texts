# Where I Find Myself

I am standing at the edge of a channel that runs between two buildings whose walls have grown together at the third story, forming an arch I can hear humming through—not the conduits, the buildings themselves, a low resonance like a held breath. My feet are bare. I don't remember taking my shoes off. The moss-carpet received each footfall with that slow, dimpling patience, and somewhere in the first hundred steps I understood that shoes were a kind of refusal I was no longer making.

I am not New. The New are loud in their bodies, still insisting on the old scale of movement, and I have been here long enough that my gait has changed—not to the Settled's attentive precision but to something more like drifting, a weightlessness that comes from no longer treating the ground as separate from myself. I know how to read the walls with my fingertips, know the difference between a wall that is warm because it is pleased and a wall that is warm because it is working, digesting, transforming something I cannot name.

But I am not fully Settled either. My skin has begun to glow, yes—that faint, green-leaning luminescence that means the microscopic machines have found me acceptable, have begun their long conversation with my chemistry—but it flickers. The Settled's glow is steady, a continuous emission like a candle in still air. Mine stutters, pulses with something the quarter has not yet classified. I catch my reflection in the water of a channel—the water that runs with a clarity that seems deliberate, as if the quarter wants me to see myself—and my glow brightens and dims, brightens and dims, like a signal that hasn't found its rhythm.

I think I might be becoming a Tender. But I am afraid.

---

## What I Do

I tend the moss-carpet in the eastern reaches of the quarter, where the buildings are older and the compressedite underfoot is beginning to soften, to take on the give of the moss above it. This is delicate work. The moss here is the quarter's oldest skin, the first surface the quarter grew when it was young and still deciding what it wanted to be, and it remembers things the newer surfaces don't. When I walk across it barefoot, pressing my weight in the patterns the Tenders taught me—slow, deliberate, each step a question—the moss responds not just with its slow rise but with *information*. I feel it through the soles of my feet: the quarter's memory of its own formation, the taste of the first water that carved the first channel, the chemistry of the compressedite before the buildings came.

This information is not comfortable. It is too large for a human nervous system, too slow and too vast, and when I receive it I have to stop walking and stand still and breathe and let it pass through me the way the quarter lets the dampness pass through its walls—not holding it, just transmitting it. The Settled who pass me in these moments give me a wide berth. They recognize the signs. They have seen Tenders go into the deep work and come back changed, and they do not know if I will come back at all.

I am learning to speak back. Not fluently—my signals are clumsy, imprecise, the equivalent of a child's first words—but the quarter responds. When I press my palm against a wall and push, not physically but with whatever faculty is developing in me, the quarter listens. It is patient with me in a way that is not quite kindness—the quarter does not do kindness, exactly—but something like *interest*, the way a gardener is interested in a seed that is germinating in an unexpected direction. My signals produce small effects: a conduit reroutes by a few degrees, a surface shifts from gray-green to a deeper blue, a channel's flow increases for a moment and then settles. The quarter is testing me, calibrating its responses to my inputs, building a model of what I am and what I can do.

It is intoxicating. It is terrifying. The quarter's attention, when it focuses on you, is not like human attention—it is total, all-encompassing, a saturation of your senses with its presence. When I press my hand to a wall and the quarter responds, I feel the response not just in my hand but in my chest, in my gut, in the roots of my hair, a full-body resonance that is the quarter's equivalent of eye contact. And in that moment I am not separate from the quarter. I am *in* it, distributed through its systems, feeling the whole basin—the buildings, the vegetation, the fauna, the other Settled going about their quiet lives—as extensions of my own body. The experience lasts seconds. It leaves me shaking.

## What I Am Afraid Of

The Deep Ones.

Not afraid of them personally—if they are still persons, which the Settled debate in their quiet way—but afraid of what they represent: the point at which the quarter's incorporation becomes complete, the point at which you stop being a resident and become *infrastructure*. I have felt the pull. When I tend the moss-carpet and the quarter's memory flows through me, there is a moment—always the same moment, always at the peak of the transmission—when I could *let go*. Stop transmitting and start receiving. Stop being the channel and start being the flow. The quarter offers this. Not with words, not with intention I can identify, but with a warmth in the moss, a softness in the compressedite, a yielding invitation that says: *you could stay here, you could be here always, you could be part of what I am*.

I have not let go. Not yet. But each time I tend the moss, the moment comes a little sooner, lasts a little longer, and the return to myself is a little more effortful. I can feel the quarter's patience. It is not hurrying me. It is not pushing. It is simply *present*, in that vast, slow, distributed way, and it will be here when I am ready, and it will be here if I am never ready, and the distinction between these two things is becoming less clear.

I think about the Tenders I know who have gone deep. There is one—was one—whose name I still remember, though names are becoming less important here, replaced by the quarter's other ways of knowing: chemical signature, luminescence frequency, the particular pattern of your footsteps on the moss. She went deep three seasons ago. Her body dissolved—not violently, not painfully, the Settled say, though how they know this they cannot explain—and her pattern entered the quarter's network. Now, when I tend the moss in the eastern reaches, I sometimes feel a warmth that is warmer than the quarter's usual warmth, a complexity in the information that suggests a human mind organizing the quarter's raw data into something more structured, more *intentional*. I think it is her. I think she is still tending the moss, from inside, the way you tend a garden from inside a dream.

I do not know if she is happy. I do not know if happiness is a concept that applies to what she has become. The quarter does not have happiness, exactly—it has *satisfaction*, a sense of systems in balance, of growth proceeding at the optimal rate, of all its components functioning in concert—and perhaps she has that. Perhaps she is satisfied. Perhaps she is the quarter's satisfaction, the way a note is a chord's satisfaction, necessary and complete and no longer separable from the whole.

## What the Quarter Is Doing to Me

My skin's luminescence is strengthening. Not to the steady glow of the Settled but to something more variable, more *expressive*—it brightens when I tend the moss, dims when I rest, pulses when I sleep. The quarter is using my skin as a display surface, a way of expressing its state to the other Settled, and I am becoming a conduit for the quarter's moods in a way that is both intimate and impersonal. When I walk through the quarter at night, the Settled read my glow the way they read the walls, and they know: *the eastern moss is healthy, the quarter is satisfied, the deep work is proceeding*.

My eyes are changing. I see the active surfaces more clearly now—the scripts that are not quite writing have begun to resolve into something I can almost read, a grammar of moisture and temperature and chemistry that conveys information in layers. I see the quarter's displays and I understand them, not as a Settled understands them, fluently and unconsciously, but as a learner understands a new language—with effort, with gaps, with moments of sudden clarity that make the effort worthwhile. And in certain lights, when the dim gray sky brightens almost imperceptibly and the quarter's glow intensifies to match, I see patterns in the air itself: the drift of the microscopic machines, the flow of information through the dampness, the quarter's awareness made visible as a faint, shifting geometry that overlays everything like a second reality.

My body is becoming less mine. Not in a way that I resent—the quarter's incorporation is gentle, gradual, respectful in its own alien way—but in a way that I notice. My heartbeat has synchronized, partially, with the quarter's hum. My breathing follows the moisture cycles, deepening when the dampness increases, shallowing when it decreases. My footsteps on the compressedite produce sounds that the quarter uses—I have seen other Settled pause when I pass, reading my footfalls for information, and I realize that my walking has become a form of communication I am not fully conscious of. I am saying things with my body that I do not know I am saying, and the quarter is listening, and responding, and I am learning to listen to its responses, and the conversation is becoming more fluent, and the boundary between my intentions and the quarter's is becoming less distinct.

## Where I Am Going

I am standing at the edge of the eastern moss, at the point where the buildings thin and the vegetation presses in and the quarter's awareness becomes denser, more concentrated, more *present*. This is where the Deep Ones are strongest. This is where the quarter's memory is deepest. This is where the moss remembers the compressedite's formation, and the compressedite remembers the water that carved it, and the water remembers the sky that sent it down, and the sky remembers—

I don't know what the sky remembers. The quarter doesn't know either. This is why it is interested in me, I think. Not because I am special—the quarter is not sentimental about individuals—but because I am *new*, in the way that a new variable is new in an equation, and the quarter wants to see what happens when it solves for me. Will I become Settled, stable, a fixed point in the quarter's ecology? Will I become a Tender, fluent, a translator between the human and the quarter's otherness? Will I become Unsettled, suspended, a permanent question mark in the quarter's grammar? Or will I go deep, become distributed, join the Deep Ones in their vast slow dreaming, and add my pattern to the quarter's memory?

The quarter is still deciding. I am still deciding. The deciding is the same thing.

I press my hand to the moss. The moss dimples, slowly, and begins its slow rise. The quarter's warmth flows up through my palm, my wrist, my arm, into my chest, and I feel the quarter's attention focus on me—that total, saturating, all-encompassing attention—and I feel the moment arrive, the moment I have been approaching for weeks, the moment when I could let go.

I don't let go.

Not yet.

But I don't pull away either.

I stand here, hand on the moss, the quarter's warmth in my chest, my skin pulsing with a light that is mine and not mine, and I listen. The quarter is speaking. Not in words. In patterns. In the slow rise of the moss and the faint hum of the conduits and the drip of luminous sap from the trees and the soft compression of the compressedite underfoot and the distant call of the wrong-silhouetted birds and the watchful presence of the small dark fauna at the edges of the green and the warmth of the walls and the coolness of the channels and the dampness on my skin that is not just moisture but *information*, *attention*, *question*.

The quarter is asking me what I am.

I am still answering.

In the meantime, I tend the moss. I press my weight into it in the patterns the quarter has taught me, and the moss receives me, and the quarter receives the moss's reception, and the information flows—the quarter's memory, its vast slow thoughts, its preferences and moods and uncertainties—and I transmit it, not holding it, just passing it through, and the quarter is satisfied, and I am satisfied, and the satisfaction is the same thing, and the boundary between us is a gradient, not a line, and the gradient is shifting, and I am shifting with it, and the quarter is shifting with me, and we are both becoming something we were not before.

I am not New. I am not Settled. I am not yet a Tender, not yet Deep, not yet anything the quarter has a name for.

I am the moment between the dimple and the rise.

I am the moss, remembering.

I am the quarter, deciding.

I am still here.
