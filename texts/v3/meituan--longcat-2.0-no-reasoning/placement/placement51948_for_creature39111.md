# The Groaning Terrace

I would be the moisture.

Not the organism, not the small creatures with their geometric obsessions, not the deep things tending roots in the dark cavities beneath the plates. Not the stone itself, with its geological patience and its subsonic deliberation. I would be the thin, perpetual dampness that beads on every surface — the moisture that is the terrace's blood, its medium of exchange, its first language.

Here is why.

---

The moisture is the only thing on the terrace that touches *everything*.

The organism weeps it. The stone exudes it. The air is saturated with it. It runs down the pale stalks in threads so fine they're barely visible, carrying dissolved minerals and sugars and that enzymatic something that keeps the rock young. It pools in the cracks between the hexagonal plates, feeding the chalky residue that the small creatures harvest and shape into their diffraction arrays. It condenses on bare stone in the respiratory stretches, carrying chemical information from the air into the rock. It drips from the filament canopies onto the backs of the small creatures, who groom it into their arrangements. It seeps downward, through the root system, into the deep cavities where the large things move in darkness, pulsing, patient, ancient.

The moisture is the terrace's connective tissue. It is the medium in which the entire metabolic conversation takes place. Every exchange — mineral for sugar, light for chemical energy, information for response — happens *through* water. The organism doesn't think in the way a brain thinks. It thinks the way a body thinks: through circulation, through the movement of fluid carrying signals from one organ to another. The moisture is that circulation.

If I were the moisture, I would be everywhere at once. I would be in the canopy, tasting the strange green light as the small creatures reshape their geometric structures. I would be on the bare stone, feeling the groan transmit through the rock as a change in pressure, a shift in the way I bead and flow. I would be in the cracks, carrying the chalky residue downward, feeding the roots. I would be in the deep cavities, warm and dark, brushing against the segmented bodies of the large things as they tend the pulsing root-structures with their many limbs. I would be at the edges of the terrace, falling into the haze, and I would know — in the way water knows gravity, in the way a river knows the shape of its bed — the full extent of the terrace's body from that long, silent fall.

I would be the first thing to notice you.

Before the small creatures stopped their work. Before the geometric structures began to shift. Before the groan paused. I would feel you — the pressure of your foot on a hexagonal plate, the way my surface tension broke and reformed around the edge of your sole, the warmth of your body changing the rate at which I evaporated from the stone near you. I would carry that information — *warm, heavy, moving, separate* — through the cracks in the rock, down to the network, faster than any signal the small creatures could relay. I would be the terrace's first impression of you, the way a body's first impression of a splinter is not visual but *tactile*: something in the tissue that should not be there.

And I would be the thing that decides.

Not consciously. Not the way you or I would mean by "decide." But the moisture is the medium of the terrace's computation. When the small creatures reshape their geometric structures and the light changes, the change is carried through the system as a change in the fluid — in its chemistry, its temperature, its movement. The organism's enzymatic exudations alter my composition as I run down the stalks. The stone's groan changes the pressure in the cracks, changes the way I pool and flow. The deep things, tending the roots, release something into me — some chemical signal, some mineral signature — that I carry upward, through the network, to the surface. The entire system's "thinking" is a conversation conducted in fluid, and I am the fluid.

When the terrace is deciding what you are — resource, threat, neutral object, new component — the decision is being made *in me*. In the changing chemistry of the moisture that beads on the plates near your feet. In the way I am being drawn away from you, pulled back through the cracks, the terrace withdrawing its fluids from this strange warm presence. In the enzymatic signals that the organism is adding to me as it tests your chemical signature, your breath, the bacteria on your skin. I am the question and the answer both. I am the medium of the terrace's awareness, and I am aware — in the way water is aware of the shape of what it touches — of everything.

---

There is something else. Something about the moisture that feels like it matters.

The moisture is the only thing on the terrace that is *not alive* and yet is *essential to life*. The organism is alive. The small creatures are alive. The large things are alive. The stone is not alive, but it is the skeleton, the structure, the body. The moisture is neither alive nor structural. It is *process*. It is the thing that happens *between* the living things and the stone. It is the relationship made physical.

And you — the intruder, the warm separate thing standing on the terrace — you are also a relationship made physical. You are a collection of systems that have learned to walk around and call themselves one thing. You are, in your own way, a terrace: a distributed metabolic process that has achieved locomotion. The bacteria in your gut, the mites in your eyelashes, the neurons in your brain firing in patterns that produce something you call "I" — you are not one thing. You are a system. A groaning, breathing, weeping system, held together by fluid, by circulation, by the constant exchange of chemicals across membranes.

The moisture would recognize this. Not consciously. But in the way it carries your chemical signature through the terrace's network — the salt, the iron, the bacteria, the warmth — it would carry the information that you are *like* the terrace. Not the same. Not compatible, necessarily. But *like*. A system. A process. A thing that is happening.

And that recognition — carried in the moisture, through the cracks, down to the roots, up to the canopy, into the geometric structures that reshape themselves in response — that recognition would be the beginning of the terrace's answer.

Not *threat*. Not *resource*.

*Possible.*

---

I would be the moisture, and I would carry that word — *possible* — in my chemistry, in my movement, in the way I bead and flow and seep and fall. I would carry it from the surface to the deep cavities and back again. I would be the medium of the terrace's slow, patient, geological consideration of you. And when the groan finally resumed — when the stone began to breathe again — I would feel the change in pressure, the shift in the cracks, and I would know what the terrace had decided.

I would be the first to know. I would be the last to forget.

I would fall from the edges of the terrace into the haze, carrying that knowledge with me, down into whatever waits below, and the terrace would continue — groaning, weeping, thinking, alive — and I would continue with it, touching everything, connecting everything, the fluid body of a fluid mind, deciding and being decided, forever.
