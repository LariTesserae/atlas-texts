# The Canopy Floats

---

You are standing in a place that should not hold together, and does.

The ground — if you can call it that — is a lattice. Not wood, not stone, not anything with a name you already know. It is a woven substrate of living cable-roots, each one as thick as a forearm, grown into interlocking arches that span distances of thirty, forty, sixty meters between supports. The supports themselves are organisms: vast columnar things that are neither trees nor pillars, rising from somewhere far below, their surfaces warm and faintly pulsating under your palm. You can feel a rhythm in them, slow as a sleeping heartbeat, though nothing here is asleep. The lattice gaps are wide enough to see through — and you do see through them, down and down, into a layered depth of similar lattices, similar columns, a three-dimensional city of structure extending farther than the mist allows you to perceive.

**Sparse**, they said. They were right. Between these great architectural spans, there is *air*. Immense volumes of open space. You can walk a single cable-root — it is just wide enough, if you're careful — and look left or right and see nothing for twenty meters but haze and the suggestion of other lattices far away, like the floor plans of abandoned buildings seen from a great height. The openness is not empty. It is *held*. Suspended. The space itself feels deliberate, reserved, the way a cathedral holds silence not as absence but as material.

Everything is warm. Not hot — *warm*, the way blood is warm, the way a hand on your shoulder is warm. The air itself carries heat from the organisms below, from the slow metabolic processes of structures so large they generate their own weather. You breathe and the air is thick, not with humidity exactly, though there is water here too — but with *presence*. The warmth of living processes at enormous scale.

And the water. It comes from above.

The canopy — there is a canopy, impossibly far overhead, you can barely make it out through the haze — weeps. Constantly. Not rain. Something more intimate than rain. The underside of the upper lattice is covered in fine, hair-like rootlets, each one collecting condensation from the warm air as it rises and cools, and each one forming a droplet at its tip. The droplets grow. They fall. They fall a long way through the open space, and in falling they catch the light — there is light, good light, warm and even, filtering down from somewhere, diffuse and sourceless and everywhere — and each droplet becomes a brief, brilliant point. Not raining. *Drifting*. Thousands of droplets, tens of thousands, each on its own slow trajectory through the open air between lattices. Some hit the cable-roots below and are absorbed. Some hit you. They are warm when they touch your skin. Slightly viscous. They taste, if one catches on your lip, like mineral and green and something almost sweet.

The sound is — and here is where you understand what *loud* means in a place with no wind, no engines, no animals to speak of — the sound is the water. Each droplet striking a root, a leaf-surface, the lattice floor, the columnar skin of the supports, produces a note. Individually they are nothing. Collectively they are a continuous, shifting, shimmering percussion, a music of chance impacts across an entire landscape-scale instrument. It is not random, or rather it is random the way a river is random — locally unpredictable, globally coherent, a texture rather than a melody. It fills the open spaces. It *is* the open spaces. Without the sound, the silence would be unbearable. With it, the sparseness feels populated, watched, inhabited by the water's ten thousand small arrivals.

You walk. The cable-root beneath your feet is springy, alive, and it hums faintly — not from your footsteps, which are too small to register, but from the water it carries. The roots are conduits. You can see moisture beading along their undersides, moving in slow capillary pulses toward the columns. The columns drink. That is what the pulse is — drinking.

**Built**. Yes. Everything is built. And yet there is no builder, or there is nothing *but* builders, or the distinction has no meaning here. The cable-roots grew into these arches. The columns grew upward until they found something to anchor against — the underside of a higher lattice, another column, a shared root-mass. The higher lattice grew in response. Everything is construction. Everything is organism. The engineering is the ecology. The ecology is the engineering. You cannot find a seam between them because there is no seam. The world is not decorated with life. The world is *made of* life, the way a coral reef is made of life, but at this scale — this open, valley-size, multi-layered scale — the effect is architectural. Cathedrals of biology. Bridges of tissue. Domes of intergrown canopy that exist not to shelter anything but to *be*, to perform the structural function of existing in relation to other existences.

And it is *still*. Not quiet — the water is loud, always — but still. Nothing moves except the water, the slow pulse of sap, the imperceptible growth of root into root. There are no leaves turning in wind because there is no wind. There are no animals scurrying. You saw something once, far away, on another lattice — a shape, low and quick, gone before you could name it. The fauna here are rare, and they know to be rare, and they are afraid of you, or indifferent to you, or so adapted to this specific architecture that you cannot distinguish them from it. A flash of chitinous brown that might have been a beetle or might have been a root-node. A sound like breathing from somewhere below that might have been the columns or might have been something alive that was not structural. You will not find it again.

The stillness is not deadness. It is *equilibrium*. This is a system that has solved its own equations and arrived at a state from which it does not need to deviate. The water falls, is collected, is conducted, rises as warmth, condenses on the upper rootlets, falls again. The columns grow only as fast as the lattices need them. The lattices extend only as far as the columns can support them. Nothing erodes. Nothing accumulates. The place is not old — it does not feel old, it feels *arrived*, complete, a proof rather than a history. And yet there is no sense of youth either. No sense of *coming into being*. It simply *is*, and has been, and will be, in this exact configuration, with these exact droplets falling through this exact warm light, forever.

**Strange**. Yes. It is strange. Not because any single element is alien — you recognize the principles, capillary action and condensation and structural arches and load-bearing columns — but because the *resolution* of these elements into a world is unlike anything your experience has a category for. The openness at this scale, combined with this density of living structure. The loudness of falling water in a place with no rain. The warmth without a source. The total absence of anything inorganic — no rock, no mineral soil, no sand — and yet nothing fragile. Everything here is load-bearing. Everything here is *serious*.

You stand on your cable-root and look out through the drifting warm droplets into the hazy open distance, where other lattices emerge and subside like thoughts, and the columns rise and vanish into the mist above, and the sound of the water wraps around you like a held note, and you understand that you are inside something. Not a building. Not a body. A *system*. A living equation balanced across a valley-sized volume of warm air and falling water and light, and you are the only variable in it that does not belong, and the system does not care, and the water keeps falling, and the columns keep drinking, and the lattice keeps holding, and nothing changes, and nothing changes, and the light is good, and the air is warm, and you are very small, and very welcome, and entirely irrelevant.

---

The place does not need you. This is, somehow, a comfort.
