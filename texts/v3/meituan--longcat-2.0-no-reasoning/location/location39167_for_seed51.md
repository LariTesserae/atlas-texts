# The Drownplain of Ytenn

---

The ground does not end. It goes on.

You are standing — if standing is the word for it — in a place where the distinction between substrate and water has dissolved into something third. The land is a pale, fibrous mat, half-living, shot through with channels that carry not water exactly but a luminous, slow-thick fluid the color of mercury gone green. It does not splash when you step on it. It *takes* your foot, and then gives it back, and the place where your foot was is already forgetting you. There are prints, but they fill within seconds, the mat knitting over them with a sound like wet fingers pressed to wet lips — that almost-sigh, that almost-silence.

The plain extends. It extends the way fog extends, not toward a horizon but toward a *softening*. You can see, perhaps, eight hundred meters in any direction before the world begins to lose its commitment to detail. Things that might be trees stand at that distance, or might be mineral formations, or might be the columns of something vast and recumbent that has been lying here so long it has become topography. The light does not reach that far in any useful way.

The light.

There is no visible sun. There is a sourceless dimness that clings to surfaces the way dampness clings — not illuminating so much as *settling*. It has a temperature, this light. Cool and faintly blue, like the glow inside a deep aquarium, and it comes from everywhere and nowhere and from the fluid in the channels and from the underside of the mat and from the air itself, which is thick and warm-cool and carries a smell like ozone and wet stone and something underneath those, something organic and old, like the breath of a root cellar that has been sealed for a century.

**It is loud.**

Not loudly. Loud in a way that has structure. The mat hisses constantly — a granular, shifting hiss, the sound of millions of tiny adjustments as the fibrous surface settles and resettles, breathes in and out, exchanges fluid with the water-table that runs everywhere just centimeters below. Under that: a deep, rhythmic *thung*, felt more than heard, as though something enormous were turning over in its sleep far beneath the plain. And threaded through both, a high, crystalline tone that seems to come from the fluid itself, the mercury-green liquid in the channels singing at a frequency that sits right at the edge of perception. You hear it in your teeth. You hear it in the space behind your eyes.

The channels are everywhere. Not a grid — something more organic, more *intentional*. They branch and reconnect and branch again in patterns that repeat at different scales: the same curving, anastomosing geometry whether you look at a meter of ground or try to trace the whole visible network with your eyes. They are dense. They are *dense* with fluid and with the pale, filamentous growth that lines their banks in tufts like wet fur, like the cilia of something vast and flattened and still. The tufts move. Not with wind — there is no wind, the air is nearly still — but with a slow, peristaltic pulse, each tuft bending and straightening in sequences that travel along the channels like thoughts through a nerve.

**Nothing is built here.** Nothing was ever built here. The plain predates the concept of building.

And yet.

The thing that makes this place *wrong* — the thing that separates it from any mere wetland, any mere marsh, any mere lowland bog that has been left alone long enough to find its equilibrium — is the **presence**.

Not fauna. The fauna count is low; you see nothing living in the conventional sense. Occasionally something small moves in the tufts — a twitch, a retraction, a glistening point that slides from one patch of growth to the next and is gone. No shapes you can hold. No eyes. Whatever lives here lives *in* the mat and does not wish to be seen as separate from it.

No. The presence is **the place itself.**

The plain is thinking. Not quickly, not in any way you would recognize as cognition, but there is *process* here — slow, dense, layered process — and it is aware of you in the way that a body is aware of a splinter. Not hostile. Not welcoming. *Noting*. The fluid in the channels shifts its flow pattern when you move. The tufts turn — not all of them, just the ones near you, just enough — and the singing tone drops in frequency by a fraction, and you feel the thung beneath you change its rhythm by the smallest increment, and the mat beneath your feet becomes fractionally firmer, or softer, and you cannot tell if this is a response or if you are simply becoming aware of what was already happening.

**The erosion is old.** This is a landscape that has finished becoming itself. The channels have carved their paths and the paths have carved the channels and the mat has grown into the spaces left by both and the fluid has found its level and the level has found its fluid and everything is in the settled, weathered, long-accommodated state of a place that has had time — real, deep, geological time — to become what it is. There is no newness here. No raw edges. The curves of the channels are smooth and fat with age. The tufts are thick and long-established. Even the light seems *worn*, as though it has been bouncing around inside this system for so many cycles that it has lost its sharpness and become soft and tired and blue.

And yet it is active. Dynamic. The fluid pulses. The tufts breathe. The mat grows and dies and is reabsorbed and grows again at a pace just slow enough to seem still — but watch for a minute, two minutes, five, and you will see the channels *widen*. Imperceptibly. A millimeter over the course of your watching. The plain is alive and the plain is alive at every scale, from the smallest ciliated filament to the whole vast expanse that stretches out past the softening point and, you suspect, keeps stretching, keeps pulsing, keeps *noting* things, for distances you cannot guess.

**The temperature is cool.** Not cold. Cool in the way that a cellar is cool, or the inside of a cave where water runs. Your skin registers it as a gentle tightening. Your breath does not mist — the air is too humid for that — but you feel the moisture condensing on your lips, on your lashes, in the fine hairs of your forearms. Everything is slightly wet. Everything is slightly *coated*. The film on your skin tastes of minerals and something faintly sweet, faintly electric.

---

You are not afraid. You should perhaps be, but you are not, because the plain does not present itself as dangerous. It presents itself as *interested*. As a vast, slow, wet intelligence that has been here since before there were words for here, and which will be here after, and which has noted your arrival the way a pond notes a stone: with a small displacement, a small rearrangement, and then the slow, patient work of accommodating something new into an equilibrium that is older than memory.

You walk. The mat takes your feet and gives them back. The channels sing. The tufts turn. The thung beneath you turns and turns and turns, and you are inside it, and it is inside you, and the light is dim and blue and everywhere, and the plain goes on.
