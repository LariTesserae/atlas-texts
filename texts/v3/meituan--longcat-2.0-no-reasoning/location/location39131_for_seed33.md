# The Vestments of Altamira

---

You arrive somewhere above the world.

That is the first thing: not *on* a mountain, not *in* a valley, but *above* — suspended in a way that makes the word "elevation" feel thin and insufficient. The ground exists. It is beneath you. You can walk on it. But the air here has a quality of *excess height*, as though the atmosphere has been peeled back layer after layer until what remains is only a suggestion of breath, a thin membrane of cool, still gas that doesn't quite support consciousness the way lower air does. Your thoughts come slower here. Not worse. Just... given more room.

The temperature is not cold the way a winter morning is cold. It is the cool of a cathedral stone that has never been touched by sun — a fundamental, structural coolness, as if warmth is a concept that belongs to some other layer of reality entirely. It does not bite. It does not discomfort. It simply *declines* to be anything else.

---

What you stand on is a city.

This is the second thing, and it contradicts nothing, though it will feel like it should.

The city was not built by hands. It was not built by minds, not in any way that the word *built* normally implies. It was *deposited*. Over spans of time so long that the concept of "construction period" becomes nonsensical, material has settled here — mineral solutions percolating through fractures in the high rock, precipitating into forms that happen to resemble architecture with a precision that should be disturbing but isn't, because intention is not required for resemblance. A frost crystal resembles a fern. This place resembles a city. The resemblance is exact and meaningless.

The structures rise from the exposed plateau in dense clusters — *density: 0.2/3* might seem to contradict *built: 2.1/3*, but look again. The structures are *present*. They occupy space in recognizable arrangements — what you could only call streets, plazas, thoroughfares, the negative space of habitation. But between them, *within* them, *through* them, there is almost nothing. The walls are lattices. The buildings are frameworks. Stand in what appears to be a grand avenue and you can see through the structures on either side to the next cluster, and through those to the next, and through those to the pale dim distance where more structures rise like the memory of a skyline. The city is *mostly absence*. It is architecture as sieve. As filter. As the ghost of density without the substance.

The material itself is a pale mineral — something between salt andite and something that doesn't appear on any periodic table you know. It has the soft, sugary texture of evaporite deposits but the structural rigidity of engineered composite. In places it has formed into columns that branch like coral, or like the representations of coral drawn by someone who was told about coral but never saw it. In other places it lies flat and tessellated, hexagonal plates fitted together with the obsessive precision of a wasp's nest. The color is always the same: a dim, warm grey, like the inside of a cloud, absorbing the already-dim light without reflecting it, so that the city seems to *drink* vision, pulling your eyes into its surfaces without giving anything back.

---

The light comes from everywhere and nowhere.

There is no sun visible. No moon. No source you can point to and say *there*. The sky above is a uniform, dark surface — not black, but the deep, bruised indigo of something that was once lit and has been allowed to slowly, slowly dim over eons. It is the color of the inside of a closed eye in a dark room, if that inside were vast and domed and *above you*. The light that reaches the plateau is diffuse, directionless, sourceless — it arrives like a property of the air itself, a slow leak from whatever thin physics still holds at this altitude. You cast no shadow. Objects have no highlights. Depth perception becomes unreliable after the first hour.

There is no day or night here. The light does not change. It simply *is* — a permanent dim overcast without clouds, a permanent 3:00 p.m. in a country that has no name, under a sky that has no mechanism.

---

You are not the only living thing.

This is the third thing, and it is the most difficult to describe, because the fauna here does not behave like fauna. They are *rare* — you may walk for what feels like days without encountering one — but they are present, and when you do encounter one, it will be *wrong* in a way that your brain will try very hard to correct.

The first one you see is at the edge of what you've been thinking of as a plaza — a wide, flat expanse of the tessellated mineral, the hexagonal plates extending in every direction until the dimness swallows them. It stands at the center of the plaza. It is about the size of a large dog. It has four legs, or four things that function as legs. It has a body. It has something at one end that could be a head.

It is looking at you.

The wrongness is not in its shape, which is almost mammalian, almost convincing. The wrongness is in its *edges*. Where its body meets the air, there is a faint shimmer, a sense that the information about where it ends is being *negotiated* rather than stated. It is as though the creature exists in slightly more dimensions than the ones you're perceiving, and what you see is the cross-section, the slice, the part that happens to intersect with your reality. When it moves — if it moves — you will not see it walk. You will see it *here*, and then it will be *there*, and the transition between those two states will not have occurred in any time you experienced.

You do not feel threatened. You do not feel safe. You feel *observed by something that is still deciding what you are*.

It turns — pivots, reorients, the shimmer at its edges intensifying for a moment — and walks into a building that is mostly holes, and does not come out, though you wait, and the waiting feels like a meaningful act.

---

The erosion is the city's true architect.

Wind exists here, but barely — a slow, mineral-laden exhalation that moves across the plateau at the speed of continental drift. You cannot feel it on your skin. But over the timescales that matter here, it has *worked*. The structures are not pristine. They are worn, scalloped, undercut in places, the soft outer layers dissolved away to reveal harder inner geometries that look like exposed bone or exposed thought. In some places the wind (or whatever slow process serves as wind) has carved the mineral into forms that are almost but not quite *deliberate* — a doorway that is too perfectly arched, a spiral staircase that descends into a darkness that has no floor, a bridge between two structures that is one atom thick and somehow still stands.

These are not ruins. The city has never been more intact than it is now. This is its settled state — its *balance point* between deposition and dissolution, the equilibrium of a system that has been running so long it has forgotten it is running. The erosion has not destroyed the city. It has *finished* it.

---

The weirdness is not an event. It is the medium.

You notice it first in small ways. You look at a structure on the horizon — a cluster of the branching columns, perhaps three kilometers away — and you *know* how far away it is. You know it the way you know the layout of your childhood home, without reference, without measurement. Then you look away, and look back, and it is closer. Not dramatically. Just... closer than it should be. The distance between things here is not fixed. It is a *preference* that the landscape holds loosely, and sometimes it changes its mind.

You find a staircase that descends into the plateau. You follow it down. It goes deeper than the plateau is thick — deeper than geology should allow — and at the bottom there is a room. The room is circular. The walls are smooth. There is a single opening in the ceiling, far above, through which the dim light falls in a column that does not reach the floor. In the center of the room, there is a *sound*.

Not a sound you hear. A sound you *are*. A low, sub-audible hum that you feel in your teeth, in your sternum, in the fluid of your eyes. It is the sound the city makes when it is not making any other sound. It is the sound of the mineral lattice vibrating at the frequency of its own structure — the resonant note of this specific arrangement of matter in this specific place, sustained forever because nothing here changes fast enough to damp it.

You stand in the room and the hum moves through you and for a moment you understand something about the city — that it is not a place, it is a *process*, a standing wave in some medium you don't have instruments for, and it has been oscillating here for so long that the oscillation has become *architecture*, has become *terrain*, has become *home* to whatever the shimmering four-legged things are, has become *this*, has become the ground under your feet and the air in your too-thin lungs and the dim light that has no source.

The understanding passes. You climb the stairs. You emerge onto the plateau. The plaza is still there. The hexagonal plates. The holes in the buildings. The vast, mostly-empty space between the mostly-empty structures.

You are very high up.

---

Far away — or not far, distance being negotiable — one of the creatures stands on a lattice wall, its edges shimmering, its head-thing oriented toward the non-sky. It is doing nothing. It has always been doing nothing. It will continue to do nothing. But the nothing it does is *specific*, is *attentive*, is a form of engagement with this place that you do not have a word for because it does not involve tools or technology or intention or even, in any way you recognize, *life*.

It is *attending*.

The city hums. The light does not change. The cool air holds its single, permanent temperature. Somewhere, mineral is depositing on a wall that has been growing for longer than your species has had a word for *wall*. Somewhere, mineral is dissolving from a bridge that has been thinning for longer than your species has had a word for *time*.

The plateau extends in every direction, and you could walk for days — for years — and the city would continue, lattice and absence and the faint shimmer of things that exist in more dimensions than you can see, and the distance between landmarks would shift like a mind changing its mind, and the light would remain the color of the inside of a closed eye, and the hum would remain below hearing, and the creatures would remain rare and attentive and wrong.

You are above the world.

The world does not know you are here.

The city doesn't care. The city has been *finished* for eons. It is simply continuing to be what it is — a standing wave in stone, a mineral thought, a place where reality has worn thin enough that the things on the other side can almost be seen, if you don't look directly at them, if you stand in the right plaza, if the distance between you and the shimmering creature happens, just then, to be *willing*.
