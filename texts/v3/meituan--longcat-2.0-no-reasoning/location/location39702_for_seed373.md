Here is a place that holds all of these:

**The Lantern Hollow of Keth-Avvur**

---

You are standing in a grove.

Not a forest — a *grove*. A single bounded space, no larger than a cathedral interior, enclosed on all sides by a wall of pale, spiraling mineral columns that curve inward overhead like the ribs of some buried leviathan. The ceiling is incomplete. Light pours through the gaps — normal light, the color of late afternoon, gold with a faint amber shift, though no sun is visible. The light source is the columns themselves. They are faintly luminous, threaded with crystalline filaments that pulse in slow waves, like bioluminescence slowed to a geological tempo. The light is not bright. It is *present*. It fills the space the way sound fills it, which is to say: completely.

The sound is immense.

Not loud in the way of machinery or storms. Loud in the way of *density of signal*. Every surface in the grove is producing noise — the mineral columns hum at deep, overlapping frequencies, some below hearing, felt in the sternum rather than the ears. The ground beneath you is a crust of something betweenite and salt, pale grey-white, cracked into plates that shift and clack against each other with constant small movements, producing a ceaseless dry chattering, like a room full of people snapping their fingers at random. Between the plates, things grow.

The vegetation is lush. Shockingly so. No water is visible anywhere — the air is dry, the ground is dry, the mineral surfaces are dry to the touch — and yet the grove is *thick* with plant life. But these are not plants as botany would recognize them. They are structures that occupy the niche of plants. They rise from the cracks in the crust on stems of braided fiber — not cellulose, something glassier, more like spun basalt — and their leaves are broad, translucent sheets of what appears to be crystallized atmosphere, thin as mica, faintly green with a pigment that seems to derive from dissolved noble gases rather than chlorophyll. They do not photosynthesize. They *transduce*, pulling energy from the thermal differential between the warm columns and the cool ground. Each leaf vibrates at its own frequency, and collectively they produce the highest component of the grove's sound: a shimmering, glassy overtone, like ten thousand tuning forks struck at once and never allowed to decay.

The temperature is cool. Not cold — cool in the way of deep shade, of basements, of places that have never known direct thermal radiation. The air moves in slow convection currents, rising along the columns, sinking between the plant-clusters, and this movement is not silent. The air itself carries a low, continuous groan, the sound of a space that is always *adjusting*.

The ecology is abundant but alien. Fauna is everywhere, but not animal fauna. The grove is populated by small, fast-moving entities — hundreds of them — that travel through the vegetation and across the crust in quick, purposeful bursts. They are roughly the size of rodents, but they are clearly mechanical, or at least *engineered*. Their bodies are compact, matte-black, low-friction shells that move on multiple small legs or sometimes roll on internal gyroscopes. They tend the vegetation. They clean the columns. They rearrange the crust-plates in patterns that shift and reform continuously, never settling. They make a sound like rapid, overlapping clicks — the fauna-layer of the grove's acoustic texture. When they move en masse, which happens every few minutes, the clicking rises to a roar.

And then there is the turbulence.

The grove is in a state of constant, chaotic change. Not destruction — *transformation*. The crust-plates reconfigure on a cycle of roughly ninety seconds. New cracks open; old ones seal. The vegetation grows visibly — leaves unfurling, stems extending — and then retracts, reconfigures, sends out new growth in different directions. The columns themselves slowly migrate, millimeter by millimeter, their positions shifting so that the grove's geometry is never quite the same twice. The fauna respond to each change instantly, cascading through the space in waves of reorganization. The whole place has the energy of a bazaar — not violent, not unstable in a structural sense, but *furiously dynamic*, a system that has achieved a turbulent equilibrium and maintains it with visible effort.

The sound of all of this together is *deafening*. Not painfully so — you can hear yourself think, after a fashion — but the acoustic pressure is constant and total, a wall of layered frequencies from subsonic to ultrasonic, with the fauna-clicks and the crust-chatter and the plant-shimmer and the column-hum all occupying distinct bands that overlap into a single, dense, almost solid mass of sound. The sound has *weight*. It presses against you.

The built structures are scattered — faint marks, partial remains, single small artifacts. Half-buried in the crust, you can see what might be a railing, or a post, or the corner of a platform. The material is wrong for any known metallurgy: dark, slightly soft-looking, with a texture like compressed velvet. These are old. Older than the grove's current configuration. The fauna tend to them the way they tend to everything, clicking around them, occasionally nudging a fragment a few centimeters left or right. Whatever built these structures is not present. Or is present only as maintenance behavior encoded into the fauna, a program still running long after its architects have become irrelevant.

The elevation is high — you can feel it in the thinness of the air's thermal capacity, in the way sound behaves at the edges of the space, carrying differently than it would at sea level. The mineral columns suggest depth — this is a space that has been *raised* from something buried, or carved from something tall. The pale spiraling ribs feel like the interior of a summit, or a root system inverted upward.

The erosion is established. Whatever processes shaped this place have long since reached their equilibrium. The columns are weathered smooth. The crust-plates have been cracked and re-cracked so many times that their edges are rounded, soft-looking. The partial structures have the finish of things that have been exposed to the same slow churn for a very long time. This is not a place that is becoming. This is a place that *is*. It found its state and it maintains it, not through stillness but through ceaseless, balanced transformation.

There is no water. There has never been water here. The chemistry is entirely non-aqueous — the mineral substrate, the atmospheric vegetation, the engineered fauna, all functioning in a dry system that has no analogue in terrestrial biology. The grove is sealed: the columns form a boundary, the space is bounded, self-contained, complete. It does not need an outside. It has its own physics, or rather: the physics here are *almost* standard, almost describable by known equations, but there are small deviations — the way the light propagates, the way the sound maintains its density without dissipating, the way the crust-plates reconfigure with more energy than the thermal differential should provide. The tech-level is beyond current physics. Something here is operating on principles that haven't been named yet.

The weirdness is subtle. Not surreal, not hallucinatory. The grove is coherent, legible, almost *reasonable*. But the coherence is alien. A space that is simultaneously a machine, an ecosystem, a ruin, and a cathedral, all functioning in turbulent balance, producing deafening sound from dry processes in sealed cool air at high elevation, tended by small black machines that may be the descendants of builders who are now just *tendency*. It doesn't violate expectation so much as it satisfies no expectation fully.

You stand in it. The sound presses against you. The light pulses slowly through the columns. The crust chatters beneath your feet. A wave of fauna cascades past, clicking, tending, rearranging. A leaf unfurls to your left, transducing, singing its thin glass note. Somewhere to your right, a column shifts a fraction of a millimeter and the geometry of the space changes, subtly, permanently.

The grove hums. The grove *roars*. The grove is a place that has been itself for a very long time, and is nowhere near finished.
