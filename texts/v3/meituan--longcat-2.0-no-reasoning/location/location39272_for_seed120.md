# The Womb of the God-Engine

---

You are inside something that is still deciding what it is.

The space is small — perhaps twenty feet across, perhaps less, though distance behaves strangely here and you cannot be sure. The walls are not walls. They are *becoming* walls. You can watch them happen: mineral-rich water seeps through a membrane of woven root-fiber and crystallizes in real time, building the surface outward in slow, branching dendrites — calcium and silica and something else, something that shouldn't precipitate at this temperature but does, because the rules here are still being negotiated. The structure is half-grown, half-built, entirely alive, and it is *hot*. The air presses against your skin like a feverish palm. Thirty-eight degrees, maybe forty. The water that weeps from every surface is warm enough to steam slightly where it meets the cooler patches of air near the floor, and the steam doesn't rise — it *lingers*, thickens, condenses back into droplets that bead on your arms and carry a faint mineral sweetness, almost like blood.

The floor is a lattice. Someone — or something — has woven a mat of split bamboo and braided vine over a shallow basin of dark, still water, and the lattice creaks and flexes under your weight with a sound like a wooden ship in a gentle sea. Below the lattice, the water is alive with movement: not fish, not quite. Pale, eyeless things the length of your finger, translucent, pulsing with bioluminescent organs that glow a deep arterial red. They are rare — you see only three or four at a time — but they are *dense* where they are, packed into the water column in tight, writhing clusters that part and reform like a single organism thinking about whether to flee. They have no mouths. They feed through their skin. The water they live in is saturated with dissolved organic matter, and they simply *absorb*, and their bodies glow brighter after each feeding pulse, and the light they cast is the primary illumination in this place — a dim, wet, red-gold glow that makes everything look like the inside of a body.

Because that is what this place is. Or is becoming.

The vegetation is not growing *on* the structure. It *is* the structure. Thick ropes of a vine with no name you would recognize — dark green, almost black, with leaves like cupped hands — have been trained and woven and *grown* into load-bearing columns, archways, a kind of ribbed ceiling that curves overhead like the inside of a skull. The leaves are broad and waxy and they weep. Every leaf-tip produces a single droplet of water that swells, holds, and falls, and the sound of all those droplets hitting the lattice floor and the water below is constant, layered, *loud* — not thunderous but pervasive, a dense patter that fills the space the way white noise fills a room, and beneath it a deeper sound: a low, rhythmic pulse, almost subsonic, that you feel in your sternum before you hear it with your ears. The heartbeat of the thing. Or your own heartbeat, reflected back at you by the wet walls, amplified and distorted until you cannot tell the difference.

The air is thick enough to chew. Humidity at saturation. Every breath tastes of green — chlorophyll and mineral water and the faintest undertone of something metallic, copper or iron, that catches in the back of your throat. The heat makes the air shimmer, and the shimmer interacts with the red bioluminescent glow from below, and the whole space seems to *breathe* — expanding and contracting by millimeters, a slow peristaltic rhythm that you feel in your inner ear as a gentle pressure, in and out, in and out.

And here is where the strangeness begins in earnest.

The walls are not stable. Not in the way walls should be. The crystalline mineral growths that form the outer surface are *emerging* — this is a place that has not finished forming, that is still in the act of coming into being — and as you watch, you can see new structures appear. A ridge of quartz-like crystal pushes outward from the wall over the course of seconds, not geological time, *seconds*, and as it grows it makes a sound like ice cracking on a winter pond, a sharp bright *tink* that cuts through the rain-sound. The crystal is not quartz. It is too warm. It pulses faintly with its own light — a blue-white that is completely different from the red glow below — and where it touches the woven vine-structure, the vine *responds*, growing toward it, wrapping around it, incorporating it. The boundary between built and grown, between made and born, between technology and biology, does not exist here. It has not been invented yet.

There are tools. You can see them, hung from pegs that are themselves living branches shaped by hand — or by something with hands. A blade of obsidian, knapped to a razor edge, lashed to a handle of bone with sinew that is still pink, still curing. A bowl carved from a single piece of dark wood, its interior polished to a mirror finish that reflects the red glow in wavering patterns. A length of cord, braided from plant fiber, coiled neatly, one end frayed where it was recently cut with the obsidian blade. These are primitive things. Simple. Made by hands that understood material and edge and tension. But they are not *old*. The bone handle is still greasy. The sinew binding is still tightening as it dries. These tools were made recently, in this place, by someone who lives here, and the craft is careful and deliberate and *present* — this is not a relic site. This is a home.

The ecology is dense and strange and *packed*. Every surface is occupied. The vine-columns are host to a moss so dark it is almost black, and the moss is host to mites — you can see them if you look closely, tiny bright specks that move in coordinated waves across the moss-surface, farming something, tending something, their movement creating patterns that look almost like writing. The water below the lattice is thick with the pale eyeless things and also with something else: a film of bacterial mat that coats the underside of the lattice in a rubbery, translucent sheet, and the sheet is *structured* — it has channels, ridges, a deliberate architecture that suggests it was seeded or guided or *designed*, though by what or whom you cannot say. The air itself carries spores — you can see them in the light, golden-green motes that drift and spiral in the convection currents created by the heat, and where they land on the weeping leaves they germinate immediately, visibly, sending out hair-thin rootlets that penetrate the leaf surface in real time. The leaf doesn't suffer. The leaf *accepts*. The new growth becomes part of the old growth within minutes. Everything is being incorporated. Everything is being *used*.

The sound is overwhelming when you stop to really hear it. The rain of droplets. The creak of the lattice. The subsonic pulse. The *tink* and *crack* of crystal growth. A high, thin whine that might be insect wings — there is something flying in here, something small, something you keep almost-seeing at the edge of your vision, a blur of iridescent wing-case and too many legs that moves too fast to track and emits a sound like a finger on the rim of a wine glass. And beneath all of it, a sound that might be breathing. Might be the structure expanding and contracting. Might be something large, somewhere deeper in the system of chambers and passages that extends beyond this room in directions that don't quite correspond to the three you're used to.

Because the geometry is wrong. Not dramatically — not Escher-stairs wrong — but *subtly* wrong, in a way that accumulates. The room is roughly circular. You can see that. But the far wall seems farther away than it should be given the size of the space, and the ceiling seems higher, and the angles where the walls meet the floor are not quite ninety degrees, not quite any degree you can name. The space is *emerging*, still forming, and the geometry hasn't settled yet. Sometimes a corner appears where there wasn't one before. Sometimes a passage opens — a dark, wet, warm throat leading deeper — and you can hear sounds from inside it: the same rain-sound, the same pulse, but *layered*, as if there are other rooms beyond this one, other chambers in various stages of becoming, and the whole structure is growing outward and inward simultaneously, building itself from both directions, meeting in the middle.

The light shifts. The bioluminescent things below pulse in unison — a wave of brightening that sweeps through the water like a breath — and for a moment the whole room flares red-gold, and you see everything with terrible clarity: the tools on the walls, the weeping leaves, the crystal growths, the mites writing their patterns in moss, the bacterial architecture, the spores germinating, the lattice flexing, the water thick with life, and on the far wall, something new — a face. Or the beginning of a face. Two indentations where eyes might be. A ridge that could become a nose. It is formed from the same crystalline mineral as the other growths, but it is *shaped*, it is *intentional*, and it is looking at you, or it will be looking at you, once it finishes emerging.

The pulse quickens. The rain intensifies. The heat presses closer.

This place is not finished with you. This place is not finished with *itself*.

You are inside something that is still deciding what it is, and you are part of the decision now.
