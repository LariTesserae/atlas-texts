# The Resonant Sprawl of Vael Muur

---

You stand at the edge of something that has no proper name for itself.

The ground is black glass — not obsidian, not manufactured tile, but something in between, something that was *grown* into its current state. It slopes gently downward under your feet, polished to a mirror sheen in places, and in others fractured into razor platelets that rise at odd angles like a field of frozen blades. This is young ground. The fractures are still sharp enough to draw blood. The glass is still settling, still *remembering* the heat that made it. Every few seconds, somewhere in your peripheral vision, a crack propagates with a sound like a finger dragged around the rim of a wet crystal glass — high, thin, and gone.

The air is warm. Not tropics warm. Warm like the air above a machine that has been running all day — a low, metabolic warmth that seems to come from everywhere and nowhere at once. It clings to your skin and carries moisture that never quite condenses into rain but never quite disappears either. Your lungs pull in air that feels slightly heavier than it should, slightly *nutrient*, as though the atmosphere itself is part of some digestive process you're not privy to.

And the sound.

The sound is *everything*.

---

## What You Hear

It starts as pressure before it starts as noise. A low-frequency hum that you feel in your sternum, in the roots of your teeth, in the fluid of your inner ear. It is not one tone but thousands — a chord so dense and layered that your brain cannot separate the individual voices. There are pulses within it, rhythms that almost resolve into pattern before sliding away. Occasional shrieks cut through — impossibly high, like metal being torn at a molecular level — and then there are the deep groans, the ones that last ten or twenty seconds, that make the glass beneath you vibrate sympathetically until your bones itch.

This is not a quiet world. There is no silence here. Even in the spaces between the louder events, there is the hiss, the rush, the *thrum* of a place that is acoustically alive. You realize after a moment that some of the sound is coming from the ground, some from the air, and some from the structures ahead of you — but the largest portion, the foundational drone that everything else plays against, has no identifiable direction. It simply *is*, the way gravity simply is.

You will not get used to it. Your ears keep trying to adjust, keep trying to find a baseline, and there isn't one.

---

## What You See — The City

Ahead of you, across perhaps two kilometers of the glass plain, the city rises.

Not rises like a skyline. Rises like a *growth*. It is urban in the sense that it is organized, intentional, shaped by intelligence — but it does not look built in any way you understand construction. The structures are *folded*. That is the closest word. They emerge from the ground in sweeping curves, as though the black glass itself was heated and bent and cooled into habitable forms. Towers, but towers that spiral and branch and reconnect with themselves. Bridges that have no ends — they loop back into the towers they connect. Surfaces that appear solid from one angle and translucent from another, not because of any material change but because the geometry itself seems to shift depending on where you stand.

The color is wrong in a way that takes you a moment to identify. It is not that the city is an unusual color. It is that the city is *too many* colors, all at once, and they don't stay still. Light moves through the surfaces the way light moves through oil on water — iridescent, shifting, alive. The effect is nauseating if you stare at any one surface for too long. The eye wants to rest and cannot.

The dimness helps. The light here is perpetual overcast — not dark, but *muted*, as though the sky is a frosted lens held at a fixed distance above everything. There is no visible sun, no point source. The illumination is diffuse and directionless, which means the city has no shadows in the traditional sense. Instead, structures seem to *glow faintly from within*, their own luminescence competing with the ambient wash. The overall effect is of a place that is always seen at dusk, always half-revealed.

Between the structures — and this is the first thing that registers as truly strange — there is *space*. Vast space. The city is enormous in its footprint but minuscule in its coverage. Buildings the size of skyscrapers stand hundreds of meters apart, connected by those looping bridges and by nothing else. The gaps between them are cavernous. You can see through the city in places, all the way to the glass plain on the other side. The density of construction is maybe five percent. Maybe less. It is a city designed by something that either needed enormous rooms or was terrified of enclosure.

---

## What Grows

And in those gaps — in the vast, empty, resonant spaces between the structures — *everything grows*.

The vegetation is lush the way a fever is hot. It is aggressive, exuberant, almost *desperate* in its fecundity. Thick ropey vines dangle from the undersides of bridges and from the spiral shoulders of towers, hanging down thirty, forty, fifty meters before ending in tasseled roots that sway in air currents you can't feel. Broad-leafed plants with leaves the size of banquet tables crowd the base of every structure, their surfaces beaded with the perpetual moisture — not dripping, not wet, but *glistening*, as though every leaf has been polished with oil. Fungi in shelf formations climb the glass ground in geometric patterns, some of them phosphorescent, adding their own faint green and blue to the city's internal glow.

But the plants are not quite plants.

They move. Not quickly — not in any way you'd notice in a single glance — but over the course of minutes, the vines shift. Leaves rotate. Fungal shelves extend by millimeters. And the movement is not random. It is *responsive*. When one of the deep groans passes through the ground — one of those bone-itchers — every plant within earshot shivers in unison, a visible wave of motion that propagates outward from the epicenter like a ripple in water. Then they settle, and resume their slow, slow reaching.

Some of the vines have grown *into* the city structures. Not against them — into them. Where a tendril meets a wall, there is no boundary. The organic and the architectural merge seamlessly, the vine's tissue becoming indistinguishable from the building's surface. You cannot tell where the city ends and the garden begins. It is possible that the distinction is meaningless here.

---

## The Water

Water does not fall from the sky here, but it is everywhere. It beads on surfaces, pools in the fractures of the glass ground, threads through the root systems in visible channels. In the lower areas — the slight depressions between the city's scattered structures — it accumulates into shallow sheets, barely a centimeter deep, that reflect the shifting light of the city like scattered mirrors. These pools have no visible inlet or outlet. They simply *are*, maintained by the ambient moisture, by condensation on the cooler surfaces of the buried structures, by processes you can't identify.

The water is not still. It vibrates constantly, dimpling with the omnipresent sound, so that every pool looks like a living membrane being played by invisible fingers. Step close and you see that the vibration creates patterns — standing waves, interference nodes, geometric arrangements that hold for seconds before dissolving and reforming. The patterns are not random. They are *almost* meaningful. Almost like language. Almost like music made visible.

In a few of the deeper pools, something moves beneath the surface. Something with too many legs, or no legs at all, or legs that are not legs. You see it only as a shadow, a disturbance, a suggestion of mass moving through water that should be too shallow to hide anything. Then it is gone, and the vibration-patterns resume.

---

## The Fauna

Because there is *life here*, and there is a lot of it, and it is not hiding.

In the spaces between structures — those vast, vaulted chambers of air and vine and water — things fly. Not birds. Not insects. Something between and beyond. They are dark-bodied and many-winged, and they move in formations that are too coordinated to be a flock and too fluid to be a machine. Their wings beat at frequencies that contribute to the omnipresent sound, you realize — those high, thin shrieks are *them*, thousands of them, calling and navigating and communicating in a register that scrapes the inside of your skull.

On the ground, larger things move through the vegetation. You catch glimpses — a segmented back rising above the broad leaves, a limb that has too many joints, a cluster of eyes that catch the dim light and throw it back in colors the eyes should not be producing. They are wary of you, or curious about you, or indifferent — it is hard to read alien body language — but they are *abundant*. The ecosystem is thriving. Whatever the rules of this place are, they support a full and vigorous web of life.

Some of the creatures are small and metallic. Some of the creatures are large and organic. Some are both. There is a creature the size of a dog that watches you from the base of a spiraling tower, and its skin is the same iridescent material as the city itself, and when it breathes, the folds of its body *fold in directions that don't exist in three-dimensional space*. You look at it and your eyes water. It looks at you and blinks with eyelids that close vertically and horizontally simultaneously.

---

## The Technology

Here is what you begin to understand, standing at the edge of this place, trying to parse the sound and the light and the wrongness:

The city is not a ruin. It is not under construction. It is *operating*.

The structures are doing something. The folding, the iridescence, the way the geometry shifts — these are not aesthetic choices. The buildings are *machines*, or they are *organisms*, or they are something for which no human language has a word. They are processing. Converting. Transforming. The sound is a byproduct of their function — or perhaps their function *is* the sound, and the architecture is merely the instrument.

You see evidence of this in small ways. A section of tower surface that ripples like the surface of a pond and then resolves into a configuration of symbols — not a display, not a screen, but the structure itself *becoming* a message for a moment before reverting. A bridge that changes its curvature in response to the passage of the flying creatures overhead, adjusting its shape to accommodate their flight path before settling back. A pool of water whose vibration-pattern suddenly synchronizes with a pulse from the ground, holds the pattern for exactly eleven seconds, then releases.

This is technology so far beyond modern that the word "technology" feels like calling the ocean "some water." It is engineering at a scale and sophistication that makes the city's sparse footprint make sense — a single structure here may do the work of an entire civilization's infrastructure. The emptiness is not absence. It is *room to operate*.

And it is recent. The fractures in the glass are still sharp. The fungi are still colonizing the newest surfaces. Some of the towers have sections that are *unfinished* — not abandoned, but actively becoming, their edges blurred and indistinct, their geometry still resolving, as though the building process is one you are watching in real time. The city is young. The city is still arriving.

---

## The Feeling

You stand at the edge of the glass plain. The sound fills you until you can't tell where your body ends and the vibration begins. The warm, heavy air presses against you from all sides. The city glows and shifts and hums ahead of you, vast and sparse and alive, threaded with vine and water and creatures that should not exist, built by minds that should not exist, operating on principles that you can observe but cannot comprehend.

It is beautiful.

It is the most unsettling place you have ever been.

You take a step forward, and the glass beneath your foot rings like a bell, and for a moment — just a moment — the entire city *responds*. Every vine shivers. Every pool shifts its pattern. Every flying creature alters its course by a fraction of a degree. Every structure brightens by a fraction of a lumen.

The city knows you are here.

It does not care, or it does, or those concepts do not apply.

You keep walking.
