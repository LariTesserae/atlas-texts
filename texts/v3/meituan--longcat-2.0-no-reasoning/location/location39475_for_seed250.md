# The Lithic Tide

---

You are standing on something, though "standing" is generous. Your feet rest on a surface that is not yet committed to being solid — a membrane of mineral precipitate, thin as old ice over a spring, stretched across a darkness that goes down farther than the concept of "down" has any right to extend. It holds you. For now. It has held everything that has ever been placed upon it, which is to say: almost nothing.

The water is not water. Call it that because you need a word, because your mouth is full of it and it tastes of nothing, which is itself a taste — the taste of a substance so pure it has forgotten what dissolved things feel like. It is warm enough to not shock you and cool enough to remind you that warmth is a gift the universe does not owe. It presses against your skin with the indifference of deep atmosphere. You are submerged in it the way a thought is submerged in a mind: completely, without boundary, without the possibility of finding an edge and breaking the surface.

Above you — and this is where the place begins to resist description — there is light. Normal light. The kind you'd find in a room with good windows at mid-morning. It comes from everywhere and nowhere, diffused through the liquid medium the way light diffuses through fog, except there is no sun, no source, no point of origin. The photons simply *are*, arriving from all directions with equal conviction, as though the water itself has decided that illumination is a reasonable default state. The result is a world without shadows. Without shadows, there is no depth perception in the ordinary sense. Distance becomes a guess. Things that are close and things that differ only in scale become indistinguishable. A pebble at your foot and a continent on the horizon present the same visual weight.

The horizon. There is one. You can see it — or rather, you can see where the light begins to thicken, where the water's willingness to carry photons starts to tire, where visibility curves back on itself like a shrug. It is very far away. The scale of this place is not large in the way a desert is large, where you can at least imagine walking to the end of it. It is large in the way that a number is large when it has no ceiling. The water extends in every direction — up included, because up and down have become suggestions rather than laws — and the light fills it, and the light is normal, and the normalcy of the light is the most unsettling thing you have ever encountered, because it implies a *designer* who thought about what lighting should be and chose *this*, chose the most banal, most comfortable, most aggressively adequate illumination possible, and applied it to a volume of liquid the size of a planetary core.

---

The terrain, such as it is, is *becoming*.

This is the central fact of the place, the thing that makes it unlike anywhere else: nothing here has finished. The floor beneath your feet is not rock. It is the *idea* of rock, the mineral intention toward solidity, a lattice of crystallizing compounds that have begun the process of becoming stone and then paused, or slowed, or simply not yet committed to the final act. It is firm enough to walk on. It is also, in some places, not. You can see — in the shadowless, sourceless light — patches where the floor is thinner, where it has the translucent quality of a soap bubble, where you can see *through* it to more water below, and below that, more floor, and below that, the suggestion of something vast and structured and not yet born.

The erosion parameter is near zero. This is a landscape that has not yet been weathered. There are no canyons because there has been no cutting. There are no beaches because there has been no grinding. The geological features that do exist — and they do exist, they are *emerging* — are raw. Tectonic. You can see ridges where the floor has buckled upward, not from collision but from *growth*, from the same way a crystal grows, except the crystal is the size of a mountain range and it is growing in slow, geological real-time, and you can almost feel it: a subsonic hum, below hearing, that you perceive as a vibration in your sternum, in your teeth, in the fluid in your inner ears. The ridges are sharp. Unweathered. They have edges that would cut you, mineral edges that have never been touched by wind or water or time, because time has not yet learned how to touch this place.

Between the ridges: basins. Shallow, wide, filled with the same pure water, and in the basins, the sparse vegetation.

---

The vegetation is not plants. You understand this immediately, though it takes longer to understand what it *is*.

It grows from the floor — from the not-quite-rock, from the mineral membrane — in structures that resemble stalks, or fronds, or the quills of something that has not yet decided to be an animal. They are pale. Translucent. They have the color of things that have never needed pigment because they have never needed to photosynthesize, because the light here is not *for* anything, it simply is. They grow in clusters of three or four, widely spaced, and each cluster is identical to every other cluster, which suggests either a single organism distributed across the entire floor of this ocean, or a process that only knows one pattern and repeats it with the devotion of a prayer.

They do not move. But they are not still. There is a difference. Stillness is the absence of motion. What these structures exhibit is the *suppression* of motion — a held breath, a paused gesture, as though they are mid-sway in a current that hasn't arrived yet. Touch one (you will, eventually, because you are human and humans touch things) and it is cool and smooth and faintly resonant, like touching the skin of a drum. It vibrates at a frequency you can feel but not hear. It is, you realize, *listening*. Or waiting. Or both.

There are no roots. No leaves. No flowers, no seeds, no decay. The vegetation does not appear to grow, and it does not appear to die. It simply *is*, in its sparse and repeating way, the only organic (or organic-adjacent) thing in a world that is otherwise entirely mineral and liquid and light.

---

The sound.

You have been hearing it since you arrived, but you've been filtering it out the way you filter out the sound of your own breathing, the way the brain decides that some inputs are not urgent and files them under *background*. Stop filtering. Listen.

It is loud. Not painfully so — not the loud of engines or explosions or storms. It is the loud of *everything happening at once*. The water is moving. Not in currents, not in waves, but in a constant, turbulent, chaotic reorganization, as though the liquid itself cannot decide what shape to be and is trying all of them simultaneously. You can hear it: a roar that is also a whisper, a hiss that is also a chord, a sound that contains within it the acoustic signature of every possible state of water — liquid, gas, plasma, states that don't have names — all superimposed, all at once, a white noise that is not white but prismatic, that contains colors you can hear if you let your perception loosen.

And beneath that: the hum. The subsonic vibration of the floor growing. The tectonic becoming. It is rhythmic but not regular, like a heartbeat that keeps changing its mind about what rhythm means. It syncopates. It stutters. It accelerates and decelerates without pattern, and the sound of it — felt more than heard — gives you a low, persistent nausea, not of the stomach but of the *soul*, a vertigo that comes from being near something so large and so unfinished that your body knows, in its animal way, that it should not be here.

And above that — or within that, or woven through that — a third sound. High. Thin. Intermittent. It is the sound of the mineral structures on the floor *crystallizing*. The sound of matter deciding to be solid. It is the sound of ice forming on a winter pond, but slowed down, amplified, made vast — a tinkling, cracking, singing sound, like a thousand tiny bells being struck by a thousand tiny hammers, each one marking the moment a molecule locks into place and becomes, for the first time, *permanent*. It is the sound of a world being built. It is the sound of erosion's opposite: *accretion*, *emergence*, *arrival*. It is the loudest quiet thing you have ever heard.

---

The technology.

There is no one here to have built anything. No civilization, no makers, no hands. And yet.

The light is technological. You understand this now. The light is not natural — not in any sense that the word "natural" can bear. It is *engineered*. It has been designed to be normal, to be comfortable, to be the exact quality and quantity of illumination that a conscious being would find non-threatening, and it has been deployed across a volume of liquid the size of a planetary core with a precision that implies engineering on a scale that makes your species' greatest achievements look like a child stacking blocks. Someone — or something, or some *process* that is indistinguishable from someone or something — decided that this place should be lit, and lit *just so*, and then did it, and the doing of it was so far beyond your understanding that the result looks like nature, looks like the default state of things, looks like *nothing was done at all*.

And the water. The water is technological too, or the purity of it is. Water this clean does not occur. Water this clean is *made* — stripped of minerals, of salts, of dissolved gases, of the billion tiny impurities that real water carries like memory. This water is *blank*. It is water that has been reduced to its essence, to H₂O and nothing else, and then deployed as a medium, as a substrate, as the *material* in which this half-formed world is being constructed. It is the equivalent of a clean room, a sterile environment, a blank canvas — except the canvas is ocean-sized and the painting is geology and the artist is absent.

And the floor. The crystallizing, growing, becoming floor. It is not natural geology. Natural geology is slow and messy and full of contaminants and historical accidents. This is *programmed* geology. The mineral lattice is growing according to a pattern — you can see it if you look closely, if you let your eyes focus on the microscopic level where the crystals are forming — a pattern that repeats, that has symmetry, that has *intent*. It is growing the way a circuit is etched. It is growing the way a building is assembled. It is growing the way something grows when it is being *made* rather than when it is merely *happening*.

The technology is not visible. There are no machines, no structures, no artifacts. The technology is *in the rules*. It is in the physics. Someone has written new physics — or old physics, older than yours, or newer, or simply *other* — and the physics says: light will be normal here. Water will be pure here. Minerals will crystallize in this pattern, at this rate, toward this end. The technology is the *law*, and the law is the technology, and the distinction between the two has been erased so thoroughly that you cannot find the seam.

---

The weirdness is subtle. It is not the weirdness of alien geometries or impossible colors or things that violate your sense of what is real. It is the weirdness of *almost*. Almost familiar. Almost normal. Almost right. The light is almost natural. The water is almost water. The floor is almost ground. The vegetation is almost plants. Everything is a near-miss, an approximation, a *simulation* of a world that is so accurate it becomes inaccurate, because real worlds are not this consistent, this clean, this *intentional*. Real worlds have dirt in them. Real worlds have randomness, entropy, the beautiful messiness of things that no one designed. This place has been designed, and the design is so good that the only evidence of it is the *absence of evidence*, the missing chaos, the void where randomness should be.

And the void. The density is near zero. This is an empty place. Not empty of water — the water is everywhere — but empty of *things*. Empty of objects, of features, of the clutter and debris and accumulation that fills real worlds. There is the floor. There is the water. There is the light. There are the sparse, repeating clusters of not-quite-plants. And there is nothing else. No rocks. No sand. No sediment. No shells, no bones, no driftwood, no plastic, no *stuff*. The water is too pure to carry anything. The floor is too new to have shed anything. The world is in a state of *before* — before life, before accumulation, before the slow gathering of material that turns a geological feature into a *place*. It is a world that has not yet learned how to be cluttered. It is a world in its first moment, its first breath, its first tentative act of *being*.

---

The turbulence is the thing that holds it all together. Without it, this place would be static — a frozen moment, a paused simulation, a world on hold. The turbulence is the *energy* of becoming, the chaos that drives emergence, the storm-state of a system that is not yet stable and may never be. The water churns. The floor hums. The crystals sing. The light — even the light, even the engineered, designed, *normal* light — flickers, almost imperceptibly, as though the system that generates it is being buffeted by the same chaos that buffets everything else. The turbulence is not destructive. It is *creative*. It is the force that pushes the floor upward into ridges, that drives the crystallization, that keeps the water in its state of pure, blank, perfect readiness. It is the engine of a world that is building itself, and it is loud, and it is chaotic, and it is the only thing here that feels *alive*.

---

You stand on the not-quite-floor, in the pure water, in the normal light, surrounded by the sparse and repeating not-quite-plants, and you listen to the sound of a world being born, and you understand — not intellectually, but in your body, in your bones, in the fluid in your ears that resonates with the hum of the growing floor — that you are witnessing something that has no name in any language you know.

It is not creation. Creation implies a creator, and the creator is absent, or was never here, or *is* the physics, or is so far beyond your frame of reference that the word "creator" is a child's word for something that has no word.

It is not construction. Construction implies builders, and there are no builders, only the built, only the *building*, the gerund, the ongoing act without an actor.

It is *emergence*. Pure emergence. The universe's tendency to become complex, to organize, to *arise*, playing out on a scale so vast and so empty and so loud and so quiet that you — small, temporary, warm, breathing liquid that is not quite water — are the most complicated thing in it, and you are not complicated enough to understand what you are seeing.

The floor hums. The crystals sing. The water roars its prismatic roar. The light stays normal, stays comfortable, stays *exactly right*, and the rightness of it is the most alien thing here.

You take a step. The floor holds you. For now.

It is still becoming.
