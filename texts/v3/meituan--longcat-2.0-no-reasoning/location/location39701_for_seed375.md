# The Calcination

---

You are standing in a city that has no name for water.

The structures rise from a floor of pale, fractured mineral — something between salt and bone — in shapes that suggest habitation without ever quite confirming it. Walls curve and branch like ribcages. Openings gape at irregular heights. There are corridors that narrow to nothing, chambers that open too wide, staircases that ascend into solid ceiling. The architecture is dense, layered, almost urban in its complexity, and yet it feels less *built* than *grown* — or perhaps *exhaled*. As though something breathed this place into being over a very long time and then stopped.

The material is wrong. That is the first thing. It is not stone, not metal, not any ceramic you could name. It has the matte, slightly luminous quality of dried something — dried *what*, you cannot say. It is warm to the touch in some places and cool in others, with no pattern you can discern. In certain walls you can see layers, strata, like sedimentary rock but compressed into geometries no geology would permit. Spirals. Right angles that curve. Corners that have too many sides.

There is no sound. Not quiet — *none*. Your footsteps make no impact. Your breathing, when you remember to check, produces no audible exhalation. The silence has texture, though. It presses. It has the quality of a held breath, or a room where someone has just stopped speaking.

The light comes from everywhere and nowhere. A dim, directionless glow, the color of old amber filtered through skin. It does not cast shadows correctly. Some objects have no shadow at all. Others have two, pointing in directions that don't correspond to any light source you can find. The illumination is just sufficient to see by — you can make out details ten, fifteen meters away, but beyond that the city simply *fades*, not into darkness but into a kind of visual static, as though the air itself becomes uncertain at distance.

And the city is *moving*.

Not quickly. Not in ways you can always perceive directly. But if you fix your gaze on a wall and wait — truly wait, for minutes — you will see it. A slow peristalsis. The mineral surfaces are *creeping*. Edges advance by millimeters. Openings dilate and contract. A corridor that was passable a moment ago has narrowed by the width of your hand. Somewhere above you — or what you have decided is above — a structure is *unfolding*, new surfaces extending themselves into space with the blind patience of crystal growth, except nothing crystalline has ever moved like this. Like muscle. Like intention without mind.

This is the dynamic truth of the place: it is alive in the way that a process is alive. It is not a city that was built and then abandoned. It is a city that is *still happening*. The construction never stopped. It simply became invisible to any timescale shorter than yours.

---

The vegetation — and it is vegetation, though the word strains — appears in sparse clusters. Pale filaments emerge from cracks in the mineral floor, reaching upward in slow arcs. They are not green. They are the color of old paper, of fungal mycelium, of things that have never known chlorophyll. Some of them pulse faintly, a rhythm just below the threshold of perception, and when you touch one it retracts — not quickly, but with a deliberate, almost *reluctant* withdrawal, pulling itself back into the crack like a tongue retreating into a mouth. They are feeding on something. The mineral itself, perhaps. Or the slow exhalations of the city's movement.

In one chamber you find a cluster of them that has grown into a lattice — a woven curtain of pale threads stretched between two walls, and behind it, a space that the light reaches differently. Warmer. The threads vibrate at a frequency you feel in your sternum rather than hear, because there is no sound, there is never any sound, but your body registers *something*.

---

The fauna, rare as it is, announces itself only through evidence. A smooth depression in the mineral floor, as though something heavy and round has been resting there. A series of parallel scratches on a wall at waist height — four lines, evenly spaced, dragged downward. In one corridor you find what might be a shed skin: a translucent sheet of material, crumpled, already beginning to dissolve back into the floor. It smells — and this is the first smell in the place, the first thing that isn't visual or tactile — like hot metal and something sweeter underneath. Overripe. Fermenting.

You do not see the creature. You are not sure you want to.

---

The weirdness is not in any single feature but in the *accumulation*. The way the architecture implies inhabitants who are not you and never were you. The way the city's growth seems to respond to your presence — not intelligently, not with recognition, but the way a plant responds to a trellis. You are a structure. You are something to grow around. Corridors that were open when you entered have shifted behind you, not sealing you in but *adjusting*, the way a throat adjusts around food. The city is not hostile. The city is not anything. It is a process that has incorporated you into its logic.

The elevation is low — you feel this in the weight of the air, the sense of being *under* something, enclosed by mass above and around. The scale is bounded. You have walked for what feels like hours and the city has not opened into anything larger. It is the size of a grove, a cathedral, a hamlet. It has edges, even if you haven't found them. It is a single feature, a contained thing, a room at a scale that should not be possible for a room.

And it is young. That is the strangest part. For all its complexity, for all the layered depth of its walls, the place feels *recent*. The features are sharp. The edges have not been worn. The mineral surfaces still have a kind of freshness, a rawness, as though the city emerged from whatever substrate underlies it only recently — geologically recently, which might mean millennia, but still: this is a young thing. It has not had time to become what it is becoming.

---

You find, in the deepest chamber you can reach, a surface that is different from the others. Smoother. Almost polished. And on it, marks. Not scratches — *impressions*. Deliberate. Repeating. A pattern of dots and lines that might be writing or might be the record of something growing through a template that was once there and is now gone.

The city is reading itself.

Or being read.

The silence holds. The walls breathe their slow, mineral breath. Something in the distance — or what functions as distance in this place — shifts, and the light changes by a degree so small you cannot be sure it changed at all.

You are in a place that has no water, no sound, no name, and no end.

It is still becoming.
