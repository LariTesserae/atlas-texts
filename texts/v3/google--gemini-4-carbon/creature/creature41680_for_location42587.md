At first, you think the city is unmanned, an automated conservatory left running by absent gods. Then you realize you have been looking at the inhabitants for an hour, mistaking them for shifts in the violet light or low-hanging sculptures. 

A silhouette detaches itself from the base of a pearl-gray spire. It does not walk so much as yield forward, moving across the razor-sharp basalt without looking down. Up close, the skin of its bare, elongated soles doesn't touch the snagging grain of the rock; a millimeter of pressurized air—the same quiet physics that governs the avenues—cushions every step, making its progress over the jagged stone entirely silent and frictionless. 

They are tall, attenuated figures, bred or tailored for low gravity and dimness, with proportions that echo the cantilevered needles around them. Because the air is warm as blood and never varies by a degree, and because there is neither wind nor harsh radiation, they have abandoned clothing entirely—or rather, they have integrated whatever clothing once did into their dermis. Their skin has the smooth, matte luster of wet slate or dark plum, drinking in the ambient indigo glow much like the broad-leaved shrubs, broken only by faint, vascular filigrees of bioluminescent jade along their collarbones, wrists, and temples. When the twenty-minute pressure drop rolls across the terrace, heavy condensation beads instantly across their shoulders and backs just as it does on the leaves. They do not wipe it away. Micro-grooves etched subtly into the contours of their skin channel the lukewarm water down their spines and off their heels in neat, synchronized rivulets, joining the *shhh-pat* of the sighing valley without breaking their stride.

To understand why they built a metropolis without roofs or rooms, you have to watch two of them meet in the center of a quarter-mile plaza. 

They stop thirty feet apart. Neither speaks. Their faces are smooth, almost severe in their tranquility, possessing large, dark, unblinking eyes with irises that dilate until they swallow the sclera, drinking in spectra far below human vision—infrared warmth bleeding from the subterranean conduits, the ultraviolet shimmer of magnetic exclusion zones. Mouths have grown small, vestigial in their stillness. Instead of speaking, the air between the two figures abruptly thickens and refracts, bending the twilight like a lens. Within that shared pocket of altered pressure, faint acoustic tremors vibrate directly against the inner ear and the teeth, accompanied by rapid, cascading shifts in the jade luminescence along their throats. They are exchanging dense, multidimensional packets of thought, sensory memory, and civic adjustment—a conversation conducted partly in sub-audible harmonics and partly in localized field-modulations. 

Walls would only muffle them. Roofs would sever their connection to the stratospheric weave miles above, which is not merely a sunshade, but a sky-wide computational loom. The inhabitants are constantly, peripherally reading the slow intersections of those dark metamaterial ribbons overhead, tracking macro-calculations written across the firmament like astrology made literal. 

Yet for all their godlike command of physics, they are creatures of immense, unhurried physical delicacy. They are gardeners of an invisible topology. 

Watch one approach a sheer cliff of dark, glossy vines pressed flat against an avenue’s unseen boundary. The figure raises a long, six-fingered hand—not to prune the plant with a blade, which would be shockingly crude here, but to press its fingertips into the intangible glass of the exclusion field itself. With a slow, balletic rotation of the wrist, the inhabitant bends the local physics, sculpting a concave niche into the force-wall. Almost immediately, sensing the vanished resistance, a nearby tendril of bruised-black foliage surges into the newly permitted space, unfurling a heavy, night-blooming flower that smells of damp cloves. The inhabitant leans in, inhaling the thick scent, allowing the pale, luminous mites on the petals to drift onto its cheekbones, where they feed harmlessly on the static charge of its skin.

This is what they *do*. The city was cracked open yesterday not as a fortress or a factory, but as an instrument—a planetary-scale resonance chamber still being tuned. 

Sometimes, you will find three or four of them gathered at the edge of one of the sunken origami courts. They stand completely motionless for hours, so still that canopy-striders step delicately from the air onto their narrow shoulders, using them as living bridges before stepping back onto the gravity-ceilings. When the matte-black stone of the court finally folds downward, exposing the cold, cerulean glare of the subterranean conduits, the figures do not pull out tools. They simply step off the ledge, drifting downward like feathers caught in an updraft, descending into the core to calibrate the young rock's thermal bleed with their own nervous systems. 

And what of the silver polyhedrons that drift at waist height, snapping the dark blossoms shut? They are not drones, not quite. If you follow one long enough, you will see it float into the open, waiting arms of an inhabitant seated on a raw basalt terrace. The rotating silver shapes fold inward, collapsing their non-repeating geometry until they settle against the figure’s chest like a heavy, metallic mantle, sinking halfway into the flesh—an externalized organ of perception returning home after wandering the avenues, pouring everything it smelled of ozone, sap, and fresh stone back into its host.

They have no possessions, no clutter, no private interiors to hide within. When fatigue—or whatever periodic stillness replaces sleep for them—arrives, an inhabitant does not go indoors. They walk to the center of the vast, empty concourses, lie flat upon a slab of sharp, unweathered vitreous stone cushioned by their perpetual millimeter of air, and let the ambient hum of the hovering carbon rings vibrate through their bones. Above them, the violet sky shifts; around them, the pressure drops; over them, the condensation beads and runs off into the new earth, leaving them indistinguishable from a dark vein of mineral in a valley still waking up to what it was built to calculate.
