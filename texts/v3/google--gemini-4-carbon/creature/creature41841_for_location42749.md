Look past the strobing throats on the fern-stems, and look past the shimmering ripples of the silver-backed bottom-dwellers, to one of the wide, shallow stretches between two plant islands where the water looks slightly thicker than it ought to be, as if a sheet of warm oil has settled over the scale-carpet.

Wait a moment for the glare to shift. The scales beneath that patch are not flashing silver when a shadow passes, nor have they scattered; they are locked into a steady, sedated ochre, sliding slowly beneath something that matches the refractive index of the lukewarm water almost to perfection. Only the faint, dimpled meniscus around its perimeter gives it away—a subtle, six-foot ring where the surface tension pulls downward by half a millimeter, bending the sunlight into a pale, hair-thin halo on the rock below.

This is one of the basin’s great, quiet custodians, though "custodian" implies an intent far too brisk for what it actually does. There are only three of them in the entire two-hundred-pace depression. More could not be supported, or perhaps three has simply been the correct number for the last four million years. 

At first, it appears to be an enormous, prostrate coelenterate or a flattened, shell-less mollusk—a broad, scalloped apron of firm, watery jelly no thicker at its center than a man’s thigh, tapering out to margins so thin they fray into clear, drifting ribbons among the ankle-deep currents. Inside its translucent dome, there is no red blood, no dark coil of gut to catch the ruthless sun and cook in the shallows. Its organs are pale, milky lattices and faintly opalescent bladders arranged in a loose, radial symmetry, resembling blown glass submerged in water. Were it to wash out over the lip of the basin into an ordinary stream, the first sharp stone would tear it to pieces, and the first cold night would kill it. Here, where grit was abolished ages ago and the temperature never wavers by more than two degrees, it has let go of armor, skeleton, and haste altogether.

Then it decides to rise, and the jellyfish illusion comes undone.

From the underside of that clear mantle, seven thick, hydraulic limbs uncoil and press downward. They do not shove into the living carpet of bottom-dwellers; instead, the tips of the limbs spread into wide, soft pads fringed with thousands of microscopic cilia that stroke the fingernail-sized organisms beneath them. In response to some chemical bribe or gentle pressure signal, the bottom-dwellers part neatly in seven small circles, exposing the bare, pinkish-gray bedrock so the creature can plant its feet on stone without crushing a single scale. 

With a slow, pneumatic shift of internal fluid from its rim to its core, the body lifts out of the water. 

Standing—if such a fluid, suspended posture can be called standing—it reaches roughly to a human chest. The wide, gelatinous apron drapes downward from a central, domed crown like a heavy wet cloak, dripping lukewarm sheets of water that fall back into the pan with a muffled, velvet *shhh*. Now that it is in the air, its skin changes. Exposed to the humid glare, millions of microscopic prisms embedded in its outer membrane tighten and align. It ceases to be invisible and becomes a muted, milky mirror, bouncing the brutal golden-white light harmlessly away from its delicate interior while throwing soft, shifting caustics across the water around its legs.

Look closely at the central dome, just beneath the frosted surface. Embedded in a ring around its upper curve are twelve eyes, though none of them have pupils or lenses like a hunter’s. Six are wide, shallow saucers lined with pale gold pigment that read the polarization of the sky and the ultraviolet strobes of the plant islands; the other six are deep, narrow pits filled with clear gel, angled downward to watch the shifting mosaics at its feet. Deeper still, suspended in the exact center of the body where a brain or a heart might sit, hangs a cluster of heavy, pearl-gray otoliths—mineral stones grown from the dissolved calcium of the ancient bedrock, floating inside a sac of dense fluid.

Those internal stones are why the creature exists in the shape it does. In a bowl where air-borne sound is strangled by dead machinery, the acoustic damping field itself has a texture. It is not uniform; it radiates from the black rods and the refractive rings in standing waves of altered pressure, invisible contours of stillness that warp slightly whenever the rings cycle and vent their ozone-scented mist. Most of the basin’s frantic, smaller inhabitants merely live inside this quiet, grateful not to be heard by things with teeth. The mantled walkers *feel* the geometry of the damping field through the heavy stones in their cores, navigating the invisible pressure-gradients the way a blind cave-fish navigates ripples off a wall.

Watch as it begins to cross the twenty yards of open water toward the eastern refractive ring. Its gait is mesmerizingly smooth—three limbs lifting, swinging forward beneath the drooping skirt, and waiting a beat at the surface while the bottom-dwellers scurry politely aside before touching down. There is no splash. Even without the basin’s unnatural hush, this thing would make scarcely a whisper. 

As it moves, a tiny ecosystem travels with it. Clinging to the shaded, damp underside of its mantle—the only movable shadow in this entire blinding court—are dozens of pale, translucent tree-frogs no bigger than thumbnails, escaping the noon glare. Along its upper creases, where warm mist condenses and runs down in steady rivulets, grows a thin, velveteen fuzz of bright green symbiotic algae that feeds directly on the nitrogen weeping through the creature’s skin. Long-legged waders trot alongside it, matching its stately pace, lunging silently into the wake of confused crustaceans stirred up as its fringe trails through the water. The walker ignores them all.

Halfway across the flat, the three-foot, ribbon-bodied blue hunter darts out from an orchid-mound, panicked by something, whipping straight across the walker’s path. A normal animal would flinch or strike. Instead, a localized flush of color blooms across the walker’s lower hem—not a panicked flash, like the silver bottom-dwellers, nor a hot territorial strobe, like the magenta amphibians. It is a slow, rhythmic wash of deep, velvety indigo, absorbsive and dark against the bright water, traveling down two of its forward limbs in a soothing, downward-pouring cadence. 

The blue predator hits the edge of that visual rhythm and goes slack, its frantic undulations smoothing out into lazy S-curves before it drifts aside and sinks peacefully to the bottom. 

Everything this creature says is like that: slow, broad-spectrum, and cooling. It is a dampener made of flesh, mirroring the physics of the ancient fixtures around it. In an ecosystem packed so densely that a single chain-reaction of visual panic could send millions of organisms boiling over one another in a suffocating crush—blinding the whole basin in a feedback loop of flashing silver and frantic ultraviolet—the three walkers drift from sector to sector, eating the hysteria.

Not metaphorically, or not *only* metaphorically.

Reaching the hovering refractive ring, the walker halts a yard away, careful not to let its trailing ribbons drift into the updraft Zone where water beads and vanishes. It waits. Its twelve eyes dim, filming over with protective nictitating membranes of milky opal. Inside its central sac, the suspended calcium stones shiver infinitesimally as tension builds in the hovering ring.

The cycle peaks. The air inside the clear ring twists the distant pinkish-gray rim into a tight corkscrew; the water lifts, hisses silently into bright steam, and the wave of warm ozone rolls outward. 

As the invisible pulse of the machine washes through the walker, the creature opens its scalloped skirt wide, spreading itself like a parasol to catch the fresh, mineral-stripped mist condensing above it. Along its upper dome, hundreds of tiny, puckered pores dilate, drinking in the ionized moisture and the trace atmospheric nitrates forged by the spatial warp inside the ring. At the same time, from the tips of its seven submerged limbs, it releases a faint, milky cloud of pheromones and excess mucilage into the half-degree-warmer water.

Instantly, the bottom-dwellers for ten yards around abandon their nervous, circulating currents and surge inward—not fleeing, but feeding. Thousands of pearl-backed crabs stream down from the nearby rock swells, piling three-deep around the pillar-like legs to graze on the shed slime. Even the gossamer aerial drifters dip low out of their thermal columns to brush against the walker’s damp, ozone-charged back, picking up tiny electrostatic charges on their wings that keep them aloft through the heavy, humid afternoon without beating a muscle.

For five or ten minutes after the ring settles, the walker remains standing there, a shimmering, chest-high pavilion of frosted glass and wet jade velvet in the blinding pan, motionless while the crowded life of the basin swarms silently over its feet and under its eaves to clean it and be fed.

If you look at the oldest, largest of the three—the one currently resting near the tilted black rod on the western slope—you can see how completely they belong to this worn-down end of time. Where the stone around the black rod has dissolved away over epochs, leaving the dark shaft jutting at its stubborn forty-five-degree angle out of nothing, the elder walker has draped half its gelatinous mantle directly around the rod’s dry, shadow-black base. No water touches that black material; no moss can grip it; even light dies on it. Yet the creature’s pale, prismatic flesh presses flush against the anomaly, utterly unharmed, using an anchor pinned to the fabric of space itself as a scratching post—or perhaps a tuning fork. 

Watch its upper dome while it leans against the rod. Faint, concentric rings of pale gold light expand outward from the point of contact across its skin, steady as a heartbeat, once every twelve seconds. Across the dazzling expanse of shallow water, thirty and sixty yards away, the other two walkers pulse faint gold along their hems on the exact same beat, keeping time across a silent world, before folding their legs up beneath theirskirts and sinking slowly back into invisibility among the warm mirrors.
