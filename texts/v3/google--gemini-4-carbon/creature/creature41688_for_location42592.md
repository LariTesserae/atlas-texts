At first, you only notice where the grazing has been interrupted. 

Along several of the thickest vascular columns, just above head height, the thousands of clear jelly-trilobites have been cleared away in neat, deliberate swaths. The dark bark underneath is wiped free of condensation, allowing the bruised violet light in the seams to shine out unhindered, slightly sharpening the gloom in the center of the room. And if you look down at the terraced basins nearest those wiped columns, the concentric grids of standing waves are not all uniform; two of the pools hold intricate, overlapping interference patterns, because someone has bent the overhanging wire-rootlets by fractions of an inch so that the heavy sap drips onto exact coordinates in the water.

Wait for the blind amphibians to startle. Wait for that sudden, ten-second storm of metallic *chinks* to fill the vault like shaken coins. 

Everything else in the chamber endures the racket passively, but high up in the compressed, tangled corner—just beneath the jagged chandeliers of black leaves—something *uses* it. During the deafening clatter of echoes, an angular mass wedged into a sixty-degree cleft between two columns tilts forward into the sound, completely still, drinking the reflections. When the chorus dies back into the slow groan of the walls, it begins to descend.

It moves faster than the jelly-grazers and slower than the drips—about the pace of the minute hand on a large clock—and it makes none of the porcelain clicking that betrays the heavy, bristle-pelted hangers in the thickets. That silence is how it respects the rawness of the room. Its four long, flat-sided limbs do not scrape or wear down the razor-sharp ceramic ridges of the wood; each ends in a wide, cleft cushion of dense, grey viscoelastic flesh that presses down over a sharp hexagonal edge like chilled putty taking a mold, locking fast by suction and molecular adhesion, then peeling cleanly away without leaving a scratch or taking a cut.

Seen against the pulsing walls, casting four overlapping bluish shadows below itself, it looks at first like an accident of the root-architecture—a gaunt, asymmetrical bundle of dark slate-colored struts, about the mass of a starved wolf folded into the posture of a mantis. Across its hunched dorsal ridge, it wears a heavy, tiered mantle of white bristles harvested from the sleeping sloth-creatures in the corners. There is no weaving or knotting here, nothing like cloth; the hollow white hairs have been meticulously cemented with hardened silicate sap onto overlapping plates of its own dark carapace, arranged in precise geometric chevrons that trap pockets of dead air and baffle stray echoes. 

Watch the base of its narrow throat, and you will understand what it is doing to the air. Your own breath rises toward the ceiling in warm, lingering clouds. Every three minutes, a pair of pleated vents beneath its collarbones parts slightly, and a thin, heavy ribbon of dense white fog spills *downward* over its chest, pooling momentarily in the hollows of its joints before cascading toward the floor like cold syrup. 

It exhales colder than the room. Like the mirror-backed leaves overhead, its core organs are biological heat-pumps, scavenging the razor-thin thermal gradients of the zero-degree air and dumping supercooled exhaust.

There is only one of it in this chamber, because a hollow twenty paces wide only leaks enough spare milliwatts of heat and coherent light to sustain a single adult mind. Thirty-five years ago, when this sunken church was still a fist-sized fissure cracking open under hydraulic root-pressure, this resident arrived from an older, deeper vault miles away—squeezing through a hairline fracture in the vascular matrix as a flat, boneless, unthinking ribbon of pale cartilage, riding the slow sap-streams until it spilled into fresh territory. It has grown up alongside the room. Its flat, blade-like limbs have hardened by drinking the suspended metallic silicates from the pools; its mind has woken up by eating the grazers on the walls.

You can watch it feed halfway down the column. Tucked beneath its thorax, shielded under the bristle-baffled mantle, are two pairs of startlingly delicate secondary arms, thin as knitting needles and segmented like brittle-star rays, tipped with tiny ceramic pincers. One of these filament-arms unfolds—taking ninety seconds of smooth, hydraulic patience to reach six inches—and plucks a hand-sized jelly creature off a glowing seam. It draws the prize under its mantle, slurps away the stiff, clear gel, and carefully extracts the branching silver organs inside, swallowing those stolen superconducting filaments whole without breaking them.

It does not digest the silver. Look closely at the translucent cartilage between the dark plates of its neck, and at the broad, eyeless dish of its head: beneath the skin, stolen cyan light flickers at blinding speed.

That is the strangeness at the center of its life. At the threshold of freezing, muscles must be hydraulic, viscous, and slow; move quickly against unweathered carbon-nanotube wood, and you will shatter a limb or slice yourself to ribbons. Yet its nervous system is wired with room-temperature superconductors scavenged from the flora. Behind a body that takes two minutes to reach out a hand lives a consciousness running at the speed of coherent light. To this creature, a falling drop of sap hangs in the air for an subjective hour before striking a pool; the ten-second clatter of the amphibians is not noise, but a leisurely, crystal-clear architectural rendering, every echo arriving distinct and countable against the velvety acoustic dish of its face, allowing it to map every fraction of a millimeter the chamber walls have extruded since yesterday.

And because a mind moving that fast in a room twenty paces wide would starve on silence, it works the hollow like an instrument. 

Reaching the lowest terrace, it settles onto three limbs and dips its fine secondary pincers into a basin's standing-wave grid—never breaking the surface tension, only nudging the microscopic cilia along the rim, retuning the geometric ripples. Beneath your boots, the high-voltage hum of the floor shifts pitch by a sliver of a hertz. Seconds later, deep inside the black, braided wall behind you, an unseen vascular column answers with a slightly altered pulse of violet photons, carrying a packet of vibration outward through the deep-earth root-web toward older cathedrals buried miles away in the freeze. It is a hermit, a relay-tuner, and an acoustic gardener, passing slow decades in conversation with sisters it has never seen, trading pressure-waves and light-pulses through forty-foot walls of living stone.

Only now does it turn that concave, silvery facedish fully toward you.

It has known you were here since the moment you existed in the space, of course. To an organism built to harvest milliwatts from freezing mist, a warm-blooded body is a roaring, wasteful sun. Your 98-degree core is scrambling the delicate convection currents of its nursery; the warm plumes of your breath are rising into the black leaf-chandeliers overhead, breaking their subtle magnetic lattices and causing dozens of levitating droplets to fall out of rhythm in sudden, pattering showers. You are blindingly bright in the infrared, catastrophically loud in the water-grid, leaking more free thermal energy every minute than this entire chamber mines from the crust in a season.

It does not flee, and it does not threaten. To strike you—to spill hot blood onto the delicate cilia mats—would ruin years of tuning. 

Instead, slowly, with glacial, unthreatening deliberation, it climbs back up a neighboring column until it hangs slightly above the level of your head, ten feet away, suspended upside down from the vaulted ribs where your exhaled breath gathers thickest. Slowly, the dark, bristle-armored plates along its back unlatch and fan wide, exposing pale, vascularized membranes veined in silver and cyan. 

It hangs motionless in the rising ghost of your breath, basking in the rich, impossible tropics of your lungs—drinking the waste heat right out of your white plumes, while from its own throat a heavier, thicker ribbon of supercooled fog begins to pour steadily toward your feet, frosting the sharp black ridges of the floor with brief, vanishing stars of ice before the humming wood drinks them away.
