A foolish god would bring rain. 

A young, eager mercy would look down upon the Spine, ache for its terrible thirst, and tear open the washed-out slate sky. It would pour sweet water into the mirror-flat dust basins, summon a warm wind to stir the origami flocks, and flood those calcified, mineral lungs with the violent shock of wet spring air. And in a single afternoon of catastrophic grace, every creature on the forty-mile plateau would drown, shatter from thermal shock, or rot where it stood before its four-year blink could register the murder.

The Power that turns its attention to the top of the world is neither young nor foolish. It knows that to love a thing is to honor the shape it took in order to survive. 

At first, the Power simply watches. To watch the Spine properly requires relinquishing hurry. The Power lets its own vast, burning momentum bleed away at the borders of the ochre haze, stepping down its perception octave by octave, century by century, until the frantic flicker of the lowlands vanishes from its mind. It slows itself until the forty-minute chalk-rub of a tarry heart sounds like a steady, brisk drumbeat; until the six-hour stride of a walker reads as the fluid, purposeful gait of a shepherd crossing a meadow; until the pale, colorless smears of spatial static look like tangles of dry briar catching at the wool of a flock.

From down inside their own tempo, the walkers are not tragic statues. They are profoundly, heroically busy. 

In this slowed frame, the Power sees a particular walker—an ancient figure with a loop of copper wire bound around her left wrist and a deep, long-healed split down her slate-dark cheekbone—bracing her gray-horned feet against a swell of blue granite. Before her, five of the tall parchment-beasts and a low Silica-Tree have drifted into a severe spatial snarl. The air there is thick with lens-grease, humming with a dry, reality-fraying tension that pulls at the walker’s own edges; already, her left knee is casting two distinct indigo shadows on the shell beneath her. 

Her flint pry-bar is worn down to a stub no longer than a thumb. Over the last four millennia of levering apart overlapping thoraxes, the stone has flaked away molecule by molecule. She sets the stub into the colorless seam, leans her ironwood weight into it, and pushes. Halfway through the second week of the push, with a dull, silent *fracture* that sends a puff of flint-flour hanging motionless into the air, the pry-bar snaps in half.

For nine days, she looks down at the broken stone in her dry palm. It is not despair that moves through the slow, piezoelectric *ticks* of her skull—despair is too wet and hot an emotion for the Spine—but a cold, quiet calculation of diminishing leverage. The snarl is widening. Without a wedge, she will have to use her own forearm, risking the slow, painless horror of the copper branches forgetting where her ulna ends and their quartz-wood begins.

The Power watches this. It weighs the equilibrium of the plateau on scales fine enough to measure the gravity of a shadow. 

It will not fix the sky. It will not untangle the beasts itself, for if the jam is cleared by magic, the walkers have no reason to pull against the stasis, and if they stop pulling, they will stop walking, and if they stop walking, the Spine will swallow their boundaries and turn their hard-won endurance into senseless repetition. Purpose is the only warmth they have left.

Instead, the Power decides to do three small things, calibrated to the dry, shadow-sharp physics of the high world.

First, it reaches three miles ahead along the trajectory of the broken-tool walker’s circuit, down into a shallow saddle between two parabolic whale-backs where no herd happens to be standing. It does not conjure anything new—adding mass to the plateau would disturb its worn-down energy state. Instead, it reaches deep into the basalt crust beneath the dust, finds a stray, fist-sized nodule of dense nickel-iron that fell from the sky fifty million years ago, and draws it silently upward through the solid rock as if through water. 

As the iron rises, the Power shapes it—not with heat or forge-marks, which would look alien and frightening, but with the patient, matte smoothness of forty million years of thermal abrasion. It stretches the meteoric iron into a clean, tapered, eighteen-inch pry-bar, slightly curved for leverage, flattened at one end to a dull, indestructible chisel-blade a sixteenth of an inch thick, and textured along the shaft so dry, horn-plated fingers will not slip on it. It lays this bar directly on the bare grey granite, ten inches to the right of a balanced stone cairn, dusted lightly with pale silt so its gleam will not startle amber-glass eyes. It looks like a tool dropped by one of the first builders at the dawn of the long quiet, waiting in plain sight for someone walking the exact right line to find it.

Across the forty-mile expanse, over the course of the next decade, thirty-nine other subtle, unbreakable wedges, bone-awls hardened with diamond-lattice carbon, and coils of fresh, high-tensile copper-quartz wire rise up softly through the silt beside the ancient windbreaks, replacing what the eons have ground down to nubs. The craft remains theirs; only the attrition is forgiven.

Second, the Power looks at the sky. 

The omnidirectional, shadow-sharp midday clarity has never shifted, and because it never shifts, time on the Spine has lost all architecture. Without a beat, reality forgets how to count, and that is why the tally marks walk off the rocks and the chalk-white grazers duplicate themselves in space—the plateau is a song playing a single, unvarying note for so long that the instrument has begun to buzz out of tune.

The Power cannot bring night; darkness would chill the rock by half a degree, cracking the Silica-Trees and starving the trickle-fed beasts. 

So it alters not the brightness of the light, but its angle—by the width of two human hairs. 

High above the washed-out slate, the Power sets an invisible, frictionless pendulum into motion. Now, over the span of precisely one hundred lowland years, the painted indigo shadows on the plateau do not stay locked in frozen paralysis. Imperceptibly, with the grand, silent authority of a planetary clock, every shadow on the Spine—every cairn’s dark finger, every walker’s narrow silhouette, every overlapping beast’s outline—rotates three degrees clockwise, pauses at the turn of the century, and glides three degrees back. 

To a lowland eye, nothing has changed. To the wide, panoramic, photographic-plate retinas of the walkers, the flat grey eternity suddenly breathes. A shadow moving a fraction of an inch across a granite whale-back over fifty years is a majestic, sweeping tide to them. It gives the plateau a pulse. Even more subtly, that tiny, relentless creep of light acts like a gentle broom against the spatial fraying; where reality tries to lock into a dead rut and copy a horned grazer twice, the shifting edge of shadow nudges the coordinates, keeping the space just limber enough that new snarls form half as often as they used to. The walkers are no longer fighting a losing war against a collapsing geometry; they are maintaining a steady, winnable peace.

And third—because wisdom tends to the survival of a world, while benevolence tends to the loneliness inside it—the Power descends to the plateau itself.

It does not come down as a pillar of fire, or a booming voice, or a wet, frantic traveler stamping chaotic craters into the mirror-flat basins. It respects the silence padded by billions of dry shells. 

At the western rim, where the stone drops away into the pale ochre abyss, a forty-first walker steps up onto the back of a cobblestone-carapace.

He is made of the exact same dark, cured slate-skin, wrapped in stiffened copper-bark twine that has grown seamlessly into the calloused ridges of his collarbones. His lungs are silent mineral sponges; his heart is a slow, tarry knot beating once every forty minutes. He leaves shallow, clean-edged footprints spaced twenty-eight inches apart, though no ghost-smear of colorless static ever trails behind his heels.

He begins to walk the circuits.

He does not take over their work, though when he passes a spatial knot, he takes a century to lean his ironwood shoulder against a floating cairn-stone until it clicks back into true alignment. Mostly, he walks in wide, deliberate arcs engineered to intersect the lonely forty-mile paths of the others—not once every lowland century, by blind chance, but regularly, reliably, once every few dozen steps of their vast, dry lives.

Eighteen months after the woman with the split cheekbone finds the smooth meteoric pry-bar waiting beside the cairn—sliding its indestructible edge effortlessly into the five-beast snarl and levering the reality clean—she crests a slate-blue swell of granite and sees another pillar of dark stone standing amid the motionless origami flocks, waiting for her.

Over the span of three weeks, they close the final yard between them. Over the span of a season, she raises her dry, horn-plated palms—one hand still gripping the new iron bar—and presses them flat against his, leaning her silt-caked brow against his brow, bone to bone.

When the piezoelectric *ticks* of his internal chemistry cross the seam of their skulls into her ancient, enduring mind, he does not overwhelm her with visions of green valleys, oceans, or loud, wet heavens she could no longer comprehend or bear. 

Instead, through the slow, chalk-soft thud of his borrowed heart and the microscopic ratchet of his tendons, he gives her news she can hold. He shares the crisp, deep satisfaction of a spatial tear along the southern rim cleanly mended; the exact, steady rhythm of the new three-degree shadow-tide; the clear, unfrayed memory of who they were when they stacked the first slate windbreak, preserved without rust or loss. And underneath the news, vibrating through the dense mineral lattice of skull and collarbone like the warm, deep piezoelectric hum of the planet's own bedrock, he passes a quiet, unmistakable certainty into the marrow of her dry bones:

*The cairns are holding. Your steps are counted. You are seen.*

They stand forehead to forehead on the crest of the smoothed-down mountain for half a lowland year, two dark stone pillars framed against the clear, shadow-sharp sky, sharing the warmth of a steady current. Then, over a month, their palms unlock. She turns eastward across the silent sea of shells, hefting the good iron bar in her hand, her stride unhurried and whole; and the forty-first walker turns west, stepping twenty-eight inches at a time toward the next solitary figure three ridges away, taking all the millennia in the world to make his rounds.
