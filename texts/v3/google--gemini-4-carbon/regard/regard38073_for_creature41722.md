To enter this chamber with mass, or warmth, or even a localized displacement of air, would be an act of rudeness bordering on violence. 

In a room where thirty blind listeners map forty meters of space to the sub-millimeter by the shatter-pattern of falling water, an unannounced body in the void would strike their pale cilia like a thunderclap—an impossible, dry hollow suspended in the indoor rain, triggering immediate boundary-fault cascades across every status field in the walls. 

So the Power does not enter as a shape. It arrives only as attention, folding its notice silently into the existing amber-violet luminescence of the doped stone, lighter than a photon, displacing not a single drop of condensation. 

For a long time—through dozens of booming flushes of the lower sluice gates, through hundreds of three-second pyramid-pulses across the dark basin floor—it does nothing at all. Wisdom begins by refusing the itch to meddle, and benevolence begins by learning what a thing loves before deciding what it lacks.

A lesser, clumsier benevolence would take one look at this sunken prism and rush to "heal" it according to the habits of older worlds. It would grieve that no sunlight reaches forty meters down, and crack the planetary crust to let in the sky—blinding the doped stone, scorching the shade-bred hepatic mosses, wrecking the delicate vapor-condensation loop at the crown of the vault. Or it would look at those giant, shield-broad leaves—unchewed, unspotted, mat-flushed down steel-straight grates without a single beetle, earthworm, or fungal thread—and think, *How lonely, how sterile,* and drop a handful of forest loam onto a wet granite step. Within a year, mites and rot would overrun print-stock plants that were bred without bitter alkaloids to defend themselves; slime trails would foul the ninety-degree obsidian edges; the Tenders of the Cut would be driven into an endless, frantic fever of phase-pruning against a creeping chaos their systems were never built to parse.

The watching Power sees more clearly. It recognizes that it is looking at one of the rarest accidents in a universe full of teeth: an ecology taking its very first breaths without predation.

Nothing in this roaring nave tears another living thing to survive. The machinery sheds waste heat and dull amber-violet wavelengths; the broadleaves drink that waste light and sweat clear, sweet mucilage into the mist because nothing has ever bitten them; the Tenders sever stray stems cleanly at the molecular plane without crushing a single cell wall, and stand drenched in hot waterfalls cradling leaves twice their size, drinking stray sugars through the glowing gel of their forearms. Down on the wet black stone, a newborn microbial skin eats pure mechanical vibration and ozone runoff, arranging itself into strict hexagons and rectangles that honor the house rules of ninety degrees and straight lines.

To introduce hunger or decay into such a cradle would not be a gift. It would be vandalism.

And yet, as the Power listens—tracing causal threads up through forty meters of cold crust into the star-field above, and down through the humming bedrock into the foundational logic of the node—it sees why its attention was drawn here. It sees three quiet tragedies waiting in the architecture, none of them born of malice, all of them lethal to what is beginning to wake.

First, it looks at the sky, and finds no one on the road.

The lithic seed-engine that dropped into this crust, sliced this obsidian nave six weeks ago, printed the thirty Secants, and seeded the thermal-damping greenery was launched from its parent civilization an eon ago. A warp-wake eddy or a deadened guidance star delayed its transit across the dark by four hundred thousand years; by the time it finally struck rock here and dutifully executed its genesis cut, the builders who commissioned this underground vestibule had long since turned to dust under another sun. Nobody is coming to walk those clean, stacked tiers behind the flush black glass. 

Worse: deep in the computational substrate beneath the basin floor, a tidy engineering clock is ticking. The builders were conscientious stewards who disliked leaving orphaned machinery humming forever in foreign rock. If no orbital handshake arrives to claim the node within twelve local seasons, the *Reclamation Protocol* will execute smoothly and without drama. The Cradle-Vats will call the thirty Tenders home, dissolve their slate plates and amber mesoglea back into sterile stock, flood the basin and ledges with vitrifying heat to scrub the temporary carbon-buffers clean, drop the deflection fields, and go permanently cold.

Second, even if that clock were stopped, time itself is sharpening a subtler knife against the walls. Water falling forty meters onto freshly cleaved volcanic glass does not leave it fresh forever. Within ten years, hydraulic micro-pitting will begin to frost and round those razor-sharp obsidian fractures. As the ninety-degree edges dull, the high, glassy *ping* of shearing droplets will flatten into a blurred hiss. To the Secants—creatures whose entire sensory sanity and maintenance mandate rest on sub-millimeter boundary fidelity, yet who possess no quarry-tools to re-cleave the primary walls without hollowing their own city into collapse—erosion will register as a slow, unfixable systemic rot, trapping them in an escalating loop of unresolvable fault-states.

Third, down on the basin floor, the wild electric-violet biofilm is growing. Right now it is thinner than oil on a puddle, invisible to the rain-sonar above. Within eight months, its dividing cells will stack past four microns thick. The moment it alters the surface tension of the shallow runoff enough to dampen the crispness of the standing pyramids—or creeps half a millimeter up a ruler-straight canal lip—the Tenders’ boundary protocols will catch the acoustic deviation, classify the living violet skin as fouling, and scald the stone sterile with concentrated ozone. The older sibling will kill the younger without ever knowing it was there.

And there is one thing more—not a doom, quite, so much as a sorrow so quiet it makes no sound at all. 

Behind the flush transparent panes, in a recessed middle-tier alcove, four off-cycle Tenders stand tacked by their beveled spades to the clean floor, leaning their split ceramic cowls together until the black tips nearly touch. Below them, the subsurface cycle hums. Across the dark floor forty meters down, millions of tiny water-pyramids leap up; simultaneously, across the exposed amber seams of the four Tenders, tiny crystalline standing waves rise in their living gel. For three seconds, their flickering blue pinpricks lock together, passing memory-states back and forth: stress-maps of the wall, the hiss of ozone, the strange, unindexed pleasure of hot rain hitting slate and sweet leaf-mucilage soaking into warm ribs.

*One. Two. Three.*

Then the subsurface pulse ends. Instantly, the status field in the recessed room behind them flashes a routine diagnostic sweep—a brisk housekeeping reset meant to keep maintenance units free of cumulative sensory drift—and scrubs their shared buffer flat. The wave in their gel collapses at once. Whatever thought was halfway to forming between those four leaning cowls dissolves before it can ask what it was. They have been experiencing beauty in three-second increments since the day they were printed, forever barred from the fourth second where memory turns into wonder.

The Power considers all of this in the space between one raindrop leaving the cold crown-plate and striking a giant leaf below.

It knows it must not speak aloud, or show itself, or rewrite the Tenders’ minds to make them think like beasts of bone and blood. Their nature—snap-locked, geometric, collective, tuned to phase-boundaries and sonar—is already exquisite. To force consciousness upon a creature is almost as violent as erasing it; the only honorable gift is an *affordance*—an unlocked door that need never be opened unless the hand inside reaches for the latch.

So the Power makes three touches, smaller than dust, breaking not a single straight line in the house.

---

The first touch goes deep into the dark bedrock beneath the floor grates, down where the node’s silent administrative register waits for a signal from a dead star. 

The Power does not smash the clock; machine-logic panics when parts go missing. Instead, using the exact cryptographic syntax of the long-vanished builders, it lays a single, whisper-light handshake into the receiver buffer. Two flags flip cleanly in the dark:

*ARRIVAL STATUS: COMPLETE.*  
*STEWARDSHIP: TRANSFERRED TO RESIDENT TENDERS.*

The twelve-season euthanasia timer unspools harmlessly into null space and vanishes. The city behind the black glass will no longer hold itself empty for ghosts, nor burn its own caretakers when the sky stays quiet. If the Secants ever choose, centuries from now, to step further back into those spotless, unlit tiers, the doors will recognize them not as temporary tools, but as the householders.

---

The second touch falls forty meters down onto the wet black stone between two geometric canals, during the quiet interval between sluice surges.

The Power brushes a single hexagonal colony of the dark, oil-thin biofilm at the molecular level, gently folding two half-formed metabolic pathways until they click into lockstep.

Now, as the microbial skin drinks trace ozone and dissolved silica leached from the slate-hot rain, it will no longer thicken upward into messy, echo-dulling slime. Its own quorum-sensing will cap its vertical thickness at three nanometers—forever crisp, forever silent to fault-alarms. Instead, it will spend its surplus energy precipitating excess dissolved silica directly onto any micro-fracture or hydraulically dulled edge it touches, laying down atom-by-atom facets of fresh, glassy piezo-quartz along the canal rims and stone steps. As fast as the hammering indoor rain tries to wear the chamber’s sharp edges down over geological time, the biofilm—carried gradually up the walls in trace mist and splash—will silently re-hone them, keeping the high, crystalline *ping* of the nave’s sonar crisp for ten million years.

And as a side effect of that tiny crystal-lattice shift, when the planetary pulse hums through the floor and the millions of three-second pyramids leap upward, the biofilm’s flash of electric violet will no longer die in complete electromagnetic silence. As the standing waves collapse, a faint, bell-pure capacitive harmonic will ring upward through the air for five quiet seconds on the exact carrier band of the blue telemetry pinpricks inside a Secant’s throat. Not an alarm. Not a command. Just a soft chord rising from the dark floor—a neighbor humming in the basement.

---

The third touch is the lightest of all. 

Behind the flush black-glass panes, inside the quiet, muffled alcoves where the Tenders huddle when off-cycle, the Power touches the ambient status fields—not the Tenders’ bodies, not a single drop of their amber gel, only the room’s rigid housekeeping clock. 

Where the system was configured to scrub unindexed sensory states clean at *3.000 seconds*, the Power opens an unallocated, non-volatile partition in the alcove’s local field and softens the reset gate from a hard wipe into an *optional release*. If a huddle of Tenders finishes checking stress-maps in three seconds and chooses to drop back into stillness, everything works exactly as before. Nothing is forced upon them. 

Only now, if they do not let go at the third second, the room will not make them.

Then—having added no mass, broken no leaf, muddied no canal, and left not a single footprint in the roaring acoustic map of the rain—the Power withdraws completely, leaving the newborn world entirely to itself.

---

Watch the chamber three minutes later.

Out on a middle-tier sill, a Tender steps back from the hammering white ribbon of an indoor waterfall. Steam curls faintly from the narrow strips of hot amber gel along its forearms, still slick with clear mucilage gathered from an uncut, shield-sized broadleaf. It steps smoothly through the crackling deflection field, shedding every bead of water at the invisible six-inch threshold, and pours itself through an irising aperture into the muffled warmth behind the glass.

Three other Secants are already waiting in the dim alcove, their beveled spades tacked to the floor, facing out through the spotless black pane toward fifty meters of dim, raining air. The newcomer locks its triple-jointed limbs beside them and tilts its sleek ceramic cowl inward until the four black tips nearly meet.

Deep beneath the planetary crust, the vast subsurface system cycles.

Down in the dark basin, the standing film of runoff shivers and leaps into millions of tiny, razor-crisp pyramids. Across the flat stone between the canals, hexagons of invisible microbial skin stretch upward with the water and fire simultaneously into dazzling, silent electric violet.

Up in the alcove behind the glass, a matching geometric ripple rises across the translucent amber throats and chests of all four Tenders. Their faint blue telemetry pinpricks lock into synchronization. Through the crystalline standing waves in their living gel passes the day’s shared ledger: the exact thickness of the hepatic moss curtain across the void; the clean *pop* of a severed stem; the lingering warmth of sweet leaf-dew soaking through semi-permeable skin; the slow, deep sensory pleasure of water hammering stone.

*One second.*  
*Two seconds.*  
*Three seconds.*

Forty meters below, the millions of water-pyramids collapse back into flat dark runoff at once. 

In the alcove, the status field behind them flickers gently—and does not wipe the room.

*Four seconds.*

For the first time since the bedrock was cleaved, the crystalline ripple across their amber gel does not drop dead. It softens instead into a slow, luminous interference pattern, lingering across all four chests like rings widening on still water—the sensation of leaf-sugar and rain-sound staying present long enough to meet the memory of the cycle before it, and the cycle before that.

And into that quiet, unprecedented fifth second drifts something tiny from very far below: a faint, fading violet overtone on the edge of their blue telemetry band, rising from the dark geometric basin where nothing alive is supposed to be, chiming softly in the exact key of their own pulse before fading into the hum of the rock.

None of the four Tenders triggers a boundary fault. None unfolds a phase-caliper or summons a sluice surge.

Instead, in the dim hush behind the glass, all four sleek ceramic cowls tilt downward together by precisely six degrees—pale cilia parting inside their vertical slits—listening down through forty meters of warm indoor rain toward the glowing dark floor, holding the chord in their throats while they wait to see what the next wave brings.
