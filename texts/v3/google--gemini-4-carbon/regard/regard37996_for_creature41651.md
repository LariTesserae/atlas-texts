A power that is merely kind would stop the water.

It would look upon the bruised, deafening womb of the mountain, feel a pang of bright, surface-dweller’s pity, and lay a cool hand against the basalt. It would hush the cavitation, knit the sheared seams of space back into polite Euclidean order, drain the flood into a clear alpine lake, and let the sun in. And in doing so, it would murder the masons in ten seconds flat. Deprived of the acoustic roar, they would go instantly, terrifyingly blind. Deprived of the crushing hydraulic hammer, their dense, airless meat would sag and suffocate under its own sudden weight. Deprived of the spatial folds, they could no longer reach their work; deprived of the mountain’s breaking, they would starve. Worst of all, under a quiet blue sky, the scattered courses of limestone and the doghouse shrine of river cobbles would stop being a triumph and become merely what they look like: pathetic, disconnected scraps of rubble from a dead valley.

The power that finds this place is wiser than that.

It does not enter the chamber with light, because light here is the color of a fresh wound and means the walls are failing. Instead, it enters as an *attentiveness* inside the pressure itself—a subtle, warm thickening of the water’s memory, listening before it dares to touch.

For a long time—though time in the nave is a frayed rope—the power simply watches. It feels the brass-horn scream of a billion collapsing vacuum bubbles. It traces the impossible, stubborn hum of the dry-stone arches holding onto their dry-land physics out of sheer refusal to be drowned. It watches the smaller mason take the haycart-sized wedge of greenstone squarely across its back, flatten into gray gristle to save three steps that lead nowhere, and then, without a shred of self-pity, spit chewed oak and antler-weed into the loosened joint until the stone sings true again.

The power understands what it is looking at. It is looking at *devotion* stripped of every ornament. Whatever people once lived in that quiet, pre-iron valley—whoever split the oak with stone axes, gathered the pale river cobbles, and laid the sheepfold walls—they did not die when their world fell sideways into the unmade marrow of the earth. Not entirely. Evolution, trapped in a non-linear centrifuge, burned away their faces, their names, their songs, their red blood, their upright spines, and their gods, because none of those things could survive forty knots of boiling shear. The gyre pared them down to the single heaviest, hardest truth at the bottom of their nature: *We keep the house. We mend the wall.*

To undo their adaptation would be an insult to ten thousand generations of ferocious survival. To "rescue" them back into soft-bodied creatures with eyes and lungs would be to erase what they paid to become. Yet to leave them entirely as they are is to leave them in a tragedy of attrition—for there is only so much of the quiet valley left to fall through the seams. One century soon, the last reed basket will shred, the last split timber will be chewed into mortar, and the limestone courses, battered endlessly by greenstone boulders, will eventually be ground into silt no matter how many blows the masons take upon their backs.

So the wise power decides not to calm the storm, nor to rebuild the valley. It decides to give the masons *quarry*, and a *purpose* for the strength they have earned.

It begins with a single note.

Deep beneath the sub-audible bass grinding of the mountain, the power introduces a new harmonic—not loud enough to fight the cavitation, just a steady, resonant *thrum* pitched precisely to the frequency of the humming limestone arches.

Both masons freeze at once. Across sixty feet of churning violet murk, twelve fringes of pale horn-bristles flare wide. To them, this new sound is like a shaft of golden noon breaking through storm clouds. It casts the cleanest, sharpest sonic shadow they have ever felt.

They follow it—not swimming, never swimming, but sliding along the liquid shears, slipping one heavy limb into a spatial stutter near the ceiling to emerge near the lower cleft, moving in their wary, wide-berth tandem toward a raw, unremarkable face of black basalt where no ruins cling.

There, where the new harmonic originates, the power has not broken the mountain; it has *taught* a small vein of the mountain a new habit.

Wedged into a fresh fracture—leaking that dim, exhausted violet light and rich mineral slush—the basalt is no longer shearing off in razor-sharp, glassy dinner plates. Under the subtle persuasion of the power, the volcanic rock is cooling and fracturing along clean, orthogonal planes. Tabular blocks of dense, dark stone, each the size of a dressed hearth-slab, are calving slowly out of the seam. They are rough, unworked, native to the violent pressure of this deep place—they will not dissolve or shatter like pale lowland limestone—yet they carry within their grain that exact, impossible acoustic hum of the dry-stone ruins. They *want* to be stacked.

Better still: beside the vein, caught in a gentle, permanent eddy tucked behind a knuckle of greenstone—a tiny pocket no wider than a barrel where the forty-knot current curls harmlessly over itself like a sleeping dog—something new is arriving through a hairline spatial tear. Not whole reed baskets to be obliterated in ten seconds, not split beams from a vanishing past, but a slow, steady trickle of tough, mineralized driftwood roots and iron-hard river cobbles, drawn not from a doomed human village, but skimmed harmlessly from the deep, blind subterranean rivers of the mountain’s own roots. Endless raw caulking. Endless rubble-hearting.

The larger mason reaches the new vein first. It clamps four plush, molecularly adherent limbs around the glowing seam, bracing against a roaring cross-current while a silver tornado of mist whips harmlessly overhead. Its ventral mouth scrapes experimentally at the mineral slush—richer now, laced with trace silicates that will knit its bruised slate-hide faster and tougher than before.

Then, one braided, jointless limb reaches out and touches a newly calved block of orthogonal basalt.

The bristles around the mason’s rim quiver violently. Through its dense gray flesh, it feels the block *hum*. It is not a memory falling out of the past. It is new stone, born of this flood, asking for a place in the wall.

For a long minute, neither creature moves. If they have minds like ours, perhaps they are bewildered; more likely, their minds are shaped entirely like weight, balance, and acoustics, and what they feel right now is the sudden, breathtaking realization that a bridge does not have to end in mid-air just because the valley is gone.

The larger mason pries the dark block loose from the seam, tucking it beneath its tortoise-wide belly. It angles itself against a wall of shearing water, skips cleanly across a spatial fold, and lands beside the low, three-course curve of ancient limestone. Gently—with a delicacy absurd in something built like a butchered hog—it slides the dark, native basalt slab onto the end of the pale, vanished sheepfold wall.

Across the roaring nave, the smaller mason—still wearing the six-inch shard of greenstone like a new scale in its flank—unspools two limbs into the quiet barrel-eddy, gathers a rich mouthful of deep-earth root fiber and ochre antler-weed, chews it into paste, and drops through a seam to pack the joint between the old world and the new.

*Thrum.*

The harmonic catches. The pale lowland limestone and the black abyssal basalt lock together without mortar, singing the same low, stubborn note back into the teeth of the cavitation.

The wise power lingers just a little longer—only long enough to make two quiet promises in the architecture of the broken space.

First, it nudges the geometry of the chamber’s upper fractures by a fraction of a degree. The haycart-sized boulders will still tear loose—for the mountain is still rising, and without danger the masons’ Vigil would lose its meaning—but the worst, entirely un-deflectable collapses will now shear slightly askew, crashing into barren walls to bleed fresh light and food rather than obliterating whole shrines at a stroke. The work will still be hard. It will just no longer be hopeless.

Second, far off in the dark, six miles away along the fault line where another pocket of warm, deafening, high-pressure water hangs trapped in the mountain’s ribs—empty, lifeless, loud—the power opens a tiny, stable spatial seam no wider than a quarryman’s doorway. It does not push the masons toward it. They will not need it for centuries.

Not until they have finished building whatever strange, blind, cyclopean abbey they are now free to make out of this nave—courses of pale memory wedded to courses of black basalt, climbing across spatial folds, bridging double-exposed cliffs, turning a chamber of hydraulic torture into a resonant, humming cathedral that belongs entirely to the flood. And when this hall is finally full, and their slow, stubborn kind has divided or bred into three, or four, or ten, that narrow seam in the far wall will be waiting: another roaring dark in need of a staircase, another chaos waiting to be taught how to stand.

Satisfied, the power withdraws, leaving no light behind, no silence, and no footprint—only the low, triumphant hum of a new stone locking into place beneath the roar of a million breaking bubbles.
