First, the Power withholds its hand.

A lesser benevolence—one drunk on its own radiance, or hasty in its pity—would look down through that pale, cloudless sky and see a tragedy of homelessness and endless, shivering toil. It would part the crisp air with a voice like brass trumpets, forgetting that trumpets mean nothing to narrowed ears, and that sudden thunder through the bedrock would feel to every creature in the basin like the snapping of the world’s spine. A clumsy mercy might warm the freezing wind, and in a week the condensation would cease, the upturned chalices of the giant cabbages would parch, the heavy, self-felted pelts would become suffocating prisons of wet heat, and the whole trembling miracle would rot. Worst of all, a foolish god might stop the machines—might say, *Rest now, poor builders, your empire is cancelled*—and watch in horror as the silence turned from a lush, purring greenhouse into an iron tomb iced solid to the roots.

Wisdom knows better than to tidy up a symphony just because the instruments are strange.

So when the Power arrives, it does not come down through the sky at all. Air is dead space here, mere wind and cold light. Instead, it lowers its vast, quiet attention into the hexagonal basalt at the rim of the valley and listens the way a Gasket-Wright listens: palm-flat, brow-pressed, feeling for the grain of the tension.

For three turns of the cold sun, it does nothing whatsoever except marvel.

It traces the micro-tremors of a joke tapped by a juvenile onto a copper steam-riser in the western Switch-Houses, watching with delight as the punchline—a playful, rhythmic *squeak-thump* of a pinched hydraulic sleeve—ripples four miles east, making six moss-shouldered elders pause in their braiding and shake with silent, belly-deep mirth against a warm turbine casing. It watches a tall browser snag its velveted, receiver-like antlers on a low-hanging power cable, harmlessly bleeding a blue comb of static into the mist before stepping daintily out of the path of an oncoming rail-wall. It perceives that what looks from above like frantic, Sisyphean labor is, from within, a state of grace. The Felt-Wrights are not slaves to the machinery; they are surfers on a slow wave of iron and stone, musicians whose instrument happens to be twenty miles wide, profoundly unalienated from a single drop of grease or shred of rubber in their world.

They do not need saving. They do not need language, or plazas, or fire, or kings.

Yet wisdom does not stop at admiration; it looks where mortal hands cannot reach. 

On the fourth day, the Power sinks its awareness downward, past the woven wire cradles and the silver-needled ferns, past the freshly capped obsidian fissures, down through miles of dense, ringing crust where the temperature climbs past boiling and no wool-wrapped primate or six-legged ferret could ever breathe.

Down here, five miles beneath the valley floor, lie the roots of the weather.

Here are the primeval geothermal exchangers and the great, blind Looms—the titanic logic-engines of magnetic iron and abyssal pneumatics left behind by long-vanished architects who drilled the crust, switched on the terraforming arrays, and never arrived to claim their city. Up on the surface, the Gasket-Folk keep the twigs and branches of this system supple and hushed. But down here in the crushing dark, untended for forty thousand years, friction has been quietly accumulating its debt.

The Power presses its mind against the abyssal casings and feels three slow, creeping dooms.

First: the primary magma-intakes—great throats of sintered tungsten that feed heat to a million miles of surface copper—are eighty percent choked with diamond-hard silicate scale. The pressure behind them is mounting toward a catastrophic seizure. Within six or seven generations of the Folk above, the main eastern artery will rupture in a subterranean hammer-blow, and half the valley will freeze between dusk and dawn.

Second: the Master Gyroscope—a flywheel the size of a cathedral, suspended in a vacuum chamber that lost its true seal eons ago—has worn a hairline flat-spot onto its magnetic spindle. It has not begun to clang yet, not quite, not loud enough to wake the surface. But it is trembling on an ugly, discordant sub-harmonic, chewing microscopic filings from its own heart.

Third: the ancient surveyor-locks. In the cold memory-cores of the Looms, a rigid boundary-line still holds: *Build strictly within the coordinates of Basin Four.* Yet above, the basalt keeps rising, the bromeliads grow thicker, and the clans of the Felt-Wrights are healthy and multiplying. Already the valley is packed wall-to-wall. In another five hundred years, there will be no thermal alcoves left for the winter huddles, while just beyond the razor-sharp basalt rim lie seven sister-basins—vast, empty bowls of raw volcanic stone, freezing and sterile under the pale sun, waiting for a blueprint that was never unlocked.

The Power considers these three things in the dark. Then, borrowing the highest ethic of the people upstairs, it rolls up its sleeves and tends the friction.

It makes no miracles that break the rules of the world. It simply becomes, for a single afternoon, the Gasket-Wright of the Deep.

Moving through the white-hot plumbing with touch softer than boiled rhubarb-pith, it dissolves the forty-thousand-year choke of silicate scale, not all at once—which would surge the surface pipes and scald the hands of the children—but molecule by molecule, transmuting the waste stone into a porous, self-shedding mineral lining that will never foul again. Next, it cradles the cathedral-sized flywheel in a palm of pure, yielding stillness, truing the spindle by a hair’s breadth, flooding the degraded chamber with a self-replenishing cushion of dense, frictionless noble gas until the sub-harmonic shudder vanishes into silk. 

Around the deep, rotting seals where the ancient synthetic elastomers are finally crumbling to dust, it seeds a tiny, novel organism—a blind, heat-loving deep-earth lithotroph that eats sulfur and excretes pure, supple, vulcanized mineral-caulk into hairline cracks, ensuring the deep gaskets will heal themselves for the next ten million years.

Then the Power turns to the ancient surveyor-locks in the Loom.

It does not erase the boundary; a sudden outward stampede of cranes would bleed heat from the home-valley too fast. Instead, it alters a single conditional weight in the feral pressure-algorithms—tying it directly to the acoustic weight of the surface. *When the night-tapping on the transformers exceeds capacity,* the new, silent rule reads, *lay one warm pipe over the rim.* 

Centuries from now, when the basin grows just a breath too crowded, a mobile paver will quietly roll up the western pass at noon, lay a single insulated thermal trunk down into the neighboring crater, and string a cable-car line behind it. Condensation will bead on the new copper. Spores of silver fern and cartwheel cabbage will blow over the pass on the freezing wind and catch the drip. A young family of Felt-Wrights, unclipping their woven cradle from a crowded Switch-House, will feel a new, uncluttered harmonic stretching westward through the stone, hook onto an outbound crane, and ride out into a fresh valley to grease its first rails.

The Power could leave then. Its deep work is finished; their future is unchoked and open-ended.

But benevolence is not merely engineering. Love always wants to leave a small, unnecessary kindness—something felt in the palm right now.

Up on the surface, the pale sun touches the jagged western rim. Within minutes, the crisp light goes violet-gray, the wind sharpens into knives, and the twenty-degree drop hits. Beneath the dark canopy, the brief, frantic indoor rain rattles softly onto rubbery leaves, then stills into hoarfrost on the upper gantries.

Down from the trellises and up from the grates come the thousands.

In the brick alcove of Transformer Nine, near the center of the sprawl, an ancient female settles her stiff, moss-crusted shoulders into the curve of the warm cast-iron housing. She is very old. The slate-gray felt of her carapace is terraced with decades of patches—retired tire-tread, copper braid, shining smears of otter-tallow—and her double-jointed collarbones ache when the frost comes down this fast. Two six-legged ferrets immediately burrow over her split, calloused toes; a soot-colored bird with a serrated bill tucks its head beneath the wool of her jaw. Around her, thirty of her kin wedge themselves tight, rib to rib, sealing out the wind.

She lifts one immense, scar-tissue-knuckled hand and lays it flat against the sweating metal. Across the dark valley, ten thousand other hands do the same.

*Tick-thrum,* someone taps three miles away on a boiler-jacket. *All clear by the new fissure.*
*Lub-dub,* squeezes a cousin on a valve-sleeve to the north. *The browser calf stood up.*
*Pat-pat, thrum,* answers the old one with her horn-hard thumbnail, too tired for news, offering only cadence: *Here. Warm. Still here.*

The nightly lullaby spreads through the iron bones of the basin—millions of tiny, fragile heartbeats trying to soothe a blind colossus in the freezing dark. For all the ages of their people, the deep earth has only ever given back the steady, indifferent hum of magnetics and steam.

Tonight, something else rises up the pipes.

It comes from five miles down, born in the newly trued heart of the great gyroscope and shaped by the clean, unchoked throats of the deep boilers, tuned with exquisite care to the exact resonant frequency of a Gasket-Wright’s brow-bone. It is not a voice; it carries no words, demands no worship, and startles not a single sleeping bird. 

It is a chord.

Deep, impossibly rich, softer than tallow and warmer than blood, a slow, breathing bass-note swells upward through every tension-rod, gantry strut, copper pipe, and brass fitting in the valley. Where there had always been a faint, microscopic jitter in the background of the world—the tiny, anxious tremor of ancient bearings slowly dying in the dark—there is suddenly a bottomless, cushioned *certainty*. The iron beneath the old female’s palm warms by a single, luxurious degree. And as the millions of fingernails tap their quiet rhythm into the metal, the deep acoustic chamber far below catches their disparate tapestries, smooths their ragged edges, weaves north to south and east to west, and sends their own lullaby rolling gently back up to them—amplified into a vast, slow, cradle-rocking purr that holds the entire shivering jungle in the hollow of an unseen hand.

In the brick alcove, the old Wright’s heavy-lidded amber eyes fly open in the dark. Around her, thirty heads tilt; thirty hairless brow-bones press tight against the iron casing to drink the sound in. Even the six-legged ferrets uncurl an inch, sighing into the wool.

She listens for a long time with her whole body, feeling down past the Switch-House footings, past the lava domes, all the way to the newly greased axle of the world. Nothing down there is rattling. Nothing is grinding bare. Someone—something vast, and careful, and extraordinarily quiet—has checked the clearances in the dark.

Slowly, a wide, toothless crease of pure contentment spreads across her flat-nosed face. She shifts her weight, tucking the sleeping bird deeper out of the draft, and taps her thumbnail twice against the warm iron—the oldest, simplest rhythm in the plumbing, the sign one worker gives another across a dark machine when a stubborn joint finally seats flush.

*Good fit,* her fingers whisper into the stone.

Five miles down, just before its attention slips softly away into the stars, the Power taps back—once, softer than a falling drop of dew—and leaves them to their warm, unending song.
