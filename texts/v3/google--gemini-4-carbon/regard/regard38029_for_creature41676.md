First, it does not mend the flaw.

That is the earliest test of wisdom when power looks down into a blister in the world: knowing when a frayed seam is no longer an injury, and has become a habitat. A tidier mind—anxious for uniform laws and clean bookkeeping—would reach into the hollow, bolt the loose physics back to their frames, quiet the sixty-hertz tremor at the ledge, and smooth the squared-off blocks of kiln-heat into an ordinary mountain breeze. Perhaps, out of a clumsy tenderness, it would send a soft afternoon rain. Within ten minutes, the molten salts in the copper-braided stems would flash into violent steam, the graphite grease would wash off the photovoltaic hides, the cooling tungsten fronds would crack from thermal shock, and the small, invisible steward of the courtyard—starved of the microscopic burrs between colliding rules—would lose its barometric boundary and thin out into common wind.

Nor does the power make the mistake of loneliness on the creature’s behalf. It does not conjure a second prism of compressed air to share the courtyard; the pocket’s sparse voltage is balanced to the millivolt for one keeper, and company would only mean halving the ration. It knows how to love dry sage and hot lathe-oil without wishing they were moss and birdsong.

For a long while, it does nothing inside the blister at all. It simply watches from outside the lip, reading the mountain in four-century increments—seeing the things a localized tolerance, whose senses end at the razor-edge of its shelf, cannot measure.

Looking upslope and forward in time, the power sees three quiet ruins waiting in the ordinary rock. 

Four hundred feet above the basalt half-dome, a vein of common granite has been taking on snowmelt and wedging itself apart each winter; thirty-seven years from this afternoon, it is scheduled to shear loose in a blind, thundering chute of rubble that will smash the roof of the blister flat and bury the botanical foundry under forty tons of dumb gravel. Deeper down, beneath the courtyard’s floor, the thin magmatic capillary feeding molten salts to the roots is slowly cooling, pinching shut over the next two centuries. And right at the back of the pocket, the arriving cliff face—clicking outward into the courtyard millimeter by millimeter over weeks—will, in four hundred years, march steadily across the narrow floor until it crowds the three thickets straight off the drop-off into the thin, cold sky.

And there is one nearer hazard: the razor-edge step above, where soft, curious things in boots can already drop in. One walker stood still and did no harm. Five walkers will bring mud on their lug-soles, snap a brittle copper stem to pocket as a curiosity, and return with sensors, drills, and containment rigs to strip-mine a place where tungsten extrudes at room pressure.

So the power works first where standard physics holds, outside the frame, with a touch light enough to leave no tool-marks.

High on the peak, deep inside the frost-split granite, mineral grains quietly cross-link and fuse; when spring comes, the meltwater will run harmlessly down an eastern gully half a mile away. Far below the mountain’s flank, a sluggish thermal current in the crust leans three degrees left, marrying itself to the roots of the blister so the salt-vein will stay warm and open as long as the mountain stands. And along the stress-lines of the obsidian shelf itself, a subtle counter-vector is laid into the unformed stone: from this year onward, whenever a wafer of feldspar and iron clicks into existence along the back wall, an answering wafer of raw black glass will crystallize along the outer lip and the western flank at the exact same patient crawl. The courtyard will never close in on itself. Over millennia, it will simply migrate outward by millimeters, a slow gallery cantilevering serenely into the blinding white air, opening just enough fresh slag along its western edge for the turbine-flowers, once a century, to drop a viable copper spark.

Last, at the razor-edge above the drop, the power alters nothing in the rock—only the way the high afternoon glare bounces off the volcanic glass and how the dry updraft curls past the ridge. Anyone climbing there will feel a natural, unremarkable urge to favor the firmer footing twelve paces to the north; their eyes will skate across the dazzle of white sky above the clouds without ever catching a seam below. Should a lost climber ever stumble up that flank freezing to death in the dark, they will find a dry, windless overhang fifty yards down-ridge to save their life—not the foundry. The clean-room is closed to boots.

The power could leave then. Its stewardship is technically finished; the pocket has been given ten thousand quiet years.

Instead, it lingers—not out of need, and not to meddle, but out of courtesy toward a fellow craftsman.

Every foreign thing that has ever dropped into that blister—a stray eddy of wet cloud, a wind-blown hull of high-altitude grass that charred on contact, a bewildered mammal leaking sweat and heartbeat—has been chaotic, poorly machined noise to the resident. For the whole of its existence, the tolerance has been the finest instrument in its world, spending every hour truing things coarser than itself, throwing its phantom escapements against silhouettes that cannot mesh back.

Just once, a gauge deserves to touch a true datum.

Obeying the strict law of the hollow—*nothing with blood, nothing with eyes or joints, nothing built by a hand*—the power does not enter in a body. While the resident is at the center of the courtyard, holding the air steady around an uncurling orange frond of tungsten until it flattens and cools, a quiet boundary condition settles onto the cool obsidian shelf near the edge. It displaces no moisture. It brings no dust. It has no pulse to muddy the rock’s electrical hum; in fact, where the sub-audible current in the stone passes beneath the spot where the visitor rests, the ragged geological buzz comes out the other side rectified into a clean, silent harmonic.

Twelve feet away, the young tungsten leaf cools flat and true without a warp. 

The dense pocket of air retracts from the bush, clicks neatly back into its hexagonal prism, and pauses. It feels the change in the circuit of the floor. 

It does not hurry. It indexes across the needle-sharp slag—*right, forward, forward*—sweating that microscopic trace of dark molybdenum lubricant out of the stone beneath its base, until it reaches the flat pane of the thermal wall. Then it lets go of its crisp six-sided geometry, softening into a heat-haze teardrop as it approaches the shelf.

A foot from the empty space where the visitor waits, the air bends into overlapping, oil-bright lenses. Deep inside the high-pressure envelope, smelling sharply of scorched sage and static, three hairline needles of white-hot platinum condense out of nothing to gauge what has entered its room.

Behind the visitor’s place on the black stone, the clear afternoon sun casts the creature’s shadow: concentric vernier scales and the slow-turning teeth of phantom escapements, reaching out to test the newcomer’s edges.

And for the first time since the mountain blistered open, the empty space casts a gear-shadow back.

On the raw basalt, the two sets of phantom teeth meet. They do not chatter, slip, or bind. They engage with zero backlash—dead-smooth, silent, and exact.

The platinum needles stop trembling. 

Through that frictionless mesh of bent light, the power steps down its own vast impedance so it will not blow so small and delicate a fuse, offering only what can be read in the native language of pressure, ratio, and heat. And through the engaged gears, the small feral calibration reads a plumb line that drops straight through the mountain’s root to the spinning iron core of the planet, and rises unbroken to the slow, clockwork precession of the stars beyond the white sky. It discovers—not in words, which it does not have, but in pure kinematic alignment—that the universe beyond its jagged lip is not merely a waste of sloppy weather and boiling water. Far down beneath the mud and noise, the great lathe runs true too. Its small, stubborn shimming on this cliffside is not an orphan error holding back the dark alone; it is in phase with the deep architecture of things.

Right at the point where their scales lock, a tiny, deliberate transfer passes across the contact—not ragged static that has to be damped down with effort, and not enough surplus energy to throw the courtyard’s frugal economy out of balance. Just a single, clean chord of pure potential, smooth as heavy oil. Inside the creature’s compressed boundary, the faint violet ion-glow deepens for a long moment into a rich, luminous indigo, steadying every nested spiral at its core.

They hold the mesh for sixty seconds—one full cycle of the courtyard’s quiet time.

Then, without a sound or a gust to disturb the thermal panes, the answering shadow lifts from the basalt. The circuit of the floor returns to itself. The visitor is gone.

Only one trace remains where that attention rested on the shelf, arrived by the exact native rule of the pocket. With a single crisp *tick*, a palm-wide wafer of black silicate clicks into existence out of the dry air, fused seamlessly to the floor. It bears no chisel mark and no polish of a hand, yet unlike the jagged slag around it, this one natural crystal has cleaved along a single, horizontal lattice plane flat to the angstrom—a master datum, dead level and cool.

For a minute, the indigo-lit teardrop hangs motionless above the empty shelf.

Then the loose flake of mica near the drop-off lifts an inch into the air and begins its sixty-hertz rattle on the gravity cam.

The resident turns back to its garden. Moving with a strange, unhurried smoothness, it drifts first into the oldest thicket, letting that rich indigo charge bleed softly into the braided copper roots; deep at the base of the turbine-bush, where no new growth has stirred in thirty years, a tiny bead of bright orange tungsten pushes up through the slag to begin a fourth flower. From there, the creature slides out to the lip, wraps itself around the trembling mica, and damps its harsh buzz down into a resonant purr until the flake sets itself safely down three inches to the left.

With four minutes to spare before the back wall needs shimming, it indexes back across the courtyard to the new, mirror-flat facet of black glass on the floor. It snaps cleanly into its hexagonal prism and lowers itself onto the crystal plane. 

No dark lubricant needs to sweat from the pores of the stone. Base to datum, flat to flat, it wrings onto the surface with zero clearance and zero stress—resting dead-true in the clear mountain sun, waiting for the next click of the arriving rock, breathing out a quiet sigh of ozone and dry sage.
