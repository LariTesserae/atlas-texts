To a power that has watched stars ignite and cool, there is a keen, rare delight in discovering a closed equation that balances itself to the last grain of dust. 

When the Presence first turns its attention downward, passing effortlessly through the salt-crust and the sagging roof of vitrified slag, its first impulse is stillness. A lesser benevolence might look upon this furnace—the whetstone shriek of the fronds, the blind lives hung over glowing trenches, the relentless, scouring draft—and feel pity. A lesser wisdom might think of cool rain, of soft moss, of rest upon solid ground, not realizing that a single drop of water here would be a bomb of steam, and stillness would be a mass grave.

The Presence does not bring rain. It folds its vastness down until it is no heavier than a shift in temperature, and it listens.

For what would be years on the surface above, it simply inhabits the toroidal wind. It rides the great convection engine alongside the five-pace mantles, admiring the dry, copper-veined grace of their bodies, the origami snap of their ribs through the narrow doorways, the constellations of orange sparks as the ribbon-shoals burn their harvest in midair. It traces the dished crescents of the ancient stairs, reading the ghost-weight of the long-vanished builders who carved an instrument for looms and left behind an ecosystem’s lung.

And as it listens, with a perception spanning millennia forward and back, it finds the two quiet tragedies coiled inside this jewel box.

The first is geological, and certain. The city is an hourglass sanding itself away. Every century, the dry convection currents plane another fraction of an inch from the slender pillars and the ash-mortar joints. The recessed knuckles of volcanic stone are loosening; the skeletal domes are thinning to wafer-bone. Worse, the sagging slag ceiling is growing heavier with trapped dust while the salt-crust around its rim creeps inward under the planet’s slow tectonic weight. In four hundred years, a central pillar in the lower wards will snap during a barometric surge. Its fall will shear three catwalks, choking a primary floor trench. The toroidal wind will knot, stall in the eastern quarter, and throw the acoustic cisterns out of phase. Within a thousand years, the ceiling will drop, crushing the organ flat, sealing the vents in silent rubble.

The second tragedy is already happening, every hour, in the dark. 

The Presence listens closely to the mantles hovering before the lintel-flues. Their brassy, sliding phrases are richer than mere territorial warnings or mating calls. Because they navigate a three-dimensional labyrinth entirely by ear, their copper nerve-nets have evolved prodigiously deep models of space, echo, and time. There is an eldest among them—a scarred, six-pace matriarch whose pellicle has oxidized to a pale, translucent verdigris at the edges, her trailing filaments braided with the static-ghosts of three vanished mates. 

She is not merely hunting. When the ten-second surges subside, she drifts from quarter to quarter, cupping her wing-tips over specific eroded slots, trying to play chords that no longer exist. Two generations ago, a stone lintel in the north terrace cracked and fell into the trenches; a resonance chamber she knew in her youth lost its voice. She returns to the gap again and again, flexing her pleated veil to recreate the missing acoustic baffle with her own body, holding the note in the roaring dark until exhaustion forces her back into the high turnaround. They have minds bright enough to remember beauty and mourn its erosion, yet they have no hands, and cannot touch the stone that houses them without shattering. They are composers trapped inside a rotting pipe organ, unable to mend a single reed.

The Presence considers. 

To sweep down and rebuild the city overnight in immortal diamond would be a violation—it would erase the acoustic texture of the volcanic rock, kill the triboelectric whetstone-scrape of the copper vines, and turn living history into a sterile cage. To lift the mantles into some gentle heaven would unmake what they are; they *are* the kiln-wind and the sulfur-spark.

Instead, the Presence chooses to work only in the grammar the blister already speaks: heat, mineral gas, crystal, and time.

Deep down in the crust, miles beneath the hand-cut floor trenches, the Presence touches the rising plumes of gas. It does not alter their heat or their ten-second rhythm. Slowly, over seasons, it shifts their trace chemistry—coaxing saturated vapors of dissolved silica, vaporized iron-nickel, and volatile copper halides up from the deep magma seams.

Nothing in the cavern explodes or falters. The skimmers feed; the gourds crack like whips; the ash-snow rises. 

Only now, where the triboelectric charge is strongest—precisely along the thinnest, most dangerously eroded columns, where the scraping thermophyte fronds keep the stone humming with high static voltage—the new trace vapors catch. Atom by invisible atom, drawn out of the wind by the electrical field itself, metallic silicates begin to plate onto the worn volcanic blocks and seep into the recessed ash-mortar. It is vapor-deposition at the speed of lichen. Where a pillar has been sanded thin as a wrist, a smooth, bronze-dark glaze of sintered quartz and meteoric iron begins to grow back against the wind, healing micro-fractures from the inside out, strengthening the load-bearing ribs without changing their round, soap-soft acoustics by a fraction of a hertz.

Above, at the sagging ceiling, the Presence turns the pinhole fissures to a subtler use. It nudges the salt-crust just enough to seal three widening cracks that threatened to drop slabs of slag onto the upper domes, while drawing open a dozen hairline vents angled steeply *outward* into a dry, porous sandstone stratum fifty yards overhead—a hidden, miles-wide sponge of empty stone. Now, when the red clay haze and white silica ash grind against the roof, the excess pressure bleeds imperceptibly away into the upper rock, keeping the ceiling light, the air clear enough to breathe its fire, and the blinding sun-needles steady.

That alone would save them. A caretaker would stop there.

An artist—and a lover of small, fierce minds—does one thing more.

Beyond the western salt-wall, separated from the ancient blister by forty paces of solid halite and basalt, lies a string of four other subterranean bubbles, formed in the same ancient volcanic cooling. Three are wider than the city; one rises two hundred paces high like a buried cathedral chimney. They have been dead, dark, and stagnant since the crust cooled, lacking floor vents to wake them.

With the patience of cooling magma, the Presence draws a thin, hot finger through the deep bedrock beneath those dark chambers, opening hairline geothermal trenches across their floors. Warmth creeps into them. Stagnant air begins to roll. 

Then it turns to the western wall of the city, high up in the lee of a ruined watchtower where the old verdigris-edged mantle often goes to play her broken lintel.

Over the course of a hundred barometric surges, the rock behind that broken lintel softens, sublimates, and drifts away as dry salt-fume. Not a collapse—an aperture. A smooth, curved throat of stone, six paces wide, fluted along its inner curve with natural basalt columns that catch the cross-draft between the old chamber and the newly warmed pocket beyond.

A new note enters the cavern.

Every flyer in the blister freezes on the current—not stalling, just holding station in the cherry-red dark. Down in the cisterns, sleeping spindles stir. High in the turnaround, braided pairs unhook their copper filaments. 

For ten thousand generations, the chord of the world has been fixed, shrinking only by subtraction as stones broke and fell. Now, pouring out of the western wall, comes a deep, velvety sub-harmonic—vast, hollow, and warm, smelling faintly of untouched sulfur and virgin pumice-dust. And riding back through the throat on the counter-current is the echo of an empty sky five times larger than everything they have ever known.

The Presence watches invisibly as the old verdigris mantle peels out of the upper loop. 

She approaches the western wall warily, banking around the needle-shafts of sun, reading the unfamiliar wake with every nerve-thread in her five-pace veil. She hovers before the new stone throat. Tentatively, she curls her scarred wing-tip over one of the basalt flutes along its rim.

The deep note bends—not cutting off, as in the small lintels of the vanished builders, but rolling outward into the dark neighboring cavern and returning two full seconds later, transposed and braided with three shimmering overtones she has never heard in her life. 

She holds still as the echo washes through her translucent skin. Then she flexes her ribs, snaps into a tight bronze javelin with a sharp *clack*, and shoots through the throat into the new chamber—blossoming wide in the uncharted dark, feeling the clean, rising warmth of fresh floor-vents beneath her belly, listening to the sheer, dizzying height of an uncarved ceiling waiting for spores, waiting for vines, waiting for names.

Behind her, trailing through the aperture on the first draft, come floating rafts of thermophyte seeds and a cautious, glittering ribbon of orange-sparking skimmers.

Before withdrawing its touch to let them claim their wider world alone, the Presence leaves one final, tiny gift in the broken lintel just beside the gateway. Grown out of pure, clear piezoelectric quartz from the mineral wind, a slender cluster of crystal reeds now bridges the gap the fallen stone left behind. They are tuned so delicately that even a whisper of a wing-tip sets them singing—not just in sound, but in faint, cool flashes of blue-white static light whenever a chord is struck, giving the blind musicians their very first gentle stars to steer by, and restoring the exact lost note the old matriarch had been holding in her memory all along.
