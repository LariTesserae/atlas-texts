A lesser benevolence would have brought the sun. It would have poured warm loam into the abyssal basement, packed red clay between the knitting-needle mortar lines, and given flesh to the shivering wire-sketches in the tenements—crushing the entire delicate pocket under a catastrophic avalanche of weight, turning the gill-swimmers to suffocating slime and snapping the perspective-bridges like dry twigs.

Wisdom knows better than to cure a thing of its own nature. 

When the Power arrived, it did not enter like a man walking a catwalk, displacing air and ruining the plumb. It entered as an adjustment to the dark itself—a quiet, attentive pressure settling into the sixty-pace boundary where the chill air turned back on itself. 

For a long cycle of sparks, the Power simply watched. It read the desperate, breathless economy of the vault. It understood what this place was: a blueprint stranded without a builder, an embryonic memory of industry trying to birth itself out of pure vegetative will, caught in a closed metabolic loop. The city could not grow solid because everything within it was starving for mass; the grazers ate the walls to live, the predators ate the grazers, and the tall, caliper-limbed artisans—the true, collective soul of the district—were slowly dismantling their own sparse bodies just to keep the idea of the people alive against the tearing vortices of the swimmers.

They were faithful unto extinction, truing the lines of a world that lacked the physics to arrive, recycling their own nerves into empty postures so the rooms would not forget why rooms exist.

The Power felt a profound, quiet ache for those stripped keels and asymmetrical struts. It saw the beauty of their draftsmanship—how cleanly a single pale limb bridged thirty yards of void by trusting the intersection of two green lines. To make this world heavy would be to make them blind and crippled. Yet to leave it untouched was to leave it trapped in a cold, frantic stasis, forever pruning and cannibalizing the same mouthful of sap.

So the Power did three small things, none of them louder than a falling drop of water.

First, it addressed the hunger in the basement. 

Down below the lowest switching yards, where the coiled black rootlets sketched the shapes of dynamos in empty air, the Power did not put soil. Instead, it altered the temperament of the abyssal cold. It thickened the boundary at the very bottom of the pocket into a slow, upwelling tide of dense, mineral-rich mist—not heavy enough to drown, not warm enough to rot, just fat with dissolved carbon, trace salts, and quiet electrostatic potential. 

When the next violet spark jumped from node to node, it did not vanish in a tenth of a second. Caught in the new, rich vapor, the violet flash softened into a steady, lingering indigo hum—an actinic twilight no brighter than deep-sea bioluminescence, gentle enough not to blind things without eyes, yet constant enough that the dark green stems began, very slightly, to drink light rather than merely leaking it.

The rootlets drank the mist. Within minutes, the sap surging upward through the phloem streetcar-cables grew richer. The apical buds along the factory gables no longer had to steal tension from the floors below to split open; they extruded a faint, sweet surplus of mucilage along their undersides—a sacrificial rind grown specifically for the ribbon-grazers and the crane-fly climbers. Full bellied on the surplus, the vermin stopped chewing through the structural welds.

Second, the Power looked to the gill-winged swimmers tearing blindly through the hollow parlors, shattering the delicate rib-sketches of the larval tenants. 

The Power did not banish the swimmers; their motion was the wind that kept the damp from stagnating. Instead, with a brush of thought lighter than static, it tuned the resonance of the green architectural outlines. The wireframe window-panes and absent brick walls began to emit an imperceptible acoustic pressure—a faint, thrumming harmonic generated by the spinning vine-wheels overhead. 

To a hand thrust through a wall, the emptiness would still feel unobstructed. Nothing solid had been added. Yet to the sensitive, boneless skins of the gill-swimmers, the harmonic registered like smooth glass. 

The next vortex of swimmers banking toward a third-story tenement did not crash through the kitchen and snap the pale tendon-sketch of the woman at the washbasin. At the exact plane of the wireframe brick, the flock banked smoothly upward, streaming up the exterior facade, riding drafts that now obeyed the *idea* of walls. Inside the rooms, the air went blessedly still. For the first time since the seed-coat of the district had split, an interior became a shelter.

Third, and most gently, the Power turned its attention to one of the tall draftsmen.

This one was near its end. Anchored between the foreground sill of a loom-shed and the distant arch of a signal box, it had already spent half its sensory drapery and one entire secondary strut into the grid. Its remaining caliper-limbs trembled slightly with hydraulic exhaustion; a fresh green vine-shoot was already creeping up its heel, preparing to encase it in bark and turn it into a permanent diagonal brace. Beside it hung the unfinished rigging of a stoker, missing the curve of a shoulder where a predator’s web had torn the threads away. The tall artisan was reaching toward its own bare keel with its split nib-palps, preparing to pluck out the last coherent ribbon of its own central nerve-film to finish the repair—an act that would lock its joints forever.

Before the nibs could pinch its own flesh, a droplet fell from sixty feet above.

It was not ordinary condensation. Gathered on a flywheel-leaf high in the indigo mist, the drop had been touched by the Power on its way down. It fell straight and true—curving slightly around the centrifugal pull of an empty turbine—and struck not the drum-leaf below, but the exact hollow intersection of the artisan’s split bone-prow.

*Plip.*

The droplet did not run off. Warmed by a fraction of a degree—just the barest whisper of springtime—it soaked instantly into the grey gristle. 

The artisan froze. Its tattered sensory membranes flared wide, trembling violently, expecting a flaw in the plumb, expecting weight or rot. Neither came. Instead, a cool, clean surge of silver-white meristem—neither plant nor animal, but the shared, primordial currency of their pocket—flowed down its narrow keel. 

Where its secondary strut had been stripped away, a pale bud swelled, split without pain, and unfolded a sleek, fresh lath of glistening cartilage, articulated and whole. Along its throat, new tiers of darkly veined soap-film unfurled, drinking in the stabilized harmonics of the quiet rooms, reading the tension of a city that was suddenly holding together on its own. And behind its ruling-pen palps, the gum-gland swelled to brimming—not with scarce, cannibalized tissue, but with an effortless, inexhaustible spool of conductive silk.

The tall thing stood suspended across thirty yards of empty air for a long, quiet minute, flexing its restored wrist. Its sensory draperies rippled—not in frantic maintenance, but in something wholly new to the vault: a slow, sweeping survey that lingered on the unbroken stoker, on the swimmers gliding harmlessly past the window-frames, on the faint, steady indigo glow pulsing softly in the sap-veins below.

With exquisite precision, the artisan leaned across the perspective-overlap. It drew a fresh, shining filament from its brimming gland—giving away nothing of its own bones—and spliced the stoker’s missing shoulder into place.

*Snick. Reeek.*

This time, when the current from the enriched dynamos flowed through the splice, it did not immediately die away. Held safe inside the windless room, nourished by the richer sap in its guy-wires, the wireframe stoker retained a faint, steady pearl-white luminescence. Its sketched chest rose in a slow, rhythmic draft of mist, and did not stop. It did not turn to heavy meat—for meat would have fallen forty feet into the dark—but something quieter and truer began to happen across the hundreds of stacked apartments. 

Within the taut, protected lattices of the tenant-sketches, gossamer planes of translucent membrane—the same weightless, electrostatic film that formed the artisans’ own throats—began to grow across the empty ribs and hollow palms, catching the indigo light like soap bubbles stretched across wire frames. They were becoming real on the city’s own terms: weightless citizens of pure tension and nerve, waking slowly into rooms made of harmonic quiet, ready at last to walk from line to line.

Down on the braided root-catwalk, nothing was out of plumb. 

The Power withdrew its pressure from the sixty-pace wall, leaving no footprint on the cables, no condensation on the stems, and no heat to wilt the crisp green angles. It left only the steady indigo twilight in the roots, the quiet indoors where the swimmers could no longer tear the sleepers, and twelve whole, unhurried architects stepping effortlessly across the dark—no longer dying to keep the blueprint intact, free at last to see what happens when a city begins to live.
