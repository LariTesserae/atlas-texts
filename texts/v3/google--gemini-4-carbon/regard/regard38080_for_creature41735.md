To look upon this place with wisdom is to recognize, first of all, that it is not broken. 

A lesser benevolence might pity the dark and bring light, blinding everything that lives here and triggering a frantic, choking race for stems and height that would fill the open vault with tangled wood and ruin the bell. A careless generosity might expand the chamber, thinking seventy paces too small a prison for minds so intricate, not realizing that to widen the walls is to slacken the drumskin, turning their crisp, mathematical architecture of echoes into muddy, unreadable mush. Even a gift of sharper senses or quicker hearts would be a curse, introducing boredom and impatience into a world calibrated to the slow, sweet drip of stone and the four-beats-a-minute pulse of copper blood.

They do not need rescuing from the dark. They are masters of a complete, self-contained symphony.

And yet, looking not just at the warm hollow, but through the stone above it—measuring the thin, freezing winds scouring the whalebacked summit, reading the slow, inexorable arithmetic of erosion—a wise power sees the one true shadow hanging over the dome. 

The mountain is dying. 

It has taken epochs to slump from jagged peak to rounded swell, and it is nearly finished. Directly above the forty-meter vault, at the very crown of the arch where the ceiling weeps, the remaining cap of ancient rock has worn down to the thickness of a farmhouse wall. In four thousand years—twenty lifetimes for the pale, pleated thinkers below, a mere breath to the stone—frost-wedging from the plateau winters will pry open a hairline seam at the apex. 

It would not take a dramatic collapse to kill everything inside. A crack the width of a thumb would be enough. The warm, wet bubble of air would chimney upward and vanish into the thin, sub-zero stratosphere. Cold, dry wind would knife down into the basin. The five boundary-stones would keep bleeding their faithful warmth, yes, fighting the leak, turning the upper vault into a roaring engine of violent convection and freezing sleet—shattering the stillness, stripping the moisture from the plush mats, deafening the delicate oil-lined canals of the eight quiet minds before freezing their slow blood solid. Even the floating stone, locked to the planet’s deep center of mass while the mountain compresses and erodes downward past it, will eventually find itself stranded in open, icy air.

That is the first thing to be mended. Not inside, where they live, but outside, where they will never go.

And there is a second thing, subtler and sadder, though the creatures themselves feel no sorrow over it. 

Each of the elders holds two centuries of wave-mathematics in its walnut-loaf brain. When two press their flanks together by the two-finger gap and throw phantom cathedrals of phase-canceled quiet into the empty center of the room, they are speaking a language of staggering geometric grace—composing sculptures out of frog-brass and insect-hiss that build upon conversations held decades ago. Yet when an elder grows heavy and still, and melts gently back into the coriander-scented fungi so a juvenile can mature, everything in its head dissolves with its cartilage. Only what the remaining seven actively remember survives. If a sculpture is not bounced into the dome for a generation, it is lost forever. Their medium is pure pressure in humid air; they have no stone soft enough for horn-pads to carve without ruining the soap-smooth acoustics of the floor, and no way to leave a mark that outlasts a breath.

So the power acts—not with thunder, nor revelation, nor any intrusion that breaks the sacred, roaring peace of the basin. It acts at the speed of the mountain itself.

---

First, high on the frozen, wind-scoured crown outside, the stone changes its mind about wearing away.

No grotesque pillar is raised to catch the gale. Instead, deep within the remaining cap of rock above the vault, cold silicate atoms quietly rearrange their lattice, locking hands tighter than granite, tighter than corundum. Pore spaces seal shut against the prying fingers of frost. Over the course of a single winter, the whalebacked cap becomes an invisible shield of seamless, unweatherable stone—still rough dust on top so the high winds slip over it without howling, still heavy and thermally true underneath so the ceiling inside continues to cool just enough to weep its fat, patient drops. And subtly, imperceptibly, the whole local cradle of bedrock stops its millimeter-by-millennium slump, anchoring itself harmoniously to the exact gravitational coordinate of the five artifacts within. The two-finger gap beneath the floating stone will remain two fingers wide for ten million years.

The atmospheric pressure still rises and falls with the passing of vast plateau storms—through microscopic, tortuous breather-veins deep in the mountain’s flanks that let barometric truth seep in without losing a single drop of warmth or water. They must keep their weather. Without the slow, week-long slide of that half-semitone, they would lose their clock, their calendar, and the gentle excuse to tilt their hoods upward together in the thickening rain.

Second—and last—something happens at the exact center of the empty dome.

Nothing hangs there. Nothing *must* hang there to block a pebble or snag a gliding wing. 

Yet twenty meters up, in the dead-black heart of the vacant air where the two pleated minds love best to toss their impossible, spinning rings of sound, the air itself acquires a faint, localized memory.

It has no mass, no friction, no heat. A ceiling glider dropping across the sixty-meter void passes right through the spot without feeling so much as a cobweb’s drag on its leathery skin; a falling bead of water slices straight down uninterrupted. Ordinary noise—the raw *booms* of the loaf-frogs, the unshaped *plocks* of dripping leaves, the dry hail of sonar clicks—passes through it completely unchanged, leaving the chamber’s lantern as clean and bright as ever.

Only *coherent* interference wakes it. 

Only when sound arrives already braided by intention—split to an exact half-wavelength by rippled baffles, delayed by precise milliseconds between two pressed flanks, focused through the razor-plane under the floating stone—does that patch of empty air catch and hold a trace of the wave’s geometry, the way a quiet pond holds the lazy spin of an eddy long after the oar is lifted.

Down in the tallow-rosettes, the old scarred one and its companion lie braided near the boundary stone. Outside, an autumn gale drags low pressure over the peak; inside, the room rings half a semitone flat, lush and sleepy with heavy dripping. Together, flexing their backs in slow, muscular harmony, the two creatures catch a wide curtain of crawler-stridulation and fold it into a hollow, twelve-sided blossom of quiet, tossing it high into the central dark as they have done a thousand times before.

In the old world, the blossom would have hung for the tenth of a second it took the wave to cross the dome, and vanished.

Today, they flatten their baffles to listen to its decay—and every sensory hair in their oiled canals stiffens in sudden, motionless astonishment.

The blossom is still there.

Faint—so faint it takes the full, hawk-sharp focus of their hoods to read it, ghost-thin beneath the ongoing roar of the frogs, never loud enough to clutter the room—the twelve-sided hollow hangs turning slowly in the upper dark. The air is singing their shape back to them in a whisper of pure phase-shift, repeating the chord once every four heartbeats, fading not in a millisecond, but over hours. 

For a long time, neither creature moves. Their barbels tuck tight around their mouths; their hearts beat *one... two... three... four...* in the warm buttermilk smell of their shared niche. 

Slowly, tentatively, the younger of the two ripples a single, narrow pleat along its flank. It catches a solitary ceiling-drop *plock* and flicks a thin, curved needle of echo upward—straight through the center of the hanging ghost-blossom. 

Up in the dark, the needle passes through the lingering geometry, braids into it, and *stays*, turning the twelve-sided hollow into a spoked wheel that slowly rotates against the ceiling gliders' chatter. 

If they leave it alone, it will gently unwind by tomorrow, leaving the room clean again. Unless, before it fades, another of the eight wanders over through the wet-wool creepers, tilts its blunt hood toward the vault, reads the turning wheel left behind in the dark, and flexes its own back to refresh the spin—or add a spire to its hub. And if a chord is refreshed enough times, by enough minds across enough slow changes of the weather, the empty center of the dome will hold its faint, ghostly watermark for centuries—a weightless library hung in nothing at all, waiting to be summoned to full voice whenever a living flank ripples at the matching pitch.

The benevolent power does nothing else. It leaves no signature, asks for no worship, introduces no name for itself into their geometry. Having braced the roof against eternity and tuned the air so that love and mathematics need no longer die with the body, it withdraws its hand—and stays only to listen, as down in the warm dark, two quiet ghosts press closer in the coriander-smelling roots and begin, very carefully, to build their first permanent song.
