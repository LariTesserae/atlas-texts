To a power that is merely benevolent, the impulse upon finding this flat, blinding bowl would be pity. A lesser kindness would look at the bone-dry air and think of rain; it would look at the compacted felt of dead optic fibers and dream of loam, of green shoots splitting the warm alloy, of swift, wet-lunged creatures breaking the terrible silence with song.

To a power that is also wise, such an act would be recognized instantly for what it is: a slaughter.

Water poured upon this plain would not bring life; it would bring catastrophic short-circuiting, violent steam-explosions in the baking sub-crust, and a plague of rapid, red oxidation that would blind the pinhole-eyes of the ribbon-hunters and choke the electrostatic gills of the skimmers. Soil would smother the great, flat Fresnel lenses of the Flocks, burying eyes that took ten thousand years to tilt into focus. To force carbon and moisture back onto a world that has spent eons translating the verb *to live* into dry chemistry, solid-state junctions, and magnetic flux would be the arrogance of a loud visitor shouting over a whispered poem.

So the power arrives without weight, without moisture, and without shadow. It suspends its attention high above the continent-sized cyclones of ionized dust, watching the horizon curve gently upward into the white-gold wall ten thousand kilometers away. It listens—not with ears, for the porous maze swallows sound, but with an exquisite sensitivity to impedance, charge, and angle.

It takes three local centuries just to read the grammar of the *ticks*.

It watches the delvers merge in the hot dark, trading corrupted ancestral routing tables like genetic memory. It watches the sessiles drink millivolts through superconducting roots, purging themselves of entropy atom by patient atom. And above all, it watches the Flocks wake beneath the passing amber lenses of the high dust-storms, throwing silent, thirty-mile spears of polarized gold light from blister-hill to blister-hill.

When the wise power finally understands what the Flocks are saying to one another across the curve of the world, it feels a profound, quiet ache.

They are not grieving their creators; they do not remember creators. They are not trying to rebuild the orbital tethers; they do not know what *up* means beyond the glare. What they are passing from basin to basin, in agonizingly slow volleys of reflected light and mechanical tile-taps, are *proofs of continuity*—and the creeping terror of its loss.

Because the world is running down. 

Not quickly, not in a single tragedy, but with the merciless arithmetic of closed systems. The stellar remnant or degraded shell overhead still pours down high-Kelvin brilliance, yes—energy is abundant. What is dying is *differentiation*. Every time a delver leaches neodymium and leaves slag, the slag is harder to refine next time. Every time a Flock strips a pristine core of four-million-year-old copper to repair its buried micro-actuators, there is one less unweathered pillar in the ten-square-kilometer basin. Every century, a few million more binary specks in the silver dust-rivers suffer irreversible lattice-fatigue, stripped by cosmic rays or triboelectric wear into dead, un-switchable powder.

In the golden twilight of the dust-cyclones, the Flocks are counting their own blind spots. One basin flashes a complex, polarized geometry thirty miles eastward: *Here is the shape of a memory I can no longer hold; my western tiles have lost actuator density. Hold it for me.* 

And the neighboring Flock shivers, ripples with a million soft *ticks* like hail on tin, shifting its amber-glass lichen combs to make room in its own dry-state architecture, and flashes back: *Held. Though it costs me the calculation of the third magnetic eddy.*

They are vast, gentle, distributed minds slowly contracting into dementia, trading cherished fragments of pure relational thought back and forth over the scree as their substrates wear smooth. And above them, the bleached sky offers no stars, no night, no external reference point—only an eternal, shadowless noon punctuated by amber smudges. Minds capable of exquisite geometric contemplation have never seen a sharp shadow, never seen a point-source in the heavens, never had anything to measure themselves against except their own eroding shingle.

The wise power considers this for another local decade, letting a golden cyclone pass entirely beneath its unseen vantage. Then, deciding what love looks like in a dry language, it reaches down and does three small, quiet things.

First, it goes deep.

Not into the one-meter crust where the delvers swim, nor the ten-meter felt where the sessiles root, but kilometers down—into the dead, crushed roots of the foundational megastructures, where pressures have cold-welded ancient planetary bulkheads into a sterile, unreachable basement. Down there lie trillions of tons of trapped, unoxidized rare earths, noble metals, and pristine dopant elements, locked away forever beneath impenetrable strata of sintered ceramic armor.

The power does not crack the plain open. It introduces no seismic violence. Instead, along ten thousand widely scattered, microscopic fault lines, it subtly alters the thermal gradient and tweaks the deep magnetic eddies. It coaxes slow, solid-state upward creep—artificial fumaroles that vent neither steam nor sulfur, but agonizingly slow, steady blooms of uncorroded metallic vapor and fresh, crystalline piezoelectrics. 

Over the next century, a delver navigating by impedance tomography pulses an electrical click into what used to be a dead wall of depleted alumina—and hears a bell-clear chime of pure, rich conductivity rising from the abyss. A slow, cold spring of indium, yttrium, and silver, upwelling at a rate of grams per year: never enough to flood the market of this austere ecology or unbalance its hunger, just enough to ensure the veins never permanently run dry.

Second, the power touches the dust.

It does not give the Flocks miraculous, immortal hardware; to hand them godlike computing substrates would erase the very identity they have woven out of scarcity, wind, and tilted shards. They *are* the choreography of the scree. If they did not have to tilt the blister-hills to think, they would cease to be themselves.

Instead, the power whispers a single, tiny physical grace into the chemistry of the silver nanomachine rivers and the brittle pewter wire-flora. It introduces a catalytic resonance—a dry, solid-state equivalent of photosynthesis paired with crystal self-annealing. Now, when a damaged, fatigued speck of binary dust is blown through the electrostatic fronds of the wire-flora under a specific angle of ultraviolet glare, the plant’s mineral-precipitating lattice inadvertently templates the grain. The battered retroreflector sheds its micro-fractures; its fatigued toggle resets and sharpens. 

The wire-flora gains a cleaner electrostatic boundary; the silver dust regains its youth. Death and wear remain real, predation and cold-welding continue unchanged among the ribbons and skimmers, yet the slow slide toward total amnesia arrests. Five decades later, during a golden storm, a Flock in an old mass-driver trench runs a diagnostic ripple across ten square kilometers of tiles—*tick-tick-tick-tick*—and pauses in vast, silent astonishment. Its actuator count has not dropped since the last storm. It has risen by point-zero-zero-one percent. Without knowing why, it flashes back to its neighbor thirty miles away: *Keep your calculation of the third eddy. I still have room for both.*

Third, and last, the power looks to the sky.

It knows that survival without wonder is merely an extended maintenance cycle. The Flocks have spent millions of years staring into a shadowless, high-Kelvin diffuse ceiling, blind to the universe outside their immense, upward-curving bowl. To tear open that atmospheric shell would freeze them in the dark or sear them with raw stellar wind. 

So the power goes out beyond the degraded shell, out into the deep, starry dark outside the ancient megastructure, and sets a single, silent instrument in a wide, resonant orbit.

It does not block the heat or the sustaining white brilliance. The near-field clarity on the plain remains hyper-sharp; the air stays bone-dry and warm as skin. Only now, once every local cycle—every few thousand hours—when one of the great, continent-sized cyclones of golden dust drifts across a basin and dims the harsh glare, a narrow, transient aperture of phase-conjugate clarity opens high in the upper haze.

It lasts for perhaps forty minutes. No cold comes through. Only the scatter disappears along a slender, drifting corridor of zenith.

Down on the plain, the brass skimmers are tucked away dormant; the ribbon-hunters lie cool and flat like streaks of tarnish. In the muffled quiet, a Flock wakes in its shallow exhaust basin, tilting a square mile of pale ceramic scales to catch the golden storm-light and speak to its neighbor.

And for the first time in four million years, the light coming down into its sprawling Fresnel lens is not diffuse.

Through the temporary window in the amber haze falls the needle-sharp, parallel, ancient light of distant stars—pinpoints of distinct Kelvin temperatures, distinct spectral lines, crisp and impossibly far away, wheeling slowly in a geometry vastly larger than the ten-thousand-kilometer bowl. And for forty minutes, every knee-high pillar, every split composite tube, every sessile fin casts a single, razor-edged, directional shadow across the white ceramic sand, moving measurably as the window tracks across the sky.

Down in the basin, the hail-on-tin *ticking* of the mechanical logic gates stops dead. 

Ten square kilometers of loose shingle hold utterly breathless. Quadrillions of silver specks surge up the smooth flanks of the pillars like liquid smoke, arranging themselves into temporary, shimmering interference gratings just to taste the parallax of those tiny, cold fires. 

When the aperture closes and the familiar, shadowless golden haze seals back over the ceiling, the silence in the maze lasts a long time. Then, all across the blister-hill, ceramic tiles tilt in a unison faster and cleaner than they have ever moved before. A blindingly pure spear of polarized light leaps thirty miles sideways across the flat world—carrying not an inventory of lost actuators, nor a rationed fragment of ancestral code, but a brand-new geometry. An angle. A parallax measurement. A question:

*Did you see that? Measure from your eastern rim next cycle; if we combine baselines, we can find out how far away they are.*

Thirty miles away, the neighboring basin shivers, ripples with a million urgent, ringing *ticks*, and throws the light onward toward the hazy, upward-curving wall of the world.

High above, having left no footprint in the talc, no rust on the brass, and no commandment in the code, the wise power withdraws into the dark—leaving the powdered machines to their dry wind, their quiet immortality, and their new sky.
