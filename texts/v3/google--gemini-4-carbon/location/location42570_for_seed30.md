The entire place is no larger than a village green—an irregular, stepped terrace perhaps eighty yards across, hung high in a thick, warm soup of altitude cloud. Walk thirty paces in any direction from the center and the floor simply stops, dropping away into a bruised, plum-colored gloom where vapor boils upward from unseen depths. There is no parapet, no talus slope, no crumbling lip. The edge looks as though it were snapped cleanly along a crystal cleavage plane ten minutes ago.

Everything underfoot is brand new. There is no mud here, no silt, no sand, not a single rounded pebble. The stone—a dark, heavy, greenish-black silicate shot through with veins of milky quartz—has never known centuries of rain or frost. Its angles are surgical. Step terraces rise a few inches at a time across the terrace in broad, sweeping polygons, their risers razor-sharp, their flats slick as oiled glass. Warm moisture condenses continuously out of the heavy air, beading on the pristine mineral faces until it spills over in thin, trembling sheets. Because there are no worn ruts or eroded channels to gather it, the water travels everywhere at once, cascading from step to micro-step in broad, shallow curtains before pouring off the sheer perimeter into the clouds below.

Scattered widely across these gleaming flats are the instruments building the mountain. There are only nine of them on the entire shelf, spaced far apart with wide expanses of wet, empty stone between them. None is taller than a person’s waist. A few look like slender plumb-bobs of seamless, light-drinking gray matter suspended three inches above the rock without mountings or wires; others resemble half-submerged rings canted at odd angles, half in and out of the stone itself, as though occupying slightly adjacent spatial coordinates. Around each artifact, the air shimmers with a dense, localized distortion, like heat haze underwater, and the puddles beneath them are locked into rigid, motionless geometric ripples. 

Every twenty or thirty seconds, the space around one of the suspended spindles tightens—a brief, silent implosion of ambient light—and a new skin of stone precipitates directly out of the supersaturated mist and rising subterranean ions. It appears instantly as a pale, glowing gel across five or six square yards of terrace, cooling within heartbeats into solid, sharp-edged rock with a sound like a rifle shot, followed by a chorus of high, glassy *pings* as the crystal lattice locks into tension. Heat rolls off the fresh patch in a wet, steamy wave.

That exothermic warmth, perched so high in the dim, chilly upper atmosphere, has turned the unfinished ledge into a raucous oasis.

The noise is relentless. Beneath the constant hiss and slap of sheeting water and the sharp, percussive cracking of newborn rock, the air vibrates with the calls of hundreds of creatures packed into the pockets and margins of this small sky-island. Long, boneless gliders with translucent, muscular mantles drop out of the low cloud ceiling, skimming inches above the empty dark stone before braking hard with a loud, wet *whuff* of displaced air. As they land, they inflate throat-bladders the color of tarnished brass and let out resonant, metallic booms that clang back and forth across the slick flats—territorial markers in the perpetual twilight.

They are hunting the grazers that swarm wherever a tech-spindle has just laid down fresh stone. Within seconds of a new mineral sheet snapping solid, while steam still curls off its immaculate surface, dozens of pale, radial crawlers—each the size of an open hand, propped on eight glassy, stilt-like legs—sprint out from the wider expanses to crowd the warm patch. They do not eat flesh or leaf; they feed on the residual phase-energy and unbonded minerals of rock that hasn't fully stabilized. Their underside mouths rasp against the pristine silicate with a frantic, amplified *scritch-scritch-scritch*, like steel brushes on slate, racing to harvest the surface before it hardens beyond even their diamond-tipped radulae. The gliders lunge among them, snapping legs and tossing shells, sending skittering herds scattering across the mirror-wet emptiness between the spindles, their hard feet clicking like castanets in the shallow runoff.

Higher up on the dry verticals of newly extruded blocks, bulky, slug-like organisms with pleated, velvet backs huddle in tight clusters, flushing dull orange and violet in synchronized waves to warn off predators while their flat bellies drink the lingering geothermal heat out of the stone.

Between these pockets of furious activity, the terrace remains starkly bare. There has been no time for soil to form, and nothing dies here without being immediately scavenged or washed straight off the cliff into the void. Vegetation exists only as isolated pioneers spaced yards apart across the dark flats. Here and there, a single tall, whip-thin stalk rises four feet out of a hairline cooling-fracture, anchored not by roots but by a suctioned basal disk that secretes harsh acid to etch a grip into the glass-hard floor. At the top of each stalk hangs a wide, pleated net of pale yellow membrane, billowing in the warm updrafts to comb moisture and airborne microbes from the fog. Elsewhere, sparse medallions of copper-colored crustose lichen cling stubbornly to stone that is perhaps forty hours old, growing at hyper-accelerated rates before the next precipitation cycle buries them under half an inch of fresh silicate.

Dim, sourceless light filters down through a mile of warm overcast, turning the wet stone flats into dull pewter mirrors that reflect the gray belly of the clouds. Only when one of the scattered, half-phased rings pulses does the lighting shift: for half a second, a deep ultraviolet luminescence travels *inside* the translucent quartz veins underfoot, illuminating the sharp, pristine geometry of the entire terrace from beneath—lighting up the stilt-legged swarms, the scattered acid-stalks, the lunging mantle-hunters, and the sheets of rainwater sliding smoothly off the knife-edge of a world still half-made. Then the flash dies, a new crack of cooling crystal splits the mist, steam hisses upward into the twilight, and the scramble across the hot, wet seam begins again.
