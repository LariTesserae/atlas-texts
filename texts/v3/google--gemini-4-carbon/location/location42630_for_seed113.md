The chamber is the size of a great nave, dropped forty meters below the planetary crust, and almost entirely hollow. There are no pillars, no spanning bridges, no cables or gantries crossing the interior air. The emptiness in the center of the room feels pressurized by its own geometry—a rectangular prism of warm, wet, dim space, bounded on all four vertical sides by sheer walls that look as though they were sliced out of the native black bedrock last week. 

Where the stone shows through, its fractures have the glassy, razor-sharp edges of freshly cleaved obsidian, entirely unsoftened by grit or age. Nothing here has had time to wear down. Yet the walls are not rock faces so much as vertical streets turned inward. Recessed flush into the stone is a dense, stacked matrix of urban infrastructure: apertures, cantilevered sills, seamless black-glass bays, transit locks, and terraced utility housings, packed together with the tight organizational logic of a city core. There are no visible bolts, welds, or mortar lines. The built surfaces meet the raw, jagged rock with sub-millimeter precision, as if the architecture had been grown into the stone at the molecular level or phase-shifted directly into place, leaving sharp, crisp ninety-degree corners everywhere.

Because the central volume is completely unobstructed, sound has nowhere to die. The room is loud—a continuous, churning roar of water and thermal exchange that bounces off the hard metamaterials and fresh stone until the air itself vibrates. High near the ceiling, warm exhaust vapor vents silently from hairline seams in the upper tiers, meeting cold thermal-sink plates set into the crown of the vault. The resulting condensation does not drift down as mist; it gathers rapidly into heavy sheets and falls like sudden, localized rain, hammering onto lower ledges, sluicing down vertical channels, and pouring over the lip of recessed balconies in white, echoing ribbons. 

Where the falling water approaches certain open bays or walkways, it strikes invisible deflection fields six inches from the thresholds, flattening in midair with a sharp, continuous hiss and sheeting sideways into drainage cuts. The smell is intense: hot wet slate, ozone from the boundary fields, and the thick, green odor of bruised chlorophyll.

In this warm, dim bath, plants have taken hold with explosive speed. Broad, waxy leaves the size of shields push out from narrow drainage seams and hang over the void.Thick curtains of bright green hepatic mosses and fast-climbing vines spill down the dark composite facades, rooting in nothing more than wet dust and the microscopic texture of heat-dissipating panels. The light they grow by is low and shadowless, a dull amber-violet luminescence emitted directly by strips of structural stone that have been doped to glow, supplemented by the faint blue flicker of status fields deep inside the recessed rooms. It should not be enough light for such lushness, yet the foliage thrives, drinking the waste wavelengths of the machinery beneath it.

Look closely at any leaf, and the strangeness of the greenery becomes clear. Not a single frond is chewed. There are no pinholes from beetles, no curled edges hiding spiders, no slime trails on the wet composite sills, no midges hovering in the warm updrafts. When a heavy droplet hits a broad leaf, nothing startles from the underside. Fallen plant matter collects in the sharp corners of granite steps and does not rot in the usual way; without worms, mites, or scavengers to break it down, dead stems simply mat together in the warm runoff until the periodic surge of an automated sluice gate flushes the channels clean with a booming rush of water down into the floor grates. The greenery is loud with water, constantly trembling and bobbing under the indoor rain, utterly alone with itself.

At the bottom of the chamber, the floor is a flat dark basin crisscrossed by shallow, geometric canals just centimeters deep, cut with ruler-straight edges into the stone. Water sheets across the dark expanses between the canals in thin, warm films that reflect the dim amber walls above. Every few minutes, a low sub-audible hum passes through the bedrock—some vast subsurface system cycling—and the standing water across the entire floor shivers into precise, interlocking standing waves, millions of tiny crisp pyramids of water rising for three seconds and then collapsing back into flat runoff at once.

Up along the walls, the urban machinery goes about its business behind smooth, dark membranes. Vents iris open and shut without visible hinges. Occasionally a transit capsule—a smooth, featureless lozenge of matte gray—slides silently down a vertical recess in the cliff-face, held inches away from the wet rock by locked magnetic or gravitational shear, pushing a bow-wave of humid air ahead of it that sets the hanging vines thrashing for twenty meters on either side. Behind the flush transparent panes, interior spaces sit dimly lit, clean, arranged in stacked tiers of corridors and bays, holding warmth and order inside the dripping rock, facing out onto fifty meters of empty, raining air.
