The entire ward occupies a brick-lined shaft no wider than a cathedral nave and perhaps four stories deep, sealed overhead by a low, vaulted ceiling of ribbed iron and fouled glass. Almost the whole volume is filled with warm, mineral-heavy water. Only a palm’s breadth of humid, lifeless air remains trapped beneath the highest arches of the vault, stagnant and dark; everywhere below that thin skin of surface, the space is liquid, bath-warm, and packed almost to immobility with machinery and leaves.

There is no open water to speak of. What might once have been narrow alleys or maintenance shafts between the workshops have long since been colonized by overlapping tiers of infrastructure and thick, tropical-warmth flora. Cast-iron gantries intersect at odd angles, their undersides hung with porcelain bell-insulators and bundled lead-sheathed cables as thick as a man’s thigh. Around these, broad-leaved ribbon weeds rise in dense, undulating curtains, their olive and burgundy fronds pressing flat against the glass faces of pressure gauges and wrapping tightly around brass valve-wheels. Between the structural columns sit the housings of the engines themselves—squat, terraced structures of glazed tile and bolted steel, stacked one atop another like cramped tenements, leaving scarcely enough room for a diver’s shoulders to pass between a vibrating boiler-wall and a counterweight shaft.

Nothing moves quickly here, though almost everything is running.

A massive spoked flywheel, twenty feet tall and furred along its rim with short, velvety green moss, rotates at the pace of a clock’s minute hand. As each spoke descends, it parts a thicket of feathery hornwort without tearing a single stem; the plants simply lean aside in the gentle displacement and drift back once the iron has passed. Nearby, long bronze piston rods slide in and out of packed stuffing-boxes, completing a single stroke every three or four minutes. Where the metal enters the seal, it is wiped bright and mirror-clean, the only unweathered surfaces in a world otherwise softened by decades of settled accretion. Everything stationary wears a rind of stabilized rust, black magnetite, and pale calcareous scale that rounds off the hex-heads of bolts and welds flange to flange, holding the whole intricate tangle in a state of calcified permanence.

The warmth radiates directly from the lower casings—deep-seated transformers filled with heavy insulating oil, and banks of galvanic cells the size of small rooms. You feel the heat changes as slow, lazy thermoclines brushing the skin as you move a few feet down or sideways: pocketed currents of ninety-degree water rising from copper busbars, lifting rafts of tiny, translucent bladder-ferns toward the ceiling until they cool and sink slowly down the masonry walls again.

Because the water is everywhere, sound is stripped of sharp edges. An industrial district that in dry air would roar and clatter operates here in a state of cushioned, velvet near-silence. When a heavy relay throws inside an oil-filled drum, it registers only as a soft, muffled *thump* felt primarily in the sternum. The great worm-gears turn without a squeal, lubricated by silt and mucilage from the plants. The dominant sound, if you hold still among the cable-vines to listen for it, is a faint, dry fizzing—millions of microscopic hydrogen and oxygen pearls forming along energized copper plates, detaching with a whisper, and threading upward through the dense canopy of leaves like slow, inverted snow.

Light has to fight to get past three yards. Nothing enters through the silted vault overhead. Instead, illumination seeps outward in small, localized pools from the hardware itself. Thick marine-glass blisters set into control panels glow a murky, amber orange from low-wattage filaments that never seem to burn out. Deeper in the tangle, where open-frame coils regulate the galvanic current, a faint, steady violet-blue corona clings to the ceramic cores, turning the surrounding water the color of diluted ink and casting sharp, layered shadows of fern-fronds and pipe-joints onto the slimed brickwork inches away. Beyond those small halos, the alleys dissolve immediately into warm, green-black gloom.

In the weird, settled chemistry of the basin, machine and marsh have reached a peculiar consensus. Thick mats of reddish root-hair feed directly on the iron oxide flaking from turbine shells, preventing further corrosion by sealing the metal under living felt. Certain pale, fleshy tubers grow only where stray electrical currents leak into the mortar, arranging themselves in neat radial rings that map invisible voltage gradients across the walls. Overhead, flexible rubber hoses pulse almost imperceptibly as peristaltic pumps move dense brine from sump to cistern, looking indistinguishable from the swollen, gas-filled stems of the water-hyacinths braided around them.

Animal life is scarce, unobtrusive, and entirely fitted to the tight geometry. Along the warm, vibrating tops of the transformer housings, a few flat-bodied, whiskerless catfish lie motionless for hours, their skin mottled to match blistered paint and lime scale. Now and then, a translucent snail the size of a walnut glides across the convex face of an ammeter, grazing a clean path through the brown diatoms so the needle behind the glass—trembling steadily at half-load—becomes briefly readable. Less often, something long, ribbon-thin, and pale slips out of a flooded exhaustmanifold, undulates unhurriedly through the teeth of a stationary gear, and vanishes into the roots, barely disturbing the hanging silt.

At the perimeter, the cut-stone retaining walls stop everything cleanly. Heavy bulkhead doors—riveted, wheel-locked, and drifted halfway up their hinges in fine, dark sediment—mark old passages that lead nowhere open now. Whatever city or surface once required the output of this sunken exchange has sealed its conduits or forgotten them, yet the circuit remains closed within the room itself: heat feeding the weeds, decaying weeds enriching the electrolyte bath, the bath driving the cells, the cells turning the slow, mossy wheels through another patient, muffled century.
