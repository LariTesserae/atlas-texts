From the rim of the shallow basin, under a clear and ordinary afternoon sun, the expanse looks at first like a crowded provincial capital caught in an earthquake that refuses to end. Roofs pitch and lock, avenues open like fissures and seal shut again, towers heave themselves three stories above the roofline and shear off at the base in clouds of pale dust. It covers perhaps four miles from edge to edge, laid out across the low depression in blocks, wards, stepped terraces, and viaducts. 

There is no stone in it. There is no brick, no mortar, no iron, no glass, and beneath it all, no dirt.

The entire valley is a single, bottomless mattress of living vegetable mass, and the city is what it is doing.

The noise hits long before the first perimeter wall. It is physical, a continuous, percussive roar that vibrates in the teeth and flattens thought. Up close, the roar resolves into millions of distinct structural violences happening at once: the cannon-crack of heartwood splitting under sudden tension, the high, wet shriek of bark planing against bark at speed, the drumroll of seed-casings machine-gunning down hollow colonnades, and beneath everything, a deep hydraulic thrumming as tons of pressurized sap slam through arterial trunks to inflate new architecture. To stand at the edge of a street is to stand inside a lumber mill the size of a county running at full tilt without blades or men.

Everything is sharp, green-broken, and raw. Nothing has had time to weather or rot. Where a three-story facade of tightly braided lianas has just torn itself loose from its neighbors, the snapped fibers gleam pale yellow and wet in the bright sunlight, edges crisp enough to slice skin. The pavement underfoot consists of flattened, hexagonal buttress-roots locked together under immense lateral pressure, their surfaces warm to the touch—almost feverish with metabolic heat, hovering near blood temperature. As new roots wedge upward from the dark, compacted mat below, old pavers buckle, snap with the report of rifle fire, and are instantly swallowed by fast-creeping callus tissue that smooths the thoroughfare back into level grade within minutes.

The dampness here is entirely internal, sweated out by the city’s exertion. The sky above is dry and bright, casting crisp, dark shadows across the plazas, yet the shadowed alleys slicken with guttation. Clear, warm fluid beads along the serrated cornices of leaf-shingled roofs and runs down grooved pilasters in sudden, steaming trickles. Where a structural bole ruptures under the weight of an upper story, pressurized xylem-water hisses out in a fine, sweet-smelling mist that hangs for twenty seconds in the warm air before thirsty aerial rootlets whip out from the nearest wall to drink it dry. Puddles of tacky, amber resin collect in the hollows of newly formed staircases, hardening into glassy amber lenses before the stairs themselves twist and fold inward.

What makes the mind slip is not merely the speed of the growth, nor its architectural mimicry, but the way space and category have broken down inside the bloom. 

The plants are not overgrowing an older settlement; there was never anything here to copy. Instead, the flora is afflicted with an orthogonal fever, a runaway morphogenetic compulsion toward municipality. Cambium grows in strict ninety-degree corners. Broad, leathery leaves stiffen with silica and arrange themselves into louvered shutters that bang open and shut in windless air, hinged on pulvinus joints pumping potassium ions back and forth in frantic, pointless rhythms. Hollow bamboo-like culms bundle themselves into load-bearing Doric shafts, flare into capitals of rigid thorns, and throw vaulted ceilings across avenues forty feet wide.

And where the growth pushes hardest, geometry gives way.

Down a sunlit side street, a row of tenement-like facades repeats with uncanny, identical precision—the same arched doorway, the same cantilevered balcony of woven rattan, the same four rectangular window-gaps—not because the shoots grew identically, but because the space itself has stuttered. Drop a broken twig on the threshold of the first doorway, and it appears simultaneously on the thresholds of the next six down the lane, trembling identically as the floorboards heave. Look at a corner building too long as it swells upward, and its two walls fail to agree on where they meet: from the left, it is a four-story watchtower; from the right, a sunken courtyard. Walk around the corner and there is no transition—one moment your hand brushes hot, ridged bark rising forty feet overhead, and the next stride places you at the lip of an open pit lined with geometric boxwood bleachers, without having crossed a threshold.

Grafts occur across empty air. A thick, cable-like tendril whips out from an upper cornice, stops dead in the bright daylight six feet above the street, and terminates in a clean cross-section—only to emerge out of empty air thirty yards away, seamlessly fused into a bridge abutment, sap pulsing steadily across the missing interval. Shadows fall in the wrong directions around the fastest-growing spires, lagging minutes behind the structures that cast them, or pooling darkly on the pavement before the arch overhead has even finished knitting itself together to block the sun. Inside the windowless ground-floor rooms—visible only when a wall splits open in a shower of fresh splinters—corridors spiral inward far deeper than the building’s exterior footprint allows, lined with dry, papery doors that open onto solid walls of green pith.

Nothing walks these streets. There are no birds on the ridges, no beetles boring into the fresh timber, no ants marching along the sap-slick gutters. In the deepest, warmest pockets of dampness where two root-walls grind together, blind, colorless nematodes writhe in the fermented slime, and occasionally a flat, motionless scale-organism clings to the underside of a balustrade, indistinguishable from a blister on the bark. Otherwise, the entire deafening metropolis is empty of eyes and legs. Broad boulevards widen themselves for traffic that does not exist; stepped marketplaces terrace the central depression, throwing up awning-leaves over stalls that immediately crush themselves into pulp to feed the eruption of a fresh aqueduct.

At the center of the basin, where the density peaks, five- and six-story masses crowd so tightly together that their upper canopies fuse into a continuous, heaving roofscape, perforated by light-wells that snap open and iris shut like pupils. Down in that warm, bright-slashed grid, whole city blocks undergo violent urban renewal every quarter hour. A plaza of white, bone-hard root-tiles bulges upward like a blister, splits with an ear-splitting *crump*, and vents a geyser of fibrous chaff and hot humidity; out of the breach, a colossal, multi-chamberedrotunda of buttress-wood jacks itself into the sunlight, clicking and ratcheting as thorny cross-beams lock into slots, its upper galleries already sprouting railings, drain-spouts, and gabled dormers—even as its eastern flank loses structural coherence, forgets how three-dimensional volume works, flattenings momentarily into a sheer, paper-thin silhouette against the sky before collapsing into a roaring avalanche of green kindling that the street-mat immediately begins to digest and pave over.

The sun hangs clear and indifferent over the low valley rim. The air smells sharply of crushed green stems, hot sap, turpentine, and sliced cucumber. Block after block, mile after mile, the empty town tears itself down and builds itself back at the top of its lungs.
