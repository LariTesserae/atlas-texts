The basin holds a broad, sunken town where the water never cools below blood heat. There is no sky directly accessible from the streets—only the heavy, ink-black press of thirty fathoms, though near the absolute ceiling of this world, where the tallest towers reach toward the distant surface, the water thins to a bruised, impenetrable slate. Down in the avenues and the plazas, the darkness is nearly absolute, broken only by the pale, drifting tallow-glow of bacterial mats and the sudden, silent flash of a passing flank.

Nothing here was built with iron, mortar, or saw. The city is an ancient, biological accretion, coaxed and calcified over centuries before whatever drowned it completed its work, or perhaps built entirely beneath the tide by hands that needed no tools. The architecture consists of tightly packed, multi-story vaults, ribbed arcades, and porous towers formed from fused limestone, river-clay, and the dead, stony skeletons of massive freshwater bryozoans. Centuries of warm, slow currents have licked every corner smooth. Doorways have no lintels or hinges; they are oval apertures worn soft as soapstone. Bridges arch over sunken canals—canals within an ocean—their balustrades eroded into continuous, undulating ridges that feel like old bone to the touch. 

Because the water is thick with suspended silt and organic snow, the city feels crowded even before its inhabitants are accounted for. The buildings lean close together, creating narrow,vertical canyons and tiered courtyards layered one atop the other. There is very little green here; light cannot reach down to feed true leaves. Instead, a sparse, stubborn flora clings to the upper cornices and the rims of sunken wells: pale, chlorophyll-starved creepers as thick as ropes, leafless and waxy, and scattered clusters of heat-loving, rust-colored sponges that filter the murky broth. 

Yet the streets are heavily populated. Life crowds every alcove, stairwell, and rafters-space, thriving in the warm, pitch-black shelter of the masonry. 

Through the wide, unlit thoroughfares drift immense, velvety-skinned rays and blind, yard-long catfish, their sensitive barbels trailing along the worn cobbles like fingers reading script. They move in slow, stacked lanes of traffic, curving seamlessly through first-floor windows and emerging from second-story balconies. Every wall is densely colonized. Thousands of pale, thumb-sized crustaceans click softly across the stucco, grazing on invisible films of algae. In the deeper interiors—rooms once meant for storage or sleep, now choked with drifts of fine, warm mud—tangles of translucent eels knot and unknot in silent, rhythmic pulses. Large, soft-shelled turtles the size of millstones rest stacked on tiered amphitheater steps, occasionally pushing off into the black water to rise unseen toward the distant air, returning hours later to settle back into the exact hollows their shells have worn into the stone.

For all this movement, the place is profoundly hushed. Water swallows vibration quickly here, muffled by the silt and the porous, labyrinthine walls. There is no roar of surf, no wind, no clatter. Sound exists only in intimate, localized textures: the dry, faint *tick-tick* of claws on calcified brick; the low, hydraulic thump of a large predator turning sharply in a narrow hallway, displacing a heavy cushion of water against the walls; the occasional, resonant groan of an ancient archway finally settling another fraction of an inch into the riverbed. 

There is an order to the motion, a city-wide metabolism governed by thermal vents hidden beneath the central plazas. Warmth wells up from the cellar-grates and cisterns in invisible, rising plumes. You feel the landscape change temperature as you drift from block to block—passing from tepid, stagnant alleys into sudden, bath-warm updrafts that carry the faint, sulfurous scent of deep earth and blooming decay. These plumes act as invisible chimneys and highways. Clouds of tiny, blind shrimp ride the warm currents upward past the darkened tiers of apartments, and waiting on the smooth, eroded window ledges are rows of patient, jawless ambush-feeders, swaying in unison like dark grass, snapping silently into the rising stream.

Occasionally, a faint, cold downdraft pushes in from the basin's unseen rim, rolling down the terraced hills of the outer districts like fog. When the cooler water hits the warm streets, the entire city shifts. The rays drop lower to hug the heated paving stones; the eels retract into the mortar-less joints of the walls; a million tiny shells clamp shut in a wave of soft, cascading clicks that travels from neighborhood to neighborhood, leaving the dark avenues momentarily empty, suspended in the warm, drifting silt, before the quiet traffic resumes.
