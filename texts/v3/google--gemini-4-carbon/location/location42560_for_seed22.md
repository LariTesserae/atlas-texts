You feel the city in your sternum three miles before you see it, and even when you are standing in the middle of its central avenue, there is almost nothing there to strike with a thrown stone.

The land itself is a wide, shallow subsidence—a lowland basin twenty miles across where the crust pulled apart too recently for wind or water to have softened the mistake. Down at the true floor, the dark vitrified rock is all razor-edged steps, fresh cleavage planes, and raw basaltic splinters that will slice a boot sole cleanly in two. There is no soil down there, no sand, no silt rounded by old streams. Every fracture looks as though it snapped yesterday afternoon. Yet the air pooled in this low bowl is thick, balmy, and close, the warmth of an exhale held under a blanket, beneath an overcast sky the color of bruised pewter that never brightens past a deep, murky twilight.

Into this wide, dim hollow, someone—or some succession of post-industrial minds—has strung an entire municipality out of little more than geometry and noise.

If you gathered all the physical matter of the settlement and compressed it into a heap, it might scarcely fill a single courtyard. Instead, it spans the basin for miles in vast, open intervals of near-total emptiness. What serves for streets, residential tiers, conduits, and towers are hair-thin filaments of dark, non-baryonic matter and wafer-sheer membranes of arrested phase-space, spaced hundreds of yards apart across the gloom. A "building" here is four shimmering corner-lines no thicker than spider silk rising three hundred feet into the murk, holding between them horizontal planes of pure, localized surface tension. You can look straight through twelve city blocks at once; the infrastructure registers only as faint, refractive hairline grids and distant knots of cold violet pinpricks suspended in the warm void. Between one elevated thoroughfare and the next yawns an immense gulf of empty air. 

What holds the city rigid—what gives those invisible floors the stiffness to bear weight, and what pumps power across miles of nothing—is sound. 

It is absolute, inescapable, and physically crushing. From deep within the fresh fissures of the basin floor, buried emitters drive colossal standing waves upward into the warm atmosphere, locking with aerial nodes to form an acoustic-gravitational scaffold. The air roars at a pitch just above and just below human tolerance simultaneously: a titanic, ceaseless chord like ten thousand brass turbines grinding against a cathedral organ, layered with the tearing-silk shriek of high-frequency containment shears. Your ribs hum with it. Your vision blurs slightly at the edges until your inner ear surrenders. There is no silence anywhere in the valley; silence would mean structural collapse. Where two pressure waves intersect in midair, the empty atmosphere grows hard enough to walk on, shimmering faintly like heat haze over asphalt, vibrating so violently that dust motes cannot settle—they are instantly atomized or flung outward into the wide dark gaps.

And because the sound shakes the warm air so mercilessly, the valley sweats.

There are no rivers here, no ponds, not even puddles in the sharp-toothed rock at the bottom. Moisture never gets the chance to pool. Instead, whenever drifting humid air crosses an acoustic node, the pressure wave wrings the vapor out of suspension instantly. Water appears out of nowhere in trembling, midair beads, suspended in geometric lattices of mist, or slicking the hairline architectural filaments in sudden, warm films of condensation. Every few minutes, a harmonic shift sweeps across a district like a passing squall, and for ten seconds the empty space between towers fills with millions of shivering droplets hanging motionless in three-dimensional grids before evaporating back into dampness or sliding down invisible walls.

It is on this suspended dampness, and on the city’s ghostly skeleton, that the jungle feeds.

Lushness here has abandoned weight entirely. Thick woody trunks would tear through the gossamer infrastructure or plummet straight to the jagged stone below, so the flora has evolved—or been tailored—to match the hollowness of the basin. Miles of sweeping, extravagant greenery drape the invisible avenues and cascade down the hairline struts, yet almost all of it is one cell thick, hollow-chambered, or inflated with warm hydrogen. Enormous, translucent emerald fronds sixty feet long unroll across empty space like bolts of wet silk, weighing less than an ounce, drinking the acoustic mist directly through millions of vibrating microscopic hairs. Great, billowing curtains of chartreuse bladder-vine hang between residential grids half a mile apart, pulsing slowly in the dim light, their skins so thin you can see the faint violet city-nodes shining right through twenty layers of foliage. 

It creates an optical impossibility: wherever you look across the broad plain of the basin, the view is riotous with overhanging leaves, coiled tendrils, weeping mist-ferns, and swollen, lantern-sized seedpods—a dense, tropical profusion in silhouette—yet ninety-nine percent of the volume around you remains uninterrupted air. A falling tool drops straight through four lush canopies, tearing harmlessly through gossamer leaf-sheets that seal themselves behind it by capillary action, plunging through empty dark halls of warm atmosphere before shattering on the fresh, sharp stone far beneath.

Nothing about this emptiness is quiet or still, because the animals have learned to use both the physics and the din.

The air between the distant structures is crowded with motion. Because screaming to be heard over the infrastructure’s roar would burst small lungs, half the fauna has joined the chorus instead, using their own bodies as resonators to surf the standing waves, while the rest hunt in frantic flashes of cold bioluminescence. Swarms of yard-long, ribbon-bodied gliders—more fin than flesh, pale and damp as cave salamanders—launch themselves off elevated transit-membranes and ride the invisible acoustic troughs across half-mile chasms, undulating through the gloom in hundreds. Huge, brittle-shelled arthropods with bodies hollow as violins cling to the mono-filament power lines, flaring acoustic baffles along their backs to saw at the standing waves; their territorial calls add a deafening, metallic clatter like sheet-iron hail on top of the city’s bass thunder, shaking loose showers of warm condensation that lower-tier epiphytes immediately drink.

Predators work the phase-boundaries. Long, whip-tailed things with suction-cupped limbs sprint upside-down beneath the invisible ceilings of deserted plazas, entirely silhouetted against the dark sky above, waiting for gas-bloated herbivores that drift lazily along the mist-currents grazing on hanging sheets of aero-moss. When a kill happens a quarter-mile away across the void, you don't hear the snap of jaws—the valley’s roar swallows all small violences—you only see a sudden burst of frantic cyan light-signaling from disturbed foliage, and a brief, drifting snow of torn green membrane settling slowly downward until an updraft catches it.

Out toward the edges of the settlement, where the urban grid thins into isolated, mile-apart needle-pylons and the jagged young rock rises slightly toward the basin rim, the mechanics of the place show their seams. Here, the standing waves fray. A massive, translucent canopy-vine that grew too far past the boundary of an acoustic field hangs limp and collapsed over sharp black crags, suddenly subjected to ordinary gravity it was never built to bear. Along those margins, the ground is wet with real runoff—trickles of warm, mineral-free water sliding down unweathered stone cracks that have not yet learned how to be streambeds, vanishing underground before they can gather into a creek.

Looking back from that raw rim across the lowland, the whole expanse lies open in the perpetual dusk: twenty miles of roaring, trembling gloom, stitched together by faint hairlines of impossible engineering, draped in vast, weightless curtains of wet green life that billow and thrash in a wind made entirely of sound, suspended above a sharp dark floor that almost nothing ever touches.
