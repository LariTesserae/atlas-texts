The basin is no wider than a cathedral’s nave and perhaps twice as long—a fresh collapse in a plateau of dark, vitreous stone. Nothing here has had time to round off. The boundary cliffs drop in clean, conchoidal fractures, their edges still sharp enough to slice leather, surrounding a sunken floor of split slate and black grit. There has never been rain to dull them. The air pooled in this low hollow is cool, bone-dry, and tastes faintly of flint and ozone. Above the rim, the sky is a permanent, lidded slate-grey, casting a shadowless twilight into the pit so even that the depth of the drop is hard to judge.

Step to the edge, and sound stops.

It is not merely quiet; the basin is an acoustic sink. A stone kicked over the lip vanishes into the dimness and shatters against the talus below without returning so much as a click. The culprit fills the pit from wall to wall: a dense, rigorously organized metropolis made almost entirely of empty space.

From the floor rise hundreds of slender towers, terraced ziggurats, tiered plazas, and multi-level causeways, laid out on a strict polygonal grid. There are clear boulevards, narrow alleys, bridged intersections, and cantilevered overhangs—all the unmistakable zoning and layered infrastructure of an urban center, compressed into a few acres of sunken rock. Yet the entire city weighs perhaps no more than a drift of dry leaves. Every wall, arch, and buttress is an open-work truss of chalk-white, dry-spun mineral foam, thinner than blown glass and riddled with microscopic pores. You can look straight through six blocks of high-rises and still see the sharp black basalt cliff on the far side, only slightly dimmed by the intervening haze of white geometry. The porous lattice drinks vibration out of the cool air, shredding sound waves before they can travel a yard.

Despite the tomb-like hush, the city is frantically, ceaselessly busy.

Everything in the basin is in motion, driven by thermal drafts and sharp electrostatic gradients that play between the fresh volcanic rock and the dry atmosphere. Ribbons of pale grey dust and sulfur-colored spores stream along the gridded avenues like silent traffic, banked around corners by curved baffle-walls built expressly to steer the current. Across the upper tiers, old districts are actively coming undone. Without moisture to bind them, aged struts oxidize in the cool air, turning brittle and flaking away in sudden, silent avalanches of white ash. A whole five-story colonnade will buckle, dissolve into powder mid-fall, and be swept instantly down a sunken boulevard toward the central plazas—a demolition without a whisper.

And just as fast, new city rises to replace it.

In the dimness, you have to watch the intersections carefully to spot the builders, because there are scarcely two dozen of them in the entire settlement, and they are nearly as sparse as their architecture. Each is the size of a greyhound, though built like a radial frame of dry, jointed quill-bone, walking on six stilt-legs that span the wide gaps between floor-beams with mechanical precision. They have no wet tissues exposed to the parching air; their carapaces are matte, papery, and dry as mica. Moving at a brisk, restless pace along a collapsing viaduct, one of the creatures presses a cluster of abdominal spinnerets against a raw basalt joint and draws back. A thread of milky, volatile resin stretches out, foaming as its non-aqueous solvent flashes instantly into gas in the chill air, freezing within seconds into a rigid, razor-sharp beam of white silicate pumice. With rapid, rhythmic nods of its whole body, the builder weaves cross-bracing, lays down a gridded roadway, and raises a serrated parapet—extruding thirty feet of fresh urban infrastructure in a matter of minutes before trotting silently off down a side street.

The city is too vast for so few inhabitants because it is not shelter; it is an externalized stomach and lung. In a world without water, life runs on dry static and airborne grit. The high, hollow spires act as electrostatic combs, stripping charge from the dry wind that spills over the sharp cliff edges; in the dim light, faint, cold violet coronas occasionally race down the spines of the tallest towers, completely noiseless, making the porous white struts glow from within for half a second. That charge draws fine mineral dust and drifting organic powder out of the air, funneling it down terraced chutes and covered aqueducts that have never known a drop of liquid, sorting the particles by weight at baffled street corners until rich, powdery drifts collect in the lower vaults.

Sparse vegetation clings to the margins of this civic machine, opportunistic and equally dry. Anchored in the knife-sharp crevices of the basalt walls and along the older, un-demolished foundation piers grow scattered clumps of wire-ferns and brittle, rust-red fan-lichens. They have no sap. Their fronds are crisp sheets of metallic-looking scale, held under dry tension. As the invisible gusts of spore-dust and static sweep down the avenues, whole rows of these rust-colored fans snap open and shut in twitching, synchronized waves, trapping charged dust against their powdery undersides. When a builder passes too close, the plants fold flat against the stone like sprung traps, swift and entirely mute.

Down in the central plaza—a wide, sunken octagon paved in sharp hexagonal tiles of secreted bone-foam—two of the stilt-limbed residents meet. Between them, a whirlwind of shed architectural ash spins tightly, corralled by low, curved wind-walls until it settles into a neat cone at the center of the court. One creature lowers a dry, fluted proboscis into the pile, inhaling the recycled mineral flour of yesterday’s collapsed tenements to fuel the secretion of tomorrow’s towers. Around them, shadows don't move, because the dim overcast above never shifts, yet the skyline changes by the quarter-hour: spires fraying into upward-drifting smoke on the windward side of town, fresh white lattices knitting swiftly into the void on the leeward, red lichens snapping at passing sparks, and dust rivers banking smoothly down silent, empty streets.
