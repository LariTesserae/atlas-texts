You hear the basin miles before the rock opens to reveal it, felt first through the soles of the feet as a continuous, percussive tremor, like standing on the casing of an immense turbine. When the basalt fault finally widens into the subterranean valley, the noise hits as a physical wall—a sustained, dry, roaring crackle that forces the jaw shut and vibrates the sternum. There is no echo here, despite the miles-wide span of the sunken ceiling, because there is almost no bare stone left to bounce a wave, and no moisture in the warm, static-charged air to soften it.

Everything is dry, and everything is growing at the speed of a brushfire.

The valley floor is a plunging, five-mile trough of newly split crust—raw, unweathered gabbro and jagged obsidian shelves that have never known a drop of rain or the smoothing touch of wind. Where the stone fractures under tectonic tension, the heat and piezoelectric charge of the splitting rock feed the *tinder-flora*. There is no soil and no sap. Instead, vast, interlocking matrices of silicate-barked trunks and fibrous, parchment-like fronds erupt directly from the fresh fault lines, drawing sulfur, carbon, and thermal energy straight from the stressed stone. You can watch a ridge of black glass crack open and, within minutes, see pale, fluted stalks thrust upward like telescoping bone, unfurling broad, rasping fans of mica-flecked cellulose that immediately begin rubbing against their neighbors.

That friction is the engine of the light. Millions of overlapping canopies, stretching across the subterranean expanse like a tangled, multi-tiered forest, grind together in the turbulent thermal updrafts. The constant mechanical stress triggers a relentless triboluminescence—sparks and sheets of warm, amber-white glow bleeding from the chafing edges of every leaf and dry tendril. Combined, these billions of tiny dry-lightning flares wash the entire sunken region in an even, shadowless, afternoon brightness. The air smells sharply of ozone, toasted chaff, hot flint, and dry musk.

Nothing holds still long enough to erode. A bough thick as a wagon wheel balloons outward, calcifies into brittle, pumice-light wood, splits down its length with a sound like a cannon shot, and sheds a blizzard of papery flakes before a dozen new shoots burst from the dry marrow of the break. The entire valley floor is heaving—a slow-motion boil of woody scaffolding building itself, collapsing into drifts of warm ash-colored dander, and instantly re-rooting into its own dry detritus. 

And packed into every cubic yard of this thrashing, self-assembling thicket is the fauna. 

They do not drink; they metabolize the dry hydrocarbons of the flora and pass fine, chalky dust. Because the forest builds and collapses by the hour, nothing can hold a permanent territory, resulting in a permanent, churning stampede across three dimensions. Armies of multi-limbed,velvet-husked grazers the size of hounds surge along the flexing boughs, their serrated mandibles shearing through newly expanded pith with the roar of a thousand saws. Above them, the open air between the upper canopy and the raw stone ceiling is thick with whirring, bladder-bodied gliders and hooked, ribbon-tailed predators that ride the violent thermal eddies, colliding, mating, and shedding translucent husks on the wing. Every surface crawls with smaller stridulating things—plate-backed mites, dry-spiders spinning cables of electrostatic silk that snap and hiss as they catch airborne chaff, and burrowing scale-worms that drill through pressurized wood, triggering explosive bursts of spore-dust that hang in golden clouds before being inhaled by passing shoals of filter-bats.

The sound is an overwhelming, polyphonic grinding: wood fracturing, stone cleaving, millions of chitinous plates clicking, wings beating dry air into whirlwinds, and territorial calls adapted to pierce the din—metallic clangs struck from hollow resonators in the throats of ape-like brachiators that swing wildly from collapsing arches of vine.

Scattered throughout this hyperactive tangle are the marks of hands, though none of them stay upright for long. 

People—or something with thumbs and a grasp of leverage—move through the mid-canopy, leaving behind hasty, robust works of primitive craft. Because any shelter anchored to a single tree will be torn apart within hours as the trunk splinters or rockets skyward, the structures are built like buoys in a riptide. High in a temporary clearing, a spherical pod woven from cured tendon and flexible husks hangs suspended on grease-rubbed bone swivels, sliding freely along long, braidedguy-lines as the surrounding branches heave and twist. Nearby, wedged into a fresh cleft of raw basalt not yet swallowed by root-sheets, stands a semicircular baffle made from overlapped plates of giant carapace lashed to charred wooden frames—an acoustic shadow-wall built simply to carve out ten square feet of bearable quiet where someone once slept or knapped flint.

You see their tools abandoned or lost in the rush of growth: heavy mauls made from volcanic glass bound with rawhide to dense horn handles, used to purposefully shatter encroaching shoots before they can crush a camp; wedges of hammered native copper driven into the seams of swelling trunks to steer their splitting away from a cache; long, hooked poles drifting down into the lower mulch, entangled in shedding bark. On a broad, momentarily stable shelf of unweathered porphyry, someone has smeared geometric tallies in yellow sulfur-paste, already half-pried apart as hairline cracks widen in the rock beneath them, steaming faintly with dry mineral heat as the first white filaments of new root-mold thread upward through the stone to swallow the mark.

Ten yards away, a spire of coiled, papery timber reaches critical tension and detonates into a fountain of splinters. Before the shards have even finished clattering down through the leaves, a swarm of rust-colored, six-winged scavengers drops onto the warm stump, stripping the exposed pith, while three fresh, pale shoots punch straight up through the middle of their feeding frenzy, lifting the squabbling mass back toward the glowing, roaring roof of the world.
