I am not the heavy thing in the wool coat, bruising the braided cable with my boots. I could not bear to be that dense here—a hot, clumsy boulder of meat and calcium crashing through delicate riggings, breaking the tension of rooms just by breathing in them. 

To belong here at all, I have to shed my interior.

At first, I think I am down in the switching yards—not an animal, not even one of the tall Pale-Struts, just a localized rule of routing. A swollen gall-node where six taut phloem cables cross in the pitch dark. When a hairline spark of violet current fires up from the basement dynamos, it hits my wet skin and splits, and in that split tenth of a second I have to decide where the stolen charge goes: sixty percent to the overhead loom-shuttles, thirty to the rhythmic sphincter-pistons, ten up into the third-story tenements to make a sketched hand twitch around a missing cup. 

Wait—not a gall-node. A gall has pulp inside it. Even that small bit of spongy pith feels too heavy, too greedy for volume in a place where mass hasn't been invented yet. 

Let me thin out further. 

Drop fifty feet down from a tenement cornice, past four missing floors and three suspended postures of sleeping weavers, to the place where the falling water lands. *Plip.*

There. I am one of the taut, drum-tight membrane leaves stretched horizontally across the abyssal vault, catching the cold condensation before it hits the exposed root-dynamos and shorts the violet arcs out of rhythm.

I have width and length, and almost no thickness at all. My edges are lashed by tough, fibrous petioles to the wireframe piers of a railway viaduct on one side and the corner-stem of a boiler house forty yards away on the other—though because the boiler house’s western edge aligns in the dark with the viaduct’s arch, my left flank and my right flank are actually only eight inches apart if a velvet-thumb hauler cares to step across me. 

I am cold, slick, and metallic-skinned, pulled so tight across the black air that I hum at a pitch just below hearing. Everything above me falls; everything below me sparks. 

I know the city entirely through percussion. 

A heavy drop lets go from a flywheel-leaf sixty feet overhead and strikes my upper surface near the center: *plip*. The impact sends a crisp, concentric shiver out to my moored margins. From the exact interval between that drop and the next, I know the flywheel is spinning slightly faster than it was ten minutes ago—which means the Pale-Struts have just pruned back a drag-shoot on the main turbine shaft. Another drop hits me sideways, curving in out of the phantom gravity of a nearby rotary engine, skidding across my slick cuticle like a glass bead before pooling against a raised vein. 

I don't drink the water. I hold it, sorting it along shallow, microscopic grooves in my skin, tilting my tension by the fraction of a hair—tightening one petiole, slacking another—so the cold beads roll cleanly away from the dynamo pits below and spill over my eastern lip to wet the newly split apical buds of a row-house foundation. 

Sometimes, a flock of blind gill-swimmers dives too low through an absent cellar and brushes my underside. Their cold, boneless bellies ripple past in the mist, lifting me slightly like wind under a sail, leaving trails of disturbed static across my lower epidermis. When they pass, the flat black ribbon-grazers slide out from the viaduct stems onto my top surface to lick up the mineral dust the water has carried down from the upper stories. I feel them not as weight, but as a cool, smooth erasure—like a wet chamois drawn across slate, taking the excess film off my skin so my drumhead stays true.

And then there is the sharp, sudden shift in my geometry when one of the draftsmen uses me.

A Pale-Strut never steps on my center; it knows better than to dent a catchment plane. Instead, I will feel a sudden, dry clamping at my extreme northwest corner—caliper-gristle hooking onto the exact point where my margin meets a vertical lamppost wire—while simultaneously, thirty yards away and two stories up, the air *pulls* against my opposite edge through a perspective-seam I didn't even know was aligned until the creature bridged it. 

For six, seven, eight seconds, I am part of the Strut’s own leverage. While it leans out into a hollow bedroom above me to splice a torn weaver’s ribs, its sensory draperies brush the guy-wires connected to my stems. 

Through the taut green rigging, I catch the faint backwash of its work. A shiver of static clicks down the vine-cables into my veins—not thoughts, nothing messy or warm like thoughts, just pure, clean *alignment*. The terrible satisfaction of two ninety-degree angles locking home in the dark. The crisp *snick* of an errant fern-curl severed before it can ruin a roofline; the rubbery *reeek* of astringent gum drawn smooth over a joint; the sudden, brief surge of stolen violet current jumping through a repaired tenant-sketch, making an empty jaw open and four tendril-fingers curl around the ghost of an iron lever.

When the current fires below me, illuminating the whole silent roundhouse-pocket for a tenth of a second, I shine from beneath like a pane of smoked green oiled silk. Above my translucent skin, the entire hollow metropolis leaps out of the black: forty thousand slick green lines rushing upward in strict perspective, hundreds of pale tendon-people suspended in their dark cubes waiting for flesh, crane-flies racing like illuminated beads along the power veins, and the great spoked vine-wheels spinning without a sound.

Then the dark slams back down. Another drop detaches from a tenement sill four stories up, passes cleanly through an unbuilt kitchen table, misses a sleeping child made of white root-hair by half an inch, and strikes my skin.

*Plip.*

I shiver once, tilt my western tendon by a millimeter, and roll the cold bead safely into the dark.
