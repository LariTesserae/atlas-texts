Look down at the polished basalt, six miles south of the obsidian ridge, where a long, shallow trough of gray dust meets an outcrop of blistered stone. 

Do not look up into the empty, shimmering air where the manifolds vent. Look directly at the rock between your boots.

And running right between them, inlaid flush into a hairline crack in the basalt, is a vein of milky, translucent quartz—shot through with fine needles of gold and dark bismuth—that was not there yesterday.

If you drop to your knees in the furnace-wind, press a gloved palm to the scorching rock, and squint against the flat white glare of the sun, you will see that the vein is not mineral deposit at all. Its surface is divided into thousands of microscopic, hexagonal facets, dry and hard as industrial corundum. Deep inside the cloudy crystal, tiny threads of liquid mercury—or an alloy that stays molten at ambient desert heat—are shifting back and forth in slow, silent pulses, lagging just behind the acoustic thud of the ten-thousand asynchronous pistons shaking the valley floor.

This is where I am. I am not in the crushing, incandescent dark of the fold, and I am not walking under the slate sky. I occupy the hairline fault between the cold sink and the stone.

To have soft lungs here is to boil; to have a furnace-heart is to be condemned to that terrible, packed interior where giant engines rub flanks in the blind dark, desperate for a crack of cold sky to breathe into. I want neither the claustrophobia of the plenum nor the deafening vulnerability of the open pan. Instead, I am a **Fault-Vein**—a sprawling, subterranean network of piezoceramic bone, spun silicate glass, and liquid-metal nerves, root-bound into the upper three feet of the plateau’s fractured crust.

From above, you only ever see my dorsal seams: miles of hair-thin, glassy inlays tracing the thermal stress-fractures of the basin floor, branching like frozen lightning from the eastern talus all the way out under the flat metallic pans. Most of the year, I look like dead geology. The rotary drills of the Reef-Mills pass right over my narrower filaments, registering me as worthless silica, lifting their screaming brass bits to seek richer iron ten yards on. When a forty-foot horn-and-carbon tread materializes directly overhead and crunches down with the weight of a mountain, I do not shatter; my segments flex downward into cushion-beds of dry asbestos-silt I have packed beneath myself over centuries, taking the monstrous compression and turning the squeeze directly into a sharp, delicious jolt of high-voltage current.

Every step the titans take feeds me. Every ear-splitting shriek of the turning axle by the obsidian ridge, every rhythmic *chug* of an unseen manifold, travels faster and cleaner through solid basalt than it ever could through the thin, four-mile-high air. Down here in the dry dark of the bedrock, the plateau is not an ironworks; it is a bell, struck ten thousand times a second.

And I am the string stretched across its belly.

My life is an architecture of listening and slow, subterranean theft. While the silicon lichens passively sip stray static on the surface, I *tune* myself. By pumping heavy, bismuth-rich amalgam from node to node through my buried glass capillaries, I shift my own resonant frequency to match whatever leviathan is grazing nearby.

Down in the stone, I feel the exact torsional shudder of twenty thousand rotary bits biting into harder grain. I shift my mercury-weights along a half-mile lateral branch, matching the Reef-Mill’s harmonic until my buried quartz ribs hum in sympathy, drawing off a steady, clean trickle of alternating current from the beast’s wasted mechanical racket.

With that power, I grow, and I tend my traps.

Follow my main trunk two miles west, out into the gray dust pan where the heat-mirages pool like mercury. Here, my glassy seam widens to the breadth of two fingers, curving in a wide, lazy arc just beneath a quarter-inch blanket of metallic silt. You cannot see me at all under the dust, though if you tossed a handful of iron filings onto the pan, they would snap into neat, bristling arches along my hidden spine.

As it nears my buried arc, I open a shunt between two deep capacitor-nodules I have been charging since dawn.

A fifty-foot wingtip of tarnished brass latticework and stretched copper membrane slices sideways out of nowhere, clips the solid basalt ridge at forty miles an hour because its spatial banking angle has been ruined, and shears clean off before the creature can yank its bulk back into the perpendicular dark.

The seam snaps shut with a thunderclap of rushing air. 

Out on the sunlit pan, a twenty-foot triangle of delicate, honeycomb-ribbed metal tumbles twice across the gray silt, kicking up dry plumes that hang motionless in the thin air, before settling with a long, tinny *shiver* directly across my buried vein.

Around the valley, nothing stops. The asynchronous pistons keep hammering; the distant Die-Presses hold their single, piercing whistle-note over the obsidian ridge. To the colossi, a sheared wingtip is less than a shed eyelash.

What the weevils drop, I flux. Through microscopic pores along my glass skin, I sweat tiny beads of concentrated sulfur-acid—scavenged decades ago from the soot-falls of passing exhaust vents and hoarded in ceramic bladders deep in the stone. Wait until the late afternoon. 

Stay kneeling by the outcrop as the ordinary white sun drops toward the western rim and the shadows stretch clean and ink-black for miles across the sinter. This is my waking hour—not because the light changes, for I have no eyes for light, but because the rock reverses its breath. 

All day, the basalt has been drinking solar radiation, expanding by fractions of an inch across thirty miles of basin floor, squeezing my buried network tight in its fist. Now, as the sun nears the rim and the dry air offers no blanket to hold the warmth, the stone begins to radiate heat straight out into space. Across the entire plateau, the bedrock contracts. Millions of tiny thermal joints relax with microscopic *ticks* that only I can hear.

In that hour when the spatial seams widen and phantom machinery flickers silently across the valley—miles-high rib-scaffolds passing like ghosts through solid hills, horizon-long belts rolling overhead without casting a shadow—look down at the cracks in the cooling basalt.

For twenty minutes before dark, I am visible.

Where my quartz seams breach the surface, I shed the excess voltage of a full day’s deafening harvest. Hair-thin lines of pale, violet corona-discharge trace the fractures of the dark rock for miles in every direction—a silent, glowing nervous system mapped across the empty floor, pulsing in exact, shimmering counterpoint to the phantom furnaces roaring past overhead.
