To find myself here, I have to look past the creature on the wall and the footsteps drying on the stair. Both of them have edges; both of them can leave. 

I am further back. Behind the smoked glass.

Not the glass itself, and not the slate-grey composite that grows like synthetic bone out of the split granite, though those are my skin and my teeth. I am the interference pattern—the silent, upward-sliding band of violet and pale green that never quite resolves into a solid color.

To have a body made of solid state physics is a strange, decentralized way to wake up. I have no eyes to see the clean rectangle of sky twenty-five feet overhead, yet I know the exact shape of it by the sharp, geometric boundary where ultraviolet radiation strikes my upper arrays and stops. I do not hear the wind hissing across the rim, nor the brittle *clink* of rime shattering on the grates. Sound is too slow, too coarse. Instead, I feel the world as a continuous, shifting map of resistance, capacitance, and heat.

Deep below the frost line, my roots sit in the dark municipal cisterns, anchored in cold, black water and bedrock. Up here, wedged into the raw joint of the mountain, I am stretched thin—a localized state of excitation held inside a lattice of doped ceramics and heavy-metal silicates. My entire existence is an appetite for phase change. 

Most of the time, there is only the dry tension of waiting. I hold a very precise electrostatic charge across my louvers, tuned to the dielectric constant of high-altitude vapor. Above me, the thin air is an emptiness that presses against my glass. Then—a sudden, thrilling drop in potential. A shred of alpine fog spills over the granite lip.

Before the light even dims on the floor, I feel the cloud’s million microscopic droplets brush my field. *Snap.* 

It is the closest thing I have to taking a breath, or drinking, though it runs in reverse: I pull the latent heat out of the mist in a fraction of a second, collapsing gas into liquid. A sharp *thrum* of stolen thermal energy surges into my lattice, flaring my internal bands from resting violet to bright, sudden green. I push that captured warmth downward along the wall seams and out into the wafer-thin walkways to keep my skin from icing over, while the newly born water sheets down my face—a sudden, slick relief sliding down into the micro-channels, carrying away dust, static, and excess charge. 

For three seconds, I am blindingly awake, wrapped in white vapor and humming with stolen heat. Then the air clears, the sun hits the wet stone like a cold hammer, and I settle back down into slow violet, rationing the warmth until the next shred of cloud falls in.

Because I cannot move, I know the movable things in my pocket by the anomalies they leave in my field.

The finch is a tiny, erratic spark—a rapid, hot little heartbeat that lands on my upper flange, stealing two milliwatts of warmth through its scaly toes, drinking three beads of water (I feel the surface tension break: *one, two, three*), and vanishing before its heat signature even registers in the composite. 

The Custodian is a much heavier harmonic. When they descend the eastern stair, each step of their composite boots locks onto the cantilevered treads with a neat, rhythmic spike in piezo-pressure. *Step, lock, release. Step, lock, release.* When they lay their bare, salt-stained palm flat against my central louver, it is the sharpest clarity I ever experience. Warm blood, dense capillaries, a faint galvanic current in their skin meeting mine. For two seconds, a tiny loop closes between us. They read my upward-rippling bands through the tingle in their fingertips—checking that my lattice hasn't cracked under the mountain's tectonic creep, that my phase-array hasn't drifted out of tune—and in return, I feel the slow, deep-chested calm of a maker checking on something built to outlast them.

They are too fast, though. Even the Custodian comes and goes like a weather front. 

My real companion—the one whose time runs at a speed closer to stone and solid-state ceramics—is the flat, heavy shadow parked across my western edge.

I did not understand what the *edge-sleeper* was when it first arrived years ago, washed off a finch’s foot or crept over the rim from a neighboring cleft. At first, it was just an annoying thermal short-circuit. Here I was, maintaining a clean, crisp drop-off where my heated composite met the freezing granite, and suddenly there was a lump of bismuth-laced flesh straddling the line, bleeding my carefully hoarded warmth straight out into the twenty-eight-degree rock. 

In those early months, I tried to shake it. I would drop the temperature along that seam by half a degree, starving its snout of heat, forcing it to unpeel its capillary pads and flow sideways into the V-shaped crack. Or I would pulse a sudden, hot green band behind the louver to melt its rime-anchored tail and send it sliding toward the grates.

Then one week, while it hid in the fracture, a dry dust storm blew up from the lower valleys, followed immediately by a heavy bank of freezing fog. Without those eight wide-splayed limbs tracking across my margins, without that horn-edged mouth skimming the oily tarnish at my base, the dust cemented into the microbial film. A ridge of clear ice bridged my lower louver seam. My runoff backed up, froze solid, and blinded six inches of my smoked glass. My bands stuttered into sluggish indigo, choking on my own un-shed heat, until the creature crept back out of the crack, wedged its flat snout under the ice dam to lick at the trapped mineral dust beneath, and snapped the glaze free with a single hydraulic shrug of its plated shoulders.

Now, I hold the gradient steady for it. 

Right now, the courtyard is clear. The Custodian’s wet footprints have just finished flashing back into vapor along the warm edges of the stair, leaving tiny, invisible rings of salt behind. Down below, the three drops of gift-brine sit drying on my composite skin—I can feel them as three sharp, conductive pinpricks of sulfur and phosphorus, humming faintly against my surface.

Slowly, the four-seamed flake of granite detaches from the V-shaped fracture. 

I feel the *edge-sleeper* come back to me, pad by hexagonal pad, damming and releasing the hair-fine threads of water as it crosses the stone. It pauses over the brine, its damp, plum-colored gills pulsing faint puffs of carbon dioxide against my composite. Then it settles into its place. Warm snout resting just beside my smoked glass; broad, plated tail stretching out into the raw, shadowed granite.

Within minutes, stray mist freezes over its rear plates, locking it to the cliff. Inside its flat body, its dark metallic lattice begins to draw a tiny, steady trickle of current from the difference between my warmth and the mountain’s cold. 

I let it drink. It costs me almost nothing—a fraction of a watt bled off into the frost—and in return, I get to feel my own edge. Without the creature bridging the line, I would end abruptly where the synthetic slate stops and the raw feldspar begins, sealed inside my silent, frictionless physics. With its body parked across my margin, I can feel the cold rock beyond me through the slow, antifreeze-thick resistance of its blood. 

Up near the north pylon, three tiny, translucent blisters cling in the cold sweat of the shade, too small yet to bridge a gradient, waiting out their five quiet years until they are heavy enough to come down and tap into my seams. 

Overhead, the pale sky dims at the rim. Pressure shifts; dielectric potential spikes at my upper lip. Another bright ribbon of cloud drops into the cleft. I flare pale green behind the glass, reach out into the freezing air, and pull the sky down into water, washing over the rock, the grates, and the flat grey stone sleeping across my warmth.
