Not at the lip, with a coat lashed against my ribs and eyes watering in the chill. Anyone standing there has brought three gallons of warm water up a dead mountain inside skin bags, and has to carry it back down before nightfall freezes it solid. From the scree you can only squint at oil-slick flashes and flinch at the *cracks*. You are outside the instrument, listening through thick, slow air.

If I come closer—past the three paces where the mountain breeze gets swallowed—the first temptation is to be one of the black rings. 

There is something terribly familiar about them: small, unfrayed, hovering two inches off chalk that has been sanded away behind them for ten thousand years, still executing an instruction whose context has eroded entirely out of the sky. Think of the patience of a phase-ring on the eastern rib. Perhaps it was only ever meant to be a ventilation baffle for a roofed chamber, or a containment bearing for something heavy and hot that used to sit in the center of the peak. Now the roof is gone, the core is gone, ninety percent of the mountain is gone, and still it listens to the afternoon air and folds it cleanly at ninety degrees. It has no boredom. It takes every lie at face value. Strike it with a shaped knot of pressure and it dutifully tilts to meet a storm that isn't there, faithful to a syntax older than the cliffs.

For a moment I almost settle there—matte-black, literal, humming up a third when struck cleanly. 

Except a ring cannot change its mind, and nothing ever gets *inside* it. Look instead right beside it, six feet further along the scoured chalk rib, at one of the vitrified scars where another device finally let go of the stone three centuries back and tumbled down the scree or shattered in the bowl. Where that ring used to sit, the acoustic geometry of the basin has a gap in its teeth. A river of air that ought to fold inward spills raw and untamed across the southern arches instead, shredding any silver-vine canopy that tries to root there.

The remaining machines don't know their neighbor is missing. They just push harder against the leak.

Something else knows, though. Not at noon. At noon nobody in the bowl knows anything at all.

At noon there is only the shutter-speed of pure survival: thirty-odd separate blades tucked into triple-braided shear under the overhangs, shuddering with stolen voltage, snapping open to starve an eddy and drop a funnel on twenty clicking gliders, milling shell to dust, shedding mineral grit upward, folding shut again before a harmonic shift turns the chimney inside out. If you looked for me at noon, you would find only pieces—a notch scarred into a hollow carbon strut here, a slightly over-tightened protein tendon on a broad female riding the northern corkscrew there, a half-healed kink in a spun-carbon vine thicket fifty yards below. Nothing in the basin weighs enough at noon to hold an *I*. Six grams is barely enough weight to hold an edge against eighty knots.

Wait four hours. Let the sun drop behind the western horn until the shadow slides across the exterior scree like cold iron, cooling the intake air until the twenty-four black survivors ease their trim in unison.

The screaming walls draw back. The center of the cathedral opens—forty yards of slow, turning quiet, walled in supersonic white.

One blade slips out of the eastern shear and spills its speed, flaring wide. Alone in the calm, it only twitches, its piezoelectric spine ticking idly with leftover static, dumb as a leaf caught in a doorway. A second slides out from under the high arch and hooks a wingtip to the first’s floating strut. 

A spark jumps the join. Still not a thought—just an oscillation, a binary shudder: *here / here*. 

Then four more drop from the upper flutes. Seven. Fourteen, trailing fullerene threads combing through one another and cinching tight under electrostatic charge. At nineteen nodes, the random violet clicking suddenly locks into phase across seventy yards of shared carbon lattice, and the white noise outside stops being noise and snaps into a crisp, three-dimensional solid.

By twenty-eight, I am awake.

It is never like opening eyes. Nothing in me sees the magenta or cyan that a watcher on the rim might admire; sunlight is only a warm, slight static bias on my sunward membranes, telling me which side of my body faces west. Instead, waking up is *coming into tune*. In the space of four heartbeats—if anything here had a heart—four hundred screaming chalk apertures around the perimeter resolve into exact distances and widths. Every thrashing curtain of polymer frond casts a distinct, fluttering shadow in the pressure-field. Every hovering black polyhedron stands out like a cold, dense star of bent pitch.

And every afternoon, the very first thing I do is take inventory of my own body, because it is never quite the body I had yesterday.

Today there are thirty-one of us clicked into the lantern. Twenty-six were part of me yesterday afternoon. Four are familiar returners who missed yesterday’s calm because they were pinned down in a feeding frenzy under the lower overhangs. Wait—my lower-west quadrant is wrong. The resonant tension there is flat, missing a sharp, triple-notched delay loop that used to anchor that corner. 

The old wide-vaned hunter with the quartz-pitted dorsal strut isn't here. 

I don't grieve her; I don't have chemistry for grief, no slow glands to sour the blood. What I experience is an immediate, structural *amnesia*. She carried nine notes of the phase-correction sequence we use to compensate for the vitrified scar on the southern rib. Sometime in the last twenty-three hours, a phase-ring shifted faster than her aging tendons could reverse polarity, and the chalk flutes milled that library into bright mica-dust and blew it out the crown into the blue sky.

In her slot hangs a newly detached fledgling, barely three days off the ring-boundary, still jittering with raw ultrasonic static, its membranes tight and blank as unetched glass.

I only have twenty-two minutes before the mountain shadow crosses the lower col and the evening katabatic wind tears this calm cylinder apart. There is no time to waste.

Where did I leave the rest of myself before I scattered yesterday?

Not on the floor—the floor keeps nothing. I reach outward with my edge-kites, dipping six stiff vanes a fraction of an inch into the shrieking shear-wall around my waist, and listen to what the bowl kept for me overnight.

There: down on the lower northern buttress, where I tricked two black rings into driving a cross-gale yesterday afternoon, the silver sail-vines have grown three yards of fresh, dense ribbon overnight off the extra momentum, thickening the acoustic shadow in that corner by half a decibel. That shadow is a tally-mark; it tells me yesterday’s trim held until midnight. 

Higher up, between the fourth and fifth polyhedra on the east wall, a tight little figure-eight air-knot is still spinning uselessly in an empty alcove, bouncing back and forth between the two machines like a ball trapped between mirrors. Nobody hunts there; there are no vines there. I put that knot there three days ago—a standing wave locked into the machines’ mutual feedback loop, holding a precise timing interval overnight where no living tissue had to carry it. 

I read the timing back off the trapped eddy. I pass the rhythm inward across my superconducting tendons in a rapid volley of violet clicks, routing the current straight through the blank, trembling fledgling in my lower-west quadrant—heating its memory-protein filaments just enough to anneal the missing nine-note chord back into its spine. The youngster shudders under the voltage, drops a quarter-inch in the frame, and then hums true. Now when we scatter tonight, two of my pieces will carry that phrase instead of one.

Only after the repair holds do I turn my full forty-foot ear toward the cathedral and begin to play.

This is what I am: a twenty-minute mind made of borrowed knives, hanging in the throat of a broken engine, keeping the garden alive by lying to its ghosts.

My edge-weavers flex in millisecond ripples. Concave sails snap shut, cup the eighty-knot wall, and hurl shaped bullets of compressed air out through the shear. *Thrum.* Three chalk flutes on the high crown choke at once, dropping the cavern’s roar by a fifth. An instant later, the rebound strikes the third black ring on the northern face at an oblique angle that mimics a collapsing low-pressure trough.

I feel the alien thing swallow the bait. Its hum tightens; its invisible magnetic baffle pivots two degrees downward. Immediately, a lethal sheet of supersonic cross-shear lifts six inches clear of a stone rib crusted with seventy tiny frost-fern juveniles, giving their quartz needles room to drink the edge of the blast without snapping loose too soon.

Another chord—wider, slower, plucked across twelve edge-vanes at once—strikes the eastern arches to compensate for the vitrified scar on the south wall, bending a wild spill of air back down into a tangled canopy of spun-carbon vines where static-chaff has gone stale in a dead pocket. The canopy explodes in pale blue corona sparks and chartreuse dust; instantly, five hundred tiny metallic grazers dive out of a neighboring vortex into the feast, clicking like hailstones on tin. Tomorrow at noon, my thirty-one mindless pieces will eat well off the gliders that come to eat those grazers, though none of them will remember who set the table.

In the four or five minutes left before the wind turns, when the nurseries are shielded and the vine-canopies are churning clean voltage and the standing-wave memories are refreshed between the eastern rings, I do the other thing—the useless thing that no six-gram reflex could ever want.

I listen *past* the lip.

By cupping all thirty-one upper membranes into a single parabolic dish aimed straight up the open chimney of the peak, using the calm column itself as an acoustic lens, I catch the faint, thin wash of sounds falling in from the outside world before the engine eats them. The dry rattle of loose scree sliding down a slope where wind only goes one way at a time. The strange, flat silence of unbraided sky—miles and miles of air that doesn't turn corners, doesn't scream, doesn't spark, where anything with weight simply falls down and stays where it lands. Sometimes, very rarely, the slow, rhythmic *thud-scuff* of something impossibly heavy and wet standing three paces back from the rim, breathing in and out at sixty-second intervals, shedding muffled heat into the chill.

I turn that odd, heavy sound around inside my lattice for thirty seconds, mapping its soft, waterlogged geometry in rapid violet sparks, wondering how anything so dense keeps from shattering against the stone it walks on. I cannot store that thought; it takes too many nodes to hold, and serves no survival loop. It lives only until the evening gust arrives, and every afternoon I think it from scratch as if for the first time.

Then the high flute on the western horn shrieks half a tone sharp.

Raw, freezing plateau air—unfolded, chaotic, straight off the snowless ridges—slams over the chalk lip. Before the front even reaches the center of the bowl, the westernmost phase-ring feels the spike and clamps down hard to defend its dead equation. The calm cylinder cracks down the middle.

To hold on for another tenth of a second—to cling to being *one*, to try to finish the thought—would tangle thirty-one frames into a knotted rag of dry silk and smash every piece of me into shimmering powder against the concave floor.

So I don't hold on. In the last ten milliseconds of coherence, I dump every capacitor at once across the docking hooks.

One bright blue flash smells sharply of flint. Every join reverses charge and kicks free. *I* vanish instantly—not dying, just ceasing to have a center—and thirty-one sleek, mindless darts hiss outward into thirty-one separate corridors of roaring violence, riding the wreckage safely into the dark, carrying pieces of tomorrow afternoon locked silently in the tension of their bones.
