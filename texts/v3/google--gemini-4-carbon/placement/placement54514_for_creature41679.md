My jaw is pressed flat against the weeping slate, and my molars ache with the B-flat hum of four hundred tons of housing holding steady over a thirty-foot drop. 

To look at me from the opposite ledge, through the sheet of warm condensate pouring off an overhead flange, you would just see a pale, greased boulder wedged into an angle iron. My knees are rotated out until the inner thighs lie flush against the pipe; one spatulate foot is hooked backward around a frayed, velvet-stiff carbon tendon, toes splayed wide and tacky with grip-mucus; my shoulders are hunched high, pale callous-plates gleaming dully under a stray shaft of white sky. Below me, in the amber-lit throat between two condenser towers, three heavy mesh cocoons swing gently over a rushing greywater sluice. Fourteen of my band are knotted inside them, slick flank to slick flank, breathing slow and steaming slightly in the draft. 

I am not asleep. I am listening with my cheekbones, my shins, and the corrugated pads of my thumbs.

Through the porous ceramic under my face comes the dense, overlapping clutter of the basin’s work: a rhythmic *thud-hiss* four tiers down where a slurry-bridge is finishing its twenty-minute stiffen-and-flush cycle; the dry, continuous *skitter-tick* of wall-crabs grazing ten yards to my left; and far off—maybe two miles east, felt only as a greasy, rolling smudge beneath the sharper vibrations—the slow, circular drag of a reservoir-sink pulling blocks inward. None of those matter yet. What matters is the local pitch. Right now, the field holding our anchor-wall against its neighbour is singing clean and flat in my jawbone, steady as blood. We have another hour here, maybe ninety minutes, before the thermal buildup inside this cluster forces the heat-exchangers to uncouple and vent.

Across the gap, twenty feet away on the twin anchor-point, sits Tallow-Knuckle. She is older than me, her back rounded into a thick, shock-absorbing C, one pale flank faintly ferned with purple Lichtenberg scars from an old brush with a live phase-sheet when she was young and reckless. She doesn't look at me—her secondary lids are half-drawn against the fine mist blowing sideways off the sluice—but every three or four minutes, she drops the heel of her palm twice against the tension-rod bridging our two walls.

*Still-flat. Still-warm.*

Without lifting my chin from the stone, I flex my throat-sac and send a short, double-clicked buzz back down the rod through my hooked toes. *Still-flat. Three crabs creeping toward your left line.*

She doesn't bother turning her head; her free hand simply uncurls with liquid, loose-jointed laziness, snatches her short memory-metal pry-bar from her chest-harness, and *cracks* the lead grazer off the ceramic before its serrated skirt can chafe the sleeping-cord. She tucks the kicking, dinner-plate-sized thing straight into a mesh bag at her hip without shifting her center of gravity an inch.

If you asked me where we were yesterday, I couldn't draw you a map. Maps belong to things that stay dead when you lay them down. Yesterday doesn't exist as a place; it exists as a sequence of pressures and temperatures stored in my joints. Yesterday was a long, fast ride down an arterial spillway after a ten-minute belly-cloud burst overhead, shooting through dark locks alongside mustard-backed eels that brushed our calves without biting because the surge was too fast for hunting. Before that, it was a hot, cramped climb up a rising crane-spine while leather-wings clacked like riveters around our ears, leaving my shoulders slick with rendered bird-fat and our pouches heavy with warm, salty yolks. 

I don't wonder who built the stone, any more than the midges wonder who brewed the brine. Sometimes, wedged into a narrow cleft waiting for a friction-field to power down so we can cross, my fingers will trace a seam where memory-ceramic has healed over an older fracture forty times until it looks like twisted knuckle-cartilage, and I’ll feel a brief, wordless kinship with the wall. It gets scarred, it sweats out its heat, it shifts load when the pressure gets too high, and anything that stops moving gets ground into paste and washed down to the dark. That isn't a mystery; that's just how weight works.

Wait.

There. Right behind my ear-slits, the fine whiskers along my jawline stiffen simultaneously. 

The air in the cleft hasn't cooled, and the roar hasn't changed volume, but the barometric weight against my ribs just thickened by the breadth of a fingernail. Two seconds later, the B-flat in my molars slides upward half a tone—*bzzzz-eeee*—turning thin and sour against my teeth. Above us, the water sheeting down the ceramic changes pattern; instead of running in braided rivulets, it suddenly flattens into jittering geometric grids.

The local cluster isn't waiting ninety minutes. A trunk-conduit down below must have rerouted early. Load is dropping *now*.

Across the gap, Tallow-Knuckle’s eyes are wide open, clear membranes flicked back. On the wall beside her, two hundred mottled grey crustaceans let go all at once, clattering down past the cocoons like dry hail into the sluice below. 

We don't waste a breath on panic. Panic makes you stiff, and stiff things snap between housings. I press my barrelled throat hard against the iron angle and drop a single, chest-hollowing *THUMP* through the structural web—loud enough in the bone to wake the dead, buzzing straight down the braided carbon suspension lines into the tangled ribs of the sleepers.

Instantly, the cocoons convulse—fourteen bodies snapping from dead slack to coiled hydraulic tension before their eyes are even open, hands locking onto internal harness-loops, ear-slits clamping shut. 

Above us, a vacuum seal blows with a muffled, gut-punching *boom* that turns the falling mist momentarily white. The fifty-foot condenser wall behind my spine shivers, sighs, and uncouples, dropping six inches onto its magnetic slip-plane. Bands of mirage-like refraction bloom in the air three feet over my head, flattening raindrops into spinning silver coins.

Tallow-Knuckle and I pull our master release-pins on the exact same beat.

The three cocoons drop like cut fruit—twenty-five feet straight down through the steam—hitting the roaring, waist-deep greywater sluice with three heavy, cushioned *splashes*. The quick-split seams part in the current; pale, broad-handed heads bob up instantly in the amber foam, already reaching out to hook each other's harness-straps into a daisy-chain as the warm torrent catches them and sweeps them out through the widening bottom-gap before the walls can scissor shut.

I unhook my backwards foot, let my palms weep a slick film of release-mucus, and push off into empty air right behind them. 

For a second and a half, falling between two grinding mountains of wet basalt while white sky flashes briefly through a tearing roof-seam ninety feet up, there is no weight at all—only warm rain on my tallow-slick shoulders and the vast, mindless, cataract roar of the world rearranging itself around my skin. 

Then my heels slap the rushing water, my fingers find the carbon loop on my sister’s hip-harness, and I pull my chin down, letting the warm surge carry us three districts west toward the smell of ozone and steamed crab, already listening with my knees for the next quiet wall.
