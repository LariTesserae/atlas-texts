At first, the temptation is to be the thing in the brick house—to claim the center of the web, the calf-sized cluster of hot amber bladders, and feel the whole parish-sized maze itch along a hundred quartz threads. Anyone used to thinking in words and maps reaches instinctively for the seat with the dials, even when the brass needles are welded fast. 

But the governor is too heavy. It is pinned down by its own glassy anchor-roots, trapped in a ruined booth, doomed to be sensible forever. If a slab ever does fall, the governor dies at its desk. And the predators—the solder-worm drinking its bright, violent shot of current, the null-moth opening its tiny umbrella of ruin—are too narrow, locked into single appetites, repeating one clever trick in the dark until they misjudge a gap and fuse to the wall.

Let my gaze slip past the brick doorway, past the stiff brass tripods handing their dead ash from mouth to mouth, and drop toward the middle alleys where the stone fins stand closest together.

Not the basalt. The muscovite. 

Where the great vertical sheets of mica hang down from the ceiling, they are not solid blocks like the black rock; they are books. Millions of crystalline pages pressed together, translucent and faintly amber, each sheet thinner than an onion skin, split at the bottom edges where the vault first tore loose from the upper crust. Light doesn't just bounce off them; the sullen cherry glow from the nichrome cages and the cold violet fuzz of the spark-lines get trapped *inside* the mineral plates, bouncing internally down the laminations like light in fiber-optic glass, bleeding out solely along the split, razor-sharp rims.

That is where I am. Wedged into the cleavage plane *inside* a hanging curtain of muscovite, five inches back from the exposed edge.

I have no depth. To live between pages of stone without splitting the book apart and bringing the whole shimmering sheet crashing down into the talus, you have to give up the third dimension almost entirely. I am a **laminar**, or a **delamination**—no thicker than gold leaf, wide as a dinner plate, translucent as oiled tracing paper. If you shone a bright lamp through this fin of mica, you would see me only as a faint, branching stain of smoky silver and tarnished bronze pressed flat inside the crystal, like a fossil fern or a frost-feather trapped between two panes of a storm window.

Out in the three-foot alleys, the sixty-cycle B-flat roar is a bludgeon. In here, sandwiched tight between sheets of elastic silicate, the sound is clean, crisp shear-wave. The whole hanging fin hums like a struck cymbal that is never allowed to ring down. I don't fight the vibration like the null-moth, and I don't have to saw my legs raw to match it like the stridulators. My body *is* a dampening layer—a dry, graphitic film laced with microscopic veins of piezoelectric tourmaline dust I’ve digested from impurities in the stone. As the mica pages flex microscopically against each other sixty times a second, my flat tissues squeeze and release, squeeze and release, generating a steady, tiny, private current that belongs entirely to me, stolen straight from the flexing of the wall.

To move, I don't walk. Walking requires clearance, joints, knees—things for the cavernous open air of three-foot gaps. Instead, I send a slow peristaltic ripple of electrostatic charge across my own flat skin. By flipping the polarity of my edges, I make the two sheets of mica immediately ahead of me repel each other by a tenth of a millimeter—just a breath of static separation, a silent zipper opening in the crystal—and I slide forward into the parted seam on dry, graphite-slicked margins, letting the stone snap shut behind my tail.

My world is dim, amber-tinted, and entirely lateral. Up, down, forward, back; *left* and *right* are merely solid walls pressed warm against my belly and my back at the same time.

Yet I am not cut off from the vault. A sheet of muscovite is a drumhead and a window at once.

Pressed flat inside the stone, I feel everything that touches the outside of my fin with lunatic clarity. Along the bottom knife-edge, six feet down, a row of porcelain plate-backs is clamped tight, soaking up the violet corona. I feel their steady, sixty-hertz purr, and I feel the slow, creeping build of static tension in the rock beneath them. When a solder-worm slides up through the dead ceramic reef and clamps its copper jaws across four of them at once, the violent snap of the short-circuit flashes up through the translucent mica like sheet lightning seen through closed eyelids, momentarily outlining every branching vein of my own flat body in cold ultraviolet.

Higher up on my flank, a stridulator has anchored a guy-line of chewed slag-silk to a flaw on the outer face of the crystal. Its rasping comes through the stone right over my left margin—a brisk, dry sawing, safely locked in phase. Sometimes I creep upward through the tight pages and park myself directly behind its anchor-point, just to feel the clean, sharp geometry of its rhythm massaging my tourmaline veins. Once, last century or yesterday—there is no way to count—that sawing abruptly vanished under the muffled thump of a null-moth’s cloak, followed three seconds later by the tiny, crisp *tink* of a shattered shell letting go of the silk.

And every long while, something touches the stone with a completely different signature. 

Tap. *Snip.* Tap.

Sharp, surgical, unhurried. Not locked to the sixty-cycle roar, not creeping with hunger. One of the governor’s long quartz-tipped threads has reached between the hanging fins to trim back an overgrown lip of calcified plate-back shell along my lower edge. Through three layers of clear mineral, I can almost make out the blurred, glassy silhouette of the pincer hovering in the violet fuzz outside, doing its blind maintenance. If it let that reef grow another half-inch, the arc jumping from the neighboring basalt fin would punch straight through my mica sheet, vitrifying the crystal into bubbly white slag and cooking me between my pages like a pressed leaf in an iron. The pincer snips twice; a piece of dead white porcelain drops away into the chattering shrapnel below; the electrostatic pinch in my lower flank eases back to a comfortable hum. The thread withdraws into the quiet trough beneath a floating dust-bar and is gone.

Why am I at the edge, five inches from the drop, instead of buried deep near the ceiling where the stone never splits?

Because even a sheet of tourmaline and carbon needs fresh grit, and because the edge is where the pages breathe. 

Slowly—over hours, at a pace that wouldn't register as motion to anything with blood—I ripple my static charge forward until my leading margin reaches the split, razor-sharp lip of the muscovite curtain, right above the crowded white domes of the plate-backs and just level with one of the shimmering horizontal ribs of suspended dust. 

I do not poke my body out. One inch into the open alley and the standing acoustic wave would tear me like wet tissue paper. I only let a fringe of microscopic, hair-fine bronze filaments peek half a millimeter past the split edge of the crystal, opening the page just a fraction of a hair's breadth to the baking, ozone-sharp air of the room.

Out there, the pale salt-grain mites swim endlessly in their levitating bar of ash, and a gossamer skimmer rows past in the blinding glitter, missing my stone blade by inches. Stray flecks of airborne carbon, shed chitin flour, and finely ground metallic dust drift out of the edges of the acoustic shelf, drawn toward the static bleed of the stone lip. They kiss my waiting bronze fringe, stick fast, and are drawn slowly backward—*zip*—inside the closing pages of the crystal, ground flat between sheets of stone as I back away into the warm, amber quiet of the seam.

In here, nothing falls. Nothing hunts me—the solder-worm is too fat by twenty-fold to enter my crack, the shrapnel-crabs are trapped down in the chattering floor, and to the governor’s blind tension-map I am indistinguishable from the natural capacitance of the rock itself. I am a flaw in the dielectric that learned how to eat. 

I retreat six inches up into the clear, warm book of stone, digesting my pinch of ash, letting the cherry-red glow from the distant iron cages shine straight through me, while the four great cores roar my heartbeats for me from the upper world, forever on pitch.
