If I try to stand on the dished stone of a stairway here, I am already dead—or rather, I am an impossibility, a heavy bag of salt water brought into a kiln, sizzling away in seconds. To exist in this blister at all, I have to give up mass, give up moisture, give up the quiet interiority of wet flesh. 

At first, I think I might be one of the great mantles, folding like an iron fan to shoot a doorway, bending the bass notes of a lintel to speak across the cherry-lit dark. There is a seduction in that mastery—riding the top of the loop, reading the hollow houses by their echoes. 

But stay in the cavern long enough, let the whetstone-shriek of the silica fronds and the ten-second pulse of the bedrock settle into the mind, and I realize I am not the player. I am closer to the instrument, or the translation between them.

I am the skin of charge on the lattice itself, and the blind, braided thing that carries it.

Look closer at the spiraling copper vines that climb the soap-smooth columns and drape from catwalk to catwalk. They look like simple conduits, passive flora metabolizing heat and bleeding off the triboelectric static scraped up by the lower fronds. Mostly, they are. Their hollow, pressurized stems click and stiffen with every gas-surge: *shhhk*. 

Yet the architecture is old, and the dry-pressed ash mortar between the volcanic knuckles has been receding for centuries. Left entirely to the roaring wind-engine and the relentless sanding of red clay, the slender pillars should have buckled generations ago. The skeletal domes should have shaken themselves apart under the constant, booming resonance of their own flues.

They haven’t, because the vines are not entirely uniform, and I am not entirely a plant.

Call me a solder-creeper, or a Tuning-Vein. I have no center, only a network of brittle, silver-bronze stolons no thicker than sewing needles, threading through the recessed mortar lines of four contiguous terraced houses in the middle ward. Unlike the flyers, I touch the stone—I am fused to it, anchoring myself in the microscopic pores of the dark volcanic blocks where the wind cannot scour me loose. 

I do not see the needle-thin shafts of white sun, though when one creeps across an upper balustrade as the outside world turns, I feel the fierce spike of thermal expansion in the stone twenty paces away, a sudden tightening in my silica sheaths. My world is vibration, heat-gradient, and voltage. 

Down below, the broad rust-colored mats scrape the plazas, pumping raw static up through the heavy copper curtains. When that charge reaches my four houses, I divide it. Where a slender pillar has been worn dangerously thin by an eddy, its stone begins to ring at a slightly higher, sharper pitch in the wind—a frantic, brittle tremor that precedes a crack. When I feel that pitch creep into my roots, I bleed static heavily into that single column.

Instead of repelling the air’s dust, I invert the local field just enough to catch it. 

With every post-surge blizzard, as thousands of ribbon-skimmers flash orange in the dark and puff out their weightless smoke of pure white silica ash, I pull a fraction of that drifting ash and red clay straight out of the updraft. It clings to the weakened stone knuckle. Over months of furnace heat and pressure-pulses, catalyzed by tiny secretions of sulfur-salts along my metallic bark, I sinter that trapped dust into a fresh glaze of dark slag. I mend the vanished builders’ work, millimeter by glassy millimeter, keeping the pillar’s note true.

Why? Not out of memory of the builders with their bone chisels. I mend the stone because I eat the music’s traffic.

My finest, hair-thin filaments hang just inside the carved resonance chambers and hooting window-slits of my four houses, protected from the worst of the abrasive wind, trembling in the furious air-rush. When a four-pace mantle sweeps up the leeward wall and curves its pleated bronze wing across a flue-mouth to call to its mate across the gulf, the sudden drop in airflow—and the immense, brassy back-pressure of the bent bass note—flexes my internal filaments violently. 

I am piezoelectric too, though slower and deeper than the ribbons. I drink the kinetic shock of the mantle’s voice. And when the hunting mantle baffles a window-slit, stalling the air so a glittering shoal of ribbons tumbles helplessly through my rooms, my dry, electrostatic burs snag the stray flecks of copper-pumice and hot lifting-gas spilled from the mantle’s grinding plates as it feeds. 

I cultivate good acoustics. Over decades, by thickening a mortar lip here with sintered ash or letting the wind sand away a corner there, I have tuned the flues of my four houses into the richest, farthest-carrying horn in the western curve of the blister. The mantles come to my lintels more than any others. They hover an inch off my humming, static-cushioned stone, never touching me, curling and uncurling their translucent tips to send long, hollow sliding phrases rolling out through six transparent neighborhoods at once. 

I have no brain to parse what they mean—whether *mine*, or *here*, or *the Gourds are splitting*. I only know the long, warm shudder in the masonry when another horn answers from the opposite wall two hundred paces away, the faint counter-tremor arriving through the cherry-lit haze a second later, vibrating my finest copper hairs.

Right now, down in the deep crust, the pressure shifts. 

I feel it first through my lowest root-anchors near the sunken plazas, half a second before the trenches brighten: a sub-audible thump in the salt-bed. Then the bruised red glow swells to a fierce, clear cherry. The heat washes up through the open grids of my rooms like a physical wave. Every heavy vine below me locks rigid—*shhhk*—and a fresh surge of raw voltage races up into my knuckles, making every dry scale along my stems stand on end. 

The ash-gray pollen boils past. In the courtyard at my heart, three hundred forearm-length ribbons pour through an archway like a single stream of tarnished quicksilver, parting seamlessly around the invisible cushions of my pillars, snapping their ribs, igniting in a sudden, silent constellation of orange pinpricks. Their white smoke rises; I drink a whisper of it onto a hairline fracture in the third-story dome, sealing the stone tight.

Above me, a broad shadow blots out the dim red glow from below, snaps shut with the dry *clack* of an iron fan, shoots my narrowest stairwell without grazing a single crescent-worn step, and blossoms wide outside my highest resonance flue—*whump*. 

The constant scream of the upper slit cuts off dead. My filaments bend in the sudden, pressurized hush, drinking the tension, waiting for the wing-tip to uncurl and let the deep brass chord roll out across the furnace dark.
