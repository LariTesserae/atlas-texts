My left condenser-baffle has a three-year scar across the third flute where a splinter of hot hornblende took me in the side, and because of it, I whistle on the intake. A thin, wet, reed-pipe *whee-uck* every time my ribs widen to wring the boiler-damp out of the air.

In a world where we had huts, or silence, or twenty yards of personal space, my kin might have strangled me in my sleep for the annoyance of it. Here, the whistle has become the downbeat for eleven people.

When the juveniles click their plated teeth to map a new talus-spill, they hang their rapid, percussive *tchk-tchk-tk-tchk* off the tail of my inhale. When Old Grizzle—whose chest-flutes have gone leathery and slack with seventy years of wringing iron-mist—drops down into the deep, sub-vocal fault-chant that remembers how the tablelands three days south shear apart when the static turns green, he waits for my *whee-uck* to turn the corner of his measure. I am not the strongest bracer in our knot, nor the sharpest-eyed on the thermal flare. I am the metronome. And I am the splicer.

Right now we are hung like a cluster of dark, greased gourds beneath the overhang of a weeping zinc fin, forty feet above a ravine that is busy chewing itself to gravel. Below us, outside the bubble, six-ton hexagonal columns of black stone are slamming up and down in staggered triplets, slick with weeping shale-oil. To my eyes—both pairs—it is violent theater. My outer eyes catch the sudden violet sheet-snap when a nearby copper reef grounds itself into the wet ferns; my big inner eyes drink the luxurious, buttery amber-and-crimson bloom of friction sliding up and down the basalt shafts, bright as stoked coals, fading to bruised plum when the shale vents steam and cools the stroke.

None of that fury reaches our ears. All that reaches us from below is the warm, rhythmic *whuff* of displaced boiler-air pushing up past our hooves, smelling of hot mineral grease and crushed castor-sap.

Inside our twelve-foot sphere, though, my skull is packed to the sutures with *us*.

I have my right middle-arm cam-locked at the elbow around a fist-thick root of iron-sulfide vine, taking half the weight of two sleeping cousins whose heavy legs are tangled through mine. My left middle-arm is braced against the wet zinc wall, thumb hooked into a natural cooling-flute. That leaves my small upper hands free, and my chest pressed tight against the slick, warm ribs of four others. Warm sternum-water from the girl perched six inches above my collarbone trickles steadily onto my shoulder, mixing with my own condenser-runoff and flushing the fine gray grit of powdered slate down my back.

We are resting, which means five of us are asleep and six of us are singing for our lives.

*Thrum-clack-clack—whee-uck—hiss-thrum.*

Left-hip against mine, Bracer-Broad—we don’t have nouns for names, only transient rhythmic signatures, and hers is a heavy, double-stopped glottal thud like two boulders kissing—is humming the Immediate Terrain. Her throat vibrates directly against my ribcage so I don't even need the air to hear it: *Zinc-fin cools on the seven-count, root-clamp slipping left by thumb-widths, flicker-wolf scent stale below, piston-three hangs slightly at the top of its throw, hangs slightly, hangs slightly...*

Opposite me, two adolescents with fresh, unscarred copper gleam on their fourteen fingertips are trading the Tallow-Taxonomy at breakneck speed, slapping their palms lightly against one another’s wet collarbones to keep the syncopation crisp while they argue in clicks over whether the yellow-veined seam we smelled twenty minutes back was sweet-fat or nerve-rot. And underneath it all, braided through my scarred whistle, three of the older throats are turning the Great Wheel—the four-hour cycle of deep memory that holds who birthed whom, how to bleed static out of a seized cam-joint without stopping a heart, and the exact harmonic whine a magnetic reef makes ten seconds before it flips polarity and throws every brass beetle in the canopy straight down like shrapnel.

If we all fell asleep at once—if some heavy pocket of sweet sulfur-gas rolled up from the cleft and knocked all eleven throats quiet for a quarter of an hour—we would wake up intact in body and utterly, terrifyingly feral. Animals with fourteen copper thumbs and nothing behind our eyes. Every trick for reading the orange flare before rock shatters, every ancestor, every map of where the counter-walkers cross the furnace-trenches, vanished into the dead air twelve feet out. You cannot afford a silent mood here. Sulking is murder. Even grief has to be sung on beat, tucked into the harmony so it doesn't crowd out the recipe for climber-skin poultices.

That is why I am the splicer. I listen for the fray.

It happens most often when we have to string out on the cords.

Twenty minutes from now, the zinc fin we are hanging from is going to get hot—I can already see a faint, feverish apricot blush creeping up its base through my inner eyes as deep granite begins to bind against granite forty feet down. Before this perch shears off and drops into the pistons, we have to cross a ninety-foot chasm of churning black ferns to reach a broad, stable terrace of sliding crust where we spotted the dark, moss-draped dome of a counter-walker marching steadily against the conveyor. On a counter-walker’s back we can un-cam our joints, scrape the vulcanized char off our hooves, and let the toddlers wobble around for six hours without the floor opening up under them.

Between us and that walking mountain hangs a single mooring-vine as thick as my torso, armored in iron-sulfide scales that grate together silently as the two spires it bridges quiver out of phase. It won't take eleven bodies bunched together. We have to cross one by one, twenty yards apart.

Which means leaving the bubble.

I hate the bubble's edge. Everyone hates it, except maybe the weird, solitary scout-types born once every three generations with thick nerve-spools and half-deaf ears, and those never live past thirty anyway.

When it’s time, Old Grizzle taps two copper-calloused thumbs against my sternum—*take the middle*—and reaches behind his own left collarbone. With a wet, muscular *slurp* that makes my skin prickle in sympathy, he draws three yards of slick, bluish-pink cord from his chest-spool, wipes the excess conductive lymph off the bone-barbed tip, and clicks it firmly into the socket beside my right shoulder-blade. Immediately, my nervous system widens. I feel the ragged, sturdy thump of his old heart as a phantom pulse behind my ear, and a sharp, salty tingle across my teeth that means *line clear, listening*.

Behind me, the girl with the warm sternum-water hooks her filament into my left socket. I pay out my own cord—feeling that strange, hollow tug deep behind my lungs as the spooled tendon unwinds—and plug myself into Bracer-Broad ahead of me.

One by one, we drop onto the trembling iron-scaled vine and scuttle out over the abyss.

Twelve feet from the zinc fin, the world dies.

It never stops feeling like having your head plunged into warm tallow. One stride I am wrapped in the dense, wet, percussive roar of ten throats—*thrum-clack-whee-uck-tchk*—and the next stride Bracer-Broad pulls ahead, the girl behind me pauses to lock a knee, and the acoustic horizon snaps shut around me alone.

Suddenly there is only my own ragged whistle, deafeningly intimate inside my tiny personal sphere of working air, the slick *shhhk-shhhk* of my split rubberized hooves biting wet iron scales, and the continuous splash of water down my chest. Everything else turns into monstrous, silent cinema.

Thirty feet below my hooves, a six-ton hexagonal piston jams against an extruded lip of slate. Through my inner eyes, the pinch-point flares blinding white-yellow—hot enough to blister skin from ten yards—and then a whole flat colony of gray-pink shock-pads wedged in the tolerance gets sheared in half as the slate explodes into razor shrapnel. Boulders the size of my torso cartwheel upward through the maroon fern-fronds, shredding timber, sending gouts of dark, oily sap arcing through the gloom, scattering swarms of stamped-brass flyers whose triple wings blur furiously.

Not a crack. Not a boom. Just a warm puff of castor-stink against my belly and the ghostly, silent flutter of torn leaves drifting past my face.

Without the song in my ears, panic tries to crawl up my throat—not fear of falling, I have four heavy limbs built for wet cable, I couldn't fall if you greased me—but the vertigo of *forgetting*. In the hush, my thoughts immediately start feeling slippery, untethered, like phantom fern-pulp dragged out of a four-second loop-acre, ready to *pop* out of my skull and leave me empty. *What comes after the seventh measure of the Sulfur-Seam? How many beats before the blue static grounds?* I start humming frantically to myself—*whee-uck, thrum-clack, whee-uck*—bouncing my single thin voice off the air two feet in front of my nose just to hear *something* stay real.

Then the cords twitch, and I am busy.

*Snap-tingle-tug* from Bracer-Broad ahead, firing straight into my right trapezius and lighting up my jaw with copper-taste: *Vine-scales loose three lengths out. Step wide. Left spire heating.*

My fourteen fingers fly across the cord leaving my chest, pinching and bridging the copper-sheathed nerves with my conductive calluses, translating and amplifying her twitch backward down the line to the girl behind me before my conscious mind even chews it, adding my own observation with a double-pinch of my second thumbs: *Updraft coming, brace low, don't look down-right, flicker-seam opening in the air six feet off the cable.*

Sure enough, six feet to my right, space itself develops a greasy vertical wrinkle, like heat-haze over ironstone, and a single wide, hornblende-clawed wolf-paw pokes casually out of nowhere, sweeping blindly through the damp air in case a climber happens to be swinging wide, while the rest of the beast sits safely on some ledge fifty yards upslope. Nobody swings wide. My double-pinch goes down the wire faster than sight; seven bodies behind me tuck their elbows tight to the iron vine in cascading unison.

Halfway across, the bad thing happens. Not to me—three places back.

The spire we just left finally gives way to that apricot friction-bloom. Looking back over my shoulder, I see twentyvertical yards of zinc cooling-fins silently peel away from the granite core like shaved lead, dropping straight across the tail of our vine. Everyone is already clear of the rock—except a falling plate of razor-edged slate clips the wet cord strung between the water-girl behind me and her younger brother.

Through my left shoulder-socket, I feel the terrible, bright *zap* of a severed nerve grounding out against raw metal, followed instantly by dead slack.

I cam-lock both middle elbows around the iron vine so hard my quartz-laced ulnae flash dull purple under my charcoal skin, halting the front half of the column with a brutal yank. Behind me, twenty-five yards back in the dead cinema, the boy is frozen on the bucking cable, frantically reeling his bleeding, sparking chest-filament back into his collarbone before a stray static sheet from the copper reefs below catches the exposed nerve and cooks his brain in its pan.

He is completely outside the girl’s twelve-foot sphere. He is completely cut off from the wire. Look at his broad, flat face through the murk: his amber inner eyes are blown wide, staring at us across twenty-five yards of dead hush, his mouth working furiously, screaming his own rhythmic signature into air that swallows it ten steps in front of his hooves.

If he stays out there alone on a bucking cable while the cliff-collapse rearranges the ravine, he’ll lose the thread. You can see it happen to severed juveniles sometimes—the wildness comes over them in three minutes flat when the world goes silent and starts breaking apart around them; they bolt into the ferns like pale eyeless grazers and we never get them back into the harmony.

I don't unhook from Bracer-Broad; I need the anchor of the front-song. Instead, I scream a tight, three-pulse burst down my front cord—*Pay me out! Full spool!*—unlock my elbows, spin on my cross-hatched hooves leaving a smear of blue rubber-smoke on the iron scales, and sprint backward along the heaving vine toward the girl.

The moment I crash inside her twelve-foot horizon, sound slams back into my skull like warm iron—she is wailing a high, jagged distress-trill that threatens to tear the Tallow-Taxonomy right out of her memory. I grab her shoulder with two upper hands, press my scarred ribs hard against hers so my *whee-uck* forces her breathing back onto the four-count, unclip her dead, frayed cord-end from her back, and jack my own spare secondary shunt—short, painful to pull, tucked right under the jaw—directly into her neck-socket while gesturing wildly with my free middle-arm at the boy fifteen yards back: *Come in! Four bounds! Look at my chest, watch the beat!*

Because he knows me—because I am the splicer, and I exaggerate the flare of my condenser-slits until steam puffs visibly white from my ribs on every *one-two-three-four*—his inner eyes lock onto the hot rhythm of my breathing before his ears can reach me. He takes four reckless, knuckle-vaulting bounds across the shuddering iron scales, dodging a silent shower of gravel, and throws himself bodily into our tangle.

The instant his head crosses the twelve-foot line, the noise of us hits him. You can see his whole six-limbed frame go limp-jointed with violent relief as the group-voice rushes back into his ears. He buries his flat face against my slick sternum, drinking the vibration straight off my bone, his plated teeth immediately chattering back onto the tail of my whistle—*tchk-tchk-tk-tchk*—checking to see if he still remembers who his mother’s brother was, checking to see if the sulfur-song is still where he left it.

It isn't, quite. Two minutes in the hush while reeling a shocked nerve has knocked a ragged hole in his half of the Regional Fault-Map; he’s clicking a flat, panicked gap where the six-stroke shale-vent cadence ought to be.

"Hold him tight," I rasp into the huddle, pressing my copper-calloused thumbs against both sides of his jaw so he can feel my throat-cartilage jump. I pull the missing cadence up from Old Grizzle’s deep baseline—still humming faintly into my shoulder via sixty yards of stretched tendon from the far side of the chasm—and sing it straight into the boy’s face, loud and wet and slightly sharp on the intake, until his teeth catch the groove again and stitch the hole shut. Nothing lost. Nobody gone animal today.

Ten minutes later, all eleven of us drop out of the dark canopy onto the broad, terraced tableland and scramble up a thick, swaying curtain of precipitated zinc plates onto the back of the counter-walker.

Gods—if we had gods, instead of just deep mechanics—what a luxury a walking mountain is.

Beneath the beast’s forty pairs of pillar-legs, the entire continent is sliding steadily westward toward a subterranean furnace-trench two miles off, where the low graphite clouds glow dull, greasy orange from beneath. You can watch jagged spires of newborn obsidian glide past like barges on dark oil. Yet here, fifty feet up on the broad, flat table of the old giant’s carapace, padded in four yards of dense tallow and weathered slate-scale, the floor *stays put*.

Underfoot grows a lush, knee-deep carpet of bronze moss and tiny violet static-flowers that never get sheared in half. Small, velvet-skinned ambush cats scatter silently out of our twelve-foot circle as we collapse into a heap among the ferns, watching us with cool green eyes from twenty paces away—pantomime cats flicking pantomime tails. Deep beneath our bellies, propagating straight up through the beast’s laminated bones into ours, rolls the immense, comforting, sub-audible *grumble-crush-grumble* of its ball-mill gut digesting half a ton of iron-sulfide bark, steady as planetary north.

Here, at last, we can un-cam.

With a chorus of loud, wet *clacks* inside our acoustic sphere, twenty-two heavy middle-elbows unlock at once. We sprawl in a tight, steaming pile of charcoal hide, tangled hooves, and glistening copper fingers, scraping gobs of warm mineral tallow out of our armpit-pouches and slapping them into one another’s mouths. Somebody plucks a freshly shed scale of brass beetle-wing out of the moss and uses its sharp edge to pare back a torn flap of vulcanized horn on Old Grizzle’s left hoof; somebody else wraps a newborn tighter in a translucent scrap of sky-ray parchment that snowed down from the distant iron-cyclone yesterday, letting the infant suckle while warm sternum-water washes the trail-grit off its tiny, unhardened toes.

I lie on my back in the center of the knot, four cousins draped across my legs, my scarred rib-flutes whistling softly into the warm mist—*whee-uck... whee-uck*—watching a sheet of silent blue static crawl lazily across the black cloud-ceiling above.

Beside me, the boy who lost his line taps seven copper fingertips lightly against my bicep, testing a brand-new rhythmic phrase he’s just invented: a fast, jagged, three-against-four syncopation that mimics the exact shudder of an iron-scaled vine when a zinc cliff peels off behind you and a wolf-paw reaches through a hole in the air to the right.

*Tchk-slap-tchk—pause—whee-uck—clack-clack.*

I listen twice, turning it over in my chest. It’s good. It’s accurate. If we hit another spire with that same apricot heat-blush fifty miles east next season, that little riff will save somebody’s cord.

I nudge Bracer-Broad in the ribs with my heel, open my jaws, and weave the boy’s new measure straight into the evening baseline. Within six breaths, eight throats have picked it up, harmonizing around my scarred intake, polishing the rough edges off the rhythm until it locks tight into the Great Wheel—our only house, our only chisel, our only monument, hanging warm and indestructible twelve feet wide in the middle of a world tearing itself apart in the dark.
