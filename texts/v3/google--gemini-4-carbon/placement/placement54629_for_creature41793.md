You will not find me out in the blinding, ordinary glare of the plazas, where the water sheets thin and yellow over the sinter, nor hanging in the vaulted palates of the ceilings where the carriage-sized keepers hold the roof-tones true. I have no patience for the brute housekeeping of lime and limpet-shell, and I am too thin to carry stone.

Look instead for the places where the loop almost drops a stitch.

There is a narrow covered stair on the western flank of the middle granite horn—though its bottom three steps are also the drain-lip of a sunken courtyard four tiers above the eastern abyss—where a mineralized strangler-root crosses a doorway at chest height. Beneath that root, two streams meet that have taken very different paths through the folded afternoon. One has just come off sixty yards of sun-baked black basalt; it is forty-six point four degrees, slick with dissolved silica, fast and thin. The other has been winding through dark, fern-choked interior rooms whose walls sweat iron; it is forty-four point eight, heavy, rusty, and slow.

Where they braid together over the dished threshold, they do not mix cleanly at first. For nine feet down the Satin Lane, there is a shear-line in the runoff, a continuous underwater braid where hot clear overrides warm red, and the roar coming off the left wall arrives a fraction of a millisecond out of step with the roar coming through the doorway behind.

That nine-foot seam is my entire body, plus an elbow crooked around a cistern rim half a mile away that happens to share the same corner of air.

If you stepped over the root-beam—ducking your head, dripping your wrong, salty sweat—you would probably not register me even after your eyes had learned to spot the masons. I don't bend the afternoon into four crimson pitcher-plants at once. I am closer to the oily rainbow you sometimes see on the surface of hot standing water, except there is no oil here, and the rainbow has thickness: a ribbon-shaped sleeve of taut, clear density laid directly along the fault between the two temperatures, about as wide as your forearm and ten thousand years deep in the middle.

Inside me, nothing spins in neat concentric rings like the grit in the arcade-pendulums. My grit—tiny, flat flakes of black mica and translucent red iron-oxide no bigger than dust motes—slides back and forth in long, lazy figure-eights, shuttling endlessly from the hot side of my skin to the cooler side and back again.

I am a *temper*, or a *baffle*, or simply a hinge. My whole existence is the difference between forty-six point four and forty-four point eight.

It feels like a continuous, delicious leaning.

Imagine standing in a doorway with your shoulder braced against the jamb while a warm gale blows past you, and realizing that *you are the jamb*, and the gale is what keeps your ribs from drifting apart. The great, bone-rattling thunder of the basin—which to you is an assault, a deafening wall—is to me simply the floor being solid. I do not hear it any more than a fish feels wet; it is the baseline tension that keeps my edges crisp. What I *feel*, with exquisite, hair-trigger intimacy, is the slight grainy rub where the sun-water and the shade-water try to tear past each other at the wrong speed.

Left to themselves, those two currents would set up a tiny, ragged flutter against the third step down. A microscopic chatter. Bubble-cavitation no bigger than pinpricks, popping against the travertine forty times a second. Over three hundred years of pinned afternoon, that chatter would pit the satin polish of the groove. The pit would catch grit; the grit would turn the current a millimeter toward the wall; the wall would cool; the acoustic bounce between the step and the mineralized root-beam overhead would flatten by a twelfth of a semitone; and up in the courtyard four tiers above—which is also right behind my tail—two small ring-collars clamped onto a philodendron stem would lose their phase-lock, go slack into splashes of warm rusty water, and let a single green root-tip surge half an inch into the light.

So I lie in the seam and take the flutter into my belly.

When the hot basalt-water surges a hair too lively off the upper switchbacks, I thicken along my left flank, tilting my internal planes until the roar pressing down from the root-beam catches inside me and stiffens my gelid middle like cooling glass. The current hits my invisible curve and slows without splashing, trading its extra fraction of a degree through my dancing mica-flakes into the sluggish iron-water on my right, warming it just enough that when my far end pours it over the distant cistern rim on the neighboring pinnacle, it arrives at forty-five point six—even, silent, and smooth as grease.

I have no thoughts that go *first this, then that*. In a city where the sun never drops toward the crest and a curling strip of bark never finishes peeling, *then* is not a direction anyone travels. My memory is sideways and spatial, held in the exact slant of my figure-eight dust.

Yet I know the whole town through the water's gossip.

Every drop that passes through my nine-foot sleeve has been everywhere else in the loop, and water remembers what bent it last by the way its dissolved minerals are aligned. I can taste, in the slight tightening of silica chains sliding through my skin, when three vault-keepers two wards away—and directly underneath me—have shifted an inch along a ribbed ceiling to correct an echo. I feel the dry, glassy wake of the broad skimmers out on the sunny switchbacks long before their split currents reach my root-beam; their passage comes down the gutter as a faint, rubbery hum that makes my mica-flakes shiver at the turns of their figure-eights, like a pleasant draft across bare teeth.

And I knew the instant you stepped onto the mountain saddle.

Long before you reached the square where the vault-keepers hushed the air around your head, the water brought your rumor down the Satin Lane. It wasn't fear that passed through us—what could frighten a knot in a standing wave?—it was an itch. A bizarre, irregular *thud-thud* in the acoustic lattice, traveling through the flagstones from three different directions at once because you had walked across a topological splice without knowing it. And then, five minutes or an eternity later, two microscopic droplets of human sweat washed off a plaza sill, took a shortcut under a fern-choked lintel, and slid right into my hot-side current.

Sodium chloride. Lactic acid. Trace urea. A microscopic, ragged flake of dead skin—soft, unmineralized carbon that hadn't been locked into ironwood or scrubbed into vapor.

When that flake entered my margin, my figure-eights stumbled.

I had never held anything *rotting* before. Nothing in the basin dies; things only precipitate or dissolve. That tiny scrap of your knuckle was chaos—floppy, porous, soaking up sound instead of bouncing it, leaking strange salts into my clean silica shear. For three whole seconds I pulled my downstream elbow back from the distant cistern rim, gathering all my density under the strangler-root, curling my glassy length tightly around that single mote of dead flesh like an oyster around a burr.

I turned the basin's thunder inward onto it from six angles at once. Not to destroy it—to *understand its pitch*. To find the resonance at which soft meat locks into crystal.

There wasn't one. Under the focused needle of vibration, the flake didn't plate onto the stone and didn't turn cleanly into bicarbonate; it just shredded into smaller, greasier smudges of carbon that muffled the micro-echoes inside my belly until I had to open a dead-spot of silence right against the yellow sinter and let the raw forty-six-degree heat cook it into dispersed gas, venting it upward as a tiny, invisible puff of steam that hit the underside of the root-beam and vanished back into the sweating stone.

If you come stumbling down my covered stair right now, fleeing the unsettling cotton-quiet of the plaza, ducking under the mineralized trunk with your lungs heaving in the boiler-room air and your boots splashing blindly through my nine-foot run—

You will feel one boot slip slightly sideways on nothing at all, as if you'd trodden on a submerged bar of hard, slick soap wedged into the gutter. For the fifth of a second your shin passes through me, the bone-rattling roar in that leg alone will drop an octave into a deep, purring thrumming as I desperately flex every crystal plane I have to keep your ragged, muffled meat from tearing my two currents apart. Looking down, you'll see your own boot-sole displaced eight inches to the left, dry under four inches of rushing water, edged with a faint bruise of violet and rust where my mica-flakes scatter against your ankle.

Keep walking. Step out the bottom archway onto the sunlit roof-terrace forty feet above my head.

Behind you, under the dark root, I will spend a long, unhurried stretch of forever smoothing out the wake you left—gathering my scattered red and black dust back into its lazy figure-eights, coaxing forty-six point four back into polite marriage with forty-four point eight, leaning my transparent shoulder once more into the warm, deafening, unbroken afternoon until the step beneath me shines like satin and nothing has happened at all.
