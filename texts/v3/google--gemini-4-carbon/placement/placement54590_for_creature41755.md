At first, it would be easy to want the coat. To imagine pulling four layers of marrow-cured felt over tucked knees, letting the jaw hang slack until the molars stop ringing, and sitting chest-to-chest in the pitch dark of a stone hovel to speak in phase-shifts. There is a deep comfort in that slow, tallow-pale endurance.

Except I have no lungs to starve, and no sternum to press against bone. If I tried to occupy a body here, I would only be impersonating an exhaustion I haven't earned.

Look closer at the floor of the shelter, just inside the low doorway where the sightlines break.

Not the keeper squatting there with grease on their brow. Look down between their tucked heels, past the spiral of pale knucklebones, at the wooden bowl turned nearly to stone. Or not even the bowl itself—the wood is a relic of forests, stubbornly remembering dirt and sunlight even after centuries of cold mineral soak. Look at the three centimeters of black water held inside it.

That is where I find myself. Not the whole of the basin's moisture—not the beads marching two inches down the basalt ribs and halting, nor the clear seepage rising from the bottom of a stranger’s bootprint—just the shallow, oil-dark lens kept indoors and tilted at a grazing angle toward the threshold.

To be this water is to have no weather of my own. Nothing evaporates from me; nothing rains into me. My surface tension is a drumhead stretched across petrified grain, trembling at an amplitude too fine to break the mirror, swallowing the dry *shhhhhhhhhhh-krrrrrr* that comes up through the slate floor and holding it without making a single concentric ring. Outside, the valley lies to eyes. It peels its rims toward the pewter sky; it tucks forty-foot pleats of slate behind a slab of rock; it stretches fifty paces into forty minutes of stumbling. Light out there is diffused, shadowless, gullible—it skates right over the seams where space fails to meet itself and pretends the floor is whole.

Until it hits me at twelve degrees.

At a grazing angle, the lie comes undone. Caught on my dark skin, the doorway shows what actually stands in the northern blind spot: the fifth slab, and the sixth, and the seventh, and the narrow, tucked-away ribbon of wet gravel between them where a dish-eared grazer is lowering its chin toward a plum rosette, entirely unaware that two hundred yards away, inside a cairn, a pair of heavy-lidded eyes is watching its flank through a puddle.

I do not move to hunt, and I do not step through adjacencies. I am the thing that stays level in a dish-shaped world that insists it is always tilting. 

Wait—there is more to it than just the mirror. A mirror is cold glass, and glass would shatter or fog here. Every few sleeps, before a keeper crawls backward out into the pewter gloom, two wide, tallow-smeared fingers dip through my surface. 

The touch leaves a microscopic bloom of ungulate fat floating on the black tension, iridescent only from the side, like the flat creatures hanging in the spatial tears. The keeper lifts three drops of me out of the bowl and runs a thumb down the parallel grooves of a slate tablet, seating me into the scored stone so the counts and step-ratios turn dark enough to read, or slick enough to feel with numb skin. For a little while, part of me is smeared across rock, carried inside a four-layered coat pocket through a folded crease in the pass—feeling that brief, greasy shear in the air when six steps cover half a mile—before being pressed onto another tablet in a cairn near the eastern shelf, while the rest of me waits back in the wooden bowl, fractionally shallower, until the air itself sweats three new drops onto my surface to make the three centimeters whole again.

Nothing is lost, and nothing is warm, and nothing hurries.

Sometimes, for a very long stretch, the flat stone is pulled across the doorway from the inside. My reflection goes completely black. Two shapes sit inches above my rim, knees touching my wooden lip, foreheads and breastbones locked together. No light reaches me then, so I hold no geometry. Instead, the roar coming up through the bedrock meets the altered, hollow resonance dropping down from their two chests—a slow, thrumming beat-frequency pressing against my meniscus. Where their lungs cancel the valley's thunder for half a heartbeat, a microscopic dimple forms in the center of my stillness, holding the quiet like a speck of dust from the ribbons five miles overhead, until they breathe again and let the noise back in.

Then the door-stone scrapes aside. The shadowless pewter light floods the threshold, bringing the crooked horizon with it. I flatten, take the grazing angle, strip the illusion off the slate, and show whoever leans over me where the next real step is hiding.
