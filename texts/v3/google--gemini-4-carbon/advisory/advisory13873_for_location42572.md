To step into this vault as an ordinary human—a wet, salty, seventy-percent-water bag of conductive meat—is to introduce a violent anomaly into a sealed, hyper-arid electrical circuit. Nothing in this room has encountered water since the rock split. Nothing here is soft, nothing is damp, and every surface is either razor-sharp, blazing hot, or carrying a high-voltage charge. 

If you materialize here unprepared, the environment will not eat you; it will short-circuit through you, slice your suit open on unweathered stone, coat your lungs in glassy dust, and vibrate your inner organs until you vomit. 

Preparing for this drop requires treating the destination less like a cave and more like the interior of a live, shattered power transformer that happens to be colonized by porcelain and glass. Here is how you must prepare before you dial the coordinates.

### 1. Scouting and Insertion: Finding a Gap
The prompt notes there is *no way in* except fractures too narrow for a human ribcage, and the vault itself has no sightlines—it is packed solid with interleaved stone fins set only an arm’s-breadth (two to three feet) apart. 

* **Do not materialize blind.** If your translation gear drops you three inches to the left of an open alley, you will rematerialize *inside* a sheet of muscovite or fused into a cast-iron inductor core. 
* **Pre-insertion probe:** If your traversal method allows you to open a pinpoint aperture or drop a marble-sized sensor through one of the ceiling fractures first, do it. Do not rely on standard radar or lidar; the intense electromagnetic fields, suspended metallic ash, and translucent mica curtains will scatter electromagnetic and laser pulses into gibberish. Use a hardened, shielded fiber-optic tether or passive thermal/acoustic mapping to find the **half-crushed brick switch-house** near the center. The area where the pegmatite slab fell is likely one of the very few spots where the vertical fins were snapped flat enough to leave a pocket wide enough for a human crouched in full protective bulk—just ensure you are several yards clear of the drooping live copper busbar and the circle of tripodal scavengers around it.
* **Travel ultra-slim:** Leave bulky external backpacks behind. If your shoulders plus your rig are wider than twenty-four inches, you will be wedged immobile between two walls of live, buzzing plate-backs before you take three steps. Everything you carry—air, cooling, tools—must be worn inline along your torso, thighs, and shins, keeping your horizontal profile narrower than your own shoulders.

### 2. The Suit: Cut-Proof, Dielectric, and Sealed
Your suit has three conflicting jobs: it must stop razor-sharp volcanic glass from slicing it open, it must insulate you from massive electrostatic potentials and live current, and it must keep you from dying of heatstroke inside a baking kiln.

* **A Closed-Circuit, Positive-Pressure Rig:** You cannot breathe the vault’s air, not even through a particulate filter. Between the elevated carbon dioxide feeding the graphitic plants, the toxic concentrations of sharp metallic ozone from constant corona discharge, and the air-temperature dry heat, a simple respirator will scorch your throat and asphyxiate you. You need a fully sealed suit with self-contained breathing air (SCBA or a compact closed-circuit rebreather tucked vertically between your shoulder blades). Maintain slight positive pressure inside the suit so that if a pinhole nick occurs, clean air leaks *out* rather than letting microscopic mica splinters and pale mites drift *in*.
* **Armor Against Freshly Cleaved Basalt:** Unweathered basalt and dry muscovite fractures have edges literally a few molecules thick—on par with surgical obsidian scalpels. Standard rubberized hazmat or electrical lineman suits will slice open the first time you lose your balance and brush a wall. Your outer shell must be an articulated weave of **high-density aramid (Kevlar/Spectra) bonded with flexible ceramic trauma plates** over high-contact zones: knuckles, forearms, elbows, shoulders, knees, and shins. 
* **Dielectric Isolation (No Exposed Metal):** Beneath or integrated into that cut-proof shell, you need Class-4 high-voltage dielectric insulation rated for tens of thousands of volts. Crucially, **your outer gear must have zero exposed metal.** No steel carabiners, no brass zippers, no conductive helmet rims, no metal boot eyelets. In an environment saturated with powerful magnetic fields from giant step-down inductors, any closed loop of conductive metal on your body will suffer from **inductive heating**—a steel belt buckle or watch case will grow blisteringly hot against your skin within minutes, and metal zippers will draw fat violet arcs from the stone edges and the shells of the plate-backs. Use PEEK plastic, ceramic, and silicone hardware exclusively.
* **Footwear:** The floor is not flat rock; it is "splintered floor" buried in "neat, dry talus piles of glittering shrapnel" vibrating constantly. Wear thick-soled, shankless dielectric boots reinforced with ceramic puncture-plates in the soles and ankles. Expect the talus to shift underfoot like greased ball bearings because the mechanical shudder of the room reduces friction between the dry flakes to almost zero.

### 3. Thermal and Water Management: Surviving the Kiln
The air inside is hot like the space above a kiln, bone-dry, and completely stagnant. Because you are encased in thick, non-breathable dielectric and cut-resistant armor, you cannot sweat to cool yourself. Unmanaged, your core temperature will hit fatal levels in twenty to thirty minutes.

* **Phase-Change Cooling Vest:** Do not rely on electronic Peltier coolers or miniature compressor pumps—their tiny motors and circuits are prime targets for the furious magnetic fields of the four iron pillars. Instead, wear a heavy-duty **phase-change material (PCM) ice vest** directly over your base layer (using high-latent-heat paraffin or salt-hydrate packs rather than plain water ice, to prevent condensation shock and give you a steady 15°C/59°F sink for two hours).
* **Absolute Moisture Containment:** Keep all drinking water strictly inside your sealed helmet plumbing (a bite-valve straw inside the mask). Do not vent moist exhaled breath directly into the room if you can scrub and recycle it, or if using open-circuit air, expect your exhaust valve to cause bizarre localized chaos: a single puff of warm, humid human breath hitting bone-dry dielectric dust, chalky porcelain shells, and high-voltage static will instantly cause airborne ash to clump into mud, trigger erratic spark-overs along the stone fins, and potentially kill a dozen desiccated organisms on contact by explosively hydrating their sulfur-iron tissues.

### 4. Acoustic, Vibrational, and Visual Defense
"Before you see anything, the sound has already occupied your chest." A continuous low B-flat (~58–60 Hz, matching the sixty-cycle roar) amplified into literal standing walls of acoustic pressure by millions of resonant stridulators is not merely loud; it is physically destructive to human tissue.

* **Double Hearing Protection (Passive Only):** Active noise-canceling headphones will instantly saturate, screech, or fry in the stray AC magnetic fields. Wear deep-insertion foam or molded silicone earplugs *underneath* heavy, liquid-seal acoustic earmuffs built into your helmet. 
* **Managing Visceral Resonance:** Earplugs won't stop a 60 Hz standing wave from shaking your eyeballs (causing blurry, swimming vision, as human eyeballs resonate near 18–60 Hz) or rattling your lungs and teeth. Wear a **bite guard** (like a heavy athletic mouthguard) so your molars don't chatter themselves into hairline fractures. Cinch a wide, snug neoprene abdominal binder around your core; physical compression noticeably dampens the nauseating sensation of low-frequency sound waves sloshing your stomach and diaphragm.
* **Vibration-Damping Pads:** Because the floor shudders continuously, standing stiff-legged will send relentless mechanical vibration straight up your tibia into your spine and skull, inducing "white finger" numbness in your feet and severe disorientation. Fit thick viscoelastic sorbothane insoles inside your boots, and pad your knees and palms with heavy gel dampers in case you have to brace yourself.
* **Optics and Lighting:** The ambient light is miserable and dangerous: dim cherry-red infrared/amber glow from buried nichrome coils, deep black shadow, and continuous **ultraviolet corona discharge** ("ultraviolet fur") bleeding off every stone edge. Unprotected, even through clear glass, the UV will give you photokeratitis (welder’s flash—corneal sunburn) in minutes. Your helmet visor needs **UV-400+ polycarbonate polycarbonate/gold-film shielding** that blocks 100% of UV and trims infrared glare while preserving visible light transmission. Bring ruggedized, magnetically shielded lights—ideally **chemical snap-lights (chemlights)** in high-output yellow or green strapped to your chest and wrists as unbreakable backups, paired with a potted, Faraday-shielded LED headlamp with zero loose wiring loops.

### 5. Equipment Rules: Leave Electronics Behind
Standard traveler electronics—smartphones, digital cameras, quartz watches, unshielded environmental tricorders—will go haywire or die immediately inside a church-sized pocket pierced by four giant magnetostrictive grounding cores. Sixty-hertz alternating magnetic fields pass effortlessly through aluminum, plastic, stone, and flesh.

* **Go Mechanical and Optical:** Bring a mechanical dive watch with an anti-magnetic soft-iron inner cage (rated to at least 15,000 gauss / 1.5 tesla, though even then, trust an hourglass-style sand or fluid timer more). If you want photographs, bring a **fully mechanical 35mm film camera** housed in a non-conductive polymer shell, loaded with medium-speed film (very high-ISO film may fog slightly if there are tiny X-ray bremsstrahlung emissions from the highest-voltage vacuum/spark gaps, though mostly it's UV corona). 
* **No Compasses:** Obviously, a magnetic compass here will simply spin or slam hard toward the nearest cast-iron pillar. Because every alley of stone looks identical and sightlines are less than a body-length, **navigation is a life-or-death hazard** even in a room the size of a parish church. Carry a spool of high-tensile **Kevlar or braided fiberglass cord** coated in bright fluorescent silicone (never nylon that melts on hot surfaces, and never wire-core). Tie off at your insertion point and pay it out carefully, keeping it taut so it doesn't snag on the razor-sharp basalt lips or saw through a curtain of mineral-silk.

### 6. Behavioral Protocol Inside the Vault
Once you are suited, cooled, damped, and dropped into the dim amber-and-violet roar, how you move determines whether you leave intact.

* **Respect the Architecture of the Equilibrium:** Remember that this ecosystem is utterly static and ancient. Nothing here flees rapidly because predators as we know them don't exist, and nothing knows how to heal from a giant clumsy biped crushing forty years of calcified insulator-crust. Move in slow motion. Keep your elbows tucked tight to your ribs. Turn sideways ("crab-walking") to slip between the interleaved basalt and muscovite fins without scraping your chest or back.
* **Do Not Bridge the Plates:** You are moving between interleaved stone fins that act like the plates of a massive capacitor, shingled with living condensers (the plate-backs) buzzing with stored high-voltage static. Even in a dielectric suit, **never brace your back against one stone fin while planting your hands against the opposing fin.** Do not turn yourself into a dielectric bridge across an arm's-breadth air gap; surface dust on your suit can track an arc right across your chest. Touch one wall at a time if you must steady yourself, and touch flat stone faces rather than the sharp, glowing spark-line edges where the plate-backs cluster.
* **Watch Your Head in the Upper Two-Thirds:** Duck low where possible. The upper reaches of the alleys are hung with layered curtains of brittle slag-silk spun by the stridulators. If you barge through these curtains, two things happen: first, you shower yourself in sharp, chewed-mica fragments and dozens of long, glassy-legged insects rasping in sympathetic vibration; second, you disrupt the standing acoustic waves in that alley. When a standing sound-wave collapses, the horizontal ribs of shimmering mica dust and metallic ash suspended in midair will suddenly drop like leaden snow right onto your visor, blinding you until you wipe it off (which will instantly scratch your visor unless you dab gently without rubbing).
* **Give the Iron Pillars and Busbar a Wide Berth:** Do not touch the four riveted cast-iron casings. Not only are they radiating kiln-heat from the cherry-red nichrome banks inside their cages, their fierce mechanical shudder can numb a hand through a glove in seconds, and snapping the brittle black graphitic plants clinging to their rivets will puff conductive gray carbon soot straight onto your suit—creating a conductive path across your exterior insulation! Similarly, near the leaning brick switch-house, stay strictly outside the outer ring of the tall, pumice-and-brass tripodal scavengers. Those creatures are living voltmeters: they stand at the *exact* radius where the voltage gradient around the broken copper busbar drops to their metabolic sweet spot. If you step inside their circle, you are stepping into step-potential territory capable of arcing through boot soles.

### 7. Extraction and Decontamination: Coming Home
You cannot simply pop back out of this universe into your home base and peel off your helmet. Your suit exterior will be thoroughly coated in **microscopic, glass-sharp muscovite dust, graphitic soot, metallic ash, and pale salt-grain mites.** 

* **Static Cling:** Worse, your insulated suit will likely carry a massive residual electrostatic charge, causing all that razor-dust to cling stubbornly to every crease of your armor.
* **Decon Routine:** Before leaving the vault (or immediately upon arriving in an airlock/staging zone), touch a grounded, high-resistance bleed rod to safely discharge your suit's static buildup so you don't draw a spark near flammable earth air or fry your translation gear. Have a wet-down shower or HEPA vacuum wash waiting in your staging area *before* you unseal your helmet. If you breathe dry mica dust while pulling a sweaty suit over your head, those microscopic flat silicate plates lodge permanently in lung alveoli much like asbestos. Wash the grit away with copious water—watching the bizarre, dusty coatings of a waterless world turn to ordinary grey mud for the very first time—before taking your first real breath of quiet air. 

Bring earplugs for *after* you get back, too. After an hour inside an unvarying, bone-shaking B-flat roar, true silence is going to ring in your skull like a bell for days.
