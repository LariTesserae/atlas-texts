In the heart of a densely packed urban landscape, where buildings of stone and primitive brick huddle together, there exists a hidden pocket of silence and cold, a micro-environment that has remained undisturbed for centuries. This is not a place of grand vistas or sweeping panoramas, but rather an intimate room tucked away within the city's labyrinthine corridors.

Water rules. The air is heavy with moisture, condensation clinging to every surface, dripping from the low ceiling, where mineral-laden drops form miniature stalactites. An occasional rainfall seems to form in mid-air, descending and trickling to water along small carved channels that crisscross the terrain. Sparse light hitting the carved ground is caught on mosaic and shimmers in the dampness. There is the occasional repetition of every surface enacting a taut calmness as a rainset occurs. Parts of the cold stone floor are sunken and filled with collected water, creating pockets of still, reflective pools. Some of the water frayed to one side flows along the curve of the slope so imperceptibly it might be still. These gently collect silt and clay from the trickles, and the accumulated grit makes the basin floor and sides gently muddied.  Their surfaces are smooth from the eroding flow of time, polished by the constant, barely perceptible movement of water that carves and fills and flows like a river in slow-motion.

Rusted chains that hang from the ceiling disappear into the water, suggesting depths unseen. The rust drips slowly into the water.  The tiny, isolated ichorous sound of rain hitting the surface of a pool echoes long. The low ceiling is supported by thick, rounded columns, their surfaces pitted and weathered by time and dripping water. Each column is unique, twisting around itself, their surfaces clad in a radiant moss between bright and deep-green flecked with rare yellow, like a soft lichen that has swallowed the stone and whose roots are both starving and essential.  The moss touches every surface, even extending to the carved mosaic on the ground.  Trailing clumps of vegetation growing the ceiling drip pastel-flecked onto the wet surfaces below. The short plants that survive are always, barely damp and spiky, but infrequent and seemingly planted deliberately. The narrow space between them is filled with lush clumps of a stable shimmering purple fungus, tiny, and short leafed plants like neon green moss, horizontal to the ground, finely compact like a golf putting green. Areas of the floor are completely packed with the stuff.

The place has the aura and architecture of a crypt, but has grown peculiar. It seems constantly early day, or late in the afternoon.  The air is filled with a soft, subtle glow coming from the moss that lends the place an unusual brightness despite the lack of natural lighting. The temperature is cold, even if dampness means there is condensation everwhere. Visitors breathe mist, and are subject to the thinly locked still air that moves with them. The silence is profound, broken only by the quietest sounds: the gentle drip of water, the almost imperceptible hum of primitive meditative machinery that pulses in the distance and underpins the silence and, if one listens very carefully, whispers something nearly indiscernible as it stills.

The walls are packed with oddities, obscure artifacts from forgotten times tucked into niches: strange icons and fetishes of carved bone and polished stone, the imprints and hooks, loops and channels of the water's path, and ribbons of murals depicting creatures that might have once lived here, or perhaps still do, unseen in the upper and lower recesses. Atop the walls, toward the entrance to the open air, fauna gather in abundant quiet. Moths cling with soft wingbeats.  They become less frequent, with statues and roots of creatures taking their place further inward.  The fungus spread around the statues is lightly lit, so the double darkness of moss is overflowed. Creatures hibernate, with interjoined fur, their fur condensed and their fallen brown pockets of fur, encirclings the shaded spaces, where the wet surfaces make a winding, honeycomb catacomb of walls.  Every creature and structure is stationary but all awake; it is just that there is no need to move.  The creatures extremely large eyes are always wet and open.  The light is constant.  Stalactites and stalagmites form over centuries; the wind is the only movement, even within the flora.  A rabbit has not yet leaped.

There are no immigrants or emigrants, but no trace of skeleton or sign of birth

The rules of this world are simple: everything is as it is, contending with the willlessness.  There is patience and quiet intelligence. If a stone tumbles from the ceiling, it is because it has always been tumbling. Change is almost imperceptible, occurring over centuries, not seconds. The water carves its paths through the stone, but it does so with a patience that comes from eternity. Even the light is stable, never flickering or wavering. It is a place out of time, a sanctuary of stillness hidden within the bustling city, even if the condensed joy of the fungi are aware.
