This is not a landscape of grand vistas or sweeping panoramas, but rather an intimate, hidden micro-environment, tucked away within the folds of a greater, harsher world.

The terrain is raw and primordial, like a planet caught in the first stages of its creation. Sharp, untouched mineral formations jut from the ground, glinting under a persistent glow around them. Yet, there are no factories or engines to be seen; the industrial echoes seem to emanate from the very fabric of reality itself, as if the world here is powered by an unseen mechanized heart. It is as though all the scaffolding of the world is being assembled by an unseen hand — or perhaps disassembled, given the sense that entropy and order seem to have blurred boundaries.

There is no water here, at least not in any familiar form. Instead, a fine, glittering dust coats every surface, shimmering like powdered diamond under the constant, normal light that suffuses the pocket. This dust is not mere mineral; it possesses a strange, almost vital quality, swirling in intricate patterns as if driven by unseen currents, like a luminous plankton in a non-aqueous sea.

Despite the lack of water, the landscape is far from barren. A strange, lush vegetation thrives here, unlike any found on Earth. Delicate, fractal tendrils sprout from the mineral formations, their translucent fronds shimmering with iridescent hues. They sway gently, not in response to any breeze, but to some internal, unknowable rhythm. This flora seems to be nourished by the glittering dust, absorbing it through their crystalline surfaces in a silent, symbiotic dance. These growths may represent the most primitive sort of neural network-networks, learning soul; the plants feel as if under radar central-planning or geomantic variegation via n-dimensional space, though we shall withhold judgement of whether it feels to them like the former or later.

There are no animals here, nor any signs of life as we know it. Instead, there is a pervasive sense of 'otherness', a weirdness that tinges every aspect with a palpable unreality. Shapes seem to shift at the edges of vision, disappearing when looked at directly. Some of the "plants" seem to epicycle between place and dimension, with sprouts on a kind of Dido-tuning-gear prognosticating and anticipating movement. The rules of the world feel fluid, malleable, as if reality itself is still in its infancy, still forming and deforming.

Time, too, seems to move at its own pace in this hidden sanctum as if submodules appear only to be felled by men with hats. Change is slow, almost imperceptible, but inexorable. The mineral formations grow and evolve over eons, their shapes recording the passage of time like the rings of a tree. The vegetation, too, seems to sway at a rate where being a plant means it has adapted to the tempo of their time-signatures.  The dynamics of this world are delicate, patient, and gradual, yet the environment seems to rest on a weirdness that verges between the carnal and operatic.
