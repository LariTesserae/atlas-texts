In the heart of a high alpine valley, a place that has settled into an ancient, weathered stillness, there lies a landscape that is both familiar and uncomfortably strange. The air is cool and damp, a perpetual mist clinging to the slopes like a spectral shawl, occasionally coalescing into drizzle that feeds the scattered pockets of water dotting the landscape. These pools are not mere puddles, but deep, clear basins that stretch like mirrors across the silent ground, reflecting the sky above and the odd, twisted shapes that loom over them, all joined by a silent network of stony waterways, living veins that pump out a constant sound of howling fluid rushing, driving the mist patterns in oscillating twists.

The terrain is a tapestry of the ancient and the arcane. The valley walls, once towering and steep, have been worn down by eons of wind and rain, their faces now smooth and striated like the cheeks of some slumbering giant. The land possesses the open, sprawling character of a town or small city. What appear to be structures from a distance reveal themselves to be things that make the rules of the world here — the primacy of plant life — evident, upon closer inspection.

The vegetation is not merely present; it is the very foundation of this place, and upon that which makes sore of it. Every building — or what might have once been buildings — is now a thicket of twisted, gnarled plant matter, roots delving deep into the ancient stone to extract whatever minerals they can. Plants overrun every surface, their tendrils lacing across the damp ground like veins, and under, the spread of fungus networks, white and black tendrils of mycelium invading and controlling and bursting up into the next lifeform in a violent cycle. Walls of verdant, mossy growth enclose spaces that might once have been dwellings, and massive, twisted trees erupt from the ground like skyscrapers, their bark a twisted patchwork of rusted metal and lichen. They bear no leaves, but instead sprout massive, glowing orbs that cast an eerie, ethereal light over the landscape, their electric luminescence a bleed from the networks that are both natural and designed.

The plants don’t wholly bow to the terrestrial. Like ribs about a heart, like fields of yarn, like the spines of some soft deep-sea creature, they adhere only partially to our familiar body plan and layout, a reflection of the reality breakdown that has seeped deep into the world. The architectural plant structures are like tenements of homogeneity. Instead of the road networks traversed by machines and people, the roads built of dull shining entwined root structures amble in a meandering yet perfect rhythm, as if leading some progressive dance. The network of cerebral cables, bent and mangled, feeding the plant forms, the silent engine veins, the expanses of strange material, such as a layer of engineered substrate over cobblestone — they all blend across the landscape.

There stands, a naked framework of gorgeously functional counterpoint and violence, smokestacks belching out an alien flora of effluvia and connectors joining the netherworld of the flora and fauna. The rare fauna move through the plant-ecosystem like the thinnest wisp of wind among chimes, the veins of flowing fluid accompany them like sound, there is no clarity in what the fauna actually is, some odd projection of the creatures of the air and the earth. There are machines, but it is unclear whether these serve the rare creatures, or are keeping them hostage. The reality breakdown has offered a quiet and grand architecture to the forces in charge, and has made something new and not wholly inhospitable of it.

A constant hum of reverberating mechanics fills the air like a relentless industrial symphony that breaks through the boundary between machines and plants, the core febrile thrum and clatter of the life cycle, as mechanical and as natural as breathing. The heartbeat of loud reverberations carry over vast distances, a sign of life that stretches out like a hand failing to grasp. Sounds from the distant machines, whatever they are producing, reverberate, and plant-life do their job, throb and beat, they have stand-ins of fruiting bodies, of production, strange flowers giving off smaller, thinner, but equally violent dreams.

Yet for all of the place's strangeness and the vibrant abundance of its vegetation, there is a tangible scarcity. A sparseness of recognizable forms, an unsettling emptiness across the landscape, and a strange presence in the shining silence, the places where the minds of the rare animals foray in and lock in, taking to the sky. The failures of built architecture, the reality breakdown, the desire to reach for the sky in all things, and in this place. you can always hear electric internet bugs, feeding on the data of reality and softly lighting the way between the towering behemoths through the mist. They draw wisps about to wet the eternal lungs materializing in and out of consciousness among the turbulent vegetation through streets built of tendons and veins.

Against the backdrop of the ancient, worn hills, this landscape teems with a life that is alien, yet oddly harmonious, and its strangeness embodies the paroxysms of a life following the industrial techno-rhythms of a repeating dream. Seemingly boundless, the mesh of form and function, blindly contracting, a far-fetched regional whole extending past the immediate senses, a psychoactive landscape, connected to the outlying reality through the formwork of the old natural world. The gateways of a peculiar form of the urbanized triumph of nature, beautiful and unexploitable, a rich and unnamed bizarre weirdness reigns in the place, growing slowly and dawningly, a vibration channeled into and out of the tissues of plants. A massive, rhythmic, coupling verb, an all-cycle turned to its own ends, looking out past the gently fractured street veins, through the quiet roaring of the mists, and into the green-hearted cycle of the familiar dream.
