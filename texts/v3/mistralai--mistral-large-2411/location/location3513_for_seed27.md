In the heart of a world suspended in the final stages of its life, there exists a landscape that is both ancient and alien, where the familiar is twisted into strange new forms. This is a place of cool, dim light that casts long, dancing shadows, a place where the air is thin and the winds sing an eternal, mournful dirge. This is the High Chthonic Plain, a world that has settled into its final, stable state, where time itself seems to have grown weary and slow.

The terrain stretches out in vast, undulating waves, like a frozen sea. The elevation is high, and the plains are punctuated by towering spires of ancient, twisted plants, their forms both haunting and hauntingly familiar. These are not the plants of Earth, but a new kind of life, a plant-as-substrate ecosystem that has evolved to fill every niche, to become the architecture of the world itself. They loom with totem-like significance on the horizon.

There is no water here, not in the traditional sense. Instead, a fine, glittering mist of frozen crystals floats on the constant wind. These crystals are the lifeblood of the alien flora (whose empty and dehydrated seeds were disseminated by accident, beyond ancient exoplanetary terrain-forming projects), replaced by alien alloys and systems that allow life to persist in this dry, cold world. The plants themselves are scatterset and sparse, their forms both alien and eerily recognizable—towering things with stalks of metallic alloy and leaves of shimmering, iridescent materials that catch the dim light and cast it back in a kaleidoscope of colors and tones. Some have structures akin to buildings, ancient tenants from far-flung regions, inbuilt and near-forgotten. They hum and click and whir with life, their sounds blending with the wind's keening to create a symphony of strange music, loud and ever-present, yet consonant in futurism.

The fauna, transgenic tool beasts used to maintain these vast urban-plant systems, are abundant and equally as strange. Creatures that resemble Earth's own slugs show skin translucent and askew in iridescence. Far below their protective uneven surface many organs and colors work away. Creatures with the general form of insects skitter and clatter through the metallic underbrush, their wings glinting with a queer tech that seems to function (but does so without being easily understood) and some far stranger tapetum lucidum phenomena. And all these beings are cast in the glow of the alien flora, in the metallic plant structures that descend beneath the softly-animated luminescence, and liken to growths of living corals.

Overhead, things float, lighter than the already thin air would expect, moving in a strange and trembling tentative world, legions of differently sized objects (a manner of nano-powered particles float via some laminar drag technology, permanent on the isobars), suspending across a deep and rich panoply of color.
