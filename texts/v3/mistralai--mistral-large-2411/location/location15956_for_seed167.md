In the heart of a vast, tepid sea, there exists a place where the remnants of a once-great city lie submerged, stretching out in a sprawling, open expanse. The water here is deep, but not abysmal, with a warmth that is both comforting and slightly unsettling, as if the very sea is alive and breathing. The city, now a sunken metropolis, is a dense labyrinth of stone and ancient materials, weathered by time and water, yet firmly established in its underwater realm.

The buildings, though crumbling, still bear the signs of their former grandeur, their shapes softened by the ceaseless caress of the sea. The streets are now canals, the squares vast underwater plazas where schools of fish dart in synchronized ballets. There is a peculiar stillness here, a quietude that is not merely the absence of sound, but a living, breathing hush, as if the city itself is listening, waiting.

Vegetation is sparse, limited to the hardiest of seaweeds and corals that cling to the stone like stubborn barnacles. Yet, there is an unusual abundance of fauna. Bioluminescent creatures drift through the darkened streets, their soft glow casting eerie, shifting shadows on the ancient walls. Strange, ethereal jellyfish pulse gently, their forms delicate and otherworldly, like living lace caught in an underwater breeze. The sounds are subtle, the occasional whisper of a school of fish changing direction, the faint clicks and hums of unseen creatures echoing through the water.

The rules of this world are simple and ancient. The sea gives, and the sea takes. The city, once a bustling hub of human activity, now belongs to the ocean, its cycles and tides the only dynamics that matter. Yet, there is a sense of balance, of harmony. The city, though drowned, is not dead, but alive in a new way, its heartbeat the steady pulse of the sea.

In this quiet, watery realm, time seems to move differently, marked not by the ticking of clocks, but by the slow dance of shadow and light, the gentle ebb and flow of the tide. There is a sense of mystery here, a weirdness that is not frightening, but awe-inspiring. It is a place of endings and beginnings, a testament to the relentless march of time and the enduring power of nature. As you move through the submerged streets, the silence seems to whisper stories of the past, and the dark water around you pulses with the promise of secrets yet to be revealed.
