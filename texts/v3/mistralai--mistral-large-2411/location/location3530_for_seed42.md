In the heart of a high, cool basin, a place both familiar and unusual has taken shape over eons. The sky is a vault of silver, whispering clouds that shed an endless drizzle, creating a wall of gentle rain that feeds the landscape below. This place is known as the Living Cistern, aCathedral-sized sanctuary perched atop an ancient, weathered plateau.

The ground is not mere earth but a interwoven tapestry of plant life, a spongy, undulating substrate that drinks deeply from the eternal rain. The vegetation is so dense and so varied that it defies categorization—a living mass that is both substrate and ecosystem. Mosses and lichens form the foundation, their undulating forms interwoven with ferns, horsetails, and a myriad of flowering plants that have adapted to this waterlogged world. Above them, towering aquatic flora stretches skyward, their roots firmly anchored in the saturated ground, creating a canopy that filters the steady rain. The plants here have evolved to thrive in this cool environment, their forms elegant yet hardy, with broad leaves that funnel water down to their roots.

Water flows freely through this landscape, meandering in rivulets and streams that crisscross the spongy ground. Pools form in depressions, their surfaces shimmering with gentle ripples, reflecting the soft, silvery light filtering through the canopy above. The air is thick with moisture, a fine mist that clings to every surface, beading on leaves and dripping from branches in a never-ending cycle.

The sound of water is ever-present, a constant symphony of drips, trickles, and gentle splashes. Yet, there is more to the acoustic tapestry of this place. The flora itself adds to the cacophony, with leaves rustling, stems creaking, and peculiar resonating chambers within some of the larger plants emitting low, resonant hums. The calls of the abundant fauna also fill the air—the chirps, croaks, and trills of amphibians and birds echoing through the mist. These creatures have adapted to their waterlogged home, evolving unique features like webbed feet, powerful swimming tails, and specialized fur that traps air for insulation and buoyancy.

Scattered throughout the Living Cistern are small, primitive dwellings, built from the very plants that make up the landscape. These structures are carefully cultivated and coaxed into shape by the inhabitants, a quiet, contemplative people who live in harmony with their environment. They use simple hand tools of bone and stone to tend their living architecture, fostering growth and adapting their homes to their needs. The buildings blend seamlessly with the surroundings, their organic forms indistinguishable from the natural growth except for the faint traces of human intention.

The weirdness of this place is subtle, revealing itself only upon close inspection. Some of the plants bear unusual features—leaves that shiver and change color with the flow of water, or flowers that bloom in intricate, seemingly impossible patterns. There are whispers of plant life that mimics animal behavior, and stories of creatures that blur the line between fauna and flora. The inhabitants embrace these peculiarities, incorporating them into their myths, stories, art and daily life.

Time moves slowly here, marked by the gradual ebb and flow of water and the patient growth of plants. The Living Cistern is a place of gentle cycles, of subtle change wrested from the steady flow of time and water. It is a sanctuary, a quiet, cool haven of life and growth, suspended high above the world in its ancient, weathered basin. A place both familiar and unusual, where the boundaries between plant, animal, and human blur, and life flows as freely as the water that sustains it.
