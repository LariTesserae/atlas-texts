In the heart of a sprawling, high-elevation plateau, a city stands, unlike any other. It is not a city of green parks and towering trees, but of mineral forests and crystalline outcrops.

Terrain is a spread of towers, not grown from seeds, but extruded from the very earth — pillars of twisted obsidian and basalt; glassy rocks weep water beneath a relentless sun. The ground is a stark mineral canvas of black and grey, veined with silver and quartz, reflecting the harsh, bright light that beats down from above. The air is thick, a humid heat that shimmers above the ground, occasionally condensing into strange, silent mists that drift through the city's streets. These mists, seemingly alive, leave trails of moisture that seep into the porous stone, only to evaporate moments later, a cycle as old as the city itself.

The streets are not paved but carved by time and wind, a labyrinth of disparate minerals stitched together in an intricate puzzle. The few constructed elements — bridges of steel, railway paths, walls of baked brick — wear an aged patina, as if even human endeavors have succumbed to the timeless erosion that has shaped this place. Buildings thrust skyward like wind-carved hoodoos, windows of steam-frosted glass and towering smokestacks merging with the mineral spires that vie for height.

The city hums with an industrial heart, yet it is eerily silent, save for the distant hum of machines buried within the factories. The silence is a thick, almost physical presence, a juxtaposition to the relentless activity within the city's core. Mechanized beasts of steel and brass trundle through the streets, their gears grinding quietly, like the hushed whispers of ancient automatons. They leave trails of glistening water, condensed from the hot air, a strange mimicry of life in this barren, fungal-dominated world.

Beasts and other lifeforms populate this landscape, born from a biome that pegs its future on heat, moisture, and inorganic chemistry. They are abundant, strange creatures with glistening, translucent forms, moving with an alien grace through the misty streets in silence. These things have hollow bones like glass pipes, complex organs like condensing pumps and valves, webs of veins that carry superheated water rather than blood. Spiral tendrils rather than limbs.

Despite the industrial density and construction made by its previous inhabitants, the emptiness is palpable. Structures are scattered, with vast, open spaces between them — plazas of serrated stone, parks of mineral pools where creatures drift like ghosts. The city extends for miles, a sprawling monument to its own strange existence, a testament to the adaptability of life in a world that seems to defy it.

This high plateau seems to float, with elevations making it hard to breathe, a dry air that defies the heat. The horizon is teased with smudges of blue that frame the city and crumble into great descents into mountainous valleys, each visible against the horizon.

Things move in this vast, open world. Things that fight, mate, grow, and change. There is a clear need, which in the organic world comes in two forms: eating and not being eaten. Things capture fleeting fog in spans of tendril mesh, or push steam from body vents. Predators work on siphoning inner heat by means of quiet arms that entangle.

This is a plateau on the edge of time, a city of industry and silence, a world of heat and water and strange, glassy life. It is a place of stark contrasts and eerie beauty, where the rules of the world as we know it bend and twist in ways both wondrous and unsettling. The eternal fog and weather carve the place; the glass life bends the order of this world, working towards the emergence of something. At night, the brightness of it lessens, but the work never ends, the tiny clicks of beasts and rigs carrying on, the shuffling of steam and pressure as all things proceed to survive.
