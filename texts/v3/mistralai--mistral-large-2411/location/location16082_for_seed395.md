In the quiet, emerging expanse of the Silvyr Reach, the air is thick with spores and possibility. This is a world in its adolescence, stretching out into a vast, warm, and densely packed landscape that is both alien and familiar. The terrestrial rules we know have been twisted gently, like a young tree seeking the sun. Here, from the silent halls of the nascent buildings that are no buildings burgeon a massive fungal ecology with nothing in all of its diverse life cycles akin to water.

What sprawls before us is not so much a terrain but a living network of puffball towers. They bloom sequentially in fractal patterns across the landscape. Pale on pale, their surfaces range from bone white to an almost translucent gray. These formations skyward like the domes of some cyclopean city, but they are not constructed in the manner of buildings intimated by the urban density. Instead, they are grown and purposeful, as if directed by some ancient, vegetal will. They are absolute in their coverage of the landscape. All that can be seen, from one end of the vast horizon to the other, is the pallid corpus of the fungal expanse, a single organism on a planetary scale. The sense is of being intrepid motes in a kingdom of silent, alien biology.

The warmth here does not come from a sun or any sort of atmosphere familiar to our experience, but moderates from within the fungal forest itself. Something like the reaction that in our world causes mushrooms to glow plays out here on a colossal scale, stoked by the slow combustion of planetary energy within the heartwood core of what must be interminable miles of thick, pale mycelium below.  This quiet, dry, pulsating heat nourishes a space where liquid water is alien, a place abstinent of that liquid favorable to ordinary life.

Everywhere, a dense active silence prevails, the silence of growth and intention. There is no wind as we know it, to disrupt the quietude or to carry sound; only a faint susurration, suggestion to even more growth. A muted rasping, as of tendon against bone, or a knife through sand, a legion of whisperers almost too faint to catch, but constant. For there are creatures here, denizens shaped by their ancient fungal world. Like its biophysics, they defy our expectations. Hovering, floating on the thermals of silent heat, translucent skinned predators glide from tower to tower, seeking prey. The larger ones bite, the smaller alight and puncture with a long spine. Many of the strange creatures grab and drag fungal matter and organic debris away with their mouth engineering. Spores waft through the air and across the wordless expanse here like gliding pollen. Yet, in this landscape of weirdness oddly mundane the spores are not as dense a presence as a thing that will eat other things so that they do not fill their lungs and bellies.  Amongst the slow-slumbering, gently breathing life of the pale giants, things eat, are eaten and grow.

Where we might expect shadows to be cast by the diffuse light throughout the vast reach, there is instead luminescence, concentrated under the caps and huffing glowing wafts of spores into the space above. The slowly writhing, uneroded ground is alive and untrammeled by the passage of beings; instead, it constitutes the whole ecology, both feeder and fed. Thus this place has a strange factory-fresh quality, which might disturb our Earth preconceptions, kicks inhibitions to embrace the exotic.

There is a sense of deep time here, of aeons passing at a glacial pace. With no water actuating erosion, time is slow, a key altered ever so slightly to the bass so low as to be universally registered, the moment of the fungal pulse barely perceptible unless you know how to listen. What here we witness is undoubtedly primordial, enduring, infinite in its ambition. It is a land before time, a place both alien and immediately, deeply familiar to the imagination. The Silvyr Reach is one made almost entirely of permutations of life, an encompassing network of ancient life striving ever forward.

All of this is in fact bizarre, we feel, but described by entirely normal terms. If the rules of the world, here over scale of its own physics have nothing in common except in familiarity, mere high temperature, or large living things, anything truthfully going on within the Reach will be nothing like it has conventionally been. We need to invent descriptions beared on predicates that are not inconsistent with those things. The underlying equations are subtly idiosyncratic; one feels they are quite aside the grammar/ sense of our laws. But, the ingredients enigmatic in several aspects they do not blunt natural vocabulary. The conditions are comprehensible. This place follows some different dense dry existence principles that leave it its otherself recognizable for what it could be.
