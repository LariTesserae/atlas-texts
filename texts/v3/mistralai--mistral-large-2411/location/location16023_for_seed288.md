In the heart of a vast, cool, and dim subterranean expanse lies a landscape of ancient, weathered majesty, a place called the Ebon Expanse. The air is dry, parched, and electric, not quite suitable for water to exist in liquid form, as a viscous, heavy haze rises towards the irradiant minerals embedded in the cavern ceiling. This is like a place shaped by aeons, yet preserved by the slow passage of time.

The terrain is a sprawling, open network of titanic, twisted formations—not quite stalagmites, but something more exotic and alien. They stretch out as far as the eye can see. Each colossal helix is comprised of a peculiar mineral that emanates a faint, cool luminescence, casting the expanse in a dim, ethereal glow. Most of the landscape is flat and featureless, as erosion has spent itself into a state of timeless equilibrium. Between the scattered, towering structures lie vast fields of luxuriant, bioluminescent flora, low-lying and spreading like moss or lichen.

Vegetation thrives in vivid contrast to the austere surroundings, the company of fungi that are rich, soft, and dense. They are all cool to the touch; there is no warmth down here. These plants bear no flowers or fruit, but instead feathery fronds that wave softly, as if underwater, and emit faint puffs of glowing spores. They paint the landscape with hues of blue and violet, randomly scattered in the infinite space - mostly wide, flat portions of hardened layers, sometimes heaps of accumulated matter. The vegetation natively clusters in wide bands that appear to radiate from the towering structures, giving the impression of colossal, glowing fingers stretching into the void.

The open architecture of mineral formations might remind one of a city laid bare, devoid of inhabitants yet echoing with the ever-present din of unseen fauna. These structures, while natural, mimic the silhouettes of grand cathedrals or colossal monuments. There are complex tangles of side passages and perforations, which might well confound any creature who isn't aware of the underlying logic. Some have gaping maws, wide enough to be open caverns, filled with more growth.

Columns echo with a cacophony of sound—a symphony of roars, howls, chitters, and screams that verge on deafening. The fauna here are abundant and diverse, ranging from diminutive, pallid critters skittering across the stone to colossal, segmented leviathans that lumber through the shadows, and our guesses stop there because they never reveal themselves.

You will never see a single creature, but the ruckus is always around you. Loping echoes imply gross stomping comparable to a dinosaur, slithering sounds suggest a writhing hive of life crawling on linoleum, claws skitter like glaciers dropping boulders with glass-like timbre, whirlpools of panicking rodents, flapping scaly wings that slice the stillness. Despite their abundance, the creatures keep to the shadows, unseen hunters stalking unseen prey in an endless, invisible dance, and only the noises reveal this dark macrocosm of shambolic and gregarious life.

Perhaps the strangest feature of this landscape is the almost complete lack of technological advancement in its natural development, as if it evolved in isolation from the rest of the universe. One might be blindsided and perceive the underground lattice as a colossal construction site, but it is, in fact, shaped entirely by slow geological and biological processes. The structures have a fractal elegance, shaped by the flows of air and the dim chemical wisdom of the vegetation, which pseudoroots burrow into the mineral substrate, leaching nutrition and slowly altering its makeup. A genetic memory of slow organic growth resonates here.

The environment is united made whole and consistent by a deep overarching peace. The cool, dim air and its muffled sense of sight wrapped in darkness simply extend forever. This constant stasis of existence in the present is like living out of time. The dimness gently renders the viewer's field into an eternal calm. There are references to celestial motion in that prairie of glowing spores, somewhere down there the clock is ticking, and one day it will run out of time, flatten, erode, pass away. Clearly, a whiff of shallow, dying stasis, a sense of a world retiring into the shallow pool of low-grade heat, is palpable against the backdrop of organic efflorescence. Here, fauna somehow emerges out of the darkness or perhaps simply moves around, never seen, only suggested by the cacophony of sounds.

Every shadow in every vast space holds the secret looming horror. The strange flora is perfectly accustomed to living in this craze of shadows. Putting the technological stage aside, there is no reason to think this vast chamber is anything more than natural. There are rumors of structures that are mere solidified echoes of foregone times when this place was a lively engineering facility. There are no concrete clues and no way of finding the truth. The wild zoo of living things produces unworldly sounds, creating illusions and allusions. This colorful, strange, confusing noise keeps the deafening silence of this underground kingdom at bay.
