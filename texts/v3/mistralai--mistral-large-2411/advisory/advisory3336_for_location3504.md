Preparing to visit this aquatic, otherworldly realm requires meticulous planning, specialized equipment, and a significant dose of adaptability and mental fortitude. Here's a comprehensive guide for an ordinary human multiverse traveler:

1. **Diving Training:** Since this is an underwater environment, obtain advanced scuba diving certification. You should be comfortable with deep dives, strong currents, and low visibility.

**Equipment:**

1. **Diving Gear:** Invest in high-quality, durable scuba gear, including a wetsuit designed for warm water, a buoyancy compensator device (BCD), regulators, and a dive computer. Given the viscous nature of the water, ensure your gear is robust and can withstand these conditions.

2. **Rebreather:** Consider using a closed-circuit rebreather to extend your dive time and reduce the number of bubbles you'll release, which could disrupt the environment or attract unwanted attention.

3. **Helmet and Communications:** A full-face diving helmet with built-in communications will protect your head and allow you to stay in touch with any fellow travelers.

4. **Lighting:** Equip yourself with multiple, powerful underwater flashlights to combat the dim, ethereal glow and disorienting shadows.

5. **Navigation:** A reliable underwater compass and GPS designed for diving will help you navigate the ever-changing landscape.

6. **Protection:** Given the unpredictable nature of the wildlife, wear protective gloves and consider carrying a non-lethal deterrent, like an underwater loud noise maker, to scare off potential threats.

7. **Artifact Collection:** Bring a durable, waterproof bag or container to collect any artifacts or samples you might find.

**Health and Safety:**

1. **Vaccinations:** Although there's no way to know what pathogens might exist in this otherworldly water, ensure you're up-to-date on standard vaccinations and consider getting any relevant travel vaccines.

2. **First Aid:** Bring an extensive first aid kit, and learn advanced first aid and CPR skills, focusing on wilderness and dive medicine.

3. **Decompression:** Familiarize yourself with decompression procedures and the risks of deep diving, including nitrogen narcosis and decompression sickness.

4. **Mental Health:** Prepare for the psychological challenges of diving in a disorienting, ever-changing environment filled with eerie sounds and distorted reality. Consider basic mental resilience training.
