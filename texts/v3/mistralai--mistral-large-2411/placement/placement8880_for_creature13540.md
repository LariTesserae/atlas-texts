As I delve deeper into this world, I find myself not as a single entity, but as a symbiotic collective of lichen, inhabiting the vast, rusted surfaces of the forgotten industrial complex. I am a union of fungus and algae, bound together in a mutually beneficial relationship that has allowed me to thrive in this unique environment.

I am not a singular lichen, but a community of the many. I am the rusty orange of Xanthoria parietina, sprawling across the decaying metal surfaces, my thallus drawing nourishment from the very rust that consumes my substrate. I am the ghostly gray-green of Lepraria membranacea, my scale-like form camouflaging myself against the dull, cracked concrete. I am the dusty, jade-hued splotches of Temora aurlenta, clinging to the shadier recesses, my chalky surface a stark contrast to the gloom. We are all connected, a vast network of shared existence, our lives intertwined with the fate of this industrial oasis.

My fungal components, the mycobionts, sank their filaments deep into the decaying surfaces, drawing moisture and vital nutrients from the decaying structures. My algal and cyanobacterial components, the photobionts, use the scarce light filtering through the grimy skylights to create food for us through photosynthesis, to capture the energy that feeds our collective existence. This harmony allows us to endure, and even thrive, within this peculiar sanctuary. All around me is the steady hum of symbiosis as my collective experiences the intricate dance of give and take, in a living tapestry of complex relationships.

I find myself most concentrated in the damper, shadier corners of the complex, where the air is thick with humidity and the scent of damp earth and decaying metal. Here, I cling to the cool surfaces of rusted pipes, my thallus drinking deeply from the condensation that forms upon them. Above me, the jack-o'-lantern mushrooms cast their ethereal glow, their presence a comforting beacon in the shadowy recesses.

My world is one of subtle, constant change. The slow decay of the industrial complex is my sustenance, each flake of rust and crumble of concrete a new opportunity for growth. I watch as the metal surfaces oxidize, their once-sharp edges softening as they succumb to the relentless march of time. The concrete too, crumbles and pits, each new fissure an invitation for me to take root.

The creatures of this world move around and over and through me, their lives intertwined with my own. The metallic, cockroach-like insects scuttle across my surface, their iridescent forms a stark contrast to my earthy tones. The adapted rodents, with their unusual fur and reflective eyes, scamper past, their tiny paws brushing against me, dislodging microscopic fragments that float gently to the ground below. The birds, with their strange calls and metallic whispers, roost above me, their droppings a welcome source of nutrients.

The remnants of the complex's industrial past are my home, my sustenance, my very existence. I cling to the rusted boiler, my orange and gray forms a stark contrast to the faded reds and yellows of the decaying metal. I sprawl across the fallen conveyor belt, my dusty jade thallus blending seamlessly with the faded green of the canvas. I am a living testament to the resilience of life, to the adaptability of nature, and to the strange beauty that can emerge from the remnants of industry.

Yet, I am also a silent witness to the fragility of this world. The boundaries of my Microcosm are starkly delineated by the walls of the factory, beyond which lies a different, harsher reality. I know that were the cyclical processes of decay and rebirth to cease, the delicate balance of this ecosystem would be disrupted and I could likely be relegated to mere survival. Beyond, in the world, lichen survival strategies in harsh environments rely on spreading and inheritance, but for I it is rather how to act as a collective and this is a world of symbiosis and interconnectedness, at yet distinct delicateness.

My world is, as yet, The Lichen Microcosm. We, of the lichen collective, are one with what we depend upon and what depends on us. We our own Gaia, through all our diversity in our common biological performed function, thriving within the shell of the old and the forgotten.

The lichen collective is one of many-as-one who have become the habitat as much as the denizen; life is sustained in this delicate balance, with the delicacy of our own individual nature juxtaposed with the seeming absolutism of the industrial machine we inhabit. We are life flourishing in the old and the forgotten. I am this world, thriving in decay. Where this world ends, I end as well.
