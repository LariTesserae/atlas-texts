In this subaqueous sanctuary, the inhabitants are as unique and adapted as the city itself. The most prominent residents are not merely creatures, but embodiments of the sea's will, its memory, and its dreams.

The **Oblivion Sirens** are the city's archivists, their forms reminiscent of humanoid shadows, with long, flowing tendrils instead of hair. Their eyes are pools of inky blackness, reflecting the city's past as if on a dark mirror. They are not malevolent, but melancholic, their songs echoing through the water, a haunting symphony that tells the tale of the city's drowning. They inhabit the grandest of the submerged buildings, their voices painting images of the past onto the canvas of the listened mind with such clarity you can see the colors of the flowers that once bloomed in the now-flooded gardens. They have been known to lead curious creatures and even the rare, intrepid human visitor to pockets of air within the ruined buildings, where they can converse in oddly comfortable, if damp, silence. While they are powerful, their intentions are benign, for they are less interested in destruction than in ensuring the city's history is not forgotten.

The **Coral constructs** are the city's builders and maintainers, ancient entities comprised of countless polyps, their forms shifting and changeless as the city itself. They move slowly, their countless tiny mouths siphoning nutrients from the water, their forms calcifying into new structures that blend seamlessly with the existing architecture. They are the reason the city, despite its submersion, has not crumbled entirely. Guided by an instinct born of the sea itself, they reinforce and rebuild, their labors often resulting in structures that defy human logic, but possess an organic, otherworldly beauty. Satellite clusters of polyps exhibit limited sentience, together constituting a hive mind which simply *knows* which fantastic architectural feats improve the city.

The **Lumina**, bioluminescent creatures akin to giant, floating comb jellies, serve as the city's light-bearers. Their delicate, translucent forms drift through the streets, their pulsating lights casting ethereal glows on the ancient walls. They are drawn to activity, their lights intensifying in response to motion, illuminating paths for the city's other inhabitants. They are revered by the other creatures, for light is life in the shadowy depths. The Lumina, in turn, seem to enjoy the attention, often congregating around other beings, their lights flickering in complex patterns that hint at a form of communication yet to be understood.

The **Scavenger Urchins** are the city's cleaners, their small, spiky forms scuttling along the sea floor, their spines collecting debris and detritus. They are not merely scavengers, but recyclers, their bodies breaking down waste, converting it into nutrients that fertilize the hardy seaweeds and corals. They are the city's unsung heroes, their tireless efforts keeping the waters clear and the ecosystem balanced. Occasionally, they gather around larger objects, a monumental shell, for instance, or the sunken debris of a more recent interloper, rotating it between their spiny masses, this too will be broken down, converted and made nutrient.

There are whispers among the city's inhabitants of another entity, one seldom seen but often felt. The **Mare Somnum**, a vast, amorphous being said to embody the spirit of the sea itself. It is said to dwell in the city's heart, its form shifting like a dream, its presence felt as a profound tranquility, a serenity that is almost sentient. It is said that the Mare Somnum can grant visions of the future to those who seek it, but such audiences are rare and unpredictable, for the spirit communicates in the language of tides and currents, a tongue few can comprehend.

This watery realm is not merely a habitat, but a community, a tapestry woven from the threads of time, memory, and adaptation. Each inhabitant plays a role, their lives intertwined, their fates bound to the city and the sea that claims it. They live in harmony, their existence a testament to nature's resilience and its profound, enduring mystery.
