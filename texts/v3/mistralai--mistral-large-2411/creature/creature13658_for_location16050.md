In this vast, hollow expanse, where the sky is an endless dome of soft, diffused light, the inhabitants are as unique and diverse as the landscape itself. The denizens of this realm are both ephemeral and enduring, shaped by the ancient, slow march of time and the dynamic, active ecology of the plateau.

The most prominent residents are the colossal, plant-formed structures that stretch towards the heavens. These are not merely plants, but living, breathing architects of the ecosystem. They are titans of the vegetal world, their bodies formed from densely interwoven vines and creepers, their foliage a dense, verdant canopy that teems with life. These plant-titans are the heart of this world, their roots delving deep into the ancient stone, creating networks of caverns and tunnels. Their branches intertwine to form lofty cathedrals, beneath which a myriad of creatures make their homes. They are the ecosystem's substrate, the architecture upon which all else is built. These plants are ancient, wise, and slow, their thoughts and movements imperceptible to the fleeting lives of the fauna that inhabit them. They communicate in whispered Chemical signals, their conversations carried on the wind, their memories stored in the very fabric of the landscape.

Among the dense foliage and the towering branches, avian creatures make their homes. They are a raucous, vibrant lot, their feathers a riot of colors that stand out against the verdant backdrop. These birds are not mere singers of songs; they are storytellers, their calls a complex narrative that speaks of the plateau's history and their own adventures. They are the plateau's memory, their songs a living, breathing record of the world's past. Among them are the tiny, iridescent hummers, their wings a blur as they dart from flower to flower, sipping nectar and pollinating the plants. There are also the massive, ponderous rocs, their wingspan casting vast shadows over the canopy as they soar on the warm, moist air currents.

In the heart of the plateau, where the warmth and moisture cling to the air like a whispered secret, insects thrive. They are the unseen orchestrators of the ecosystem's symphony, their constant, throbbing hum the heartbeat of the world. They are everywhere, from the tiniest of mites that dwell in the soil, to the colossal, lumbering beetles that trundle along the branches of the plant-titans. They are the plateau's recyclers, their mandibles and mouthparts constantly breaking down and rebuilding, turning decay into new life. Among them are the industrious weavers, their silk a shimmering tapestry that stretches across the branches, capturing moisture and prey in equal measure. There are also the fearsome hunters, their bodies armored and weaponized, their lives a constant, dynamic struggle for survival.

The shallow depressions and meandering rivulets that pock the plateau are home to a different kind of life. Here, in the transient pools that shimmer with life, amphibious creatures dwell. They are the plateau's opportunists, their lives played out in bursts of activity and periods of dormancy. They are masters of metamorphosis, their bodies shifting and changing with the coming and going of the water. Among them are the croaking singers, their voices a deep, resonant bass that echoes through the emptiness. There are also the silent hunters, their bodies sleek and streamlined, their movements a barely perceptible ripple on the water's surface.

The ancient, jagged edges of the plateau are home to a different kind of life. Here, where the warm, drying caress of the atmosphere is strongest, reptilian creatures bask in the golden glow. They are the plateau's sentinels, their eyes constantly scanning the horizon, their bodies perfectly still as they soak up the heat. Among them are the scaled leviathans, their bodies massive and ponderous, their tails ending in a whip-like appendage that can shatter stone. There are also the fleet-footed runners, their bodies lithe and agile, their movements a blur as they dart across the ancient stone.

The networks of caverns and tunnels created by the roots of the plant-titans are not empty. Here, in the cool, damp darkness, mammalian creatures make their homes. They are the plateau's engineers, their powerful claws and teeth constantly reshaping the tunnels, creating complex, interconnected warrens. Among them are the blind, pale diggers, their bodies perfectly adapted to a life in the dark. There are also the sleek, furry hunters, their bodies agile and powerful, their senses honed to perfection.

Yet, even in this world of contradictions and wonders, there are whispers of a long-forgotten people. The crumbling stone walls and weed-choked plinths speak of a time when this realm was not quite so empty, when hands wielding simple tools shaped stone and wood to their will. The inhabitants of this realm, though diverse and unique, share a common thread. They are all shaped by the slow, relentless march of time, their lives played out against the unchanging backdrop of the plateau. They are the denizens of this ancient, active, unusual yet strangely familiar world. They are the heart of this deafening symphony, the essence of this profoundly alive realm.

Look more closely now. Squatting or walking just under gravity's scattered archers, you may see the shadows. Or rather, perhaps it's easier to see that they are not shadows, but a new scale of life. They are fungal, soft, lumpy pillows, mobile and quorate, all linked by mycelial sheets, talking to each other in little chemically amorous murmurations. They seem to roll across the landscape, with tiny smoky spores curling up in deliberately ghostly writing. You will find them where life has forgotten to look after itself.

They whisper.

Some people say that they hold the essence of the ancient folk. The forgetful, hidden, but not gone. Perhaps they were stopping making their handprints. No one quite remembers why.

The fungi can rise from the ground and hover for a while, leaning over the remaining neolithic monuments whispering every year fewer letters in a lost language, of an ever-scattering mythical history.
