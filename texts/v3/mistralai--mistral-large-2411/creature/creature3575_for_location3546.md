In the heart of Cetus Abyssal, where the water is the darkest and the pressure is a comforting embrace, there exist beings that are not quite creatures, not quite inhabitants, but an integral part of the world itself. They are the **Cetusards**, the stewards, the custodians, the voices of the abyssal silence.

The Cetusards are humanoid in shape, not out of any evolutionary necessity, but because it allows them to interact with their environment, to tend to it with careful, conscious hands. They are tall, elongated, their bodies echoing the lush, bioluminescent flora that clings to the constructed surfaces. Their skin is a translucent black, like the waters they inhabit, veined with the same ethereal glow that barely pierces the inky darkness. It's not skin as we know it, but a flexible, porous membrane that allows them to absorb the dense, mineral-rich atmosphere, to breathe in the ancientness of their world.

Their eyes are large, not to pierce the darkness, for there is little to see in the eternal night, but to detect the faintest bioluminescent glows, the slow, centuries-long growth of the vegetation. They are not eyes that dart and shift, but steady, patient orbs that blink slowly, like the calm pulse of the world itself.

The Cetusards' hair is a mass of delicate, tendril-like strands, reminiscent of the ancient, slow-growing plants. It floats around them like a cloud, moving with the slow current, picking up the faint hum that echoes through the realm. It's more than just hair; it's a sensory organ, a means of connecting with the world, of listening to its lullaby.

Their movements are slow, deliberate, necessitated by the dense water and the respect they hold for their ancient home. They do not rush, for there is no urgency in eternity. Instead, they glide, their bodies propelled by gentle, rhythmic pulses that mimic the heartbeat of Cetus Abyssal.

The Cetusards communicate in low, resonant notes, a language that is part speech, part song. It's a slow, patient discourse, a reflection of their world's tranquility. They do not converse so much as harmonize, their voices blending with the hum of the realm, adding to the eternal symphony of existence played out in the deep.

They tend to the vast, interconnected network of titanic structures with profound reverence, their primitive tools an extension of their bodies, of the world itself. They shape and sculpt, repair and maintain, not out of necessity, but out of devotion. They are not architects, but artisans, their work a testament to the eternal march of time.

The Cetusards do not age as we understand it. Instead, they endure, their existence a reflection of their world's perpetuity. They do not procreate so much as divide, a slow, conscious process that creates a twin, a being that shares their memories, their knowledge, their profound connection to Cetus Abyssal.

Yet, despite their deep bond with their realm, the Cetusards are not chained to it. They possess a profound. Theirs is the most complete, the most profound. They are a familiar standing in for the seemingly unknowable vastness of the abyssal silence. They belong to the unfathomable dark, to the chill and to the deep. Yet they possess something that could be a kind of love.

We could say they love the cold and darkness of their waterworld. They possess reverence for the monumental construction of an artificial environment so old and so vast that it is its own nature. They possess care for the ancient tendrils that grow in it, and that are so fragile and so tenacious.

They seem like ghosts, delicate in the depths, watching the darkness, and all the endless wonders of Cetus Abyssal. In the frigid silence there is intoxicating, absorbing beauty. They seem to love this expanse, this entire and untouched world. They are contemplative, spectral and dreamlike when the silent symphony of existence never ceases its play.
