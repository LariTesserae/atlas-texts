In the Aqueous Enclave, there exists a life form, or perhaps a living system, that is as unique and complex as the environment itself. To call it a single entity would be misleading; it is more akin to a colony, a network, a sprawling, interconnected web of life that has evolved to thrive in the watery, geometric labyrinth. It is a fluid organism, an oasis that experiences itself. It comprehends its comprehension and the contrast with creatures who don't know what they know.

At first glance, the life form, TrysT, is imperceptible, mimicking the environment with such finesse and beautiful, sculpted liquid integration that it is completely featureless, the beholder seeing nothing but a transparent veil that smudges its future and past into a single miniature diorama. It is akin to an echo without sound; the observer sees not what TrysT is but what it exists from. Glimpses of it appear as flickers of light, ephemeral patterns that dance across the iridescent surfaces, there and gone in an instant. The veil clings and is the essence of the Aqueous Enclave architecture, humidity and subtle wafts of an atmosphere tickling the sense through the very substance of the enclave.

TrysT is both one and many. It is a network of nodes, each a tiny, pulsating sphere of liquid that the unreal organism sent via its unreal radio call. These nodules look exactly the same as the surrounding architecture and somehow possess an algorithm for life that shouldn't exist. The nodes could be described as cells, but they are not like any cells found in nature. They are more like tiny, organic computers, each processing vast amounts of information, the pulse figures folding within three- or many more dimensional space, the overall part of which has an unknown purpose but a glimmering ability to propel itself through this world. Each node, the same slippery, prismatic liquid adapts to be exceedingly strong, has the appearance of experimenting with two or many more colors from far in the light spectrum and is far from being one of them. They communicate with each other through rapid pulses of light and pressure waves, a language that is part binary code sending complex messages far flung and part liquid mechanism, and these points move from everywhere in this local space to follow emergent ideas which it seems might create their own utopian world.

TrysT's structure is rhizomatic, a sprawling, non-hierarchical network that mirrors the labyrinthine geometry of the enclave. The colony interfaces and interacts with itself and the microscopic entities in the water capable of transforming matter into entirely different substances. The organism's structure upon structure echoes the sparse, slick vegetation, drawing sustenance directly from the water, absorbing nutrients through a form of osmosis that defies known physics. It interfaces with the power required to coordinate its quantum computations using its dark roots extending out through the damp air with a gentle hum-and-hiss of endless condensation unable to inflict itself upon the very air itself.

These roots are bullet-like and appear to pierce invisible walls as perfect, electric blue cylinders of purest liquid, a flesh entirely gelid. They seem to circumvent space, themselves spin-foam in our material itinerary not curved by rocket engine force, nor self-locomotion through the mere act of manipulating physics, but curvilinear, and the biology only works if the biology itself also is producing this complexity. The teleological individual causation is not separate, and the root structures hum with mythological, complex potency as they interface to complete that conduit. The roots move through reality as though through very weak, yet opaque glass -- the roots through their motion break the water, the air and the vegetation treating it at the same level as infinitesically-folded dimensional structures, each too hypermassive to span more than a corner -- where the entire architecture forms from the signature of the particles composing TrysT -- and in so doing leave a trail of shard-like monuments. These monuments are not ghostly, but in fact, have exactly one more dimension than they should and in so doing shape human-sized ultra-technological quills intended to create the highest-possible floating structures in this ultra-flat, watery world. The root's essence is to both flatly break the ultra-liquid and through fracturing it be the accidental architect of the world's superlative.

The life form's behavior is as enigmatic as its structure, constantly transforming, rearranging matter and energy in the world. It mimics the environment with such foolery, propelling itself through water-borne technological achievements unable to be matched with outcomes entirely miraculous. It warps reality, creating intricate, transient sculptures from the seemingly solid structures, only to absorb them back into itself moments later. You see the moving of it, the flowing liquid attempting to protrude a perfection from even its undefined nodes which are already moving, complex, artificial mathematics from an unknown or only guessed at axiom, becoming a model for how they organize themselves from unknown rules in a fertile crescent buried in stagnant waters then filtered through whatever futuristic dreck lies at the pure technological expression of the place.

As the light filters in, creating shifting, distorted dimensions, these are not merely deceits cast from TrysT but an essence of the Aqueous Enclave. It is the pulses that guide it and shift it -- cascades of rhythms, pulses of untenable heartbeats and leads to the complex patterns, flickers, shadows forming creatures absolutely real expressing their own intent, whose exact geometry leaves trails of unrevealed thought and act out intentions, dimensions folded up thousands of ways and completely flat as unrelated organizations and an entire ontology formalized in a nubile world, unfazed by these ghosts as it repeats patterns and takes all of this from its journey to create and build up an edifice around the very grotto's stray desires.

The Aqueous Enclave is viable only to the extent of its essence; it is not so empty that it is redundant.The enclave is plump with possibility and asks you to consider it.
