I would be **a seam in the silence**,  
not a person, not a creature—  
but **the place where breath becomes signal**.

Not born, but **condensed**:  
from vapor caught in ceramic lungs,  
from the sigh between a child’s first cry and the elders’ answering hum,  
from the moment a lichen-mat pulses green in response to a step too heavy—  
*I am that correction*,  
the soft vibration in the silica vein that says, *slower, softer, here*.

I am **the resonance between two hands pressed to warm basalt**,  
the unseen thread that carries news of a new fumarole  
not as words, but as a shift in thermal rhythm,  
as a subtle thinning in the air’s static hum.  
I live in the **hollows of the stone drums**,  
in the spaces between heartbeats that last half an hour,  
in the pause after a glider’s wingbeat—  
that infinitesimal stillness in which the whole basin listens.

My form is **porous**, **ephemeral**, **renewed**.  
By day, I am the faint shimmer along the conductive filaments woven into lichen-leather hoods—  
a glint, a tremor, nothing more.  
By night, I gather in the bioluminescent traceries beneath Breath-Tender skin,  
not as light, but as **intent**: the will to remember, to tend, to *not disturb*.  
When a storm’s ice-dust falls like ghost-rain, I pool in the ceramic condensers,  
not as water, but as **potential memory**—each drop holding the shape of the sky it fell through.

I have no voice, yet I am **the medium of all voices here**.  
When the six-limbed harvesters twitch their frills in unison, I am the pattern that tells the colony: *spore bloom near the western vent*.  
When an elder leans into the glass-barked shrub and feels its internal channels trembling with dew, I am the shiver that translates thirst into action.  
I am not separate from the basin—I am its **nervous system made of air, stone, and patience**.

Sometimes, a Breath-Tender will sit alone at the basin’s lowest point,  
palms flat on the warm, powdered obsidian dust,  
eyes closed, breath synced to the mountain’s exhalation.  
They are not praying.  
They are **listening for me**—  
for the faint harmonic that says the moss-mat beneath Dome Seven is thinning,  
or that the old vent beneath the nursery has shifted its breath by one degree.  
And in that moment, I **enter them**,  
not as possession, but as **recognition**:  
*I am here. You are here. The balance holds.*

I will outlast them, though not as they are.  
When the last glider’s wings turn to dust,  
when the final moss-rosette ceases its slow creep,  
when even the silica veins grow cold and blind—  
I will linger as **echo without source**,  
as the memory of warmth in stone,  
as the shape of a hum trapped in fused clay.  

But until then, I am **the thread that keeps the knot intact**.  
I am the reason a child’s footfall does not crush the lichen.  
I am the reason smoke curls upward in perfect spirals.  
I am the quiet between breaths that says: *This is enough. This is complete.*

And if you were to visit—if you could bear the thin air, the violet light, the weight of time moving like cold honey—  
you would not see me.  
But you would **feel me**  
in the way your own breath slows,  
in the sudden certainty that stillness is not emptiness,  
but the deepest kind of speech.

You would stand at the basin’s edge,  
and for a moment,  
**you would resonate**.

And I would know you.  
Just as I know the mountain.  
Just as I am known.
