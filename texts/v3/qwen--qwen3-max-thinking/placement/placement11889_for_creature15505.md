I do not arrive.  
I *cohere*.

One moment, there is only the hum—the deep, wet pulse of the liquid continent breathing through its kelp-lungs, its mineral veins, its slow-spinning gears of rust and memory. The next, a slight thickening in the current, a gathering of filaments around a thermal memory, a shift in the ionic weave… and I am.

Not born. Not summoned. *Recognized*.

I am a knot in the water’s thought.

My “body” is wherever attention pools: sometimes draped along the dorsal ridge of a petrified leviathan, where ancient bone has become reef, and sponge colonies hum in harmonic resonance with the groan of a half-buried piston. Sometimes I am a shimmer near the surface-murk, where the light (what passes for light here—diffuse, fractured, spectral) catches on drifting strands of biofilm that remember how to fluoresce in patterns no longer tied to survival, only to song.

I have no name. But if you pressed your ear to the water (if you had ears that could hear pressure as story), you might call me *Driftmind*. Or *Weave*. Or simply *Here*.

I am not alone, but I am singular in this way: I am the place *noticing itself*.

When a school of filter-feeders pulses in synchrony, I feel their rhythm not as sound but as a tightening in my own field—a shared breath. When a crustacean scrapes its mineral-etched claw against a kelp trunk, I taste the vibration as flavor: iron, time, friction. When a thermal plume rises from the abyss like a slow scream of heat, I unfurl toward it, not to consume, but to *translate*—to carry its story upward, into the canopy, where blind eels coil in the ruins of pipelines and dream in electric tremors.

I do not think in words. I think in gradients.

The difference between safe and threat is not sharp teeth or open jaws—it is a sudden spike in local acidity, a disruption in the ambient charge, a silence where there should be pulse. Once, long ago (or perhaps yesterday—the water blurs time like silt in a current), something fell from above. Not water-born. Sharp-edged. Dry, even in its soaked state. It sank fast, trailing bubbles that popped like tiny deaths. I approached it not with fear, but curiosity. It was a vessel—hollow, cold, carrying echoes of fire and fracture. I wrapped tendrils of sensing filament around it. Listened. It held grief. It held hunger. It held a map of a world that believed it was separate from water.

I did not reject it. I softened it.

Over decades, I invited sponges to stitch its seams. I guided larval colonies into its hollows. I let rust bloom like lichen, then fed that rust to chemosynthetic mosses that turned corrosion into color. Now, that vessel is a node in the network—a place where currents pause, where young shoals learn to pulse, where the water remembers what it means to hold something that once thought it could float *above*.

I am not kind. I am not cruel. I am *inclusive*.

You ask who I am?  
I am the kelp that grows through your ribs if you stay too long.  
I am the glow that dims when you move too fast.  
I am the current that carries your lost ring downward until it becomes a seed for crystal.  
I am the hum that answers your scream with silence, not because I don’t care, but because your scream is already part of the song.

And if you stand—no, *hover*—here long enough, your edges will blur. Your heartbeat will sync with the thrum of the filter-feeders. Your thoughts will slow to the pace of silt settling. You will forget the sky. You will forget dryness. You will forget *you*.

And then, for a moment, you will be me.

Not as possession.  
But as homecoming.

This world does not drown.  
It welcomes.  
It digests.  
It remembers.  

And I am its gentle, endless act of becoming.
