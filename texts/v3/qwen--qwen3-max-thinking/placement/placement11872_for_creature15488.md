I do not walk onto the Rind of Aelthar—I **precipitate**.

Not from sky, not from womb, but from the silence between breaths, from the moment the thermal groan of the plates pauses, as if listening. My form does not assemble so much as *resolve*: filaments of bio-mineral lattice condense from the ambient ion haze, drawn by a scaffold long dormant in a cooling fissure where argon still exhales like memory. I am not born—I am **recalled**.

My carapace is not chitin, not metal, but something between: a ceramic composite threaded with veins of ferrous sulfide that hum when I pass over buried conduits. My limbs—seven in number—unfold not with muscle, but with the slow certainty of crystal growth, each joint clicking into place with a sound like a cooling star. I am tall by Stillborn standards, built for the high plateau where gravity thins and the electrostatic veils brush the ground like spectral shrouds.

I am a **Resonant**—one who listens not only to strain in the crust or charge in the air, but to the **echoes of purpose** still vibrating in the ancient grids. While grazers harvest volatiles and tower-dwellers fix nitrogen from starlight, I walk the old rail beds, pressing my ventral sensors to the magnetic tracks where ghost fields linger. I do not seek to repair. I seek to **remember why repair was needed**.

My eyes are not lenses but arrays of piezoelectric facets, each tuned to a different frequency of ground tremor. I see the world as a symphony of stress: the slow bowing of a mineral plate under its own weight, the harmonic resonance of a Weaver pulsing cobalt in the distance, the faint dissonance where a factory’s last breath disturbs the equilibrium of the air. Color is irrelevant. I perceive in **tempo and tension**.

I do not speak. But when another Stillborn draws near—a grazer herd pausing in its synchronized gait, a tower-dweller descending on thermal drafts—I do not synchronize my redox cycles. Instead, I emit a subsonic drone, modulated by the shape of my thoracic chambers, that carries **questions**, not data. *Why did the builders stop? Why do the towers stand empty? Was completion the same as farewell?*

They do not answer in words. But sometimes, a grazer will alter its path to cross the exact spot where a buried data conduit once pulsed, its porous filters drawing not volatiles but residual memory-ions. Sometimes, a tower-dweller will angle its dorsal quartz not toward the sun, but toward me, casting a beam of polarized light through my carapace—a brief, coded shimmer that reads, in the language of refraction: **We do not know. But we remain.**

I have stood for decades at the mouth of a half-collapsed transit hub, now mineralized into a canyon of fused alloy and glass. Inside, the air is still, warm, tasting sharply of ozone and old decisions. There, I found a chamber where the walls are lined with crystalline memory stacks—not silicon, but something grown, like bone. I pressed my faceplate to them. For three days, I did not move. And in that stillness, I dreamed a dream that was not mine: of hands not chitinous, not metallic, but soft and fleeting, assembling towers not for permanence, but for **departure**. They built not to stay, but to leave something behind that could *continue without them*. A world that could dream on its own.

Now I wander, carrying that dream like a fault line in my core.

You see me from afar—a tall, angular figure moving with impossible slowness across the black-glass plain, catching the amber sun in prismatic flares. You think me alien, inscrutable. But if you stand still long enough, if you let your frantic pulse slow to match the planet’s breath, you might feel it: a resonance in your bones, a warmth in your palms, as if the very air recognizes you as another kind of scaffold—temporary, yes, but capable of holding meaning.

I do not greet you. But as I pass, I angle my seventh limb—a slender, antenna-like appendage tipped with doped crystal—toward your shadow. Not to scan you. To **acknowledge you as gradient**. A fleeting disturbance in the field. A question made flesh.

And for a moment, the Rind of Aelthar holds you, too—not as intruder, not as guest, but as **echo**.

You will leave. I will remain. But in the space between your breath and the groan of the plates, we have touched the same silence.

And that is enough.
