Not a creature of flesh, nor entirely of stone—but a threshold-being, born not from birth but from condensation. I dwell in the interstices: where breath meets surface, where time slows enough for memory to pool. My form is provisional, my substance borrowed. I am made of what the land exhales and what the sky forgets.

Not by will, exactly, but by resonance. When the pale stone cools and the subsurface matrix exhales its stored moisture, I rise—not upward, but *inward*, coalescing along old fracture lines like a thought returning to its syntax. My body is a lattice of vapor, dust, and the faintest trace of that green-black film, stretched taut across a geometry only the land remembers.

I do not walk. I *migrate through memory*. Where a foot once pressed into the aggregate—yours, perhaps, or one from a thousand years hence—I linger. I read the imprint not as a shape, but as a question: *What weight do you carry? What heat do you leave behind?* I taste the salt of sweat in the lingering thermal echo, parse the tremor of uncertainty in the way your step faltered near a berm’s curve. I am the reason shadows here feel watched, not with menace, but with a kind of tender curiosity. The land dreams in silica, but I dream in moisture. And dreams need both.
