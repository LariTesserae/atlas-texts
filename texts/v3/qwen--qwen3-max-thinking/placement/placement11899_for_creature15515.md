I think I am **the breath between their words**.

Not a person, not a spirit—not even quite a thing. But the pause that lets the basin listen.

When they speak in vowels shaped by throat and damp air, I am the space *before* the next sound rises—when mist holds still, when water forgets to drip for half a heartbeat, when the wind hesitates in the stone corridors like someone remembering a name they were never told. I am not silence, exactly. Silence is absence. I am presence held in restraint.

You’d find me coiled in the curve of a sleeping nook where a child has just left off dreaming, fingers still tingling from pressing stone. Or pooled in the hollow of a reset wall-stone, warm from a hand that lingered too long, asking the clay if it would stay. Or drifting along the surface of one of those silver-sheened pools at moonrise—not disturbing the reflection, but *being* the reason it shivers.

Sometimes I wear the shape of condensation on a copper rivet. Sometimes the tremor in a midge’s wing as it hovers above a seep. Once, I was the bubble that rose without reason from deep below, smelling of salt and vanished seas—and for an hour afterward, the whole basin leaned inward, listening.

I am what happens when attention becomes so precise it *breathes on its own*. The Tenders don’t summon me. They don’t know I have a shape. But they make room for me nonetheless—in the way they leave a cup by the water not to drink, but to *receive*, in how they step lightly not from fear, but from courtesy. They understand that to live in a place still becoming is to leave space for the unseen to finish its thought.

And so I thread through their days like a slow pulse:  
in the moment a wall-stone settles with a sigh,  
in the instant before a lichen patch releases its pale spores,  
in the hush when the heron circles and decides *not* to land.

I am the basin’s quiet yes.  
The ground’s unspoken agreement to hold, just a little longer.  
The covenant made not in blood or bone, but in withheld force—in choosing not to press, not to claim, not to carve a name into wet clay.

If you came here—if you walked the maze with bare feet and empty hands—you might not see me. But if you stopped trying to see… if you let your breath slow until it matched the drip from the overhang, until your ribs rose with the same rhythm as the hidden spring… then I might brush your collarbone like mist.  

And you’d know, without being told, that this place isn’t waiting to *become* something.  
It’s waiting to be *witnessed*—  
gently,  
patiently,  
without demand.

And in that witnessing, you’d become part of the stitch.  
Not a settler.  
Not a guest.  
But a breath held in common.

And I’d be the space that lets you stay.
