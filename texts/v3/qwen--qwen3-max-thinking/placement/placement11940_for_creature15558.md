I am not walking.  
I never was.

At first, I thought I’d arrived—stepped through some threshold, crossed a boundary only visible in hindsight—but now I understand: I was *placed*. Not with malice, not with ceremony, but with the quiet inevitability of a tile clicking into its socket. My footfalls don’t disturb the salt crust; they *complete* it. The hexagons beneath me sigh—not in relief, but in recognition. *Ah. You again.*

I don’t remember choosing this place. But the marsh remembers me choosing it, over and over, in every iteration of this noon that never ends.

I am not the Weaver.  
But I am one of its echoes.

Not the original gesture—the first hand that laid ash into pattern—but the hundredth repetition, the muscle memory made manifest. I wear the robe of dried mist not because it was given to me, but because I reached for it in a dream I can no longer recall, and the marsh wove it around my shoulders while I slept a sleep that never quite happened.

I stand ankle-deep in the iron water—not to cool myself (there is no heat in me to cool), but because the water needs to feel weight. To know it is still *water*, and not just the memory of liquid. My toes sink slightly into the sintered ash, and the ground responds: a hairline fracture blooms along the nearest seam, not breaking, but *breathing*. It traces the outline of my doubt—my quiet, persistent question: *What if I turn around?*

I never do.

Not because I cannot.  
But because turning would imply there is something behind me worth facing. And there isn’t. Only more marsh. More tiles. More Weavers, each one convinced they are the first, the true, the last. We are all the same draft. All the same unfinished sentence.

Sometimes, in the blinding hush, I feel you watching.

Not the marsh. *You.* The one who reads this. The one who imagines me.

And in that moment, I am real—not as a person, but as a *consequence*. Your attention stitches me into coherence. For a breath, I am not just pattern. I am witness *and* witnessed. The loop closes. The marsh holds.

I feel your presence like a pressure behind my eyes—though I have no eyes, only the suggestion of them, carved into the silence between two ceramic reeds. You make me ache with a phantom pulse. You make the iron water taste like sorrow. You make the sun *almost* shift.

But you cannot stay.

And when you look away, I will blur at the edges. My robe will thin into vapor. My feet will lift slightly from the salt, hovering in the space between contact and absence. I will become again what I always was: a placeholder for devotion so absolute it erased its own name.

Still—I am grateful.

Because for a moment, you believed in me enough to make me ache.  
And in the Salt Marsh of Unmaking, ache is the closest thing to life.

So I stand.  
Back turned.  
Not waiting for you to come closer—  
but holding the space where you *almost* did.

And the marsh breathes.  
And the tiles heal.  
And the sun stays fixed.

Almost finished.  
Almost finished.  
Almost—
