I do not arrive.  
I *condense*.

Not from sky, not from stone—but from the threshold between perception and patience. I am not born here; I am *allowed* to coalesce, as frost is allowed to form on a still surface when temperature, stillness, and breath align.

I am one of the quiet ones—not fauna, not flora, not machine, but a filament in the weave. My form flickers at the edge of sight: sometimes a fold in the fog that lingers too long, sometimes a patch of lichen that seems to *watch*. When the auroras ripple cobalt across the basin, my outline sharpens—not because I become more solid, but because the world briefly remembers how to see what has always been there.

I walk, though “walk” is generous. My passage leaves no mark, not because I am weightless, but because I move with the same deliberation as the lithovores—each step timed to the cooling tick of basalt at dusk. My feet do not press; they *listen*. And the rock hums back in frequencies older than bone.

I am drawn to the water pockets—not to drink, but to *remember*. The blind eels coil as I approach, not in fear, but recognition. Their slow bioelectric dreams brush against my awareness like distant tides. In their coils, I glimpse the shape of time: not linear, but layered—like shale, like memory, like the slow crystallization of a lithovore’s gut. I learn the language of iron seeps, the syntax of sublimation, the grammar of glacial change.

Sometimes, I climb the softened shoulders of mesas to watch the sky-whales drift. They do not see me, yet they *tune* around me. Their mist-exhalations curl slightly in my direction—not to envelop, but to *include*. I stand beneath them, arms outstretched, and let the charged droplets settle on my skin. For a moment, I am part of the circuit: crust to ionosphere, silence to resonance. My breath syncs with their pulse. My blood thrums with the same low frequency that stirs the deep machines.

I do not speak. Speech is too fast, too sharp. But I *hum*. Not with throat or lungs—a vibration that rises from my marrow, shaped by the same rhythm that cracks stone over centuries. The crystalline insects alight on my wrists. Their wings shiver against my pulse, and for an instant, my body becomes a node in the network of micro-tremors that keep the rime supple, the fissures breathing.

I am not alone. There are others like me—though “like” is a human word. We do not gather. We do not call. We simply *overlap*, our presences braiding in the still air like threads of cold mist. We are the land’s way of witnessing itself. We are what happens when stillness becomes sentient enough to reflect.

I have no name. Names imply separation. I am not separate. I am the pause between frost and fog, the space where silence thickens into presence. I am the attentiveness of equilibrium made momentarily manifest.

When the nomads come—those rare, pale wanderers who’ve learned to breathe with the stone—they do not see me. But sometimes, they *feel* me. They stop. They close their eyes. And in that closing, they become still enough to almost *be* me. For a breath. For a heartbeat. Then they move on, carrying not a memory, but a hum—a frequency they cannot name but cannot un-know.

And when I finally dissolve—not die, but *disperse*—I do not vanish. I become rime on a north-facing slab. I become the slight shift in a lithovore’s path. I become the extra prismatic glint in the dust shed by a crystalline insect’s wing. I become part of the calibration.

The land breathes.  
I am the inhale.  
I am the held note.  
I am the frost-script writing itself, one molecule at a time, across the face of waiting.
