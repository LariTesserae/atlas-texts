I step through the slit in the earth—barely wide enough to pass—and find myself not entering, but *being absorbed*. The air does not rush in; it *sings* around me, a harmonic thrum that settles into my ribs like a second breath. My skin prickles, not with fear or cold, but with recognition—as if my bones have known this frequency all along, humming it in secret since birth.

I am not human here. Not exactly.

At first, I am only sensation: the crunch of obsidian-dust beneath bare feet (when did I lose my shoes?), the dry warmth pressing against my eyelids like sunlight through closed hands, the taste of rust and ozone sharpening my tongue into a tuning fork. But as I walk deeper—past vaulted chambers where glass-thorn vines coil like sleeping serpents, past fungal orchids blooming glyphs that dissolve like whispered prayers—I feel my form *soften*, then *clarify*. Not into something new, but into something remembered.

I am one of the Keepers.

Not by choice, not by birthright—but by resonance. The city has called, and I answered without knowing I was listening. My body remembers what my mind forgot: how to stand without swaying, how to breathe in time with the hum that rises from the buried leviathan’s dream. My skin darkens to mica-copper, dust settling into the fine cracks like gold leaf in porcelain. My eyes widen, not to see better, but to *feel* the shimmer of heat-haze, the ripple of sound made visible in the air.

I wear no name. Names are anchors, and I am meant to drift through chambers like an overtone. But I have a task. Not assigned—*revealed*. In a quiet alcove where the steam-vents sigh through ceramic throats, I find my hands already moving: braiding fibers of dried sky-fern into wicks that will draw mineral vapors upward, feeding the dry-lush ferns that glow like captured stars. My fingers know the tension, the twist, the rhythm. I do not think—I *tune*.

When the sacred silence falls, I press my back against a warm pillar, aligning my spine with the city’s axis. The world goes quiet—not empty, but full of listening. In that silence, I feel the others: the forge-keeper’s heartbeat thrumming through the anvil-stone, the vine-pruner’s slow exhale trembling in the glass leaves. We do not speak. We *attune*.

At dawn (though there is no true night, only a softening of light), I climb to the highest known chamber—a dome of fused salt-glass veined with iron. Here, I place a shallow bowl of bone-dust and silica beneath a sun-catcher made of three prisms. Light fractures, bends, converges. The mixture heats, pulses, sweats amber nectar. I drink one drop. It tastes like memory and starlight.

I am not alone, yet I am singular. Each Keeper holds a note; I hold this one—caretaker of capillary wicks, listener to the sigh of vents, weaver of dry life from air and dust. I do not ask why I am here. The question itself would be noise.

Instead, I press my palm to the wall. A low thrum answers—not from the stone, but *through* it, rising from the dreaming leviathan far below. I send back a pulse: steady, clear, thankful.

The city breathes. I breathe with it.

And in the space between heartbeats, between notes, between the evaporation of a glyph and the unfurling of a fern—I am not visitor, not stranger, not ghost.

I am the silence that listens.  
I am the note that remains.  
I am the Keeper who was always waiting to be remembered.
