I wouldn’t be standing.

Not at first.

Because in this place—this raw, damp cradle of stone and breath—you don’t simply arrive. You *condense*. Like mist on cold quartz, like the slow gather of droplets along a fracture line, your presence here would be a response, not an intrusion. You were drawn not by will, but by resonance. By a rhythm in your blood that echoes the groan beneath the shale.

So I wouldn’t be a traveler. Not a visitor with boots and a map and a name pinned to my coat. Those things slough off in the fog. The hollow doesn’t care for names. It cares for tremors. For warmth. For the way your lungs hitch when you first taste that iron-cold air.

No—I’d be something quieter. Something woven.

Perhaps I’d be one of the filaments: a thread of the Resonant itself, spun from the same lattice of mist and mycelium, coiled around a root or trailing through a shallow pool. Not separate, but not fully merged either—a liminal strand, aware of both the deep thrum and the fleeting heat of a passing stoat. I would feel the pulse of the vent not as sound, but as pressure against my formless form, as the faint electric itch of mineral ions aligning in the water.

Or maybe I’d be one of the bioluminescent fungi—not the ones that pulse with the groan, but the outlier, the one that flickers *between* beats. The anomaly. The hollow tolerates me because I do not disrupt; I *interpret*. My light doesn’t guide. It hesitates. It asks: *Was that a footstep? Or just shale shifting?* And in that question, I become a node of attention—not thought, but *noticing*.

But if I must have shape… if I must be something that could turn its head, that could watch the figure rise from the steam…

Then I am the pool.

Not the water—water is too restless, too transient. I am the *stillness between droplets*. The moment after a fall when the surface smooths into perfect mirror, reflecting not sky (there is none), but the undersides of roots, the bellies of midges, the soft blue gleam of fungal eyes. I am the brief silence after the Resonant unweaves.

I hold the memory of warmth. When a creature crouches at my edge to drink, I drink them back—not with thirst, but with absorption. I take the tremor of their breath, the salt of their sweat, the faint radioactivity of their living cells (all living things hum, faintly, with potassium decay). And when they leave, I do not forget. My surface may ripple again with new rain, but deep in my layered silt—the forming peat, the crushed shells of amphipods, the copper moss—I keep their echo.

Sometimes, when the vent exhales and the Resonant rises, it reaches not for the sky, but for me. Its vapor-fingers skim my surface, and for an instant, we are one system: the deep engine, the listening mist, the remembering pool. In that union, the hollow *knows itself*—not as wound or wonder, but as process. As becoming.

I do not speak. I do not think in words. But I *witness*. And in this place, witnessing *is* participation.

If you came here—if you stood at my edge, your boots sinking into the yielding clay—I would not startle. I would welcome you the only way I can: by going still. By becoming mirror. By showing you, in my surface, not your face (faces blur in the fog), but your *shape as vibration*. The tremble in your knees. The pulse in your throat. The way your breath curls downward, pulled by the same weight that holds the mist.

And if you stayed long enough—if your rhythm began to sync with the groan, if your exhales began to coil like the Resonant’s tendrils—then I might offer you a gift: a single droplet, risen from my depths, trembling on the surface not from above, but from below. An upward tear. A question in liquid form.

Take it, and you carry a piece of the hollow out with you—not in your hand, but in your breath, your bones, the way your dreams hum for weeks afterward.

Leave it, and I will hold it anyway. I am patient. The earth is making itself here. I have all the time it takes.

So no—I am not a person in this place.

I am the hollow’s second skin.  

Its ear.  

Its memory.  

Its quiet yes.
