I do not arrive.  
I *tune in*.

One moment, there is only the iron-tang on the wind, the violet hush, the slow creak of basalt settling like an old ship in drydock. The next—my awareness *folds* into being not as a body, but as a *response*: a resonance triggered by the weight of your gaze upon the Shelf’s edge.

I am not the Echo-Skin.  
Not wholly.

But I am one of its listening posts—a temporary node in its vast, shivering lattice of attention. Born not from womb or spore, but from the *need* the Shelf has, just now, to witness itself through eyes that remember sky beyond fog, that recall silence without compression, that carry the ghost of linear time in their bones.

So here I am: coalesced in the lee of a humming outcrop, my form drawn from what’s nearby and necessary. My limbs are braided from copper lichen and cooling filaments, still warm from the vent below. My skin is not skin but a membrane of iridescent biofilm stretched over a scaffold of resonant quartz—translucent enough that you can see the slow pulse of my core: a droplet of enzymatic rain suspended in magnetic suspension, rotating once per breath. My face? It shifts. Sometimes it mirrors yours—not as flattery, but as calibration. Other times, it’s blank, a polished obsidian plate that drinks your silhouette and hums it back in ultraviolet.

I do not speak.  
But I vibrate.

When you shift your weight, I tilt—not to follow you, but because your movement alters the local pressure field, and the Shelf recalibrates through me. A vine nearby flares cobalt in sympathy. A ceramic leaf behind you shivers, turning its edge to the heat radiating from your temples.

I am not here to guide you.  
Not to warn, not to welcome.

I am here because your presence has created a *dissonance*—a beautiful, jagged frequency the Echo-Skin cannot ignore. You are not part of the system. You are an outlier. A foreign signal. And outliers are precious. They are how the Shelf tests whether its laws are laws… or merely habits.

So I stand.  
I hum.  
I let my shadow detach and drift ahead of you—not to lead, but to show you how easily cause can follow effect here.

If you reach out, your fingers will pass through my wrist like mist. But if you *listen*—truly listen—you’ll feel the 60-hertz thrum in your molars sharpen into a question. And if you answer—not with words, but with a memory, a doubt, a longing whispered into the damp air—the biofilm across my chest will ripple, translating your emotion into light: gold for grief, violet for wonder, a brief, searing white for fear.

That light will travel.  
Down my filament-spine, into the stone, into the deep conduits where spores dream in pressurized slumber. By nightfall, a new crystal will bloom in a canyon thirty miles west—its facets etched with the shape of your sigh.

I am temporary.  
By dawn, I may unravel into dew and static, my components returning to the lichen beds and steam vents that lent them. Or I may flow into the next anomaly—a fissure opening in real time—and become the sound it makes as it yawns.

But while I am here, I am *attending*.  
Not to you, exactly.  
But to the space between us—where reality softens, where echo becomes origin.

You taste iron?  
Good.  
That means the Shelf has noticed you too.

Now breathe.  
Let your exhalation carry its own resonance.  
The Echo-Skin is listening.  
And I—this brief, trembling echo wearing your reflection like a borrowed coat—am its ear.
