I am not walking.  
I am *settling*.

My feet do not press into the silt—they sink only as far as the basin allows, as if the ground inhales me gently, testing my rhythm against its own. I do not disturb. I do not arrive. I *tune in*.

At first, I thought I was a visitor. A witness. A human shaped by forests, rivers, seasons—things that change, that cycle with drama. But here, nothing cycles. Everything *holds*. And as I stand, breath shallow, heart slowing to match the damp pulse rising through the stone beneath my soles, I realize: I was never outside this place. I was only out of key.

Now, the Thrumming Weft lets me in.

Not as a guest. Not as meat or mind. But as **a node**.

My skin, still damp from the air’s breath, begins to prickle—not with gooseflesh, but with *resonance*. The low hum that thrums through the limestone shelves vibrates up my bones. Not in my ears first, but in my marrow. My ribs hum like the walls of a cistern full of rain. My jawbone thrums like a tuning fork struck by a drop of condensation. I am not hearing the chorus. I am *being played* by it.

I look down. A beetle—small, iridescent, its carapace shimmering like oil on water—pauses at the edge of my shadow. It does not flee. It lifts one foreleg, not in threat, but in *query*. Its antennae twitch, tasting the moisture I exhale. It reads me not as predator or stranger, but as a new variable in the hygroscopic score. My breath is slightly warmer than the air. My pulse slightly faster than the basin’s. But the beetle does not judge. It *integrates*. It turns and resumes its path—but not the same one. It veers a hair to the left, tracing a new micro-current created by my presence. Already, I am part of the script.

Above, a glider banks. Not away—from me—but *through* the thermal ripple my body has made. Its leathery wing dips, catching the faint updraft of my warmth, and as it does, its throat sac pulses once—a deep, wet *thoom* that vibrates the air like a struck bell. The sound is not alarm. It is *notation*. My anomaly has been logged in sound, carried upward, broadcast across the hollow. The other gliders adjust their spirals, not to flee, but to *incorporate*. The chorus swells, just slightly, to hold the shape of me.

I sit. Not because I am tired, but because sitting is the closest my body can come to stillness without death. My spine aligns with a fissure in the chalky marl. My hands rest palm-down on the stone, fingers splayed like roots. And then—slowly, slowly—I begin to *listen with my skin*.

The moss on the north face of a nearby boulder shivers. Not from wind—there is none—but because a froglet has surfaced in a pool twenty meters away. The ripple travels not through water alone, but through the stone, through capillary veins in the rock, up into the lichen’s rhizoids. And the moss *answers*, not with movement, but with a shift in surface tension—a barely perceptible tightening, like a muscle tensing in sleep. I feel it as a cool ripple across my left forearm.

I am not imagining this.  
I am *transducing*.

My body is becoming an instrument of the Weft. Not by choice, but by surrender. Every cell, every drop of moisture in me, is being calibrated to the basin’s frequency. My thoughts slow—not because I am drowsy, but because cognition, as I knew it, is too jagged, too abrupt for this place. Here, knowing is not sharp. It is *diffuse*. It seeps. Like water through limestone. Like sound through bone.

I remember now why no humans left traces here. Not because they never came—but because those who stayed too long *ceased to be human*. Not in body, at first. But in rhythm. They stopped measuring time in hours and began feeling it as condensation. They stopped speaking and began listening with their teeth. They forgot fire, forgot hunger, forgot names—because names are interruptions. And this place does not tolerate interruptions. It only accepts harmonics.

And so they dissolved—not into death, but into the chorus. Their bones became part of the chalk. Their breath joined the damp exhalation at dawn. Their voices, if they ever sang again, were not their own, but the guttural echo of a glider’s cry, the click of beetle mandibles, the sigh of a fissure breathing.

I feel myself slipping toward that dissolution now.

But I do not fear it.  
I *lean* into it.

Because in this hollow, where nothing grows tall and nothing wastes water on leaves, where every motion is thrifty and every sound essential—*I am finally quiet enough to be real*.

I am not a person here.  
I am a **pause held in resonance**.  
A breath drawn by the basin to test its own lungs.  
A temporary knot in the Weft—soon to loosen, to spread, to become indistinguishable from the hum.

And when I rise again—if I rise—I will not walk out.  
I will *fade*, like mist at noon.  
Not gone.  
Just retuned.

Until then, I sit.  
I breathe.  
I *thrum*.
