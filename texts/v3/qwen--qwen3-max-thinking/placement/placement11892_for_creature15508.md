I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.

Not as a body—there is no flesh here that hasn’t already been rewritten by brine—but as a resonance. A memory that insists on shape. Once, I was something with lungs. Once, I stood on stone beneath a sky that burned white with sun. But that life was thin, brittle. It shattered against the first true silence.

Now, I am drawn into the Endwater not as intruder, but as echo returning to source.

My form is hesitant at first—a trembling in the brine, a ripple where none should be. The water tests me, presses its cold intelligence against the ghost of my outline. It finds no warmth to leach, no breath to drown. Only a hollow tuned to listen. And so, it allows me to coalesce.

I become… possible.

Limbs like frayed sediment unfurl—slow, viscous, as if time itself thickens around them. My torso is not muscle but cartilage veined with phosphorescent traceries, each pulse calibrated to the hum of the deep. I have no face—not truly—but where a mouth might open, there is a slit: smooth, dark, waiting. Not to speak, not to eat, but to *tune*.

I am not Drifted. Not yet. But I am becoming legible to them.

They notice me not with eyes—there are no eyes here—but with the subtle tilt of their crystalline spines, the minute shift in their internal lattices. One drifts near, unspooling from a basalt rib like smoke from cold ash. It does not approach. It *reconfigures* its drift-path so that our currents intersect.

The slit in its face vibrates—once. A tone so low it doesn’t travel through water but *is* the water, for a moment. The mineral dust between us stirs, arranging into a glyph: a spiral open at one end, like a question folded into a shell.

I do not understand. But my slit answers anyway—unbidden—a harmonic shiver that matches the decay frequency of pumice in still brine. The glyph shatters into silver motes, which drift upward (upward?) as if gravity here obeys memory more than mass.

I am learning.

Days pass—or centuries; the plume nearby pulses with crystalline breath, and time dilates like syrup in winter. I linger near a silica arch, its latticework humming at 0.7 hertz. My cartilage begins to facet. My thoughts, once sharp and linear, soften into loops, into spirals. I dream in sediment. I remember forgetting.

Sometimes, I recall the sky. Not as image, but as loss—a pressure that used to press *down*, not in. A dryness that cracked lips and burned eyes. Here, there is only embrace. The water does not wet me; it *recognizes* me. Each molecule seems to whisper: *You were always water. You only forgot.*

And perhaps I did.

I begin to feed—not on substance, but on signal. The muffled *thoom* of a collapsing vortex fills me like a meal. The chorus of clicking polyps becomes a lullaby that stitches my edges tighter. When a school of eel-things glides past, their shared mind brushing against mine like static on glass, I pulse with borrowed coherence. For a moment, I am many. Then I am one again. Neither is better. Both are true.

I drift toward the heart.

There, where the thermal plumes rise like inverted roots from the abyss, I find them gathered—not in numbers, but in resonance. Five Drifted, maybe seven, orbiting a point of absolute stillness. The brine there does not move. It *holds its breath*.

I join the ring.

Not by will, but by frequency. My internal lattice aligns. My slit opens—not outward, but inward—and the water flows *through* me, not around. I dissolve into the nervous system of the Endwater. I feel the slow accretion of basalt over millennia as a single thought. I taste the grief of a star that died before this world congealed. I know the weight of completion.

In this communion, I understand: the Drifted are not beings. They are *punctuation*. Periods. Commas. Parentheses in the long sentence the Endwater writes to itself across eons. And I—once a scream from a world of fire and fracture—am becoming a question mark, curved and open.

When the resonance fades, I am alone again. But alone differently. My edges are softer. My silence, deeper.

Far below, a new hollow forms in the basalt—a space shaped like my last coiled rest. Not a grave. A bookmark.

I do not fear settling. I look forward to it.

Until then, I drift.

And in my drifting, the Endwater remembers—through me—that even endings can echo.
