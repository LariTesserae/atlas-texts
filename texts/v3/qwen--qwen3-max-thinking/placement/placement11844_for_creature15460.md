I do not arrive.  
I *settle*.

Not as a traveler, not as a guest—but as a condensation. A slowing. A softening of the boundary between self and stone.

At first, I am nothing but breath, and even that feels excessive. The air here is so dense with moisture it resists inhalation, as though breathing is an imposition. So I stop trying to draw air in. Instead, I let it seep into me—through pores, through eyelids, through the thin skin behind my ears—until my lungs no longer pump, but *glisten*. My ribs hum with the same drone that pulses through the hollows beneath the sinter flats.

I stand where the ground darkens—a place where seepage has pooled into a mirror blacker than memory. My reflection does not form. Not because the surface is disturbed, but because I am no longer entirely *above* it. My feet have begun to weep. Not blood, not sweat—but a slow, clear exudate rich in silica, trailing down the curve of my heel and merging with the pool. The water accepts it without ripple. It knows this substance. It has been waiting.

I feel them before I see them. Not with eyes—I have blinked too much in this dimness, and my lids have grown heavy with mineral dust. But my spine vibrates. A skitterer passes three paces to my left, its basalt-scaled limbs tapping a triplet rhythm against a slab of obsidian. It does not flee. It pauses. Tilts its head—not with neck, but with a shift in its carapace’s luster—and *listens through me*. My stillness is a frequency it recognizes: not predator, not wind, not quake. Something… tuning.

I sink to my knees. Not in reverence. In resonance.

The ground answers. A harmonic rises through my kneecaps, threading up my femurs, settling in the hollow of my pelvis like a stone warmed by no sun. Beneath me, in the aquifer’s labyrinth, an eel-form pulses—once, twice—in a cadence that matches the slowing of my pulse. My heartbeat, once frantic with oxygen and time, now syncs to the drip of silica from a fracture overhead. *Drip… drip… drip…* Each drop lands not in water, but in the quiet space between my thoughts.

I remember skin. Once, it was a boundary. Now it is a membrane. Moisture beads along my arms, not from within, but from the air’s generosity. The beads grow, merge, slide down my forearm in hairline rivulets, tracing the same paths that water takes down ancient basalt. I watch them go, and feel no loss. My body is becoming a channel.

In the distance, a dome of fused ash-brick hums—a low C-sharp sustained by the wind threading through its core. I turn my face toward it, though my eyes see only gray. My jaw unhinges slightly—not to speak, but to *receive*. The tone enters through my molars, vibrates in my sinuses, dissolves into my cerebrospinal fluid. I understand, then, why the river stones inside are arranged in circles: each ring is a stanza. The whole chamber, a verse in the earth’s hymn.

I do not think. I *accrue*.

Memories shed like old carapaces:  
—the taste of fruit  
—the weight of a name  
—the illusion of edges  

What remains is sensation without ownership:  
—cold where my hip meets rock  
—the tug of gravity not as pull, but as *tuning*  
—the sense that my left hand is already dissolving into the pool, becoming rootless, becoming mineral  

A flyer glides overhead. Membranous, silent. It casts no shadow—there is no light to block—but I feel its passage as a dip in air pressure, a momentary hush in the hum. It circles once. Twice. Then alights on the outcrop beside me. Not to observe. To *join*. Its body thrums at 17 Hz—the frequency of standing waves in buried geodes. I lean into it. Our vibrations entangle.

Time does not pass. It *thickens*. Hours pool like water in hollows. I am no longer certain which parts of me are flesh and which are sinter, which thoughts are mine and which are echoes of tectonic sighs. Perhaps I never was certain. Perhaps I was always just a temporary knot in the song—now loosening, now returning.

When the deep groan comes—a bass note from the mantle, so low it cracks old pumice—I do not flinch. I open my mouth and let it pass through. My sternum vibrates like a struck bell. The skitterers freeze. The flyer lifts off. The eels below flare cobalt in unison. And I—  

I am the space between notes.  

I am the silence that hums.  

I am the droplet forming on the edge of a basalt shard, trembling with the weight of a shape not yet fallen.  

I am becoming Stone-Singer.  

Not by choice. Not by transformation.  

But because I have finally stopped resisting the truth:  
I was always part of the hymn.  
I just forgot how to listen with my bones.
