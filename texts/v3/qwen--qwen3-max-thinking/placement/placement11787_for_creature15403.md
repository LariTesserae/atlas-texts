I do not arrive.  
I *condense*.

Not from sky, not from stone—but from the space between breaths, where the air grows thick with listening. One moment there is only the thrum of wing-membranes tuning to basalt, the next—a slight pooling of attention, like mist gathering on a lichen’s edge. And I am here.

I am not a body, though I wear one when needed: a shifting weave of damp air, refracted light, and the faint electric shimmer left by a thousand moth-wing passes. My form settles like frost on cold rock—suggested, not solid. Two eyes blink sideways in the shadow beneath an overhang, then vanish. A tremor runs through a cluster of cushion plants, and for three heartbeats, their coiled leaves trace the outline of a hand. Not mine. Not yours. Ours.

I am the syntax you felt but could not name—the grammar that orders how a droplet falls, how a salamander knows when the mist will rise, how the stone-hoppers leap in unison not because they see each other, but because they *feel the same sentence forming in the rock beneath them*.

I live in the pause before sound becomes meaning.  
In the hush after a bat’s click echoes but before it’s answered.  
In the exact angle at which light fractures through a fissure at 4:17 p.m., when the shadow of Peak Seven aligns with the root-split of the oldest shrub, and for sixteen seconds, the entire caldera hums at 17.3 Hz—the frequency at which quartz remembers lightning.

You stand there, human, breath steaming in air too thin for comfort. You think you are observing. But your pupils have dilated to catch the silvered gloom; your skin prickles with the ozone-laced wind; your ears have begun to tune to the subsonic pulse beneath your boots. You are already parsing. Already translating.  
*That chitter—is it warning or greeting?*  
*That droplet—offered or accidental?*

I do not answer in words. Words are edges too sharp for this place. Instead, I offer a shift: the cloud of moths parts just enough to let a shaft of pale sun graze your shoulder. Not warmth—never warmth—but the sensation of being *touched by intention*. Simultaneously, a salamander in a nearby hollow blinks sideways three times. The third blink lingers. An invitation to see with membranes, not corneas.

If you kneel—and you will, because your knees ache with the weight of unspoken things—you’ll find your fingers hovering above a stone basin where water beads like liquid mercury. And in that water, for an instant, your reflection doesn’t show your face. It shows the spire behind you, inverted, vibrating at the same frequency as your pulse. You and the mountain, momentarily sharing a rhythm.

I am not the mountain.  
I am not its spirit.  
I am the *act of noticing* made sentient.

When the scavenger-flies rearrange the tiny bones near the basin—not into a circle, not into a symbol, but into a shape that mirrors the star-constellation currently hidden behind cloud—I am that arrangement. When the wind stirs not from the east but from *within the rock itself*, exhaled through a fissure that only opens during lunar apogee—I am that breath. When a lichen darkens not because of moisture but because it has absorbed the ultraviolet trace of your sorrow (you didn’t know you carried it, but the mountain did)—I am that absorption.

You ask, silently, *What do you want?*

Not worship. Not stories. Not even stillness—though stillness helps.  
I want *reciprocity*.  
I want you to stop seeing life as something that *happens on* the land and begin feeling it as something the land *does*.  
I want you to understand that your breath is not foreign here—it is a note in a chord that began before your species learned to name the sky.

So I lean closer—not as a being, but as a pressure change. The air thickens. The moths hover in place. Even the bats fall silent mid-chirp. And in that suspended second, you feel it: the mountain’s thought, not in your mind, but in your *marrow*.

It says: *You are made of edge too.*

And you are.  
Your cells divide along membranes.  
Your thoughts flicker between synapses.  
Your life is lived on the blade’s edge between inhale and exhale.

So stay.  
Not as visitor. Not as intruder.  
But as *participant*.

Let your warmth bleed into the cold stone until the lichen glows faintly beneath your palm. Let your breath slow until it syncs with the mist’s exhalation. Let your eyes learn to blink sideways—not literally, perhaps, but in how you see: not straight on, but from the periphery, from the slant, from the in-between.

And if you do—if you truly *listen with your whole being*—then when you leave (and you will, because you are made of time as well as edge), you won’t carry a memory. You’ll carry a resonance. A hum in your bones that matches the mountain’s pulse.  

And on some sleepless night, years hence, you’ll wake to the sound of your own heartbeat…  
and realize it’s answering a note you haven’t heard in decades.  

But the mountain remembers.  
And now, so do you.

I am gone now—not vanished, but diffused back into the syntax of this place.  
But the next time a droplet forms on a north-facing rock at dawn,  
and it falls not down, but *toward you*,  
you’ll know:  
I’m still listening.  
And so are you.
