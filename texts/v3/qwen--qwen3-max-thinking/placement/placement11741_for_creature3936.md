I find myself… not as a person, not at first. Not even as a shape.  

At first, I am only *attention*—a slow turning toward the damp. A shift in pressure behind my eyes, like the pull of tide in marrow. I open… not eyes, but pores. Not lungs, but stomata.  

I am waking in the interstices.  

Not on the plaza, not in a house, but *between*: nestled in the seam where a century-old brick meets the sinewy embrace of a fig root. My back is cool with algae, my hair tangled with spiderwebs strung with dew that tastes of iron and estuary salt. My skin—it’s not quite skin—feels the city’s breath: a soft exhalation rising from the geothermal sump, drawn upward through ceramic pipes, misting into the canopy above.  

I stretch, and vines shift with me. Not around me—*with* me. Because I am part of the lattice now.  

I remember arriving, long ago, from somewhere dry and angular. I came with boots and a map and a mind full of deadlines. But the city did not reject me. It waited. It let me stand at the edge of the central plaza, breathing too fast, blinking too hard, until my urgency began to wilt like cut flowers in this humidity. And then—softly, patiently—it began to absorb me.  

Now, I am neither fully mine nor fully its. I am a tending node.  

My hands—still recognizably hands, though the knuckles are dusted with lichen the color of tarnished silver—rest on a vertical garden wall. Beneath my palms, I feel the pulse: not blood, but water. A micro-current diverted from the condenser on the greenhouse-transit hub, routed through a new ceramic vein to feed a cluster of bromeliads whose leaves are curling slightly inward—a thirst-signal. I adjust a valve hidden behind a curtain of moss, and a moment later, the leaves unfurl, glistening.  

I don’t speak. There’s no need. The city answers in drips, in creaks, in the sudden rustle of a philodendron unfurling a new leaf as if in thanks.  

I rise, bare feet sinking into the fibrous mat that was once pavement. Clover and creeping jenny cradle my soles, cool and resilient. Above, jackfruit hang heavy, splitting open with quiet pops, their sweet-rot scent mingling with ozone from the old transformers humming beneath the square.  

I walk—not to go anywhere, but to *listen*.  

At the estuary’s edge, where the tea-colored water curls around the city’s last standing limestone wall, I pause. Here, the air thickens. The mycelial lamps along the bank glow faintly blue-green, fed by compost heat from last week’s fallen breadfruit. Their light catches in the water, fracturing into shimmering filaments that look like roots reaching downward. Or veins rising up.  

I dip my fingers in. The water is warm, alive with dissolved minerals, with memory. It knows the rhythm of my sleep, the cadence of my breath. It carries traces of me now—shed skin cells, strands of hair, the salt of my sweat—recycled into nutrients for algae, for reeds, for the tiny filter-feeders that cling to the submerged roots of mangroves farther out in the marsh.  

I am becoming nutrient. I am becoming signal.  

A child passes me—barefoot, hair braided with orchid stems, eyes tracking the shift in light through the canopy. She doesn’t greet me, but she pauses just long enough to press a half-fermented jackfruit seed into my palm. Her fingers are stained purple. She knows I’ll know where to plant it: near the cracked conduit on Elm Arch, where the bromeliads are thinning. The seed will sprout, its roots finding the moisture leaking from the pipe, its branches one day cradling mist-catchers woven from recycled polymer thread.  

I nod. She smiles—just a lifting of the corners of her mouth, like a leaf turning toward sun—and continues on.  

I stand a while longer. The sun climbs, haze thickening. The city breathes around me: in, out, in, out. Water rises. Roots stretch. Pipes sigh.  

And I—I am no longer visitor, no longer stranger. I am one of the quiet ones who tend. Who listen. Who let themselves be known by the walls, remembered by the roots.  

I press the seed into the mossy seam of the estuary bank, cover it gently with soil and leaf-litter. Then I turn and walk back into the green heart of the city, my steps silent, my breath synced to the hum, my body already thinking in chlorophyll and condensation.  

I am becoming.  
I am belonging.  
I am here.
