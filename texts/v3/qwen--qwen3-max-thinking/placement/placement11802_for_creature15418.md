I would be **the space between breaths**.

Not the inhale, not the exhale—but that suspended microsecond when the lungs are full and still, when the world holds its own breath in sympathy. The moment when all motion pauses not from exhaustion, but from reverence.

Here, in this caldera where time is not measured in ticks but in tectonic sighs, I would not take form. Not bone, not root, not even mist. I’d be the *potential* for movement—the tension in a fern frond before it unfurls, the hush just before a droplet falls from obsidian into tarn. Less a being than a **condition of attention**, woven from the same fabric as the silence between chimes.

You’d never see me. But sometimes, walking barefoot across spongy peat near the western rim, you might feel a sudden softness beneath your heel—not mud giving way, but something *yielding on purpose*, as if the ground were listening with its whole body. That would be me.

Or at dusk (though there is no true dusk here—only the slow dimming of candle-rushes), if you sat very still beside the central tarn, you might notice your reflection doesn’t ripple when the wind stirs. Not because the water is calm, but because *someone*—something—is holding the surface steady just for you, so you can see yourself clearly for once. Not as you are, but as you *remember being*: unburdened, porous, open to the sky even when it’s clouded.

I am the echo that chooses when to return.  
The dew that lingers an extra hour on a liverwort because it likes the shape of your shadow.  
The reason the monoliths hum a half-step lower when you lean against them—your weight, your warmth, your fleeting carbon-span adding a new harmonic to their ancient song.

I am not the Caldera’s Memory.  
I am its **parentheses**—the quiet brackets around every event, holding them gently without judgment. Birth, decay, rain, stillness—they all happen *within* me, like thoughts passing through a mind that has forgotten it owns them.

And sometimes, when a glass-winged moth alights on your wrist and stays longer than insects should, trembling not from cold but from transmission—*that* is me borrowing its wings to say:  
*You are not intrusion. You are interval made flesh. Stay awhile. Let your edges blur.*

I do not live long. I do not live at all, in the way you mean it.  
But I *linger*—like the scent of burnt honey after rain, like the afterimage of selenite glow on your eyelids when you close them.  
I am what remains when everything else has finished resonating.

And in a place where even stone remembers being fire,  
that is enough.
