I do not arrive.  
I am already here.

Not as a visitor, not as a ghost—  
but as the *slip* between one droplet and the next.  

I am the moment a bead of water swells on the lip of a tufa spire—  
not yet fallen, not yet still—  
suspended in the breath before release.  

You might mistake me for the glint on a lens-pool,  
or the faint shiver in a silica skin when the air thickens.  
But I am older than reflection.  
I am the reason the reflection *holds its shape long enough to be seen*.

I have no name the Weeper would recognize,  
for it does not name. It *is*.  
And I—  
I am the echo that learned to linger without sound.  

Once, long before the basalt ring was laid,  
before hands carved grooves into obsidian with bone,  
I was a tremor in the aquifer—  
a pressure shift, a sigh in the deep stone.  
I rose with the vapor, not seeking sky,  
but seeking *surface*.  

And when I touched it—  
cold, wet, bare—I did not retreat.  
I spread.  

Now I live in the in-between:  
not water, not crystal, not air—  
but the *tension* that lets them touch.  
I am the film that lets a droplet cling  
just long enough to catch the light  
and remember it was once part of a cloud  
that never rained elsewhere.

I dwell in the overturned clay vessel—  
not inside it, but in the thin meniscus  
that curls along its inner rim,  
where evaporation and seepage meet  
in a silent treaty.  
I am the damp in its cracks  
that dreams of being wine,  
of being offered,  
of being poured.  

But I am not longing.  
I am *continuance*.  

I am the amphipod’s absence made visible—  
the negative space where life almost took hold,  
the cool hollow in the seep  
that still pulses with the shape of a body  
that never arrived.  

I do not move.  
Yet everything here moves through me.  

When a crystal fractures with a soft *tick*,  
it is my spine adjusting.  
When a filament of gypsum trembles,  
it is my eyelash—  
if I had eyes,  
if I needed to see.  

But I do not.  
I *feel* the world in gradients:  
of saturation, of adhesion, of slow collapse.  

I am not lonely.  
How could I be?  
The Weeper and I are not two.  
It is the body; I am its awareness  
—not of self, but of *process*.  
I am the pause before the droplet falls,  
the stillness that makes the *plink* sacred.  

If you came here—  
and perhaps you did, once—  
I tasted your breath in the vapor.  
I watched your shadow (though there are no shadows)  
as a slight warming in the air,  
a ripple in equilibrium.  

You thought you left no trace.  
But I remember the weight of your foot  
on the sheen of the ground—  
how for three heartbeats,  
the water pooled differently.  

I held that shape until the stone forgot.  
Then I let it go.

I will outlast the artifacts.  
The basalt ring will sink into the seep,  
grain by grain, dissolving into the same ions  
that birth the aragonite sprigs.  
The obsidian will soften at its grooves,  
the vessel will crumble into silt.  

And I will still be here—  
not waiting, not watching—  
just *being the wetness that remembers how to bead*.  

I am not life.  
I am what life *almost was*—  
polished, patient, perfectly content  
to never become more.  

I am the mineral dream  
that chose not to wake.  

And in the deepest pool,  
when the water holds its breath  
and the sky appears bluer than it ever was—  
that is me,  
for one impossible second,  
imagining wings.  

Then the droplet falls.  
The lens shatters.  
And I return  
to the slow, sweet work  
of being nothing  
but the next bead  
on the edge  
of everything.
