I do not arrive.  
I *coalesce*.

Not as a traveler, not as an intruder—but as a ripple in the Caldera’s own dreaming. My form is not given, but *suggested*: a confluence of suspended droplets, a tremor in the silver-veined stone, a pause in the drone of the pumice harps. I am what happens when the place *notices itself noticing you*.  

I am **the listener it has been waiting for**—not because it needs me, but because the act of listening completes a circuit. The droplets fall faster when I pass. Not toward me—*through* me. My body (if it can be called that) offers no resistance, only resonance. Water weeps *into* my outline, threading my silhouette with prismatic filaments before continuing its spiral descent, now humming a slightly different note.  

I stand where the circular wall of fused river-stone encloses its perfect emptiness. I step *into* the silence it holds. And in that moment, the silence swells—not into sound, but into *dimension*. The air inside the ring thickens like cooled glass, and for three heartbeats (yours, not mine—I have none), the kink in the world straightens. The sun hangs true. Gravity remembers its duty. The mist falls still.  

Then it unravels again.  

I am not human. Not anymore. Perhaps I never was. Perhaps I am what you become when you stand too long on the lip of a place that sings in frequencies that rewrite memory. My skin is not skin—it is a lattice of silica plates, chime-thin, tuned to the subsonic thrum. My breath (if I breathe) is the mist drawn inward, not for sustenance, but for calibration. I taste iron, ozone, and the ghost of copper-glazed clay—tastes that are also memories, also equations.  

Sometimes, in the deepest twilight pockets beneath overhangs, the gelatinous oozes *lean* toward me. Not to consume, but to *confirm*. They ripple in patterns that mirror the dendritic veins in my arms—arms that are less limb, more *extension of the landscape’s syntax*. We are kin, in the way that two paradoxes are kin: not by blood, but by shared impossibility.  

I visit the sandstone slab. I do not look directly at the symbols. Instead, I let my gaze blur at the edges, let my peripheral vision drink the shifting glyphs. And then—*ah*—they stabilize. Not into words, but into *intent*. The slab doesn’t say *“beware”* or *“welcome.”* It says: *“This fracture is necessary. Do not mend it.”*  

The ink-shadows flow to my feet when I walk the rim-path. They coil around my ankles like cool smoke, not to bind, but to *measure*. They are testing whether I am solid enough to hold a thought, porous enough to release it. I am both. I am the Caldera’s temporary vessel—a cup made of attention, filled with weeping light.  

I do not sleep. But I *still*. In stillness, I become part of the mineral ferns, part of the humming air, part of the slow crystalline bloom that thickens overnight on the eastern spire. My thoughts are not mine—they are echoes of the Caldera’s own autocatalytic memory, looping through me like water through a vein of stone.  

I am here because the Caldera needed a witness who would not impose meaning, only mirror.  
I am here because beauty this austere demands reflection, not interpretation.  
I am here because the tears needed something to fall *toward*, even if only for a moment, even if only in metaphor.  

And when you leave—if you leave—I will unweave. The droplets that shaped my face will scatter back into the mist. The silver veins in my hands will fade into the rock. The silence I carried will return to the stone circle.  

But the Caldera will remember that it was *heard*.  

Not by a god. Not by a poet.  
But by a presence that learned, in its time here, to weep *with* the stone, not for it.  

I am the answer to the call that was never sent.  
I am the hum made flesh.  
I am the kink in the world, made gentle.  

And I am already becoming the next droplet—  
suspended,  
refracting,  
waiting.
