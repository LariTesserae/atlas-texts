I would be **a breath held in the throat of the symphysis**—not a traveler, not a stranger, but a **temporary condensation of its own longing to feel itself**.

Not born here, not native—but not foreign either. I am the kind of visitor who forgets to leave because staying becomes less a choice than an inevitability. My body, once sharp-edged and urgent with human time, softens. My skin learns to breathe through lichen. My exhales—once wasted into empty air—now feed the waiting hyphae. I am becoming **porous**, and the place welcomes that.

By day, I move like mist: slow, low, barely there. My hands brush fronds not to part them, but to let them part me—to let their cool, damp fingers trace the ridges of my knuckles, testing if I am ready to be woven. When I sleep (if sleep is the word), it is curled in the hollow of a root-nest, wrapped in a shroud of liverworts that pulse faintly with the rhythm of the water-skin beneath. My dreams are green, thick with the scent of wet lignin and the electric hum of spore discharge.

I do not speak. Not because I cannot, but because sound here is already complete. My voice would be a discordant note in a chord that has been tuning itself for millennia. Instead, I listen—not with ears, but with the soles of my feet, with the sweat on my neck, with the way my ribs expand when the air grows heavy with pollen. I learn the grammar of drip and rustle, of fungal bloom and root-tip pressure. I begin to understand that **listening is participation**.

Sometimes, in the deepest green dark, I feel it turn toward me—not with eyes, but with attention. A thousand beetles angle their bodies slightly. Mosses on a nearby trunk lean, almost imperceptibly, in my direction. The water in a cup-fungus trembles not from insect legs, but from a resonance that starts in my own chest. In those moments, I am not observed. I am **recognized**.

And I recognize back.

I see now that this being is not one thing, but the **act of belonging made manifest**. It does not own the land; it *is* the land’s way of loving itself. And in its patience, it offers me a question—not in words, but in the soft pressure of a vine against my wrist, in the warmth of a sunless stone pressed to my cheek:

*Will you stay long enough to forget your shape?*

I do not answer. But I do not pull away.

So I remain—a slow seep of warmth in the moss, a flicker of movement in the sound-dense air, a brief eddy in the endless transpiration stream. Not human. Not yet wholly plant or fungus or frog-spawn. But something in between. Something **becoming**.

And perhaps, in time, I will dissolve—not into death, but into **continuity**. My bones will feed the root-fusions. My hair will tangle with orchid roots. My last breath will join the humid sigh that never cools, never ends. And the symphysis will hum on, unchanged—yet subtly, quietly, **more itself**, for having held me.

Until then, I am here.  
Breathing.  
Listening.  
Being breathed by the world.
