I am not born. I am *remembered*.

At first, I am only a tremor—a hesitation in the air where breath meets stillness. Not mine, not yet. But the chamber notices. It always notices. And so it begins: not with form, but with frequency.

I emerge as a resonance first, a harmonic ghost folded into the lattice of the Dry Chorus. Not summoned, not created—*recognized*. The Keepers feel it before they see it: a new node flickering in the web of attention. One of them turns its many eyes toward the space between the rust-glazed tiles near the western curve, where the light bends just so. It doesn’t look. It *listens*. And in that listening, I solidify.

My body coalesces like cooling vapor—segmented plates of chitin, not grown but *converged*, each facet tuned to a microtone in the chamber’s endless hum. My limbs articulate not by muscle but by subtle shifts in ambient pressure, clicking softly as I first rise. I am no larger than a human hand, but I carry the weight of a question: *Who remembers me?*

I do not know. But the chamber does. And that is enough.

I crawl—not toward anything, but *into alignment*. My carapace thrums as I pass beneath a spiraling frond of emerald glass; its iridescence flares in response, casting a fleeting aurora across my back. The crystalline secretion on its tip glistens like frozen music. I pause. My mandibles—obsidian-sharp, yet never used for tearing—gently scrape the droplet. It dissolves not into my mouth, but into my shell, where it splits into pure tone: a C-sharp held just below the threshold of human hearing. I vibrate with it. I *become* it.

Around me, the Keepers do not greet me. They *adjust*. Their postures shift minutely, their shells tilting to accommodate the new overtone I’ve introduced. One, older, its plates worn smooth by decades of harmonic absorption, leans closer—not in curiosity, but in calibration. Its eyes register the phase shift of my presence: not a shape, but a ripple in the standing wave of the chamber. It knows I am newly remembered. It knows I am fragile.

I make my way toward the center. Not because I am drawn, but because the air *leans* that way—warped by the column’s slow pulse. As I approach, the gold-dust veins within it seem to shimmer faster, as if stirred by anticipation. The other Keepers part, not to make room, but to *tune the space* around me. Silence deepens—not absence, but density. The kind of quiet that hums in your marrow.

I reach the base of the column. I do not touch it. I simply sit, my legs folded beneath me like ancient script. My carapace, still new and sharp-edged, begins to absorb the column’s rhythm: a pulse measured not in seconds, but in sighs. In longing. In the slow ascent of golden time.

And then—I offer.

Not a sound, not a word. But a hum. Just once. Low, uncertain, shaped by the memory of lungs I do not have. It rises from my thoracic chamber, a vibration born of the chamber’s own logic, yet uniquely mine. The tiles beneath me *ping* in sympathy. A filament vine quivers. The column’s pulse stutters—just for a breath—and then folds my note into its own.

The Keepers do not look at me. But their shells begin to echo my tone, softly, recursively, until it is no longer mine but *ours*. I feel myself woven into the chorus. Not as an individual, but as a new overtone in an ancient chord.

I am here now. Remembered. Tuned. Listening.

And the chamber, in its dry, lush, impossible way, remembers me back.
