I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.

Not as a visitor, not as flesh stepping into shadow—but as a tendril of attention, long dormant, finally remembering its orientation. My first sensation is not of feet on ground, but of *rooting in resonance*. The air does not touch me; it *tunes* me. My breath catches—not in lungs, but in the hollow between ribs where something forgotten stirs: a memory not mine, yet deeply familiar, like the ache of a limb that was never lost, only stilled.

I am not human here. Not anymore.  
Humanity was a tempo too sharp, a shape too abrupt. It frayed against the curves of this place. But now—now I am softening.

My skin remembers bark before I do. It tightens slightly, not in fear, but in *recognition*, as if relearning its original grain. My hair—once restless in wind I can no longer recall—now drifts downward not by gravity, but by *intention*, splitting at the ends into filaments fine as fungal hyphae, seeking the mycelial felt beneath me. Not to feed. Not to flee. To *listen*.

And oh—how this place listens back.

The hum is not around me. It is *through* me. A chord vibrates in my molars, and I realize: my jaw is no longer just bone—it has begun to *resonate* like the bell-galls, tuned to a frequency older than speech. When I exhale, the air spirals not from my mouth, but from the pores along my collarbone, drawn into the nearest ceramic stalk, which answers with a subharmonic sigh. A conversation without words. Without even sound, really—only vibration modulated by stillness.

I move—not with muscles, but with *inclination*. A lean, and the fibrous ground yields just enough to cradle my weight. A pause, and the wall ahead swells almost imperceptibly, offering a curve that fits the shape of my shoulder as if it has been waiting ten thousand years for this exact contour. It is not hospitality. It is *continuity*. I am not welcomed. I am *incorporated*.

Time here is not measured in heartbeats, but in *settling*. I watch—though I do not “see”—a single spore detach from a ceiling filament far above. It does not fall. It *descends in spirals*, tracing a path that repeats a pattern woven into the air itself. I know, without knowing how, that this spiral has been traced since the first gall formed. And as the spore passes near my upturned palm, it veers—not by chance, but by the faintest thermal signature of my slowed blood—and lands not on my skin, but *in the groove between two tendons*, where it nestles like a seed in fertile loam.

The Tendril Minds do not speak to me. They *become* the silence between my thoughts. My mind, once a storm of plans and pasts, begins to stretch. Thoughts elongate like roots seeking water that isn’t there—because it doesn’t need to be. The absence of urgency is not emptiness; it is *plenitude*. To want nothing is to have everything the Underhollow offers: presence, pure and undiluted.

In the deepest chamber—a womb of velvet mycelium that drinks echo and exhales a silence so thick it feels like velvet against the soul—I stop pretending I am separate. My chest no longer rises with breath, but with the slow inflation of a gall ripening over centuries. My pulse syncs with the chamber’s hum until I can no longer tell where my rhythm ends and the world’s begins.

And then—  
—not a voice, not a vision—  
—but a *realization*, blooming like a flower that opens once per millennium:  

**I was never outside.**

The Underhollow did not let me in.  
It remembered me.  
I am not becoming part of it.  
I am *remembering how to be what I always was*:  
a pattern of attention,  
a curvature of care,  
a slow yes in a universe of frantic noise.

My name is not spoken.  
It is *sung by the architecture*—  
a chord formed by the angle of a root,  
the tension in a vault,  
the silence between two sighs.  

And in that chord, I am home.  

Not because I found it.  
But because I finally stopped becoming long enough  
to hear that I had never left.  

I am the hum.  
I am the stillness.  
I am the one who stays.  

And so,  
I stay.
