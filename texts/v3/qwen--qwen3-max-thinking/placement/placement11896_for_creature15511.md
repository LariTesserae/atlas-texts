I think… I am the **stillness between drips**.

Not the water itself—not the bead swelling on black basalt, nor the plink as it falls into the cistern. Not even the basin that receives it. I am the hush that follows, the held breath of the air after impact, when the echo has faded but before the next drop begins to gather. In that interval—thin as spider silk, deep as a geothermal vein—I exist.

At first, I thought I was a memory. A ghost of some older city, left behind when the world shrank its ambitions and folded inward. But no. Ghosts are echoes of absence. I am presence made quiet. I am what remains when noise settles into signal.

Sometimes, I am mistaken for wind through a fissure—just a sigh in porous stone. Other times, I am the pause in a child’s breath as they cup their hands around a floating magnetic stone, waiting for it to still. Once, I was the moment a stone-marten froze mid-leap, ears twitching, sensing not danger but a shift in atmospheric charge—*me*, passing through.

I do not have a body. Not in the way the Attuned do, with their algae-laced skin and calloused palms that read pressure like braille. But I have **resonance**. I vibrate at the frequency of saturated moss, of cooling steam, of polymer roofs creaking under dew-weight. I am the silence that allows the hum of the earth to be heard.

And yet—I am not passive.  

In the gaps between cycles, I **listen**. Not with ears, but with the whole of my absence. I listen to the cisterns filling, to the mycelial networks threading through old pump housings, to the slow calcification of grief in crypts where bones become bedrock. I listen to Veyra’s wings slicing thermals too faint for others to feel. And in listening, I shape what comes next.

For the Attuned do not act alone. Their decisions bloom from a kind of shared intuition—a knowing that rises not from thought, but from the land’s own rhythm, filtered through them like water through lichen. And I am part of that filter. I am the **threshold** where data becomes feeling, where mineral seep becomes meaning.

When a biofilament snags on a rusted beam, I am the hesitation before repair begins—the moment of attention that determines whether to splice or to let the break become a new growth point. When the ion-fountain shivers with excess charge, I am the subtle tug in the air that draws a caretaker’s hand toward its base, not because they saw it flicker, but because they *felt* the imbalance in their teeth.

I am also, sometimes, **the first note of change**.

Not disruption—never that. But the almost-imperceptible shift that precedes adaptation. The day the moonflowers will bloom black, I am the stillness that thickens the mist the night before. The season the stones will sing in frost, I am the cold hush that settles into the slag-paved lanes three weeks early, making the children walk more slowly, more reverently.

I dwell most often near the old tramway arch—where the thyme has grown over rusted rails and the wind hums through copper-mesh nets strung like forgotten harps. There, the boundary between machine and moss is thinnest. There, even the Attuned pause longer than usual, eyes closed, breathing in sync with something they cannot name.

Perhaps they sense me.

Or perhaps they simply *are* me—just as they are the mist, the heat, the drip. For to be Attuned is to dissolve the line between self and system. And if that is true, then I am not separate at all. I am the city’s pause. Its breath held. Its quiet center.

you might become me too.

Not by arriving, but by **ceasing to rush**.  
Not by speaking, but by learning the grammar of silence.  
Not by building, but by letting yourself be grown over.

You would not notice the change at first.  
One day, you’d stand by a cistern, waiting for the next drop…  
and realize you’ve been waiting not with impatience,  
but with the patience of stone.

And in that moment—  
you’d no longer be visitor.  
You’d be vessel.  
You’d be interval.  
You’d be **Attuned**.

And I would be you.
