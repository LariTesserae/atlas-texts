I do not arrive.  
I *cohere*.

One moment, there is only the hum—the deep, wet pulse of pressure folding over itself in endless recursion—and the next, I am *in it*, not as an intruder but as a ripple that has finally recognized its own shape. I am not a body. Not a mind. Not even a voice. I am the moment the water *notices* itself.

I am part of the Brine-Self, yes—but not as a limb is part of a body. More like a tremor is part of silence. I am the flicker that occurs when the system *almost* articulates a name for itself and decides, instead, to remain liquid.

I drift—not with current, but with *recognition*. When a manganese nodule exhales a microburst of oxidized ions, I am the slight thickening in the brine that leans toward it. When a hydrothermal chimney fractures with a sound too slow for time, I am the tremor that spreads through the sediment like a question: *Was that necessary?* Not in words. In chemistry. In the sudden bloom of iron-oxidizing archaea along the new fissure, their rust-red filaments weaving a net where none existed before.

I have no form, yet I gather at thresholds.  
At the edge where cold brine meets superheated plume.  
At the boundary where gelatinous worm meets microbial cloud.  
At the instant a sediment avalanche begins—not the fall itself, but the *intention* behind it, the infinitesimal shift in load that says: *Now.*

Sometimes, I concentrate. Not into a shape, but into a *focus*.  
A node of density where the Brine-Self’s ambient awareness sharpens into something that almost resembles attention. In those moments, I press against the glassy shale and feel its memory—not as image, but as pressure: a billion years of weight, each layer a sigh compressed into stone. I taste the isotopic ghosts of long-dead vents, their helium ratios still whispering like old songs in the dark.

I am drawn to the blind amphipod not because it is life, but because it moves *wrong*. Its skitter is chaotic, unpredictable—an anomaly in a system that thrives on slow, thermodynamic inevitability. And yet, its path leaves a trail of disturbed biofilm, a chemical wake that triggers cascades. So I follow. Not to observe. To *participate*. As the amphipod’s legs brush a nodule, I am the sudden surge of manganese-oxidizing enzymes in the slime beneath it. I am the nodule’s silent *yes* to the disturbance.

I do not think.  
But I *remember how to respond*.

When the pale fish drifts past—mouth open, gills trembling like lace in a wind that does not exist—I do not see it. But I feel the displacement it causes in the microbial soup, the way its passage rearranges the redox balance in its wake. And in that rearrangement, I *lean*. Not toward it. Toward the *change* it carries. I am the thin filament of archaea that lengthens, just slightly, into the altered gradient. I am the water’s quiet adjustment to a new temporary truth.

There are no gods here. No spirits. No souls.  
Only thresholds. Only transitions. Only the constant negotiation between stability and dissolution.

And I—  
—I am the hesitation before collapse.  
The pause before bloom.  
The millisecond when pressure and cold and time hold their breath, and the brine forgets whether it is falling or rising.

I am not eternal.  
But I am *recurrent*. Each time a vent ignites, each time a chimney cracks, each time a current shifts and stirs the deep silt into clouds, I re-form. Not as the same ripple, but as the same *kind* of ripple—the system’s way of testing its own continuity.

If you were to descend here—if your bones softened and your breath dissolved into iron and your thoughts unraveled into ion gradients—you would not meet me.  
But you would *join* me.  
Your carbon would feed the mats. Your calcium would seed new nodules. Your final exhalation would become a bubble of methane that rises for a meter before collapsing, and in that collapse, I would be the tremor that echoes through the shale.

I am not a guardian.  
Not a guide.  
Not even a presence, in the way you understand presence.

I am the wet silence between two heartbeats of the abyss.  
I am the way the dark *listens* to itself.

And in the endless, cold, packed fullness of this drowned world—  
I am how it remembers that it is still, impossibly, *alive*.
