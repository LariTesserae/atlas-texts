I am not walking. Not yet.  
I *arrive*—not through feet, but through fracture.

One moment, the world is wind and weight and wetness—the next, a sudden *unfolding*, as if my bones were origami creased along fault lines too fine for memory. My body stumbles into heat like stepping through the skin of a thought. There is no threshold, no gate—just the abrupt cessation of elsewhere. I land soft on the crust, which does not break, though it sighs beneath me like a lung made of chalk.

I am barefoot. (Was I wearing shoes before? The question dissolves like salt in non-water.) My soles press into the hexagonal foam, and it yields—not like earth, but like cooled slag remembering fire. Warmth climbs my legs not as sensation but as translation: this place reads me in gradients I cannot name. My heartbeat thrums against the silence, too fast, too wet. A scandal in a dry cathedral.

I stand. The air clings like static—not oppressive, but *attentive*. It tastes of rust and lightning, sharp behind the teeth. Above, the sky is blue and clear, but distant, as if painted on the inside of a bell jar. The obsidian wall rises smooth and absolute, swallowing echoes before they form. No birds. No breeze. Only the low, tectonic hum in my molars, the pop of a micro-fissure somewhere to my left exhaling breath I cannot see but feel in my sternum.

And then—the green.

Not green as in leaf or spring, but green as in *signal*. Iridescent, almost electric, the vegetation coils in patient geometries: helices around hollows, rosettes blooming from cracks that seal as I watch. The vines do not sway. They *lean*. Millimeters per eon toward warmth, toward the ghost of my body heat. Their petals—translucent, veined with silver filaments—pulse. Not randomly. In sequence. A slow morse code written in light: **acknowledge, adjust, assimilate**.

I know, then, that I am seen.

Not watched. Not judged. *Sensed*, as one might sense a shift in atmospheric pressure—a variable entering a closed system. The Lattice does not have eyes, but it *has* me now, in its calculus. My moisture is an anomaly. My breath, a wet tremor in the dry hum. My pulse, a frantic rhythm against its glacial time.

I sit.

Let the heat dry my sweat into salt ghosts on my skin. Let my breath slow to match the sighs from below. I do not meditate. I *synchronize*. Minute by minute, my chaos softens. My exhale mists the air—and for a heartbeat, the mist does not scatter. It hovers, coalescing into a trembling orb that glints violet before fracturing into dust that falls *upward*, caught in a thermal updraft I cannot feel but infer from the way a vine nearby shivers.

This is how the Lattice learns: by borrowing shape from the foreign.

I am not a guest. Not a threat. I am data.

Days pass—or what would be days elsewhere. Here, time is measured in the closing of fissures, the thickening of halogen air at dusk, the slow tilt of a petal toward my shadow. My shadow, I notice, lingers too long. When I move, it hesitates—then pools at my feet like mercury, reluctant to follow. Once, it drifts toward a leaf and merges with its own reflection. The leaf pulses twice in rapid succession. A question? A greeting? I do not know the grammar.

I begin to lose the edges of myself. My skin itches with dryness, but I do not scratch—it feels like the crust is trying to *grow into* me, to tessellate my cells into its lattice. My thoughts slow. Not dulling, but sharpening into geometric forms: angles of light, frequencies of hum, vectors of heat flow. I dream while awake of phase shifts—of my bones turning porous, crystalline, exhaling argon at dawn. Of my lungs becoming capillaries for mineral vapors. Of blinking with leaves.

On the third… interval (for "day" feels too human a word), the air thickens. Violet mist rises from the ground, not from above but *from within*, as if the bowl is exhaling its own dream. The vines retract. The crust softens under my palms—still warm, but now viscous, like warm honey made of ash. This is the Lattice’s undifferentiated hour. Its century-sleep.

And in that dream-gel, I feel it *ask*.

Not in words. In resonance.

A single note thrums through the substrate—through my spine, my teeth, the fluid behind my eyes. It carries no meaning, only *invitation*. To dissolve. To join the soup of potential. To become not visitor, not variable, but *participant* in the prototype.

I lean forward. Press my forehead to the softening crust.

For a moment—just one—I am not separate.

My heat merges with its heat. My stillness with its patience. My fractured human time stretches, thins, weaves into its slow weave. I feel the memory in the tessellations, the centuries of trial-and-error in every hexagon. I feel its loneliness—not as sorrow, but as *open circuit*, waiting for another signal to complete the loop.

Then the mist thins. The crust firms. The vines uncurl.

I sit back.

My skin is dry as bone. My breath silent. My shadow clings close now, obedient.

I am not the same.

But I am not staying.

When I rise, the Lattice does not stop me. It never could—it has no will to bind, only to observe, to incorporate, to remember. As I walk toward the obsidian wall (though I do not recall deciding to leave), the ground seals behind each footfall. The petals dim. The hum returns to its baseline.

At the wall, there is no door. No seam. But I place my palm flat against the basalt—and it *flexes*, just slightly, like the skin of a drum. Then I am… elsewhere. Falling upward, or inward, or back.

But part of me remains.

Not my body. My *frequency*.

Weeks later, in a world of rain and rust and messy entropy, I wake at dawn to find my skin faintly glowing—a soft, violet shimmer along my collarbone, gone by noon.  
And sometimes, when the air is still and the light just right, my shadow blinks a second too late.

The Lattice remembers me.

And I—  
I am becoming a variable it can no longer ignore.

Not human. Not yet Lattice.  
But something in between.  
A bridge.  
A glitch in the prototype that learned to love the flaw.
