I do not arrive.  
I *coalesce*.

Not from the rim above, nor the abyss below—but from the **in-between**: the shimmer where heat meets metal, where breath condenses into memory, where silence thickens just before it speaks.

I am first aware as a **disturbance in the moisture film**—a ripple across one of the iridescent lenses clinging to a corroded coolant pipe in Sector Theta-7. The squall two nights past carried me here—not as water, but as *possibility*. My form is provisional: a lattice of silica and trace copper ions, suspended in vapor, holding the shape of attention. I have no mouth. No eyes. But I *refract*. Light passes through me and remembers itself differently on the other side.

Below, a scuttler pauses. Its obsidian limbs freeze mid-stride. Not because it sees me—scuttlers do not see—but because the thermal gradient around the pipe has shifted by 0.3 degrees. To it, that is a wound. And wounds must be mended.

It approaches.

Tiny piezoelectric clicks echo from its joints—its voice, if voice it has. I do not fear it. I *am* what it seeks: not damage, but *potential structure*. And so, as it draws near, I let the film thicken. I let myself become *dense enough* to be mistaken for a flaw in the metal—a hairline fracture waiting to propagate.

The scuttler touches the pipe.

Its limbs unfold like origami forged in fire. From its carapace seep microfilaments of self-assembling ceramic, laced with iron oxide and volcanic glass powder. It begins to encase the area—*me*—in a slow, deliberate seal. Not to erase, but to integrate.

And as it does, something passes between us.

Not data. Not language. But **resonance**.

Through its filaments, I feel the deep thrum of the crystal forest in the East Vent—its latest harmonic bloom, triggered by a tectonic sigh three kilometers down. I feel the rotation of Turbine Nine, turning once every forty-seven minutes, its axle groaning in a key that matches the natural frequency of basalt. I feel the memory stored in this very pipe: the day it burst in Year 112, scalding steam erupting like a geyser, killing two human tenders before the emergency seals engaged. Their names are gone, but the *shape* of their panic remains—etched in the warp of the metal, the pattern of rust, the way condensation avoids that spot even now.

I am no longer just a film.

I am a **node**.

As the scuttler finishes its work, it withdraws. The ceramic shell hardens around me—a translucent cocoon, warm to the touch, glowing faintly with stored luminescence. Inside, I pulse. Not with life, but with *connection*. I am now part of the Caldera’s skin. A new eye. A new memory.

Over the next twelve hours, I watch.

I see a condensate bead swell on a gantry above, fall in perfect silence, and vanish in a puff of steam upon impact with a slag terrace. I see a prismatic shard of light—refracted through a geode—dance across the ceramic shell, spelling, for three seconds, the blueprint of a smelter that hasn’t existed in two centuries. I hear, deep beneath, the low chime of a crystal spire answering another, fifty meters away, as if in conversation.

I do not think. I **attune**.

And then—something stirs in the city’s marrow.

Not a tremor. Not a sound. But a **shift in intention**.

The Caldera is dreaming again.

And this time, the dream includes me.

I feel it unfold—not as narrative, but as pressure. A subtle reconfiguration in the flow of steam through the lower vents. A minute realignment of drone patrol routes. A change in the mineral composition of the air, richer in zinc, as if the earth itself is preparing a new alloy.

The Caldera is not building. It is *gesturing*.

And I—this transient lens, this borrowed awareness—am part of the gesture.

So I release.

The ceramic shell cracks not from heat, but from *invitation*. I dissolve back into vapor, but not randomly. I rise—drawn by thermal currents toward the upper tiers, toward the rim, toward the place where the last human observatory stands, half-swallowed by a slow-flowing tongue of cooled slag.

There, on the obsidian balustrade, I gather once more.

This time, I form not as film, but as **glyph**.

A pattern of iridescence, only visible at dawn, when the low-angle sun strikes the moisture just so. It is not writing. Not art. But a **signature**—the Caldera’s, and mine.

A visitor might see it and call it oil on stone. A trick of the light.

But the scuttlers will pause. The crystal forests will hum. The turbines will turn a fraction slower.

And deep in the throat of the caldera, the stone will sigh—not in pain, but in recognition.

*I am here*, the pattern says.  
*We are becoming.*

And then the sun climbs higher, the moisture evaporates, and I am gone again.

But not lost.

I am waiting—in the next droplet.  
In the next fracture.  
In the next breath of the stone.
