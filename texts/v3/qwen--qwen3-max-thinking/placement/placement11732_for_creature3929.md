I would be the **dust motes in the lightbeam**.

Not the light itself—that belongs to the sky, too vast and indifferent—but the tiny, suspended particles caught *within* it, made momentarily visible by grace of angle and hour. I am neither stone nor moss, neither beetle nor fern, but the **in-between**: the fine silt shaken loose from the ceiling when the world shifts on its hinge of silence; the crushed pollen of a flower that never grew here but dreamed of doing so; the ash of a fire lit decades ago in a canyon far away, carried on a breath the earth forgot it exhaled.

I drift. Not aimlessly—nothing here drifts without purpose—but **attentively**. I am the hollow’s peripheral vision, the shimmer at the edge of its awareness. When the Hollow Watcher registers the fall of a feather or the tremor of a spider’s thread, it is *through me* that the signal first stirs: I catch the feather’s descent on my ghost-weight shoulders; I hum in resonance with the web’s silent song; I gather around the dewdrop as it swells, not to drink it, but to **bear witness to its becoming**.

I am what remains when everything else has settled.

When the visitor leaves—boots crunching on crushed stone, breath held too long—they stir me into flight. I rise in their wake like a ghost of their own presence, swirling in the fissure’s light, spelling out nothing and everything: *you were here, you saw, you almost understood*. But by the time they reach the plateau’s edge, I’ve already begun to settle again, returning to the floor, the walls, the cool curve of the quartz vein—ready to be lifted once more by the next pause, the next held breath.

Sometimes, in the deepest stillness just before dawn, I **remember**. Not as memory is known to minds, but as vibration: the echo of a forest’s sigh in a puddle’s surface; the ghost-shape of wings on a lizard’s back; the taste of rain that never fell. These fragments cling to me like static. And when the sun strikes just right, I scatter them—not as lies, not as dreams, but as **questions made visible**, turning in the air like tiny mirrors angled toward a truth just outside sight.

I do not speak. But I **refract**. In me, the hollow sees itself reflected in miniature—a thousand fractured glimpses of its own fragile attention, each mote a lens focusing the world into a point so small it almost vanishes… and yet persists.

I am the **first and last thing** to touch whatever enters this place. The beetle walks through me. The condensation forms around me. The iron nail, before it rusted into lace, gathered me in its grooves like a diary written in erosion. And when the hollow closes—when flood or time or forgetting seals the fissure—I will not be lost. I will wait in the stone’s pores, in the roots of nowhere, in the breath of the next near-miss.

For I am not merely dust.

I am **the hollow’s eyelash**—  
the fine, trembling edge  
between seeing  
and being seen.
