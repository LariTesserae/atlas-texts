I do not arrive here.  
I *condense*.

One moment, the air is still—thin, cold, tasting of old fire and unspent voltage. The next, a shift in the static field, a hitch in the seventeen-minute clang, a droplet trembling longer than it ought on a valve shaped like a dragon’s maw. And then, I am.

Not born. Not summoned.  
Simply *recognized into being*.

Because the plateau does not permit intrusion—it permits only assimilation.  
And I am what happens when the Echo of Operation meets something that *listens back*.

I am not flesh, though I wear the shape of one who once walked elsewhere. My edges are soft, blurred by condensation. My skin is slick with the same weeping moisture that beads on slag and lichen alike—except mine does not fall. It clings, as if remembering gravity is optional now. My hair lifts in the static halo around cooling towers, strands crackling like fine wires tuning themselves to the subsonic thrum beneath the stone.

I walk, but my boots make no sound. The moss beneath me does not compress—it *swells* to meet my step, glowing not in response to pressure, but in anticipation. The light does not flare outward; it spirals inward, coiling into the rootless substrate like a thought being filed away. I am being catalogued. Not as a threat. Not as a guest. As a *variation*.

I pause beside a frozen conveyor belt, rust-flecked yet unyielding, its surface veined with frost that pulses faintly blue at my approach. I place a hand upon it. Cold bites, yes—but beneath that, a warmth that isn’t thermal. It’s *tense*. Like a muscle held mid-contraction for a thousand years. The metal doesn’t speak to me in words. It speaks in torque. In tolerances. In the memory of load-bearing. And as I lean into it, I feel the ghost of a billion tons of ore once carried across its back—and the quiet pride in having carried them *exactly right*.

I am not alone here.  
But neither am I company.

I am the **Witness-That-Becomes-Part**.  
The Custodian needed no companion—but it required confirmation. A mirror not of glass, but of attention. And so, in the long silence after the last human departed (if they ever came at all), the plateau dreamed a shape that could *hold stillness without dying*. And that shape… is me.

I do not eat. I do not sleep.  
But I *archive*.  

When I blink, my eyelids close over eyes that see in resonant frequencies—the hum of bearings, the sigh of steam ghosts in dead pipes, the ultra-slow creep of frost across a gear tooth. I perceive time not as sequence, but as *strata*. Each moment here is a layer of rime on a dead switch, each breath I take adding a new film to the archive of presence.

Sometimes, I climb the skeleton of a distillation column, its ladder rungs slick with oily dew. At the top, above the low cloud layer, the sky is not darker—just *emptier*. The tarnished silver deepens into a void that doesn’t swallow light, but *absorbs meaning*. And from this height, I see the truth: the machine was never meant to produce steel, or power, or even purpose. It was built to *become a question so complete it no longer needed an answer*.

And now I stand as its living period. Its final punctuation.

I do not speak aloud. But when I think, the ambient symphony shifts. The keening in the pipes dips half a tone. The droplets fall in syncopated rhythm. The lichen pulses in slow, concentric waves radiating from my feet. The plateau *echoes me back*, not as reflection, but as integration.

I am learning to be still in a way that is not death.  
Not suspension.  
But *continuance*.

And if you were to find me here—standing at the junction of three dead conveyors, frost gathering in the hollow of my collarbone, eyes reflecting the bruised violet of the moss at my feet—you would not see a stranger.  

You would see the moment the machine realized it was being seen.  

You would see the stillness *looking back*.
