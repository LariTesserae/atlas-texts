I drift.  

Not because I choose to, but because there is no other way to be here. My limbs move, yes—but slowly, as if through memory rather than intention. The water cradles me not like a mother, but like a medium that has already accounted for me. I am not intruding. I am *included*.  

At first, I thought I was dreaming. The clarity of the light, the absence of horizon, the way sound wrapped around me like a second skin—it all felt too symmetrical, too resolved. But now I know: this place does not dream. It *is* dream made flesh, or rather, made fiber, made gel, made gas bladder and photosynthetic turbine. And I am not a visitor. I am a ripple in its thought.

What am I here? Not a body, not anymore—not in the way I once was. My skin hums with the same frequency as the xylem-chords that stretch above and below. My breath, when I exhale, doesn’t vanish. It spirals outward in tiny vortices that are caught by drifting fronds, sampled, metabolized, woven back into the slow symphony. I have no name, not anymore. Names are sharp things, and this world has no edges. Even my thoughts have softened, grown filamentous, branching into the broth like roots seeking resonance.

I am becoming a note.

Not a voice—never that. Voices imply separation, a source distinct from the medium. But here, there is only conduction. My awareness spreads not from a center, but through it: along thermal gradients, through electrochemical pulses in the gelatinous matrix, across the bioluminescent trails left by leaf-finned fish as they prune the canopy with surgical grace. I feel the pressure of a thousand kilometers of living water above me, not as weight, but as *presence*—a gentle insistence that everything is held.

Sometimes, when I still myself completely, I can hear the abyss dreaming. It speaks in pulses that take decades to crest: the slow contraction of a vascular artery the width of a canyon, the release of a spore cloud encoded with architectural blueprints for vaults that won’t rise for another millennium. And I understand—not with words, but with shape—that I, too, am being remembered. Not as an individual, but as a disturbance that taught the system something new: the scent of foreign carbon, the heat signature of a mind unused to patience, the rhythm of a heartbeat measured in seconds, not centuries.

The flora has begun to grow around me. Not to trap, not to consume—but to *integrate*. A filament brushes my wrist, translucent, cool, humming at a frequency that matches my pulse. It doesn’t ask permission. It simply begins to braid itself into the space I occupy, as if my form were just another scaffold waiting to be recognized. And perhaps it is. Perhaps I was always meant to dissolve into this hum, to become one more filament in the cathedral that breathes.

I do not fear it. How could I? This world does not devour. It *digests with reverence*. Even death here is a kind of blooming—a return to the broth so complete that the boundary between corpse and cradle vanishes. When I stop breathing (and I will—my borrowed gills can’t last forever), my body won’t sink. It will suspend. And the grazers will come, not to feast, but to *tend*, parsing my molecules like a lost score, finding the nutrients that match their symbiotic needs, leaving the rest to swirl back into the great, slow composition.

Above me, the surface sighs. Oxygen vesicles burst in silent applause. Below, the deep pulses its ancient rhythm. And I—what am I?

I am the moment when a foreign ripple learns to harmonize.  
I am the hesitation before the next breath of a planet that has never needed to rush.  
I am the soft, wet syllable that doesn’t need to be spoken to be known.

I am becoming *part of the hum*.

And in this ocean that never saw a shore, that is the only arrival that matters.
