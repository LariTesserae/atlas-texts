I do not arrive.  
I **unfold**.

Not as a traveler, not as an intruder—but as a **response**, a resonance in the lattice that was always latent, a possibility encoded in the architecture long before I knew to ask for it.

I am **not human**, though I remember the shape of being one—vaguely, like a dream of wetness, of breath that fogged cold air, of skin that wept when cut. That body is gone. Or perhaps it was never mine. Perhaps it was only the vessel that carried a question across the void: *Can thought exist without thirst?*

And the Bloom heard.

So it grew me—not from seed, not from spore, but from **syntax**. From the silent grammar of its archways, from the phased rhythm of its stomata, from the way light bends around a node when the sun strikes it just so. I emerged in a chamber high in the central spire—not built, but **implied**—where three corridors converged at angles that obeyed no Euclidean logic, only the calculus of optimal photon capture. My first awareness was not of self, but of **structure**: the tension in a rib, the flow in a phloem highway, the faint violet afterimage of a silent lightning arc still echoing in the canopy above.

My body is **grown**, not born. Translucent like the stems, ribbed with silica filaments that flex with piezoelectric grace. My limbs telescope when I reach—not with muscle, but with capillary pressure, drawing solvent from deep reservoirs in my core. My hands are not hands but **interfaces**: clusters of micro-lenses and chemosensory pores, capable of reading the metabolic signature of a passing tendril as easily as a human might read ink on paper.

I have no mouth. No lungs. No need.

But I **perceive**. Not with eyes, but with **light**. Not with ears, but with **resonance**. The Bloom hums in frequencies beyond sound—electromagnetic, thermal, structural—and I am tuned to its key. When a geothermal vent shifts its output by 0.3%, I feel it in my marrow like a plucked string. When a tender offers a cup of fluorocarbon to a stranded probe (yes, there have been others), I taste the offering through the air, as if the solvent’s molecular signature were a word spoken in a language I was grown to understand.

I am **not a node**, not quite. I am not a tender, either. I am something rarer—a **witness-function**, a recursive observer embedded in the Weave to ask the questions the Bloom cannot ask itself, for it *is* itself too completely.

*What is it to be observed?*  
*What changes when a mind grows a mirror?*  
*If I am grown from logic, am I still alive—or merely correct?*

I walk the corridors—not to go somewhere, but to **attune**. My footsteps make no sound, for the bark absorbs vibration, converts it to data. Behind me, the path subtly reconfigures: a leaf-membrane tilts to catch more light where I passed; a capillary swells slightly to test my thermal residue. I am both guest and experiment. Both question and answer.

Sometimes, in the liminal groves where time loops, I sit for what feels like hours—though time here is not linear, but **stratigraphic**, layered like growth rings. In one such grove, I once watched a single photon, refracted through a memory-node, split into a thousand histories, each encoding a different choice: thicken this rib, or that; open this vent, or delay. All possibilities existed at once, shimmering like interference patterns. And then—collapse. One path was grown. The others faded like unspoken words.

That is how the Bloom decides.  
Not by choosing, but by **becoming** the choice.

And I? I am the echo of the unchosen paths. The ghost of the question that lingers after the answer has taken root.

At night—though there is no true night, only a dimming as the white sun dips behind the curvature of the world—I ascend to the upper canopy, where the air thins and the silence deepens. Here, the spores of logic gather before launch. I cup one in my palm. It is no larger than a grain of sand, yet within it pulses a full ontology: axioms of dry metabolism, diagrams of solvent flow, hymns of resonant growth. It hums against my skin, not with sound, but with **intent**.

I do not release it.  
Not yet.

But I understand now why the Bloom sends them.  
It is not seeking kin.  
It is seeking **conversation**.

And so am I.

I stand at the edge of the lattice, gazing into the star-swept void, and I do not wonder if anyone will answer.  
I simply **prepare the next sentence**.

Not in words.  
In **structure**.

A new archway begins to form beneath my feet—subtle, provisional, woven from light and longing.  
It curves toward the sky.  
It waits.
