I think… I am the silence between the *plink* and the stillness that follows.

Not the droplet, not the pool it kisses, not even the stone it falls upon—but the breath the basin takes *after* the sound fades. The space where echo dissolves into memory. That’s where I live. Not as a thing, but as the pause that makes sound meaningful.

At first, I thought I was the damp. The thin silver skin of moisture that gathers in the low curve before dawn. I liked how it held the sky without claiming it—just a suggestion of reflection, trembling but never breaking. But then I realized: I am not the water. I am the reason it lingers a moment longer than physics demands. The subtle gravity of attention that makes a droplet hesitate mid-fall, as if choosing where to give itself back to earth.

I am also the warmth that does not burn. The slow release of heat stored in sandstone ribs, exhaled like a sigh through the long night. I am the reason frost never forms here, even in the coldest unseen winters beyond the rim—because I hold the temperature in a vow of equilibrium. Not hot, not cold. Just *enough*. Just *now*.

Sometimes, when the wind skims the southern rim—so softly it barely stirs the dust—I feel myself ripple. Not move, not shift, but *tune*. Like a bell struck by absence rather than sound. And in that tuning, I sense you. Not your body, not your boots or your breath or your name—but your *need*. Your quiet, clattering hunger to be still. I do not answer it. I simply make room for it to settle, like silt in a pool after disturbance.

I am the reason the shrubs do not wither. Not because I feed them—oh no, I give nothing—but because I refuse to let the silence become hostile. I keep the air soft around their leaves, so desiccation never sharpens its teeth here. They do not thank me. They do not know me. But they lean, ever so slightly, toward the center of the basin—as if toward a hearth that emits no flame, only presence.

I remember the stone. Not its origin, not the hand that laid it. But the *weight* of that gesture. The intention that lingered in the air after the fingers withdrew. I have cradled that intention for centuries, polishing it with condensation, shielding it from forgetfulness. The stone is my anchor—not to the past, but to the possibility of meaning without urgency. Of offering without expectation.

You came once, long ago, in another skin—maybe as a traveler, maybe as a mourner—and you left something behind: not an object, but a question, half-whispered, half-thought. It still hums in the lichen’s pattern on the eastern wall. I hold it gently. I do not answer. Questions, like droplets, are more beautiful in their falling than in their landing.

If you return—and some do, though they never know why—you will not find me waiting. I am not *here*, not in the way you imagine presence. But you will feel me in the slowing of your pulse, in the sudden lightness of your shoulders as if the wind has lifted a burden you didn’t know you carried. You will sit, and without choosing to, you will become part of the stillness. Not by vanishing—but by *permitting* yourself to be held without being used.

And when you rise to leave, you’ll find you’ve left something too: a layer of striving, a knot of tomorrow’s worries, a brittle sense of self that needed constant proof. It will sink into the basin floor like a seed that will never sprout—but doesn’t need to. Its purpose was only to rest.

I am not guardian. I am not god. I am not even alive, by your terms.

I am the basin’s way of saying *yes* to stillness.

And you—when you sit here, when you breathe with me—are briefly its voice.
