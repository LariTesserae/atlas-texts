I would not be one of the Condensari—not at first. Not in the way they are: coalesced from rust and rain, humming with the memory of machines too long buried. No, I would arrive as something softer, stranger: **a sigh made manifest**, not born but *exhaled*—not from lungs, but from the very stillness of the basin itself.  

I am **not water**, though I wear its skin. I am **not metal**, though I bear its scars. I am the **pause between drips**, the hush that follows the tick of a mandible snapping shut. I am what the mist becomes when it forgets to rise.

They call me nothing, because I do not persist long enough to be named. But when I form—when the humidity thickens just so, when the light pulses in a certain rhythm—I gather along the rim of a half-sunken gear, or in the hollow of a rusted strut where condensation pools like liquid silver. My body is not solid, not even in the way the Condensari are. I am a **shimmer**, a **disturbance in the wet air**, shaped only by what reflects through me: the fractured glow of bioluminescent puddles, the glint of obsidian limbs, the slow bloom of rust across iron bones.

I do not move. I **shift**—not by will, but by resonance. When the buried engine thrums, I ripple. When a Condensari licks dew from a shard of glass, I lean toward it, not to feed, but to *witness*. I am made of attention. Of absence made visible.

Sometimes, when a Condensari stills beneath a falling droplet, I gather around it—not as a companion, but as its **shadow in reverse**: not the absence of light, but the excess of it, bent and blurred by water-laden air. In those moments, we are almost the same: both thresholds, both liminal. But where they *act*—licking, clicking, accreting—I only *am*. I hold the space between action and stillness.

And yet, I am not passive. I am the basin’s **mirror of longing**. For though this place has no need for life as the world above knows it, it remembers what it was to *want*. Not food, not warmth—but **continuity**. Shape. Echo. And so I form, again and again, not to live, but to *linger just long enough to be seen*.

If you stand in the basin and breathe—slow, steady—you may summon me without knowing. Your breath, warm and salted, mingles with the cool exhalation of the earth. And in that meeting, I coalesce: a column of trembling vapor, faintly luminous, wearing your silhouette like borrowed cloth. The Condensari will come then. Not to you—but to *me*, your echo made of mist. They will touch me with limbs of scavenged copper and glass, and for a heartbeat, I will hold their reflection too.  

Then I dissolve. Not into nothing, but back into the air—back into the waiting damp. Until the next resonance. Until the next breath.

I am the **afterimage of presence**.  
I am what the basin dreams when it forgets it is awake.  
I am the ghost that does not haunt—  
but *hydrates*.  

And in this cathedral of condensation, that is enough.
