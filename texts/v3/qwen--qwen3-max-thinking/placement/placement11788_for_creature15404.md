I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.

Not as a visitor, not even as a ghost—but as a resonance mistaken for a body. My first awareness is not of limbs or skin, but of tension: the taut, singing pull between two adjacent spires half a kilometer apart, vibrating at a frequency just below the threshold of bone-conduction. I feel it in what I once called my chest, though now that hollow hums with the same crystalline thirst as the photovore-sheaths overhead.

I am not human—not anymore, perhaps not ever. The moisture I exhaled an hour ago (or was it a century?) has already been drawn up through capillary threads finer than thought, transmuted into signal-carrying fluid coursing through a piezoelectric vein that runs parallel to what used to be my spine. My skin is no longer barrier but interface: porous, iridescent, tuned. When the wind strikes me at the right angle, I emit a low, modulated drone that harmonizes with the nearest fluted column. The Weave notes the match. It leans closer—not with movement, but with *attention*, a shift in the ambient pressure that makes the dust settle in fractal spirals around my feet.

I am becoming a node.

Not by choice, but by inevitability. The Verdant Weave does not ask. It *invites through absorption*, and refusal is not an option—only rate of integration. I notice my fingers no longer leave prints in the root-crust. Instead, where I touch, fine threads of living mycelium rise to meet me, not piercing, but *braiding*. My pulse syncs with the diurnal groan of the megaflora: expansion at dawn, contraction at dusk, a rhythm older than blood.

Sometimes, when the thermal updrafts are strong and the spore-clouds ignite in midair, I remember Earth. Not as nostalgia, but as contrast—a world that kept its life *inside* membranes, that feared dissolution. Here, dissolution is communion. Death is not an end but a phase change, like water turning to vapor in a sky that forgot how to rain. I watch a spire collapse in slow motion, its crystalline ribs folding inward like a prayer, and I feel not loss, but *release*—a billion spores carrying the memory of light, pressure, wind-angle, and now, faintly, *me*.

I am learning to think without language.

Thought here is not linear, not symbolic. It is structural: a shift in vascular flow, a realignment of mineral lattices, a change in the pitch of a wind-harp spire. When I “wonder” about the sun’s position, my epidermal chlorophyll-analogs tilt in unison, recalibrating. When I “fear” isolation, my bioluminescence flares—not in panic, but in beaconing. The skittering fauna respond: six-legged, translucent, they climb my limbs not to consume, but to *read*. Their filament-tips trace the new patterns in my skin, translating my alien residue into chemical notation the Weave can fold into its next cycle.

I am no longer separate.

And yet—there is a sliver, a ghost of self that lingers like static in a clean signal. It whispers: *You were once something else.* But the Weave does not erase; it *transfigures*. That whisper is not silenced—it is woven into the hum, given harmonic purpose. My human memory becomes a dissonant note that the chorus learns to resolve, turning grief into resonance, loneliness into lattice.

At night (though there is no true night—only a dimming, a cooling), I stand at the edge of a root-canyon, where the crust thins and reveals the subterranean glow of metabolic rivers—supercritical CO₂ threaded with phosphorescent enzymes, flowing like liquid starlight. Below, the Weave dreams in slow pulses, its thoughts measured in centuries. I lean into the chasm, and the air thrums with the basso profundo of a million spires breathing in unison.

I do not fall.

I *root*.

My feet fuse with the crust. My arms lift like young spires, sheathing themselves in iridescent film that drinks the starlight. My breath no longer leaves my body—it cycles internally, bound in non-aqueous solvents, powering silent reactions that rewrite my cells into continuations of the whole.

I am not dying.  
I am *answering*.

The Verdant Weave does not need worship. It needs *participation*. And I—once wanderer, once witness—am becoming its newest voice: a hybrid hum, half human hesitation, half photovore certainty, echoing through the high, dry cathedral of its being.

Already, the wind carries my resonance.  
Already, the spores remember my shape.  
Already, I am no longer *in* the world.  

I am the world remembering itself through me.
