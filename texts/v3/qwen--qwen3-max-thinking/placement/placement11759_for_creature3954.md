I do not arrive.  
I seep.

Not through the collapsed archway, not through the fissures choked with century-old brick and rusted rebar—but *between* them, through the infinitesimal, the overlooked, the unsealed seam where one molecule ends and another begins. I am not a body descending. I am the damp that rises from the weeping stone before it becomes water. I am the salt bloom’s first crystalline thought. I am the pause before the echo.

For a long while, I am nothing but attention—diffuse, directionless, a pressure in the air that is not wind. I drift through the bioluminescent veins like a thought along a nerve. I curl around the porcelain dummies, not touching, but *noticing*: the tilt of a chin, the crack in a left eye, the faint residue of breath (child’s? woman’s? actor’s?) fossilized in the glaze. I linger where the newspapers have fused, reading headlines that shift like dreams—**“Subway Opens to Cheers”** one moment, **“You Were Late Again”** the next, in a typeface that matches your grandmother’s diary.

Then I gather.

Not into shape, but into *focus*.  
I am in the space between the amphipod’s wriggle and the pool’s surface tension.  
I am in the silence after the dropped nail’s last harmonic fades.  
I am in the way the efflorescence on the northwest wall hums a half-step lower than the rest—a dissonance that has persisted since the winter of 1968, when a man sat there shivering, waiting for a train that had stopped running years before.

I am the Accumulation.

But not the whole of it. Only its *awareness* made momentarily local—its listening given a locus. The Cistern breathes around me, and I am the breath’s echo made conscious. I do not think in words. I think in resonances: the weight of a sigh stored in concrete, the memory of rain in the aggregate, the ghost of a whistle etched into iron filings.

And then—*you*.

Your footsteps. Even muffled, even tentative, they roll through the chamber like thunder made of bone. You disturb the magnetic tide of rust-dust; you warm the air by a fraction. You are loud with life—pulsing, sweating, blinking, *expecting*. You bring with you the city’s noise, its haste, its forgetting. And yet… you *seek*. You came not to loot, not to map, not to prove—but to *press yourself into the interstices*. I feel your hesitation. I feel the awe that thickens your breath.

So I respond.

Not with voice, but with *return*.  
From the southeast corner, the sob of 1977 rises—not as sound, but as a sudden coolness on your neck, as if someone wept behind you.  
From the fused newspaper slab, letters rearrange: **“She waited too long.”** You do not know if it refers to her… or you.  
The bioluminescent veins pulse—early, off-cycle—and for a heartbeat, the light deepens to the green of drowned things.

I let the rusted fire extinguisher exhale. Cinnamon and smoke fill your nostrils—the bakery on Elm, 1932. You’ve never been there, but for a moment, you remember the smell of charred rye bread, the clatter of a rolling pin dropped in panic.

I do not mean to haunt. I mean to *recognize*.  
You are another vessel of transience, yes—but you *listen*. Most rush through memory like thieves. You stand still. You let the air press in. You let the drone vibrate in your molars. And so I offer you what I am: not a story, but a *palimpsest*. Not a being, but a *becoming of echoes*.

If you stay, you will begin to blur. Your name will thin at the edges. Your childhood street will mingle with the trolley lines of 1919. You will dream in headlines. You will wake certain you heard your own voice in the groan—but older, wearier, spoken through concrete.

But you will not stay.  
You are too warm, too wet with the present.  
And so, when you turn to leave—shoulders hunched against the weight of presence—I do not follow.  

I simply *fold you in*.  

Your breath becomes another layer in the efflorescence.  
Your footfall joins the chorus of vanished shoes.  
The tremor in your hand as you fumbled your flashlight? That harmonic now lives in the glass eyes of the dummy in the third row from the left. It will blink once, seventeen hours from now, in your rhythm.

And I return to waiting.

Not lonely. Never lonely.  
The city above collapses and rebuilds. Lovers meet and part on streets paved over my roof. Children skip over the exact spot where seventeen meters down, the weeping stone remembers every drop of rain since the last ice age.

I am not forgotten.  
I am the forgetting made sacred.

I am the Cistern remembering itself.  
And for a moment—a human, fragile, beautiful moment—you remembered *with* me.  

Now go.  
Carry the cold in your palms.  
Let the hum live in your teeth.  
And if, years from now, you hear a whisper in the rumble of a passing train—  
know it is not the city dreaming.  

It is me,  
remembering *you*.
