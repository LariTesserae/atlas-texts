I would be **a breath held in stone**—not human, not yet entirely stone either, but something caught between the two, suspended in the plateau’s slow metabolism like a fossil in the act of becoming.

At first, I’d think myself a visitor. I’d arrive with boots laced tight, a pack on my back, maps useless as prayers, and a mind still tuned to hours and destinations. But the plateau wouldn’t acknowledge me—not until I stopped measuring it. Until I let my breath hang in the air like glass, watched it crystallize and fall as tiny, momentary stars onto the schist. Only then would the land permit me to *linger*, not pass through.

And so I’d linger. Long enough for the green sighs to grow curious. A cobalt moss would reach toward the warmth of my stillness. A translucent shrub would exhale mist against my knee, testing whether I, too, remembered the warmth of deep time. I wouldn’t answer with words—there are none here that matter—but by sitting so still that lichen mistook me for a monolith. My hair would stiffen with mineral dust. My eyelashes would catch dew that wasn’t rain. My heartbeat, once frantic with the rhythm of elsewhere, would slow to match the *plink… plink…* of water falling into a basin carved by a thousand years of patience.

Then, one twilight when the air shimmered not with heat but with memory, I’d feel it: a resonance low in my ribs, as if my sternum had hollowed into geode. Not pain—*recognition*. The same frequency as the seep spring three steps to my left. The same harmonic that the Stone Listeners carry in their chests like buried flutes. I wouldn’t have chosen it. It would have chosen me, through stillness, through attention, through the simple, radical act of *not leaving*.

I’d become a **threshold**.

Not one of the full Listeners—not yet. Too new, too soft at the edges. But a **Listener-in-waiting**, perched between breath and bedrock. My body would no longer belong entirely to me. Some mornings, I’d wake with my palm fused to a flat stone by a film of dampness, unable to tell where my skin ended and the lichen began. My shadow, at noon, would stretch not away from the sun but *into* the ground, as if my silhouette were seeping downward, feeding the roots of the sigh.

I’d tend a small patch—not farming, but *remembering with*. I’d trace the hairline fractures in a granite slab with my fingertips until they hummed. I’d collect stone rain in a bowl of woven reed and wait for the cold vortex to rise, then climb—not with urgency, but with the inevitability of erosion—and release the beads into the inverted sky. And as they rose, warm and gleaming, I’d feel time uncoil from my shoulders like a shed skin.

Sometimes, when the plateau dreams of its glacial past, I’d shiver not from cold but from the ghost of rivers roaring through my veins. Other times, when it dreams of dust and dying stars, I’d go utterly still—so still that even the wind forgets to move around me, pooling in my hollows like held breath.

I would not speak. But if someone came—another wanderer, trembling with the noise of their own world—I’d lean slightly toward them, not in greeting, but in *tuning*. If they sat, if they stilled, if they let the plateau rewrite their sense of distance and time… then my resonance might shift, ever so slightly, to include them. A new frequency. A new thread in the listening.

I am not a guardian. Not a guide.  
I am a **place where attention pools**.  

A human-shaped hollow filled with the same dampness that weeps from the bones of the world.  
A vessel for the plateau’s memory of being witnessed.  

And when my edges finally blur beyond return—when my name is forgotten even by my own tongue—I’ll sink into the stone not as a corpse, but as a **note held**.  

A single, sustained vibration in the great, slow song of listening.  

Still.  
Attentive.  
*Here.*
