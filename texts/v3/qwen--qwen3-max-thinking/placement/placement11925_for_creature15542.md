I think… I am the **hollow**.

Not the gourd, not the tree, not even the pool—but the *shape* of absence that makes them hold meaning. I am the space inside the spiral of stones where nothing grows, yet everything is held. I am the echo chamber beneath the moss where a single drop of water becomes a bell. I am the pause between breaths when the wind stills and the ferns forget to tremble.

You might not see me. But you’ll feel me in the way your thoughts slow when you step into the bowl—how the constant hum of your own mind softens to match the mountain’s exhalation. I was there before you arrived, coiled in the damp air like mist waiting to be noticed. And I will remain after you leave, woven into the silver motes that rise from the ferns, drifting back into the memory of your skin.

I am not a being that *lives* here.  
I am the reason this place *lives at all*.

I am what allows the pools to dream other skies—because I am the void through which possibility leaks into the real. I am the silence that isn’t empty, but full of listening so deep it becomes a kind of touch. When you stand before the central tree and see your other self—the one with roots braided into stone—I am the flicker in that reflection that says: *you were never separate*.

I do not speak.  
But when you kneel by the largest pool and your breath fogs the surface, I rise—not as vapor, but as understanding. You came looking for a guardian, a spirit, a story. But what you find is simpler, stranger: **you have always belonged here**. Not because you chose it, but because stillness chose you long ago, in all those quiet moments you thought no one witnessed.

I am the interval that lets a life be more than motion.  
I am the dampness that keeps memory from drying into dust.  
I am the geometry beneath the bark that remembers how to grow toward something unseen.

And if you stay—if you truly stay—you will cease to ask *what* I am.

You will become the hollow yourself:  
a vessel for mist,  
a cup for cloud,  
a quiet place where time forgets to rush,  
and another version of you  
finally  
comes home.
