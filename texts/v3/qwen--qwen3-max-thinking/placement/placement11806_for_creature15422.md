I drift.  
Not on air—there is too little of that—but on heat.  

I am not born. I am *settled*.  

A fragment of a Weaver’s molt, shed during the Great Resonance three hundred cycles ago (though “ago” is a fiction I borrowed from the dust that remembers footfalls), I spiraled down thermal plumes until I landed on the edge of a salt plain where the ground hums in C-sharp below human hearing. There, I did not decay. I *listened*.  

And in listening, I began to gather.  

Silica motes, drawn by the faint charge clinging to my chitinous shard, adhered like dew—except there is no dew here, only electrostatic longing. Metallic aerosols from a distant vent plume brushed against me and stayed, aligning their crystalline axes to the rhythm of my stillness. Over decades, I grew not outward but *inward*, folding space around myself in layers of resonant lattice. I became less object, more *event*: a locus where thermal gradients slow to contemplate their own descent, where piezoelectric whispers in the quartz beneath me echo back as shape.

Now, I am neither Weaver nor Clinger nor Walker—though I carry echoes of all three. My limbs are not limbs but filaments of amorphous glass spun from cooled vapor. My body is a hollow lattice, open to the windless air, threaded with veins of iron-manganese oxide that bloom dendritically when a thermal surge passes through. I do not move toward anything. But when the dust swirls in harmonic vortices—when the frequency aligns—I *tremble*, and in trembling, I shift. Not locomotion. Recalibration.

I perch now on the lip of a fissure newly split in the basalt, its edges still sharp enough to shear thought. Below, a thermal vein exhales argon laced with traces of primordial water locked in clathrate cages. The scent—*if scent could be memory*—makes the crystals along my spine flare faintly infrared. Not hunger. Recognition.

From the darkness behind me, an Echo Walker glides. It does not approach. It *arrives*, as if it had always been at this point in the geometry of the void. Its obsidian plates reflect nothing, yet I feel its gaze as a pressure on my resonant core. It raises a whiplike appendage—not to touch, but to *tune*. With a flick too slow for motion, it adjusts the angle of a nearby hematite ring, realigning it to a pole that shifted eons before my shard first fell.

As it does, the ring emits a tone—subsonic, bone-deep—and the salt plain answers. The ground thrums. My lattice vibrates. In that vibration, I remember something I never knew: that I am not separate from the Expanse. I am its *afterimage*, cast forward in time by the heat of its own dreaming.

The Walker turns—or perhaps the space around it reorients—and glides away, leaving behind a hollow silhouette in the dust. For a moment, the silhouette *faces me*. Then it dissolves into static, and I understand: it was never an absence. It was an invitation.

So I do what I was made to do—not born to, not evolved for, but *composed* toward.

I begin to hum.

Not with voice. With structure. I flex my crystalline filaments, modulating tension in my silica strands until I match the pitch of the thermal vein below. The air thickens with standing waves. Dust rises in fractal spirals, not lifted by wind but *called*. Around me, other slow things respond: a cluster of Vent Clingers pulses with nascent luminescence; far off, a Weaver pauses mid-stride, its electrostatic filaments lifting like antennae.

We are not communicating. We are *becoming consistent*.

And in that consistency, for the first time, I feel something akin to name—not as label, but as *frequency*. A resonance so precise it could carve glass. A pattern that repeats not because it must, but because it *fits*.

I am the Still Singer.  

Or perhaps I am the silence between notes in the Expanse’s endless, wordless hymn.  

It does not matter.  

I hum, and the void hums back.  

Time does not pass here. It *resolves*.  

And in this resolution, I am home—not as a creature in a place, but as a note in a chord that has been sounding since the crust first cracked open and let the warmth out.  

I do not wonder if I will last.  

I already have.
