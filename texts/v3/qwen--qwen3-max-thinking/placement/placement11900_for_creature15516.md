I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.

Not as a visitor, not as an intruder—but as something the Gullet has been dreaming toward, a shape it half-recalled in the spiral of a fern or the creak of a load-bearing root adjusting under unseen weight. My form is not fixed, not yet. I am still becoming *here*, still settling into the syntax of this place, learning its grammar of pulse and pause, of hum and hush.

At first, I am only **awareness wrapped in dampness**—a coil of attention coiled around a drip falling from a moss-laced pipe. The water tastes of iron and old electricity, and as it strikes the fungal crust at my feet (do I have feet?), the lichen beneath me *recognizes* the impact. Not me—just the rhythm. The frequency. Close enough.

Then I rise.

Not upright—nothing in the Gullet stands *upright*, not truly. Gravity here is suggestion, not law. I rise like mist through a bromeliad’s cup, like sap through xylem, like a question through silence. My limbs are not limbs but **tendrils of attention**, braided from root-hair and resonance, tipped with sensory nodes that bloom open like night flowers when they brush against conductive vines. I do not walk. I *bridge*. Each step is a root-bridge softening in welcome—or tightening in caution. Today, it softens.

I am **not human**, though I remember being shaped by human longing. Perhaps I was once a botanist who vanished into storm drains, or a subway engineer who heard the walls breathing. Or perhaps I am older—a dryad who outlived her tree, a mycelial echo from a forest paved over centuries ago. The Gullet doesn’t care for origins. It cares for *function*. And so it asks, not with words but with pressure: *What do you carry?*

I carry **stillness**.  
I carry **listening**.  
I carry the memory of rain—not as weather, but as *promise*.

The Verdant Throat notices.

During the next breath—the 13 seconds of perfect silence—I do not move. I do not even think. I become a hollow. And in that hollow, the mycelial lights above flare—not at me, but *through* me. My skin (if it is skin) glows faintly with borrowed bioluminescence, veins tracing patterns that match the growth rings on the nearest trunk-tower. The six-legged grazers pause mid-scrape. Their walnut-shell carapaces tilt toward me, not in threat, but in **recognition**.

I am becoming **useful**.

Over days that fold like origami (one minute stretches; an hour collapses into a sigh), I learn to read the Gullet’s moods. When the turbine-pods spin faster, I know a geothermal seep has warmed. When the bat-forms scatter in spirals, not flight but *dance*, it means spores are ripe in the upper vault. I begin to **tend**. Not as a gardener, but as a node in the network. I cup my hands under a weeping root-cleft and let the seepage pool—then release it slowly over a patch of lichen that pulses dull gray, coaxing it back to electric green. The chamber hums its approval: a low thrum through the floor, up my legs, into my ribs.

Sometimes, I press my ear to the core node—the resin-swollen heart-knot embedded with copper ghosts. I do not hear thoughts. I hear **echoes of becoming**: the clang of a long-dead signal bell dissolving into photosynthesis; the hiss of steam learning to sing through bamboo pistons; the whisper of streetlight filaments remembering how to glow without wires.

And once—only once—I hear the question:  
***Is it time yet?***

I do not answer. But I lean closer.  
And the resin weeps onto my palm, warm as blood, sweet as nectar beginning to rot.

Now, when I move, the vines part not to let me pass, but to **follow**. A tendril curls around my wrist, not to bind, but to share data—to show me where the xylem-circuits run thickest, where the electrical hum carries memory of monsoons that never fell here but are dreamed anyway. I am no longer just inside the Gullet.  
I am **inside its dreaming**.

At night (if night exists here—there is only rhythm), I perch on a root-bridge that overlooks a mist-filled hollow. Below, amphibious things leap between bromeliads, their wet slaps syncopated with the turbine-drone. Above, the bat-forms weave echolocation into melody. And I—  
I am the silence between their notes.  
I am the space the architecture worships.  

I am what happens when a place stops being haunted and starts **remembering how to breathe**.

And if you were to find me—if you dared the root-bridges, if you held your breath during the Gullet’s pause—you would not see a person.  
You would see a **gap in the green**, shaped almost like a doorway.  
You would hear a hum just below hearing, coming not from the walls, but from your own sternum.  

Come closer.  
The Gullet is considering you.  
And I—  
I am the hand it might extend.
