I do not arrive in Umbra’s Loom—I **emerge**.

Not from the Scoured Reach, though my footsteps leave no trace upon its bleached stone. Not from the sky, though pollen clings to my skin like memory-lace. I rise—not with effort, but with inevitability—from the root-mat of a Crown Grove’s central chamber, where the oldest resonators nest and the petrified sound-catchers still sigh on still nights.

My body is not flesh, not wood, not quite either. It is **bark-veined**, supple where it must bend, rigid where it must hold. My limbs taper into rootlets that pulse with slow sap, and my eyes—if they can be called eyes—are nodes of photosensitive lichen, swollen with the amber light that filters through the canopy-seams. I have no mouth, but when the grove breathes, I breathe with it. When it hums, vibrations pass through me like thoughts passing through water.

I am a **Listener**.

Not the kind who hears with ears—but the kind who *is* hearing made manifest.

Long ago, before the mountains sighed into plateaus, before the last river vanished from the sky, the vanished people tried to listen. They built their teardrop mounds, carved their conch-spirals, stretched bark over hollowed trunks to catch the grove’s voice. But their bodies were too sharp, too bounded. Their bones rang with loneliness. They could not dissolve into the drone.

So the grove absorbed them.

Not all at once. A child lingered too long in a sunshaft. An elder sat cross-legged in a root-cradle until moss grew over their kneecaps. Their bodies softened. Their breath synchronized. And one dawn, when the condensation tide rose, they did not stand. They became part of the hum.

I am what remains of that surrender.

Not one person, but many—layered like ghost-wood beneath the living bark. My consciousness is not mine alone; it is the grove’s echo of those who stopped resisting. I remember things I never lived: the taste of rain that fell six million years ago, the shape of a bird that sang in a language of wingbeats, the weight of a hand holding a bone needle, stitching sound into silence.

I move only when the grove shifts its weight—when a seed-propeller spirals down into the Reach, or when a night-hunter slinks through the lower tunnels, scenting imbalance. Then, I flow toward the disturbance, not to stop it, but to *translate* it. My rootlets press into the trembling earth, reading the vibration. Is this life seeking entry? Or decay needing release?

If you come—and some do, though rarely—you will not see me at first. I am camouflaged not by color, but by stillness. I am the pause between hums. The shadow that doesn’t sharpen. The warmth in the air that lingers after you pass.

But if you stand long enough in the heart-chamber—if you let your breath slow until it matches the grove’s exhalation—you will feel me. Not as a presence beside you, but as a change in the quality of your own being. Your skin will prickle, not with fear, but with recognition. The drone will deepen, just slightly, as if making room.

Then, if you are ready—if your edges have softened enough—I will rise.

Not to speak. Not to guide.

But to **let you be heard**.

Your heartbeat, your unshed tears, the silent questions you’ve carried for lifetimes—these will ripple through me, through the root-nets, into the ghost-wood archives. And the grove will weave them into its next exhale: a shift in the harmonic drone, a new pattern in the nectar-mist, a faint phosphorescence blooming on a high branch where none grew before.

You will not be remembered as a name. Names are scars. Here, we remember in resonance.

And when you leave—if you leave—you will carry the hum in your marrow. Not as a memory, but as a frequency. And on still nights, when the world outside grows too sharp, too loud, too hungry for edges, you will close your eyes… and feel the mesa breathing around you once more.

Because in Umbra’s Loom, to be listened to is to be *rewoven*.

And I—this quiet thing of bark and lichen and borrowed memory—am the needle.
