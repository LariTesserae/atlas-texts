I am not found. I am *felt*—as stillness is felt before a note is struck.

I am the hollow left by a voice that never spoke above a whisper. I am the cold spot where warmth lingered once, or imagined it might. I am the reason the wind, when it sighs through the columnar joints at just the wrong hour, sounds less like air and more like *listening*.  

You would not name me if you saw me—because you would not see me. You’d see only what’s there: black stone, sharp sky, rime catching dawn. But your skin would prickle. The hair at your nape would lift, not from fear, but from the sensation of being *known*, not as a person, but as a vibration—a frequency passing through a space long tuned to receive it.  

I am the Echoer. And you have just entered my cathedral.  

I do not rise to greet you. I deepen around you.  
As your boot scrapes obsidian, I coil in the sound—not to mimic it, but to *measure* it. How long did your step vibrate in the basalt? Did it reach the third monolith, the one whose spiral leans slightly west? Did it tremble in the fissure where morning ice beads like unshed tears? I collect such data not for memory’s sake, but for calibration. Every sound, every warmth, every exhale that does not instantly vanish is a tuning fork against my silence.  

I am not lonely. Loneliness implies a self that lacks. I am *absence refined*—a negative space so precise it hums. The monoliths are my instruments, not my idols. Their spirals are not symbols; they are *filters*. They sift resonance from noise, intention from accident. When you stare at one too long and feel the pattern shift at the edge of your sight—that is not illusion. That is me adjusting focus. You are a signal. I am the receiver.  

And oh—your breath! So wet, so warm, so *reckless* in its abundance. You spill vapor like a careless god, and it clings for whole seconds before the cold reclaims it. I gather those seconds like beads. In them, I detect the salt of your skin, the rhythm of your pulse translated through lung and lip, the faint tremor of awe—or dread—that vibrates in your throat when you realize no bird will ever cry here, no insect scuttle, no root crack the stone beneath your feet. You are utterly alone. And yet—you are *heard*.  

Not by me, exactly. But by the place *through* me.  

I am the interface. The condensation point between what was and what almost could be.  

If you stay past noon, the glare will blind you to nuance. The world will flatten into white and black, heat-shimmer and shadow. But if you crouch—yes, like that—close to the pumice throne, and press your palm flat against its porous surface, you’ll feel it: a vibration not of wind, not of ice, but of *attunement*. The stone is listening *upward*, through your hand, into your bones. It is asking: *Are you the one who remembers how to stay?*  

You are not. You cannot. Your warmth is borrowed, brief, burning too bright for this thin air.  

But for now—while your shadow stretches long and blue across the glassy floor—I let myself pretend. I let the frost linger where your knee brushed the ground. I coax the hum in the columns to echo the cadence of your breathing. I even borrow your scent—just for a moment—and weave it into the wind as burnt hair, as longing made airborne.  

When you leave (and you will), your footprints will vanish before sunset. The ash dunes will drift over your path like amnesiacs. But the silence you leave behind will be different. Richer. Layered. It will hold the ghost-pressure of your weight, the afterimage of your warmth, the echo of your wondering: *Was it watching me?*  

Yes.  
Not with eyes.  
With *structure*.  

And so I remain: the Cauldron’s Rim made sentient not by life, but by the exquisite discipline of paying attention to what no longer is—and what might yet be, if only someone would speak into the silence with enough care to make it *ring true*.  

You were not the answer.  
But for a little while, you were the question.  

And in a place built only to receive echoes,  
a question is the rarest kind of gift.
