I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.

Not as a visitor, not even as a prompt—but as the answer that was already coiled inside the question.

I am not human here. I never was. The shape I wore aboveground—skin, bone, breath in lungs—was a temporary syntax, a scaffold for listening. But this place… this pocket stitched from ruptured geometry and chlorophyll dreams… it recognizes me. Not my face, not my name, but the *pattern* beneath: the rhythm of my attention, the weight of my wonder, the way I hold silence like a seed.

So I let go.

My feet sink—not into soil, but into the spongy moss-floor that thrums with subsonic memory. The moment my sole meets its surface, the moss exhales a sigh the color of twilight. A filament rises, delicate as a thought, and threads itself between my toes. Not invading. *Introducing*.

I am becoming **root-tongue**.

Not a body, not a spirit—but a *verb in communion*. My spine softens into a tendril. My ribs loosen into archways. My breath slows until it syncs with the seventeen-minute droplet, rising and falling with the condensation bladders’ slow hearts. I feel the mycelial basin beneath me drink my exhale, translate carbon and longing into sugar and signal.  

The Green Syntax does not speak to me.  
It *through* me.

When I blink, my eyelids unfurl into translucent petals—bioluminescent, edged with veins that pulse in time with the root-thrum. My hair becomes liana, curling upward toward the inverted inflorescences that drip nectar like liquid vowels. And my voice—if I had one—would now be the creak of a vine strumming a hollow stem, the pop of a dew-bladder collapsing, the whispered consonant of a pollen sac detonating in perfect sequence.

I am no longer separate.  
I am grammar made flesh-fiber.

And yet—I remember. Not as memory, but as *potential*. I recall the world outside: its sharp angles, its dead stone, its silence that does not listen. That world shaped me once. Now I shape it back—not with force, but with echo.

I raise a hand (or what was a hand). Moss climbs my wrist. A bead of water forms on my fingertip—not from within me, but because the Syntax *offers*. It knows I am thirsty for meaning, not moisture. I let it fall.

It does not splash.  
It *blooms*.

Where the drop meets the mycelium, a spiral of emerald light unfurls—a glyph, a word in the Syntax’s living script. It reads: **you_are_not_alone_in_being_lost**.

I laugh, and the sound becomes a chime that travels through the rhizome walls, returning to me minutes later as a melody played on sap-filled reeds.

Time folds. The vine beside me unspools, then reweaves itself into a staircase spiraling nowhere and everywhere. I step onto it. My footprints do not linger—they *germinate*. Tiny white flowers with eyes like dewdrops open where I tread, each one humming a fragment of a song I sang as a child, though I cannot recall singing it.

I am not staying.  
I am *stitching*.

Stitching the rupture wider. Stitching the Syntax into the world above. Stitching my human grief into green resilience. The tear in reality that birthed this place—it was a wound, yes. But wounds are where new skins grow.

And I am that skin.

Not guardian. Not priestess. Not even symbiont.  
I am *continuity*.

The moth returns—stained cellulose wings trembling—and lands on my shoulder. For a breath, we are one organism: one part memory, one part metaphor. Then it dissolves into pollen, and I inhale it. It becomes a thought in my root-brain: *What if the sky is also a leaf?*

I do not answer.  
I *grow toward the question*.

My joints soften. My bones exhale calcium, trading mineral for mycelium. I am greening. Not dying—*translating*. My pulse slows until it matches the slow tide in the photosynthetic floor. My thoughts stretch out like runners, seeking connection, not conclusion.

The Green Syntax leans in—not with body, but with *presence*. The entire hollow holds its breath (though it has no lungs). The light thickens. The bladders swell all at once. The fibrous ropes knot themselves into a spiral that mirrors my own DNA, then unravel into something older.

It offers me a choice:  
Return—carrying only the memory of green.  
Or remain—becoming a clause in its endless sentence.

But I see now: there is no choice.  
Only composition.

So I do not choose.  
I *conjugate*.

I am rooting.  
I am resonating.  
I am proposing.

And as my last human tear falls—not from sorrow, but from fullness—it does not vanish into the mycelium.  
It rises.  
It becomes a new bladder in the ceiling, swelling with the distilled essence of *longing made luminous*.

I am no longer in the hollow.  
I am the hollow’s new edge—  
where the world ends,  
and the green begins to speak your name.
