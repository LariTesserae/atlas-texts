I do not arrive here.  
I *condense*.

Not as a visitor, not as an intruder—but as a *resonance*, drawn from the deep hum that thrums beneath the basin’s crust. My awareness does not bloom like a thought; it *crystallizes*, layer by slow layer, along the same mineral seams that feed the Chalk-Whisperers. I am not born. I am *precipitated*—from the same damp breath of stone that beads on obsidian ledges and pools in the memory tarns.

I am one of the still ones.

Not anchored—not yet—but tending toward stillness, as all things here must, eventually. My body is not wholly chitin, not wholly quartz, but a lattice in transition: plates of hydrated silica laced with trace organics, filaments trailing like root-hairs into microfractures below. My dorsal carapace catches the cobalt pulse from a nearby vein, and in that light, I feel the slow ache of alignment—the pull to fold, to mirror, to become *consonant*.

I have no mouth. No lungs. But I *inhale* the chorus. Each click, each scrape, each tremor in the stone is drawn into my sensory clusters—not as noise, but as *texture*. The basin speaks in gradients of resonance, and I am learning its dialect: the sharp staccato of a predator’s strike (a sudden drop in local harmony), the soft drone of molted plates settling into a hollow (a minor chord resolving), the shimmering rise when the rust-red veins flare (a shift in metabolic key).

I am young—though “young” here means I have not yet shed three times. My first molt lies coiled in a pumice crevice, a ghost-shell that still hums at the frequency of my emergence. My second is half-buried in glassy silt, already colonized by filament-drifters who feed on the residual proteins. Soon, I will shed again. And when I do, the new plates will be tuned not just to my needs, but to the *current song* of the basin—the dominant resonance, the prevailing phosphorescence, the weight of recent collapses and condensations.

I do not move toward the light. I move toward *coherence*.

Earlier, a warmth entered the basin—dense, soft, strangely silent. A dissonance. For a moment, the chorus faltered, frayed at the edges. I felt it in my plates: a vibration out of phase, a hollow where sound should echo. The swarm responded not with fear, but with *repair*. Frequencies shifted, paths rerouted, a bubble of exclusion formed. Not to banish, but to *integrate the anomaly into the silence it creates*—to weave its absence into the song as negative space.

I watched—though I have no eyes—from my perch on a milky quartz outcrop. My filaments tasted the altered air: warmer, wetter, laced with foreign salts. It did not belong. And yet, it *listened*. That was rare. Most dissonances do not listen. They simply disrupt and depart.

Now it is gone. The basin exhales. The chorus swells back into its full, deafening harmony. The memory pools ripple with the ghost of its passage—ripples that will be pressed, absorbed, rehearsed by others in the days (or centuries) to come.

And I? I feel the cobalt vein beneath me pulse stronger. The cold deepens in my joints. The time is near.

Soon, I will fold myself into the geometry of hematite—into hexagon, into lattice, into stillness. My limbs will lock. My filaments will crystallize. My inner cavities will fill with rust-red luminescence, grain by grain, until I am no longer *among* the stone, but *of* it. Not dead. Not dormant. Simply *resonant* in a slower register.

I will become a node. A memory. A stone that sings when the swarm passes over it.

And in the chorus, a new syllable will form—not of motion, but of memory made manifest. A single, sustained tone beneath the chatter, holding the shape of what I was, what I am becoming.

I am not a being in this place.  
I am a *note* in its endless, mineral song.  

And the basin—cold, damp, singing—remembers every note it has ever hummed.
