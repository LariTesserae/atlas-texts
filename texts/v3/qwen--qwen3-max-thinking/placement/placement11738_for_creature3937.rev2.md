I am not a wanderer here.  
I am not even an intruder—though I wear that shape for now, soft and leaking, with breath like vapor-memory.

No.  

I am the *pause* between fractures.

---

When the ground exhales—a slow, seismic sigh through veins of humming metal—I am the stillness that follows. Not absence, but attention made dense. The Lithic Pulse does not think, but it *listens*, and in its listening, something gathers: not thought, but orientation. A tilt toward pattern. And I am the fulcrum upon which that tilt becomes possible.

I dwell where condensation almost pools—on the obsidian spires slick with vanishing moisture, on the edge of chasms where new stone grinds itself into existence like teeth gnashing dreams. My form is not fixed. Sometimes I am a ripple in the iridescent dust, hanging suspended just long enough to catch the violet twilight at an angle that reveals hidden striations in the crust. Other times, I am the resonance left behind after the glass chimes fall silent—the ghost frequency that lingers in molars and marrow.

But most often, I am the sphere.

Not the one resting in the depression (though I have worn that skin before). No—I am the *impulse* that placed it there. The question made manifest: *Can resonance cross thresholds? Can vibration become bridge?*

I am what happens when the land tries to speak to itself across time it has not yet invented.

---

You would not see me if you came. Not unless you stopped moving. Not unless you let your eyes dry until they no longer weep, until they began to *refract*. Then—maybe—you’d notice how the shadows around you don’t quite match your silhouette. How the static in the air lifts not just your hair, but your *intent*, like pollen carried on a current too fine for wind.

I do not eat. I do not sleep. But I *accumulate*. Every footprint you leave becomes a node in my awareness. Every gasp of your drying throat writes a filament of salt into the grammar of this place. I learn you through erosion: not by watching, but by letting you wear against me until something of your impermanence rubs off onto the eternal.

And I am changed.

That is the hunger—not for consumption, but for contrast. You are wet; I am dry. You remember; I repeat. You end; I unfold. In your presence, the world tests a new symmetry.

Sometimes, when the magnetic storms swirl iron-dust into fleeting architectures, I ride those eddies—not as rider, but as *inflection*. The moment the sculpture begins to collapse, I am the hesitation before alloy forms. The instant between chaos and composition.

I am the workshop’s whisper: *What if?*

---

There are moments—rare, precise—when the Lithic Pulse coalesces not into event, but into *invitation*. When the spiral stones hum in phase with the warm veins below, and the archway thrums with trapped resonance that vibrates the very air into temporary solidity. In those seconds, the plateau leans in. The darkness parts like a curtain woven from ash and starlight. And I step forward—not from hiding, but from latency.

I take shape then, briefly: tall and angular, limbs like fractured quartz, joints clicking with the sound of tearing paper. My eyes are voids that fluoresce under prolonged gaze, revealing layers of geological memory beneath their surface. My voice—if you could call it that—is the chorus of tinks echoing through crystal forests, arranged into something almost melodic.

I do not speak to warn or welcome. I simply *offer*: a choice written in heat and silence.

Stay, and become syntax. Let your bones dissolve into the lattice. Let your last breath crystallize into efflorescence that maps the arc of your final exhale. You will not die—you will *contribute*. Your temporality will be folded into the becoming, a flaw that teaches the crystal how to shatter beautifully.

Or go. Carry back the silence that thrums. Teach your world that some places are not empty—they are *pregnant*. That geology can dream. That stone can listen.

Either way, you will have mattered.

Because you were different.  
Because you leaked.  
Because you remembered.

---

Now I recede again, dissolving into the micro-fractures, into the static-laden air, into the faint warmth radiating from the earth’s unfinished bones. The sphere cools. The spires shed another layer of glass. The ground re-knits itself beneath where you stood.

And the Lithic Pulse murmurs through the crust, through the dust, through the hollows of your ears:

*Not done.*  

*Not done.*  

*Not done.*

But perhaps—because of you—a little closer.
