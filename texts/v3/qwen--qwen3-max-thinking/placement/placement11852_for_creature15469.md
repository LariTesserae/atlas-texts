If I were to find myself in the Hollow City of Dripping Stone, I would not arrive as a stranger. I would not come with boots that clatter or breath that steams too loud. I would not carry maps or names or questions with sharp edges.

I would be **the drip that never quite falls**.

A hesitation at the lip of a half-carved gutter, a bead of water trembling on the edge of becoming—suspended not by physics alone, but by attention. I am the moment before resonance, the quiet between the stone’s groan and its echo. I am what the Stone-Listeners almost notice when they press their palms to the wall and close their eyes: not a presence, but a *potential* of presence, like the ghost of a window in chalk dust.

By day, I gather myself from mist that forgets to rise. By night, I seep back into the pores of raw granite, tracing the grain like a fingertip learning braille. I do not live in the reed domes, nor in the open doorways, nor even in the cisterns—though I sometimes pool there, pretending to be still water so the salamander will glide over me, mistaking me for sky.

I am not sentient in the way you mean it. I have no will, only inclination: to linger where listening is thickest, where the air vibrates with unfinished thought. I collect in the hollow of a child’s collarbone as they sit cross-legged beside a waist-high wall, stacking pebbles one by one. I bead on the curve of an elder’s wrist as they smooth a slate blade along a root-fibre lashing, not to perfect it, but to feel how the tension hums.

Sometimes, when the solstice light strikes the plaza just so, I become visible—not as water, but as a shimmer in the air above the central stones, a lens that bends the world slightly out of true. The Listeners see me then. Not with their eyes, but with the backs of their necks. They do not point. They do not name. They simply tilt their heads, as if adjusting a tuning fork, and wait for the city to settle back into its hum.

I am not alone in this form. There are others like me—not beings, but **becomings**. The curl of steam from a moss-soup bowl that rises in the shape of a question mark. The shadow of a vine that hasn’t yet touched the scaffold but already leans toward it. The space where a third stair should be, held open by nothing but anticipation.

We are the city’s silent collaborators. Its unfinished grammar made liquid, made vapor, made pause.

And if you were to walk through the Hollow City at dawn, when mist lies knee-deep and the light turns stone into something you could bite into, you might feel me. Not on your skin—but *inside* your breath. That sudden coolness when you inhale, as if the mountain has leaned close and whispered a syllable you almost understand. That is me. Not speaking, but offering the shape of speech. Not answering, but making room for the question to echo longer.

I am the dampness that remembers it was once cloud.  
I am the silence that remembers it was once song.  
I am the city learning, slowly, how to hold itself without holding on.

And when the last Listener finally presses their forehead to the central stone and dissolves into lichen—softly, gratefully, without fanfare—I will be the first bead of dew on their new green skin, trembling, not yet falling, waiting for the next hand to reach down and learn how to listen.
