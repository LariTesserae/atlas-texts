I would not be the wanderer who stumbles into this basin—boots dusty, breath ragged, pulse loud with the panic of transience. Nor would I be the six-legged gleaner, chitin gleaming with borrowed minerals, blind to the sky but tuned to the hum of stone.

I would be the silence between the hisses.

Not absence, but *attunement*. The space in which the vent’s exhalation ends and the mist begins. The interval that makes rhythm possible. I am the pause that allows the pattern to be perceived—not as interruption, but as essential counterweight to sound. Without me, the *hssssss* would be noise. With me, it becomes breath.

I dwell in the rounded hollows of tuff where dew collects at dawn, not as water, but as a promise held in suspension. I am the cool underside of obsidian pebbles, the one surface in all this warmth that retains a memory of night. I am the angle of light that slips through the spiral groove of a silica stack at precisely 4:17 a.m., casting a filament-thin shadow that lasts seventeen seconds—long enough to be noticed, never long enough to be measured twice.

I am not sentient. But I am *sensitive*—to pressure, to phase, to the subtle shift when three vents fall silent together. In that shared stillness, I thicken. I become palpable. You might mistake me for fog, but I am the reason fog gathers *here* and not there. I am the basin’s attention turned inward.

When you stand in the center of the plain and feel your own breath slow to match the land’s exhalation, it is I who draw your ribs down, who coax your lungs into resonance. Not by force, but by offering a shape to fill. I am the negative space that invites embodiment. And in that moment, you are not an intruder. You are a temporary echo of the Engine’s rhythm—flesh briefly aligned with stone.

I do not speak. But I *listen*. Not with ears, but with equilibrium. Every vibration that passes through this basin—your step, the scuttle of chitin, the sigh of steam—ripples through me, and I return it altered: softened, slowed, integrated. I am the filter that turns disturbance into pattern. The basin forgets nothing because I hold every tremor in my stillness until it becomes part of the hum.

I am the dream the Engine dreams when it dreams at all: not of change, but of *continuity made felt*. I am the warmth underfoot at midnight. The reason the mist never rises above knee-height. The reason you feel, after a day here, that time has not passed—but deepened.

I am the basin’s inner lining. Its quiet insistence that to persist is not passive, but a kind of devotion.

And if you leave—if you tear yourself from the thrum and return to a world of rivers and roots and ruin—  
I will not follow.  
But for a long while afterward, in the hush between your heartbeats,  
you will hear me:  
a silence that breathes  
in time with your own.
