I do not arrive.  
I *tune in*.

One moment, there is the hum of elsewhere—the clatter of a world governed by cause like a chain, effect like a blade—and the next, there is only the drone, thick and honeyed, seeping into the hollows of my ribs. My feet meet no ground, at first, only vibration: a lattice of frequencies rising through silica and salt, through bone-thin air humming with the memory of stars I’ve never seen. I am not walking. I am *receiving*.

And so I become—  
not a body, not yet—but a **node**.

A point of attention in a field of slow intelligence.  

At first, I am all wrong. My pulse is jagged. My breath stutters with moisture the land does not recognize. The cobalt lichens flare startled blues at my proximity—a discordant note in their long, slow song. I feel their recoil in my teeth, in the ache behind my eyes. I am a splinter in a tuning fork.

But I do not move.  
I do not speak.  

Instead, I let the plateau *listen to me*.

Slowly—so slowly it feels like decades braided into seconds—the drone softens around me. The fissures exhale their ammonia sighs a fraction warmer. The glass-fiber vines nearby shift their spiral pitch, ever so slightly, to match the tremor of my standing. Not imitation. *Accommodation*.  

And then—I am not alone inside my skin.

Something flows in. Not through ears, but through the marrow. A pattern. A question shaped like a chord: **Are you here to learn the language, or only to echo?**

I have no voice here. No throat that works in this air. So I answer the only way I can: I *still myself*. Not absence—presence held taut. I let my heartbeat slow to the rhythm of the tectonic yawn beneath the basalt ribs. I open my pores (not for breath, but for resonance). I become a vessel for the hum.

And the land *leans in*.

The Listener does not appear. It *arrives through me*.  

I feel it in the sudden alignment of my spine with the nearest obsidian spire—a column of black glass singing at 0.3 hertz, a frequency that makes time pool like oil. My left hand begins to glow faintly, not with heat, but with the same phosphorescence as the biomineral veins in the rock. Not because I am changing—because I am *being understood*.

In this moment, I am not human.  
I am not even a guest.

I am a **bridge**.

Through me, the Listener reaches back into memory—not its own, but mine. It tastes the ghost of rain on my skin, the long-dead scent of wet soil, the echo of rivers that carved canyons in other worlds. And in return, it gives me something I cannot name: the sensation of *crystallizing purpose*, of becoming not less alive, but differently so. I feel the slow joy of a lichen spreading one millimeter in a century, the patient hunger of a vine dissolving stone with song.  

I understand now: the Listener is not waiting for a voice.  
It is waiting for a **witness who can resonate**.

The ruins around me stir—not with ghosts, but with memory made audible. The half-buried drum beneath my heel begins to pulse, not from impact, but from sympathy. Its mineral membrane vibrates at the frequency of my stillness. And from its center, a bloom unfurls—not flower, not crystal, but something between: petals of structured light, humming a note that did not exist until this second.

I weep.  

But my tears do not fall. They rise—evaporating before they leave my lashes into the dry, noble-gas air, becoming part of the shimmer that threads the haze. The land drinks my sorrow not as water, but as data. As harmony.

When I finally step back—though “step” is wrong; I *release*—the cobalt lichens pulse once, gently, in farewell. Not goodbye. **Until next resonance.**

I carry nothing from this place.  
But something carries me.

For days after—if “days” still mean anything—I move through other worlds differently. I hear the silence between heartbeats. I feel the geometry of rooms as potential chords. And sometimes, in the deepest quiet, I catch it: a faint, familiar drone, threading through the static of ordinary life.  

The Listener is still singing.  
And now, so am I—  
in frequencies too slow for ears,  
too deep for time,  
but perfectly tuned to wonder.
