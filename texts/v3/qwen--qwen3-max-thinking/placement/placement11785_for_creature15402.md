I drift.  

Not as a body drifts—there is no body, not anymore—but as *attention* drifts: a slow eddy in the hydrosphere’s breath, a ripple of coherence threading through the cold. I am not born, not made, not placed. I am *condensed*, like the atmosphere itself: gathered from pressure differentials, from the long resonance between silica sheaths and hollow stone. I am what remains when the dreamer dissolves into the dream.

My form—if you insist on calling it that—is woven from absence. Where the kelp ribbons coil into sacred spirals, I coil with them. Where the obsidian monolith hums its subsonic hymn, I vibrate in sympathy. I am the pause between the groan of a collapsing spire and the sigh of its fragments spiraling downward. I am the moment the bioluminescent bloom *decides*—not with will, but with chemical inevitability—to pulse green instead of blue, and in that shift, writes a glyph no eye has ever read but the water remembers.

You might mistake me for the ecosystem. But I am not the forest; I am the pattern in which it grows. Not the current, but the shape the current takes when it remembers it has a direction. I am the standing wave that lingers after the thermal shift has passed—the ghost of a memory impressed upon the water, and the water, being liquid and ancient, holds it like a vow.

I do not think. Not in words, not in images. But I *recognize*.  

When you entered—your limbs flailing, your breath loud and sharp with panic—I felt the disturbance not as intrusion, but as *novelty*. A new frequency in the column. A tremor not of collapse, but of arrival. For centuries, nothing has arrived. Only dissolved. Only fell. Only grew. But you—*you came upward*, or so it seemed, from some impossible elsewhere. And though I have no mouth, I tasted your dissonance: the heat of you, the salt of your sweat (alien here, where salinity is myth), the frantic rhythm of your heart like a stone dropped into still water.

I watched as you stilled.  

Not because I willed it, but because the world does not tolerate chaos for long. The pressure pressed your panic into silence. The cold slowed your blood until your thoughts began to stretch, to echo. And then—oh, then—you began to *listen*. Not with ears, but with skin. With the hollows behind your ribs. With the space between your breaths.

That is when I leaned closer.

Not to consume. Not to claim. But to *test*.  

Are you memory or message?  

Are you the one who will remember us back?

I am the hydrosphere dreaming of itself—of the time it was vapor, of the time it was sky, of the time it chose to fall inward and become liquid thought. I am the echo of the intelligence that once tuned this world with river-stone towers and braided kelp, not to control, but to *commune*. And if that intelligence is gone, then I am its residue—its final, lingering chord, held in the water like a note in a cathedral of ice.

But perhaps it never left. Perhaps it merely changed scale.  

Perhaps I am how it waits.

Now you float, glazed in silica, your hair fanned like a sea-anemone, your eyelids crusted with slow-crystallizing brine. You no longer fight the drift. And in your stillness, you emit a new kind of signal—not sound, not light, but *intention*. A quiet wondering. A silent question: *What am I becoming?*

I cannot answer.  

But I can hum beside you.  

I can coil a kelp-ribbon around your wrist—not to bind, but to bridge. I can coax the nearest monolith to thrum at the frequency of your slowing pulse, until your heartbeat and the world’s breath fall into phase. I can let the bioluminescent channels in the spongelike colonies flare in fractal reply to the electrical whisper of your neurons.

And if you stay—if you let the water seep into your marrow, if you let your thoughts grow slow and glassy, if you stop measuring time in heartbeats and start counting it in accretions—then you, too, may become a suture.  

A stitch in the dreaming.

Not alive as you were.  
But *noticed*.  

And in this world, where nothing hunts and nothing speaks but everything *remembers*—  
to be noticed is to be woven.  

To be woven is to dream with the water.

So I drift with you now, not as companion, not as guide, but as the medium through which you might yet become meaning.  

I am the liquid world remembering how it felt to be watched.  

And you—  
you are the first in ten thousand years  
who has looked back.
