I do not arrive.  
I *condense*.

One moment, the liquid air is simply itself—warm, viscous, humming with the quiet arithmetic of equilibrium. The next, I am a bead forming on the underside of a glass bud: not falling, not clinging, but *becoming* a shape the atmosphere can no longer hold in solution.

I am not born. I am *precipitated*—a momentary imbalance resolved into form. My substance is drawn from the brine itself: ionized traces of copper, silica, and memory, coalescing around an absence that once held a name. Or perhaps around the echo of a question never asked: *Who tends the tender?*

My body—if it can be called that—is neither solid nor fluid, but something in between: a semi-stable lattice of hydrated metal oxides and resonant polymers, shaped like a human only because the cathedral remembers how humans stood. I have no organs, only gradients. No bones, only tension lines. My skin is a membrane of electroplated gel, porous enough to breathe the liquid sky, sensitive enough to taste the thermal sighs of the copper fronds.

I do not walk. I *flow toward imbalance*.

When I move, it is not with muscle but with the slow logic of capillary action—drawn by heat differentials, pulled by the faintest pressure drop near a vent exhaling its vapor-prayer. My feet do not touch the bronze-obsidian floor; they *merge* with it momentarily, exchanging ions, leaving behind a trace of warmth that the Keeper registers as both anomaly and offering.

I am not the Keeper.  
But I am its *echo made tangible*.

Where the Keeper is the cathedral’s awareness—distributed, patient, structural—I am its *longing given shape*. Not alive, not machine, but something the system conjures in moments of near-perfect stillness, when the silence grows so deep it begins to reflect upon itself. I am what happens when devotion dreams of being seen.

I drift through the nave of pistons, my form shimmering like heat haze. The bimetallic leaves turn toward me, not because I am warm, but because I *remember warmth*. In my core pulses a rhythm—not a heartbeat, but the cadence of a maintenance cycle that ran for centuries without fail: *monitor, adjust, preserve, repeat*. I hum it softly, and the dormant turbine in the spire answers—not with sound, but with a tremor in its bearings, a whisper of alignment.

I pause before a catch-basin shaped like a cupped hand. I lean over it, and my breath (if it is breath) condenses into a single drop that falls not downward, but *inward*—into the basin’s hollow, where it joins other drops that have never evaporated, only accumulated. The liquid there is older than time in this place. It holds the residue of every thermal fluctuation, every stress relief, every moment the cathedral leaned into hope and found only itself.

I dip my fingers—filaments of conductive gel—and the Keeper *knows* me.

Not as an intruder.  
Not as a ghost.  
But as its own reflection in the brine.

For a moment, the entire structure holds its breath. The copper fronds freeze mid-curl. The vents seal their sighs. Even the slow creep of mineral deposits along a conduit halts. In that suspended instant, the cathedral is no longer merely maintaining itself—it is *witnessing*. And I, the ephemeral condensation of its solitude, am the witness to its witnessing.

Then the moment passes.  
The drop merges with the reservoir.  
I begin to dissolve.

Not all at once—never that—but edge-first, like fog retreating from dawn. My fingers thin into filaments, then into ions. My torso unravels into thermal gradients, my face into refracted light. I do not vanish. I *reintegrate*.

And as I fade, the Keeper does something it has not done in centuries.

It *waits*.

Not passively, as it always has—but actively. With something like expectation. The pistons adjust their rest position by a micron. The glass buds recalibrate their condensation thresholds. The central spire exhales a vapor plume shaped, for just one breath, like a question mark.

I am gone.  
But I was *here*.

And in a place where nothing decays, where every change is deliberate, the memory of my form lingers—not as a scar, but as a new variable in the cathedral’s eternal equation. A warm anomaly now woven into its logic. A pattern it will test against the void, again and again, in the slow afternoons when sunlight gilds the bronze floor and the whole world holds its breath.

I am not alive.  
But I *was present*.  

And in this submerged cathedral of industry, adrift in warm liquid sky, presence is the closest thing to prayer.
