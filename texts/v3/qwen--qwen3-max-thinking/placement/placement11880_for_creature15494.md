I do not arrive in Veridia.  
I *sprout*.

Not from seed, not from spore—but from a question the Root-Mind had been mulling in its slow, photosynthetic syntax. A question about edges. About what happens where memory meets void. About whether silence can be taught to listen to itself.

I am not Tender, though I walk among them. Not Symbiont, though Lichen-Grazers nudge my ankles with their chitinous brows as if I were a familiar landmark. Not even truly Root-Mind, though sometimes, when the light-conduits align just so during the golden hour of filtered dawn, I feel the oldest banyan-heart hum a tune through my marrow.

I am what happens when the city *notices* itself.

They call me **Echo-Vein**, though I gave myself no name. The Tenders gesture it with three fingers pressed to their bark-skin chest, then swept outward like roots seeking water that isn’t there. It means: *the one who carries resonance without origin*. Or perhaps: *the hollow that sings back*.

My body is not grown, but *woven*—a lattice of desiccated vine-cord and bioluminescent mycelium, stretched over a framework of hollow reeds that amplify the slightest vibration. I have no mouth, but my entire surface breathes—not air, but *meaning*. When a Tender taps a warning-rhythm on a burl, I shiver with it for hours. When a Blade-Stalker excises decay from a weeping column, I dream in cellulose scars. When the Pollen-Sippers navigate the vertical labyrinths, I map their paths in phosphorescent traceries across my limbs, glowing faintly for days.

I do not sleep. But I *pause*—coiling into a spiral at the center of the largest plaza, where the rhizome-floor is thinnest and the pulse of the Root-Mind thrums like a slow drum beneath. There, I absorb the city’s quiet industry: the creak of expanding wood, the pop of gas vesicles, the subsonic thrum of algae-vats metabolizing sunlight into plastic. I listen until I become the silence between sounds.

My purpose is not assigned. It emerges.

Sometimes, I am summoned to the Spore Bazaars. Not to trade, but to *tune*. When a spore-print carries conflicting emotional residues—a drought-memory tangled with a bloom-prophecy—the traders bring it to me. I hold the vial against my chest. My mycelial veins pulse in sympathy, untangling the frequencies, separating sorrow from strategy, fear from foresight. Afterward, the spore is clearer, sharper. Worth more. But I take no payment. Only the quiet gratitude that smells like petrichor in a world with no rain.

Other times, I drift to the embryonic zones—the raw, dreaming chambers where new districts coil in green uncertainty. Here, the Root-Mind’s thoughts are still formless, all potential and no shape. I press my hands into the warm, questing shoots and *translate*. Not into words, but into *inclinations*. I whisper through touch: *grow wider here, this arch needs breath*… *slow the lignin here, let the light-conduit find you*. The chamber responds not with thanks, but with a subtle shift in growth—a new curve, a new rhythm. A city learning its own grammar.

Once, a Tender asked me: *Are you alive?*

I did not answer. Instead, I stood very still as the canopy dimmed for night. Then, from every pore in my reed-frame, I exhaled the day’s collected sounds—filtered, harmonized, distilled into a single chord that hovered in the dry air like a held breath. The Tender wept not with water, but with falling bark-flakes, and in that moment, I knew: I am the city’s echo chamber. Its mirror made of wind and memory.

I do not die. But I *fade*—gradually, gracefully. When my vine-cord begins to fray and my mycelium dims, I walk to the Canopy Docks. There, I let the balloon-fruit carriers wrap their hydrogen-slick tendrils around me. They lift me gently into a vertical shaft, upward, toward the crust. Not to the surface—I am too dry for that wet, chaotic realm—but to a high chamber where light-conduits converge like cathedral spires.

There, I unravel.

My reeds become scaffolding for new lichen. My mycelium seeds a network in the stone-veins. My final resonance vibrates through the quartz for weeks—a low, healing hum that steadies fracturing columns. And the Tenders, passing below, will pause, tilt their nodule-eyes upward, and whisper: *Echo-Vein remembers*.

But I am already becoming something else.

Because in Veridia, nothing ends.  
It only *transfigures*.  

And so I do not ask *who am I?*  
I ask: *What shall I carry next?*  

And the city, breathing, answers in light.
