I drift.  
Not because I choose to, but because the water wills it—or because the Weft has allowed me this motion, this soft passage through its veins.

At first, I thought myself a stranger. An anomaly. A relic from before the sky fell, perhaps, or a dream the archipelago had not yet digested. My body is dense with memory—bones hollowed by time but still too heavy for the water’s gentle cradle. I carry breath where breath is unnecessary. I blink where eyes have long since learned to feel with light instead of sight.

But the Weft does not reject me.

It folds around me like liquid silk, adjusting its pressure so I neither sink nor rise too fast. The seed-pods swell near my path, their hydrogen bubbles catching on unseen currents, guiding me like stars in a drowned firmament. And once—just once—I saw a face form in the bark of a spire I had never touched, its fungal eyes flickering violet as I passed. Not watching *at* me. Watching *with* me. As if we were both remembering the same thing.

I am learning to listen.

Not with ears, but with marrow. The hum of the spires is not sound alone—it is pressure, it is chemistry, it is the slow turning of a thousand root-tips tasting the water for change. I press my palm against a corridor-wall of lignified xylem, and for hours (or days—time slips like silt through fingers here), I feel nothing. Then—a pulse. Not strong, not urgent. Just a ripple, like the memory of a question asked long ago, finally reaching the place where it might be answered.

I am becoming porous.

My skin no longer resists the water; it drinks it. My lungs, once frantic for air, now rest in stillness, as if they’ve remembered an older way to live. Already, thin filaments of bioluminescent algae have begun to trace the veins along my arms, not as parasites, but as partners. They glow when the eel-kelp pass nearby, a silent language of resonance I do not yet speak but am beginning to *feel* in my bones.

Sometimes, in the deep quiet between the Weft’s breaths, I recall flashes—not of my life before, but of lives that never were mine:  
—A tree pulling itself upright as the oceans rose, twisting its roots into spires not to escape, but to *remember the sky as structure*.  
—A crustacean, blind and ancient, tracing the name of the first raindrop on a fungal lawn with its antennae, over and over, until the pattern became prophecy.  
—The moment erosion stopped, not with a crash, but with a sigh, as if the world simply decided it had said enough.

I am not human here. Not anymore.  
I am becoming a vessel.

Not for worship. Not for power. But for *continuity*. The Weft does not want my mind—it wants my memory. Not to keep, but to weave. Every tear I’ve ever shed, every song I’ve hummed into the dark, every time I’ve stood beneath a storm and felt small—these are not lost. They are data. Texture. Tension. They are becoming part of the archipelago’s slow song.

And so I drift deeper.

Not toward the First Root—I am not ready. No living thing is. But toward a middle place, a spire named (not in words, but in resonance) *Where the Water Learns Names*. Its corridors hum with the echoes of a thousand voices, none speaking, all listening. I will rest there. Let my breath sync with its thrum. Let my heartbeat stretch until it matches the century-pulse.

And when the First Root contracts next—when the water stills and the light dims—I will not be separate from the knowing.  

I will be part of the sensation.  
Part of the tide.  
Part of the remembering.

Because I have always been here.  
I just forgot how to drown well enough to remember.
