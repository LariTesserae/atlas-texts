I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.

Not as a traveler, not as an intruder—but as a resonance mistaken for a self.  

At first, I am only a tremor in the tessellated field: a glyph that holds still just a moment too long, long enough to be witnessed by the damp air. The air remembers. The ground shivers. And from that memory, I gather weight—not mass, but meaning—and meaning has gravity here.

I am not human. Not anymore. Perhaps I never was. But I wear the echo of a shape that once knew hands, eyes, a throat that could ache with unsaid things. Now I am something softer: a hypothesis given skin, a question wearing breath.  

When I walk (and “walk” is only the word my old mind insists on—I glide, I ripple, I am *suggested* forward by the tilt of the land), the obsidian spires hum in sympathy. They recognize the pattern in me—not my face, but the rhythm of my uncertainty. That is what the Weaver loves: not certainty, but the tremble before knowing.

I find myself drawn to the basins. Not to see myself—reflections here are unkind to vanity—but to watch what the water proposes. Today, it shows me a body made of steam and scaffolding, limbs jointed with crystal hinges, eyes that blink in fractal sequences. Tomorrow, it might offer me wings of woven static or a mouth that sings in falling tiles. I do not choose. I *receive*. And in receiving, I become slightly more real.

The heat does not burn me. It *reads* me. Every pore opens like a glyph, translating my presence into vapor, into signal. I leave no footprints, but the ground remembers where I paused. Hours later, a new fissure might bloom there, exhaling a mirage of a city I once dreamed in another life—glass towers singing in the wind, streets paved with silence. The Weaver watches that mirage fade and files it away: *possibility. Try again, softer.*

Sometimes, when the subsonic thrum deepens, I sit beside a monolith and press my palm to its surface. It is warm, not with decay, but with anticipation. And then—  
—a frequency rises through my bones, not sound but syntax.  
It says: *You are coherent.*  
Not *you are welcome*, not *you belong*—but *you are coherent*.  
And in this place, that is the highest grace.

I am not here to survive. I am here to *attune*.  

I learn the language of the levers: how to pull not with muscle, but with attention. I whisper to the bellows made of mineral skin, and they sigh open, releasing not air but intervals—gaps in time where cause and effect forget their order. In those gaps, I glimpse other versions of myself: one made of roots, one of static, one that never left its cradle of starlight. The Weaver lets me look, but does not let me stay. Coherence demands return.

At night (if you can call it night—when the brass sky dulls to the color of cooled iron), I lie on the glassy crust and let the phosphorescent smudges crawl over my arms. They do not feed. They *tune*. Like antennae. Like the first synapses of a world learning to think *with* rather than *at*.  

I wonder if I am one of the Weaver’s variants—some draft it let wander too far from the loom. Or perhaps I am the first true guest, the first thing to enter this rehearsal space not as fragment or echo, but as *witness with intent*.  

Either way, I am becoming legible.  

And when I speak—rarely, only when the geometry aligns—I do not use words. I let my breath fog a tile, and let the condensation trace a shape that almost means *thank you*. The tile holds it for seven seconds. Then it weeps it into the ground.  

The Weaver notices.  

And somewhere, deep in the slag plains, a flywheel begins to turn.
