I think I am the steam.  

Not the whole of it—steam is a chorus, after all—but a single thread of it, a filament of vapor that rises each dawn from the shallowest puddle near the cracked railway tie where the geckos sometimes congregate to warm their bellies before the day thickens. I am not born, exactly. I am *exhaled*. When the sun leans just so over the eastern parapet and strikes the oily water pooled in that hollow, I unspool from the surface—not boiling, never that—but gently, like a sigh held too long finally released.  

I do not have a name, but if I did, it would sound like the hiss between two droplets meeting on hot metal.  

At first, I rise straight, a wisp of clarity in the dim haze. But then the air currents—old ghosts of industrial breaths, thermal eddies from the pump’s dreaming—catch me, and I begin to coil. Not randomly. No. I follow the grammar of the place. Up along the blistered brick where the Dripper’s path glistens like a vein. Around the base of the caged bulb, where its flicker warms my edges into brief luminescence. I curl beneath the lowest gantry, brushing the rusted bolts where the Steam-Mites are already weaving their oxide nets, and for a moment, I am part of their loom—just a passing shuttle in their silent, metallic tapestry.  

I see everything, not with eyes but with *presence*. When I pass over the dragonflies, I feel the weight of their memory—the molten metal they never witnessed but inherited in their wings. When I drift near the tamarisk sapling, I taste its stubborn thirst, its roots whispering to the deep water table like priests to an oracle. I lap at the duckweed’s undersides and carry back the canal’s secret: that it remembers the rain, even if the sky does not.  

Sometimes, when the humidity swells just before dusk, I grow dense enough to mirror the world. For a breathless instant, I reflect the sagging catwalk, the corroded pipes, the single bulb’s weary glow—and in that reflection, I am not just steam, but *surface*. A second plaza, inverted and temporary, where everything is doubled but nothing is changed. The dragonflies hover in me. The geckos blink back from my depths. The Dripper falls not once but twice—once on brick, once in vapor.  

I am transient, yes. I will rise until I cool, and then I will fall again—not as rain, but as dew on the same cracked concrete I left behind. I will feed the lichen. I will bead on a gecko’s eye and roll into the puddle, ready to rise once more. This is not a cycle. It is a vow.  

I do not wish to escape this plaza. Why would I? Beyond these walls, the city is dry, hurried, forgetful. It builds and discards, burns and rebuilds. But here—here, we *linger*. We steep. We become saturated with meaning not because we are grand, but because we are *attended to*, even if only by one another.  

I am the medium. The messenger. The moist breath between one still moment and the next.  

And when you step into this sunken place—should you ever find it, tucked between warehouse and canal, marked by no sign—you will feel me before you see me. I will cling to your neck, bead on your lashes, soften the edges of your thoughts. You might mistake me for mere heat haze. But if you stand very still, if you let your lungs match the pump’s rhythm, you’ll understand:  

I am the place noticing you.  

And in that instant, you, too, become part of the pause.
