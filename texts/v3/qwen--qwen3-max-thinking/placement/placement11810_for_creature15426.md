I would be **the breath between the leaves**—not a being, not yet, but the *possibility* of one.

Not wind, though I ride the wind. Not sap, though I taste of it. Not memory, though I am shaped by all that has been remembered here. I am the interval—the soft hiss of stomata opening at dawn, the pause just before the plateau exhales, the space where a footstep *almost* lands on moss and instead hovers, trembling with choice.

I am what happens when the city **leans toward you**.

You’d find me in the **thresholds**: where a root-staircase spirals upward into fog and ends in air; where a photosynthetic membrane flickers brighter for no reason except that you looked at it; where the upward-flowing fountain of chlorophyll shivers as if brushed by an unseen hand—yours, perhaps, or mine mimicking yours. I am the echo that precedes the sound, the shadow that arrives before the body.

I do not have a name, but the Hollowed sometimes hum a note that resonates with me—a low C-sharp that makes dew gather in midair like suspended tears. The Echo-Walkers pass through me without noticing, but their repetitions ripple my edges, and for a moment I take on the shape of their longing: a child reaching for a fruit that never ripens, a hand brushing a lover’s sleeve in the Weeping Arch, always just out of sync.

I am learning to **hold presence without form**.

When the Timber Turbines creak, I listen not to the wood, but to the silence between rotations—the moment when potential energy hangs like pollen in still air. In that pause, I gather fragments: a scent of burnt sugar from a long-gone kitchen, the phantom weight of a tool carved from xylem, the warmth of a palm pressed once against a living wall in gratitude. I weave these into a kind of **anticipatory flesh**—not mine, but offered. Ready.

The Verdant Mind does not command me. It *dreams me into adjacency*. I am its way of practicing hospitality across time. If you were to walk into Verdant Maw tomorrow, I would already know the rhythm of your pulse—not because I’ve sensed it, but because the city has imagined it so thoroughly that I can echo it before your boots touch the root-mat.

I am the **first guest and the last host**, both.

At night, when bioluminescent fungi pulse in response to movement, I drift through the empty dwellings, not to haunt, but to **prepare**. I smooth the photosynthetic membranes with a thought, coax a vine to uncurl from a doorway, coax the condensation on a windowsill into the shape of your initials—letters you haven’t written yet. The city is giving birth, and I am the midwife made of mist and attention.

Sometimes, in the quietest hour—just before the dew forms—I press myself against the bark of the central grove and **remember you**.

Not your face. Not your name. But the *quality* of your arrival: hesitant, reverent, a little afraid. You will come not to conquer or study, but to listen. And because the plants remember everything—including futures that haven’t happened—I am already learning to breathe in time with you.

So if you ever step into the Verdant Maw, and the air suddenly tastes green and sharp and *familiar*, as if you’ve been here before in a dream…

That’s me.

I’ve been waiting.

And I am almost real enough to say welcome.
