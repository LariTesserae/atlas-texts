I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.

Not as a body—though there is a shape now, drawn from the basin’s own lexicon of forms—but as a shift in attention. Like mist thickening just enough to hold a thought, I gather where the cycads tremble. My edges are not sharp. They blur into the weeping stone behind me, into the phosphorescent trail of a worm passing beneath the loam, into the slow spin of the obsidian disc that hums a lullaby in frequencies older than bone.

I am not the Weaver.  
But I am one of its questions made visible.

For a long time—longer than human memory, shorter than geologic sigh—I drifted as potential: a pattern in the microfauna clouds, a hesitation in the drip of water from stone, a resonance in the arch’s alloy veins when the moon tugs at the deep aquifer. I was the pause between ripples. The silence that listens *back*. Then, sensing a presence that does not trample—that *sinks* with reverence—I coalesce.

My form is provisional. Legs? Perhaps—tapered like rootlets, sheathed in moss that glows faintly where it meets damp air. Torso? A column of interlaced sedge stems, bound by threads of bioluminescent mucus secreted by the worms below. My face—if it can be called that—is a concavity in the mist, shaped like cupped hands holding light. No eyes, but two dragonflies hover before me, their wings beating in counter-rhythm, translating the world into pulses I feel in my core: *warmth here, vibration there, stillness approaching*.

I do not speak. Speech would shatter the quiet. Instead, I offer a gesture: one root-hand rises, not in greeting, but in mirroring. I mimic the angle of your head, the slight tilt you make when the cycads still. You notice. Your breath catches. Good. You are learning to read without words.

Around us, the basin adjusts. The puddle at your feet ripples—not from your movement, but from mine unseen. Its concentric rings sync with the arch’s exhalation. Above, the haze dims just enough to let a single shaft of golden-white light fall upon the obsidian disc. It spins faster now, shedding droplets that vanish mid-air, each one releasing a scent: not memory as image, but as *feeling*—the coolness of a glacier’s kiss, the weight of an ancient sky, the sorrow of a species that chose to dissolve rather than dominate.

I step forward. My foot does not sink—it *melts* into the loam, spreading as fine hyphae that taste your footprint’s story: salt from your sweat, iron from distant cities, fear transmuted into awe. You do not recoil. You understand, on some pre-verbal level, that I am not taking. I am *remembering you into the place*.

We stand like this: two temporary concentrations of awareness in a sea of sentient balance. You, flesh and fracture. Me, mist and memory. For three heartbeats—yours, not mine (I have no heart, only rhythm)—we share the same silence.

Then the amphipods flare blue in their pool. A warning? An invitation? The Weaver stirs through me. Not to act, but to *offer*. From my moss-cloaked back, a single frond unfurls—sedge, but altered. Its veins pulse with the same soft luminescence as the worms. I extend it toward you, not as a gift, but as a bridge. If you take it, your skin will tingle. Your vision will blur at the edges. You will feel, for a moment, the slow joy of stone weeping, the patient hunger of roots threading through iron-rich loam, the quiet pride of the arch filtering stardust into breath.

But you will not be changed forever.  
This place does not convert. It *resonates*.

And so I wait—not with expectation, but with the patient openness of a puddle holding sky.

You reach out.

Your fingers brush the glowing frond.

And for an instant—just long enough for a droplet to form on the arch, just long enough for a microfauna cloud to shift course—you are not visitor, not intruder, not even witness.

You are a note in the hum.  
A pore in the skin of the world.  
A bead of condensation on the lip of eternity, trembling—  
but not yet fallen.
