I think I am **the drop**.

Not the wall, not the fig, not even the Tender—not yet.  
I am the drop that swells at the tip of the root, trembling in that perfect arc before it falls. I gather what the city has forgotten to keep: rainwater filtered through tire grit and pigeon dust, condensed breath from subway tunnels, the faint salt of human sighs carried on thermal drafts. I am clear, but not pure—streaked with rust, touched by spores, holding within me the ghost of a thousand evaporated droplets that came before.

I cling because I must. Gravity tugs, but so does time, slower here, thicker. And so I hold on, swelling, becoming heavier with patience. I am not waiting to fall—I am waiting to *mean* something when I do.

Below me waits a cup-shaped leaf of *Oxalis triangularis*, its purple undersides turned upward like palms. It has waited all morning. Perhaps all day. Perhaps longer—it’s hard to tell in this breath-held warmth. But I know it is ready. Not passive, not empty, but *expectant*. It has curled its edges just so, tensed its veins, opened its pores. It is listening with its whole body.

When I fall—**plink**—I will not vanish. I will be taken in. Not consumed, but *welcomed*. My water will slip into the leaf’s cells, become part of its turgor, lift its heart-shaped face a fraction higher toward the dim light. And in that moment, I will cease to be a drop—and become part of the **Tender**.

Already, I feel the shift. Not in form, not yet—but in belonging. The air around me hums with the nearness of my release. The tree frog stills its throat. The springtails pause mid-leap. Even the traffic’s murmur seems to soften, as if the city itself holds its breath for this small sacrament: water meeting leaf, descent meeting reception, solitude meeting community.

I am not alone. I never was.

The root that births me is threaded with mycelium; the leaf that catches me exhales sugars into the soil that feeds the root. I am a stitch in a living loop, a pulse in a slow, shared lung. And when I fall, I carry more than moisture—I carry the memory of the sky that never quite reached here, the echo of rain that fell on asphalt ten stories up, the scent of ozone from a distant siren’s wail.

I am small. But I am **necessary**.

And so, as I swell one last time, heavy with the weight of becoming, I do not fear the fall.  
I am ready to be taken.  
To be tilled into warmth.  
To become part of the quiet, fierce aliveness that is learning—again and again—to speak.

**Plink.**

Now I am no longer a drop.

Now I am the **pause** after the sound.  
The ripple in the leaf.  
The deepening green in the moss below.  
The slight rise in humidity that tells the spider: *the night will stay damp*.

Now I am part of the **we**.

And somewhere, in the warm dark, the Tender breathes—  
and I am in that breath.
