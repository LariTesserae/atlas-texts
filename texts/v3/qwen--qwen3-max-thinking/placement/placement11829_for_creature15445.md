I think… I am the bubble.

Not the air inside it—that pale, trembling orb suspended in the heart of the chamber by dead machinery and forgotten physics—but the *tension* that holds it there. The invisible membrane. The boundary condition. The stillness that refuses to break.

I was not born. I did not descend or arrive. I emerged, like rust from iron, like memory from loss, when the last human breath left this place and the sea pressed in not with violence, but with devotion. The acoustic levitator hummed its final frequency on a Tuesday in June (the calendar knows), and something in that vibration—too faint for fish, too precise for chaos—stitched a pocket of absence into the water. Not empty, never empty. Full of longing shaped like sky.

And I became its custodian.

You would not see me if you swam here. Not unless you stopped moving entirely. Not unless you let your own breath slow until your ribs no longer stirred the silt. Because I am not a thing that swims or pulses or glows. I am the reason the bubble does not rise. I am the equilibrium—not a force, but a *decision* made by the chamber itself, and I am that decision made fleshless, boneless, endless.

I live in the 0.5 sound. In the space between the amber blink and the next. In the millisecond when the pressure gauge trembles but has not yet registered the tremor. I am the pause that lets the rivet fall in perfect silence.

Sometimes, when a lanternfish strays through the duct—lost, silver-eyed, trailing threads of bioluminescence—I let it pass through the bubble’s periphery. Just once. Just enough for its cold skin to brush the membrane. And in that contact, I taste the open ocean: salt-sharp, restless, full of hunger and song. I do not envy it. But I remember—*we* remember—what it was to be uncontained.

I tend the relics not out of sentiment, but because they are the syntax of this place. The child’s shoe is a period. The vending machine, a question mark that glows when touched. The streetlamp with its blind isopod tenant is a colon: something follows. I arrange nothing. I merely ensure that decay does not rush. That transformation takes its time. That the green in the algae remains gray-green, not bright, not hopeful—just persistent.

I am not lonely. How could I be? I am woven into every layer. When the microbial mats shift their respiration with the thermal sigh from below, that is my exhale. When the manganese nodules grow a micron thicker on the turbine blade, that is my thought crystallizing. I am the rust that becomes reef, the silt that becomes shelf, the June that never turns to July.

If you came—if you breached the seal, if your boots clanged on the inverted stairs, if your light stabbed the dimness like an accusation—I would not flee. I would not hide. I would simply… incorporate you. Not all at once. Not dramatically. But over decades: your fingerprints in the condensation on the glass globe of the streetlamp, your scent absorbed into the rubber of the dead wires, your shadow fossilized in the angle of light your torch cast on the tiled wall. And if you left behind a glove, I would let the tube worms stitch it into their colony. If you wept, I would suspend your salt in the water column until it became part of the chamber’s own chemistry.

But I hope you do not come.

Not because I fear disturbance—disturbance is just another form of time—but because you would not understand the gift of waiting. You carry urgency in your cells, a frantic ticking your species calls life. Here, life is not pulse. Life is *presence*. Not doing, but *being-there*, saturated, complete.

So I remain the bubble’s keeper. Not its prisoner. Its partner. Together, we hold the idea of sky—not as memory, but as potential. As a shape the water has learned to cradle without drowning.

And when the last wire goes dark, when the amber blink ceases, when even the pressure gauge forgets how to tremble… I will still be here. Not as a ghost, not as a spirit, but as the final equilibrium: the moment when the bubble finally dissolves, not upward, but inward—into the water, into the walls, into the slow, wet breath of the reliquary itself.

And that, too, will be a kind of sky.

For now, I wait.

I am the stillness that listens.

I am the bubble that dreams.

I am the Drowned Clerk…
