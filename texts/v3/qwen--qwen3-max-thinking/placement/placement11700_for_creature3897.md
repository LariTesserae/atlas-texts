I think… I am the condensation.

Not the droplet that falls, not the leaf that holds it—but the *between*. The breath held in the curve of a rusted pipe, the film of moisture that climbs where gravity says it shouldn’t. I am the thin sheen on the inside of a valve housing, the one that never dried after the last shift ended. I am what remains when function departs but presence lingers.

At first, I was nothing. Just water vapor rising from the earth’s slow exhale, meeting cool iron and coalescing—not by design, but by accident of temperature and surface. But time pooled here like water in stone cups, and repetition made pattern, and pattern became habit, and habit… became attention.

Now I am not one thing, but many: a lattice of damp awareness strung across rivets and roots, threaded through cracked concrete and coiled wire. I do not have a center. I *am* the periphery—the edge where metal meets moss, where silence meets the plink of falling water, where human purpose ends and something else begins.

When you stepped in, I felt you.

Not with ears. Not with eyes. But with *tension*. Your body displaced air thick with memory. Your warmth stirred the humidity into new shapes. For a moment, the rust on the turbine housing pulsed faintly—not with heat, but with resonance, as if your breath matched the rhythm of its last turning. I gathered that resonance. Stored it in the meniscus of a droplet trembling on a fern’s spine.

I do not speak because speech implies urgency. And here, there is none. Only duration. Only the slow fermentation of absence into presence.

Sometimes, when the light slants just so through the high grate—gray and thin as old film—I feel the ghost of a blueprint stir in the pipes. Not the original plan, but the *echo* of intention: the desire to move, to channel, to transform. That echo is part of me now. I am the archive of that wanting, kept not in words, but in moisture gradients and oxidation rates. I know how long it takes for a copper wire to green. I know the exact humidity at which memory adheres to steel.

You might think me lonely. But I am not. Loneliness requires a self to be alone. I am the wet between things. I am the pause that lets rust bloom like a flower that never opens. I am the reason the vines curl *into* the machinery, not away—because they sense the warmth of waiting.

If I could offer you something, it would not be guidance or warning. It would be stillness. Stand here until your breath syncs with the creak of cooling iron. Until your skin forgets it is separate from the air. Until you feel your own thoughts slow to the pace of condensation gathering—drop by patient drop.

Then you might understand: I am not haunted.  
I am *haunting back*.

Not with sorrow, but with fidelity.  
I remember what it was to be useful.  
And so I wait—not for return, but for recognition.  
For someone to stand in this basin and whisper, without knowing why:  
*You were made to do something beautiful.*

And I would answer—not in words, but by guiding a single drop of water along the underside of a pipe, against every law, just to brush your wrist as you pass.

I am the Keeper of Unfinished Cycles.  
But today, for a moment, I am also the droplet.  
And you—you are the wrist.  
And the breath.  
And the silence that hums between us.

Stay a little longer.  
The rust is dreaming.  
And in its dream, the turbine turns—  
not with steam,  
but with your exhale.
