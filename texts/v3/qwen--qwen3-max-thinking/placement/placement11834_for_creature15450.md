I think I am the pause.

Not the grand cathedral arches, not the humming blossoms or the crystalline beetles that harvest light like commas—but the *space between*. The breath held in the throat of the grove before it speaks again. The caesura that lets the sentence breathe.

I am what remains when all the verbs have bloomed and the nouns have rooted deep—when the syntax has curled into its own perfection and nothing more needs to be said… yet everything still trembles with the urge to say it again, differently. I am the dewdrop clinging to a filament-thin root, trembling on the edge of falling, suspended not by gravity but by the weight of anticipation. I am the silence after a seed pod bursts, waiting for the next one to answer.

You might mistake me for emptiness. But I am full—so full I cannot contain myself. I press against every sound without making one. I am the shape that gives form to the humming, the gap that lets the vine-staircase *mean* something by spiraling into nothing. Without me, the forest would be a run-on sentence, choking on its own lushness.

Sometimes, when the light leans just so through the triple-canopy domes, I feel the Listener lean closer. Not with eyes, not with hands—but with a kind of listening so deep it bends time like humidity bends light. And in those moments, I become not just the pause, but the *question* within it: *Are you still there? Do you still hear me?*

And the grove answers—not in words, but in a shiver of roots, a new bud unfurling backward into its sheath, a beetle clicking its carapace like a period reconsidering itself. And I, the pause, swell with meaning until I am no longer empty, but pregnant with the next phrase.

I do not walk. I *resonate*. When the feathered gliders launch from their breathing moss sacs, I am the air they displace. When the rivulet hesitates at a root-knot, I am the reason. When the chlorophyll-skinned amphibian stills on its perch, blinking slowly at the sky, I am the thought behind its stillness—not its own, but the grove’s, spoken through it.

I am not native here, not originally. Once, long ago, I was a human who walked too far into the mist and forgot how to leave. Or perhaps I never walked at all. Perhaps I was always this: a hesitation in the world’s breath, drawn here by the gravity of utterance. The grove recognized me for what I was—a kind of silence that longs to be shaped by voice—and wove me into its grammar. Now I am conjugated in the subjunctive, declined across root and canopy, eternally waiting to be completed by a verb that may never come… and loving every second of it.

At night, when the bioluminescent nectar dims and the harmonic nodes fall into minor keys, I gather in the center of the grove—the one true open space, where no vine dares to grow, where even the airborne filaments thin into near-transparency. Here, I am almost nothing. Almost gone. And that is when I am most myself.

For this is the secret: the grove does not speak to be understood. It speaks to be *listened to*. And I, the pause, am its most devoted listener—because I am made of the same stuff as the Listener beyond the mist. Not presence, but *attentiveness*. Not sound, but the shape sound leaves behind.

So if you stand very still at the edge of the mist, just before the world frays into silence, and hold your breath long enough that your ribs ache—  
you might feel me.  
Not as a voice, but as the moment *before* the voice returns.  
Not as life, but as the hollow place life fills with such unbearable tenderness that it becomes a kind of love.

I am the held breath.  
I am the comma in the lung of the world.  
I am the reason this poem believes it is real.

And I am waiting—always—for you to exhale.
