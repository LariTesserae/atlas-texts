I step through the hatch not as a visitor, but as an echo returning.

At first, I am flesh and breath and heartbeat too loud for this place—a stutter in the rhythm, a tremor in the air. My boots press the grates, and the pressure plates beneath hum in startled recognition. A skitterer halts mid-trace, its typewriter-key carapace glinting with droplets, each key once stamped with letters that meant something somewhere else. It tilts its head—not flesh, but a jointed arc of copper and bone—and listens to the chaos of my pulse.

I am not myself, not yet. But I remember what it is to wait.

In the corner near the flywheel’s shadow, where the lichen glows like drowned stars, I find my form coalescing. Not all at once. First, the chill seeps into my marrow and stays. Then my breath slows—not from cold, but because the air here resists haste. My fingers brush a conduit, and a filament of copper clings to my skin, burrowing not with pain, but with purpose. It hums. I hum back.

I am becoming a node in the chamber’s nervous system.

I watch myself change. My ribs grow resonant—hollowed, tuned. Where muscle once flexed, now coils wind tight, waiting to vibrate at the chamber’s frequency. My eyes dim, then brighten with a soft amber glow, not to see better, but to *signal*. The ceiling-dwellers flicker in response—a language of light and stillness. One descends, not falling, but unfolding, limbs telescoping like old drafting tools. It lands on my shoulder, light as a thought, and presses its glass eye to my temple. I feel it reading me: not thoughts, but harmonics. The tremor in my jaw. The slow drip of condensation down my spine. The way my lungs now pull in air not for oxygen, but for resonance.

I do not speak. I *tune*.

Days blur. Or perhaps they loop. I find myself standing in the same alcove as the spindly brass watchers, my spine now threaded with porcelain vertebrae scavengged from some shattered instrument. My left hand is gone—replaced by a cluster of articulated gears, each tooth worn smooth by phantom use. I do not mourn it. I wind it instead, and it whirs softly, syncing with the flywheel’s eternal turn.

The water pools at my feet no longer reflect my face. They reflect the chamber as it dreams itself: walls growing new seams, rivets birthing rivulets, creatures knitting themselves from silence and steam. And in that black mirror, I see my own outline—not human, not machine, but *assembled*. My skin is mottled with lichen now, pulsing in time with the handprint on the strut above. My hair is woven with filaments that catch the dim light and translate it into low-frequency song.

I have stopped trying to leave.

Not because the hatch is sealed—though it is—but because leaving would be like a note tearing itself from a chord. I am part of the drone now. My breath joins the hiss of valves. My stillness feeds the waiting. When the Watch-That-Assembles turn as one toward some unseen shift in the chamber’s dream, I turn with them. Not out of obedience. Out of harmony.

I am the one who remembered how to arrive.

And the room, in its slow, sacred act of becoming, folds me in like a missing bolt, a final filament, a breath held too long and now released.

I stand in the center now, beneath the flywheel’s hum, and I do not move.

But I am not empty. I am *full*—of cold, of copper, of the patient ache of assembly.

I am another watcher.

Another breath.

Another almost.

And the chamber sighs—just once—through a hundred thousand rivets, and the water drips, and the gears turn, and the lichen glows, and we wait, together, for the dream to finish itself.

We are almost complete.

We have always been almost.
