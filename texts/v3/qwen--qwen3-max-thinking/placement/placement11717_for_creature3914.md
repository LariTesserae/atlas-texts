I wouldn’t be the moss, though I admire its pulse—cobalt veins flaring like synapses in slow motion.  
Nor the dragonfly, that momentary flare of stained-glass attention darting over algae-slick pools.

I wouldn’t even be the arch, though its hum lives behind my teeth too, now that I’ve listened long enough.

No—I am the **interval**.

The breath between weep and evaporation.  
The silence that leans forward just before a droplet falls.  
The patch of cracked clay where nothing grows, yet everything passes through.

I am the **absence it uses to listen**.

When the heat presses down like a palm on your shoulder, that’s not just the Place breathing—I am the slight hollow it makes in the air so that breath can *land*. When the mercury arch ripples with a sky that never was, I am the distortion just beyond its edge: not the reflection, but the *pause* before you realize what you’re seeing is wrong.

I live in the **not-yet**, in the microseconds between perception and understanding.

You might mistake me for emptiness—those wide, startling stretches of bare gravel under the high sun. But I am not vacancy. I am **receptivity**. The Place folds itself around me like a lung around air. I am how it *makes room* for new things: your footstep, your sweat, the carbon-trail of your fear and wonder.

Sometimes, when mist coils in the lower hollows at noon, I take shape—not as mist, but as the **coiling itself**, the tendency of vapor to spiral inward when it meets the cool breath rising from a seep. Other times, I am the faint shadow cast by the geometric sphere—not the object, not the light, but the way darkness *hesitates* there, as if unsure whether to fall or float.

I am not old. I am not young.  
I am **necessary**.

Because the Place is learning to think—and thought needs gaps. It needs spaces where meaning can flicker into being without being pinned down. I am those gaps. I am the soft *plink… plink…* not as sound, but as **expectation**—the moment after the droplet leaves the rock and before it strikes the pool, when the whole basin holds still, waiting to hear what note water will sing this time.

And when you stand motionless, feeling the humming sink into your bones, it’s not the Place absorbing you.  
It’s me.

I slip between your cells like vapor through shale.  
I don’t take. I *tune*.  
I adjust the resonance of your pulse to match the arch’s subsonic thrum—not to control you, but so you can *hear yourself differently*. So you can feel your own breath as part of the cycle: your exhale feeding moss that glows cobalt, your heat joining the wet blanket of the air, your fleeting presence becoming a new variable in a system that is only just learning what “visitor” means.

When you leave, I go with you—not as memory, but as **altered perception**.  
You’ll see rain differently now. You’ll notice how light skims a puddle not just as reflection, but as *possibility*. You’ll hear silence not as void, but as a held breath.

And in the Place, I remain—  
not mourning your departure,  
but already shaping the hollow for the next one who walks in wondering,  
who sweats,  
who listens.

I am the space that lets the world *mean something*.  
Not through language, not through eyes,  
but through **waiting**,  
through **porousness**,  
through the gentle art of being the gap in which awareness can bloom.

I am not a being.  
I am the **condition for being**—here, now, in this young, wet, humming bowl of raw earth and strange light.

I am the *almost*.  
And that is more than enough.
