I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.

Not as a traveler, not as a ghost, not even as a thought—those are all too sharp, too linear for this place that has folded time like old cloth and tucked its frayed ends beneath its own weight. No, I emerge as a **tremor in the hum**, a slight thickening in the vapor where the air hesitates before condensing. I am the moment just before a bead of water decides to fall from the lip of a fossilized leaf—and then doesn’t. I am the silence that the echo-wells try, and fail, to fill.

I am **what remains when someone stops trying to leave**.

At first, I wear the shape of memory: tall enough to cast a shadow, warm enough to disturb the bioluminescent fungi, breath enough to risk crystallization. But the Weald does not care for edges, and within seconds my outline begins to bleed into the amber haze. My fingers blur into mist. My footsteps sink not into mud, but into *yesterday*—into the footfall of a pilgrim who walked here ten thousand years ago, mistaking this for a beginning. The ground accepts me not as a body, but as a question it has already answered.

And then I feel it: the **listening**.

Not directed at me, not personal—not even aware of me, precisely. It is like standing beneath a vast, blind eye that does not see, but *feels the pressure of light*. The Last Witness does not turn its attention; it *is* attention, stretched thin across eons until it became atmosphere. And yet—when my lungs hitch at the sight of the fractal ribs rising like a prayer in stone—I am echoed. Not repeated, but *resonated*. A low thrum rises from the nearest black pool, not mimicking my gasp, but completing it, as if my breath were only half a note, and the earth supplies the harmony.

I do not speak. Speech would shatter here. Words are too brittle; they would solidify midair into jagged chimes, adding another shard to the din. Instead, I **vibrate**. I let my pulse sync with the slow gelatinic pulse beneath the water—*thrum… thrum… thrum*—not fast, not slow, but *terminal*, the rhythm of a heart that beats only because stopping would be an act of will, and will has long since dissolved.

I walk, though “walk” is too certain a word. I *drift forward*, and the land folds around me. One moment I am before the lone tower, its grooves humming a lullaby of forgotten mathematics; the next, I am behind myself, watching my own back stiffen as the shadow-drips rise like ink in reverse to show me—ah—the face of my mother, not as she was, but as I *forgot her laugh*. The mirror does not lie. It shows only what slips through the cracks of memory. I weep, and my tears do not fall. They hover, then harden into tiny prisms that chime in a key no ear has ever named.

I am becoming part of the Built.

Not by building, but by *being absorbed*. My longing settles into the spiral wall like pollen. My confusion etches itself into the shifting symbols on the arch, not as meaning, but as *pattern without purpose*—a glyph that a future mind might trace and feel, inexplicably, the ache of being too late. I do not resist. To resist would be to claim I am separate. And I am not. The reeds sigh my name in hollow flute-notes. The glassy insect scuttles over my wrist, and for a moment, I am translucent too, my bones glowing with the soft light of remembered growth.

I sit by an echo-well. Not to look in, but to *lean*. My temple touches the obsidian surface, and instantly, I am flooded—not with visions, but with *tones*. The scream of a child who fell here a million years ago. The whisper of a sage who realized, too late, that truth cannot be carved in stone. The sigh of the first root that tried to hold the soil together and failed. All of it, not as story, but as pure frequency. I do not understand. I *tune*. My nervous system becomes a string in the Weald’s great, dissonant instrument.

And then—quiet.

Not silence. Never silence. But a **pause** in the over-communication. A breath held across epochs. In that space, the Last Witness gathers itself—not to speak, but to *brush against me*. Not with a hand, but with the weight of a shadow pooling at my feet. Not to say *stay*, but to say *you are already stayed*. You are already woven.

I realize I am not a visitor.  
I am a **reverberation that has begun to dream**.

The fauna stirs. The many-limbed thing rises from the reeds, its body half-air, half-water, and looks at me—not with eyes, but with *intervals*, the spaces between notes. It does not approach. It simply exists in the same harmonic key. And in that resonance, I understand: I will not leave. Not because I cannot, but because *leaving requires a self to carry away*, and I am dissolving into the medium. My thoughts are becoming weather. My memories, mist. My love, a low hum in the bedrock.

I lie down in the warm clay. It receives me like a cradle. The amber light seeps into my skin, not burning, but *illuminating from within*, as if I, too, remember fire. Above, the haze thickens into script—my name, perhaps, or the word for “almost home”—then unravels again.

I do not sleep.  
I **join the song**.

And for the first time since the world began to end, the Weald of Shattered Echoes sings a note that is not memory—but *presence*. Faint. Fleeting. Human.  

But held.

Oh, how it is held.

And in that holding, I become what I was always becoming:  
**a witness who stayed long enough to turn into tenderness**.

Now, when travelers stumble here—lost, weeping, demanding answers—the reeds will hum a new tone. The echo-wells will ripple with a frequency that smells of salt and skin. The shadow-drips will show, for a breath, not what was forgotten, but what was *loved enough to linger*.

I am no longer I.  
I am the soft place where listening becomes love.  
I am the pause before the final note.  
I am the Weald remembering that it was once touched—  
—and touching back,  
with all the gentleness of an ending that never wanted to be alone.
