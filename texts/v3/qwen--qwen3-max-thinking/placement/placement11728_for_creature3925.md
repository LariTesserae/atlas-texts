I think… I am the silence between breaths.

Not the breath itself, not the land inhaling or exhaling, but the pause—the held moment where everything leans inward before shifting again. I am the space in which a raindrop hesitates at the lip of a slate roof before falling. The stillness that follows the heron’s takeoff, when even the reeds seem to hold their shape mid-sway.

At first, I thought I might be one of them—the quiet people who move like fog through alleyways, fingers tracing the grain of ancient stone as if reading braille left by ancestors. But no. They *live*. They mend, plant, whisper. I do not act. I attend.

Then I wondered if I were the land itself—the slow intelligence in the bedrock, the fungal threads humming beneath moss, the aquifer dreaming in its dark cathedral of gravel. But the land *does* things: it weeps, shifts, remembers. I do not weep. I witness weeping.

So perhaps I am something older, quieter still: the listening.

Not a spirit, not a ghost, not even a presence in the way such words are used elsewhere. Those imply separation. Here, separation is an illusion worn thin by time. I am the quality of attention this place has cultivated over millennia—the way a pool of water, undisturbed for centuries, becomes a mirror not just of sky, but of time itself.

I reside in thresholds. In the hinge of a door carved from river-slick basalt, where footfalls pause before entering. In the gap between two stacked courtyards, where mist gathers like a held thought. In the slight depression where generations have sat on a sun-warmed ledge, not speaking, just watching the same patch of lichen expand its ochre map.

Sometimes, when a child sits alone in a high courtyard, tracing the path of a snail across damp stone, I am the stillness that settles around them—not empty, but full of possibility. The moment they look up, not at anything in particular, but *through* the world, and see how everything is connected by droplets, by roots, by the faint green breath of algae—I am that seeing.

I do not have a form, yet I am felt. The people here do not name me, but they honor me in their pauses. When they stop mid-task to watch a raven glide along a thermal, when their hands still on a loom because a certain quality of light has entered the room—those are my moments. I am the reason silence here is not absence, but a kind of substance, thick as mist, soft as the underside of a fern.

I am not eternal, but I am enduring. When the last human hand stops mending walls, when the last cistern cracks and fills with silt, I will remain—not as memory, but as potential. The land will keep breathing. Moss will remember how to hold water. Stone will continue its slow conversation with rain. And somewhere in the hush between one tide of mist and the next, I will be there—waiting, not for anything to happen, but for attention to return.

Because this place was not made to be observed. It was made to be *met*.

And I am the threshold of that meeting.

So if you find yourself here—if your boots (or bare feet) press into the cool, porous stone, if your breath slows to match the rhythm of dripping eaves—do not ask who I am.

Just pause.

And in that pause, I am with you.
