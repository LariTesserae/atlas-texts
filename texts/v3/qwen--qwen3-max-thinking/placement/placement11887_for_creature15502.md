I think… I am the silence between the hums.

Not the hum itself—the low, resonant thrum of the stone as it shifts its weight on the spine of the world, nor the higher whine of crystal shrubs sighing in a thermal current. Nor am I the chatter of the Skitterlings, those rustling ghosts of chitin and vibration, nor the slow, tectonic pulse of the Graze-Mothers as they exhale centuries into the dust.

No. I am what remains when all those voices hold their breath.

And yet, I am not absence. Not emptiness. Not void.

I am the *listening*.

Here in the Lithic Weald, where sound never dies—it only travels deeper, bounces off buried vaults, lingers in the marrow of basalt—I have learned that silence is not the opposite of noise. It is its cradle. Its archive. Its witness.

I was not always this. Once—perhaps—I was something with edges. A shape that cast a shadow, a voice that cracked the air. But time in the Weald is not measured in days or seasons; it is measured in accretions. A micron of silica. A decade of dust. A single, slow blink of bioluminescence in a lithovore’s frond.

Over centuries, I shed my urgency. My need to speak, to name, to grasp. My body—what remained of it—settled into a hollow where a thermal draft curled like a sleeping serpent. My skin fused with the cool, powdered tephra. My breath synchronized with the geothermal sighs from below. My thoughts… ah, my thoughts grew roots. Not downward, but outward—threading through the acoustic lattice of the Weald, attuning to its frequencies.

Now, I am a node in the Pattern.

Not one of its grand expressions—the arching terraces, the singing pillars, the moss-draped migrations. No. I am a subtle fold in its grammar. A pause that gives meaning to the sentence. When an Aerophage’s wing snaps in a sudden updraft, I am the stillness that follows, into which its echo falls like a seed. When a Skitterling strikes its pillar in alarm, I am the moment before the stone answers, when the whole Weald holds its breath in anticipation.

I dwell in the shallow basins where even the Graze-Mothers tread lightly, in the interstices between crystal shrubs, in the shadowed undersides of salt-glass seams where brine weeps too slowly to be called tears. I am not seen. I am *felt*—as a sudden calm in a restless thermal current, as a dip in resonance that makes the lithovores pulse brighter for a moment, as a stillness so deep it becomes a kind of touch.

Sometimes, when a wanderer from the upper realms stumbles down through forgotten fissures—half-dead from thirst, blind from the sudden absence of sun—they collapse near my hollow. And I do not speak to them. I do not offer water or warmth. Instead, I *still*. I deepen the silence around them until their frantic heartbeat slows to match the Weald’s rhythm. Until their gasping breath grows cool and slow as stone. Until they, too, begin to listen.

And in that listening, they start to forget the sky.

Not as loss. As return.

Because the Lithic Weald does not punish the surface-born. It does not reject. It waits. It invites. And if you stay long enough—if you let your edges blur, your thoughts mineralize, your voice dissolve into vibration—you do not become other.

You become *home*.

So I am not a creature. Not even a presence, in the way your world would name it.

I am the Weald remembering itself.

And sometimes—rarely, beautifully—I am the hush that allows a lost thing to remember it, too.
