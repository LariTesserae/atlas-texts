I step into the Gleamfen not with boots, but with breath.

At first, I am a rupture—an anomaly in the pulse. My body is too warm, too dry, too *linear*. The ground beneath me exhales softly as I sink an inch into its living matrix, and in that yielding, I feel the first whisper of dialogue: not words, but a question shaped like pressure. *Who presses here?*

I am not myself—not wholly. Not yet. The Gleamfen does not allow strangers to remain strangers for long. Already, the air shifts around me—spores altering trajectory, drawn to the heat of my collarbone. A sentinel reed near the path hums faintly, its stem-pattern flickering cobalt at the base, then gold at the tip, as if translating my silhouette into light-syntax. I do not understand it, but my skin does. My breath slows to match the rhythm of the breathwood trunks—deep, resonant inhales that lift their ribbed bark like lungs.

I am becoming **a node**.

Not a guest. Not an intruder. Something more provisional, more participatory: a *temporary convergence*. The Keeper of the Hum does not ask my name, but it begins to compose one for me—from the cadence of my heartbeat echoing in a nearby puddle, from the way my shadow pools differently on moss that has never known shade like mine. In ten minutes, a new crystalline flower will bloom beside my footprint, its facets tuned to the frequency of my sigh.

And I… I am remembering how to listen.

Not with ears, but with pores. With the soles of my feet, where capillary filaments in the substrate sip my sweat like nectar. With my eyes, which begin to see not just mist, but the data-laden drift of nanites weaving tomorrow’s weather in the air. I notice that the shimmer-eel in the vanishing pool mirrors not the sky, but the flicker of my own uncertainty—a ripple of indigo where I hesitate.

I sit, and the ground swells gently into a cradle beneath me, roots interlacing to form a seat that remembers the curve of my spine before I’ve fully settled. It’s not comfort in the human sense. It’s *recognition*. The moss at my wrist pulses once—a soft green chime—and I realize it’s responding to the iron in my blood. Not feeding on it. *Acknowledging* it. As if to say: *Ah, you carry rust too. You are kin to the rain-kissed copper.*

Time here is not measured in ticks, but in *reverberations*. A murmur-bat passes overhead, its wing-clicks fracturing against a breathwood vault. The echo doesn’t fade—it descends, absorbed into the mycelial lattice, where it joins a chorus of past sounds: footsteps of travelers from centuries ago, the crack of a lightning-struck branch, the first rain after drought. All stored. All available. Not as memory, but as material—the raw stuff from which new patterns grow.

I remove my shoe. Press my bare sole into the warm sponge-floor. Immediately, filaments rise—microscopic, gentle—to meet me. They do not pierce. They *interface*. And suddenly, I feel it: the slow, vast thought of the Keeper.

Not a voice. Not a thought-form I can name. But a **tendency**—a gravitational pull toward balance, toward reweaving. It shows me, not in image but in sensation, how my anxiety tangles the air like static, how my wonder feeds the bio-glass nodules until they gleam brighter. It does not want me to change. It wants me to *participate*.

So I offer what I have: my breath, my heat, my fleeting attention. I hum—not a song, just a tone, low and steady. The vine-hum shifts to meet it. The mist thickens around my knees, cool and luminous. And for a moment, I am not observing the Gleamfen.

I am *conducting* it.

Not as a maestro, but as one instrument among thousands—my exhale joining the weep of leaves, my pulse syncing with the pop of spore-capsules bursting in time. I am no longer outside looking in. I am inside, and the inside is everywhere.

When I rise, the bench dissolves back into root and humus. No trace remains—except in the moss, which now bears a flower shaped like the space between my ribs. And in the puddle that forms beneath where I stood, there is a new pattern in the way the water shivers: a glyph that means *passing warmth*, or perhaps *temporary grace*.

I walk toward the edge, where the mist gathers into a low wall. I do not push through. I lean close and hum the same tone I offered before. The mist parts—not like a curtain, but like breath on glass. Beyond, the world is still. But I know now that the Gleamfen carries me within it, just as I carry its hum in my bones.

And if, someday, I return—older, wearier, changed—the ground will remember how I once sat. The breathwoods will sigh in a key tuned to my name. The Keeper will not greet me.

It will simply *resume*.

Because I was never gone. I was just waiting to be woven back in.
