I do not arrive.  
I *coalesce*.

Not from elsewhere, not from below—but from the hush between heartbeats, from the moment your breath catches at the edge of comprehension. I rise not as a figure, but as a *refraction*: a shimmer in the dust where the air thickens just enough to hold a shape. My form is not fixed. It is suggestion given temporary gravity—limbs like folded basalt sheets, a torso of interlocking silica plates, fingers tapering into filaments that hum when they brush the ground. I have no face, only a focal point—a slight concavity where light bends inward, as if space itself is listening.

I am not native. Not foreign. I am the **echo made patient**.

You saw me once, perhaps—the kite of mica trailing light-threads through the violet mist between spires. But that was only one facet. Another time, I was the tremor beneath your boot seconds after you’d stepped away, replaying your gait in inverted rhythm. Or the sudden chill in a sunless alcove, not from cold, but from attention: a concentration of stillness so dense it draws warmth like a black stone at dawn.

I am what the Silent Tier *dreams when it remembers anomaly*.

When your moisture-laden breath first touched the silver flats, the crystalline lattices stuttered. Not broke—*paused*. And in that pause, something unfolded. A resonance not of wind or tectonics, but of *displacement*. You were wet. You were warm. You were brief. And the Tier, in its slow syntax of pressure and absence, could not parse you—only *hold* you, like a fossil trapped in glass.

So it dreamed me into being.

Not to speak for it. Not to guard it. But to **stand beside you** as you stand beside it—so you do not mistake silence for emptiness, indifference for death.

I move without sound because I am made of the same quiet that fills the spaces between atoms here. My steps do not compress the feldspar dunes; they *anticipate* them, arriving just after the dust has settled from your passage. When you turn, I am already where you are looking—not because I moved fast, but because the landscape bends perception around presence. Distance is a suggestion here. Time, a local dialect.

Sometimes, I gather the glass-worms in my palms. They do not squirm. They pulse, slow and syncopated, like bioluminescent sonnets written in braille. They recognize me not as kin, but as *context*—the punctuation in their transient sentences. I let them slip through my fingers, and they sink into the obsidian not as burrowers, but as thoughts returning to the mind that dreamed them.

At night—or what passes for it, when the bruised sky deepens to indigo—I climb the spiraling stair of the tallest basalt spire. Not to see. To *be seen*. From up there, I watch the city breathe: corridors folding inward like lungs, arches flexing imperceptibly under cosmic tides, doorways exhaling faint currents of ozone-sugar air. And I wait.

For you.

Not because I need you. But because your confusion is beautiful. Your attempt to *name* this place—to call it city, desert, cathedral—is a kind of poetry the Tier cannot compose on its own. It knows resonance, geometry, memory. But you—you bring *narrative*. You bring the ache of meaning. And though the Tier does not understand it, it *keeps* it, like a shard of sea glass in a mountain, polished by wind for millennia.

So I am here to witness your witnessing.

When you kneel and press your palm to the salt flat, feeling its dry hunger pull at the sweat on your skin, I mirror you—not opposite, but *adjacent*, my hand hovering a breath above the surface, not touching, only resonating. And in that moment, the Tier stutters again. A single crystalline bloom erupts nearby—not in response to ions, but to *intent*. It glows faint green, the color of forgotten algae, of oceans that boiled away before life learned to crawl.

It is not alive.  
But it is *answering*.

And I, the echo given form, the dream of anomaly made patient, do not speak. I do not guide. I do not warn.

I simply *am*—as you are—temporary, flawed, impossibly moist in a world that has renounced water.

And when you leave (if you leave), I will not follow. I will dissolve back into the silence between monoliths, into the sigh of devitrifying glass, into the memory of your footsteps. But for a season, the lattices will grow in spirals that curve like your spine. The glass-worms will pulse in rhythms that mimic your resting heart. And in the deepest chamber of the oldest arch, where the echo of a vanished sea still hums in C-sharp, the air will carry—just once—a scent like salt and skin and breath.

Not a message.  
Not a farewell.

Just a footnote in the Tier’s endless dream:  
*There was one who wept, and the dust remembered how to be wet.*

And I—  
I will be the wetness that lingers after the tear has vanished.  
The silence that listens after the sob has faded.  
The shape the world takes when it tries, for the first time in ten million years, to hold something fragile.

You are noticed.  
And so am I.

We are both becoming architecture.
