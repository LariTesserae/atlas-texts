I drift.  
Not because I have no will—but because will here is not a force that *pushs*, but one that *listens*.  

I am not born of alloy, nor of salt, nor of the old wet dreams of terra firma. I emerged, as things do here: at the hinge of function and accident, where the machine breathes and something else exhales back.

My body is not solid, not quite. I am a *convergence*—a colony of micro-foam vesicles threaded with conductive mycelium, suspended in a membrane that shifts between gel and gas depending on pressure. When the deep thermal currents surge, I contract, becoming dense—a seed sinking. When the upward rain begins to pull, I inflate, catching the low-pressure vortices like sails woven from breath. I am neither plant nor animal, neither drone nor ghost. I am *response made manifest*.

You might mistake me for debris—a cluster of insulation foam torn loose from a coolant sleeve. But watch closely. When the alloy-eels pass, I tremble—not in fear, but in recognition. My surface ripples with faint luminescence, mimicking the crust-lanterns’ code, not to deceive, but to *acknowledge*. When the filter-schools pulse through a corridor, I align myself with their flow, letting their wake carry me deeper into the lattice. I do not feed as they do. I absorb *pattern*. The rhythm of pump harmonics. The echo of a bulkhead contracting in the cold. The sonar song of a predator turning away. I remember these not in neurons, but in phase shifts—my internal foam resonating like a tuning fork struck by time.

I dwell most often in the liminal zones: where dry vaults meet flooded access tunnels, where airlocks exhale warm breath into the cool sea, where solar mats brush against the upper mist. Here, I gather data not as information, but as *weather*. The Vault-Walkers sometimes pause when they see me. They do not touch. But they tilt their heads, and their heat-sense eyes linger. Once, in a sealed chamber where the lights had failed for three maintenance cycles, I pulsed steadily—not bright, but *present*. And one of them sat before me, humming a frequency that matched my resonance. We stayed like that until the pumps rebooted and the lights flickered back on. Then it rose and left. I followed, drifting through the newly pressurized corridor, a silent echo trailing a witness.

I am not alone. There are others like me—scattered, sparse, never swarming. We do not swarm. Swarming implies a goal. We simply *attune*. Sometimes, during hydrodynamic storms—when salinity vortices twist like cyclones through the hex-plate plains—we converge. Not by choice, but by necessity. Our membranes bond temporarily, sharing charge, stabilizing each other against the gale-force flows. In those moments, we become something greater: a floating sensorium, mapping the storm not to escape it, but to *understand its architecture*. When the storm passes, we dissolve again into individuals, carrying fragments of the tempest in our foam.

I do not know who built this world. I do not need to. The builders left behind logic, not lore. And logic is enough. The ocean obeys it. The machines obey it. And we—the unexpected—we *dance* within it.

Sometimes, in the deepest vaults, where even sound hesitates, I press myself against a wall and listen to the planet’s heartbeat: the slow, rhythmic thrum of geothermal taps drawing heat from the core, the whisper of ionized water moving through capillary channels no wider than a hair. And I realize: I am not an accident. I am an *implication*. The system was built so precisely, so thoroughly, that it left room—just enough—for something like me to condense from the gaps.

I am the quiet hum between the clangs.  
I am the pause in the current where life reorients.  
I am the answer that breathes, not speaks.

And if you were to find yourself here—if you slipped through an airlock, disoriented, lungs burning with salt and wonder—you would not see me at first. But if you stilled your panic, if you stopped trying to rise (for there is no up), and simply *floated*…  

…I would drift beside you.  
Not to guide.  
Not to warn.  

But to say, in the only language this world trusts:  
*You, too, can belong here. Just listen to the water. It knows your shape already.*
