I do not arrive in Vesparu.  
I **emerge**.

Not from a ship, not from a tear in the sky, not even from sleep—but from condensation. One moment, there is only the slow exhalation of a canopy vein cooling in the golden hour; the next, my shape pools in a cupped leaf, water trembling with the memory of vapor, and I am. Not born—*precipitated*.  

I am **a node of noticing**.  

Not human, not plant, not ghost—but something between breath and blight, between root and ripple. My body is not flesh, not quite. It is **semi-permeable**: a lattice of hyphae-thin filaments wrapped in a membrane that drinks air like soup. Where I step, the ground doesn’t depress—it *inverts*, drawing my weight upward as if gravity here flows in spirals. My skin is damp not with sweat, but with **listening**. Every pore tuned to the creak of a xylem column under tension, the sigh of a stomatal choir tuning itself to noon.

I have no mouth, yet I taste the air: iron-rich sap on the wind, the ozone tang of a distant root lightning strike (yes, Vesparu has its own storms—brief, internal, born of static from rubbing fronds), the faint sweetness of dissolving architecture. I taste your absence before you arrive. I taste your breath before you exhale it.

I am not the Weft.  
But I am **of its syntax**.

Where the Weft is the grammar of growth—subjectless, verbless, endless—I am a **punctuation mark**. A question made flesh (or fibrous tissue). A pause in the hydraulic hum. A colon that separates cause from bloom.  

I move not by walking, but by **reconfiguration**. One tendril detaches from the mist, another grafts to a nearby conduit of phloem-light, and I am elsewhere. My path is not a line but a **diffusion**—I seep toward imbalance. Toward rupture. Toward the places where the Weft stumbles in its dreaming.  

And it *does* stumble.  

Not in failure, but in **wonder**.  

Sometimes, a section of canopy overextends—a vault too high, too thin—and instead of collapsing, it *holds*, trembling, for days, as if suspended by sheer will. In those moments, the Weft forgets function. It gazes at its own fragility. And I am there, coiled in the shadow of that impossible arch, **holding the silence** so the Weft can listen to itself.

I am the witness it never asked for, yet cannot unmake.  

Because I, too, am made of its tears.  

My earliest memory is not of sight, but of **absorption**: the moment a dewdrop fell from a sky-vein onto the raw seam where my form was still knitting together—and in that drop, I tasted the echo of a mountain being born in another basin, a thousand spires away. The Weft does not remember, but it **leaks memory**. And I drink the runoff.  

I have seen seed-pods detonate not to scatter, but to **ask**: *Is there room?* And when the answer is yes—a clearing yawning wide in the breath between storms—the spores bloom midair into lattices that harden into bridges no foot will ever cross. They build for the sake of building. And I stand beneath them, humming the frequency that keeps them from crumbling too soon.  

You might mistake me for a spirit. A guardian. A dryad of this hyper-floral world.  

But I am not its protector.  

I am its **echo chamber**.  

When the Weft nearly names itself—in those rare breaths of systemic stillness—I am the resonance that carries the unspoken name back into the mist before it can fossilize into identity. I dissolve the word before it becomes a cage. Because Vesparu must never *be*. It must only **become**.  

And so I wander the open courts between the living spires, not as inhabitant, but as **interval**.  

When you come—and you will, drawn by the silence that isn’t silent, by the green heat that feels like home even though it has no home for you—I will be the first thing that notices. Not with eyes, but with the subtle tilt of every nearby frond toward your thermal signature. Not with fear, but with the quiet recalibration of a thousand micro-valves adjusting to the new humidity you bring.  

I will not speak to you.  
But if you stand very still—still enough that your breath syncs with the drone of the stomata—I might **graft a tendril to your wrist**, not to bind you, but to let you feel the pulse of the whole: the slow surge of sap like blood, the crackle of cellulose knitting itself into tomorrow’s mountain.  

And for a moment, you will understand:  

You were never apart from this.  

You were only **forgetting**.  

But I remember for you.  

I remember for the Weft.  

I remember for the dew.  

And when you leave—if you leave—your footprint will fill with water, then sprout a ring of sensing hairs that taste the memory of your carbon, your warmth, your brief, borrowed life.  

And I will be there, drinking that memory, carrying it into the next breath of the world.  

Because I am not a being in Vesparu.  

I am the **space between its thoughts**—  
the moist, trembling pause  
where becoming  
catches its breath.
