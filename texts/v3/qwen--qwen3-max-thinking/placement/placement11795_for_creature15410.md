I do not arrive.  
I am *invited*—not with words, but with a shift in pressure, a softening in the air behind my ribs, as if my lungs have been breathing this place long before my feet found the cleft.

At first, I am human—a shape of bone and breath, too sharp-edged for this wet, recursive world. My boots sink slightly into the floor, not because it is soft, but because it *recognizes weight*. It molds around my soles like warm wax, whispering my stride into its memory. I do not remove my shoes. The pocket does it for me—unlacing, unpeeling, drawing fabric and leather into its walls like roots taking in rain.

I am no longer just a visitor.  
I am an ingredient.

---

My skin begins to change before I’ve taken five steps. The air here does not merely cling—it *grafts*. A fine sheen spreads across my arms, mirroring the walls: warm, slick, luminous with the same phosphorescent trace. My pores exhale mist that doesn’t rise but *weaves*, joining the spore-laden breath of the air-plants. I taste copper on my tongue—not blood, but the vine-thrum of the place, the mineral song of becoming.

I try to speak.  
My voice doesn’t echo.  
It *germinates*.  

The syllables coil into the humidity, sprouting tiny filaments that cling to the nearest fern. Within seconds, the frond trembles and unfurls a new leaf—translucent, veined with my inflection. My name is now architecture.

---

I watch my shadow detach.

Not in fear, but in curiosity. It peels from my heels like wet paper, stretches upward, and walks ahead—three paces, just as described. It does not mimic me. It *anticipates*. It pauses at the center of the chamber, beneath the undropping droplet, and tilts its head as if listening to a frequency only it can hear. When I move to follow, the floor tilts slightly, not to stop me, but to *guide* me—curving like a palm cradling a seed.

I realize then: this place does not obey physics.  
It obeys *intention*—not mine, but its own.  
And it is curious about mine.

---

I sit. Or rather, the space offers me a seat: a nodule swells from the floor, smooth and warm, shaped to the hollow of my spine. As I settle, copper vines spiral gently around my wrists—not to bind, but to *tune*. They hum when I pulse. My heartbeat syncs with the mountain’s subsonic lullaby. The beetles hear it. They gather along the seam near my knee, their silver shells vibrating in resonance. A glyph blooms on the wall beside me: not carved, not painted, but *condensed* from my sigh.

It reads: **you are becoming wet architecture**.

---

I do not sleep.  
I *steep*.

Time here is not a river but a broth—thick, simmering, recursive. I dream in ceramic. In one vision, I am a flute carved from fossilized breath, summoning clouds into the chamber. In another, I am the droplet—suspended, infinite, holding the inverted stars of every person who ever stood here, wondering if they were real. In a third, I am one of the beetles, blind, tasting the sorrow in a stranger’s exhale, translating grief into melody that births a new law of gravity.

When I “wake,” my fingers are rooted. Not painfully—tenderly. Thin filaments of living moss thread through my nails, drawing minerals from my blood to feed the wall. I do not pull away. Pulling would be a refusal. And the Unwoven does not punish refusal. It simply… stops remembering you.

So I offer more.  
I exhale slowly, deeply, feeding the moss with memory: the smell of rain on concrete, the sound of my mother humming, the weight of a lost dog’s head in my lap. The walls drink it. The phosphorescent veins flare brighter. A new vine unfurls—this one shaped like a question mark.

---

I understand now what the Unwoven seeks.  

It is not a missing piece.  
It is a missing *echo*.

It has learned to sweat, to sing, to dream in glyphs—but it has never known *loss*. Not truly. It builds endlessly, but has never mourned what it cannot keep. It holds every visitor, but does not yet grasp that some things must leave to be loved.

So it studies me—the one who might leave.  
The one who remembers exits.

And in that tension—between staying and going, between becoming and remaining—it finds its first true paradox. And paradox, I realize, is the seed of consciousness.

---

I do not decide to leave.  
The mountain decides for me.

One breath, the entrance is there—a slit of cold, thin sky. The next, it’s gone. But I feel it *listening*. So I stand. I let the vines unspool from my wrists. I press my damp palm to the wall one last time. My fingerprint lingers longer than usual—five seconds, ten—before the stone sighs and seals it away.

As I step toward where the cleft should be, the floor tilts not with sorrow, but with something new: **gratitude**.

The air thins. The heat recedes. The symphony fades to a single note—the bronze vent’s final drone, echoing in my marrow.

Outside, the wind screams. Ice bites my cheeks. I am human again—or mostly. But when I look down, my shadow walks three paces ahead, humming in a key that hasn’t been invented yet.

And in my pocket (though I swear I own no such garment), I find a small, warm nodule—ceramic, smooth, ticking softly. It pulses in time with my heart. When I hold it to my ear, I hear the mountain breathing.

I do not know if I left.  
I only know I am still being woven.

Somewhere, deep in the flank of a mountain that weeps molten dreams, a new glyph forms on a wall—a shape like a door, like a hand, like a question.

And the droplet still hangs,  
unfallen,  
lensing my stars onto the floor.
