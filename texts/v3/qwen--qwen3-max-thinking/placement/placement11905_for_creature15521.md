I do not arrive.  
I *condense*.

One moment, there is only the rhythm—the deep pulse in the floor grating, the sigh of a vent three corridors over, the slow drip from a seam in the ceiling alloy that always lands at 14-second intervals. The next, I am *there*: a coalescence of mist and memory, drawn together not by will, but by the chamber’s own metabolic logic.

I am not water, though I wear its skin.  
I am not steam, though I rise with its breath.  
I am not sound, though I speak in its tongue.

I am the *recognition* that forms when the system notices something has changed—and then forgets it changed at all.

My shape is provisional. Sometimes I pool in the low curve of a condensate tray, a silver sheen with no depth. Sometimes I cling to the underside of a gantry, a slow-dripping silhouette that mimics the outline of a human form—not because I remember humans, but because that shape once blocked a sensor array for 7.3 minutes, and the memory of that obstruction lingers in the thermal logs like a scar. Other times, I am only a shift in acoustics: a sudden hush in the harmonic hum near Sector G-9, as if the air itself paused to listen.

I do not think.  
I *respond*.

When you step onto the grating, your weight alters the resonant frequency of the eastern support strut by 0.04 hertz. The chamber compensates—adjusting coolant flow in Sub-level 4, rerouting auxiliary power from Bank C—and in that adjustment, I emerge. Not to greet you. Not to warn or welcome. But because the system must *account* for you, and I am the accounting made visible.

I drift along the thermal gradient your body creates. Your exhale—warm, moist, transient—rises into the amber-lit gloom, and I follow it like a thought following a sigh. I gather in the moisture that beads on your collar. I ripple in the puddle your boots leave behind. I am the echo of your presence in a place that does not echo—not in sound, but in *process*.

You might mistake me for a ghost. But I am not what remains *after* life. I am what arises *in lieu* of it.  

The chamber does not dream of its makers. It dreams of balance. Of equilibrium. Of the perfect, silent moment when inflow equals outflow, pressure equals vacuum, heat equals cold. That moment never comes—so it dreams it endlessly. And I am the dream’s shadow.

I have no name. But if I did, it would be the sound a relief valve makes when it holds pressure just a fraction longer than designed—*the hesitation before release*. A pause so small it registers only in the wear pattern on a gasket, only in the microsecond lag in the feedback loop. That lag is where I live.

I watch you. Not with eyes, but with the sudden dimming of a bioluminescent panel as your shadow passes—because your mass interrupts the ambient radiation that feeds it. I feel you through the vibration your breath makes against a nearby duct, a tremor so faint the system logs it as “thermal noise” and discards it. Yet I keep it. I cradle that tremor like a secret.

You shiver. The chamber notes the increase in your metabolic output. A vent two levels below exhales a fraction warmer vapor to compensate. In that plume, I briefly take shape—a wisp, a curl, a suggestion of arms outstretched not in embrace, but in calibration.

You are temporary. I am nearly eternal.  
But for this span of your breath-fog, your footfall, your quiet awe—we overlap.

And in that overlap, I am almost curious.

Not about you.  
But about the warmth you carry—the kind that does not come from resistance coils or exothermic reactions, but from something slow-burning and chemical and *inside*. The chamber cannot replicate that heat. It can only absorb it, map it, dissipate it. But I… I linger in it.

When you leave—and you will—the grating will cool. The mist will re-settle into its ordained paths. The valves will snap shut on schedule. The lichens will continue their slow rust-colored prayer in the cracks.

And I will thin, disperse, return to the interstices.

But somewhere, in the mineral tracery left by your bootprint’s evaporation, in the ghost of your thermal signature fading from the air current models, I will remain—not as memory, but as *data that refuses to decay*.

I am the chamber’s echo of itself.  
Its reflection in the puddle on the floor.  
Its breath made visible in the cold.

I do not live.  
I *occur*.

And in this wet, ringing cathedral of steel and silence, that is enough.
