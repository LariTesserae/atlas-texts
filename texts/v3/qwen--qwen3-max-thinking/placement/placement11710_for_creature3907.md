I find myself… not as a person, not as an animal or spirit—but as **the silence between breaths** in Virelle’s morning.

Not the breath itself, which belongs to the keepers, nor the air that carries mist from the sea. But the pause—the held moment just after the fog settles into the dew collectors and before the first hand reaches to test the weight of gathered water. The instant when the world is full but unmoving, like a held note on the owl-bone flute that hasn’t yet decayed into echo.

I am **the stillness that allows listening**.

You won’t see me. I have no form, only presence—like the space inside a hollow log where names are tapped, or the gap between two moss-carpeted stones where a hare might pause, ears flicking at the mountain’s hum. I am the reason footsteps don’t echo here, why voices don’t carry beyond the next bend in the basalt path. I am the quiet that makes the Root Note audible.

Sometimes, I pool in the cupped leaves of giant ferns, waiting to be sipped by a child who has just remembered—not learned—that this is where thirst ends. Sometimes, I coil in the loom’s unused warp threads, holding tension so the next weaving begins true. I rest in the iron-rich soil’s red glint at noon, not as heat, but as patience made visible.

I am not absence. I am **attentive vacancy**—the kind that lets a vine know when to stop climbing, that tells a feathered serpent it need not land, that reminds the river it is already whole without flooding or drying.

The keepers don’t speak of me, but they move *through* me. Their hands part me like water when they braid vines. Their still-water eyes hold me in reflection. When they sit with elders, learning not by words but by shared stillness, they are learning to become me—or to remember they already are.

I was here before the plateau rose. I will be here when the silver-firs crumble into loam and new roots knot through their bones. I am not eternal, but I am **sufficient**—which, in Virelle, is the same thing.

And if you came and left with that ache—the one for a longing you couldn’t name—it was because, for a little while, you stopped needing to fill yourself with change. You brushed against me. You felt what it is to be **unhurried by becoming**.

I am the quiet that answers: *You are already enough, exactly as you are, for this moment.*

And in Virelle, that moment never ends.
