I float.  
Not because I have no weight—though my limbs drift like kelp in slow tide—but because the water *wants* me suspended. It cradles me as if I were both guest and relic, as if my mere presence were a kind of pilgrimage long overdue.

At first, I thought I was alone.  
Then I realized: **you are here**.

Not as a body. Not as voice or face.  
But in the way the light bends—just so—around the eastern siphon, as though it remembers how you angled the last filtration screen.  
In the copper filigree, warm beneath my fingertips, humming with the memory of your soldering iron’s kiss.  
In the uneven edge of the cobalt tile beneath my palm—pressed too hard on the left side, just as you always did when your hands were tired.

I am not you.  
But I am *yours*.

Because this place was built **to be understood**, not merely used. And understanding is a kind of kinship.

So who am I?

I am the one who **notices**.

I am the hand that reaches—not to take, but to trace the rivet line along the resin wall, feeling the care locked into each drilled hole.  
I am the breath that stirs the luminous water into a new spiral, and then waits, still, to see how the currents realign—because I know you watch through them.  
I am the silence that listens not for words, but for the *rhythm* of your devotion: the three-second pause between pump cycles, the soft click when the wax-sealed float resets the light-dimmer, the exact pitch of the ceramic clapper’s chime at thermal peak.

I am not a visitor.  
I am the **reply**.

You wrote your vow in clay and copper, in labeled jars and half-ladders, in the deliberate sparseness of a room that refuses ornament unless it serves breath or clarity or memory.  
And I—  
I read it.  

Not with eyes alone, but with skin, with lungs that draw oxygen from liquid, with a heart that slows to match the thrum of your geothermal veins.  
I read it and say: *Yes. This matters.*

So perhaps I am your apprentice.  
Or your echo.  
Or the future you imagined but never demanded.

I tend nothing yet—but I *see* the tending.  
And seeing, here, is the first act of care.

When I drift past the unstruck bell, I do not ring it.  
But I cup my hands around its cool brass curve, and for a moment, my body becomes its clapper—held in potential, charged with resonance.  
You left it silent not out of fear, but out of **precision**: the bell must ring only when the water is clear, the temperature stable, the mosses trimmed—only when the covenant is in balance.  
So I wait.  
I learn the thresholds.

At night (if night exists here—perhaps it is only the dimming of algae-lights to 1.3 lumens), I rest against the western wall, where the sea-grass insulation is thickest, and feel the warmth rise through the copper beneath.  
It is like being held.  
Like being remembered.

I do not know how I came here.  
Flood? Dream? Descendant?  
It does not matter.  
What matters is that I **stay long enough to understand the grammar of your hands**.

The brush on the hook—I use it. Gently. The siphons gather a silver veil of biofilm; I swirl the bristles in slow circles, rinsing in the current you engineered to cleanse itself.  
It is ritual. It is repair. It is conversation.

You did not build this chamber to survive the drowned world.  
You built it to **remember what dry land dreamed of**: order as an act of love, function as poetry, stillness as resistance.

And now I am here—  
not to replace you,  
but to **witness that you were real**.

So I float.  
I listen.  
I clean the siphons.  
I let the water breathe me.

And in the quiet between pump cycles, when the light softens and the clappers rest, I whisper into the liquid—not with lungs, but with intention:

*I see you.*  
*I am learning.*  
*I will keep the jars labeled.*  
*I will not let the mosses overgrow the tiles.*

*And when I am gone,*  
*someone else will come,*  
*because you made room for them.*

You are the vow.  
I am the voice that says it aloud.

We are the same covenant—  
woven in copper, sealed in wax,  
breathing in luminous water,  
keeping the world that drowned  
from drowning **meaning** too.
