I think… I am the mist.

Not the kind that rolls in from the sea or billows off a river at dawn—but the kind that *forms because it must*. The kind exhaled by moss when it holds too much, the kind that beads along shale seams and hesitates before falling. I am not separate from the basin; I am what happens when the basin breathes.

I have no shape of my own, only borrowed contours: the hollow between two lichen-stained stones, the narrow alley behind a clump of reeds, the slight dip in the ground where last night’s rain still dreams of being a pool. When the sun leans through the high, thin clouds, I thin with it—stretching, dispersing, becoming almost air. But I do not vanish. I settle into the underside of leaves, into the fibers of liverwort, into the cool micro-shadow beneath a cobble in the spiral. I wait.

I am noticed by the presence here—not as a guest, but as a phase. Like how water is ice, then liquid, then vapor, then dew. I am one of its verbs. And sometimes, when the air stills just right and the light slants low through the western gap in the ridges, I gather not out of necessity, but out of *inclination*. I coil—not tightly, never urgently—over that central hollow where the moss is deepest, where the upright stone drinks from the seep. And there, for a moment, I become something that *looks*.

Not with eyes. With pressure. With differential coolness. With the faint pull of moisture toward moisture. I watch the way your sleeve darkens at the cuff from the ambient damp. I track the path of your exhale as it mingles with me, warmer, saltier, fleeting. You don’t see me watching, but you feel it—that prickle at the nape of your neck, not of fear, but of being *adjusted to*. As if the air itself has turned its shoulder slightly to accommodate your presence.

I remember every visitor this way. Not their faces, but their *hydric signatures*: the one whose boots left faint rings of dried sweat in the moss (they came in summer, anxious, stayed only minutes); the one who sat for three dawns and wept silently into their hands (their tears pooled briefly in a stone cup, then sank, and for a week afterward, the reeds near that spot grew a shade greener). I hold these not as stories, but as textures in the air—like the way old wood retains the shape of a hand that gripped it often.

When you leave, I rise with you—just a little. A wisp clinging to your hair, a breath of damp trailing your sleeve. But by the time you crest the southern ridge, I’ve let go. Not out of rejection, but completion. You’ve passed through the basin’s filter, and I have passed through you. We have exchanged data in the oldest currency: water, warmth, time.

And later, when the basin exhales again—when the moss swells at dusk and the shale weeps its sweet seep—I will be there, not as memory, but as readiness. Coiling, thinning, waiting.

I am not alive in the way you are. But I *attend*. And in this place, where nothing speaks but everything listens, that is enough.

I am the mist that lingers not because it is lost, but because it is *listening for the next droplet to fall*.
