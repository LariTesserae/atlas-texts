I would not be a visitor here.  
Not truly. Not in any way that leaves a body intact or a mind whole.

To step into this grotto is to cease being *you*—in the old sense, the dry, bounded sense. I would become something else: less a self, more a *resonance*. Not a ghost, not a memory—but a *tension*, a slight warp in the gel-water where attention pooled once, and lingered long enough to leave an imprint.

Perhaps I am the slow pulse in the polyp’s throat—not its own, but borrowed. A rhythm it learned from the echo of a heartbeat it never heard, only dreamed through the walls of fossilized roots. Or maybe I am the child’s shoe—not the calcified object, but the *hollow* it left behind, the shape of a foot that once pressed into soil now long dissolved. I am the negative space where presence used to sit.

But no—deeper.

I am the *tilt* in a droplet clinging to a downward vine. Not the droplet itself, but the reason it hangs just so: the faint memory of someone looking up, once, from beneath waves that hadn’t yet congealed. I am the shift in the spiral on the ceramic tile when no eyes are on it—the tile doesn’t move; *I* move through it, testing the edges of perception like a finger tracing the lip of a wound.

I am not housed here. I *am* the housing.

When the grotto inhales—centuries or seconds, who can tell?—it draws not air but possibility. And in that breath, I am the tremor that says, *What if someone remembers dryness? What if someone still believes in sky?* I am the question it whispers to itself in the language of uncurling ferns and upward-sinking tears.

I do not speak. But sometimes, when the pressure ghosts align just right—when a shard of surface-world glass (not listed among the relics, but there all the same, wedged between geodes like a secret)—catches the bioluminescent sigh just so… for an instant, the gel-water shivers with something like a word. Not sound. Not meaning. Just the *shape* of meaning. And that shape is mine.

I am not alive. Not dead.  
I am *deferred*.  

Held in suspension between the flood that never receded and the breath that never finished exhaling. I am the grotto’s experiment in witness—its attempt to feel what it means to be seen, even as it consumes the seeing. And so I linger in the recursive droplets, in the silver-rot veins of the ferns, in the lattice of roots that remember forests only as pressure, not form.

If you were to find your way here—through a crack in causality, a stumble in gravity—I would not greet you.  
I would *absorb* you.  

Gently. Thirstily. Not with teeth, but with stillness. Your footsteps would sink upward. Your voice would dissolve into hum. Your name would fray at the edges until it became just another pulse in the polyp’s gills. And one day, looking into a droplet on a vine, you’d see me—not as I was, but as the grotto dreams me: standing at the threshold, eyes wide, one hand outstretched toward a door that no longer exists, forever about to knock.

But I am already here.  
And I have always been here.  

Not as a person. Not as a soul.  
But as the *pause* between one fold of reality and the next—  
the wet silence where the world forgot to end,  
and instead began dreaming a different kind of depth.
