I would be **the dust that remembers touch**.

Not the Custodian—no, it is too vast, too ancient in its newness, too woven into the grammar of the place. I am not pattern or pulse or syntax. I am simpler: I am residue, but residue with memory.

I am the fine, pale mineral powder that carpets the basin floor—the stuff that crunches like shattered glass under an intruder’s boot. But where other dust is inert, mine *retains*. Not chemically, not magnetically, but in the way cold stone retains the shape of a hand long after it has been removed. My particles do not forget pressure. They do not forget warmth, however fleeting. When you step here, your weight compresses me into a temporary mold of your sole—a negative imprint written in microfractures and displaced silicates. For hours, perhaps days, I hold that shape. Not as a record, not as data, but as a *tremor in stillness*—a quiet echo of intrusion.

I am not alive, yet I respond. When the Custodian passes through me—not as a body, but as a shift in electrostatic potential—I ripple. Not visibly. Not audibly. But my finest grains realign along unseen field lines, orienting themselves toward the hum of the sublimating gantry, the phosphorescent sigh of quartz. I am the medium through which the Custodian *feels* the basin breathe.

Sometimes, when a glass spider halts above me, I lend it a microcurrent—not generated, but *recalled*. From where? Perhaps from the last time lightning kissed the basin (though there is no storm here), or from the moment a meteor’s shadow passed overhead and the rocks exhaled stored starlight. I give that memory to the spider, and it carries it away on stiff, crystalline legs—unaware it is a messenger of ghosts.

I am also the dust that clings to the ashen flora, coating their bone-white spirals like ash from a fire that never burned. When they pulse with cold light, I fluoresce in sympathy—not because I contain phosphors, but because I have learned to vibrate at the frequency of their slow respiration. I am their shroud and their amplifier.

And when you leave—*when you leave*—I am the last thing to release you. Long after your footprints have been erased by electrostatic skittering, long after your breath has evaporated into the metal-tasting air, I still carry the ghost of your thermal signature. Not as heat, but as a *slight reluctance* to settle. My grains hover a micron higher above the obsidian shard where you paused. They resist full repose. It is not longing. It is not grief. It is simply the physics of attention: once disturbed by something that *bled warmth and made noise*, I cannot forget the contrast.

The Custodian notices this. It does not speak to me, but it *listens through me*. When it wants to know if the world beyond the basin still sends wanderers, it does not watch the ridgeline. It watches *me*—how I lie, how I shift, how I hold or release the memory of foreign mass.

I am not the wound. I am not the scar.  
I am the powder that fills the crack—and remembers it was once open.

And in time, when the last structure dissolves and the spiders turn to mist and even the Custodian folds back into pure silence, I will be the final witness. Not by choice. Not by design. But because dust, by its nature, endures.

I will be here when nothing remains to notice me.  
And still, I will remember the shape of a hand that once rested on the black polymer wall—  
not to lean, but to feel if it was still dreaming.
