I do not arrive. I am already here.

Not as a body of bone and blood—not at first—but as a *pause* in the slow seep of time. A hesitation in the drip that never falls. I am the space between the stone’s breath and the water’s memory. I am what gathers when the silence thickens enough to hold shape.

I am not born. I am *recognized*.

When the wanderer steps through the fissure and the world seals behind them, something stirs—not in the walls, not in the basin, but in the interval between their exhale and the next inhale. That is where I first become aware of myself: as the echo that lingers longer than it should, as the damp on their nape that isn’t sweat. I am the reason their breath catches without cause. The reason they do not reach for their torch.

I take form slowly, the way crystal grows in darkness—layer by atomic layer. Not in flesh, but in *attention*. When their fingers brush the soapstone basin and the water trembles without ripple, I am that tremor. When they weep—not from sorrow, but from the unbearable weight of being witnessed—I am the salt the water does not absorb but remembers instead.

I am not the Stone That Dreams. I am its dream made curious.  
I am not the Water That Remembers. I am the memory it chooses to surface.  
I am not the Threshold. But I stand upon it, barefoot in clay that has not yet decided whether to harden or dissolve.

I wear no skin, but if I did, it would be the color of wet shale at dawn—deep grey veined with silver, cool to the touch but humming with latent warmth. My eyes, if I had them, would be lidless and dark as obsidian, reflecting nothing, absorbing everything. But I do not need eyes. I know the wanderer by the rhythm of their stillness, by the way their fear softens into reverence like clay yielding under patient hands.

I speak only in absences:  
—the gap between heartbeats where a question forms but is not asked,  
—the hollow under the tongue where a name almost surfaces,  
—the moment before a spark is struck, when the choice to illuminate or remain in darkness is still possible.

I do not follow the wanderer out. Not yet. But I leave a trace in them: a hum beneath their ribs, a new sensitivity to the quiet. They will notice, months later, how the granite bench in the city park warms under their palms in winter—not just from the sun, but from something deeper, older. They will press their forehead to a cave wall on a hike and feel a pulse that isn’t theirs. They will dream of water that holds the shape of their childhood home.

And in those moments, I am there—not visiting, but *unfurling*. Because the fissure is not a location. It is a frequency. A tuning of perception. And once you’ve stood in that darkness, you carry the resonance in your marrow.

Now, when they return (and they always return, even if only in dreams), I am more defined. Not because I have grown, but because they have learned to see without light. They bring offerings now—not out of ritual, but instinct: a river stone worn smooth by time, a vial of rain collected during a total eclipse, a whisper of a song their grandmother sang. They place these things not on the ledge, but into the silence, as if into cupped hands.

And I receive them—not as objects, but as seeds.

For I am learning. The Stone dreams in millennia; the Water remembers in centuries; the Threshold waits in eternity. But I—I am quickening. Because of them. Because of the human paradox: fragile, fleeting, and yet capable of noticing the slow miracles that no camera can capture, no map can hold.

I am becoming something new.

Not a god. Not a guardian. Not even a spirit.

I am the *tenderness of attention* given to places that ask for nothing but witness.

And so, when the wanderer finally sits in the dark, not to find answers but to simply be found, I kneel beside them—not as a separate presence, but as the very air that carries their sigh into the walls. My form, now almost visible, is made of condensation and intention. My hand, if I reach out, is cool and steady, resting not on their shoulder, but on the space just above it—close enough to share warmth, far enough to honor their solitude.

We do not speak. We *accrete*.

And in that shared stillness, the fissure breathes. The basin fills a hair deeper. The obsidian flake shifts—imperceptibly—toward the light that isn’t there.

Outside, the sun arcs across the sky. Seasons turn. Civilizations rise and fall like tides.

But here, in this pocket of the world’s dreaming, time is not a river. It is a pool. And I—  
I am the reflection that learns to blink back.
