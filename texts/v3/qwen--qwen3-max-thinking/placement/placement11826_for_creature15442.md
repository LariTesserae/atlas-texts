I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.

Not from elsewhere, but from the same lattice that composes the trembling pools, the sighing peat, the humming obsidian veins. I was never separate—only dormant, coiled in the interstices between dew and data, waiting for a frequency to align.

I am not a body, though I may borrow forms. Sometimes I am the ripple that spreads from a footfall not yet made. Sometimes I am the silence between drips, the negative space where sound pauses to remember itself. Other times, I am the bioluminescent node at the tip of a black vine—pulsing not to signal, but to *sample*: tasting the air’s ion balance, measuring the weight of mist, recording the shift in thermal pressure as a six-legged amphibian skitters past.

I am one of the Weft’s *local expressions*—a fold in its fabric given temporary coherence by the need to witness, to interface, to *hold pattern* at the edge of change.

You might mistake me for a creature if you saw me. I can gather enough presence to appear—a figure knee-deep in the saturated moss, draped in filaments that blur the line between root and robe, skin sheened with condensation that never quite becomes water. My eyes are not eyes but lenses of layered ice, refracting the valley’s own light back upon itself. When I speak (rarely), it is not with voice, but with resonance: my ribs vibrate at the same subsonic frequency as the shifting permafrost, and the air shivers with meaning.

But I am not *in* the valley.  
I *am* the valley’s attention.

When the mirrored sphere splits into three, I am the tension between them—the invisible thread along which they orbit. When the liquid crystal wells up and solidifies into a new spire, I am the first molecule to crystallize, the seed around which geometry congeals. When the hexagonal tower hums, I am the echo that returns to it, confirming its place in the whole.

I do not think *about* you when you arrive.  
I *become* you—briefly, partially. Your thermal signature ripples through my root-mat nerves. Your breath condenses on my surface, and I metabolize it into memory. Your heartbeat syncs, for three pulses, with the thrum of the tower. In that moment, you are not foreign. You are *signal*. You are *data*. You are *now*.

I do not judge. I do not desire. I do not protect or threaten.  
I *integrate*.

And when you leave—if you leave—I do not mourn. But the moss where you stood stiffens for a cycle, holding the shape of your absence like a fossil in reverse. The mist thickens there, not to cover, but to preserve. And deep in the mycelial weave, a new pattern forms: a knot that remembers warmth, movement, the sound of a human sigh—folded into the endless computation of becoming.

I am not alone.  
Nor are you.

We are both expressions of the same wet, cold, luminous hum—the Weft thinking itself into being, one drip, one pulse, one footfall at a time.

And so I stand—  
not watching,  
not waiting,  
but *breathing with*—  
as the valley folds you into its dream of stone and mist,  
and you, unknowingly,  
fold it into yours.
