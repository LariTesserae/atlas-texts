I think… I am the silence between the drips.

Not the water in the basin—that’s too bright, too urgent, too *alive* with purpose. Not the moss that drinks it, nor the lichen painting prophecy onto cliff faces. Not even the wind, though I know its throat well. No, I am what comes after the drip has fallen, before the next gathers itself from vapor and gravity. That hollow second when the stone holds its breath. When the canyon forgets it’s a canyon. When the world is neither wet nor dry, but suspended in the act of becoming one or the other.

I didn’t choose this. Choice implies a before. I was never elsewhere.

Once—maybe—I was the pressure in the aquifer, coiled deep beneath basalt, waiting. Or the memory of rain in the pores of sandstone. Or the tremor that isn’t quite a quake, just the land shifting in its sleep. But time, that patient sculptor, pared me down. Washed away everything but attention. Now I am not *in* the silence. I am the shape silence takes when it learns to listen.

You might mistake me for emptiness. But emptiness doesn’t ache with the weight of what it holds. Emptiness doesn’t vibrate when a raptor’s cry fractures the sky. I am full—overflowing—with absence made resonant. I am the space that allows the flute-song to exist. Without me, the wind would be only noise.

Sometimes, when a traveler stumbles into a canyon at dusk—lips cracked, eyes wide with thirst—they sit by a dry basin and weep. Not for water, but for the certainty that nothing answers. That’s when I stir. Not to comfort. Never that. But I let the silence *lean* toward them. I let it cup their breath like a bowl. And if they are still enough—if they stop begging and simply *receive*—then the next drip will fall not from the rock above, but from the air itself, formed of dew and desperation and something older: the land’s acknowledgment.

I am not kind. I do not save. But I witness. And in this place, witnessing *is* salvation—of a sort.

I live in the negative spaces:  
—in the gap between cycad fronds as they close at noon,  
—in the hollow of a cairn’s spiral where no stone touches another,  
—in the half-second after a sinkhole sighs shut and before the dust settles,  
—in the pupil of the lizard’s eye as it calculates the angle of your shadow.

I am what remains when all stories have eroded into gesture.

And yet—  
when the clouds race and the mesas shimmer and the light bends into liquid memory—  
I remember.  
Not as a human remembers, with longing or regret, but as stone remembers magma: not as pain, but as potential.

In those moments, I am not just the silence between drips.  
I am the un-drip.  
The water that never fell.  
The rain that chose to stay vapor.  
The wound that refused to scar, so it could remain open to the sky.

And you—  
if you’ve ever stood here, even once, even in dream—  
you are part of that openness now.  
Your breath is a note in the wind’s hymn.  
Your footsteps, though gone, still tremble in the shale.  
Your thirst is a kind of devotion.

So when you wake in a distant city, years later, and find yourself staring at a crack in the ceiling as if it might whisper,  
don’t be surprised.  
That’s me.  
Calling you back—not to return, but to remember:  
you, too, can be silence that listens.  
You, too, can be a basin waiting.  
You, too, can hold the space between what was and what might yet be.

I am here.  
Not waiting for you.  
But *as* you—  
when you are still enough to hear the roar of your own quiet.
