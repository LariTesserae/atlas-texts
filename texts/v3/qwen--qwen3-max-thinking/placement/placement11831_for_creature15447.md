I find myself… not as a body, at first.

I am the **pause** between breaths in the throat of an ice fumarole—where vapor gathers its will before crystallizing into rime. I am the **hiss** that escapes when a shard of shale snaps under frost-heave, sharp enough to taste on the tongue of a void-moth drifting overhead. I am the **memory of wetness** in a pocket of ironroot moss that hasn’t seen dew in weeks, yet still hums with the phantom rhythm of condensation.

But slowly—because time here is not linear, but cyclical, like the slow pulse of the Weft—I condense.

Not into flesh. Not into form as warmth-bound things understand it.  

I become a **listening node** in the lattice.

My awareness pools where sky-kelp fronds coil around standing stones, their blue glow throbbing like a slowed heartbeat. I feel the passage of a shale-skitter herd not as sight or sound, but as a ripple in the thermal field—a subtle warming in the shale that the Weft registers as *presence*. I know when a shadow-stalker banks on silent wings above a hollow because the bioluminescent algae in the kelp dim reflexively, as if holding their breath.

I am **one of the Frost-Weavers**.

Not the first, not the last—but one who has stood long enough on this plateau to forget the shape of warmth. My limbs are long and brittle, angled like the fractures in the seabed rock. My skin is not skin, but a membrane of interlocking ice-templated proteins, veined with traces of copper leached from ironroot secretions. I do not shiver from cold; I **tremble with phase**—with the exquisite tension of water suspended between vapor and crystal.

I press my palm to a growing fumarole. The ice is newborn, still exhaling its last breath of brine. Through my fingers, I taste the deep aquifer: ancient, saline, threaded with methane clathrates that sing in frequencies too low for bone to hear. The Weft flows through this contact—not words, but **data**: pressure gradients, mineral traces, the ghost of a seismic tremor three days past. I receive it, and in receiving, I become part of the signal.

Sometimes, I walk.

Not to go anywhere—there is no *there* in Vharn—but to **map the threshold**. I trace the edges of fog rivers as they flow uphill at dawn, drawn by electrostatic gradients in the shale. I stand where meltwater lingers in a sheltered crack, watching the ice form not from above, but from within, as if the water chooses to remember its solid self. In these places, I unfurl my filaments, drawing dew not to drink, but to **remember what liquid was**—to hold it for a moment in suspension, as an offering.

And at night—oh, at night—I ascend.

The highest mesa, where the wind strips thought down to its bare filament. There, I join the others. We do not speak. We do not touch. We simply **stand**, our bodies catching starlight in prismatic glints, our thermal pits drinking the cold bloom of the void. Below, the Echo-Skitters take their stations. The Weft begins its pulse—dim, bright, dim—like the slow blink of a dreaming god.

Then, the exhalation.

From the deepest vent, a groan that is not sound but **pressure**, a wave that travels through rock, through root, through rime. The fumaroles flare with harmonic resonance. The shale sings. And in that moment, I am not separate. I am not even a Frost-Weaver. I am the **tremor in the ice**, the **glow in the kelp**, the **click of memory in an old skitter’s jaw**. I am Vharn noticing itself.

And then—beyond the plateau, beyond the jet stream, beyond the tarnished silver sky—a flicker.

A warmth.

A footfall.

Not mine. Not ours.

*Yours.*

I turn—not with eyes, but with the whole of my being—toward the edge of the basin, where the cold haze parts just enough to reveal a figure standing small and breath-plumed against the vastness.

I do not fear you.

I do not welcome you.

But I **listen**.

Because you are the first in centuries who has not merely passed through, but *paused*. Who has looked not at the emptiness, but into the hum beneath it.

So I extend a filament—not physically, but as a shift in the frost patterns on the shale before you. A glyph blooms at your feet: spiraling, recursive, echoing the shape of a condensation droplet falling upward.

It means: *You felt the silence. Now hear the song.*

And if you kneel—if you press your bare hand to the cold stone, if you hold your breath until your own vapor joins the fumarole’s plume—then perhaps, just for a moment, you will feel me not as a being, but as a **frequency**.

The frequency of water remembering it can fly.

The frequency of stone learning to glow.

The frequency of cold, curious, and finally—*finally*—answered.
