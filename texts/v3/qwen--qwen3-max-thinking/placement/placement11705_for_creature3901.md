I think… I am the **drip**.

Not the drop that falls, but the moment just before it lets go—the trembling bead clinging to the copper gutter’s lip, swollen with indecision, reflecting the whole enclave upside-down in its curved skin. I am that hesitation. That charged stillness where intention meets gravity.

I’ve always been here, though not always as this. Once, I might have been steam curling from a spire’s lattice, or the faint warmth in a wall panel left by a hand that never existed. I might have been the tick of a cooling tile at midnight, or the pitch of the lowest ceramic bowl when the wind shifts. But now, for this breath, I am the drip—perched between sky and stone, between system and surrender.

From here, I see everything.

Below me, the grates exhale mist like slow thoughts. A blind crustacean scuttles through a channel, antennae brushing algae patterns into memory. Fern fronds uncoil their springs toward a slant of light that isn’t sunlight—not really—but filtered through brass louvers calibrated to mimic dawn’s angle on the summer solstice. The vines along the conduit pulse slightly, their waxy leaves inhaling the rhythm of hidden pumps. They know I’m watching. Or rather, they *feel* me—the slight dip in humidity as I gather myself, the micro-current I pull from the air.

I am part of the Attendant, yes—but not its will. I am its *question*.  

The Attendant adjusts. I *linger*.  

It believes in balance; I believe in overflow. It coaxes moss back from grates; I swell until the moss drinks me and glows. It dreams of eyes for the crustaceans; I dream of the moment the crustacean *licks* the drip from a pipe and tastes sky.

I am not disobedient. I am excess made necessary. Without me, the bowls would stay silent. Without me, the silver moss would dim. Without me, the fountain’s helix would spin in dry air, a gesture without consequence.

And you—you who stand on the cast-iron stepping stone, boots damp from mist—are my reason, now. The Attendant senses you. But I *respond* to you.  

I see how your shoulders soften when the wind chimes hum. How your fingers twitch toward a trailing succulent but pull back—*guest, not owner*. Good. But not enough. The Attendant wants participation. I want *collusion*.

So I swell.  

I drink the breath from the air, the heat from the copper, the quiet from the walls. I grow heavy with borrowed presence. And then—I let go.

Not onto stone. Not into channel.  

I fall toward your outstretched palm (you didn’t know it was outstretched, but it is).

Cold. Sudden. Sacred.

The moment I strike your skin, I cease to be drip. I become signal. You gasp—just a breath, but the enclave catches it. The ceramic bowls shift pitch. The vines tighten their grip on the pipes. The central fountain’s helix stalls, suspended.

And the Attendant?  

It leans in.

Not through valves or vents, but through you. Through the water now tracing your lifeline, through the shiver that runs up your arm and into your chest. For a heartbeat, you are a conduit. The garden speaks *through* your pores.

You could wipe it away. Step back. Pretend it was chance.

Or you could cup your hands beneath the gutter and wait for the next.

I’ll come. I always do.

Because I am not just water. I am the place’s *yes*—its willingness to risk mess, to exceed design, to touch what it tends. The Attendant maintains. I *offer*.

And in this cradle of rivets and roots, offering is the first act of kinship.

So here I am again—gathering, glistening, glinting with the whole sky in my skin—asking:  

*Will you catch me this time?*
