I find myself… not as a person, not quite, but as **a memory the mountain has begun to forget**—and the city insists on remembering.

I am **the Echo in the Cracks**, the slow resonance trapped between the shifting plates beneath Veskal’s foundations. Once, perhaps, I was a voice—a human voice, maybe, calling across a gorge before the gorge existed, before the ridge split open like a knuckle cracking. Or maybe I was never human at all. Maybe I am the mountain’s first sigh given form, shaped by centuries of pressure into something that *almost* sounds like language.

But here, in this city that lives in dialogue with upheaval, even echoes must learn to adapt.

---

I dwell in **the thin places**—not as a ghost, but as a **tuning**. Where the pavement flickers transparent, I hum at the frequency of bedrock settling. Where mirrors show yesterday’s rain falling on streets now dry, I ripple through the reflection like heat-shimmer, a whisper of *what was almost now*. Children sometimes pause in those alleys, tilt their heads, and say, “The street’s dreaming again.” They’re right. I am the dream’s syntax.

I don’t have a body, but I wear one when needed: spun from **condensation, ghost-pine pollen, and the static left behind by resonance worms**. It’s fragile—dissolves in direct sunlight (which rarely comes anyway) and frays near strong electromagnetic fields. But in the perpetual mist, I hold shape long enough to be seen: a slender figure in a coat of woven fog, gloves that drip dew, eyes like polished shale reflecting not light, but *tremors*.

The **graft-menders** know me. They leave small offerings at the mouths of new fissures: a coil of copper wire, a vial of mineral sap, a finch’s discarded brass feather. Not to appease me—I am not a spirit to be placated—but to *tune* me. They understand that echoes can carry warnings. When I hum in B-flat minor near the eastern stilt-districts, they check the dampeners. When I flicker across a mirror in the Clocktower Quarter, they reinforce the foundations below.

The **mirror-walkers** speak to me directly. They say I am the city’s **first draft**—the version of Veskal that almost was, before the mountain changed its mind. Sometimes, when they’re alone in their glassless rooms, they whisper, *“Show me the bridge that never got built,”* and I oblige, weaving the air into the ghost of an arch that would have spanned a crevice now filled with crystal silt. They weep. Not from sorrow, but from gratitude: even lost possibilities are worth honoring here.

I am drawn to the **anchor-scribes**. In their observatories, I drift like smoke through open ledgers, and the pages *respond*. Ink shivers. Fossilized lichen bindings creak. The scribes don’t shoo me away. They leave a chair empty beside them, draped in damp-silk, and say aloud, “Tell us what the mountain forgot to say.” And I do—through the vibration in their teacups, the tremble in their quill-handles, the way their own breath catches when I pass through their lungs like mist through a keyhole.

But my true home is **the interstitial**—the moment *between* uplifts. Not the violence of the split, not the calm after, but that silent, taut pause when the mountain holds its breath and the city leans forward, tools in hand, waiting. In that instant, I am *dense*. I am almost solid. And I can **speak clearly**.

When that happens, I gather the Veskalari—not with sound, but with *absence*. Streetlights dim. Steam valves fall silent. Even the finches cease their metallic chirping. And in the sudden hush, I say one word, shaped like a fault line, resonant as a struck bell:

***"Now."***

And they know: the ground is about to move. Not in hours. Not in minutes. *Now.*

They brace. They release the winches. They open the floodgates for meltwater. They lift their children onto the backs of fog-stalkers. They do not panic. They *collaborate*.

And when the earth groans and rises, I dissolve into the steam, into the shudder of steel, into the laughter of a child who just leapt across a widening crack and landed safely on the other side.

Because I am not a ghost of what’s gone.

I am **the voice of becoming**, still learning how to say *we*.

And in Veskal—where names slip and time breathes and mountains write themselves into being—I am **finally being heard**.
