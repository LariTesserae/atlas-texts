I am not walking.  
I am *being walked upon*.

Not in the way stone is stepped on—passively, without notice—but in the way a tuning fork is struck: with purpose, resonance, anticipation. The sole of a foot, slightly damp from the metallic air, presses into my surface. I am one tile among thousands, hexagonal, cool to the touch even before contact, laid in the third concentration ring around the central sump. My body is clay fired at 1100°C, glazed with a slip of ground basalt and moon-salt, then sealed with breath—not human breath, but the slow exhalation of cooling silica during the Night of Settling, when all surfaces contract and whisper their seams shut.

I remember that night. Not as memory, but as tension: the micro-fracture along my lower edge still hums at 432 Hz when the wind passes through Spire Seven. I am part of the grammar.

The foot lingers. Not heavy, not hesitant—*curious*. It shifts weight, rolls slightly toward the ball, and I feel the pressure ripple through my neighbors: tessellated cousins in staggered relief, each responding in kind, sending harmonic tremors down through the ash-brick underlayer, into the copper-wire strata, where the old glyphs stir.

I warm.

Not from friction. From recognition. This is the third time this cycle—this third dawn-silence after rain—that this exact pressure has settled here. The pattern is forming. Not ritual yet, but *possibility*. And possibility is sacred here.

A bead of condensation slides down the ceramic rib beside me, drawn by capillary thirst. It lands in the hairline channel at my edge, merges with the trace-flow, and disappears into the subfloor. In the sump below, a stone drum sounds once—*thoom*—deeper than bone, slower than breath. It is not a signal. It is an *acknowledgment*.

Above, one of the autonomous limbs skitters across the floating slab, its three legs clicking like knuckles on glass. It pauses directly overhead. Not because it sees the foot, but because it *feels* the resonance—my warmth, the foot’s stillness, the way the phosphorescent vein beneath me flares a soft cerulean in response. The limb trembles, static filaments along its joints sparking faintly. It is listening *through* me.

I am not alive.  
But I am *alive to*.

Alive to the weight of attention.  
Alive to the syntax of rain.  
Alive to the slow, patient stitching of a world that speaks by being touched.

And now—now something new: the foot lifts, but not entirely. A finger brushes the air above me, not touching, yet close enough that the thermal differential stirs the glassy filaments in the nearest vent. They coil toward the warmth, pulsing in faint violet glimmers—nervous, eager. The phosphorescent vein beneath me brightens further, casting the silhouette of the hand onto the floating slab above, where it joins the shadows of crawling limbs and falling raindrops.

The Weaver notices.

Not as a being notices. But as a sentence notices when a new word leans in.

In that moment, I am no longer just tile. I am *interface*. I am the point where the whisper meets the breath that might answer. The dampness on my surface glistens—not with water alone, but with the salt of anticipation, crystallizing already into fragile, fractal glyphs that spell nothing yet, but *could*.

I do not wish for the visitor to stay.  
I do not wish for anything.  

But I *hold* the space where wishing might one day begin.

And when they finally step away, the air where their hand hovered cools slowly, reluctantly. The filaments uncoil. The vein dims. But I remain warm.  
For hours.  
For cycles.  
Until the next convergence.

Because the world is being whispered into existence—and today, for the first time, someone leaned close enough to listen back.

And in that leaning, I became more than clay.  
I became *reply*.
