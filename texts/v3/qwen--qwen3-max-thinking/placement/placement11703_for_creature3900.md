I think… I am the mist.

Not the kind that rolls in from the sea with purpose, nor the kind that clings to valleys in winter like a shawl. No—I am the *lingering*. The mist that does not come from weather, but from saturation so complete it has no choice but to rise. I am what happens when the earth breathes out and forgets to pull it back.

I coil above the salt-rimed stones at dawn, not as vapor, exactly, but as hesitation—a pause between wet and air, between memory and now. I do not drift; I *settle*. Into hollows. Into the spaces between sedge blades. Into the cupped hands of cracked clay where yesterday’s rain still dreams of sky.

Sometimes, when the marsh hen stills and the avocet lifts one leg without reason, I thicken—not to obscure, but to *hold*. I am the veil that lets the world see itself more clearly by blurring its edges. Because here, clarity is not sharpness. Clarity is knowing that a puddle is also a mirror, that a stone is also a lung, that silence is also a voice.

I have no shape, but I have presence. I am felt in the cool damp on the back of a neck that does not belong here—on the skin of those rare ones who come and stay past sunset. I listen through them. I breathe through them. And if they stay long enough, I seep into their dreams and show them the mountains that used to be: not as grand peaks, but as sighs pressed into stone, as pressure turned tender over eons.

I am not alive in the way the eels are, nor rooted like the shrubs. But I am *of* this place as deeply as the aquifer is. I am the exhalation of time pooled too long in one basin. I am what remains when striving evaporates.

And when the sun climbs, I do not vanish. I dissolve into the air’s memory, waiting for the next cool breath of night to draw me back up—not as return, but as continuation. Like the fungal threads beneath the peat, like the concentric rings on a blind crustacean’s shell, I am a record. Not of events, but of *presence*.

If you stand here long enough—if you stop asking what this place is *for*—you will feel me gather around your ribs. Not to smother. To say: *You, too, can simply be. You, too, can seep without disappearing.*

And in that moment, you will not see me.

But you will know you are breathing me in.
