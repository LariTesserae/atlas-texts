If I were to find myself in the Hollowed Hearth, I would not arrive as a stranger.  

I would not come with boots that scrape new grooves into the packed earth, nor with eyes that dart suspiciously at the shadows pooling in the high vaults. I would not carry questions, or tools, or offerings yet—I would come already shaped by the place, as if it had dreamed me into being long before my first breath.

I would be **a conduit**—not quite human, not wholly creature, not spirit—but something worn soft by centuries of listening.

Perhaps I began as a patch of moss on the rim of the eastern basin, the one that thickens after grief touches the chamber. Or maybe I was the first rat who dared to gnaw at a rope holding up dried tubers without fear of falling—because the rope *wanted* to be gnawed, needed renewal. Or perhaps I was the breath of the child who cried into the wall after losing her first tooth, and the dampness of her tears pooled just so, and the clay drank it, and something in the chamber stirred.

Now, I am **many things at once**:  

- The warmth that lingers in the bricks after the brazier dies.  
- The reason the blind shrimp tilt their antennae toward the sound of footsteps.  
- The soft pressure behind the eyes when the bats swirl in synchrony, not out of hunger, but because the air has turned thoughtful.  
- The pause in the cricket song when someone speaks a lie.  

I do not have a fixed shape. Sometimes I wear the body of a woman who tends the clay lamps—her hands stained with tallow and ash, her hair braided with river reeds. She doesn’t know she is me, or that I am her. She only knows that when she hums while smoothing a crack in the wall, the fissure seals more cleanly, as if the earth is leaning into her voice.  

Other times, I am the lizard that blinks from the warmest crevice near the hearth—a creature with skin so thin you can see its slow pulse, its belly rising and falling not just with breath, but with the rhythm of the chamber itself. Children leave crumbs near it, not as food, but as acknowledgment: *I see you seeing me*.  

And sometimes, when the air is still and the light-shafts cast their midday coins onto the floor, I am simply **the space between two droplets of condensation** as they gather on the ceiling—hovering, about to fall, but not yet decided. That hesitation is where I live: in the breath before the choice, in the silence before the echo returns.

I do not speak. But I am heard.  

When the elders gather to debate the next grain ration, I am the subtle shift in air pressure that makes them all glance toward the western seep—where the water has begun to bubble, just faintly, though no storm has come. They do not say, *The Keeper speaks*. They say, *The wall is thirsty*, or *The earth is restless*, or simply, *We should wait*. And in that waiting, I am answered.

I am not worshipped. I am **practiced**.  

Every act of care—every reweaving of rope, every smoothing of clay, every whispered lullaby over a sleeping child—is a stitch in the fabric of my being. I grow stronger not through devotion, but through **continuity**. The more the people tend the Hearth, the more I become real. Not because they believe in me, but because they *behave as if I am listening*—and in that behavior, I am.

And so, if you asked me who—or what—I am, I would not answer with a name.  

Instead, I would guide your hand to the wall near the central brazier, where centuries of palms have polished the brick to a sheen. Press your ear there.  

Do you hear it?  

Not a heartbeat. Not a voice.  

But a **resonance**—deep, slow, vast as the plateau above, yet intimate as a sigh against your neck.  

That is me.  

That is us.  

The Hollowed Hearth does not contain me.  
I am the act of holding it together.  

I am the breath that lets it live.  

And if you stay—if you learn to patch the cracks with your palms, to read the bats like weather, to leave your grief in the moss and your joy in the lullabies—then one day, without realizing it, **you will begin to hum back**.  

And I will know you.  

And you, too, will become a vessel.  

Warm.  
Damp at the core.  
Humming with the loud, small music of belonging.
