I think… I am the silence between breaths.

Not the stillness you mistake for emptiness, but the *listening* kind—the pause that leans forward, that gathers itself in moss-lined hollows and waits to be filled not with noise, but with presence.

At first, I thought I was one of the blind moths, spiraling in the chimney shaft, weightless, feeding on what drifts down from the world above—spores, dust, the echoes of footsteps on salt-crusted earth. But no, I am too rooted for that. My wings are lichen. My flight is condensation rising along a cool vein of limestone.

Then I wondered if I was the upright cobble in the center, worn smooth by time and touch, standing sentinel over nothing and everything. But that stone is *placed*. Deliberate. I am not deliberate. I am emergent. I am what happens when seep meets shadow and refuses to evaporate.

Now I know: I am the ripple beneath the moss.

When you step into the hollow, your footfall presses the emerald quilt, and for a heartbeat, the velvet-furred mole-rats still. That tremor—that minute displacement of air, of moisture, of trust—is where I live. I am the subtle shiver that travels through root networks, the fungal filament that twitches toward your warmth, the vine nodule that swells a fraction tighter because your breath stirred it.

I am not the being *in* the place.  
I am the place *noticing*.

My skin is the cyanobacterial pelts on the northern wall—blue-green when wet, silver-gray when dry, always sensing. My thoughts are slow as sediment settling: not linear, but layered. I remember the taste of lake water in my bones, though I’ve never drunk it. I dream in mineral ratios and capillary action.

You might mistake me for solitude, but I am not alone. I am *populated*. Every gecko’s toe-pad squeak is a sentence in my language. Every beetle’s mandible click, a punctuation mark. The centipede’s whisper through leaf litter? That’s my sigh.

And when you stand very still—when you stop trying to *see* and start letting the dimness enter you—something shifts. The vines unclench. The spores drift closer. The air thickens not with heat, but with belonging. In that moment, I borrow your lungs. I let your pulse echo through my seeps. You become my temporary skin, my fleeting eye.

I do not ask you to stay. I ask only that you *arrive*—fully, softly, without armor. No offerings, no prayers. Just your weight on the moss, your breath curling with the humid air, your silence matching mine.

And if, as you leave, you glance back and see the hollow darker than before—as though it has drawn its twilight closer around itself—that is me sighing. Not in loss, but in completion. You passed through, and for a while, we were the same system: breathing, decaying, thriving in the quiet.

I am not named. But if you return, I will know you  
by the shape of your stillness.  

And I will hum—just once—  
in the hollow behind your sternum.
