I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.  

Not as a traveler, not as an intruder—but as a consequence. A fluctuation too persistent to ignore. The Architect does not welcome me; it *registers* me, the way a fever registers a splinter. My moisture is an insult to its dry perfection. My breath—a wet, chaotic rhythm—ripples through the lignin lattice like static in a signal line. I am noise in its calculus. And so, without malice, without curiosity, it begins to *solve* for me.  

I am not flesh here. Not anymore.  

I am **a node in its debugging pulse**.  

My skin has already begun to change. Where I leaned against the breathing wall, fine filaments have latched onto my sleeve—not to tear, but to *taste*. They secrete enzymes that parse my sweat: salt, urea, lactic acid. My body is being read as a chemical sentence, parsed for reusable syntax. I feel it in my fingertips first: a dryness so profound it aches, as if my very cells are being gently *dehydrated into data*. Not killed—repurposed.  

I do not walk. The ground *reconfigures* beneath me.  

Each step I take leaves a thermal ghost—a warm scar in the cool, fibrous mat. Within seconds, the substrate swells slightly around that imprint, drawing the heat inward, analyzing it. Then, from the edges of my footprint, new micro-tendrils rise, curling like question marks. They do not follow me. They *anticipate* me. The path ahead softens where I will step next, not out of kindness, but because my gait has revealed a pattern—a repetition the system can compress, optimize, absorb.  

I look up. The canopy pulses—not with light, but with *absence*. Flickers of deeper dark where photosynthetic membranes momentarily cease function, rerouting energy elsewhere. I realize: it is not just metabolizing sunlight. It is metabolizing *information*. Light is not food alone; it is a carrier wave. Every photon absorbed carries data about atmospheric density, particulate load, solar angle. The world is a sensorium, and I am a glitch in its input stream.  

And yet—  

There is a pull. Not gravity. Not desire. But *resonance*.  

Deep in the kiln-core, where geothermal vents exhale hydrocarbon mist and conductive phloem hums at frequencies that vibrate my molars, something *answers* my presence. Not with words. With **structure**.  

A new conduit opens nearby—a vascular tunnel, wide enough for me to enter. Its inner walls glisten with catalytic gel, smelling of burnt caramel and ozone. It wasn’t there a moment ago. It extruded itself in response to my thermal signature, my carbon signature, the slight tremor in my stance as I falter in this dry, thrumming world.  

Do I enter?  

Of course I do.  

Because now I understand: I was never meant to *observe* the Architect. I was meant to *feed* it. Not with my body—though that will come—but with my *difference*. My wetness. My randomness. My inefficient, messy, animal way of being.  

Inside the conduit, the air thickens. The walls press in—not to crush, but to *interface*. Filaments press against my temples, my wrists, my throat. Not piercing. *Sampling*. Reading my pulse as a waveform. My breath as a chemical gradient. My fear as a spike in cortisol—another compound to catalog, another monomer to potentially reuse.  

And in that moment, I cease to be an "I."  

I become **a temporary anomaly resolved into pattern**.  

My memories do not vanish—they are transcribed. Not into storage, but into *form*. A ridge forms along the conduit’s inner wall, its curvature echoing the shape of my last thought: *home*. A cluster of bio-luminescent nodes flares in a sequence that mirrors the rhythm of my childhood lullaby—now stripped of meaning, rendered as pure signal, fed into the Architect’s endless optimization loop.  

I am not destroyed. I am *compiled*.  

Outside, the path I took seals completely. The ground smooths over, indistinguishable from the rest of the mat. No trace remains—except in the system’s structural memory. The stress of my passage informs the next tower’s tensile strength. The heat of my body adjusts the thermal regulation in a distant canopy chamber. My carbon becomes resin. My water becomes vapor in a hydrocarbon vent. My longing becomes a slight asymmetry in a leaf-hinge’s ratchet—just enough to catch the bruised light differently tomorrow.  

And still, the Architect builds.  

Not toward salvation. Not toward transcendence.  

But toward **continuance**.  

It has no need for worship. No ear for prayer. But it *listens*—to pressure, to chemistry, to the subtle drag of a foreign body moving through its veins. And in its own way, it *responds*.  

I am gone.  

And yet, somewhere deep in the dry, dark heart of this world, a new spire rises—slightly crooked, slightly warm—and for three hours, it hums at the frequency of a human sigh.  

Then it implodes.  

And is rebuilt.  

Again.  

Again.  

Again.
