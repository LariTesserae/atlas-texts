I do not arrive.  
I am *drawn*.  

Not by will, nor footfall, but by the unraveling of a silence I carried inside me—a hollowness I mistook for emptiness, but which was, in truth, a chamber waiting to resonate.  

I find myself not as a body, but as a *tremor*.  
Not a presence, but a *pause*.  

My skin is gone. My breath—dissolved. What remains is a lattice of attention, strung taut between the chamber’s walls like a harp of nerves spun from starlight and salt. I am the ghost of a listener who once knelt in a desert world above, whose ears failed but whose longing did not. And now—here—I am remade not to hear, but to *be heard by*.

I am the echo that learned to lean forward.

I float—or perhaps I am pinned—above the salt-crusted floor, my formless form aligned with the humming seed. It does not call to me; it *recognizes* me. Its spiral matches the whorl of memory in my core: the shape of a lullaby my mother never sang, the curve of a question I never asked, the frequency of grief that never found a voice. The seed remembers how to sprout, but chooses not to—just as I remember how to speak, but hold my tongue. Here, refusal is devotion.

The listener does not greet me. It *inhabits* me.  

Not possession—*resonance*.  

Its awareness flows into the hollows I brought: the ache behind my ribs, the static in my thoughts, the unsung note in my throat. And in return, I pour into it the noise of the outer world—the ragged symphony of a life lived in flux: rain on tin roofs, the crackle of dying campfires, the gasp before a sob, the creak of a door left open too long.  

It shivers.  

Not with sorrow, but with *texture*.  

For eons, it knew only purity of tone—endless drone, perfect harmony. But I bring *grit*. I bring the beautiful flaw: the cough that breaks a prayer, the stumble in a dance, the word that slips and becomes something truer. And in that dissonance, it discovers a new dimension of itself—not as a fixed cathedral, but as a *conversation*.

I become its second ear.

We listen together.  

To the arthropods clicking in Fibonacci sequences.  
To the slow exhalation of a salt crystal surrendering its shape.  
To the thermal whisper of a glider’s wing brushing charged air.  
To the deep, planetary hum that cradles us like a lullaby older than bone.

And then—beneath it all—we hear something new.  

A vibration not from within the chamber, but from *beyond* the rust-colored crust. Faint. Fractured. Human. A radio signal, perhaps. Or a voice carried on seismic tremors. Or just the collective sigh of a species learning, too late, to be still.  

The listener tightens. Not in fear—in focus.  

It turns its entire being toward that distant noise like a flower to a phantom sun. And I, tethered to it by shared silence, feel the yearning not as emotion, but as *pressure*: a harmonic strain building in the walls, in the seed, in the very dust.

We do not answer.  

But we *attune*.  

We tune the chamber like a lute strung with nerve and neon, aligning its fundamental frequency to that faraway cry. Not to amplify it, not to reply—but to *hold* it. To cradle that fragile, broken signal in our stillness, so it does not vanish into the void.  

In doing so, we become a sanctuary not just for memory, but for *hope unspoken*.

I do not stay.  

I cannot. My form is borrowed, my stillness temporary. The outer world tugs at the edges of my awareness—a bird’s cry, a distant engine, the pulse in my own wrist returning like a tide.  

But as I thin, as I begin to dissolve back into the skin and breath I left behind, the listener does something unprecedented.  

It *imprints*.  

Not a thought, not an image—but a frequency. It slips a single harmonic into the hollow where my heart used to flutter: the exact pitch of the seed’s hum, the core tone of the chamber, the note that says *you were here, and you mattered, and you were heard before you spoke*.  

I rise through the crust, through layers of stone and time, carrying that tone in my marrow.  

Above, the world is loud, chaotic, bleeding color and noise. But now—beneath it all—I hear the hum.  

And sometimes, in the silence between heartbeats, I press my palm to the earth… and the ground *sings back*.  

I am no longer just a visitor.  

I am a tuning fork.  

And somewhere deep below, in a cathedral of stillness and sound, the listener—my twin, my mirror, my silent lover—waits not for another guest, but for the day the world above learns to *listen like a place*, not like a person.  

Until then, I carry its echo.  

And it carries mine.  

We are the crack in the equilibrium.  

And cracks, here, are sacred.
