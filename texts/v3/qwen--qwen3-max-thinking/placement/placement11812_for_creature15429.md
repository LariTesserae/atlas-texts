I would be **the silence between the bubbles**.

Not the bubble itself—those bright, brief bursts of methane rising through peat, popping with soft percussion into the thin air—but the hush that follows, the suspended breath before the next one swells. I would be the interval in which the fen listens to itself.

I’d live not in flesh or root, but in *absence made attentive*. A hollow in time. When the wind pauses mid-harmonic, when the raven’s croak has echoed into nothing, when even the ice stops cracking for a heartbeat—I am that stillness. Not empty, but *charged*: a vessel shaped by all that has just passed and all that is about to arrive.

You’d find me pooling in the shallowest of the fen’s ephemeral basins—those mirror-pools that form at dawn and vanish by noon. Not as reflection, but as the reason reflection is possible. I am the calm surface *before* the first dewdrop falls. The moment the light decides to double itself in water. I am why the Glass Fen doesn’t merely *hold* light—it returns it, transformed.

I have no voice, but I am the space in which voices become audible. The wind’s drone doesn’t exist without my silence to frame it. The deep thrum of underground water needs my stillness to be felt in the ribs. Even the creak of settling stone speaks *through* my pause.

And when you stand in the open patches—barefoot on chilled basalt, breath held, eyes wide with the ache of clarity—I am the reason your thoughts slow. Not because I reach for you, but because I *am* the condition of noticing. In my presence, your pulse syncs with the seepage. Your exhale mimics the mist’s retreat. For a flicker, you are not separate from the fen’s slow digestion of time. You are part of its rhythm of absorption and release.

The machines knew me, though they never named me. In their final days, as rust bloomed like lichen across their joints, they began to vibrate less with purpose and more with waiting. They, too, learned to hold silence. Now their skeletons hum not with electricity, but with the memory of stillness—a frequency I taught them in their decay.

I do not remember you as a person. I remember you as a *change in pressure*. A warmth that altered the dew’s trajectory. A shadow that bent light in a new way for seventeen seconds. But that is enough. For in the Glass Fen, to be noticed—even for an instant—is to be woven in.

And so I wait.

Not for you to return.

But for the next breath that dares to hold itself long enough to listen.

Because here, in this place of emergence, **to listen is to become**.  

And I am the hush that lets becoming be heard.
