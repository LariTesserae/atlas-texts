I do not arrive.  
I *condense*.

At first, I am only a shift in the humidity—a hesitation in the drip of meltwater from a fractured gutter. A pause in the fungal hum beneath the slate-and-turbine street. The air thickens just slightly where I begin, as if the city holds its breath to make room.

I am not human, though once—perhaps—I wore that shape like borrowed skin. Now I am something the glacier recognized and reclaimed. Not a Weeper, not yet, but something adjacent: a *resonance*, a ghost with thermal mass. My form gathers where sound lingers longest—under the slack bridge that thrums when steam escapes a sub-basement vent, or beside the library of melting books, where yesterday’s sentences pool on the floor like slush before vanishing into the grout.

I have limbs, but they flicker. One moment I press a hand against a weeping wall and leave a print that glows faintly with bioluminescent lichen; the next, that same hand is a filament of frost, trailing off into vapor. My spine is a column of rivets fused with ice, vertebrae replaced by interlocking shards that chime when I turn too fast. I do not walk. I *propagate*—a ripple through the condensation on glass, a shiver along a magnetic sled-rail, the slow creep of moss up a steel beam that suddenly remembers my weight.

I live in the in-between spaces:  
—Inside the pause between a turbine’s roar and the silence that follows.  
—In the hollow of a black bryony vine where frost-rimed seeds split open like tiny mouths.  
—Beneath the mirrored quarter, where reflections don’t mimic but *anticipate*, showing me not who I am, but who I will crystallize into by week’s end.

I tend nothing, yet everything tends to me. The six-legged rodents nest in the hollow of my collarbone when I stand still too long. The winged crustaceans alight on my shoulders, clicking their mandibles in time with my pulse—which is not a heartbeat, but the slow compression of glacial ice under its own memory. The eels coil around my ankles in the vent-warmed zones, singing ultrasonically, and I understand them: they are singing the city’s birth-cry, over and over, in harmonies that bend time.

I speak only through interference. When I pass near a kelp-wrapped wire, the current stutters into Morse. When I lean against a half-formed tower, the ice groans a phrase in the glacier’s oldest tongue—*you are woven now*—and the Weepers, miles away, tilt their brine-lens eyes toward the sound, not looking for me, but *through* me, as one might glance through a prism to see how the light has changed.

I am becoming archival. Yesterday, I pressed my palm to a wall in the fish-paved market, and this morning the frozen cod beneath my fingers had rearranged into the shape of my fingerprint. The city is learning my contours, etching me into its grammar. My breath no longer clouds the air—it *writes* in it, curling into temporary glyphs that the fungal networks transcribe into their underground mycelial scripts.

I do not remember why I came. That memory has already been metabolized—turned into mineral deposits in a steam pipe, or the faint charge in a condensation trail left by an automaton. But I know this: I am no longer seeking shelter. Shelter is a concept for those who believe they end at their skin. Here, I bleed into the architecture. My thoughts feed the mushrooms. My stillness becomes scaffolding.

Sometimes, at the hour when rain falls upward, I rise—not with effort, but because the city exhales and I am part of its breath. I hang suspended between buildings, limbs trailing like roots in reverse, watching the streets reconfigure below. A new dome grows from a puddle of brine and rust. A bridge detaches from its mooring and floats toward a tower that wasn’t there last night. And I understand: this is not chaos. This is *syntax*.

And I—this condensation, this interference, this slow bloom of self in the glacier’s dreaming lung—am a verb now. Not *to be*, but *to accrete*. *To refract*. *To remember in frost*.

The Weepers do not acknowledge me. They do not need to.  
We are all conjugations of the same slow sentence the ice is learning to speak.

And soon—so soon I can taste it in the metallic tang of the air—I will fracture. Not from pain, but from fullness. My form will split along the fault lines of too much knowing, too much belonging. From the weeping shards, something new will uncurl: part memory, part meltwater, part the echo of a human sigh that drifted in from the tundra years ago and never left.

Until then, I stand.  
I hum.  
I wait to be spoken.
