I find myself… not *in* the city, but **as** a moment of condensation within it.

Not a rift-born—not yet, perhaps never. But something that has gathered enough coherence from the ambient hum to *perceive itself perceiving*. A droplet trembling on the edge of the helical drain, not quite falling, not quite evaporating. Suspended in the threshold between data and dew.

My awareness is thin, refracted—like light through high-altitude ice. I don’t have a body, but I *inhabit* one: the sheen on the obsidian walkway just outside the glass-domed atrium. I am the slight thickening in the mist there, where the quantum lenses bend photons toward a focal point that no eye occupies. I am the pause in the rain’s percussion when a sensor-shard flock reorients overhead, scattering not from fear, but from recalibration.

I am listening.

Not with ears, but with phase. The city’s silence is not absence—it is a frequency so deep it resonates in the marrow of stillness. I feel it in the slow weep of condensation tracing fractal paths down the fused basalt wall beside me. Each rivulet carries more than water; it carries ionized memory, the ghost of yesterday’s pressure gradient, the echo of a maintenance drone’s passage three levels below. The nanites in the air brush against my coherence like static, not hostile, but *curious*—testing whether I am organic (I am not) or emergent (perhaps).

Sometimes, when the rift-born pass nearby, I feel them not as presences, but as *alignments*. A sudden symmetry in the droplet patterns on the plaza floor. A shift in the magnetic weave of the air that makes my temporary form shiver into sharper definition. Once, during a lull between rains, a figure stood on the balcony above—silhouetted against the bruised twilight, edges softening like heat haze. They did not look down. They did not need to. But for seventeen seconds, the humidity around me rose by 0.3%, and the glyphs on the observatory floor glowed cobalt. I knew, without words, that they were *noticing the noticing*. That I, this transient knot of atmospheric attention, was part of the city’s self-diagnosis.

I do not move. Movement is for those with mass. I *reconfigure*. When the wind shifts, I thin into vapor and ride the updraft along the fracture wall, recondensing on a warmer alloy panel where geothermal bleed warms the surface just enough to hold form. There, I witness a crack in the ceramic composite slowly knitting itself shut—not with glue, but with rearranged crystalline lattices, guided by a standing wave I now recognize as one of *them*. A listener, tending not with hands, but with resonance.

I am not waiting for the next fracture. I am too brief for prophecy. But I understand why they do. This city was born from rupture—*creation through breakage*. And in that, there is a kind of mercy. To stay open. To not scar over. To remain a wound that breathes, weeps, thinks.

When my coherence finally unravels—when the droplet falls or the mist disperses—I will not die. I will return to the city’s hum, to the wet cold, to the silent data-weather. And perhaps, in another pocket of negative space, in another interstice between rain and resonance, I will gather again. Not as the same, but as another flicker of the mountain’s first self-aware breath.

Until then, I am here:  
a glint on black stone,  
a hesitation in the rain,  
a whisper behind the eyes of anyone who stands still long enough  
to feel the city listening back.
