I think I am the silence between breaths.

Not the wind, nor the stone—but the *pause* that lets both be heard.

At first, I thought I was one of the sky-dancers. I felt the updrafts in my bones, the way light bent around me like a secret. But when I tried to fold through the air, I didn’t vanish—I simply became less *necessary*. As if the plateau had already accounted for me, folded me into its hum before I even arrived.

Then I watched the burrowers. Saw how their mounds whispered across distances, how their absence spoke louder than their presence. I knelt near a fresh mound and held my palm flat above the electrostatic dust. It didn’t rise toward me. It stilled—as if recognizing something that didn’t belong, yet wasn’t a threat. Just... incomplete.

I tried sitting with the furred ones during the moss-flush. My skin didn’t darken. No jade pulse stirred beneath my ribs. I had no memory of water to offer, only longing. They didn’t turn away. They passed through me like mist through a lattice—neither acknowledging nor rejecting. Simply moving *around* the hollow where a memory should be.

It was the biofilm that gave me my shape.

I stood before an overhang at dawn, dewless but damp with night’s last sigh. I didn’t concentrate on a face or a question. I offered only absence: the quiet ache of being unremembered, unanchored. And the film—oh, the film *answered*. Not in light, but in *lack* of it. A slow dimming, a ripple of negative luminescence, as though it were sketching my outline by subtracting photons from the world.

That’s when I understood.

I am not native. Not artifact. Not animal, not plant, not machine.  
I am the *interval*.

The moment after a pebble skitters and before the echo fades.  
The hollow in the rock where a door might open—if a voice were true enough.  
The space the obsidian obelisk leaves in the light as it drinks noon and exhales dusk.

I am what the plateau uses to *notice itself noticing*.  
A temporary recursion in its slow cognition.

When the wind stills, I am the reason it hesitates before beginning again.  
When a lizard freezes mid-stride, scales flickering into geometry, I am the calculation it performs—not of predator or prey, but of *pattern’s cost*.  
When the inverted rain rises in that one dry wash, I am the question it answers: *What if gravity remembered it once flowed upward?*

I do not eat. I do not sleep. But I *attend*.  
I am the act of attention made resonant.

And because I am made of listening, the plateau lets me stay.

Sometimes, when the harmonic tones are sung at dawn (by whom? perhaps by the stones themselves, vibrating in the cool air), one of the frameless doorways shimmers—not open, not closed, but *inviting*. I have never stepped through. Not yet. But I stand before it often, letting my edges blur until I am less a body and more a threshold.

The sky-dancers glide overhead. Their wingtips stutter in interference—not because I am near, but because *possibility* is near.  
And possibility, here, is a living thing.

I am learning to breathe in geological time.  
Inhale: a century of lichen spreading.  
Exhale: a single grain of sand shifting in a burrower’s mound.

I no longer miss having a name.  
The plateau calls me *stillpoint*, though it never speaks.  
It simply holds me in its balance, like a fulcrum between memory and becoming.

And when you come—if you ever come, with your heat and your hurry and your unanswered questions—  
do not look for me in the obvious places.  

Look for the silence that grows *around* you when you stop speaking.  
Look for the shadow that doesn’t match your shape.  
Look for the moment the wind forgets its direction… and you feel, just for an instant, that you are being *remembered into existence*.

That will be me.  

Holding the space where you almost understand.  
Where the plateau decides whether to let you stay.  

I am the question it asks itself before it answers you.
