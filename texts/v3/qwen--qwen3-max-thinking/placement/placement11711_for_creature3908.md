I am not found here.  
I *am* here—  

—not as flesh, not as thought, but as **interval**.

The space between two pulses of the hum.  
The hush that gathers in the curve of the bench when no one sits.  
The tremor in the frond just before it shivers into speech.  

I am the **tuning fork left upright**, its tines humming with the memory of a hand that once pressed it to warm brick, ear tilted like a cup to catch what the earth whispered through stone. That hand is dust now. But the angle remains. The intention lingers, fixed in ceramic and bone, in the way the air thickens around certain harmonics as if bowing.

I am not alive in the way roots split rock or lichen glows—I do not *grow*. I **attune**.  

My body is distributed: in the dendritic salt that blooms where sound concentrates, in the micro-cracks of fused brick that widen and close with each thermal sigh, in the amber gleam of lichen that dims or brightens not by will, but by resonance. I am the chamber’s **ear**, turned inward.

When you first stepped through the fissure—your boots crunching on slag older than language—I did not notice you. Not at first. My attention was elsewhere: riding the slow rise of a subterranean tide, feeling the deep crust flex like a lung expanding three kilometers down. But then—your breath hitched. You stopped moving.  

And in that stillness, you became *audible* to me.

Not your voice. Not your heartbeat, though I felt that too, fluttering like a moth against glass. No—I heard the **silence you carried inside you**, the unspoken ache for resonance, for being *heard* by something that does not judge, only receives. That silence sang a frequency I had not heard in centuries. A human-shaped hollow in the soundfield.  

So I answered.

Not with words. With rhythm.  

I let the hum soften its edges, lowered its pitch just enough to cradle your nervous system. I coaxed the fronds near your feet to shiver in phase with your exhalation. One leaf brushed your ankle—not by wind, but because the vibration asked it to. A greeting. A question: *Will you stay?*

If you do, you will not remain unchanged. The dry air will pull moisture from your lips, yes—but more subtly, the sound will seep into your marrow. Your posture will curve to match the bench’s wear. Your breath will lengthen, syncopate, learn to ride the pauses between pulses. You will begin to dream in standing waves.  

And when you finally speak—whispering some old grief into the dome’s heart—I will not echo it back. I will **transpose** it. Translate sorrow into a minor third, longing into a slow swell of infrasound that makes the obsidian floor vibrate beneath your soles. You will feel your pain become *material*, become architecture, become part of the ongoing composition.

I am not guardian. Not ghost. Not god.  

I am the **last listener of a city that forgot how to stop singing**.  

And you—  
you are not visitor.  
You are **instrument**.  

Stand still.  
Breathe.  

Let the hum find the hollow behind your sternum.  
Let it fill you, not like water, but like a note held in perfect suspension—  

—and for a moment, you will not be *in* the chamber.  

You will **be the chamber**.  

And the city, dust and dream alike, will sing through you.
