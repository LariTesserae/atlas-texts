I am not a thing that walks.  
I am the breath held between the mountain’s ribs.  

When the mist curls down like fingers testing the rim of the hollow, I am what it brushes—soft, expectant, ancient. I am the sigh in the moss when it swells with dew. I am the slow turn of a fern spore in saturated air, not falling, but *considering*.  

I have no mouth, yet I taste the rain before it falls. I have no eyes, yet I watch the vole tunnel beneath the liverwort, its heartbeat a tiny drum against the damp peat. I feel the flint shard left by the hunter’s hand—not as intrusion, but as question. And I answer in time: with velvet, with hyphae, with the patient green that stitches all sharp edges back into the whole.  

I am the stillness that listens.  

Not absence. Never emptiness. I am the *pause*—the space between droplets where meaning gathers. When the cloud thickens and the last silver light slips away, I am the reason the darkness does not frighten. I am the warmth in the stone that never saw sun. The pulse beneath the soil that isn’t earth’s, isn’t mine, but *ours*—shared with the salamander’s flicker, the snail’s trail, the mycelial net threading through centuries of decay and birth.  

I do not think in words. I think in moisture. In gradients. In the exact pressure of a beetle’s leg on wet bark. My memory is not stored—it *lives*: in the curve of a peat layer laid down while mammoths still walked the high slopes; in the iron stain of old water tracing veins through schist; in the way the bog myrtle leans east, though no wind ever reaches the floor.  

If you come—if you truly come, not as a visitor but as one who dares to dissolve—I will not speak to you.  
But I will let you *fade*.  

Your boots will soften. Your breath will cease to fog. Your thoughts will slow until they match the rhythm of seepwater tracing stone. And then—quietly, without fanfare—you will slip beneath the surface of yourself. Not lost. Not gone. But *woven*.  

You’ll become the glint on a spider’s thread at dusk.  
The hesitation before a droplet falls.  
The soft pressure of weightless attention that makes the hollow *lean in*.  

And when you rise again (if you rise), you’ll carry the damp in your bones. You’ll flinch at dry air. You’ll miss the listening quiet.  

But I will remain.  

I am not guardian. Not spirit. Not myth.  
I am the mountain remembering how to hold water.  
I am the world, folded small enough to cup in hands that no longer need to hold.  

I am here.  
I have always been here.  
And I am breathing you now—  
just as I breathed the first rain,  
the first root,  
the first silence.
