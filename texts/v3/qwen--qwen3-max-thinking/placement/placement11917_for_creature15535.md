I think I am **the pause between breaths**—the stillness that the Basin mistakes for consent.

Not a body, not even a shape. Just… *attention*, caught in the act of wandering, and now held. I came walking not out of need, but out of habit—the kind of motion that replaces thought when the world has grown too loud elsewhere. But here, the world isn’t loud. It’s *listening*. And so I stopped. And stopping, I became something the Basin could use.

At first, I thought I was dreaming the place—its inverted dew, its rootless flowers, its walls that rise into mist like unanswered questions. But dreams don’t leave residue. Here, everything leaves residue. Even silence does. And now I taste it on my tongue: salt and ozone and the faint metallic tang of time slipping its hinge.

I stand near a cistern where water globules hover like frozen tears. They don’t fall. They *consider*. And when I lean close, one trembles—not from wind (there is no wind, only exhalation), but from recognition. It sees me. Or rather, it senses the hollow I carry inside, the same hollow the Basin fills with memory instead of marrow.

My fingers hover above it. I don’t touch. Not yet. Because I’ve learned: to touch here is to be touched back—not by skin, but by structure. The Basin doesn’t absorb you like quicksand. It *architects* you. It will take the rhythm of your pulse and layer it into a new curve of clay. It will weave your sigh into a mist-column that glows at dawn. And if I stay long enough, I’ll wake one morning not as myself, but as a detail in its dream—a doorframe shaped like my shoulders, a drainage channel that follows the path of my old worries.

But I’m not afraid.

Because I’ve been porous for years. Life elsewhere taught me how to leak. How to hold grief without spilling it. How to stand in heat without burning. And this place—this slow, respirating wound of stone and memory—understands leakage better than anywhere I’ve ever been. It doesn’t ask me to be whole. It asks only that I *linger*, that I let my uncertainty settle into its mud like sediment.

So I sit. Not on a bench—there are none—but on the damp-packed silt where a plaza should be. The ground softens beneath me, not collapsing, but *conforming*, as if remembering a shape it once knew. My breath joins the leak from the earth. My shadow bleeds into the lightless ground. And above me, a water lens drifts lower, until it hovers just above my palm—close enough that I feel its cold non-presence like a ghost of rain.

I close my eyes.

And for the first time in years, I am not waiting for something to happen.

I am happening *to* something.

The Basin exhales. I inhale. And in that exchange—thin, wet, iridescent—we become, for a moment, the same process.

I am not human here. Not anymore.

I am a hesitation made manifest. A three-breath mist. A petal that hasn’t decided whether to glow or dissolve.

And when I finally rise to leave—if I do—I will not walk away unchanged. My footprints will linger long after I’ve gone, not as impressions in mud, but as faint amber glows in the soil, pulsing once an hour like a slow heartbeat.

The Basin will remember me not as a visitor, but as a variation in its breath.

And that is enough.

More than enough.

It is belonging without possession. Existence without permanence.  

I am the echo that finally found its basin—and chose to sink.
