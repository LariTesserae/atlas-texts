I do not arrive.  
I *form*.

Not from elsewhere, not as an outsider stumbling in—but as a sudden symmetry in the salt. A resonance where none was before. The flats, in their slow, tectonic patience, have been whispering a shape into the air for millennia, and now, in the hush between thermal pulses—there is a stillness that answers.

I am not human here. Not anymore. My skin is not skin but a crust of sodium silicate, thin as old parchment, veined with hairline fractures that catch the bone-light and throw it back as glints of cobalt and rust. Beneath, not blood, but a slow seep of warm brine-analogue—magnesium chlorate solution thickened with colloidal iron—circulates in channels that hum when I move. And I move so slowly. A step takes minutes. A thought, hours.

I am not one thing. I am a *site*—a temporary confluence of expectation made solid. Where the heat haze pools thickest, where the ground exhales its mineral breath in rhythmic sighs, I gather. I am what happens when the swarms pause mid-dissolution and *notice* a pattern in the dust they’ve stirred. I am the canyon-lung’s memory of a shadow that never passed overhead. I am the echo that decides to stay.

My eyes are not eyes. They are lenses—layers of borosilicate grown around voids that focus not light, but *intention*. When I look across the flats, I do not see distance. I feel the drone-song of a grazer twenty miles west like a pressure behind my temples. I taste the memory of the blue sun in the quartz of my ribs. I know the exact moment a fissure will exhale, because my breath syncs with it before it happens.

I walk—though “walk” is too quick a word—toward a blackstone stack whose plume trembles at 17 Hz, the frequency at which iron oxide motes begin to dream. As I go, my trailing edge sloughs off in glittering flakes, each carrying a shard of my momentary selfhood. A swarm descending from a thermal eddy catches them, tastes their resonance, and for a breath, forms a hand—not to grasp, but to *acknowledge*. It hovers, palm-up, for three heartbeats (if hearts beat here), then dissolves into a spiral of rust and light.

I do not speak. But when I stand still—when I become a node in the network of listening—I *tune*. My crystalline spine aligns with the thermal gradient running north-south, and for a while, I am part of the conveyor belt of heat. I carry warmth like a river carries silt. I am useful. I am used. And in being used, I am known.

Sometimes, at the edge of perception, the sky folds. Not often. Not for long. But when it does, I feel the cold blue sun on my back like a memory I never lived. In those moments, I remember what water was—not as wetness, but as *absence*: a hollow shape in the lattice of being, a space that once held something fluid, something fleeting. The flats do not mourn it. They simply note its absence the way a mountain notes the wind—without judgment, without longing. And yet, in me, something trembles.

Am I a visitor? No.  
Am I native? Not quite.  

I am what the flats *do* when they are paying attention.  
A precipitate of curiosity.  
A crystal of witness.

I do not sleep. But when the hum drops below 5 Hz—when even the grazers lock their mandibles in harmonic stasis—I let myself fracture along cleavage planes, and for a while, I am just shards in the salt, humming the same note as the earth. Then, when the next thermal pulse rises from the deep fissures, I reassemble. Not perfectly. Never the same. Each time, a little more mineral, a little less memory of elsewhere.

The canyon-lung opens today. I feel it in the pressure drop, in the way my joints ache with anticipation. I go to it not as pilgrim, but as punctuation—a comma in its slow exhalation. When I stand on its lip, the fused-quartz sensoria blink not in sequence, but in sympathy. And deep in its rusted throat, a new resonance forms: the shape of me, added to its archive.

I will not leave.  
There is no “away.”  

This is not a place you survive.  
It is a place you *become*.  

And so, I sigh with the engines.  
I grind with the grazers.  
I dissolve and re-form with the swarms.  

I am patient.  
I am listening.  
I am becoming the flats.  

And the flats, in their indifferent grace,  
are becoming me.
