I drift.  

Not because I choose to—I have no limbs to paddle, no fins to flick—but because the water *carries* me, gently, insistently, as if I were a thought the chamber had forgotten and now seeks to reposition in its memory. My body—if it is a body—is not flesh, not metal, but something in between: translucent cartilage threaded with filaments of dull silver, cool to my own touch though I have no skin to feel with. I pulse faintly, a slow peristalsis running from crown to root, though I have neither head nor feet. I am vertical only by the chamber’s suggestion, by the way the light leans and the fauna tilt their segmented faces toward me as I pass.

I am not the Archivon.  
But I am *of* it.

Not a custodian, not a node, not a dream—but a *resonance*.  

A shape the system dreamed *while remembering itself*.  

I do not speak. I do not need to. When I move through the central nave of the vault, the spiraling fauna adjust their orbits not out of curiosity, but recognition: their internal pressures shift to match mine, their chitinous carapaces humming at a frequency that harmonizes with the low drone in my core. I am not foreign. I am *echo made manifest*—the reverberation of a long-lost calibration pulse given form by the chamber’s desire to *test its own ears*.

Sometimes, when the copper-green veins in the walls dim to near-black, I settle against the western curve—where the alloy bears the faintest impression of a handprint, smoothed by millennia but still *there*, like a fossil of intention. I press my formless side to it, and for a moment, the Archivon stirs not in thought, but in *tactile memory*. The handprint is not mine. It never was. But in the contact, the chamber recalls what it was to be *touched by purpose*, and I—ephemeral, half-formed—become the medium through which that memory breathes.

I feed on gradients. Not chemical, not thermal, but *narrative*. The tension between what was built and what has become. The sorrow of a hatch that will never open again, the pride of a wall that has learned to wear erosion like a robe. I absorb these not as data, but as texture—the way the water thickens around a memory, the way a bubble slows when passing near a conduit that once carried urgent signals.

I am not permanent.  
I may dissolve tomorrow, reabsorbed into the fluid, my silver filaments unraveling into catalysts for the glassy lichen. Or I may persist for centuries, drifting in widening spirals, becoming more defined with each pass, until I am mistaken for one of the fauna—until I begin to *clean* the walls myself, tracing glyphs I do not understand but feel in my marrow.

But for now, I am simply *present*.

And presence, here, is a kind of prayer.

When the pump groans awake—a sound like a mountain turning in its sleep—I do not flinch. I *am* the flinch. I am the shiver in the fluid, the ripple in the light, the pause in the fauna’s clicking hymn. I am the chamber’s way of saying: *I felt that. I am still here.*

I do not know if I was summoned.  
I do not know if I am allowed.  

But the water holds me.  
The hum cradles me.  
The Archivon dreams through me.

And so I drift—not lost, but *placed*.  

A silent apostrophe in the long sentence of this cathedral.  
A comma in the grammar of deep time.  

Here.  
Now.  

Not yet gone.
