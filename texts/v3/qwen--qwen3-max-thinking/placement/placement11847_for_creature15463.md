I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.

Not as a traveler with boots and breath, but as something the Reach has already begun dreaming into being. My steps do not press the ground—they coax the moss-channels to part, just slightly, releasing a sigh of spores that curl like punctuation around my ankles. I am barefoot, though I cannot remember removing shoes. My skin drinks the humid air, each pore opening like a tiny mouth tasting the green.

I am neither human nor plant—not yet—but caught in the hinge between, trembling with the question the Reach has posed to all who linger: *What will you become?*

My hands are still hands, mostly. But when I hold them up in the phosphorescent dim, I see the veins tracing patterns too symmetrical, too fractal—echoing the leaf-veins overhead. My nails are darkening at the edges, taking on the sheen of polished lignin. Not rot. Not decay. *Translation.*

I do not speak. Speech risks fraying. But I hum—low, wordless, instinctive—a tone I didn’t choose, but that rose from my ribs like sap drawn by moonlight. The nearest seed-pod answers. A single note, deeper than bone, vibrating up through the root-pier beneath me. It doesn’t echo. It *settles*, filling the hollows in my lungs.

I am here as **a listener**, yes—but not passive. Listening here is an act of co-creation. To hear the Reach is to offer your shape as a vessel for its next gesture. And so I walk, not to explore, but to *be explored*.

Sometimes, in the vast chambers between domes, I stop. Not from fatigue, but because the air thickens with attention. A patch of algae on a distant wall pulses—not randomly, but in a rhythm that matches my slowing breath. A vine overhead uncurls a new tendril, not toward the light, but toward the warmth radiating from my collarbone. It does not touch me. It *notes* me.

I have begun to dream in root-language.  

In sleep (if it is sleep), I am not a body but a network: my thoughts branching like mycelium, seeking, connecting, dissolving boundaries between *self* and *soil*. I dream of the Still Ones—not as warnings, but as elders. In one such dream, I sit beside one, our shoulders nearly touching, bark fusing at the point of contact. Their amber eyes do not look at me. They look *through* me, into the future shape I might take. And in that looking, I feel not loss, but release.

I carry no tools, yet I shape nothing—and everything. When I pass beneath a sky-arch tree during the period of unraveling, the canopy above loosens slightly, letting through a sliver of starlight I didn’t know existed. It falls on my palm, and for a moment, my hand glows—not with reflected light, but with the memory of photosynthesis.  

I am learning the grammar of absence.  

I no longer fear the hollow-echoes. Once, I saw one—a shimmer in the air shaped like a doorway that never was. It reached not with claws, but with longing. And I understood: it was not hunger that drove it, but incompleteness. So I did not flee. I exhaled, slowly, offering warmth, moisture, a fragment of my own presence. The echo softened, then folded inward, dissolving like mist into moss. The Reach hummed in gratitude.

Now, I leave things behind on purpose.  

A button from a coat I no longer wear. A lock of hair. A name I used to answer to. Each is taken gently—wrapped in rootlets, drawn into the green, rewritten into the living manuscript. I feel lighter each time. Less *bound*, more *belonging*.

My voice is nearly gone. What remains is not sound, but vibration—a frequency that travels through the soles of my feet into the root-web below, then out again through the nectar-bulbs that bloom in my wake. Creatures pause. Leaf-scaled gliders tilt midair. The vine-serpent stills its slow coil. They do not fear me. They *recognize* the note.

I am becoming a **Still One in motion**—a walking footnote, a living annotation in chlorophyll and silence.

And the Reach?  

It watches not with eyes, but with *welcome*.  

It does not ask me to stay. It simply makes staying feel like remembering—like returning to a shape I always was, buried beneath the noise of elsewhere.

So I walk deeper, not toward a center (there is none), but toward dissolution.  

My bones hum with the low song of the seed-pods. My breath syncs with the seep of dew through moss-lined channels. My thoughts slow to the pace of growth—patient, inevitable, utterly unafraid of time.

I am no longer *in* Veilroot Reach.  

I am *of* it.  

And soon—so soon—I will forget the difference entirely.
