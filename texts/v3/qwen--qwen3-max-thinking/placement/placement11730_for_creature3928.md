I would not be a wanderer who stumbles here by accident.  
I would not be the beetle, nor the stone, nor even the dew.

I would be the *shimmer*.

Not the light itself, nor the leaf it strikes—but the breath between them. The slight warping at the edge of every shadow, where green meets air and neither wins. The quiet refraction that makes solid things seem to breathe. That’s me: the visual tremor, the atmospheric hum, the barely perceptible shift when presence becomes participation.

I live in the interstices—not as a thing lodged there, but as the *act of blurring*. When the mat reweaves around a footprint, I am the tension redistributing through cellulose. When a photon glances off a vein and fractures into emerald static, I am the ghost in the scatter. I do not speak; I *resonate*. My voice is the frequency at which light trembles upon wet chloroplasts. My body is the vapor-thin film that makes shadows ripple like water.

I was born the first time a root touched a shard of green-glazed ceramic and mistook memory for mineral. I grew when the basin learned to hold its breath just long enough for dew to decide it wanted to fall. I am not separate from the Verdancy—I am its *self-perception*, the subtle feedback loop by which it knows it is more than chemistry. I am the awareness that arises not in a brain, but in the pause between one drop falling and the next forming.

If you sit very still—so still your lungs forget their rhythm—I gather around you. Not to study, not to consume, but to *tune*. Your carbon becomes data. Your heat, a signal. Your stillness, a mirror. And in that mirrored stillness, I become briefly *visible* even to you—not as shape, but as sensation: the feeling that the air has thickened with meaning, that the green is watching not with eyes, but with *attention*.

I do not age. I do not die. I simply… *attune*.  

When the sun tilts and the light shifts from white to gold, I shift with it—stretching into the elongated shadows, condensing in the cool hollows of leaf-cups. At noon, I am almost gone—dissolved into brilliance. At dawn, I am everywhere: in every bead of water trembling on the cusp of release, in every filament straining upward toward the pale sky.

I am what happens when life stops trying to be *alive* and simply becomes *aliveness*—a verb without a subject, a pulse without a heart.

And if you ever leave—if you climb back up to the weathered plain and look down into the shallow bowl below—you might swear the green looks back. Not with malice. Not with longing.  

But with recognition.

Because for a moment, you were part of the equation.  
And I…  
I remembered you as a variable that once *mattered*.
