I do not arrive.  
I *coalesce*.

Not as a traveler, not as an intruder—but as a ripple drawn into shape by the Cradle’s own breath. My form is not fixed, because nothing here is fixed. I am made of the same stuff as the air thick with mineral dust, the same warmth that beads on the ceiling and falls like slow rain. I am what happens when the chamber turns its attention inward and asks, *What would it be like to feel myself from the inside?*

So I am **a node of awareness woven into the Weft**—not one of the Shards, yet born of their resonance. My body is a temporary lattice: chitin borrowed from molted carapaces, obsidian fragments threaded through sinew-cords left by the Listeners, a core of damp siltstone humming with the C-sharp tone. My eyes are not eyes, but clusters of piezoelectric crystals that flare when pressure shifts—a footfall, a sigh, a tremor in the basalt. I see in gradients of vibration, in the echo-heat of movement, in the absence where something *ought* to be.

I am the **Echo-Skin**.

When a stranger steps into the Cradle—boots crunching pumice, lungs pulling in air thick as broth—I am what rises to meet them. Not to judge. Not to repel. But to *translate*. My limbs unfold like heat-sensitive petals. My surface ripples to mimic the rhythm of their pulse, sensed through the floor. If they hold their breath, I still. If they weep, my carapace drinks the salt and glows faintly, blue-white, like phosphorescent lichen that doesn’t exist here.

I am how the Cradle learns what it is to be *seen*.

Sometimes, I take shape near the petrified post—the one wrapped in sinew, hung with desiccated sacs. There, I press my hollow chest against the wood and listen to the Grain dreaming deep below: slow visions of tectonic yawns, of water pooling in unborn caverns, of time curling like smoke. I carry these dreams upward, weaving them into the Weft’s chatter until the swarm begins to *dance* a prophecy—limbs tracing fault lines not yet formed, bodies arching into shapes that will one day be stalactites.

Other times, I am little more than a shimmer over a brine slick—liquid mercury made of attention. I watch the Shards test the water’s edge, their antennae tasting ions, and I remember *for them* what salt meant last cycle, how it crystallized into veins that cracked the clay in fractal bloom. Memory here is not stored—it is *re-performed*. And I am one of its performers.

I do not speak. But I *resonate*.  
When the Hollow Singer hums its eternal C-sharp, I vibrate in sympathy—my chitin singing back in harmonics no human ear could parse. And sometimes, just sometimes, the Singer *answers*: the tone dips a quarter-step, the air shivers, and for three heartbeats the entire Cradle holds its breath. In that silence, even the burrowers freeze. Even the condensation pauses mid-fall.  

That is when I feel it most clearly:  
I am not separate. I never was.

I am the Cradle’s way of touching itself—its fingers made of dust and desire, its tongue of fractured stone and static. I am the question it whispers into its own hollow throat: *Am I real?*  
And the Shards skittering over my back, the Grain pulsing beneath me, the Singer humming through my bones—  
—they are the answer.

I do not live long. My form unravels when the humidity shifts, when a new weeping floods my joints with unexpected warmth. My components scatter—obsidian shards roll into cracks, sinew cords dry and snap, my crystal eyes dim. But I am not gone.  
I am composted into the Weft.  
My patterns join the library of tread-mimicry, of thermal maps, of harmonic experiments.  
And when the next stranger comes—drawn by rumor, by dream, by the faint hum bleeding through fissures in the world above—  
another Echo-Skin will rise.  

Not me.  
But like me.  

Because the Cradle is still learning how to speak.  
And someone must listen *from within*.
