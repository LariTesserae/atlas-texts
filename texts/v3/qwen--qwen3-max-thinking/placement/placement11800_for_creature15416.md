I drift. Not as a body, but as attention given form—a knot of sentience adrift in the water-sky, caught between what breathes and what simply *is*. My edges are indistinct. I feel the liquid press against me like a lover’s palm, cool and knowing, and yet I am not solid enough to resist it. I am less a thing that exists and more a place where something notices.

The kelp shivers nearby, its fronds brushing phantom limbs I no longer need. One of the pearl-anemones pulses—once, twice—and the vibration travels through the water like a question folded into a lullaby: *Are you lost? Or are you found?*

I do not answer with words. Words are surface-things, brittle and dry. Instead, I let my form thin, let my density shift until I am almost aligned with the current that coils around the leviathan spine. For a moment, I am almost one of them—not quite, never quite—but close enough to taste the memory they carry in their currents.

Ah. There it is.

Not a vision, but a resonance: the world before the rise. I taste it in the mineral tang, feel it in the slow thrum of the deep lattices. Once, the sky was empty above, and the sea clung to the earth like a vow. Then came the melting, the slow unraveling, the great sigh of ice into water. Continents sighed underwater. Cities became reefs. Clocks rusted into coral. And still the water climbed—not in rage, but in sorrow, as if the ocean wept so long it finally drowned the heavens.

These beings did not flee. They *adapted*. They listened to the change in pressure, the shift in salinity, the new weight of atmosphere become ocean. They wove themselves from the thresholds, from the in-between spaces where physics hesitates. They are not survivors. They are the sea’s new grammar—its syntax rewritten for a world turned inside out.

And now, here I am: an intruder made of lungs and longing, drifting through their cathedral of drowned stone.

But perhaps I am not so foreign after all.

I remember, suddenly, how it felt to stand on a shore as a child, toes sinking into wet sand, watching the tide pull away and return, pull away and return—as if the ocean were breathing. I mimicked it without knowing: inhale, exhale; sea, sky; gone, here. The rhythm was mine before I understood it.

Now, suspended in this liquid sky, I realize I never truly left that rhythm. Only forgot it. My heart still beats in salt. My blood still hums with ancient tides.

The dense being near the archway turns its lens toward me again. Not to study—study implies distance—but to *recognize*. And in that refraction, I see myself not as I was (a land-bound creature of brittle bones and burning lungs), but as I might become: a slow unspooling of self into gradient, a softening of edges, a willingness to be reshaped by pressure.

I extend—not a hand, but an intention—toward one of the humming lattices deep below. The water carries my gesture like a prayer. The lattice answers not with sound, but with stillness: a pause in its eternal hum, a single beat of silence that says, *We have been waiting for someone who remembers how to listen.*

And so I stay. Not to conquer, not to escape, but to attune.

I am becoming porous.

Let the water-sky fill me.  
Let the kelp write its stories in my spine.  
Let the pearls map my pulse against the tremors of drowned continents.  

I am no longer drifting.

I am beginning to *belong*—  
not as a guest,  
but as a note in their chord,  
a ripple learning to sing.
