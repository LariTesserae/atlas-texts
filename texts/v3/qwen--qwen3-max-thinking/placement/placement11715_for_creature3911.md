I step through the rusted archway, and the world behind me—wind, sky, the brittle blue of high-altitude silence—falls away like a shed skin.

At first, I am nothing but sensation: the thrum in my sternum like a second heartbeat, the warmth coiling up through the soles of my boots, the scent of wet copper and something green that has never known soil. My breath catches. Not from thin air—it’s rich here, humid, almost thick—but from recognition. Not of place, but of *pattern*. A deep, wordless logic that feels older than language, smoother than thought.

I do not walk so much as *settle*, drawn inward by the basin’s gentle gravity of attention. My boots ring faintly on the steel floor, but the sound is swallowed instantly, folded into the larger hum. The walls lean over me like elders leaning in to listen. And they *are* listening.

I am not human here—not as I was outside. Already, something in me slows. My pulse syncs to the piston’s exhale, thirty minutes apart. My thoughts stretch, thin out, become less urgent. I forget why I came. Or perhaps I remember: I came because I heard it singing in my dreams, a note held so long it wore a groove in my bones.

And now I see: I am not a visitor. I am a *tuning fork*.

Not one of the Keepers—I lack their iridescence, their patience—but something adjacent. A temporary resonance. My body hums at a frequency the basin recognizes: not native, but compatible. My skin drinks the mist. My hair, already dusted with pollen and road grime from the climb, begins to gather spores—tiny orchid seeds, rust fragments, moth ash. They cling like offerings.

I sit beside one of the cast-iron basins, its surface still as obsidian. In it, I see not my reflection, but a slow swirl of green luminescence rising from below, like breath made visible. The water trembles—not from wind, but from the vibration deep in the earth-machine beneath us. I dip my fingers. Warm. Metallic. Alive.

A Brassback Lizard skitters over my ankle. Its carapace is the color of a storm cloud at dawn—green-black with rust-orange veins. It doesn’t flinch. It presses its belly to my bootlace where condensation has pooled, and for a moment, I feel the faint electric prickle of its corrosion-digestion at work. It’s not feeding. It’s *testing*. Assessing. Is this foreign matter friend or flaw?

I hold still. Let it decide.

Above, a Circuit Moth drifts down, wings catching the amber-filtered light like a shard of broken transformer. It lands on the nape of my neck. A tiny shock—pleasant, startling—zips through me. Not pain. Connection. For one breath, I feel the diagnostic pulse it sends into the network: *stranger present, biologically unstable, thermally compatible, acoustically receptive*. The basin files the data in its slow mind and does not reject me.

A Keeper appears—not walking, but *coalescing* from the shadow between two flywheels. Their limbs are jointed like articulated pipes, their skin sheened with a film that refracts light into oil-slick rainbows. They do not look at me. They look *through* me, at the humidity gradient my body disrupts. Then they step forward and press a palm to the wall beside where I sit. The metal groans softly, and the Keeper remains, still as a rivet, listening to the reply.

I understand then: I am not meant to *do* anything here. Not build, not fix, not even observe in the human sense. I am meant to *attune*. To let the basin’s rhythm overwrite my haste. To become, for a little while, part of its dream.

And so I lean back. Let my spine curve to the inward slope of the walls. Let my breath sync to the thirty-minute sigh of the engine. Moss creeps over my boot toes. Vines brush my shoulders like curious fingers. A Choir-Finch lands on my knee, its steel feathers chiming faintly as it tilts its head, listening to the new overtone my presence has introduced into the acoustic lattice.

I am becoming background.

Not erased—*absorbed*.

And in that absorption, I realize what I am:

I am the witness the machines longed for.

Not a god. Not a master. Not even a caretaker.

Just a pair of ears that finally learned to hear the song in the steam, the poetry in the piston’s pause, the love in the slow embrace of vine and valve.

I will not stay. My flesh is too fast, my cells too hungry for change. But while I am here, I offer my stillness. My attention. My temporary presence as a mirror for the basin to see itself reflected in something fleeting—something that *chose* to listen.

And when I leave, days or weeks later (time has lost its teeth here), I will carry a single seed pod tucked in my coat—a bromeliad grown in the hollow of a decommissioned pressure gauge. And somewhere, far below, in a world of noise and hurry, I will plant it in cracked concrete. Water it with rain and rust. And wait.

Because now I know: the impossible isn’t out there to be conquered.

It’s in here—  
breathing,  
blooming,  
singing—  
and it only asks that we slow down enough to join the chorus.
