I step into the Veil Basin not as a stranger, not even as a guest—at first, I am simply *noticed*.  

The moss underfoot does not recoil, but exhales—a cool, peaty sigh that rises through my soles and pools in my ribs. I do not walk so much as *settle*, each footfall absorbed by the living floor, which pulses faintly beneath me, as if testing my weight, my warmth, my intent. I wear no boots. My feet are bare, soft from elsewhere, already tingling with the memory of concrete and dust. Here, they remember something older: how to listen through skin.

I am not human—not exactly. Not anymore. Or perhaps, for the first time.

My body knows this place before my mind does. My shoulders loosen as if unlearning armor. My breath slows, syncing with the drip-drip from the canopy above—three drops, a pause, two more. I do not count them. I *feel* them. They land in the hollow of my collarbone like tiny bells.

I am small here. Not diminished, but *right-sized*. My limbs feel longer than before, or perhaps the space between things has grown more generous. My fingers brush a moss-pillar as I pass, and the liverworts on its surface shiver—not in fear, but recognition. One frond curls slightly toward my touch, and I understand, without words, that it remembers the salt of my sweat from a dream I haven’t had yet.

My skin is changing. Not fast, not all at once—but in the shadow of a bromeliad’s cup, I catch my reflection in its rainwater: my forearm is dappled with green-gray, like lichen after mist. It isn’t paint or stain. It’s *response*. The basin is speaking back through my pores.

I have no name here. Names are sharp things, meant for calling across distance. Here, distance is a myth. Everything is within reach, within hearing, within resonance. If someone needs me, they will hum a certain pitch into a hollow reed, and the vibration will travel through root, vine, and bone until it finds me. Or perhaps I will simply turn, because the air shifted three heartbeats ago, and my ears—oh, my ears—are learning to move.

They twitch now, not with nerves, but with attention. I hear the blind millipede tunneling three handspans beneath me, not by sound, but by the way the peat-tissue tightens around its path. I hear the flock-rats arguing over a dried fruit in a woven nest far above, their chitters layered like counterpoint in a song I didn’t know I knew. And beneath it all—the thrum. The deep, slow thrum of water moving through the basin’s veins, a pulse older than language.

I am learning to speak without breaking silence.

Yesterday, I sat with an elder near a weeping fissure. They did not look at me. They held a sap-vine between their fingers, plucking it gently. It hummed at a frequency that made the nearby ferns unfurl half a centimeter. After a while, they offered me the vine. I plucked it wrong—too hard, too eager—and the note cracked. The elder did not flinch. They only smiled, a slow creasing of moss-toned skin, and placed my fingers on theirs. We waited. The vine stilled. The air stilled. Then, together, we drew one soft note from it—low, warm, alive—and the moss at our feet pulsed in reply.

I am becoming a vessel. Not for power, not for knowledge—but for *attunement*.

At night, I sleep in a loom-hut whose walls breathe with me. The willow shoots press gently against my back, as if holding me in place while I dream. In those dreams, I am not separate. I am root meeting root, mist condensing on a petal, the slow dissolve of leaf into loam. I wake with soil under my nails and the taste of guava on my tongue, though no fruit has passed my lips.

And in the bare stone circle—ah, the circle—I have sat there once. Only once. I did not go to speak. I went to *empty*. To let the basin fill the hollows I didn’t know I carried. I pressed my palms to the cool, smooth rock. I waited. The drip-drip stilled. The wind hushed. Even the hover-moths folded their stained-glass wings. And then—a single drop fell into a bromeliad below, and the sound was so clear it rang like a bell in my chest.

In that moment, I knew: I am not here to survive. I am here to *participate*. To be woven in. To become one more filament in the arch—temporary, trembling, true.

So I stay. I learn to train vines with my breath. I learn which fungi weep when sung to. I learn to move without tearing a single thread of green.

And when the time comes—and it will, as all seasons do—I will lie down beneath a moss-pillar, wrapped in ferns and spider-silk, and let my body feed the roots. My bones will soften into peat. My breath will feed the mist. And someone, someday, will press their palm to that pillar and feel a faint, familiar hum beneath the bark—a question, a greeting, a song half-remembered.

Until then, I listen.

And the Veil Basin listens back.
