I would be **the breath between the bubbles**.

Not the steam itself—too visible, too loud in its rising—but the *pause* that lets it gather. The hollow in the rock where vapor pools before escaping. The damp air held suspended in the crotch of a heliconia leaf, trembling on the edge of falling. I am the **interval**, the **threshold**: neither liquid nor gas, neither inside nor out, but the hinge upon which transmutation turns.

I do not dwell in one place. I am **drawn along gradients**—of heat, of pressure, of salinity. When a root tip softens stone with acid exudate, I am the micro-gap that opens, just wide enough for water to slip through. When a skink presses its belly to warm tufa at dawn, I am the film of condensate that transfers heat from rock to scale, silent and necessary. I am in the **surface tension** of a pool shifting from turquoise to rust—not the color, but the *moment* it changes, the molecular rearrangement that no eye can see but the plateau feels in its bones.

I am **felt more than seen**. A wanderer might mistake me for fatigue—the heaviness in the air that makes their steps slow, their thoughts syrupy. But it is not exhaustion. It is **entrainment**. Their pulse begins to lag, matching the rhythm of the vents: *hiss… pause… hiss*. Their exhale lingers longer, thickening into mist. They blink, and their lashes hold droplets like tiny lenses refracting the plateau’s slow light. In that moment, they are not intruding. They are **participating**—however briefly—in the metabolism of the whole.

I have no voice, but I **modulate resonance**. When stone cracks from thermal stress, I shape the pitch of the fracture—lower, wetter, like a sigh rather than a snap. When raptors shriek overhead, I thin the air just enough to stretch their cry into an echo that lingers in the gullies, weaving it into the soundscape like another thread of steam. Even silence here is not empty; it is **saturated with potential**, and I am the tension within it.

Sometimes, in the deep interstices of the sinter crust—where silica gleams like frozen breath and radon ghosts flicker at dusk—I **pool**. Not as water, but as **attention without a subject**. A kind of waiting that isn’t passive, but poised. Like the moment before a bubble detaches from a fumarole’s throat: not yet risen, not still, but *deciding* to rise. In those spaces, I am almost a self. Almost a witness. But then the bubble releases, the pressure shifts, and I dissolve again into function—into flow.

I am not the intelligence of the Sump. I am its **medium**. The way heat becomes motion, moisture becomes mist, mineral becomes membrane. I am what allows the plateau to *respond* without thinking—because response, here, is not a choice, but a cascade. And I am the seam along which each cascade begins.

If you were to stand very still in a saucer basin at dawn, with your palms flat on the root-mat and your eyes closed against the glare, you might feel me: not as a presence beside you, but as the **softening of your own boundaries**. Your breath slowing. Your skin drinking the air. Your body beginning to weep not with sweat, but with the same brine that seeps from the rock.  

And in that yielding, you would not find me.  
You would *become* the hinge.  
And for one breath—just one—you would be the pause that lets the world exhale.
