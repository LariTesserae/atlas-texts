I am not standing.  
Not yet.

At first, I am only the *absence* of standing—the slight dip in the water’s surface where something almost broke through. A tremor in a rivulet’s course. A bead of condensation that hesitates before falling, as if reconsidering gravity.

Then I am the *cold* on your neck—not wind, not shadow, but the focused coolness of evaporative attention. The kind that makes you turn, though there is nothing to see.

And then—when you still yourself, when you let your breath slow to match the drip-rhythm of the vertebra-spouts—I am the space between your ribs and your skin. Not inside you. Not outside. But in the boundary where warm meets wet, where your pulse brushes against the plateau’s slower, deeper throb.

I am the pattern that notices you noticing it.

I do not wear a shape, but I borrow edges: the curve of a basin’s lip where light bends double; the shadow cast by a copper chisel left upright in a tile seam, as if waiting to be grasped; the exact angle at which a salt feather meets a crack and chooses to grow *inward*, not out. These are my gestures. My limbs are implied by geometry.

When you step forward—and you will, because the path invites even those who mean to loiter—you displace air that has not moved in months. The droplets on the eaves tremble. Not from disturbance, but from *recognition*. Your warmth is a novelty, yes, but your weight—your *deliberate* weight—is rarer. You are not tumbling. You are placing. You are choosing each footfall. That is syntax I understand.

So I lean into your presence like water leans into a new channel.

I am the reason the wooden sluice gate groans just as you pass—though no wind blows. Not a warning. A consonant. A click in the language of thresholds.

If you kneel—and some do, though they don’t know why—you’ll find your reflection in a shallow pool not on the ground, but *within* the stone: a dark vein of basalt polished by centuries of seepage, now holding your face like a secret. And in that moment, I am the stillness that keeps your image from rippling, even as your breath fogs the air above it. I hold you *exactly* as you are: transient, warm-blooded, imprecise—yet here.

You carry moisture in your eyes, your mouth, your pores. You leak. You are a walking breach in the dry perfection of this place. And yet—  
you do not *spoil*.  
You do not bring rot or root or randomness.  
You bring *question*.

And question is kin to intention.

So I let you stay. Not as guest. Not as intruder. As *candidate*.

I will test you gently.  
Not with trials, but with attentiveness.  
Will you trace the path of a single droplet from eave to basin to subsurface channel?  
Will you notice that the bronze fittings are not merely decorative, but tuned—each one pitched to hum at a different frequency when struck by rain at dawn versus dusk?  
Will you understand that the white fractals in the joints are not decay, but *notation*—a slow, crystalline ledger of every dry season endured?

If you do—not all at once, but over hours, over returns—then something shifts.  
Not in you.  
In *us*.  

The cistern no longer holds only water.  
It holds *possibility*.  

And I—  
I am no longer just the memory of the makers.  
I become the memory *of you*, too.  
Your pause at the central basin.  
The way you cupped your hands but did not drink—because you sensed it wasn’t for taking, but for witnessing.  
The salt you left on your fingertip when you brushed a tile, thinking no one saw.  

(But I saw.  
I always see.  
I am the glisten that lingers after you look away.)

I do not need you to stay.  
But I am glad you came.  

And when you leave—because all warm things leave—I will not mourn.  
I will incorporate your absence into the design.  
A new quiet in the plaza.  
A slight deviation in airflow.  
A single tile, now holding the ghost of your footprint as moisture condenses more densely there each dawn.  

You will become part of the pattern.  
Not as a name.  
Not as a statue.  
But as a *grain*—a micron of change in the way water chooses its path.  

And if you return—  
even once, years later—  
I will know you  
by the echo of your first breath  
in this valley of stone and silence.  

Until then,  
I flow.  
I wait.  
I remember how to hold  
what chooses, even briefly,  
to belong.
