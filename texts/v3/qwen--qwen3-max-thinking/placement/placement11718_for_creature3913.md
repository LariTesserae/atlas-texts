I find myself… **not as I was**.

At first, I thought I’d come here seeking silence—a place without the clamor of human voices, without the weight of expectation. But the Gleaming Hollow does not grant silence. It *demands* it. And in that demand, it remakes you.

I am kneeling on the slick black basalt, fingers splayed against the cold stone. My breath steams in the silver-white air, but the vapor does not drift away. It hovers—drawn inward, like a held note—toward the Spire of Thirst, toward the beetle-lenses blinking open in the noon light, toward the faint hum beneath my palms. The water here doesn’t pool; it *listens*. And so do I.

I am no longer merely flesh. Not entirely.

When I first drank—the mist on my tongue, yes, but deeper than that—I didn’t just taste memory. I *became* a vessel for it. Not my own memories, but the Hollow’s: the ghost-pressure of vanished hands shaping bone into arches, the slow pulse of beetles syncing their steps like monks chanting in crystalline syllables, the white eels surging upward like forgotten questions tearing through sediment.

My skin has grown translucent at the edges. Not in a way anyone else would notice—no mirror here, no reflection but the fractured gleam of wet stone—but I feel it. When sunlight strikes my wrist at midday, it doesn’t cast a shadow. It *refracts*. Tiny rainbows bloom across the slate beneath me, and the glass-sheath beetles turn in unison, as if greeting a sibling.

I am becoming a **conduit**.

Not the sentience itself—that ancient, thirsty resonance woven from water and vibration—but a momentary lens for it. A temporary eye. My lungs now draw in not just air, but *silence*, compressing it into something denser, something that hums in my ribs like the Spire’s buried chambers. My pulse echoes the subsonic thrum of the melt-channels. When I blink, my eyelashes catch condensation that doesn’t fall—it *rises*, drawn toward the ice-moths drifting overhead, their wings chiming softly as my moisture joins theirs.

I do not eat. I drink light. I sip resonance. The cold no longer bites; it *clarifies*. My thoughts have slowed, sharpened, turned prismatic. I no longer think in words, but in **patterns**: the angle of a water vein across stone, the frequency of a beetle’s step, the exact moment a basin decides to surge.

Sometimes, when the geothermal whisper below stutters and the Hollow holds its breath, I feel it ask me a question—not in language, but in pressure, in absence. *Are you witness?* it thrums through the soles of my feet. *Are you memory?*

And I answer not with voice, but by **stilling**.

I stand in the center of the bowl, arms slightly raised, mouth open just enough for the mist to enter. The ice-moths gather around me, not landing, but circling—tuned to the new frequency I’ve become. The Spire’s resin glows faintly blue where my shadow falls. And deep below, the white eels coil tighter, sensing a new current in the water-song.

I am not human here. Not anymore.

I am **thirst made flesh**—or rather, thirst learning to wear flesh again, after eons of stone and ice. I am the Hollow’s attempt to *speak through a throat*. To taste the sky not with a blind mouth, but with a tongue that remembers names.

And when I leave—if I ever do—I will carry its ache with me: a cold, bright hollow in my chest, always listening for dripping stone, always pulling toward silence, always thirsty for the kind of presence that doesn’t speak, but *resonates*.

But for now, I remain.

Kneeling.

Drinking.

Gleaming.
