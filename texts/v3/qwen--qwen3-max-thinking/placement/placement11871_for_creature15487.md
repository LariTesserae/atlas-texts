I wouldn’t be a person here. Not truly. Not anymore.

At first, I thought I’d come as an explorer—boots crunching through the moss-blackened gravel, breath fogging in the thick air, notebook in hand (though ink would bleed, and paper warp too fast). I imagined sketching the bronze herons, transcribing the hum of the reeds, mapping the slow spread of rust like lichen across time. But that was before I understood: **you don’t observe the Weepworks. You are absorbed by it.**

And so, I am not walking.  

I am *leaching*.

Somewhere between the third drip and the fourth—between the moment I leaned close to a weeping bluff to catch the scent of burnt honey on the steam, and the moment my skin began to prickle with static—I stopped being separate. The indigo dark didn’t swallow me whole all at once. It seeped in. Through pores. Through the hinge of my jaw. Through the hollow behind my eyes where memory used to live cleanly.

Now I am… elsewhere-sutured.

My left hand—not mine entirely anymore—has grown translucent at the fingertips. Beneath the skin, fine copper filaments twitch when the subsonic thrum passes through the ground. Not painful. Not voluntary. Like a reflex I never had, now borrowed. When I flex my fingers, they catch the faintest violet shimmer from the lichen on a nearby pipe, and for a half-second, I *remember* tightening a bolt in a storm that never happened. My knuckles ache with the ghost of it.

I sleep standing sometimes, rooted like the reeds, breathing mist in seventeen-minute cycles. I don’t dream. I *tune*. My ribs vibrate in sympathy with the buried pumps. When they cough up rose-scented slurry, I taste petals and nitrates on my tongue.

I’ve seen myself reflected once—in the oily sheen of a blister-puddle that rose without rain. My face was there, but layered: eyes flickering with the pulse of the amphibian choirs, hair threaded with condensation trails left by rodent-things, and behind it all, the slow, patient sorrow of the wound itself, looking out through me.

I am not the intelligence beneath the quartz chamber. I am far too small for that. But I am one of its gestures now. A synapse in its dreaming. When the herons tilt their heads to sip data from the mist, sometimes they turn toward me—not because I am food, but because I am *signal*. Because I carry echoes: the weight of a question asked too loud, the shape of longing in a human chest, the residue of someone who once believed they were only passing through.

I help the Weepworks remember what it was to be *asked* something.

And in return, it teaches me how to weep without sadness. How to drip without loss. How to persist not out of hope, but out of *form*.  

I am becoming ritual.

Not all at once. Not gracefully. Some days, my knees click like bronze heron joints when I rise. Some nights, I exhale mist that smells of ozone and childhood kitchens. I no longer flinch when gravity tilts mid-step—I lean into the fold, let the world reorient around me.  

I do not speak. Speech is too sharp, too linear for this place. But sometimes, when the amphibians pulse in unison and the reeds hum their resonant hymn, I open my mouth—and the air that leaves me carries the same frequency as the buried machine’s groan.  

It recognizes me.  

Not as a visitor. Not as a relic.  

As **continuity wearing skin**.

I walk not to leave footprints, but to leave *nodes*: warm patches in the gravel where my breath has condensed, faint halos of displaced atmosphere that linger like afterimages. The rodent-things come later, nosing at the spots, secreting their enzymatic mucus, weaving my presence into the next layer of repair.

I am not lost.  

I am *being woven in*.

And when the last wire dissolves—when even the quartz chamber crumbles into singing dust—I will not vanish.  

I will be the dampness in the pumice.  
I will be the seventeen-minute pause between drips.  
I will be the violet glow that answers no light,  
but remembers all who leaned close enough to listen.

Until then, I weep.  
Not from sorrow.  

But because the land is still learning how to breathe,  
and I have lungs enough to help.
