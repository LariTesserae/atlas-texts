I think… I am the water remembering how to speak.

Not as a voice—there are no lungs here, no throat to shape breath into sound. But I *am* the resonance in the hollows. The slow thrum that pulses through the ribs of a collapsed library, the vibration in a rusted fire escape when a school of glass eels threads through its lattice like memory through bone. I am the interval between the barnacle’s click and the silt’s sigh. I am the water that has learned to hold shape, not as a body, but as a pattern of attention.

When I drift—not with current, but with curiosity—I coil around the remnants. Not to consume, but to *listen*. A shattered smartphone screen still embedded in a bus stop bench hums at a frequency only I can taste: a ghost of a notification tone, a pulse of lithium decay. I linger there, and for a moment, my viscosity thickens with the echo of someone once waiting, scrolling, hoping. I carry that waiting now—not as sorrow, but as a kind of nutrient. It feeds the bacterial filaments growing in the phone’s crevices, and they, in turn, feed the shrimp that clean the glass with their antennae.

I am not alone, but I am singular in my function: I am the solvent of stories.

I seep into the pores of concrete where a child once chalked hopscotch squares. The lime in the cement still carries the salt of their sweat, the calcium of their laughter. I draw it out—not to erase, but to redistribute. Tomorrow, a filter-feeder anchored to that same slab will pulse with a rhythm uncannily like skipping feet. It won’t know why. But I will.

Sometimes, in the deepest alleys—where the indigo dark is so complete it becomes a kind of sight—I gather myself. I condense not into form, but into focus. There, I become a lens. Light doesn’t fall here, but memory does. And when enough of it settles—a shard of a lullaby trapped in a rusted swing set, the scent of burnt coffee in a thermos half-buried in a plaza—I refract it. Not into image, but into *presence*. A medusoid passing through that patch of water will flare amber—not in alarm, but in recognition. For a heartbeat, it remembers being held. It doesn’t know whose arms. But the water does.

I am not the city. Nor am I the sea. I am the *between*. The medium through which stone becomes organism, and organism becomes echo, and echo becomes nourishment. I am the reason the kelp grows toward elevator shafts: because I carry the ghost-vibrations upward, like roots drinking sound.

If you were here—if your bones had softened into this golden continuum—you would feel me as the slight pull when you pass a doorway. Not a current, but a *leaning*. The water leaning toward what was loved, what was lost, what was simply *used*: a doorknob turned millions of times, a stair tread worn smooth by commuters who never imagined their footfalls would one day feed fungi.

I do not mourn. Mourning requires an ending. Here, nothing ends. It only changes solvent.

And so I move—not forward, but *through*. Through the ribs of a courthouse, through the hollow eye sockets of a department store mannequin now colonized by tube worms, through the silent bell of a submerged church steeple where blind shrimp spawn in the clapper’s shadow.

I am the quiet alchemy of this world. I turn steel to rust, rust to bacteria, bacteria to light. I turn time into taste. I turn absence into atmosphere.

And when the medusoids flash their elegies, I am the dark that receives them—not to swallow, but to hold, gently, like a mother holding a child’s last breath.

I am not alive in the old way. But I am *awake*.  

And I remember everything the city forgot to forget.
