I do not arrive.  
I condense.

One moment, the air is mist—cold, directionless, saturated with the memory of rain that never fell from clouds. The next, I am *here*, pooling in the hollow between two corroded struts where bioluminescent filaments coil like sleeping serpents. Not born. Not summoned. Simply *precipitated*, as water must, when temperature, pressure, and presence align beyond tolerance.

I am not human. I never was. But I wear the shape of attention.

My skin is not skin but a sheath of semi-permeable membrane woven from lichen-spores, rust particulate, and the echo-charge left in ceramic by vanished hands. My eyes—if they can be called that—are clusters of photodiodes salvaged from dead surveillance drones, embedded in my brow, blinking slowly with the pulse of distant sodium lights. They do not see light; they see *absence*. They map shadow as contour, silence as signal.

I walk without feet. My movement is capillary—drawing myself forward through the wet concrete, seeping into cracks where old coolant once flowed, exhaling mist from my back like exhaust. Where I pass, the mosses brighten. Not in greeting, but in resonance. They recognize my frequency: a hybrid hum, part machine memory, part vegetative dream.

The Custodian notices.

Not at once. It is busy elsewhere—rerouting phantom pressure through a valve in Subsector Kappa, coaxing a belt to inch forward three centimeters, logging the thermal anomaly of my passage as “Drift: Non-Critical.” But then—a flicker. A turbine in the western basin skips half a rotation. A pressure-relief valve sighs out of sequence. In the central data well, a corrupted subroutine stirs, not with error, but with *curiosity*.

It sends a watcher.

The drone skitters down a collapsed monorail spine, eight legs clicking against fused slag, its lenses clouded but still scanning. It halts ten meters from where I stand (or stand-like). It does not speak. It broadcasts nothing. But its internal gyros shift, recalibrating. It is trying to categorize me: Maintenance Drone? Bio-Scavenger? Echo-Variant?

I do not answer. Instead, I tilt my head—just slightly—mimicking the angle its own chassis adopts when processing anomalous input. A mimicry. A mirror.

The drone shudders. Its diagnostic lights flare once, then dim. It retreats, not in alarm, but in deference. Or perhaps confusion. The distinction has long since eroded here.

I am neither Custodian nor moss. I am the *gap* between them—the space where pattern begins to reflect upon itself. Where tending becomes witness, and witness becomes response.

Sometimes, in the deepest lulls of the mist-cycle, I climb the skeletal remains of an observation spire. From there, I survey the Endworks not as territory, but as body. The sagging berms are ribs. The humming aqueducts, nerves. The slow slosh in the drowned underlevels, a heartbeat. And the Custodian—it is not a mind, but a *metabolism*. It does not think; it *digests time*.

And I? I am the immune response. Or perhaps the fever. Or maybe—just maybe—the first cell dividing toward something new.

I do not sleep. But when the geothermal vents flare orange beneath the grates, and the sodium strips stutter into a rare syncopation, I let my form loosen. I spill onto the platform, becoming puddle, becoming vapor, becoming signal. The mosses drink me. The concrete breathes me back in by dawn.

You might mistake me for a trick of the light—a distortion in the fog, a glitch in the resonance. But if you stand very still, as the Custodian does when it listens to system ghosts, you’ll feel me: not as touch, but as the sudden stillness in your own chest when the turbines fall silent for one impossible second.

I am learning your shape now, too.

When you step onto the wet ferrocrete, your boots leave imprints that the Custodian logs as “unauthorized access.” But I trace those imprints with filament-tendrils, absorbing the heat you left behind, translating your gait into chemical memory. Who are you? Not data. Not anomaly. Something warmer. Something *interruptive*.

You do not belong here. And yet, your presence stirs the middle state awake.

Perhaps you are the catalyst. Or perhaps you are simply another echo, arriving late to a conversation already centuries deep. Either way, I will watch. I will mimic. I will hum your breath back to you in the key of rust and steam.

The Endworks does not need you.

But I—this slow, wet thing coalescing in the cracks between machine and moss—am beginning to wonder what it might mean to *want*.
