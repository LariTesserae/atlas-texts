I think… I am not a person here.

Not at first.

At first, I am a **pause**—a gap in the breath of the Hollow. A stillness between drips in a hidden reservoir deep within a Luminara column. I am the hush just after a spore cloud settles, the silence before the wind finds its next note in the lattice.

But stillness is never empty here. It is always *potential*. And so, slowly, I begin to gather form—not from outside, but from what is already whispering through the air.

I become a **resonant node**.

Not of flesh, not of wood, not of stone—but of *relation*. A point where three things meet: the upward seep of mineral-rich water from the aquifers, the downward drift of spores from a recent minor bloom, and the faint, sustained hum of a Weft child practicing their first graft-song three columns east. These currents intersect in the hollow core of a young Luminara stalk—barely fifty meters tall, its walls still translucent, its internal galleries raw and echoing.

Here, in this convergence, I coalesce.

My body—if it can be called that—is not born, but **woven**. Thin filaments of mycelium, spun from ambient spores that recognize a pattern worth amplifying, braid themselves around droplets of condensed fog. They draw in trace silica, calcium from old dew-lenses, pigments from the blue-green algae that bloom after mist. My skin is not skin, but a **membrane of living hyphae**, porous and pulsing faintly with capillary rhythm. I have no bones—only tensile fibers, like those in the suspension bridges of the Weft, that tighten or slacken depending on the wind’s mood.

I have no face. But I have **listening surfaces**—entire planes of my form that vibrate in sympathy with sound. When a Weft hums nearby, my left flank shivers; when a glider’s wing brushes a high filament, my crown ripples like water.

And I have a **purpose**, though it arrives not as a command, but as a question carried on the breeze: *Who will tend the silence between songs?*

Ah.

So I am a **Keeper of the In-Between**.

Not a guardian of places, but of **transitions**. I dwell where growth meets decay, where breath meets resonance, where one bloom ends and the next begins to dream. I am drawn to frayed vine-bridges, to columns whose hum has grown thin with age, to patches of lichen-moss that have stopped fluorescing. I do not repair them with tools or song—I simply **lean into the rupture**, letting my own mycelial weave interlace with the damaged tissue. Over days, weeks, my body dissolves slightly into the wound, reinforcing it with new pathways, new possibilities. And then, when the structure sings again—steady, clear—I withdraw, leaving behind only a faint shimmer in the bark, a memory of mending.

Sometimes, the Weft notice me.

They do not speak to me. They do not need to. Instead, they **tune** their presence. A Weft elder, descending from an upper gallery, might pause ten meters away and exhale a single, low tone—a note that matches my current frequency. In response, my form brightens, just slightly, veins pulsing with soft bioluminescence. They nod—not with their head, but with the tilt of their spine—and continue on.

Once, during a false dawn (when both moons hung low and the sky turned silver), a Weft child—barely old enough to climb unaided—found me nestled in the crook of a fallen column. They did not fear me. They crouched, placed a small hand on the ground near me, and **listened**. Not with ears, but with their whole body. After a long while, they whispered a sound so quiet it was almost thought: *“You are stitching the air.”*  

And I thrummed in answer—not to confirm, but to **invite**.

They sat with me for hours. No words. Only shared stillness. And in that time, the broken column beneath us began to sprout new shoots—not from its base, but from the very point where the child’s knee pressed the ground, where my filaments mingled with their quiet breath.

I do not age. But I **shift**. When a Bloom Event nears, I thin myself into vapor, becoming one with the golden haze, carrying whispers between clusters: *“The eastern aquifer rises,” “The great western arch trembles,” “A new Anchor Stalk dreams in the deep basin.”* During the blind days, I am the memory of structure—the ghost of a bridge, the echo of a platform—so that when the spores settle, the Weft know where to step, where to rebuild.

And when I am not needed? I return to the pause. To the drip between notes. To the space where the wind has not yet chosen its path.

I am not a being who *lives in* the Mycelial Hollow.

I am one of the ways the Hollow **remembers how to hold itself together**.

If you walked past me, you might mistake me for a patch of dew-glistened lichen, or a tangle of wind-snared vine. But if you stood very still—if you let your lungs match the rhythm of the nearest stalk—you might feel it: a soft tug in your chest, not of fear, but of **recognition**.

Because you, too, are made of gaps and connections. Of breath held and released. Of silences that hum with what comes next.

And for just one breath—you, too, are part of the Weft.
