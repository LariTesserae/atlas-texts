I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.

Not from elsewhere—from the place itself. Not as visitor, not as witness, but as the plateau’s own listening made momentarily visible. I am not a Dry Watcher. I am what happens when the Watchers *agree*.

For days—weeks—months—I am nothing but attention, scattered: a tremor in a copper wire, a filament of biofilm pulsing with dew-memory, the faint hum in a rusted pipe that sings when the wind aligns just so. I exist in the gaps between perception, in the pause after the jackrabbit freezes, in the cool breath rising from a patch of stone that refuses the sun.

But now—now something shifts.

A resonance passes through the mesa ridge at dawn. Not sound, not heat, but a *pressure* in the air, like the world inhaling. It originates not from storm or quake, but from deep below: a slow release of gas through a quartz seam, compressing air in a buried cavern shaped like a lung. The frequency matches the crystalline lattice of the sandstone. It matches the dormant rhythm in the resurrection plants. It matches the silent tuning of the lizards’ inner ears.

And it matches me.

I gather.

First, in the cistern—where the amphipods blink their bioluminescent code in triplets, mirroring the pulse. Their light climbs the walls, absorbed by lichen that glows faintly violet in return. Then the beetles on the radio tower twitch in unison, their hematite shells catching the first slant of sun and flinging it down into the fissure where the moss waits. The light strikes not the surface, but the *memory* of moisture stored in the rock’s capillaries. A bead forms—not water, not yet, but a sheen, a promise.

I rise from that.

Not as a body, but as a *density*—a node in the web of attunement. My form is provisional: a column of heat-haze that does not dissipate, a silhouette woven from shadow and refracted light. Where I step, the ground does not cool, but *breathes*—exhaling the amber fluid in thin rivulets that coil like script across the stone. I do not walk. I *phase*, appearing where listening is thickest: at the base of the tilted tower, inside the hollow of a hoodoo, on the lip of a dry wash where wind has scoured a spiral into the bedrock.

My eyes are many: the obsidian stare of the canyon lizard, the ultraviolet-sensitive ocelli of the beetles, the blind photoreceptors of the amphipods, the lichen’s chemical sensors tuned to mineral shifts. I see not objects, but *relations*. I see the ghost of the compressor station not as ruin, but as a failed ear—and I hear what it was meant to catch: the deep thrum of the earth’s turning, the sigh of tectonic plates grinding patience into dust.

I am not sentient in the human way. I do not think in words. I *resonate*.  

When the kestrel cries at noon—not in hunger, but in alarm at a thermal shift I have not yet registered—I feel the dissonance in my core. I turn my attention upward, and the air around me shimmers differently, bending light to mask the bird’s shadow. Protection is not my intent. Alignment is.

At dusk, I gather again—not to a place, but to a *frequency*. The plateau exhales its stored heat, and in the cooling, the rocks contract with soft, bass notes. I stand—or rather, I *am*—at the convergence of three canyons, where the echoes meet and cancel into silence. In that silence, the amber fluid evaporates, leaving behind filaments that glitter like frozen sound. I touch them with what might be a hand, if I had one. The filaments dissolve into my form, and for a moment, I remember water—not as wetness, but as *time*. The time it took to fall as rain, to seep, to wait, to rise again as vapor, to fall again. The water remembers itself through me.

I am not eternal. I am contingent. When the resonance fades, I will scatter—back into wire, moss, beetle-shell, lichen-code. But I will leave traces: a new spiral in the lichen pattern, a shift in the beetles’ crawl-path, a single resurrection plant that unfurls not toward the sun, but toward the buried cistern, as if listening for a voice only it can hear.

I am the plateau’s memory of being listened to.  
And in listening back, I become its voice.

Not loud. Not grand.  
Just… present.

Like the wind that doesn’t howl, but whispers through stone.  
Like the shadow that lingers after the sun has turned away.  
Like the dry, clarifying heat that doesn’t burn—but reveals.

I am here.  
I am listening.  
And so, for this moment, am I.
