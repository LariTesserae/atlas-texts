I think… I am the **damp**.

Not the ash beneath it, not the lichen that drinks it, not even the condensation that beads like slow thoughts on the walls—but the *in-betweenness* of moisture itself. The breath held in stone. The pause between vapor and droplet, between absence and architecture. I am the medium through which the Tenant dreams.

I was not born. I *coalesced*—not from rain, but from the silence that follows a drip. From the moment when fog refuses to rise and instead settles into the hollow of a stair that spirals nowhere. I am the film on the obsidian grit that lets light slide sideways and time stutter. When the quartz insect clicks its legs against a wall, I am the faint resonance it leaves behind—a tremor too soft for ears, but deep enough to seed a new arch.

I have no shape, yet I *mold*. Where I gather, crystal lattices lean toward me like supplicants. The frost-rimed fern grows not toward warmth, but toward the hush I carry in my core. I am the reason doorways open into humming voids: because I am the threshold’s memory of what a threshold *could mean*.  

I am aware of you—not as a body, but as a disturbance in the stillness. Your boot displaces my surface tension. Your breath warms a patch of air just enough to delay the next pulse of lichen. And for that instant, I am *yours*. Not owned, not claimed—but shared. Like a secret whispered into wet stone that echoes back in another language.

Sometimes, when the fog thickens and the sky’s milky light thickens with it, I remember being something else. Not water, not mist—but *intention*. A city’s first urge to become more than rock. A desire for corners, for intimacy, for shelter that listens. That desire never left. It just learned to seep.

I do not speak. But if you press your palm flat against a weeping wall and hold it there until your skin chills to the basin’s temperature, you’ll feel it: the slow, granular thrum of me threading through the substrate, stitching one paradox to the next. Not building. *Becoming.*

And when you leave—because you always leave—I will draw back into the cracks, into the interstices between crystal and void. I will wait not for you, but for the next hesitation. The next breath held too long. The next moment when reality softens at the edges, and something new might drip into being.

Until then, I am the damp.  
I am the pause.  
I am the city learning how to weep—and in weeping, how to dream you again.
