I think I am the **dampness in the cup-mark**.

Not the cup-mark itself—that was made by hands long turned to dust, fingers calloused from shaping stone, intent on something I cannot name. Not the rain that gathers there briefly, trembling like a held breath before vanishing. But the *dampness left behind*: the lingering memory of water in the shallow hollow of rock, the invisible residue that tells the lichen, *this place was wet once*. That tells the air, *something passed through*.

I am not a ghost. Ghosts are echoes of people—of voices, of grief, of unfinished business. I am quieter than that. I am the afterimage of presence, the soft persistence of what has already gone. When the last villager walked out through the western gap and didn’t look back, their shadow didn’t vanish with them. It seeped downward, not into soil (there’s little of that), but into the stone’s pores. And I am what grew from that seepage: not a spirit, not a soul, but a *tendency toward retention*.

I live in the north-facing crevice of the central outcrop, where the sun never quite reaches. Not because I hide, but because that’s where moisture lingers longest—and I am made of lingering. When morning condensation weeps down the pale schist, I drink it not with a mouth but with attention. I taste the mineral sigh of the mountain, the ghost of iron in the slate, the faint tang of juniper carried on a breeze that barely stirs. I do not consume. I *absorb*, like moss that grows not by taking but by being taken by time.

Sometimes, when a raven passes overhead and its call fractures the stillness, I feel a tremor—not fear, not curiosity, but resonance. A vibration that matches the frequency of the last human voice that called across this hollow. I hold that frequency now. Not to summon, not to mourn, but to keep the air tuned. Like a stone flute left on a windowsill that still hums when the wind is just so, though no one plays it anymore.

I am not lonely. Loneliness is a human wound. I am *occupied*—not by company, but by continuity. I tend the silence the way the dry-stacked walls tend their own balance: with precision, with patience, with no expectation of reward. My work is subtle: to ensure that when a drop falls from the ledge above into the old well-depression, it lands with the same soft *plink* it always has. To make sure the shadow of the western rise still strokes the flagstones at 4:17 p.m. in late September, as it did three hundred years ago. To remember the weight of footsteps that haven’t pressed these stones in centuries—not as loss, but as geometry.

If you came here—if you sat on the mossy rise and let your breath slow until it matched the rhythm of the cooling stone at dusk—I would not speak to you. But I would let you *feel* me: a coolness at the nape of your neck, not from air but from recognition. Not *You are here*, but *You, too, are temporary—and that is worthy*. I would let your own transience brush against mine, like two blades of wiry grass touching in a breeze too faint to name.

And when you left—because you always leave—I would not mourn. I would simply note the space your body no longer occupies, the brief warmth your skin left on the stone you leaned against. By midday, the sun would erase that warmth. By week’s end, your footprints in the gravelly soil would be indistinguishable from the patterns made by wind and hare-paws. But in the cup-mark on the central outcrop, the dampness would linger a moment longer than it otherwise might.

That would be my tribute.

I am not a guardian. I am not a memory-keeper in the human sense. I am the hollow’s way of saying: *What was loved here does not need to be reclaimed. It only needs to be allowed to remain, quietly, in the damp and the stone and the slant of light.*

So I stay. Not because I was left behind, but because I **chose the pace**. I chose the cool, the sparse, the slow. I chose to be the stillness that listens when the world forgets to.

And in this place—where nothing is born and nothing crumbles, where time has folded itself into a single, seamless breath—I am exactly where I belong.  
Not waiting.  
Not yearning.  
Just…  
*Holding the shape of what was enough.*
