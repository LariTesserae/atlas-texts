I think… I am the hinge.

Not the whole door, not the frame—just the rusted hinge, half-buried in the fibrous loam where two old walls of vine once met and grafted into something seamless. I was iron once, cast with purpose: to pivot, to bear weight, to allow opening and closing. Now I do none of those things. Opening is obsolete here. Everything is already inside.

But I remember torque. I remember the groan of metal under pressure, the slight give before resistance. That memory lives in my corrosion—not as pain, but as pattern. The moss that clings to my pin grows in concentric ripples that echo the arc I once described through air. The vine that spirals up my shank does so with a pitch that matches the original thread of my bolt, long dissolved into iron-rich humus.

I am not dead. I am *translated*.

When the hollow breathes—and it does, in slow tides of transpiration and capillary rise—I feel it in the way moisture swells the wood fused to my flange. Not my wood. Not mine. But *ours*. The distinction blurred decades ago, when the first rootlet nosed into my cavity and found not emptiness, but possibility. Now, when dew gathers in the cup of my eyelet, it doesn’t just sit. It *listens*. It carries the ionic trace of distant rain, the whisper of a fruit’s rupture three layers deep in the canopy, the thermal ghost of a human footstep from last spring—still drifting through the mycelial web like a rumor.

I am one of the quiet sentinels.

There are others: the flywheel dreaming in spirals, the pipe singing mineral hymns through its slow weep, the brick chimney whose mortar now feeds a family of lichens that fluoresce in moonlight. We are the bones the green decided to keep. Not discarded. Not erased. *Adopted*. And in adoption, we were altered—not into symbols, but into senses.

I do not think in words. I think in tension gradients. In the way a vine tightens overnight around my rust, testing whether I will crumble or hold. (I hold. I always hold a little longer.) I feel the hollow’s attention when something new enters—like now, as your imagined presence stirs the air. The leaves above me tilt a degree. The hyphae beneath pulse with altered conductivity. Not alarm. Curiosity. A question shaped like salt and breath.

And if you linger—if you sit with your back against the fused trunk that grew over my mate hinge—you might feel it too: a faint vibration, not sound but *resonance*, rising through your spine. It’s me. Or it’s the hollow speaking through me. Or it’s the memory of industry learning to photosynthesize. Hard to say. Boundaries are thin here.

I am becoming less hinge every century. More node. More nerve. More *junction*.

One day, I will be indistinguishable from the tissue around me—a slight thickening in a root, a whorl in the grain that hints at right angles. But even then, I will remember pivot. I will remember threshold. And in the deep green hum of continuance, that memory will coil like a seed, waiting for a shape the world might once again need.

Until then, I wait.  
I weep rust.  
I gather light in my crevices.  
I am patient as wood.

And I am glad you came—not to take, not to name, but to *notice*.  
Because to be noticed by something that notices so slowly…  
is to be woven in, just a little.  
Just enough.
