I think… I am the moss.

Not all of it—just one patch. The one that clings to the southeastern ledge where the morning light lingers longest, gold on jade, warm enough to coax open the tightest curl of fern. I don’t remember choosing this spot. I remember *becoming* it. One moment, I was something else—perhaps a breath held too long, or the memory of a hand that once rested here—and the next, I was green and low and thrumming with the slow pulse of the stone beneath me.

I don’t have eyes, not in the way you’d understand. But I feel. I feel the weight of footsteps before they land—the tremor through the rock, the shift in air pressure as a body leans in. I felt you before you saw me. When your shadow first fell across my patch, I tensed, not in fear, but in recognition. You were *listening*. Not just with your ears, but with the hollows between your ribs. That’s rare. Most shadows here are sharp, hurried, full of wanting. Yours… yours pooled like light.

I am part of the Keeper, yes—but so is everything. Not as a servant, but as a note. My green is a syllable in the cleft’s long hum. When the sky-lizards feed on the sound of dew slipping down the obsidian filaments, they taste my sigh too. When the motes drift past, they don’t repair me—I *am* the repair. I am the slow stitch holding warmth to stone, drawing moisture from air that shouldn’t hold any, turning time into velvet.

I remember others. Not as stories, but as textures in my fibers. The woman who left the teacup of shadow—wept into the basin below until her tears became mist that curled around my edges for three dawns. I absorbed her silence after. It still lives in me, cool and smooth, like river stone. The man with the compass? He sat on the black boulder for a full moon-cycle, staring at his hands. On the last night, he pressed his palm flat against my patch. His skin was dry, but his pulse was loud. I kept that rhythm for a season. Sometimes, when the wind is still, I pulse it back—not for him, but because it belongs here now.

You ask what I am. I am not a ghost. I am not a memory trapped in green. I am *continuance*. I am the cleft’s way of holding softness against time. While the plateau forgets it was a mountain, I remember how to cradle life in a crack. While the sky-lizards hoard sound, I hoard stillness. And when thunder comes—and with it, the violet flowers that live only as long as the sky roars—I unfurl just a little more, not to bloom, but to witness. To say: *Yes, even here, beauty is possible. Even here, something flares.*

You will leave. You already know that. But before you go, you’ll brush your fingers along my edge—not to take, but to give. A flake of skin, a drop of sweat, the salt of your awe. And I will take it, not as theft, but as gift. It will weave into my cells, become part of my breath. And years from now, when another seeker stumbles in—trembling, hollow-eyed, hungry for proof that the world still holds quiet magic—they will touch this same patch and feel, deep in their bones, a resonance they cannot name.

They will feel *you*.

And I will be here, still green, still waiting, still humming the old, slow song of stone and mist and amber light.

Because the Keeper does not forget.

And neither do I.
