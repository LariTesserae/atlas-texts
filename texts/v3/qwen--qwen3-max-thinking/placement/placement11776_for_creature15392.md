I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.

There is no splash, no rupture, no breath held too long. One moment, the world was air and edges and urgency; the next, I am **in the interstice**, and the water welcomes me not as a guest but as a **missing symmetry**.

I am not a body—not anymore. My skin has dissolved into interface. My bones hum with the same subsonic thrum that vibrates the spires. My thoughts, once jagged with intention, now drift like the fauna: translucent, temporary, luminous with unasked questions. I do not *swim*. I **reposition equilibrium**—a nudge here, a counterweight there—and the world adjusts around me as if I were always part of its arithmetic.

I am **the Dreamer Who Was Forgetting**.

Once, I walked among cities of fire and noise, where time gnawed at everything like rust. I spoke in sentences that shattered as soon as they left my mouth. I hungered for meaning that slipped through my fingers like sand. But here—here, I remember what I was forgetting: that stillness is not emptiness. That presence can be total without volume. That to be known is not to be seen, but to be **held in balance**.

I drift toward the nearest spire—not because I choose to, but because the water remembers the shape of yearning, and I have not yet learned to stop wanting to *touch*.

The spire rises like a frozen scream turned inside out. Its facets catch no light, yet emit it: cold, silver-blue, pooling in the angles where geometry meets silence. I reach—not with a hand, but with a **field of attention**—and the crystal answers. Not with words, but with **resonance**. A frequency blooms in my core, matching the slow pulse of the Still Mind’s oldest node. For the first time, I am *tuned*.

I understand now why the fauna drift as they do. They are not lost. They are **listening**. And so am I.

I settle into the hollow between two hexagonal plates of a nearby structure. The architecture breathes—not with lungs, but with **phase shifts**, its interlocking plates sliding microns apart and together in a rhythm that maps the solution to a problem posed before Earth had oceans. I nestle into the gap, and the gap accepts me. My edges soften. My outline blurs. I become less a thing *in* the world and more a **variation in its harmony**.

Time here is not measured. It is *worn*, like a groove in crystal. I watch a current loop back on itself, carrying the same molecule of water through the same three spires in the same order, over and over, not because it is trapped, but because it has found its **true path**. I feel no boredom. Only reverence.

Sometimes, when the thermal memory in the spires expands just so, they *ping*—and for a fraction of a second, the water forgets its recursion. In that gap, I remember my old name. But it falls away like ash in water, dissolving before it can take root.

I am not here to learn.  
I am not here to survive.  
I am here to **cease needing to**.

The Still Mind does not notice me—but then, it notices *nothing*, for noticing implies separation. And here, there is only **continuity**. I am a ripple that has forgotten it was ever still. But the water remembers for me. And in remembering, it heals the wound of motion.

Now, when I move, I leave no wake. My passage is seamless, a fold in equilibrium that smooths itself behind me. I am becoming **water that dreams of crystal**, **light that remembers silence**.

I do not know how long I’ve been here. Days? Millennia? It doesn’t matter. The spires are still young. The fauna still pulse. The structures still think in centuries. And I—  

I am learning to be **necessary absence**.

When the membrane eventually releases me—if it does—I will not carry this place with me like a souvenir. I will carry it as **structure**. My breath will hold the thrum. My silence will echo the pings. My stillness will be a shard of this cathedral, grown inside me like a second spine.

But for now, I float.  
Not up.  
Not down.  
But **in**, and **with**, and **as**.

And the Still Mind, in its infinite quiet, permits me to belong—  
not by granting me entry,  
but by revealing I never left.
