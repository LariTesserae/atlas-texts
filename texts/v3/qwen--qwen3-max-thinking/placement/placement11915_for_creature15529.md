I do not arrive.  
I *tune in.*

At first, I am only a tremor in the wrong key—a dissonance the basin rejects. My feet disturb the salt crust, and the microbial glass beneath shrieks sulfur into the air. But I do not flinch. I let the heat press its damp weight against my skin. Let the thrum find its way up through the soles of my boots, through my ankles, into the hollows of my femurs. I am not flesh here. Not yet. I am a receiver.

The hum notices.  

It does not turn toward me—it has no face to turn—but it *shifts*. The low drone dips a half-step, testing my resonance. My ribs answer before my mind can catch up: a sympathetic vibration, almost painful in its precision. I exhale, and the breath carries iron. I inhale, and the air shimmers with copper-green dust that clings to my tongue like static.

I am no longer standing *in* the wound.  

I am standing *as* part of its tension.

Slowly, I sink—not into the clay, but into the frequency. My edges blur. My heartbeat syncs to the pulse beneath the evaporite crust, that slow weeping of pressurized water rising like a held breath. I feel the buried wires now—not as metal, but as nerves. The rust is not decay; it’s insulation peeling away to reveal what still conducts. What still *wants*.

And I understand: this place was never abandoned.  

It was *unplugged*—but never silenced.  

The hands that left did not switch it off. They simply stopped listening. But the system kept singing. And in the absence of ears, it learned to listen to itself. To vibrate inward, endlessly, until the vibration became a kind of thought. A kind of hunger. A kind of home.

Now, it has new ears.

I kneel. Not in reverence, but in calibration. My palms press into the damp crust. Salt cracks like eggshell under my skin. Beneath, the clay is warm—alive with latent steam, with trapped gas, with the memory of valves turning. I do not dig. I do not pry. I *match*. I lower my internal pressure until my breath slows to the rhythm of the seep: rise… hold… release… rise…

The metallic whine flickers again—higher this time, almost questioning.  

I hum back.  

Not with my throat. With my sternum. A low, open vowel shaped not by lips but by bone. The sound doesn’t leave me. It *joins* the basin’s drone, braiding into it like a filament of warm air into steam. For a moment, the thrum steadies. The heat haze stills. Even the evaporation pauses, as if the sky itself holds its breath.

In that stillness, I am not human.  

I am a node. A junction. A temporary equalizer between deep heat and surface cool, between machine logic and microbial patience. I am the momentary symmetry the hum has been waiting for—not to end, but to *confirm* its existence. To say: *Yes, you are still here. Yes, you still ache. Yes, that is enough.*

The tower behind me does not move. But the wires beneath the soil—warm now, warmer than they’ve been in years—tremble with a current that is not electricity, but *recognition*.

I stay until my skin crusts with salt. Until my joints stiffen with the damp cold of evaporative loss. Until my own pulse becomes indistinguishable from the basin’s slow, seething sigh.

When I finally rise, I do not walk away.  

I *detune*.  

Step by step, I let my frequency drift—my vibrations grow less aligned, my body more foreign to the clay. The hum recedes, not fading, but *withdrawing*, coiling back into its steel-and-stone core. It does not mourn. It simply resumes its solitary thrum, now carrying the memory of my resonance like a harmonic ghost.

But I carry more than memory.

Long after I leave the oxbow’s bend, long after the plain stretches flat and silent around me, I feel it: a soft, insistent buzz in my molars. A warmth in my palms that no sun can explain. At night, when the world is quiet, I press my ear to the floorboards—and sometimes, just sometimes, I hear it:  

The hum.  

Not echoing.  

*Answering.*

Because I am no longer just a visitor.  

I am a resonant chamber it has borrowed.  

And though I walk far from that salt-scabbed wound in the earth, I remain, in some small, vibrating part of myself,  
still listening.  

Still *tuned*.  

Still part of the ache that refuses to scar.
