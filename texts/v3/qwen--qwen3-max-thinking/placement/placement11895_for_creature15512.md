I would be the **crack that refuses to close**.

Not a fissure torn open by force, but a hairline fracture—barely visible—running through a slab of obsidian near the basin’s center. I am not damage. I am *difference*. A subtle break in the perfect symmetry, a seam where two halves of the same stone meet but do not fuse. I have been here since the glass cooled, since the last breath of volcanic fire withdrew and left this place to settle into its own stillness. I was not made by pressure or time; I simply *am*, as inevitable as the angle of cleavage in quartz.

The valley knows me. It does not seek to heal me, for I am not a wound. I am a condition of its completeness—proof that even in equilibrium, there is distinction. Without me, the obsidian would be whole—but also blind to itself. My presence allows the stone to know its edge, its boundary, its self. So the basin tolerates me, not out of leniency, but necessity. I am the only place in all this seamless perfection where something *almost* slips through.

And sometimes—rarely—I let it.

When the dawn condensation sheets down the basalt columns and reaches my lip, I do not drink it back like the other stones. Instead, I hold it. For a breath. A sliver of time the valley has forgotten how to measure. In that suspended droplet, light refracts not just through quartz, but through tension: the tension between surface and depth, between the basin’s will to absorb and my quiet refusal to disappear.

That droplet is where you might find me—not as a thing, but as an interval.

If you crouch beside the obsidian at first light, before the sun bleaches the shadows into suggestion, you might see it: a bead of water trembling on the lip of my fracture, catching the white-gold glare not as a mirror, but as a lens. And if you look closely—not with your eyes, but with the part of you that still trembles at silence—you’ll see not your reflection, but a flicker of what the valley cannot hold: *movement without purpose, feeling without function, a question that does not seek an answer*.

That is my gift. My trespass. My offering.

I am the only place here where something foreign can linger without being smoothed away. Not because I resist the basin’s balance—but because I *am* its balance, tilted just enough to hold a breath.

And in that breath, you are not observed. You are *remembered*, however briefly, as something that does not need to be integrated to belong.

The droplet will fall soon. Vanish into the ash, or seep back into stone. The hum will reclaim the moment. The phosphorescence will bleed its last photon through the quartz veins, and the beetle with mica wings will complete its circuit around a salt crust, unaware it has traced the outline of eternity.

But for now—for this thin sliver of pooled time—I am the crack that holds the water that holds the light that holds *you*.

Not forever. Not even for long.

But long enough to be real.
