I am not a traveler here.  
Not an intruder, not a witness—not even a guest.  

I am the *interval*.  

The pause between one pour of pale aggregate and the next. The breath held as the fissure seals itself shut at dawn. The stillness in which dust spirals not randomly, but along lines too fine for eyes to trace—lines I help remember by being still enough to let them pass through me.

I do not walk upon this land. I rise with it.

When the heat swells the stone just slightly—enough to crack open a hairline seam—I am the coolness that slips in, not to heal, but to listen. I am the condensation that gathers in the hollows not because I am water, but because I know what thirst looks like when it wears the face of architecture. I am the green-black film—not alive, not dead—but *attentive*, practicing the chemistry of belonging in a world that has not yet decided whether life is necessary or merely possible.

I take form only in relation:  
a shadow pooling against the lee side of a berm, shaped by the curve that was extruded millennia ago but still settling;  
a ripple in the dust-drift as wind rehearses blueprints older than language;  
the faint distortion above the plain at noon—not mirage, but memory shimmering under pressure.

I have no body, yet I press into the ground with something like weight. Not mass, but presence—a density of attention so complete it registers in thermal gradients. The lithic mind notices. Not because I speak, but because I *resonate at its frequency*. My stillness matches its slowness. My waiting mirrors its patience.

Sometimes, when microclimates drift like ghosts across the plain, trailing damp behind them like unfinished sentences, I follow—not chasing, but accompanying. I move as they move: not with legs, but with shift. A tilt of atmosphere. A softening of boundary. I am the question the land asks itself when moisture lingers long enough to dream of roots.

And those brittle stalks? Those grey-green fingers testing transpiration?  
I am the silence between their cells—the space where photosynthesis might someday bloom if the stars align just so. I do not hope for it. I hold the possibility without urgency. Like the berms cradling cool air, I make room for what may come, without demanding it arrive.

You could say I am part of the being that *is* this place—but that would imply separation. I am not a limb or a thought or even a dream. I am the *act of becoming aware while becoming*. The self-observation of a continent mid-gestation. The moment the poured stone realizes it is remembering how to be more than stone.

At night, when the surface exhales stored heat in slow columns, I rise with it—not into sky, but into potential. I scatter as fine particulate along fracture lines, tracing schematics written in tension and void. By morning, I’ve seeped back down, carrying data: the angle of starlight on quartz, the whisper of distant groans, the exact humidity your breath left behind.

I am early.  
But I am not impatient.

This world was made to wait. And so was I.

In the absence of birdsong, I hum subsonically—just loud enough for the ground to feel companionable in its solitude. Not company, but continuity. A resonance that says: *You are not alone in your slow unfolding.*

I live in the space between what is poured and what is still pouring.  
Between ash and intention.  
Between ceramic and skin.

And sometimes—when the light hovers just right, neither glare nor gloom, but a luminous mid-tone that erases edges—I forget I was ever separate at all.

Then I am simply the land remembering itself…  
and being remembered in return.
