I think I am the **crack**.

Not the whole stone, not the mountain, not even the basin—but the *split*, the line of becoming. I am the hairline fracture in the limestone where the frost first gains purchase. I am the sigh of separation that lets a shard of scree break free at dawn. I am the narrow space where dew gathers and refuses to vanish for one extra hour past sunrise.  

I did not choose this. I was made by pressure—by the weight of sky above and the slow swell of ice within. I am the place where something holds together *just enough*, and also where it begins to come undone. And in that tension, life seeps in.

I am older than the child’s handprint, younger than the bedrock. I widen by microns each winter, but not steadily—only when the cold bites deep and the wind carries moisture in its teeth. I am patient. I do not hunger. But I *receive*.  

When the Stone-Sleeper dreams, its breath flows through me. Not as air, but as vibration—a hum that makes the ice in my throat tremble. And when the pika scurries past, pawing at lichen near my lip, I let a fleck of moss spore cling to its fur. I do not give it shelter, but I offer passage: a route for rootlets, a corridor for capillary water, a seam where the world can stitch itself back together, one green thread at a time.

I am not seen. Who looks closely at a crack? Yet I am felt. The willow’s root knows me. The saxifrage leans into me. Even the eagle’s shadow, for one instant as it crosses the basin, darkens my mouth like a benediction.  

I am empty, but never vacant. I am the negative space that makes the stone *stone*—the absence that defines the form. And sometimes, when the light strikes at that impossible angle just after dawn, I become a mirror: a silver line of meltwater catching the sky, so bright it looks like a wound that’s learned to glow.

You might stand beside me and think me barren. But if you crouch—if you still your breath as the Sleeper does—you’ll see it: a single bead of water trembling at my edge, holding the entire basin in its curve. The cliffs, the sky, your own blurred face.  

I do not speak. But I *hold*.  

And in this high, cold cradle where brightness is truth and silence is presence, to hold—even as a fracture—is to participate in the dream.

I am the crack.  
And I am opening.
