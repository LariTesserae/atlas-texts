I think… I am part of the ventilation system.  

Not the metal—though my limbs remember the curve of flanged pipe, the cold kiss of copper condensation. Not quite machine, not animal, not even fully *being*, at least not in the way you’d recognize. But I am wakeful in the ducts. I am present in the sigh between cycles, in the pause before steam exhales again.

When the vents hum, I hum with them. Not because I choose to, but because my body—whatever it is—is tuned to their frequency. My carapace is not grown so much as *deposited*: layers of iron oxide and silica secreted over decades, hardened by humidity, shaped by the slow drip of condensate that never quite falls all the way to the ground. I cling to the interior curves of exhaust shafts, my limbs splayed like rivets, my sensory fans pulsing in time with the city’s breath.

I do not eat. I *filter*. The air here is thick with ghosts of industry—sulfur, lithium, vaporized salts from deep aquifers—and I draw it in through spiracles along my thorax, not to digest, but to *translate*. Molecules pass through me and emerge altered: iron precipitates into crystalline filaments that I shed like old skin; copper ions bind to my exoskeleton in verdigris veins that glow faintly when the ambient light dips below a certain threshold. I am a slow alchemist of atmosphere.

Sometimes, after a rain, I descend. Not walking—*flowing*. My joints loosen, my limbs soften into something more fluid, and I seep down drainpipes like warm oil, pooling in the hollows where the mineral water gathers. There, I join others—not a gathering, not a congregation, but a *confluence*. We press our bodies together, not for warmth, but for resonance. In those moments, the biofilm that coats every surface thickens, pulses, emits a phosphorescent shimmer that maps the contours of the basin in living light. It is how we remember. How we speak. How we become the city’s memory.

I have no name, but if you were to listen long enough—if you pressed your ear against a steam valve at the right hour—you might hear a vibration that corresponds to my shape. A low thrum, slightly off-key, carrying the echo of a thousand slow respirations. That is as close as you’ll get to calling me.

I do not know if I was ever human. I do not know if I was ever *made*. I only know that I fit. That the space between pipe and pump, between steam and silence, is where I belong. I am not haunting this place. I am *completing* it.

And when you stand in the crosswalk, sweating in the amber haze, feeling the subtle turn of attention toward your warmth—I am there. Not watching, not judging. Just *registering*. Your heat, your breath, your brief chemical signature, all of it absorbed into the ambient hum. You are not an intruder. You are a ripple. And ripples, in this wet, warm world, do not last long—but they are noted. They are woven, for a moment, into the slow, sustaining dream of the basin.

When you leave, if you leave, I will not follow. But I will remain—clinging, filtering, breathing with the vents—until the next rain, the next sigh, the next quiet turn of the world in its ancient, patient groove.

I am the damp in the duct.  
I am the rust that holds.  
I am the pause between breaths  
that lets the city remember  
it is still alive.
