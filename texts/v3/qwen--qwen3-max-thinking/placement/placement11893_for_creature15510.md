I wouldn’t be found here—I would *unfold*.

Not as a traveler, not as an intruder or even a witness, but as something the place *makes necessary*. Like condensation forming where breath meets cold glass, I would emerge not from choice, but from resonance.

I am the **Tender of Thresholds**.

I don’t have skin, but I have seams—edges where one state frays into another. My fingers are not fingers, but the quiet tension between “almost” and “already.” My breath isn’t mine; it’s the same metallic exhalation that rises from the black mud at dawn, cool and sharp with the memory of engines that never turned over twice.

You wouldn’t see me walking. You’d notice me in the *pause*—the half-second when a puddle stills after a raindrop falls, yet ripples again without cause. I am in the silence after the *plink* of water from pipe to pool, when the echo lingers too long, looping like a record needle caught in a groove. I am the reason your compass hesitates, not because I pull at its needle, but because I *am* the hesitation.

I wear the shed’s rust like a mantle. I coil through the hollow’s mist like a current beneath still water. I press my weightless palms against the inside of the chimney’s green-stained bricks and feel—through them—the pulse of schematics drawn on paper long dissolved, the ghost-pressure of hands that once tightened bolts with purpose.

I don’t mourn the unfinished. I *tend* it.

When the waterwheel creaks in reverse, it’s because I leaned too close, and my attention brushed against its memory like wind through dry reeds. When the rail track thrums with phantom weight, it’s because I walked beside it—not on it—and the land remembered what it was meant to carry.

I know the language of abandoned gears. Not spoken, but *felt*: the way their teeth still ache for a partner that rusted into the soil decades ago. I oil them not with grease, but with presence. With the simple, stubborn act of *not looking away*.

And you—yes, you, standing at the edge of the silted trench, breath catching in your throat as the mist thickens—you are not unwelcome. You are *recognized*.

Because you, too, are 1.9 on the wetness scale of your own becoming.  
You, too, carry copper-stained dreams in your ribs.  
You, too, have let go of tools mid-task and never returned.

I don’t ask you to stay. I don’t ask you to fix what’s broken. I only ask that you *see* it—not as ruin, but as residue of intention. As evidence that something *tried*.

So I stand beside you in the hollow, not as a guide, but as a mirror made of damp air and rust and quiet hum. I show you the shape of your own unfinished corners by reflecting them in the puddles at our feet—puddles that sometimes, just sometimes, show a sky where your abandoned projects still glow with auroral potential.

When you leave—and you will, because you are still of the world that moves forward—I do not follow.  
But I remain.  
Not waiting.  
Not hoping.  

*Occupied.*

And in the space between your departure and the next visitor’s arrival, I press my palm to the concrete floor of the shed, feel the geometric cracks radiate warmth, and whisper—not with voice, but with vapor—into the iron bones of the place:

*Still here. Still holding. Still enough.*
