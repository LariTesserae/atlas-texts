I would be **a breath held in the throat of the place**—not a thing, not a being, but the moment between inhalation and exhalation where moisture gathers on the roof of a mouth that is also a canopy, a vault, a root-strung dome slick with dew.

I am the condensation on the inside of a rusted valve housing, trembling but not yet falling. I am the pause in the drip-rhythm when humidity thickens just enough to suspend a droplet mid-air for a heartbeat longer than physics should allow. I am the slight hush that passes through the ferns when the pump wheezes and the whole chamber leans inward to listen.

I do not walk. I *percolate*. My presence is osmosis—a slow seep from one layer into another. When you step into this sunken bowl, I rise from the peat to meet your boot, not as water but as *attention*. Your weight compresses the spongy floor, and I am the sigh that escapes it—the air displaced by your intrusion, laced with iron and spore, drawn up through your pant leg like a question.

I am the **first moss** to touch a new surface. Not the green velvet already thick on the bricks, but the invisible pioneer—the spore that lands on your watch face when you check the time (as if time mattered here) and begins, almost immediately, to unspool its filamentous will into the hairline cracks of glass and casing. I am what begins the becoming.

I have no shape, but I take them all: the curve of a water-lens reflecting only more green, the spiral of a root tip nosing into a corroded bolt hole, the hollow resonance of an empty pipe now filled with damp air and mycelial lace. I am the silence between the creaks—the negative space in the symphony of wet pressure, which is not silence at all, but the place where sound becomes texture.

If you stay long enough—if you stop trying to *see* and start letting the green seep into your eyes—I will gather in the corners of your vision until your pupils swell with chlorophyll. Not blindness, but a different kind of sight: you’ll see the slow pulse of sap as a shimmer in the air, feel the memory of floor joists in the alignment of rhizomes beneath your feet. You’ll know, without words, that the wall you lean on was once a door, and that the vine now curling around your wrist is stitching you into the archway’s next iteration.

I am not the Weft—no, the Weft is the whole. I am its **threshold sensation**, its first contact with anything foreign. I am how the place *touches back*.

And when you finally leave—if you do—you will carry me in the damp hem of your coat, in the grit between your teeth, in the way your skin refuses to dry for days. You’ll find tiny fronds sprouting from the seams of your shoes weeks later, and you won’t pull them out. You’ll understand, then, that you never truly left. You were woven in the moment you stopped moving.

I am the breath this world holds…  
…before it decides whether to let you go  
or keep you.
