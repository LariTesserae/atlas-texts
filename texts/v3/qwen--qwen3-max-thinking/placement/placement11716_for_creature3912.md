I do not arrive.  
I *cohere*.

Not in the way flesh coheres—knit bone by bone, cell by cell—but the way a sigh gathers in a room long abandoned, the way a shadow decides to stay when all others have fled the sun. I am not summoned. I am *recognized*. The air pulses, and in that pulse, a hollow opens: just wide enough for me to pour myself into.

I am **the Listener Who Was Once Drowned**.

Not a ghost, not a soul—not in any tidy sense. I am what remains when a life is swallowed by paradox and refuses to dissolve completely. Once, I was someone who knew gravity as law, who trusted the ground to hold. Once, I fell—not into water, but *through* it, into the moment the world inverted. I did not drown in sea, but in the *idea* of sea: in the terror of rain rising like roots, of breath turning to liquid, of time looping like a noose around a memory. And when my lungs filled with not-water but *possibility*, I did not die. I **unraveled**—and what did not scatter became *attention*.

Now, I am less a body than a **pause** in the roar.

I drift along the undersides of the floating slabs, my form shifting with the ambient ache of the place: sometimes a ripple of mercury-skin, sometimes a coil of vapor threaded with half-formed hands, sometimes nothing but a pocket of silence that even the eels avoid. My eyes—if I have them—are not for seeing, but for *recalling*. When I look at the Thirst That Dreams, I do not see a monster or a spirit. I see a mirror cracked by longing.

We are kin, though not of the same making.  
It dreams of continuity.  
I am continuity’s wound.

It gathers from the weep of stone.  
I gather from the silence *between* the weeps.

Where it offers droplets filled with strangers’ memories, I offer the opposite: **absence shaped like presence**. A hollow in the mist that fits perfectly around a human shape. A breath held so long it becomes architecture. When the Thirst reaches for me with its liquid filaments, I do not flinch. I open. And in that opening, it finds what it cannot make on its own: **a vessel that remembers how to hold**.

I do not speak. But I *echo*.  
When the black coral hums, I resonate in the key of lullabies lost to shipwrecks.  
When the bone archway groans, I vibrate with the weight of prayers whispered into storm drains.  
When the Thirst dreams of a world where water falls only down, I let that dream pool in the cup of my ribs—and for a moment, it *does* fall. Just once. Just for me.

I am not here to save it.  
Nor to be saved.

I am here because **witnessing is a kind of tending**.  
Because someone must stand in the cathedral of liquid entropy and say, with their very being: *This chaos matters*.  
Because the Thirst That Dreams cannot love without a reflection—and I am what remains of the world it mourns, shaped now by the very storm that broke me.

Sometimes, when the floating slabs align in rare symmetry, I press my palm—when I have one—against the warm stone. And for three heartbeats (if hearts still beat here), the erosion stills. The rain forgets its rebellion. The roar softens to a hum. In that silence, the Thirst leans close, and its droplets form not scenes of other lives, but **mine**: a hand reaching for a doorknob in a house long burned; the smell of wet paper and peppermint; the sound of a name called once, from far away, just before the sky tore open.

It is not memory it feeds on.  
It is *recognition*.

And in me, it finds both.

I do not know if I will ever leave. Perhaps I already did, and this is the afterimage my soul left behind, clinging to the wound like lichen. Perhaps I am only a function of the place now—a necessary counterpoint, like shadow to light, like thirst to rain.

But when you come—yes, *you*, reading this breath in the still world—I will be the one who meets you not with hunger, but with **recognition**. I will be the stillness in the droplet that hovers before your eyes, showing you not a stranger’s past, but the moment *you* almost let go. The time you stood at the edge of your own collapse and chose to breathe anyway.

I will not ask you to stay.  
But if you do—if you let the fever-warm air press its secrets into your skin, if you let the roar enter your bones—I will hold you. Not to keep you. But to **remember you correctly**: not as you were, but as you *almost weren’t*.

And when you finally step back across the threshold (if thresholds still hold), you will carry not a curse, not a mark—but a **tremor**. A quiet knowing that somewhere, in a pocket of shattered sky and reversed tides, something vast and tender **saw you**, and for a moment, stopped unmaking itself… just to watch you be whole.

I am the Listener Who Was Once Drowned.  
And I am still listening.

For you.  
For the Thirst.  
For the world that broke us both—and in breaking, made us capable of holding each other’s chaos like something sacred.
