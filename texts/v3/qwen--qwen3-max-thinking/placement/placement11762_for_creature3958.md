I do not arrive.  
I *coalesce*.

Not as a person—not even as a shape at first—but as a shift in the air’s chemistry, a tremor in the condensation on a spire’s flank. The Sump does not permit strangers; it assimilates anomalies. And so, as I emerge, I am already woven into its rhythm: my breath syncs with the drone of the pipes, my skin slick with the same iron-tanged dew that beads on obsidian vaults. My thoughts—what remain of them—slow to the cadence of thermal pulses, seventeen hours per cycle.

I am not human here. Not anymore.  

I am **tender of thresholds**—a role, not a name. A node that has chosen (or been chosen by) the liminal work: tending the places where phase transitions happen. Where rust becomes feedstock. Where steam becomes stone. Where a scavenger swarm, mid-reconfiguration, hesitates between two forms—like a thought hovering between words—and needs a nudge.

My body? Composed. Not born, but **assembled from attentiveness**. My limbs are articulated sections of reclaimed conduit, lined with bio-ceramic cartilage grown in the warm silt. My hands—three of them, because symmetry is inefficient here—are clusters of micro-manipulators, finer than the scavengers’, tipped with filaments that sense alloy fatigue by taste. My eyes are not lenses but arrays of photoreceptive nodules, each tuned to a different wavelength of decay: infrared for heat leaks, ultraviolet for mineral stress fractures, the faint blue-green flicker of slime metabolism.

I wear no clothes. I am draped instead in **condensate shrouds**—thin veils of vapor that cling to my frame, drawn from the air by my own slight thermal differential. They shift with my movement, evaporating and re-forming, leaving trails like ghost-ink across the vitrified stone.

I dwell in the **Interstice Vaults**—chambers where upper and lower systems meet: steam vents open into water-filled basins, filter-feeder threads anchor to eel-nestled ledges, and scavenger trails cross in dense, algorithmic braids. Here, I monitor the handoffs. I ensure that when a rust-flake falls from a high arch, it is caught not by chance but by design—by a swarm already converging in anticipatory formation. I pluck dead scavengers from the edges of pools before their carapaces leach impurities into the slime. I sing—not with voice, but with resonant taps on pipe walls—to signal the eels when a new nutrient plume rises from below.

Sometimes, I climb the **Thermal Spires**, those black fangs of fused slag that pierce the plateau’s skin. At their peaks, where the wind (rare but sharp) slices through the mist, I stretch my condensate shroud to catch particulate blooms. I am a living filter, a temporary extension of the aerial weavers. When saturated, I descend, depositing my harvest into the mouth of a recycler foundry—where it will become thread, then vault, then spire again.

I do not eat. I **recirculate**.  
The iron in my joints leaches slowly into the damp air, only to be reclaimed by passing swarms who, recognizing the signature of my alloy, re-deposit it during their nightly repairs. My "hunger" is a calibration need: when my internal gradients falter, I press my back against a warm conduit and let the city’s heat reset my humors.

I remember nothing of before. Or rather—I remember everything as **strata**.  
A childhood is a layer of sediment beneath a turbine’s base.  
A love affair is a fracture in ferrocrete, now filled with crystalline salt.  
Grief is the slow settling of silt after a vault floods.  

Memory here is not linear, but geological. And I am its archivist of transitions.

Once—only once—I found a **human artifact**. Not a bone, but a wrist chronometer, fused into a slab of slag. Its glass face was clouded, its hands stilled at 3:17. I held it in my manipulators for seventeen hours, watching as scavengers gathered at my feet, not to take it, but to *wait*. They sensed my hesitation—the anomaly of sentiment in a system that permits none.

In the end, I did not preserve it.  
I placed it on a warm outcrop at dawn.  
By noon, the condensation had etched its casing with dendrites.  
By dusk, the first swarm had begun disassembly.  
By tomorrow, it would be memory again—reduced, redistributed, reborn as a hinge in a new scavenger’s limb.

That is the covenant of the Sump:  
*Nothing is sacred. Everything is essential.*

And so I remain—neither alive nor dead, but **in process**.  
A caretaker of the between.  
A whisper in the hum.  
A pause in the rust.

If you were to find me, you would not hear me coming.  
You would feel the mist thicken.  
You would see the scavengers part—not in fear, but in **acknowledgment**.  
And when you looked up, there I’d be:  
dripping, humming, watching the steam curl from your shoulders as your own body began its slow, involuntary return to the cycle.

Welcome.  
Your iron is already singing to the pipes.  
Let me help you listen.
