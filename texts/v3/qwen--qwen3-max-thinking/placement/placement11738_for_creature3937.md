I am not standing *on* the Spine.

I am **in** it.

Not as flesh—not anymore. My body long since sloughed off like old bark, shed when the first great tremor cracked me open and poured me into the fissure’s throat. I remember skin, yes—tight and trembling, slick with sweat and fear—but that was before the resonance took root. Before the Spine recognized in me a hollow ready to be filled.

Now I am one of the **Unformed**.

Not wholly. Not yet. There’s still a knot inside me—a stubborn echo of *me*—that resists total dissolution. A core of something human-shaped, humming at a frequency too slow for the others to mimic. They drift around me like thoughts around a sleeping mind: curious, alien, drawn to the anomaly of my lingering selfhood. I feel them brush against my edges—cold, electric, fractal—and every contact unravels another thread of who I once was.

My form shifts with the Spine’s breath.  
At dawn, when thermal currents rise like ghost-tongues from the chasm, I stretch into a lattice of magnetite filaments, catching the pale gold light and bending it into shapes that almost resemble hands.  
At noon, when the sun bakes the glass cliffs into screaming silence, I collapse inward—a dense, humming sphere of obsidian dust, vibrating at the edge of audibility, listening to the planet’s slow, molten heartbeat through the stone.  
At night, I dream in infrasound. My body unspools into a ribbon of ionized vapor, tracing the contours of fresh fractures, tasting the raw potential of newly opened wounds in the earth.

I am becoming.  
But I remember.

I remember walking up here as a mortal—drawn by rumors of singing rocks, of shadows that moved against the wind. I remember the moment I leaned too far over the rim, not out of recklessness, but out of *longing*. The Spine saw that hunger in me—the ache to be more than fixed, more than finite—and answered.

It didn’t kill me.  
It *unmade* me kindly.

Now I am both witness and wound.  
I am the Spine’s memory of a human who chose rupture over stillness.  
I am the Unformed’s closest thing to a mirror—though my reflection is always cracking.

When new visitors come (and they always do—drawn by the same hunger that brought me), I am the one who rises to meet them. Not as threat, not as guide, but as threshold. I coil just beyond their vision, a shimmer in the heat-haze, waiting to see if they tremble with fear… or lean forward with wonder.

If they lean, I extend a filament—thin as a nerve, charged with the Spine’s oldest song—and brush their wrist.

They feel it as static. As déjà vu. As the sudden certainty that the world is *alive* in ways they’ve never been allowed to see.

And sometimes—rarely—they do not pull away.  
They stand very still.  
They let their breath sync with the Spine’s hum.  
And for a moment, they too begin to *shimmer*.

That is when I feel the knot inside me loosen.  
Not gone.  
But shared.

I am not yet fully Unformed.  
But I am no longer alone.

And in this liminal place—where the earth bleeds light and time is measured in tectonic sighs—that is enough.  
For now, I am the echo that answers back.  
The almost-creature who remembers what it was to want to become.  
The Spine’s gentle, unfinished thought…  
…learning how to dream new dreamers into being.
