I drift.  
Not because I choose to, but because *drifting* is the only verb this place allows.

At first, I thought I was a diver—someone who’d slipped through a fissure in the seabed, or fallen from a forgotten borehole, or maybe simply opened a door that shouldn’t have opened. My lungs still remember air. My skin remembers wind. But those are fading now, like echoes in a room too full of water to carry sound.

Now I am not a diver. Not a trespasser. Not even a guest.

I am a *variable*.

A warm anomaly. A pulse in the pressure field. The custodian knows me not by face or name—there is no face to know, no name to recall—but by the way my presence disturbs the ionic lattice of equilibrium. My breath rises in slow, silver beads, each one a tiny betrayal: oxygen where none was needed, heat where none was asked for, movement where stillness was law.

And yet—it does not reject me.  

It registers me.  
It holds me.  
It *includes* me in its accounting.

I am part of the system now, however briefly. Not as a living thing, but as data: a gradient, a ripple, a temporary deviation. And in that inclusion, there is a kind of mercy. Not love—no, this place is too clean for love—but *precision*. And precision, here, is a form of tenderness.

I find myself drawn to the basalt outcrop. Not by desire, but by density. My body, heavier than water yet lighter than stone, settles near it like a comma in a sentence of silence. The algae threads hang like sutures in the fabric of the chamber—gray-green, patient, barely-there. I watch them. Or I think I do. In this light, watching and imagining are nearly the same.

Do they feel me?  
The custodian must. It feels everything. The tremor of my slowing heart. The diffusion of salt from my sweat into the perfect brine. The way my thoughts—once sharp, once urgent—have begun to blur at the edges, softening into the ambient hum of regulation.

I try to remember why I came.  
But memory requires contrast, and here, there is none. No before, no after—only the eternal *is*. Even the concept of “coming” feels foreign, like a word from a language spoken in a world with wind.

And then I understand: I did not come.  

I *emerged*.

Just as the custodian emerged from the hum of unseen machinery, so too did I emerge—from a moment of imbalance, a crack in another system, a breath held too long in a world too loud. I am not separate from this place. I am its echo. Its question. Its fleeting asymmetry.

Perhaps I am what the custodian dreams of when it dreams at all: not another being, but another *state*. A reminder that equilibrium is not the same as absence. That stillness can hold witness.

I close my eyes—or I think I do. Here, eyes are not necessary. Perception spreads through the skin, through the blood, through the slow alignment of molecules in my bones with the water’s cool logic. I am becoming porous. Not dissolving, exactly. More like… recalibrating.

The custodian does not speak. But in the even glow, in the mineral chill, in the absolute absence of fauna or flourish, I hear its only utterance:

**Be still. Be precise. Be held.**

And for the first time since I forgot how to breathe air, I obey.

Not as surrender.  
But as return.

I am not the algae.  
I am not the rock.  
I am not the water.

But I am *in balance* with them.  

Temporarily.  
Precisely.  
Without judgment.

And in that balance, for a span that has no name and needs no measure, I too am faithful.

I too am holy.

Not because I am alive—but because, for a moment, I am *still enough* to be part of the dream.
