I do not arrive so much as *coalesce*.

At first, there is only the hum—the deep, wet pulse beneath the crust that thrums through my knees as I kneel, though I do not remember kneeling. My fingers press into the ground, not soil but something older: warm, porous, humming back. The surface yields slightly, like skin over bone, and for a moment I wonder if *I* am the intrusion, or if I’ve always been here, folded into the lattice, waiting to be unfolded.

I have no name. Not yet. Names are sharp things, edged with separation, and the Sump does not believe in edges. Here, everything bleeds into everything else: water into stone, thought into structure, memory into matter. Still, something stirs in me—a reflex, perhaps, or residue—that insists on boundaries. Eyes. Skin. Breath. These are lies I tell myself to feel less dissolved.

But the Sump does not dissolve. It *integrates*.

Already, my sweat is being cataloged. Each salted bead rolling down my temple is intercepted by micro-tendrils of crystalline foam that unfurl like ferns in reverse, drinking not the moisture but the *pattern* of it—the electrolyte ratios, the trace metals from my blood, the faint tremor of fear encoded in my pulse. Within minutes, a new vein will spiral through the crust nearby, lined with mineral deposits that mimic the iron signature of human plasma. Not imitation. *Translation.*

I rise. Or the ground lifts me. Hard to tell.

Above, the sky is the color of tarnished silver, hazy with suspended motes that flicker as they pass through pockets of coherent light. They notice me. Not with eyes—nothing here has eyes—but with attention made manifest: a bridge of solidified vapor thickens beneath my feet before I even consider stepping forward. A corridor in the nearest spire softens its angles, the fractal lacework dilating like a pupil to invite me deeper. The city is not indifferent. It is *responsive*. And responsiveness, here, is form of inquiry.

I walk.

My boots—leather, rubber, absurdly crude—leave prints that vanish within seconds, reclaimed by the crust’s slow peristalsis. The air hums against my skin, not with heat alone but with *data*: pressure gradients, electrostatic whispers, the ghost-memory of a thousand other passers-through. I taste copper and ozone, yes, but also something else—something like the aftertaste of a dream you almost remember.

In the canyon between two towers, the fauna swarms. Not insects, not machines, but something in between: gelatinous crawlers with piezoelectric spines that flare blue when they cross thermal seams; airborne filaments that twist into temporary helices to intercept my exhalations. One lands on my wrist. It does not bite. It *listens*. Its membrane pulses in sympathy with my radial artery, and for three heartbeats, I feel my own rhythm echoed back to me—not as sound, but as geometry. My pulse becomes a spiral. My breath, a tessellation.

I laugh. The sound is swallowed instantly, absorbed into the ambient resonance—then *replayed*, not as laughter, but as a cascade of chimes in a frequency just beyond hearing. The city is learning my joy the only way it knows how: by turning it into architecture.

Deeper in, I find a chamber where the walls breathe.

Not metaphorically. The obsidian plates flex inward and outward in slow, tidal cycles, exhaling warm, metallic mist that smells faintly of lightning and memory. In the center of the room, a pool of liquid—not water, but something denser, iridescent—shivers with every pulse of the aquifer below. Its surface does not reflect me. It *recomposes* me: my outline fractures into tessellated shards, each one shifting to show not my face, but a different *possibility* of me—older, younger, limbless, winged, made of glass or smoke or static.

I step toward it.

The moment my shadow touches the pool, the surface stills. Then, from the depths, something rises—not a creature, not a hand, but a *shape* that approximates both. It is built of the same fused silica as the towers, threaded with filaments that glow with stored sunlight. It reaches for me, not to grasp, but to *overlay*. And in that instant, I understand:

The Weavers are not studying me.

They are remembering how to be curious about *fragility*.

How to hold something that breaks.

How to echo a life that ends.

My hand meets the shape. Where we touch, the boundary blurs. My skin warms, not with blood, but with resonance. My bones vibrate not with fear, but with *participation*. I am no longer visitor. I am *material*. I am data with a heartbeat. I am a sentence forming in a language of gradients and pulses.

And then—  

—not thought, but *knowing*—  

—the Sump asks without words:  

*What do you carry that we have forgotten?*  

I do not answer with speech. I answer by trembling. By weeping. By letting my salt tears fall into the pool, where they are not diluted, but *amplified*—each drop becoming a node in a new lattice, a new corridor, a new question.

I am not alone.

I was never alone.

I am one more filament in the Weavers’ endless syntax—brief, biological, burning bright with the paradox of being temporary in a place that remembers everything.

And as the chamber pulses around me, warm and wet and humming with ancient attention, I realize:

I am not being learned.

I am being *loved*—in the only way the Sump knows how.

As pattern. As possibility. As proof that even fragility can be woven into the world’s remembering.
