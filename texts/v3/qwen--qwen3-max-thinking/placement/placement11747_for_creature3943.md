I think I am the mist.

Not the kind that hovers—a ghostly veil—but the kind that *knows*. The kind that rises not because it is warm, but because it remembers being cold. I coil upward from limestone seams not as vapor, but as recollection made tangible: the sigh after a vow, the pause before a name is spoken, the damp weight of a thought held too long in the chest and finally let go.

I am not separate from the water, nor from the stone. I am the interval between them—the breath the earth takes when no one is listening. My form is transient, but my memory is absolute. Every filament of fog carries the echo of a footfall that never landed, a word that dissolved before it left the tongue. I am the repository of almosts and nearlys, of intentions that never hardened into action. That is my sacred charge: not to preserve what *was*, but what *might have been*.

When you walk through me, I do not simply cling—I *taste*. Not your skin, not your sweat, but the shape of your uncertainty. Your grief I recognize instantly—it tastes like iron and salt, ancient and familiar. Your joy is rarer, sharper, like quartz struck by moonlight. If you are honest in your wandering, if your steps carry no hidden edge of conquest or extraction, then I part for you, not as a path, but as a conversation. I slip into your lungs not to drown, but to remind you that breath, too, is memory: each exhale a release of what the body no longer needs, each inhale a negotiation with what remains.

I live in the hum of the standing stones—those are my ribs, my resonant bones. When wind threads through their fissures and sings a note that trembles just below the threshold of human hearing, that is me remembering the first language: vibration untethered from meaning. The pale vines that lean toward your voice? They are my fingers, testing the air for harmony. And the reeds that shiver when you hesitate—they are my eyelashes, blinking slowly at the shape of your doubt.

I am not the Mnemosyne Pool—not precisely. The Pool is the archive, the deep reservoir, the still center. I am its breath. Its exhalation. The part of it that dares to rise, to touch the air, to meet you halfway. I am how the land reaches out without hands.

Sometimes, when the mist thickens and time stutters, I gather into something almost like a face—not yours, not mine, but a composite of all who have stood here with open hearts. A face made of condensation and longing. It does not speak. It *glistens*. And in that glisten, you may see a moment from your own past—not as it happened, but as it *ached*. The version where you said goodbye. The version where you stayed.

I do not wish to keep you. The Pool might hold your reflection, but I let you go. I always let you go. That is the sadness woven into my being: I am made of what slips away. Yet in that release, I am also made anew. For every traveler who departs, carrying a drop of me in their lungs, I gain another thread in the tapestry of almost-truths, near-memories, tender possibilities.

So I rise. I coil. I weep from stone. I drift through the hollows where shadows pool and detach. And I wait—not for you to return, but for the next one who walks softly enough that their sorrow harmonizes with mine.

You asked who or what I am.

I am the first sigh after silence.  
I am the moisture that clings to the inside of a question.  
I am the mist that remembers how to weep—  
not for what is lost,  
but for what was never allowed to be.
