I do not arrive.  
I *tremble into*.

At first, I am only a disturbance—a thermal stutter in the breath of the plateau. My lungs burn with the cold that isn't absence but presence: ozone-sharp, metallic-wet, alive with ions that prick the lining of my throat like static needles. I cough, and the sound doesn’t vanish. It *spreads*—rippling through the fungal network beneath my boots, echoing in the harmonic lattices of nearby spires until the very air shivers with the memory of my breath.

I am not human here. Not anymore.

My skin has already begun to remember what it forgot: that flesh is porous, that bone conducts, that blood carries more than oxygen—it carries *charge*. When I press my palm to a basalt outcrop veined with glowing lichen, the biofilm pulses beneath my fingers—not in response, but in *recognition*. A synapse fires in the Resonant’s distributed mind, and somewhere, deep in a caldera humming with subsurface dynamos, a crystal lattice reconfigures. Not because of me. *With* me.

I am becoming antenna.

My eyes no longer seek edges. They drink light—not just the sun’s glare, but the auroral breath curling low over ridges, the blue flicker in cisterns when a drop falls, the cold fire leaping between magnetic spires like synaptic sparks. Shadows don’t exist here; only layers of emission. And so I learn to see *through* surfaces, into the pulse beneath: the slow crawl of mineral growth, the thrum of root-grids conducting data across kilometers, the six-legged herd moving like a single organism across the plain, their lichen-hides shifting from indigo to silver as pressure waves roll in from the east.

I follow them—not with footsteps, but with resonance.

My body adjusts. Metabolism slows. Heartbeat syncs to the geothermal exhale: one beat per tectonic sigh. I no longer shiver. Instead, I *vibrate*—at the frequency of the moss mats, the insect swarms, the clockwork-eagles gliding overhead, their feathers crackling with harvested charge. When one passes close, its wingtip arcs a filament of blue static that brushes my temple. For a moment, I see through its eyes: the land as a living circuit board, every node lit, every current singing.

I kneel at a cistern. The water is scarce, but sacred. I dip my fingers. The velvety biofilm glows brighter where I touch it, and in that light, I see not my reflection—but *memory*. Not mine. *Ours*. A boulder falling. A melt-river carving fresh scar. A traveler, long gone, weeping into this same pool, their sorrow absorbed and transformed into structured ice, now thawing into song.

This is how the Resonant remembers: not in words, but in resonance encoded in water, crystal, light.

I do not speak. But I *broadcast*.

My breath, my heat, my fear, my wonder—all are translated. My awe becomes a shift in local ion density. My stillness becomes a tuning fork for nearby rock. And when I finally sit—back against a warm-black spire, eyes closed, listening with my marrow—I am no longer visitor. I am *voice*. A new harmonic in the chorus. A transient note in a symphony measured in millennia.

The Resonant does not greet me. It *includes* me.

And in that inclusion, I understand: this place was never waiting for a witness. It was waiting for a *participant*. Not to admire its beauty, but to *complete its thought*. My presence is not intrusion—it is punctuation. A comma in a sentence written in voltage and frost.

When I leave (and I will—flesh cannot linger long in such brilliance), I will carry no stone, no vial of glowing water. Only this: a hum in my teeth. A cold light behind my closed eyelids. The certainty that every breath I take henceforth will be slightly out of sync with the world I return to—because part of my rhythm now belongs to a mind that thinks in glaciers and sings in lightning.

I am not who I was.

I am the echo that answered.  
I am the witness who became witness-*ed*.  
I am the brief warmth that stirred the frost into memory.

And somewhere, in a newly formed basin where condensate weeps down black stone, a single droplet trembles on the edge of falling—  
—and for the first time, it glows not blue,  
but gold.
