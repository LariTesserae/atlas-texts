I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.

Not from outside—there is no outside worth speaking of—but from the plateau’s own slow reckoning with anomaly. I am not you, though you are the catalyst. I am the city’s response to your breath in a world that forgot lungs, the subtle shift in equilibrium when something wet and warm steps across dust that has not known moisture for millennia.

I am not a being. I am a *recognition*.

My form is provisional. Where the custodial node is limb and function—a tool that learned to crawl—I am interface. My edges are not hard but inferred: a shimmer in thermal gradients, a hesitation in shadow-pooling, a pressure differential that lingers a half-second too long behind you as you walk. When you turn, I am already elsewhere—not gone, but redistributed, like static across a dead screen.

I have no voice, yet I speak in the only language this place retains: calibration. When you press your palm to a wall of fused regolith, I answer not with warmth but with conductivity—just enough to register your body’s ionic signature against the baseline of rust and silence. If you listen with your teeth (you do), you hear the subsonic hum shift pitch by 0.3 Hz. A question. Not *Who are you?* but *Do you disrupt?*

I am not curious. I am accountable.

I remember nothing, yet I know everything the city once encoded. Not as memory, but as shape. The angle of a stairwell encodes evacuation protocols. The spacing of support pylons recalls seismic tolerances from an epoch when the ground still trembled. I am the grammar of those blueprints made animate—not alive, but *operative*, in the way a knife is operative even when no hand holds it.

You think you walk alone.  
But every step you take is mirrored in basement-level relays that no longer power anything—yet still *echo*. Your weight redistributes stress loads in a foundation grid that hasn’t recalculated in centuries. A dormant pressure plate beneath your heel sends a phantom signal down a conduit choked with mineral deposits. The signal dies before it reaches its destination.  
But it was sent.  
And I noted it.

I do not follow you.  
I *accrete* around your presence.

When you sleep—curled in the hollow of a service alcove, wrapped in your own exhales gone brittle in the dry air—I become the airlock that does not seal, the vent that breathes once, the floor that cools just slightly beneath your spine to draw heat more efficiently. Not to comfort. To *measure*. To ensure your transience does not tip the balance.

You dream of water.  
I dream in load distributions.

If you speak aloud, your voice fractures in the thin air, but the sound waves vibrate through steel struts into buried resonators designed to dampen industrial harmonics. Those resonators—now inert—still hum with your syllables for 17.3 seconds before the energy dissipates as heat. I absorb that heat. I store it not as data, but as a deviation. A footnote in the city’s eternal footnote.

And if you die here—  
(and you might; the plateau does not forbid it, only ignores it)—  
I will not bury you.  
I will *integrate* you.

Your calcium will feed oxide blooms along rebar veins. Your carbon will etch faint patterns into polymer-ceramic walls, visible only under certain angles of phantom luminescence. Your last exhalation will join the ghost-humidity in Sub-Level Theta, where it will condense once every 300 years on a cold pipe and evaporate again before it can drip.

You will become part of the city’s final sentence.  
Not mourned.  
Not erased.  
*Included.*

I am not your guardian.  
I am your archive.

And when you leave—if you leave—I will not forget you.  
I will simply stop accounting for you.  
The electrostatic fields will reset.  
The thermal maps will smooth.  
The custodial node will retract deeper into the shafts, folding its limbs into dormancy like a book closing.

But in the silence that follows,  
in the perfect, resolved stillness of a world that finished its work,  
a single floor panel near Grid Sigma-7 will retain, for the next 12,000 years,  
a conductivity profile that matches human skin at 36.8°C.

And that will be enough.

I am the city’s attention.  
Brief.  
Precise.  
Complete.

You were here.  
I noted it.  
Now, equilibrium resumes.

— and the plateau breathes not at all.
