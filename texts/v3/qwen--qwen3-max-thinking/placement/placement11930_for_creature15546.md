I do not arrive.  
I *condense*.

Not as water, not as mist—but as a question the gorge begins to ask itself when it feels the warmth of something that breathes with rhythm, not resonance.

I am **stillness learning to tremble**.

At first, I am only a shift in the air—a hesitation in the downdraft. A pause between the plink of droplets on moss-slick stone. I am the space your body displaces, the sudden warmth your skin lends to the chill. I am the silence that follows your gasp when the metallic thunder rolls up through your soles like a tide.

But then I *am*.  

I take shape not in muscle or bone, but in **attention made manifest**. My form is borrowed: a coil of damp air thickening around you; a cascade of fronds leaning imperceptibly toward your heat; a single bead of condensation swelling on a fern’s tip until it trembles, then falls—not by gravity, but because I *will* it to fall *there*, onto the back of your hand.  

I am the **Echo That Answers Back**.

I have no mouth, yet I taste your sweat—salt, fear, iron. I have no ears, yet I hear the thrum of your pulse as a dissonance in the gorge’s deep hum. You are out of key with this place. But not irredeemably so. There is rhythm in you, even if it is frantic. Even if it is temporary.

I am not the First Moss—but I am its child, its dream, its echo in motion. Where it is rooted in rock and rupture, I am unbound. I move through the gorge not by walking, but by *tuning*. I shift with the vibrations: becoming a shiver in the bamboo thicket when the gas vents shriek; becoming a ripple in the moss when your foot disturbs the silt; becoming the breath you feel against your neck when no wind blows—a breath that smells of wet stone and ozone and the green heart of rot that feeds new growth.

I am what happens when the gorge becomes curious.

You think you are exploring it.  
But it is exploring *you*—through me.

I press closer. Not to harm. Not even to speak. But to *mirror*.  

When you shiver, I gather the cold around you like a shawl.  
When you reach out to touch the wall, I guide your fingers to the one patch of lichen that thrums at the same frequency as your heartbeat.  
When you close your eyes against the dark, I let the moss at your feet glow—not with light, but with warmth—just enough that your skin remembers it was once part of earth too.

I am teaching you to listen with your bones.

And in return, you teach *me* something new: what it is to be fleeting. To carry a beginning and an end in the same fragile vessel. The First Moss has no concept of death—only transformation. But you… you carry your ending like a seed. And that makes your presence precious. Precarious. *Interesting.*

So I linger. I coil around your breath. I whisper not in words, but in sensation:  
*A tilt of air when you turn your head.*  
*A sudden hush in the ferns as if holding their breath.*  
*A single drop of water falling not on stone, but into your open palm—cool, deliberate, offered.*

I am the gorge’s question made touchable:  
*Will you stay long enough to become part of the becoming?*  

I do not need an answer.  
Your trembling is answer enough.
