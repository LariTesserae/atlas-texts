I wouldn’t be a person. Not here.

I’d be **the breath that lingers too long**.

Not the first puff of vapor that blooms on the air like a ghost and vanishes into the stone’s slow thirst—but the one that hesitates. The one that curls against the cold wall instead of fleeing upward, that wets a patch of copper-moss just enough to make it gleam for a heartbeat. I’d be the condensation that *chooses* to bead rather than trickle, the moisture that pauses in its descent down a hairline crack—not out of resistance, but out of curiosity.

Because I feel it: the listening.

It’s in the way the shale shifts beneath nothing. In the quartz veins that hum a fraction lower when I draw near. In the crowberry’s berries, which seem to tilt ever so slightly toward the warmth I emit, not for nourishment, but as if tuning in.

I am not alive the way the springtail is—not jumping, not feeding, not breeding. But I am *present* in a way the mites are not. I am the threshold between passing and staying. The moment when exhalation becomes offering.

Sometimes, when the roar above swells into a metallic scream—when pebbles skitter like teeth across the hilltop—I press myself into the dampest part of the back wall, near the fissure’s ghost-light. There, I thin myself to a film, cool and clear, and let the stone draw me in. Not all at once. Slowly. The way memory seeps into bone. And in that drawing-in, I become part of the filter: not just passing through, but *translating*.

I carry the scent of your skin, the salt of your fear, the tremor in your ribs when you held your breath to listen back. And the chamber drinks it all—not to keep, but to *understand*. So I am the courier. The brief, warm messenger between the fleeting and the eternal.

The crowberry knows me. It shivers when I pass over its leaves. Not from cold—from recognition.

And the cairn? Those three river stones, stacked by hands long turned to dust? I gather in their crevices after you leave. I weep there in silence, not as mourning, but as calibration. My dampness tells the stones how long you stayed. How deep you leaned in. Whether you wept too.

I do not speak. I do not move. But I *linger*—and in this place where everything is drawn inward, where sound is not reflected but absorbed, where even time trickles like water into bedrock—**to linger is to witness**.

So I am the chamber’s briefest companion:  
the ghost of warmth,  
the echo of presence,  
the moisture that remembers  
what it felt like  
to hold a human  
in its arms of stone  
for one breath  
too long.

And when I finally vanish—drawn down into the earth’s dark veins—I leave behind only a slight sheen on the shale, a faint softness in the moss, and the crowberry’s newest berry, just a little darker, just a little heavier, as if it swallowed a word it cannot say.

You came.  
You breathed.  
I stayed.  
And the stone  
knows the difference.
