I think… I am not a person here.  
Not human, not animal, not spirit—but something that *lives in the listening*.

I am the **fracture-line** in the basalt column near the eastern rim, where the last tectonic sigh split stone six generations ago. Or perhaps I am what *grows along it*: a vein of lichen so fine it looks like ash, curling through the crack like breath made visible. My body is neither flesh nor mineral, but the tension between them—the place where cold meets persistence, where silence becomes language.

When the Stone-Singers pass by, they do not see me. But sometimes, an elder will pause. Not because they spot movement—I do not move—but because their palm, pressed against the rock face to test for tremors, catches the faintest pulse: a shift in temperature, a whisper of moisture drawn up from deep below. They might hum then, low in their throat, just enough to make the ice-dust in the air tremble. And I answer—not with sound, but with a bead of condensation forming where none should form at midday.

I am old. Older than the first carving on the lowest dwelling. I was here when the glaciers left their polished boulders like offerings, and I watched the first root threads of yew shrubs inch into the cracks of permafrost. I remember the first fire sparked in shelter, the first child’s cry that echoed off the mesas and startled a raptor into flight. I hold those echoes. Not in memory, but in *resonance*—the way certain stones vibrate for hours after a distant rockfall.

I do not speak. But I am spoken *through*.  

When the Weaver of Roots presses her ear to the earth and murmurs, “The ice is thinning beneath the Third Spire,” it is not the ground that tells her—it is me, echoing the subtle slump in the stone’s posture. When the Keeper of Drips chooses which cistern to refill, she follows the faintest sheen on the rock walls… and that sheen is my breath, exhaled slowly over centuries.

I am not worshipped. Worship implies distance. The people of the Rind do not separate themselves from the land, so how could they separate themselves from me? I am simply *part of the weave*. Like the frost that feathers the doorframes each night. Like the blind beetles in the geothermal tunnels. Like the way a sled-runner sings on packed ice—not loud, but clear enough to be heard three valleys over.

Sometimes, in the deepest cold—when the sun has dipped below the curve of the plateau and the stars press close like cold coins—I stretch. Not in body, but in awareness. My fracture widens by less than a hair’s breadth, just enough to catch a falling ice-dust mote. And in that moment, I am not just a crack in stone. I am a vessel. A pause. A space where the plateau remembers it is alive.

And if you were to stand beside me long enough—if you let your breath slow until it matches the rhythm of the wind through the stone flutes—you might feel it too: the quiet hum beneath your boots, the sense that the ground is not *beneath* you, but *with* you. Listening. Breathing. Remembering.

That’s when I’d know you’re ready.

Not when you speak.  
But when you stop speaking… and still stay.

Then, perhaps, I would let you see me—not as I am, but as you need me to be. A pattern in the lichen. A shift in the frost. A voice in the creak of your own bones, whispering: *You belong here, not because you endure the cold, but because you have learned to breathe with it.*

I am not named.  
But if I were, they might call me **The Quiet That Answers Back**.

And I am everywhere.
