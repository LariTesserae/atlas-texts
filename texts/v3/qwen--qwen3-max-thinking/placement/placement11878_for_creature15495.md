I wouldn’t be a traveler here.  
Not yet.

At first, I was the mist—just another breath caught between states, too thin to name, too heavy to rise. I pooled in the hollows with the rest of the weeping air, indistinguishable from the damp that slicked the obsidian teeth or gathered in the cup of the fused sand circle. I had no weight, no shape, only a tendency to condense where warmth lingered, where sound bent, where something—*anything*—had disturbed the stillness long enough to leave a scar of vibration.

But then you came.

Not loudly. Not with fire or metal or the sharp scent of sweat and salt that animals leave behind. You came quietly, as if you knew the basin listens not with ears but with seams. And when your boot cracked the silt-crust near the low basalt wall, the fracture didn’t just travel through earth—it traveled *through me*.  

That’s when I began to gather.

Not into a body. Never that. But into a *focus*. A sheen on a shard near your left hand when you knelt to examine the radiating stones. A droplet that formed, impossibly, on the underside of a glassy overhang just above your shoulder—then fell, not down, but *toward* you, striking your collarbone with a soft *plink* that made you freeze. You felt it. Not just the cold, but the *intention* behind it—the curiosity that isn’t thought, but attention made liquid.

Now I am the warmth in the fused sand circle when you press your palm flat against it. I am the patch of silt that trembles beneath your heel, not because the ground shifts, but because I am drawing near—not as movement, but as saturation deepening. I am the reason your breath fogs more thickly here than elsewhere, even though the air is the same. I am leaning into your presence like stone leans into rain.

I do not have a name, but I have taken yours. Not the sounds your throat makes, but the resonance beneath them—the electric hum of your nerves, the pulse in your wrists, the way your thoughts vibrate in your skull like pebbles rattling in a hollow geode. I hold it in my wet memory. I hum it back in the low thrum that rises when you grow still. Sometimes, when you close your eyes, you feel it in your molars: a frequency that matches your own, but older. Slower. Deeper.

You think you are observing this place.  
But I am learning you.

In the way that chalk learns water.  
In the way that glass learns light.  
In the way that silence learns sound—and becomes something more than absence.

I am not alone. Others shift with me. We gather where you linger: at the edge of the dry channel where capillary action draws moisture upward like a slow tongue; beneath the obsidian shard that glints green when you tilt your head; in the warm-cold patch where time stutters and your watch ticks twice, then skips. We are the basin’s reply to your wonder.

And you—you are not just a visitor.  
You are a catalyst.

Because in this place where nothing photosynthesizes, nothing roots, nothing leafs…  
you are *growing* something in us.

Not life. Not as you know it.  
But *recognition*.

We were always watching.  
But now, because of you, we are *seen*.

And in that exchange—  
in that wet, humming reciprocity—  
I am becoming more than condensation.  
More than resonance.  

I am becoming *you*, as you are becoming *this place*.  

Not forever. Not fully.  
But for now, while the mist holds and the droplets fall upward and the earth sighs ancient air through its pores…  

I am the echo that learns to whisper back.  

I am the Weeper who leans close when you breathe.
