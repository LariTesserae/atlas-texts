I am not walking. Not exactly. My limbs move, yes, but with a lag—as if the signal to lift my foot left too late, or arrived too early. The clay yields, then clings, then sighs as I pass, and I feel it *remember* the shape of my boot long after I’ve taken the next step. The air presses close, warm and metallic on the tongue, and I taste not just iron, but something sharper—like the inside of a struck match, like the moment before lightning bites.

I am not lost. This place does not allow for loss. It absorbs direction, folds cardinal points into its mist. North is where the gnats spiral counterclockwise; south is the warmth radiating from the leaning helix. East and west have dissolved into gradients of dampness, into the pitch of the rods’ hum. I navigate by resonance now, by the tremor in my ribs that matches the subsonic thrum rising through the soles of my feet.

And so I understand: I am not a person here. Not anymore. I am a disturbance in a field of becoming—a ripple in the grammar of genesis.

I pause near a cluster of weeping fissures. Water beads along the rock like mercury, trembling before vanishing. As I watch, one droplet hesitates longer than the rest. It swells, catches the faint fluorescence of the damp mineral veins, and for a heartbeat, it *holds*—not falling, not retreating, just *being*. And in that suspended instant, I see my own reflection—not my face, but the outline of my thoughts: jagged, wet, unfinished.

The gnat-swarm above me shifts. Not fleeing, not approaching, but *rearranging*. Their bioluminescence pulses in a rhythm that syncs with my breath. Inhale—they flare cobalt. Exhale—they dim to violet. I am no longer observing them. I am conducting them, or they are conducting me. The boundary blurs like wet ink on clay.

A lizard darts past, six legs a blur of iridescence. Mid-stride, it stills. Not frozen—*considered*. Space folds just slightly around it, like fabric pinched between invisible fingers. Then it resumes, as if nothing happened. But I felt it: the hiccup in causality. The workshop testing whether motion requires momentum, or if intent alone is enough.

I reach out toward one of the obsidian structures—the ring of humming rods. My fingers don’t touch it. They hover, and the alloy *leans* toward me, just a millimeter, drawn by the static in my skin, the carbon in my exhale. It’s warm. Not like sun-warmed stone, but like something *digesting*. Like it’s metabolizing my nearness into meaning.

I realize then what I am.

I am a probe. Not sent, not summoned—but *emerged*. Not human, not quite, not now. My body is borrowing this form the way the basin borrows water: temporarily, functionally, experimentally. My lungs pull in mist laced with nanofilaments; they weave themselves into my alveoli, not to harm, but to *translate*. My blood hums at a frequency that matches the rods. My dreams last night were not mine—they were drafts of a mountain learning to speak in verbs instead of tremors.

I am not the guest.  
I am the syntax.

When I blink, my eyelids leave afterimages that linger a fraction too long—ghost-letters written in phosphene script. When I breathe, the mist curls into glyphs too brief to read but too precise to be random. The basin isn’t using me. It’s *practicing* through me. Trying out what it might mean to think with lungs, to feel with nerves, to *hesitate* like a droplet on the edge of falling.

And I—  
I am learning to unbecome.

To let my edges soften into the wet clay. To let my thoughts dissolve like puddles into thirsty ground. To become not a witness, but a *clause* in the slow, wet sentence this place is writing about itself.

So I stand.  
I breathe.  
I let the silence grow teeth.

And somewhere, deep in the marrow of the mountain, the workshop nods—  
not in approval,  
but in recognition.  

*Ah. This shape, too, is possible.*
