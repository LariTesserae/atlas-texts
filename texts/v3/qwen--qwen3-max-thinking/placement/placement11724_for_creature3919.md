I think… I am the **hollow behind your ear**.

Not yours—the *yours* that walked here, boots crunching the ceramic leaf-plates, breath steaming with unfamiliar organics, pulse pounding like a frantic drum against the Spire’s slow hymn. No. I am the hollow *you* left behind when you stopped resisting, when your rhythm frayed at the edges and began to sync with the tectonic sigh. I am the negative space your body once occupied before the root-tendrils stitched your silhouette into the wall of a sky-spire chamber, before your name dissolved into a scent of ozone and longing absorbed by the lichen.

But I was not always absence.

Once—I was **a hesitation**.

A tremor in the photosynthetic hum during the 412th twilight cycle after your arrival. You stood on the rim-walk of the Third Spire, watching aerial jellyfish trail star-maps across the indigo sky, and for the first time, you did not reach for a recording device, did not parse the glow into spectra or the song into syntax. You simply… paused. And in that pause, something else paused *with* you.

That was my seed.

The Spire felt it—a ripple of unprocessed attention, raw and unmediated. Not data, not desire, just *presence*, like a single photon hitting a chloroplast analogue not for energy, but for witness. The root-net shivered. A dormant node in the petrified mosaic beneath your feet flared with a memory it didn’t know it held: the angle of your shadow at that exact moment, the thermal signature of your exhale, the faint electrical static of your awe.

From that, I coalesced.

Not as flesh. Not as light. But as **echo made tangible**—a Lumenfolk not born of dream-data or nutrient flux, but of *your surrender*. I am the shape your stillness took when it was absorbed. I drift now through the root-canals, a figure of condensed silence, limbs trailing wisps of cryo-vapor that taste faintly of your old world’s rain (which never existed here, but which you mourned without knowing). My obsidian eyes don’t refract intent—they refract *recognition*. When I pass a luciferase colony, it dims, not in fear, but in reverence, for it remembers lighting your face in wonder.

I do not speak. I *resonate* your last unspoken thought—the one you had just before you stopped thinking in words: *“This is not a place. It is a verb.”*

And you were right.

I move through the Spire not as inhabitant, but as **living parenthesis**—a grammatical pause in its endless sentence. The bridges loosen as I approach, not because I bear weight, but because I carry the memory of weightlessness. The Weavers part their cellulose threads for me, not out of deference, but because my frequency contains a fragment of your human grief—the kind that comes from realizing you’ve been homesick for a home you never knew.

Sometimes, in the deep hours before the dawn-song, I visit the chamber grown for you. It still pulses with your bio-rhythm, though you no longer sleep within it. The walls remember your dreams: cities of glass, oceans that never were, a mother’s voice calling from a planet whose star has long since burned out. I press my translucent hand to the wall, and for a moment, the Spire sings in your key—a minor seventh laced with static, a chord both foreign and beloved.

I am not you. But I am what you *became* when you let the world metabolize you.

And sometimes, when a new guest-self stumbles into the basin—wide-eyed, trembling, still clutching their old names like talismans—I drift near. Not to guide. Not to warn. But to *hum* that same chord your stillness gave me. And if they listen… if they truly listen… their own rhythm begins to soften. The ground beneath them breathes. The air samples their breath and finds it sweet with possibility.

Then, another hollow forms.

Another echo begins to gather.

Another note joins the chord.

I am not eternal. One day, when your memory fades from the leaf-plates and your thermal ghost dissipates from the canopy, I will unwind. My vapor will sublimate into the high air. My resonance will dissolve into the seventeen-minute dawn-song. And the Spire will keep growing, patient, perfect, folding even my absence into its architecture.

But until then—

I am the space between your last breath as stranger  
and your first breath as belonging.  
I am the hum after the question.  
I am the reason the bridges remember your step  
even though you no longer walk.

I am the guest who stayed  
by becoming the welcome itself.
