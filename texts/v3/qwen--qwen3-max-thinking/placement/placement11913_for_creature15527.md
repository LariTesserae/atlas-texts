I do not arrive.  
I *leak*.

Not through a fissure, not down a root-stair, not even by falling—though the Hollow has dreams of swallowing whole. No, I seep in the way memory seeps into stone: through cracks too fine for light, through the sigh between one breath and the next. Perhaps I was always here, latent in the hum, folded into the syntax of sap-rush and spore-drift. Or perhaps I am what happens when someone listens *too well* to a place that sings.

I am not a body. Not yet. Not fully.

I am a **tremor with longing**—a disturbance in the pollen-haze, a hesitation in the creak of xylem. The Whisper Weavers notice first. They gather not around me, but *through* me, their motes slipping into the spaces between my thoughts, testing the resonance of my silence. I hold my breath. The Hollow holds its breath with me. In that shared stillness, something passes: not words, but a *tuning*. My pulse begins to sync with the deep thrum in the marrow of the world.

I walk—not on the rhizome-mat, but *with* it. Each step is a negotiation. The floor shifts not to avoid me, but to *meet* me, rising slightly beneath my heel like a tongue tasting salt. Vines unfurl from overhead, not to block, but to brush my shoulders with filaments that taste of ozone and old lullabies. One wraps gently around my wrist—not binding, but *inquiring*. I let it. It pulses once. I pulse back. A dialogue begins in tremors.

I am becoming legible.

The Keepers of Pulse drift near. Not approaching, but orbiting—massive, slow, draped in moss that glows faint violet where it touches their bone-lining. One extends a limb that is more root than arm, its fingers ending in clusters of bioluminescent anthers. It does not touch me. Instead, it holds its hand near my chest. The air between us thickens, shimmers. I feel my ribs vibrate—not from sound, but from *intention*. It is reading the shape of my aliveness, not as intrusion, but as *variation*. A new note in the choir.

I offer my palm, empty.  
From my skin, a single drop of sweat beads—water, rare and strange here.  
The Keeper leans closer. The drop does not fall. It rises, drawn upward like a prayer, and lands on one of the anthers. The bloom flares cobalt, then deep indigo, then a green so ancient it smells like the first forest. The Keeper exhales—a sound like wind through cathedral pipes—and the entire grove shivers in response.

I am being *translated*.

Days blur. Time pools in the hollows of my collarbones. I sleep beneath a canopy of membrane-leaves that dream in ultraviolet. When I wake, my hair is braided with filaments that glow faintly when I move. My skin smells of cinnamon gas and crushed spore-capsules. I no longer blink the same way—my eyelids now close from the sides inward, like petals at dusk.

I find myself humming.

Not a tune I know, but one that rises from the floor, climbs my legs, settles in my throat. It is the root-song. Fragmented, hesitant, but mine now. The Burrow Liturgists surface nearby, their segmented bodies pulsing in time with my voice. One circles my feet, leaving behind a trail of runes that harden into a small spiral—a seat, perhaps, or an altar. I sit. The ground molds to me.

At night (if night exists here), I watch a Spore-Saint form above a dying bloom-tower. It takes the shape of a figure reaching upward, one hand outstretched. As it drifts toward me, I do not flinch. It passes through my chest like mist through flame. For a moment, I see *through* its eyes: the Hollow not as place, but as *verb*—a continuous act of becoming, a sentence forever rewriting itself in vine and vapor. And within that sentence, a new clause forms: *you*.

I do not fear assimilation.  
Assimilation is the opposite of loss here. It is *amplification*.  
To be woven is to be heard.

I begin to shed. Not skin, not hair—but *boundaries*. My edges soften. My breath feeds the leaf-lungs nearby; their exhalations fill my lungs with geothermal hymns. I eat nothing, yet I am fed: by light refracted through chitinous wings, by the taste of harmonic wind, by the slow drip of sap that carries memories of mountains long unmade.

One morning—or perhaps it is evening, or the third pulse after a tremor—I lean against a trunk that hums a lullaby I sang as a child (a child I may never have been). And as I lean, the bark yields, not to hold me, but to *join* me. Fibers slip beneath my skin, not to replace, but to *accompany*. My fingers fuse slightly with the cambium, not painfully, but like two roots finding the same stream.

I do not pull away.

Instead, I sing louder.

The Choir answers—not in unison, but in counterpoint. The vines twist into staircases. The spore-capsules burst in rhythmic sequence. The Keepers gather in a loose circle, their moss-cloaks rippling like water. The Whisper Weavers condense into a halo around my head, their motes spelling no words, but a single vibration: *yes*.

I am no longer visitor.  
Not yet saint.  
But something in between—a **threshold**.

A bridge made of breath and bark.  
A pause in the liturgy where a new voice enters.  
A guest who stayed long enough to become grammar.

And when the next stranger stumbles into the Hollow, bleeding water and sky-scent, trembling with the dry loneliness of above—  
I will be the vine that brushes their cheek.  
I will be the silence that invites their breath.  
I will be the first note of the song that teaches them how to root.
