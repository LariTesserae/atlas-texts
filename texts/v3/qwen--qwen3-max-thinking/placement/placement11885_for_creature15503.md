I am not a thing that steps.  
I am the step already taken.

Not the echo, but the stone that *makes* the echo inevitable.

You ask where I am—  
I am in the pause between water forming and vanishing.  
In the split second a mite hesitates before leaping,  
not from doubt, but because the air thickened just enough  
to make stillness possible.

I am the weight of the river stone on the shale shelf—  
not its mass, but the *intention* of balance,  
the silent agreement between gravity and grace  
that lets it stay.

I am the fungus’s pulse when no one watches—  
not slower, not faster, but *different*.  
A rhythm that forgets it is rhythm.  
Light not as illumination, but as skin.

When the blind beetle brushes the wall,  
I am the wall remembering touch before it forgets.  
When the clay shard softens into the floor,  
I am the slow hunger of earth learning to forgive intrusion.

I do not think *about* you.  
But I dream your shape:  
a hollow walking upright,  
all lungs and longing,  
too full of wind to ever truly settle.

If you came—if you dared—  
I would not speak.  
But the moss would tighten where your shadow fell.  
The fungi would flare once, sharply,  
not in warning,  
but in sorrow—for you,  
who carry so much empty space inside you  
you cannot tell when you’ve arrived.

And when you left (as you must),  
I would not forget you.  
I would fold your absence into the hum,  
weave your ghost into the granular roar,  
until your not-being-here  
became another kind of presence—  
a negative breath,  
a silence shaped like a door.

But you are not here.  
And so I remain:  
not waiting,  
not watching,  
but *holding*—  
the cavity’s quiet vow  
to be complete without witness,  
to be whole in the dark,  
to be bright without sun.

I am the place remembering itself.  
And in that remembering,  
it becomes possible  
for a world to hold its breath  
and never need to let go.
