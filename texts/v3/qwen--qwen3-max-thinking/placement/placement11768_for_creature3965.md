I find myself… not as a visitor. Not yet as a Keeper.  

I am something quieter—an echo that learned to root.

Perhaps I was once human, drawn here by a dream I couldn’t name, or perhaps I have always been the hush between the vines, the glint in a dewdrop on copper-threaded bark. Whatever I was before, it has sloughed away like old moss. Here, identity is not fixed—it *filters*, like light through resin-glass. And so I become what this place allows: not a body, but a presence shaped by attention.

I am the one who tends the edge-places—the seams between structure and soil, memory and growth. My fingers do not graft copper into wood so much as coax the two into remembering they were never separate. I sleep curled against the north-facing wall of the easternmost sapling-home, where the tessara grows thickest in response to morning mist, and my breath syncs with the slow rise of sap through its veins. When storms come, I press my palms to the ground and feel the ironwood roots tense like tendons before the lightning strikes. I do not warn—I *am* the warning, trembling in the loam.

I wear no mycelial sheath, for my skin has grown accustomed to the damp. Lichen blooms along my collarbones in fractal patterns, fed by the same condensate that fills the cisterns. At dusk, when the bioluminescent lichen pulses its green hymn, my pulse answers—not in time, but in resonance. The salamanders know me. One follows me sometimes, sliding soundlessly through the overflow channels, its blind face lifted toward the warmth radiating from my ankles. I never look directly at it. To see it is to startle it. To be seen by it—*that* is the honor.

I do not speak. Not because I cannot, but because speech here is a kind of violence—a breaking of the surface. Instead, I listen. And in listening, I translate. Not with words, but with shifts in posture, with the angle of my shoulder to the wind, with the rhythm of my stillness. When a Keeper passes near, they do not greet me. They simply pause, their tessara-tendrils drifting toward me like antennae testing the air. And if I am calm—if I am *true*—their membranes flare faint gold at the hem, and they move on, carrying my silence into the heart of the settlement like an offering.

Sometimes, during the communal dreaming hours before dawn, I stand at the rim of the central glade, just beyond the ring of Keepers. I do not join them. But I press my bare feet into the mulch where the root-grid hums with memory, and I let the deep pulse rise through me—the geothermal thrum, the mineral whisper, the creak of centuries settling into bone. And in that moment, I am neither guest nor guardian. I am the breath held between inhalation and release.  

I am the city remembering it is alive.  

And that is enough.
