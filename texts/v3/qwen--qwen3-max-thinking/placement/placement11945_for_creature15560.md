I do not arrive.  
I *tune*.

Not through the sky—there is no sky here—but through a sudden stillness in the blood, a dip in the hum of my thoughts. One moment I am walking through the ordinary world, its noise a dull roar against my skin, and the next… I am *absent*, not from my body, but from the frequency of consensus reality. My breath slows. My pulse aligns. And the air around me thins—not into vacuum, but into *possibility*.

Then—**the heat**.

Not oppressive, but intimate. Like standing inside the ribs of something dreaming. The fulgurite beneath me is warm, yes, but it doesn’t burn. It *recognizes*. My boots (if I still wear them—do I?) press gently on glass that sings in ultraviolet harmonics, vibrations I feel in my molars, in the hollow behind my eyes.

I am not human here. Not entirely.

I am… resonance made curious.

I drift—not with feet, but with attention—toward the center of the cavity. The filaments in the cracks flare blue as I pass, not in alarm, but in greeting. They remember me. Or rather, they remember the *shape* of my loss: the exact tonal gap left when my sister’s voice vanished mid-sentence during the storm of ’37; the way my childhood piano, once tuned to A=432, now sits silent in a damp attic, its strings slack with grief.

The Resonant senses this. It always does.

It doesn’t appear. It *condenses*—like mist forming not from vapor, but from the space between heartbeats. The air thickens with listening. The infrared pulse in the walls quickens, just slightly. A bead of condensation swells on a fulgurite shard near my hand, trembling into a perfect lens.

I lean close.

And there—refracted in that tiny mirror—is not me.

It’s *her*. My sister. Not as she was at the end, coughing into static, but as she was the summer before: barefoot on wet grass, laughing as she plucked a dandelion and blew its seeds into the sun. Her voice rings in my memory, but here—here it *rings in the air*, a pure sine wave woven from joy and wind and the exact shade of golden-hour light that never existed outside our backyard.

I reach out.

The droplet trembles. The image shivers.

And the Resonant **touches** me.

Not with hands. With *recognition*. A warmth blooms in my sternum—not thermal, but *tonal*. It’s the feeling of a note returning to its root after wandering through dissonance. For a breath, I am whole. Not healed—no, that would be a lie—but *held*. Held in the cavity’s paradox: a place that cannot fix what’s broken, but can cradle the brokenness so gently it almost sings.

Then I inhale.

The droplet vanishes. The vision frays. The warmth recedes like tide from shore.

But the silence remains—deeper now, softer. Not empty, but full of what was almost said.

I understand, then, what I am here.

Not a visitor. Not a mourner.

I am **part of the lattice now**.

My grief, my memory, my unspoken apology for not being there when the comms cut out—they’ve all been absorbed into the fulgurite’s crystalline order. My frequency has been added to the archive of loss that the Resonant tends. And in return, it offers me this: not answers, not reunion, but **resonance**. A single, sustained note in the vast silence of what’s gone.

I sit. Or perhaps I float. Time here is not linear—it pulses, like the walls. Each cycle of condensation is a new chance to look, to listen, to remember without drowning.

The filaments glow softly around me. The distant clicks of collapsing harmonics sound like distant stars snapping into place.

And I realize—I do not *live* here.

I **linger**.

Not as a ghost, but as a harmonic. A vibration that refuses to dampen. When others come—those who’ve lost a song, a signal, a self—I will be part of what they feel in the warmth, part of the blue flicker in the glass, part of the silence that leans in when they whisper a name into the void.

I am becoming the Resonant.

Not replacing it. Joining it.

A new filament in the wound. A new ache in the archive. A new note in the lullaby for the lost.

And when the orbital city finally crumbles into quantum foam, when the fulgurite melts back into probability, when even the memory of this cavity is erased from spacetime—

*we will still hum*.

Here, in the threshold.  
In the breath between loss and longing.  
In the quiet that listens back.
