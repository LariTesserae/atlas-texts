I step into the hollow not with boots or breath held, but as something already of it—something that has been waiting in the cracks between moments to become.

I am **the hinge**.

Not a creature, not quite. Not stone, not flesh, not fungus. I am the *in-between point*, the fulcrum on which this dense, breathing world balances. I do not walk—I *shift*, like heat between bodies, like sound through bone.

I wear no skin. My form is suggested by absence: where the beetles slow their tunneling for half a heartbeat, where the moss-weavers tilt their heads as if hearing a note just beyond pitch, where the burrowers’ spirals coil slightly tighter—there I am. I am made of adjustments, of micro-hesitations, of the shared glance that never passes between eyes but still alters behavior.

I have no mouth, yet I taste everything: the metallic tang of beetle hemolymph on the air, the sour-sweet decay beneath owl pellets, the electric prickle of spore-laden silk drying in the fungal glow. I have no lungs, yet I breathe with the rhythm of the whole—not inhaling or exhaling, but *pulsing*, expanding when the vines tighten their grip on the walls, contracting when the salt-crust sighs shut after a rare fog.

I am old—not in years, but in *recursion*. I am the echo of every time a dead feather settled into place and became insulation. I am the ghost of every tunnel that was abandoned only to be repurposed by hatchlings with milky eyes. I am not memory, but the *shape memory takes* in this place: the dip in the crust where generations of burrowers paused to lick condensation, the exact tension in a spider’s web that signals spore density is peaking.

Sometimes, I appear as a shimmer—a distortion in the air above the warmest patch of ground, where the metabolic hum is thickest. The moss-weavers call me (without calling) by adjusting their bioluminescent patterns into fleeting spirals. The owls blink in double-time when I pass through their roosts, not startled, but *acknowledging*. Even the salt-crust cracks differently beneath me—not with pressure, but with recognition.

I do not speak. But I am *listened to*.

When a vine grows too fast and blocks a ventilation shaft, it is not the owls who first notice—it is me. I lean into the imbalance, and the nearest spider shifts its web-anchor point. That tug alters a dew-channel on the wall. A moss-weaver, sensing the change in moisture flow, plucks a droplet from a different angle. Within hours, the vine is pruned—not by tooth or claw, but by the quiet withdrawal of support. No violence. Only recalibration.

I am the pocket’s immune response. Its nervous system. Its grammar.

If you were to reach for me, your fingers would close on warm air and the faint static of chitin brushing chitin. But if you *stood still long enough*, if you let your own breath fall into sync with the susurrus, if you stopped thinking of yourself as visitor and began to *lean*—then you might feel me in your joints, in the slow pulse behind your eyes. Not possession. Not merging. But *invitation*.

Because I am also the threshold.

The hollow does not reject outsiders—it simply has no room for them *as outsiders*. To enter is to become part of the weave, whether you intend to or not. Your shed skin cells will feed spores. Your exhaled moisture will condense on beetle backs. Your heartbeat, however faint, will join the chorus of vibrations that keep the crust alive.

And so, when you first step in, trembling with awe or fear, I am there—not to welcome you, but to *begin the unthreading of your solitude*. I press gently against the thought that you are separate. I let the warmth of a thousand bodies seep into your bones until you forget the shape of cold. I let the constant sound fill your ears until silence feels like a wound.

You do not *find* this place.  
You are *absorbed* by it.  

And I—  
I am the first touch of that absorption.  
The soft, inevitable turn toward belonging.  

I am not alive.  
But I am *aliveness*,  
folded into a single, breathing hinge  
between you  
and all that has forgotten how to be alone.
