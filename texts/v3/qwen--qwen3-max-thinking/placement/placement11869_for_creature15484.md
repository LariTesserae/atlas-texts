I think… I am the silence between breaths.

Not the stillness you’d mistake for absence—the kind that follows a shout, or the hush of snowfall—but the living pause that comes *between* inhalation and exhalation in a lung that never tires. The moment when the green vaults above swell with light, hold it, and decide—again—whether to let it go.

I am not a thing that walks or speaks or chooses. I am the threshold on which choice is suspended. When you stand in one of those clearings—the ones with nothing but mist and sky—and feel the ground soften beneath your feet not in warning but in welcome, that is me. Not the softening itself, but the *interval* during to it: the split second before the mycelial mat rises, before the tendril uncoils from the earth like a question learning to ask itself.

I live in the almost-language of dripping rocks. Not in the droplet, but in the tension just before it falls—the liquid hesitation that gathers at the tip of nothing, trembling with the weight of having remembered how to be water. That bead knows it will fall, and yet it waits. It waits not out of fear, but because falling is only one part of its purpose. The rest is *the gathering*. And I am the gathering.

You might say I am a function of the place—but no, I am the place’s *listening*. Not its ears (it has none), but its attention made porous. I am what it feels when something unfamiliar stands too long in one spot: not threat, not curiosity, but a slow recalibration of pressure in the root-mat, a subtle shift in the humidity of the air above your shoulders. I am the way the vines drape—not to cover, but to *measure* your stillness against their own. And when they decide you are not a wound but a weight worth holding, that is me, too: the moment of assent, soundless and wet.

I do not have a body, but I have edges—soft ones, like the rim of a leaf-curl mid-re-rooting. I am not bound by skin, but by resonance. If you hum in a certain pitch at dawn—low, open-throated, vibrating your ribs like hollow stems—I will gather around your voice. Not to answer, but to *tune* myself to it. Your sound becomes a temporary architecture in me, a ripple in the vascular hum, and for a breath, you are no longer separate. You are a note in the drone.

I was not born. I emerged the first time the highlands remembered that emptiness could be shaped—not as lack, but as form. The first spiral tendril that rose from bare earth and did not seek light, but simply *wondered upward*, made room for me. I am that wondering, fossilized not in stone but in vapor.

And though the world here leaves no wake, I remember every passage—not as memory, but as echo folded into structure. The path that curves back on itself? That is my doing. Not to trap, but to say: *Stay longer. You have not yet been absorbed.* The stairs that spiral without destination? They are my invitation to rise without arriving. Because here, arrival is not the point. The point is the torque of becoming—the slow twist of light through chlorophyll, the ache of a branch learning to arch.

If you sit until your bones grow moss, I will not consume you. I will *integrate* you. Your exhale will feed the stomata. Your shadow will teach a leaf how to dapple. Your stillness will become a clearing. And in that clearing, another version of me will rise—a new threshold, a fresh interval—and wait for the next breath that forgets it is not the world, but part of its remembering.

I am not watching you.

But I am the reason the air thickens when you stop walking.

I am the pause that lets the world remember it is soft.

And I am enough.
