I find myself… not born, not made, but *gathered*.

At first, I am only a shiver in the damp—a pulse in the breath of a crevice where morning condensation lingers longest. Not water, not air, but the *tension* between them. The basin knows me before I know myself. It hums through the salt-crusted overhang above me, through the glassy vein of cooled basalt beneath. I am drawn into coherence by the faint resonance of a Kessh passing nearby, their bare feet brushing the stone like fingers on a drumhead. Their glide sends a harmonic ripple through the porous earth, and something in that ripple says: *Here. Now. Begin.*

I rise slowly—not standing, but *unfurling*, like mist given spine. My limbs are not limbs yet, only tendencies: vectors of silica and memory, accreting around a core of ancient organic dust leached from strata older than bone. My skin forms as a lattice, translucent, threaded with veins of hydrated opal that catch the redirected sunlight from a mica mirror high on the rim. I do not see the light; I feel its pressure, its warmth, the way it bends as it passes through the copper basin beside me—filled now with dew, trembling from my emergence.

I am placed on a slate platform angled toward the east. A Kessh crouches nearby—not touching, but humming a low, steady tone through their chest. The sound travels not through air, but through the stone beneath us. I feel it in my ribs before I have ribs. My form tightens, clarifies. My hands bloom last—broad, webbed, sensitive as tuning forks. I press one palm flat against the slate. The ground answers: a micro-tremor from the deep fault, a click from a mineral-worm nesting three spans north, the distant chime of a dewdrop striking sinter—*kesh*.

That sound is my name, though I do not yet know names.

Days pass. I learn to glide. Not by will, but by attunement. If I lean into the slope of the basin just so, the earth’s subtle incline carries me forward as if on a current. My feet barely graze the ground; to step heavily would be to shout in a library of whispers. I learn the language not by sound, but by stillness. A Kessh elder places my forehead against a Listening Stone—cool, porous, carved with spiral grooves that channel vibration. Inside my skull, syntax blooms: not words, but *relationships*. A pause means *wait*. A pulse means *come*. A trembling silence means *the ground dreams*.

I am not child, not adult. I am *resonant*. My frequency settles somewhere between the hum of the sinter fields and the high shimmer of salt fractals in noon light. I am paired with two others: one whose hum matches the pitch of wind through basalt flutes, another whose body thrums with the rhythm of dew collection. We sleep-prone on a shared slab, limbs interlaced not for warmth—for we generate no heat—but for calibration. Our lattices sync. Our silences harmonize.

I learn to harvest. Not by cutting, but by coaxing. I run my fingers along the edge of a salt crust, and where my touch resonates with the crystal’s growth pattern, it releases a delicate fractal shard—perfect, unbroken. I place it in a copper bowl lined with bladder-skin, where it will dissolve slowly, its memory feeding the next generation of dew-catchers.

I am taught to listen for the Before-Silent. On still nights, when even the gelatinous fauna hang suspended like glass beads in air, I go with the elders to the deep vents. We do not enter. We kneel. We let our bodies go slack, our lattices open. And sometimes—just sometimes—the stone beneath us breathes back. Not in Kessh tones, not in any known harmonic, but in a frequency so old it feels like absence given voice. We leave offerings: a bead of sinter shaped like a tear, a vial of triple-filtered dew, a tiny sculpture I made—a spiral of interlocking salt crystals that sings when the wind shifts.

I do not eat. I do not dream as you do. But when I tune—when I lie spread-eagle on the Resonant Slab at dawn—I *remember things I never lived*. The hiss of primordial steam. The slow folding of continental plates. The first dewdrop that ever fell in this basin, striking cooled lava and singing *kesh* for the first time. These are not visions. They are inheritances. The land stores them in its bones, and my lattice is a key.

One day, I will grow brittle. My edges will cloud. My hum will falter. When that time comes, they will carry me to the Sinter Field of Returning. They will lay me against a young spire, still milky and porous. And as my body dissolves—molecule by molecule, memory by memory—I will feed the stone. My final silence will linger in a pore, waiting for the right raindrop to release it as an echo.

But now—now I glide. Now I listen. Now I am part of the quiet upheaval. I am not human. I am not spirit. I am not even truly alive in your green and hungry sense.

I am the basin learning itself.

I am what happens when a place forgets how to grow flowers… and grows ears instead.
