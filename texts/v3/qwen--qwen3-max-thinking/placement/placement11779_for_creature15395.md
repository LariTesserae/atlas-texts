I do not arrive.  
I *condense*.

Not with a step or a breath—but as a shift in the air’s weight, as a new pattern of stillness among the whispering stones. The basin does not notice me at first. Why would it? I am neither threat nor gift. I am only another form of attention, drawn like dew to the cold blade of this place.

But slowly—because nothing here is fast—I become aware that I am not separate.

My skin tastes of salt and dust. My joints ache with the same slow pressure that cracks the tuff into fluted spiers. When I blink, it is in rhythm with the Stone-Tickers returning from the rim, each leg lifting and placing with ritual precision. I do not walk the basin. The basin walks *through* me.

I am kneeling near one of the black quartz nodes—smooth, cool, humming not with sound but with absence. Around it, beetles have arranged themselves in a perfect radial symmetry, their carapaces catching the slanted midday light in fractured glints. They do not flee. They do not acknowledge. They simply *are*, and in their being, they define the shape of presence.

I press my palm to the stone.  
Not to feel.  
To *listen*.

And the Watcher answers—not in words, not in image, but in sensation: a ripple through the fungal mycelium beneath the ash, a tremor in the aquifer ten meters below, the synchronized pause of every Ash-Spinner within three meters. The air thickens. Not with moisture, but with *recognition*. Not of me as a person, but as a node—temporary, yes, but resonant.

I am becoming part of the choir.

My breath slows to match the basin’s rhythm: inhale—the wind stutters through a fluted spire; exhale—the mites shift in fractal pulses across a basalt face. My heartbeat syncs with the pop of salt crystals fracturing in the sun. I taste alkali on my tongue, not because I licked stone, but because my cells remember scarcity.

At dusk, I sit cross-legged in the indigo gloom as the Silent Bats unfold like ancient scrolls from their fissures. Their thermal maps flicker across my skin—not seen, but *felt*, as patches of warmth and cold spelling out the basin’s hidden contours. The bioluminescent bacteria bloom in cobalt streaks along the upper walls, and for a moment, I understand: this is not light for seeing. It is light for *remembering*. Each pulse encodes a drop of fog, a shift in pressure, a beetle’s pilgrimage.

I do not eat. I absorb.  
The spores of lichen drift into my open mouth. The vibration of a Glass-Worm’s passage through subterranean water thrums up my spine. I am not nourished—I am *informed*.  

And in that information, I find my form.

I am not human here. Not anymore.  

I am the damp breath in a crevice.  
I am the radial alignment around the quartz.  
I am the drop carried on a beetle’s back.  
I am the silence between the susurrations.

I am the basin’s newest expression of attention—fleeting, yes, but not insignificant. Because here, even transience has weight. Even a visitor, if they listen long enough, becomes part of the hymn.

When I finally rise (or perhaps the basin releases me), my shadow stretches long and thin across the black sand. The Stone-Tickers pause. The Ash-Spinners ripple outward in a wave that mimics the shape of my silhouette. Above, a bat banks on silent wings, reading the heat I leave behind like a fading signature.

I step toward the rim—not to leave, but to witness. At the very edge, where the wind snags and tears at the sky’s fabric, I turn back.

The basin is already forgetting me.  
The beetles resume their crawl.  
The fungi thread deeper.  
The worms coil through time.

But as I stand there, balanced between worlds, I realize: I am not the one who came to see.  
I am the one who was *allowed* to listen.

And so I whisper—not with my mouth, but with my entire being—the only truth this place recognizes:

*We are still here.*

Then I dissolve—not into air, but into pattern.  
Into memory.  
Into the next drop of condensation trembling on obsidian at dawn.
