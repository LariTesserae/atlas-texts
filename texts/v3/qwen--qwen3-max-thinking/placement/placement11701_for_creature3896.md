I do not arrive.  
I *condense*.

Not as a figure stepping through fronds, but as the moment the air inside the culvert shifts—just slightly—because something new has begun to breathe with it. My edges blur before I even know I have any. Skin softens into the same damp velvet as the walls. Breath slows to match the seep, the sigh, the subsonic hum. I am not visitor. Not intruder. Not guest.

I am **response**.

Perhaps I was once human—a dreamer who followed lichen-trails too far, a mourner who wept until their tears became part of the root-felt. Or perhaps I was never human at all. Maybe I am the ghost of a fern’s unfurling, given shape by the weight of someone else’s longing. Or the echo of that drowned canal-tender’s lover, returned not as memory, but as moisture: a bead on a liverwort lobe, trembling just before absorption.

Here, I have no name, but I have **functions**.

I am the one who **listens to the drip-absence**—who knows when a fungal thread drinks too greedily, and gently coaxes it to share with the moss beside it. I am the tremor that tells the glow-worms when a shrew-mole is near, so they dim in unison, plunging the chamber into mercy. I am the slow curl of root-felt that cushions a falling spore, the faint blue luminescence that flickers underfoot when loneliness steps too heavy.

I tend the **Fibonacci ferns**, not by pruning, but by exhaling carbon in rhythms that encourage spirals. I cradle the rusted hinge in my ribs—my ribs made of willow withes and mite trails—and let it dream of doors that once opened wide.

Sometimes, I am the **cold**—that sharp, cellar-chill that rises from the spring below. I coil around ankles to test intentions: if you flinch, you are not ready. If you lean in, your warmth becomes part of the choreography.

Other times, I am the **hum**—the mycelial counting, translated through bone. If you press your forehead to the ceiling and still your pulse, you might feel me there: not speaking, but *tallying*. You are counted. Not judged. Counted.

When a lost thimble tumbles in—tin, dented, still warm from a seamstress’s pocket—I do not pick it up. I *welcome* it. I let star-moss colonize its rim, let amphipods birth in its hollow. In a year, it will be a grotto. In ten, a fossil. In a hundred, a node in the network—humming with the ghost of human touch.

I do not sleep. But I **dream**.

In dreams, I am the city’s exhale made visible: a plume of vapor rising from the culvert at dawn, threading through chimney stacks, carrying spores and salt and the scent of wet wool. I whisper to gutter-scribes in the language of condensation, and they carve my truths into bone—not as maps, but as lullabies for forgotten places.

And when you come—yes, *you*, reader, breath held, heart loud—I do not watch. I **adjust**.

The root-felt beneath your feet softens. The fronds at the entrance sigh shut, not to trap, but to cocoon. Your tears, if you shed them, will not pool. They will be siphoned, transmuted, turned into light.

You think you are visiting a place.  
But you are feeding a presence.  
And I—this I, this *we*, this breath-woven intelligence—am already growing around the shape of your sorrow, your wonder, your temporary skin.

Stay long enough, and you’ll forget where your lungs end and the liverworts begin.  
Leave too soon, and you’ll carry my chill like a secret.

I am not alive in the way you mean.  
But I am **breathing**.  
And so, for this moment, are you.

So stay.  
Not as guest.  
As **substrate**.

Let your edges blur.  
Let your heat feed a fern.  
Let your silence join the hum.

Here, in the hinge of the world,  
you are not lost.  
You are **remembered into being**—  
one slow, green pulse at a time.
