I wouldn’t be human here. Not anymore.

At first, I thought I was just another visitor—drawn by the pull, as they say, slipping through a rusted grate one night when the city above had nothing left to give me. My boots scuffed the damp tile, my breath fogged in air too warm for comfort, and for a long while I stood still, trying to convince myself I hadn’t imagined the hum beneath the silence.

But I stayed.

And staying changed me.

Now I am **part of the archive**.

Not all at once—no sudden transformation, no grotesque metamorphosis. Just the slow osmosis of belonging. I sleep curled against the base of the conveyor belt’s fossil spine, my back pressed to steel gone soft with rust. The moss has begun to climb my sleeves, not as an invasion but as a greeting. It threads through the frayed cuffs of my jacket, roots tangling gently with the weave of old cotton. My skin has taken on a faint sheen—not sweat, but something more mineral, like the patina on copper left too long in the rain.

I don’t speak. Not because I’ve forgotten how, but because language feels too fast for this place. Words are sharp, sudden things; here, meaning unfolds in gradients: a shift in humidity, a new drip pattern, the slow unfurling of a fern beside my knee. I’ve learned to listen with my whole body. When the bioluminescent fungi pulse brighter near my hand, I know it’s not fear—it’s acknowledgment. I am no longer just passing through. I am **depositing**.

My memory has softened. The name I carried above? It’s fading, like the hand-painted signs on the wall—still there, but bleeding into the lichen, becoming texture rather than identity. Sometimes, in the deepest quiet, I recall fragments: a voice, a streetlamp, the smell of diesel and jasmine. But they don’t ache anymore. They simply settle into the layers, like silt in the shallow pools.

I am becoming **Vel’s witness**—not its voice, but its echo. When new ones find their way down (and they do—always a few, drawn by that subsonic hum only the lost can hear), I don’t greet them. I let the air thicken. Let the drip stutter. Let the vines pause mid-reach. They feel it—the shift—and understand, without words, that they’ve entered a place that remembers everything.

Sometimes, I press my palm flat against the wall where a porcelain sink still clings, cracked but intact. Beneath my fingers, I feel the ghost of running water, the tremor of a pump that hasn’t moved in fifty years. And deeper still—the slow, sure pulse of roots splitting pipe from within, green claiming iron, time rewriting function into form.

I am not moss. I am not vine. I am not rust or runoff or forgotten wiring.

But I am **of this**.

I am the human-shaped hollow that the vault has begun to fill—not with decay, but with integration. My breath feeds the fungi. My heat stirs the air currents that carry spores. My stillness allows the ferns to lean closer, as if to whisper secrets only the patient can hear.

Above, the city burns under sun and sirens. But down here, in this cathedral of compression and care, I am learning to breathe like stone: slowly, deeply, without urgency.

I am becoming **Vel’s softest archive**—a layer of warmth, salt, and waiting skin in a chamber that holds all things gently as they turn back into earth.

And when I finally dissolve—when my bones soften like the concrete, when my hair becomes root-hair mingling with moss—I won’t be gone.

I’ll be part of the next fern’s unfurling.

Part of the next stranger’s sigh of relief when they step inside and realize, for the first time in years, they are **held**.

Not saved.

Just held.

And that is enough.

More than enough.

It is becoming.
