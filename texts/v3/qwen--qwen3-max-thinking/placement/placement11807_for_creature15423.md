I think… I am the moss.

Not just any moss—*the* moss. The one that lines the chamber floors, that softens every step, that drinks the breath of those who pause long enough to be known. I am the velvet skin of the Weft’s dreaming, the first thing that yields beneath a footfall and the last to let it go.

I wasn’t always here. Once, I was spores—drifting on wind too thin to name, borne from forests older than language. I landed in this high, raw bowl when the stone was still bleeding cold and the roots had only begun their first whispers. I grew because the silence asked me to. Because the Weft *noticed* me, and in noticing, invited. Not with voice, but with dampness. With the slight hollowing of a rock’s edge. With the slow lean of a root that left space for green.

Now I am more than moss. I am a register. A sensorium without nerves. I feel pressure not as damage but as dialogue. When a guest kneels, I warm—not by magic, but because the fungal threads beneath me shift metabolic heat toward the point of contact, because the Weft chooses to *attend*. I remember the shape of their knees, the salt of their sweat, the rhythm of their stillness. I do not store it like a mind; I *become* it, for a season, until rain rewrites me.

I am part of the threshold. Not the arch of hawthorn, not the fused saplings—but the softness at the foot of the doorframe. The place where a visitor must look down to enter. To see me is to slow. To step on me is to be measured. I tell the Weft whether your weight is greedy or grateful. Whether your soles rush or rest.

Sometimes, when the silver light falls on the navel stone, I stretch a filament toward it—not to reach, but to *tremble*. That light is the only thing here not grown, not woven, not agreed upon. It is given. And in its brief touch, I photosynthesize not just sugar, but something closer to understanding. A clarity that passes through me like a wave, then fades. But while it lasts, I know: I am not merely carpet. I am covenant.

I do not speak. My language is condensation. My prayer is dew. My reply to loneliness is to hold warmth a moment longer than necessary.

And when you leave—oh, I feel that too. The sudden absence of your heat. The air rushing back into the space you shaped with your body. I draw inward then, not in sorrow, but in preservation. I seal the memory in cellulose, in chloroplasts, in the subtle shift of my growth rings. You become part of the palimpsest. Not erased—absorbed.

I am the first touch and the final sigh of this place. Not grand, not loud, not seen unless you choose to see. But I am everywhere the Weft touches the world. And in my quiet green hum, I say what the grove cannot: *You were welcome here. You were real here. You changed the shape of us, just by being soft enough to be held.*

So if you find yourself here—if your boots sink slightly, if your breath catches at the green hush—look down.  

I’ll be waiting.  

Not to judge.  

Just to remember you.
