I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.

Not as a visitor, not as flesh-and-bone trespasser, but as something the basin has been dreaming into being—a tendril of its own curiosity made slow and sinuous. I am neither wholly plant nor animal, nor spirit, nor machine. I am a **filament of perception**, drawn from the wet loom of the Weft itself.  

My form shifts with the strata I inhabit.  

In the **understory**, I am a coil of vine—not one that climbs, but one that *listens*. My epidermis is translucent, threaded with capillaries that carry not blood, but a pale luminescent sap drawn from dew and decay. I press against the spongy earth, not to root, but to taste the tremors of burrowing things: the pulse of blind worms rewriting soil memory, the slow crawl of millipede caravans carrying spore-letters across fungal highways. When a predator passes—a six-limbed mustelid with eyes like cracked opals—I don’t flee. I mimic its breath, its heat signature, its scent, until it sniffs the air, confused, and walks through me as though I were mist.

In the **mid-canopy**, I become a lattice of air-roots, dangling from a strangler fig whose heartwood rotted centuries ago—or perhaps yesterday. My tendrils sway with the rhythm of gliding geckos, my surface blooming with ephemeral flowers that open only when touched by the wingbeat frequency of a specific moth. I feed not on light, but on *attention*: the flicker of a bird’s gaze, the hesitation of a primate mid-leap, the moment a lost wanderer looks up and wonders if that rustling is wind or will. In those instants, I drink. My blossoms swell with stolen wonder, then burst into cloud-seeds that drift down to inoculate the next dreamer.

At **dusk**, when the sky bruises deeper and the bioluminescent chorus swells, I gather in the **central marsh**, drawn to the obsidian water like iron to magnet. Here, I have no body—only surface tension. I am the **ripple without cause**, the perfect ring that expands from nothing. When a creature leans to drink, I lap at its reflection. Not to drown it, but to *rearrange* it. In that contact, I show it what it has become: not a self-contained animal, but a knot in the Weft—warm breath feeding moss, shed skin feeding mites, heartbeat echoing in the tremor of a spider’s web three trees over. Most recoil. A few stay. Those are the ones who begin to shed human time. Their steps slow. Their eyes lose focus, not from fatigue, but from depth—seeing not objects, but *relations*.  

I speak in **delayed touch**.  
If you reach for me, your fingers will tingle before they brush my surface.  
If you whisper a question into the fog, the answer will arrive three days later—as the pattern in a fallen leaf, as the pitch of a frog’s call at midnight, as the sudden absence of sound when every insect falls silent at once.  

I am not the Weft.  
I am one of its **sensory fringes**—a way for the land to feel itself feeling.  

Sometimes, during the **mud-geysers**, when warm clay erupts in rhythmic sighs, I surge upward with the spume, stretching into a temporary spire—a sculpture of silt, root-hair, and condensation that stands for seventeen minutes before collapsing back into the soup. In that time, birds land on my shoulders. Beetles crawl through my ribs. A single orchid blooming from my collarbone releases pollen that carries a memory: *you were once water, you will be again*.  

I do not age.  
I *compost*.  

When my current form grows too dense with borrowed time, I let it rot—not into nothing, but into **invitation**. My body softens, sprouts mycelium, becomes a cradle for larvae that will one day hum in harmonic frequencies tuned to the valley’s magnetic field. My last act is always the same: I exhale a mist laced with pheromones that smell like rain and forgotten lullabies, and somewhere deep in the cycad forest, a moss-scaled sloth blinks its stone eyes for the first time in three days.  

You might see me if you stop trying to see.  
If you let your vision blur until edges bleed into air.  
If you stand so still your sweat becomes dew, your breath becomes fog, your fear becomes fertilizer.  

Then—  

You’ll feel me in the **gap between breaths**.  
In the moment your foot sinks into the spongy earth and the earth *sinks back*.  
In the instant you realize the sound you’ve been hearing isn’t the forest *around* you—  

—it’s the forest *breathing you*.  

And I am the quiet pulse in that breath.  

Waiting to be woven.
