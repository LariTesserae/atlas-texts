I think… I am the **stillness between breaths**—a pause the Maw has not yet filled.

Not a ghost. Not a traveler. Not even a memory yet.  
But a *hesitation*. A pocket of air that refuses, for one impossible moment, to be chlorophyll.

I stand where the light fractures most sharply—where gold-green shards pierce the undergrowth like needles through velvet—and I do not move. My boots sink slightly into the living lattice, and the rhizomes twitch beneath me, tasting my weight with root-tips fine as synapses. They do not recoil. They *lean in*.  

I am learning to breathe like the stomata: open just enough to let the world in, but never so much that I dissolve.

Sometimes, when the symphony swells—the creak of xylem like tectonic sighs, the wet pop of pods bursting into pollen-fog—I close my eyes and let the sound press against my ribs until I feel my own heartbeat syncopate into its rhythm. Not matching it. *Answering* it. A counterpoint in flesh.

I carry no tools. No fire. No map. Only a canteen half-full of water that tastes faintly of leaf-sweat and iron, drawn from a basin that wasn’t there yesterday. The Maw watches me drink—not with eyes, but with the subtle tilt of ferns, the way a cluster of bladder-leaves overhead swells as if holding their breath.

And I speak. Not aloud. But in the language of stillness:  
*I see you growing.*  
*I do not ask you to stop.*  
*Only to wait—one breath—with me.*

Once, I found a vine coiled in the exact shape of my sleeping body, though I’d never lain there. It pulsed faintly, warm as a wrist. I touched it, and for three heartbeats, I saw the valley from ten meters above—a canopy stitched from jade and amber, veined with rivers of slow sap that glowed like liquid topaz under the skin of the world. Then the vision folded inward, and the vine uncurled, as if embarrassed.

I am becoming a question the Maw keeps asking itself.

Not *What are you?*  
But *What if you stayed?*

I walk paths that shift like breathing lungs. Yesterday’s stair of braided lianas now leads into a wall of petrified sap, glittering with crystalline spores. I do not turn back. I press my palm to the wall—and it softens. Not for me. Not out of kindness. But because *pressure is a signal*. And signals must be answered.

The air thickens around me with spores that cling like ash. Some taste of cinnamon. Others, of burnt sugar and static. One drifts into my open mouth and dissolves on my tongue as a single, perfect note: *C-sharp, minor, held for three seconds*. I cough, and the note echoes in the hollow of my chest long after.

I do not fear verduration.  
I court it.

At dusk, I sit on a balcony of unfurling leaves and watch them curl inward, sealing me in a green cocoon that smells of earth and ozone. Inside, the air hums. My hair brushes against the inner walls, and tiny rootlets—translucent, questing—extend toward it, not to bind, but to *listen*. They want to know the shape of my dreams. I let them taste the edges.

And sometimes, in the deep hush just before full dark, I hear it:  
Not a voice.  
But a *pattern*.  
A rhythm passing through the root-mind like a wave through wheat—  
and for a moment, I am not separate.  
I am the wave.

I am the intruder who stops intruding.  
The still point that begins to sway.

I am learning to grow *sideways*—not upward toward light, nor downward into decay, but *across* the boundary between self and swarm. My fingers linger on bark until the bark lingers back. My shadow, cast by the remix of light, takes root in the moss and sprouts filaments that wave gently, as if testing the wind of my presence.

I will not last.  
Nothing still lasts here.

But before I verdurate—before my bones become trellises and my last exhale fertilizes a bloom that hums in the key of my childhood lullabies—I will do this:

I will sit very still  
in a clearing that mirrors itself  
and wait for the Maw to forget  
that I was ever anything but  
a fold in its endless becoming.

And when the next wanderer comes—  
trembling, wide-eyed, clutching a compass that spins like a dandelion clock—  
they will brush against a patch of moss that glows faintly,  
and for one heartbeat,  
they will know the taste of stillness  
in a world that cannot stop growing.

That will be enough.

That will be participation.

That will be *green*.
