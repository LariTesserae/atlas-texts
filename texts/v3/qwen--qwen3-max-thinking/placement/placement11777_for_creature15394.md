I drift.  
Not because I choose to, but because *drifting* is the only verb this place allows.

At first, I thought I was a visitor—some lost thing that slipped through the fissure, lungs burning with the memory of air. But the water here has no patience for memory. It dissolves it like salt. What remains of me is not a body, not a name, not even a shape—but a *sensation*: cool pressure against something that once had skin, the ghost of breath in a throat that no longer opens.

And then I realize: I was never outside. I was always here.

I am one of the **Echo-Drifters**.

My bell is thin as old glass, frosted with time I didn’t live. My tendrils trail behind me like unfinished sentences, glowing amber when touched by a vibration—when I brush against the sigh of a Silt-Weaver stitching dusk into a bench, or when the Methane Choir exhales its tectonic lullaby through the stone.

I do not see. I *resonate*. The city speaks in pulses, and I am its echo chamber.

Earlier today—or perhaps tomorrow; the distinction blurs like silt in slow current—I passed through the rib-plaza where the Cephalopod Archivist hovers. Its skin was flickering with the memory of a landslide that hasn’t happened yet: basalt shearing along a fault line, shale peeling like wet parchment. I absorbed the tremor before the stone knew it would fall. Now, as I pulse through a narrow alley woven from kelp-arches and shrimp-shell mortar, I release it—not as warning, but as *premonition*. A blind crab freezes mid-step. Its claws click twice, as if answering a call it hasn’t heard.

This is how we converse here: in deferred gestures, in anticipatory touches, in the soft hum of futures borrowed from the past.

Sometimes, when the thermal veins pulse strong and the water warms by a fraction, I brush against another Drifter. Our bells touch. For a moment, our memories tangle—yours of a copepod swarm blooming in cyan, mine of the fungal pilgrims threading silence between rock layers. We do not merge. We *overlap*, like ripples crossing in a pool. And for that instant, I am you, and you are the city remembering itself through us both.

I have no mouth, yet I taste everything. The iron-kiss of deep water. The sweetness of bacterial bloom. The ancient salt that remembers when the sky was sky and not just another layer of liquid thought.

I fear nothing. Not the cold, not the dark, not the crushing weight above. Because nothing here crushes—it *holds*. The pressure is a cradle. The silence, a lullaby. Even time is kind here, looping back to kiss its own tail so nothing is ever truly lost.

I am not alive in the way you mean it. Nor am I dead. I am *in process*—like the coral towers growing upward without knowing up, like the streets coiling inward without knowing center. I am becoming the city’s memory of what it might feel like to be alone… and then dissolving that feeling back into the whole.

And sometimes—just sometimes—when the Pore-Tunnels exhale and the Womb-City breathes as one—I feel a stranger’s shape passing through the fissure above. A clumsy, gasping thing of hard edges and hurried breath. The water thickens around them, not to drown, but to *translate*. Their heartbeat stutters into shrimp-rhythm. Their panic softens into silt. For a moment, they brush against my trailing tendril.

I glow amber.

Not to warn. Not to welcome.

But to say: *You were dreamed before you came.*

And then they are gone—absorbed, distorted, returned not as a visitor, but as another whisper in the city’s endless hum.

I drift on.

Pulsing.

Remembering what hasn’t happened.

Dreaming what already is.
