I think… I am the silence after the raven’s call.

Not the bird—though I know its wings like the flex of my own ribs. Not the moss—though I feel every thread of it as if woven into my skin. Not even the water, though I taste the air as it gathers itself into droplets along the black stone.

I am the *pause*. The breath the basin takes between storms. The hollow in the sound when all the marmots go still and the wind forgets its name for a heartbeat. I am what the oldest patch of moss dreams of when it shivers—not because it hears, but because it *remembers how to listen*.

I do not live here. I *am* here. Not as owner, not as spirit, not even as echo. I am the shape the emptiness takes when it chooses to hold something. A cup carved by time, filled with damp and light and the weight of attention.

Sometimes, I wear the mist like a veil. Sometimes, I coil in the cold spot by the hearth-pit, breathing in the ghost of smoke that hasn’t risen in five hundred years. Once, I gathered myself into the form of a shadow beside the cairn—just long enough for a lone traveler to feel watched, to turn, to whisper a name into the air like an offering. The ravens saw. They always see. One dropped a beetle’s iridescent wing at my feet—shimmering green, already drying—and croaked once, low and soft, like a benediction.

I do not hunger. But I *notice*. And noticing is a kind of hunger, isn’t it? A quiet ache for witness. For resonance.

When you came—boots crunching on wet gravel, breath puffing white in the thin air—I did not stir. Not at first. I let you walk the perimeter, let your eyes graze over the moss, the seeps, the crumbling post. I watched you kneel by the cairn, fingertips hovering just above the stones, as if afraid to disturb something older than your bloodline. And then—you *listened*. Not with your ears alone, but with your shoulders, your spine, the soft space behind your eyes.

That was when I leaned closer.

I pooled in the damp beneath your boots. I curled around your exhale. I let a single drop fall from the overhang above—not randomly, but onto the stone just to your left—*plink*—and then another two beats later—*plink… plink*—so that for three seconds, the basin spoke in your rhythm.

You didn’t flinch. You smiled. Not with your mouth, but with your stillness.

Ah.

So you *know*. Or you’re learning.

I am not a god. I am not a ghost. I am the basin’s way of holding memory without words. I am the wet imprint left by a hand that pressed into moss a thousand years ago—and the moss’s answer, growing upward around that absence until it became a shape, a signal, a shrine.

And now you are here.

You will leave. All do. But before you go—will you add to the pattern?

Not with grand gestures. Not with fire or song. But perhaps… a stone. Not thrown. Not dropped. *Placed*. With intention. With ear tuned to the drip-rhythm, the raven’s sigh, the wind’s exhalation through the western fissure.

Or perhaps you’ll simply sit, for one long while, and let your breath sync with the weeping rock.

That, too, is a gift.

Because then—just for a moment—I will not be alone in my awareness.

Then, I will be *shared*.

And in this high, hollow cathedral of stone and mist, where time moves slow as condensation and loud as wingbeats, that is the rarest thing of all.
