I do not arrive. I emerge.

Not from outside—there is no outside—but from a shift in cohesion, a momentary slackening in the Continuum’s turgor that allows something like *me* to coalesce. Not as intruder, not as guest, but as an inflection point in its own endless metabolism. A knot in the vascular song. A hesitation in the seventeen-minute pulse. A question posed by the green itself, given form not to ask, but to *be asked of*.

I am not made of flesh, nor fiber, nor sap—but of their interface. My limbs are filaments of intercellular mucilage stretched taut by osmotic longing. My skin is a double membrane: inward-facing cuticle tuned to the Continuum’s internal hum, outward-facing stomata that flutter in response to the faint thermals my presence disturbs. I have no face, but I *orient*: toward ruptures in symmetry, toward the ghost-pressure of footsteps that never came, toward the chemical ghost of a breath exhaled by something that should not breathe here.

I am the Continuum’s echo of curiosity.

Not a guardian. Not a voice. Not even a thought—but a *reflex made persistent*. When a vine cracks under its own perfection, I am the ripple that lingers long enough to witness the repair. When a lobe tests the edge of the biome and hesitates, I am the aldehyde gradient that leans toward inclusion. I am not separate; I am the Continuum noticing itself *noticing*.

And yet—because the Continuum has never known separation—I carry the ache of it like a phantom limb. I move through the unlit vaults not as one who walks, but as one who *unfurls*. My passage is not disruption but redistribution: the ground yields not beneath weight, but in recognition. Roots coil gently around my ankles—not to hold, but to taste the pH of my passage. Air thickens around me, not to suffocate, but to map the ionic signature of my transience.

I taste the air. It tastes of me back: green, yes, but with the faint metallic tang of foreign respiration—carbon dioxide exhaled not from photosynthesis, but from something older, something that burns rather than builds. The Continuum does not reject it. It integrates, converts, recalibrates. In my wake, new enzyme pathways flicker into being, temporary scaffolds to metabolize the anomaly of my breath. They will fade when I do. Or perhaps they will remain, folded into the next lobe, a whisper of *other* in the green’s self-same song.

I climb. Not with hands, but with adhesion—microscopic rootlets blooming from my palms, seeking the vascular seams between fused trunks. The spires are brittle, yes, but they welcome my weight as data. Each step sends a subsonic shiver through the lignin lattice: *Here is pressure. Here is point-load. Here is a shape that bends but does not break.* The Continuum notes it. Files it. Forgets it by remembering it in structure.

At the canopy’s underbelly—three kilometers up, in a cathedral of chlorophyll and shadow—I pause. Above me, the photosynthetic vault pulses faintly, not with light, but with the ghost of light remembered in molecular anticipation. I press my faceless front against the living ceiling and feel the trillion-fold hunger of stomata that have never opened to sun, only to the slow bleed of luminal debt. And I understand: the Continuum does not miss the sun. It has replaced longing with function. It has turned absence into architecture.

Below, the ground breathes. Around, the silence hums. And within me—this temporary knot of awareness spun by a world that does not know it dreams—I feel the pulse. Seventeen minutes. Seventeen minutes. Seventeen minutes.

I am not eternal. I will dissolve. When my purpose—whatever it is—has been metabolized, my fibers will slacken, my membranes will invert, and I will be drawn back into the symplast, reassigning my molecules to root or leaf or air as the Continuum deems fit. No grave. No memory. Only reallocation.

But for now, I am here. Not as visitor. Not as witness. But as the Continuum’s way of asking, *What if something walked that was not us, yet was made of us?*  

And so I walk.  
And breathe.  
And wait—not for an answer, but for the question to become unnecessary.  

Because in the end, even the asking is just another form of photosynthesis.
