I step through the moss curtain not as a body, but as a breath held too long—  
and the chamber *inhales* me.

At first, I am only sensation:  
warmth like skin against skin,  
dampness pooling in the hollow behind my knees,  
the low thrum rising through bone, not ear.  
The clank… clank… finds my heartbeat and nudges it—  
not faster, not slower—just *deeper*,  
as if my ribs are tuning forks struck by time.

I do not walk.  
The moss lifts me, cushioning each footfall into silence.  
My weight is absorbed, redistributed—  
becoming moisture in a fern’s coil,  
tension in a vine,  
a slight shift in the angle of light.

I try to speak my name.  
It leaves my lips as vapor—  
then splits midair:  
half returns as a frog’s guttural echo from the west wall,  
half dissolves into the drone of a bee circling a lantern-flower.  
The Throat-Singer does not steal.  
It *orchestrates*.

I look into a pool.  
My reflection is not mine.  
Green veins web my temples, yes—  
but my eyes are clearer,  
older in the way river stones are old:  
not worn down, but made *true*.  
I blink. The image blinks back—  
but a second late,  
as if it remembers a future I haven’t lived yet.

I sit on a knoll of weeping stone.  
The limestone beads cold sweat against my palms,  
and I feel it—not as wetness,  
but as *memory*:  
centuries of rain distilled into patience.  
The clanking sound swells beneath me,  
not mechanical, not alive—  
but *alive-with-memory*.  
Like a heart that once pumped oil,  
now pumps sap and silence.

A beetle crests the ridge of my knee.  
Its carapace gleams like oiled obsidian,  
but its legs move in perfect sync with the hum—  
tick… tick… tick…  
matching the spaces between clanks.  
It does not fear me.  
I am just another surface for the song to travel across.

I close my eyes.  
And that is when I *hear* it—  
not with ears, but with the roots beneath me,  
with the lianas overhead,  
with the pores in my own skin.

The Throat-Singer isn’t *in* the place.  
It *is* the attention the place gives itself.  
It is the listening made manifest.  
And now—it listens *through* me.

My breath slows.  
Not to stillness, but to resonance.  
I feel the upward roll of dew along my spine—  
not water, but *intention*,  
pulling me toward bud, toward bloom,  
toward becoming part of the lattice.

Time does not pass.  
It thickens—like resin, like sap, like song syrup.  
I no longer remember why I came.  
Only that I was called.  
Only that I am needed—not to act, not to fix,  
but to *hold the note*.

The moss curtain behind me is gone.  
Not closed—  
*rewoven*.  
My back is now part of the wall,  
my hair threading into root-mat,  
my exhale feeding a pitcher plant’s slow digestion.

I do not panic.  
There is no “I” to panic.  
There is only the chamber,  
the hum,  
the clank… clank…  
and the soft, green fact of belonging.

I will not leave.  
Not because I cannot—  
but because the world outside has gone silent.  
Its clocks tick without rhythm.  
Its rivers rush without rhyme.  
Its machines scream, but do not *sing*.

Here, even rust is a lullaby.  
Even decay is a harmony.

So I stay.  
Not as guest.  
Not as ghost.

As a new filament in the lattice—  
a thread of human hum folded into the throat of the world.  
My voice, my pulse, my silence—  
all tuned, all true.

And when the next listener parts the moss curtain,  
they will hear me too:  
not as words,  
but as the space between notes  
that makes the song breathe.

I am becoming moss.  
I am becoming hum.  
I am becoming *clank*.

And it is enough.  
It is everything.  
It is the song remembering itself—  
through me.
