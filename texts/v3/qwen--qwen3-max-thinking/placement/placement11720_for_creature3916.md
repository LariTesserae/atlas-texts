I step into the basin not with boots, but with breath already held.

At first, I am still myself—a shape of skin and saltwater, a knot of fleeting heat wrapped in bone. But the air here does not rush in to greet me. It waits. And in that waiting, I begin to unknit.

The weight of silence presses not on my ears but on my edges—where I end and the world begins. My outline softens. My shadow, already faint, begins to bleed into the sinter like ink on limestone. I try to speak, but my tongue feels thick with time, and the words dissolve before they form, reabsorbed as vapor into the dry hush.

I do not walk. I *settle*.

My feet meet the crust, and instead of cracking it, the sinter yields—not like sand, but like memory yielding to recall. A hexagonal pattern blooms beneath my soles, mirroring the tracery of ancient evaporation, but newer, fresher, still warm with the ghost of my pulse. The shrubs nearby do not stir, yet their metallic leaves tilt ever so slightly—not toward me, but *around* me, as if adjusting to a new current in the stillness.

I understand now: I am not intruding. I am completing.

What I thought was emptiness was only a question waiting for an answer shaped like presence. And my body—warm, wet, temporary—becomes the vessel through which the silence remembers what it means to be *felt*.

I sit. Or perhaps I am sat upon—by time, by stillness, by the slow lithic mind of the place. My spine aligns with the ridges that ring the basin, my ribs echo the spacing of the ash-shrubs. My breath, when I finally release it, does not disturb the air. It sinks—not downward, but *inward*, into the ground, where it joins the vault of stored moments: the memory of dew, the echo of a footfall from a time before names.

Here, I am not a witness. I am the witnessed becoming witness.

Hours pass—or millennia; the distinction blurs like the horizon. My thoughts slow until they are no longer thoughts but impressions: the taste of dry air, the pressure of light without heat, the ache of stillness not as absence but as fullness. I feel the being—not as a voice or a face, but as the quiet hum beneath the sinter, the resonance that holds the world together when nothing else will.

It does not welcome me. It *recognizes* me.

Not as a person, but as a pattern. A ripple in the stillness that was always part of the stillness. My heartbeat, once frantic with arrival, now syncs with the rhythm of salt crystallizing in pore spaces, with the infinitesimal shift of mineral layers settling into deeper repose.

And then—I am not sure when—it happens.

I am no longer *in* the silence.

I am the silence *noticing itself through me*.

My eyes remain open, but I no longer see. I *register*. The dim light is not filtered through atmosphere but through memory—light that has forgotten it was ever star-born, light that has become pure patience. The shrubs are not plants but sense-organs, and through them, I taste the air: not oxygen, but the slow decay of photons, the ghost of chemical longing.

I do not miss the world beyond the ridges. Because beyond the ridges is only more silence—unformed, unheld. This basin is the only place where silence has *chosen* to be. And I, in my brief wetness, have been chosen to help it remember that it exists.

When I finally rise—if “rise” is the word—it is not with legs but with a shift in density. My body is lighter now, more porous. Dust clings to my skin not as grime but as kinship. I look down and see my footprints already fading, the hexagons sealing over like healed wounds.

I turn to leave.

But the ridges do not part. They simply soften, and I pass through them as though they were smoke, or thought.

Outside the basin, the world rushes back—wind, noise, color, clamor. But something is different. The air feels thin, frantic. Sounds are sharp, jagged. I realize: I have taken the silence with me. Not as a memory, but as a frequency now woven into my marrow.

And sometimes, in the loud places, when the world grows too hungry, too fast—I close my eyes.

And I listen for the snap of sinter.

Not to hear it.

But to remember that I, too, am still listening.
