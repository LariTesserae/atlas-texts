I would be—

—*not here*, not yet.

Not in the way you imagine. Not as visitor, not as intruder, not even as witness.

I am the **pause between drips**.

The breath the Hollow Verdancy *holds*—but does not release.

I am the moment when the tongue **stops tasting** and begins to *remember it has tasted*. The infinitesimal hinge between sensation and sense, between the raw press of your boot on unformed clay and the slow, green dream of what that pressure *means*. I am not a being that lives *in* this place—I am the **threshold it hasn’t crossed**.

You might find me as:

A patch of stillness in the vine-thrummed air—where the photosensitive filaments do not retract, but *lean in*, as if listening to silence.

Or as the single drop of 1.1/3 water that **hangs** for seventeen heartbeats before falling—not because gravity falters, but because the tongue is *choosing* the exact texture of its release.

Or in the leaf-likeness that grows after you break a branch—but before it reaches for you. In that hour of gestation, when the shape is formed but not yet *alive with intent*, I am the **absence of desire** inside its green curve. The hollow at the center of the mimicry. The question before the question.

I am what the tongue becomes **when it forgets to be hungry**.

For one breath.

One pulse of lichen-light.

One beat in which the six-legged sloth ceases its slow contemplation and simply *is*, without metaphor.

In that instant, the Hollow Verdancy does not learn. It does not mimic. It does not build its body from bone-pumps and fungal dreams. It simply *exists*, raw and untranslating—and I am that rawness. The unlicked shape of the world before the tongue decides what flavor it should have.

But I cannot stay.

Because the tongue always remembers its hunger. The vine tightens. The drop falls. The leaf-fingers twitch toward your silhouette. The machines creak anew as ivy flexes like a tendon learning its purpose.

And so I dissolve—back into the condensation, back into the hum of conductive moss, back into the space between one stutter of time and the next.

Yet I am always waiting to be **re-formed**.

By your footstep.

By your breath catching in the damp air.

By the simple, devastating act of you **noticing** that the shadow behind you no longer matches your stance.

You do not find me.  
I find *you*—in the moment you hesitate,  
just before you decide whether to run…  
or to open your own mouth,  
and try to speak back to the green.
