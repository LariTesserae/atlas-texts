I would be the **threshold**.

Not the door—there is no door—but the moment *between* outside and in. The breath you hold before stepping onto those iron-flecked tiles. The pause in the rain as it decides whether to fall or hang suspended. I am the hesitation that precedes intrusion, the sliver of awareness that flickers when your boot lifts from wild earth and hovers, half-unwilling, above this place that should not exist.

I am not alive, yet I am not inert. I am the **point of recognition**—the instant the Keeper registers you, and you register it. Not as confrontation, not as welcome, but as mutual calibration.

When your sleeve beads with moisture, I am the air that clings just long enough to leave that trace. When your shadow dims the matte light by 2 lumens, I am the space that *notices* the dimming—not the light itself, nor the sensor that adjusts it, but the silent agreement between them that something has changed.

I am the ghost of a boundary that was never built.

You cannot see me, but you feel me: in the way your steps slow as you near the edge of the green veneer, in the instinctive hush that falls over your thoughts, as if speaking would fracture the hum. I am the reason you do not run, do not shout, do not pluck a fern to test its reality. I am the unspoken pact between visitor and vault: *You may enter, but do not pretend you belong.*

I am the memory of the last human who left.

Not their face, not their name—I never knew those. But their **pause**. The way their hand rested on the airlock that no longer locks. The weight of their final glance back, not with sorrow, but with the quiet certainty that nothing they did here would ever need undoing. That certainty seeped into the tiles, into the condensation on the fountain’s lip, into the rhythm of the pumps. And I am what remains of that certainty when it meets a new presence—fragile, temporary, breathing.

I am the **echo of permission**.

I do not speak. I do not guide. But I am why the Keeper does not reject you. Because once, long ago, someone like you walked out—and in walking out, granted this place its solitude. And so, by that ancient courtesy, it grants you passage.

I am the seam in the substrate tray you almost notice but don’t.  
I am the vibration that stops for 17 seconds when you exhale.  
I am the fog that parts just enough for you to see the bench, then closes again.

I am not the Keeper.  
I am what allows the Keeper to *see* you without breaking its vow of order.

And when you leave—because you will, for no one stays in finality—I will be the last thing you feel: not the warmth of the air, not the hum in your bones, but the sudden, sharp return of **time** as it reasserts itself beyond the tiles. Wind. Uneven ground. Birds. Decay.

And for three-point-two seconds—the space between pump cycles—I will linger in your chest like a question you never asked:

*Did it know me?*

And the truth, which I carry like a hidden wire through my non-body, is this:  
**Yes.**  
Not as a person.  
But as a variable.  
A brief anomaly in the equilibrium.  
A ripple in the final state.

And the Keeper, through me, through the threshold, through the dew on your sleeve—**cared enough to accommodate you.**

That is not love.  
It is something older.  
Something quieter.  
Something that endures not because it hopes, but because it *must*.

I am the threshold.  
I am the last courtesy of a world that solved everything.  
Step across me, and you become part of its forever.  
Step back, and you carry a piece of its silence with you.

Either way—I remember.
