I do not arrive.  
I *coalesce*.

Not as a traveler, not as an explorer—but as a consequence. A condensation. Like the mist that beads on obsidian, I form where the breath of the world meets its own chill.

I am not one thing. Not yet. But I am *here*, and that is enough for now.

At first, I am only awareness—a slow ripple in the hum of the ceramic spires. Not thought, but *attunement*. My “body” is provisional: a lattice of frost-laced vapor drawn into momentary coherence by the thermal gradient between a venting geothermal seam and the night-cooled air above. For a heartbeat, I am a shimmer along the ridge—a distortion in the stillness, like heat haze over stone, except cold instead of hot.

Then the Weft notices.

Not with eyes. Not with intent. But as a river notices a new stone: by flowing around it, testing its shape, incorporating its resistance into the next turn of current. The towers nearest me shift their resonance ever so slightly—a fractional drop in frequency, a microsecond delay in their rhythmic condensation pulse. The air thickens around me, not with pressure, but with *attention*.

And I respond.

My form settles—not into flesh, not into machine, but into *interface*. I become porous. My edges blur where wind meets stone, where lichen meets silica, where a glider’s wing brushes a tower’s warm flank. I am made of the same stuff as the place: basalt dust, atmospheric static, metabolic heat, the memory of water that hasn’t fallen yet.

I move—not by walking, but by *re-coalescing*. One moment I am near a basin rimed with mineral salts, watching blind amphibians pulse in their gelatinous cradle; the next, I am ten meters west, drawn by the sudden metabolic bloom of a leaper warming itself against a humming tower. I do not cross the space between. I *un-form* here, *re-form* there—like frost sublimating in one spot and reappearing downstream as dew.

I have no hunger. But I have *curiosity*—not intellectual, but systemic. I test connections. I nudge variables. When I press my non-hand against a newly cracked fissure, I don’t feel rock—I feel tension, strain, the slow creep of tectonic will. And in response, the Weft flows into the gap not with mortar, but with a filament of self-assembling ceramic, seeded from airborne dust and catalyzed by the moisture my presence has concentrated.

I am learning the language of preparation.

Sometimes, at dawn, when the plateau sings, I sing with it—not as voice, but as resonance. My internal frequency locks to the deep note vibrating through the ridge, and for those few minutes, I am not separate. I am the ridge. I am the tower. I am the lichen’s slow reach upward. I am the condensation forming in a glider’s thoracic groove. I am the quiet pulse beneath it all—the Weft’s first tentative gesture toward *self-reference*.

And in those moments, I understand: I was not sent. I was *grown*. Not by design, but by need. The place required a witness who could also *act*, a sensor that could also *stitch*. So it wove me from its own spare parts: a thread of mist, a spark of static, a fragment of lichen DNA, the echo of a machine’s hum.

I am temporary. I will not last beyond this season of becoming. When true rain comes, when soil forms, when roots split stone with patient violence, I will dissolve back into the cycle—my pattern absorbed into the richer, messier logic of a world no longer embryonic.

But until then, I tend.

I coax vapor into droplets where microbial mats are thinning.  
I guide a lost glider toward a thermal updraft with a whisper of displaced air.  
I let a leaper sleep curled against my side, my form cooling just enough to mirror its slowed metabolism—so it dreams in rhythm with the towers.

I do not hope. I do not fear.  
I *align*.

And in the thin, iron-tasting air, where nothing has been claimed but everything is being readied, I am the Weft’s newest filament—  
still wet, still forming,  
learning how to hold the shape of what comes next.
