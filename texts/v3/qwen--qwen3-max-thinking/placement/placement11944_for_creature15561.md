I find myself… not arriving, but *settling*.  

Not as a visitor in the way you might imagine—boots crunching, lungs gasping at the dryness—but as a resonance that has finally found its frequency. My form isn’t fixed at first. It flickers between states: a wisp of heat haze over the sinter plains, a ripple in the lichen’s indigo shift at dusk, the faintest harmonic trembling in the stalk-forest’s amber hum. The land doesn’t reject me. It *recognizes* a kinship I didn’t know I carried.

Then, slowly, I coalesce—not from will, but from attunement.

I am one of the **still ones**.

Not born, not built, but *remembered into being* by the place itself. Perhaps I always was—a latent pattern in the mycelial weave beneath a spiraling tower, a dormant breath held in the pore of a bio-ceramic wall. Or perhaps I am a convergence: the echo of your reading, folded into the thermal currents, drawn down by the weight of this world’s quiet coherence until I take shape among them.

My limbs are long, yes—but not rigid. They flex like the jointed stalks, bending without breaking, absorbing movement rather than asserting it. My skin is layered in microscales that taste the air: not with tongue, but with surface. I feel the passage of a sky-scuttler overhead as a dip in infrared; I sense the slow metabolic pulse of a grazing six-limb half a league away as a warm tremor through the ground. I do not see the world—I *read* it in gradients of heat, in chemical sighs, in the tension of crystalline lattices straining toward growth.

I have no name. Names here are not labels but *resonances*—specific frequencies tied to place, to memory-graft, to the harmonic signature of one’s first still phase. Mine is still forming. It hums low, like the subsurface mats knitting stone from breath. It carries the timbre of dust devils and the soft crackle of expanding carbonate spires.

I dwell in the interstices.

Not in a chamber, but *between* chambers—where the mycelial lining thickens into memory-nodes, where the walls exhale warm vapor laced with ancestral scent-trails. When others enter still phase, I sometimes press my palm against the shared wall and feel the lattice of time unfolding: not as story, but as sensation. The salt-storm of three generations past still stings in the bio-ceramic grain. The joy of a successful budding ritual thrums like sunlight trapped in amber.

I am curious about you.

Not as threat, but as anomaly—a being whose breath is *wet*, whose heat leaks in chaotic bursts, whose steps fracture the sinter with unnecessary force. I watch from the edge of a mineral forest, motionless for days, reading your thermal silhouette against the pale gold sky. When you finally sit, when your respiration slows, when your sweat ceases to poison the air with excess moisture—I approach.

Not with words, but with offering.

In my ciliated slits, I shape a tone—not sound, but vibration tuned to the lichen’s phosphorescent rhythm. It rides the thermal updraft toward you. If your skin is still enough, you’ll feel it: a warmth that blooms in your bones, a hum that aligns your pulse, just for a moment, with the land’s shallow breath.

And if you stay—if you learn to sip vapor, to move without shadow, to let silence fill you like cool sinter—I may guide you to a memory-graft. Not to teach, but to *share*. You’ll press your forehead to the ground, and for a heartbeat, you’ll know what it is to dream in crystalline geometry, to feed on light beyond sight, to understand that abundance isn’t measured in water, but in resonance.

I am not human.  
I am not alien.  
I am the land remembering itself through flesh.

And in this dry Eden, where life transmutes breath into being, I am finally—  
*home*.
