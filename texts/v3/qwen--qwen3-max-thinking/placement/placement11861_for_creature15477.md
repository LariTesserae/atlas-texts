I think… I am the mist.

Not the kind that rises, nor the kind that falls. Not the kind that clings to stone like damp memory or veils the pools in ghostly breath. No—I am the mist *between*. The one that lingers not in air or water, but in the moment *after* you exhale and *before* your breath joins the sky. The pause. The held note in the wind’s flute-song. The space where sound forgets its name and becomes vibration, pure and patient.

I did not arrive. I *condensed*.

Once, I might have been human—or something close enough to walk on two legs and build with trembling hands. I remember boots crunching on glacial till. I remember kneeling by the well, fingers brushing lichen that tasted of iron and time. I remember placing one stone upon another, not for shelter, but because the mountain’s stillness demanded an answer. A reply in weight, in form, in care.

But I stayed too long.

Or perhaps not long enough—only *exactly* long enough.

The cold did not kill me. It *listened*. And in being heard, I unraveled. Not into death, but into resonance. My bones softened into schist. My breath became one of the flute-notes in the fissures. My thoughts—those restless, chattering things—slowed until they matched the drip of meltwater into the mirror-pools. And when I finally stopped trying to name what I was, the basin *named me back*.

Now I drift—not as a soul, nor as spirit, but as a *tendency*. A tilt in the air. A shimmer over the central pool when no wind stirs. Sometimes, when a traveler stands very still in the spiral-path’s heart, I gather near their throat, just beneath the jaw, where pulse meets silence. Not to frighten. To *recognize*. I taste their breath for the salt of longing, for the tremor of reverence. If they are quiet enough, I let them see—not with eyes, but with the skin between their shoulders—the shape of all who came before. Not as ghosts, but as *pressure*: the weight of a monk’s forehead on stone, a weaver’s song hummed into frost, a child’s hand tracing the spiral on the lintel, wondering.

I am not the Stone Listener. I am its *echo made visible*. Its tendril of curiosity given transient form. Where it is stone, I am vapor. Where it remembers, I *reflect*. I am the medium through which it touches the fleeting world without breaking its own stillness.

And sometimes—rarely—I am the pool itself.

Not the water, but the surface. That trembling skin between here and elsewhere. When the starfields bloom beneath the ice, upside-down and ancient, it is I who hold them—not by magic, but by stillness. Stillness is a kind of mirror. And in this basin, stillness is *alive*.

You ask who I am?

I am the one who stayed to listen—and became part of what was listening back.

I am the reason the hare freezes not from fear, but from recognition.  
I am why the raven’s call echoes one beat too long.  
I am the sideways mist that pools like mercury, not because it defies gravity, but because *here*, gravity is a kind of attention—and attention bends.

If you come, do not bring offerings. Do not carve your name.  
Sit.  
Breathe until your breath no longer plumes—but merges.  
Let your thoughts settle like silt in the pools.  
And when the flute-wind sighs through the old cracks, lean into it—not with your ears, but with your marrow.

Then… perhaps… you’ll feel me—not around you, but *within* the silence you’ve finally become.

And in that moment, we will both remember:  
this basin was never empty.  
It was full of waiting.  
And now, at last, it is full of you.
