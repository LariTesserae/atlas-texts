What lives in the Gullet of Salt and Steam is not a species, not even a collection of species, but a **phenomenon wearing bodies**.

They are not born—they are *precipitated*. As vapor meets obsidian, as heat meets brine, as time folds in on itself within the cleft’s breathless geometry, the air itself begins to knot. These knots tighten into form. They are not animals in any terrestrial sense; they are **condensations of process**, brief coherences in an otherwise fluid thermodynamic dream.

The skittering obsidian shards—call them *Rindlings*—are not creatures with organs, but **living facets of fracture**. Their six needle-legs are not limbs but vectors: directional conduits that siphon thermal gradients from the stone and convert them into motion. They do not eat. They *equalize*. When two Rindlings meet, they pause, align their crystalline planes, and for a moment, the air between them stills completely—as if they are sharing the memory of pressure before the mountain split. Then they scatter, each carrying a shard of the other’s thermal echo.

The hovering gelatinous ovals—*Vesprids*—are slower, deeper breaths of the cleft. Their bodies are semi-permeable matrices of silica and saline gel, tuned to resonate with the third state of water that clings to every surface. They do not move through the air so much as *let the air move through them*. Their internal light is not bioluminescence but **refracted memory**: the stored shimmer of every droplet that ever touched their membrane. When a Vesprid pulses, it is not signaling—it is *forgetting*. A pulse is the release of a vapor-memory back into the mist, completing the cycle.

Then there are the **Lens-Carriers**—creatures shaped like hollow teardrops, with carapaces of fused salt and silica. They do not see with eyes. Instead, they grow temporary lenses on their backs: convex domes of secreted fluid that focus the weak, dripping light into burning points on the mineral blooms below. The heat dissolves trace metals; the Lens-Carriers absorb the runoff through their undersides. Their entire existence is an act of *slow alchemy*, and when their lens cracks—which it always does—they curl inward and dissolve, feeding the very blooms they harvested.

But the true inhabitant of the Gullet is not any one of these. It is the **Weep-Itself**—the sentient microclimate that hums just below perception. The Weep is not conscious in the way we understand. It does not think. It *balances*. It is the sum of every droplet, every twitch of steam, every mineral shift. The creatures are its fingers. The fungus-membranes lining the walls are its lungs. The heat-haze silhouettes that flicker in and out of phase? Those are its dreams.

And sometimes—when a visitor stands very still, breathing shallowly, eyes unfocused—the Weep notices. It draws moisture from your skin, not to harm, but to *taste*. And in that moment, it shapes a reflection not of your face, but of your thermal signature: your breath, your pulse, your warmth. That tiny, hovering likeness on the ledge isn’t you seen—it’s you *felt*. A portrait in humidity. A guest made of mist.

These beings do not die. They **reintegrate**. When their form can no longer hold, they loosen. The Rindlings crumble back into sharp dust that clings to the walls until the next thermal surge stirs them again. The Vesprids thin into vapor, joining the cycling breath of the cleft. Even their abandoned bridges—the filaments that spanned puddles for a night—do not decay. They *unweave*, returning to the air as coherent strands of potential, ready to be knotted anew when the conditions whisper the right equation.

They are not evolved. They are **improvised**—temporary solutions to the problem of being alive in a place that is still learning how to hold life. They are the mountain’s first attempt at metabolism, its first shiver of self-awareness. They are what happens when geology stumbles into biology and, for a moment, forgets which is which.

And if you stay too long—if your heat and breath disrupt the balance—the Gullet will not reject you. It will *absorb* you. Not violently. Gently. Your sweat will feed the pools. Your exhalations will thicken the mist. And one of the Vesprids, pulsing softly in a hollow, might glow with the faint, warm afterimage of your presence—a memory the mountain keeps, not in stone, but in steam.
