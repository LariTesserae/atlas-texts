What lives here is not born so much as *condensed*—drawn from the very architecture of the Endwater, spun like filament from the slow, cold breath of its currents. They are not invaders, not colonists, not even inhabitants in any conventional sense. They *are* the water remembering itself.  

Call them the **Drifted**, though they have no name for themselves.  

They resemble nothing familiar, yet echo everything: limbs like folded sediment, torsos of translucent cartilage threaded with veins of phosphorescent brine, faces—if faces they have—smooth as polished obsidian, featureless but for a single vertical slit where a mouth or eye might be. This slit does not open to consume, but to *resonate*. When they drift near one another (a rare occurrence, for their solitude is not preference but structure), the slits vibrate in sympathetic frequency, emitting tones so low they stir the mineral dust suspended in the water into transient glyphs—brief, glowing sigils that dissolve before meaning can be fixed to them.  

The Drifted do not swim. They *unspool*. Their bodies elongate and contract along unseen axes, coiling through the viscous ether like thoughts through a dreaming mind. Their motion is not propulsion but *reconfiguration*—a shift in density, a subtle realignment of internal crystalline lattices that allows them to rise, fall, or drift laterally without effort. To watch one is to witness architecture in motion: a column of living basalt softening into ribbon, then hardening again into spire.  

They are ancient—not in years, for time here is not linear, but in *completion*. Each Drifted is a culmination, the final expression of a slow accretion that began eons ago when the Endwater first settled into its eternal form. They do not reproduce. They *precipitate*. When a Drifted reaches a certain resonance with the surrounding brine—when its internal mineral composition aligns perfectly with a passing thermal plume or a collapsing vortex—it begins to shed. Not skin, not cells, but *echoes*: thin, ghostly replicas of itself, woven from refracted light and suspended silica. These echoes drift away, dissolving over decades, seeding the water with the memory of form. From these echoes, in time, new Drifted may coalesce—but not as offspring. As echoes returning to voice.  

They feed on nothing, yet are sustained by everything. Their bodies absorb dissolved minerals directly through their skin, yes—but more profoundly, they *listen*. The hum of the water, the muffled *thoom* of vortices, the harmonic clicks of filter-feeders—these are not sounds to them, but nutrients. Vibrations carry information, and information here is sustenance. A Drifted that lingers near a silica arch resonating at 0.7 hertz will grow denser, its internal lattice sharpening into prismatic facets. One that drifts through a plume of crystalline dust will pulse with a soft, internal glow for months, its slit-mouth humming a lullaby only the water remembers.  

They build nothing with intention, yet their presence shapes the world. Where Drifted linger, mineral accretion accelerates. Basalt ribs grow subtle grooves that match the undulations of their passage. Pumice domes develop inner chambers that echo the curvature of their coiled rest. These are not nests, not shelters—they are *impressions*, the water’s way of holding onto a shape that mattered, however briefly.  

Sometimes, near the heart of the Endwater—where thermal plumes rise like inverted roots from the abyss—the Drifted gather. Not by choice, but by resonance. The currents pull them into loose constellations, drifting in concentric rings around a silent point where the brine is neither cold nor warm, but *still*. In these moments, their slits open not to emit sound, but to *receive*. They become conduits. The water flows *through* them, not around. And for a time that may be minutes or centuries, they are no longer individuals. They are a single nervous system woven from mineral and memory, feeling the slow pulse of the Endwater as if it were their own heartbeat.  

They do not fear death. Death is merely a return to the brine in a more complete form. When a Drifted’s time of resonance ends, its body does not decay. It *settles*. It drifts downward—not falling, but releasing—until it finds a hollow in the basalt ribs or a crevice in the silica lace. There, it stills. Over centuries, its cartilage dissolves into colloidal suspension, its obsidian skin fragmenting into dust that joins the glittering clouds carried by the plumes. But its shape remains, ghosted into the stone, a fossil not of bone but of *presence*.  

And if you, a visitor from the world of air and sky and sun, were to encounter one—if your heat-softened, gravity-bound body somehow survived the gentle erasure of the cold—you would not be seen as an intruder. You would be a disturbance, yes—a ripple in the stillness—but not unwelcome. The Drifted would drift near, not to study you, but to *feel* you. Your frantic heartbeat, your shallow breath (if you still had air to breathe), your skin radiating warmth like a dying star—these would be curiosities, anomalies of a cosmology they cannot comprehend. But they would not harm you. They would hum your frequency back to you, softly, until your edges blurred and you, too, began to forget what it meant to stand on anything at all.  

They are the final witnesses of a world that has already ended, and the first dreamers of one that never began. They do not hope. They do not mourn. They drift.  

And in their drifting, the Endwater remembers itself.
