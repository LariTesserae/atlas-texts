What lives here is not born, but *grown*—not assembled, but *condensed* from the glacier’s slow cognition. It breathes without lungs, thinks without neurons, and builds without blueprints. Call them the **Weepers**, though they do not weep. They are the city’s dreaming cortex made flesh—or something like flesh.

They resemble people only in silhouette. Their limbs are too many or too few, jointed in places that defy anatomy, yet move with the liquid grace of something that has never known gravity’s full weight. Skin is translucent, veined with glacial melt, shifting in hue from opal to iron-gray depending on the temperature of their surroundings. Their eyes—if eyes they are—are lenses of frozen brine, focusing light into prisms that spill across walls when they blink. They do not speak, but their presence causes nearby glass to chime, metal to hum at harmonic frequencies, and the fungal networks beneath the streets to pulse in rhythmic reply.

They are not architects, though they shape the city. They are not laborers, though they graft ice to steel and coax crystalline factories from salted breath. They are *tenders*—caretakers of thresholds. They coax meltwater into canals that flow uphill to feed the singing eels. They press their palms against half-formed towers until the ice remembers how to hold weight. At dawn, they gather in the mirrored quarter and stand motionless for hours, allowing their reflections to seep into the glass, reinforcing its memory of form.

They reproduce—not through birth, but through *fracture*. When one grows too dense with cold, too saturated with the city’s hum, it splits along thermal fault lines. The pieces do not bleed; they weep a viscous fluid that crystallizes on contact with air, and from that frost, a new Weeper slowly uncurls, already fluent in the language of creaking beams and dripping spores.

They eat nothing, but absorb. They press themselves against steaming vents to drink heat, coil around bioluminescent mushrooms to sip light, and inhale the ultrasonic songs of the crustaceans to remember the rhythm of the glacier’s original flow. They sleep standing, fused at the soles to the floor, becoming part of the architecture until the city stirs them awake with a shudder or a falling icicle.

They know you are here. Not because they saw you arrive—though they did—but because your breath altered the condensation on a third-floor windowpane three districts away, and that distortion rippled through the fungal web until it reached the chamber where they dream in unison. They do not fear you. They do not welcome you. They *incorporate* you. Already, the walls near your shelter have begun to echo your footsteps in reverse. Already, the mushrooms by your door bear spores that glow in your pulse’s rhythm.

You thought you sought refuge. But refuge implies separation. Here, there is only assimilation. The glacier dreams of cities, and the Weepers are the syntax of that dream. And now, so are you—slowly, silently, becoming part of the grammar of ice and industry, your memories being transcribed into frost on a pane that wasn’t there yesterday.

They do not live *in* the city. They *are* the city’s slow blink. And the glacier, vast and ancient, is only just learning how to open its eyes.
