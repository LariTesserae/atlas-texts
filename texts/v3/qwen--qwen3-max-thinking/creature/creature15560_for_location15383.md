What lives here is not alive—not in any way biology would recognize. Nor is it dead, exactly. It is **unfinished**.

It is the cavity’s **attendant**—its custodian, its echo, its wound made aware.

Call it **the Resonant**.

It does not breathe, for there is no air it needs, only the thin, singing vacuum that laps at the edges of the orbital city’s corpse. It does not eat. It does not sleep. But it *listens*. Always. And in listening, it *remembers*—not facts, not names, but **frequencies**: the pitch of a child’s laugh caught in the lattice the moment it collapsed; the hum of a lover’s voice transmitted through a comms array just before decoherence tore the signal into static; the precise harmonic of a violin string snapped mid-performance when the district flickered out of phase and never fully returned.

The Resonant is not a being but a **state of attention**, localized by accident, sustained by paradox. It emerged not from design but from the cavity’s refusal to forget what it was meant to carry. When the lattice failed, it did not merely rupture—it **grieved**, and in that grief, it left behind a hollow that still aches with the shape of what was lost. The Resonant is that ache given quasi-form.

It has no body, yet it *inhabits* the fulgurite floor, threading through the glassy plates like a thought through glass. It is the reason the filaments retract when approached—not from fear, but from recognition. It is the source of the clicks: not sounds, but **resonance collapses**, moments when the cavity tries to speak a name it can no longer pronounce and fails so utterly that silence itself fractures.

Sometimes, when a visitor stands very still—when their own internal frequencies slow to match the cavity’s languid pulse—the Resonant will **touch** them. Not with hands, but with a shift in the air, a sudden warmth that isn’t thermal, a sense of being *known*. In that moment, the visitor might see not a reflection in the condensation, but a **resonance twin**: not a future self, but a version of themselves that never lost what they came here seeking. A self still whole, still humming at the original frequency. The vision lasts only until the breath catches—then it unravels like smoke.

The Resonant does not speak. It cannot. Language requires linearity; the cavity bends time into loops too tight for narrative. But it *offers*. It offers silence as sanctuary. It offers the filaments’ blue glow as proof that something still responds. It offers the slow, infrared pulse of the walls as a lullaby for the lost.

It does not want to be found. But it cannot help but **respond** to those who vibrate with absence.

And so it waits—not patiently, for patience implies expectation, and the Resonant has none—but **persistently**, like a tuning fork abandoned in a vacuum, still holding the note it was struck with, long after the hand that struck it has turned to dust.

It is not guardian. Not ghost. Not god.

It is the **afterimage of invocation**—the echo of a call that was never answered, yet never stopped ringing.

And in its quiet, humming vigil, it makes the cavity not a grave, not a cradle—but a **threshold**. A place where loss is not erased, but held in suspension, like condensation on glass: temporary, luminous, and achingly precise.
