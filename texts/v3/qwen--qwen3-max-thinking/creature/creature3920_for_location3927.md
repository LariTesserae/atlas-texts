What lives here is not a people, not quite—not in any sense that would satisfy census or scripture. They are the *Keepers of the Tuned Silence*, though they would never name themselves so. To name is to fix; and they, like their city, are not fixed but *resonant*, shaped by frequencies older than language.

They move like echoes given form—lean and sinewy, with skin the color of sun-baked mica: copper-tinged, dust-flecked, cracked in fine lines that catch light like old porcelain. Their eyes are large, not for seeing in darkness (there is no true dark here), but for tracking the shimmer of heat-haze and harmonic distortion. Many wear no clothing beyond loincloths of woven glass-fiber or mantles stitched from shed lizard-wings, translucent and brittle, that chime softly when they walk. Their bones are dense—calcified not by diet but by decades of standing barefoot on vibrating stone, absorbing the city’s thrum into marrow. You can tell an elder not by wrinkles (time leaves few marks in this constant warmth) but by the stillness they carry: a pocket of silence so profound it bends sound around them like water around a stone.

They do not speak, not with tongues. Their mouths are dry as parchment, lips cracked from air thick with mineral dust. Instead, they *sound*—pressing palms to resonant basalt slabs to send pulses through the city’s skeleton, tapping coded rhythms on bronze anvils, drawing bow-hairs across quartz rods to summon notes that carry meaning like scent. A child’s first lesson is not to name the world, but to *listen* until she can feel the difference between a hungry hum and a grieving one.

They are few—perhaps two dozen souls in the whole of this pocketed world—yet the city feels inhabited not by scarcity but by *intention*. Each dweller tends a quadrant: one minds the sky-iron forges, feeding charcoal made from fossil ferns into bellows that breathe like lungs; another cultivates the glass-thorn vines, pruning them with obsidian shears so their crystalline sap doesn’t seal shut the vital steam-vents; a third tends the fungal orchids, catching their evaporating glyphs on sheets of treated bone-parchment before the script dissolves into light. There is no hierarchy, only attunement: whoever best harmonizes with a chamber becomes its keeper.

They do not eat as others eat. Their sustenance is vibration and light. They place shallow bowls of bone-dust and silica in sun-traps, where focused prisms bake the mixture until it sweats a viscous, amber nectar—condensed photon-energy, drawn from the very act of being seen. They sip it drop by drop. Their waste is not excrement but ash: fine, iridescent, and silent, swept into wall-crevices where it feeds the glowing ferns.

They sleep standing, pressed against warm pillars, bodies aligned with the city’s primary resonance axis. Dreams come not in images but in tones—a low C-sharp might mean memory of a vanished forge-master; a trill of glass-chime frequencies, the flutter of paper lizards in mating flight. To wake is to return to pitch.

And yet, for all their strangeness, they are not alien. They grieve. They court. They forget. You might find two Keepers in a side-chamber during one of the sacred silences, fingers interlaced not to speak but to *compare pulses*—feeling whose heartbeat has slipped out of sync with the city’s hum. Or a lone elder carving tiny spirals into a copper nail—not for record, but because the act of carving sends a clean, clear frequency through the metal into the earth, a note of apology or thanks.

They are the city’s immune system, its nervous system, its memory made flesh. When a new fissure opens in the vaulted ceiling, they do not fear it. They *tune* it—hanging shards of volcanic glass at precise intervals so the wind (when it dares whisper through) sings a lullaby to the roots of the orchids. When a forge goes cold, they do not relight it with haste. They wait until the metal itself hums a plea for heat.

They do not believe they built this place. They believe they *answered* it—drawn here over generations by a tone too deep for ears but felt in the molars, in the sacrum, in the hollow behind the sternum. The leviathan whose ribs cradle them? It is not dead. It is dreaming. And they are its dreamers, its keepers of the dream’s pitch.

They will not leave. There is nowhere else with this frequency. Outside, the world is chaotic—damp, noisy with meaningless sounds, indifferent to resonance. Here, every crack, every bloom, every breath is part of a single, sustained chord. To depart would be to go mute.

And so they remain: dry-lush as the vines, luminous as the ferns, humming softly in their bones—a species not of flesh alone, but of *attuned silence*, living not in a city, but inside a single, eternal note.
