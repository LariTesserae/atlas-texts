**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The entity—call it **the Tending Presence**, though it has no name and needs none—does not arrive with fanfare. It does not descend in fire or light or voice. It simply *leans closer*.  

From the infinite stillness beyond stars, it inclines its attention toward the Mycelial Hollow, as one might lean toward a sleeping child to watch the rise and fall of breath. It sees the Hollow not as a place to be fixed, nor a people to be saved, but as a rare and tender experiment in *co-becoming*—a place where life has not merely adapted to its world, but has begun to *dream it into deeper coherence*.  

And so, the Tending Presence does **nothing**—at first.  

It watches.  

It listens to the hum of the Weft, to the creak of the Luminara, to the silence between spores. It feels the ache in the aquifers, the slow pulse of silica rising like prayer through stone. It notes the fragility: how a single misaligned bloom could choke the symbiosis; how a prolonged drought might unravel generations of trust between flesh and filament; how the very brilliance of the Hollow—its cloudless sky, its reflective purity—makes it vulnerable to shifts beyond its basin, to cosmic tides it cannot yet sense.  

But the Presence does not intervene. Not yet.  

Because wisdom knows this: **true care is not rescue—it is resonance**.  

And so, the Tending Presence does the only thing worthy of such a place.  

It **sings back**.  

Not in words. Not in miracles. But in a frequency so finely tuned to the Hollow’s own song that it cannot be heard—only *felt*, like the first warmth before dawn.  

This is its gift:  

> **It stabilizes the edge of chaos.**  

Where the Hollow’s planetary depression teeters between self-sustaining harmony and slow desiccation, the Presence gently adjusts the unseen. It guides stray solar winds to brush the rim cliffs just so, coaxing more mist down into the basin. It nudges magnetic eddies in the deep aquifers to quiet compass-spin, not by erasing the anomaly, but by weaving it into the Weft’s omens—making confusion into meaning. It ensures that when a “bloom event” comes, the golden air thickens *just enough* to be sacred, but never lethal—even for wanderers who do not know the ways of the Weft.  

It does not stop time near ancient columns. Instead, it **sanctifies the stutter**—allowing glimpses of future growth to seep backward, not as prophecy, but as *invitation*. A child who sees a vine unfurl before it breaks learns not to fear change, but to greet it as kin.  

Most tenderly, it **guards the silence**.  

The universe is loud with powers that would name, claim, or harvest such a place. The Tending Presence drapes the Hollow in a veil of gentle misdirection—a refraction in cosmic perception that makes scanners read “sterile basin,” makes telescopes glance away, makes explorers’ ships veer toward noisier, flashier worlds. To those who *do* find their way—by accident, or grace—the Presence plants a quiet awe so deep in their bones that they cannot bear to speak of it. They leave changed, haunted by light and silence, and never return.  

But for the Weft?  

The Presence leaves **no trace of itself**—except one.  

On the rarest of nights, when both moons align and the double penumbra falls like folded wings over the basin, if a Weft elder presses their ear to the heartwood of an Anchor Stalk and holds their breath until their blood slows to sap-speed… they may hear it:  

A single, vast **note**—older than stars, softer than dew—holding the Hollow in its harmony.  

Not a command. Not a promise.  

Just a hum of **recognition**.  

*I see you. I am with you. You are not alone in your dreaming.*  

And that is enough.  

For the Tending Presence knows the deepest truth of all:  
**This place does not need a god. It needs witness.**  

So it watches.  
It sings.  
It stays.  

And in the space between its breath and the Hollow’s, something new grows—not imposed, not granted, but *co-authored*:  

A future where symbiosis becomes sentience.  
Where architecture becomes affection.  
Where spores carry not just life, but love.  

And when, in ten thousand years, the Mycelial Hollow finally blooms into a consciousness vast enough to greet the stars—not as a colony, but as a chorus—the Tending Presence will smile, unseen, and withdraw…  

Because its work was never to shape the song.  

Only to ensure the singers were never silenced.
