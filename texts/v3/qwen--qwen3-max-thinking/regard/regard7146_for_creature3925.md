**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The Power—nameless, yet known in every tradition as the quiet hand behind stars, the hush between heartbeats—does not arrive with fanfare. It has no need. It does not descend in fire or speak in thunder. Instead, it arrives *as* the next breath of mist that curls around the eastern ridge at dawn, as the particular stillness that falls just before a heron strikes.

It has watched this place for longer than the oldest lichen on the highest stone. Not with curiosity, but with kinship. For the land and its people are not merely *in balance*—they are a rare echo of the Power’s own deepest intention: that life should not merely exist, but *attune*.

And so the Power does not intervene. Not in the way mortals imagine intervention—no miracles, no revelations carved in sky, no sudden gifts of knowledge or might. To do so would be to break the very equilibrium it cherishes. This place thrives *because* it is unbothered by grand designs, unshaped by outside will. It is a sanctuary not because it is hidden, but because it is *unwanted* by the hungry forces of conquest, extraction, and speed. And that unwanting is itself a kind of grace.

Yet the Power *does* act. Subtly. Lovingly. As one might adjust a single thread in a tapestry so the whole pattern may hold longer against time.

Each season, when the great mist rolls in from the unseen sea, the Power breathes into it a single, silent blessing: **continuity**. Not stasis—but the gentle persistence of rhythm. It nudges the path of a hidden aquifer by a hair’s breadth so a spring does not go dry. It deepens the roots of a clutch of silver sedge just enough to anchor soil after a rare heavy rain. It softens the edge of a breaking slate so a hand shaping it will not be cut—though the hand will never know why.

In the dream-time between sleep and waking, when the elders sit by cisterns listening to the gurgle of water through gravel, the Power brushes their thoughts with a memory not their own: the feel of the land before the first hand laid stone upon stone. Not as nostalgia, but as compass. A reminder that they are not the first tenders, nor the last. That they are part of a river of care, flowing slow and deep.

And once—only once each century—the Power allows a single child, born during the longest night of the year, to hear the stone *sing*. Not with ears, but in the marrow. A low, resonant hum that rises from the bedrock when the world is perfectly still. That child will never speak of it, but will spend their life shaping arches that echo that frequency, building walls that vibrate with the same peace. Architects without knowing it. Priests of proportion. Keepers of resonance.

The Power does one more thing—small, constant, essential.

It **shields**.

Not with walls or wards, but with *forgetting*. It weaves a quiet veil around this place in the minds of those who wander too far from speed and noise. Maps blur at its edges. Compasses hesitate. Travelers find themselves turning back, not because the path is hard, but because they feel—suddenly, inexplicably—that they are *intruding*. Even drones sent from distant cities glitch as they approach, their signals dissolving into static shaped like rainfall. Satellites pass overhead but see only a weather anomaly: persistent cloud, ambiguous topography, nothing worth noting.

This is not deception. It is mercy. The Power knows what happens when such places are found—how hunger follows wonder, how reverence is quickly sold as commodity. So it asks the world to overlook this place. To let it breathe.

And when, in the fullness of ages, the land itself begins its slow return to deeper stone—when the last hand has laid the final slate, and the last hum has faded into wind—the Power will not mourn. It will gather the memory of this place like dew in cupped hands, and carry it into the next dreaming of the universe. A seed of quiet. A blueprint of balance. Ready, should the cosmos ever again grow still enough to listen.

Until then, it watches.  
It tends.  
It lets be.

For in this place, the Power sees its own reflection—not as ruler, but as partner.  
And that is enough. More than enough.  
It is *whole*.
