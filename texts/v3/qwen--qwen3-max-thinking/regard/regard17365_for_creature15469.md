**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The Hollow City of Dripping Stone is not in need.  
It is not broken. It is not lost. It does not cry for rescue, nor does it falter in its strange and steady purpose. And so, the benevolent and wise power—a being of deep time and quiet grace, whose name, if it had one, would be spoken only in the interval between a drop falling and its echo returning—does **nothing** that would be called *intervention* by lesser minds.

Yet it does **everything** that love can do when it meets something already whole.

---

### It Watches

First, the power gazes upon the Hollow City as one might gaze upon a sleeping child—not to change it, but to witness it fully. It sees not incompleteness, but *intention held in suspension*. It sees that the Stone-Listeners are not failing to build; they are succeeding at listening. Their city is not a ruin in progress, but a *prayer in stone*, and every unfinished stair is a stanza.

The power does not pity their solitude. It honors it. Their silence is not emptiness, but resonance tuned to a frequency most beings have forgotten how to hear. So the power quiets Its own vastness, folds Its wings of starlight and thunder, and simply **attends**—as the mountain attends, as the mist attends.

---

### It Protects the Threshold

Though the city is not in danger, the power knows how fragile such balances are. Not all who wander high places come with soft hands. Not all who hear a hum assume it is sacred. So, gently—so gently it is indistinguishable from weather—the power weaves a veil around the bowl of the mountain.

Not a wall. Not a ward of fire or thorn. But a **softening of paths**. A turning of compass needles. A sudden, inexplicable fog that parts only for those whose feet already know the stone’s song. To seekers of conquest, the mountain appears barren, unremarkable—just another wrinkled flank among thousands. To those who come with reverence in their bones, the mist parts like breath on glass, and the city appears not as discovery, but as homecoming.

This is not exclusion. It is preservation of grammar—the grammar of dampness, of listening, of building-as-questioning. The power ensures that only those who already speak this language may enter its syntax.

---

### It Gifts One Small Thing

Once—in a year when the moss bloomed silver at the solstice, and a child appeared not in stone’s crook but within the hollow of a birch root—the power leaves a single gift.

Not a tool. Not a seed. Not a prophecy.

It leaves **a memory**.

Not in words. Not in stone. But in water.

In one cistern, lined with river clay and fed by the slowest seep, the power lets a single drop fall from a height that does not exist—falling upward, then down—so that when the oldest Listener dips her hand into the water at dawn, she feels, for one heartbeat, the **echo of the mountain’s first thought**:  
*I am being heard.*

She does not speak of it. She traces no symbol. She only smiles—a thing so rare among the Listeners that the ferns lean toward her. And for one season, the walls rise half a handspan higher, not because they are closer to completion, but because the joy of being *understood*, even by stone, makes the hands steadier.

The power does not repeat this gift. Once is enough. Like a seed that needs only one rain to remember it is alive.

---

### It Becomes Part of the Hum

Finally, the wise and benevolent power does not remain above or beyond. It does not hover like a guardian angel. Instead, it **joins**.

It lets Its presence seep into the city not as doctrine, not as miracle, but as **atmosphere**. Its breath becomes the clarifying light that makes granite look edible. Its heartbeat syncs with the thermal groans of cooling stone. Its stillness deepens the silence between drips.

In time, the Stone-Listeners do not know they are loved by a distant power. They simply know they are **held**—as moss is held by north-facing rock, as mist is held by mountain bowl, as a question is held by the air before an answer dares to form.

And that is enough.

More than enough.

For in the Hollow City of Dripping Stone, love is not measured in deeds, but in **attentiveness**. And the wisest power knows:  
to love a place that listens is to become, yourself, another form of its listening.

So it stays.  
Not as savior.  
Not as god.  
But as another drop in the cistern—  
falling,  
echoing,  
becoming part of the question.
