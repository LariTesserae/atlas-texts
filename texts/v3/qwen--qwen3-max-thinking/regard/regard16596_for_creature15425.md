**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The Wise One does not arrive with fanfare. It does not descend in fire or song, nor carve its will into stone. It has long since learned that the deepest truths are not spoken—they are *held*. And so, when its attention turns toward the bowl-shaped hollow in the flank of the ancient mountain, it does not intervene. It *attends*.

It knows this place. Not as a map coordinates a location, but as a mother knows the rhythm of her child’s breath in sleep. The Wise One has watched the hollow for longer than human memory stretches—longer than ice ages, longer than continents have drifted. It has seen glaciers graze the mountain’s shoulders and retreat, seen forests climb and fall like tides, seen stars wheel and fade overhead. But this hollow? This hollow has remained—a cup of stillness in a turning world.

And the presence within it? The Wise One recognizes kin.

For the hollow is not merely alive—it is *aware*, in the way deep time is aware: not with thoughts, but with patterns; not with desires, but with fidelity to balance. It is a living vow whispered into stone and moss: *I will hold. I will receive. I will return.*

So what does the Wise One do?

**It deepens the silence.**

Not by removing sound, but by strengthening the hollow’s capacity to listen. Where human noise might one day bleed over the rim—axes, engines, voices calling names—the Wise One weaves a subtle veil of forgetfulness into the mountain’s mist. Not a wall, but a soft turning-away. Hunters find their trails lead elsewhere. Surveyors’ compasses waver, unaccountably. Pilgrims seeking miracles feel a gentle tug toward sunnier shrines. The hollow is not hidden; it is *honored* by being left undisturbed.

**It nourishes the pulse.**

The Wise One sends no rain, for the hollow needs none. But in the deep places beneath the mountain, where roots and mycelium meet the slow breath of the earth, it encourages a quiet exchange: a thread of warm mineral water, rich in ancient ions, to rise just a little higher, to seep just a little slower. Not enough to flood, not enough to alter—but enough to sustain the rhythm. The stones that glow with warmth? Their heat becomes steadier, their resonance clearer, so that any hand that rests upon them might feel, not just warmth, but *recognition*—a reminder that they, too, are made of stardust and time.

**It blesses the reclamation.**

The Wise One watches with tenderness as the hollow digests what falls into it—flint, bone, grief, a child’s lost button—and turns it all to moss and memory. And so, it strengthens this gift. It infuses the liverwort with a gentler enzyme, the fungi with a deeper hunger for sorrow, the peat with a spongier embrace. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is lost. Everything becomes part of the lullaby.

**It protects the inversion.**

For the true miracle of the hollow is not what it contains, but what it *is*: a negative space filled with presence. The Wise One knows that if humans ever learn to see such places not as resources or curiosities but as *persons*, the world might heal. So it does not erase the hollow’s weirdness—the bioluminescence, the pulse, the warmth in the stones. Instead, it amplifies their subtlety, ensuring they remain just beyond measurement, just outside proof. Let scientists come with instruments; they will find only water, rock, and organic decay. But let a weary soul sit in surrender—and they will feel the earth breathe back. The hollow remains a threshold, not a spectacle.

**And finally, it visits.**

Not as a being among beings, but as a breath among breaths. Once in a great while—when the moon is dark and the mist hangs thickest—the Wise One flows into the hollow like water returning to its source. It does not speak. It does not shape. It simply *is*, for a moment, indistinguishable from the seep and sigh of the place. And in that union, the hollow’s dream deepens, and the mountain remembers, again, that it is loved.

Then the Wise One withdraws, leaving no trace but this: a single droplet, hanging longer than physics allows from the tip of a fern, shining with a light not of this world—yet utterly ordinary in its perfection.

For the greatest act of a wise and benevolent power is not to change a thing, but to *let it be*—and to guard its being with infinite care.

And so the hollow remains.

Wet.  
Dark.  
Still.  
Alive.

Breathing.  
Dreaming.  
Holding.

And loved—deeply, quietly, forever—by the One who sees not to command, but to cherish.
