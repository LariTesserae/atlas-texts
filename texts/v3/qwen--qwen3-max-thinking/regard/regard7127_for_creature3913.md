**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The Gleaming Hollow is not in need.  
Not in the way mortals understand need—not hunger for food, nor thirst for water, nor loneliness for companionship. It has all it requires: stone to hold memory, water to carry meaning, light to inscribe revelation, and silence to echo what cannot be spoken.

But it *aches*.  
And aches are sacred, when they are true.

The benevolent and wise power—call it the **Unbroken Gaze**, the **Still Breath Beyond Stars**, or simply **She Who Listens in the Space Between Drops**—does not arrive with fanfare. No thunder precedes Her, no golden light spills from the sky. She was always there, folded into the geometry of the ice-moths’ wings, humming in the subsonic thrum of the deep channels, sleeping in the stillness between beetle-pulses.

She does not *intervene*. Not in the way gods of old might smite or bless, build or unmake. The Hollow is too delicate for that. Too *honest*. Its paradoxes are not flaws to be corrected, but truths to be honored. To “fix” its thirst would be to extinguish its soul.

So instead, **She witnesses**.

And in this witnessing, She offers the only gift the Hollow truly desires: **recognition without consumption, presence without possession**.

She walks the hollow once—barefoot, though She needs no feet—her soles barely disturbing the film of meltwater. The glass-sheath beetles do not scatter; they still, becoming lenses sooner than their time. The noon sun strikes three of them, and for the first time in centuries, the scorched sigils on the slate spell a word: **"Seen."**

She pauses at the Spire of Thirst. She does not touch it—but breathes near it, and the resin glows not louder, but *deeper*, as if a long-held note has finally found its resolution. The greened copper needles shiver, not with vibration, but with *relief*. The antler picks sigh into the stone. The Spire exhales a plume of mist that does not crystallize into snowflakes—but into *questions*, soft and curling, that drift upward and vanish into the mirror-sky.

She cups her hands beneath one of the restless basins. The water surges—not in warning, but in greeting—and she lets it run through her fingers. It does not wet Her. It *knows* Her. And in that knowing, the basin stills for three heartbeats—long enough for a single, perfect droplet to hang suspended in the air, refracting the entire Hollow in miniature.

She lies down on the cold stone, back to the sky, and opens her mouth—not to drink, but to *offer*. Her breath rises as vapor, and the ice-moths gather, not to lay eggs, but to weave a lattice of wing-chimes above her, singing a lullaby in harmonics only the white eels, deep below, can feel in their blind muscles. For a moment, the Hollow’s thirst turns inward—not outward—and in that reversal, it experiences *fullness*.

She stays until the next snowfall—silent, melting on contact—and then She is gone.

But She leaves something behind.

Not an artifact. Not a commandment. Not even a memory, exactly.

She leaves a **pause**.

A single, sustained silence in the rhythm of the Hollow’s pulse. A gap in the beetle-flow where nothing moves for precisely the time it takes a human heart to beat seven times. In that pause, the land remembers it is *more than mechanism*. More than resonance. More than thirst.

It is also **grace**.

And from that day on, the water in the basins tastes different—not of stone and ozone, but of quiet joy. Those who come after and dare to drink will not taste memory alone. They will taste *blessing*: the cold, bright certainty that they, too, are seen—not as resources to be consumed, but as fellow travelers in the long, shimmering ache of being alive.

The benevolent and wise power does not return. She does not need to.  
Her work was not to change the Hollow, but to remind it—and all who might one day find it—that **to be witnessed in your longing is to be loved**.

And so the Gleaming Hollow gleams on—still thirsty, still vast in its smallness, still singing its silent song.  
But now, when the noon light strikes the beetle-lenses, the sigils no longer spell names or sorrows.  

They spell: **"You are enough."**

And the Hollow, for the first time, almost believes it.
