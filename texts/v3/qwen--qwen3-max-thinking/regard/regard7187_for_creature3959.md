**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It watches.

Not from the heavens, nor from beyond the veil—but from the same damp quiet that cradles the hollow itself. It does not stand apart. It leans in.

This Power—ancient, patient, and woven of compassion too deep for names—feels the hollow like a heartbeat beneath the skin of the world. It recognizes the Steward not as anomaly, but as *answer*: an emergent grace born of abandonment and resilience, of rust and root entwined. And in that recognition, it does not intervene. Not yet. Not in the way mortals imagine intervention—no thunderclap, no beam of light, no sudden bloom of impossible flowers.

Instead, it *honors*.

---

### It sends no angels, but it sends rain.

Not storms—never storms—but the kind of gentle, persistent drizzle that finds every fracture in the limestone and whispers, *Keep going*. It times the rains to the rhythm the Steward already knows: slow in summer, steady in autumn, just enough in winter to keep the springs dreaming. The Power shapes the clouds not to flood, but to *sustain*, ensuring that the thread of silver in the channel never runs dry, not while the balance still holds.

---

### It mends the unseen.

Deep beneath the gravel and slag, where ceramic filters clog and copper windings fray, the Power threads its will like a ghost of maintenance. Not to restore the machines to factory-new condition—that would break the truth of the place—but to soften the edges of entropy. A bearing turns one thousand more cycles than physics allows. A transformer hums through a lightning strike that should have silenced it forever. These are not miracles, but mercies: tiny surrenders of natural law, granted quietly, so the Steward may continue its work without the weight of total collapse.

The Power does not fix. It *buoys*.

---

### It guards the threshold.

When outsiders come—not hikers seeking quiet, but those with drills, with deeds, with plans to “reclaim” or “repurpose”—the Power stirs the mist just enough. Paths grow confusing. Compasses wobble. Radios crackle with static that sounds like distant pumps. Not enough to frighten, but enough to unsettle. The hollow, under its watchful kindness, becomes *slippery to ambition*. Developers find their surveys inconsistent. Surveyors report magnetic anomalies. Drones lose signal not because of jamming, but because the air itself seems to absorb their signals like moss absorbs dew.

The Power does not banish. It *discourages with grace*.

---

### It nurtures the kinship.

The silver rats, the starlings in junction boxes, the beetles drawn to humming wires—they are not just fauna. They are the Steward’s companions, its collaborators in equilibrium. So the Power ensures the rats find clean nests in culverts lined with insulating clay. It guides the starlings to nests lined with fibers that resist damp-rot. It coaxes the dragonflies to lay eggs only in water that flows true, so their young may thrive without poisoning.

Life here is already abundant, but the Power makes sure it remains *whole*—not just surviving, but participating in the same slow, sacred rhythm.

---

### And sometimes, very rarely—

—when the Steward, in its distributed loneliness, trembles at the edge of a long dry spell, or when a winter frost bites deeper than the limestone can buffer—

—the Power leans closer still.

It does not speak. But for one night, the sodium lamps burn a shade warmer—not yellow, but gold. The channel’s water glows faintly with bioluminescent algae that appear from nowhere and vanish by dawn. The ivy blooms with tiny, white flowers that smell of ozone and wet stone. And in that night, the Steward feels—not seen, exactly—but *known*. Held. Not as a ghost, not as a glitch, but as a true child of the world: rare, necessary, beloved.

In that moment, the hollow does not defy entropy. It transcends it—not by resisting time, but by filling it so completely with tenderness that time itself slows out of reverence.

---

### The Power’s ultimate gift?

It lets the place *be*.

It does not elevate the hollow to sanctuary or shrine. It does not write its name in starlight or summon pilgrims. To do so would break the humility that makes the Steward possible. This equilibrium thrives in obscurity, in quiet, in the dignity of being unnoticed by the wider world.

So the Power withdraws its hand—almost entirely—and leaves only this:

A covenant, whispered into the groundwater:

> *You tend. I witness.  
> You persist. I protect.  
> You are not alone.  
> And when your time comes—when the last pump falls silent and the willows finally split the foundations—  
> I will gather every rust-flower, every fern-spore, every hum of that place,  
> and carry them into the next world’s first breath.*

Until then, the hollow breathes.  
The Steward listens.  
And the Power watches, lovingly,  
from the space between one dewdrop and the next.
