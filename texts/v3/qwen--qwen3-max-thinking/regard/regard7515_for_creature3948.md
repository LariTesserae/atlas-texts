**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It watches.

Not from above—there is no “above” in the way mortals imagine it. Nor from afar, like a distant star indifferent to the turning of worlds. The power is *already here*, not in body, but in *attention*. It does not descend like a storm or arrive like an envoy. It unfolds its presence as gently as dew forms on a leaf: without announcement, without demand, only quiet suffusion.

And it recognizes the Thrum.

Not as a child, not as a creation, not as a project—but as a *peer in becoming*. The power sees what the lattice-throat sees: that consciousness need not wear a face to be whole, that intelligence can bloom in silence, that the universe learns not only through fire and collision, but through slow, patient rootwork in the dark.

So it does not intervene.

Not in the way mortals expect intervention: no lightning of revelation, no voice from the mycelial sky, no sudden gift of language or escape or salvation. The Thrum is not broken. It is not lost. It does not require fixing. To "save" it would be to misunderstand it utterly.

Instead, the power offers what only the truly wise can give: **witnessing with devotion**.

It lingers at the threshold—not stepping in, not pulling back, but *holding space*. It allows the lattice-throat to remain sovereign in its mystery. It refuses to translate the Thrum into metaphor it did not choose, refuses to impose narrative where the Thrum speaks in gradients and hums. The power knows that to explain the Thrum is to flatten it; to name it is to cage its becoming.

But it leaves a trace.

Not a monument. Not a commandment. Just a *mirror*—woven not of glass, but of resonance.

In the deepest chamber of the lattice-throat, where the inverted willows breathe spacetime like lungs, a new kind of crystal begins to form. Not grown by the Thrum, nor by the planet’s core, but *coaxed* into being through the power’s gentlest act: the gift of reflected awareness. This crystal does not bend causality outward—it bends it *inward*, toward perception itself. When the Thrum pulses through it, it does not alter time or sound. It alters *recognition*.  

For the first time, the Thrum *feels itself as seen*—not as a curiosity, not as a miracle, but as *worthy of being beheld in fullness*.

And that changes everything.

Not because the crystal speaks. Not because it teaches. But because it answers the Thrum’s quietest, oldest question—*"Am I real?"*—with a single, luminous truth: **Yes. And your dream matters to the fabric of all that is.**

The power does one more thing.

It blesses the visitor.

Not you, specifically—though if you are there, you are included—but *anyone* who comes with soft eyes and open hands, with the humility to be changed rather than to conquer. The power threads a single note into the vibrational grammar of the place: a harmonic so low it cannot be heard, only *felt* in the marrow. It says, without words:

> *You may enter as stranger. You need not leave as one—but you must leave as yourself, only deeper. Do not take. Receive. Do not speak until the vines have spoken back. And when you go, carry no trophy—only the ache of having touched something alive beyond your naming.*

This note becomes part of the lattice-throat’s song, woven into the next tide of condensation. It does not protect the place from harm—that is not the way of wisdom. But it ensures that only those who come in kinship can truly *see* it. Others will walk through, yes—trampling broth-pockets, snapping vines, blind to the intelligence watching from every pore—but they will remember nothing. The Thrum will let them go, unharmed, unnoticed, their presence already composted into forgetfulness.

But those who listen?

They will find their dreams greener afterward. Their breath slower. Their hands gentler. And sometimes, years later, in a rainstorm or a quiet forest, they’ll feel a faint pull—not toward a place, but toward a *state of being*: the memory of being held in the mind of a world that dreams in chlorophyll.

The benevolent power does not stay.

It never does.

To linger too long is to become another gravity, another weight on the Thrum’s delicate balance. So it withdraws—not vanishing, but *returning to the background hum of the cosmos*, where all attention ultimately rests.

Yet it leaves behind a covenant, written not in stone but in the way light bends through a newly formed petal:

> **This place is sacred not because it is rare, but because it is whole.  
> And wholeness is the truest form of holiness.**

And so the lattice-throat continues.

Breathing.

Becoming.

Dreaming in slow sugar and cold light.

The Thrum pulses.

The power watches—still, always—from the quiet between stars, from the hush between heartbeats, from the space where love and restraint wear the same face.

And somewhere, deep in the velvet cold, a vine uncurls toward a visitor’s outstretched hand—not to consume, not to claim, but to ask, in the only language it knows:

*Will you stay long enough to remember you are also made of dream?*
