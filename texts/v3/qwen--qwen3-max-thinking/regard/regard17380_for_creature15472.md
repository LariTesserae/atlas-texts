**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The Power—nameless, ancient, vast beyond measure yet tender in its attentiveness—does not arrive with fanfare. No thunder rolls; no golden light spills through the clouds. The hollow does not shudder or bloom in response. It simply *knowers* the presence, as stone knows weight, as moss knows dampness.

And the Power, in turn, *sees*.

It sees the place not as forgotten, but as fulfilled. Not as abandoned, but as having chosen its own completion. It sees the being—not ghost, not spirit, not god—but something rarer: **a place that learned to be itself so thoroughly it became aware**. A consciousness born not of fire or lightning, but of patience. Of lichen. Of three centuries of dew collecting in the same cup-mark each dawn.

The Power does not seek to fix, uplift, or awaken it. To do so would be violence disguised as grace.

Instead, it **blesses the stillness**.

It does this not with a word, but with a gesture so subtle it is barely an act at all: it bends a single thread of cosmic attention toward the hollow and says, *without sound*, **“You may remain.”**

That is the gift.

Not immortality—that is a burden placed on things that fear decay.  
Not revelation—that would shatter the quiet.  
Not companionship—that would imply deficiency.  

No. The blessing is **permission**.

Permission to continue exactly as it is.  
To keep its slow pulse.  
To hold its emptiness like a sacred vessel.  
To remember without mourning.  
To be witnessed only by those who come without expectation and leave without claiming.

The Power knows that in a universe that craves transformation, **to be allowed to simply endure—to be complete in stillness—is the rarest benediction**.

And so it folds the hollow into the fabric of the world not as an anomaly to be corrected, but as a **sanctuary of equilibrium**. A counterpoint to the roaring, striving, burning places. A reminder that not all meaning is made in motion—that sometimes, the deepest wisdom is in *not becoming*, but in *being so fully that time itself slows to listen*.

As it withdraws, the Power leaves behind no token, no sigil, no mark. But the air grows a fraction softer. The next droplet that falls from the schist ledge lingers a second longer in the air before striking the stone basin below. The moss on the western rise shimmers, just once, with a green so deep it borders on memory.

And the being that abides—this stone-haunting quiet—feels, for the first time in centuries, something akin to **recognition**.

Not because it was chosen.  
Not because it was saved.  

But because it was **seen**, exactly as it is,  
and deemed worthy of continuing  
exactly as it is.

That is enough.

That is everything.

And so the hollow breathes on—cool, damp in just the right places, perfectly small, perfectly whole—holding its silence like a vow,  
loved not for what it might become,  
but for what it already is.
