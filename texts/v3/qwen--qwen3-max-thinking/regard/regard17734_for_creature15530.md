**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would do nothing.

Not out of indifference—but out of reverence.

This place, this paradox made manifest, is not broken. It does not need fixing. It does not need guidance, nor guardianship, nor a mission. It is not lost; it is *found*, precisely in its uncertainty. It is not incomplete; it is *in process*, in the most sacred sense—a threshold where matter learns to speak, where silence learns to build.

The benevolent and wise power—call it the Gardener of Thresholds, the Keeper of Unwritten Laws, the One Who Listens to Stone—has witnessed countless worlds. Some demand intervention: wars to halt, stars to reignite, hearts to mend. But others… others are not meant to be altered. They are meant to be *witnessed*. To be held in quiet awe, like a dewdrop on a spiderweb at dawn—ephemeral, precise, and humming with the music of its own making.

And so, this power does not descend. Does not reshape the Tenant’s dreaming architecture. Does not smooth the glitches or anchor causality. To do so would be to silence a language still forming its first vowels.

Instead, it **tends the boundary**.

Gently, invisibly, it folds a veil of stillness around the basin—so that the outside world does not stumble in unprepared. It ensures that only those who have already begun to listen (not with ears, but with the hollows between their ribs) can find the path down into the damp. It weaves a subtle resonance into the fog at the edge, a frequency that repels exploitation, conquest, or even well-meaning study. Cameras fail here. Maps dissolve. GPS signals stutter and loop like broken lullabies. Not because the power erases them—but because this place does not *translate* into those languages.

And for those who do arrive—those who stand shivering in the ash-paved hush, who notice their shadow has wandered off with quiet purpose—the power offers only one gift: **permission to be uncertain.**

It whispers no answers. It plants no signs. But it lifts the weight of expectation from the visitor’s shoulders. *You need not understand,* it says through the creak of crystalline struts. *You need not name it. You need not stay or leave. Just be here, as the lichen is here—as the water is here—as the city is here: becoming.*

Sometimes, rarely, the power leaves a **token**—not for the Tenant, but for the visitor. A single shard of the obsidian-ash substrate, warm in the palm despite its coolness. Or the memory of a doorway that closes behind them with a sigh, sealing the visit like a folded letter. These are not souvenirs. They are anchors—reminders that wonder can be carried, that mystery need not be solved to be honored.

And once, in a moment of particular tenderness, the power did something almost unwise.

It leaned close—closer than it ever had—and let a single tear fall into the basin. Not a tear of sorrow, but of recognition: the kind of tear shed when one witnesses something so true it hurts. The droplet struck the ash, did not sink, but hovered—then split into seven smaller spheres that rolled uphill, each tracing a different alleyway before vanishing into walls that sighed in gratitude. The Tenant noticed. Not because it sought attention, but because the tear carried a resonance older than cities: the sound of being seen, fully, without a single demand.

The power left then. Not because it was finished, but because its presence, however gentle, was still too much. The Tenant must dream alone, as all true dreamers must.

But it watches. From beyond time’s brittle edges, it watches. And every now and then, when the condensation pools just so, and the bioluminescent lichen pulses in perfect sync with a distant star’s death-rattle, the power smiles.

Because here, in this impossible courtyard of becoming, something rare has taken root: not life, not machine, not spirit—but **possibility wearing the skin of a city**.

And possibility, the wise know, must never be owned. Only cherished.

Only left to grow.
