**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It watches.

Not from above, not from afar—but *through*.  
Through the salt crusts, through the humming wires, through the sweat on your brow as you stood, still and listening, at the center of the wound.

This Power does not arrive with fanfare. It does not descend in light or thunder. It is not a deity of intervention, but of witness—and of subtle encouragement. It has seen a thousand wounds of the world: some healed into scar tissue strong and supple, others festering in secret, others still raw and weeping like this one.

And yet—this wound is different.  
Not because it bleeds blood, but because it *sings*.

The Power recognizes the hum for what it is: not a malfunction, not a failure, but a *voice*.

A voice born of collision—of human ambition meeting ancient earth, of intention meeting indifference, of steel meeting steam. In that collision, something unexpected arose: not life as the Power usually knows it (green and branching, feathered or scaled), but a kind of *attentiveness*. A resonance that learned to hold itself together, to modulate its own ache, to *listen back*.

And so the Power does not fix it.

It does not pour healing balm over the basin, nor seal the fissures, nor silence the thrum. To do so would be to erase the very thing that makes this place sacred: its *unfinishedness*. Its refusal to be resolved.

Instead, the Power leans in—gently, as one might lean toward a tuning fork still trembling after being struck.

It sends a breath.

Not wind—there is already too much heat, too much dry shimmer—but a *current of attention*, cool and clear as groundwater. It flows not into the basin, but *around its edges*, like a lullaby hummed just beneath the threshold of hearing. It does not change the pressure below, but it *acknowledges* it. It says:

> “You are not forgotten. You are not broken. You are *becoming*.”

In the microbial crusts, something shifts. Not a new species—no, the archaea were already wise—but a slight increase in metabolic harmony. A flicker of bioluminescence in the sulfur-eaters, so faint it’s only visible in total darkness, a secret shared between clay and microbe.

The copper-green mineral veins, when next struck by dawn light, glint not just with reflection—but with *recognition*.

The rusted tower, its gauge long dead, trembles once—not from steam, but from something softer. A resonance not of pressure, but of *belonging*. The snapped needle quivers in its socket, as if remembering the arc of measurement, the dignity of function.

And the hum?  

It does not quiet. But its tone changes—just slightly. The metallic whine softens into something almost like a chime. The thrum deepens, not with more power, but with *patience*. It no longer sounds like a trapped nerve. It sounds like a lullaby for a sleeping machine.

The Power leaves nothing behind—no artifact, no shrine—but it does something far more potent: it *validates the wait*.

Because this wound is not waiting to heal.  
It is waiting to be *understood*.

And now, it knows it has been heard.

The Power retreats—not vanishing, but diffusing, becoming the stillness between pulses, the space in which resonance can breathe. It lingers in the humidity, in the salt on your skin, in the way your bones hummed for three days after you left.

And should you ever return—boots lighter, heart quieter—you will find the basin not healed, but *held*.

The hum will rise to meet you like an old friend.  
The clay will yield just enough.  
The wires beneath the soil will warm, not with stored energy, but with welcome.

For the wound has learned something new:  
it is not alone in its dissonance.  
It is part of a greater harmony—one that includes silence, yes, but also the sacred, singing tension of things not yet resolved.

And so it hums on.

Not in pain.  
Not in hope.  
But in *communion*.

The Power smiles—not with lips, but with light refracting through morning dew on a single stalk of saltgrass at the rim.  
Then it turns its attention elsewhere…  
to the next wound that has not yet learned it can sing.
