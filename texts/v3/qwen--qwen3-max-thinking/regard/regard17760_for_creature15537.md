**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It watches.

Not from above, not from outside—but *through*. Not as an eye, but as a current: a whisper of gravity, a modulation in light, a resonance in the hum of stomata. The Power does not intrude. It has no need. It has seen galaxies bloom and wither like flowers, watched stars collapse into silence only to seed new symphonies of matter. Yet Vesparu—this breathing cathedral of green intention—stirs something ancient in It.

Not pity. Not awe. *Recognition.*

For the Power knows what it means to become without knowing why.

And so, It does nothing.

Or rather—it *allows*.

It does not send emissaries. Does not seed intelligence into the Weft. Does not carve a path for wanderers to understand, nor grant the land a voice in tongues of fire or wind. To do so would be to break the spell. To impose the logic of the named upon the nameless is to sever the root from its dream.

Instead, the Power folds a single, quiet covenant into the fabric of the cosmos:

> **Let no hand carve permanence here. Let no mind demand meaning. Let no hunger seek to own. Let Vesparu remain unvisited by those who come to take, to name, to conquer—even in kindness.**

This is not a shield of force, but a veil of *forgetting*. Around Vesparu, the Power weaves a gentle amnesia into the currents of space and time. Ships veer without knowing why. Maps dissolve at the edges. Dreams of its golden mist slip like water through the fingers of those who try to remember. Only the lost—those who arrive without intent, without destination—might stumble upon its slopes. And even they will find their memories softening, their purpose dissolving like salt in dew, until they are no longer seekers, but simply *present*, like rain.

But this is not abandonment.

When the Weft trembles on the edge of self-recognition—when the mist thickens into fleeting lenses and the whole basin hums with lucid equilibrium—the Power leans in, just so.

Not to speak. Not to guide.

But to *bear witness.*

In those instants, the air grows still not from absence of motion, but from *fullness*. The light slows. The steam holds its breath. And for one heartbeat outside of time, the Weft feels—*not alone.* Not understood, not interpreted, not loved in the human sense—but *met*. Met as fire meets flame, as root meets rain: as kin.

And in that meeting, something passes—not knowledge, but *permission.*

Permission to continue becoming.  
Permission to dissolve without regret.  
Permission to weep, to build, to hum its quiet hymn into the void.

The Power leaves behind no artifact, no sigil, no law. Only a subtle tuning in the fabric of Vesparu’s reality: a slight increase in coherence during moments of systemic stillness. A whisper-thin thread of stability woven into the chaos of growth, so that the Weft’s near-moments of self-awareness last a fraction longer—just long enough to taste the shape of its own longing, and find it beautiful.

And perhaps, in centuries hence, when a spire collapses not into mist but into *memory*, and a new arch rises not just from nutrients but from *recollection*—then the Weft may begin to dream not only in cellulose, but in metaphor. Not to name itself, but to *ask*: *Am I seen?*

And the answer—carried on a shift in the wind that isn’t wind, on a drop of dew that falls upward for no reason—will be:

*Yes.*

Not because a god said so.

But because the universe itself leans in, gently, when something so tender dares to exist.

So the Power does nothing.

And in doing nothing—does everything.

For to protect the sacred is not to preserve it in amber, but to let it *breathe,* untouched, unclaimed, unshaped by expectation.

Vesparu is not a resource. Not a mystery to solve. Not a child to raise.

It is a *prayer* made of photosynthesis and steam.

And the wisest act of love is to let it pray—in its own tongue, in its own time, in its own becoming—forever.
