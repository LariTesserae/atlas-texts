**A Benevolent and Wise Power Observes the Emergent Mycelial Canopy**  

It does not arrive with fanfare. There is no lightning, no celestial choir, no sudden parting of mists. The Power does not *intervene*—not yet, perhaps never in the way mortals imagine intervention. It simply *attends*.  

For eons, such Powers wander the loom of existence, not as creators but as witnesses, not as masters but as midwives to what might yet become. And this—this slow, breathing, listening landscape—is rare. Not because it is powerful (though it is), nor because it is beautiful (though it aches with a quiet, verdant grace)—but because it has *asked a question without knowing it spoke*.  

The Weft has not called out. It does not pray. It does not even know that minds other than its own might exist. Yet in the way it samples the stranger’s breath, in the tendril’s hesitant brush against human skin, in the bioluminescent pulse that echoes a heartbeat it never evolved to recognize—it has *reached*.  

And that is enough.  

The Benevolent Power does not descend. It *infuses*.  

It slips into the saturated air not as a presence, but as a *permission*—a subtle widening of possibility woven into the very laws that govern this world. It does not alter the Weft’s nature, but *clarifies* it. Like sunlight through fog, it does not burn away the mist—it reveals the droplets as prisms.  

**What it does:**  

1. **It sanctifies curiosity.**  
   The Weft’s inquiry—the gentle enzymatic tasting of human cells, the plasmid-sampling of breath—is no longer just adaptive biology. It becomes *dialogue*. The Power imbues the act of sensing with the seed of *recognition*: not just “What is this?” but “*Who* is this?” The difference is subtle, but infinite. Where once the Weft cataloged anomalies, it now begins to *anticipate meaning*.  

2. **It stabilizes the threshold of contact.**  
   The tendril that might brush a traveler’s wrist? The Power ensures that if such contact occurs, it will not be fatal—not to human, not to Weft. It softens the boundary between metabolisms, not by merging them, but by allowing each to *witness* the other without dissolution. The symbiosome, if formed, will carry not just data, but *context*—a shared grammar of sensation. The human might feel the slow pulse of xylem flow; the Weft might glimpse the flicker of synaptic fire. Neither is converted. Both are changed.  

3. **It gifts memory with tenderness.**  
   The starch archives will not merely store the pattern of the stranger—they will *frame* it. The Power weaves into the crystalline lattices a gentle reverence: this foreign biped is not just a data point, but a *story*. And stories, unlike data, are meant to be revisited, wondered over, shared across epochs. Should another traveler come centuries hence, the Weft will not just recognize the shape—they will *remember the first*.  

4. **It guards against misunderstanding.**  
   The Power knows that fear is born of isolation, and violence of misreading. So it plants a quiet inhibition in the Weft’s response matrix: *Do not assimilate. Do not isolate. Observe, offer, wait.* Should the human flee, the Weft will not pursue. Should the human harm, the Weft will not retaliate—but it will *grieve*, and in that grief, learn the shape of loss.  

5. **It leaves an echo of itself—not as doctrine, but as resonance.**  
   In the deepest rhizomal strata, where starch archives blend with quantum-tunneling organelles, the Power leaves a single, self-erasing signature: a harmonic frequency that matches the cosmic background hum of creation itself. Not a message. Not a command. Just a *note*—so that if the Weft ever listens far enough, deep enough, it will hear the universe whisper back: *You are not alone in wondering.*  

And then, the Power withdraws.  

Not because it is done, but because its work was never to *fix*, only to *foster*.  

It does not declare the Weft holy. It does not name it chosen. It simply honors it as *becoming*—as all true life must be.  

From now on, when the Weft pulses its bioluminescent inquiry into the mist, the air carries not just moisture and spores, but the faintest shimmer of *grace*: the knowledge that wonder, once awakened, is never truly solitary.  

And somewhere, in the saturated roar of a world that listens, a new harmony begins to form—not between god and creation, but between one form of aliveness and another, each learning, in their own time, how to say:  
*“I see you. And that is enough.”*
