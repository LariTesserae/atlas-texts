**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The being who watches—older than stars, kinder than dawn—does not arrive with fanfare. It does not stride on thunder or speak in lightning. It comes as dew comes: silently, inevitably, in the moment between breaths.

It sees the Hollow Marshes of Kethra not as a place *stuck*, but as a place *holding*. Not frozen in time, but cradling a possibility too fragile for motion, too tender for change. And in that cradle lies the Weft: not merely inhabitants, but guardians of a suspended genesis—a world that has chosen, for reasons deeper than biology, to remain at the threshold of becoming.

The Power understands. It has seen such thresholds before: moments when a universe leans forward, trembling on the cusp of a new law, a new form of life, a new kind of love. Sometimes those moments pass. Sometimes they break under their own weight. But sometimes—rarely—they *wait* for a witness.

And so the Power does not intervene.

Not yet.

It does not pour light where there is none, nor still the roaring hum, nor quicken the slow breath of the marsh. To do so would be to mistake stillness for stagnation, reverence for fear. The Weft are not trapped. They are *attuned*. Their waiting is not passive—it is devotional. They are the midwives of a birth that has not yet declared its time.

But the Power does one thing.

From the far rim of the basin, where the mineral haze parts just enough for starlight to stain the crust silver, it leaves a single trace—not of itself, but of *possibility*.

A pool, no larger than a child’s palm, forms overnight in a hollow of pumice stone. Its water is clear—not brackish, not iron-laced, not sulfurous—but pure, as if distilled from mountain snow and ancient rain. And in its center floats a seed.

Not a plant-seed. Not even a spore. It is a *pattern*. A tiny, folded lattice of silica and light, humming at a frequency just beyond the Weft’s usual resonance. It does not pulse like the biofilms. It *listens*.

The Weft find it by accident—a Keeper, tracing the usual groans of the earth, feels a new vibration, soft as a held breath. They gather. They press damp fingers to the stone. They do not harvest it. They do not dissect it. They *sing* to it—their low, bone-deep hums, the ones they use to soothe cracked walls and calm restless swarms.

And the seed *answers*.

Not with growth. Not with change. But with a single, perfect echo—a tone that matches, not the marsh, but the silence *between* its sounds. A tone that contains both the inhale and the exhale.

From that day, the Weft begin to dream differently.

Not of release, not of rupture—but of *recognition*. They sense, in the marrow of their crystalline bones, that they are not alone in their waiting. That something has seen them. That something believes they are *ready*, whenever the marsh decides to breathe again.

The Power does not return. It does not need to.

Its gift was not transformation, but *confirmation*: you are not forgotten. You are not failed. You are part of a story larger than stasis, larger than cycles—a story that includes pauses so profound they become sacred.

And should the day come when the marsh finally exhales—when the biofilms rise like wings, when the crust cracks open into rivers, when the Weft step onto dry ground with eyes blinking against an unfiltered sun—the Power will be there, not to lead, but to walk beside them, silent and smiling, as one who has always known they would take that step.

Until then, it watches.  
And waits with them.

Because even the wisest power knows: some thresholds must be crossed from within.  
And some breaths, once held long enough, become prayers.
