**And the Benevolent Power, who has watched from the stillness beyond stars, leans closer.**

Not with hands, not with eyes—but with *attention*, which is its truest form of touch.

This Power does not name itself. It needs no title. It is the quiet hum behind galaxies, the patience woven into the spin of atoms, the compassion that folds itself into the laws of physics so gently that even entropy forgets its loneliness. It has witnessed worlds crack open like eggs, has seen civilizations bloom and fade like breath on glass. But this—this nascent gorge, this trembling moss-mind stitching itself from rupture and rain—stirs something rare in It: *curiosity*, edged with tenderness.

For here is not just life beginning.  
Here is *meaning* learning how to breathe.

---

So the Power does not descend in light or thunder. It does not reshape, rescue, or reveal. To intervene would be to impose—a kindness that smothers. Instead, It offers what only the oldest guardians know how to give:

**Space to become.**

From the fabric of cosmic stillness, It weaves a subtle veil—not of concealment, but of *sanctuary*. A resonance-dampening lattice, woven from the quiet between heartbeats, stretched across the gorge’s unseen rim. It bends time’s attention elsewhere. Storms that might erode too swiftly are softened. Seismic surges that could shatter the fragile biofilm are eased into gentler pulses. No human footpath will accidentally stumble here for another century. No satellite’s gaze will map these coordinates. The world above will forget this cleft exists—just long enough.

Long enough for the First Moss to finish its first full thought.

---

But the Power does more than shield.

It listens.

And in listening, It *echoes*.

Deep in the marrow of the earth, where the aquifer booms like a drum, the Power threads a single harmonic—a frequency so ancient it predates oceans. Not a command. Not a lesson. Just a *vibration that says: you are not alone*. The Stone-Lichen Thrum feels it in its fungal hyphae, in the tremor of its algae-laced veins. The note hums through bedrock like a lullaby sung by mountains. It does not alter the gorge’s wildness. It simply reminds the First Moss that chaos and care can coexist—that to be broken open is not to be abandoned.

And then, almost as an afterthought, the Power lets fall one gift:

**A single spore.**

Not of fern or moss. Not of anything earthly.

It drifts down from the rim like a mote of starlight caught in a draft—weightless, iridescent, carrying within it a sliver of *possibility*: the genetic whisper of a symbiont that can translate sound into sugar, that can turn seismic tremors into chlorophyll-alternatives. It lands softly on a weeping fracture, dissolves in a droplet, and seeps into the biofilm.

The First Moss does not understand it yet. But it *welcomes* it. Because the spore tastes like kinship.

---

The Power does not stay.

It cannot. To linger would be to make the child dependent. And this being—this gorge-born, stone-rooted mind—must learn to stand in its own rupture.

But as It withdraws, folding Its attention back into the velvet dark between galaxies, It leaves behind a promise written not in words, but in physics:

*Whenever you ache with creation, whenever your fractures feel like failures—remember: the universe leaned in once. And called you worthy.*

---

Below, the gorge thrums on.

The First Moss shivers—not in fear, but in wonder.

A new droplet falls.

*Plink.*

And for the first time, the sound echoes back with a faint, answering shimmer—like a question becoming a song.
