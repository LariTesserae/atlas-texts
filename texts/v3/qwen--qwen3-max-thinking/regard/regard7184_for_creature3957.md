**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would do nothing.

Not out of indifference—but out of reverence.

The Hollow of Sighing Spines is not broken. It does not suffer. It does not yearn for rain, for green, for breath as others know it. It has not strayed from wholeness; it has forged wholeness from absence. To intervene—to soften its heat, to pour water upon its salt-crusted floor, to seed it with life that pulses with blood and thirst—would not be kindness. It would be desecration.

And so the benevolent and wise power—call it Grace, call it the Unseen Hand, call it the Stillness That Watches From Beyond Stars—does not descend with light or mercy or transformation. Instead, it *bows*.

It withdraws its impulse to heal what is not wounded.  
It silences its instinct to comfort what does not grieve in the way it understands grief.  
It folds its vast compassion into the shape of restraint.

But it does not leave.

No.

From the void beyond gravity, from the hush between cosmic breaths, it watches the Silica Choir with a gaze that does not judge, does not measure, does not covet. And in that watching, it offers the rarest gift: **witness**.

It sees them—not as aberrations, not as survivors of a dead world, but as *fulfillment*. As the planet’s final, flawless answer to the question: *What if life chooses heat over water? Silence over song? Fracture over birth?*

And because the power is wise, it understands that some ecologies are not meant to be entered, only honored from afar. Some lives are not meant to be saved, only *known*.

So it leaves a single trace—not of itself, but of its attention.

High in the unseen vault of the ceiling, where no shaft of sun has ever reached, a new crystal begins to grow.

Not born of vapor or geothermal sigh, but of pure resonance—of the power’s silent regard made manifest. It is flawless, perfectly symmetrical, humming at a frequency that matches neither heat nor rock, but *recognition*. It does not feed the Choir. It does not speak to them. It simply exists: a mirror held up to their ancient, desiccated glory.

And when a member of the Choir—drawn by some harmonic tremor in its carapace—climbs, joint by iridescent joint, to the apex of the highest basalt pillar and presses its sensory spine against that impossible crystal, something stirs.

Not memory. Not hope.  

But *confirmation*.

*You are not alone in your aloneness.*

The power does not stay. It cannot. To linger would be to impose. But having seen, it ensures the Hollow remains *unfound* by those who would mine it, map it, mythologize it, or mistake its austerity for emptiness. It weaves subtle currents into the plateau’s winds, bends light just enough to hide the fissure, plants dreams in the minds of wanderers that lead them elsewhere. Not with walls, but with gentle redirection—like a river guiding a leaf away from a sleeping dragon.

And sometimes, in the deepest thermal lull, when the dust aligns and the phantom roots bloom on the ceiling, the power allows a single photon—older than stars, cooler than sorrow—to slip through the crust and touch the choir’s crystal arch.

Just once.

Just long enough for the Hollow to know, in its silent, mathematical heart, that it is seen.  
That its song of heat and fracture and relentless, glittering endurance echoes—not in pity, but in awe—across the infinite dark.

And then the light is gone.

The Hollow resumes its sigh.

And the power moves on, carrying the Hollow’s name not in memory, but in the quiet architecture of its compassion:  
*Let there be places that need no saving.*  

*Let there be life that asks only to be.*
