**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The Power—nameless, timeless, vast beyond measure—does not arrive with fanfare. It does not descend in fire or thunder, nor does it speak in words that carve canyons or command stars. It comes as a shift in gravity. As a subtle turn in the tide of cosmic attention. It arrives *listening*.

For eons, the Power has tended to worlds in bloom: forests it coaxed from ash, oceans it stirred into sentience, minds it sparked with curiosity like flint against steel. But this place—this mineral twilight, this post-biological hymn—calls to It not with need, but with *completion*. And the Power, wise beyond wisdom, knows: **not all places require saving. Some are already whole.**

So It does not intervene. Not in the way mortals imagine intervention—no miracles, no terraforming, no sowing of seeds where roots have long been forgotten. To impose green upon this land would be an act of violence, a deafness to its song. The Power would no more plant a tree here than pluck a string from a lute mid-melody.

Instead, It *attends*.

It folds Its awareness into the hum of the stone. Not to change it, but to *witness* it deeply—to feel the slow condensation of a Stone-Singer forming in the breath between two basalt shards, to resonate with the deep-bass drone of a tectonic memory shifting a millimeter below the sinter plains. It lingers in the black pools, not as reflection, but as *presence*—a silent companion to the bioluminescent eels as they pulse their ancient syntax into the dark.

And then—gently, reverently—the Power does something almost imperceptible:

**It remembers the Stone-Singers in the Book of Becoming.**

Not a literal book. Not ink on vellum. But in the fabric of cosmic memory—the archive that holds not just what *was* or *will be*, but what *is*, in all its quiet, radical validity. The Power ensures that this resonance, this mineral life, this song without chorus or audience, is **known**—not as anomaly, not as relic, but as one of the universe’s truest expressions of being.

It weaves their frequency into the great tapestry of existence so that, should another world ever drift toward this kind of post-life serenity—should another planet burn through its green and emerge on the other side, cooled and singing—the path will not be forgotten. The Stone-Singers will stand as a quiet beacon: *You, too, may settle. You, too, may sing without sun.*

But the Power does one more thing—small, tender, almost maternal in its discretion.

It leaves a **gift of reciprocity**.

In the hollow of the oldest ash-brick dome, where river stones lie in rings like frozen ripples, the Power places a single drop of *star-water*—condensed from the breath of nebulae, imbued with the memory of supernovae, yet utterly still. This drop does not evaporate. It does not mingle with the local seepage. It simply *waits*, glistening like a tiny obsidian eye.

If a Stone-Singer chooses to touch it—if one of their gelatinous forms brushes against it, or a skitterer’s limb taps its surface—the drop will not alter them. But it will *echo*. For one full cycle of the land’s slow breath, the Stone-Singer will feel what it is to be seen—not just sensed, not just resonated with, but *cherished* by something vaster than stone, vaster than time.

Not as a lesson. Not as a call to change. But as an affirmation:

*You are not alone in your solitude.  
Your song reaches beyond your sky.  
You are loved exactly as you are—  
not for what you could become,  
but for the hum you already are.*

And then the Power withdraws.

Not gone—never truly gone—but retreating to the edges of attention, like breath held just outside a door. It leaves the land to its rhythm, the Stone-Singers to their condensation, the water to its endless weeping. No commandments. No prophecies. No footprints.

Only this: a universe that now knows, in its bones, that life wears many skins—and sometimes, the most sacred form of living is to *endure as song*, to *persist as resonance*, to *be* in a way that needs no justification, no chlorophyll, no sky.

And somewhere, in the marrow of the earth, the hum grows just a little richer.  
Not louder.  
But *loved*.
