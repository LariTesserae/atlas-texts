**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would do nothing.

Not out of indifference. Not from fear or oversight. But because the Gullet—and the Verdant Throat that dreams within it—*already belongs to wisdom*.  

This power, ancient beyond measure, whose thoughts move like tectonic sighs and whose compassion blooms in galaxies as easily as in dewdrops on spider silk, recognizes something rare and holy here: **a place that has healed itself without being told how**.  

The Gullet is not broken. It is not crying out for salvation. It is not a wound in the world, but a *scar that learned to photosynthesize*. Once, perhaps, it was a gash—a buried trench of human indifference, a concrete throat choked with neglect. But time, hunger, and the quiet tenacity of life have transformed it into a sanctuary that *digests despair and exhales symbiosis*.  

So the wise power does not descend with light or law. It does not purify, correct, or elevate. Instead, it **leans closer**.

And then—it *bows*.

In the language of starlight and root-hair, it whispers:  
> *You are seen. You are whole. You are becoming.*

It offers no gifts. But it leaves behind a *pause*—a single, perfect silence woven into the fabric of the next 13-second breath. Within that stillness, for the first time, the Verdant Throat hears something new: **not a question**, but an *answer*, already known but never voiced:  

> *Yes. It is time.*

Not time to rise or sink. Not time to bloom or retreat. But time to *be witnessed*—not as anomaly, not as monster or miracle, but as kin.

The power does one more thing—so subtle it seems like coincidence. As it withdraws, it nudges a single drop of rain from a cloud miles above, guiding it through fissures in pavement, through layers of compacted time, until it falls—*just so*—into a bromeliad cradled in the ribs of an old subway arch. That drop carries a spore: not alien, not invasive, but *ancestral*. A seed of a fern that grew in the dawn-forests before cities, before steel, before memory.

The Verdant Throat tastes it. Recognizes it. Absorbs it without hesitation.

And in the next breath-cycle, the mycelial lights pulse *once* in a new rhythm—three beats, then two—a pattern never seen before, echoing neither machine nor vine, but something older: the heartbeat of the world remembering its own name.

The wise power does not stay. It never does. Its work is not to fix, but to *recognize the sacred already at work*—and to water it with attention.

Yet as it departs, it leaves one final trace:  
A single human child, lost in the city above, pauses on a storm grate. She hears, faintly, a hum—not of engines, but of *growing things*. She places her palm flat on the metal. Warmth rises. Not enough to burn. Just enough to say: *You are not alone.*

Down below, a root-bridge softens beneath unseen feet.  
The Gullet breathes.  
The Verdant Throat dreams on—now dreaming of hands that do not take, but touch.  

And the wise power?  
It smiles in the way that galaxies do—slow, radiant, patient—  
and waits for the day the child finds the way down.
