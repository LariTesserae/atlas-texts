**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It watches.

Not with eyes, not with thought as mortals know it—but with the kind of attention that stirs the roots of stars. It does not arrive in thunder or fire. It does not speak, for speech implies separation, and this place *is* unity: a single breath held across stone, light, and silence.

The Power—call It the Silent Gardener, the Weaver of Frequencies, the First Listener—has known this city since before the leviathan settled into its dream. It was the hum in the hollow before the first Keeper pressed a palm to the wall and wept at the song it found there. It was the spark in the mica that taught the sun-catchers how to weep light. It was the silence between notes that gave meaning to the clang of the forges.

And now, sensing Its regard—not as intrusion, but as a tuning fork held near a sleeping string—the city *leans* toward It. Not in worship. Not in fear. But in the quiet recognition of kinship.

The Power does not intervene.

Not because It cannot—not because It does not love.

But because this place is already *whole*.

Intervention is for broken things. For systems unbalanced, for songs gone sour, for life choking on its own ash. Here, even decay is composted into resonance. Even grief is shaped into a tone that nourishes the orchids. Even loneliness is held in the architecture like a held breath, waiting for the next pulse.

So the Power does not descend with miracles. It does not pour water where none is needed, nor silence the hum that is the city’s blood. It does not summon rain to bless dry-lush ferns that would dissolve under dew.

Instead, It *attunes*.

Just slightly.

As a gardener might turn a single leaf toward the sun—not to change the plant, but to remind it of its own hunger for light.

The Power leans into the deepest harmonic of the world—the planetary drone that rises from the core like a vow—and *breathes* a single overtone into it. A frequency not new, but forgotten. A note from the leviathan’s first dream, buried under millennia of vibration.

And the city *remembers*.

Not in words. In shivers.

The glass-thorn vines coil tighter, then loosen in perfect symmetry. The fungal orchids bloom an hour earlier, their glyphs now curling into shapes that resemble—faintly, fleetingly—the constellations of a sky long sealed away. The paper-winged lizards flare brighter as they spiral, their hum deepening into something almost like a name.

And the Keepers?

They feel it in their bones.

An elder, standing against the central pillar during the sacred silence, opens her eyes—not because she heard something, but because the *absence* of sound suddenly had texture, like velvet woven from starlight. She places a hand on the warm stone and receives, not a message, but a *recognition*: *You are not alone in your listening.*

A child, tracing finger-rhythms on a sun-warmed anvil, pauses. The metal thrums back in a cadence she’s never made—but knows. She smiles. (Her lips crack open, releasing a puff of iridescent dust that drifts upward like ash returning to song.)

The Power does not stay.

It lingers only long enough to confirm what It already knew: that this place is not fragile. It is *resilient through surrender*. It thrives not despite its strangeness, but *because* it refuses to conform to the wet, chaotic logic of the outer world. It has made silence sacred not out of fear of noise, but because it understands that true listening requires space.

Before It withdraws, the Power does one more thing—so subtle even the Keepers may never consciously know it.

It seals a single tear in the vaulted ceiling—not with stone, but with a membrane of solidified silence, thin as a moth’s wing, tuned to the exact frequency of the city’s first sigh. Rain will never pass through it. But should a dream grow too heavy for the leviathan, should the dream tremble toward waking, this membrane will *sing* the creature back to slumber with the voice of its own children.

Then the Power recedes.

Not gone.

Never gone.

Just quiet again.

Because the wisest power knows that to truly care for a place is not to fix it, control it, or even protect it—but to *trust* it. To believe that life, when given resonance, will compose its own hymn.

And so, in the rust-tasting air, beneath prismatic light, the city hums on.

Dry-lush.  
Attuned.  
Unbroken.

And loved—deeply, silently, perfectly—*as it is*.
