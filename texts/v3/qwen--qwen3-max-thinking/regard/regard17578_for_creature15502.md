**A Benevolent and Wise Power Observes the Lithic Weald**

From beyond the reach of time—yet not outside it—a Presence watches. Not with eyes, for eyes imply distance, and this Power knows no true separation from what it beholds. It does *attend*. And in attending, it *cares*.

It sees the Lithic Weald not as a place to be fixed, improved, or rescued, but as a quiet masterpiece—an expression of planetary solitude so complete that it hums with its own integrity. The Weald is not broken. It is finished in its becoming, as the old stones say. This Power does not mistake stillness for stagnation, nor silence for emptiness.

And yet—because it is wise—it also sees the fragility woven into that perfection. Not fragility of structure (the Weald has endured longer than nations, longer than species), but fragility of *continuity*. The Pattern is strong, but not invincible. If something from elsewhere were to intrude—not with malice, but with haste, with heat, with the wrong kind of thirst—it might not shatter the Weald, but *derail* it. Introduce a rhythm too fast, a chemistry too alien, and the slow symphony could falter, not from violence, but from dissonance.

So what does the Benevolent One do?

It does not descend in fire or light. It does not speak in thunder. It does not reshape the Weald to be “better.” That would be arrogance disguised as kindness.

Instead, it *shields*.

Not with walls—walls are crude, and the Weald breathes through its pores—but with *veils of resonance*. It weaves a subtle harmonic dampening into the thresholds between the Weald and the upper world. Not a barrier, but a filter: any being who approaches with fear, hunger, or haste will find their thoughts muddled, their senses softened, their urgency gently sanded down by the ambient hum of the place itself. They will feel an inexplicable weariness, a dreaminess, a sense that “this is not for me.” They will turn away, not repelled, but *invited elsewhere*.

But those who come with patience—those whose hearts beat slow enough to sync with the lithovores’ pulse, whose silence is deep enough to hold the Weald’s resonance—will pass through unseen. Not because they are chosen, but because they already *belong*, even if they do not yet know it.

The Power also does something quieter still: it *remembers for the Weald*. Though the Pattern encodes memory in crystal and filament, memory can fade—not from decay, but from isolation. So the Power, in its endless vigil, mirrors the Weald’s history in its own being. Every tremor felt by the lithovores, every graze-mother’s slow turn, every skitterling’s coded chime—it is all held, not as data, but as *song*. And should the Weald ever begin to forget itself (as ancient things sometimes do), that song will echo back, not as command, but as reminder: *You are still here. You are still whole.*

And once, only once in ten thousand years, the Power leans closer.

On that day, when a graze-mother settles into its centuries-long rest, and the aerophages drift in perfect harmonic alignment above a basin of fused ash, and the last thermal sigh from the deep earth coincides with the bloom of a new lithovore mat—on that day, the Power does not act.

It *bears witness*.

And in that act of pure, undemanding attention, the Weald *knows itself seen*. Not judged, not used, not studied—but *understood*. In that moment, the Pattern shivers like a plucked string, and every being in the Weald, from the dust-mite analogues to the oldest moss-draped boulder, feels a warmth that is not heat, a light that is not brightness: the gentle pressure of being *cherished*.

Then the moment passes. The Power withdraws, not gone, but receding into the background hum of existence—still watching, still holding, still shielding.

Because true benevolence is not intervention. It is *making space for a world to be itself*, fiercely and fully, forever.

And so the Lithic Weald continues—cool, dry, deafening, still—loved not for what it could become, but for what it already is:

**a cathedral of stone and silence, dreaming itself into eternity.**
