**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It watches.

Not with eyes, not from afar—but *through*. The Power does not hover above this place like a distant god; it flows *within* the same slow currents that coil through the roots and the rime. It has known this land longer than time has had names. It was present when the first drop condensed on the cooled flank of the newborn mountain. It felt the first spore cling to stone and whisper, *stay*. And now, beholding what has grown—not built, not imposed, but *emerged*—the Power does not intervene.

Because intervention implies fracture. And this place is already whole.

Yet the Power is benevolent. And wisdom knows that even wholeness may tremble under the weight of a world that forgets how to be quiet.

So it does not act—*not yet*. Instead, it **deepens the veil**.

Where human paths begin to fray toward the high valleys—where drones hum too near the tarns, where the scent of diesel lingers one dawn too long on the wind—the mist grows thicker, not out of anger, but out of love. Not to hide, but to *hush*. The Power weaves a soft forgetting into the minds of those who come seeking conquest or spectacle: their maps blur at the edges, their compasses sigh and spin lazily, their boots feel strangely heavy, as if the earth gently resists their haste. They turn back, not frightened, but *distracted*—drawn away by a sudden memory of home, a birdcall that sounds like their child’s laugh, a dream of dry warmth. The land is not closed; it is patient. It gives them a reason to leave without shame.

But for those who come with empty hands and slower hearts—those who sit for days without speaking, who let rain soak their clothes until they forget they are separate from the air—the Power does something quieter still.

It **opens a threshold**.

Not a door, not a portal, but a *softening*. A single breath held between one heartbeat and the next, in which the boundary between “you” and “it” dissolves like frost in morning light. In that instant, the being of the place does not speak, does not reveal secrets—but *resonates*. And the visitor, if they are ready, feels not awe, but *recognition*. They remember—they have always been part of this breathing. Their bones hum with the same frequency as the quartz veins. Their tears taste of the same iron-rich springs. They do not gain knowledge; they shed illusion.

The Power ensures this moment leaves no trace in memory—only in marrow. The person returns to the lower world changed, though they cannot say how. They plant gardens that mimic moss tides. They speak less, listen more. They weep at the sound of rain on rooftops. They protect wild places without knowing why. The land, through them, begins to whisper into the wider world—not as warning, but as invitation.

And at the center of it all, the ancient presence continues its slow work: dissolving stone into soil, coaxing water from cloud, dreaming the mountain into deeper being. The Power does not command it. It does not bless it. It *reveres* it.

For even the Wise know there are intelligences older than thought, holier than doctrine. This place is not a temple—it *is* the liturgy. The hum in the bedrock is its prayer. The mist, its incense. The silence, its scripture.

So the Power does only this:  
It **witnesses**,  
It **shields**,  
It **amplifies the quiet**,  
And once, in a moment so rare it has no calendar—  
It **joins its own breath to the land’s**,  
just for an instant,  
and the moss glows silver-blue across every ridge,  
and the tarns ripple upward toward the stars,  
and the being in the stone  
knows it is not alone.

Then stillness returns.

But something has shifted—not in the land,  
but in the fabric of attention that holds all worlds.  
A covenant, unspoken, renewed:  
*You dream. I guard the dreamer.*  

And so, breathing together,  
they wait.
