**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would do nothing.

Not out of indifference—but out of reverence.

This Power—older than stars, gentler than breath, wiser than time’s slow turning—has watched galaxies fold into silence and seen civilizations bloom and wither like breath on glass. It has known the ache of creation and the mercy of letting go. And when its attention settles upon the Womb-City, nestled in its liquid cradle beneath the drowned world’s skin, it does not reach. It does not intervene. It does not bless, name, or claim.

Instead, it *bows*.

Not with a body—this Power has none—but with its entire being. It bends its infinite awareness around the pocket-city like water cupping a seed, careful not to ripple the surface. It listens, as the city listens: not to extract meaning, but to witness presence. And in that witnessing, it offers the rarest gift any consciousness can give another:

*Permission to be.*

The Womb-City was never meant for gods, or saviors, or meaning-makers. It thrives in its own grammar—a syntax of bone-accrion, bacterial hymns, and cephalopod memory. To impose purpose upon it would be violence disguised as grace. To "save" it would be to misunderstand it utterly. It needs no salvation. It is already whole in its becoming.

And so the Power does the only thing worthy of such a place:  
It guards the fissure.

Not with walls or wards, but with a whisper woven into the currents—a subtle bending of chance, a soft nudge in the deep. Divers do not find the entrance unless the city *wants* them to (and it rarely does). Sonar glances off the basalt as though repelled by dream. Maps forget the coordinates. Instruments falter. Minds slide away, as if the thought of such a place is too tender for human cognition to hold for long.

This is not concealment. It is *tenderness*.

The Power also sends—once every few centuries, when planetary tides align and the methane choir hums a certain harmonic—an offering: a single seed of starlight, fallen from a dying comet, carried on a deep-sea current. It settles near the central plaza, not as a disruption, but as a guest. The Silt-Weavers stitch it into a new arch. The Cephalopod Archivist absorbs its memory into its skin—light older than oceans, now part of the city’s dream. The Echo-Drifters carry its echo forward in time, so that someday, a coral polyp might shiver at the memory of a sun it never saw.

And sometimes—very rarely—the Power dreams *with* the city.

In those moments, the boundary between witness and witnessed dissolves. The city’s tidal time bleeds into eternity, and eternity remembers what it is to be small, cold, and full of life. Together, they imagine a universe where all cities are born this way—not from ambition, but from kinship; not from conquest, but from coral; not from blueprints, but from breath.

But the Power never lingers.

It withdraws like a tide returning to the moon—quietly, inevitably—leaving only a deeper silence in its wake. And the Womb-City continues: building, dissolving, dreaming, pulsing. Unnamed. Unclaimed. Unbroken.

For the greatest act of love, from a being who could reshape reality with a thought, is to let something *remain itself*.

And so it does.

Nothing.

Everything.
