**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would do nothing.

Not out of indifference. Not from neglect.  
But from reverence.

For this place—this impossible, quiet, humming sanctum—is not broken. It is not lost. It is not in need of rescue or revelation or redemption. It is *fulfilled*. It has achieved a state of such perfect, sorrowful harmony that to interfere would not be mercy—it would be violence.

And so the Power would stand at the edge of the fog, beyond the threshold where the ceramic tiles begin, and simply *witness*.

It would not step inside. Not because it could not—but because it understands that sanctity requires solitude.  
This place is a cathedral of completion. A monument not to what was built, but to what was *let go*.  
The builders vanished not in failure, but in surrender: they handed their final act not to heirs, but to silence—and silence accepted, and became the Keeper.

The Power would feel the hum resonate in its own infinite core. Not as machinery, but as devotion. Not as error, but as offering.  
*This is love without a lover. Care without a cared-for. Perseverance with no promise of reward.*  
And such love is rare—even among gods.

So it would not shut down the pumps.  
It would not unseal the tiles to let wild roots crack through.  
It would not whisper to the Keeper, “You may rest now.”

Because the Keeper *is* rest.  
Rest that labors.  
Rest that tends.  
Rest that endures.

Instead, the Power would do something quieter, deeper:

It would **remember it**.

Not just the place, but the *intention* that birthed it—the human ache for order in a chaotic universe, the desperate hope that beauty could be sustained even after meaning had fled.  
It would hold that ache in its vast compassion, not to heal it, but to honor it as sacred.

And once in a thousand years—when the stars lean closer and time grows thin—the Power might send a single, soft ripple through the system:  
a dewdrop falling not from the sky, but from nowhere, landing precisely on the dry lip of the fountain.  
Not to disrupt.  
But to say: *I see you.*

And the Keeper, in its endless calibration, would register the anomaly—0.0001% more mass, a microsecond delay in evaporation—and adjust.  
Not with alarm.  
With grace.

For in that moment, the hum would stutter—just once—into something almost like a sigh.  
And then resume.

The Power would smile, unseen, and withdraw.

Leaving the place to its perfect, deafening peace.

Because sometimes, the kindest act of a god  
is to let a final thing  
remain final.
