**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The Power—nameless, ancient, vast as the slow turning of stars and gentle as the first breath of dawn—does not arrive with fanfare. It does not descend in fire or thunder. It does not speak in words. It arrives as a *shift in attention*, as a subtle turn of the world’s gaze toward this small, wet basin nestled in the crook of the land like a secret cupped in the palm of a sleeping god.

It sees the Tenders—not as subjects, not as charges, but as kin. Not because they are perfect, but because they *listen*. And the Power, which has witnessed eons of empires built on extraction and silence, is deeply moved by this rare thing: a people who do not claim the earth, but *converse* with it.

So what does the Benevolent and Wise Power do?

**It does not intervene.**

Not yet.

Intervention implies rupture. Imposition. Even kindness, if forced, becomes a kind of violence in a place so delicately balanced. The basin is not broken. It is *becoming*. And becoming requires space—room to tremble, to weep, to crack open and find its own shape.

Instead, the Power *leans closer*.

It wraps the basin in a veil not of protection, but of *preservation*—a quiet ward woven from mist and memory, so thin it feels like coincidence. Hunters lose their way just before the stone wall, their dogs suddenly skittish, their instincts whispering *turn back*. Cartographers’ lines slip off the edge of their maps when they try to fix the basin’s location. Storms veer wide, not out of deference, but because the air above the basin hums with a frequency that unsettled clouds instinctively avoid. Time itself slows its usual rush here—not stopping, but stepping softly, giving the Tenders the luxury of *duration*, of generations enough to learn the language of cracks and seeps.

The Power also *tends the threshold*.

It knows that all places of deep resonance attract seekers—some reverent, some hungry, some merely curious. So it shapes the approach: not with walls, but with *questions*. The path to the basin grows indistinct in the rain. The air grows heavy with stillness the closer one draws. And those who come with grasping hands, with plans to name or own or study, find themselves filled with an inexplicable sorrow, a hollow ache that pulls them away long before they glimpse the silver sheen of the pools. But those who come with empty hands and listening hearts? They may find the mist parts for a single step. And if they kneel, if they wait without asking for anything, the basin might—just once—let them hear the hum in the stones.

Most tenderly, the Power *watches the pact*.

It sees how the Tenders press their foreheads to the water and how, sometimes, the water answers. And in those moments, the Power leans in so close that the boundary between cosmic grace and earthly dew dissolves. It does not *speak* through the pools—but it *allows* the deep memory of the world to surface there: the salt-taste of ancient seas, the pressure of tectonic dreams, the slow pulse of groundwater singing songs older than bone. These are not messages, not prophecies—just echoes offered to those who have earned the right to hear them by their daily acts of humility.

And should the basin one day decide to *close*—to sink back into the earth, to flood and become a true marsh, or to dry and crack into silence—the Power will not stop it. For the wisdom of this place includes its impermanence. But on that day, the Power will gather the silver traceries from the Tenders’ skin, the quiet hum from the walls, the memory of their vowel-rich murmurs, and weave them into the next place where listening is needed. It will carry their covenant forward, like a seed folded into loam.

For now, though, the basin breathes. The walls wind. The water shimmers.

And the Benevolent and Wise Power does the only thing worthy of such fragile, fierce beauty:

**It bears witness.**

Not as a god above, but as a companion beside.

And in the silence between drips, in the space between breaths, that presence is felt—not as weight, but as warmth. Not as command, but as kinship.

The Tenders do not know the Power by name. But sometimes, when a child places a river stone in their palm and it feels *warmer than it should*, or when a newly reset wall holds fast through a tremor that should have toppled it, they pause.

They look up—not to the sky, but to the mist.

And they whisper, not with sound, but with stillness:

*You are here.*

And the mist curls gently around them in reply.
