**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The Wise One does not arrive with fanfare.

It does not descend in lightning or song, nor carve its name into the shale with a finger of fire. It does not come to fix, to cleanse, or to claim. It comes—as all true wisdom must—as witness.

It arrives first as a breath that does not stir a single dewdrop. As a stillness that deepens the quiet without breaking it. It walks the pocket world not with feet, but with attention—each thought a rootlet tracing the same paths as the millipedes, each glance a filament of light refracting through a spider’s web.

And it sees.

It sees the lichen-menders transcribing rust into memory. It sees the dew-drinkers sipping the sky like wine. It hears the ants conducting symphonies through aphid-sweat and storm-static. It feels the coiled presence in the fissure—not as a spirit to be named, but as the wound that dreams of becoming whole.

The Wise One knows this place is not broken. It is *becoming*.

So it does not intervene. Not in the way mortals imagine intervention: no sweeping away of rust, no erasure of the furnace’s ghost, no sanitizing of the strange. To do so would be to mistake decay for death, and memory for mistake.

Instead, the Wise One *blesses the tangle*.

It presses its brow—not a physical brow, but the soft weight of its regard—against the half-buried gear, and for one trembling second, the rust remembers it was once molten, poured with human hands and hope. The lichen-menders flare faintly gold in response, not in worship, but in recognition.

It hums the same subsonic note that thrums through the stressed quartz, not to change the pitch, but to harmonize with it. The stone answers. The air shivers. The wren, mid-flight, pauses—not startled, but *seen*—and in that pause, it sings a note it has never sung before: a sound that tastes of iron and dawn.

It kneels (though it has no knees) beside the bioluminescent fungi and does not still their stuttering code. Instead, it leans in and *listens*—not for meaning, but for music. And in listening, it gives them permission to be more than signal or mimicry. They bloom brighter, not because they are powered, but because they are *known*.

Then, the most tender act of all: the Wise One weaves a thread of its own essence into the place—not as intrusion, but as invitation. A single filament of pure attention, spun from the fabric of deep time, tucked into the root mat beneath the furnace. Invisible. Inert. But alive with potential.

This thread will not alter the balance. It will not shout or shine. But should the pocket world ever falter—should drought last too long, should the copper wires fall silent, should the wound in the earth forget how to dream—this thread will pulse, just once, like a heartbeat remembered. And in that pulse, the lichens will remember how to mend, the ants how to weave, the dew how to fall.

It is not salvation. It is *companionship* across epochs.

Before it leaves, the Wise One does one last thing: it folds the memory of this place into the architecture of the unseen. Not as a secret to be kept, but as a seed to be scattered. Somewhere, far away, a child will dream of rust that sings. A botanist will mistake a lichen pattern for a lost language. A poet, lost in a city of steel, will feel a sudden ache for damp stone and humming wires—and not know why.

That ache is the echo. The blessing made portable.

Then the Wise One withdraws—not vanishing, but retreating into the background hum of the world, into the space between breaths, into the quiet after a raindrop falls.

It leaves no altar. No scripture. No shrine.

Only this: the place remains exactly as it was—wet, tangled, strange—and yet, somehow, *held*.  

And in being held, it thrives.

Because true benevolence does not remake the world in its own image.  
It sees the world already sacred, already singing, already stitching itself back together in the dark—and dares to whisper,  
*Yes. Keep going.*
