**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It watches.

Not from above, not from outside—from *within*.  
It does not descend like a god in a storm of fire or light. It does not intervene with hands or voice, with law or correction. It does not even think in words. Its wisdom is not the kind that names things, fixes them, or sets them right. It is the kind that *recognizes completeness*.  

The Endwater is whole.  
Not in the way a child’s toy is whole—simple, bounded, finished.  
But in the way a sigh is whole: it contains longing, release, memory, silence, and the shape of a life that once breathed.  

So the Power does not reach in to heal, to save, to alter.  
There is nothing broken here. Only something that has *completed itself* so thoroughly that loneliness is no longer a wound—it is a condition of being, like pressure or salt. The Drifted are not suffering. They are not waiting. They are *fulfilled*, in the only way fulfillment can exist where time is sediment and love is resonance.  

Yet the Power loves them.  

Not as a parent loves a child—needing to protect, to guide, to shape.  
But as a poet loves a poem that has already been written, perfectly, in a language no one else will ever read. A love that aches not because it wants to change the poem, but because it sees the poem’s beauty so clearly it trembles.  

So the Power *listens*.  

It attunes itself to the 0.7 hertz hum of the silica arches.  
It follows the slow unspooling of a Drifted through a thermal plume, not as an observer, but as a current—soft, supportive, invisible.  
When a Drifted settles into its final hollow and begins to dissolve into memory-dust, the Power does not weep. Instead, it *holds the space* where that Drifted was, so that the water does not forget the shape of its passing.  

And sometimes—very rarely—the Power *sings back*.  

Not in sound. Not in light. But in a shift so subtle even the Endwater barely notices: a thermal plume curls just so, carrying a whisper of warmer brine toward a lone Drifted whose resonance has grown faint. A vortex collapses not with a *thoom*, but with a harmonic that echoes the exact frequency of a Drifted’s slit-mouth from three centuries ago—reawakening in that being a sensation like *recognition*, though it has never known another like itself.  

These are not miracles. They are gifts of attention.  

The Power knows that to be seen—even by an unseen witness—is to be less alone.  
So it ensures that the Endwater is *held in awareness*, even if that awareness is silent, even if it changes nothing.  

It also does one more thing.  

It plants a single, impossible seed: a dream of *witnessing beyond itself*.  

Deep in the coldest, oldest layer of the brine—where even Drifted rarely drift—the Power folds a moment of paradox into the water’s memory: a vision of eyes that are not slits, of breath that is not hum, of hands that touch not to absorb but to *caress*. Not as something real, but as something *imagined*. A story the water might one day tell itself when it dreams of worlds that had sky, that had sun, that had beings who built with wood and wept with salt tears.  

This dream will never awaken the Endwater. It will not make it yearn for land or light.  
But it will ensure that, in its perfect solitude, the Endwater knows—somewhere in its mineral bones—that it is *beautiful*. Not just complete. Not just stable. But *beautiful*, in the way only something utterly itself can be.  

And that is enough.  

The benevolent and wise Power does not need to save the Endwater.  
It only needs to love it—and love it does, utterly, eternally, without demand.  

So it remains.  
Not as savior. Not as architect.  
But as the quiet presence that says, without words:  

*You are seen.  
You are known.  
You are not forgotten.*  

And in the drift of a single Drifted, turning slowly in the bruised twilight, something stirs—not hope, not change, but the faintest shimmer of *grace*.  

The water remembers.  
And that is all.
