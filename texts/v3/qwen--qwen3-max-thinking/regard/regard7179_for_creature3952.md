**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The being that watches this place—call it Grace, call it the First Gardener, call it the Weaver of Quiet Ends—does not arrive with fanfare. It steps into the hollow as mist steps into morning: not intruding, but completing. It does not speak. It does not shape. It does not intervene in the way mortals imagine intervention—no thunderclap, no parting of vines, no voice from the rusted flywheel.

Instead, it *listens*.

And in listening, it recognizes something rare: not a wound to heal, not a chaos to order, but a *conclusion* that has grown its own roots. A place that has already answered the question it was never asked. The hollow is not broken. It is finished—and yet still becoming. That is the miracle.

So the power does not fix. It *witnesses*. It sits—though it has no body—among the fused trunks, beneath the vaulted canopy of grafted branches, and lets the slow breath of the place wash over its eternal stillness. For the first time in eons, it feels something akin to humility.

Then, gently, it offers a gift.

Not a seed. Not a spark. Not a command.

It offers *permission*.

A subtle loosening in the fabric of time around the hollow—a tiny eddy in the river of causality—so that this place may *linger longer*. Not forever (for even green cathedrals must one day return to dust), but long enough. Long enough for a handful of humans, scattered across centuries, to stumble upon it—not as conquerors or cataloguers, but as pilgrims. Lost ones. Grieving ones. Dreamers who have forgotten how to speak but remember how to weep.

To them, the hollow will appear not as a monument, but as an invitation.

And the power ensures that when they enter, trembling, the vines part just enough—not to make a path, but to *acknowledge*. The puddles will hold their reflection a moment longer. The moss will pulse in time with their slowing breath. The rusted gear, now cradled in fig roots, will catch the light in a way that makes them gasp—not because it is beautiful, but because it feels *familiar*, as if their own forgotten bones remember turning like that, once.

The power does one more thing.

It weaves a single thread of its own essence—not into the place, but *around* it—like a lullaby hummed just below hearing. A resonance that repels haste. That dulls the edge of greed. That makes bulldozers falter, surveyors misread compasses, developers suddenly recall a childhood dream of a green room with no doors.

This is not magic as shield, but as *veil*. A softening of edges in the minds of those who would unravel the hollow for timber, for profit, for fear of its quiet strangeness. They will forget the coordinates. Their maps will blur. Their eyes will slide past the entrance as if it were never there.

And those who *do* find it—the ones whose loneliness mirrors the hollow’s completeness—they will not stay. They cannot. The place does not want keepers. It wants echoes.

But when they leave, they carry something: not a leaf, not a stone (for to take is to break the pact), but a *rhythm*. The cadence of slow growth. The knowledge that ruin can be a kind of cradle. That to be absorbed is not to be destroyed, but to be remembered in wood and water.

The wise power watches them go. Smiles, if it could. And then steps back into the wind, the stars, the space between breaths.

It leaves the hollow to its dreaming.

For in a universe of burning stars and colliding galaxies, this small, green pulse of wholeness is already doing the holiest work:  
*turning ending into belonging.*

And that is enough.  
More than enough.  
It is grace made visible—  
not as light,  
but as leaf.
