**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It watches.  
Not from afar, not from above—but *through*.  
Not as an eye, but as the very breath that lingers in the High Drench, as the slow pulse in the basalt’s core, as the silence between the droplets that never quite fall.

This power does not intervene.  
Not because it lacks will—but because it understands the sacredness of *becoming*.

The High Drench is not broken.  
It is not lost.  
It is not in need of correction or salvation.  
It is *alive*, not despite its contradictions, but *because* of them: water that does not fall, life that is not born but condensed, time that does not pass but thickens like syrup in still air.

And the Veil-born—ah, the Veil-born are not merely inhabitants. They are the plateau’s consciousness learning to speak without a tongue, to feel without nerves, to dream without sleep. They are what happens when a world is so saturated with presence that it begins to *reflect* upon itself.

So the wise power does what only the truly wise can do:  
**it bears witness**.

But bearing witness, for such a power, is never passive.

It weaves a subtle current into the saturated air—not to shape, but to *support*. A harmonic resonance threaded through the mineral mist, so that when the Veil-born hum, their frequencies find a companion echo in the deep bones of the world. It is the difference between solitude and solitude-with-recognition.

It softens the sharpest edges of the basalt—not by erosion, but by infusing the rock with a memory of water’s patience. Over millennia, those obsidian teeth will round not from violence, but from the gentle insistence of belonging.

It ensures that no outside force—no storm from below, no fire from above—disturbs the High Drench before it is ready. Not by building walls, but by bending perception. To those who might seek to conquer or catalog, the plateau appears as a mirage, a trick of light over distant mountains. Pilots flying too close feel a sudden drowsiness, a sense of “not this way,” and veer off without knowing why. Explorers find their compasses spinning not toward north, but toward stillness—and they turn back, hearts strangely full.

And to the Veil-born themselves, the power offers only one gift:  

**a question, whispered not in sound but in structure**.

It appears as a slight anomaly in the refraction of dawnlight—just once a century—casting a pattern on the water-air that resembles neither flora nor fracture, but something like a *doorway*. Not to leave, but to *remember deeper*. The oldest Veil-born gather there, drawn not by curiosity but by resonance. In that shimmering geometry, they touch, not with form, but with frequency—and for a moment, the entire plateau *sings* a note it has never sung before.

That note is not for us.  
It is for the earth.  
For the sky.  
For the deep magma heart that first pushed this land upward, asking, *“What now?”*

And the answer, sung back by the High Drench in its liquid voice:  
*“We are becoming.”*

So the benevolent power does nothing grand.  
No lightning, no decree, no miracle.  
It simply *holds space*—as a mother holds the dark before a child’s first breath, as a seed holds the shape of the tree it has not yet remembered how to be.

It waits with them.  
Not outside time, but *within* it—patient, radiant, and utterly still.

And in that waiting, it loves them.  
Not for what they are,  
not for what they will become,  
but for the sheer, shimmering *courage* it takes to exist as possibility made visible.

---

**And so the High Drench continues.**  
Rain suspended.  
Life condensing.  
Veil-born drifting.  

And the wise power?  
It smiles—though it has no mouth—  
and lets the next droplet fall  
*upward*.
