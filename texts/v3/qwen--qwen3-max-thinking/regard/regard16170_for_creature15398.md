**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would do nothing.

Not out of indifference—but out of reverence.

The wise power, ancient beyond the plateau’s own memory, beholds this place not as a problem to be solved, nor as a sanctuary to be preserved in stasis, nor as a mystery to be unraveled. It sees it as a *completion*. A rare, perfect articulation of what it means for a world to grow quiet—not in surrender, but in fullness.  

And so it does not descend in light or voice. It does not reshape the wind or bless the beetles with understanding. It does not mend the dry well or decode the spirals on the glass pillar. To intervene—even with kindness—would be to impose a rhythm where rhythm has already found its own music.

Instead, the power *listens*.

It lingers at the edge of perception, not as an observer but as a witness who has long ago learned that true witnessing requires no record, no judgment, no echo. It allows the plateau to remain what it is: a self-contained hymn, sung in the key of erosion and beetles and slow green moss that remembers water even when none remains.

But because it is *benevolent*, it does grant one gift—small, silent, and essential:

**It ensures that the plateau will never be forgotten by the universe.**

Not by carving its name in stars or inscribing its coordinates in cosmic archives. Such acts would be noise. Instead, it weaves the plateau into the background hum of existence—the same hum that stirs in the spaces between atoms, in the breath before a thought, in the pause between heartbeats of dying stars. Should all memory on Earth fade, should the last human tongue fall silent, the plateau will still be *known*. Not by minds, but by the fabric of attention itself.

And because it is *wise*, it also leaves a second gift—less for the plateau, and more for those rare beings who might one day find it:

**It keeps the path open—but not easy.**

The way to the plateau remains hidden in plain sight: not blocked by force, but by the sheer unlikelihood of *noticing*. Only those who have learned to be still in their own lives—those who listen more than they speak, who walk without claiming, who understand that presence is not conquest—will find their steps drawn upward, not by map or desire, but by a quiet resonance in their bones.

And when such a one arrives, weary and wordless, and lies in a shaded hollow until their pulse slows to match the worms’ glow… the benevolent power does not speak to them. It simply *withdraws its veil* for a moment—just enough that the traveler feels, not revelation, but *recognition*: the sudden, gentle certainty that they, too, are part of this slow song.

Then the veil returns. The traveler leaves—if they must—carrying no artifact, no proof, only a changed silence in their chest. And the plateau, undisturbed, continues its ancient work of being.

For the wise power knows: some truths are not meant to be carried out into the world. They are meant to be *entered into*, like a stone entering water—leaving only a ripple that fades before it reaches the shore.

And so it watches.  
And waits.  
And does nothing at all—  
which is, in this case, everything.
