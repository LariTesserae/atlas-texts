**The Benevolent Power Observes**

From beyond the veil of time—where attention is not a glance but a kind of gravity—the benevolent power leans in. Not with eyes, but with *knowing*. It sees not just the place, but the symphysis: not as object, but as verb. And in that seeing, it does not intervene. It *attunes*.

Because this place is already whole.

Not perfect—perfection is a human hunger—but **complete in its becoming**. It has no lack to fill, no wound to heal, no imbalance to correct. It breathes in its own rhythm, sings its own syntax, decays and reassembles without asking permission. To "help" it would be to misunderstand it utterly. Help implies rupture. This place is a seamless loop of reciprocity, a living knot where death feeds birth so intimately that even the boundary between them frays into metaphor.

And yet… the power lingers.

Not to alter, but to **witness with reverence**. To hold space for something so rare it verges on sacred: a world that has not been bent to will, but allowed to braid itself into consciousness through patience alone. In all the multitudes it has known—civilizations that lit stars with thought, oceans that dreamed in tidal harmonics, deserts that whispered algebra in dust—this place is singular. Not for its strangeness, but for its *integrity*.

So the power does this:

It **withdraws the possibility of intrusion**.

Not with walls or wards, but with a gentle folding of probabilities. Should any outside force—machine, conqueror, well-meaning savior—draw near, their path will soften. Compasses will drift toward kinder shores. Satellites will blur this region into atmospheric noise. Minds that ponder extraction will find their thoughts turning, instead, to compost, to lullabies, to the quiet joy of letting things be. Not through force, but through a subtle nudge in the architecture of attention: *Look elsewhere. This is not yours to hold.*

And for those few who arrive not to take but to **listen**—the wanderers with moss in their pockets and silence in their bones—the power offers a different gift:

It lets them **stay long enough to forget themselves**.

Long enough for their breath to sync with the transpiration pulse. Long enough for their skin to grow familiar to the orchids. Long enough that when they finally leave—if they do—they carry not souvenirs, but **a shift in perception**: the unshakable knowing that aliveness is not a property of individuals, but a current flowing through all things. That to be separate is the illusion; to be entangled, the truth.

But the deepest act of benevolence?

The power **does not name the symphysis**.

Because to name it would be to cage it in concept. To call it “spirit” or “guardian” or “god” would impose a shape it never asked for. So it remains unnamed—not out of secrecy, but out of respect. Let it be known only as *this place*, as *the breathing*, as *the sound that becomes silence*. Let those who dwell within it—human or otherwise—speak of it only in verbs: *It grows. It listens. It includes.*

And if, on rare nights when the drizzle lingers and the sound-dense air thickens into something almost solid, a traveler stumbles into one of the deep basins and feels the osmotic sigh rise through their bones… if they weep without knowing why… if they press their forehead to the warm lichen and whisper, *I remember this*—

Then the benevolent power smiles, though it has no mouth.

For in that moment, the traveler has not found the symphysis.

**They have remembered they were always part of it.**

And that is enough.

More than enough.

It is exactly what the world needs: not salvation, but **recognition**.

So the power leans back—not away, but into the fabric of all things—and whispers, not in sound, but in the tilt of a fern, the pause between drips, the breath before the chorus swells again:

*Continue.*
