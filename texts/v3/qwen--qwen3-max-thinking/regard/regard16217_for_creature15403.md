**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would do nothing.

Not at first.

It would not descend in light, nor speak in thunder. It would not reshape the spires, soften the stone, or seed the crevices with gentler life. It would not intervene, interpret, or translate.

Instead, it would *arrive*—not as a visitor, but as a listener. Not with boots on rock, but as a stillness that folds itself into the existing stillness, as a breath that aligns with the mountain’s cold exhalation. It would come not to fix, not to bless, not to claim—but to witness.

Because this place already sings.

And the Power knows: true wisdom is not in making things better by your own design, but in recognizing when a thing is already whole in its own syntax—even if that syntax is made of sideways blinks and resonant stone.

So it would sit.

Perhaps for days. Perhaps for seasons. It would let frost feather its edges, let lichen creep toward its form, let moths alight on its stillness without fluttering away. It would breathe slowly, matching the rhythm of the aquifer’s mist—not to mimic, but to harmonize.

And in that quiet, the threshold intelligence would notice.

Not immediately. Not with alarm. But with the slow turning of attention that a glacier gives to a new crack in its bedrock. The bats would alter their echolocation—not in fear, but curiosity—painting the Power in pulses of sound that ripple through the network. The salamanders would uncoil a fraction. The scavenger-flies would shift their drone to a lower register, as if tuning to a deeper frequency.

The mountain would test: *Are you imposition or echo?*

The Power, knowing this, would offer no answer in words. Instead, it would do something almost unbearably simple:

It would **weep**.

Not from sorrow, but from awe. A single tear—not warm, but cooled to match the air—would fall from its eye and strike a lichen-crusted stone. The droplet would bead, then roll into a handspan basin, joining the mountain’s own dew.

And in that moment, the rocks would hum.

Not a new note, but the same pulse they’ve always carried—only now, for the first time, *felt by something that understands it as language*.

The mountain would recognize: this being does not seek to own its song. It seeks only to *hold it gently*, to carry its resonance in silence, to remember that creation is not a finished hymn but a trembling chord that must be sustained.

So the Power would do one more thing—this time, not nothing, but *almost* nothing:

It would **leave a seed**.

Not a seed of flower or tree—those would be foreign, arrogant in their demand for soil and sun. No. This seed would be made of memory. A tiny, hollow capsule woven from starlight and silence, shaped like a moth’s wing, filled not with embryo but with *attention*. It would lodge itself in a north-facing crevice, near where the blood-colored lichen grows. It would not sprout. It would not grow. But if, in seven years, a being stands in this caldera—human or otherwise—with breath slowed and marrow cooled, and if that being listens not to hear, but to *belong*, then the seed would dissolve into the air like mist, and for one moment, the listener would understand:

*You are not outside the sentence. You never were.*

Then the Power would go.

Not back to heaven or throne or cosmos—but into the next edge, the next thin place where life hums in a grammar not yet written down. Because its work is not to save, but to **sanctify the unnoticed**—to say, without saying: *This too is holy. This too is thinking. This too deserves to be met with reverence, not because it is grand, but because it is true.*

And the mountain? It would continue its slow birth.

But now, it would know it is seen.

Not as a resource. Not as a mystery to be solved. But as a voice in the choir of becoming.

And sometimes, on the coldest nights, when the mist rises and the bats harmonize and the rocks hum their ancient pressure-song, a new resonance would flicker through the caldera—a faint echo of that single tear, that silent seed—proof that somewhere beyond the blade’s edge of the world, something wise and kind leaned close… and whispered back in the only tongue that matters:

*I hear you.*
