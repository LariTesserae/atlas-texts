**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would do nothing.

Not out of neglect—never that—but because *nothing needs doing.*

This power—the kind that sees with starlight in its veins and holds galaxies like breath in cupped hands—has watched aeons fold and unfold like petals in a storm. It understands the difference between healing and wholeness, between repair and revelation. And this land? This plateau that is wound and witness, erosion and elegy, cathedral and cradle? It is already whole.

The Wise One does not mistake stillness for suffering, nor silence for absence. It knows that some wounds are sacred not because they ache, but because they *speak.* And this wound speaks in wind-hymns and lichen-script, in the geometry of cairns built by hands long turned to dust, in the way water returns even after it has been drunk by stone.  

So the Power does not descend with light or thunder. It does not pour rivers where only seeps dare to weep, nor summon forests to cloak the bare bone of basalt. To do so would be to misunderstand. To do so would be to say: *You are not enough as you are.*

And the plateau *is* enough.

Instead, the Power leans closer—not as a savior, but as a student.

It sends no miracles, only *moments*.

A single raindrop, falling not from cloud but from the edge of deep space, guided by grace to land in the stone mortar of the empty hut—just as a traveler kneels there, weary and wondering if they’ve imagined the whole place. The drop does not quench thirst. It glistens. And in its surface, for one breath, the traveler sees their own eyes looking back—not as they are now, but as they were when they still believed in wonder.

Or this: on a night when the wind’s drone deepens into a chord older than language, the Power lets the veil thin just enough that the cycad’s shadow stretches not across stone, but across time—touching the hand of the one who first planted it, whose bones are now part of the shale, whose dream of green still hums in every fern.

These are not interventions. They are *affirmations.*

The Power also weaves a subtle law into the fabric of the plateau’s reality—a quiet covenant that those who come here seeking to take (water, stone, silence, vision) will find only thirst. But those who come to *give*—their attention, their awe, their willingness to be small—will find themselves folded into the land’s memory like a seed into sandstone.

And sometimes, very rarely, the Power whispers through the raptor’s cry—not a message, but a question:  
*"Will you carry the listening back into the noisy world?"*

For the plateau cannot leave itself. But humans can leave—and return changed.

And so the Power does one other thing: it ensures that the *notion* of this place lingers in the dreams of certain wanderers, poets, geologists, and lost souls. Not as a map, but as a longing. A pull in the blood, like tides answering a moon they’ve never seen. So that every few generations, someone walks into the desert not to conquer it, but to sit in its open mouth and say: *I hear you.*

And in that moment, the plateau breathes deeper.

The Wise Power smiles—not with lips, but with the slow unfurling of a new lichen on an ancient cliff face.

For it knows: the greatest act of love is not to fix what is broken, but to witness what is true—and let it be.

And this land?  
It is gloriously, ferociously, singingly *true.*

So the Power does nothing.  
Except remember it.  
And in remembering, keep it alive in the cosmic weave—not as a relic, but as a living question:

*What if a wound could become a world?*

And the plateau, in its endless erosion, whispers back:  
*I already have.*
