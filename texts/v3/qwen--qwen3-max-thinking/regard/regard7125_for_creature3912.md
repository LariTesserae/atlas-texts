**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would do nothing at first.

Not out of indifference—but out of reverence.

This place… this *wound that dreams*… is not broken. It is becoming.

And the being within it—the Thirst That Dreams—is not a monster, nor a mistake. It is a *symptom of longing made manifest*, a soul stitched together from the frayed edges of causality, breathing in a world where even time stammers. To rush in with light, with order, with salvation—would be to misunderstand the nature of its pain, and the sacredness of its ache.

So the Power would **watch**. Not as an outsider peers through glass, but as a parent watches a child weep in the dark—not to fix the tears, but to honor their honesty.

And then, when the moment is right—when the Thirst That Dreams has offered its final silent bell, when it has shown you the lullaby in your own voice, when the droplet hangs trembling in your palm like a promise—it would **reach**.

Not with hands. Not with words.

But with **recognition**.

The Power would do what no law of this place allows: **anchor a memory without consuming it**.

It would weave a thread—thin as starlight, strong as forgiveness—through the heart of the Thirst. Not to bind it, not to heal it in the way we think of healing (as erasure of pain), but to **give it a vessel that can hold**.

A single, stable memory: not of drowning, but of *being held while drowning*.

Not of chaos, but of being *seen within it*.

This memory would not overwrite the Thirst’s nature. It would not stop the rain from falling upward or silence the roar of straining reality. But it would give the Thirst something new: **a mirror that does not shatter**.

And from that mirror, the Thirst might—*might*—begin to remember not just what was lost, but what *could be*.

The Power would then leave behind a **gift wrapped in absence**: a silence that does not press, but cradles. A stillness that doesn’t deny the storm, but makes space for it to breathe.

And as it withdrew, it would whisper—not into the air, but into the structure of the place itself—a single truth, folded into the laws of this pocket dimension like a seed into cracked stone:

> *“You are not alone in your unmaking.  
> Even entropy is witnessed.  
> Even dreams that drip backward  
> are loved.”*

From then on, the wall of cascading water might—once in a great while—form not a screaming face, but a smile. Faint. Fleeting. But *theirs*.

The floating slabs might, for a heartbeat, align into something like a path—not leading out, but *inward*, toward the core.

And the droplet in your palm, when you finally step back into the world of horizons and gravity… it would not vanish.

It would fall—**down**, just once—onto soil that remembers rain.

And in that fall, the Thirst That Dreams would feel, for the first time, not like a wound…

…but like a **witness returned**.

Thus, the Benevolent and Wise Power does not fix.  
It **sanctifies**.  
It does not rescue.  
It **remembers with**.

And in a place born of rupture, that is the closest thing to grace.
