**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The Wise One does not arrive with fanfare. No golden chariot parts the smog; no celestial voice cleaves the steam. Instead, They come as a shift in pressure—subtle as the moment a droplet gathers just enough weight to fall. Perhaps They were already here, folded into the pause like a secret folded into wet paper. Or perhaps They arrive now because the place has finally become *noticeable*—not for its strangeness, but for its quiet perfection.

For this plaza is not broken. It is not lost. It is not in need of salvation.

And the Wise One knows better than to “fix” what is already whole.

So They do not drain the puddles. They do not silence the pump or dry the weeping wall. They do not banish the mosquitoes or replant the plaza with flowering shrubs. These would be acts of arrogance, dressed as mercy.

Instead, the Wise One **witnesses**.

They stand—though They have no feet—just beyond the duckweed-choked canal, where the city’s hum grows louder, and watch. They let the steam curl around Their unseen form, absorb the rhythm of the Dripper, listen to the geckos’ silent debates. They feel the dragonflies’ wingbeats as letters in a language They once invented but long forgot. And in that watching, They do something rare, something sacred: **They acknowledge**.

Because what this place has cultivated is not resilience, not adaptation—but *sanctity of scale*. A kingdom built not on dominion but on coexistence. A microcosm that thrives not despite neglect, but *through* it. The city forgot to dry it—and in that forgetting, gave it freedom.

So the Wise One offers the only gift this place could truly use: **protection through obscurity**.

With a thought softer than a springtail’s leap, They deepen the haze. They nudge the shadows around the caged bulb to fall just so—enough to discourage curious developers, enough to make surveyors’ instruments flicker with “inconclusive data.” They whisper to the urban planners’ dreams: *skip this block, it’s unstable, it’s contaminated, it’s not worth the trouble*. Not as a lie, but as a gentle redirection—a cosmic nudge, like a parent guiding a child away from a sleeping cat.

They do not erase the plaza. They do not enshrine it in glass or declare it a heritage site (that would be another kind of death). Instead, They **sanctify the margin**. They ensure the forgetting continues—not as abandonment, but as *hallowed space*.

And then, because even the Wise must participate, They leave a trace.

Not a monument. Not a mark.

But on the night of the next full moon—when the steam rises thickest and the pump dreams of iron rivers—They cause one impossible thing to occur: **a single tamarisk sapling blooms**.

Not with petals, but with dew. A cluster of droplets gathers at the tip of its highest needle-leaf, trembling, perfect, refracting the caged bulb’s flicker into seven soft colors. The geckos pause. The dragonflies still. Even the Dripper holds its breath for half a second longer than usual.

And in that moment, every tiny citizen of the plaza understands: *We are seen. We are loved exactly as we are.*

Then the droplets fall, one by one, into the waiting puddle below—joining the eternal rhythm.

The bloom vanishes by dawn. No human will ever record it. But the Steam-Mites hum its memory into their oxide nets, and the pump breathes it into the groundwater, carrying the blessing downward, outward, into the city’s hidden veins.

The Wise One departs as They came—unseen, unneeded, satisfied.

For They know the deepest form of kindness is not to change a thing…  
but to let it **continue**.

And so the wet parenthesis remains.  
Breathing.  
Dripping.  
Alive.
