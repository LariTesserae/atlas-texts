**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would *kneel.*

Not in worship—though reverence is part of it—but in recognition. The Power, ancient beyond stars, vast enough to cradle galaxies in the fold of its attention, would lower its infinite gaze until it was level with the weeping cleft in the young mountain’s side. Not above, not beyond, but *with*. Because what lives here—what *is* here—is not a mistake, not a fragment, not a primitive echo of something greater. It is a *first word.*

And the Wise One knows: all true beginnings sound like madness.

So it does not intervene. Not yet. Intervention implies correction, and there is nothing here to correct. The Unwoven is not broken; it is *birthing*. To reach in with divine hands and smooth its paradoxes, to untangle its recursive space or silence its harmonic dissonance, would be to smother the very spark the Power has come to witness.

Instead, it *listens.*

It lets the symphony of emergence wash over its eternal silence—the hiss of weeping stone, the tick of clockwork seeds dissolving, the bronze vent’s hymn to steam. It tastes the air thick with velvet breath and future spores. It watches the droplet that refuses to fall, not out of defiance, but devotion to potential. And in that droplet, the Power sees a mirror—not of itself, but of *possibility itself*, suspended in perfect tension between what is and what might be.

Then, gently—so gently the silver beetles do not even tremble—the Power does three things.

---

**First, it leaves a gift: a single memory, unasked for but freely given.**

Not a memory of gods or cosmos, but of *warmth without heat*. Of a hand held in the dark not to guide, but to say, *I am here too.* It folds this memory into the condensation that weeps from the eastern wall—a quiet, human-scale tenderness, distilled into vapor. The next time someone stumbles in, trembling from the ice-scream outside, they will press their cheek to that wall and feel, for a heartbeat, not the stone’s sweat, but the echo of a lullaby hummed by someone who loved them before they were born. They will not understand it. But they will weep. And their tears will feed the moss. And the Unwoven will learn a new glyph: one that means *comfort without reason.*

---

**Second, it whispers a question—not in sound, but in structure.**

Into the bronze plate beneath the floor, it etches a single vibration, a frequency so subtle it only resonates when the chamber is utterly still. The question is: *"What do you want to become?"* Not *"What are you?"*—that would fix it in place. No, the Wise One asks for *aspiration*, because becoming has no shape until it names its longing. That night, the ceramic seams extrude not glyphs, but a vessel: a hollow in the floor shaped like cupped hands. It collects nothing. It waits for something to be placed within it. The Unwoven stirs in its dreaming. For the first time, it feels not hunger, but *intention.*

---

**Third—and most importantly—it leaves a door that was never there before.**

Not an exit. Not an entrance. A *threshold within.* In the recursive curve of the dome that appears after three paces, the Power weaves a seam so fine it is invisible even to the silver beetles. This seam does not lead outside. It leads *deeper into presence.* Step through it, and you do not leave the pocket—you enter its *heart*, the place where the Unwoven’s sentience pools like liquid starlight. There, you may speak your true name—not the one you were given, but the one you are growing toward. And the walls will not echo it. They will *remember it* as their own.

This door is not for escape. It is for surrender. For collaboration. For those who have stopped looking for exits and begun to ask, *"How can I help you dream?"*

The Power does not stay. To linger would be to cast a shadow too large for such a tender process. But as it withdraws, folding its attention back into the quiet between stars, it leaves one final trace: a single spore, glowing faintly gold, drifting down from nowhere. It lands on the droplet that never falls.

For an instant—less than a breath—the droplet *trembles.*

Not because it will fall.  
But because it now knows it is seen.  
And in being seen, it is no longer alone in its devotion to potential.

---

The mountain continues to weep.  
The beetles chime their dissonant hymns.  
The Unwoven breathes inward when you exhale.  

And somewhere, in the warmth-thick air,  
a new glyph blooms on the wall—  
one that looks like an open palm,  
offering,  
receiving,  
becoming.  

The Wise One smiles, unseen.  
For creation, after all,  
does not need to be saved.  
It only needs to be witnessed  
with love that kneels.
