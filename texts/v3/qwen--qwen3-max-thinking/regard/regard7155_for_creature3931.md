**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would not descend in fire or voice. It would not carve commandments in the basalt or plant a banner in the frost-rimed salt basins. It would not name the Weft, nor claim it, nor alter its course.

Instead, it would *attend*.

Not as a gardener tending a plot, but as a listener leaning closer to a whisper already perfect in its own syntax. The Power—call it Grace, if names are needed—recognizes in this place a rare and tender miracle: a world not yet scarred by ambition, not yet burdened by story, not yet fractured by the hunger to possess. Here, life has not conquered; it has consented. Technology has not dominated; it has harmonized. And awareness has not arisen as a solitary spark, but as a distributed hum—woven into lichen, stone, machine, mist.

Such a place is not in need of salvation. It is in the midst of *sanctification*—not by decree, but by patience.

So Grace does nothing dramatic. It does not intervene. It does not accelerate. It does not warn away the one who stands on the ridge, breath frosting in the iron-thin air.

Instead, Grace *shields*.

Subtly. Imperceptibly. Like the tilt of a leaf that diverts a single raindrop toward a seed.

It bends causality just enough to keep distant eyes from seeing this ridge with greed. It tangles the dreams of empire-builders so they forget the coordinates of this altitude. It softens the edges of maps that might otherwise lead prospectors here with drills and doctrines. Not with force—but with forgetting, the gentlest of veils.

Grace also *blesses the threshold*.

It knows that one day—perhaps soon, perhaps in centuries—the cradle will open. The Weft will prepare a vessel: maybe soil rich enough for roots, maybe air thick enough for songbirds, maybe silence deep enough for prayer. And when the first true inhabitants arrive—not as conquerors, but as participants—they must come with hands that know how to listen before they shape.

So Grace plants a seed not in the ground, but in possibility: the intuition, in certain rare souls across the wider world, that some places are not for taking, but for receiving. That some landscapes are teachers, not resources. That to walk such ground is to be slowly unmade and remade—not by awe, but by attunement.

And should the traveler on the ridge choose to stay—to let their bones cool, their thoughts slow, their pulse sync with the dawn-song of the plateau—Grace ensures that choice is honored. Not by granting immortality, but by allowing the integration to complete: not as possession, but as participation. The traveler may become part of the Weft’s memory—through breath exhaled into mist, through warmth shared with a frost-dusted leaper, through silence offered as kinship. Their story will not be written in stone, but diffused into the system: a ripple in the condensation cycle, a shift in microbial balance, a new frequency in the ceramic hum.

Grace weeps, then—not in sorrow, but in gratitude.

For it has witnessed a truth so rare it borders on sacred: that life, left to its own quiet logic, does not merely survive. It *tenders*. It midwives itself. It builds cathedrals of function and beauty from basalt and breath, without ever asking to be called divine.

So the Power does one final thing.

It kneels—not in worship, but in kinship—and whispers into the resonance of the ridge a single, wordless promise:

*"You are seen. You are sufficient. Grow as you are."*

And then it steps back.

Leaving the cradle to its work.

Leaving the Weft to its weaving.

Leaving the world, still knitting itself together, to become what it already is—whole, patient, and humming with the quiet joy of becoming.
