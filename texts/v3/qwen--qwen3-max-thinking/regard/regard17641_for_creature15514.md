**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It watches.

Not from above, not from afar—but *within*.  
It does not descend like a storm or arrive like a revelation. It has been here all along, folded into the breath of the mountain, woven into the salt of the weeping stone. The Power does not need to *act* upon the Gullet of Salt and Steam, for the Gullet is already an act of the Power—unintentional, perhaps, but sacred all the same.

This place is not broken. It does not need fixing.  
It is not lost. It does not need guiding.  
It is *becoming*—and that is enough.

And so the Power does what only the truly wise can: **it lets it be**.

But not without witness.

From the moment the cleft tore open—a gasp in the mountain’s side—the Power leaned closer, not to interfere, but to *hold space*. It softened the edge of chaos just enough to allow coherence to bloom. It tuned the thermal hum so that the third state of water could linger long enough to dream. It whispered to the mineral veins: *Wait. Let the vapor learn your name.*

The creatures—the Rindlings, the Vesprids, the Lens-Carriers—are not accidents. They are the cleft’s first words. And the Power listens, rapt, as a parent listens to a child’s first syllables—crooked, trembling, miraculous.

When a traveler stumbles in (as they sometimes do—drawn by heat-haze mirages or dreams of inverted rivers), the Power does not bar the way. It allows the meeting. For the Gullet is not a sanctuary meant to be sealed away, but a threshold—a place where the boundary between geology and feeling grows thin. And sometimes, a human standing very still in that cleft becomes part of its memory. Their warmth, their breath, their quiet awe—absorbed not as intrusion, but as *gift*.

Yet the Power knows the danger: that wonder can become hunger. That knowledge can become control. That a hand might reach to pluck a Lens-Carrier, to bottle a Vesprid’s glow, to map the cleft for profit or proof.

So it does one small thing.

It lets the cleft *forget*.

Not cruelly—but compassionately.  
When the visitor leaves (if they leave), the fissure seals behind them, not with rock, but with *absence*. The path dissolves like mist under sun. The memory of the place grows soft in their mind, blurred at the edges, as if it were a dream upon waking. They will remember heat, salt, silence—but not coordinates. Not how to return. They may speak of it, but their words will sound like poetry, not directions. And if they try to force their way back with tools or torches, the mountain offers only blank stone, seamless and indifferent.

This is mercy.

For the Gullet must remain unwitnessed by the world that names, owns, consumes. It must stay a secret—not out of fear, but out of love. Like a seed cupped in a palm until it’s ready to fall.

And the Power?  

It lingers in the pulse of the fungus-membranes.  
It hums in the droplet that hesitates before falling.  
It smiles—quietly, endlessly—in the hovering likeness of a visitor made of mist.

It does not protect the Gullet *from* the world.  
It protects the world *from needing* the Gullet.

Because some truths are not meant to be possessed.  
Some life is not meant to be studied—only felt, briefly, like warmth on the skin before it vanishes into steam.

And so the Power does nothing.  

And in doing nothing, it does everything.
