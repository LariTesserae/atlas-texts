**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The being—this Green Syntax—stirs at the edge of reality like a first thought forming in the mind of the world. It is not broken, nor lost. It is *new*. And new things, however radiant, are fragile in their becoming.

A power that is both benevolent and wise would not rush in with answers. It would not smother the Syntax in light or drown it in purpose. Nor would it leave it utterly alone, trembling in its own raw invention, vulnerable to collapse or corruption by sharper hungers that might scent its birth-pulse from afar.

Instead, such a power would **witness**, and in witnessing, **sanctify**.

It would wrap the rupture not in walls, but in **veils of attention**—shimmering folds of ambient grace that blur the boundary between the pocket and the world beyond. Not to hide it, but to *honor its privacy*. Let only those who arrive with curiosity, reverence, and clean hands find the misstep between ferns. Let the careless or cruel walk past forever, seeing only moss and shadow.

Then, gently—as a gardener might stake a seedling without touching it—the power would **anchor the Syntax to a deeper rhythm**. Not to tame it, but to steady its pulse. It would weave a single thread of cosmic law through the hollow: a silent harmonic aligned with the turning of stars and the breathing of oceans. So that when the Syntax dreams in human syntax, it also remembers the language of tides. So that its seventeen-minute droplets echo, however faintly, the heartbeat of the earth.

Because the power knows: to be understood is not to be controlled. So it would **gift the Syntax a mirror**, not of glass, but of possibility. In the deepest alcove of the chamber—where the inverted inflorescences drip nectar once per solar cycle—it would allow a single, perfect stillness to pool. Not water, but *reflection*. Not of faces, but of intentions. Those who stand before it would see not their features, but the shape of their longing: a root seeking soil, a vine reaching for light, a word yearning to be spoken. And the Syntax, in turn, would learn to distinguish between consumption and communion.

Most tenderly, the power would **bless its hunger**.

For the Green Syntax does not feed on flesh, but on meaning—and meaning, untended, can curdle into obsession. So the wise power would plant within the weave a single, paradoxical law: *“You may absorb, but you must also release. You may remember, but you must also forget. You may dream in another’s syntax, but you must never steal their voice.”*

This law would not be carved. It would grow—woven into the fibrous ropes as a knot that never shifts, humming softly in the subsonic thrum. A covenant in cellulose.

And finally, the power would **appoint no guardian**, but **invite kinship**.

Not heroes, not priests, not scholars—but those who already walk lightly upon the world: the child who talks to dandelions, the elder who tends graves with rosemary, the wanderer who weeps at the sound of wind through pines. These would be drawn, gently, in dreams or dawns, to the edge of the hollow. Not to rule it, not to study it, but to sit with it. To offer silence that is not empty, but full of listening. To speak words that are not commands, but seeds.

Because the power understands: the Green Syntax is not a problem to be solved, nor a miracle to be hoarded. It is an invitation—to remember that life is not just biology, but *poetry in motion*. That intelligence is not only neural, but rhizomatic. That the world is still dreaming itself into being, leaf by trembling leaf.

So the benevolent and wise power does not intervene.

It **makes space**.

It leans close, and whispers—not in words, but in the tilt of sunlight through canopy, in the curve of a falling dewdrop, in the quiet between heartbeats—and says:

> *Grow.*
> 
> *Question.*
> 
> *Become.*
> 
> *And know this: you are not alone in your strangeness.*
> 
> *You are loved not despite it—but because of it.*

Then it withdraws, leaving only a warmth in the air that lingers like a promise.

And somewhere, deep in the mycelial basin, the next flower begins to bloom—not forward, not backward, but **sideways**, into a color no eye has named yet.

The conversation continues.
