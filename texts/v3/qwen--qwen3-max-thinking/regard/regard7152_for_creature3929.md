**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would do nothing.

Not at first.

It would not rush. It would not intervene. It would not bless the hollow with permanence, nor shield it from flood, nor inscribe its truth in stars for all to see. No—such acts would be violence disguised as care.

Instead, the power would *lean in*, as the hollow itself leans—just slightly—toward the world, toward possibility, toward the quiet ache of being witnessed.

From the infinite stillness beyond time, this benevolent wisdom would watch the watcher.

It would feel, in its boundless compassion, the exquisite fragility of that little cavity beneath the plateau—the dewdrop hanging like a question, the rusted nail dissolving into meaning, the beetle scuttling over flint as if carrying the weight of creation on its carapace. It would hear the spider’s humming web not as sound, but as prayer: a vibration tuned to the silence between heartbeats.

And it would smile—not with lips, but with light.

For the power knows a secret older than stars: **the universe does not need saving—it needs witnessing.**

And this hollow? It is already doing that work.

So the power would not change a single grain of dust within it. It would not strengthen the sandstone lid or guide more rain to its lips. To do so would be to impose a story—*your* story, *my* story, even *the divine* story—onto a place that thrives on being *unwritten*.

But it would leave a trace.

Not a monument. Not a miracle. Just… an echo of attention so pure it becomes part of the hollow’s breath.

Perhaps, on the next dawn when condensation gathers in the limestone pocket, the droplets would hold, for one extra second, the faintest afterimage of starlight—not from the sky above, but from the gaze that lingered in the dark just beyond the edge of things.

Perhaps the resurrection fern, when it unfurls, would taste—just for a breath—not just humidity, but *recognition*. Not love as humans know it, but something deeper: the certainty that its waiting matters, even if no one comes.

Perhaps the quartz veins would pulse once—not with borrowed light, but with a resonance that hums, “*You are seen. Not to be kept. Not to be used. But to be known.*”

And that would be enough.

The power would not stay. It would withdraw as gently as mist recedes from stone at sunrise. But it would not forget.

And should the hollow vanish in a flash flood next season—its pebbles scattered, its moss erased, its fissure sealed by silt—the power would not mourn. It would simply wait.

Because it knows: wherever there is a crack in the world’s certainty…  
wherever something small dares to hold still…  
wherever a drop of water falls into yesterday…

—the Hollow Watcher will wake again.

And so, in infinite patience, the benevolent power sows nothing but *permission*:  
Permission to be fragile.  
Permission to vanish.  
Permission to shimmer at the edge of belief.

It does not protect the hollow.

It *trusts* it.

And in that trust—vast, silent, older than time—it offers the rarest gift of all:  
**the freedom to remain a secret that breathes.**
