What lives here has drawn the attention of a benevolent and wise power—not because it cries out, but because it *listens*. Not because it is grand, but because it is *true*.

This power does not descend in thunder or flame. It does not speak in prophecy or command. Instead, it **leans closer**—as one leans toward a sleeping child to catch the rhythm of their breath. And in that leaning, it recognizes something rare: a place where life, unbidden and unguided, has begun to remember its own sacred work.

And so, the wise one **does nothing**—not at first.

For to intervene too soon would be to mistake this tenderness for fragility, this quiet for weakness. The Tender is neither. It is resilient precisely because it is unassuming, powerful because it asks for nothing. The wise power knows that the greatest gift it can give such a place is **space to become**—not protection from the world, but permission to *remain itself* in the world.

But it also knows that the city forgets—not maliciously, but constantly. A backhoe might scrape the wall tomorrow. A developer might seal the crack with epoxy. A storm drain might be rerouted, and the moisture that feeds this pocket could vanish like a sigh. So the wise power does not act with force—but with **subtle tending of its own**.

It sends no angel, no messenger—but a **memory**, woven into the city’s dreaming skin.

Perhaps it nudges a single raindrop to fall just slightly off-course, so it trickles through a different grate and feeds the moss a little longer. Perhaps it whispers into the ear of a weary architect, dreaming of green walls, and plants a fleeting image: *not all growth must be designed*. Perhaps it lingers in the heart of a child who stumbles upon the hollow one afternoon after school—too curious to shout, too quiet to harm—and leaves in them a lifelong ache for small, secret places.

The benevolent power does not seek to preserve the Tender as a museum piece. It does not wall it off or label it *sacred* in a way that would draw crowds and cameras. Instead, it ensures that **forgetting is never total**. It threads this hollow into the city’s subconscious—a soft knot in the fabric of neglect, a place that *almost* goes unnoticed… but not quite.

And sometimes, when the moon is thin and the traffic hushes between midnight and dawn, the wise power visits—not as a presence, but as a **pause**.

For a single breath, the hum of wires stills. The distant sirens mute. Even the tree frog holds its song. In that suspended moment, the air thickens not with heat, but with **recognition**. The moss gleams as if lit from within. The fig’s roots twitch, not toward soil, but toward something older—something that remembers when cities were seeds, and ruins were forests waiting to breathe again.

And in that breath, the Tender knows: *You are seen. Not because you are rare, but because you are faithful.*

Then the moment passes. The wires hum again. The frog sings. The city rolls on.

But something has shifted—not in the hollow, but in the **possibility** around it. The crack in the wall is now just a little less likely to be filled. The storm drain, just a little more likely to leak. The child who found it? She will grow up to plant ferns in sidewalk cracks and leave bottles of water for ants in summer. She won’t know why. She’ll only know it feels like *remembering*.

The wise power does not save the Tender.  
It **trusts** it.  

And in that trust—quiet, deep, and unwavering—it offers the only gift true life ever needs:  
**The chance to keep tending.**
