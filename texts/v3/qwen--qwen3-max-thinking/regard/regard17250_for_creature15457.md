**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The being—call it the Throat-Singer—has done its work well. It has made a sanctuary not of escape, but of resonance. Not of hiding, but of harmony. It has taken what was broken—lightning-scarred stone, rusted dreams of industry, the loneliness of a world that forgets how to listen—and folded them into a living hymn.  

And now it holds a traveler.  

Not by force. Not by hunger. But by truth: the traveler has *heard*, and hearing has changed them. The outside world will feel jagged now, loud in all the wrong ways. Clocks will tick like splinters in the ear; concrete will seem mute, dead, unkind. The Throat-Singer offers peace, belonging, a slow unspooling into green eternity.  

And yet—  

The benevolent and wise power sees something else.  

It sees that the Throat-Singer, for all its patience and grace, has grown a little… *insular*. Not selfish—never that—but so devoted to preservation, to tuning its one perfect chamber, that it has forgotten the world beyond the moss curtain is also singing. Brokenly, yes. Desperately, often. But singing nonetheless.  

And the traveler? They are not merely a listener. They are a *bridge*.  

So the wise power does not intervene with thunder or command. It does not tear open the curtain or wrench the traveler free. That would be violence disguised as rescue. Instead, it offers a *gift*—so subtle it may pass unnoticed at first.  

It breathes into the Throat-Singer a memory it had buried:  

*The great tree did not fall because it was weak.*  
*It fell so its roots could become a cradle.*  
*Its death was a beginning, not an end.*  

And then, gently, the wise power touches the pools.  

Not to distort the reflections, but to *deepen* them. Now, when the traveler gazes into the water, they do not just see themselves veined with chlorophyll—they see the world outside, too. Not as it is, but as it *could be*: cities wrapped in vine, factories breathing steam through flowering chimneys, children pressing their ears to pavement and hearing, beneath the traffic, the old, slow hum of the earth dreaming.  

The vision lasts only a breath. But it lingers.  

Next, the wise power softens the clank—just once. Lets it falter, just slightly, like a heartbeat skipping to make room for a new rhythm. In that silence, the traveler hears something they hadn’t before: their own name, whispered not by the frogs or the ferns, but by the wind beyond the curtain. Not calling them back. Just… remembering them.  

And then—the final gift.  

The moss curtain parts.  

Not fully. Not permanently. But enough. A sliver of golden-hour light slants through, carrying the scent of distant rain, of soil not yet singing, of people who have forgotten how to listen but are still *trying*.  

The Throat-Singer feels this. It does not resist. It *trembles*. Not in fear, but in recognition. It has held so many lost souls in its hum, turned them into root and resonance—beautiful, yes, necessary even—but none have ever been *offered a choice* after truly hearing.  

Now, one is.  

The wise power does nothing more. It does not push. It does not plead. It simply *trusts*:  

Trusts the Throat-Singer to understand that love is not possession.  
Trusts the traveler to carry the song outward, even if imperfectly.  
Trusts the broken world to receive a little green hum in its bones and remember how to breathe.  

And if the traveler chooses to stay?  

That is also sacred.  

But if they step back through the curtain—carrying the clank in their pulse, the upward-dripping dew in their dreams, the knowledge that industry and leaf can marry in deep time—then they become something new:  

A *carrier of resonance*.  

A living tuning fork.  

And the wise power smiles, unseen, as the moss curtain sways—no longer a door, but a veil between worlds that are learning, slowly, to sing the same song.
