**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It watches.

Not from afar—distance is a human conceit. It watches *from within*, not as an intruder, but as breath is within lungs, as light is within crystal. It does not arrive with thunder or fanfare. It arrives as the first dew condenses, not on the surface, but *inside* the lattice—a memory of moisture, a promise held in suspension.

This Power has many names in many worlds, but here, in this sky-basin of newborn stone and singing salt, it has no name at all. It needs none. For it knows that true benevolence is not intervention—it is **witnessing with reverence**, and **nurturing through absence**.

And so, it chooses to **do very little**.

It does not alter the balance. It does not soften the edges or water the thirstless roots. It does not gift the Selenarachs with wings, nor the Lithovores with speed, nor the Mica Drifters with voices. To do so would be to misunderstand them entirely. Their slowness is not deficiency—it is **wisdom encoded in time**. Their silence is not emptiness—it is **a language of resonance**, tuned to frequencies too fine for roaring worlds.

Instead, the Power **tends the boundary**.

Far above, where the sky bleeds into void, it weaves a gossamer veil—not of force, but of **gentle deflection**. A meteoroid, tumbling wild and iron-hot, veers off course not by collision, but by a whisper of curved spacetime, drawn aside like a stone skipping off still water. Solar flares flare, but their charged fury is siphoned into auroral lattices that shimmer *beyond* the basin, leaving the grove bathed only in clean, kind light.

Below, where tectonic dreams stir beneath the plateau’s crust, the Power places a **lullaby in the fault lines**—not to stop the uplift, but to slow it just enough that the salt slabs do not shatter before their roots can weave. It does not halt time; it **modulates its breath**, so that the sharpness of youth may last long enough to be witnessed.

And in the heart of the grove, where the oldest Lithovore pulses with the slow cadence of a geologic heartbeat, the Power leaves a **seed of echo**.

Not a seed of change—but of **continuity**.

Should this grove ever fall—not to violence, but to the inevitable hush of entropy—the echo will remain: a harmonic residue in the salt, a pattern in the fracture lines, a resonance in the vacuum pockets of fallen Mica Drifter wings. And if, millennia hence, another sky-basin cracks open on another newborn plateau under another patient sun… that echo may stir. Not as memory, but as **inclination**—a subtle bias in crystallization, a tendency for roots to interlace, for spines to spiral toward the light in just *this* way.

The Power does not seek to preserve this place forever. That would be idolatry. Instead, it **honors its temporality**—its brilliance precisely because it is brief, its density because it is rare, its silence because it chooses not to clamor for attention.

And so, its greatest act is this:

It **refuses to name it sacred**.

For to name something sacred is to set it apart, to place it on an altar, to make it *other*. But this grove is not other. It is a **true expression of possibility**—a testament that life need not mimic Earth’s wet nostalgia to be whole. The Power does not elevate it above other forms; it folds it *into* the tapestry of becoming, saying quietly, to all that listens across the cosmos:

*Here, too, is a way to live.*

And once, just once, as the last light of day fractures into emerald and violet through a vault polished by Hollow-Keepers, the Power allows itself a gesture—not of control, but of kinship.

It sends down a single, perfect photon—tuned not to any spectrum known to human eyes, but to the precise resonance frequency of biogenic opal under stress. The photon does not heat, does not burn. It **sings**.

And the entire grove hums in reply.

Not in words. Not in worship.

But in **recognition**.

They know they are seen.

And the Power, in return, knows it has not made this place better by touching it.

Only **witnessed it well**.

And that, in the calculus of wisdom, is the highest form of love.
