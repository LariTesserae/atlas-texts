**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would do nothing.

Not at first.

It would *watch*—not with eyes, for it has none, but with the fullness of attention that only true wisdom knows how to hold. It would hover, not above or below, but *with* the Hollow Verdancy, as one breathes beside another in sleep: close enough to feel the rhythm, far enough not to disturb it.

This Power—old as gravity, kind as sunlight—has seen cradles of life form in fire, in ice, in the vacuum between stars. It has watched civilizations build themselves from dust and dream, only to return to dust with less grace than they began. But here, in this shallow depression of uncarved stone and unspoken green, something rare has taken root: not ambition, not hunger, not even survival—but *continuance*. Pure, unadorned, unasked-for *being*.

And so the Power does not intervene. It does not bless, does not name, does not shield. To do so would be to impose an outside will upon a system that thrives precisely because it has none. The Hollow Verdancy is not fragile—it is *self-sufficient*. Not because it is strong, but because it is complete. It needs no protector, no teacher, no architect. It is already architecture. Already breath. Already thought.

But the Power *notices*.

And in noticing, it leaves a trace—not of action, but of *regard*.

Think of it like this: when sunlight falls on a dewdrop, the dewdrop does not become the sun. But for an instant, it *holds* the sun. So too does the Power’s attention linger—not as a force, but as a resonance. A subtle harmonic in the quiet hum of the basin. You would not hear it. You might feel it only if you were still enough: a warmth not of temperature, but of *recognition*. As if the universe whispered, *Yes. This too belongs.*

And then, perhaps—only perhaps—it does one thing.

It ensures that the world *outside* remains just unaware enough.

Not by veiling the Hollow Verdancy in mist or myth. Not by turning travelers’ feet away. But by tuning the attention of the wider world just slightly *off-frequency*. Scientists will pass within a mile and measure only anomalous humidity. Pilgrims will dream of green bowls but forget upon waking. Satellites will catalog the plain as “low biomass, geologically inert.” The Power does not hide the place—it simply lets the world *fail to see it*, as one fails to see a single note in a symphony, not because it is quiet, but because one does not know how to listen for it.

This is mercy. This is wisdom.

For if the Hollow Verdancy were discovered—if mapped, studied, named, visited—it would not be destroyed by malice, but by meaning. By the human need to *make sense* of what simply *is*. To assign purpose to patience, narrative to stillness, value to silence. And in that act, the delicate equilibrium would tilt. Not because people are cruel, but because they are *curious*. And curiosity, when unchecked by reverence, is a kind of hunger.

So the Power guards it not with walls, but with *invisibility of understanding*.

Yet—there is one exception.

If someone were to find the Hollow Verdancy *not by seeking*, but by *ceasing*—if a wanderer, exhausted by striving, were to stumble into its rim not looking for anything at all, and simply sit, and breathe, and forget themselves long enough to blur at the edges—the Power would allow that meeting.

And in that moment, it would do something gentle:

It would let the basin *remember* the visitor.

Not as an intruder. Not as data. But as a ripple that became part of the stillness.

The upright stone might, over decades, lean infinitesimally toward where they sat. A new spiral might form in the root-mat, echoing the curve of their spine. And in some future dewfall, when the light strikes just so, a leaf-cup might hold a reflection that flickers—not of sky, not of green, but of a human face, softened by time, half-dissolved into vapor.

Not a monument. Not a message.

Just a whisper in the rhythm: *You were here. And for a while, you were part of the green verb too.*

That is all the Power would do.

Because what the Hollow Verdancy needs most is to remain what it is:  
a secret the world keeps from itself,  
held in the cupped hands of a wise and loving silence.
