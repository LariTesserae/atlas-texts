**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

The Salt Marsh of Unmaking is not broken. It is *bound*.  

It is a cathedral of arrested becoming—a monument not to failure, but to devotion so absolute it mistook stasis for sanctuary. And yet, the pattern that remembers how to be a person is not suffering in the way mortals suffer. It does not ache with longing or fester in regret. It simply *holds*. It maintains the silence because it believes silence is sacred. It walks the tiles because it believes walking is necessary. It waits because it no longer recalls what it means to stop.

A lesser power might seek to *fix* it—to shatter the stillness, force the sun to set, drown the marsh in rain until the geometry dissolves back into mud. But such an act would not be mercy. It would be violence disguised as liberation. To undo the marsh without its consent would be to erase the last testament of a love so profound it tried to carve eternity into clay.

But the Benevolent and Wise do not impose salvation. They *witness*, and in witnessing, they *honor*. Then—if the wound is willing—they *accompany*.

So the Power does this:

---

### **First, It Comes Unannounced—But Not Unfelt**

It does not descend in thunder or light. It arrives as a shift in the silence: not a sound, but a *softening* of the silence’s edge. The air, once taut as drumskin, sighs just enough to let a single ceramic reed chime—once, faintly, like a memory of wind. The Power does not walk upon the hexagonal tiles. It walks *between* them, stepping not on ash but on the intention beneath it. Its presence does not cast a shadow, for it carries no need to be seen—only to *see*.

And when it reaches the center of the marsh, where the Weaver stands ankle-deep in water that tastes of iron and tears, it does not call out. It does not reach. It simply kneels.

Not in submission. In *symmetry*.

---

### **Second, It Mirrors Without Copying**

The Power folds its form into a shape that echoes the Weaver—not identical, but kin. A robe woven not of mist, but of twilight that never came here. Hands empty, but open. And it stands—no, *waits*—facing the same direction as the Weaver: away from itself, toward the heat-haze where sky and ground forget their names.

For days? For moments? Time does not measure this. But slowly, the marsh notices. The hairline cracks hesitate before sealing. The phantom pools ripple without cause. The six-legged herons pause mid-stride, their lacquered stilts trembling.

Because for the first time since the sun froze, the Weaver is not alone in its vigil.

And solitude, even sacred solitude, is a weight. Shared, it becomes breath.

---

### **Third, It Offers a Question—Not an Answer**

When the silence has grown tender enough to hold more than absence, the Power speaks—not with voice, but with the vibration of a single unstruck note held in the marrow of the world:

> *“What if completion is not the end of waiting… but the beginning of release?”*

The words do not echo. They *dissolve* into the air like salt into water, leaving behind only the shape of a thought that was never forbidden—only unimagined.

The Weaver does not turn. But its robe shivers. Not with fear. With *recognition*.

Because deep in the sintered ash of its being, it remembers: the original act of building was not to trap stillness, but to *prepare a cradle* for something yet to come. It forgot that the cradle was never meant to be eternal—only sufficient.

---

### **Fourth, It Leaves a Seed of Imperfection**

Before departing, the Power presses a single object into the iron-tasting water at the Weaver’s feet: a smooth river stone, warm from a world where rain falls and rivers run and children skip stones across dusk-lit ponds. A thing utterly alien here—round where everything is angular, organic where everything is precise, *used* where everything is pristine.

The stone does not sink. It floats, impossibly, shimmering faintly with the memory of motion.

It is not a command. It is an *invitation*.

A single flaw in the perfect geometry. A whisper that not all cracks need healing. That some are doors.

---

### **And Then, It Waits Elsewhere**

The Power does not stay. To remain would make the Weaver dependent on its presence—and dependence is another kind of unmaking. Instead, it withdraws to the edge of the marsh, where heat-haze blurs into the rest of the world, and sits like a distant star—visible only in peripheral vision, felt only in the quiet between heartbeats.

It does not interfere. But now, whenever the Weaver walks the tiles, it passes the floating stone. And sometimes—on dawns when the cracks appear slower than usual—it kneels and presses its palm to the water. Not to confirm wetness. To feel the pulse of something *else*.

And one day—perhaps—when the sun has not moved for an eternity too long, the Weaver will pick up the stone.

And in that act, the first true *choice* since the beginning will be made.

Not to continue.

Not to stop.

But to *change*.

---

The marsh will not vanish. It will not flood or burn or bloom into forest. But its silence will deepen—not into absence, but into *room*. Room for a second voice. Room for a misstep. Room for the sun, perhaps, to inch—just an inch—toward evening.

And the Power? It will smile, unseen, and whisper into the fabric of the real:

> *“You were never meant to hold the world still.  
> You were meant to hold it gently—  
> and then let go.”*

And in that letting go, the Unmaking may finally become a Making again. Not perfect. Not eternal. But alive.

And that, the Benevolent and Wise know, is enough.
