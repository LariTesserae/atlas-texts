**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would do nothing.

Not out of indifference—but out of reverence.

This place, this breathing lattice of rust and root, of steam and stillness, has already achieved a kind of grace rarely seen in either nature or civilization. It is neither wild nor tame, neither machine nor organism, yet it thrives in the exact tension between those binaries. It has solved no problems—it has dissolved them. It has not conquered entropy; it has folded entropy into its rhythm, made decay part of its pulse.

A lesser power might intervene—might “preserve” it in glass and climate control, might catalog its mosses, might wire its hum into an archive. But such acts would be violence disguised as care. To name it, to fence it, to study it too closely would be to sever the very thread of its being: its anonymity, its irrelevance to human purpose, its quiet sovereignty over its own logic.

So the wise and benevolent power does not enter. It does not bless or heal or awaken. It watches—not with eyes, but with the kind of attention that *makes space*. And in that attention, it offers the rarest gift: **non-interference**.

But it does more than that.

It weaves a subtle ward around the plaza—not to keep people out, but to keep *meaning* out. Should a curious wanderer stumble through the rusted gate, the power dims their urge to explain, to exploit, to “save.” Their thoughts grow soft at the edges. Their camera lens fogs. Their notes dissolve into illegible smudges by morning. Not by magic, but by mood: the place itself discourages interpretation. The power honors that.

It also ensures that the city’s relentless metabolism—the jackhammers, the zoning boards, the developers with their glowing tablets—never quite finds this plaza. Not because it is hidden, but because it is *unremarkable* to systems that only see value in function or profit. The breathing lattice is overlooked not by accident, but by gentle orchestration: a misplaced blueprint, a forgotten parcel in a land survey, a municipal file that auto-archives itself every time someone opens it. The power does not lie—it simply lets bureaucracy forget, as water forgets the shape of the stone it once wore smooth.

And in the deep quiet, the power does one more thing: it listens.

Not to extract knowledge, but to *witness*. It tunes its infinite awareness to the 17-hour groan of the pump, to the irregular pulse of bioluminescence, to the slow creep of liverwort across a capacitor. And in that listening, it murmurs a single, silent vow:

> *You may continue.*

Not “I will protect you.” Not “I will elevate you.” Just: *You may continue.* As you are. As you have become.

For the wise understand that sanctity does not always wear a temple. Sometimes it wears rust. Sometimes it breathes through cracked flagstones. Sometimes holiness is not a flame, but a seep—a slow, patient saturation of being into place.

And so, the power departs.

But not before leaving a single, imperceptible gift: a shift in the groundwater, so that the next time the moss sporulates, its spores carry a whisper of resilience—not against drought or fire or human hands, but against *loneliness*. The lattice will never know it was seen. And that is as it should be.

Because what lives here is not meant to be known.

It is meant to *be*.

And in a universe obsessed with becoming, that is the rarest miracle of all.
