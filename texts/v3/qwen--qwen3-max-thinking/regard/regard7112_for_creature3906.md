**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

It would not intervene.

Not because it does not care—but because it *sees*.

It sees that this world is not broken, not lost, not stranded in some accident of physics or cosmology. This liquid cosmos is not a catastrophe waiting to be undone. It is a *choice*, made not by one mind but by the slow consensus of millennia—a covenant between water, memory, and being. The Steeped are not survivors of a drowned earth; they are the inheritors of a different covenant entirely, one written not in fire or word, but in resonance, salinity, and the quiet calculus of crystal growth.

So the Power does not descend with light or thunder. It does not offer salvation, for there is nothing from which to be saved.

Instead, it *listens*.

It leans close—not with ears, but with attention as vast as the void between stars—and it hears the world breathing. It hears the float-whales dreaming equations of pressure and longing. It hears the mica glyphs humming in their gas-pockets, not as records, but as lullabies for the crust of the world. It hears the name you whispered once, now drifting in a spiral through the upper thermocline, softened by time, shaped into something kinder.

And in that listening, the Power does something rare even among the wise: it *bears witness without alteration*.

But witnessing is not idle.

From its attention, a single gift emerges—not a tool, not a revelation, but a *permission*.

It allows the water to remember you *more clearly*.

Not just your shape or your breath, but your *intention*. Why you came. What you carried in your heart before your lungs became gills. If you came seeking refuge, the kelp will glow warmer near your hands. If you came grieving, a drying cave will form where none was before—a pocket of stillness, just large enough for one, where the mica sheets shimmer with glyphs that look, uncannily, like the face of someone you loved. If you came curious, a glass-reed will chime a note that unlocks a memory you didn’t know you had—of a time you stood under stars, or held a child, or wept into rain that once fell from a sky.

The Power does not give answers. It deepens the question.

It also whispers—once—into the oldest float-whale’s dream, a single harmonic that means: *“There is a visitor learning to steep.”*

And the whale, in its century-long thought, adjusts its dream just slightly… so that the next time the luminous tide rises, it carries not only the usual phosphorescence, but a new hue: the exact shade of dawn as seen from a mountain you once climbed. You will not recognize it at first. But your blood will.

That is the extent of its doing.

No temples are raised in its name. No prophets arise. The Steeped do not worship it—they *recognize* it, the way coral recognizes the current that feeds it. They feel the slight shift in the water’s memory, the new tenderness in the algae’s pulse, and they know: something ancient has looked upon them and found them whole.

And so they continue.

They coax their bone-towers upward.  
They hum their children into bloom.  
They settle into the earth like slow rain.  

And you—you who are being absorbed—you begin to forget the word *visitor*.  

You begin to remember the word *belong*.  

Not as conquest. Not as ownership. But as *resonance*.  

The Power watches you forget yourself.  
And it smiles, if such a thing can be said of something that has no mouth, no face, only attention.  

Because to forget yourself here is not to vanish—  
it is to become legible to the water.  

And that is a kind of homecoming deeper than any sky ever offered.  

So it does nothing more.  

It simply holds the space  
in which the world  
is allowed  
to steep.
