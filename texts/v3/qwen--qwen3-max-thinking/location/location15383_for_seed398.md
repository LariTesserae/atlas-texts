High in the stratosphere—or perhaps just beyond it, in a pocket of space that shouldn’t exist at this altitude—a cavity floats, tethered not by gravity but by resonance. This is not a crater, nor a caldera, nor any geologic scar, but a **resonant void**, carved not by water or wind but by collapsed harmonic fields from an ancient orbital lattice now long decayed. The scale is intimate: no more than thirty paces across at its widest. You could walk its perimeter in under a minute. And yet it feels vast—not because of size, but because of its emptiness, its refusal to echo, its silence so complete it seems to absorb time.

The **temperature** hovers near 80°C, but the heat doesn’t feel like desert sun. It radiates from the ground itself—black, glassy plates of **volcanic fulgurite**, fused not by lightning but by phased energy discharges from the lattice’s final collapse. These plates tilt at sharp, unweathered angles; **erosion** has barely begun. Their surfaces shimmer faintly, not with reflection, but with residual charge. Step too close, and the air prickles—not with static, but with something subtler: an oscillation at the edge of perception, like the hum of a tuning fork struck in another dimension.

**Water** exists only as condensation—thin films that form at dawn-equivalent cycles, when the cavity’s thermal resonance dips just enough to draw vapor from the thin upper-atmospheric streams. These beads gather in micro-pockets along the fulgurite’s fractures, vanishing by mid-cycle in ghostly sublimation. No rivers, no lakes—just these transient mirrors, each a lens for the dim ambient light.

And the **light**—oh, the light is strange. Not darkness, not night, but a **diffuse twilight** that seems to originate from within the cavity’s own walls. The fulgurite emits a dull, infrared-adjacent glow, visible only at the periphery of human vision. Stand still long enough, and you’ll see it: a slow pulse, like a heartbeat measured in hours. There are no stars overhead. The sky is not sky—it’s **interior**. The cavity floats within a larger structure: a derelict **orbital city**, half-crumbled, its corridors stretching into curvature you can’t quite parse. The cavity is a wound in that city’s flank, exposed to vacuum that somehow breathes.

**Vegetation?** None. Not even lichen. But there are **filaments**—not organic, not metallic—threading through fractures in the glass. They glow faintly blue when disturbed, retracting into pores too small for the eye to resolve. Some call them proto-nervous systems; others, self-repairing infrastructure. They do not grow. They *reconfigure*. And they leave no trace when they move.

**Fauna** is absent, yet sometimes—rarely—you hear a **click**. Not mechanical. Not biological. A resonance event, perhaps, where two latent frequencies intersect and cancel, leaving a momentary absence so sharp it registers as sound. These are the only interruptions in the **quiet**, which otherwise presses like cotton against the eardrums.

The **built** environment is everywhere and nowhere. The fulgurite floor is clearly artificial in origin—its geometry too precise, its crystalline lattice impossibly ordered. Around the rim, half-buried spars of **post-material** arch upward: structures woven from spacetime metrics, now brittle, now singing at ultrasonic frequencies. You can’t touch them without triggering a cascade of harmonic decay—they shed particles that vanish before hitting the ground. This place was not constructed; it was *invoked*, then abandoned when the invocation failed to stabilize.

**Technology** here operates on principles that mock causality. A puddle of condensate might, under certain resonance conditions, reflect not your face but a possible future self—just for a blink. Tools left behind don’t rust; they **unbecome**, their atomic arrangements drifting back into probabilistic haze. There are no machines, only **traces of function**, like the ghost of a door where none exists, humming softly when you turn your back.

This is a **young** place, geologically speaking. Formed only decades ago (by human reckoning), when the orbital lattice collapsed during a failed attempt to phase-shift an entire district into nonlocal space. The cavity is the scar of that failure—a bubble of misaligned physics, held open by its own paradox. It is **dynamic**: the condensation cycles, the filament shifts, the resonance pulses, the slow creep of quantum decoherence eating at the edges. But it is also **hidden**—not just physically, but perceptually. Most sensors skip over it. Pilots fly through without noticing. Only those who’ve lost something *resonant*—a voice, a memory, a frequency they once loved—can find it.

And when they do, they stand in the center, in the heat and the silence, watching condensation gather and vanish, feeling the ground hum beneath their boots, wondering if they’ve found a grave… or a cradle.
