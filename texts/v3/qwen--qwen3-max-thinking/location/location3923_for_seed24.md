This is a place where silence has weight.

The ground is not soil but sinter: a fused crust of pale minerals, bleached and brittle, like the residue of some ancient evaporative rite. It spreads flat and unbroken, low as promised—barely a ripple, no dune, no ravine, just a plain that stretches to a horizon blurred by heat-haze even though the air is still. The water score is near-zero, yet the crust bears faint hexagonal cracks, ghost-patterns of moisture that vanished epochs ago, baked into memory by relentless warmth. The temperature isn’t scorching, not quite—it hovers in that deceptive band where the air feels benign but the ground leeches dryness from anything organic that dares touch it. Warm, yes, but sterile-warm, like the inside of an unlit kiln left to cool over centuries.

Sparse vegetation clings in the form of desiccated shrubs the color of ash, no taller than a handspan, spaced so widely they might never have known kinship. Their roots don’t seek water—they seek cracks, fissures in the sinter where microscopic salts accumulate. They don’t photosynthesize in the usual green way; their leaves are grey-filmed, waxy, almost metallic, harvesting ambient infrared or perhaps some trace chemical energy from the air itself. They are survivors of a metabolism pared down to its barest possibility.

No animals stir. No insects hum. No birds silhouette against the sky. The fauna score confirms it: this is a stage without actors, only props and light. And the light—oh, the light is the true strangeness. Though the sun must burn somewhere beyond the veil, the illumination here is dim, diffuse, as if filtered through miles of high-altrope haze or a permanent dust-shroud. It’s not night, but a perpetual twilight, the kind that makes shadows indistinct and distances unreliable. You could stand at the center and not know where the edges are, though the scale is small—a single depression in the plain, perhaps a shallow basin no wider than a village green, ringed by low, crumbling mineral ridges that serve as soft boundaries. This is a contained world, a diorama of desolation.

The erosion score speaks of deep time. This landscape isn’t young desert—it’s older than desert. It has settled into its final form. Wind has long since carried away the loose grains; what remains is the armature, the skeleton left after aeons of subtraction. There are no shifting sands here, only fixed planes and powdery veneers that haven’t moved in lifetimes. The silence is not absence but presence: a held breath, a resonance so low it borders on tinnitus. Occasionally, a flake of sinter detaches with a sound like a dry leaf snapping—tiny, sharp, swallowed instantly by the stillness.

No human hand has touched this place. No postholes, no fire rings, no shards of pottery. The tech parameter allows only for the possibility that someone, once, might have walked here with a stone blade or a woven pouch—but if they did, they left no trace. This is pre-ruin. Pre-memory.

And nothing changes. The dynamic score is near-zero: no seasons, no tides, no diurnal bloom. The warmth is constant. The dimness unvarying. The shrubs neither grow nor die; they simply persist in suspended animation, metabolizing time itself. If you stayed a year, a decade, you would see no difference—only your own shadow, faint and wavering, shrinking slightly at midday, then stretching again as the eternal dusk returns.

This is not a wasteland. It is a complete world, perfectly balanced in its barrenness. A place that has answered all its own questions and now rests in silence, waiting for nothing.
