The Plateau of Still Engines stretches beneath a sky the color of tarnished silver—vast, yes, continental in scale, yet hollowed out by an emptiness so profound it feels less like absence and more like negation. This is a high, cold world: elevation places you near the roof of a breathable atmosphere, where the air is thin and bites with a dry, persistent chill (temperature 0.5). Frost feathers the lee sides of every structure, glittering in the normal, indifferent daylight (light 1.8), but never melts. There is no sun-warmth to speak of, only a flat, diffuse illumination that casts no sharp shadows, as if the sky itself were a frosted lamp.

Water (1.6) is everywhere and nowhere. It doesn’t pool or flow—it weeps. From seams in the pale, striated rock (young, sharp-featured, erosion 0.8), from joints in the colossal, half-buried machinery, from the very air itself in a perpetual, silent condensation. Thin films of liquid sheen over every cold surface, gathering into trembling droplets that fall with agonizing slowness into shallow, transient lenses on the ground—only to vanish not by evaporation, but by sinking instantly into a substrate that drinks greedily. Marsh-edge humidity hangs in the air, a clammy kiss against exposed skin, yet the ground underfoot is mostly bare, gritty stone or fused industrial slag. Patches of vegetation (1.6) cling stubbornly in these damp micro-zones: not forests, but dense, spongy mats of mosses, liverworts, and strange, rubbery lichens in improbable hues—bruised violets, deep teals, rust-reds—that seem to absorb sound rather than reflect it. They grow thick but low, never taller than a handspan, thriving in the constant moisture yet stunted by the cold and the altitude.

This is a built landscape (1.5), but not a city as you know it. There are no streets, no homes, no market squares. Instead, the plateau is studded with the carcasses and skeletons of industrial ambition on a scale that defies comprehension (tech 2.5, scale 3.0). Imagine blast furnaces the size of mesas, their brickwork cracked and spewing not fire, but slow, cold steam. Conveyor belts wider than highways, frozen mid-rotation, sagging under their own weight like petrified serpents. Vast, open-air refineries of interlocking pipes and ducts, now choked with ice and veiled in those weeping condensations, stretch to every horizon. The structures are layered, yes—but not with human habitation. They are layered with purpose: one titanic gear meshed into another, pipelines feeding into distillation columns feeding into cooling towers that pierce the low cloud layer. It feels less like a town and more like a single, continent-spanning machine that was erected here, ran for a time, and then simply… stopped. Not broken. Not ruined. Just halted mid-task, perfectly preserved.

And it is loud (sound 2.4). Not with life—there is no fauna (0.1), not even an insect’s buzz, not a bird’s cry, not the scuttle of a rodent. The silence of the living is absolute. But the machine itself sings a dirge of residual energy. Deep, subsonic thrums vibrate up through the soles of your boots, felt more than heard, the ghost of a million pistons that once drove this place. High above, in the lattice of frozen pipes, the wind (what little there is) whistles through apertures with the pitch of a steam valve bleeding pressure—a constant, mournful keening. Somewhere, a colossal bearing, suspended in a cradle of rust-flecked steel, emits a single, resonant *clang* every seventeen minutes, like a slow, metallic heartbeat. Condensation dripping onto hot (or once-hot) metal hisses and spits. The sound isn’t chaotic; it’s a stable, unchanging symphony of arrested motion (dynamic 0.4). This noise has been playing, unchanged, for decades, centuries, maybe millennia. It is the sound of equilibrium.

The weirdness (2.2) is in the details, the subtle violations of expectation. The water that weeps from the rocks tastes faintly of ozone and machine oil. The lichen glows faintly when touched, then dims. The colossal gears, though frozen, show no sign of rust—only a fine, grey powder that flakes off like dried skin. The air crackles with static near the larger structures, making your hair stand on end, yet there are no visible power sources. And the emptiness (density 0.3) is profound. You can stand in the center of a plaza-sized junction of frozen conveyor belts and see for miles in every direction—acres of dead machinery, slick with condensation, draped in cold moss, humming its eternal, lonely song—without spotting another soul, another moving thing, another *presence*. The scale (3.0) ensures that even the largest structure feels dwarfed by the sheer, hollow immensity of the plateau itself. You are not in a place that was abandoned. You are in a place that was completed. Its purpose was fulfilled the moment it fell silent, and now it exists in perfect, loud, wet, cold, empty stasis—a monument to industry that achieved its final state not through decay, but through flawless, eternal cessation. Time doesn’t pass here; it accretes, layer upon silent layer, like frost on a dead switchyard.
