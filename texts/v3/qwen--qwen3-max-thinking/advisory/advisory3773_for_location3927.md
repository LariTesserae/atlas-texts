To visit this place—this humming, rust-scented, bone-dry city folded inside the earth like a secret in a clenched fist—you must prepare not as an explorer, but as a guest. Not as someone arriving with tools and maps, but as a body learning a new language of breath, balance, and resonance. Here is how you ready yourself.

---

### 1. **Purge Your Expectations of Water**
Forget everything you know about hydration, irrigation, condensation, or thirst as a linear need. There is no water here—not in any form you recognize. Do not bring canteens or desalination tablets. Instead, bring *mineral salts*—electrolyte powders rich in magnesium, potassium, and silica. Your body will draw moisture not from liquid, but from humidity stripped from the air by your own breath, and from the mineral-rich dust that clings to your skin. The locals survive by licking salted stone tablets or chewing silica-rich lichen that secretes a viscous, gelatinous fluid when warmed by body heat. You will learn to do the same. Before arrival, fast for a day on dry foods only—dates, dried seaweed, roasted seeds—to train your metabolism toward internal moisture synthesis.

---

### 2. **Tune Your Ears Before Your Feet**
This city communicates through vibration. Your eardrums will ache within minutes of entry if you are unprepared. Pack *resonance dampeners*—not earplugs (which deaden all sound and risk isolation), but finely tuned bone-conduction filters: lightweight brass or quartz earpieces that allow harmonic frequencies to pass while muting dissonant overtones. These can be crafted from tuning forks wrapped in cured membrane. Practice beforehand by sleeping near wind chimes or Tibetan singing bowls until your nervous system stops interpreting constant resonance as threat. Learn to *listen with your sternum*. The locals do.

Most importantly: **do not speak aloud upon arrival**. Your voice will be a crude, disruptive blare. Instead, bring a set of *tapping stones*—smooth, resonant pebbles of varying densities (basalt, quartz, obsidian). Practice simple rhythmic sequences: one tap for greeting, three for “I mean no harm,” a slow ripple for curiosity. The city will answer in kind—through the hum of its walls, the creak of a vine, the shift of dust.

---

### 3. **Wear Only What the City Can "Hear"**
Dress in layered, loose-fitting fabrics woven from *acoustic fibers*: spider silk, flax treated with conductive clay, or the shed skins of paper-winged lizards (if ethically sourced from molted remains). Avoid synthetics—they create static friction that disrupts the ambient resonance. Your clothing should *breathe*, but also *vibrate*. Attach small, hollow brass beads or glass bells to your cuffs and hems; their chiming should harmonize with the city’s baseline hum, not clash with it. Shoes must be soft-soled—rawhide or compressed moss—so your steps contribute to, rather than interrupt, the city’s rhythm. Bare feet are acceptable only after your soles have calloused enough to feel the pulse of the ground without pain.

---

### 4. **Bring No Light Sources**
The city is already saturated with prismatic daylight, refracted endlessly through mica veins and silica walls. Artificial light—lanterns, torches, LEDs—will be perceived as violence, a falsification of truth. Instead, bring *light-catchers*: prisms of your own, cut from clear quartz or ice-glass, to redirect and soften beams for reading or close work. The locals use lenses made from the petrified eyes of blind worms, polished into perfect circles that focus light without distorting color. If you must navigate darkness (though there is no true night), rely on the bioluminescent nectar of the translucent ferns. You may gently coax a droplet from a mature frond by humming a low C—many ferns respond to specific frequencies—but never pluck or break the plant. To do so is to silence a voice.

---

### 5. **Prepare Your Body for Dry-Lushness**
Your skin will crack. Your eyes will sting from airborne silica. Your sinuses will clog with the metallic tang of rust and ozone. Bring *mineral-infused ointments*: salves made from tallow and ground obsidian, mixed with crushed fungal orchid pollen. Apply nightly. Drink infusions of *dry-bloom tea*—a blend of powdered glass-thorn bark and evaporated nectar crystals—to stimulate internal mucous production. Breathe slowly and deeply; the air is thin in moisture but rich in ionized particles that can energize or overwhelm your nervous system. Meditate each morning with your back against a warm wall, letting the city’s vibrations recalibrate your internal rhythm.

---

### 6. **Understand the Sacredness of Silence**
Once per local cycle—usually when the fungal orchids’ glyphs begin to fade—the entire city observes *The Still*. All forging stops. All tapping ceases. Even the glass leaves seem to hold their breath. This is not emptiness—it is *listening*. To break The Still is the gravest offense, worse than theft or violence. Before you arrive, practice absolute silence for increasing intervals: 5 minutes, then 15, then an hour. Learn to still not just your voice, but your *intent*. During The Still, you must not even *think* loudly. Stand or sit with palms flat against the ground, eyes closed, heart open. In that silence, the city may choose to speak to you—not in words, but in a sudden warmth in your spine, a shift in air pressure, or the scent of blooming orchids where none were before.

---

### 7. **Leave No Trace—Except Resonance**
You will be tempted to take a glass-thorn, a shard of iridescent soil, a petal of evaporating glyph. Resist. These are not souvenirs; they are *notes* in a song that has played for millennia. Removing them is like plucking a string from a harp mid-performance. Instead, offer something that *adds* to the harmony: a small bell you’ve tuned to match the city’s fundamental frequency, a woven mat of your own hair and local fiber, a drop of your blood mixed with salt (if offered freely) to feed the mineral veins. Your presence should leave behind not footprints, but a new echo.

---

### 8. **Accept That You Are Being Watched—By Everything**
The architecture observes. The dust remembers. The blind worms trace your path in phosphorescent script. This is not surveillance—it is *communion*. Move with humility. Pause often. Bow your head when passing beneath arches. Touch walls gently, with open palms, as if greeting an elder. If a vine coils near your arm, do not pull away; let it taste your vibration. You are not an intruder, but a guest in a body that has learned to live without water, with only song and stone. And if, after many days, the city begins to hum a tune you recognize from your childhood—do not be surprised. It has been listening to you longer than you’ve been listening to it.

---

Come not to conquer, not to document, not even to understand.  
Come to *resonate*.  
And when you finally leave—through that narrow slit in the earth—you will carry no artifacts, only a silence that hums.
