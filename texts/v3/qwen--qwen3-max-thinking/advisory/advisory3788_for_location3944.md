To visit **The Hollowed Hearth**, you must prepare not as an explorer of ruins, but as a guest in a living lung—a place that breathes, listens, and remembers. This is not a site to be *conquered* or merely observed; it is to be *entered with humility*. The following guidance blends practical survival, cultural sensitivity, and metaphysical attunement, because in The Hollowed Hearth, all three are inseparable.

---

### **I. Before Descent: Understanding the Threshold**

The entrance to The Hollowed Hearth is unmarked by grand architecture. There is no door, only a narrow fissure in the plateau’s crust—perhaps hidden beneath a slab of sun-cracked stone or disguised by a tumble of wind-scoured boulders. It may be guarded only by silence or by the watchful presence of a single elder sitting nearby, weaving reeds with eyes half-closed.

**Do not seek it with maps or drones.** The Hearth reveals itself to those who are *listening*, not searching. Locals (if any surface dwellers remain nearby) might speak of it only in proverbs: *“Breathe where the earth exhales,”* or *“Dig with your ears.”*

**Bring only what you can carry in your hands and leave behind nothing but your breath.** Metal tools, synthetic fabrics, or electronic devices will feel alien here—disruptive to the chamber’s delicate acoustic and energetic balance. They may even *fail*: compasses spin, batteries drain, and screens fog inexplicably. Not from magic, but from resonance—the Hearth’s dense hum interferes with frequencies it does not recognize.

---

### **II. Physical Preparation**

#### **Clothing & Gear**
- **Wear natural fibers only**: wool, linen, raw cotton, or leather. Dyes should be plant-based. Synthetic materials (nylon, polyester) will trap moisture unnaturally and emit static that agitates the bats and crickets.
- **Soft-soled footwear**: Bare feet are acceptable if your soles are tough, but thin-hide moccasins or felt slippers are better—they allow you to feel the floor’s subtle gradients of warmth and dampness.
- **No heavy packs**. Carry essentials in a woven satchel or cloth bundle tied with sinew or plant fiber.
- **Bring a small clay cup or gourd**—not to take water, but to drink *if offered*. Refusing water here is a grave insult.

#### **Supplies (Minimal)**
- **A personal offering**: A smooth river stone (not from your homeland), a sprig of non-invasive herb (sage, thyme), or a few dried seeds. Place it gently in one of the basins upon entry—not as payment, but as introduction.
- **A beeswax candle or tallow lamp** (if permitted). Do not bring matches; fire here is kindled by friction or ember-sharing. Ask before lighting anything.
- **No food unless gifted**. Eating uninvited food—especially anything grown in the Hearth—is taboo. But if offered fermented tuber or smoked fish, accept with both hands and eat slowly.

---

### **III. Sensory & Psychological Readiness**

#### **Adjust to the Soundscape**
The constant chorus of crickets, bat clicks, dripping seeps, and rustling rats is not noise—it is **language**. Newcomers often experience anxiety or vertigo from this “dense quiet.” To acclimate:
- Sit still for at least 30 minutes upon entry. Close your eyes. Map the sounds: *Where is the drip coming from? Which wall hums after a gust above?*
- Do not speak loudly. Voices carry strangely—whispers echo; shouts fracture into dissonance. Speak low, and only when necessary.

#### **See in Layers**
Light is not uniform. Learn to read by gradation:
- The amber glow of lamps reveals texture, not distance.
- The midday sun-shafts are brief but potent—they mark time, ritual, and seasonal shifts.
- Shadows are not empty. They *hold* things. Do not thrust your hand into a dark alcove without invitation or warning.

#### **Accept the Uncanny**
- If a pool shows you something that isn’t your reflection, **do not stare**. Blink slowly, bow your head slightly, and move on. The “echo-keepers” are not malevolent—they are the chamber’s memory made visible.
- If you hear your name spoken in a voice you don’t recognize, **do not answer immediately**. Wait. Sometimes it’s the wind in a flue. Sometimes it’s a child testing your attention. Answer only if the call repeats thrice.

---

### **IV. Behavioral Protocols**

#### **Movement**
- **Walk with weight**. Light, hurried steps signal fear or theft. Place your whole foot down, as if greeting the floor.
- **Never block a seep channel**. These are the Hearth’s veins. Step over them with care.
- **Do not touch the walls without reason**. Each brick has been shaped by generations. If you must steady yourself, use the designated handholds—often marked by darker patina or smoother texture.

#### **Interaction with Inhabitants**
The caretakers (if present) may appear as elders, children, or solitary figures tending fungi or patching clay. They may not speak your language, but they understand gesture.
- **Greet with open palms**, facing slightly downward—not in surrender, but in sharing.
- **Never point**. Use your chin or an open hand to indicate.
- **Join labor if invited**. Reweaving a basket, smoothing clay, or sorting tubers is how trust is built. Refusing is polite only if you are clearly unwell.
- **Ask permission before sketching, recording, or taking soil**. Most will refuse—and that refusal is final.

---

### **V. Metaphysical Etiquette**

The Hollowed Hearth is **alive in the way ecosystems are alive—not sentient, but responsive**. It remembers who enters with greed and who enters with gratitude.

- **Leave no trace—but leave a trace of respect**. Your footprints will fade into the packed earth, but your *intent* lingers.
- **Do not stay more than three days** unless invited to stay longer. The chamber becomes “full” with strangers; its balance shifts.
- **On departure**, pause at the threshold. Turn slowly in place. Let the sounds settle into your bones. Then climb out backward, feet first—a sign that you carry nothing away but memory.

---

### **VI. After Leaving**

You may feel **disoriented on the surface**. The sun will seem too bright, the wind too loud, the ground too solid. This is normal. The Hearth rewires your senses.

- **Drink only clean water for three days**. Your body may still “taste” the mineral seeps.
- **Dreams will be vivid**, often involving water, whispering walls, or small animals guiding you.
- **Do not boast** of your visit. Speak of it only in hushed tones, or in writing that uses metaphor (“I walked through a lung made of clay and cricket-song…”).
- **If you feel called to return**, wait one full moon cycle. The Hearth reveals its deeper layers only to those who forget it long enough to miss it.

---

### Final Note:

The Hollowed Hearth is not a destination. It is a **threshold between human making and planetary memory**. You do not “discover” it—you are *permitted* to witness it. Go not to learn its secrets, but to remember your own: that you, too, are made of breath, damp earth, and the quiet music of small things enduring.
