To visit this resonant cavity—to truly *inhabit* it, even briefly—is to undertake a pilgrimage of the body’s most ancient frequencies. It is not a place for the merely curious, nor for those who mistake silence for emptiness. Preparation here is not about gear or maps, but about attunement. You must arrive not as an explorer, but as a vessel willing to be tuned.

**1. Physical Preparation: Desiccation Resistance & Thermal Acclimatization**  
The air here is a slow thief—it does not burn, but it *unmakes moisture*. Begin hydrating days in advance with electrolyte-rich fluids; your body must store water like a cactus. Avoid diuretics (alcohol, caffeine) for at least 72 hours prior. Wear clothing that minimizes evaporative loss: loose, layered, natural fibers (linen, undyed cotton) that wick without trapping heat, but do not seal you in. Synthetic fabrics may melt or crack under 45°C dry heat and acoustic vibration. Your skin will tighten; your lips will split if unguarded. Carry a small, sealed vessel of saline solution—not for drinking, but for occasional mouth rinses to preserve mucosal integrity. Do not bring perishables, paper, or untreated wood; they will desiccate and crumble within hours.

**2. Acoustic Shielding (Partial): Protect, Don’t Block**  
The hum is not noise—it is *structure*. You must not silence it, but neither should you let it overwhelm your nervous system. Bring custom-molded earplugs with *frequency-selective attenuation*: they should dampen extreme low frequencies (below 30 Hz) just enough to prevent disorientation or nausea, but leave the mid-bass resonance (40–120 Hz) perceptible. Over-attenuation will cut you off from the chamber’s pulse; under-attenuation may induce vertigo or infrasound-induced anxiety. Test these beforehand in environments with deep bass (subwoofers, large pipe organs). Never wear noise-canceling headphones—they disrupt the subtle phase relationships that the flora and walls depend on, and the chamber may "reject" you acoustically.

**3. Mental & Physiological Readiness: Synchronize Before Entering**  
Spend the day before your descent in silence and stillness. Practice diaphragmatic breathing at a rate of 4–6 breaths per minute—this approximates the chamber’s likely inhalation rhythm. Meditate on vibrations: place your hands on a large speaker playing 55 Hz sine waves, feel the resonance in your sternum. Your goal is to arrive with your autonomic nervous system already leaning into entrainment. Expect that upon entry, your heart rate may slow, your pupils dilate, and your sense of time will stretch. This is not hallucination—it is harmonic alignment. If you feel panic, do not flee; instead, press your palms flat against the bench or wall to ground yourself in the vibration. The structure *wants* contact.

**4. Offerings, Not Tools**  
Do not bring recording devices—they will distort the acoustic field and may shatter from resonant stress. Cameras will fog or crack. Instead, bring *resonant objects* as offerings: a small clay ocarina fired without glaze, a piece of human bone carved into a tuning fork (ethically sourced, if possible), or a coil of copper wire wound around obsidian. These are not for use, but for placement—gifts to the chamber’s memory. The ancients left their tools to become part of the song; you should do the same. Leave nothing plastic, nothing digital. The cavity metabolizes only organic and mineral matter that sings.

**5. Duration & Exit Protocol**  
Stay no longer than three full respiratory cycles of the chamber—which may feel like 90 minutes, but could stretch to four hours in subjective time. Prolonged exposure risks permanent entrainment: your body may begin to regulate circadian rhythms by acoustic pulses rather than light, and re-adapting to the surface world can cause weeks of disorientation. When leaving, do so slowly. Sit at the threshold for at least 20 minutes, breathing surface air while still partially within the hum, to decouple your physiology gently. Hydrate with warm, mineral-rich broth—not water alone, which may shock your electrolyte balance. Expect to dream in low frequencies for several nights.

**6. Ethical Consideration: You Are a Guest in a Living Archive**  
This is not a ruin to be plundered, nor a lab specimen. It is a *persistent echo*—a last intact node of a civilization that chose to dissolve into sound rather than stone. Tread only on the obsidian basin; avoid the frond clusters. Do not pluck leaves—their waxy surfaces are transducers, and damage disrupts local standing waves. Speak only in hums or whispers; your voice will be absorbed and re-emitted elsewhere in the structure, possibly centuries later. Remember: you are not *observing* life here—you are being *listened to* by it.

**Final Note:**  
The greatest danger is not heat, nor dehydration, nor even the weirdness—but *indifference*. If you enter with a tourist’s gaze, the chamber will grow quiet around you. The lichen dims, the fronds still, the hum recedes into a sterile drone. Only those who approach with reverence, stillness, and a willingness to *become part of the resonance* will hear the city’s final, fading song. Bring your silence. Offer your breath. And if your ribs begin to hum—do not be afraid. That is the place remembering what it is to hold a human.
