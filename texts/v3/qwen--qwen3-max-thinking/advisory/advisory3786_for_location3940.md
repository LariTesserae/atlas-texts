**Preparation Guide for Visiting the Sunken Grove Beneath the Salt Pan**

*For the human multiverse traveler: curious, vulnerable, and profoundly mortal.*

---

### **I. Mindset & Orientation**

Before boots touch salt-crusted earth or fingers brush moss-slick stone, prepare your **perception**. This is not a place to be “conquered,” documented, or even fully *understood*. It is a pocket of **deep time made legible through intimacy**, not spectacle.  

- **Discard expectations of drama.** No thunderous waterfalls, no howling beasts, no hidden temples. The grove’s power lies in its **quiet saturation**—a world humming at the edge of hearing, blooming in micro-detail.
- **Embrace slowness.** Time here moves like sap through a winter branch: present, purposeful, invisible. Plan to sit still for at least one full local twilight cycle before moving deliberately.
- **Reframe “emptiness.”** Silence does not mean absence. The low sound rating (0.2/3) is a veil—not a void. Train your ears to the subvocal: the rustle beneath silence is where life thrives.

---

### **II. Physical Preparation**

#### **Clothing & Gear**
- **Footwear:** Soft-soled, closed-toe boots with minimal tread. You’ll walk on living moss carpets and delicate mineral crusts. Aggressive soles will crush ecosystems that took decades to stabilize.
- **Clothing:** Lightweight but full-coverage natural fibers (linen, cotton, untreated wool). The humidity (2.6/3 heat with damp pockets) encourages fungal and microbial adhesion. Avoid synthetics—they trap moisture and amplify heat stress.
- **Gloves:** Thin, breathable fingerless gloves for handling surfaces without transferring oils or disturbing spore-laden vines.
- **No headlamps or bright lights.** Bring only a **red-filtered, dimmable biolight** (max 5 lumens) for emergencies. The grove operates in perpetual twilight; your eyes will adapt within 30 minutes. Artificial white light disrupts nocturnal pollinators and spore cycles.
- **Water:** Carry your own. **Do not drink the pooled water**, however sweet it tastes. Though chemically benign, it contains bioactive compounds that may interact unpredictably with offworld metabolisms. (Local life has co-evolved with it; you have not.)

#### **Body Readiness**
- **Hydrate deeply** before descent. The humid heat induces subtle sweat loss you won’t notice until dizzy.
- **Apply mineral-based barrier cream** to wrists, neck, and ankles. Not for insects (they’re harmless), but to prevent biofilm transfer—some cyanobacteria here produce mild dermal enzymes that can trigger rashes in non-native skin.
- **Bring ear protection—but use sparingly.** Not for noise, but to **modulate auditory sensitivity**. In the profound quiet, your own heartbeat may seem loud. Use soft earplugs with variable attenuation if overwhelmed.

---

### **III. Behavioral Protocol**

#### **Movement**
- **Step only where moss is thick and emerald-green.** Rust or silver moss indicates drier, more fragile strata. The floor is a living membrane over blind tunnels; missteps may collapse micro-habitats or startle burrowers.
- **Never touch the upright cobble or stone ring.** These are not artifacts but **anchor points** in the grove’s ecological memory. Disturbing them may disrupt unseen mycorrhizal networks.
- **Breathe steadily and low.** Remember: certain vines curl in response to exhalation. Rapid or loud breathing may trigger defensive spore releases or cause tendrils to retract—a minor ecological trauma.

#### **Observation**
- **Use peripheral vision.** Central focus misses the subtle motions: the ripple of mole-rats beneath moss, the inching progress of a centipede, the slow unfurling of a succulent leaf.
- **Look downward more than upward.** The true density (2.1/3) is in the floor—not the ceiling. The “living manuscript” is written underfoot.
- **Watch for bioluminescent spores at dusk-equivalent.** They glow for precisely 53–61 minutes after release. Do not chase or collect them; their light is part of a reproductive signaling cascade.

#### **Interaction**
- **Take nothing but memory.** Not a leaf, not a pebble, not a drop of water. The ceramic shard embedded in the wall is part of the strata now—removing it would be like plucking a tooth from a skeleton.
- **Leave no trace—especially of sound.** Humming, whispering, or tapping stones may seem harmless, but the grove’s acoustics amplify internal resonance. That “distant chime” you hear? It may be your own voice echoing through limestone pores hours later.

---

### **IV. Psychological Safeguards**

- **Expect mild temporal disorientation.** The dynamic rating (1.9/3) means constant micro-change, yet the scale (1.3/3) creates a womb-like stasis. You may lose track of hours. Bring a **non-digital chronometer** (sand-timer, candle clock) if timekeeping is essential.
- **Prepare for solitude-induced euphoria.** Many travelers report a deep, wordless calm bordering on trance. This is normal—but **do not sleep here**. The humid air, combined with bioactive spores, can induce vivid, disorienting dreams that blur self and surroundings.
- **If you hear “chimes”:** Do not seek their source. They are likely piezoelectric resonance from shifting mineral strata—or, as locals whisper, the **stone memory** of ancient rains. Either way, they are not meant to be found, only witnessed.

---

### **V. Exit Protocol**

- **Cleanse your soles** before climbing out. Brush off all organic matter; rinse with your carried water if possible. You carry microbes now—don’t spread them to the salt pan above.
- **Sit on the rim for 15 minutes** after emerging. Let your eyes re-adjust to the harsh light. The grove’s twilight recalibrates your visual cortex; sudden brightness can cause nausea or afterimages of glowing spores.
- **Do not speak of what you saw for at least one full day.** Let the experience settle. The grove rewards silence with revelation—but only if you return the gift.

---

### **Final Note**

This is not a destination. It is a **conversation**—one conducted in moss, moisture, and millimeters. You are not an explorer here. You are a guest in the earth’s quiet lung. Move like one.

And if, in the deepest hush, you feel a warmth not your own pressing gently against your palm—do not pull away. It might be the grove, finally acknowledging you.
