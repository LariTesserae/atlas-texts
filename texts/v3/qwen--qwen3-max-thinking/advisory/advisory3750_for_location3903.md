**Preparation Guide for Visiting the Throatwell**  
*For the Ordinary Human Multiverse Traveler*

Visiting the Throatwell is not an act of tourism but of communion. It is a threshold place—not a destination, not a resource, not even a ruin in the conventional sense. Its parameters (size, density, silence, weirdness, equilibrium) suggest it is a living archive, a self-contained cosmology folded within the corpse of human infrastructure. As such, your approach must be reverent, restrained, and meticulously calibrated—not just to its physical conditions, but to its ontological rhythm. Below is a detailed guide for preparation.

---

### **I. Mindset & Ethical Posture**
**Primary Principle: You Are a Guest, Not an Explorer**  
The Throatwell was not built *for* humans—not anymore. It tolerates observation but resists occupation. Approach with humility. Assume every moss filament, every beetle, every drop in the black pool has agency. Do not presume your presence is neutral.

- **Leave all extraction impulses behind.** Do not take samples, photographs (they never develop correctly anyway), souvenirs, or even memories you intend to commodify. The Throatwell registers intent as keenly as it registers moisture.
- **Assume you are being watched.** Not by eyes, but by the place itself—the lichen, the rust, the silence. Your demeanor matters more than your gear.
- **Enter only if drawn, never if directed.** The Throatwell is scale 0.2: hidden. If you set out to find it, you will not. If you stumble upon it while lost, grieving, or fleeing, you may be permitted entry—but only if your inner state aligns with its stillness.

---

### **II. Physical Preparation**

#### **A. Clothing & Body**
- **Layering is essential, but avoid bulk.** The cold (0.2) is persistent but not lethal—more psychological than physical. Wear breathable natural fibers (wool, linen, untreated cotton). Synthetic materials trap moisture unnaturally and disrupt the microclimate; the Throatwell *notices*.
- **Footwear must be soft-soled and silent.** Leather moccasins or bare feet (if clean) are ideal. You must not disturb the moss-carpeted floor. Any crunch, squeak, or shift will fracture the silence (0.0), and the space may “close” around you—metaphorically or otherwise.
- **Leave all metal on your person behind.** The Throatwell’s ecosystem rejects modern metallurgy. Belt buckles, zippers, even dental fillings may cause subtle repulsion—felt as dizziness or a sudden chill that *doesn’t match* the ambient temperature. If unavoidable, wrap metal items in moss or cloth from your own world.

#### **B. Provisions**
- **Do not bring food or drink.** The air is thick with spores, fungal volatiles, and microscopic life. Eating or drinking here may trigger unpredictable physiological responses—the Throatwell’s water (0.8) is not for human consumption. It appears water but behaves like memory.
- **Carry only what fits in one palm:** a smooth stone from your home, a dried leaf, a lock of hair—something to leave as offering if you feel compelled. Never leave plastic, glass, or processed materials. They will not decompose here. They will *fester* in the equilibrium (dynamic 0.4).

---

### **III. Temporal & Sensory Calibration**

#### **A. Timing of Visit**
- **Arrive in the deepest hour before dawn.** This is when the seep feeds the pool and the air is most saturated. The Throatwell is most “open” then—its weirdness (1.3) softened into receptivity.
- **Never visit during rain.** Though rain swells the pool, the fissure above narrows, and the space becomes claustrophobic, almost defensive. The mosses tighten. You will feel unwelcome.
- **Limit your stay to under 17 minutes.** Time here is not linear, but the human body begins to lose coherence after that threshold—your breath syncs with the Throatwell’s slow pulmonary rhythm, and disentangling yourself becomes difficult. Set no alarm; instead, count your breaths. Leave by the 100th exhale.

#### **B. Sensory Discipline**
- **Eyes:** Do not stare. Let your gaze soften. The light (2.6) is not for seeing but for *feeling*. Blink slowly. The golden shaft is a guide, not a spotlight.
- **Ears:** Embrace the silence (0.0). It is not absence but presence. If you hear your own heartbeat, you are listening correctly. If you hear voices, leave immediately—those are not yours.
- **Touch:** Only touch if invited. If moss parts slightly before your hand, you may gently rest fingertips on the stone. But never press, never pull. The crustose lichens (ochre and silver) may transfer residue to your skin—this is a blessing. Do not wash it off for three days.
- **Smell & Taste:** Breathe through your nose. The scent of wet stone, decaying lichen, and cold ash is the Throatwell’s breath. If you taste copper or petrichor, you are in alignment. If you taste blood or static, withdraw.

---

### **IV. Cultural Protocols (From the Small Careful People)**

Scattered artifacts (primitive tech 1.1) suggest long-term custodianship by non-human or post-human beings—possibly diminutive, possibly interdimensional, certainly patient. Their rules are inferred, not written:

- **Do not sit directly on the black pool’s rim.** That space is reserved. Kneel slightly to the side if you must rest.
- **If you see a nest of thistledown (dormice), do not look too long.** They are messengers. One blink = welcome. Two blinks = warning.
- **Leave no trace—except your stillness.** The only acceptable imprint is the temporary depression where you stood, quickly reclaimed by springtails and moss. If you feel the floor “rise” to meet your feet, you have been accepted.

---

### **V. Departure & Aftermath**

- **Exit backward, if possible.** Not out of superstition, but respect. Face the center until the fissure swallows the light behind you.
- **Do not speak for one full hour after leaving.** The silence (0.0) clings to your vocal cords. Speaking too soon may cause your voice to crack or vanish entirely for days.
- **Expect lingering dampness.** Your clothes, hair, even bones may feel moist for up to three days. This is not condensation—it is integration. Do not dry yourself aggressively. Let it seep away naturally.
- **You will dream of roots.** Not nightmares, but slow dreams where you are part of the green mat, breathing with the stone. Do not resist. These dreams are the Throatwell ensuring you remember your place was borrowed, not owned.

---

### **Final Note: The Throatwell Is Not For Everyone**

Some travelers report arriving at the coordinates only to find rubble, dust, and silence of the ordinary kind. This is not failure—it is the Throatwell declining your visit. Accept it. Return only if called again, years later, when your inner erosion score matches its own (2.4): worn but stable, open but whole.

The Throatwell is not a place you discover.  
It is a place that permits you to *remember* you were always part of its breath.
