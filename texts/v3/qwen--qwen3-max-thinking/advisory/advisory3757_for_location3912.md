To visit this world—this liquid cosmos suspended not by physics alone but by the quiet will of memory, pressure, and time—you must not pack a bag. You must unbecome.

### **I. Biological Surrender**
You cannot enter with lungs. Lungs are relics of a dry, frantic world of breath and burst. Here, respiration is woven into your skin, your blood, your very structure. Before you even glimpse the threshold of this place, you must undergo the **Rite of Gilling**—not surgery, but symbiosis. Tiny filaments of native biota, cultured from the ancestral waters, are introduced into your lymphatic system while you sleep in a saline womb beneath a quartz moon. Over weeks, your neck softens, splits like a lotus, and blooms with gills that flutter when you dream. These are not tools—they are *kin*. They will taste the water for you, interpret its moods, and if you disrespect the currents, they may close forever.

You must also shed your need for air as a concept. There is no “surface” to gasp toward. Panic is a foreign language here; it will drown you faster than depth. Train yourself in **liquid meditation**: submerge in deep tanks for days at a time, breathing only through gills, until your heartbeat slows to match the pulse of abyssal tides. Learn to sleep while weightless, cradled not by a bed but by the water’s even pressure.

### **II. Sensory Recalibration**
Your eyes are too sharp, too hungry for edges. In this world, light is not illumination but intimacy—a shared secret between organisms. You must **dull your sight** and **sharpen your lateral line**. Wear lenses made of cured jellyfish mesoglea that diffuse bright points into ambient glow. Practice navigating by vibration: let your palms and soles become sonar. In time, you’ll feel a distant bridge’s creak in your molars, sense a float-whale’s dream as a warmth in your sternum.

Sound here is not carried—it is *delivered*. Voices are not for broadcasting but for anchoring. Learn to speak in **subvocal hums**, felt more than heard. Practice silence not as emptiness but as active listening—the water is always whispering. Carry no devices that beep, buzz, or flash; they are blasphemy. Instead, bring a **resonance stone**: a smooth, hollow nodule of fossil coral that you can press to your chest to feel the world’s low-frequency song.

### **III. Temporal Reorientation**
You cannot visit on a schedule. Time here is not linear but **sedimentary**—layered, compressive, slow-dissolving. Arrive with no return date. Leave no itinerary. The world will absorb you only if you cease measuring your stay in hours. Instead, mark time by **biological events**: the blooming of the violet kelp, the awakening sigh of a nearby float-whale, the hardening of your own temporary shelter (more on that soon).

Bring no clocks. Carry no calendar. Let your body forget the sun’s tyranny. Sleep when the algae dim; wake when the thermal vents sigh louder. If you feel urgency, you are still foreign. If you feel boredom, you are not listening closely enough. **Patience is your passport.**

### **IV. Material Humility**
Forget all you know of technology. No metals. No plastics. No fire. Even wood is alien here. Your tools must be **grown or gifted**. Before departure, commission a **bone-tine comb** from a collaborator who works with sustainably harvested whale ivory (not from this world—yet). Learn to carve with volcanic glass, polished by river silt. Your clothing should be woven from **tendon-silk**, dyed with crushed bioluminescent spores—light-emitting, breathable, and dissolvable if lost.

Most importantly: bring **nothing you cannot return to the water**. Every object you carry must be compostable by the ecosystem—edible to filter-feeders, absorbable by coral, or sinkable into the soft crust without scar. If you must record your journey, use **mica tablets** and etch with clam-shell styluses. Your words will fade, as all things must. That is not loss—it is participation.

### **V. Architectural Integration**
You will not stay in a guest house. You will **co-grow your dwelling**. Upon arrival, the locals (if they accept you) will guide you to a soft outcrop of nascent coral. There, you will press your body into its warm, malleable surface while humming your name in subsonic tones. Over days, the structure will remember your shape, your heat, your scent—and begin to curve around you. This is your **coco-home**. It will pulse gently with your breath. Do not rush it. Do not decorate it. Let it remain translucent, so the ambient light can pass through your walls like thought through skull.

If you are granted this, you are no longer a visitor. You are becoming **local geology**.

### **VI. Ethical Preparation: The Covenant of Absorption**
This world does not allow observers. It only allows participants. Before stepping through the threshold (a thermal rift? a singing crevasse? a dream shared by three elders?), you must swear the **Covenant of Becoming**:

> *I will not extract.  
> I will not document for the sake of the dry world.  
> I will not name things without asking their permission.  
> I will let the water remember me as I remember it.*  

Break this, and the gills will seal. The coral will reject your touch. The currents will carry your name back to you not as echo, but as warning.

### **VII. Departure Is a Myth**
There is no “going back.” Even if your body returns to air, your cells will carry traces of the deep water—its minerals, its memories, its slow time. You may find yourself unable to tolerate silence on land, or unable to speak above a whisper. You may weep when you see rain, not from sadness, but because it is *wrong*—droplets falling, not pooling, not connecting.

But perhaps you will not leave. Perhaps the water will decide you fit. Perhaps your bones will begin to hum with the same frequency as the float-whales. Perhaps your skin will grow translucent, your thoughts slower, your dreams shared.

That is not death. That is **completion**.

---

So prepare not with gear, but with surrender. Not with curiosity, but with reverence. Not as a traveler, but as a seed.

And when you arrive—**do not move quickly**. Let the water taste you. Let the coral consider you. Let the silence decide if you belong.

Because here, you are not entering a world.

You are entering a **womb that has been waiting**.
