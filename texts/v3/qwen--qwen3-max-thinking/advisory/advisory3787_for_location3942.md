To visit the **Drowned Spires of Virel** as an ordinary human multiverse traveler—one still tethered to flesh, breath, and the fragile physics of home—is to prepare not for survival, but for **surrender**. This is not a place you conquer or document; it is a place you *attune* to. The danger here is not predation or collapse, but misalignment. Arrive with haste, noise, or intent to extract, and you will find yourself alien, unwelcome, and slowly forgotten by the place itself. But arrive with patience, humility, and a willingness to become part of its rhythm, and the Spires may let you linger—even, perhaps, remember you.

Below is your guide to preparation—not in gear alone, but in **mind, body, and gesture**.

---

### **I. Before Departure: Internal Calibration**

**1. Unlearn Urgency**  
Time in Virel is not linear but **cyclical and vegetative**. Your wristwatch, your itinerary, your “efficient” mindset—these are foreign toxins. Before stepping through the veil into the basin, practice stillness. Sit for hours without purpose. Learn the weight of waiting. Meditate on growth: not the explosive kind, but the incremental thickening of wood, the slow unfurling of a leaf. If you cannot sit quietly for an afternoon without checking a device, do not go. The Spires will sense your impatience like a fever.

**2. Reconsider “Ownership”**  
You will not build here. You will not dig, cut, or claim. Every structure is grown, tended, and returned. Bring no tools of extraction—no drills, saws, or containers for samples. Even sketching must be done with permission, using only charcoal on biodegradable bark-paper (offered by locals, if they deem you worthy). Your role is **guest**, not observer-exploiter. Internalize this: you are not here to *take knowledge*, but to *receive it*.

**3. Whisper Your Name**  
Local belief holds that the spireweave remembers names spoken into its pores. Whether this is true or not is irrelevant—it is *true enough* to shape behavior. Before arrival, choose a name you are willing to offer the place. Not your legal name, but a softer one: a childhood nickname, a word in a forgotten tongue, a sound that feels like rain on stone. Practice whispering it gently into your palm. You may be asked to speak it into the bark of a young spire upon welcome. If you refuse or mock the ritual, you will be tolerated—but never *known*.

---

### **II. Physical Preparation: What to Bring (and What to Leave)**

**Clothing:**  
- **Layered, breathable, plant-dyed fabrics** (linen, hemp, or silk). Synthetic fibers shed microplastics—unthinkable contamination.  
- **Water-resistant but not waterproof**: you *will* get wet, frequently. Embrace it. Rubber boots or sealed shoes are taboo; bare feet or soft hide sandals are expected. Tactile connection to wet wood and moss is part of integration.  
- **No bright colors**. Wear moss-green, slate-gray, bone-white, or lichen-brown. Flashy hues disturb the visual harmony and attract aggressive glass-scales (who mistake you for a predator).

**Gear:**  
- **A single copper vessel** (bowl or cup). Copper is the only metal accepted, and even then, sparingly. It must be unadorned, hammered by hand. This will be your eating/drinking vessel and possibly your offering bowl.  
- **Bone or wood utensils**—curved, polished, no sharp edges. Knives are communal and kept in resin-sealed sheaths; you will not carry one.  
- **A woven reed satchel**, no larger than your torso. Waterproofed with sap-resin, not plastic. It should hold only:  
  - A small pouch of dried lichen-bread (for trade or offering)  
  - A bone hook (as gesture of goodwill—you will not use it)  
  - Your whisper-name token (a smooth stone or carved seed)

**Absolutely Forbidden:**  
- Electronics (they corrode instantly in the mist; their hum disturbs the vascular thrum)  
- Plastic, glass, or steel  
- Perfumes, soaps, or synthetic lotions (your skin must smell of salt, sweat, and rain—nothing artificial)  
- Maps or compasses (the basin has no cardinal directions; paths shift with growth)

---

### **III. Arrival Protocol**

You will arrive at the **Mist’s Edge**, where the granite rim meets the water. Do not attempt to scale the rock. Wait. A boat—grown from buoyant gourd, lashed with vine—will come if the Spires permit. It may take hours. Do not call out. Sit on the wet stone. Observe the frogs. Let the mist settle on your skin.

When the boat arrives, it will be steered by a child or elder—never an adult in their prime. They will not speak first. You must:  
1. **Kneel** (not bow—kneel, both knees to wet wood)  
2. **Hold out your copper bowl**, empty  
3. **Whisper your chosen name** into the water  

If they take your bowl and fill it with clear water, you are welcome. Drink it slowly. If they turn away, leave quietly. Do not argue.

---

### **IV. Living in Virel: Daily Practice**

**Movement:**  
- Walk softly. Platforms flex. Sudden steps cause vibrations that alarm the spireweave—it may retract tendrils, destabilizing paths.  
- Paddle boats with flat-palmed pushes, not oars. Let your hands feel the water’s pulse.  
- Never run. Never shout. Even joy must be expressed in hums, not laughter.

**Eating & Waste:**  
- Food is shared communally: steamed glass-scale fillets, kelp-fermented tubers, spore-paste bread. Eat slowly; meals last hours.  
- Waste is composted into nutrient slurry fed directly to young spires. Urinate only in designated root-chambers (marked by blue lichen). Defecation is done submerged in deep channels—never on platforms.

**Sleep:**  
- You will be given a hollow in a buoyant trunk-chamber, lined with dried moss and frond-matting. Sleep on your back. Do not thrash. The chamber sways with water movement; fight it, and you’ll wake seasick. Surrender, and you’ll dream of root-networks.

**Interaction:**  
- Speak sparingly. Conversations are punctuated by long silences—these are not awkward, but necessary.  
- Children will touch your skin, hair, clothing. Let them. They are learning if you are real.  
- Elders may ask you to press your ear to a trunk. Do so. You will hear the thrum—the slow, wet pulse of sap and symbiosis. Do not describe it as “like a heartbeat.” It is not. It is older.

---

### **V. The Risk of Forgetting**

The greatest danger is not physical harm—it is **dissolution of self**. In the soft light, the constant sound, the slow rhythm, your edges may blur. You may forget your origin world. You may start tending spires as if you were born to it. This is not possession; it is absorption. Some travelers never return because they *choose* to stay, whispering their true name into the bark until the Spires claim them as kin.

If you wish to leave, you must:  
- Announce your intent **one full moon cycle** in advance  
- Return every borrowed item (even a dropped hairpin)  
- Perform a **parting ritual**: carve your whisper-name into a dead frond (never live wood) and let it sink  
- Leave your copper bowl behind, filled with rainwater  

Only then will a boat come to take you back to the Mist’s Edge.

---

### **Final Note**

The Drowned Spires of Virel is not a destination. It is a **state of being**. You do not visit it—you *enter into relation* with it. And like all true relationships, it demands presence, reciprocity, and the courage to be changed.

Pack light.  
Breathe deep.  
Whisper gently.

And remember: the water tastes of moss and stone—not because it lacks flavor, but because it has **remembered everything**.
