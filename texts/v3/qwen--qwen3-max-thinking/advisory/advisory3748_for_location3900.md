### **I. PHYSICAL PREPARATION**

#### **1. Respiratory Acclimatization  
**  
At this altitude, the air isn’t just thin—it’s *sharp*, like breathing ground glass filtered through silk. Begin acclimatizing **weeks in advance**:  
- Use a **hypoxic training mask** or sleep in a simulated high-altitude tent (target: ~5,000–6,000 meters).  
- Practice **diaphragmatic breathing**: shallow, rhythmic inhalations that mimic Veskal’s “sipping” rhythm. Panic or deep gasps may trigger hyperventilation or paradoxical suffocation—your body expects sea-level oxygen, but the air here offers only whispers of it.  
- Carry **portable oxygen micro-canisters** (discreet, pressurized, refillable at city air-purification kiosks). Do **not** rely on standard medical O₂—Veskal’s atmosphere contains trace geothermal volatiles (mercury vapors, hydrogen sulfide, rare earth aerosols) that require catalytic filtration.

#### **2. Thermal Regulation**  
The “coolness (1.1)” is deceptive. It won’t freeze you, but it will *leach* warmth relentlessly:  
- Wear **phase-change thermal layers**: garments embedded with microcapsules that absorb, store, and release heat as ambient temperature shifts.  
- Avoid cotton—it retains moisture and accelerates conductive cooling. Opt for **merino-aramid blends** that wick, insulate, and resist static buildup (static sparks can trigger resonance worm swarms near power conduits).  
- **Heated insoles and gloves** are essential. Veskal’s chill seeps into bone, not skin.

#### **3. Footwear & Mobility**  
The ground is *alive*. Pavements tilt. Grates sigh open. Stairs might lead to yesterday.  
- **Articulated boots** with magnetic soles and ankle gyroscopes help maintain balance on shifting surfaces.  
- Never wear rigid footwear—it won’t flex with the tectonic pulses.  
- Carry **collapsible trekking poles with seismic sensors**—they’ll vibrate subtly before a micro-uplift event.

### **II. PERCEPTUAL & COGNITIVE ADAPTATION**

#### **1. Light & Vision**  
With **dim light (0.6)**, your pupils will stay dilated. This makes sudden flashes (e.g., bioluminescent lichen flares, mirror anomalies) dangerously disorienting.  
- Wear **adaptive electrochromic lenses** that auto-tint based on ambient luminance gradients.  
- **Avoid direct eye contact with mirrors** in residential or historic sectors. If you must look, cover one eye—binocular vision increases susceptibility to temporal bleed-through.  
- Learn to “see” with your periphery. Central vision fails in the mist; motion and shadow bloom better at the edges.

#### **2. Temporal Awareness**  
Time is **non-uniform**. In magnetic alleys, your watch may tick normally while your body ages a millisecond faster—or slower.  
- Use **quantum-entangled chronometers** (available from Veskal’s Bureau of Temporal Cartography) synced to the city’s “baseline resonance.”  
- Never schedule meetings within 100 meters of a fault line without confirming temporal sync with your contact.  
- If you feel “stuck” (e.g., repeating the same sentence, watching the same fog wisp drift for minutes), **hum loudly**. Sonic vibration disrupts localized time loops.

#### **3. Gravity Sensitivity**  
Near uplift zones, gravity may **pulse**—0.98g one moment, 1.03g the next.  
- Practice **microgravity balance drills**: standing on unstable surfaces, shifting weight constantly.  
- Carry a **personal gravimeter pendant**—it glows amber when local g-forces deviate beyond safe thresholds.  
- If objects hover when dropped, **do not reach for them**. Wait. Let physics resolve itself.

### **III. CLOTHING & EQUIPMENT**

#### **Essential Gear:**  
- **Modular urban survival vest**: with compartments for oxygen, emergency rations (see below), signal flares (infrared—visible light scatters in mist), and a **seismic earpiece** that translates tectonic groans into audible warnings.  
- **Bioluminescent ink tattoos**: temporary, water-soluble. Apply directional arrows or “safe path” sigils on your arms—useful when mist turns streets into voids.  
- **Resonance-dampening earplugs**: not for silence, but to filter harmful frequencies. The city’s “hymn” can entrap unwary minds in harmonic trances.

#### **Avoid:**  
- Electronics with unshielded circuits—they may synchronize with resonance worms and overheat.  
- Perfumes or strong scents—they interfere with ghost-pine pollen navigation (locals use scent trails to orient in fog).  
- Metal jewelry (except titanium or obsidian)—ferrous metals attract magnetic anomalies.

### **IV. SUSTENANCE & HYDRATION**

Water is **fugitive (1.1)**—present but elusive.  
- Carry a **condensation harvester**: a collapsible dome lined with hydrophilic mesh that collects dew from your own breath and ambient mist overnight.  
- Drink only **filtered geothermal condensate** or city-sanctioned meltwater. Untreated sources may contain dissolved heavy metals or temporal isotopes.  
- Eat **dense, slow-metabolizing foods**: lichen cakes, compressed protein wafers infused with ghost-pine resin (a natural mild stimulant that sharpens focus in thin air).  
- **Never consume resonance worm secretions**—rumored to grant prophetic visions, but often cause “chronal vertigo.”

### **V. SOCIAL & CULTURAL PROTOCOLS**

Veskalians are **pragmatic mystics**. They don’t fear the weird—they *maintain* it.  
- **Greet with open palms**—showing tools or weapons implies you believe the city is hostile. It isn’t. It’s just *unfinished*.  
- **Never mock instability**. Saying “this bridge looks unsafe” is like calling someone’s child ugly. Bridges *know*.  
- Learn **three phrases**:  
  - *“The stone dreams upward.”* (Acknowledges the mountain’s agency.)  
  - *“I walk with my weight.”* (Means: I accept gravity’s terms.)  
  - *“The mist is thick, but not blind.”* (A request for guidance.)  
- **Do not photograph mirrors**. Images may capture echoes that “leak” into your home universe.

### **VI. PSYCHOLOGICAL PREPARATION**

Veskal induces **ontological vertigo**—the sensation that reality is provisional.  
- Before arrival, practice **grounding meditations** focused on impermanence: watch ice melt, sand shift, light fade. Build tolerance for flux.  
- Bring a **touchstone object** from your home world—something dense, familiar, unchanging (a river stone, a cast-iron key). Hold it when the pavement flickers.  
- Accept that **you will get lost**—not spatially, but *temporally*. You may exit a shop and find your hotel has tilted 20 degrees since breakfast. This is not failure. It is participation.

Leaving Veskal is harder than arriving.  
- Your body may retain **resonant echoes**: humming at turbine frequencies, dreaming in tectonic time.  
- Undergo **de-resonance therapy** before returning to stable worlds: immersion in still water, silence, and flat, unchanging horizons.  
- **Do not bring back souvenirs** from “thin places.” A shard of vibrating basalt might unravel your home’s spacetime over months.
