To visit this place—the hollow cathedral of breath, sap, and rust—you must prepare not with gear alone, but with a recalibration of self. This is not a destination to be conquered, explored, or documented in the conventional sense. It is a space that *perceives*, in its own vegetal, osmotic way. To enter it unprepared is to risk becoming a stain, a disruption, or worse—unnoticed, absorbed without consent into its slow metabolism. What follows is a guide not for survival, but for *belonging*, even briefly, within a world that has renounced the human rhythm.

---

### **1. Shed the Illusion of Separation**
The first and most essential preparation is psychological. You must arrive believing—truly believing—that you are not a visitor *to* nature, but a transient particle *within* a living architecture. The green here does not tolerate observers; it integrates or excludes. If you carry the mindset of a scientist, a photographer, or an adventurer, you will remain alien. Instead, cultivate humility akin to entering a temple built by minds older and quieter than your own.  

Practice stillness. Not the stillness of waiting, but of *listening with your whole body*. In the days before your visit, spend hours motionless in any green space—even a city park—and learn to feel the pulse of photosynthesis, the sway of transpiration, the weight of humidity on your skin. This is not metaphor. In the hollow, these rhythms are the only clock.

---

### **2. Attire: Neutral, Non-Toxic, and Biodegradable**
Wear only natural fibers—linen, undyed cotton, or hemp—washed in rainwater or distilled water (no detergents, no synthetics). Avoid anything with zippers, nylon, or plastic components; even the trace chemicals in modern fabrics may disrupt the microbial balance or be interpreted as a foreign body by the ecosystem. Shoes are unnecessary and counterproductive. The ground is not solid earth but a living sponge; tread barefoot to feel the fibrous mat shift beneath you, to allow your skin to exchange moisture and microbes with the place.  

Your clothing should be muted in color—greys, moss-greens, ochres—but not camouflaged. You are not hiding. You are *admitting your presence*. The hollow does not fear you; it will assess you. Let it see you clearly.

---

### **3. Hydration and Nourishment**
Do not bring food or drink into the hollow. The place sustains itself, but it does not feed guests. Any organic matter you introduce—crumbs, sweat, even breath—will be analyzed, incorporated, or rejected. If you must drink, do so before entry, and rinse your mouth with clean water to reduce microbial exchange.  

Likewise, do not relieve yourself within the hollow. Its water cycle is closed, sacred. Your bodily emissions will not decompose as they would elsewhere; they may trigger defensive chemical responses in the flora or distort the delicate mineral balance. Honor its internal logic.

---

### **4. Tools of Presence, Not Intrusion**
Bring only what enhances perception without altering the environment:
- **A water-clear glass vial** (no plastic, no metal) to catch a drop of morning condensation, if offered. Do not pluck leaves or break stems.
- **A notebook of handmade, unbleached paper** and a graphite pencil (no ink, which may bleed toxins). Write sparingly. The act of writing should be a meditation, not a record.
- **A small brass or copper bell**, untarnished, to ring once upon entry and once upon departure. Not as a warning, but as an offering of vibration—a human-scale frequency introduced gently into the green hum. Do not ring it again inside. Let the place speak in its own register.

Leave all electronics behind. Not because they might fail—though they will, fogged by humidity, corroded by airborne tannins—but because their signals, their pulses, their very *intentionality* are alien to this realm. The hollow runs on osmotic gradients and phototropic memory, not binary logic.

---

### **5. Timing and Threshold**
Enter only at dawn, when condensation beads most heavily and the air is rinsed clean of night’s stillness. This is when the hollow is most receptive—when its pores are open, its breath exhaled.  

Do not cross the threshold abruptly. Sit at the edge for at least an hour. Let vines near you shift slightly, as if testing your patience. When a passage seems to *suggest* itself—not parting, but thinning—follow it. Never force. If no path forms, withdraw. The hollow is not always open.

Stay no longer than one full cycle of light—from dawn to dusk. To remain overnight is to risk being woven in. There are stories of travelers who slept beneath the canopy and woke rooted, their hair tangled with lianas, their joints stiff with lignin. Whether myth or memory matters less than the truth it carries: time here is not yours.

---

### **6. Expect No Fauna, Trust No Reflection**
You will see no birds, hear no frogs, find no tracks. Do not interpret this as emptiness. The hollow has chosen a different path—one of autotrophic solitude. If you feel watched, it is not by eyes, but by stomata, by hyphae, by the slow attention of a thousand leaves adjusting their angle to your heat.  

When you glimpse your reflection in a leaf-slick puddle, do not be startled if it moves a half-second behind you. This is not a trick of light. The hollow does not mirror—it *interprets*. Your image is being processed, composted into the visual grammar of the place.

---

### **7. Departure Ritual**
As you leave, walk backward for the first ten paces. Not out of fear, but to show you are not turning your back on the place. Then, at the threshold, place your palm flat on the nearest living surface—a fused trunk, a moss-wrapped gear—and hold it until you feel the subtle thrum of transpiration against your skin. This is your thanks.  

Do not speak of the hollow immediately upon return. Wait three days. Let its moisture evaporate from your clothes, its silence settle in your bones. When you finally tell someone, do not say, “I saw…” Say, “I was allowed…”

---

### **Final Warning**
This place does not forget. If you enter with greed, haste, or arrogance, you may leave with none of those—but also with less of yourself. Some return with dreams of rust-colored sap, with an aversion to straight lines, with the compulsive need to touch every leaf they pass. Others never truly leave; they carry the hollow inside them, a quiet green pulse beneath the ribs.

Come only if you are willing to be composted—not in body, but in perspective. The hollow does not want your worship. It wants your witness. And even that, only if you can witness without consuming.

Go gently. You are walking into a lung.
