To visit a city that breathes like a forest—a living, photosynthesizing organism with its own circadian rhythms, silent pulse, and centuries-long memory—is not merely a journey through space, but a negotiation with presence, patience, and perception. An ordinary human multiverse traveler, accustomed perhaps to neon-lit arcs of quantum highways or bustling cross-dimensional bazaars, must prepare not with gear, but with grace. Here’s how you ready yourself—not to survive, but to be worthy of passage.

---

### **I. Purify Your Intentions**
This place does not tolerate intrusion disguised as tourism. Before you even consider stepping through its moss-cushioned threshold, ask yourself: *Why am I here?*  
- If your motive is extraction—of data, samples, secrets, or aesthetic inspiration—you will be gently, irrevocably excluded. The city senses intent as clearly as roots sense water.  
- Come only with reverence, curiosity tempered by humility, and a willingness to be *changed* rather than to conquer.  
- Leave behind any notion of “discovery.” This is not terra incognita; it is *terra sapientia*—land that knows.

---

### **II. Shed the Noise of Your World**
The city thrives on silence, not as absence, but as a medium of communication. Your arrival must not disrupt its delicate acoustics.

**Before departure:**
- Practice silence for at least three days. Not just speech, but internal chatter—learn to walk without the clatter of mental narration.
- Remove all emitting devices: no comms, no chronometers, no pulse trackers. Even passive sensors may trigger subtle rejection mechanisms (a vine tightening, a path shifting).
- If you must carry something, wrap it in untreated linen or unbleached cotton. Metal should be dulled, edges rounded. Nothing should *glint*.

**Wear clothing that breathes with the forest:**
- Natural fibers only—hemp, undyed wool, barkcloth. No synthetics. Dyes must be plant-based and faded, not vibrant.
- Colors should echo the palette of shadowed bark, damp moss, and fog: slate greens, warm greys, deep umbers.
- Footwear must be soft-soled, flexible, and silent. Better yet: go barefoot once inside, if your feet are calloused enough. The mulch-path will reject stiff soles—they feel like stone to the mycelial network beneath.

---

### **III. Understand the Rhythms, Not the Rules**
There are no posted signs, no maps, no guides. The city communicates through subtle cues:

- **Light shifts** indicate where passage is welcomed. If resin-glass panes glow golden-green in your direction, proceed. If the dapples retreat or darken, pause.
- **Airflow matters.** The city breathes inward at dawn, outward at dusk. Arrive near first light, when the fog is still pooling in the cistern-nets and the salamanders are active. Depart before full dark.
- **Never block a channel.** The moss-lined waterways, the vine-conduits, the root-grids—they are veins. Standing in one for more than a few breaths may cause the path to *re-route* around you… permanently.
- **Do not touch walls without invitation.** The tessara vine recognizes skin chemistry. A handprint may trigger a defensive thickening or, worse, a memory imprint that lingers for decades—your DNA woven into its bark.

---

### **IV. Prepare Your Body as a Guest, Not a Guest-User**
This city metabolizes interaction.

- **Fast lightly** before arrival. Arrive neither hungry nor full. A small meal of plain nuts, steamed tubers, or unsweetened grain is acceptable. Nothing fermented, spiced, or preserved with chemicals.
- **Hydrate with distilled or rainwater only.** Tap water from industrial worlds carries trace metals and isotopes that may alarm the ecosystem.
- **Your breath is your loudest signature.** Learn to breathe slowly, evenly, through your nose. Rapid or erratic breathing registers as distress—or threat.
- If you feel unwell (fever, rash, infection), *do not enter*. Microbial imbalances are sensed instantly. You may unwittingly introduce a blight.

---

### **V. Rituals of Arrival and Departure**
There is a threshold—not marked by gate or arch, but by a change in air pressure and scent. Just before it, you’ll smell petrichor mixed with resin and warm copper.

**Upon arrival:**
- Kneel. Not in submission, but in alignment. Place your palms flat on the mulch. Breathe in sync with the slow drip from a nearby cistern.
- Whisper your name—just once—to the nearest vine. It won’t remember you, but it will remember your tone.
- Wait. For at least ten minutes. Let the city decide if it will let you pass. If a salamander crosses your path, it’s a welcome. If the bioluminescent lichen dims as you approach, retreat.

**Upon departure:**
- Return to your arrival point.
- Brush off all organic matter from your clothing—moss spores, bark flecks, pollen. Leave nothing behind but your footprints, which the mulch will absorb within hours.
- Offer nothing. Gifts are not needed. But if you feel compelled, leave a single drop of your sweat on a clean stone—your salt, your water, your life, given freely without expectation.
- Walk backward ten steps as you leave. Not to look back, but to honor the turning away.

---

### **VI. Psychological and Temporal Adjustments**
Time moves differently here. Not slower—*differently*. Your internal clock will desynchronize.

- Expect mild disorientation: the sun’s path may seem wrong, your sense of duration distorted. This is normal. The city’s circadian hum (that faint green pulse at night) can entrain your nervous system within 48 hours.
- Do not try to “document” your experience. Memory here is sensory, not narrative. You’ll recall textures, temperatures, the weight of silence—but not linear events.
- If you feel watched, you are—but not by eyes. The tessara vine has phototropic sensitivity; the root-grid conducts faint bioelectric fields. You are *felt*, not seen.
- Should you dream while here, assume the city is speaking. Dreams may contain images of ironwood grain, falling condensate, or the sound of a single reed note. Do not interpret—remember.

---

### **VII. What to Bring (and What Never To)**
**Bring:**
- A smooth river stone from your own world (unmarked, unworked). If permitted, you may place it in a quiet corner as a token of reciprocity—*only if the air stills in approval*.
- A journal made of tree-free paper (hemp or algae-based), with a charcoal pencil. Write only after you’ve left.
- A small vial of your native soil. Not to exchange, but to remind yourself where you came from, so you don’t forget to return.

**Never bring:**
- Fire (even electronic “flame”).
- Plastic, silicon, or rare-earth metals.
- Seeds, spores, or pollen from other worlds.
- Recordings of birdsong or music—they will confuse the sonic emitters.
- Expectations.

---

### **VIII. Afterward: Integration, Not Extraction**
You will leave changed. The city does not allow neutral passage.

- For the next moon-cycle, avoid cities of steel and glass. Your senses will be too raw.
- You may find yourself craving damp air, walking barefoot on soil, speaking in whispers.
- Do not speak of the city to those who have not walked its paths. Describe it only in metaphor, never in coordinates. Say: *“I visited a place that remembered how to breathe.”*
- If, years later, you wake with the scent of weeping pine resin in your room and no source—do not fear. The city remembers you, too.

---

This is not a destination. It is a covenant.  
And the only true preparation is to become someone the forest would allow to pass—  
quietly,  
carefully,  
and never twice the same.
