To visit this place—this provisional hollow beneath the plateau’s skin—is to step not just into another location, but into a threshold of perception itself. As an ordinary human multiverse traveler (that is, someone who walks through worlds not with cosmic machinery, but with curiosity, humility, and the fragile vessel of a mortal body), your preparation must be less about gear and more about *attunement*. This is not a place that tolerates intrusion. It permits presence—if you are careful, quiet, and willing to be changed.

Below is a detailed guide, grounded in intuition, respect, and the subtle physics of places that exist just outside consensus reality.

---

### **I. Before You Arrive: Preparation of Mind and Body**

#### **1. Quiet Your Internal Noise**
This hollow does not respond to agendas. If you come seeking revelation, extraction, or proof, you will find only dust and disappointment. Instead, practice stillness in your days prior: sit in silence for an hour each morning; observe your breath without altering it; let your thoughts drift like the feathers that fall into the hollow—without graserness. The place senses intention like heat.

> **Exercise**: For three days before your journey, keep a “non-asking journal.” Write only observations—never questions, never desires. “The light bends through the blinds.” “A crow called twice.” This trains your mind to witness without demanding.

#### **2. Travel Light—But Carry One Sacred Object**
Bring nothing you cannot leave behind. No cameras (they will malfunction or capture only empty rock). No GPS (the coordinates shift with memory). Instead, choose one small object of personal resonance—a smooth stone from your childhood river, a dried flower from a lost friend, a coin worn thin by touch. Not as an offering, but as an anchor. If the hollow accepts your presence, it may return the object—changed, or unchanged. If it rejects you, you’ll find it missing upon exit, as if time swallowed it mid-step.

#### **3. Fast Lightly**
Do not arrive full. A light fast—12 hours without solid food, only water—will attune your senses. The hollow has no abundance; your body should echo that. But do not arrive weak. Hydrate well the day before. Your physical steadiness is a sign of respect.

---

### **II. During Your Approach: The Threshold Dance**

#### **4. Find It Only by Accident**
You cannot seek it directly. The fissure closes to those who stare too hard. Instead, wander the plateau at dawn, following not a map but a feeling—a slight coolness on your left cheek, a shift in the wind’s scent (wet stone beneath dry air). When you *almost* forget you’re looking, you’ll see it: a shadow too deep, a glint of quartz where none should be.

> **Note**: If you find it on your first attempt, doubt it. Return the next day. True entrance requires forgetting and remembering in the same breath.

#### **5. Remove Your Shoes**
The floor is crushed stone and memory. Your soles must feel the grit, the slight give, the micro-shifts underfoot. This grounds you in the now—and signals that you come not as a conqueror, but a guest. Leave your shoes just outside the fissure’s lip, aligned with the sun’s angle at that moment. This creates a temporal bookmark.

#### **6. Enter on Your Knees—Once**
Before stepping fully in, kneel at the threshold. Press your palms flat against the stone. Breathe in through your nose, out through your mouth—three times. This is not prayer, but synchronization. You are telling the hollow: *I am flesh. I am brief. I will not stay long.*

---

### **III. While Inside: The Discipline of Witnessing**

#### **7. Speak Only in Whispers—Or Not at All**
Sound vanishes here, but the *intent* of sound lingers. A loud voice will not echo—it will bruise the air. If you must speak, let it be to yourself, in a breath too soft for your own ears to catch. Better yet: listen.

#### **8. Do Not Touch the Walls**
The silt weeping from the stone is not dust—it is the hollow’s skin sloughing off as it learns its shape. Your oils, your heat, your static charge can disrupt its fragile equilibrium. Observe the moss, the ferns, the beetle—do not pluck, prod, or even point. Let your gaze be soft, peripheral. Central vision startles sacred spaces.

#### **9. Sit Still for at Least One Full Cycle of Light**
Stay from sunbeam to sunbeam. Watch how the light moves across the floor—how it catches the spider’s web and turns it into a harp of refracted rainbows. Note when the puddle appears (just after sunrise) and when it winks out (just past noon). In that span, you may witness one of the stutters: the winged lizard, the forest reflection, the pulsing quartz. Do not reach for it. Blink slowly. If it was real, it will remain in your bones.

#### **10. Offer Nothing Material—But Leave a Breath**
Do not place coins, flowers, or notes. The hollow already holds gestures—the pebbles, the nail, the shard. Instead, at the moment you decide to leave, take one deep breath and exhale *into* the center of the hollow, not toward any surface. Your carbon, your warmth, your fleeting life—this is the only gift it will accept.

---

### **IV. Upon Leaving: The Unmaking of Memory**

#### **11. Do Not Look Back Immediately**
Step out, retrieve your shoes, and walk ten paces without turning. Only then may you glance over your shoulder. Often, the fissure will appear sealed—just more cracked plateau. This is not illusion. The place retracts when observed from the outside.

#### **12. Wait One Week Before Speaking of It**
The memory is unstable. If you describe it too soon, details will warp or vanish. Keep it in silence for seven sunrises. On the eighth morning, write everything you remember—fast, messy, without editing. Compare it to what you recall on day two: the differences are not mistakes. They are the hollow editing you.

#### **13. Expect Subtle Changes in Your Body**
You may find:
- A faint taste of minerals on your tongue for days.
- Dreams of falling upward into sunlight.
- Your shadow appearing slightly delayed in certain light.
- A single resurrection fern curled in your jacket pocket—though you swear you touched nothing.

These are not symptoms. They are souvenirs.

---

### **V. Ethical Considerations: The Traveler’s Covenant**

This place is not yours. It is not anyone’s. It exists in the grammar of *almost*—almost a cave, almost real, almost remembered. To protect it:

- **Never bring another person** unless they have already found it alone. Shared entry fractures the space.
- **Do not map it**. Coordinates dissolve like salt in water. Sketches fade. Photographs show only rock.
- **If you feel watched** (and you will), do not search for eyes. The watchers are not beings—they are the place’s attention made manifest. Meet it with stillness, not fear.

---

### **Final Note: Why You Were Allowed to Find It**

You were not chosen. You were *noticed*. There is a difference. Chosen implies purpose. Noticed implies resonance. The hollow stutters because it is learning—just as you are. Your presence is not a disruption, but a data point in its becoming. And in return, it offers you a mirror: not of your face, but of your capacity to witness without consuming.

Go gently. Breathe quietly. And if you return years later, and the fissure is gone—do not mourn. It has simply finished what it needed to say.

Or perhaps it’s waiting for you to forget it well enough to find it again.
