The power did not descend.

Descent would have chosen an above and a below, and the plateau had suffered enough from directions that disagreed. Nor did it appear at the horizon, where anything large became indistinguishable from the reclining beings. It did not take the ordinary sun for an eye or speak through all the mouths at once. Such gestures would have been easy, and therefore unwise.

It attended.

For a span that could not be measured, the power regarded the plateau without touching it. It learned the long-backed herds as they learned themselves: not as multitudes, not as one distributed body, but as the exact tension between those truths. It perceived each clasping predator, each wound held open without injury, each scavenger carrying the unused image of decay. It followed the shell colonies inward, past mites and rings and hooked grains, past chambers inhabited by chambers, until scale ceased to be a useful form of humility.

It attended the suspended wings without asking them to fly.

It attended the newborns without urging them to grow.

It attended the horizon-beings without naming them.

It did not mistake stillness for imprisonment merely because it possessed the power to move. It did not mistake persistence for consent merely because nothing cried out.

The distinction mattered.

Many things on the plateau had no desire for change. Desire belongs partly to succession: first an absence, then the thought of what might fill it. The animals lived otherwise. The tooth at the hollow in the hide did not wish to bite or refrain. The wing did not long for distance. The unconsumed prey did not await rescue. Their relations were not unfinished acts. They were the organs by which the whole place knew its shape.

To separate them indiscriminately would not have been liberation.

A lesser mercy might have made rain. It would have seen the dry mouths and filled them, seen the old channels and restored their flow. But rain would have required falling, striking, gathering, passing. It would have entered the powder and forced the footprint to choose between before and after. It would have loosened the talus, softened the ceramic roots, blurred the white chips that distinguished insect from cliff. It would have brought weather into a country whose smallest identities depended on weather’s absence.

A lesser mercy might have moved the sun.

Then shadows would have left their creatures. The copper fans would have lost the lives reflected on their blades. North-facing scars would have dragged across bodies like cuts. Night would have fallen everywhere at once, and many things that existed only by permanent visibility would have become synonymous in the dark.

A lesser mercy might have granted death.

But death, too, is an event. Here it would not have released the living. It would have stood beside them, another complete posture added to birth, feeding, predation, and rest. The plateau would only have grown more crowded.

The power understood this.

So it looked for suffering that was not merely strange.

It found some.

Not in the jaws enclosing necks. Not in the hands beneath scales. Not in the young who remained young beside their elder forms. It found suffering in the makers, though even there it was fine enough to be mistaken for devotion.

Their labor had no interval.

Each maker held some difference in place: this horn and not all horns; this child and not every form of the child; this hollow with its own depth; this path connecting these two stones and no others. The work was delicate, local, and without completion. If a maker lowered a screen, the framed animal began to spread into its repetitions. If a line of chips went unattended, the insect and cliff approached a resemblance from which neither might return. If a hand relaxed too fully, its fingers ceased to know how many they had been.

The makers could rest in posture.

They could not rest in responsibility.

Among them, the power found the summit sitter.

The figure occupied the narrow chair with closed eyes and hands laid upon its knees. Around it, the tools waited in their exact disorder. At intervals that did not pass, one hand lifted a shell lens or copper point and touched the air.

Something somewhere became singular.

A pale animal separated from the pale idea of the herd.

A wing acquired one body.

An opened eye retained an eyelid of its own.

The sitter held these distinctions briefly. Other makers secured them with walls, bowls, chips, hooks, or acts of looking. Then the sitter lifted another tool.

The power watched.

At first nothing changed. The plateau had always been attended, and another attention, however vast, was still attention. But the power’s regard differed from the regard already present.

It did not require the sitter to perform.

The sitter’s work was made of selection. This creature. This surface. This count of fingers. The benevolent power selected nothing. It regarded the sitter in every version without confusing those versions and without forcing them apart. It knew the child lying beneath the tendon roof, the thin adult placing white chips, the aged figure upon the summit, and the maker whose track began before its foot. It knew them as one being, many beings, and the unresolved mercy between.

For the first time, the sitter was itself clarified.

No screen framed it.

No chip connected it to a cliff.

No hook attached its name to the jaw of an animal.

It was held in distinction by nothing made on the plateau.

The sitter’s right hand lifted toward a green ceramic flake.

Then stopped.

Stopping was common there, but this was not the old stillness. The old stillness contained the complete act. This hand contained an act that was no longer required.

The fingers changed from six to five.

Then from five to four.

Then, very slowly, the hand closed.

Far below, a white beast began to spread through its herd. The boundaries of its flank softened. Its clouded eye appeared in several neighboring skulls.

The power looked at it.

Not more strongly. Strength was irrelevant.

It looked exactly.

The white beast remained one beast.

Its counterparts remained its counterparts. The herd retained its shared stomachs and distant hearing. Nothing was severed. Yet the clouded eye belonged locally to the creature that bore it. Its split hoof was neither erased nor repeated. For the duration of that regard, the animal required no wall, frame, or summit tool to be itself.

The sitter opened its eyes.

Its branching pupils contracted around the ordinary sun. The narrow mouth parted. Its divided tongue moved once against the lower teeth, but the sound it made was not one of the clicks used to identify a silence.

It was a breath.

The transparent inhabitants of the air entered its chest. Until then, breath had remained beside breath, none replacing another. But this inhalation came after an emptiness and was followed by a release.

The exhalation crossed the sitter’s lower lip.

Powder stirred beside the chair.

Only three grains moved.

They traveled less than the width of a mite’s body. One struck the base of a shell lens. One settled in the shallow mark left by a tool. One did not come down.

Across the plateau, every wing felt the movement.

None flew.

Every spoon-shaped ear received it.

None turned.

The makers raised their heads.

The power did not speak in language. Language there would have become architecture, and each word would have required a shelter to keep it from meaning all words. Instead, it offered an arrangement.

Beside each maker appeared two stones.

One was dark.

One was pale.

Paired stones already meant that a journey could occur between them. But these pairs differed from the path markers. The dark stone lay behind the pale at every angle. Viewed from the opposite side, it remained behind. Viewed from above, it lay beneath. Closed in the hand, it occupied the place the hand had just ceased to touch.

The makers approached them cautiously.

One extended a five-fingered hand, required a sixth, and found that no sixth finger came. The pale stone was lifted anyway. It was lighter than the act demanded.

Beneath it, the mineral surface held no corresponding impression.

This frightened the maker.

The absence of a mark was not itself unusual. Many absences inhabited the plateau. But this one had not been preserved by a body. It was merely where the stone had been, and now was not.

The maker set the stone down.

The stone returned to the ground, but not to its former position.

The first position remained intelligible without remaining present.

That was the gift.

Not time, for time could not be poured upon the plateau without drowning it in sequence. Not change, which would have spread catastrophically through beings whose anatomies crossed distance and scale.

The power introduced release.

It made a distinction between what must be preserved and what may be remembered.

Until then, every instant had remained materially beside the next because nothing could be trusted to depart without being lost. The untouched powder persisted beside the footprint. The unopened eye remained within the opened one. Every near-gesture required a place, and the creatures nesting in alternatives had grown populous on the accumulation of all that had not ceased.

The power did not take these prior states away.

It gave them somewhere else to be.

Not another country. The plateau could not support an outside without folding it into the horizon. Not a vault, paradise, archive, or mind. Such places would only reproduce the burden at a hidden scale.

It gave them trustworthy absence.

An event might end and remain true.

A touch might cease without having never occurred.

A maker might lower a hand, and the distinction held by that hand could survive as having been held.

This was a small alteration. The power made it small deliberately.

The plateau resisted.

Along the central scarps, the old channels brightened with mutually incompatible courses. Suspended flocks thickened as abandoned wingbeats sought bodies. Powder showed prints belonging to feet that had not yet decided whether to lift. The scavengers’ transparent bellies filled with images of skins no longer required to remain available.

For one dangerous instant, the seated absence among the herds opened what might have been a mouth.

Every animal facing it displayed the same eye.

The power did not command the instability to stop. Command would have made obedience one more fixed relation. Instead, it took upon itself the work the makers could not safely lay down.

It remembered without retaining.

This was something the plateau had never known.

Its remembrance was complete but did not occupy space. It did not crowd the present. It did not demand that the former thing remain accessible as matter. What had been could be absent and still be loved precisely.

Under that regard, the bright channels dimmed.

The unused wingbeats did not enter the birds. They became past flight.

The tracks settled behind their feet.

The seated absence closed its mouth, if it had one.

Then the power established no law, but a custom.

At any one place, for any one being, one thing might be allowed to finish.

No more than one.

Wisdom refused abundance even in mercy.

A shell might release a mite, and the opened chamber need not eternally contain the moment before release.

One copper blade might cease reflecting its assigned wing.

One predator might withdraw one tooth from one hollow, not severing predator from prey, but allowing the contact to become an act rather than an everlasting anatomy.

One maker might complete one knot.

The custom moved slowly across the plateau. It did not spread by distance. It passed by care.

The first to use it was not the summit sitter.

It was a curled insect holding an antler branch in its mouth.

For a duration shaped like years, the capsule had occupied both gripping and release. Its jaws were open and closed; the branch held and unheld. Makers had built no marker around it. No animal watched. Its uncertainty was too small for the plateau’s broad geometries.

The power attended it.

The insect opened its mouth.

The branch remained where it was, fitted among the others.

The insect fell.

It fell only the length of its own body, struck the mineral ground, righted itself, and took three steps.

The steps came one after another.

After the third, it stopped.

Its first position was no longer present.

Nothing replaced it there.

The plateau did not collapse.

A maker nearby lowered itself until its face almost touched the insect. Its branching pupils widened. A sixth finger emerged, then a seventh, as though the hand required new anatomy merely to understand so modest a journey.

The maker placed a pierced bowl over the insect, then reconsidered.

It removed the bowl.

The insect took a fourth step.

The maker made the new sound: a breath entering, a breath leaving.

Elsewhere, the custom reached a suspended feather.

The feather descended.

Not the bird. Not the flock. One loose feather, attached until then to every available instant of its attachment, separated and drifted through the still air. It turned once. Its shadow crossed a copper fan. For the first time the blade’s reflection differed from the thing reflected.

The feather settled upon the back of an armored animal.

The animal closed one eye.

Afterward, it opened it.

The closure did not remain inside the open eye.

Across the uplands, makers began to sit down.

Some had always sat, but this was different. They ceased a task and did not preserve their hands in the shape of continuing it. Their fingers withdrew to five, or four, or whatever number belonged to no work. The power held the unattended distinctions while they rested.

It did not hold them forever.

That would merely have replaced the makers with a kinder jailer.

Instead it taught the distinctions a limited resilience. A creature, once precisely known, could retain some singularity without continuous external labor. Not absolute singularity—the plateau’s beings were truly shared, folded, repeated, and communal. Mercy did not require converting them into sealed individuals. It only meant they need not be incessantly prevented from vanishing into totality.

The white beast remained part of its herd and also itself.

The maker remained all its ages and also the one now seated beside two stones.

The shell was colony, province, aperture, and shell.

The horizon-being was not clarified.

The power left it so.

There are immensities that benevolence does not reduce for the comfort of understanding.

Last, the power returned its attention to the summit.

The sitter had risen from the chair.

The polished seat stood empty, though emptiness there had never meant vacancy. The sitter faced the fixed sun. Its pale wrappings made small sounds against one another.

It looked down at the plateau.

Here a feather lay where it had not always lain.

There an insect’s four prints followed its body.

Farther away, a predator’s jaw remained around a neck, but one tooth had been withdrawn. The hollow it had occupied was visible, clean, and dark. Neither animal blurred.

The sitter touched the back of the chair.

Its hand had five fingers.

It lifted the hand.

The touch ended.

The chair remained polished, but the act of touching it did not remain beside the untouched stone. The sitter trembled. Trembling had previously existed only as a complete arrangement of positions. This tremor traveled from shoulder to wrist and was gone.

The power offered no instruction.

It made no demand that the sitter descend, sleep, abandon the work, or accept a future. A benevolent power does not force movement upon those shaped by stillness and call the result freedom.

The sitter chose.

It sat once more.

For a moment, the power may have wondered whether anything had been changed.

Then the sitter leaned back.

The chair had not been built to support that posture. Its narrow back ended below the shoulders. The maker’s long head tilted until it rested against the lintel’s shadow.

Its eyes closed.

Previously, closed eyes had contained open ones. Every act of seeing remained available behind the lids.

Now the sitter ceased to see.

The power saw it.

That was enough.

A darkness appeared beneath the four pillars.

It was no larger than the structure and did not come from the sun. It covered the chair, the tiny implements, the maker’s resting hands. At its boundary, permanent daylight continued unchanged.

This was the first night on the plateau.

It lasted for one sleeper.

Within it, no creature nested in the moment before sleep. No cup collected the almost-gesture of waking. The sitter’s identity did not spread into the herds or the cliffs. The power attended without summoning, remembered without fixing, and waited without making waiting a prison.

The sleeper dreamed.

Its dream did not become architecture.

No animal was required to embody it. No scar copied its shape. No distant shelf rose to accommodate its impossible valley. It remained private, unbuilt and unshared.

This, more than movement, was the mercy: for the first time, something belonged to one being without requiring that being to be held apart from all others.

When the sitter woke, the darkness ended.

It did not remain beside daylight.

Only the memory of rest remained, and memory occupied no chair.

The power then withdrew, though withdrawal did not mean inattention. Its regard ceased to be a presence the plateau had to support. It left no temple, no commandment, no new species carrying its name. The fifth pillar was not added. The sun did not move.

But from then on, night traveled.

Never broadly. Never as weather.

It gathered beneath one shelter where a maker laid down its hooks. It enclosed one newborn while all its other ages continued their tasks. It entered a shell so a mite might cease attending the dark center around which it had always stood. Once, it appeared in the enormous open eye of a horizon-being, though whether that being slept could not be known.

And wherever that small night went, the power took up no throne.

It simply kept watch.

A few things began to happen.

Nothing was forced to.

The chord remained sounded in the cool, ordinary light, but now a note could fall silent without being erased from the music. Another could enter after it. Between them appeared an interval that was not an inhabitant, not a posture, not a body made of exclusion.

It was room.

Not much.

Enough.
