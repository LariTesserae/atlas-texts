The power does not bring rain.

Rain would be an extinction.

It does not cool the stone, soften the air, sow the shelves with green, or fill the fractures with water. These would be kindnesses only to another world. Here they would poison catalytic blood, drown the plate-worm colonies, burst hot organs with steam, and turn the black carbide basins into fields of rust.

Nor does the power silence the industrial noise. It understands soon enough that the hammering is speech, circulation, boundary, warning, and perhaps thought. To quiet the plateau would be to lay a hand over its mouth.

The power does not descend as a star.

Too many mouths are already open.

Instead it remains beyond every direction by which it could be approached, and attends.

It watches one full White Circuit. Then another. It follows a fragment down a ratchet line for fourteen thousand years. It learns the pressure grammar of the kiln animals: the declarative stamp, the doubled refusal, the long subsurface tremor by which an old animal announces that its next step will occur in three places.

It waits beneath interval grazers without being delayed. It permits convergents to close paths around its attention and discovers that even a benevolent gaze can become a kind of hunting if it insists upon understanding.

So it ceases to insist.

It allows some mysteries to remain alive.

Only after many cycles does the power perceive what is wrong.

Not wrong as drought is wrong for a forest, nor as predation is wrong to the animal being eaten. The plateau has no wish to become gentle. Its furnace lives do not secretly yearn for blood or leaves. They are not failed versions of softer creatures.

What is wrong is smaller.

Hidden beneath the forty-three-second hammer is a second impact.

It has always been there, following the first too closely for any native organ to distinguish. The two pulses have traveled together through every exposed age. One is the plateau’s deep rhythm. The other is an answer that began long ago and has never finished arriving.

Something under the White Circuit is attempting to respond.

The Circuit’s great operation keeps that answer from tearing the plateau apart. Every seventy years the fauna gather, not by command and not entirely by instinct, to redistribute the unfinished pressure. Kilns take it into their furnaces. Sky-borers extend it across adjacent hours. Ratchets divide it into fragments too small to rupture stone. Interval grazers provide the extra duration in which these acts can be completed.

The process succeeds.

The process also never ends.

The plateau is not dying. It is caught in the act of saying something larger than its world can bear.

The wise power does not complete the answer for it. That would replace the being’s meaning with its own. It does not extract the second impact or seal it away. That would be a cure indistinguishable from mutilation.

It makes room.

After the next hammer blow, the forty-third second passes.

Then, for the first time, there is another kind of interval.

It is not a second, though later observers might call it the forty-fourth. It has no measurable length. It does not delay what follows. No surface ages inside it. Heat does not accumulate. Reactions do not advance toward completion. It cannot be eaten by a grazer, transmitted by a ratchet, closed by a convergent, or multiplied beneath the foot of a kiln.

It is time in which nothing is required to continue.

The gift enters the plateau as a softness too slight to feel.

A ratchet holding a crystal fragment reaches the new interval. For all its species’ history, receipt has necessitated alteration, and alteration has necessitated transfer. Now, between receiving and changing, there is room enough for neither.

The ratchet holds the fragment.

The creature behind it waits.

The one ahead receives nothing.

The procession does not fail. It simply contains, for the first time, a member that is not passing anything.

After three days, the ratchet resumes.

The fragment continues down the line, altered by no known crystalline degree. Yet every ratchet that receives it afterward pauses. Some pause for less than the width of a vibration. Some for centuries. One reorganizes its anatomy toward no neighbor at all and walks out of the procession.

It travels seven finger-lengths before becoming still.

The line bends around it.

Much later, ratchets lay pieces beside the small body, not as bridgework or raw material, but in a pattern that performs no conductive function.

Elsewhere, a kiln animal raises one foot.

Its three simultaneous positions lift together. Ordinarily the animal must set them down in sequence, preserving enough disagreement between its histories to evade convergents. This time the foot enters the unrequired interval.

It remains raised.

The animal’s furnace quiets. Its tower vents a thin band of clear metal vapor never before produced by its species. Kilns across the plateau turn their sensory plates toward it.

The standing animal is not trapped. It is not feeding, mating, arguing, migrating, defending, assembling, or participating in the Circuit. For nine years it does nothing that another kiln can interpret.

Then it sets its foot down in only one place.

Two of its former locations remain empty.

The abandoned tracks do not vanish. They continue without it for several kilometers, impressions appearing one after another across the pale ceramic shelves. A convergent follows them, narrows them, and closes upon an absent prey. For the first time, a hunter of possibilities encounters a path that belongs to no creature.

It starves there—not painfully, but conclusively.

Its death appears as a widening.

Two routes that had been approaching each other for geological ages begin to separate. Between them emerges a strip of country no prior geometry contained. It is only a few meters across at first. In later centuries it broadens into a valley.

Nothing unusual grows there. Nothing grows anywhere.

But juvenile kilns begin to surface along its margins.

The interval grazers react most strangely. They cannot feed on the gift. Under their arches, noon still lengthens and heat still gathers, but within every stretched duration there is now an opening that does not stretch.

The oldest grazer settles over one such opening and brightens to white. Ordinarily, when sated, it would depart by becoming intermittent. Instead its red plates descend gently to the ground.

For the first time, the emptiness beneath an interval grazer closes.

Inside is a small space untouched by heat.

Not cold. Cold would be hostile. Merely untouched.

Larval grazers leave unfinished impacts and gather there. Falling plates complete their falls. Vents finish opening. Sounds arrive. Across the plateau, ancient accidents are released from the obligation to keep happening.

The noise changes.

It does not lessen. It acquires rests.

The power waits to learn whether its gift has been accepted or merely survived.

At the next White Circuit, the animals assemble as before. Kilns arrange themselves along the conductive bands. Sky-borers turn their chambers through dawn, noon, and night. Ratchets enter the fissures. Swivel stones orient toward subterranean light. The horizontal lightning begins.

But participation is no longer inevitable.

One kiln remains outside the outermost band.

Then another.

A ratchet procession reaches a fissure and divides, half descending, half continuing across the valley.

Several sky-borers leave portions of themselves in other afternoons.

The Circuit falters.

For eleven days the plateau approaches discontinuity. Southeastern animals separate from their other locations. Shadows break loose and stand independently. A black basin appears overhead, pouring its grains upward into the sky. The margins of the plateau become visible for the first time, not beyond the haze but directly beneath every point.

The benevolent power could stabilize the operation.

It does not.

A gift of freedom secretly guarded against consequence would be another mechanism.

The plateau trembles. The deep hammer misses one beat.

Then the animals change the Circuit.

The remaining kilns widen their spacing. The absent bodies are not replaced; their absences become components. Current leaps across gaps without crossing them. Ratchets pass empty space as though it were a substance. Grazers lay the unrequired interval beneath reactions that otherwise could not finish. The two old pulses—the blow and its trapped answer—begin to separate.

The White Circuit no longer resembles a dynamo.

It resembles a mouth learning to open.

In the final month, every sound on the plateau ceases.

Not because it has been silenced. Because all its noises have entered the same pause.

The kiln towers vent nothing. Sky-borers halt their rotary organs. Plate-worm mouths remain open. Even the hammer beneath the crust waits after striking.

The unfinished answer rises.

It does not emerge as language. It has no proposition that can be translated. It is pressure becoming geometry, geometry becoming duration, duration becoming a single alteration in the relationship between one moment and the next.

Far above the atmosphere, stars shift by less than their own width.

Across the plateau, all shadows turn toward no source.

The answer passes through the opening the power made and leaves the world without damaging it.

What receives the answer, if anything, is beyond even the power’s attention.

Afterward, the plateau resumes.

The kiln animals disperse. Ratchet lines continue their destinationless transfers. Sky-borers draw charged dust through several hours at once. Convergents hunt. Juveniles fail beneath the stone. Adults collide. Furnace bodies cool and are dismantled. Nothing becomes harmless.

The power does not remain as ruler, custodian, or god.

It leaves no monument. A monument would distort migrations. It establishes no law against suffering, because life here cannot be separated cleanly into harm and function. It promises no immortality to creatures whose selves already extend across ages and bodies.

It leaves only the interval.

Henceforth, after every deep hammer blow, there is room for an answer—and room for none.

Some beings use it to rest. Some to deviate. Some to die without becoming material for another process. Some refuse it completely and continue in the old inevitability. During later Circuits, the interval becomes one component among many: not an absence in the great operation, but the place where the operation decides what it will be.

The plateau remains above the breathable weather.

It remains hot, dry, poisonous, vast, and loud. No grass enters its fractures. No water softens its stone. The fauna still resemble factories, pylons, seams, stars, and mistakes in distance.

But now the emptiness is inhabited by one more thing.

Not mercy as rescue.

Mercy as room.
