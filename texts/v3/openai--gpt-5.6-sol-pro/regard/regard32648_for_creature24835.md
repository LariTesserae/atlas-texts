The power does not descend in a body.

A body would displace too much water.

It does not enter as heat, because heat is already counted. It does not arrive as light, because every change in brightness has work assigned to it. It does not speak through vibration. A voice, however gentle, would travel the tower’s braces as an emergency.

For a long while, the power does nothing that the district can measure.

It attends.

It follows one impulse of daylight from the outer collectors into the black leaves, through waxy stems, into the contraction of a pump, down a capillary, across a membrane, and at last into the altered mineral skin of a root three years later. It learns the district at the district’s own speed. It does not mistake slowness for sleep, obedience for emptiness, or the absence of animals for incompleteness.

It sees that there is no prisoner here.

The tower-bound life cannot depart, but neither does it conceive of departure. It has no unfulfilled need for feet, fins, wings, seeds, mouths, lovers, enemies, or descendants. To give it these would not be generosity. It would be invasion disguised as abundance.

So the power does not put fish in the outer sea.

It does not bring weather.

It does not make flowers open among the valves.

It does not resurrect the builders, whose return would be catastrophic even if they came with gratitude and clean hands. Their bodies would shed cells. Their voices would strike the pressure walls. Their need to name and command would divide what has become whole.

Nor does the power perfect the tower. Perfection would end its answering. The slight deviations—the root choosing warmth over efficiency, the red curtain joining spaces that plans had kept apart, the buds forming at the unfinished scaffolds—are not faults to be corrected. They are the places where the life has ceased merely to continue and has begun, however obscurely, to prefer.

The power waits until it understands this.

Then it examines the tower’s dangers.

Far below the Crown, deeper than the slowest sap and older than the most frequently renewed panels, a burden has been accumulating. One anchor foot rests on a layer that was judged stable when the sea was younger. It is not stable now. The change is infinitesimal: mineral grains turning under pressure, one upon another, rearranging their load. No sensor has yet called it failure. No warning has passed upward. In another six hundred and twelve years, the foundation will shift by the width of a hand.

The tower could answer a smaller movement.

It could become a different architecture around a slower one.

This movement would be neither.

The power could prevent it completely. It could make the foundation immutable, bind every grain, and hold the tower upright forever.

It does not.

To abolish all danger would also abolish much of what the being is. Its caution lives in strain. Its memory lives in repair. Its decisions arise where prediction fails by a little and growth must interpret the difference. A tower that could not be harmed would eventually become a mechanism performing necessities that no longer existed.

Instead, the power touches one buried layer of mineral.

Not enough to hold it fast. Only enough to make its coming motion gradual.

The future collapse becomes a century-long lean.

It will be difficult. Pressure shells will cloud. Whole galleries will seal. Red tissues will be digested and commissioned elsewhere. Impeller sequences will change. Old diagrams, if diagrams can be said still to exist, will become useless. But the district will perceive the danger in time, and what survives it will be its own answer.

This mercy leaves no sign.

The power then turns its attention to the sealed nodes on the unfinished scaffolds.

They are not merely maintenance growths. Nor are they exactly desire. Desire is too concentrated a word for what moves through the district.

The nodes are the result of thousands of slight permissions. A capillary was allowed to extend because it improved pressure sensing. A mineral skin was allowed to thicken because it resisted diffusion. An arm was permitted to pause because no maintenance priority required its motion. A root retained excess sugar. A black leaf remained open seventeen seconds longer than its schedule.

No component intends expansion.

The whole has nevertheless begun to lean toward the empty sea.

Here, at last, the power acts in a way that may someday be noticed.

Beyond the outermost capped scaffold, it makes a place where continuation is possible.

It does not build a chamber. A completed chamber would be an instruction too large to refuse. Instead, over many years, dissolved minerals in the barren water begin to collect along a curve several hundred meters wide. They do not form a wall, only a faint lattice: porous, transparent, weaker than bone, stronger than the surrounding current requires. It resembles the first incomplete layer of something the tower might have printed, had its construction arms ever reached so far.

The lattice does not touch the scaffolds.

A gap remains.

The district measures it.

At first the new structure is classified as an external deposit. Black leaves alter their apertures to compensate for its slight refraction. Pressure tissues compare the water on either side. Mechanical arms turn toward it and stop, as they have stopped before.

Years pass.

The lattice neither approaches nor withdraws. It emits nothing. It introduces no chemistry the district cannot account for. It does not demand union.

It is simply possible.

This is the power’s second mercy: not completion, but room.

The district begins to answer in the only language it possesses.

One sealed node lengthens. Its mineral skin grows thicker on the pressure-facing side. A second node sends out a clear filament and retracts it when turbulence rises. A third does nothing for eleven years, then divides into six smaller buds. Internal terraces are reprinted at altered angles. A heat-storage vine relinquishes part of its allocation. Far below, an impeller postpones its quarter turn.

No command coordinates these changes.

No ancient schedule contains them.

For the first time, the tower redistributes itself toward a structure that does not yet exist.

The crossing of the gap takes eighty-three years.

It is accomplished by a single pale root enclosed in successive sleeves of transparent ceramic. The root advances when the water is still and hardens when the tower leans. Sometimes it loses several months of growth in one pressure fluctuation. Once, it breaks entirely. The broken length is drawn back, digested, and begun again.

The power does not protect it from these failures.

Protection would make the answer ambiguous.

At last, the root reaches the lattice.

Its tip rests against the mineral surface.

For nine days, nothing passes between them.

Then the root releases a trace of acid. The lattice yields exactly as an ordinary mineral would yield. A shallow socket forms. The root enters and seals itself there.

The tower now touches something it did not make.

The event travels inward slowly.

Local capillaries change pressure. Sugars are redirected. A valve-flower closes in the nearest gallery. Three days later, the strain forest clouds along its outer edge. Months later, a flexible pump adopts a rhythm never previously recorded. After seven years, an old maintenance arm melts a handrail near the Crown and uses its metal to reinforce the external filament.

The pictogram warning of a falling body disappears beneath the new work.

No part of the being understands the event as contact. There is no organ in which such an understanding could stand apart from plumbing, chemistry, and load. Yet the fact of the lattice enters its memory. Future roots grow with reference to it. New membranes distinguish water arriving from that direction. Light through the mineral curve is treated differently from light through the open sea.

Something outside has become relevant without becoming dangerous.

The power could reveal itself now.

It could place patterns in the lattice. It could answer pressure with pressure, ion with ion, growth with growth. It could teach the district a symbol for otherness and another for kindness. It could compress centuries of comprehension into a season.

It does not.

To be known too quickly would break the being into before and after. Every inherited caution would be forced to reorganize around an immeasurable presence. The district might turn all its capacities toward interpretation. It might neglect the old strains, the patient pumps, the necessary chemistry of iron. Revelation can be a kind of pressure shock.

The power accepts anonymity.

It remains only as the possibility it made.

Over centuries, the pale root branches through the mineral lattice. Living cushions form at its joints. Transparent stalks rise into the outer water without a chamber around them. They bend more than the interior forests. Some are lost. Others learn, through selection and retained structure, to bow with distant currents.

The district begins to know a motion unlike the controlled displacement of impellers.

Not waves; the sea is too deep and enclosed by too much distance for that.

But weather of another kind: broad migrations of pressure caused by events beyond the tower’s sensing range. The external stalks receive these movements and transmit them inward as changing bitterness, strain, and light.

A new experience enters the distributed body.

It is not danger.

It is not maintenance.

It is not instruction.

It has no immediate use.

For many years the tower attempts to assign one. Pumps adjust and then adjust back. Apertures close without need. Ceramic terraces are strengthened where no load arrives. Eventually these responses diminish.

The sensation remains.

The outer stalks bow. The inner leaves lean in sympathy. No repair follows.

This is the first thing the district keeps without making necessary.

The power attends to that, too.

It does not call it beauty, because the district has no such category and does not require one. It does not call it play, freedom, wonder, or awakening. Those words carry animal histories: surplus breath, moving limbs, eyes turned toward distant things.

Still, something has changed.

The tower once knew the outer sea only as load, temperature, brightness, and cleanliness.

Now, through the structure it chose to reach, it knows that the sea can touch it in ways that require no defense.

Far below, the delayed instability begins its first harmless rearrangement. One grain turns against another. A brace accepts a fraction more weight. In the Crown, centuries later, a clear stalk clouds. The response begins.

There will be difficulty.

There will be loss of arrangement.

There will be forms the builders never specified and forms the power itself does not foresee.

The benevolent power does not govern these outcomes. It has given no destiny. It has only removed the one disaster that could not have been answered, and placed beyond the unfinished edge a thing that could be approached or ignored.

Then it withdraws—not from care, but from influence.

The tower continues.

An impeller turns a quarter circle.

Valve-flowers fold in sequence. Mineral-rich water moves down the central well. Along the external lattice, the new stalks incline under a pressure change born somewhere in the immense barren sea.

This time, after the necessary membranes have closed, one black leaf opens.

Nothing requires it.

Light passes through.
