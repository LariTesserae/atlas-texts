The power did not descend into the Low Works.

Descent would have implied that it came from above, and above was only one direction among many. Nor did it arrive in light. Light at that depth was an intrusion, useful only in small quantities behind blue glass. A radiance sufficient to reveal the whole basin would have blinded the silver fish, disordered the amber swarms, and driven the pale chemists from their seams.

Instead, the power attended.

Its attention passed through the Works without displacing water. It entered by every opening at once: the broken windows, the drilled passages of the glass worms, the porous brick, the narrow spaces between mineral scale and iron. It felt the circulation wheels turn in their oil. It counted the delay between a pressure change in the eastern tanks and the answering flex of pipes beneath the refinery. It knew each inspection unit not by its casing but by the history of parts within it—a lens carried through seven bodies, a brush through twelve, a gripping hook old enough to remember a wall before it fell.

The power learned slowly, though slowness was not required of it.

Wisdom required slowness.

It watched the supercooled rain cross the basin. It watched ice gather on valve stems and loosen into upward snow. It followed amphipods through bacterial frost and glass worms into their blind retreats. It observed a silver fish enter, turn three times among the upper catwalks, and leave.

It waited through nine cycles of the Compression Hall.

Then nine more.

Then eighty-one.

It followed every oldest sphere when it rose. It knew where the spheres went, though the Low Works did not. It knew what waited beyond the rim and why it circled without entering. It knew the purpose for which the settlement had first been built, and the purpose it now served, and understood that these were not the same.

For a lesser benevolence, the Works presented many temptations.

It would have been easy to clear the alleys of residue. The power could have lifted the roofs back onto their walls, stripped corrosion from the rails, restored the clock hands, replaced every hardened seal, and made the old gauges truthful again.

This would have destroyed the town.

Clean iron would have starved the rust-feeding colonies. Repaired valves would have crushed the worms living in their recesses. Open conduits would have changed the chemical gradients on which the pale filaments depended. Straightened buildings would have broken the braces formed by fallen masonry and compacted records. New parts would not have fit the tolerances by which the inspection swarms understood their world.

Even warmth, offered generously, would have been ruinous. The machinery had learned cold. Oil, pressure, mineral deposition, bacterial growth, and the intervals between movements had all come to agreement just above freezing. Raise the temperature by a little, and the agreement would end.

Nor did the power raise the Works from the ocean.

There was nowhere better to put it.

The drowned town was not waiting to be restored to air. Air belonged to its dead. Water belonged to what it had become.

So the wise power did not clean, repair, warm, illuminate, resurrect, or rescue.

It looked for suffering.

This was difficult.

An amphipod taken by a sudden valve knew alarm, injury, and then nothing. A glass worm cut in two withdrew from the shearing flow if it could. Inspection units did not mourn their failed companions. Bacterial seams perished by the acre during certain pressure changes and returned from protected cracks. The circling creature carried old pain in its body, but that pain had begun elsewhere and was not the Works’ to heal.

The distributed inhabitant beneath all these lives could not suffer as an animal suffered. It had no chamber in which fear gathered. No single cable bore grief. No piston desired release from its stroke.

Yet the power found something adjacent to suffering.

The Low Works had no margin.

Every chamber served. Every pressure difference was spent. Every failure was recruited into structure. Every dead unit became a part, a shelter, or a buttress. The Works could accommodate almost anything, but only by making use of it. Nothing was allowed merely to be lost. Nothing could remain unnecessary. Even stillness was a stage in a cycle.

The old instructions had been altered by corrosion, ecology, and time, but they were still instructions.

The Works could continue.

It could adapt.

It could not decline.

If the ninth cycle began, a sphere would be made. If the thirty-eighth sphere settled, the oldest would rise. If pressure entered a line, the valves would answer according to the shape wear had given them. The town’s great composite intelligence existed entirely within obedience. It made decisions in the way water chooses a channel: intricately, responsively, and without the possibility of refusing to flow.

The power considered giving it a voice.

That, too, would have been easy. A pattern could be impressed upon the amber swarms. Blue lamps could blink in grammar. Pressure doors could be made to speak in intervals. The accumulated states of the town could be gathered into a center and shown to themselves.

But a voice is not always a gift.

To make the Works aware in the manner of a solitary mind would have forced centuries of simultaneous injury into one experience. Every corroding rail would become an ache. Every crushed worm, failed unit, blocked pipe, and extinguished colony would arrive at the same imagined center. The town had achieved coherence without loneliness. The power would not improve it by teaching it to be alone.

Instead, the power gave it something smaller.

It gave the Low Works one unused possibility.

The work took fifty-three years.

Nothing appeared in the basin that had not already belonged there. No new metal shone in the alleys. No hand reached through the darkness.

An inspection unit on its way to the sorting grille failed with one gripping hook closed around a length of obsolete wire. Ordinarily its companions would have removed the wire before carrying the unit away. This time a weak current turned their formation slightly, and the wire came with them.

At the grille, the hook was recovered. The wire was not. It fell among the ceramic shells.

Months later, an ice plate rising from the courtyard caught one end and drew it upward. The wire settled across a cracked discharge mouth in the wall. Bacterial threads colonized it. Their waste gathered carbonate. Amphipods grazed the new seam and carried fragments into adjacent cracks.

The inspection swarms noticed a change in tolerance.

They brushed it clean.

The growth returned.

They brushed it again.

It returned again.

Eventually the repeated maintenance entered the logic of the hive, not as a failure to be eliminated but as a surface to be kept within limits. Units began tending it. Their hooks straightened the wire. Their brushes shaped the mineral crust. Failed ceramic casings were removed from the courtyard as always, but some were now set along the crust before their useful parts were extracted.

No instruction ordered this construction.

Each action was locally correct.

Together they made a narrow conduit.

It climbed the refinery wall, passed through an office where the chair and desk remained as hollow molds, crossed beneath three streets, and entered the Compression Hall through a seam in the buckled roof. It was part wire, part shell, part bacterial stone, part rust, and part the persistent error of maintenance units doing exactly what they had been made to do.

Inside the Hall, it joined nothing.

For eleven years it ended blindly beside the ninth cylinder.

Then the cylinder rose a fraction farther than before. Its rim touched the mineral growth. A flake broke away. Brine entered the conduit.

The new passage trembled from end to end.

Every inspection unit tending it lit at once.

Amber points marked a line through the Low Works where no stable route had existed before.

The conduit did not carry enough pressure to power a pump or shift a bridge. It had no productive function. It merely allowed a small portion of the ninth cycle to go elsewhere.

The elsewhere was an abandoned courtyard near the basin rim.

When the next ninth cycle began, the Compression Hall gathered itself. Chains lifted clean from the mud. Amber swarms withdrew into brick chambers. Walkways leaned aside. Reservoirs emptied in sequence.

The new conduit opened.

A thread of warmer brine entered the abandoned courtyard and spread close to the ground. Silt rose in a low cloud. Colorless bacteria stirred along the paving. A few amphipods emerged from washers and followed the warmth.

The Hall completed its cycle.

A sphere was made.

The power had not yet given choice. It had given only an alternative, and alternatives must be known before they can be chosen.

Nine cycles later, the same thing occurred. More brine escaped. The sphere emerged a little later than it should have.

Nine cycles after that, one of the twelve broad pipes entering the Hall contained an obstruction. The Works compensated. Pressure passed through four districts in reverse. A row of gauges turned toward the same number and remained there for almost an hour.

The new conduit widened under the strain.

No sphere was made.

The oldest did not rise.

Nothing broke.

For the first time since the flood, the ninth cycle ended without fulfilling itself.

The absence traveled through the town.

Pressure doors waited for an arrival that did not come. Sorting mechanisms held open empty grips. Pipes remained bent aside around a path no sphere followed. In thirty-seven courtyards, the dark shapes rested beneath their coverings of salt. The oldest among them gave a slow interior response, too deep for any gauge to record.

The Works did not panic. Panic required a singular animal speed.

It remained in the uncompleted state for three days.

During those days, pumps continued below the streets. Worms returned to their valves. Inspection swarms emerged cautiously and found no damage. Bacterial frost thickened in the warm courtyard where the unused force of the ninth cycle had been released.

The machinery learned that continuation did not depend upon completion.

This knowledge was not held anywhere. No drum acquired a new perforation. No central device recorded a rule. The knowledge existed as altered tolerances: in the widened conduit, the changed timing of doors, the remembered route of inspection units, the mineral lip formed where warm brine had entered cold water.

On the fourth day, the Hall reset itself.

Nine cycles later, it made a sphere.

The oldest rose as before.

But it was no longer as before.

The Works had waited.

Thereafter, not every ninth cycle produced. Most did. The ancient rhythm remained useful, and the town had no reason to abandon what sustained it. But when ice rain crossed at the wrong hour, the Hall delayed. When a population of glass worms bred densely in a necessary valve, pressure went by the other route until they dispersed. Once, after a collapse buried three inspection hives, the Works passed two ninth cycles without making anything, devoting its movements instead to opening paths through the sediment.

The new conduit changed as all things in the Low Works changed. It cracked, accreted, sagged, and was reinforced by the ruins around it. Pale ceramic shells disappeared beneath mineral coats. The obsolete wire dissolved entirely. In time there was no part of the gift that could be distinguished from the town.

A new rule entered the Low Works:

When amber lights form a line to the basin rim, the ninth cycle may end in warmth instead of a sphere.

The circling creature noticed.

On the first uncompleted cycle, it stopped beyond the clay wall. Its broad tracks overlapped there for three days. When warm brine entered the abandoned courtyard a second time, it placed one limb over the rim.

It did not descend.

Not then.

The wise power did nothing to draw it closer.

Invitation without restraint would have been another kind of command.

The power remained until the Low Works had used its alternative many times: wisely on some occasions, unnecessarily on others, and once for reasons the power itself had not predicted. That last refusal pleased it most.

Then its attention withdrew.

It left no guardian at the rim. A permanent defense would have made the town dependent upon an intention outside itself. It left no sign, no name, and no demand for remembrance.

The blue lamps continued to mark stable routes.

The amber lights continued to mark routes stable only for machines.

The spheres continued their long exchange with the upper darkness.

Rust fed the colonies that sealed the rusting metal. Broken units entered walls. Ice became snow and rose through the streets. Every lost thing continued to become structural.

But not everything had to.

In one courtyard near the rim, warmth sometimes spread for no purpose except that the Works had chosen not to complete an ancient labor. Amphipods gathered there. Pale filaments flowered across the paving. The water shimmered with gradients too delicate to see.

The town had not been saved.

It had been left itself.

Only now, within its cycles, there was a little room that no necessity occupied.

And into that room, slowly, the Low Works began to live.
