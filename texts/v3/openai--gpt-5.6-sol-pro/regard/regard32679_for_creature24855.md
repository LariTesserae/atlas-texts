The power did not descend in fire.

Fire would have been a catastrophe here. It would have run through the dry grasses, baked the mud around sleeping bodies, and driven what little air remained from the shallow pools. Nor did the power arrive as a voice. A voice loud enough for the whole plain would have silenced the frogs and sent the birds lifting darkly toward the horizon.

It came as attention.

For a long while, nothing changed.

The power regarded the shelves and pans, the channels without banks, the water entering stone and returning years later. It perceived the lives too small to cast shadows. It followed dissolved limestone through the bodies of snails, through the combs inside a feeding hollowback, into the pale crust on an old animal’s back, and from there into the sediment of a basin not yet formed.

It knew every death occurring beneath the apparent peace.

A larva caught in drying mud. A bird taken by a snake. A transparent hatchling swallowed by a beetle. An ancient hollowback whose breathing slits would not open after rain. The power was benevolent, but not sentimental in the manner of those who mistake kindness for the prevention of all endings. It did not remove hunger. It did not forbid predation. It did not make the eggs imperishable or the old animals immortal.

To do so would have destroyed the plain as surely as fire.

The power saw that this place was not poor. It was exact.

Its abundance depended upon intervals: wet and dry, connection and separation, exposure and concealment. Too much water would drown the algae of the high shelves and invite fish into every nursery cup. Too little would divide the hollowbacks until their low calls traveled nowhere. Rich soil would close the open stone beneath plants. Perfect weather would favor a few lives until they displaced the rest.

Nothing asked to be perfected.

Yet there was a fragility in the plain that did not belong to its ancient rhythm.

The power found it not in the present drought, nor in the salt already breathing faintly from the coastal basin, but in a convergence of years not yet arrived. Rains would fall heavily in places where the channels could not receive them. Elsewhere, seasons would pass under tall white clouds that gave no water. The largest pans would endure, but the seams between them would fail. Adults would sleep and wake and sleep again without sensing a moving sheet. Their calls would enter isolated basins and return from the rims without reply.

The hollowbacks could survive dryness.

They could not survive a world broken into pieces.

The power considered calling the rains.

But rain given in abundance would wash the nursery hollows clean. It would alter the migration of birds, carry salt into fresh seams, and wake every sleeper at once into a flood that might not return for years. A miracle at the scale of weather was too blunt.

It considered deepening the pools.

But deeper water would favor swift fish, tall reeds, and hunters that had never before crossed the warm shallows. The hollowbacks themselves were shaped for a depth measured in fingers. A lake would be as much a barrier to them as a desert.

It considered changing the animals.

It could have hardened them against heat, taught their skins to retain water, lengthened their limbs for travel across exposed stone. But then they would no longer belong so precisely to the place that had made them. Benevolence did not grant the right to erase a being in order to save something bearing its name.

So the power chose the smallest measure that could answer the danger.

One night, while mist lay over the lowest country, it entered the limestone.

No surface cracked. No shelf lifted. Beneath the plain, among flooded seams too narrow even for young hollowbacks, the power touched certain grains of stone and made them slightly more willing to dissolve. Elsewhere it made other grains slightly less willing. It adjusted no passage by more than the breadth of a root hair.

Water noticed.

During the next rain, a minute portion of the flood entered seams it had not entered before. It moved underground, slowly enough to lose its mud, and gathered in old cavities below the highest pans. When the surface began to dry, the stored water returned—not as a spring, not as a stream, but as a darkening along the bottoms of certain cracks.

A drop formed beneath one low overhang.

It fell.

Much later, another followed.

The change was almost beneath detection. Moss grew on no new acreage. The water level of no basin visibly rose. But one shaded cup remained wet eleven days longer than it had before. A runnel thought dry retained a film beneath its algae. A chain of hoofprints did not harden completely. Between two pans, a limestone seam held enough moisture for a transparent juvenile to pass through it at night.

The power repeated this work across the plain, never making a river where there had been none, never forcing water to travel against the old gradients. It gave the land no abundance it could not bear.

It gave it continuity.

The hollowbacks were not told.

They had no revelation. No symbol appeared in their resting hollows. Their dark eyes did not turn upward in gratitude. The oldest among them felt only that certain routes tasted faintly different after rain. Where pressure once faded to nothing, there was now a cool mineral trace continuing through the stone.

They followed it.

Because the power was wise, it did not make the hidden connections permanent. Permanent water would soon cease to be mercy and become dominion. The seams opened only under particular pressures. Some years they held. Some years they dried. Their usefulness shifted from century to century as mud settled, roots entered cracks, and the limestone continued its patient surrender.

Nor did every young hollowback find them.

Many still perished. The birds still watched the shrinking pools. Beetle larvae still hunted among the eggs. Age still closed over the largest bodies in their familiar basins. The gift was not rescue from the terms of life.

It was a refusal to let accident become extinction.

There was one other thing the power did, though even the power could not know all that would follow from it.

In the deep chambers with which hollowbacks called through water, it placed no new organ and no new faculty. It altered only the faintest overtone of their pulse. Every call now carried a vibration so low that it entered the porous limestone as well as the water above it.

Most of the note was lost.

A little traveled through the newly wetted seams.

Thus, in a year when the surface lay divided into separate pools, an animal calling at dusk sometimes received an answer from beyond dry stone.

The answer was weak and delayed. It might come after so long that the caller had settled back into mud. Then the floor of the basin would tremble once beneath its body.

The hollowback would raise its head.

It did not understand distance as a bird did, nor hope as a person might. It knew only that the world continued in that direction.

Sometimes it waited for rain.

Sometimes, if the night was wet enough, it began to move.

Its passage remained slow. It crossed the exposed shelves under darkness, mucus shining along its sides, flexible digits spread against the stone. It rested in shallow cups and pressed itself beneath grass. More than once the route failed. More than once the animal turned back.

But occasionally, before dawn, it reached a narrow seam where coolness persisted. It lowered its broad head and tasted water that had passed beneath the plain.

Then it went on.

Ages later, no monument marked the power’s intervention.

There were only consequences that appeared natural.

A population endured in a western chain of pans after vanishing from the east. Young emerged from cracks where no adult had been seen for generations. Old channels crossed and recrossed the limestone, some made by water, some by hollowbacks, and some by both acting upon the other until no distinction remained.

In one unusually dry century, the coastal salt advanced far inland. Sedges yellowed. Fish disappeared from several basins. Birds altered their routes. For nine years, no broad flood joined the central plain.

In the tenth year, rain fell briefly on distant shelves.

It was not enough to cover the land. From any height, the pans still appeared separate. Yet beneath the rock, water entered the hair-fine mercies prepared long before. It moved through darkness and emerged as dampness under a series of rounded ledges.

An old female hollowback woke.

Mineral crust had sealed half her back in pale plates. A flowering herb had rooted in mud against her tail. She opened one eye, then the other, and remained still for most of the night.

At last she called.

The basin trembled. Rings moved outward, touched the rim, and vanished.

There was no reply.

She called again.

Far away, beyond shelves no hollowback had crossed in her lifetime, another animal lay beneath a film of black water. The note reached it through stone, diminished almost past existence.

It answered.

Between them were miles of interrupted country, drying cups, narrow seams, and no certainty. Neither animal knew the other. Neither could imagine the future basin in which their descendants might one day leave eggs.

But each turned toward the vibration.

Above them, clouds were already thinning. Frogs called from the remaining water. A wader stepped carefully through a pan and drew a silver fish into the air. Dew gathered on the exposed limestone, insufficient to save anything by itself.

The benevolent power watched and did not push.

It did not shorten the distance. It did not conceal the travelers from every predator or soften every stone beneath them. Wisdom had required intervention; wisdom also required restraint.

By dawn, the old female had crossed only a few body lengths. Her broad track shone behind her. Ahead, under an overhang no higher than her eye, a single drop of stored rain hung from the limestone.

She reached it after sunrise.

The drop fell onto her skin and spread into the folds beside her breathing slit.

There was almost nothing in it.

A little water. A little dissolved stone. The faint chemical history of distant rain.

And the direction in which the plain remained whole.
