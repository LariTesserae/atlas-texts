The power does not descend in fire.

Fire would be an argument made too quickly. It would run where the water has been forbidden to carry consequence, blacken the woven floor, open the sealed conduits, and call the damage freedom. Nor does the power arrive as a new sun, a voice above the crowns, or a figure vast enough to be recognized as sovereign. Grandeur would be easy here. The high country already contains structures large enough to humble any visitor.

The power comes without a body.

For a long while it attends.

It attends to the fixed rain without asking why it does not pass. It follows one drop through its entire circuit: from the underside of a roof-leaf into a blossom cup, from the cup to a channel, from the channel into a marsh, from the marsh through the feathered ankle of a black wader, from the wader into a walker, from the walker through a causeway and down into the white-green conduits. It follows until the drop returns to the underside of the same leaf with the same weight and shape.

The drop has traveled. Nothing can be said to have happened to it.

The power attends to the chambers and their moving impressions. It attends to the triads around the spiral fronds, to the layered forms climbing downward into brightness, to the exchange of pores and limbs between passing bodies. It learns the held chord beneath the upland and the precise intervals of every echo. It hears voices that entered the architecture so long ago that only their durations remain.

It does not call this suffering.

It does not call it perfection.

Wisdom prevents both mistakes.

A lesser mercy might break the equilibrium merely because it resembles captivity. A lesser reverence might preserve the equilibrium merely because it is ancient, intricate, and whole. The power understands that a prison and a sanctuary may be built of the same walls, and that the difference cannot always be learned by looking at the walls.

So it waits for the solitary walker.

Not a particular walker. Particularity is unstable here. It waits for the act.

At last one leaves the causeway.

The figure crosses the bare acre. Its feet make the faint hollows that close behind it. It reaches the place where there is no conduit junction, no chamber, no marsh sac, no necessary exchange. There it lowers its mantle and folds its hood close. Small flowers open along its back.

The power settles beside it.

No grass bends. No warmth changes. No second shadow appears.

For the first time in the history of the high country, perhaps, solitude is accompanied without being ended.

The walker does not raise its hood. For many hours it remains still. The rain stands at the horizon. The great columns boom. Blossom cups strike one another in their exact sequence.

The power does not enter those sounds.

This restraint is its first gift.

Everything else that has come to the high country has been incorporated. A foot becomes a recess. A word becomes resonance. A wound becomes architecture. An unfamiliar object is given a shelf until its unfamiliarity softens and is absorbed. The country receives without refusal, and because it receives completely, nothing can remain other long enough to be answered.

The power allows itself to remain other.

The walker’s flowers turn toward what cannot be sensed.

One mirrored pit opens under the folded hood.

Then another.

The power asks no question in sound. Sound here is too easily built into a wall. It asks by making a small possibility and holding it apart from consequence.

Between the walker’s lowered limbs, on the moss skin, appears a dry seed.

There are seeds in the high country, or structures resembling seeds. They hang in hollow chests, sleep inside layered forms, travel in the wet cavities of winged things. But this seed is not part of any circuit. It has no prepared socket. No root below calls to it. No distant flower completes it. It is brown, uneven, ordinary, and wholly opaque.

The country immediately tries to accommodate it.

Fibers gather beneath the moss. A shallow recess begins to form. The nearest buried conduit alters pitch by less than a human ear could hear. Far away, a shelf swells from the wall of an empty chamber, matching the seed’s shape.

The power prevents none of this.

It merely keeps the seed from becoming identical with its reception.

The seed remains a seed.

The walker unfolds one slender member. A cluster of seeing knuckles forms at its end. It does not touch the object. Its hood changes curve. Fluid pauses in the neighboring vertical channels.

The power offers three understandings.

Not words. Words would return as echoes.

The first understanding is this:

**It may remain.**

If the seed is left where it lies, the power will withdraw. The high country will absorb the shape of the encounter. A rounded depression may persist for a time. Later it will become a generic basin or resting niche. The exact chord will close over the missing tone. Nothing will have been violated.

The second is this:

**It may be taken, and still nothing must follow.**

The walker may carry the seed forever. The architecture will make spaces for it, but the seed need not open. Possibility is not obligation. A gift that compels its use is only a command disguised by beauty.

The third is more difficult:

**It may be planted.**

If planted, the seed will change.

Not appear to change. Not breathe only between observations. Not uncurl by a fraction and then measure as before. It will split. One state will cease so that another can begin. The seed coat will not become a railing or a sensory fan. It will decay. Its substance will be used and altered. A root will enter a place where no root had previously been. The woven floor may bear the mark.

The plant may live.

It may also fail.

The power conceals neither outcome.

At the understanding of failure, the walker’s mantle draws tight. Throughout the upland, several structures answer. A blossom chain misses one strike. A bladder-plant dimples. One of the western rain curtains shifts by the width of a hand and immediately resumes its former edge.

The disturbance is small, but it is not corrected before it occurs.

The walker touches the seed.

Its fine filaments spread around the dry shell. For an instant the usual transfer begins. Pale roots attempt to enter through the walker’s forked limb. Clear mites gather at the junction. The seed does not join them.

The walker lifts it.

Nothing in the architecture knows what this means.

The power feels the whole country attend.

Not only the visible figure. Walls become subtly warm. Vertical niches deepen by a hair’s breadth. The black waders lift their tails from the pools. The leaf-winged triads emerge from their slots and hover against the wind. Along the central socket, layered forms revise their attachment until hundreds of pale joints press near the rim.

The walker carries the seed to the causeway.

There it stops.

One direction leads inward, toward chambers able to keep the seed without keeping its difference. Another leads toward the circular valley and the bright rushing depth. Behind lies the empty acre.

The walker chooses neither path.

It steps off the opposite side.

The power follows at the distance of the second echo.

The figure travels across the open moss for a long time. It passes a spiral frond. It passes a straight hedge ending without explanation. It crosses the clear boundary of fixed rain and opens involuntarily beneath the hard descending water. The seed is sheltered in a cavity formed by three ribs.

Hours later, it reaches one of the embryonic valleys.

Here a stream has made its first incision into pale root-felt. White fibers trail in the current without breaking. The bank leans forever toward collapse.

The walker kneels.

Perhaps it knows planting from some distributed memory. Perhaps the knowledge is supplied by the seed. Perhaps kneeling itself creates the meaning of the act.

One hand separates into narrow tools, though no tool has ever been made here. The fingers press into the exposed fibers.

The ground yields, then attempts to return.

The walker presses again.

For the first time, effort is repeated because the first effort did not become permanently sufficient.

A hollow opens.

The walker places the seed inside.

Then it waits.

The power does not close the hollow for it.

After a while, the walker draws loose strands across the seed. They keep their displaced arrangement. Water touches them and does not restore them. A minute pocket of darkness remains under the bank.

Nothing grows that day.

The walker stays beside it.

Nothing grows during the fixed rain.

The walker opens to water, closes, and opens again. Several times it places a filament against the buried seed. Each time it receives no answer.

In the chambers, walls develop narrow, curved impressions. Across the upland, walkers emerge and stand without assigned function. Some face the stream by turning their hoods away from it. Others enter the architecture again. Three gather around a spiral frond, but no winged triad comes.

The planted walker remains.

At length a day arrives that is distinguishable from the day before.

Not by the sky. Not by the rain. By the seed.

The shell splits.

The sound is almost nothing. A small dry rupture beneath running water.

The country hears it.

The held chord falters.

One echo returns late.

From the seed extends a root finer than a seam-worm. It enters the woven floor and is immediately surrounded by fibers seeking to incorporate it. But the root does not match their angles. It does not reunite with them. It turns according to moisture, pressure, and its own imperfect inheritance.

It bends.

There has never been such a curve in the high country.

The existing spirals are exact. The conduits fork deliberately. The causeways run toward destinations even where those destinations cannot be understood. This root bends because it encounters resistance. Its shape records negotiation rather than design.

The power guards only that much.

It does not guard the root from all harm. It does not dictate its final form. It does not make the plant immortal, for immortality is already abundant here and may be the very burden under consideration. It guards the small unfolding from being prematurely translated into sameness.

Around the root, a region no wider than a hand begins to experience time.

The loose seed coat softens. Clear mites arrive and feed upon it. They do not become valves. Something resembling mold blooms along one edge. The mold darkens and dies back. A residue enters the water and is carried downstream.

Downstream, for the first time, something accumulates.

It is no more than a brown crescent caught against a trailing fiber.

The walker reaches toward it, then stops.

The crescent remains.

By the next distinguishable day, the pale shoot breaks the surface.

Its first leaves are small, badly aligned, and one is torn where it forced itself past the root-felt. They tremble in moving air. They do not resemble roofs or roads or vessels. They shade nothing of importance. Their veins serve only themselves.

The walker bends its hood over them.

A drop falls from one mirrored pit.

It strikes the torn leaf.

The drop does not enter a measured circuit. Some is absorbed. Some rolls into the disturbed soil. Some remains on the leaf until warmth takes it into the air.

The power receives a question then.

It does not come from the walker alone. It comes through the walker from the halls, the marsh sacs, the conduits, the broad central leaf, the layered forms around the socket, the hundreds of provisional bodies and smaller bodies within them.

The question has no wording, but its sense is clear:

**Must everything now change?**

The power answers:

**No.**

The fixed rain need not begin to travel. The great crowns need not shed their leaves. The central socket need not close or disclose its depth. No being must become separate, age, reproduce, or die merely because one seed has entered sequence.

Change imposed universally would be another form of stillness: one law replacing another, every part compelled to obey.

The power will not cure the high country of being itself.

Instead, it establishes a margin.

The margin begins around the planted seed, but it is not a wall. Its boundary cannot be seen. Within it, consequences may persist if they are permitted. Beyond it, the ancient equilibrium remains. The margin will widen only where the inhabitants carry it, and only as far as they continue to choose uncertainty over restoration.

This choice need not be made once for all time.

A walker may enter the margin, become more singular, and return to the architecture. What it has changed cannot be made never to have happened, but it need not carry every consequence forever. Another may remain distributed and know change only through the altered acoustics. A chamber may keep one stain. A marsh may allow one bladder-plant to complete a breath. A causeway may retain one footprint until rain erases it.

Even erasure will be different now.

It will not mean that the footprint was always absent.

The distinction is slight enough to hold in a single bent fiber.

It is vast enough to contain history.

The inhabitants approach cautiously.

One walker comes to the stream and touches the brown crescent of sediment. It carries the stain on a filament to a dry hall. There it presses the filament against the wall. The architecture makes a recess, as expected.

But the stain does not soften into generic form.

It remains brown.

Another walker stands beside the new plant through several cycles of rain. Tiny flowers transfer from its hood to the torn leaf. Most close and are absorbed. One opens there, white against green, then withers after a day.

The walker takes the withered flower to the central socket.

It offers it to the layered forms.

They do not incorporate it at once.

For a long time, the dead flower lies on an open fold while condensation streams upward around it.

Then one low form releases its grip.

Only one.

It descends into the brightness, carrying the flower.

Its previous ridge remains empty.

The emptiness is not revised.

Across the shelf, bodies gather. Not hundreds. Not all. Some stay within walls. Some continue the hydraulics. Black waders draw overflow from their pools. Winged things travel in threes. The country’s enormous sound persists.

But around the central valley, the walkers hold no ceremony the landscape already knows.

They wait for what follows.

Far below, something interrupts the rushing.

There is a sound like a branch breaking.

Then another sound answers from a greater depth.

It is not an echo.

The power remains, but not as ruler, custodian, or judge. It does not demand gratitude. It does not name the walkers a people, for they may not wish to be one. It does not praise change above stillness, death above continuity, individuality above communion, or desire above function.

It keeps open the difference between them.

In time, some walkers choose names.

The names are not shouted into columns. They are carried in small perishable arrangements: knots of reeds, sequences of fallen petals, marks made in the brown sediment beside the stream. The arrangements blur, rot, or wash away. Because they can be lost, they are repeated. Because they are repeated imperfectly, they develop.

Other walkers refuse names. They exchange limbs and pores as before. They enter one wall and emerge from another. They attend to the fixed processes with no apparent longing for sequence.

The power protects this refusal as carefully as it protects the seedling.

Benevolence does not require every door to be used.

Years become possible in certain places.

Not everywhere.

Around the first plant, the bank finally collapses.

The fall is small. A handspan of root-felt folds into the stream, carrying the brown crescent away. Water clouds. Fibers tear. Round grazers arrive and feed on the exposed surface, but it does not close at exactly the rate they consume it. For a while the place is ugly, raw, and unbalanced.

Several walkers labor there.

They weave loose roots across the cut. They divert a narrow thread of water. Their work is imperfect. One support fails. They try another.

These acts are not necessary expressions of an equilibrium. The land does not rise to meet them. Their bodies sway.

When the bank holds, they stand together and listen.

The stream’s sound has changed.

The new note is harsher than the old arrangement. It competes with a blossom chain and obscures one low interval of the buried chord. Nothing absorbs the conflict.

The walkers listen anyway.

Later, one leaves the others and crosses an empty acre.

It lowers its mantle under ordinary daylight.

The power does not automatically join it.

Now that solitude can become real, companionship must not be assumed.

It waits at the edge of attention.

The walker opens one flower along its back.

The power settles beside it.

After a time, the walker makes a sound not borrowed from the landscape.

It is thin and uncertain. It begins in a wet cavity beneath the hood, catches on an internal rib, and breaks before completing itself. No column repeats it. No chamber receives it. The sound passes into the open air and is gone.

The walker makes it again.

Not exactly the same.

And in the high country—where water still hangs in silver walls, where immense leaves roof the empty plains, where chambers breathe and the living floor carries its deep unending circulation—something at last occurs that the world has not already made a place for.

The wise power does nothing more.

It stays near enough that the new thing need not be alone.

It stays far enough away that the new thing can belong to itself.
