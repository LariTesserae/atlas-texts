The power does not descend.

Descent would be too much like a falling body. It would bend reeds, scatter fish, alter the spacing of calls. Anything with weight would immediately become habitat: moths would settle beneath it, mites would enter its seams, vines would fasten to it, mouths would test its surface. Anything giving light would summon wings. Anything giving warmth would receive eggs.

Nor does the power speak.

There is nowhere in the plain where a voice could remain one voice. Frogs would answer its rhythm. Birds would divide it into paired notes. Vibrations would pass down stems into water and outward through the root lattice. Before a sentence ended, it would belong to every throat and no throat at all.

So the power attends.

Its attention rests first upon the hunters and the hunted. It feels the grazer’s jaw closing over the reed and the cat’s teeth closing beneath the grazer’s jaw. It feels the beetle entering blood, the fungus entering flesh, the root entering what the fungus leaves. It finds no clean point at which mercy could be inserted. To spare the grazer would starve the cat, overcrowd the herd, strip the reeds, empty the sheltering water. To feed the cat without death would deprive flies, fungi, larvae, roots, flowers, mites, birds, fish, and young carried in warm folds.

Every rescue, followed far enough, becomes an injury.

The power is wise enough not to mistake innocence for harmlessness, or painlessness for good. It does not remove teeth. It does not sweeten blood. It does not make the bodies immortal. Immortality here would be suffocation: every space occupied forever, every birth denied by those who came before.

It looks deeper.

Beneath predation, beneath hunger, beneath the swift erasure of carcasses, it finds the stranger deprivation.

Nothing can be remembered because nothing is allowed to remain.

A hoofprint closes. A bent stem rises. A body vanishes. A displaced grain is replaced from below. The calls alter and return. Even grief, where it occurs in the social animals—a calf nosing the wet place where its mother fell, a climber searching the crowns for a vanished companion—is taken almost immediately into appetite, danger, heat, mating, movement. The plain does not forbid memory. It simply gives memory nothing to hold.

The power sees that the great living exchange is not suffering from disorder.

It is suffering, if suffering is the word, from perfect correction.

Every difference is absorbed before it can become consequence. Every event is answered so completely that it cannot become a past. The plain is alive everywhere and awake always, but it has never slept and never awakened. It has no yesterday against which to feel the shape of today.

The power could break the equilibrium. It could raise mountains, open rivers, turn the clouds aside, send cold over the shallows. Given ages, such acts might create shores, droughts, migrations, burrows, tools, graves, songs. They might also erase nearly every form now breathing.

Wisdom refuses the grandeur of that cure.

Instead, the power searches for the least gift that cannot be immediately eaten.

At last it finds one.

It withholds replacement from a single grain of pale mineral.

The grain lies on the slope of a sweating marl swell. A clear thread of water touches it. As always, the grain loosens. As always, it slides the width of an insect’s leg.

But this time no fresh mineral rises beneath it.

The absence is microscopic. Algae cross it within moments. A beetle walks over it. Condensation fills it and evaporates into air already wet. No eye notices.

Yet the hollow remains.

The next droplet enters it and waits a fraction longer than droplets have waited there before. Its weight loosens another grain. That grain also is not replaced.

The power does nothing further.

For many cycles of flowering that are not seasons, the hollow deepens. It is less than a claw mark, then the size of a claw mark, then a narrow groove beneath the moss. Water begins to prefer it. Preference becomes repetition. Repetition becomes direction.

The first channel in the plain is no wider than a root.

It does not drain the marsh. It does not expose soil to the sky. It merely allows water from one side of the mineral swell to arrive on the other side sooner than before.

But because it arrives sooner, something changes.

A colony of submerged mouths opens before the usual mineral pulse. Fish gather. A frog lays its eggs there, though frogs of its kind have never laid there before. Larvae hatch into a current too slight to deserve the name and are carried several body lengths from the leaf that held them.

Most are eaten.

One is not.

That one matures among frogs whose pulse differs faintly from the pulse of its birthplace. When it calls, its timing contains both patterns. The altered rhythm passes from stem to stem. It fades, but does not disappear entirely.

The plain has received its first event.

The power waits.

It does not guard the channel. Moss narrows it. Roots enter it. A shelled reptile rests upon the swell and presses part of it closed. For a long while the water returns to sheets and beads.

Then another droplet finds the old depression.

Because something happened there before, something happens there more easily again.

That is all a path is at first.

The gift spreads with unbearable slowness. Here a hoofprint remains until the next condensation fall. There a vine fails to lift after a herd passes, and the bent place becomes a low passage used by small animals. Elsewhere a fallen shell plate resists consumption for three days. Moths shelter beneath it. A climber turns it over, not to use it, but because it remembers finding grubs beneath such surfaces.

The creature leaves the shell where it falls.

Later, it returns.

This return is new.

The power does not place intention in the climber’s mind. It does not enlarge the clever hands or whisper instructions about levers, shelters, blades, or names. It merely permits the world to answer the same act similarly twice.

Memory, which had existed only inside flesh, begins to find correspondences outside it.

The change is not wholly gentle.

A persistent pool gathers waste and briefly sickens a school of fish. A trail helps grazers reach tender growth and also helps a cat follow them. One frog colony fails when the new channel diverts mineral seepage from its roots. Its absence leaves a silence in the pulsing calls, and neighboring colonies answer too long before understanding that no answer will return.

The benevolent power feels each loss.

It does not reverse them.

To reverse every loss would be to revoke the gift. A world in which nothing can be lost is also a world in which nothing can be kept, because keeping is loss made selective. A memory preserves one moment by allowing others to pass away. A path honors one direction by neglecting all the ground beside it. A choice is impossible until alternatives can have different consequences.

Still, the power places bounds upon its intervention.

The channel will not become a sudden gorge. The clouds will not tear open into killing light. The water will not abandon the roots in one vast retreat. Change enters by tolerable degrees, slowly enough for bodies to answer it. Variation, not disaster. History, not judgment.

On a low swell, where seepage begins to follow the new groove, a patch of mineral dries.

Not much. No larger than the spread hand of a climber.

The dry patch is the first surface in the plain that does not immediately shine. Algae darken at its edge but cannot cross its center. Gnats do not rise from it. No fish passes over it. Roots approach and turn aside.

For several days nothing touches it.

The emptiness alarms the nearby life. Frogs avoid the bordering stems. Beetles circle and depart. A bird shouts at it from above. The place has no nectar, no shelter, no digestible film. It offers nothing.

Then one of the climbers descends.

It is young, though youth and age have never mattered much here. Its slick flanks are patterned in bruised green. A healing bite closes along one forelimb. Two members of its group watch from the communal canopy, giving soft warning clicks.

The climber extends one grasping foot onto the pale patch.

Dry mineral feels strange against its skin. There is no yielding root mat, no warm seep rising between its toes, no tiny bodies disturbed by its pressure. Its foot comes away dusted white.

The mark remains.

The climber stares at it.

Not because it knows what a footprint is. Not because self-recognition arrives whole and splendid in its mind. It stares because the shape on the ground corresponds to the shape of its own spread toes, and because when it raises its foot, the shape does not close.

It places the foot down again.

A second print appears beside the first.

The watching climbers fall silent. The young one looks from the two marks to its feet, then back again. It shifts its weight and presses both hands into the mineral. When it lifts them, five narrow rays remain beneath each palm.

For the first time, an inhabitant of the plain encounters an action after the action is over.

The climber gives a low call.

No other creature has a call for this. The nearest companions repeat it imperfectly. A bird catches one note and shouts it from the crown. Frogs incorporate its interval into a pulse. The sound spreads, becomes ordinary, and is nearly lost.

But the prints remain until moisture returns.

That night—though the plain has no true night—the climber comes back.

The power watches without approaching.

The climber presses its hand into the whitening mineral again. Then it takes the hand of a smaller companion and pushes that hand gently beside its own.

Two shapes remain.

The creatures crouch over them while condensation gathers along their backs. Around them the plain continues feeding, coupling, hatching, killing, carrying, cleaning, dissolving. Fish kiss the water. Cats lower themselves through reeds. Larvae open their mouths beneath the surface film. A dead reptile begins to flower with fungi before its last muscles cool.

Nothing has been redeemed from the exchange.

Nothing has been crowned above it.

The plain is not made into a garden, because it was already more alive than any garden. The climbers are not made its masters. The power gives them no commandment to separate themselves from the bodies that sustain them.

It gives the whole place only a slight imperfection in its forgetting.

In time there may be channels. There may be trails. A branch may be left against a pool and remain where it was placed. A shell may be carried back to a remembered feeding place. Some creature may scratch a call-pattern into drying marl. There may be graves, though the mouths of the plain will contest them. There may be clearings, though vines will lean inward. There may be errors that last long enough to become teachings and kindnesses that last long enough to become customs.

There will also be scars.

The power knows this.

A scar is not the opposite of healing. It is healing that remembers what happened.

For now, the dry patch is covered again by condensation. Water softens the handprints. Their edges slump. Mites cross them. A pale worm explores one narrow finger mark. By morning, the shapes are gone.

But the climber remembers where they were.

And beneath the moss, the first channel continues carrying water in one direction.
