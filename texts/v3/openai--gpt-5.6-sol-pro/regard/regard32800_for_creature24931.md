The power does not descend.

Descent would mean choosing a direction, and direction is already a disputed matter in the city. Nor does it appear above the highest terrace, because above is only one of the mountain’s temporary opinions. It does not take the shape of a traveler. The city has learned too much from travelers already, and too imperfectly. Another upright figure would be copied into alleys, arches, false shoulders, faces made of doors.

The power does not speak its name.

It understands what names do here.

Instead, it notices the city completely.

This is the first thing it gives.

Not approval. Not judgment. Not the hard light of comprehension under which mysteries become mechanisms. Its attention is neither an instrument nor a demand. It does not simplify what it sees in order to see it. It beholds the lintel animals as creatures and as measurements and as the city’s minute closing eyelids. It beholds the folded black flocks as individuals, as exchanges of individuality, and as moving seams in a single larger thought. It beholds the residents in their overlap without separating them, and beholds each of them without denying the overlap.

It sees the mountain-being, if there is one.

It sees the city-being, whenever there is one.

It sees the seasons in which nothing here is alive.

It does not look away during those seasons.

For a long while, nothing changes.

This is how the city knows the attention is benevolent.

Every previous attention has altered what it touched. A traveler’s glance shortens an avenue. A spoken word feeds distance. Fear makes gravity remember itself. Curiosity closes side streets around its object. Even the inhabitants cannot observe one another without entering into the arrangement observed.

But this attention leaves room.

Under it, a tower may lean without being corrected. A bridge may question vacancy without receiving an answer. A young resident may gather three stairs and a shadow into itself, then abandon them without the failure becoming permanent.

The wise power waits through an elevation tide.

It waits through a pressure season in which emptiness becomes almost impassable. It waits while two districts exchange places seven times and insist afterward that they have never moved. It waits through the passage of the vast transparent being beyond the eastern wall.

When that being comes, the city contracts as before.

Lintel animals draw tight.

Black fliers enter angles.

White networks withdraw from events that have not yet happened.

The channels fill with upward darkness, and the residents pull their awareness beneath the exposed stone.

The power does not defend the city.

Not yet.

Defense offered too early would teach the city nothing except a new dependence. Worse, it might mistake the abyssal being for an enemy when the being may be wounded, lost, migrating, or part of the city’s own life cast outward. Wisdom does not make innocence from ignorance.

So the power attends to the passage also.

For the first time, the enormous transparency is wholly seen.

It is not a predator.

It is not harmless.

Long ago—though “ago” is inaccurate at that scale—it belonged to an arrangement that included the eastern districts. It grew through them until every relation became compulsory. Every tower answered every other. Every threshold measured the same passage. Every plaza reached the same conclusion. Unable to become more unified without extinguishing all difference, the arrangement tore itself from locality.

It has been passing the city ever since.

What the residents experience as fear is partly recognition.

What the abyssal being experiences, if experience is the word, is hunger for the body from which it exiled itself.

The power does not return it.

It does not drive it away.

It sees that these would be the same act performed in opposite directions: an answer imposed before either city or abyss has learned to choose.

At last, the power enters the civic life of the mountain.

It does so by taking the only place prepared for it.

In every hexagonal square, the central plinth remains empty.

The power remains absent there.

Its absence differs from ordinary absence. Ordinary absence erases, burdens, displaces, or waits to be filled. This absence does none of these. It is not vacancy but restraint: the shape made by one who could enter and chooses not to.

Dust gathers around the plinths.

For one dim hour it attempts proposals.

A throne forms in a basin and collapses.

Then a figure with many hands.

Then a flame, though no creature in the city remembers flame.

Then a perfect copy of the nearest tower.

Each proposal falls soundlessly into powder.

At last the dust makes a doorway standing alone, with no wall around it.

The doorway remains open.

The residents convene.

Columns multiply. Seven, eleven, thirteen, uncountable, one, none. Gravity separates into fine layers. The basins deepen without changing their dimensions. Towers across remote peaks incline by fractions. Crack-flowers whiten chambers around events that may now be permitted to occur.

The absent participant offers no plan.

It presents a relation.

The city understands relation.

Two distances agree to meet, and between them appears an arch unlike the city’s other arches. It bears no load. It joins no walls. Its only property is that neither side requires the other to be crossed.

This is new.

Every existing threshold participates in consequence. Passage closes something, recruits something, measures something, feeds something, leads upward, inward, elsewhere, or never back. To cross is to enter a rule.

But the new arch permits approach without capture.

A lintel animal forms above it and waits.

A young resident extends a stair toward the arch. The stair reaches the basin, hesitates, and withdraws. Nothing follows it. No avenue shortens. No courtyard closes around its retreat.

The young resident approaches again.

Again it withdraws.

The third time, it passes beneath.

The lintel animal does not contract.

For a moment the resident is smaller than it has ever been. It is not a district, not a recruited sequence of changes, not a preference spreading from stone into stone. It is only one threshold’s inclination toward a patch of shadow.

Yet it does not cease to exist.

It crosses back.

The city alters.

Not dramatically. No mountain breaks. No new light opens in the charcoal sky. The great residents do not bow, awaken, or become kind. Benevolence imposed upon beings is merely obedience taught to wear a gentle face.

Instead, one possibility enters the local weather:

A relation may be refused without becoming a wound.

The city studies this for a very long time.

At first it copies only the shape. Unsupported arches appear in plazas, on bridges, inside sealed rooms. Most are useless. Some turn passage into endless hesitation. One divides whatever crosses into its arrival and departure. Another permits return but not memory. The residents dispute the form, competing to recruit it into their existing anatomies.

The power does not correct them.

It preserves only the original possibility, faintly, wherever the city manages to express it without coercion.

Gradually, differences emerge.

A gate opens onto the back of a wall and is understood not as refusal, but as refusal without retaliation.

Two residents release a contested plaza. Gravity reunites there, not because one has won, but because both have ceased to insist.

A bridge extended into vacancy retracts. The vacancy is allowed to remain unanswered.

In a grief-stricken district, buildings assume their impossible pristine forms. Cracks seal. Stairs sharpen. Facades remember an origin that never occurred. As always, the district prepares to exclude every intrusion until the strain of perfection reduces it to powder.

This time, one doorway stays imperfect.

Its lintel is chipped. Its sides do not quite agree on height. The grieving resident could repair it.

It does not.

Through that uncorrected opening, dust leaves.

The district survives its grief with three walls missing and no need to pretend they were never there.

Elsewhere, a room with two shadows becomes larger than its exterior. A traveler enters and finds the old conversations trembling in its corners: apology without speaker, warning without danger, affection without an object.

Formerly, the room would have enlarged around the traveler’s attention. The resident containing it would have fed space to every spoken name until departure became unreachable.

Now a second door appears.

It leads exactly where it appears to lead.

The traveler weeps, though the tears do not reach the floor. They separate into clear spheres and hang in the cold air.

The city has never encountered liquid water.

Every nearby arrangement turns toward it.

Channels darken. Crack-flowers anticipate the spheres’ descent. Folded black creatures gather in angles, their surfaces showing sealed chambers and remote bridges. Lintel animals shorten all across the district. Deep beneath the saltstone, one of the oldest inhabitants brightens wafer by wafer.

The traveler fears the tears will be taken apart and studied forever.

The wise power does not conceal them.

Nor does it preserve them.

It allows the city to notice, and allows the tears to remain the traveler’s.

One sphere strikes the raised lip of the dry basin.

There is almost no sound.

But almost is more sound than the city has known in an age.

The drop spreads across the mineral surface. It darkens an area no wider than a fingernail. Then the air steadily removes its warmth, the stone takes its substance, and it is gone.

The mark remains until the next absence season.

The city does not create rivers.

It does not fill its channels, seed gardens, raise moss, or imitate the lower world. It understands that the drop was not an instruction. To love a thing is not necessarily to reproduce it.

Instead, in several protected crevices, the deep-red stone becomes a little warmer than before.

Only a little.

Enough that a hand placed there does not lose heat so quickly.

The wise power attends to the eastern abyss again.

The city still contracts when the transparent being passes, but its contraction has changed. Peripheral streets are released rather than abandoned. Lesser residents simplify without vanishing into panic. The upward-flowing darkness enters the mountain, yet not every surface is emptied of awareness.

On the easternmost wall, one arch remains awake.

It stands alone on the parapet.

Beyond it is granular depth and the serial disappearance of peaks.

The abyssal being passes behind the arch, though it is too vast to pass through. For one interval, its transparent immensity and the small unoccupied opening coincide.

The city offers neither reunion nor banishment.

It offers a threshold that does not compel crossing.

The passage in the abyss stops.

Distant portions of the city remain concealed. The eastern towers disappear and do not reappear. Pressure gathers. The sky develops cracks. Every lintel animal contracts to its smallest possible length.

The wise power could intervene now.

It could divide the abyssal being into gentler creatures. It could restore it to the mountain. It could build a boundary no enormity could cross. It could resolve the city into one immortal consciousness or many inviolable selves. Any of these acts would be within its strength.

It does none of them.

It holds the possibility open.

After a duration no inhabitant can measure, one distant tower becomes visible again.

Then a bridge.

Then a peak.

The abyssal being has moved aside.

Not departed. Not reconciled. Merely moved aside.

For such a being, this is the first gesture it has made that is not hunger.

The city answers by extending a bridge toward it.

The bridge stops before contact.

For the first time, an unfinished bridge is not a question demanding completion.

It is companionship at a distance.

After this, the power begins to withdraw.

Not because its work is finished. Nothing living is finished except by being made unable to change. It withdraws because attention, even benevolent attention, may become a climate under which others forget how to live unwitnessed.

It leaves no law.

Laws here are weather, and weather passes.

It leaves no temple. The city would make a body of the temple, then an organ, then a hunger.

It leaves no name. Distance has enough causes.

It leaves the arches, but not as permanent objects. Many erode. Some are recruited into residents and lose their freedom. Some become ordinary thresholds, measuring and altering those who pass. A few survive for ages in remote districts. Others appear only during certain elevation tides.

What remains is not architecture.

It is a habit the city did not possess before:

The pause between attention and possession.

The narrow interval between fear and contraction.

The incompleteness through which grief can breathe.

The possibility that a road may approach without shortening, that a door may open without consuming departure, that one thing may touch another and afterward they may still be two things.

Travelers continue to vanish.

Residents still quarrel through incompatible geography. Absence seasons erase whole quarters. Gravity divides. Dust makes proposals and most collapse. The vast transparent being still crosses the eastern depths, and sometimes the city is too afraid to leave even one arch awake.

Benevolence does not make the mountain safe.

Wisdom does not make it simple.

But now and then a traveler reaches a hexagonal square and finds the central plinth empty among an uncountable colonnade. At the far end stands the impression of a narrow figure.

The avenue shortens once.

Then stops.

Traveler and resident remain separated by the width of the square.

Neither is forced to approach.

If the traveler turns away, the streets permit departure. If the traveler waits, the figure becomes no clearer, but one column in the surrounding arcade inclines almost imperceptibly, like a being trying to bow without yet understanding the shape of courtesy.

Sometimes the traveler bows in return.

Nothing is solved.

A meeting occurs.

And in the shallow basin, the dust arranges itself into two open hands that do not close.
