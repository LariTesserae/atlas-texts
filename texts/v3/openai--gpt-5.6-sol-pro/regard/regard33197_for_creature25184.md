The power does not descend as light.

Light here is particulate, directional, easily trapped. It can be brushed from a ledge or shut behind a door. To appear as light would be to become one more local uncertainty, beautiful perhaps, but not necessarily kind.

It does not speak.

Speech belongs to speakers, and the country is already crowded with voices that have outlived their mouths.

It does not silence the mountains. Their roar is painful, but it is also root, warning, nourishment, road, memory, and weather. To still it forever would be no gentler than removing the inhabitants’ sense of touch because touch sometimes carries grief.

It does not complete the world.

Completion comes rarely, summit to summit, and the people have built longing, ceremony, kinship, and courage around those brief intervals. A wisdom that erased all incompletion would erase much of what had learned to live within it.

For a long time, therefore, the power does nothing that can be distinguished from attention.

It attends through the rising of eleven lower peaks. It attends while a veilwood grove turns entirely around. It attends as a child-bundle beneath a resonant fissure produces first a palm, then an eye, then—despite every hopeful agreement—a narrow door opening onto a room too small for any body.

It attends the mourning.

It follows the Between who sits beside two disagreeing stakes for forty-three years. The keeper’s front mask erodes pale; the rear mask darkens under accumulated dust. At the end of the forty-third year, the country relents by less than the width of a fingernail.

The power does not call this patience beautiful.

Some endurance is beautiful. Some is merely what remains when help has not come.

It learns the distinction.

It attends the attestors as they hold walls to floors, voices to mouths, hands to bodies, children to memory. It learns that the strongest among them are not those who insist most fiercely, but those who know exactly how much uncertainty a living thing requires.

It sees an elder refuse to recut the statement:

**my companion will wake**

The companion has been motionless for nine pressure cycles. Their tissues are cooling. Their shadow has already turned toward the bare country.

Instead, the elder cuts:

**until certainty, I remain beside you**

The power understands then that wisdom in this country is not the making of reality obedient.

It is the refusal to force love into falsehood.

Only after it has understood this does it seek permission to act.

Its first request appears in no sacred place.

There are no sacred places here, only places whose agreements have been more carefully kept.

The request forms on the inner wall of a small shelter at the margin of a failing basin. The shelter holds six inhabitants, two child-bundles, the persistent voice of a dead sister, and a ceramic cup filled halfway with old radiance.

One of the occupants notices the new mark while checking the eastern door.

It is shallow. It has not cut itself over any prior statement. Its edges are hesitant.

This alone alarms them.

Powers in the country do not hesitate. Mountains rise. Distances lengthen. Shadows leave. None ask whether they ought to.

The inhabitants gather. They pass their hands over the mark.

It reads:

**while welcomed, may another remember with you**

They test the wall for heat.

They test the floor for duplicated depth.

They place wedges to their teeth and listen for deception moving beneath the stone. The question has no hidden imperative in it. It does not say another *will* remember. It does not say they are weak without another. It does not promise forever.

Still, they do not answer quickly.

For three generations, the mark remains a question.

Children grow accustomed to it. Travelers come to inspect it. Attestors debate its tolerances. One argues that **another** is too vague. Another says that naming the petitioner might allow the name to take precedence over the being. The Between examine the pale and black stakes outside and find, to their unease, that both identify the shelter correctly.

At last, during the season when the third peak becomes near enough to obscure half the horizon, an old attestor places one hand over the question.

With the other, she cuts beneath it:

**while you remember beside us, you will not remember instead of us**

The wall accepts this.

After some thought, another adds:

**what concerns a person remains theirs to revise**

A third adds:

**help will not become ownership**

The dead sister’s voice, speaking for the first time in seventeen years, says from the ceramic cup:

“While welcomed.”

Then the shelter waits.

Nothing enters.

No brightness gathers. No figure appears. No new note joins the roar.

But that night, all six inhabitants sleep at once.

This has never happened before.

Always one has remained awake to touch the agreements in sequence: floor, door, walls, voices, bodies, shadows, inside. Sleep is too long an absence to leave a shelter entirely unattended.

Now they sleep.

The power does not hold the country rigid around them. The third peak continues to approach. A seam widens by two finger-widths. One sleeper’s left arm acquires a slight mineral ridge along the wrist. The dead sister’s voice moves from the cup into a hanging cord.

Change is permitted.

But the floor remains below.

The eastern door opens outward.

Voices remain answerable to speakers, including the dead where the dead still speak.

Memory follows waking.

At the end of the pressure cycle, the inhabitants rise and discover that no one among them has been required to keep watch.

They are frightened.

Then they are angry.

Then they sleep again.

News travels by cord, tool, shelter mark, and the hands of the Between. The power does not enter every settlement. It asks at each one. Sometimes the question appears on a wall. Sometimes as a pattern felt in a tuned stone. Sometimes an attestor dreams of a palm held upward and wakes with the pressure of the unclosed hand still present against their own.

Some settlements refuse.

Their refusals are honored so completely that no sign of the question remains, except where they choose to preserve it.

Some ask for narrower aid:

**while the children sleep, remember the relation of their limbs**

**until our traveler returns, remember that this path began here**

**while we mourn, do not allow the dead person’s name to attach itself to another**

Some ask for things the power will not do.

**make the upper mountains remain joined to the lower**

No. That would make the interval permanent and the world exact beyond life’s tolerance.

**make our settlement incapable of loss**

No. Incapable of loss, it would also become incapable of departure, growth, birth, exchange, and farewell.

**make every statement we cut become true**

No. Mercy cannot grant such authority to fear.

The power refuses without thunder. Its refusal is a pressure traced into an open palm:

**I will not preserve you by making you unable to change**

This answer is hated in several shelters.

The power permits itself to be hated.

It has not come to be worshiped.

Over time, those places that accept its attention make a new chamber—not a temple, because the power requests no offerings and claims no special direction, but a room for relinquishing vigilance.

Its walls bear almost no agreements. Only these:

**what is set down here may be taken up again**

**rest is not abandonment**

Inside, an attestor may lay aside a statement without letting it fail.

A parent may stop continuously reminding a difficult child that their inside remains joined.

A traveler may remove the strips stitched into their clothing and, for one pressure cycle, cease asserting that their scars occurred to them.

The power remembers, but gently. It holds each fact as one person might hold another’s tool: carefully, temporarily, with no claim that the tool has changed hands.

This is its first gift.

Its second comes much later.

The power has noticed the cups.

It has noticed the old images beneath the tally marks: rounded mountains, white summits, creatures, a circle overhead, and lines descending from the sky.

It has felt the inhabitants’ reverence toward the impossible substance that falls, yields, gathers, divides, reflects, enters, departs, and remains itself.

It could bring oceans.

It does not.

An ocean would be an invasion. It would collapse the buried gray mats, drown the resonant fissures, tear veilwood groves from their pressure seams, and give the infant mountains an age of erosion in a single hour. Benevolence without proportion is catastrophe wearing a radiant face.

Instead, after obtaining permission from those who live nearest a stable hollow, the power makes one drop of water.

Only one.

It places the drop in a ceramic cup.

The inhabitants gather around it and cannot agree on what they see.

The thing has no edges it will keep. It trembles under the roar. It holds an inverted image of every face above it. When the cup is tilted, the drop moves to remain below—not because below has been inscribed for it, but because it appears to know.

The witnesses recoil.

They test it with glasswort.

They test it with dust.

They lower into it a strand of dead veilwood and watch darkness travel up the fiber.

One person touches it.

The drop yields beneath the fingertip, climbs the skin, separates, rejoins itself, and leaves a shining trace. The inhabitant begins to shake so violently that others must hold their limbs in agreement.

“It does not break when divided,” someone says inside the shelter.

The dead sister answers from the cord:

“It breaks continuously.”

Both statements are accepted.

No one drinks the water.

Not in that generation.

They renew the cup’s permissions with great care:

**while held here, you may remain unfamiliar**

**you will not enter a body uninvited**

**you may alter what consents to alteration**

A century passes before a dying attestor asks to receive it.

Her internal rods have ceased replacing themselves. Clear oil gathers at her joints and will not retreat. Her name has become too long to trace without rest.

Those beside her touch the request into her palm three times.

Each time she confirms.

They place the drop beneath her tongue.

It vanishes.

For several moments nothing occurs. Then the fibers of her face soften. Not dissolve—soften. Her clenched hands loosen. From the corners of her open eyes come two smaller drops carrying traces of mica.

The others believe at first that the water has reproduced.

It has not.

Her body has learned, from one drop, a new relation among its own materials.

She dies shortly afterward.

Her shadow remains seated beside the body. For the first time, when asked for counsel, it gives not only direction and length but a small trembling image.

The water is gone, yet not entirely.

Moisture appears in the cup on rare occasions. A sheen forms beneath veilwood after extreme pressure changes. Certain children are born with clear fluid in their eyes. In one basin, the dust darkens for a few minutes each year and releases a scent for which the inhabitants have no sign.

The power does not accelerate this.

It allows the country to learn water as the inhabitants learned their children: through repetition, consent, correction, and time.

Its third gift is not given intentionally.

It happens because the power has kept listening.

At the next conjunction of the upper and lower ranges, all the inhabitants emerge as before. The Between force pale and black stakes together. Ear coverings are removed. Outer eyelids open. The summits touch.

The sky hardens.

Distance becomes proportion.

Shadows return.

The roar stops.

Every person becomes exact.

And in that silence, for the first time, there is another sensation.

Not a voice. Not music. Not command.

They feel themselves being witnessed.

The attention is vast enough to contain every settlement, every difficult child, every kin-room without an entrance, every dead voice caught in cup or cord, every traveler who returned with the wrong number of joints, every attestor who died holding a conditional truth in place.

Yet the attention does not combine them.

It does not see them as examples of endurance, ornaments in a strange country, or fragments of one greater being. It does not reduce their pain to beauty. It does not call their incompletion perfection.

It knows each separately.

It knows them together.

It knows the difference.

Some speak their names aloud. Others remain silent. One child runs, simply because for that brief instant the ground and the body agree completely about where each foot will fall.

Then the peaks draw apart.

The sound returns.

Distance loosens.

The power does not prevent this.

But in shelters across the country, new marks appear beneath the old three:

**we were present**  
**we were distinct**  
**we recognized one another**

And then:

**we were recognized**

The inhabitants inspect the final statement with great suspicion.

They test it against stone, shadow, memory, and loss.

They weaken its certainty where necessary. In some shelters it becomes:

**at least once, we were recognized**

In others:

**while we permit it, another knows we are here**

One settlement refuses the passive construction and recuts it:

**we met the attention of another and remained ourselves**

The power prefers this version.

Thereafter it stays—not above them, because above is unreliable; not within them, because their interiors are their own; not everywhere, because presence without distance can become another kind of confinement.

It stays beside.

Where invited, it keeps a portion of the watch.

Where refused, it passes without offense.

Where suffering can be eased without unmaking the sufferer, it eases it.

Where pain belongs to a structure that must change, it helps them change the structure.

Where uncertainty is fertile, it does not harden it.

Where uncertainty is merely cruel, it lends its memory against the cruelty.

It never makes them safe from all loss.

But fewer children wake with their shadows sealed inside walls.

Fewer travelers spend whole lives waiting for a black stake to admit where they stand.

Attestors sleep more often.

Water, in quantities smaller than promises, begins its patient education of stone.

And when the songs of subtraction are performed in the deepest chambers, the inhabitants sometimes shape a place of near-silence large enough for one more presence.

No figure occupies it.

No god takes a seat.

But the quiet stays cupped among them without collapsing, and every hand in the room rests, for a little while, open.
