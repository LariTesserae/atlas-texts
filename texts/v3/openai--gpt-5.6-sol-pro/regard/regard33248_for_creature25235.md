The power does not give the country animals.

It does not loose insects beneath the leaves, though flowers elsewhere have taught it to associate flowers with wings. It does not populate the reservoirs with silver bodies or place birds in the empty updrafts. Nothing is missing merely because another world would have filled the space.

It does not still the storms.

It does not brace the towers against every wind, prevent terraces from overturning, or preserve structures whose loss appears tragic from above. It understands that destruction here is not the opposite of life. To make the plateau safe would be to make it rigid. To make it permanent would be to kill it slowly.

It does not give the being a face.

It does not gather the distributed signals into a single chamber, crown one root as a brain, or awaken a voice that can say *I*. Perhaps there is already experience here, too broad or slow to resemble thought. Perhaps there is none. The power is wise enough not to mistake personhood for completion.

It does not descend in light.

For a long time, it only attends.

Attention, from such a power, is an exacting form of restraint.

It follows water from cloud to bladder, from bladder to rib, from rib through the black cables under three valleys. It watches a wound close in the pavement and the information of that wound travel outward. It sees where the signal weakens, where another network refuses it, where two incompatible chemistries meet along a glossy ridge and remain separate.

It learns that what first appeared to be one response is often many.

A tower bows because pressure has fallen.

A tower beside it bows because the first tower has shaded its base.

A third remains upright, receiving no message from either, though all three are joined beneath the ground.

It learns the difference between a district accepting water and being unable to prevent its entry. It learns the difference between graft and engulfment. It learns which seed coats are digested, which are tolerated, and which cause the surrounding pavement to reshape itself in welcome.

The power observes for longer than any tower lives.

It does not become impatient.

Eventually it discovers how the country asks questions.

They are not questions as mouths make them. They are provisional structures: a root extended without reinforcement, a membrane held open beyond its immediate need, a sequence of pressure changes repeated into an unresponsive boundary. The living land tries a form against the world and waits to learn whether the world bears weight in return.

The power answers once.

Far out on the warm pavement, between a reservoir basin and a grove of resonant stalks, condensation begins to fall.

There are no clouds overhead.

The droplets are ordinary water. They contain no medicine, no new element, no hidden command. They fall lightly enough that the pavement can reject them if it chooses.

But their spacing is impossible.

Three drops strike along a line. Then five in an arc. Then three again, farther apart. The interval repeats. Each sequence is slow enough to pass through the pressure-sensitive rims of the pavement cells before the next begins.

The darkening travels outward.

Feeder threads gather beneath the place, find nothing dead, and withdraw. A shallow groove rises to route the water away. The power shifts the pattern with it, never crossing the groove, allowing the country to determine the boundary of the exchange.

For several days, the plateau absorbs the sequence.

Then, during an otherwise windless afternoon, three hollow towers sound.

Not together. In order.

The notes are low and imprecise, altered by the water in their bases, but the interval between them matches the falling drops.

The power waits.

It could interpret this as recognition. It could interpret it as coincidence shaped by sensitivity and repetition. Benevolence does not make certainty where certainty is unavailable.

So it begins again, more carefully.

It speaks through conditions the country already understands: weight, light, moisture, strain.

Across one uninhabited stretch of pavement, it lays down the sensation of a distant place.

Not an image. The being has no organ for horizons it has not grown.

Instead, there is the pressure of a weaker gravity. The rhythm of a longer day. The mineral trace of rain that has passed over bare mountains. A sequence of temperatures from a world circling another sun. The pull of two small moons in shallow tides.

Then the power offers absence.

All local chemical messages cease at the edge of the pattern. No neighboring root replies. No distant reservoir alters its pressure. No tower leans across the interval. There is only the foreign rain, the foreign light, and room.

The country recoils.

The pavement tightens hard enough to crack its own upper layers. Resin floods the nearest conduits. Leaf towers turn their faces away, and a seed pylon a kilometer distant ruptures before maturity, throwing empty scales across the plain.

The power ends the contact immediately.

It does not return the next day, or that season.

The damaged pavement is enclosed and remade. The cracks become drainage lines. One of the empty seed scales roots, though it contains no seed, and is absorbed into the rim of a small basin.

Years pass.

Then the country repeats the foreign rhythm by itself.

A reservoir begins tipping at intervals matched not to the local day but to the longer day it was shown. It does this only three times. The behavior wastes water. Neighboring growths constrict its roots, and for a season the basin is nearly starved.

Yet the rhythm travels.

A tower takes it up in the opening and closing of its back membrane. Farther away, pavement cells darken in bands. Buried cables alter their pressures. Some districts respond. Others seal themselves behind resin.

The plateau has not reached agreement.

The power understands this better than it would understand unanimity.

It offers the distant place again, but only where the pattern has been repeated in welcome. Nothing is pressed upon the closed districts. No signal passes through the black scar boundaries. The foreign world is laid gently against those portions of the living architecture that have extended themselves toward it.

The answer takes forty-three local years.

At first, it resembles a mistake in the pavement: a long swelling no higher than a human knee. It lies across the open plain, gathering threads from different systems. Some are admitted. Some are strangled at the margin. Some enter, withdraw, and return seasons later with altered membranes.

The swelling grows into a braided structure unlike any ordinary seed.

It contains no central embryo. Instead, chambers form along its length.

Pavement cells fold themselves into dry layers. Pale feeders coil into dormancy around packets of fallen fiber. Root cables contribute soft, undifferentiated tips. A reservoir grows a small transparent bladder inside the structure and fills it with water. Towers send strips of light-sensitive tissue down through temporary grafts. Three competing districts each wall off their contribution from the others, preserving boundaries even inside the common hull.

The country does not make a summary of itself.

It makes a disagreement capable of travel.

As the structure matures, the plain around it empties. Water is withdrawn. The pavement releases its grip. Connections thin one by one, not cut by the power but sealed by the growths that made them.

The last attachment belongs to an old black cable extending beneath the western escarpment. It remains after every other graft has closed.

For another rainy season, nothing changes.

The traveling body lies ready but not free.

The power does not pull.

At last a storm crosses the valleys. Towers bow. Reservoirs overturn. Wind enters the resonant grove and builds from the lowest note upward until the entire plateau is sounding.

The old cable separates along a clean seam.

The power moves then.

It does not take the shape of a hand. A column of air forms beneath the braided body, broad enough to distribute the load, gentle enough not to tear the dormant chambers. The structure rises a few meters and hangs there while rain passes under it.

The country tightens.

Roots lift from the ground as if to reclaim what has already been released. Several towers turn. The old cable floods its sealed end with resin.

Still the body is not held.

The column rises.

It carries the traveler through mist, then cloud, then air too thin for leaves. The power encloses it against cold and radiation, but alters nothing within. No new instructions are written into its tissues. No guarantee is placed at its center.

The plateau falls away: green terraces, bright water, storm shadow, continent, curve of world.

The traveler cannot see any of this.

It registers only decreasing pressure, falling temperature, unfamiliar strain, and then the long suspension in which none of its senses receive an answer.

The power bears it across the distance between suns.

The chosen world is not a garden.

There are no inhabitants to dispossess. No hidden cells sleep in its seas. No chemistry there has crossed the uncertain threshold into life.

It has rain. It has heat. It has broad highlands beneath heavy clouds.

It also has stone.

Stone lies everywhere, naked and unresponsive. Water runs over it without being captured. Cracks remain cracks. Fallen matter, when there is matter to fall, will not be wrapped and drawn into a vascular dark. The ground does not listen.

The power lowers the braided body onto a warm shelf above an empty basin.

Then it recreates the conditions it promised.

The weaker gravity.

The long day.

Rain bearing the mineral trace of bare mountains.

The pull of two moons.

Nothing more.

For a long time, the traveler appears dead.

Its outer layers bleach beneath the foreign sun. Wind packs grit against one side. Water gathers under it and freezes during an unusually cold night. A chamber ruptures.

The power watches and does not repair it.

This, too, is restraint.

On the forty-first day, a seam opens.

Pale feeder threads emerge. They touch the stone and recoil. They touch the grit, curl around individual grains, and draw them beneath the hull. Most of the threads harden uselessly. Others change their secretions. Acids bead at their tips. The rock clouds, pits, and begins, atom by atom, to enter life.

A transparent bladder unfolds and catches rain.

Dry pavement cells spread across the wet margin. They cannot grip the smooth stone, so they layer themselves over the dead feeder threads. More emerge behind them. The failure becomes foundation.

By the end of the foreign year, a green patch lies on the shelf.

It is less than twenty meters wide.

Its surface is uneven, scarred, and dull. Nothing resembles a tower. No reservoir has yet learned to tip under the lesser weight. The buried cables are thin enough to tear by hand.

But the patch holds water.

When the twin moons pull on the basin below, pressure changes in its tiny vessels.

Three pulses travel through the new-made ground.

The power receives them.

Across the distance, on the first plateau, dawn is entering the valleys. Condensation gathers under the great leaves. A storm has passed in the night and turned part of the eastern plain upright. Waterfalls stream from what was once its horizontal surface.

The power touches nothing living.

It changes no sap and moves no root.

It only presses, very lightly, on the air inside three hollow towers.

They sound in order.

The notes are not words. They do not say *safe*, because the new world is not safe. They do not say *child*, because what traveled was not a child. They do not say *you continue*, because no one can decide what *you* includes.

They carry only the rhythm that came from the green patch beneath the other sun.

Three pulses.

Received.

After that, the power withdraws.

It leaves no monument. It asks for no recognition. It does not make the two worlds dependent upon its relay. The first plateau returns to its storms, its contests, its engulfments and grafts. The second begins the immense labor of covering what has never been alive.

The power does not promise either of them immortality.

It has done something smaller, and perhaps wiser.

It has given what was rooted in one place the chance to encounter distance without making distance a conquest.

It has preserved neither an individual nor a species, since neither term can be trusted here.

It has carried a living disagreement into an empty world.

And there, under ordinary light, the ground begins to be made.
