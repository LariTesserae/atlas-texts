The power does nothing at first.

This is not neglect.

It has found places where mercy must arrive as rain, places where it must arrive as fire, and places where the kindest act is to break a gate before those behind it have learned to hope. But here, where one drop waters half a city and the removal of one calcite grain may alter a labor older than nations, haste would be another form of violence.

So the power attends.

It does not enter the hollow. Entry would displace air, water, sound, perhaps stone. It does not pour light through the ceiling. Light would mean little to the Kept and much to the organisms that have never learned to defend themselves against it. It does not stop the springs, even for a breath. It does not lift the pressure from above. The city has shaped itself around that pressure so completely that sudden relief might shatter what weight has held together.

Instead, the power lays its awareness against the limestone.

It listens as the Kept listen.

For a long while it knows only the coarse things: the fall at the central shaft, the deep basin note, the water wheels, the city’s crowded shell within the seam. Then it learns to distinguish the finer motions. A scraper’s tooth lifting scale from a collar joint. Four hooked feet crossing the underside of a bridge. A cradle keeper touching a sealed ancestor. The brief absences made by musicians around a singing channel.

It learns that the pillar is not merely a pillar. It is duty, uncertainty, inherited fear, and the place where the city’s conscience rests its hands.

It learns the difference between an unused chamber and an abandoned one.

It learns not to count the walking bodies alone.

It listens through several emergences and several failures to emerge. It listens while one of the Kept dies at the central collar with its forelimbs still pressed to the joint. It listens while another takes the dead one’s place before the vibration of departure has faded from the stone.

It listens to the Listeners to the Unreturned, who permit a white crust to close an old stair and discover beneath it a nourishing orange seam. It listens to the orthodox inspectors at the district margin, who record the new resonance with disapproval so exact that it resembles longing.

The power comes to love them.

This, too, is not yet permission to act.

Love that cannot bear to learn the shape of another’s necessity is appetite.

So the power waits until it understands enough to ask.

---

Its first message is almost mistaken for damage.

In a disused passage above the seventh silt terrace, a section of blank wall gives three minute taps.

Not the three strokes for danger. The intervals are wrong.

The nearest citizen presses its jaw plates to the floor. The taps come again, this time from beneath, though there is no chamber beneath that passage. Then from the ceiling. Then from the wall.

The citizen reports an unclassified resonance.

Inspectors arrive. They search for cracks, trapped water, settling clay, loose shell, wheel-transmitted chatter, deception, and signs of structural movement. They find none.

For nine labor divisions, there is nothing.

Then the stone taps a sequence corresponding to:

**Listening.  
Not danger.  
Request assembly.**

The Kept do not assemble.

First they verify.

They close nearby channels one at a time. The tapping adjusts to each altered resonance. They scrape the wall. It waits. They strike test plates in distant districts. It answers from the same blank stone, preserving meaning despite the changed route. They send a deliberately false report through the wall, saying that a retaining ledge has split. The answer comes:

**Report does not match pressure. Inspect again.**

The second inspection finds no split.

This is more persuasive to them than any miracle.

The caretakers of the central pillar are summoned. So are burial keepers, feeder-mappers, wheel tenders, channel arbiters, two silt-lifters, and—after dispute—three Listeners to the Unreturned.

They gather in the passage until their shells touch.

The power does not speak into their minds. It does not assume that an inward voice would be understood as anything but injury. It taps the stone. Where condition and certainty would ordinarily be carried by flavored droplets, it causes minute traces of mineral to gather on the wall: iron for direct witness, salt for intention, sulfur for warning, copper for uncertainty.

Its speech is slow and clumsy.

**I have listened.**

Iron.

**I am outside the hollow.**

Iron again, though the Kept test the grain repeatedly.

**I can alter pressure, stone, and water.**

Iron touched with sulfur.

**I do not know your city as you do.**

Copper.

This last statement causes the longest discussion.

A being able to speak through unbroken stone, they reason, may be powerful. A being that distinguishes its power from its knowledge may possibly be sane.

The eldest pillar caretaker rests four hands against the wall and taps:

**Are you pressure?**

The answer:

**No.**

**Are you water?**

**No.**

**Are you alive?**

A long pause.

Then:

**In a meaning larger than yours and smaller than some.**

This answer is regarded as irritating but not evasive.

A burial keeper asks:

**Have you come to open the hollow?**

The power answers at once.

**Not without being asked.**

Sulfur. Iron. Salt.

The entire assembly feels the certainty of it.

Then the power says:

**I have found a burden falling where you cannot return it. I ask permission to show you.**

---

They do not agree immediately.

Some maintain that anything outside the hollow is, by definition, unmeasured pressure and therefore cannot be admitted to civic consequence. Some insist the speaker is an unrecorded property of limestone. One citizen argues that it is the city speaking from a district too large for ordinary hearing. A Listener to the Unreturned suggests that the distinction may not matter.

The debate circulates through the walls.

For the first time in the city’s embedded history, every district answers the same question: whether knowledge may be accepted from something that does not bear a named maintenance duty.

The eventual decision is not trust.

It is permission for one inspection.

The conditions are exact. No spring may be altered. No living film may be touched. No load may be shifted. No sealed chamber may be opened. No object may be taken from or returned to the basin. The demonstration must be reversible, though several citizens point out that knowledge itself cannot be returned.

The power accepts.

It asks them to place resonant plates at seventeen points: beside the pillar, under the highest burial galleries, along the central cascade, in the altered districts, and at three sealed walls where the city believes there is only solid rock.

When the plates are ready, the power touches the limestone far outside the cavity.

The touch is gentle enough that no grain moves.

A deep vibration passes through the bed of stone.

The Kept feel the world.

Not all of it. Not the surface, not the weather, not the lands under sun. The power knows better than to put an ocean into a cistern. It gives them only the nearby pressure: the folds, faults, closed pockets, drowned fissures, and load paths in the rock surrounding their seam.

For the first time, the hollow is revealed not as an absence resisting a roof, but as one chamber among many.

Some nearby cavities are no larger than a cradle. Some are filled with clay. One contains air that has not moved since before the city’s first foundation. Another holds water under such pressure that opening it would cut the central district apart.

And beside the pillar, beyond a wall no Kept has ever crossed, lies a wedge of dense limestone descending from the upper bed into the lower.

It bears much of the load they thought the pillar carried alone.

The pillar remains necessary. The collar remains necessary. But their measurements have confused vibration with weight. The central cascade causes the pillar to speak more loudly than the hidden wedge. Generations have listened most closely to the structure that can be heard.

The burden falling where it cannot be returned is not yet stone.

It is certainty.

Their reverence has concentrated labor at the city’s center while quieter supports have gone nearly unexamined.

The power does not say this.

It lets the plates speak.

---

For a long interval afterward, the city is disturbed.

Nothing cracks. No channel overflows. No citizen is harmed. Yet routes change. Names lengthen. Inspectors cross into districts they have never had cause to enter. The pillar caretakers resist transferring even one labor division away from the collar. The keepers of the newly discovered load paths refuse responsibility until the stone has been listened to across a full cycle of scale removal and renewal.

The Listeners to the Unreturned say the hidden wedge proves that the city has mistaken the audible for the real.

The orthodox reply that the wedge endured precisely because no Listener had been allowed to improve it.

Both claims circulate.

The power remains against the outer stone but sends no further message.

At last the eldest pillar caretaker returns to the speaking wall.

**You showed. You did not alter.**

**Yes.**

**Can the uncertainty of the collar be inspected in the same way?**

Here the power could simply answer. It knows the composition of the calcite, the depth of the microfractures, the paths by which the water deposits new crystal. It knows that the old collar is neither wholly helped nor wholly harmed by accretion. Some joints are strengthening. Others are being forced apart beneath apparently smooth scale.

But an answer would become doctrine. The Kept would preserve it after its conditions had changed. A truth delivered without method might become a more dangerous inheritance than ignorance.

So the power says:

**Yes, but not by a single inspection. You require a place where consequence can be made small.**

They have no term for experiment that does not also mean damage.

The power teaches them one.

Not by definition, but by architecture.

---

With permission, it maps a narrow region beyond the upper west wall. There, a shallow natural cavity lies separated from the city by less than the width of a Kept’s forelimb. The cavity is dry by their standards, structurally quiet, and crossed by two nearly identical calcite ribs.

The power offers to open it.

The city imposes conditions again. The opening must be no larger than necessary. Dust must fall into prepared clay. The first breach must occur behind a sealed spray curtain. All new water routes must possess closures. No unfamiliar substance may be introduced.

The power could dissolve the wall without debris. Instead it guides the Kept’s tools.

For the first time, the city expands not by preserving an ancient accident but by knowingly entering a place that has never been part of it.

They name the chamber **The Room Where Error Is Given Walls**.

In it they build two small collars around the twin calcite ribs. They scrape one according to inherited practice. On the other, they allow accretion to remain. The power does not tell them what will happen. It helps them arrange resonant plates fine enough to detect load, hidden splitting, and crystal growth separately.

The results take years.

This is appropriate.

The scraped collar remains flexible but slowly loses material at three inner joints. The encrusted collar strengthens along its lower arc and begins to split beneath its upper seam. Neither tradition is vindicated. Neither is condemned.

The Kept develop a third practice: permitting growth where growth distributes pressure, removing it where it conceals or concentrates pressure.

The first time this practice is applied to the sacred pillar, the entire city gathers through the walls.

A caretaker leaves one pale line of calcite unscraped.

Nothing collapses.

The line thickens by less than a grain.

It is the smallest visible change made intentionally in the central court for longer than any count can prove.

The Kept light a lamp.

---

Only then does the power offer another gift.

It has tasted, through the stone, the impoverishment of the feeding films. It has traced the old chemical routes by which the city once supported more walking citizens. The ancient population marks were not entirely misread. There had been more.

A shift in water far above the cave—far beyond anything the Kept could sense—had closed several microscopic paths through iron-rich stone. The springs did not weaken, but their composition narrowed. The oldest households survived inside the Kept while losing the exterior films that allowed them to flourish. Birth became rarer. Cradles remained closed. The city adapted so perfectly that starvation came to resemble balance.

The power could restore the old routes at once.

It does not.

A sudden iron bloom would poison half the living bowls, clog channels, favor some inner households over others, and perhaps awaken more cradle-knots than the city could feed. Plenty, arriving without sequence, would be a flood.

Instead the power shows them three sealed seams.

It tells them what lies beyond each: iron in one, sulfur compounds in another, a trace mineral missing from several of the oldest interior households in the third. It shows where a bore no wider than a mouth comb might meet each seep. It helps them calculate the rates.

Then it asks:

**Do you wish for more?**

The answer is not immediate.

More food means more citizens. More citizens mean more labor, but also more consumption, more cradle chambers, more disputes, more pressure upon districts built around rarity. Some Kept fear not scarcity but multitude. The city prepared for possible arrivals in principle; actual arrivals would alter every duty.

One burial keeper taps:

**If the sealed emerge, are they returned or new?**

The power answers:

**Both.**

Copper and iron together.

**Will they live?**

**Some. If you prepare.**

**Will they die?**

**Yes.**

There is no softening mineral for this certainty.

At last the city chooses one seam, the narrowest. Not restoration. Not abundance. A measured opening.

The Kept drill it themselves.

The work takes the remainder of one caretaker’s life. When the final stone skin grows thin, the power does not pierce it. A young feeder—young only in the sense that its shell bears little calcite—places a flint point against the membrane and turns.

A bead appears.

It is yellow at the edge and dark at the center.

The feeder tastes it, recoils, tastes it again, and begins to tremble so strongly that the floor carries the motion into three adjacent districts.

One of its inherited households, known only from internal residue and old flavor records, has found its exterior nourishment.

The new seep is caught in a cup with five closures. For many cycles it is opened only long enough to wet the tip of a palp. The city learns its concentration. The brine bowls are tested. Feeding films are established in isolated niches. Nothing is allowed to spread without a return channel and a means of interruption.

In time, the mineral is carried to one of the sealed elders.

Not the most promising cradle. Not the oldest. The burial keepers choose the one whose chemical changes most closely match the revived household.

They introduce a quantity too small to see.

For a long while, nothing happens.

Then the opened shell answers a tap.

Once.

The attendants wait, pressed together around the chamber.

It answers again.

The vibration is weak, irregular, and unmistakably from within.

The city does not ring plates or light lamps. Celebration would overwhelm the signal. Instead, district by district, shutters close over the channels.

Not completely. Water must continue.

But throughout the hollow, the roar is diminished.

Silences move outward from the cradle chamber in carefully timed intervals. The music is the largest the city has ever performed. A shape made of absence passes under bridges, through workshops, around cistern throats, over the pillar collar, and down to the margin of the black basin.

At its center is a small, uneven tapping.

The unsealed citizen takes many years to emerge. When it does, it is dangerously hungry, as all new citizens are, and softer than the records prepared anyone to expect. It closes the cradle chamber with plates from the body that made it. Then it is brought to the new seep.

Its first civic name is the resonance of the chamber.

Its second is the route from the dead to nourishment.

Later, after it has lived long enough to earn a work-name, it will be called:

**One Who Entered Because Enough Was Not Mistaken for All.**

---

The Kept eventually ask the power about the surface.

They have inferred its existence from the outside pressure-map. The question circulates for a long time before anyone gives it tapping.

**Is there a greater hollow?**

The power considers images that would be true and useless: sky, distance, weather, green things drinking light, cities taller than mountains from the Kept’s measure, oceans so broad that their black basin would vanish within a wave’s reflection.

It answers:

**There are places where stone does not close above the living.**

The assembly has difficulty receiving this.

**What bears the roof?**

**There is no roof.**

Many conclude that the power’s translation has failed.

A Listener asks:

**Would we live there?**

The power answers:

**Not as you are.**

**Is it better?**

The power waits.

Then:

**It is more open. This is not the same as better.**

The Kept understand that distinction.

They do not ask to see it.

Not then.

---

The power never retrieves anything from the basin.

It could.

It knows where many sounding weights lie. It knows the folded fissures that make depth contradictory. It knows that some water descends, travels through stone for centuries, and rises far away beneath an open sky. It knows that what the city calls unreturned is not annihilated, only removed beyond the reach of its circuits.

But to return an ancient tool as proof would make the basin a treasury. Citizens might risk lines, bodies, channels, whole duties against the promise of recovery. More importantly, the basin’s moral meaning is not a geological mistake. Some consequences do pass beyond the power of those who caused them to repair.

Benevolence does not teach that nothing is ever lost.

It teaches where loss is real, where it is only hidden, and where another may carry what cannot be brought home.

So when the Kept finally ask:

**Does the basin end?**

The power replies:

**Yes.**

**Does it return?**

**Not here.**

**Is what enters it destroyed?**

**No.**

**Can you bring it back?**

**Some things.**

**Will you?**

The power places iron, salt, and copper in the wall.

**Not unless need is greater than the danger of return.**

This answer becomes the first civic record that is neither wholly embedded nor wholly circulating. They carve no inscription for it. Instead they build a small spillway at the basin’s edge with one channel entering and another permanently sealed.

The sealed channel records the possibility.

The flowing one records the choice.

---

In the end, the power does not remain as ruler, oracle, or hidden support.

It will not become the structure whose failure the city cannot survive.

It gives the Kept a tapping sequence by which they may call its attention. The sequence is long enough that accident cannot reproduce it and ends with a statement rather than a plea:

**Consequence exceeds our measure. Come listen.**

Then it withdraws its immediate touch from the limestone.

Not entirely. Benevolent powers, when they are wise, do not confuse departure with abandonment. It continues to know the hollow. It keeps storms above from driving poison into the springs. It turns aside one future drilling machine whose operators would never have noticed the city before destroying it. It leaves other dangers untouched when the Kept can perceive and answer them themselves.

Most of all, it does not complete their world for them.

The city continues to be wet, loud, burdened, and small. The black basin still trembles. The pillar still requires hands. Citizens still die. Some cradles never open. The Listeners still permit certain stairs to vanish beneath crystal, and the orthodox still stand at the margins recording every dangerous note.

But now there are chambers where error can be made small.

There are new feeding films under measured flow.

There are districts allowed to become, and districts charged to remain.

There is a support in the western stone that is listened to despite its quietness.

There is one line of calcite on the central collar that no caretaker scrapes merely because the previous caretaker scraped it.

And once in a great while, when a decision exceeds all inherited duties, the Kept gather at the speaking wall. They place their hands against the stone. They tap the sequence.

Sometimes the power answers.

Sometimes it does not, because the consequence is already within their measure and wisdom would be diminished by replacing it.

On those occasions they wait, inspect, argue, taste the evidence, and decide.

Far above, landscapes flood and dry. Green things root, flower, and vanish. Cities rise under the open rooflessness of the sky.

Below, in the seam of pale limestone, a drop crosses the same crowded roofs.

Not quite the same.

At one ledge, where the old gutter once returned every displacement to its inherited route, the water now divides. Most of it follows the ancient channel.

A minute portion enters a newer one.

The Kept built its closure themselves. They inspect it without pause. Beyond it lie rooms that were not prepared for departed citizens or citizens who failed to arrive.

They are rooms for those who may come because the city has chosen to make room.

The new channel has a faint, uncertain note.

The city listens to it.

And does not silence it.
