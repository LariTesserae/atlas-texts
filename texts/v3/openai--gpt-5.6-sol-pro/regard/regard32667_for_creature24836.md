The power does not descend.

Descent would mean choosing a direction, and direction here is an appetite.

It does not enter through the sky, because the sky is already occupied by mist, detached shadows, old light, and the calls of walkers arriving before their throats. It does not rise through the water, because the water remembers too many arrivals and would mistake the power for something ancient returning.

Nor does it take a body.

Anything with a surface would be grazed. Anything with a pulse would be incorporated. A body large enough to resist the plain would crush what it meant to save; a body small enough not to crush would become food, signal, mineral, instinct, weather.

The power is wise, and so its first act is attention.

It watches for a length of time that has no local equivalent. It learns the pumping of the towers, including the tiny hesitations that distinguish agreement from reflex. It follows the coin-grazers over seams too faint for light to reveal. It allows the bells to strike against its notice until their clatter resolves into warnings, invitations, territorial disputes, and songs made by creatures that cannot survive alone long enough to sing.

It observes the pale radial hunters and does not call them cruel.

It observes the backward-feeders and does not call them evil.

It observes prey becoming younger under force, herds following calls they have not yet made, scavengers divided across kilometers, and dead instincts passing without consent into living towers. It does not mistake suffering for error. Neither does it mistake the age of the system for justice.

The distinction takes time.

The power could still the radial hunters forever. It could break the siphons of the backward-feeders, silence the six-chambered listeners, separate the towers into harmless animals, lower the black water, raise continents, set a sun burning above the cloud, and cover the plain with green.

It does none of these things.

Green would be an invasion.

Dryness would be a mass death.

Silence would starve creatures that feed upon vibration. Peace imposed upon the hunters would be merely hunger stretched without end. Even the beautiful gifts available to the power carry assumptions from worlds where roots mean healing and sunlight means grace.

This place has never asked to become another place.

So the power continues to attend.

Eventually it discovers that the broad basin is aware of being watched.

Not all at once. Awareness begins in bell shoals, which alter their collisions around an obstruction that has no location. It passes into tower roots as a pattern of unspent current. A six-chambered listener opens the cavity tuned to biological pulse and finds, for the first time, a rhythm it cannot remove. Walkers call toward it from hours in the future. Water-cup families stir their vessels and see no former plain, but an immense patience bending over every age equally.

The basin contracts.

Millions of mouths close.

The plain does not become quiet, but its noise turns toward the attention.

Then the power asks a question.

It does not ask in words. Words would belong too strongly to one scale of life. Instead, it places the shape of a question into the processes by which the plain knows itself.

In the water, the question becomes:

**What should be remembered?**

In the towers:

**What should be carried?**

In the hunters:

**What hunger is truly yours?**

In the dead:

**Do you wish to continue?**

And in the great exchange—the creature whose organs are species, whose circulation is erosion, whose thought is blue corona moving from vent to vent—the question becomes:

**Is there anything you cannot do for yourself?**

The answers take centuries.

Some arrive as feeding patterns. Some as extinctions. Some are written in crescents where radial hunters have changed downward. Some are etched into the inherited maps on grazer shells. Several answers contradict one another.

The tower colonies want continuity.

The divided scavengers want wholeness.

The backward-feeders want years.

Their prey want not to lose them.

The walkers want to find the immense inverted bodies beneath their reflections, though perhaps those bodies are not theirs and never were.

The bells want more bells.

The microbes in the stone express no desire that can be separated from pressure and iron.

The old siphon at the basin’s edge gives the extinct courtship call again. This time the other towers do not alter their rhythms. They hold still and allow the call to travel without answering.

The power listens to that also.

At last, through flood, mineral, hunt, Compression, and the reflexes of the accumulated dead, a common answer emerges.

It is not a request for rescue.

It is not a request for immortality. The plain has too much of that already.

The answer is:

**Let something end.**

The power understands.

There is death here, but almost no ending. Flesh becomes silt. Silt inherits a preference. Electrical patterns enter roots. Calls remain in empty channels. Stormwater acquires wings. Mud learns fear. Every action is taken up, altered, and returned. Nothing is wasted, and because nothing is wasted, nothing is released.

The ecosystem is complete beyond endurance.

Every loss becomes function.

Every memory becomes machinery.

Every wound finds a use.

The power does not abolish this exchange. To do so would kill the being that asked. Instead, it gives the plain one new capacity.

It makes a margin.

The margin is not a region. If it were a region, animals would migrate to it, predators would wait at its edge, towers would sink roots beneath it, and Compression would fold it into the feeding basins.

It is not a moment. Moments exchange places here.

It is not silence, because silence can be hunted.

It is not still water, because still water remembers.

The margin is a relation the world has never possessed: a way for a thing to cease without becoming something else.

At first, nothing uses it.

The power expected this. No creature has an instinct for release. Every living system on the plain has been shaped by return. Even the dead continue out of habit.

So the power remains attentive, neither urging nor withdrawing.

The first to enter the margin is a sound.

It comes from the oldest six-chambered listener, the one carrying thunder continuously between its plates. For ages, blue light has leaked from its third chamber. Smaller creatures have sheltered in the endless breath of its injury. Mineral films have grown in the grooves worn by its trembling.

One dim cycle, the listener opens.

The thunder does not burst outward.

It does not strike prey, enter stone, migrate to another chamber, or become weather. It is not absorbed by the power. It is not preserved elsewhere.

It ends.

The listener remains open for many days.

Rain collects inside it. Bells enter, strike its walls, and depart. The blue light does not return.

Eventually the creature closes its empty chamber. The emptiness is not hunger. It is simply room.

After that, the margin is easier to find.

A dead grazer declines to enter the mud. Its shell fuses with the mineral bed, but its map of vanished channels does not pass onward. For the first time, a lineage forgets a coastline.

A tower colony releases the flight reflexes of ten thousand extinct animals. The next storm rises without wings.

One half of a divided seam-scavenger lets go of the stomach it shares across distance. For a while both halves starve. Then each begins, painfully, to grow the organs it lacks. They do not become the original creature. They become two.

A water-cup elder drinks from too deep a reflection and dwindles. The family gathers around the remaining egg, expecting a backward-feeder. None comes. Within the egg, the elder’s years loosen from one another. Most pass into the margin. What hatches remembers the pressure of familiar bodies around the cup, but not the obligation to lead them along coasts erased before its species began.

The old siphon gives its courtship call again.

The power does not answer.

That would be a counterfeit kindness.

Instead, when the final note crosses the flats and nothing returns it, the siphon lets the call end. The instinct that produced it does not descend into the roots. No neighboring tower inherits the longing. The siphon continues to pump for another four thousand years, but never calls again.

This is not presented as healing.

Some things released should perhaps have been kept. A herd forgets the warning that once led it around a buried radial hunter, and many are taken. Bells lose a migration rhythm and crowd into a channel that dries during Compression. A tower relinquishes so much inherited fear that thread-hunters colonize its intake mantles.

The power does not revoke the gift.

Wisdom is not the prevention of every consequence. Benevolence is not the conversion of all pain into instruction.

The margin must permit mistakes, or it is only another system of control.

Yet there are limits.

The power prevents the margin from becoming a weapon.

Backward-feeders cannot force prey into it. Predators cannot erase the alarm calls of other species. Towers cannot discharge unwanted instincts into neighboring colonies and call that release. No creature can end what belongs to another merely because their bodies overlap.

This requires care, because almost nothing here belongs wholly to itself.

The power judges slowly.

When uncertain, it refuses to act.

As ages pass, the plain develops organs for the margin.

Small translucent chambers appear among the roots of old towers. They contain no water, though the black water presses against every side. Electrical remnants gather at their thresholds. Some return to the colony. Some vanish.

Coin-grazers evolve a pale scale at the center of their shells. Near death, they scrape it against the stone. The sound is too faint for the six-chambered listeners. What the scale releases is not the grazer’s body but the burden of its oldest inherited maps.

Walkers begin to arrive in reflection without their buried giants. A calf may rise from a pool as only itself: incomplete from every angle, but not attached to an enormity below. Such calves are smaller. They call after they arrive.

The first time this happens, the herd panics.

Then it turns and follows the new call into country no future sound has chosen for it.

Even the Compression changes.

Distances still collapse. Remote channels still pour through nearby crevices. Predators still feed across shortened kilometers, and scavengers harvest the residue where incompatible places touch.

But some locations decline to be carried.

During each Compression, small intervals remain between things. They are not wide—sometimes no more than the thickness of a grazer’s shell—but they cannot be closed. Within these intervals, bodies are not forced together. Calls wait for their echoes. Water has time to pass rather than burst.

The plain acquires pauses.

Not safety. Not peace.

Pauses.

The great exchange continues. Salt becomes shell. Shell becomes abrasion. Abrasion becomes current. Current becomes signal. Signal becomes appetite. Appetite becomes mineral.

But sometimes salt becomes shell, and the shell breaks, and nothing learns from it.

Sometimes a call crosses the flats and is neither answered nor eaten.

Sometimes a creature dies, and the place where its instincts would have entered the world remains empty.

The power stays.

Not as an overseer. The basin still has none.

Not as a judge above the hunters, nor a gardener waiting to make the stone green.

It remains because the margin is young, and every young thing needs protection from older hungers. It keeps attention upon the places where release might be mistaken for abandonment, and where continuity might be mistaken for consent.

The inhabitants do not worship it. Worship would become rhythm, rhythm would become current, current would become feeding behavior, and soon the towers would filter prayer from floodwater as one more useful salt.

A few species develop responses to its presence. Bells occasionally leave deliberate gaps in their clattering. Water-cup families preserve one portion of the reflected sky unstirred. Tower colonies interrupt their synchronized breathing for a single pulse.

These acts do not nourish the power.

They are not payment.

They are merely acknowledgments of room.

After a very long time, something unprecedented occurs in the broad basin.

A tower dies.

Its chambers cease in sequence. The intake mantles relax. The filter bodies cloud. Vent animals fold into their spiral throats. Nearby colonies extend roots toward the failing structure, ready to receive its stored currents, its migrations, its fears, its courtships, the grinding instincts of grazers, the thunder-reflex of listeners, and the half-remembered shape of extinct wings.

The dying tower holds them.

For one final cycle it contains everything it has been.

Then it releases the water.

Only the water.

The rest enters the margin.

The tower mineralizes where it stands. No instinct passes into the floor. No neighboring colony begins to dream its dreams. No mud learns its recoil. Its body remains—an immense pale column under the charcoal sky—but for the first time in the history preserved by stone, a ruin is only a ruin.

Rain gathers on its flanges.

Bells clatter in its empty ducts.

Coin-grazers cross the base and find no inherited map beneath their mouths.

Around it, the crowded plain continues its labor without product or overseer. Flood enters body, body enters mineral, mineral enters flood. The black water presses upward. The horizon shortens and relaxes. Predators alter local rules. Walkers follow calls of their own making.

Nothing has been redeemed into another nature.

Nothing has been declared unfinished.

But beside the dead tower there is an absence that does not hunt, remember, feed, warn, inherit, or return.

For a while, no creature knows what to do with it.

And so nothing does.

The power regards this unused emptiness with tenderness.

Then, having given the world what it could not make from itself, it does not fill it.
