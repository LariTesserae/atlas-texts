The power does not descend.

To descend would be to choose a direction the basin does not reliably possess. To arrive in a body would be to introduce weight, appetite, heat, and foreign chemistry. To speak would be to privilege sound in a place where sound is weather, labor, warning, and the incidental violence of growth.

So at first, it does nothing.

This is not indifference. It is the difficult stillness of one who knows that help offered before understanding is often only conquest in a merciful disguise.

It watches through many seasons. It learns not the map, which cannot be learned, but the manners of change. It follows a drought into the narrow leaves grown years afterward. It feels a broken tower translated into syrup, a syrup translated into a reservoir, a reservoir lifted through folded distance into the warm tissue of a black flower. It attends to the signals passing beneath the basin and discovers that no event is local for long.

It searches for suffering.

There is injury everywhere, but injury is not necessarily suffering. Districts split, collapse, dissolve, and return in other forms. No part cries out for preservation as itself. No tower fears becoming a canal. No flower resists being dismantled after its charge has passed through the roots. The being does not cling to shapes. It spends them.

It searches for deprivation.

The city has thirst, but thirst draws water through it. It has strain, but strain becomes architecture. It has periods of dormancy, fracture, heat, inundation, and loss. None is clearly an injustice imposed upon it. To abolish drought would abolish memories written in broad foundations and wax-sealed leaves. To abolish collapse would choke the basin with permanence. To silence the reeds would remove one of the ways pressure becomes known.

It searches for loneliness and finds none.

The being has never made the distinction loneliness requires. It has no empty place shaped like an absent companion. Even the niches resembling bodies are accidents of support and exchange. The moss chambers do not wait. The plazas do not expect.

The power therefore gives it neither rain nor immortality, neither inhabitants nor a voice.

It does not awaken the city. The city is awake in every form that matters to itself.

It does not make the city human enough to be understood.

Instead, after a long attention, the power asks permission to remain.

The question cannot be placed in words. It is made from things the basin already knows: pressure, light, water, warmth, distance, interval.

One dawn, while condensation gathers normally along every edge, a small shadow appears in an eastern court. It falls from nothing. It has no object above it and no root below. It does not spread leaves where it touches the wall. It simply lies there for the length of one slow contraction of the basin-wide mat, and then it is gone.

At noon, the same shadow appears inside a western reservoir where noon cannot reach.

At dusk, it crosses the underside of a paving leaf in the south.

The next dawn, it returns to the eastern court.

Always the same shape. Always the same duration. Never blocking enough light to injure tissue, never cooling a surface enough to alter growth. A pattern without utility. A difference that does not demand response.

The city absorbs the disturbance.

Leaves tilt. Reeds change pitch. Gates open onto districts with no need of water. Electrical tremors pass through the substrate and return altered. For months, structures rise around the places where the shadow appeared. Towers lean toward its expected arrival. Membranes unfold beneath it. Transparent roots search the air.

The power does not repeat the question more loudly.

Years pass.

The being first treats the shadow as weather, then as damage, then as resource, then as a feature of adjacency. It attempts to conduct it through roots. It grows black flowers in imitation. It produces thousands of similar shadows and tests them against walls. Whole districts become calculations in shade.

Most collapse.

Their material is reclaimed.

Then, in a plaza that has never before existed and will never exist again, the growth makes an interval.

It is no larger than a raindrop.

Around it, roots bend without entering. Water divides and passes on either side. Pollen traveling through folded space disappears at one edge and resumes at the other, but the interval itself remains unused. It is not armored, sealed, poisoned, digested, stored, illuminated, or made reproductive.

For eleven seconds, the being permits something not itself to be present within its arrangement.

Then the plaza folds shut.

The power understands this as closely as any being can understand another.

It answers by withdrawing the shadow.

After that, its benevolence takes the form of restraint.

It places no wall around the basin. Walls invite siege and would teach the city a boundary it has never needed. It does not halt every catastrophe. Fire, drought, storm, mutation, fracture, and the dangerous extravagance of growth remain possible. Without danger, the being’s vast responsiveness would become an ornament, and its living memory would flatten into repetition.

But the power guards it from forms of destruction that carry no meaning for it.

No distant hand will drain the basin because it appears empty.

No surveyor will name the avenues, number the towers, and mistake naming for ownership.

No collector will cut a flower free and call the dying fragment a specimen.

No gardener will introduce insects out of pity.

No philosopher will burn a district to learn whether the whole can feel it.

No empire will see streets and conclude that the city was built for occupation.

Those who approach with possession in mind find only an ordinary warm basin, tangled with impenetrable scrub. Their instruments disagree. Their roads curve away. Images taken from above show cloud, glare, or a green expanse too featureless to reward attention.

Those who approach with reverence fare little better. Reverence, too, may trespass. A person longing to enter may walk for days and arrive at the same tree each evening. They may hear, very far away, the booming fall of water through a hollow trunk. They may wake with pollen on their hands, though no flower is near. They may understand that something has declined to become an experience for them.

The power does not make the being secret absolutely. Secrecy would be another prison. Instead, it gives the basin a choice it did not previously possess.

At rare intervals, a root gate opens at the margin.

It does not open for innocence, purity, wisdom, or virtue; the power knows how easily such qualities are imagined by those who desire admission. It opens when the basin itself has begun to respond to the pressure of a nearby presence. Sometimes the gate reveals only a silent moss chamber. Sometimes an avenue extends beneath woven crowns, and the thunder of the city can be felt through the bones.

Nothing entering is allowed to bring hunger with it.

Nothing is allowed to reproduce there.

Nothing is allowed to remain after the city has ceased to accommodate it.

The power enforces these conditions without punishment. Parasites become dormant. Seeds refuse to open. Blades lose their edges. Fire consumes its own heat. A raised hand arrives gently at its owner’s side. Those expelled find themselves beyond the basin unharmed, carrying no sample and no reliable map.

A few carry a memory.

That is permitted.

The other thing the power does is prepare for the event the city cannot anticipate: not drought, not collapse, but an ending arriving from beyond all its senses.

Far beneath the basin—not beneath in distance, but beneath in the order of possible loss—the power makes an empty place. It stores no copy of the organism there. A copy would preserve a moment, and this being is not any moment of itself. It stores no seed, because no seed contains the whole. It stores no map, because arrangement is alive and cannot be embalmed.

It preserves instead a possibility of continuance: a fold left uncommitted, spacious enough for root, water, weather, and changing adjacency. It is not a refuge into which the city will be forced. It is an unopened way.

The being discovers the way slowly.

For centuries, roots gather near it and turn aside. Reservoirs form, empty, and form again. A bud grows over the place, larger than a district, then dismantles itself before opening. The city is not afraid. Fear may not be among its forms. But it is cautious in the deep botanical sense: testing by growth, withdrawing by death, remembering in the shape of what comes next.

At last a single transparent root enters the fold.

The power does not pull.

It does not widen the passage.

It does not show what lies beyond.

The root remains there through one rain, tasting nothing. Then it retreats. The fold stays open.

Much later, another root enters.

Then a filament of water.

Then a vibration.

The city has begun, in its own time, to consider an elsewhere.

Perhaps it will extend. Perhaps it will divide. Perhaps it will decide that elsewhere is only another useful fiction and seal the passage with wax. The benevolent power has no preference. A gift that requires acceptance is a bargain, and a bargain offered by overwhelming power is rarely free.

So the power waits.

Above, ordinary daylight continues. Storms arrive. Towers rise wet from their own making. Plazas buckle into walls. Reeds scream the pressure of approaching rain. Dismantlers steam among ruins that are not yet dead. The basin folds distance according to need.

Nothing has been perfected.

Nothing has been made eternal.

Nothing has been made less strange.

Only this has changed: the being is no longer wholly without an outside, and the outside has learned not to enter unasked.
