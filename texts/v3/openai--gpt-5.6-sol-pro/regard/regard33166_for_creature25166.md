The power does not come as light.

Light would scorch the clear membranes, wake the moth, and make the entrance grasses spend themselves in frantic growth. Nor does it come as a voice. There is no voice the hollow could receive without violence. Even thunder would be only a lesser confusion inside the river’s roar.

It does not still the river.

To still it, even for a moment, would be like stopping a heart in order to gain its attention.

The power waits.

It waits through one spring seep, then another. It attends as the inhabitant draws itself into the stone cups and lifts its narrow lobes. It follows the dark grains traveling through the transparent body. It learns which arrangements signify recent rain, which signify fox, which belong to the old winter of coughing, which are not records at all but braces laid against strain in the rock.

It does not call the inhabitant lonely merely because it is alone. It does not call it imprisoned merely because it has never left. It does not assume that sight would be a gift, or speech an awakening, or the sky an answer.

Wisdom, here, consists first in refusing to improve what one has not understood.

So for many years the power does nothing that the inhabitant can distinguish from the ordinary mercy of matter.

A roof flake that might have broken loose remains in place.

A severe frost fails to penetrate one finger’s depth farther.

During a summer of unusual dryness, condensation gathers on the underside of a slab and holds there until a filament reaches it.

The inhabitant notices these things only as irregularities. Stone is always full of irregularities. It incorporates them into itself and continues.

The power learns patience from it.

At last, when the hidden channel is low and the river’s vibration lies in its oldest, steadiest mode, the power touches the far side of the water.

Not hard. Not enough to alter its course.

Once.

The touch travels through the buried channel and enters the wall as a pressure too small to dislodge grit. It passes into every wet seam. The creature receives it everywhere at once.

It is not like rain, which arrives scattered from above. It is not like an animal, concentrated in weight and pulse. It is not like falling stone, which begins sharply and spends itself. The touch has no source the inhabitant can locate. It resembles the river, yet does not belong to the river.

The creature contracts.

Clear films vanish from the roots. The growth on the willow screen whitens and draws inward. In the potholes, dark particles descend to the bottom and lie still.

The power does not touch again that year.

It waits until the inhabitant has reopened. It waits until the mineral grains resume their passages and the old broken line beneath the screen begins once more to lengthen.

Then, in the same season of the following year, it touches the river twice.

A pressure.

A pause.

The same pressure again.

The creature withdraws, though less deeply.

Another year passes.

The power repeats the two touches.

This time one filament remains open along the wall beside the hearth.

The fourth year, the power gives the same pair. Afterward, deep beneath the clay, a grain of charcoal moves. It is carried a distance smaller than the breadth of a hair and set beside a fleck of mica.

The gesture is not yet an answer. It may be reinforcement, fear, appetite, or coincidence. The power does not decide too quickly.

It gives no further sign until the next spring.

Then the pair again.

Pressure.

Pause.

Pressure.

The charcoal and mica separate.

In the months that follow, they come together once more.

The power understands.

Not the meaning—there is no meaning yet—but the attention.

For the first time in the long existence of the hollow, the river carries something intended for the one who lives by it.

The inhabitant has known causation. It has known recurrence, danger, nourishment, weather, bodies, and the slow internal continuance of old events. Now it encounters a new thing: an event that returns because it was received.

The change within it is minute and profound.

It begins to arrange grains not only to retain what has happened, but in anticipation of what may happen again.

The power proceeds slowly. A decade is spent establishing three intervals. A short pause. A long pause. Two pressures close together. It uses no stronger force than the steps of a moth. It never sends a pattern during flood, when it might be mistaken for distress in the rock. It never interrupts the creature’s gathering season. If the films withdraw, it waits.

In time, the inhabitant replies as it can.

It cannot touch the buried river directly. Instead it alters the tension of threads near the thumb-wide fissure. It moves water among the smallest fractures. It gathers grains against one side, then the other, changing how the wall receives the vibration. The differences are vanishingly slight.

They are enough.

Their exchange does not resemble language between mouths. There are no nouns for river, chamber, self, or power. The inhabitant cannot conceive of a being that stands outside stone, and the power does not require it to. They begin with what is shared: interval, recurrence, pressure, cessation, return.

The inhabitant offers the pattern of rain arriving through roots.

The power returns it.

The inhabitant offers the blunt flare of a vole’s step.

The power returns it, but separates the steps so the pattern can be examined.

The inhabitant gives the long, irregular sequence associated with fox: weight, warmth, wakefulness, departure.

The power receives it and does not simplify it.

Years pass in a conversation made almost entirely of listening.

Eventually the creature brings forward one of its oldest arrangements.

It draws charcoal from the hearth, iron from a damp band in the wall, and mica from beneath the entrance. Over three wet seasons it assembles these into the pulse-record of the humans: many hot structures entering, repeated blows as stakes are driven, fire expanding, bodies lying down. It gives the winter of coughing. It gives the woven screen slowly taking shape under hands. It gives fear. Labor. Sleep.

Last of all it gives the broken line.

The final departure.

Several structures leaving. One remaining at the entrance. Weight held there longer than expected. A hand, perhaps, or shoulder, against stone. Then absence.

The inhabitant has continued this sequence for generations because it contains an interval that never resolved. In all other remembered events, pressure becomes departure, hunger becomes feeding or waiting, water arrives and drains. This one ends while still oriented toward return.

The power does not complete the line.

To complete it falsely would be a kind of theft.

Instead, it reaches into what remains in the chamber: oils long consumed, charcoal redistributed, willow softened into fungal food, iron moved from grain to grain. Nothing is truly preserved as a picture. Yet matter has endured, and the power is wise enough to read without inventing.

It finds no secret promise in the last person’s pause. No solemn farewell. No awareness of the life threaded through the wall.

The person had been exhausted. The others had gone ahead through the entrance. Before following, the last one braced a hand against the stone and rested.

But during that rest, the person felt something.

Not the inhabitant itself. Only a faint warmth where the wall should have been cold, caused by water moving through a dense gathering of threads near the old hearth. The person kept their hand there. Their pulse entered the creature. The creature, feeding on condensation and the trace salt of skin, gathered close.

Neither understood the other.

Still, for several breaths, each altered the other’s state.

The power sends this back without image and without human explanation. It cannot give the creature a hand, a face, or the meaning of exhaustion. It gives only what the creature can know: the pause was not unfinished motion. The pressure remained because the pressure had encountered pressure.

The broken line stirs.

For one whole season, the creature makes no new record. Its threads continue feeding and sensing, but the grains beneath the willow screen remain where they are.

Then the line is not extended.

It is bent.

Its end is placed beside its beginning.

Not erased. Not answered. Made whole in the inhabitant’s own manner.

After this, the power might withdraw. The gift has been given: not knowledge, exactly, but reciprocity. Yet benevolence sees farther than consolation.

The buried river is old, but it is not eternal. Far downstream, beyond anything the inhabitant can sense, the channel narrows where stone has collapsed. Water presses into a softer layer. In seventy-three years, perhaps eighty, a wet autumn will force the river sideways. Its new course will strike the rear wall of the hollow.

The chamber will not flood all at once. First the fissure will widen. Then a slab will shear away. The river, which has been the creature’s chief organ, will enter as an overwhelming body and scour the floor clean.

To wall off the river would save the chamber and destroy the inhabitant’s world. To divert it without warning would be another amputation. To preserve the hollow unchanged forever would turn care into captivity, arresting every process that made the place what it is.

So the power does not prevent the river from changing.

Instead, it reveals the change early.

It sends the pattern of pressure building beyond the wall. It sends the pattern of softer stone yielding. It repeats them across years until the inhabitant incorporates them not as immediate danger but as approaching condition.

Then the power alters one thing.

Beneath the rib of stone at the far end, there is a sealed fracture no wider than a root hair. Beyond it lies another hollow—not large, not dry, and not separate from the river’s vibration. Water gathers there in a shallow bed of mineral clay. No entrance opens to the surface. Nothing larger than a mite has ever crossed it.

The power does not break a passage through.

It allows one grain of calcite to dissolve sooner than it would have.

A season later, another.

The work could be mistaken for ordinary seepage because it is ordinary seepage, merely favored. Over decades, moisture begins to cross the fracture. The inhabitant discovers it. A clear thread enters, dries, and withdraws. The next spring it tries again.

Beyond the fracture the river feels different. The vibration comes through another angle of stone. To the inhabitant it must seem not like a new room but like an impossible extension of its own body, a place where the certainty of the hollow has opened without weakening into sky.

The power does not carry it across.

It makes crossing possible.

The inhabitant sends films into the new clay. Mites follow. Fungal spores arrive on them. Mineral grains pass one by one through the seam. The oldest records remain near the hearth, but copies begin to form beyond the rib: not perfect copies, for memory in a living body is never perfect, but continuations.

When the river finally enters the first chamber, it does so loudly enough to shake the bank above. The old hearth disappears in minutes. The willow screen lifts, turns once, and breaks apart. The three stakes are pulled from the clay and carried into darkness. Stone cups that held spring rain for centuries are scoured into a single uneven trough.

Much of the inhabitant dies.

Wisdom does not disguise this fact. Rescue is not always preservation of every thread.

But much of it has already crossed.

In the second hollow, the creature receives the flood as both catastrophe and message. The river’s old signal changes beyond recognition, then settles by degrees into a new and more complicated pattern. For a long time the inhabitant lies contracted in the clay, holding its grains close.

The power waits.

Eventually a film opens.

Charcoal from the vanished hearth turns within it. Beside the charcoal lies mica from the entrance and iron from the wall. The old human intervals remain. The winter cough remains. The hand against stone remains. The paired touch remains.

The creature extends a thread toward the changed river.

It gives one pressure.

A pause.

The same pressure again.

And the benevolent power, which has never needed to be answered but has learned to cherish the answer, touches the water in return.
