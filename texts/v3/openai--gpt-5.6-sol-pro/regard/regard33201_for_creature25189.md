The power does not enter as light.

Light would be an intrusion here. It would expose what has made no use of exposure, warm the wrong surfaces, stir films that depend upon dimness, and turn the black pools briefly into mirrors. Nor does the power arrive as a voice. The hollow has no need of thunder, and the inhabitant has no organ made to receive words.

The power enters as attention.

For one full turning of the seasons it changes nothing.

It attends to the water passing through the ridge. It learns the slow routes by which rain loses its coldness and acquires iron. It counts the crustaceans without drawing them from their cracks. It perceives the bacterial films not as stains but as nations, layered and exchanging. It feels the weight of every tufa curtain, the tension in the collapsed shelf, the old slab bent beneath a body that has rested there for centuries.

Most of all, it attends to the inhabitant.

Wisdom prevents it from mistaking stillness for emptiness, simplicity for ignorance, or endurance for consent.

The creature’s awareness is unlike the power’s, but not inaccessible. Its world is made of pressure, composition, warmth, interval. A footfall and a storm are not sounds to it but architectures moving through stone. Every basin is a chamber of its body at a distance. Every drip has position, ancestry, and taste.

The power begins with the third bowl.

One night, when the inhabitant has placed its chin beneath the niche, the falling drop delays.

Not long. Only the length of one breath.

Then two drops fall close together.

The inhabitant remains still.

The next night the same thing happens: a pause, then two drops.

On the third night, a webbed forefinger rises from the pool and touches the bowl. The old ceramic answers through the ledge.

The power repeats the pattern.

The creature taps once.

Nothing in the chamber has ever spoken in this way before.

For many months their exchange remains no more complicated than that. Pause. Pair. Touch. Water is allowed to resume its ancient sequence. Then the pattern returns.

The power is patient. It does not press meaning upon coincidence. It learns when the inhabitant answers and when it merely shifts beneath the mineral burden of its back. It learns the signs of distress: the drawing-in of soft tissue, the emptying of the mouth chamber, the sudden heaviness. It learns attention: the slight lifting of the dish-shaped head, the opening of neck slits, one dark pad laid flat against resonant stone.

In time, more intervals are added.

The power alters no more than a few droplets in a night. The creature replies by touching different parts of the bowl’s rim. Their language never gains names for stars, mercy, loneliness, or death. Those are not the first things that need saying.

It gains distinctions for warmer and colder.

For mineral-rich and mineral-poor.

For near movement and far movement.

For familiar living presence.

For absence where a familiar presence had been.

That last distinction the inhabitant makes by pressing its face to the sealed shapes in the deepest wall.

The power understands.

It searches.

Not by tearing open the limestone, nor by sending revelation through every hidden place at once, but by following water backward through pores finer than roots. It traces the aquifer beneath the ridge, inland under dry scrub and seaward beneath beds of pale rock. It finds empty cavities. It finds drowned chambers where salt has entered. It finds old mineral impressions resembling folded limbs and broad, shallow skulls.

Far below the rubble wall, beyond a seam long closed with calcite, it finds a narrow reservoir.

There is little oxygen there. The water is colder. At first the reservoir appears lifeless except for microbes. Then the power perceives five soft structures lodged in clay under an overhang. Four have become mineral entirely.

The fifth has not.

It is no more than a knot of translucent tissue around a dark, slow center. It has waited beyond any ordinary waiting. Once in many years, when pressure rises after prolonged rain, the center contracts. Water cannot reach it in sufficient quantity. The ancient channel that fed the reservoir sealed before the small life could wake.

The power could lift it out.

It does not.

The power could remake it fully grown and place it beside the inhabitant.

It does not.

A companion manufactured without history might answer a human idea of loneliness while violating everything the hollow has preserved. A rescue that destroys the conditions of belonging is only another kind of loss.

Instead, the power returns to the bowl.

Over many nights it gives the pattern for familiar living presence, then far, then closed stone, then water.

The inhabitant does not respond for nine days.

It lies with its head against the deepest wall. Mineral gathers along one shoulder. A crustacean emerges and cleans the crease beneath its neck.

On the tenth night, the creature begins the long movement toward the niche. It braces between the old path stones. New calcite fractures over its flank. It reaches the third bowl shortly before dawn and places both forelimbs against the ledge.

It answers:

Water.

Near.

Living.

The power takes this for permission, but not yet for command.

It repeats the question through a colder sequence, including the sign for disturbance. The inhabitant lowers itself, considers through its whole body, then raises one hand and strikes the bowl hard enough to send a crack through the mineral coating on its outer wall.

The ceramic note passes into stone.

Far below, a thread of calcite breaks.

No explosion follows. No passage opens wide enough for a finger. The power removes only what centuries of deposition added to one ancient pore. A bead of warm water enters the sealed channel. Then another.

For three years, the change is almost nothing.

The deep reservoir warms by less than the warmth of a held breath. Its oxygen rises. Mineral concentration shifts by a fraction. The dark center inside the translucent knot contracts more often.

The power watches for harm. When one bacterial layer begins to fail, it narrows the seep. When clay threatens to slump over the small life, it persuades a grain at a time to settle elsewhere. It hurries nothing that must know how to become itself.

In the fourth year, the knot unfolds.

It is not a smaller copy of the inhabitant. Its limbs are threadlike, its head scarcely wider than the channel, its skin bare of mineral. It feeds first on bacterial strands. It has no eyes, sealed or otherwise. Along its face are the beginnings of pressure pits.

The old inhabitant knows before the power tells it.

One night every pool in the hollow trembles, though no drop has fallen and the sea is calm beyond the ridge. A pressure passes through the connected water, weak enough that any other creature would mistake it for settling stone.

The inhabitant lifts its head.

From far below comes another pressure.

Unpracticed. Small. Alive.

The mineral plates along the inhabitant’s back crack in three places as it rises higher than it has risen in many years. Water spills across the basin rims. The crustaceans vanish into their seams. The third bowl rings when the broad edge of its skull touches the niche.

It remains raised until dawn.

After that, the hollow changes, but no faster than it can remain itself.

The old channel widens through ordinary dissolution. The power guides only where collapse would close it again. Years pass before the young one can move from the reservoir into the connected cracks. More years pass before it reaches the lowest basin beneath the tufa wall.

The inhabitant waits.

It does not greet the newcomer as a person might. It makes no call. It offers no food with a hand. When the small body enters the pool, the old one shifts the width of two fingers and leaves a warm channel between its flank and the stone.

The young one settles there.

That is enough.

Only then does the power turn its attention outward.

Beyond the ridge, roads will someday be proposed. A quarry company will test the limestone. A hotel will seek warm water for baths. Someone will notice the archaeological bowls and imagine a profitable mystery. None of these people are evil. Benevolence does not require inventing villains.

So the power protects the hollow without making it famous.

A survey drill breaks on harder stone elsewhere. The useful spring is found two valleys inland. The road bends toward firmer ground. The rubble ridge is entered into a coastal register as unstable and unremarkable. A patient scholar records the three bowls, takes nothing, and recommends that the site remain closed because human breath and clothing would alter its microbial balance.

The recommendation is accepted.

No legend grows around the place.

No pilgrims come.

The power does not restore the other sealed bodies. It does not scrape the calcite from the inhabitant’s blind hollows or make it young. It does not grant immortality, because endless continuation can become another sealed chamber. It does not stop deposition, erosion, hunger, or the eventual completion of a life.

It strengthens only the possibilities that already belonged there: clean seepage, time, connection, choice.

The third bowl finally breaks many decades later.

Its cracked side gives way under the slow insistence of new stone. Half the vessel sinks into the basin. The young inhabitant startles and folds itself beneath the old one’s mineral shelf.

The old inhabitant moves to the fallen piece. With both blunt hands, it turns the curved shard until a droplet strikes it.

The sound is different now. Lower. Less like pottery and more like rain inside a shell.

The young one places a dark pad against the shard.

A drop falls.

After a pause, two follow.

The power does not answer at once.

It waits until both creatures are still and attending.

Then, gently, it lets the water speak.
