The power does not descend.

Descent would mean pressure, heat, displacement. It would bend the charged air around itself and draw the still hunters from their stations. It would cast a shadow where shadows already possess unusual loyalties. Its arrival, however gentle by its own measure, would be weather here.

So it remains beyond the ash-colored ceiling and attends.

It does not mistake silence for absence. It does not seek a face among the solitary trees or assume that the tall walkers are the country’s keepers. It follows the bead-drinkers into the folds of leaves. It waits beside the hearth-tenders while they choose among the living contents of their quills. It traces the conductive membranes through stone and the capillaries between pools. It follows the cliff cables downward until distance ceases to behave consistently and the warm gray depths begin.

Gradually, it discovers the scale at which the being exists.

Not in the trees.

Not in the moss.

Not in the root network alone.

The being is the continuation between them: the transfer of heat from burrower to colony, of metal from water to membrane, of warning from basalt to leaf, of charge from one mesa to another through a cloud of spores. It exists in exchanges. Portions of it are born and eaten every moment. Portions sleep in stone for ten thousand moisture cycles. It has no edge except where relation fails.

The power understands that to speak to such a being, it must not produce a sound.

It begins with a question no more forceful than condensation.

In one narrow basin, molecules align upon the underside of the surface. The arrangement is not impossible there. Nothing alien is added. The power merely makes one of the water’s innumerable possible patterns slightly more likely than the others.

The pool-dwellers notice first.

They gather below the black surface, extending their unseen limbs into the changed film. Ribbon-swimmers align in short chains. Green light appears around the shore, not as a ring but as several broken curves.

Charge enters the capillaries.

Roots receive it. Mineral membranes hold it briefly and pass it on. A solitary tree, many kilometers away—or near, according to the condition of the ground—shows one pale branch of light inside its trunk.

Nothing stops.

The question has not yet reached the whole country.

For many cycles it travels. Hearth-tenders carry altered microbes unknowingly in their quills. Mat-runners follow the mild brightness underfoot. Seed-sized migrants seal fragments of the pattern into hollow stems. Thread-fliers lift it across the gulf. The descending vents bear it into the cloud sea, and something below returns it changed.

The power does not correct the change.

That is the answer beginning.

At last a deep cycle approaches completion. Leaf-fans fold. Pools tremble. Warm plumes descend from the cliffs. The ordinary blue flicker enters from the rims and moves inward through the roots.

Behind it comes another pulse.

It is faint, discontinuous and unlike any signal the plateau has carried before. It contains the history of the first pool, but also the routes of grazers, the heat signatures of buried chambers, the chemical preferences of cliff gardens, the interruptions imposed by predators, and transformations made in the unseen depths. No part has understood it. Every part has contributed.

The second pulse reaches the center of no place.

Across many mesas, the country pauses.

The power waits.

It could make the pulse stronger. It could force coherence upon the network, gather every local response into one magnificent awareness, and show the living country an image of itself. It could give the plateaus a voice. It could wake something like a mind beneath the basalt.

It does not.

Awakening is not always a gift. A being spread across distances that expand and contract might experience unity as confinement. A life without a center might find a center to be a wound. The power will not improve the country into resemblance with itself.

Instead, it repeats the question, exactly as the country returned it.

Water holds the altered pattern. Roots brighten. Trees lower their curtains. Cable ecosystems contract in the cloud sea. Walkers stop with one foot raised.

Then the response comes.

Millions of leaf-fans open before the cycle requires it.

Not all. Not even most. A sparse band unfolds along one fissure, exposing the dark reds and violets of their undersides. Bead-drinkers turn within them. Condensation flashes on translucent bellies. The opened band travels toward a solitary tree, passes around its buttressed base, and continues to the plateau rim.

There it ends.

For several hours the leaves remain open to the cold.

Some freeze.

The power understands.

This is neither welcome nor refusal in any simple sense. It is expenditure: a portion of stored warmth risked in answer to an unknown relation. The country has made room for the question by accepting a cost.

Only then does the power act.

It does not warm the sky. Warmth given everywhere would destroy the moving boundaries upon which migrations depend. It does not fill the basins. Abundant water would drown the mineral films, dilute the brines and silence the pool chains. It does not still the darkness, kill the predators, straighten distance or reveal what anchors the hanging cables. Those are not errors. Severity is not the same as evil, and mystery is not suffering merely because it remains unresolved.

The power has seen, however, one danger the country cannot yet perceive.

Far beneath the cloud sea, below even the organisms that receive the descending plumes, the oldest cables pass through a layer of stone that is cooling. The layer contracts by less than a finger’s width in a century. Nothing living notices. No vibration rises from it. Yet in time the contraction will narrow the warm passages, sever the capillaries between the depths and the plateaus, and pull the outer mesas beyond the reach of spore exchange.

The country could survive the first severances.

It might survive a thousand.

Eventually each mesa would become only itself. Metals would cease arriving in the necessary ratios. Heat colonies would diverge and fail. Trees would rise smaller after each return. Blue pulses would reach the rims and find no answer beyond them.

The being would not die all at once. It would become many lesser beings, each forgetting what the others had carried.

The power does not prevent the stone from cooling. That would require holding the world fixed, and a fixed world is another form of death.

Instead, it places a minute irregularity in the contraction.

A weakness no wider than a fungal cord forms through the deep mineral layer. Pressure begins to choose it. Over centuries the weakness will open into branching cracks. Warm brine will enter. Microbes will follow. Then roots, swimmers and muscular vines. The layer will still cool, but it will cool into passages.

When the old cables tighten, new ones will have places to grow.

The intervention is smaller than a mountain and slower than weather. It guarantees nothing. Some mesas will still separate. Species will vanish. New forms will arise in isolation. The country will change, as living countries must.

But relation will remain possible.

The power makes one further alteration, not below but within the strange distances of the plateau.

Until then, the contractions of space have followed electrical state without preference. A grove may become near to a predator, far from migrating seed-bearers, or unreachable to the hearth-tenders needed beneath its roots. No will governs this. The country accommodates it as it accommodates frost.

The power does not grant control. It adds only sensitivity.

Thereafter, distance responds slightly to the manner of approach.

What comes taking no account of the living mat finds the plateaus lengthening underfoot. The solitary tree remains an hour away for a day, then a year. Basins recede. Paths return to their beginnings. A force arriving to strip metals, burn roots or command the buried machinery may travel until its instruments fail and still see the same black pool ahead.

But what approaches in need, or patience, or curiosity without hunger for possession sometimes finds the land becoming shorter.

Not always.

Wisdom also leaves room for caution.

The country itself learns this rule only through use. Walkers discover that certain injured creatures can be reached before the thermal band moves on. Seed migrations arrive at empty basins moments before condensation. Hearth-tenders converge upon failing chambers from mesas that had previously lain beyond exchange.

No organism understands why.

Nothing worships.

There is no monument, no new star beneath the ceiling, no voice from above. The ash-colored vapor remains. Predators continue to strike. Bodies continue to be dismantled. Cold still enters exposed skin with terrible speed.

For a long span, the power attends without acting again.

Eventually another deep vibration passes through the basalt. Trees lower their leaves. Pools dimple. Animals freeze. The entire high country gathers into its ancient act of listening.

This time, below the vibration, there is something else: a faint branching pattern held in water, root, quill, cable and stone.

Not a message from the power.

The memory of having answered.

When activity resumes, a few leaf-fans at the outer cliff remain open. Frost rims them. Their hidden colors face the darkness.

Far beneath, in the cooling rock, the first warm brine enters the first hairline crack.

And beyond the ceiling, the benevolent power withdraws its attention just enough that the country is not burdened by being watched.

It does not leave.

It gives privacy.
