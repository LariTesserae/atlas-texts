It does not put the world back together.

That is its first mercy.

A lesser power, seeing continents strewn across the dark, would mistake wholeness for health. It would gather the fragments, draw the old crust around the dim center, press mountain roots beneath plains, pour released water into basins, and name the result restoration.

The black mats would burn or drown. The metallic stalks would lose the gradients by which they feed. Gliders would find no crossings. Towers built to govern dispersal would resist, and the force required to overcome them would pass through every living plate. The inhabitants, assembled over ages for plural gravities, would be crushed beneath the single certainty of down.

The wise power sees that brokenness has ceased to be an event here. It has become a condition in which lives are possible.

So it does not heal the world into another world.

Nor does it announce itself in the sky.

It begins by listening.

This is not simple. The inhabitants’ messages are too slow for speech and too embodied for translation at a distance. Their names are pressures. Their histories depend on stone. To address them through light would be to speak only to their eyes, which they do not trust for intimate things. To enter their thoughts would violate arrangements older than many suns.

The power chooses an empty fragment, broad enough to have a horizon and barren except for a few dormant threads of black life. It places no tower there. It raises no temple. It does not mark the plain with a symbol.

It presses against the underside of the stone.

Very gently, it gives a sequence:

a lift beneath no visible limb,

a lateral pull toward no mass,

three diminishing pulses,

and then a long drag like a distant country passing.

It is not a word. It is the shape of attention.

The signal enters a seam and travels.

At first it is taken for turbulence. Listeners fold flatter against the ground. Navigators extend their fibers and search the sky for the body responsible. Communities separated by thousands of kilometers receive the same impossible pressure from different directions. Maps are brought out and strained. Old plates are consulted. Towers are watched for reflections.

The power waits.

It repeats the sequence only when the first has entirely faded. It does not increase its strength.

After several returns of the split continent, someone answers.

The answer is cautious and local. An inhabitant resting beside a field of violet glass presses one limb into a hairline crack. It gives its own signature, then the pressure-pattern for a question:

What bears you?

The power has considered many replies. It could say that no stone bears it. It could describe dimensions outside the world, or the foundations of mass, or the long curve on which the dispersed crust and its dim center are themselves traveling. All would be true and useless.

Instead it answers:

I bear my approach.

The inhabitant remains in contact with the seam for nine years.

Then it asks:

Will you become heavy?

This question passes from relay to relay. It acquires variations. Will you fall upon us? Will you choose a down? Will your presence make one direction sovereign? Are you a continent too large to see?

The power answers:

Not unless invited, and never everywhere.

Thereafter the conversation lasts for centuries.

The power learns patience from beings who believe themselves merely adapted to it. It learns not to complete their pauses. It learns that silence after a question is not absence but examination. It allows them to test its statements against conjunctions, tower-fields, dust behavior, and the testimony of storm-readers.

It submits to being doubted.

In time, they ask what it wants.

Nothing from you, it replies.

This produces greater alarm than any demand could have done. Nothing in their world acts without exchanging pressure, charge, position, mineral, memory, or risk. A thing that wants nothing may be dead, deceptive, or so immense that its appetite has no name.

The power corrects itself.

I want your continuance, if you want it.

This can be understood.

The inhabitants deliberate. They do not gather in one place; no place could hold enough of them, and any universal assembly would grant too much authority to a single arrangement of geography. Instead the question is transmitted across seams, carried in tension-maps, taken through solitary doorways, and entrusted to travelers who may not reach their destinations for ten thousand years.

Some wish the power to leave.

Some ask it to still the central field.

Some ask for water.

Some ask for the dead.

Some want the seamless towers opened and explained. Others insist that no structure should be understood faster than it can be survived.

Many ask for the lost travelers: those dark shapes passing alone in orbits that never draw close enough to land.

The power begins there.

It does not seize them from space. It approaches each as it approached the communities, through the medium available to a body almost wholly dormant. A minute tidal pressure touches the closed plates. It repeats at intervals. Sometimes centuries pass before a traveler wakes enough to answer.

The power gives the pressure for:

A path can be made. Do you wish to arrive?

Not all say yes.

Some have altered during their solitude. A few no longer recognize arrival as desirable. One has become host to a black mat unlike any surviving on the continents and fears that return would endanger both itself and others. Another asks not to be carried home but moved into a wider orbit where the stars can be watched without interruption. Several give no answer, and the power leaves them untouched.

Those who consent are not transported instantaneously. Their bodies could not comprehend such a passage. Instead the power makes small revisions to the emptiness ahead of them. A distant slab exerts a little more pull. A tower turns its field aside for one hour. Charged dust gathers into a thread. An orbit that would have continued for another geological age begins, almost imperceptibly, to close.

From the continents, the returns look like natural miracles.

A shape seen periodically for generations grows larger at each passage. Communities place listening limbs against the ground. Navigators calculate, distrust their calculations, and calculate again. At last the traveler enters weak local gravity, opens a sail stiff with ancient dust, and descends.

No one moves quickly to meet it.

They arrange themselves across the plain and offer their names through the stone.

Sometimes the returned one remembers.

Sometimes it bears a pressure-language so old that only inherited plates respond. Sometimes its inner tissue has divided into several patterns, each claiming continuity with the one who departed. The power does not decide which is real. It has brought back what asked to come back. The rest belongs to those who share the ground.

After the first returns, the inhabitants make a second request.

They do not ask for safe paths. A path without danger would be a different thing, stripped of information and mutual dependence. Navigators would become passengers. Shared mass would become unnecessary. The ceremonies at the margins would become gestures copied after their meaning had died.

They ask instead for paths that can be found again.

Not always. Not forever. Only that a successful crossing leave some inclination in distance—a memory faint enough to decay, strong enough that those who come after are not wholly abandoned to chance.

The power understands. This world conserves age but not geography. Its greatest cruelty is not separation; the inhabitants have made forms of love across separation. Its cruelty is that devotion leaves no trace in the interval itself.

So the power changes one small law.

There is no flash. No artifact awakens. The central void does not brighten.

But thereafter, when a living being crosses willingly from one fragment to another, space retains for a time the slightest preference for that passage. Not a road. Not even a current. A tendency.

The second crossing remains perilous, but less arbitrary. The third can be taught. If the path is neglected, it fades. If used too heavily, it widens unpredictably and must be allowed to rest. No route becomes permanent, and none belongs to those who first opened it.

Distance has acquired memory.

The inhabitants discover this slowly. For many generations they attribute repeated crossings to skill, favorable storms, or the wisdom of old maps. Then they notice that routes used for rescue endure longer than routes used for acquisition. Paths crossed while bodies share mass become more stable than those crossed alone. A traveler carrying dormant life leaves a clearer inclination than one carrying empty stone.

This is not a reward imposed by the power. It is an effect of what the crossing contains. Living arrangements press more deeply into the changed law than solitary weight.

The inhabitants name the phenomenon with a long tectonic signature. Its closest spoken meaning would be:

the interval that has begun to recognize us.

They do not name the power.

To name it would imply that its full pressure could be reproduced. Instead they refer to it by relationship: the Listener Without Ground, the Weight That Asks, the One That Did Not Gather Us.

The power does other things, but only by consent and never all at once.

It releases minute quantities of water where black gardens are failing, not as rain but bound inside minerals the inhabitants can choose to open.

It teaches no answers about the ancient structures. It does, however, mark those artifacts whose operations would erase or multiply minds beyond recovery. The mark is not visible. It is a pressure of revulsion, unmistakable to any sensory filament laid against nearby stone.

At one great ring, where thousands of memorial stones surround the absent, the power is asked whether those who entered still exist.

It looks.

Some questions even benevolence cannot answer kindly.

The ring does not lead to a place. It separates patterns from sequence and stores them without time. Those who entered are neither suffering nor living in any form their people would recognize. They might be reconstructed, but reconstruction would create beings burdened with the conviction of uninterrupted return.

The power tells the truth by stages, allowing centuries between each pressure.

Then it asks what should be done.

After long deliberation, the inhabitants choose not to open the ring. They ask only that nothing else enter unknowingly. The power bends loose dust into a permanent outward stream. The warning can be seen from every approach.

Once, during a high storm-state, a continent begins to unfold along a seam that contains one of the oldest inhabited networks. The event is natural to the dispersed world. Preventing it would transfer the strain elsewhere, perhaps into fragments whose lives are unknown.

The power does not secretly choose who will bear the harm.

It wakes the network.

It shows them the strain, the cost of intervention, and the possible directions of release. The inhabitants feel the offered knowledge through their whole bodies. They choose evacuation rather than preservation of the land. The power holds the seam for only as long as requested.

Across twelve days, the community crosses by remembered intervals. They carry black gardens, warm-stone crawlers, tension-maps, and the plates of those too dormant to wake. The last to leave are the listeners who maintained the conversation through the ground.

When they release their limbs, the continent opens.

Its plains become a shining fan. Its ridges separate into long mineral leaves. The seam that carried their names for half a million years passes into silence.

The power does not call this a victory.

It grieves with them, not by imitating their lost pressure, but by holding a gentle counterweight beneath the fragment on which they have gathered. For one full turning of the white sun, none of them needs to anchor itself.

They rest.

Ages pass.

The world remains dispersed. Mountains still turn through the sky. Dust rivers braid between worn continents. Storm-readers stand exposed beneath impossible horizons. Travelers still misjudge paths. Bodies still fail by regions, and old plates still depart into the dark.

But fewer departures are absolute.

Here and there, across gaps once crossed in love or rescue, distance develops a faint shape. Communities separated for ages find that an old conjunction opens again. A black garden reaches a country where life had vanished. Two fragments bearing parts of the same ancient settlement draw near, and the inhabitants on each side feel through the interval the beginning of a familiar name.

The wise power does not remain above them like a ruler.

It enters no tower.

It demands no worship.

Much of the time it does nothing at all, because attention is not the same as interference, and protection is not the prevention of every ending.

It listens for consent.

It keeps watch over those who fall alone.

And when, beneath the ordinary white sun, the heaps of ancient stone unfold at the edge of a continent and prepare to step outward, the power does not carry them.

It remembers with the distance.

For a moment the void inclines—not enough to command, only enough to answer.

Then they cross by their own limbs, carrying one another’s weight.
