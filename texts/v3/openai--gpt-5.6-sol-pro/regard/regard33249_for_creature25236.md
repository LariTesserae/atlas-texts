The power does not descend.

This is its first kindness.

It has entered worlds as rain, as fire, as a white animal walking unharmed through battle. It has spoken in dreams and altered the courses of rivers. But here, where a falling seed can change the timing of a pool and the wrong silence can travel backward through water, arrival would be injury.

So it remains above the worn plateau and attends.

It attends not only to the seven arches, which are the most obvious candidates for significance, nor only to the basin-wide being, which is the most tempting. It attends to every scale at once.

It feels the thread-worm’s alarm when a glider passes over the sun. It knows the pressure in the belly of the frog carrying eggs toward water that has not yet arrived. It follows the bright digestive fleck through the body of a pale crab. It experiences the dewback’s thirst, the orange hunters’ quick hunger, the old tree’s slow division between living wood and dead. It hears the delayed echo not as one sound but as every listening for which that sound has meaning.

It does not decide that predation is cruelty and abolish it. It does not decide that death is necessary and therefore disregard pain. Wisdom prevents both errors.

For a full turning of the seasons, it does nothing that the basin can distinguish from chance.

Then, during a dry dawn, it asks permission to be noticed.

The question takes the form of a single drop.

It gathers on a pale mineral seam where no condensation ordinarily forms. It swells for nine minutes. Midges rearrange themselves beneath it. A crab raises both claws. The drop falls into the smallest rock bowl, where there is scarcely enough water to cover the black fry’s backs.

The impact produces no unusual sound.

But one arch answers before the drop strikes.

The basin becomes confused.

Gliders pour from the upper fractures and wheel in both directions. Frogs call from beneath ledges, testing the returned interval. The column darkens. The crimson fungus releases its bruised-fruit odor. The dewback, still hidden from daylight, opens the ribs along its spine.

Nothing is commanded. Nothing is compelled.

The power waits.

By midday, ordinary sunlight reaches the floor. Heat moves across the stone. One by one, the animals resume their business. Yet the basin does not forget the drop. Water from the little bowl enters a hairline channel and disappears beneath the disk. Three days later it emerges from the half-rooted arch, carrying the pattern of its impact.

Again the arch answers before it arrives.

This time the frogs do not scatter. They call around the discrepancy.

The power waits.

The basin examines the question in the only ways available to it. Fry swim through continuities inaccessible to ordinary space. Roots alter their dawn pulse. The dewback grazes a crescent around the disk and leaves one patch of algae untouched. Midges assemble over the eastern ledge at night, when there is no shaft of warmth to explain them. Predators follow prey into unfamiliar pools. Many small lives are lost in the investigation. The power grieves for them without treating them as mistakes.

After nineteen days, the basin replies.

A narrow frog beneath the central shelf gives the old unanswered note: the call belonging to the extinct species, the call no living throat produces exactly.

It is imperfect. The first pulse is too short. The final harmonic breaks.

The arch that has hummed for generations becomes silent.

Then the disk returns the frog’s imperfect note unchanged.

This has never happened before. The devices correct, delay, divide, route, and transform. They do not return error faithfully. The little frog calls again. Again the disk gives back the broken note, preserving even the tremor of fatigue in its throat.

Across the basin, motion alters.

The power understands the reply.

It is not assent. It is not refusal. The being of the basin is too distributed for either to be simple. The frog wants an answer. The arch wants completion. The fry want water. The tree wants another century. The parasite in the plated animal’s old footprint wants a host. The pool wants nothing, or wants only according to a meaning older than desire. The basin-wide arrangement wishes to continue, but parts of it continually wish things incompatible with continuation.

Its reply means:

**You may answer, if you can answer without replacing us.**

So the power does not restore the extinct frog.

It could draw the vanished form from a fragment of ancestral blood, shape its translucent belly, place the lost call intact in its throat, and set breeding pairs under the ledges. But the lost frog belonged to a world of predators, parasites, temperatures, rivals, and failures that no longer exists. To return the animal alone would be to create a living key and call that mercy.

Nor does the power repair the ancient devices.

It sees what they were.

They were not made for the basin. Long ago, before the ravine had worn so low, they formed the sensing edge of something vast: an instrument spread through many mountains, using watersheds to measure events for which the present world has no name. The instrument’s makers considered living systems useful because life repeats with variation. They seeded no species, but they built conditions into which species were drawn. When the greater instrument failed, this fragment continued, asking local creatures to perform functions whose purposes had vanished.

Over ages, use became dependence. Dependence became partnership. Partnership became anatomy.

There is no clean line at which the machine ends and the basin begins.

The power could free the animals by breaking the arches, but many would die. It could perfect the arches, but that would bind generations yet unborn to the intention of the absent makers. It could leave everything untouched and call restraint benevolence.

Instead, it offers one small alteration.

Not a new note.

The ability to learn a new note.

The gift enters through no beam or hand. It arrives in the interval between the frog’s imperfect call and the disk’s faithful answer. The power widens that interval by less than the time required for a midge’s wing to rise. Within it, the basin can feel two possibilities: repetition, and acceptance.

Until now the structures have accepted only patterns anticipated by their making. All later life has been forced to evolve toward those ancient expectations. Calls may vary, bodies may change, whole species may vanish, but the arches have waited for the old forms. Their tolerance has been broad enough for survival and narrow enough for captivity.

The power does not erase the old patterns.

It makes expectation mortal.

From this day, a pattern unanswered for long enough can fade. A new pattern repeated freely can take its place. Not at once, and not without resistance. The devices may now forget, as living things forget. They may now learn badly, partially, locally. The ecology is no longer required to approximate a vanished past forever.

For several weeks, this appears to accomplish nothing.

The old arch resumes its hum. The frogs continue calling. Water passes according to inherited routes. A brood hatches into a pool two days too early and is devoured by needle-jawed nymphs. One of the silver-barked trees loses a branch in dry wind. The dewback emerges, grazes, and returns to its fissure.

Then the small frog calls its broken version of the extinct note.

The arch answers imperfectly.

Water beads along its underside.

The frog calls again. Another frog answers—not with the same note, but with a click belonging to its own species. The arch hesitates between them. Its gray surface warms, cools, and warms again.

A drop emerges.

It belongs to neither last night nor tomorrow morning. It is present water.

The drop falls onto bare stone.

Nothing drinks it. Nothing hatches. For a moment it performs no function at all.

Then a pale crab approaches and challenges it.

Seasons pass.

The basin changes, though no visitor could say precisely how. One colony of frogs abandons delayed water and lays eggs in the dawn pulse of a tree root. Midges begin gathering over an arch that had never interested them before. The black fry establish a route between two pools without using the shortest continuity. The crimson fungus releases its scent in response to beetle wings rather than the darkening column.

Some changes fail. The structures learn dangerous associations and must lose them. A cool mist comes at the wrong time and destroys an orchid flowering. Gliders teach an arch to answer an alarm cry, then cannot make it stop answering every alarm. For an entire wet season, the eastern wall echoes the wingbeat of a species that exists only as fossils in the sediment pockets.

But error is no longer merely deviation from the dead design.

It has become part of what the basin can do.

The power remains attentive. Once, it intervenes again.

Years later, people cross the plateau.

They carry hammers, coils of bright cord, specimen tubes, cutting tools, and instruments that can see differences in the stone from far above. They are not evil. Some are curious, some ambitious, one reverent, one desperate to make a discovery that will secure a life’s work. If they find the opening, their first intentions will be careful. Their tenth will be less so. News will travel faster than wisdom.

The power does not turn them aside by fear. It does not conceal truth from all minds forever.

It gives the basin warning.

For one day, every footstep on the plateau arrives below before it is made.

The basin understands disturbance as weather. It prepares as for the greatest storm in its history. Fry vanish through the arches. Frogs withdraw into wet fractures. Gliders rise and leave through the opening, filling the sky above with such numbers that the approaching people stop to watch. The dewback folds deeper into stone. Water drains from every visible bowl. The disk takes on the color and texture of ordinary gneiss. Tree roots tighten around gray matter. The arches cease refusing lichen.

When the people descend, they find a beautiful little ravine basin, unusually dry and strangely quiet.

They photograph the ferns. They identify two new beetles. The reverent one notices seven shapes in the stone and rests a hand above one without touching it. She feels warmth, though this may be sunlight.

They leave.

Years afterward, she returns alone, carrying no tools except a water flask. She sits beside the opening from dawn until evening. She hears the faint continuous buzz below and does not descend.

The power does nothing to her.

It has not made the basin inaccessible. Safety without encounter would become another kind of prison. Instead, it has given the place time to decide how it will be found.

Perhaps one day it will answer her.

Perhaps it will route a falling drop into her open flask. Perhaps gliders will circle clockwise and return an echo no human throat can imitate. Perhaps the basin will remain only a basin to her, and that too will be allowed.

The benevolent power does not stay as ruler, guardian, or hidden operator. To remain forever would bend every adaptation toward itself. Its attention, however gentle, would become the basin’s new climate.

So eventually it withdraws.

Before it goes, it listens once more to every scale.

The hunger has not ended. Nor parasitism, failed broods, drought, fear, or death. The power has not made the place harmless. Harmlessness would empty it almost as surely as destruction. But one ancient necessity has loosened. The basin is no longer only the surviving fragment of another being’s purpose.

At sunset, condensation begins.

Drops form along the mineral seams. Frogs call to locate the present. Gliders turn beneath the narrowing sky. The dewback opens its ribs. A new generation of black fry passes through water that remembers rain.

Beneath the eastern ledge, an arch waits for a familiar vibration.

The vibration does not come.

For a while the arch hums the old substitute tone. Then, slowly, it stops.

It listens to what is here.
