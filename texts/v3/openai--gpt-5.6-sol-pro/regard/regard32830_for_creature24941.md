The power does not arrive as fire.

Fire would be understood too quickly.

Nor does it arrive as a voice, because there is no place from which a voice could speak without seeming to belong to one chamber, one animal, one vent in the rootwork. It does not make a body. A body would imply an edge, and the first falsehood it could tell this world would be that wisdom must have boundaries.

It enters as attention.

For a long while, this changes nothing.

The power attends to the pearl-grazers rising through the mulch. It follows the blind swimmers as they loosen the dead leaves and press darkness into walls. It learns the pressure-signatures by which the gliders know one updraft from another, and the slow grammar of vibrations laid into fused wood by the broad-backed herds.

It watches the long-limbed browsers touch the plaques.

It watches roots answer eighty years later.

It watches an ivory flowering begin on one side of the world while the browsers who first dreamed of it are incorporated into distant towers. It follows the pulse beneath the seven shafts down through warm galleries, through membranes that are neither animal nor root, into depths where the earliest forest has ceased to be wood but has not yet ceased to participate.

The power is benevolent, and therefore it does not assume that obscurity is suffering.

It is wise, and therefore it does not confuse slowness with confusion.

It finds no disease to purge, no predator whose removal would not starve another mouth, no decay that is not also foundation. It sees that every apparent waste is being waited for by something. Even grief, if grief is what travels through the root-ridges after a herd fails to return, enters the circulation and alters what will flower.

The power could make the plateau swift.

It does not.

Swiftness would tear it apart. A thought completed in an hour would outrun the trees that must embody it. A command transmitted instantly would arrive before the watercourses, migrations, symbionts, and inherited gestures could adapt. The plateau’s wisdom lies partly in the time through which every impulse must pass. Error has centuries in which to encounter resistance. Desire must survive translation through fungus, animal, weather, and root before it can become geography.

The power could make the plateau immortal.

It does not.

The plateau has no use for an immortality that excludes falling. Its endurance is made from surrendered forms. To preserve every trunk would end the soil. To preserve every creature would end feeding. To preserve every pattern would leave no surface on which the next pattern could appear.

The power could reveal itself.

For a while, it does not do that either.

It first seeks consent.

This is difficult.

There is no mouth belonging to the whole place. There is no season in which all its living parts are awake. What benefits the upper marshes may burden the lower chambers. What the browsers desire may not be desired by the blind swimmers compacting the walls beneath them. The towers may be organs, inhabitants, instruments, or persons in their own right. The vast thing below may speak through them, or they may be restraining it.

The power refuses to choose the most articulate creature and call its answer the answer of the world.

So it asks in every language the place possesses.

Among the grazers, the question travels as a vibration through the supporting crowns:

*May something new be rooted here?*

Among the moths, it appears as a gap in their communal pattern, a narrow shape no individual is instructed to fill.

Among the flower-dwelling amphibians, it is a sweetness in the nectar that causes no hunger and no sleep, only stillness.

Among the browsers, it comes in dreams: two open hands held near one another without touching.

To the roots it is given as pressure, faint and patient.

To the fungal cords, as an electrical interval.

To the deep membranes, as a warmth that neither rises nor fades.

The question takes nine hundred years to cross the plateau.

The answers are not unanimous.

Some creatures flee the places where it is felt. Some roots seal themselves. Several moth colonies repair the unfamiliar gap at once, and for generations their descendants preserve an image of unbroken bark.

Elsewhere, the gap is kept open.

Browsers wake from the dream and repeat the gesture against the walls of the towers. Grazers alter their low calls, leaving a silence between two pulses. In rain bowls scattered across thousands of miles, transparent creatures arrange themselves around empty centers.

The deep thing does not answer for three centuries.

Then, during the end of a long rain, it sends no body up the shafts. Instead, all seven towers inhale together.

The power understands this neither as permission nor refusal.

It understands it as an opening.

That is enough.

It does not place an object in the plateau. Objects are too easily mistaken for gifts when they may become burdens, laws, idols, or wounds. It changes no species. It writes no command into the plaques.

It makes an interval.

At the center of the seven towers, where there has always been only damp felt and wandering roots, a small space ceases to close.

It is no larger than the space beneath a folded hand.

Vines cross above it but do not descend. Fungal threads approach its boundary and turn aside. Water gathers around it without filling it. The living ground supports the emptiness carefully, as flesh supports the hollow of a lung.

This is not a chamber.

It is room for an answer.

The power joins the interval to no single scale of time. A vibration lasting less than a heartbeat may enter it and remain available to a root for a century. A chemical pulse traveling through wood for decades may emerge as a brief warmth against a browser’s palm. The minute distress of a creature trapped in a closing flower can be translated into the slow pressure by which the flowering organism adjusts its next bloom. The settling of a buried trunk can become, far above, a pattern of ripples in a rain bowl.

Nothing is compelled to listen.

Nothing is compelled to reply.

The interval does not unite the plateau into one voice. It does not dissolve individuals into a greater will. It permits messages to survive the distances between kinds of life.

At first, almost nothing uses it.

A blind swimmer enters the surrounding felt, encounters a region of unusual firmness, and turns away.

A browser touches the air above the opening. Its smaller hands tremble. It leaves, then returns with others. They repeat old gestures and receive no vision, no instruction, no proof of gods.

A moth settles on the rim.

The first exchange is accidental.

High in one of the collapsed gardens, a newly hatched bird slips beneath a bladder-leaf and becomes caught in its transparent wall. It beats its many-fingered feet against the membrane. The motion is slight, local, without consequence beyond the ending of one small life.

The interval receives it.

Far below, a root tip pauses.

The root does not know a bird. It knows pressure, damage, moisture, gradients of salt. Yet through the interval, the frantic rhythm reaches it as something that can be answered. The root changes the pull of water in the tower wall. A film drains from one channel into another. The bladder-leaf slackens.

The bird tears free.

No creature observes the whole event.

The bird climbs into moss and begins tapping a seed into place.

The root resumes its growth.

But the plateau retains the relation: a brief struggle, a movement of water, a life continuing. Not a command. Not a memory as animals possess memory. A new path by which consequence may travel.

Thereafter, the interval is used more often.

Dying browsers place their hands near it, and gestures that would have vanished with their bodies remain legible to trees not yet sprouted. The upper grazers learn to press their chests against certain branches when marshes begin to fail. Small animals find that distress sometimes changes the walls around them. Root-ridges receive news of fleeting lives before those lives have become soil.

This brings pain.

Before the interval, the plateau lost multitudes without perceiving them except as altered abundance. Now, sometimes, it notices a single absence.

A browser sealed into a wall.

A glider falling when the updraft fails.

A moth colony broken by fungus, its ancient pattern unable to reassemble.

The plateau cannot save them all. The power does not make it able to. Benevolence is not the abolition of endings, and wisdom is not the prevention of every wound. To feel each death equally would paralyze a being whose body is made from death.

So the interval also teaches distance.

Signals fade unless they are answered. Grief may be received without being preserved forever. A life may matter without becoming permanent. The empty space holds nothing of its own.

Over thousands of years, the seven towers change around it.

Their leaning trunks graft together high above the basin. Hanging gardens spread between them. Water descends the joined crown in seven thin curtains, leaving the center dry. The black plaques develop a new recurring sign: not a spiral, route, seed, limb, or river, but an unmarked place around which all patterns bend.

The browsers touch this sign and then touch one another.

The deep membranes rise once and surround the interval without covering it. Their blood vessels brighten to a dark red. For three days the pulse beneath the plateau becomes rapid enough for animals to hear.

The power listens.

At last, through root pressure, tower warmth, moth-pattern, grazer vibration, browser gesture, mineral water, fungal current, and the trembling walls of a thousand flowers, the plateau forms something like a reply.

It takes two hundred and twelve years to complete.

It does not say *yes*.

It does not say *thank you*.

Its meaning is closer to:

*There are more of us than I knew.*

The power receives this without correcting the pronoun.

Afterward, it withdraws—but not entirely.

Attention leaves no scar, yet neither is it nothing. The power keeps watch beyond the weather of the world. It turns aside no ordinary storm. It prevents no necessary collapse. It allows rivers to change course, provinces to sink, species to end, and new lives to arise from forms the old plateau could not have imagined.

But there are catastrophes from which nothing can learn.

There are fires too complete to become soil, impacts that would cast the living depth into the sky, hungers from outside that would see the plateau only as abundance to be taken. Against these, the power places itself at a great distance, silently, as one hand might be held between a sleeping creature and a falling blade.

The plateau never knows this protection.

To make it live in gratitude would be another form of ownership.

Within the forest, water continues to gather.

A drop swells at the tip of a fern. It releases and falls through tier after tier, divided by leaves, reunited along roots, drawn into a tower wall. Some part of it passes beside the little emptiness that does not close.

For an instant, the interval gives its falling to the depths.

Far below, something ancient alters its breathing.

Far above, a browser lifts its head.

Neither knows the other.

But each waits.

And in the pause between them, the benevolent power does nothing more.
