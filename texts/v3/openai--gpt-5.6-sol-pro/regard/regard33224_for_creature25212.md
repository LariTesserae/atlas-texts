### What the Rain Was Given

The power did not descend.

Descent was one of the hollow’s uncertainties, and the power was wise enough not to choose a direction on its behalf.

Nor did it enter as flame, voice, wing, hand, or figure made radiant. Radiance would have altered the growth of the mineral colonies. A voice might have traveled through the walls and struck every foot at once. A hand, however gentle by the measures of larger worlds, would have crushed lakes, migrations, tools, histories.

So the power attended.

For one season of the mountain, it learned to notice at the hollow’s scale. It felt the drying of a bead as the withdrawal of an ocean. It waited through the long generations between two raindrops. It allowed itself to be divided at the hanging planes and did not insist that its divided portions remain in agreement.

One portion accompanied the heat-mites along their branching paths.

One rested beneath the card-crawlers and endured the rock.

One traveled through pores with the knot animals, emerging before it had entered.

One became thin enough to live in a reflection and was hunted there.

One watched a gray glass-worker strike a flake from the wall. When the impact passed through the mountain, the power received it not as noise but as touch.

Only then did it understand that there was indeed a being in the hollow.

Not one hidden sovereign among the multitudes. Not the largest animal, nor the oldest mineral crust, nor a mind sealed beneath the uncertain floor.

The being was the continuance itself.

It was the gesture passing from limb to limb after bodies changed. It was thirst translated into migration, pressure into tool use, danger into transparency. It was the relation between the rising water and the descending water. It was what remained recognizable when nothing retained its form.

The power could have made the hollow stable.

It could have cooled the rock, widened the entrance, gathered the water into a permanent pool. It could have corrected gravity and made distance honest. It could have prevented the summit from settling or sealed every fracture against eruption.

But stability, here, would not have been preservation.

A permanent lake would drown the walkers and imprison the swimmers in a single shape. Cooler stone would starve the heat-feeders. Honest distance would sever the knot animals. One gravity would exile half the congregation at the divided stream. If every creature kept one body, the being of the hollow would be broken into species and each species into solitude.

The power could have ended predation.

It considered this longer.

It felt the ribbon closing around shell-backs. It felt the mites drinking the ribbon’s water. It felt the glass-worker split and disperse as pale lives. Hunger was everywhere, but cruelty was not. Nothing killed to enlarge an idea of itself. Nothing stored suffering for later use. Appetite moved through the hollow as heat moved through stone, terrible nearby and impersonal at a distance.

To remove it entirely would require remaking every mouth, every mineral exchange, every cycle by which one form entered another. The hollow might become peaceful for an hour and lifeless soon afterward.

So the power did not abolish hunger.

It did not abolish death.

It did not name the being, because a name given by such a power might have become a wall around it.

Instead, the power asked a question.

It asked without sound. The question entered as a slight equality among droplets. For one instant, all the water in the hollow—rising, falling, suspended, hidden in bodies, sealed in stone—held the same temperature.

The mites stopped drinking.

The card-crawlers paused halfway through turning themselves inside out.

Needle-legged hunters felt no curvature pointing toward prey.

The knot animals tightened throughout the mountain.

Every sensory droplet along the wrists and heads of the glass-workers became still.

The question was:

**What must not be lost?**

No creature answered.

Then all of them did.

A shell-back altered its path by the width of a crystal. A swimmer struck the boundary of its bead. A ribbon opened mouths at both ends. Three gray workers, separated by folds of stone and several generations of local time, raised one forelimb at the same angle.

The mineral colonies grew while the power watched them directly.

That had never happened before.

Their clear branches spread over the basalt, following no footprint. They made a sign too large for any one inhabitant to perceive. Seen from the entrance, it resembled nothing. Seen from within the wall, it resembled a fracture. Seen across time, it was a movement repeated:

Not a plea to remain unchanged.

Not a plea to remain here.

The answer was:

**Let the gesture continue.**

The wise power understood.

For the hollow had survived many endings already. Every evaporation was an ending. Every crossing of a surface ended one body and began another. What threatened it was not death, but conclusion: the coming hour when the young mountain would cease to be young, when its heat would fade or surge, when the summit would split open, when the crack would widen to ordinary dimensions and the impossible relations inside it would be exposed, dispersed, and made merely small.

The power could preserve the chamber forever. But forever would turn refuge into captivity.

Instead, it touched the rain.

The change was so slight that no cloud altered course.

Thereafter, whenever water entered the summit crack, one droplet among the thousands failed to surrender all of itself. It evaporated, but not completely. It entered bodies, but was never wholly digested. It passed into stone, steam, shell, tooth, sensory pit, and mineral film. In every form it retained no substance that an instrument could isolate—only an inclination.

The inclination was to repeat one useful gesture elsewhere.

A mite carried the way condensation could be divided without being destroyed.

A crawler carried the knowledge of shelter made from one another.

A hunter carried the art of changing a slope rather than pursuing prey.

A shell-back carried the memory of growing stone as part of the body.

A glass-worker carried the angle of the wrist.

The droplet moved through all of them. When steam rose from the summit, a portion of that inclination rose with it.

Clouds received it.

Rain bore it down the mountain’s slopes, into lava tubes, coastal fissures, deep wells, the cracks of other peaks. Most places could not answer it. There, the water became ordinary water and the gesture slept.

But now and then rain entered young stone still uncertain of its shape.

A mineral film thickened where no life had been.

A white angular speck appeared at the edge of a bead.

A darkness between two wet surfaces peeled free and acquired a border.

These were not colonies sent out from the summit. They were not copies. The power had not made the hollow invasive, nor multiplied its every hunger across the world. What traveled was subtler: permission for stone, water, heat, and fracture to attempt the relation again.

Elsewhere, the result was never exactly the same.

In one volcanic seam, the descendants of the gesture lived only as moving reflections.

In another, no animals appeared, but the rock learned to pass warmth from surface to surface without crossing the distance between.

Far beneath an island, a population of soft gray beings worked not glass but salt. They built nothing. After using a tool, they dissolved it in water.

In a desert fault that received rain once in a century, transparent husks waited between storms. When moisture came, they stood and walked toward it.

Thus the summit hollow was not saved from ending.

It was saved from being the only beginning of its kind.

The power did one other thing.

This act was smaller and more dangerous.

It placed mercy into the hollow—not as a law, but as an available mistake.

Before, a glass-worker might cut another creature free from mineral crust because the flake happened to move that way, because trapped flesh attracted feeding, or because the gesture of cutting had survived without purpose.

Afterward, on rare occasions, an animal interrupted its own advantage.

A needle-legged hunter flattened the water beneath its feet and allowed prey to slide away.

A ribbon formed a mouth around a cluster of shell-backs, held them briefly in the clear outline of its hunger, then opened.

Card-crawlers drew a burned stranger between their layers.

A gray worker used a fresh edge to release a knot animal and did not eat what emerged.

These acts did not become universal. Had they done so, they might have destroyed the hollow as surely as drought. Nor were they rewarded. The spared prey sometimes became a predator moments later. The sheltering colony sometimes starved. Mercy accumulated no monument and founded no doctrine.

It survived as everything survived there: as gesture.

The angle of a limb.

A pressure withheld.

A mouth becoming a tail.

A choice carried across a surface into a body not yet formed.

The inhabitants did not worship the power. Most never perceived that anything had attended them. No image appeared in mineral crust. No glass-worker raised a shrine. The altered rain was drunk without ceremony.

This pleased the power.

When at last it withdrew its concentrated attention, it left no guardian at the entrance. A guardian would have made the crack conspicuous. It placed no curse on intruders and no veil against discovery.

It only arranged that anyone who looked inside with the wish to possess would see a wet fracture, too narrow and hot to explore.

Anyone who looked with the wish to understand would see movement, but would doubt their eyes.

And anyone who looked with patience, without demanding that the hollow become simple, might hear through one resting hand the impact of an obsidian flake struck far below.

They might feel, following the blow, a second vibration:

not an echo, but the same gesture taking place in another mountain.

The summit will still weather. Its heat will change. One day the throat may open to the sky or close beneath cooled stone. Its airborne lakes will vanish. Its opposing streams will find a common gravity. The last glass-worker, if there is a last, may leave a tool where no later limb will claim it.

The benevolent power did not prevent this.

Wisdom did not require the small world to last forever.

It required that its ending not mean it had been a mistake.

And so rain continues to enter the seam.

Multitudes drink. Hunters wait upon curvature. Dark colonies turn themselves inside out. Gray hands strike newborn glass. Sometimes a mouth closes while prey is still within reach.

Steam leaves the summit carrying no creature anyone could find.

Far away, on wet stone that has scarcely finished becoming stone, something without a name repeats the angle of the hand.
